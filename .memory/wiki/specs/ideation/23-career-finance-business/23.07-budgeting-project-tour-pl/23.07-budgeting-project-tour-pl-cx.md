## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Budget & Actuals Tracking ↔ Project & Tour P&L

**Relationship**: The same rows viewed at two moments. During the work, actuals-vs-budget is a **control** question ("can we afford the extra night?"). After it, the same rows are a **result** ("did this make money?"). Modelling them as two data sets would guarantee they disagree, and a tour whose live view and final P&L disagree is a tour whose members will not trust either.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: for a self-managed act the budget is a rough plan in someone's head and the actuals are receipts in a glovebox. Both halves of this are currently vapour.
- **Producer**: a recording budget is real, contractual, and recoupable against it (23.06.03) — their budget has legal weight the Musician's does not.
- **Operator**: budgets a show or a room refit; their P&L is per-event (17), not per-tour.
- **Fan**: not affected — no access to this sub-domain.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One set of rows, scoped to the project. The P&L is a **closing view**, not a copy.
2. **Trigger chain**: Expense/income lands with a project reference → actuals move → P&L derives. If the row has no project reference it is invisible to both, which makes tagging-at-capture (23.02.01) load-bearing.
3. **Permission intersection**: A band member seeing the tour budget sees what everyone else is being paid — a genuine intra-band disclosure, not a technicality.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Budget overrun is worth alerting during, useless after.
5. **State transition conflict**: Late costs arriving after the P&L closed and money was distributed (CX-02) — money already paid out has to come back from friends, which is the worst conversation in the sub-domain.

### CX-02: Project & Tour P&L ↔ Band Entity Treasury & Member Distribution

**Relationship**: The P&L's bottom line is the distributable amount. This chain — "what did we make" → "what's my share" — is the sequence that ends bands, and today both halves are guesses. The platform can make the first half a fact, which is the only way the second half becomes negotiable rather than accusatory.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the member being distributed to, and often the one who paid for the van (CX-03).
- **Producer**: read-only — frequently paid *out of* the budget, ranking as a cost above the distribution.
- **Operator**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The P&L owns the result; the treasury owns the distribution rule and the payments. A distribution must **pin** to a closed P&L version, or a late cost silently changes what everyone was told they earned.
2. **Trigger chain**: P&L closes → distributable computed → rule applies → members paid. If a cost lands afterwards, the distribution was wrong and the money is already gone.
3. **Permission intersection**: Who may close a P&L, and who may distribute? Concentrating both in one member recreates the treasurer problem the platform should be dissolving.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every member sees the same numbers at the same time — which is itself the product (23.07.03 DT-01).
5. **State transition conflict**: A member leaving between the P&L closing and the distribution.

### CX-03: Budget & Actuals Tracking ↔ Band Entity Treasury & Member Distribution

**Relationship**: The unglamorous one that actually causes the arguments. Members personally fund costs mid-tour — the drummer's card pays for the van in Leeds, the guitarist buys the strings, someone's mum lends them £300 for the ferry. Each is a **debt of the entity to a member**, ranking ahead of any profit distribution, and none of them are written down. At the end of a tour that lost money, these claims are all that is left to argue about, and everyone remembers their own spending best.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the one who paid, and the one who will not raise it because it feels petty — until it does not.
- **Producer**: not typically a member; their fee is a cost.
- **Operator**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: A member-funded cost is simultaneously an expense of the entity (23.07.01) and a receivable of the member. Recording it once and deriving both views is mandatory; recording it twice guarantees drift.
2. **Trigger chain**: Member pays → claim recorded → ranks ahead of distribution → settled or netted from their share.
3. **Permission intersection**: Every member must see every claim. A private claim against a shared pot is how trust dies.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A claim is a small social act — logging one should feel like bookkeeping, not like an accusation. That framing is the whole design problem.
5. **State transition conflict**: A disputed claim ("we agreed you'd cover the van") blocking a distribution everyone else wants.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 23.07.03 Band Entity Treasury & Member Distribution | 09 Rights & Ownership (splits) | Cross-level pair considered and **firmly** rejected (parent D-03). A touring partnership distributing trading profit and a songwriting split are different objects with different bases: one divides a night's takings between five people who played, the other divides a copyright between whoever wrote it. The same five humans, two unrelated percentages — the drummer who gets 20% of the door may hold 0% of the publishing, and that is normal, not an error. Conflating them would put touring income into the rights ledger and invite the platform to "reconcile" two numbers that were never meant to match. |
| R-02 | 23.07.01 Budget & Actuals Tracking | 23.05.01 Career Goals | Considered — a budget target looks like a goal — and dismissed. A budget is a **constraint on a specific project**; a goal is an aspiration about a career. Wiring them would import project overruns into career progress and make a bad tour look like a failed life. The domain's recurring invariant applies (see `23.06-advances-commission-recoupment-cx.md#R-02`): forecasts, plans and aspirations must never touch the records of what actually happened. |
