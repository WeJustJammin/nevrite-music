# Feature: Project & Tour P&L

> **Number**: 23.07.02
> **Parent**: [Budgeting & Project/Tour P&L](./23.07-budgeting-project-tour-pl-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Did it make money? For a tour, a record, a project — a question musicians ask constantly and answer with a shrug, because the income arrived from six places over eighteen months and the costs were paid by four people out of whatever they had. This is the closing view over the same rows the budget tracked (`#CX-01`), and its bottom line is what gets distributed to the humans (`#CX-02`), which is why being approximately right is not good enough.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds out whether the tour they just did was worth doing — usually for the first time, and often the answer is no. That fact should change the next decision, and currently cannot, because nobody has ever assembled it. |
| Producer | Full | A record's P&L against its budget, with recoupment (23.06.03) sitting on top: the project can be profitable while they personally receive nothing until points recoup. Two different questions that look like one number. |
| Operator | Reports | A **show** has a P&L for them — a promoter's settlement is a per-event P&L, and it belongs to 17 Live Booking & Settlement. A tour or a record does not describe their business; their unit is the room and the night. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no projects, no professional stake. |

## Behavior

The P&L is a **closing view** over project-tagged rows, not a new store. Closing it pins a version, because a distribution (23.07.03) must attach to a fixed number — otherwise a late cost silently changes what everyone was told they earned, after they have spent it.

### Happy Path

1. The 12-date tour ends. All costs tagged; all settlements (17) and merch (20) landed.
2. The P&L assembles: gross income by source, costs by category, member-funded claims (`#CX-03`) ranking ahead of profit.
3. Bottom line: £4,200 distributable, after the drummer's £600 van claim is settled.
4. The band closes the P&L; the version pins.
5. Distribution (23.07.03) runs against that pinned version, and every member sees the same arithmetic at the same time.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Late income or costs after closing | **The nastiest transition in the sub-domain.** Money may already be distributed and spent; recovering it means asking friends for money back | Supersede with an explicit delta and a settlement path — never silently restate a number people acted on |
| The tour lost money | The common case. A loss is distributable too — as a **liability** between members | Loss allocation is a real and much harder conversation than profit allocation, and the platform must model it rather than only handling the happy version |
| Untagged costs (23.07.01 DT-02) | The P&L overstates profit; the distribution then overpays; the money is gone | Coverage statement is mandatory. This is where a missing tag becomes a transfer between people |
| Recoupment sits on top (a Producer's points) | The project is profitable *and* they receive nothing | Both figures shown, or the number lies (mirrors 23.01.05's gross-vs-net-to-me problem) |
| Members disagree about a cost | "We never agreed to that hotel" | The P&L is evidence, not adjudication — the domain's recurring posture (23.04.04 D-01, 23.06.03 DT-03) |
| Merch income reconciles months later | Common; box counts and consignment settle slowly | Provisional close vs final close are genuinely different states |
| Currency across a European tour | FX movement is a real result, not an artefact | 23.01.03's policy applies |
| A member left mid-tour | Their share of what, computed to when? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — and it is exactly when relations are worst |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Assembling | Skeleton |
| Empty | Project has no rows | Honest |
| Populated | Rows exist | Income, costs, claims, bottom line |
| Error | Assembly failed | No bottom line rather than a wrong one — a wrong bottom line becomes a wrong payment |
| Partial | **Provisional** — income still landing, costs still arriving | The honest default for months after a tour. Distribution against a provisional P&L must warn, and closing early is the most common way money gets distributed wrongly |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The P&L answers "did we make money" and that is the value | The obvious framing | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — it answers it, but the value is what comes **next**: the bottom line is the distributable amount (`#CX-02`), and the reason it must be right is that people get paid from it. A P&L nobody pays out of is a curiosity; a P&L that pays five people is infrastructure. This reframes the feature's quality bar from "informative" to "defensible", which is a much higher one. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **most tours to lose money**, and would expect loss allocation to be the harder feature | Anyone who has toured at working level: the tour is a marketing cost for the record, or a loss carried for the exposure. Profit is the exception. Yet every P&L design assumes profit and treats loss as a red number — when the actual product question is "who absorbs the £2,000 we are down?" That is a **liability** allocation between five friends, and it is the conversation that ends bands. | ✅ CONFIRMED: loss allocation is first-class, not an error state. The feature must model a negative distributable amount as a real object with a settlement path, not paint the profit view red. This is a genuine gap in how every tool in this space works, and it is the case the personas will actually hit. |
| DT-03 | Closing the P&L is a clean event | Naive | ❌ REJECTED: music income **trickles**. Merch reconciles in weeks, settlements in months, royalties in years. There is no clean moment when a record's P&L is final — a record's costs are known long before its income is. So "closed" is a decision someone makes for a practical reason (we want to get paid), not a fact the data announces. That means provisional-vs-final is a first-class distinction, and that distributing against a provisional P&L — which everyone will do, because they want their money — is the normal case rather than the exception. The design must make that safe rather than pretend it will not happen. |
