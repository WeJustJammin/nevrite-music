# Feature: Band Entity Treasury & Member Distribution

> **Number**: 23.07.03
> **Parent**: [Budgeting & Project/Tour P&L](./23.07-budgeting-project-tour-pl-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The band earned £4,200 on the tour. Now what? `meta/personas.md` states that bands are first-class entities that "book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members" — which means a band has money, and that money has to reach humans, according to a rule the band probably never wrote down. Today this runs through one member's personal bank account, a mental note about who paid for the van, and a conversation that happens late, badly, and only once someone is already angry.

**This node was not in the candidate list.** The sweep's provisional personas included a "band treasurer"; D-19 removed that persona without relocating the capability, leaving band money with no home anywhere in domain 23 (parent D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the band's position and their own share, computed from a rule everyone agreed to when nobody was angry. Also logs the £600 they personally put on their card for the van in Leeds — a debt of the band to them, ranking ahead of anyone's profit. |
| Producer | Read-only | Frequently paid **out of** a band's budget — their fee is a cost that ranks above the distribution. They need to see the project's position without being a member of the partnership that divides what is left. Read-only is the precise expression of that: adjacent to the money, not party to it. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue pays the band and is done — the band's internal division is none of their business, and surfacing it to them would be a disclosure with no purpose. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. What a band earns and how they split it is private. This is not fan-facing content in any form. |

## Behavior

The Band entity (01) holds a **position**, not a balance: WeJammin computes and displays how the band's money should divide; it does not, in the Level-1 model, custody the funds (DT-03, Q-01). The position is derived in a **fixed waterfall order** so the arithmetic is never ambiguous:

1. **Distributable base** — the closed P&L bottom line for the project/tour (`#CX-02`), pinned to a specific closed version so nobody distributes against a moving number.
2. **Member claims (debts) settle first** — money a member personally advanced for the band (the van, the deposit, the merch print run) is a debt of the entity that ranks *ahead* of anyone's profit (`#CX-03`, D-02). Claims net against arrears where a member also owes the band.
3. **Remainder divides by the distribution rule** — captured early, ideally on day one (D-03). The rule is not this feature's to define: it is a governance term owned by [01.04.03 Treasury Mandate](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.03-treasury-mandate.md), whose Behavior fixes it to "**exactly three shapes**" (D-05, Q-02):
   - **Equal** — split N ways; remainder ÷ N members.
   - **By income share** — the per-member percentages fixed in [01.04.01 Partnership Terms](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.01-partnership-terms-decision-rules.md). A band that wants shares keyed to role or contribution — touring members vs. writers — expresses that here, as percentages; it is not a fourth shape.
   - **Manual per event** — the mandate holder proposes a split, every member sees it, and above the spend ceiling governance's threshold from 01.04.01 must clear it. A band picks one default rule, and a single inbound event may override to `manual` when the split is genuinely bespoke (a guest player on one gig).
4. **Result is shown to every member simultaneously** — the same income, costs, each claim, and each member's computed share, with no member holding a private view. Symmetric visibility is the mechanism that dissolves the treasurer role (DT-01): nobody has to be trusted because nobody can see more than anyone else.

A distribution is **not** a royalty/publishing split (D-04, rejected pair `#R-01`) — the drummer with 20% of the door may hold 0% of the songwriting, and the two numbers are unrelated. The rule is captured as **evidence at agreement time**, timestamped, so a later "we always said X" is answered by the record rather than by memory (DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. Tour P&L closes at £4,200 distributable (23.07.02).
2. The drummer's £600 van claim settles first — a debt, not a share (`#CX-03`).
3. The remaining £3,600 divides by the rule the band agreed at the start: equal fifths, £720 each.
4. Every member sees the same numbers simultaneously — the income, the costs, the claim, their share.
5. Nobody has to trust the member who happens to hold the bank card, because nobody has to.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **No distribution rule exists** | The overwhelming majority of bands. No recorded rule means no distribution computes — and the platform never guesses one | Parent Q-03 is **resolved**: neither prompt nor silence. The band is shown a plain statement of the defaults already governing them — the legal equal-shares general partnership plus the platform default mandate — because "bands do not lack governance; they lack *awareness* of the governance imposed on them" (01.04.01 D-04). Capture of their actual intent is offered, never demanded. Whether that *disclosed* default may itself drive a computed split is Q-04 |
| The tour lost money | Loss allocation: who absorbs it? A liability between friends, not a share | 23.07.02 DT-02 — first-class, and much harder than profit |
| A member paid personally | A debt of the entity ranking before profit (`#CX-03`) | Claim, not expense. Every member sees every claim — a private claim against a shared pot is how trust dies |
| A member leaves | Their share of what, computed to when? Their claims? Their ongoing rights? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — collides with 01's entity model and 23.04.01's departing-member question. Occurs exactly when relations are worst |
| Late income after distribution | Money already paid and spent (23.07.02 DT-03) | Top-up or carry-forward; never a clawback from friends |
| One member controls the money | The status quo, and the problem | Concentrating close + distribute rights in one member recreates the treasurer problem the feature exists to dissolve |
| Members disagree about the rule after the fact | "We always said the songwriter gets more" | The rule is evidence if captured early; a memory if not. This *is* the platform thesis |
| A member is in arrears to the band | Netting a member's debt against their share | Real; ordering matters, same as 23.06.03 |
| The band splits up mid-distribution | The worst case, and not rare | The record is what survives — which is the entire argument for capturing the rule on day one |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A band needs a treasurer — one member who runs the money, and the product should give them tools | The obvious model, and the sweep's own instinct (its provisional personas listed "band treasurer") | ❌ REJECTED: the treasurer **is the problem**. One member holding the card, the passwords and the spreadsheet is how bands end — not usually through theft, but through opacity: nobody else can see, so everybody suspects. The product opportunity is to **dissolve the role**, not tool it. Shared visibility over one computed position means nobody has to be trusted, which is strictly better than making one person trustworthy. This also retroactively justifies D-19's removal of "band treasurer" as a persona — it is not a person, it is a symptom of missing infrastructure. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **money to end more bands than creative differences**, and the fix to be capturing the rule while everyone is still friendly | Anyone who has been in a band, and any music lawyer: the split conversation happens when there is finally money to argue about, which is exactly when it is hardest and when memories conveniently diverge. It is the **identical failure** to the one `meta/problem-statement.md` names as the platform's reason to exist — "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable" — applied to trading profit instead of copyright. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the sharpest finding in this sub-domain: **band money is a provenance problem, not an accounting problem.** The thesis (D-18) says capture the fact at the moment it is true; that principle has been applied to credits (02) and splits (09) but nobody applied it to the band's own money. A distribution rule agreed on day one, when the band is five friends with nothing to divide, costs one conversation. The same rule agreed after a profitable tour costs the band. This is the platform's core move, and the candidate sweep missed it entirely. |
| DT-03 | The platform should hold the band's money to make distribution real | The natural completion — and the one that must be interrogated, not assumed | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and it is parent Q-01. Holding pooled funds for a multi-member entity is **custody**: safeguarding rules, e-money implications, and a platform deciding who may withdraw from a shared pot during a band's breakup. That is the same pattern this domain keeps hitting — 23.02.03 (filing), 23.06.01 (lending), 23.06.02 (routing commission) — where the maximal version requires a regulated role. **Four occurrences is not a coincidence; it is the domain's defining characteristic**, and it deserves to be a domain-level finding rather than four separate footnotes. The alternative — compute the shares, show everyone, let the money move in a real bank — captures most of DT-02's value (the *rule* and the *visibility* are what save the band, not the custody) at a fraction of the exposure. |

| D-02 | Member-funded costs are **debts** ranking ahead of profit, visible to all members | `#CX-03` — private claims against a shared pot destroy trust |

| D-04 | Partnership distribution is not a royalty split | Parent D-03 |
