# Feature: Point-of-Need Insurance

> **Number**: 23.08
> **Parent**: [Career, Finance & Business Management](./career-finance-business-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Offer cover at the moment the need becomes real: you just bought a £3,000 guitar (13), you just booked a 12-date tour (17/18), you just took a booking at a venue that requires public liability (16). Musicians are chronically underinsured — not from indifference but because insurance is bought in a separate, unpleasant errand that never rises above the next gig. The platform's genuine edge is the **gear registry (15)**: an insurer's hardest problem is knowing what you own and what it is worth, and the registry already knows.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees relevant cover at the trigger moment — instrument cover on a purchase, public liability when a venue's booking requires it, travel/equipment cover when a tour is confirmed. Their gear is their means of production and typically uninsured or covered only by a home policy that excludes professional use, which they do not know. |
| Producer | Full | Studio contents, outboard, professional indemnity (a client claiming a delivery failure). Higher values, and business rather than personal cover — a home policy will not touch it. |
| Operator | Full | Public liability and premises cover are **licence conditions**, not optional — a venue without them cannot legally open. But they already have a broker and a policy; WeJammin has no advantage here and should not pretend to. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A fan buying a ticket has no insurable professional interest. (Ticket refund protection is a 19 Ticketing product and a different thing entirely.) |

## Behavior

The platform **surfaces and refers**; it does not underwrite (that is licensed and capitalised). A trigger event produces a contextual offer with the data the platform already holds — the instrument, its value, the tour dates, the venue's requirement — pre-populated.

### Happy Path

1. Musician buys a £3,000 guitar through the gear marketplace (13); it registers in the gear registry (15).
2. A trigger fires: high-value instrument, newly owned, not covered.
3. An offer surfaces with the make, model, serial and value already filled in — the friction that normally kills the purchase is gone, because the platform knows the answers.
4. They take cover in two taps instead of a Saturday.
5. The premium is an expense row (23.02.01), auto-categorized, and the policy sits in the vault (23.04.01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The offer is wrong for them (already covered, ineligible, wrong territory) | An insurance offer at a moment of purchase excitement is a **sales interruption**, and a wrong one is worse than none | Suppressible; never nagging. The trigger must be a fact, not a guess |
| The user relies on cover they did not actually buy | Believing you are insured when you are not is worse than knowing you are not | Cover status must be unambiguous, never implied by having seen an offer |
| A claim is made | The platform is not the insurer and cannot help — but the user will come here first, because this is where they bought it | Honest routing; the referral model's real weakness, and it lands at the worst moment |
| Gear registry data is wrong (13/15) | Underwriting on wrong data voids the claim — the exact moment it matters | The registry's accuracy becomes an insurance-grade dependency, which 15 was probably not designed to be |
| Tour crosses borders | Territory limits are the classic exclusion; equipment cover that stops at Dover is useless on a European tour | Territory must be explicit; a tour trigger implies a territory question |
| Venue requires proof of liability cover | Real, common, and currently handled by emailing a PDF the night before | Proof-of-cover at booking (17) is arguably the better feature — see DT-02 |
| Instrument is vintage / irreplaceable | Agreed-value vs market-value cover is the whole argument for a musician, and generic insurers get it wrong | Specialist need; a generic offer here is worse than nothing |
| Commission/affiliate incentive | The platform earns from the referral | Disclosure. Same conflict as 23.06.01's brokering — the platform profits from the user's purchase |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Offer resolving | Should never block the underlying action (the purchase, the booking) |
| Empty | No trigger | Invisible. There is no "insurance section" to browse — the feature only exists at trigger moments |
| Populated | Trigger fired | Contextual offer with pre-filled data |
| Error | Partner unavailable | Silent. A failed insurance offer must never interrupt a gear purchase |
| Partial | Eligibility unknown | Honest; no implied cover |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is an affiliate/monetisation feature — insurance is a revenue line bolted to a marketplace | The cynical reading of the candidate, and the one that would produce a bad feature | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the tension is real. It **is** a referral business. But the platform has a genuine informational edge that is not fake: the **gear registry (15)** solves the insurer's hardest problem — proving what someone owns and what it is worth. That is a real underwriting input nobody else has, and it means the offer can be cheaper and faster rather than merely more convenient. The feature is legitimate *only* to the extent that edge is passed to the user; if it degrades to a banner ad at checkout, it is monetisation wearing a product costume and should be cut. |
| DT-02 | The strongest trigger is the **gear purchase** | The candidate's own framing ("point-of-need"), and the obvious one | ❌ REJECTED: the strongest trigger is the **venue's requirement**. Public liability cover is a condition of playing many rooms — it is not optional, not a nice-to-have, and it is currently handled by a panicked email the night before with a PDF nobody checks. A booking (17) that says "this venue requires £2m public liability; you do not have it; here it is" is a **blocker being removed**, not a product being sold. That is a categorically better feature than an upsell after a guitar purchase, and it relocates the trigger from 13 to 17/16. It also means the feature is really about **proof of cover at booking**, which is closer to compliance than to insurance sales. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect musicians' gear to be **uninsured or wrongly insured**, not simply uninsured | Any music insurance broker: the common case is not "no policy" — it is a **home contents policy that excludes professional use**. The musician believes they are covered; they are not; they find out when a stolen guitar claim is refused because they were paid to play. That is a worse state than knowing you are uninsured, and no generic insurer will ever tell them, because the exclusion is in their favour. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it changes the feature's job: the value is **telling someone their existing cover does not apply**, not selling them a new policy. That is genuinely useful, genuinely on the user's side, and it costs the platform nothing to say — but it only works if the platform is willing to give advice that does not end in a referral. Which is the same test as 23.06.01 Q-02: does the product ever say something that loses it a fee? |

- Triggered by **13 Gear Marketplace** (purchase), **17 Live Booking & Settlement** and **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** (the venue's liability requirement — the real trigger, DT-02), **18 Show Production & Touring** (tour cover, territory).
- Feeds **23.02.01 Expense & Receipt Capture** — premiums are deductible expenses, auto-categorized.
- Feeds **23.04.01 Contract Vault** — a policy is a document with renewal dates, and **23.04.03 Alerts** — lapsed cover is a dated risk.
- Related to **23.06.01 Advance Underwriting** — the same referral-with-a-conflict pattern; both monetise the platform's informational edge in someone else's regulated product.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
