## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Quote & Estimate Builder ↔ Invoice Issuance

**Relationship**: The quote records what was agreed **before** the work; the invoice bills for what happened. In music these routinely differ — the session ran to a second day, the mix went to five revisions, the function ran an hour over. The gap between quote and invoice is where payment disputes are born, and the platform's advantage is that it can hold both artefacts and show the delta rather than leaving two people arguing from memory. That is the same reconstruction failure as `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis, applied to money instead of credits.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: quotes for a function, a session, a teaching block. Their quotes are small and frequent; scope creep is constant and rarely re-papered.
- **Producer**: quotes for a mix or a production. Revision count is *the* scope variable and the classic overrun — "unlimited revisions" is a quote that cannot be invoiced honestly.
- **Operator**: quotes for room hire, often with a deposit structure and a cancellation policy attached; their quote is closer to a contract than the others'.
- **Fan**: not affected — no access to this sub-domain.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The quote is immutable once sent (it is an offer). Changes are new versions, not edits. The invoice references the quote version it bills against.
2. **Trigger chain**: Quote accepted → work happens → invoice generated pre-filled from the quote. If the invoice exceeds the quote, the excess must be explicit and attributable, not silently absorbed into a bigger number.
3. **Permission intersection**: The recipient of a quote may not be a WeJammin user at all — much off-platform work is for people who are not here. Both artefacts must work for non-users, which constrains the whole sub-domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Quote accepted/declined/expired notifies the issuer. An invoice exceeding its quote should arguably require the payer's acknowledgement before it enters the dunning ladder.
5. **State transition conflict**: A quote expiring while the work is already underway — common and awkward; the artefact says no, the reality says yes.

### CX-02: Invoice Issuance ↔ Payment Chasing & Aging

**Relationship**: Aging is a pure derivation of issuance date plus terms. The ladder's escalation steps, the statutory late-payment interest entitlement, and the aging buckets all resolve from fields set at issuance — which means a sloppy issuance (no terms, no due date) produces an unchaseable invoice. The feature that looks like the boring one (issuance) determines whether the feature that carries the value (chasing) can function at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the chaser. Their entire receivables position is other people's admin backlogs.
- **Producer**: chaser and payer simultaneously — they owe contributors while a label owes them, which is precisely the cashflow squeeze that makes them withhold stems (the anti-persona behaviour in `meta/personas.md`).
- **Operator**: the archetypal slow payer in the ecosystem *and* a chaser of their own deposits. Both directions, high volume.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The invoice owns status (`issued` → `paid`/`overdue`/`disputed`/`written-off`). The ladder reads it and writes only chase events.
2. **Trigger chain**: Due date passes → ladder starts → escalation by rule. A payment landing (via the ledger, 23.01.01) must stop the ladder **immediately** — chasing someone who has already paid is a relationship-damaging bug, not a cosmetic one.
3. **Permission intersection**: A delegated manager or bookkeeper chasing on the user's behalf is the ideal case — it removes the emotional cost that stops musicians chasing at all (see 23.03.03 DT-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every ladder step is an outbound message to a counterparty who may not be a WeJammin user; deliverability and tone are the product here.
5. **State transition conflict**: Payment and escalation racing — the reconciliation lag between money arriving and the ledger knowing about it is exactly when a wrongful chase gets sent.

### CX-03: Payment Chasing & Aging ↔ Quote & Estimate Builder

**Relationship**: The feed-forward loop. A counterparty's payment history is knowledge the musician currently keeps in their head, badly, and forgets under the pressure of wanting the gig. The platform can put it in front of them at quoting time: "this promoter took 94 days last time." That does not tell them to decline — it tells them to ask for a deposit.

**Role scoping**: Musician and Producer primarily. Operator less so — their protection is a deposit taken up front (domain 17), a mechanism they already have.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — deferred per template guidance. The unresolved question is whether payment history is **private to the user** (my experience of this promoter) or **aggregated across the platform** (this promoter pays everyone late). Aggregation is far more valuable and far more dangerous — it is a de-facto credit rating on a named business, with defamation and competition exposure. See 23.03.03 Q-01.
