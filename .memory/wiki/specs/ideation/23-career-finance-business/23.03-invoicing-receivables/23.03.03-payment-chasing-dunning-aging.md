# Feature: Payment Chasing, Dunning & Receivables Aging

> **Number**: 23.03.03
> **Parent**: [Invoicing & Receivables](./23.03-invoicing-receivables-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who owes you what, how old it is, and what happens next. The aging view is easy; the hard part is that **musicians do not chase**, because the person who owes them £400 is the promoter who books the room they want to play next spring. The unpaid session is a switching trigger in `meta/personas.md` — "losing money to a fact they couldn't prove" — and the reason it stays unpaid is social, not technical. This feature's real job is to remove the emotional cost of asking.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their aged debt in one place for the first time — usually a bigger number than they expected, spread over people they are afraid to annoy. Runs a ladder that escalates without them having to write the awkward message. |
| Producer | Full | Both directions at once: a label owes them while they owe contributors. That squeeze is what produces the anti-persona behaviour in `meta/personas.md` — withholding stems as leverage. Seeing the two positions together is the intervention: they are not refusing to pay, they have not been paid. |
| Operator | Full | Chases deposits and settlement shortfalls, and is simultaneously the ecosystem's archetypal slow payer. Their aging view is high-volume and their counterparties are acts they will book again — the same social bind as the Musician, from the other side. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Fans have no receivables. A fan who is owed a refund is a support case in 19/24, not a creditor with an aging bucket. |

## Behavior

The ladder is a **rule-driven escalation** from a gentle reminder to a formal demand, with statutory late-payment interest available where the jurisdiction grants it. It reads invoice status (23.03.02) and stops the instant the ledger (23.01.01) sees the money. Every step is an outbound message to a counterparty who is usually not a WeJammin user.

### Happy Path

1. Invoice hits its due date unpaid; aging starts.
2. Day 3: an automatic, neutral reminder goes out — no tone, no accusation, sent by the platform rather than by the musician.
3. It surfaces probable mechanical causes first (`#CX-02`, DT-02 on 23.03.02): missing PO, wrong recipient, missed payment run.
4. Day 14: a firmer reminder naming the statutory interest entitlement.
5. Payment lands; the ledger sees it; the ladder stops the same moment and the invoice closes.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Payment arrives but the ledger has not reconciled it yet | **The worst bug in the feature** — chasing someone who has paid damages the relationship the musician was protecting by not chasing | Reconciliation lag must be treated as a hard constraint: pause the ladder on any signal of payment, and prefer a missed chase to a wrongful one |
| Short payment that is actually **withholding** (23.02.04) | Not a shortfall; chasing it is wrong and looks ignorant to a foreign payer | Detected and reclassified before escalation |
| Counterparty disputes the work or the scope | The ladder must stop — dunning a disputed invoice is escalation, not collection | Dispute state halts automation; routes to the quote/invoice delta (`#CX-01`) and, if needed, domain 24 |
| The user does not want to chase this person, ever | Must be honoured. The relationship is worth more than the invoice, and that is the user's call to make, not the platform's | Per-counterparty pause/exclude. Never auto-escalate against an excluded party |
| Debt is uncollectable | Write-off is a real state with a tax consequence (a bad-debt relief in some jurisdictions) | Write-off path feeding 23.02.03 |
| Counterparty is on WeJammin | Chasing becomes an in-platform notification — much stronger, and much more socially loaded | See DT-03; the reputational dimension is a live risk |
| Ladder message is delivered but ignored repeatedly | Escalation runs out of runway; formal demand or write-off | Honest about the end of the ladder — the platform cannot make anyone pay |
| Musician wants a human to chase | Delegated bookkeeper/manager (Representation cross-cut) | Delegate can run the ladder — this is DT-02's real answer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aging computing | Skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing outstanding | Genuinely good news, said plainly |
| Populated | Outstanding invoices | Aging buckets (current / 30 / 60 / 90+), total owed, per-counterparty grouping |
| Error | Aging query failed | Error with retry; never a wrong total on a debt view |
| Partial | Some invoices in dispute or paused | Excluded from automation, counted separately — a paused debt is still a debt |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The problem is visibility — show musicians their aged debt and they will chase it | The dashboard instinct | ❌ REJECTED: they already know, roughly, and they still do not chase. The blocker is **social, not informational**: the debtor is a future employer. A better aging table changes nothing. The intervention is that the *platform* asks, in a neutral voice, so the musician never has to be the one who brought it up. That is a positioning decision (who is the sender?), not a feature. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **automation to be the point, because delegation is what the professional class actually buys** | Anyone with a manager or a bookkeeper: the reason established artists get paid and session players do not is that somebody whose job it is asks, and that person is not the artist. The platform can be that third party for people who cannot afford one — which is a real widening of access, not just a convenience. | ✅ CONFIRMED: the ladder is a **synthetic third party**. It also makes the Representation & Delegated Access cross-cut concrete: a real bookkeeper should be able to run the same ladder. This is the strongest argument in the sub-domain and the reason the feature is `should` rather than `could`. |
| DT-03 | Chasing is stronger if the counterparty is on WeJammin, so we should lean into that | Platform-leverage instinct — and it is true that in-platform pressure works | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a serious caveat nobody asked for: leaning in leads directly to **payment reputation on a business's profile**, which is a de-facto credit rating on a named party. It would be enormously valuable to musicians (`#CX-03`), and it exposes WeJammin to defamation claims, competition-law questions, and a retaliation dynamic where a promoter blacklists the musician who dinged their rating. A promoter with a public "pays in 94 days" badge has a legal budget and a grudge. Escalated as Q-01 — this is a **product and values decision**, not an implementation one. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Driven by **23.03.02 Invoice Issuance** — terms and due date are the ladder's only inputs (`23.03-invoicing-receivables-cx.md#CX-02`).
- Feeds forward into **23.03.01 Quote & Estimate Builder** — payment history should change future terms (`#CX-03`), which is where the reputation question (DT-03) actually bites.
- Reads **23.01.01 Income Event Ledger** to stop on payment — the reconciliation lag is the feature's most damaging failure mode.
- Touches **23.02.04 Cross-Border Withholding** — a short payment may be withholding, not a shortfall.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** — a disputed invoice is a dispute, and non-payment at scale is a trust signal about a counterparty.
- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** — settlement shortfalls are receivables, and the promoter is both counterparty and future employer.
- Depends on **Representation & Delegated Access** (a bookkeeper runs the ladder — DT-02) and the **Notifications & Messaging** cross-cut (every ladder step is an outbound message to a non-user).
- Feeds **23.02.03 Tax Pack** — bad-debt write-offs have a tax consequence.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform is the sender, in a neutral voice | See DT-01 — removing the social cost is the entire feature |
| D-02 | Payment signal halts the ladder immediately; prefer a missed chase to a wrongful one | Chasing someone who paid destroys the relationship the user was protecting |
| D-03 | Per-counterparty exclusion is always available and always honoured | The relationship may be worth more than the invoice — the user decides that, not the platform |
| D-04 | Mechanical causes are surfaced before tone escalates | See 23.03.02 DT-02 — most non-payment is an AP process failure, not a refusal |
