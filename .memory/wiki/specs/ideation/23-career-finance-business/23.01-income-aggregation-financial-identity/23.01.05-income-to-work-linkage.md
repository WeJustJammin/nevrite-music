# Feature: Income-to-Work Linkage

> **Number**: 23.01.05
> **Parent**: [Income Aggregation & Financial Identity](./23.01-income-aggregation-financial-identity-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Every income event that came from creative work carries a reference to the work that produced it, so the credits graph and the ledger become one record: "this session has paid me £340 across three years." This is where the finance domain touches the platform thesis directly — provenance (D-18) says the record of who did what must survive; this feature says the record of **what that work paid** survives with it. It is also the answer to the only question that makes a credits database worth opening twice.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees lifetime earnings per credit across every income type — the session fee on the day, the royalty trickle for years after, a sync fee out of nowhere. This is the view that makes the credits graph *pay* rather than just flatter. Strictly private to them. |
| Producer | Full | Same for works they ran, plus points/backend — the income that arrives long after the session and that they currently reconstruct from memory (`meta/personas.md`). A producer with three years of backend on a record can finally see whether their points were worth taking. |
| Operator | None | No linkage. Their income attaches to a **room and a date**, not a work — a venue does not appear in the credits graph. Explicit absence: do not build a work-attribution surface into the Operator's ledger. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Fans see public credits (domain 02); they must never see what anyone was paid. The join between the two is precisely the leak to prevent. |

## Behavior

The linkage is a **read projection** joining ledger rows (23.01.01) to the credits graph (domain 02) via the `work_id` the originating domain supplied. It stores no money and no credits of its own. Rows without a work reference (a gear sale, a rehearsal room hire) simply do not appear here.

For each linked work the projection presents three things, and only these three: a **total to date**, a **breakdown by income type** (the income type is carried on the ledger row by the emitting domain — session fee, royalty line, sync fee, backend/points, advance), and a **timeline** of when each event settled. Because a work can be advanced against (23.06.03), every figure is shown as **two numbers — gross and net-to-me** — so a fully-recouped work reads honestly as "earned, received nothing" rather than as a plain total that lies. The projection is scoped to a single earner: it answers "what did *this* work pay *me*", never "what did this work pay anyone".

### Happy Path

1. Producer runs a session; contributors and splits are captured on the day (domain 02/09 — the moment that matters).
2. The session fee settles; the income event carries the `work_id`.
3. Two years later royalty lines for the same work arrive from domain 10, carrying the same `work_id`.
4. The Musician opens their credit for that track and sees total earnings to date, by income type, over time.
5. The number is real because the platform was in the room when the fact became true — no reconstruction.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A credit is contested or revoked (domain 24) while earnings display against it | Attribution degrades to "unattributed income"; **the money is still real and stays in the ledger** | Money intact, attribution marked disputed — never delete income because a credit is questioned |
| Income arrives with no `work_id` (gear sale, room hire, teaching) | Not shown here at all | Absent, not zero |
| One payment covers several works (an album session, a multi-track sync) | Requires allocation across works | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether allocation is user-declared, pro-rata, or simply refused is unresolved and consequential |
| Earnings-per-credit visible to someone who can see the credit | **The leak.** Credit visibility is public; earnings visibility is private. Same join, two permissions | Earnings never render outside the earner's own context |
| A work's income is a Band entity's, not the member's | The member sees their distribution (23.07.03), not the band's gross | No inflation of personal figures via band works |
| Advance/recoupment against a work (23.06.03) | Gross earnings for a work may be fully recouped — the user has "earned" and received nothing | Gross and net-to-me must both be visible or the number lies |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Opening a credit's earnings view | Skeleton |
| Empty | Credit exists, no income yet | Honest "no income recorded against this work yet" — common and not a failure; most works earn nothing for a long time |
| Populated | Income linked | Total, breakdown by income type, timeline |
| Error | Join failed | Error with retry; never show £0 when the truth is "unknown" |
| Partial | Some income unlinked (pre-platform work, missing `work_id`) | Explicit "excludes N unlinked payments" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a nice-to-have reporting garnish on the credits graph | Naive framing — it looks like a chart | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **answer to why anyone maintains credits at all**. `meta/problem-statement.md` names Jaxsta/Muso.AI/Sound Credit as the failure mode — "nobody wakes up wanting a credits database." Money is the daily hook that a credits database has never had. This is the cheapest available bridge between the wedge (provenance) and the platform (consolidation), because both halves of the data already exist for other reasons. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect this to expose **who is underpaid**, which is politically explosive | Any session player: seeing that a track you played on has generated royalties for the artist while your one-off fee was £150 is exactly the realisation that starts disputes. The platform is choosing to make an invisible asymmetry visible. That is arguably the right thing and definitely a consequence nobody asked for. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real second-order effect. Constrained by the permission split: a user sees **their own** earnings per work, never anyone else's. The asymmetry becomes visible only to the person experiencing it. Feeds 23.05.03 benchmarking, which generalises it safely via cohorts. |
| DT-03 | The credits graph and the ledger can be joined freely because both are ours | Convenience assumption | ❌ REJECTED: they have **opposite privacy postures**. Credits are public by design (they are reputation); earnings are private by design. Joining two stores with opposite defaults is how leaks happen — the join must be permission-checked on the earnings side, never on the credit side. The default answer for any viewer who is not the earner is "no", including for a delegated manager unless finance access was explicitly granted. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Projects **23.01.01 Income Event Ledger** — see `23.01-income-aggregation-financial-identity-cx.md#CX-03`. No independent storage.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** — consumes `work_id` and the credit record; writes nothing back. A revoked credit must not delete money.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** and **10 Royalties & Collections** — royalty income is the long-tail half of any work's earnings and the reason the timeline matters.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** — a credit dispute has a money shadow here, and the money is often the *motive* for the dispute.
- Excluded from **23.01.04 Verified Income Statement** — see rejected pair `#R-02`.
- Depends on the **Income Event Emission Contract** cross-cut: `work_id` is only present if the emitting domain supplies it. Domains that forget to populate it silently break this feature with no error anywhere.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Read projection only — stores neither money nor credits | Two owners already exist (23.01.01, domain 02); a third copy would be a third truth |
| D-02 | A revoked or disputed credit degrades attribution but never removes income | The money happened. Attribution is a claim; payment is a fact |
| D-03 | Permission-checked on the earnings side; default deny for every viewer but the earner | See DT-03 — public credits joined to private money is the leak |
