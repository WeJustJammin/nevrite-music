# Feature: Multi-Currency Normalization & FX

> **Number**: 23.01.03
> **Parent**: [Income Aggregation & Financial Identity](./23.01-income-aggregation-financial-identity-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

A working musician is paid in EUR for a German festival, GBP for a home function, and USD for streaming royalties — three currencies before anyone has left the country. The ledger cannot show a single total without deciding **which rate on which date**, and that decision is not a preference: the user's tax jurisdiction largely dictates it, and changing it restates every historical figure. This feature owns the policy, the rate source, and the honesty about which numbers are estimates.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets a reporting currency; sees native amount and converted amount side by side. Touring and streaming both create cross-border income, so this arrives early for them, not as an advanced feature. |
| Producer | Full | Remote sessions for overseas clients are routine post-2020 (`meta/problem-statement.md` — "the room is already digital"), so their exposure matches the Musician's. Points/backend royalty income is typically USD regardless of where they live. |
| Operator | Config | Sets the room's currency once and rarely thinks about it again — their inventory is time and space in **one** country. They see FX only when an overseas act invoices them in another currency. Config, not a working surface. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no income. (Fans do meet currency at checkout in 13/14/19, which is a different mechanism owned elsewhere.) |

## Behavior

Every ledger row stores native currency and amount **immutably**. The reporting-currency figure is a derived projection, cached, and always recomputable. The rate-date policy is account-level configuration with a jurisdiction-driven default.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is paid €1,200 for a Berlin show; the event lands with native currency EUR.
2. FX policy (reporting GBP, rate at transaction date) resolves a rate for that date.
3. Ledger shows `€1,200` with `≈ £1,031` beneath it — the native figure is the fact, the conversion is the projection.
4. Year-to-date totals and the tax pack (23.02.03) use the projection consistently.
5. The native €1,200 never changes, whatever happens to rates afterwards.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Rate provider unavailable for a date | Row stays visible with an unresolved projection; it is **excluded from totals with an explicit count**, never silently dropped or zeroed | "Total excludes 2 rows pending conversion" |
| User changes reporting currency or rate-date policy | Every historical projection restates; artefacts already exported no longer match | Warning naming the exported artefacts affected before the change commits |
| Payment received in a currency, converted by the payment provider before payout | Two conversions exist — the provider's real one and ours. The **provider's actual rate is the truth** for what hit the account; ours is only an estimate where no real conversion happened | Actual rate marked as such; estimate marked as such |
| Historical rate is revised by the provider | A period already exported/locked must freeze | Locked periods do not silently move |
| Tax jurisdiction mandates a specific rate (e.g. an official annual/monthly average) | Policy must support jurisdiction-mandated rate tables, not just spot rates | Policy preset per jurisdiction |
| Delegated accountant (Config access) sets or changes the FX policy | Permitted — the delegate is exactly the party who should set it (CX-02 permission intersection). But policy change is a restatement event and inherits the same exported-artefact warning | Same restatement warning, attributed to the delegate in the audit trail |
| Two policy changes committed near-simultaneously (owner + delegate) | Restatement is a single ordered operation on the account's FX policy; the second write is applied against the result of the first, never interleaved, so projections never end up half-restated | Second actor sees the current (post-first-change) policy before their change commits |
| Operator sees FX at all (their inventory is single-country) | Feature stays Config-only for them; it surfaces solely when an overseas act invoices them in a foreign currency, never as a working ledger surface | An FX line on that one invoice; no reporting-currency dashboard |
| Crypto or non-fiat payment | Out of scope for this pass | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rates resolving | Native amounts render immediately; projections fill in — never block the real number on the estimate |
| Empty | Single-currency user | Feature is invisible. No config surface until a second currency appears |
| Populated | 2+ currencies present | Dual display, policy summary, per-currency subtotals |
| Error | Rate source failed | Native figures intact, projections marked unresolved, totals honest about the gap |
| Partial | Some rows converted, some not | Total with explicit exclusion count |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | FX is a display convenience — pick a rate and show a number | Naive framing | ❌ REJECTED: the rate-date rule is a **tax position**, not a display choice. HMRC, the IRS and most authorities each constrain which rate is acceptable, and the figure produced feeds a filed return. A "convenience" conversion that ends up on a tax pack is a compliance artefact wearing a UI costume. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **provider's actual conversion to outrank our estimate** | Anyone who has reconciled a payout: the money that arrived was converted once, for real, at a real rate with a real spread. Storing our own spot estimate as if it were the fact makes the ledger disagree with the bank — and the bank wins every argument. | ✅ CONFIRMED: rows distinguish actual conversions from estimated ones. Real conversions come from the payments cross-cut, not a rate API. |
| DT-03 | Withholding tax at source interacts with FX and nobody mentioned it | A German festival withholds tax before paying; the withheld amount is in EUR, the reclaim (23.02.04) is in EUR, but the user's return is in GBP. Converting gross, deductions and net at different dates silently breaks the arithmetic — net ≠ gross − deductions after conversion. | ✅ CONFIRMED: gross, deductions and net on a row must convert at **one** rate/date, or the row must not balance. Direct constraint on 23.01.01's D-03 and on 23.02.04. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Constrains **23.01.01 Income Event Ledger** — see `23.01-income-aggregation-financial-identity-cx.md#CX-02`. No totals without a policy.
- Feeds **23.02.03 Tax Pack Generation** and **23.02.04 Cross-Border Withholding & Treaty Reclaim** — the reclaim is denominated in the withholding country's currency.
- Consumes the platform **Payments, Payouts & Escrow** cross-cut for actual realised rates (DT-02).
- Touches **23.07 Budgeting & Project/Tour P&L** — a tour budgeted in GBP and spent in EUR has FX exposure that is a real tour cost, not an accounting artefact.
- Touches **18 Show Production & Touring** — the same multi-country reality that creates FX creates withholding and per-diems.
- Depends on the **delegation / account-access** model (accountant, manager) — FX policy is `⚙️ Config`, and CX-02 argues the delegated accountant is the correct party to set it; policy changes must be attributable to the actor in the audit trail.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Native amount is immutable; the conversion is a derived projection | Overwriting the native amount destroys the only fact in the row |
| D-02 | Actual provider conversions outrank estimated ones and are marked distinctly | See DT-02 — otherwise the ledger disagrees with the bank |
| D-03 | Gross, deductions and net on one row convert at one rate/date | See DT-03 — otherwise the row does not balance |
