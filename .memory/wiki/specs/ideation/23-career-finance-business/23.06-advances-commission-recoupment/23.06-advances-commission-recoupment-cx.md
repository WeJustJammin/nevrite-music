## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Recoupment & Netting Ledger ↔ Commission & Representation Deductions

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's spine (parent D-02). When £10,000 of royalty income arrives against an unrecouped £8,000 advance and a 20% management commission, the answer is either "manager gets £2,000, then £8,000 recoups, musician gets £0" or "£8,000 recoups, manager gets 20% of the remaining £2,000 = £400, musician gets £1,600". Same facts, materially different outcomes, and real contracts specify both. The platform must implement the **contract's** sequence, not a default — which means the sequence is a term extracted from the deal vault (23.04.02), not a setting.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the party who loses if the sequence is wrong, and the party least likely to know which sequence their contract specifies.
- **Producer**: same exposure via production agreements and points.
- **Operator**: not affected — no commission, no recoupment (parent Role Matrix).
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The netting ledger owns the sequence and the balances; commission owns the rate and its sunset. Neither may assume a default order — an unspecified order must **block**, not guess.
2. **Trigger chain**: Income event → netting sequence applies → deductions computed → net-to-me. If the sequence is unknown, the row must be held as unresolved rather than netted wrongly. A wrong netting is silent money loss.
3. **Permission intersection**: The manager is a **delegate and a beneficiary of the same calculation** — the sharpest conflict of interest in the domain. They must never be able to alter the sequence or the rate.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A change to the sequence or rate must notify the musician, not just the manager who requested it.
5. **State transition conflict**: A commission sunsetting (23.04.03) mid-period while recoupment is live — the sequence changes underneath an in-flight calculation.

### CX-02: Advance Underwriting & Offers ↔ Recoupment & Netting Ledger

**Relationship**: Taking an advance creates a liability that silently attaches to every future income event. This is the same classification trap 23.01.02 flags on import: an advance landing in a bank account **looks exactly like income** and is not. If the ledger records it as income, the musician's tax pack overstates their earnings, their proof-of-earnings artefact overstates their income, and their runway forecast tells them they are richer than they are — three wrong answers from one misclassification.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the borrower.
- **Producer**: same.
- **Operator**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The advance is a liability record; income events are facts. Never merged.
2. **Trigger chain**: Advance taken → recoupment position opens → every subsequent income event of the covered class nets against it → position closes at zero. Which income classes are covered is a contract term, not a default (a royalty advance does not recoup against session fees — usually).
3. **Permission intersection**: The funder may need visibility of the recoupment position — a third party reading the user's income. That is a consent question, not a plumbing one.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Position cleared is a genuinely good notification, and rare in this sub-domain.
5. **State transition conflict**: Income reversal (a refund, a chargeback) after it recouped — the position must re-open, and the arithmetic must survive going backwards.

### CX-03: Recoupment & Netting Ledger ↔ Cashflow Smoothing & Runway Forecast

**Relationship**: Runway is the sub-domain's honest output, and it is only honest if it forecasts **net-to-me**. A musician with £4,000/month of royalty income against an unrecouped advance receives nothing — a gross-based runway would tell them they are fine while they cannot make rent. Given that lumpy income is the persona's defining financial condition, a runway that lies is worse than no runway.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: portfolio income with deductions is exactly where gross and net diverge most.
- **Producer**: same, plus they owe contributors out of what arrives.
- **Operator**: read-only runway — perishable inventory means cashflow visibility matters even without the instruments.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Netting owns balances; runway owns projection. Runway reads, never writes.
2. **Trigger chain**: Netting position changes → runway re-forecasts.
3. **Permission intersection**: A delegate seeing runway sees a very intimate number — whether someone can make rent.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A runway warning is the most sensitive notification in the product; see 23.06.04 DT-03.
5. **State transition conflict**: Deferred — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

### CX-04: Catalogue Stake Sale ↔ Recoupment & Netting Ledger

**Relationship**: A sold stake diverts a share of future income permanently — mechanically like recoupment, but with no balance and no end. That difference is the entire risk of the instrument, and it is exactly what a musician in a cash crisis is least equipped to weigh. Medium confidence: synthesis deferred per template guidance, pending the `wont` decision (parent D-04).

### CX-05: Advance Underwriting & Offers ↔ Catalogue Stake Sale

**Relationship**: Two answers to one question — "I need money now." An advance ends when it recoups; a stake sale never ends. Presenting them side by side is genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous: the stake sale will often be the worse deal and the more attractive-looking offer, because the headline number is bigger. Medium confidence; synthesis deferred.
