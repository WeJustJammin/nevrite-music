# Feature: Advance Underwriting & Offers

> **Number**: 23.06.01
> **Parent**: [Advances, Commission & Recoupment](./23.06-advances-commission-recoupment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Cash now against income later. Royalty-advance companies already do this — for the top few percent, underwriting streaming royalties only, because streaming royalties are the only music income anyone can see. WeJammin can see the whole picture (23.01): session fees, settlements, teaching, marketplace sales, royalties. That makes it possible to underwrite a working musician nobody else can assess. It is also lending, which is licensed, capitalised and regulated — so the platform's realistic role is to hold the data, not the money (parent D-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this exists for: lumpy portfolio income, no salary, no way to prove creditworthiness to anyone. Sees what they could raise against their verified income and on what terms — and, critically, what it costs. |
| Producer | Full | Same mechanics. Their income is lumpier still (a production fee then nothing for four months) and their backend royalty income is genuinely forecastable, which makes them arguably the better credit. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue's financing needs are ordinary small-business ones — a bank, a lease, an overdraft — where WeJammin has no informational advantage and no business competing. Explicit absence (parent Role Matrix). |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no income, no professional stake. |

## Behavior

Underwriting reads **verified income only** (23.01.04) — declared income carries no weight, which is the whole reason the trust tiering exists (23.01 D-03). The platform assesses and presents; a third-party funder lends (parent D-03). An accepted advance creates a recoupment position (`#CX-02`) and is recorded as a **liability, never as income**.

### Happy Path

1. Musician has 18 months of verified income across sessions, shows and royalties — a picture no bank has ever been able to see.
2. They ask what they can raise.
3. The platform assesses verified income, its stability and its payer diversity (23.01.04 DT-02) — not just the total.
4. Offers come back from funders with a rate, a recoupment share and a term, and the **total cost is stated in money, not in a percentage**.
5. They accept; a recoupment position opens; future income nets against it (23.06.03) until it clears.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The advance lands in their bank and is imported as income (23.01.02) | The classification trap (`#CX-02`) — it would overstate their tax pack, their proof-of-earnings and their runway from one mistake | Detected and reclassified as a liability |
| Verified history is too short | Cannot be manufactured — the platform was not there before | Honest "N months of verified history; funders typically want M". This is the cold-start wall for the whole sub-domain |
| Income drops after the advance | Recoupment stretches; the debt outlives the income that justified it | The core risk of the instrument, and it must be stated **before** acceptance, not discovered after |
| User does not understand the cost | A musician in a cash crisis is the least equipped person to price a financial product, and the most motivated to skip the detail | Cost in **money over the expected term**, never only an APR or a percentage. See DT-03 |
| Funder wants to see the user's income data | A third party reading someone's earnings — consent, not plumbing | Explicit, scoped, revocable consent |
| User has a manager taking commission | Does not affect underwritability — see rejected pair `#R-01`; the income exists either way | No interaction |
| Default | The platform brokered this. It is now adjacent to someone's debt and possibly their collections process | Parent Q-01 — the reputational and relational exposure is real |
| Same user takes advances from two funders | Neither can see the other's position; the platform can see both | A duty nobody asked about; see Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Assessing | Skeleton |
| Empty | Insufficient verified history | Honest, with what would change it — this will be the normal state for most users for a long time |
| Populated | Offers available | Offers with total cost in money, term, and recoupment share |
| Error | Assessment failed | No offer rather than a wrong one |
| Partial | Assessable but no funder interest | Honest — "we can assess you; no funder is offering", which is information, not a failure |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | WeJammin should originate advances itself — it has the data, and D-03 (global) says ambition is maximal | The maximal reading of the candidate | ❌ REJECTED: lending is a **licensed activity** requiring regulatory permission, capital adequacy, KYC/AML and a balance sheet. This is not an effort or cost objection (the cost-blind rule would dismiss that) — it is a legal-capability constraint, like 23.02.03's filing rejection. The platform's unique asset is the **underwriting data**, not the money. Referral against verified income captures the differentiated half; originating captures the commoditised, regulated half. Recorded as parent D-03. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **income stability, not income total, to be the underwritable signal** — and that inverts the musician's usual disadvantage | Any underwriter: £30k from twenty payers over three years is a better credit than £30k from one payer who has now gone. Musicians are refused credit because their income *looks* chaotic on a bank statement — but a diversified portfolio is genuinely more resilient than a single salary, and nobody has ever been able to show that. The platform can. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the sharpest idea in the sub-domain: the same data that makes musicians look uncreditworthy to a bank makes them look **good** when it is legible. It also confirms 23.01.04 D-03 (stability and payer diversity are first-class content) from a second direction — the artefact and the underwriting want the same thing. |
| DT-03 | An advance is a product decision the user makes; our job is to present offers neutrally | The marketplace framing | ⏳ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — with an uncomfortable finding.** The user reaching for an advance is, by construction, in a cash crisis: the least equipped moment to price a financial product and the most motivated to skip the terms. Presenting offers "neutrally" to someone in that state is not neutral — it is a dark pattern with good manners. A platform whose thesis is being on the musician's side (`meta/problem-statement.md`) cannot broker debt to desperate users and call the outcome their choice. Concretely: cost must be stated **in money over the expected term**, the cheaper alternative must be shown when one exists (23.06.04's runway may say they do not need it), and the platform should be willing to say "do not take this." Escalated as Q-02 — a values decision the owner must make explicitly. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Referral, not origination | Parent D-03, DT-01 — lending is a licensed activity; the data is the asset |
| D-02 | Verified income only; declared carries no weight | The trust tiering exists for exactly this |
| D-03 | Cost stated in money over the expected term, not as a rate | See DT-03 — the user is in a crisis, not a comparison-shopping mood |
| D-04 | Stability and payer diversity are the underwriting signal, not the total | See DT-02 |
