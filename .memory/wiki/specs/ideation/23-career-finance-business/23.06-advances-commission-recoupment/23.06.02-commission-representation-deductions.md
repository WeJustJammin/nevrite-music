# Feature: Commission & Representation Deductions

> **Number**: 23.06.02
> **Parent**: [Advances, Commission & Recoupment](./23.06-advances-commission-recoupment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A manager takes 20%, an agent takes 10%, a business manager takes 5%. Those percentages apply to some income and not others, start on some date, and stop — in theory — when a sunset clause expires. In practice nobody tracks the "stop", so former managers keep collecting on income they have no claim to, for years, quietly. The platform holds both the contract terms (23.04.02) and the income (23.01), which makes it the only party positioned to notice.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees what each representative takes, on which income classes, and when it should stop. Most have never seen this laid out — commission is deducted upstream and they receive a net figure with no working shown. |
| Producer | Full | Less likely to have a manager, more likely to have a production agreement with points flowing the other way. Their distinctive case is being the one who **pays** rather than the one deducted from. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue has no manager taking a percentage of the door. Explicit absence (parent Role Matrix). |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no income, no representation. |

## Behavior

A commission rule is a rate, a scope (which income classes), a start, and a **sunset** — all of which are contract terms extracted from the deal vault (23.04.02), not settings a user invents. Rules apply through the netting ledger's sequence (`#CX-01`), never independently, because the order relative to recoupment changes the number.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's management agreement is in the vault; its terms are confirmed: 20%, all music income, 18-month sunset at 10% on post-term income from deals made during the term.
2. A £2,000 session fee lands.
3. The netting ledger applies the contract's sequence; the commission is computed with its working shown.
4. Musician sees gross £2,000, commission £400, net £1,600 — and *why*, citing the clause.
5. Eighteen months after the term ends, the sunset expires and the commission **stops on its own** — which is the entire feature.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The sunset expires and nobody notices | **The feature's reason to exist.** A former manager collecting for years on income they have no claim to is routine, quiet, and nobody's job to catch | Alert (23.04.03) + automatic stop. Money that would otherwise leave silently |
| Commission scope is ambiguous (does "music income" include a gear sale? a teaching hour?) | Contracts are frequently vague on exactly the income classes this platform newly makes visible — a manager's agreement written in 2019 never contemplated marketplace income | Must refuse to guess. An unresolved scope holds the row rather than deducting wrongly |
| Order vs recoupment unspecified | Blocks rather than defaults (`#CX-01`, parent D-02) | Unresolved, flagged — a wrong netting is silent money loss |
| The manager is also the user's **delegate** | The sharpest conflict of interest in the domain: they administer the account and benefit from the calculation | They must never be able to alter the rate, scope or sequence. Hard rule, not a permission preference |
| Commission taken ≠ commission agreed | The most checkable finding in the domain (23.04.04) | Reconciliation surfaces the drift |
| The platform **routes** the commission to the manager | Makes WeJammin a payment intermediary for a third party who is not a persona and never signed anything here | Parent Q-02 — unresolved and consequential |
| A manager leaves / is fired mid-term | Post-term commission is precisely what sunset clauses govern, and precisely when relations are worst | Sunset terms drive it; the platform is the neutral record |
| Band has a manager; members have their own | Overlapping commissions on the same income at different levels | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

**Open-question governance — overlapping commissions.** The **Owner** is User; the hard decision
deadline is immediately before `/create-prd` begins; and it blocks the commission-precedence,
allocation, and netting behavior for one income event. No downstream calculation may deduct or
prioritize either claim until the owner resolves or formally supersedes this decision.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The value is automating the deduction — commission calculation is arithmetic | Candidate 14's framing ("Commission Automation") | ❌ REJECTED: the arithmetic is trivial and already happens (upstream, opaquely, by the manager's bookkeeper). The value is **the stop** — the sunset that expires and that nobody has ever had an incentive to notice. Automating a deduction serves the manager; tracking its expiry serves the musician. The feature's identity depends on which of those it is for, and the candidate's name points at the wrong one. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **scope** of commission to be the contested term, not the rate | Any music lawyer: 20% is 20% and nobody argues. What gets argued is *of what* — does the manager commission touring gross or net of costs, merch, publishing, a gear sale, a teaching income? A manager's agreement written before this platform existed never contemplated marketplace or teaching income, and the platform is about to make those income classes visible and deductible for the first time. | ✅ CONFIRMED, with a consequence nobody asked for: **WeJammin's consolidation creates a commission question that did not previously exist.** A manager who never knew about their client's gear sales might now argue they are commissionable — because the platform made them legible. The product could actively transfer money from musicians to managers by aggregating income. That is a direct, unintended contradiction of the platform's stated side, and it argues for scope defaulting to the **narrowest defensible reading** with anything ambiguous held rather than deducted. |
| DT-03 | Commission should be routed automatically at payout — that is the "automation" | The natural completion of the feature | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and it is parent Q-02. Routing is what a manager would pay for and is genuinely useful. But it makes WeJammin a payment intermediary for a **third party who is not one of the four personas** and never agreed to anything here — with the platform enforcing a private contract it never saw, against a user whose interests may diverge from the party being paid. Calculating and showing is defensible; moving the money is a different product with different duties. Note the pattern: this is the third feature in the domain (with 23.02.03 filing and 23.06.01 lending) where the maximal version requires the platform to take on a regulated or fiduciary role — that recurrence is itself a finding about the domain. |
