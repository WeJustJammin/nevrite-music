# Feature: Peer Cohort Benchmarking

> **Number**: 23.05.03
> **Parent**: [Career Progression & Benchmarking](./23.05-career-progression-benchmarking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Tell a musician what comparable musicians actually charge, earn and do — because right now they have no reference class at all. Session rates are folklore passed around in WhatsApp groups; a drummer undercharges for a decade because nobody ever told them the number. The platform sees real rates and real income across thousands of comparable people, which makes it the only party that could ever answer "am I underpriced?" honestly. It is also the feature most likely to hurt someone if the aggregation is careless.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The target user. A working musician with no manager and no mentor has literally no reference class — this is the feature that tells them their day rate is 30% below comparable players in their city, which is information nobody in their life has. |
| Producer | Full | Benchmarks on rates, project values and client mix. Their reference class is smaller and more specialised, which makes cohorts thinner and re-identification easier — the same feature is more dangerous for them. |
| Operator | Reports | Room-rate and occupancy benchmarks are a genuinely different product — closer to 22 Analytics' market intelligence than to a career question. Reports-only reflects that this feature does not really describe them (parent D-04, Q-02). |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No professional stake, and nothing here is fan-facing. |

## Behavior

Cohorts are formed from career-stage signals the timeline supplies (`#CX-03`) — instrument/role, years active, region, activity level, income band. Benchmarks are **aggregates with a minimum cohort size**, below which nothing is shown at all. Computation belongs to 22 Analytics; the career framing belongs here (parent D-04).

### Happy Path

1. Musician is a session drummer, 6 years active, London, ~40 sessions/year.
2. Cohort forms from comparable players; the cohort is large enough to aggregate safely.
3. They see a distribution of day rates — not a single "market rate", because a single number is a lie about a distribution.
4. They are at the 25th percentile.
5. They raise their rate on their next quote (23.03.01), and the platform never told them what to charge — it told them where they sit.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cohort is too small | **Re-identification.** Four session drummers in one city is four named people; one of them can work out the others' rates | Hard minimum cohort size, enforced. Show nothing rather than something. This is a privacy control, not a UX preference |
| Cold start — not enough users on the platform | The feature simply cannot exist until density arrives. It is a **network-effect** feature in a domain of standalone ones | Honest absence. This alone argues `could`, not `should` |
| The multi-hyphenate does not fit one cohort | The central persona is a drummer *and* a mixer *and* a teacher — cohorting them as "a drummer" is wrong | Per-role cohorts, never one identity-level cohort. Fitting them into one bucket repeats the archetype failure `meta/personas.md` names as fatal |
| Benchmark reveals the user is **overpaid** | Nobody wants this, and it may cost them work if they act on it | Honest in both directions, or the feature is flattery |
| Benchmark data drives everyone toward the mean | A real second-order effect: publishing rates could **compress** them, not raise them. Buyers benefit from transparency too, and the platform is host to both sides | Genuinely unresolved — see DT-03. The feature could lower musicians' rates and thereby harm the users it was built for |
| Aggregating one user's private income into another's benchmark | A use they did not obviously consent to at signup (`#CX-03`) | Consent/purpose-limitation question — GDPR, not manners |
| Rates differ by genre, city, client type, union status | A single benchmark is meaningless across those splits | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — how many dimensions before the cohort is too small to show? |
| Users game the data by inflating declared income | Trust tiering (23.01.02) — benchmarks should use **verified** income only | Declared income excluded from aggregates |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Cohort forming | Skeleton |
| Empty | Cohort below minimum | "Not enough comparable data" — and this will be the normal state for a long time, so it must be a dignified state, not a failure |
| Populated | Cohort sufficient | Distribution with the user's position, never a single number |
| Error | Aggregation failed | Nothing shown; a wrong benchmark changes what someone charges for a year |
| Partial | Some dimensions available (city yes, genre no) | Explicit about what the cohort is and is not |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The value is showing musicians the market rate | The obvious framing | ❌ REJECTED on two grounds. First, there is **no single rate** — there is a distribution, and collapsing it to a number is a lie that would anchor a whole profession. Second, the useful output is not the market's number but **the user's position in it**: "you are at the 25th percentile" is actionable; "the market rate is £250" is a fiction that helps nobody and misleads everyone at the tails. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **survivorship bias** to make cohort-derived advice actively dangerous | Any statistician looking at career data: "musicians who broke through played 40 shows a year" says nothing about the far larger number who played 40 shows and did not break through. The platform only sees the people who are here, still active, still earning — the ones who quit are invisible by construction. A benchmark built on survivors and presented as a goal (`#CX-02`) is advice with the failures deleted, and it could cost someone a year of their life. | ✅ CONFIRMED: **descriptive benchmarks only** ("here is where you sit"), never prescriptive ones ("do this to succeed"). This kills the most seductive version of the feature — the "career roadmap based on what worked for others" — and it should be killed, because the data cannot support it and the users cannot check it. |
| DT-03 | Rate transparency helps musicians | The assumption the feature is built on, and it deserves interrogation rather than acceptance | ⏳ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and this is a values decision, not a product one.** Transparency helps the underpriced individual and may harm the profession: published rate distributions give **buyers** a reference class too, and the platform hosts both sides. Every promoter, label and studio on WeJammin would learn the going rate is lower than they were paying some people. The literature on pay transparency is genuinely mixed — it compresses distributions, which lifts the bottom and lowers the top. WeJammin cannot serve buyers and sellers this data neutrally and pretend it is neutral. Escalated as Q-01: **who sees benchmarks?** If the answer is "only the person being benchmarked", the feature is defensible. If buyers see them, the platform has built a wage-suppression tool for the users it claims to serve. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Distributions and positions, never a single "market rate" | See DT-01 — a number is a lie about a distribution |
| D-02 | Descriptive, never prescriptive | See DT-02 — survivorship bias makes prescription unsupportable |
| D-03 | Hard minimum cohort size, enforced; show nothing below it | Small cohorts are named individuals |
| D-04 | Verified income only in aggregates | Declared income is gameable, and a gamed benchmark misleads everyone |
| D-05 | Per-role cohorts, never one identity-level cohort | The multi-hyphenate is three cohorts at once |
