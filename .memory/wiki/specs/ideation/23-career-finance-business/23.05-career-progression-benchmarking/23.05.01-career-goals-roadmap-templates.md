# Feature: Career Goals & Roadmap Templates

> **Number**: 23.05.01
> **Parent**: [Career Progression & Benchmarking](./23.05-career-progression-benchmarking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Goals expressed as **queries over real platform activity**, not as a to-do list: "play 20 shows this year", "earn £2,000/month from sessions", "get 5 credits on released records". Roadmap templates are preset bundles of such goals for a recognisable situation (going full-time, first release, building a session career). This is the feature the parent index calls out by name as the thing that usually dies — `idea.md`'s "Career Planning: goal setting, milestone tracking", which as described is a generic goal tracker nobody opens twice.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets goals spanning the whole portfolio — shows, sessions, income, credits — which is the one thing no single-purpose tool can do, because no single-purpose tool sees the portfolio. Never updates them; they update themselves (`#CX-01`). |
| Producer | Full | Goals are business-shaped rather than aspirational: income mix, client quality, credit quality. Their meaningful goal is usually "fewer, better-paid projects", which is a query over 23.01 and 02 rather than a count of anything. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue does not have a career — it has a business with a P&L (23.07) and an occupancy problem. A career roadmap for a room would be nonsense, and offering one would signal the product does not understand them. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no professional stake, no career. |

## Behavior

A goal is a **target plus a derivation** — the query that decides whether it is met. If a goal cannot be expressed as a query over events the platform already records, it is not offerable (parent D-03). Progress and completion are computed, never stored and never manually ticked.

### Happy Path

1. Musician picks the "going full-time" roadmap template.
2. It seeds goals grounded in what the platform can actually see: monthly income from music ≥ £X (23.01), ≥ N shows/quarter (17), ≥ N sessions/quarter (05).
3. They adjust the numbers and set no reminders — there is nothing to maintain.
4. Over the year, income events and bookings land; goals advance on their own.
5. The income goal completes; they are told. They never once opened the goals screen to update it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The goal is about something the platform cannot see (off-platform gigs, a publishing deal) | Cannot be tracked honestly. This is the sub-domain's structural limit — the platform only knows the part of the career that happens here | Either not offerable, or explicitly partial. **Never** a progress bar over data we do not have |
| A goal is met by activity WeJammin does not see | Progress bar says 60%, the truth is 100% — actively demoralising and wrong | Argues that declared/imported data (23.01.02) should count, with the same trust segregation |
| An event is reversed (cancelled show, refunded fee, revoked credit) | A completed, celebrated goal un-completes | Awkward and rare; must not be silently papered over |
| User sets an unreachable goal | The platform is not their manager and should not editorialise on ambition | No opinion offered. Benchmarks (`#CX-02`) inform; they do not judge |
| Goal completion when the user has quietly given up | A cheerful notification on a career someone is grieving | Real, and the reason none of this should be gamified |
| Band-level goals | The band's goals vs the member's are different objects (01) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Template assumes a career shape the user does not have | The multi-hyphenate (`meta/personas.md`) does not fit "artist" or "session player" — they are three things at once | Templates must compose, not choose. A template that forces "are you an artist OR a producer?" fails the central persona immediately |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Deriving progress | Skeleton |
| Empty | No goals | Templates offered. This is the **only** moment this feature gets attention — it must earn the whole relationship here |
| Populated | Goals active | Progress derived live from real activity |
| Error | Derivation failed | No progress shown rather than a wrong one. A wrong progress number on a career goal is worse than a blank |
| Partial | Goal partly derivable (some sources unseen) | Explicit "based on activity we can see" — never a confident bar over a partial view |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Goal tracking is a legitimate standalone feature | `idea.md`'s framing — "Career Planning: goal setting, milestone tracking" | ❌ REJECTED, and the parent index rejects it first: a generic goal tracker is something nobody opens twice. The **only** version that survives is one where the platform already holds the evidence, which is exactly why this cannot be built before the domains that generate the activity (17, 05, 02, 23.01). It is a **derived** feature and it must ship late or not at all. Building it early produces the dead tracker `idea.md` described. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the meaningful goals to be about **income stability**, not income totals or activity counts | Any working musician: the fear is not "earn more", it is "will there be work in March". Portfolio careers fail on gaps and lumpiness, not on annual totals — you can earn £30k and still not make rent in a bad quarter. A goal framed as "£X/month" is a stability goal wearing a total's clothing, and the platform can see stability (23.01) in a way the musician themselves cannot. | ✅ CONFIRMED: stability-shaped goals are the honest default, and they connect directly to 23.06.04 (runway) and to 23.01.04 DT-02's insight that payer diversity is what underwriters actually assess. The same data point serves the user's anxiety and their mortgage application. |
| DT-03 | Templates should map to career archetypes (artist / session player / producer / teacher) | The obvious information architecture | ❌ REJECTED: it reproduces the **exact UX failure `meta/personas.md` names as immediately fatal** — "any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer OR a seller?' fails them". The Musician persona is a multi-hyphenate holding three roles simultaneously; archetype templates force a choice they cannot make. Templates must **compose** — a session career *and* a teaching income *and* a band — which is harder to design and is the only shape that fits the persona at the structural centre of the product. |
