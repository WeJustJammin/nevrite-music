## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Auto-Milestone Timeline ↔ Career Goals & Roadmap Templates

**Relationship**: This is the cross-cut that decides whether the sub-domain exists. The parent's rationale — and D-03 — say a manually-maintained goal dies in a fortnight. The only thing that saves goals is that the platform already knows whether they happened: shows played (17), credits earned (02), income reached (23.01), releases shipped (12). The timeline is the evidence stream; goals are a query over it. Without this link, goals are a to-do list in a finance product and should be cut.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: goals span the whole portfolio — shows, sessions, income, releases — which is exactly why no single-purpose tool can track them and why fragmentation (`meta/problem-statement.md`) is the reason this has never worked before.
- **Producer**: goals are more business-shaped (income, client mix, credit quality) than aspirational.
- **Operator**: no goals — a room has a P&L, not a career (parent Role Matrix).
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The timeline owns events; goals own targets and their derivation. Goals never write events — a "completed" goal is a computed state, not a stored one.
2. **Trigger chain**: Event lands → goals re-derive → completion may fire a notification. If a goal cannot be expressed as a query over real events, it should not be offerable (D-03).
3. **Permission intersection**: Goals are private; the timeline is partly public (`#CX-03` and 23.05.02 DT-03). A goal must never leak through a public timeline — "3 shows away from their target" is nobody's business.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Goal completion is a positive notification, and one of very few in domain 23.
5. **State transition conflict**: An event being reversed (a cancelled show, a revoked credit) un-completes a goal that was already celebrated. Rare, awkward, and must not be papered over.

### CX-02: Peer Cohort Benchmarking ↔ Career Goals & Roadmap Templates

**Relationship**: Templates seed goals; benchmarks make the seeds honest. A roadmap template written by the platform is opinion; a template grounded in what comparable musicians actually did is evidence. This is the difference between a career-advice blog post and something worth opening.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the target — a working musician with no manager and no mentor has no reference class at all.
- **Producer**: reference class is smaller and more specialised, which makes cohorting harder (23.05.03 Q-01).
- **Operator**: reports only — venue benchmarks are a different product (occupancy, yield) and closer to 22.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — deferred per template guidance. The core unresolved risk: a benchmark-derived goal encodes **survivorship bias**. "Musicians who broke through played 40 shows a year" says nothing about the many who played 40 shows and did not. Presenting that as a goal is not neutral — it is advice, dressed as data, that could cost someone a year. See 23.05.03 DT-02.

### CX-03: Auto-Milestone Timeline ↔ Peer Cohort Benchmarking

**Relationship**: Cohorting requires a machine-readable definition of "career stage", and the timeline is where that definition would come from — years active, credit count, show count, release count, income band. This makes the timeline load-bearing for benchmarking rather than merely decorative, and it means the timeline's data is **aggregated across users**, which its Role Matrix (`👁️ Read-only` for Fans on the public half) does not make obvious.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their timeline both receives benchmarks and contributes to everyone else's cohort.
- **Producer**: same.
- **Operator**: contributes nothing to musician cohorts.
- **Fan**: contributes nothing; sees only public artist timelines.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The timeline owns per-user events; benchmarking owns aggregates. Aggregates must never be reversible to an individual — with a small cohort, they trivially are.
2. **Trigger chain**: Timeline events → cohort membership → aggregate stats. Cohort membership changes as a career moves, so a user's benchmark shifts under them without them doing anything.
3. **Permission intersection**: **The consent question.** A user's private career data feeding other users' benchmarks is a use they did not obviously agree to by signing up to a booking platform. This is a GDPR purpose-limitation issue, not a nicety.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Deferred — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: Small cohorts are re-identifiable; a "cohort" of four session drummers in one city is four named people, and one of them can work out the other three's rates.
