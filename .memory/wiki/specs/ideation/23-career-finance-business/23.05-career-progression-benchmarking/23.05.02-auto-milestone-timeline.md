# Feature: Auto-Milestone Timeline

> **Number**: 23.05.02
> **Parent**: [Career Progression & Benchmarking](./23.05-career-progression-benchmarking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The career, assembled from facts the platform already holds: first credit, first paid session, first headline show, first release, first sync, the year income crossed a threshold, the record that changed things. Nobody maintains it — that is the entire point, and the reason it might survive where `idea.md`'s "milestone tracking" would not. It is also the closest thing in domain 23 to the platform thesis: the proof of a career, accumulated rather than reconstructed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their career as a record rather than a memory. For the multi-hyphenate whose work is scattered across six platforms and whose proof evaporates (`meta/problem-statement.md`), this is the accumulation they have never had — and controls what part of it is public. |
| Producer | Full | Their timeline is credits and projects — the discography they currently reconstruct from memory for every new client. |
| Operator | Read-only | A venue's timeline is shows hosted, not a career. Genuinely different content; they read the *artist's* public timeline when deciding whether to book them, which is a real use and the reason this is not `❌ None`. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The only `👁️` a Fan holds in domain 23.** An artist's public timeline is fan-facing content — the story of the act. They see the public projection; nothing financial, nothing unreleased, nothing the artist has not chosen to show. |

## Behavior

Milestones are **derived from events in other domains** — credits (02), shows (17), releases (12), income thresholds (23.01) — never entered by hand. Each milestone has a private form (complete, factual) and a public projection (curated, artist-controlled). The two are one entity with a visibility rule, not two stores.

### Happy Path

1. Musician plays their first headline show; domain 17 records it.
2. A milestone derives itself: "first headline show — The Lexington, 2026-09-14."
3. It appears on the private timeline immediately, with no action from anyone.
4. The Musician toggles it public; it joins their artist profile story (01/20).
5. Years later it is still there, evidenced by the booking record — not remembered, not claimed.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The career started before WeJammin | The timeline begins mid-story, which for an established musician makes the platform look ignorant of who they are | Declared/imported history (23.01.02, 02) fills it as `declared`. The trust tiering that protects the income statement here mostly just looks like honesty |
| A milestone is embarrassing or unwanted (a project the artist has disowned) | The private timeline is factual; the public one is curated | Hide from public, always. Deleting from the private record is a harder question — it is *evidence*, and this is a provenance platform |
| An event is reversed (cancelled show, revoked credit) | The milestone derives away | Rare; must not silently rewrite a public story a fan already saw |
| "First" milestones are wrong because earlier work is off-platform | "First credit" is only first *here* | Framing must say "on WeJammin" or it is a false claim — and a false claim on a provenance platform is a self-inflicted wound |
| A milestone reveals a private fact (income crossing a threshold) | Income milestones must default private, hard | The public/private split is load-bearing; a leak here publishes someone's earnings |
| The timeline is used as a **credential** by a booker or a client | Then it needs the same verified/declared distinction as 23.01.04 | Verified milestones cite their source event; declared ones say so |
| Fan sees a timeline of an artist who has quit | A career artefact becomes a memorial | Real; no product answer offered here |
| Two artists dispute the same milestone (whose headline show was it) | Derives from the booking record, which has one answer | Evidence beats claim — the platform's whole reason for existing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Deriving | Skeleton |
| Empty | New user, no events | Honest — the timeline starts when the platform starts seeing the work. Prompt to import history |
| Populated | Events exist | Chronological, grouped by year, with public/private markers |
| Error | Derivation failed | No timeline rather than a partial one presented as complete — a gap in a career record reads as a gap in the career |
| Partial | Some domains unseen | "Based on activity we can see" |

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A timeline is a nice visualisation of the credits graph | The obvious framing — and the reason it looks cuttable | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **input to cohorting** (`#CX-03`). "Career stage" has no machine-readable definition anywhere else in the product, and without it 23.05.03 cannot form a cohort and 23.05.01 cannot suggest a grounded goal. The decorative-looking feature is load-bearing for the two features that carry the sub-domain's value — which is not obvious from its name and is exactly the kind of thing a MoSCoW cut would get wrong. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the timeline's real user to be **someone else** — a booker, a client, a label — not the musician | Any working musician: they do not need reminding what they did. What they need is to stop re-proving it. `meta/personas.md` names this precisely — "every new client requires re-proving who they are and what they've played on", and success is "credits are cited rather than claimed". The timeline's value is as a **credential surface**, not a nostalgia surface. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it relocates the feature: it belongs as much to 01 Identity and 03 Community as to a finance domain. That is parent Q-03 restated with force — if the public timeline is the artist profile, this feature is in the wrong domain, and 23 keeps only the private career-stage derivation that feeds cohorting. |
| DT-03 | Public and private timelines are two features | Naive separation, and the tidier architecture | ❌ REJECTED: one entity, one visibility rule. Two stores would drift, and a drifted public timeline is a false claim on a platform whose entire thesis is provable truth. But the rejection creates the feature's central design problem: the same object is a **private career artefact** (income thresholds, unreleased projects, disowned work) and a **public artist story** — with a Fan reading one projection and an underwriter-adjacent use reading the other. Getting the visibility rule wrong publishes someone's earnings. Load-bearing, and the reason the Fan `👁️` in the parent Role Matrix deserves scrutiny rather than a shrug. |
