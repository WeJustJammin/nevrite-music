# Feature: Cross-Border Withholding & Treaty Reclaim

> **Number**: 23.02.04
> **Parent**: [Expenses & Tax Readiness](./23.02-expenses-tax-readiness-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A musician who plays abroad is taxed at source before they are paid: the US withholds 30% from foreign performers by default, the UK's Foreign Entertainers Unit withholds on non-resident performances, Germany and most of Europe do the same. Under double-taxation treaties much of that is reclaimable or creditable — and almost nobody reclaims it, because the paperwork is per-country, deadline-bound, and denominated in a currency and a language the musician does not work in. This is money the user has already earned, already lost, and could get back.

## Behavior

Withholding is recorded as a **deduction on the income row** (23.01.01's gross/deductions/net decomposition), not as an expense (`#R-01` — the intuitive instinct is the wrong one). Where a treaty applies, the feature identifies the reclaim or credit route, the deadline, and the evidence required. The platform **prepares**; it does not file (parent D-03).

### Happy Path

1. Musician plays a German festival for €3,000; the promoter withholds and pays €3,000 minus the withholding.
2. The income event lands with gross €3,000, deductions (withholding) and net — all converted at one rate/date (23.01.03 DT-03), or the row does not balance.
3. Feature identifies a UK–Germany treaty position and a reclaim route with a statutory deadline.
4. It assembles the evidence it already holds — the booking (17), the contract, the withholding figure — and lists what it does not.
5. The claim is prepared for the user or their accountant; the treaty credit appears in the tax pack (`#CX-03`).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reclaim deadline passes | The money is gone permanently — a statutory window, not a policy | Deadline alerting is the feature's core value, not a nicety. A missed window is unrecoverable |
| No treaty between the pair | No reclaim; may still be a credit at home | Honest — some withholding is simply lost |
| Treaty relief available **at source** if claimed in advance | Far better than reclaiming afterwards, and requires action **before** the show — which means this feature must reach backwards into booking (17), not just report after the fact | Pre-show prompt; this is the highest-leverage moment |
| Withholding certificate never provided by the promoter | The claim usually needs it; the musician must chase a foreign counterparty | Evidence gap named, chase prompt |
| Withheld amount misclassified as an expense | Understates relief; the user overpays | Prevented structurally — withholding lives on the income row (`#R-01`) |
| Band entity performed, individuals taxed | Withholding and reclaim entitlement may sit differently for the entity and the members | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — genuinely complex and jurisdiction-dependent |
| User's residency is ambiguous or dual | Treaty position may be undeterminable by the platform | Refuse to assert a position; flag for the accountant |
| Reclaim succeeds; refund arrives | A new income event? A negative deduction? It restates a possibly-filed year (`#CX-03`) | Must be modelled explicitly, not improvised |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Withheld foreign tax is a cost of touring, so it is an expense | The intuitive framing, and the wrong one | ❌ REJECTED: it is tax **already paid** on the user's behalf — a credit against home liability under a treaty, not a deductible cost. Treating it as an expense understates relief and the user overpays; ignoring it double-taxes them. Structurally prevented by keeping withholding on the income row. Recorded as rejected pair `#R-01` because the wrong instinct is the natural one. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **relief at source** to beat reclaim, which means this feature must act before the show | Anyone who has toured: reclaiming from a foreign treasury takes months to years and often fails on evidence. Claiming the treaty rate *in advance* (a US Central Withholding Agreement, a UK FEU reduced-rate application) means the money is never withheld at all. That requires action at **booking** time — weeks before the income event this feature is nominally about. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it relocates the feature's centre of gravity: the highest-value moment is in domain 17/18 (booking and advancing a foreign show), not in the tax sub-domain. Strong argument that a booking-time trigger is mandatory, recorded as Q-01. A tax feature that only ever looks backwards captures the smaller half of the value. |
| DT-03 | This is a niche feature for a handful of touring professionals | Scope-cutting instinct — and it is why the MoSCoW proposal is `could`, not `should` | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED: the affected population is narrow (musicians who work across borders) but the per-user value is the highest in the domain — often four figures of recoverable money per year — and **no competitor touches it**, because it requires music-specific knowledge (performance withholding is an entertainer-specific tax regime that generic accounting software has no reason to encode). Narrow and deep, not thin. The honest tension is that it is also the most expensive rule work per user served. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Reads withholding from **23.01.01 Income Event Ledger** (deductions field) — never from an expense row (`#R-01`).
- Feeds **23.02.03 Tax Pack** as **credits**, not costs — see `23.02-expenses-tax-readiness-cx.md#CX-03`.
- Shares a rules-engine question with **23.02.02** — treaty-pair rules are two-dimensional where deduction rules are one (`#CX-04`).
- Touches **23.01.03 Multi-Currency & FX** — the withholding is in the foreign currency; gross/deductions/net must convert at one rate/date or the row will not balance (23.01.03 DT-03).
- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** and **18 Show Production & Touring** — see DT-02; relief at source is a **booking-time** action. Also the mirror duty: an Operator booking a foreign act may be the withholding agent.
- Touches **23.04 Deal & Contract Vault** — the performance contract usually specifies who bears the withholding, and that clause decides who actually loses the money.
