## Overview

The other half of the financial picture: what went out, what is deductible, and what the tax authority needs by January. Because 23.01 already holds the income side and the platform already knows the shape of the work (a tour, a session, a gear purchase), the domain rationale's claim that "the tax pack largely generates itself" is credible — but only for the sole-trader case, and only where jurisdiction rules are actually encoded rather than assumed.

**Interacting capabilities** (what justifies sub-domain status): expense capture; deduction categorization whose rules are dictated by jurisdiction; the tax pack that assembles income + expenses into an accountant-ready artefact; and cross-border withholding reclaim, which is simultaneously an expense problem, an income problem and a treaty problem.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Expenses and tax merged into one sub-domain rather than two | They are not independent: the deduction category set is *defined by* the tax jurisdiction, and the tax pack is meaningless without the expense side. Splitting them yields two nodes that cannot be specified without each other. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 — merged candidates 02 and 03 |
| D-02 | Scope is **sole trader / self-employed**, not incorporated-company accounting | The Musician and Producer personas are self-employed portfolio earners. Corporation tax, payroll and statutory accounts are a different product (Xero/QuickBooks) and a losing fight. This is the honest boundary; it is also why Operator access is reduced across this sub-domain. | Agent, persona analysis |
| D-03 | WeJammin produces an **accountant-ready pack**, it does not file returns | Filing is a regulated activity in most jurisdictions and an unbounded per-jurisdiction integration cost. Generating the pack captures most of the value at a fraction of the exposure and liability. | Agent, Deep Think DT-01 on 23.02.03 |
