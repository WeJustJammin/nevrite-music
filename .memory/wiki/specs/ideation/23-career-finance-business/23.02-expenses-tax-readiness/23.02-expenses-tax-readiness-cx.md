## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Deduction Categorization ↔ Expense & Receipt Capture

**Relationship**: Capture appears to be the primary feature and categorization a downstream nicety. It is the reverse: the jurisdiction rule set decides what categories can even be offered, and a category captured against the wrong vocabulary is worthless. This means capture **cannot ship jurisdiction-neutral** — there is no generic "expenses" product that later gets tax rules bolted on.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the most category-ambiguous spend in the product — strings, a guitar, a rehearsal room, a train to a gig, a laptop that is 60% work. Nearly every item needs apportionment or a rule.
- **Producer**: cleaner categories (studio time, plugins, hardware) but higher values and higher capital-vs-expense stakes.
- **Operator**: business-shaped costs (rent, licences, staff) that their existing accountant already categorizes — which is why Operator is Config only.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The rule engine owns the vocabulary; the expense row owns the chosen category. A jurisdiction change re-maps historic categories and may orphan some — the row must survive re-mapping.
2. **Trigger chain**: Capture → suggest category by rule → user confirms. If no rule matches, the row is `uncategorized`, which is a real state, not an error.
3. **Permission intersection**: A delegated accountant should be able to re-categorize; the user should see that they did. Re-categorization by a delegate is the single most likely delegate action in the sub-domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Deferred — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: Re-categorizing a row inside a period already exported (CX-02) must supersede the export, not silently alter it.

### CX-02: Expense & Receipt Capture ↔ Tax Pack Generation

**Relationship**: The pack is an assembly, not a computation of its own — it is only as good as the rows underneath. The pack's honest job is to be explicit about what is missing (uncategorized rows, receipts without evidence, unresolved FX from 23.01.03, unadjudicated duplicates from 23.01.02) rather than to produce a confident-looking total over incomplete data.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: generates the pack for their accountant or their own filing; most likely to have gaps.
- **Producer**: same flow.
- **Operator**: reports export into their existing books (D-02).
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pack is an issued snapshot (same posture as 23.01.04). Expense rows keep moving; the pack does not.
2. **Trigger chain**: Generate → validate completeness → block or warn. A pack must **refuse** to present a final figure while blocking gaps exist.
3. **Permission intersection**: Handoff to an accountant is a delegated-access grant scoped to a period — the clearest real use of the Representation cross-cut in the whole domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Late income/expense arriving after a pack was handed off must notify the accountant, not just the user.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two supersessions racing (a user edit and a late platform event) — last-writer-wins on the snapshot is acceptable because snapshots are immutable and additive.

### CX-03: Cross-Border Withholding ↔ Tax Pack Generation

**Relationship**: Withheld foreign tax is money the user earned and never received, already paid to a foreign treasury on their behalf. It is a **credit** against home-country liability under a double-taxation treaty — not an expense. Getting this wrong is not a rounding error: treating it as an expense understates the relief and the user overpays; ignoring it entirely means they are taxed twice on the same money.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the affected persona — foreign performance income is withheld at source (US 30% default on foreign performers absent a treaty claim; UK FEU; German withholding).
- **Producer**: read-only — their fee may be withheld from, but the reclaim mechanics attach to the performer/payee of record.
- **Operator**: not affected — a room does not tour.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The withholding figure originates on the income row (23.01.01's deductions field), not on an expense row. Two features read one fact; neither should copy it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Income event with withholding → reclaim candidate → treaty rule → credit line in the pack.
3. **Permission intersection**: Same delegated-accountant path.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Reclaim windows expire (statutory deadlines) — this is an alerting obligation, and a missed window is real money lost forever.
5. **State transition conflict**: A reclaim filed and refunded changes the credit position for a year that may already be filed.

### CX-04: Deduction Categorization ↔ Cross-Border Withholding

**Relationship**: Both are rule engines, which makes them look like one feature. They are not: deduction rules are single-jurisdiction (what can *I* deduct where I live), withholding rules are **treaty-pair** (what does country A withhold from a resident of country B, and what does B's treaty with A allow back). One dimension versus two.

**Role scoping**: Musician only in practice; Producer read-only.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — deferred per template guidance. The open question is whether these share a rules substrate at all, which is an architecture question (see Q-01).
