## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Contract Vault ↔ Key Term Extraction

**Relationship**: Storage is custody; extraction is comprehension. The vault's value is entirely downstream of extraction — a folder of PDFs is what the musician already has (a Dropbox named `FINAL_v3_ACTUAL`, per `meta/personas.md`). Extraction is what turns custody into a product, and it is also where the liability lives (parent Q-02).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: signs deals young, often without a lawyer, and frequently does not know what they agreed — the anti-persona failure in `meta/personas.md` is signing a split sheet you do not understand and finding out years later you gave away your publishing. Extraction is the intervention.
- **Producer**: production agreements and points; more commercially literate on average but with more deals in flight.
- **Operator**: their contracts are commercial-property-shaped; music-deal extraction does not describe them (read-only).
- **Fan**: not affected — no access to this sub-domain.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The document is immutable (it is signed paper). Extracted terms are a derived, correctable layer — the user must be able to fix a bad extraction without touching the document.
2. **Trigger chain**: Upload → extract → user confirms each term. Confirmation is mandatory, not optional: an unconfirmed extraction must never silently drive an alert or a reconciliation finding.
3. **Permission intersection**: A delegated manager or lawyer is exactly who should review extractions — but a manager is also a **counterparty** to the management agreement they would be reviewing. That conflict of interest is real and is why delegation here needs per-document scoping, not blanket access.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Deferred — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: An amended or superseded contract arriving — the old extraction must not keep driving alerts.

### CX-02: Key Term Extraction ↔ Option, Reversion & Obligation Alerts

**Relationship**: This is the chain that carries the sub-domain's value. Extraction finds the dates; alerts fire before them. The asymmetry that defines the design: a false alert costs a minute of attention; a **missed** alert costs a catalogue, permanently. There is no symmetric tuning here — the system must be biased hard toward over-alerting.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the reversion window is the highest-value date in their professional life and the one nobody tells them about — the counterparty has every incentive not to.
- **Producer**: option exercises and delivery obligations.
- **Operator**: licence renewals and lease dates — config only, and generic rather than music-specific.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Extracted terms own the dates; alerts own the schedule and delivery state. Neither writes the other.
2. **Trigger chain**: Term confirmed → alert scheduled → fires with lead time proportional to the action required (a reversion needs months of notice, not a week's).
3. **Permission intersection**: Alerts must reach the user even if a delegate manages the vault — especially when the delegate is the counterparty (CX-01, point 3).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Multi-channel and escalating. A single email for a date worth a catalogue is negligent design.
5. **State transition conflict**: A date passing while the extraction is still unconfirmed — the alert must fire anyway, flagged as unconfirmed. Silence is the one unacceptable outcome.

### CX-03: Key Term Extraction ↔ Deal-to-Rights Reconciliation

**Relationship**: Where the vault meets the platform thesis. Provenance (D-18) says WeJammin holds the ownership record end-to-end (D-10); this cross-cut asks whether that record **agrees with the paper**. Two structured sources — the contract's terms and 09's rights ledger — describing one reality. Disagreement is a finding, and findings here are worth real money.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: discovers their split sheet and their publishing deal say different things — usually years late, usually in a dispute.
- **Producer**: discovers their points were never registered anywhere.
- **Operator**: holds no rights; not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Neither source is authoritative over the other. **The platform must not resolve the disagreement** — it surfaces it. A signed contract and a rights ledger disagreeing is a legal question, not a merge conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: Terms confirmed → compare against 09 → findings. Findings notify but never auto-correct: silently "fixing" a rights record to match a contract could itself transfer ownership.
3. **Permission intersection**: A finding may implicate a counterparty who is also a WeJammin user with their own view of the same rights.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Whether the counterparty is told is a genuinely hard call — see 23.04.04 Q-01.
5. **State transition conflict**: Rights changing in 09 after reconciliation ran — findings must be re-derivable, never cached as truth.

### CX-04: Alerts ↔ Deal-to-Rights Reconciliation

**Relationship**: A reversion date passing should cause the rights position in 09 to change. Very often it does not — the paper reverted the rights and nobody moved the record, so the old owner keeps collecting. That silent failure is detectable only by joining a date (alerts) to a ledger state (reconciliation).

**Role scoping**: Musician and Producer. Operator and Fan not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — deferred per template guidance. The open question is whether the platform can assert that a reversion *happened* (a legal conclusion) or only that a date passed and the record did not change (an observation). The second is defensible; the first is not.
