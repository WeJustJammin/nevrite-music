# Feature: Option, Reversion & Obligation Alerts

> **Number**: 23.04.03
> **Parent**: [Deal & Contract Vault](./23.04-deal-contract-vault-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Shout before a date passes. Music deals are full of dates that transfer value silently: an option window the label must exercise (and you should know if they did not), a reversion window that opens for 90 days and closes forever, a sunset clause that should stop a former manager's commission, a delivery obligation whose breach hands someone else a remedy. Nobody sends a reminder — the counterparty's incentive is usually silence. This feature is the reason the vault exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Alerted, with lead time proportional to the action needed: months for a reversion (it may require a lawyer and a letter), weeks for an option. The single highest-value notification in the entire platform — the difference between owning your catalogue and not. |
| Producer | Full | Option exercises and delivery obligations. Their dates are more operational than existential, but a missed delivery date can forfeit points. |
| Operator | Config | Lease breaks, licence renewals, insurance expiry. Real and useful but generic — this is a calendar with consequences, not music-deal comprehension. Config-only reflects that honest boundary. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no deals, no dates. |

## Behavior

Confirmed dated terms (23.04.02) schedule alerts. Lead time is a function of the **action required**, not a fixed reminder interval. Delivery is escalating and multi-channel, and the design is deliberately asymmetric: over-alerting is cheap, silence is catastrophic.

Concretely:

- **Lead-time bands scale with the action.** A reversion (needs a lawyer + a letter) schedules alerts at roughly 6 months, 3 months, 1 month, on-open, and inside the window. An option exercise (needs a decision, maybe a call) schedules weeks out. An operator lease break / insurance renewal schedules on a generic calendar interval. The bands are seeded per term-type at extraction time (23.04.02) and are user-adjustable.
- **Escalation is per-alert, not per-schedule.** Each scheduled point starts in-app, and if not acknowledged escalates to email, then to push (once mobile exists — `meta/constraints.md` open surface question). The highest-consequence points (reversion opening, final window) escalate hardest; a positive/counterparty-lapse alert (DT-02) informs but does not escalate — there is no deadline to miss.
- **Acknowledgement, not action, closes an alert.** The system cannot verify the user acted (that is reconciliation's job — 23.04.04); it can only record that the user was told and that they saw it. An acknowledged alert stops escalating but stays on the timeline until its date passes.
- **Every alert states the consequence, not just the date.** "Option window closes in 30 days" is insufficient; the alert names what is lost if the window closes and, where a route exists, links to it (a music lawyer via 05 — DT-03).

### Happy Path

1. Extraction finds and the Musician confirms a reversion window opening 2029-03-01, open for 90 days.
2. The system schedules alerts working backwards from the action, not from the date: six months out (start finding a lawyer), three months, one month, on opening, and inside the window.
3. Six months out, the first alert fires and explains what the window is and what happens if it closes.
4. Musician acts inside the window.
5. Reconciliation (23.04.04) then checks whether the rights actually moved in 09 — because a reversion exercised on paper and never recorded is still a loss (`#CX-04`).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The date passes with no alert (extraction missed the clause) | **The catastrophic failure.** Unrecoverable, and the user believed they were covered — the platform's presence made them stop worrying | Mitigated only by absence-reporting (23.04.02 DT-02) and by manual entry being first-class. A vault that silently misses a reversion is worse than no vault, because it displaced the user's own vigilance |
| Extraction unconfirmed when the date approaches | Fire anyway, flagged as unconfirmed | Silence is the one unacceptable outcome (`#CX-02`) |
| User ignores the alerts | Escalate; then record that they were told | The platform cannot act for them |
| Alert requires action the user cannot afford (a lawyer) | Common and demoralising — knowing does not equal being able | A `05 Services Marketplace` path to a music lawyer is the honest completion of this feature |
| The counterparty's option **lapses** — good news for the user | Nobody tells the artist when a label fails to exercise. It is value falling into their lap unnoticed | A positive alert, not just a warning. Genuinely novel and nobody asked for it |
| Date depends on an event, not a calendar (reversion on delivery + N years) | Requires knowing the event happened, which may live in 12 Release & Distribution | Event-triggered dates are a real class and materially harder than calendar dates |
| User leaves WeJammin | The alerts stop and the user does not know they stopped | Honest limit; touches the earned-vs-hostile lock-in question (`meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02) — an alert you only get by staying is uncomfortably close to hostile |
| Band deal — who gets alerted? | All members, or the entity's admin? A member who is not told loses the same rights | Entity fan-out, defaulting to everyone affected |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Schedule computing | Skeleton |
| Empty | No dated terms | Honest: "no dates tracked" — and if that is because extraction found none, say so; it is a warning, not a clean bill |
| Populated | Dates scheduled | Timeline of upcoming dates with consequences stated |
| Error | Scheduler failed | **Must fail loudly.** A silently broken alert scheduler is indistinguishable from having no dates, which is exactly the state the user must never be lulled into |
| Partial | Some terms confirmed, some not | Unconfirmed dates alert anyway, flagged |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a reminder feature; the design question is notification frequency | The obvious framing | ❌ REJECTED: it is an **asymmetric-risk** feature. A false alert costs a minute; a missed one costs a catalogue, permanently, with no remedy at any price. That asymmetry means over-alerting is the correct bias, loud failure is mandatory, and lead time must scale with the action (a reversion needs months because it needs a lawyer, not because the date is important). Tuning this like a normal notification system optimises the wrong quantity. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **counterparty's silence to be adversarial**, which changes what we alert on | Any music lawyer: when a label fails to exercise an option, or a reversion window opens, the counterparty's interest is that the artist does not notice. There is no neutral party sending reminders — the whole industry runs on the artist not knowing. This means the valuable alerts are the ones about **the other side's inaction**, which no artist currently tracks because they have never had visibility into it. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it produces a feature nobody proposed: **positive alerts** — "your label did not exercise; those rights are yours." That is value the platform hands the user that literally nobody else in their life will tell them, and it is the sharpest expression of whose side the product is on. |
| DT-03 | Alerting is enough — tell the user and the job is done | Comfortable assumption | ❌ REJECTED: knowing and being able are different things. A reversion window that needs a lawyer's letter is useless to a musician with £400 in the bank — and this is the persona whose whole problem is a portfolio of small income streams. Without a route to action (a music lawyer via 05, a template letter, or a cost), the alert is a notification that you are about to lose something and can do nothing about it. That is arguably a worse user experience than not knowing. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Over-alert deliberately; lead time scales with the action required | See DT-01 — the risk is asymmetric and so is the design |
| D-02 | Unconfirmed dates alert anyway, flagged | Silence is the only unacceptable outcome |
| D-03 | Positive alerts on counterparty inaction are first-class | See DT-02 — nobody else will ever tell the artist |
| D-04 | Alert scheduler failures must be loud | A silent scheduler is indistinguishable from safety |
