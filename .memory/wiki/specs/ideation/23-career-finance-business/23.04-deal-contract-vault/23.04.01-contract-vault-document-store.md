# Feature: Contract Vault & Document Store

> **Number**: 23.04.01
> **Parent**: [Deal & Contract Vault](./23.04-deal-contract-vault-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Custody of the paper that governs a career — management, agency, label, publishing, production, endorsement — in one place that survives a lost laptop, a dead email account and a former manager who has the only copy. This is deliberately the **thin** feature of the sub-domain: the storage mechanics are a platform cross-cut (parent D-03), and what is owned here is the deal-shaped organisation of documents that everything downstream reads.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Uploads the deals they have signed, often years after signing and often without ever having read them. The archetypal case in `meta/personas.md` — a split sheet or a publishing deal agreed young whose consequences surface later. Frequently does not have a copy at all, which is itself a finding. |
| Producer | Full | Production agreements, points letters, work-for-hire terms. More deals in flight, more of them half-papered — the "we'll sort it out later" that never gets sorted, which is their named accidental failure mode. |
| Operator | Full | Lease, licences to operate, supplier and insurance contracts. Real documents with real dates, but commercial-property-shaped rather than music-shaped — they get custody and alerting, not music-deal comprehension. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Fans sign nothing. No document surface should appear in a Fan context. |

## Behavior

Documents are stored **immutably** — signed paper cannot be edited — with versioning for amendments and supersession. Each document is typed (management / agency / label / publishing / production / endorsement / other), scoped to an entity (person or Band), and linked to its counterparties. Storage, versioning, retention and e-signature are consumed from the platform cross-cut, not built here.

Concretely, what this feature owns:

- **Accepted inputs**: a signed instrument in any of the forms musicians actually hold it — a born-digital PDF, a scan of a wet-signed page, or a phone photo of a page. Input form is captured and carried forward because it governs downstream extraction quality (a photo is not a searchable PDF, and the feature must say so rather than pretend).
- **The deal, not the file** (D-02): a document is one node in a **version chain**. An amendment, side letter, or renegotiation is appended as a new version that references what it supersedes; nothing is ever overwritten or deleted from the chain. The current effective instrument is the head of the chain; earlier versions remain visible and retrievable.
- **Typing and entity scope**: every document carries a type and is scoped to exactly one entity — a person or a Band (per 01's entity model). Band-scoped documents are the band's, and member access follows the entity, not the uploader.
- **Counterparty links**: each document names its counterparties (label, manager, agency, co-writer). Where a counterparty is themselves a WeJammin user, the link is to that identity; otherwise it is a named external party.
- **Custody is the whole job here**: comprehension (extraction), scheduling (alerts), and cross-checking (reconciliation) are separate features (23.04.02–04) that read this store. This feature does not read the *contents* of the paper — it guarantees the paper exists, is intact, is typed, and is findable four years later.

### Happy Path

1. Musician signs a management agreement and receives a PDF.
2. They upload it, type it as `management`, name the counterparty.
3. It is stored immutably against their identity, not their laptop.
4. Extraction (23.04.02) proposes key terms; alerts (23.04.03) follow from confirmed dates.
5. Four years later, when the sunset clause matters, the document is still there and still findable.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User does not have a copy of their own deal | Extremely common, and a real finding — the counterparty holds the only copy | Honest: the vault cannot conjure it. A "request a copy from your counterparty" path is the only product answer, and it is a socially loaded ask |
| Contract is amended by a side letter or an email | Amendments are versions, not replacements; the chain matters | Version chain with supersession explicit |
| Document is a photo of a page, not a PDF | Common for older deals | Accepted; extraction quality degrades, and it must say so rather than guess |
| Contract belongs to a **Band entity** | Band deals are the band's, and members' access follows the entity model (01) | Entity scoping; a departing member's access is a genuinely hard question |
| A member leaves the band | Do they keep access to the deals they were party to? Legally yes, probably; practically the platform must decide | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — collides with 01's entity model |
| The counterparty is the user's **manager**, who is also their delegate | The delegate can read the agreement that governs them, and could delete it | Per-document delegation scoping; the vault must not be manager-administered by default (`#CX-01`) |
| Deletion request under GDPR | A contract is evidence with retention obligations that outlive the relationship | Collides with 23.01 Q-03; deletion of a mutual contract is not the user's decision alone |
| Document contains counterparties' PII | It is a contract; of course it does | Storage is cross-cut; PII handling follows platform rules |
| Two amendments to the same deal uploaded near-simultaneously (e.g. user and delegate both act) | The version chain must stay a single ordered lineage — two heads is a corrupted deal | Both are recorded; the chain surfaces the ambiguity for the user to order, and never silently picks a winner. Head-of-chain resolution deep-cases at `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` |
| The wrong file is uploaded (a receipt, a press photo, an unrelated PDF) or the "contract" is not one | Custody cannot detect intent, and typing is user-asserted | Accepted and stored under the user's chosen type; a mistyped or non-contract document is a correction, not an error — the user can retype or supersede it. Extraction (23.04.02) is where "this doesn't read like a management agreement" surfaces |
| A very large or malformed/encrypted file, or an upload that fails partway | Real for scanned multi-hundred-page deals and password-locked label PDFs | Rejected cleanly with the reason (too large / unreadable / locked); never a partial or truncated document enters the chain (see Error state) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Opening the vault | Skeleton |
| Empty | No documents | Not a generic empty state — a prompt naming the deals a musician typically has, because many users do not realise they *have* deals to upload |
| Populated | Documents stored | Grouped by type and entity, with extraction status |
| Error | Upload failed | Retry; never a partial or silently truncated document |
| Partial | Stored but unextracted | Custody without comprehension — honest about what the vault can and cannot do for it yet |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The vault is the feature; storing contracts safely is the value | The obvious framing | ❌ REJECTED: storage is a solved commodity and the musician already has Dropbox. A vault that only stores is `FINAL_v3_ACTUAL` with a nicer logo. All the value is downstream in extraction, alerting and reconciliation — this feature is deliberately thin and exists to serve them. It also means storage mechanics are a **cross-cut** (parent D-03), not a build. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the most common state to be **"the artist does not have the contract"** | Any music lawyer: the single most frequent problem is that the artist never received, never kept, or cannot find their own agreement — the label or the manager holds the only executed copy, and asking for it signals distrust. A vault that assumes the user has their paper is designed for a case that often does not exist. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it produces a feature nobody proposed: a **request-a-copy** path, and — more interestingly — the observation that if both parties were on WeJammin the document would have been deposited at signature and this whole problem would not exist. That is exactly the platform thesis (`meta/problem-statement.md`: be present when the fact becomes true) applied to contracts instead of credits. Recorded as Q-01: execution-at-signature may be the real product, and custody merely the migration path for the pre-platform past. |
| DT-03 | Immutability is straightforward — store the PDF and never change it | Naive | ❌ REJECTED: contracts are amended by side letters, superseded by renegotiations, and partially varied by email. The **document** is immutable; the **deal** is not. Modelling a deal as one file is wrong: a deal is a chain of instruments over time, and the current effective terms are a computed view over that chain. This directly complicates extraction (which terms are live?) and alerts (which dates still apply?). |
