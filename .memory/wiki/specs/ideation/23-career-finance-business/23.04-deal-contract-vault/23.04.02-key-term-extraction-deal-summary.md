# Feature: Key Term Extraction & Deal Summary

> **Number**: 23.04.02
> **Parent**: [Deal & Contract Vault](./23.04-deal-contract-vault-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turn a signed contract into a structured deal summary a musician can actually understand: the term, the territory, the commission rate, the option windows, the reversion trigger, the sunset clause, the delivery obligations. This is the feature that answers "what did I actually agree to?" — a question musicians routinely cannot answer about their own careers — and it is simultaneously the highest-liability feature in the domain, because being confidently wrong about a contract term is worse than saying nothing.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Gets a plain-language summary of a deal they may have signed at 22 without a lawyer. The named accidental failure in `meta/personas.md` is signing something you do not understand and discovering years later you gave away your publishing — this is the direct intervention on that failure. |
| Producer | Full | Points, delivery obligations, work-for-hire language. Their key term is whether they hold points or a flat fee, and whether "work for hire" quietly took their copyright — a distinction with career-scale consequences that hides in one clause. |
| Operator | Read-only | Their contracts are leases and licences; music-deal term extraction does not describe them. They see whatever generic dates extraction can find, and should not be sold comprehension the feature does not have for their document type. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no deals, no professional stake. |

## Behavior

Extraction proposes structured terms from a stored document; the **user confirms each one**. Unconfirmed extractions are visibly provisional and must never silently drive a reconciliation finding. Confirmed dates flow to alerts (`#CX-02`); confirmed commercial terms flow to reconciliation (`#CX-03`) and to commission automation (23.06.02).

### Happy Path

1. Musician uploads a management agreement (23.04.01).
2. Extraction proposes: term 3 years + 2×1-year options, 20% commission, worldwide, 18-month sunset at 10%.
3. Each proposed term shows **the clause it came from** — the citation is the feature; a number without provenance is unusable.
4. Musician confirms each, correcting the sunset rate the extractor misread.
5. Confirmed terms schedule the option-window alerts and populate the deal summary.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Extraction is wrong about a material term | **The core risk.** A wrong reversion date means an alert on the wrong day, which is worse than no alert — the user relied on it | Every term cites its clause and requires confirmation; confidence is shown, never hidden |
| Term is genuinely ambiguous (they often are) | Real contracts contain clauses that lawyers disagree about | Must refuse rather than pick: "we cannot determine this — read this clause" beats a confident guess |
| Deal is a chain (amendments, side letters) — 23.04.01 DT-03 | Live terms are a computed view over the chain, not the terms of one file | Effective terms with the instruments that produced them |
| Document is a photo of a fax from 2009 | Extraction quality collapses | Says so; degrades to custody-only rather than pretending |
| User relies on a wrong extraction and loses a right | Parent Q-02 — the liability question, unresolved and serious | Framing, confirmation and citation are the mitigation; they may not be sufficient |
| Extraction of a **counterparty's** obligations | Their duties are as material as the user's (accounting frequency, audit rights) and never read | Both sides extracted, not just the user's |
| Contract sent to an AI extractor contains PII and confidential terms | Contract text is highly sensitive commercial data | `security-first` — the PII/confidentiality posture for any model call is a hard constraint, not a detail |
| Multi-language contracts | Territory-dependent | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Extraction running | Document readable immediately; terms fill in after |
| Empty | No extraction attempted / unsupported type | Custody-only, said plainly |
| Populated | Terms extracted | Summary with per-term citation, confidence and confirmation state |
| Error | Extraction failed | Document intact; manual term entry available. Manual entry is a **first-class path**, not a fallback |
| Partial | Some terms found, some not | Explicit gaps — "we did not find a reversion clause" is information, not silence |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Extraction accuracy is the feature's quality bar | The obvious metric | ❌ REJECTED: **calibration** is the quality bar, not accuracy. An extractor that is right 95% of the time and silent about which 5% is dangerous, because the user cannot tell which terms to check. One that is right 80% of the time and honest about its uncertainty is usable. This inverts the normal ML product instinct and it is why every term must cite its clause and require confirmation. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **reversion clause** to be the single most valuable term, and the one most likely to be missing | Any music lawyer: rights reversion is where catalogues are won and lost, and reversion terms are often absent entirely from older deals (rights assigned for life of copyright), or buried in a definition rather than stated as a date. An extractor tuned to find dates will miss "reverts upon the expiry of the Term as defined in Clause 1.1" — the value hides behind a cross-reference, which is precisely what naive extraction cannot follow. | ✅ CONFIRMED: reversion is the priority term, the hardest to extract, and often a **finding by absence** — "this deal has no reversion" is itself the most valuable thing the feature can tell a musician. Absence-reporting is not a normal extraction output and must be designed for deliberately. |
| DT-03 | Manual term entry is the fallback when extraction fails | Framing extraction as primary | ❌ REJECTED: manual entry should be **first-class and complete on its own**. A musician who knows their commission is 20% and their option window is next March gets the entire value of alerts and reconciliation without any extraction at all. Making extraction a prerequisite gates the sub-domain's value behind its riskiest component. This also means the sub-domain could ship, honestly and usefully, with **no extraction at all** — which materially changes the MoSCoW shape (extraction `could`, alerts `should`). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Calibration over accuracy; every term cites its clause and requires confirmation | See DT-01 — silent confidence is the dangerous failure |
| D-02 | Manual term entry is first-class, not a fallback | See DT-03 — it unlocks the sub-domain's value without the risky component |
| D-03 | Absence is a reportable finding | See DT-02 — "no reversion clause" is the most valuable sentence available |
