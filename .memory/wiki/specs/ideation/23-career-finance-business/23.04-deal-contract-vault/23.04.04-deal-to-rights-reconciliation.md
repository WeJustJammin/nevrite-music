# Feature: Deal-to-Rights Reconciliation

> **Number**: 23.04.04
> **Parent**: [Deal & Contract Vault](./23.04-deal-contract-vault-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Compare what the paper says against what the platform's own records say. The contract says the publishing split is 50/50; the split sheet in domain 09 says 60/40. The management agreement says 20% commission; the deductions actually taken (23.06.02) say 25%. The deal says rights revert in 2029; it is 2030 and 09 still shows the label as owner. Each disagreement is a finding worth real money, and none of them are currently discoverable by anyone — because no single party has ever held both the paper and the ledger.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds out that the deal they signed and the splits that were filed are not the same document — usually the first time anyone has ever checked. The failure named in `meta/personas.md` (signing a split sheet you do not understand and finding out years later) becomes detectable in year one instead of year nine. |
| Producer | Full | Finds that their points were never registered anywhere, or that the production agreement's terms never made it into the rights record. They are the party best placed to have prevented it and — per their anti-persona note — the party most likely to have closed the project without capturing it. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue or studio holds no rights in works; their contracts are hire agreements with dates but no chain of title. Explicit absence: no reconciliation surface in an Operator context. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no deals, no rights, no professional stake. |

## Behavior

Reconciliation joins **confirmed** contract terms (23.04.02) against the rights ledger (09), the royalty flows (10) and the deductions actually taken (23.06.02), and reports disagreements. It **never auto-corrects**: a contract and a ledger disagreeing is a legal question, and silently "fixing" a rights record could itself transfer ownership.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's publishing agreement is stored and its terms confirmed (50/50 with the co-writer).
2. Domain 09 holds a split sheet for the same work showing 60/40.
3. Reconciliation surfaces the disagreement, citing both sources and the dates each was created.
4. Musician discovers the split sheet was filed after the fact, from memory, and does not match the signed deal — the exact reconstruction failure in `meta/problem-statement.md`.
5. They act on it while the co-writer is still reachable and still friendly, rather than in a dispute a decade later.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Contract and ledger disagree | **Never auto-resolved.** Surfaced with both sources cited | A finding, not a fix. The platform is not a party to the deal and must not act like an adjudicator (`#CX-03`) |
| The disagreement implicates another WeJammin user | Their view of the same rights may differ, and they may be right | Whether the counterparty is notified is Q-01 — genuinely hard both ways |
| Extraction was wrong, so the "finding" is false | A false accusation of a co-writer is a relationship-destroying bug | Only **confirmed** terms reconcile; findings cite the clause; confidence is always visible |
| A reversion date passed and 09 never changed | The silent failure — the old owner keeps collecting (`#CX-04`) | Reported as an observation ("the date passed and the record did not change"), never as a legal conclusion ("the rights reverted") |
| Contract is a chain of amendments (23.04.01 DT-03) | Reconcile against **effective** terms, not the original file | Effective terms with their instrument chain |
| No contract exists for a rights position | Extremely common, and a finding in itself: rights recorded with no paper behind them | Absence reported — the mirror of 23.04.02 DT-02 |
| Rights change in 09 after reconciliation ran | Findings must be re-derivable, never cached as truth | Live derivation |
| The finding is that **the user** is over-credited | Nobody wants this one; it must still be reported | Honest in both directions or the feature is a weapon, not a tool |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Reconciling | Skeleton |
| Empty | No confirmed terms or no rights records to compare | Honest about **why** — "nothing to compare" is not "everything agrees", and conflating them would be the most dangerous UI lie in the domain |
| Populated | Comparison complete | Findings, or an explicit "no disagreements found" |
| Error | A source unavailable | Never present a clean result over an unread source |
| Partial | Some terms confirmed, some rights records missing | Coverage stated explicitly — what was compared, and what was not |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should fix the disagreement — it has both records, so it can just correct the wrong one | The automation instinct | ❌ REJECTED: a signed contract and a rights ledger disagreeing is a **legal question between parties**, not a data-quality problem. Auto-correcting 09 to match a contract could transfer ownership on the strength of a machine's reading of a clause — and the platform is not a party to the deal. Surface, cite, never resolve. Recorded as `#CX-03`. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the most common finding to be **"no contract behind this rights position"** | Any music lawyer or royalty auditor: rights records routinely exist with no executed paper behind them — a split agreed verbally, a "work for hire" nobody papered, points promised in a text message. The industry's default state is rights without documents, which is the same absence-at-the-point-of-truth root cause named in `meta/problem-statement.md`. | ✅ CONFIRMED: absence is the headline finding class, not disagreement. This also reframes the feature — it is less an auditor and more a **gap detector**, and it argues the feature has value from day one even with almost no contracts in the vault, because "you have rights and no paper" is derivable from 09 alone. That materially changes its cold-start story. |
| DT-03 | This is a finance feature and belongs in domain 23 | Inherited from the candidate list (candidate 05's "deal vault") | ⏳ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the boundary is genuinely uncomfortable. The **reconciliation** is a rights operation reading rights data (09) — it arguably belongs there. What keeps it here is that its input is the contract vault, which is business-shaped, and its most valuable findings are money-shaped (commission drift, unstopped sunsets). But if 09 grows its own contract awareness, this feature moves. Flagged as Q-03 — an honest boundary risk, not a settled call. D-17 in `ideation-index.md` already warns that domain-count inflation is a live hypothesis, and this is exactly the kind of node that inflates it. |
