# Feature: Valuation Confidence & Thin-Market Handling

> **Number**: 13.04.03
> **Parent**: [Price Discovery & Market Data](./13.04-price-discovery-market-data-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list)
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The model that decides whether the price guide is allowed to speak, and how loudly. Most gear models are thin: a boutique fuzz pedal sells four times a year, a specific 1962 Jazzmaster finish sells twice a decade, and a modified unit has no peers at all. A guide that answers every question with equal confidence is wrong exactly where it matters most — on rare, valuable, hard-to-price gear, which is precisely what people consult a guide about.

**Why this exists as a node**: the sweep listed the price guide (#15) and repricing (#16) but nothing governing when either may speak. Without this, both features default to always answering, and the failure is silent — a confidently wrong number looks identical to a well-evidenced one.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees "based on 12 sales in 18 months" vs "based on 2 sales in 6 years" and calibrates. The persona has good instincts and mostly needs to know whether to trust the number or their gut. |
| Producer | Read-only | **Lives in the thin-market case.** Rare outboard is the norm; a guide that pretends otherwise is useless to this persona. "We can't price this" is the correct and respected answer. |
| Operator | Read-only | Needs confidence at fleet scale for asset valuation — an insurance schedule built on low-confidence numbers is a claim that gets rejected. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The persona this exists for.** Zero calibration, will read any number as a fact. Cannot be expected to notice "n=2" — so at low confidence the number must be *withheld or reframed*, not annotated with a caveat the persona won't read. |

## Behavior

Confidence is computed per bucket from comp count, recency, dispersion and bucket integrity (was it widened? are the comps really this model, this condition, this originality?). It produces a **gate, not a label**: above threshold, the guide renders normally; below it, the guide reframes ("2 sales in 6 years, ranging £900–£2,400") or declines entirely. Confidence is not a decoration on an answer — it decides whether there is an answer.

### Happy Path

1. User requests a guide figure for a model × condition × originality bucket
2. System assembles comps and computes confidence
3. High confidence: distribution renders with comp count and period
4. Low confidence: raw comps shown as *examples*, no median, no suggestion
5. No confidence: "Not enough sales data" with an invitation to look at related buckets — disclosed as related, not passed off as this one

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Single comp (n=1) | One sale is an anecdote, never a distribution — cannot be above threshold no matter how recent or clean | The one sale shown as an example; explicitly "1 recorded sale", no median, no suggestion |
| 2 comps, 6 years apart, 3× apart in price | Below any threshold — the dispersion says the market has no consensus | Both sales shown as examples; no median, no suggestion |
| 30 comps, all within 3 months, tight range | High confidence | Full distribution + suggestion enabled (13.04.02) |
| Bucket widened to reach a comp count | Confidence must drop, not rise — more comps of the *wrong thing* is worse, not better | Widening disclosed (13.04.01 D-02) and penalised in confidence |
| Modified unit (13.02.03) | No comparable peers; the base-model bucket describes a different object | Guide declines; reason stated |
| Provenanced unit (13.03.06) | One-of-one; unpriceable by comparison per 13.04.01 DT-02 | Guide declines; reason stated |
| Comps are real but stale (market moved) | Recency weighting drops confidence as comps age; direction is settled (older = less trusted), the exact decay curve is not | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` — decay rate is category-dependent (vintage slow, current-production fast) and unmodelled at Level 1 |
| Wash sales inflate a thin bucket | Small samples are cheapest to manipulate — thin markets are exactly where gaming is easiest and confidence is highest-stakes | Feeds 13.04.01 Q-02; confidence alone cannot detect this |
| Confidence is high but simply wrong | Possible — a run of comps can be coincidence | The published comp count is the user's own check; evidence is always expandable |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Guide requested | Skeleton |
| Empty | Zero comps | "No sales recorded" |
| Populated | Above threshold | Distribution + confidence + evidence |
| Error | Confidence computation fails | **Fail closed** — no confidence means no number. The one place in this domain where failing closed is right |
| Partial | Below threshold | Raw comps as examples; no median, no suggestion |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Confidence is a label attached to an answer — show the number, caveat it | The standard treatment, and what every existing price guide does | ❌ REJECTED: caveats are not read, and the persona least likely to read one (Fan) is the one most likely to be harmed. A caveated number and an uncaveated number are the same number to a non-expert; "£1,240 (low confidence)" is remembered as "£1,240". Confidence must **gate** the output — change what is shown, not annotate it. Below threshold there is no median at all, because the median is the artifact that does the lying. |
| DT-02 | A thin market is a data problem that more data will fix | It reads as a cold-start issue, temporary by nature | ❌ REJECTED: thin is the **permanent, structural** condition of most of this catalog, not a bootstrapping phase. A 1963 Jazzmaster in Foam Green with all-original electronics will *never* have 30 comps a year — there are only so many, and they trade rarely. Combined with 13.04.01 DT-01 (the guide can't launch without liquidity) this produces the sub-domain's real shape: **the guide will be confident on the common and mute on the rare, permanently, and the rare is where people most want an answer.** That is not a defect to engineer away; it is the honest boundary of what comps can know, and pretending otherwise is how price guides lose credibility. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Confidence gates the output; it is not a label on it | Per DT-01 — the median is the artifact that lies, so at low confidence there is no median. |
| D-02 | Bucket widening lowers confidence | More comps of the wrong thing is worse than fewer of the right thing. |
| D-03 | Fail closed on confidence computation failure | The only fail-closed rule in the domain, and it is correct: the alternative is an ungated number. |
| D-04 | Thin markets are permanent, not a cold-start problem | Per DT-02 — sets expectations for what this sub-domain can ever deliver. |
