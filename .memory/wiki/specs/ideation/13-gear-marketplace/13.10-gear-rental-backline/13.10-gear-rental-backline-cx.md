## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Rate Cards ↔ Availability

**Relationship**: The rate is a function of the range, and the range is only bookable if the calendar allows it — so a renter asking "how much for Friday to Monday?" is asking one question that both features answer jointly. Non-linear tiers (13.10.01 D-02) mean the price cannot be computed until the exact range is known and confirmed available.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: books a weekend; the weekend rate is why they book a weekend rather than three days.
- **Producer**: books a tracking week where the week rate makes the project viable.
- **Operator**: sets both, and prices the tiers to shape demand toward the utilisation they want.
- **Fan**: sees rates, books nothing.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The rate card and the calendar are both properties of the listing. No write contention — one owner, the lender.
2. **Trigger chain**: Range selected → availability check (including turnaround buffer, 13.10.02 D-02) → tier resolution → quote. A range that is *partly* available yields a partial offer (13.10.02 States), which may fall into a different rate tier — a 4-day booking trimmed to 2 days is not two-sevenths of a week rate.
3. **Permission intersection**: None distinct.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: A lender changing rates while a quote is live. Low stakes; the quote should pin. Contrast the sale side, where a price edit under a live offer is blocked outright (13.03.02 D-03) — a rental quote is shorter-lived and lower-stakes.

### CX-02: Return Condition ↔ Availability — the cascade

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's defining failure. Rental inventory is a dependency chain: one unit, sequential commitments, each with an immovable date. A late or damaged return doesn't inconvenience one party — it propagates into every downstream booking, and the next renter's show is tonight. 13.10.02 DT-02 rejects the late-fee answer precisely because a fee compensates the lender and does nothing for the person standing on a stage without an amp.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: absorbs the cascade and must re-source under time pressure. The persona doc's stated failure mode — "a show that failed on a preventable technical mismatch" — is exactly this.
- **Musician (as next renter)**: the victim, and the persona with the least slack; they cannot conjure another amp on a Friday evening.
- **Producer**: a session with paid contributors booked around gear that hasn't arrived.
- **Fan**: absent — no deposit access, no rental role. The sub-domain's weakest Fan story, and this is why.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The unit's availability is the shared entity. The return event mutates it — and the mutation may be *unavailable indefinitely* if the damage is serious.
2. **Trigger chain**: Late/damaged return → assessment → unit blocked → **forward bookings invalidated** → re-source or cancel. Per 13.10.02 D-04, this must be proactive: the next renter should be told (and ideally re-sourced) before they discover it themselves at the loading dock.
3. **Permission intersection**: The assessing lender is the party whose forward bookings are at risk — an incentive to assess quickly and, potentially, to under-report damage to keep the unit rentable. The mirror image of 13.09.03 DT-02's grader conflict.
4. **Notification fan-out**: One late return notifies the assessor, the late renter, and **every downstream renter in the chain**. Unbounded in principle, and time-critical — the only notification path in the domain where minutes matter.
5. **State transition conflict**: Assessment racing the next collection. 13.10.03 D-04 bounds the window precisely because the unit goes out again tomorrow — but a short window plus a queued next renter means the assessment happens under exactly the pressure that produces bad assessments.

### CX-03: Availability ↔ Return Condition — a live design conflict

**Relationship**: Two confirmed decisions that contradict each other. 13.10.02 D-03 assigns pooled units **at collection** to maximise fleet flexibility — you rent "an SM58", the specific one is chosen as you walk out. 13.10.03 D-02 assesses returns against **this hire's collection state**, and the sub-domain's D-02 records condition history **per unit**. Both are correct in isolation; together they require knowing which unit went out, which late assignment defers and pooling deliberately blurs.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: the only persona affected, and the persona both decisions serve — flexibility is their business, and condition accountability protects their fleet.
- **Musician/Producer**: renting a pooled item, unaware which specific unit they have.
- **Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The unit identity itself is the contested entity — pooling wants it fungible, condition history wants it specific. **This is the same tension 13.03.03 DT-01 found on the sales side** (qty=1 vs. counted stock), arriving here in a sharper form because rental units return and accumulate history that counted stock never does.
2. **Trigger chain**: Book pool → assign at collection → capture condition (13.10.03 D-01, both parties present) → return → assess against collection. **The chain works if assignment is recorded at collection** — which is also the moment condition is captured, so the two can resolve together. That is probably the answer: pooling is a *booking-time* abstraction, and identity resolves at the collection capture.
3. **Permission intersection**: None distinct.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: If assignment is *never* recorded — true pooling all the way through — then a damaged unit cannot be attributed to a renter and the deposit mechanism collapses. **The conflict is resolvable only by conceding that pooling ends at collection**, which 13.10.03 Q-01 leaves open and Step 5 should close.
