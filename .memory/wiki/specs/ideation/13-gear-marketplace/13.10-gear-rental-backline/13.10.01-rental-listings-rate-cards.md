# Feature: Rental Listings & Rate Cards

> **Number**: 13.10.01
> **Parent**: [Gear Rental & Backline Hire](./13.10-gear-rental-backline-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Listing gear for hire rather than sale, with rates that vary by duration — day, weekend, week, month — and terms covering delivery, deposit and what the renter is liable for. The same physical unit that could be sold is instead let out repeatedly, which makes it an income stream rather than a transaction.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both) | Rents out a spare amp for weekend income; hires a specific vintage piece for a session rather than buying one. The persona's "portfolio of small income streams" made literal — gear they already own earning money between gigs. |
| Producer | Full (both) | Hires outboard for a specific project — the honest alternative to buying a £6k compressor for one record. Rents out studio gear when it's idle. |
| Operator | Full (the primary supplier) | **The persona this exists for.** Backline fleets, PA, rehearsal-room gear. Already sells time and space (per the persona doc); renting gear is the same business logic against a different asset. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees rates. Would not rent professional gear — no deposit context, no liability context, no reason. |

## Behavior

A rental listing binds a specific unit (or a pool of interchangeable units — a fleet of five identical SM58s) to a rate card. Rates are duration-tiered and non-linear: a week is not seven day-rates. Terms carry deposit, delivery/collection, and liability. The listing is a **standing offer against a calendar** (13.10.02), not a one-time sale.

### Happy Path

1. Operator lists a backline amp for hire with day/weekend/week rates
2. Musician needs it for a Friday show; checks availability
3. Rate resolves from the duration tier
4. Terms — deposit, delivery, liability — are stated up-front
5. Reservation proceeds (13.10.02)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fleet of identical units vs. a specific unit | Renting "an SM58" differs from renting "this 1968 Deluxe Reverb" | **Both regimes needed** — pooled fungible and specific non-fungible. See DT-01. |
| Same unit listed for sale *and* for hire | Legitimate — sell it if someone wants it, rent it meanwhile | A sale mid-rental is a real conflict; who wins? |
| Rate card doesn't cover an unusual duration | Non-linear tiers leave gaps (a 10-day hire) | Interpolation rules, or "ask" |
| Delivery of backline to a venue | It's a freight/logistics problem (13.07.01) with a deadline that cannot slip | Late backline is a cancelled show — a severity no sale has |
| Renter wants to buy it after hiring | Try-before-buy is a genuinely good funnel | Rent-to-own is a real model nobody has scoped |
| Long-term hire drifts into a finance arrangement | Regulatory line | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Gear is registered (domain 15) with provenance | "Hire the amp that played on X" | Provenance as a rental premium — real, and 13.03.06's consent model applies |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rental inventory is qty=1 non-fungible, like the rest of the domain | The domain's founding premise (D-14: "qty=1 non-fungible stock with condition-as-price") | ❌ REJECTED — and it is the **second** crack in that premise, after 13.03.03 DT-01 (counted stock). Most backline is deliberately **fungible**: a hire company with five identical SM58s rents "an SM58", not a specific one, and pooling is the whole point of a fleet. But high-value rental is the opposite — you hire *that* 1968 Deluxe Reverb, and no substitute exists. So rental needs **both** regimes, and the pooled one shares nothing with the qty=1 model the domain is built around. Taken with 13.03.03 DT-01, the domain now has *three* inventory regimes: qty=1 sale, counted-stock sale, and pooled time-boxed rental. **That is a real strain on the D-14 boundary and directly supports the promotion question (sub-domain Q-01).** |
| DT-02 | Rental listings are sale listings with a rate instead of a price | The cheapest way to ship it — reuse the listing model | ❌ REJECTED: a sale listing is an offer to transfer an object once; a rental listing is a **standing offer against a calendar**, valid indefinitely, generating revenue repeatedly from an asset that never leaves the owner. It doesn't have a lifecycle terminating in `sold` (13.03.02); it has utilisation. It doesn't produce a comp (13.04.01) — a day rate says nothing about the unit's value. It doesn't transfer ownership (13.06.05) — the domain's thesis contribution simply doesn't fire. **Almost every structural decision in this domain assumes an object that leaves and doesn't come back.** Rental violates that assumption at every point, which is the strongest evidence yet that it is a domain-shaped thing, not a feature of this one. |
