# Feature: Deposits, Damage Assessment & Return Condition

> **Number**: 13.10.03
> **Parent**: [Gear Rental & Backline Hire](./13.10-gear-rental-backline-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Taking a deposit, comparing the unit's condition on return against its condition on collection, and deciding what — if anything — comes out of the deposit. This is where rental's money actually gets decided, and it is a dispute engine: rented gear is *used* gear by definition, and the line between "normal wear from doing exactly what it was hired to do" and "damage" is genuinely blurry.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | As renter: gets the deposit back, or doesn't, and needs to know why. As lender: assesses their own gear coming home from a stranger's show. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Assesses outboard on return — where "damage" may be a drifted calibration nobody can see, which is a harder assessment than a scuff. |
| Operator | Full | The primary assessor, at fleet volume, under time pressure — the unit needs to go out again tomorrow. Speed matters more here than anywhere in the domain. |
| Fan | None | No deposit, no assessment, no liability. Consistent with the sub-domain's Role Matrix — this is the weakest Fan story in the domain. |

## Behavior

Condition is captured at **collection** and again at **return**, and the deposit resolves against the difference. This is the evidence-pack pattern (13.02.04) applied to a round trip rather than a one-way sale — and it needs the same property: capture as a byproduct at the moment it's true, not a reconstruction afterwards.

### Happy Path

1. Renter collects; condition is captured with both parties present — the strongest evidence moment available
2. Gear is used at the show
3. Return; condition captured again
4. Normal wear; deposit released in full
5. Condition history appends to the unit's registry record (sub-domain D-02)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **"Normal wear" vs. "damage"** | A rented amp is *meant* to be gigged. Scuffs are the product working | The definitional problem. See DT-01. |
| Damage disputed | Deposit withheld against the renter's account | Collection evidence is the arbiter — without it, one memory against another |
| Damage found after the unit is already out again | Assessment window is minutes at a loading dock, not days | Late findings are unattributable — which renter? |
| Pooled unit (13.10.02 D-03) | Assigned at collection; but condition history is per-unit | **Direct conflict** — see Q-01 |
| Deposit doesn't cover the damage | A destroyed vintage amp against a £200 deposit | Deposit is not insurance; the gap is real |
| Renter returns it better than it went out (serviced it) | Should be recognised | Rare, and a nice signal |
| Return is late *and* damaged | Cascade (13.10.02 DT-02) plus a claim | Compounds |
| Gear stolen during the hire | Van theft — endemic per the persona doc | Routes to 13.03.07/domain 15; the renter's liability may be ruinous |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The domain's condition grading (13.02.01) can assess rental returns | Same scale, same vocabulary, same photos — reuse it | ❌ REJECTED: sale grading answers **"what is this worth?"**; rental return assessment answers **"did you break it, or did you use it?"** Those are different questions and the second has no scale. A hired amp is *supposed* to come back with new scuffs — that is the product being consumed. A grade band drop from Excellent to Very Good over 40 hires is not damage; it is depreciation the lender priced into the rate. So the assessment is **delta-based and intent-laden**, not absolute — and the domain has no vocabulary for "expected deterioration". This is the fourth structure in the sub-domain that rental cannot reuse (with the order model, the comp model and the ownership model), and it strengthens the promotion case (sub-domain Q-01). |
| DT-02 | Capture condition at return and compare it to the listing | The obvious implementation — the listing has photos | ❌ REJECTED: the listing describes the unit *before 40 hires*. Comparing a return against it charges this renter for four years of everyone's wear. The comparison must be against **this hire's collection state**, which means capturing at collection — and collection is the one moment in the entire domain where **both parties are physically present with the object**. That is the strongest evidence opportunity anywhere in domain 13, stronger even than dispatch photos (13.02.04), because it is mutual and contemporaneous rather than one-sided. Skipping it makes every deposit dispute unresolvable. Same principle as 13.02.04 DT-01 — capture at the moment the fact is true — but with a better moment available. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Condition is captured at collection, with both parties present | Per DT-02 — the best evidence moment in the entire domain. |
| D-02 | Return is assessed against this hire's collection state, never against the listing | Per DT-02 — otherwise each renter pays for everyone's wear. |
| D-03 | Expected wear is priced into the rate, not charged to the renter | Per DT-01 — a gigged amp coming back gigged is the product working. |
| D-04 | The assessment window is bounded and short | An open-ended window on a unit that goes out again tomorrow makes findings unattributable. |
