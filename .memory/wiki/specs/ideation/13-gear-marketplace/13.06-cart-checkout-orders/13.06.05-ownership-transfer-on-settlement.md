# Feature: Ownership Transfer on Settlement

> **Number**: 13.06.05
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list)
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

When a gear order settles, the buyer becomes the recorded custodian of that serial in the registry (domain 15) — automatically, as a byproduct of the sale, with no separate act of registration. This is the gear domain's expression of the platform thesis: **ownership captured at the moment of transfer**, exactly as splits are captured at the moment of creation. It is the mechanism by which the ownership chain accretes without anyone choosing to maintain it.

**Why this exists as a node**: the sweep produced 28 candidates describing a complete marketplace and **not one of them wrote anything to the registry**. Domain 15 was left to accumulate ownership records by asking people to enter them — which is the reconstruction failure the problem statement identifies as the platform's root cause, reproduced inside the platform that exists to fix it. Without this node, 13 and 15 are two products in one folder tree.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Buys an amp; it appears in their registry without them doing anything. Configures whether their custody is public, private, or recorded-but-hidden. As seller: their custody closes, and the unit's history stays intact without them. |
| Producer | Config | Same. Their studio inventory accrues from purchases rather than from an afternoon with a spreadsheet — the persona whose defining trait is never having time for admin. |
| Operator | Config | Fleet registration accrues from buying. The asset register maintains itself, which is the only way an asset register ever stays current. |
| Fan | Config | Buys a first guitar; it is recorded as theirs. **The persona least likely to ever register gear deliberately and most likely to have it stolen from a car.** This is the only realistic path to that instrument having a stolen-registry record (13.03.07) at all. |

## Behavior

On settlement (13.06.02's terminal state), the system appends an ownership event to the unit's registry record: previous custodian's tenure closes, buyer's opens, timestamped and evidenced by the order. If the unit has no registry record, one is created from the listing's model binding, serial and evidence pack. **Consent governs visibility, never recording** — the chain records; who can see it is a separate question.

### Happy Path

1. Order settles (inspection window closes, funds release)
2. System appends the transfer event to the unit's registry record
3. Seller's custody closes; buyer's custody opens
4. Buyer sees the unit in their registry, with its history intact — including provenance (13.03.06) and condition evidence (13.02.04)
5. The chain is one link longer, and nobody typed anything

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Order refunded/returned after transfer** | Ownership must **reverse** — but the chain is append-only, so it reverses by appending a compensating event, never by deletion | Registry shows transfer and reversal; the history is that a sale happened and unwound. Deleting it would be a lie |
| Listing has no serial | Nothing to key on. Most launch listings | No registry record created; recording an unidentifiable unit creates a phantom |
| Buyer doesn't want to be recorded as owner | Consent governs **visibility**; the chain still records the transfer, or the registry has holes exactly where a theft claim would need it | See DT-02 — the hardest question in this file |
| Bundle sale (13.03.05) | One order → N transfers, atomically | Per 13.03.05 D-02 |
| Serial is wrong/mistyped | Transfers custody of the wrong unit — possibly someone else's registered instrument | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — serial collision handling is unresolved and nasty |
| Local pickup (13.11) with no platform settlement | No settlement event → no transfer. The domain's most common cash sale is invisible to the chain | Real gap; see Q-03 |
| Sold off-platform after being bought here | The chain ends with a stale custodian who no longer holds it | Chain is honest about last-known, never asserts current |
| Unit had a stolen report land post-settlement | Ownership transferred to a buyer of stolen goods | 13.03.07 Q-02 — unresolved, and this node is where the damage lands |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Settlement processing | Invisible — this is a byproduct, not a user task |
| Empty | No serial → no transfer | Nothing; optionally "add a serial to track this" |
| Populated | Transfer recorded | Unit appears in the buyer's registry with history |
| Error | Registry write fails | **The order must still settle.** A registry failure cannot hold a buyer's money — queue and retry |
| Partial | Bundle with some constituents unserialised | Transfers what it can; records what it couldn't |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership recording belongs to domain 15, so 13 just needs to not interfere | 15 owns "ownership & provenance chain" outright in its Overview — writing to it from 13 looks like a boundary violation | ❌ REJECTED: 15 owns the **record**; 13 owns the **event**. A settlement is the only moment on the entire platform when a chattel's ownership demonstrably changes, with money, two identified parties and a timestamp as evidence — and 15 has no visibility into it. If 13 doesn't write, 15's chain is populated by self-assertion, which is precisely the "asking people to remember" failure the problem statement names as the root cause. **This is the gear-domain analogue of the Producer capturing splits in the session rather than reconstructing them at year three.** Same thesis (D-18), same mechanism, different asset class. Being *in the room* for a gear sale means being the checkout. |
| DT-02 | Ownership recording should be opt-in, since it's the buyer's private business | Privacy instinct, and 13.05.03 DT-02 already established that registry visibility is a *safety* matter (theft targeting), which argues for restraint | ⚠️ REFRAMED — and this is the sharpest tension in the domain. Opt-in **visibility** is required (13.05.03 DT-02 is right: broadcasting who owns a $40k instrument is targeting data). But opt-in **recording** destroys the mechanism: an ownership chain with consent-shaped holes cannot establish provenance, cannot support a theft claim (you cannot prove a stolen guitar was yours if you opted out of the record that says so), and cannot survive as evidence. The resolution is to **separate recording from visibility** — the chain always records, the owner controls who sees it, and the default is private. That gives the Fan persona a theft record they never asked for and never has to show anyone. **But "we always record who owns what, you only control who sees it" is a real values position** — precisely what problem-statement Q-02 asks about lock-in being earned rather than hostile. This needs the owner, explicitly. It cannot be defaulted. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Settlement writes the ownership event; 15 owns the record, 13 owns the event | Per DT-01. |
| D-02 | Recording and visibility are separate concerns; the chain always records, the owner controls visibility, default private | Per DT-02 — **requires owner ratification; this is a values position, not an agent call.** |
| D-03 | Reversal is a compensating append, never a deletion | An append-only chain that can be rewritten is not a chain. |
| D-04 | A registry write failure never blocks settlement | The buyer's money cannot be held hostage to a provenance nicety. Queue and retry. |
