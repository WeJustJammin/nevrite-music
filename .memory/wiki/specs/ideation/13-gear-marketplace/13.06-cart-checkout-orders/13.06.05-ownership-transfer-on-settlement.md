# Feature: Ownership Transfer on Settlement

> **Number**: 13.06.05
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list)
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

When a gear order settles, the buyer becomes the recorded custodian of that serial in the registry (domain 15) — automatically, as a byproduct of the sale, with no separate act of registration. This is the gear domain's expression of the platform thesis: **ownership captured at the moment of transfer**, exactly as splits are captured at the moment of creation. It is the mechanism by which the ownership chain accretes without anyone choosing to maintain it.

**Why this exists as a node**: the sweep produced 28 candidates describing a complete marketplace and **not one of them wrote anything to the registry**. Domain 15 was left to accumulate ownership records by asking people to enter them — which is the reconstruction failure the problem statement identifies as the platform's root cause, reproduced inside the platform that exists to fix it. Without this node, 13 and 15 are two products in one folder tree.

**What this node is not**: it does not *effect* ownership and it does not *adjudicate* title. Per DT-08, the platform cannot make someone own a guitar; it can only record, on good evidence, that they do. This node is **evidentiary, not constitutive** — a distinction that turns out to decide the CX-M04 question (Q-02) and to keep this file consistent with 15 D-07.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Buys an amp; it appears in their registry with no action taken. Two levers only: **which identity the order is against** (themselves or a band/entity per domain 01 — see DT-06, the case that silently mis-records the band's PA as personally owned), and the per-item visibility inherited from 15.04 (private by default). As seller: their tenure closes and the unit's history survives without them. The multi-hyphenate case bites here — a Musician selling a preamp into their own studio company is a **related-party transfer** (DT-09), recorded and marked, never blocked. |
| Producer | Config | Same levers. Their studio inventory accrues from purchases rather than from an afternoon with a spreadsheet — the persona whose defining trait is never having time for admin, and whose *accidental* anti-behaviour (`personas.md`: "we'll do it later") this node exists to make impossible. The lazy path is the only path. |
| Operator | Config | Fleet registration accrues from buying; the asset register maintains itself, which is the only way an asset register ever stays current. The persona most exposed to DT-05 (consignment): an Operator selling stock they hold but do not own must **not** be recorded as ever having owned it. |
| Fan | Config | Buys a first guitar; it is recorded as theirs. **The persona least likely to ever register gear deliberately and most likely to have it stolen from a car.** This is the only realistic path to that instrument having a stolen-registry record (13.03.07) at all. Will not configure anything — which is precisely why the default (record, private) has to be right without them.

> All four are **Config** per the sub-domain Role Matrix. The transfer itself is not user-operable by anyone: it is a byproduct with no button. What is configurable is the buying identity and the visibility of the resulting record — and the visibility control surface belongs to **15.04**, not to this node (see Cross-Cut Notes; this file does not build a settings page).

## Behavior

On settlement (13.06.02's terminal state), the system appends an ownership event to the unit's registry record: the previous custodian's tenure closes, the buyer's opens, timestamped and evidenced by the order. If the unit has no registry record, one is created from the listing's model binding (13.01.04), serial and evidence pack (13.02.04). **Consent governs visibility, never recording** — the chain records; who can see it is a separate question, owned by 15.04.

Four rules govern the shape of every event this node writes:

1. **The trigger is settlement, never delivery and never payment.** Payment is not finality (a cart authorisation precedes dispatch by weeks); delivery is not finality (the inspection window sits between delivery and settlement, and a return inside it means ownership never should have moved). Only settlement — escrow released, sale final — is the moment the fact is true. This is the load-bearing distinction in the file and it currently **contradicts 15.01.03's stated trigger**; see DT-03.
2. **Ownership follows the object, not the money.** A return reverses the transfer; a refund on its own does not. The partial-refund-with-retention remedy (13.08.01 D-03) leaves the buyer holding the guitar, so the buyer still owns it. This one rule resolves five otherwise-contradictory edge cases (partial refund, chargeback, keep-it remedy, damage claim, price adjustment) and corrects an error carried in this file's own breadth pass.
3. **The event's timestamp is the settlement time, not the write time.** The write is asynchronous and may be retried for hours (D-04). A chain that records when the row landed rather than when ownership changed is lying about the only thing it exists to know.
4. **The chain never fabricates a tenure it did not observe.** If the seller was never the recorded owner, the closing tenure renders as `prior tenure — unrecorded`, not as an invented one. This is 15 D-06 applied at the write side: a registry that cannot say "I don't know" will fabricate, and this node is the largest single writer into it.

### Happy Path

1. Order reaches `settled` (13.06.02 step 5): inspection window closed without dispute, escrow released.
2. System resolves the unit's registry identity on the composite key `(maker, model, era, serial)` per 15 D-05 — **never on the serial alone** — using the listing's model binding (13.01.04) as the maker/model/era source.
3. No record matches → one is created from the listing's binding, serial and sealed evidence pack (13.02.04). A record matches and the seller holds it → resolved to that record.
4. System appends one transfer event: seller's tenure closes, buyer's opens, `reason: sale`, `at:` the settlement timestamp, `evidence:` the order.
5. Claim minted at the **Witnessed** tier (15.01.02) — the platform observed money move between two identified parties against an identified object. No buyer confirmation is requested: per DT-04, for an on-platform sale the settlement record *is* the witness, and asking the buyer to attest would substitute self-assertion for platform-observed fact.
6. Buyer sees the unit in their registry, private by default (15.04 D-01), with its history intact — provenance (13.03.06) and condition evidence (13.02.04) attached.
7. Seller's collection moves the unit to "formerly owned" (15.04 DT-03) — their gear CV, retained.
8. The chain is one link longer, and nobody typed anything.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Goods returned after transfer** | Ownership reverses by appending a compensating event that **references the transfer it compensates** (D-03, D-06). The link is what lets a renderer tell a sale-and-return from a genuine buy-back years later — without it the two are indistinguishable, and they are different facts | Registry shows transfer and reversal; the history is that a sale happened and unwound. Deleting it would be a lie. Owner count is **not** incremented — see DT-07 |
| **Refund with no return** (13.08.01 D-03 partial-refund-with-retention; return freight exceeds item value) | **No reversal.** The buyer still has the guitar, so the buyer still owns it. Ownership follows the object, not the money | Chain unchanged. The money event is the order's, not the chain's |
| **Chargeback months after settlement, goods not returned** | Same rule: no reversal. The seller has lost the money and the buyer has the object; that is a dispute (domain 24), not an ownership fact | Chain unchanged; order shows the dispute. The chain does not arbitrate who *deserves* the object |
| Listing has no serial | Nothing to key on. Most launch listings | No registry record created; recording an unidentifiable unit creates a phantom. Optional prompt: "add a serial to track this" |
| **Serial mistyped, matches nothing** | Creates a record under a serial that does not exist. Low harm, permanent litter, and it will not reconcile when the real unit surfaces | Buyer's registry shows the unit; the serial is correctable by the owner (15.01.01), and a correction is an amendment on the record, not a new record |
| **Serial mistyped, collides with a record another user actively claims** | **The nasty case.** The transfer must NOT silently attach to a stranger's instrument. Resolution per DT-05: composite key narrows it; if a match still carries an active ownership claim held by anyone other than the seller, the append lands as a **contested transfer** — both claims held, neither auto-wins, no tenure closed | Per 15 D-07 the platform surfaces the contest and does not adjudicate. Both parties notified; routed to the shared dispute path (15.02.04). The buyer's order is unaffected — this never touches their money |
| **Seller was never the recorded owner** (bought off-platform, never registered) | The chain gains a link with no predecessor. It must not invent one | `prior tenure — unrecorded`, rendered as a gap. Per 15 D-06 — the honest majority case at launch |
| **Consignment sale** (13.09.02): the seller holds the unit with selling rights but does not own it | The tenure that closes belongs to the **consignor**, not the selling dealer. A naive seller→buyer append records the dealer as having owned it — a phantom tenure and a factually false link | Requires 15.08's custody model (**Should**, and this node is **Must** — see DT-10). Absent 15.08, the closing tenure renders `unrecorded` rather than asserting the seller. Never fabricate |
| **Trade-in / part-exchange** (13.09.01) | **Two transfers, opposite directions, one order**: the buyer's part-exchanged unit moves buyer→dealer at the same settlement that moves dealer→buyer. Nobody had specified the second one | Both units move in the buyer's registry — one in, one out |
| Bundle sale (13.03.05) | One order → N transfers, one per constituent unit. The bundle's atomicity (13.03.05 D-02) is a claim about the **sale**, not about the chain | Per 13.03.05 D-02 |
| **Bundle partially returned** | Reverses exactly the returned unit's event. Because each constituent got its own event (above), this needs no special case — which is the argument for per-unit events over a bundle-level one | Chain shows the retained units transferred and the returned one reversed |
| Bundle with some constituents unserialised | Transfers what it can; records what it couldn't | Partial state — "3 of 5 items registered; 2 have no serial" |
| **Registry write fails** | **The order must still settle** (D-04). The write is retried; if it cannot land within a bounded window it is escalated to a human operator (CX-M27) rather than abandoned. Never blocks, never silently drops | Buyer sees the unit as **`registration pending`** — never absent. A silent drop is the one outcome D-06 cannot tolerate, because an invisible gap cannot be noticed by anyone |
| **Settlement retried; transfer fires twice** | **Exactly one transfer event per order line per unit**, regardless of how many times settlement re-runs or how many workers process it concurrently — a re-run adds nothing | No user-visible effect. 15.01.03's Error state requires this too ("a double-tap must not double-transfer") |
| **Registration still pending, buyer lists the unit for sale meanwhile** | Race: listing rights derive from custody + selling rights (15.08 D-02), and the claim has not landed — so the buyer cannot sell gear they legitimately own | Listing check must treat a `registration pending` order as sufficient provenance for the buyer's own listing. The buyer must never be punished for the platform's delay |
| **Two orders settle for the same unit** (oversell escaped 13.03.03) | Two conflicting transfers. Neither auto-wins | Contested; both buyers and the seller notified; routed to domain 24. The chain records both attempts — the honest record of a platform failure |
| **Buyer re-purchases a unit they previously owned** | Legitimate: A→B→A with a genuine gap between. **Two real tenures**, not a reversal | Chain shows both tenures. Distinguishable from a sale-and-return only because the reversal carries a compensating link (D-06) and this does not |
| **Buyer and seller are the same beneficial owner** (personal → own studio Ltd; two accounts, one human) | Legitimate and common — musicians sell gear into their own company for tax reasons. But repeated, it manufactures Witnessed links to make a fake look well-provenanced (DT-09) | Recorded and **marked as a related-party transfer**, never blocked and never hidden. Blocking breaks a normal transaction; hiding launders it |
| **Purchase made for a band/studio entity but ordered on a personal account** | The chain records the identity on the order. If checkout never captured an entity, the band's PA is recorded as personally owned — a systematic error discovered years later at dissolution (15.01.03 DT-03) | See DT-06 and Q-05. The rule this node owns: **the custodian recorded is the identity on the order, never the human who clicked** |
| **Local pickup with cash off-platform** (13.11) | No settlement event → no automatic transfer. The chain is not silent, though: it falls back to 15.01.03's manual handshake at a lower evidence tier | Per DT-11 — the chain gets a **weaker link, not a hole**. The revenue half remains 13.11 Q-01 (User) |
| Sold off-platform after being bought here | The chain ends with a stale custodian who no longer holds it | Chain is honest about last-known, never asserts current. Bears on 15.01.02 Q-02 (claim decay) |
| **Stolen report lands post-settlement** | Ownership transferred to a buyer of stolen goods. The chain records the transfer **faithfully and does not reverse it** — the sale happened, and no automatic rewrite is defensible. Under *nemo dat* the buyer may hold no title at all, but the platform does not adjudicate title (15 D-07) | Flag renders **on the chain** (15.04 precedent: flag state makes publication a recovery asset). If the goods are surrendered, that is a further transfer with `reason: recovery` — **recovery is not reversal**; the buyer's tenure was real and the record says so. Legal remedy remains 13.03.07 Q-02 |
| **Layaway: unit destroyed at instalment 3 of 6** | No transfer ever fired, so there is nothing to reverse and the chain correctly records nothing | Confirms the trigger choice: had transfer fired at commitment, the chain would assert ownership of an object that no longer exists and was never delivered. Loss allocation is 13.06.03 Q-01 |
| Ownership contested at settlement (15.01.02) | 15.01.03 blocks *manual* transfers on a contested record. This transfer must **not** block — the sale already completed and the money already moved | Appends as contested. Blocking here would leave the money moved and the chain silent, which is the worst of both |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Settlement processing | Invisible — this is a byproduct, not a user task. No spinner, because no one is waiting on it |
| Empty | No serial → no transfer | Nothing; optionally "add a serial to track this". Absence of a record is never rendered as an absence of ownership |
| Populated | Transfer recorded | Unit appears in the buyer's registry, private by default, Witnessed tier, with history and evidence attached |
| Error | Registry write fails | **The order still settles** (D-04). Unit shows `registration pending` with a retry in flight; if the write cannot land within a bounded window it is escalated to an operator, and the buyer still sees `registration pending` rather than nothing |
| Partial | Bundle with some constituents unserialised; or composite key resolved but claim contested | Transfers what it can, names what it couldn't: "3 of 5 registered — 2 have no serial". Contested constituents render as contested, not as failed |

- Reversed by **13.08.01 Returns & Refunds** ([path](../13.08-returns-rma-warranty/13.08.01-returns-refunds.md)) — **only on return of the goods, never on refund alone**. Its D-03 (partial refund with retention) is the case that proves ownership follows the object, not the money.
- Fans out for **13.03.05 Bundles** ([path](../13.03-listings-inventory/13.03.05-parts-bundles-bstock.md)) — one order, N per-unit transfers. Per-unit events (not a bundle-level event) are what make partial returns work without a special case.
- **Fires twice for 13.09.01 Trade-In / Part-Exchange** ([path](../13.09-tradein-consignment/13.09.01-tradein-part-exchange.md)) — two transfers in opposite directions on one settlement. Previously unspecified anywhere.
- **Depends on Domain 01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) for the entity-buyer case — bands and studios own gear independently of their members (`personas.md`). Per DT-06 the recorded custodian is the identity on the order; whether checkout captures an entity is **13.06.01**'s, and it does not currently say. **Q-05**.
- **Feeds 13.03.06 Provenance Display** ([path](../13.03-listings-inventory/13.03.06-provenance-session-history-display.md)) and **15.01.04 Provenance Chain View** — the chain this writes is what they read. Per DT-07 they need the reversal's compensating link to render owner count truthfully, and per DT-09 they must never render chain depth as chain strength.
- **Enables 13.03.07 Stolen-Serial Screening** ([path](../13.03-listings-inventory/13.03.07-stolen-serial-screening.md)) — you cannot report stolen what was never recorded as yours. Its Q-02 (post-sale theft report) lands here: the chain records faithfully and does not reverse; `recovery` is a further transfer, not a reversal.
- **Degrades to 13.11 Local Pickup** ([path](../13.11-local-pickup-meetup-safety.md)) — per DT-11 the cash branch falls back to 15.01.03's manual handshake at a lower tier. The revenue half stays 13.11 Q-01.
- Escalates to **Domain 24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — contested transfers, oversell collisions, related-party patterns (DT-09).
- Consumes **CX-M19 Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** — append-only, compensating events. CX-M19 already serves gear-marketplace; no gap here.
- Consumes **CX-M27 Admin, Back-Office & Support** — operator escalation on a registry write that cannot land (D-04).
- **CX-M04 Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer should NOT be extended to this node** — per DT-08, CX-M04 is constitutive and this is evidentiary. Reverses the breadth pass's recommendation. See Q-03.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Settlement writes the ownership event; 15 owns the record, 13 owns the event | Per DT-01. |
| D-02 | Recording and visibility are separate concerns; the chain always records, the owner controls visibility, default private | Per DT-02 — **the always-record half requires owner ratification; this is a values position, not an agent call.** The visibility half is settled by 15.04 D-01/D-02. Per DT-10, not ratifiable independently of 15.04's sequencing. |
| D-03 | Reversal is a compensating append, never a deletion | An append-only chain that can be rewritten is not a chain. |
| D-04 | A registry write failure never blocks settlement | The buyer's money cannot be held hostage to a provenance nicety. The write is retried, and if it cannot land within a bounded window it is escalated to an operator rather than dropped; the buyer sees `registration pending`, never nothing. |
| D-05 | **The trigger is settlement — never payment, never delivery** | Per DT-03. Payment precedes dispatch; delivery precedes the inspection window. Only settlement is final, and firing early forces a reversal on every in-window return. |
| D-06 | **A reversal references the transfer it compensates** | Per DT-07. Without the link, sale-and-return is indistinguishable from a genuine buy-back, and owner count — a real price signal in vintage gear — inflates. Costs nothing at write time; impossible to retrofit. |
| D-07 | **Ownership follows the object, not the money** | Return reverses; refund alone does not. Resolves partial-refund-with-retention (13.08.01 D-03), chargeback, and keep-it remedies with one rule. Corrects the breadth pass, which had refund reversing ownership. |
| D-08 | **The event timestamp is the settlement time, not the write time** | The write is async and may retry for hours (D-04). Recording when the row landed rather than when ownership changed misrepresents the only fact the chain exists to hold. |
| D-09 | **Resolution is on the composite key `(maker, model, era, serial)`, never on serial alone; a collision with another party's active claim appends as contested and never silently attaches** | Per DT-05, applying 15 D-05 and 15 D-07. |
| D-10 | **The recorded custodian is the identity on the order, never the human who clicked** | Per DT-06. Bands and studios own gear independently of their members (`personas.md`). |
| D-11 | **No buyer confirmation gates the settlement path** | Per DT-04. Escrow release between two identified parties *is* the witness; a buyer's tap is self-assertion, a weaker tier. |
| D-12 | **Related-party transfers are recorded and marked, never blocked and never hidden** | Per DT-09. Selling into your own company is legitimate; blocking breaks it, hiding launders it. |
| D-13 | **A re-run of settlement produces exactly one transfer per order line per unit** | Settlement retries and concurrent workers must not double-append; the outcome is the same whether settlement fires once or many times. |
| D-14 | **Never fabricate a tenure the platform did not observe** | Per 15 D-06. An unregistered seller's prior tenure renders `unrecorded`; a consignment sale without 15.08 renders the closing tenure `unrecorded` rather than asserting the dealer. |
