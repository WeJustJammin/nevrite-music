## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Cart & Checkout ↔ Order Lifecycle

**Relationship**: Checkout is a fan-out — one cart, one payment, N vendor orders. The gear-specific problem (13.06.01 DT-01) is that each vendor's lines carry independent feasibility, so the fan-out can partially fail: two orders created, one line undeliverable to this country at any price.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: full primary access here and nowhere else in the domain. A partial checkout failure is exactly the "professional complexity" the persona doc says they will not tolerate — this is where Fan abandons.
- **Musician**: hits the freight cliff mid-cart without expecting it.
- **Producer**: international landed cost must resolve *before* commitment (13.06.01 Edge Cases).
- **Operator**: buys at volume; most likely to hit oversize freight quoting.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The cart holds intent; orders hold commitments. The units are the contested entities and they are **not held by the cart** (13.06.01 D-01) — so a cart's contents can evaporate before checkout. That is correct and it makes the checkout moment the only consistency point.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cart → per-item eligibility → per-vendor quote → claims (13.03.03) → authorisation → N orders. Per 13.06.01 D-03 claims precede or accompany authorisation. **If claim N fails after claim 1 succeeded, the chain has no clean unwind** — this is 13.06.01 Q-01, the sub-domain's hardest open question, and it is joint with CX-M01's escrow model.
3. **Permission intersection**: All four personas may buy. Only three may sell, so the seller side of every order is professional.
4. **Notification fan-out**: One checkout notifies N sellers. Bounded by cart size.
5. **State transition conflict**: A unit claimed by another buyer between cart-add and checkout (13.03.03 D-02, expected and designed). A stolen-registry hit landing between cart and capture (13.03.07 D-02, re-screen catches it). Both are races the checkout must lose gracefully rather than prevent.

### CX-02: Order Lifecycle ↔ Ownership Transfer

**Relationship**: The thesis seam. Settlement — the moment escrow releases and the sale is final — is the only instant on the platform when a chattel's ownership demonstrably changes with money, two identified parties and a timestamp as evidence. 13.06.05 D-01 makes that instant write the chain. Everything domain 15 is worth depends on this edge existing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: gear accretes into a registry they never maintained.
- **Producer**: studio inventory maintains itself — the persona defined by having no time for admin.
- **Operator**: fleet asset register stays current because buying updates it.
- **Fan**: the persona who will never register anything deliberately, whose first guitar now has a theft record they didn't ask for. The clearest case for D-02's separate-recording-from-visibility model.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The registry record is domain 15's; this appends to it. Append-only with compensating events (13.06.05 D-03) means no merge strategy is needed — the chain is the merge strategy.
2. **Trigger chain**: Settle → append transfer. **Registry failure must not block settlement** (13.06.05 D-04) — queue and retry. This is an async, eventually-consistent edge on purpose: the money path cannot depend on the provenance path.
3. **Permission intersection**: Consent governs visibility, never recording (13.06.05 D-02) — **requires owner ratification** (that file's Q-01). If the owner rules the other way, this cross-cut's value collapses and 13.03.06/13.03.07 lose their data source with it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Buyer gains a registry entry; seller's custody closes. Low fan-out, high meaning.
5. **State transition conflict**: A refund after transfer reverses by appending (13.06.05 D-03). A stolen report landing post-settlement (13.03.07 Q-02) means ownership transferred to a buyer of stolen goods — the chain records it faithfully, which is right, and the legal remedy is unresolved.

### CX-03: Layaway ↔ Ownership Transfer

**Relationship**: Layaway inserts months between commitment and transfer, creating a state nothing else in the domain has: an object physically held by the seller, partially paid for by the buyer, owned by neither in any useful sense. 13.06.03 DT-01 asks the question this cross-cut exists to hold: **if it burns during the hold, whose loss is it?**

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona layaway exists for (a £3k instrument on a portfolio of small income streams) and therefore the persona holding the risk.
- **Producer**: same shape, higher values.
- **Operator**: offers it as shop policy; holds the object and therefore the custody risk.
- **Fan**: could use it; least able to evaluate what "held" means for risk.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The unit is held by 13.03.03 as a long-lived claim, far outside the reservation timeouts that file contemplates (13.03.03 Q-03). Custody sits with the seller; ownership sits nowhere. **Domain 15 distinguishes custody from ownership** — that vocabulary is exactly what this needs, and neither file currently uses it (13.06.03 Q-01).
2. **Trigger chain**: Commit → hold → instalments → final payment → transfer. A default mid-chain releases the hold and forfeits money (13.06.03 D-02) with **no transfer ever occurring** — so the chain records nothing, correctly.
3. **Permission intersection**: Operator configures terms; the other personas agree to them. The asymmetry is why forfeit must be stated in money at commitment (13.06.03 D-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Instalment reminders, and forfeit terms restated at each one.
5. **State transition conflict**: The unresolved case. If the held unit is destroyed at instalment 3 of 6, there is no transfer to reverse and no ownership to claim against — the buyer has paid toward an object that no longer exists and never was theirs. **Nothing in the domain answers this** (13.06.03 Q-01).

### CX-04: Service Add-Ons ↔ Order Lifecycle

**Relationship**: Add-ons insert a `pre-dispatch service` state into the order and, per 13.06.04 DT-01, can generate evidence that contradicts the listing the order is against — an inspection finding an undisclosed fault, paid for by the buyer, performed while the seller still holds the object and before money settled.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: highest value — a bench test converts "probably works" into a tested claim before settlement.
- **Musician**: buys setups; may also fulfil them (the multi-hyphenate is both sides).
- **Operator**: offers add-ons from an in-house bench.
- **Fan**: needs the setup most, can evaluate the upsell least (13.06.04 DT-02).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The order holds the add-on line; domain 05 holds the service job. Two records, one object, and possibly a third party holding it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Order paid → `pre-dispatch service` → work → evidence into the pack (13.02.04) → dispatch. If the work reveals a fault, the chain **must be able to fork** to renegotiation or cancellation — and currently has no defined outcome (13.06.04 Q-01). That is a gap at the most valuable moment the feature has.
3. **Permission intersection**: A third-party fulfiller takes custody of an object the buyer has paid for and never seen (13.06.04 Q-02) — insurance and liability both move, and CX-M09 delegated authority is implicated.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Buyer, seller and fulfiller on a single order line.
5. **State transition conflict**: Work started makes the add-on non-refundable (13.06.04 D-03) while the *gear* remains refundable — one order line hardens while another stays soft. A cancellation after work is done is therefore a partial unwind, which the order model must support.

### CX-05: Cart & Checkout ↔ Layaway

**Relationship**: Layaway does not fit the cart. A cart is a momentary basket resolved in one authorisation (13.06.01); layaway is a per-unit, per-seller agreement with contract terms (CX-M03), a months-long inventory hold, and a forfeit schedule. Recorded at Medium confidence because the *incompatibility* is inferred from both files' semantics rather than stated in either.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer**: would encounter this by trying to layaway one item while buying others normally.
- **Operator**: offers layaway as policy; would be the one surprised if the cart tried to compose it.
- **Fan**: unlikely to encounter.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. Step 5 should confirm the expected resolution: layaway is initiated **from a listing, not from a cart**, and a cart containing a layaway-intended item either rejects it or splits it out. The open risk is a mixed cart where one line is layaway and three are normal — one payment, four fulfilment models, months apart. That should be prohibited rather than engineered.
