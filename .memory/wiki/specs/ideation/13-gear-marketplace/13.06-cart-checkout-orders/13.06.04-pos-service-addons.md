# Feature: Point-of-Sale Service Add-Ons

> **Number**: 13.06.04
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Buying work on the gear at the moment you buy the gear — a professional setup on a guitar, an inspection on a vintage amp, a re-tube, a fret level. The work happens before dispatch, by the seller or by a third party, and it arrives as part of the purchase rather than as a separate errand two weeks later. It exists because a used instrument almost always needs something, and the buyer's willingness to pay for it is highest at the moment of purchase.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Adds "pro setup" to a used guitar purchase. Also **fulfils** add-ons if they're the multi-hyphenate who does setups — the persona is explicitly both sides of this platform. |
| Producer | Full | Adds an inspection/bench-test on used outboard — the highest-value add-on in the domain, because it converts "probably works" into a tested claim before money settles. |
| Operator | Config | Offers add-ons on their own listings (a shop that does setups in-house). Configures a menu rather than buying. |
| Fan | Read-only (may buy) | The persona who most *needs* a setup on a first guitar and least knows it exists. Also the persona most vulnerable to an upsell they can't evaluate — see DT-02. |

## Behavior

An add-on is a service line item on a gear order, fulfilled **before dispatch**. The service itself is domain 05's (Services Marketplace); the *offer at the point of sale* and its ordering relationship to dispatch is this domain's. The add-on may be fulfilled by the seller (a shop with a bench) or dispatched to a third party (a local luthier), which is a custody detour on someone else's object.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer views a used guitar; seller offers "professional setup — £65"
2. Buyer adds it at checkout
3. Order enters a `pre-dispatch service` state; the work happens
4. Work is evidenced into the pack (13.02.04) — what was done, before shipping
5. Unit dispatches; order proceeds normally

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The setup reveals a fault the seller didn't disclose | The add-on has just produced evidence against the seller's own listing | See DT-01 — genuinely valuable and genuinely awkward. Buyer must be told; likely a return right (13.08.01) |
| Third-party fulfilment adds a custody hop | The object leaves the seller before it reaches the buyer, with a stranger in between | Custody is tracked; the evidence pack must capture hand-off, or damage becomes unattributable |
| Work delays dispatch beyond the buyer's expectation | The buyer bought an object and got a wait | Stated lead time at purchase, not after |
| Buyer cancels after work is done | The work is not returnable | Add-on becomes non-refundable once started; must be stated at purchase |
| Add-on offered on an item that doesn't need it | Upsell on the persona who can't evaluate it | See DT-02 |
| Seller has no capacity to fulfil | Add-on availability must reflect real capacity, not a menu | Consumes CX-M05 |
| Add-on on a local pickup order (13.11) | No dispatch — the work happens before collection | Different sequencing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing/checkout render | Add-on menu inline |
| Empty | Seller offers none | Section absent |
| Populated | Add-ons available | Menu with price and lead time |
| Error | Add-on unavailable at checkout | Removed with reason; the gear purchase proceeds |
| Partial | Work in progress | "In the workshop — dispatching after setup" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An add-on is a simple upsell line item | The sweep's framing (#28), and how it looks on a checkout page | ❌ REJECTED: a pre-dispatch inspection is an **independent examination of the seller's own disclosure**, paid for by the buyer, performed while the seller still has the object. When the setup finds a truss rod issue the seller never disclosed (13.02.02), the add-on has generated evidence against the listing it's attached to — *before* money settled and *before* the object crossed a border. That is enormously valuable (it prevents the expensive-unwind case 13.06.02 DT-02 worries about) and structurally awkward (the seller may be the one performing the examination of their own claim). **A seller-fulfilled inspection is not an independent one, and the UI must not imply it is.** Third-party inspection is the version with real value. |
| DT-02 | Offering add-ons is straightforwardly good for buyers | More service, better outcome | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: good for the three professional personas, who know what a setup costs and whether the instrument needs one. For the **Fan** it is an upsell they cannot evaluate, attached to a purchase they're already nervous about, from a seller with an obvious interest. The persona doc is explicit that Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" and has no professional stake — that is exactly the profile a low-value high-margin add-on preys on. Not a reason to withhold it: a first guitar genuinely benefits from a setup more than a pro's does. But the framing must be **need-based and honest** ("new guitars usually need this; here's what it does"), and a seller marking every listing as needing an expensive setup is a pattern for domain 24. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The service is domain 05's; the point-of-sale offer and pre-dispatch sequencing are this domain's | Clean seam, consistent with D-14. |
| D-02 | A seller-fulfilled inspection must not be presented as independent | Per DT-01 — implying independence where none exists is worse than not offering it. |
| D-03 | Add-ons become non-refundable once work starts, stated at purchase | Work is not returnable; discovering that at cancellation is a dispute. |
| D-04 | Add-on framing is need-based and honest, not maximal | Per DT-02 — the Fan persona is the one who can't evaluate the upsell. |
