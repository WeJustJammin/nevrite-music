# Feature: Order Management & Lifecycle

> **Number**: 13.06.02
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The order's life from payment to settled: paid → packed → dispatched → in transit → delivered → settled, plus every way it deviates. Gear orders run longer and fail differently from retail — a freight shipment takes weeks, an international one clears customs, a $40k instrument gets inspected, and the thing being shipped is irreplaceable, so "we'll send another" is never available.

The order is also the domain's **clock-keeper**. Money, an irreplaceable object, a statutory return right, a carrier insurance window and a provenance write are all in flight at once, on different timers, owned by different parties. The order is the only artifact that sees all of them, and the failure this feature exists to prevent is a remedy expiring silently while the person who needed it was waiting for a different clock (DT-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own orders, both sides) | Tracks the amp they bought; as a seller works a short queue of one or two units. Wants to know where it is and when the money lands — the persona's stated pain is income scattered and invisible, and the inspection window is precisely the gap between "sold" and "paid" that makes it invisible (DT-02). **The persona most likely to be on tour when a re-consent gate or a delivery appointment fires** (D-06, D-09) — their order actions must survive being answered three days late from a phone in a van. |
| Producer | Full (own, both sides) | Longer international orders with customs holds. Needs "stuck in customs for 11 days" to be legible rather than a silent gap. Their "not as described" is **functional, not cosmetic** — a compressor that pumps wrong is only discoverable in a real mix, which is the concrete argument for the inspection window's length (DT-02). Also the persona who is routinely **seller and inspector on the same order** (13.06.04 D-02) — the order labels that, never launders it. |
| Operator | Full (own, both sides) | Runs an order queue at volume from a back office; needs bulk operations and a work-queue view keyed on **what is due today**, not an order-detail page per unit. Two staff on one account act on the same order concurrently (D-13). Sets the handling time that their own auto-cancel clock is derived from (D-04). Takes freight delivery appointments at a loading dock, from a phone, offline (CX-M32). |
| Fan | Read-only (own orders, buyer side) | Tracks what they bought. No seller side. Needs plain "where is it" answers; will not interpret carrier status codes or a customs hold. **The persona whose remedies expire silently**: they do not know a statutory return window exists, that a carrier claim window is shorter than it, or that a delivery scan started a clock. The order must run their clocks *for* them and tell them in time — D-03 is written for this persona specifically. Read-only means they never transition an order by hand; they confirm, dispute, or do nothing, and doing nothing must be safe. |

## Behavior

The order is authoritative once the listing hits `sold` (13.03.02 hands off here). It composes escrow state (CX-M01), fulfilment state (CX-M14), and the gear-specific detours: customs holds, freight scheduling, damage claims (13.07.04), and returns (13.08.01). **Settlement releases the seller's money and triggers ownership transfer (13.06.05)** — but settlement closes the *money*, not the *record* (DT-04): the order survives as the reference object for warranty claims, theft reports and statutory rights that outlive it.

Five properties define the order:

1. **It composes, never reimplements** (D-01). Escrow is CX-M01's, tracking is CX-M14's. What is domain-owned is the *shape* — the states that exist in no other commerce domain (DT-01).
2. **Every stall is bounded and resolves automatically** (D-02). Money plus a unique object in indefinite limbo is the worst state available.
3. **It runs concurrent clocks, not one** (D-03). Settlement, statutory return, carrier claim and dispute clocks run simultaneously, start at different events, and expire out of order.
4. **It snapshots what was agreed** (D-05). The delivery address as entered at checkout and the disclosure state as it stood at purchase (13.02.04 D-05) are immutable on the order. Every "who was wrong?" question in this file is adjudicable *because* those snapshots exist.
5. **Carrier evidence is weighted by carrier incentive** (D-08). An acceptance weight is uncontestable; a delivery scan is the one carrier event the carrier is motivated to fake.

### The Clock Model

Four clocks run on a delivered order. They start at different events, have different owners, and **the shortest one is rarely the one the user is watching**.

| Clock | Owner | Starts at | Expiry destroys |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Settlement** (inspection window) | Platform | Verified delivery (D-07) | The buyer's right to dispute before funds release |
| **Statutory return** | Law (jurisdiction) | Delivery | The buyer's change-of-mind right (13.08.01 D-04) |
| **Carrier / insurance claim** | Carrier or insurer | Delivery, sometimes acceptance | The *funding* for a transit-damage remedy (13.07.04) |
| **Dispute** | Domain 24 | Dispute opened | Escalation rights |

**The rule** (D-03): any clock whose expiry destroys a remedy is **protected by filing before it closes**, not by waiting for the dispute to resolve. The moment a buyer reports transit damage, the platform files a **protective carrier claim** immediately — before adjudicating anything — because the carrier claim window is routinely shorter than the statutory return window it is competing with, and once it closes the money to fund *any* remedy is gone.

### Verified Delivery

The settlement clock does not start on a bare carrier scan (DT-06). **Verified delivery** is, in precedence order:

1. **Buyer confirmation** — always sufficient, at any value.
2. **Carrier POD with signature or delivery photo** — sufficient at or above the value threshold that makes insurance structural (13.07.04 D-01). Above that threshold, signature/photo POD is a **required carrier service**, purchased with the label, not an option.
3. **Bare delivery scan** — sufficient below that threshold only.
4. **Backstop**: where no delivery event of any kind exists, the clock starts at `dispatch + carrier transit SLA + 7 days`. The order renders `no-delivery-confirmation` throughout, and the arrival evidence prompt fires here instead, labelled `post-delivery` by definition (13.02.04's fallback).

### Happy Path

1. Payment captured; system creates one order per vendor and snapshots the delivery address and the disclosure state as they stood at purchase (D-05)
2. Seller notified; the order enters the seller's queue with a due date derived from **their own stated handling time** (13.12), not a platform default (D-04)
3. Seller packs and dispatches; the evidence pack seals at label print + 15 min or carrier acceptance scan (13.02.04 D-03)
4. Carrier acceptance scan ingests; tracking flows; buyer sees progress, and sees **carrier silence as silence** (D-11) rather than a stale status implying movement
5. Carrier delivers; POD satisfies verified delivery (D-07); the arrival evidence prompt fires within the delivery notification (13.02.04 step 8)
6. Settlement clock opens. The statutory return clock and the carrier claim clock open alongside it, and the order shows **which expires first** (D-03)
7. Window closes with no open dispute or damage claim → `settled` → escrow releases to the seller (CX-M01) → ownership transfer appends to the registry (13.06.05)
8. The order stays queryable forever as the reference object for warranty, theft and statutory claims that outlive settlement (D-14)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Seller doesn't dispatch** | Clock derived from the seller's own stated handling time (13.12), never a platform default — the seller set the term, so the term is theirs (D-04). Notice at `due`, `due+1d`, `due+3d`; auto-cancel and full refund at `due+5d` | Buyer: full refund, no action required. Seller: cancellation lands on their scorecard (CX-M08) with the handling time they published quoted back at them |
| **Held in customs for weeks** | Not a failure — normal for international gear (13.07.02). The settlement clock has not started; nothing is expiring | Explicit `in-customs` state with the honest answer: this can take weeks. Days-in-customs counter, no fake ETA. Duty owed under DAP is surfaced here, not at the door |
| **Customs seizes the item** (CITES violation — 13.07.03) | **Not a hold. The object is gone and is not coming back.** No return exists — there is nothing to return | Order → `seized`. Full refund from escrow to the buyer immediately; the buyer did not cause this. Seller bears (13.07.03 D-02 made CITES a hard gate precisely so this is rare); routes to domain 24. No registry transfer — settlement never occurred |
| **Arrives damaged** | A claim received before the inspection deadline atomically moves the order to `claim-open`, stopping settlement and ownership transfer; it routes to 13.07.04 with the sealed pack as baseline. **A protective carrier claim files immediately** (D-03), before adjudication | Claim flow, not a return flow — different remedy, different payer. Buyer sees the claim already filed, not a request to file one |
| **Not as described** | Routes to 13.08.01 against the disclosure-at-purchase snapshot (D-05) | Return flow |
| **Freight delivery needs scheduling** | Buyer must be present with a way to receive 200kg. Requirements were stated at checkout, not at the kerb (13.07.01 D-04) | `delivery-appointment` state. Parcel logic does not apply — an appointment is a two-party commitment, not a tracking status |
| **Buyer refuses the freight delivery at the door** (no lift gate, won't fit the stairs) | The object is on a truck, undelivered, and redelivery costs real money. Not a return — nothing was received | `delivery-refused`. Cost allocation follows where delivery legally ends (13.07.01 Q-03, unresolved) — the order holds the state and the evidence; it does not invent the liability rule |
| **Freight line needs a human quote and cannot resolve in a checkout session** | Resolves 13.06.01 Q-03 / 13.07.01 Q-02 **on the order side**: the `awaiting-quote` state exists. No payment is authorised, no claim is taken, the listing does not go `sold`, and the unit stays buyable by others (D-10) | "We'll quote this within 2 business days — it's not held for you until you accept." Honest: a quote request is not a reservation. Expires at 7 days if the seller never quotes |
| **Seller amends a disclosure after purchase, before dispatch** | Resolves 13.03.02 Q-01 / 13.02.04's CX-02 handoff. Dispatch is **blocked**; order → `disclosure-amended`; the buyer bought against a snapshot and the object changed. **Only the buyer may cancel** (D-06) — the seller cannot use an amendment to exit a sale they regret | Buyer: the diff, both photos, "this wasn't in what you bought — keep it or cancel?" 72h to answer; silence auto-cancels with full refund. Seller: **no cancellation penalty for a volunteered amendment** (D-06) — penalising honesty rebuilds the concealment incentive the domain exists to remove |
| **Seller amends a disclosure to escape a sale** (better offer arrived) | The abuse case D-06 opens. Three counterweights: the amendment requires evidence (a flaw photo — 13.02.02 D-03); **only the buyer elects**, so if the buyer says "proceed" the seller is bound; and a seller whose amendment rate is anomalous fans out to CX-M08 and domain 24 | Seller cannot cancel from this state at all. The exit they wanted does not exist here — it exists as an ordinary seller cancellation, with its cost |
| **Pre-dispatch add-on inspection contradicts the seller's disclosure** (CX-04) | The order **forks** rather than dead-ending — it enters `disclosure-amended` by the same mechanism as a volunteered amendment, with the finding attached and labelled by capturer role (13.02.04 D-07). The order supplies the fork; **which remedies sit on it is 13.06.04 Q-01's call, not this file's** | Buyer sees the inspection finding against what they bought. The add-on's own refundability is 13.06.04 D-03's |
| **Carrier marks it delivered and the buyer did not receive it** | The one carrier event with an incentive to lie (DT-06). Below the value threshold a bare scan starts the clock; **at or above it, POD is required and a bare scan does not** (D-07). On a buyer non-receipt report, the settlement clock **stops immediately** — it does not pause pending investigation, it stops | "You told us it hasn't arrived — nothing is counting down." The burden is the carrier's POD, not the buyer's disproof of a scan |
| **Seller ships to the wrong address** | Resolves Q-03. **The checkout address snapshot (D-05) makes this adjudicable**: label ≠ order address → seller error; label = order address, address wrong → buyer error. Remedy ladder (D-12): (1) platform requests carrier intercept/recall **immediately on detection, without waiting for the seller**; (2) recovered → re-dispatch, clock restarts at the new delivery, buyer unaffected; (3) not recovered → the object is gone and there is no replacement — routes to 13.07.04 as a **loss claim**, never to 13.08.01 (nothing to return) | Seller error: buyer refunded in full from escrow **regardless of the insurance outcome** — the buyer does not carry the seller's typo or the insurer's timeline; seller and insurer settle between themselves. Buyer error: platform requests recall and promises nothing; the buyer bears, stated plainly and immediately rather than after three weeks of hope |
| **Buyer never confirms delivery** | The window is a right, not an obligation. Auto-settle fires at the settlement clock's expiry from verified delivery (D-07); with no delivery event at all, from the D-07 backstop | Countdown with the consequence stated: "Funds release to the seller on the 14th unless you raise something." The Fan persona is told what the clock is *for*, once, in plain words |
| **Carrier goes silent for days mid-transit** | Reported as silence, never as a stale status implying progress (D-11). No clock is running — the settlement clock has not started | "No update since Tuesday 14:02" — the last real event and its age. Never a synthesised "in transit" |
| **Buyer disputes at the same instant auto-settle fires** | Race. **Dispute wins on tie** (D-15): a dispute registered at any moment before funds actually release blocks the release. A dispute landing after release routes to 13.08.01/13.07.04 as a post-settlement claim — the money moved, the remedy did not evaporate (D-14) | Never "your dispute arrived 200ms late". Either it blocked the release or it opened a post-settlement claim — both are real outcomes; neither is a loss |
| **Escrow release fails at settlement** (payout rail down) | Retried automatically; if the rail stays down, escalates to a human (CX-M27). The order does **not** revert to `delivered` — settlement is decided, the payout is pending | Seller: "Settled — payout is retrying, we're on it." Never a silent stall, and never a state that implies the buyer can still dispute when they cannot (D-16) |
| **Registry write fails at settlement** | **Settlement is unaffected** (13.06.05 D-04). Queue and retry; the buyer's money is never hostage to a provenance nicety | Nothing. This is a byproduct, not a user task |
| **Duplicate settlement / double payout** | First settlement wins; a repeated attempt pays no one twice and changes nothing (D-16). Same rule as the pack's seal (13.02.04 D-03) | Nothing. A retry storm must not pay a seller twice |
| **Order settles, then a stolen report lands** | Settlement is not terminal (DT-04), so there is an order to act on. The order → `flagged-post-settlement`; the registry transfer is **not reversed** — a sale genuinely happened and the chain must stay honest (13.06.05 D-03); a `theft-claim` event appends; the unit is suppressed from relisting (13.03.02) pending resolution; the pack is preserved; escalates to domain 24. **Who bears the loss is not this file's call** | Both parties told, with the order and pack attached. Loss allocation → 13.03.07 Q-02 (User, `/create-prd-security`) — legally loaded, and an agent inventing an answer here would be a fabrication with real victims |
| **Buyer and seller are the same human** (two accounts, or selling to their own band entity) | The order runs — the registry chain, the tax record and the fee are all real regardless of intent. But **a self-dealt sale manufactures a comp and a clean provenance link** (DT-05), attacking the two artifacts this domain is proudest of. Settled orders between linked parties are **excluded from the comp set** (13.04) and flagged on the registry chain as related-party | The order looks ordinary to the parties. The comp guide never sees it; the chain records the transfer *and* that it was related-party. Recording it honestly beats refusing it — a refusal just teaches the ring to use a third account |
| **Wash-trading ring: three accounts cycling one unit at escalating prices** | The generalisation of the above, and the one that actually pays. Detection is not this file's — the **signals** are: repeat counterparties, unit velocity, price trajectory against the guide (13.04), and shipment distance ≈ 0. The order emits them; domain 24 and 13.04 decide | Nothing, to the ring. See Q-04 — this needs a real owner and currently has none |
| **Order is for a Band entity** (domain 01), and the member who bought it leaves the band mid-inspection-window | Buyer rights sit with the **entity**, exercisable by any member holding commerce authority (CX-M09) — never with the individual who clicked buy (DT-08). A departing member loses the rights with their authority; the band does not lose its remedy because its bassist quit | Band members with authority see the order and can dispute. The departed member sees it in their history and cannot act on it |
| **Seller's account is deleted mid-order** | The order survives; the counterparty's live rights do not evaporate because the other side left (13.02.04 D-09's rule, applied to the order). The seller becomes a de-identified role reference bound to the order. Payout obligations survive as an obligation of the closed account | Buyer: order proceeds normally; the counterparty renders as "the seller" without a name. Nothing about the buyer's remedy changes |
| **Buyer requests erasure mid-order** | Erasure does not defeat a live order. It cannot: an object is in transit to a physical address, and money is in escrow. Scoped, not refused — de-identified after the order closes and the longest claim window expires (CX-M18) | "Your order is still running — we'll erase when it's closed and the claim windows have passed." Exact durations are statutory → 13.02.04 Q-02 (`/create-prd-security`) |
| **Partial cart failure** — one vendor's order proceeds, another's doesn't | Whichever way 13.06.01 Q-01 resolves, **the order side is the same**: orders that were created are real, independent, and proceed; the failed vendor's order never existed and has no state. There is no half-order (D-17) | Per vendor, itemised: what you bought, what you didn't, what was refunded. Never a single cart-level "something went wrong" |
| **Seller dispatches after the auto-cancel already fired** | The object is in transit and no order exists. Real, and endemic among sellers who don't read notifications | Order re-opens as `unwind-in-transit` and routes to 13.07.04's recall path (D-12), not to 13.08.01 — the buyer has no obligation to receive, pay for, or return a thing they were already refunded for. Seller bears the recall |
| **Two orders exist for the same unit** (oversell race escaped 13.03.03) | Should be impossible — claims are atomic with authorisation (13.06.01 D-03). If it happens: **first claim wins**; the second order cancels with full refund and routes onward (13.03.03 D-01) — a refund is not a fix, because the buyer wanted *that* amp | Loser: "Someone bought it 200ms before you" + routes to alternatives. Never two buyers both told they own one guitar |
| **Local pickup order** (13.11) — no dispatch, no carrier, no scans | The clock model has no delivery event to key on. The **handover is the delivery**: verified delivery = both parties confirming collection. Where money moved through the platform, settlement runs normally from that confirmation. Where cash changed hands off-platform, no order exists at all — the domain's most common sale is invisible here | Platform-settled pickup: joint confirm-collection, then a normal inspection window. Off-platform cash: nothing. That hole is 13.11 DT-01's, and which branch a listing takes is the seller's per-listing choice under 13.11 D-04 (P-09) — never a global rule |
| **Layaway order** (13.06.03) | The order is created at **commitment**, not at final payment — months of instalments need a record to hang on, and the hold is live throughout (D-18). It sits in `layaway-active`, dispatch-blocked, until final payment moves it to `awaiting-dispatch`. Default forfeits per 13.06.03 D-02 and the order closes with **no transfer ever written** (CX-03) | A live order that isn't going anywhere yet, with the schedule and the forfeit figure restated at every instalment |
| **Order-scoped chat used to stall settlement** (CX-M06) | Messaging does **not** pause any clock (D-19). Chat is not a dispute | A buyer wanting the clock stopped must open a dispute, which has consequences. "Message the seller" and "dispute this order" are visibly different actions with different effects |
| **Serial disputer** — buyer opens a dispute on every order | Disputes stop the settlement clock (D-15), so the pattern is a free lever against every seller they buy from | Not adjudicated here — the order emits dispute-rate to CX-M08 and domain 24. The lever stays available (evidence changes weight, never eligibility — 13.02.04 D-06); the *pattern* is what gets acted on |
| **Two Operator staff act on one order concurrently** | The second write must not silently win. Unlike the pack's append-only evidence, an order's state is a single fact, so overwriting it loses information: a transition that finds the state already moved is **refused, not applied** (D-13) | Second actor: "This order was dispatched by Sam 20 seconds ago" — the current state, not a stale form silently overwriting it |
| **Buyer confirms delivery while the item is demonstrably still in transit** | Confirmation is always sufficient at any value (D-07) and this is usually a mis-tap, occasionally a mistake the buyer will regret | Confirmation is **reversible until the settlement clock expires** — the clock started, and the buyer can say "actually it hasn't arrived", which stops it (DT-06's rule). An irreversible tap that starts a countdown on a $40k instrument is a trap |
| **Order spans a jurisdiction whose statutory return window outlives the inspection window** | Guaranteed, not hypothetical — they will not naturally align (13.08.01 Q-03). **The order does not collapse them.** Settlement releases funds at the inspection window's end; the statutory right survives settlement and routes to 13.08.01 as a post-settlement return (DT-04) | Buyer: their statutory right, still live, stated in their own terms. Seller: "settled, and returnable until the 28th" — an honest, uncomfortable fact, which is better than a surprise clawback |

### States

The five UI states, then the order's own lifecycle. The lifecycle states are the domain content (DT-01) — customs, freight appointments, disclosure amendments and post-settlement flags exist in no other commerce domain here.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Order view opened | Timeline skeleton |
| Empty | No orders | "No orders yet" with a route to browse |
| Populated | Order active | Timeline with the current state, what happens next, and **which clock expires first** (D-03) |
| Error | State transition fails | Never leave state ambiguous — money and a unique object are both in flight. The last known state, the failure, and who is on it (D-16) |
| Partial | In transit, no carrier update for days | "No update since Tuesday 14:02" — the last real event and its age, never a stale status implying progress (D-11) |
| `awaiting-quote` | Freight line needs a human quote | Not held, not sold, not paid. Expires at 7 days (D-10) |
| `awaiting-dispatch` | Payment captured | Seller queue; due date from the seller's own handling time (D-04) |
| `disclosure-amended` | Seller amends, or an add-on inspection contradicts (CX-04) | Dispatch blocked. Buyer's diff + 72h to elect. Only the buyer may cancel (D-06) |
| `layaway-active` | Layaway commitment (13.06.03) | Schedule, forfeit figure, dispatch-blocked |
| `pre-dispatch-service` | Add-on purchased (13.06.04) | Work in progress on an object the buyer paid for and has never seen |
| `dispatched` / `in-transit` | Carrier acceptance scan | Tracking, with silence rendered as silence |
| `in-customs` | Customs hold (13.07.02) | Days-in-customs counter. "This can take weeks." No fake ETA |
| `seized` | Customs seizure (13.07.03) | The object is gone. Immediate full refund; no transfer |
| `delivery-appointment` | Freight (13.07.01) | A two-party commitment with a date, not a tracking status |
| `delivery-refused` | Buyer refuses at the door | Object on a truck, undelivered. Cost per 13.07.01 Q-03 |
| `no-delivery-confirmation` | No delivery event of any kind | Backstop clock running (D-07), stated plainly |
| `inspection-window` | Verified delivery (D-07) | Countdown + the consequence + the first-expiring clock |
| `misdelivered` | Label vs. order-address mismatch, or non-receipt against a POD | Recall requested already. Fault attributed from the snapshot (D-05, D-12) |
| `unwind-in-transit` | Seller dispatched post-cancel | Recall path. Buyer owes nothing and receives nothing |
| `disputed` | Dispute opened | Settlement clock stopped. Protective claims already filed (D-03) |
| `claim-open` | Timely damage claim opened | Settlement and ownership transfer suspended until the claim is withdrawn, rejected, or resolved |
| `settled` | Window expired, no dispute | Funds released; transfer written. **Financially closed, evidentially open** (D-14) |
| `flagged-post-settlement` | Theft report, warranty or statutory return after settlement | The order is back, without the money being back |
| `cancelled` | Auto-cancel, buyer election, or claim failure | Full refund; reason recorded; scorecard effect per cause |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The order lifecycle is generic e-commerce and should be a cross-cut | CX-M01 owns escrow, CX-M14 owns fulfilment — what's left looks like a shell | ❌ REJECTED: what's left is the gear-specific *shape*. The states that matter here — customs hold, freight delivery appointment, high-value inspection window, damage claim against a sealed evidence pack — exist in none of the other commerce domains. Digital goods (14) settle instantly; services (05) settle on revision rounds; ticketing (19) settles at the event. The order composes CX-M01 and CX-M14 rather than reimplementing them, but the composition *is* domain content. |
| DT-02 | The inspection window is a buyer-protection convenience | It reads as a grace period, so it gets defaulted to a round number of days | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **structural counterweight to non-fungibility**, and its length is a real economic decision, not a default. In fungible retail a bad unit is replaced; here there is no second unit, so the only remedy is unwind — and unwinding after a $40k instrument has crossed a border is expensive and slow. Too short and the buyer cannot realistically assess (a vintage amp's fault may only appear after an hour at volume; a Producer needs to hear a compressor in a real mix, not in the hallway). Too long and the seller's money is hostage while an irreplaceable object sits in a stranger's house. **The window is also where the seller's cash-flow pain lives**, which is the Musician persona's stated problem ("income visible in one place") pointing the opposite way from buyer protection. This tension is unresolved and belongs to the owner (Q-01). |
| DT-03 | The order runs one clock — the inspection window — and everything else is downstream | The happy path has exactly one countdown, so the model looks single-threaded | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **defect in the single-clock framing**, and it is the sharpest thing in this file. A delivered gear order runs **four clocks concurrently**, started by different events, owned by different parties, expiring out of order — and **the carrier's insurance claim window is routinely shorter than the statutory return window it competes with**. The lethal sequence: buyer returns at day 13 under a 14-day statutory right citing transit damage; the carrier's claim window closed at day 7; the remedy is owed and there is no longer any money to fund it, so the platform or the seller eats a loss that was insured the whole time. Nobody designs for this because each clock looks correct in isolation and no single feature sees all four. The order is the only artifact that does. Resolution: **file protectively before the shortest clock closes** (D-03) rather than waiting for adjudication — standard freight practice, absent from every candidate in the sweep. Confirms 13.08.01 Q-03 and 13.02.04 Q-02 are the same seam viewed from two sides. |
| DT-04 | Settlement is the terminal state | It is where money releases and ownership transfers, which reads as the end | ⚠️ REFRAMED: settlement is terminal for the **money**, not for the **record**. At least four things outlive it — a theft report against the serial (13.03.07 Q-02), a warranty claim (13.08.03), a statutory return window longer than the inspection window (13.08.01 Q-03), and the registry chain itself (13.06.05). An order model that closes and discards at settlement destroys the reference object every one of those needs, and it is the same mistake 13.02.04 D-09 refused when it kept the pack alive past account deletion. The correct framing is **financially closed, evidentially open** (D-14) — which is also why `flagged-post-settlement` has to be a real state rather than a dispute with nowhere to attach. This file's own Overview asserted settlement as "the terminal state that matters"; that was too strong and is now corrected. |
| DT-05 | Self-dealing on an order is a payments problem — fees are paid, so the platform is indifferent | The fee is collected either way, and both parties consented, so it looks victimless | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **real attack on this domain's two crown jewels**, and no file in the sweep mentions it. Sold listings are the comp set (13.03.02 D-01) and settlement writes the ownership chain (13.06.05 D-01). So a sale between two accounts controlled by one person does two things at once: it **manufactures a comp** — the recognised way to build a fake price floor under a "collectible", and gear is exactly the category where thin markets make one comp move a valuation (13.04.03) — and it **manufactures a clean provenance link**, laundering a unit's history through a transfer that looks identical to a real one. The wedge (D-18) is that provenance here is captured at source and therefore trustworthy; a self-dealt order is provenance captured at source and *false*, which is worse than no record, because it carries the platform's authority. The resolution is not refusal (a refusal just teaches the ring to use a third account) but **honest recording plus comp exclusion**: the chain records the transfer *and* that it was related-party; the guide never sees it. The order emits the signals — repeat counterparties, unit velocity, price trajectory vs. the guide, shipment distance ≈ 0 — and 13.04 and domain 24 own the judgement (Q-04). |
| DT-06 | Carrier scan events are uniformly trustworthy third-party evidence | 13.02.04 DT-06/D-14 establishes carrier scans as the most valuable material in the pack — neither party produced them, so neither can contest them | ⚠️ REFRAMED, and this narrows a sibling's decision rather than contradicting it. Carrier evidence is trustworthy **where the carrier has no incentive**, and the delivery scan is the one event where it has a strong one: drivers are measured on delivery completion and mark parcels delivered that are not. An acceptance weight is uncontestable — nobody at the carrier benefits from a wrong number. "Delivered" is different in kind. This matters because a false delivery scan **starts the settlement clock**, so the buyer is now fighting a countdown that began on an event that never happened, and the Fan persona will not know a clock started at all. Resolution: **verified delivery** (D-07) — buyer confirmation always suffices; a signature/photo POD is required above the value threshold that already makes insurance structural (13.07.04 D-01), so it costs no new decision; a bare scan suffices only below it. And a non-receipt report **stops** the clock rather than pausing it pending investigation — the burden belongs to the carrier's POD, not to a buyer disproving a scan. 13.02.04 D-14 stays correct for the pack's evidentiary purposes; what changes is that a scan cannot, by itself, start a countdown on someone's money. |
| DT-07 | Order state should be **derived** from escrow state ⊕ fulfilment state — the order needs no state machine of its own | Follows directly from D-01 (compose, never reimplement); a derived state cannot drift from its sources, which is architecturally attractive | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection is what vindicates DT-01 concretely rather than rhetorically. Two independent proofs. **(a) States exist in neither source**: `awaiting-quote` has no payment and no shipment; `disclosure-amended` is paid, unshipped, and the *description* changed — CX-M01 and CX-M14 both see a perfectly ordinary paid-awaiting-dispatch order; `misdelivered`, `seized`, `flagged-post-settlement` and `layaway-active` are all invisible to both. **(b) The sources actively disagree, and the disagreement is the feature**: at `inspection-window`, fulfilment says complete and escrow says open. **The inspection window *is* the gap between them.** A derived model would have to resolve that disagreement into one value and would thereby delete the domain's single most important state — the one DT-02 identifies as the structural counterweight to non-fungibility. Composition is the right relationship; derivation is not composition. |
| DT-08 | The buyer on an order is the human who clicked buy | Every checkout has an authenticated user attached, so the buyer is obvious | ❌ REJECTED: bands, studios and venues are first-class entities that buy independently of their members (`personas.md`, domain 01), and gear is the category they buy most. If buyer rights attach to the individual, then **the band loses its remedy when its bassist quits mid-inspection-window** — the person holding the right to dispute a £4k amp the band paid for is no longer in the band, and may be actively hostile to it. Rights sit with the **entity**, exercisable by any member with commerce authority (CX-M09) — the same rule 13.02.04 already reached for pack visibility, arrived at independently here, which is corroboration rather than duplication. It also means "who can settle, dispute, or accept a delivery appointment" is an authority question, not an identity one, on every entity order. |
| DT-09 | An active order-scoped conversation (CX-M06) should pause the settlement clock — a buyer mid-conversation with a seller shouldn't be timed out | Reads as basic fairness, and it is what a support team would do by hand | ❌ REJECTED: it hands either party a free, unaccountable lever over the other's money. A buyer who sends one message every few days freezes a seller's funds indefinitely with no dispute, no evidence and no consequence — and the seller's cash flow is the Musician persona's stated pain (DT-02), so the lever aims at exactly the thing the platform promised to fix. The dispute mechanism pauses the clock (D-15) because a dispute is accountable: it is recorded, it is adjudicated, and a pattern of them is visible (CX-M08). Chat is not. Keeping "message the seller" and "dispute this order" visibly different actions with visibly different effects is the honest design (D-19). |
