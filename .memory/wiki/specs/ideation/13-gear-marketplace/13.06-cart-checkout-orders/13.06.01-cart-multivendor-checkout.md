# Feature: Cart & Multi-Vendor Checkout

> **Number**: 13.06.01
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Buying from several independent sellers in one transaction. The gear-specific difficulty is that a cart containing a pedal and a Hammond organ has two fulfilment paths with nothing in common — one goes in a padded envelope, the other needs a freight quote, a lift gate and possibly a refusal — and one of them may be legally unshippable to the buyer's country. This is not standard multi-vendor checkout with different sellers; it is checkout where line items have **irreconcilable physics**.

The feature's job is therefore not to split a payment (that is CX-M01's, per sub-domain D-01). It is to **answer N independent feasibility questions before a single pound moves**, and to make the answers legible to a Fan who will abandon at the first thing they don't understand.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as buyer) | Buys a pedal and a set of strings from two sellers; expects one payment and gets it. Hits the **freight cliff** — adds a 2×12 combo to a £180 cart and the shipping is £140 — which is why freight must be signalled at add-to-cart, not at the quote step (DT-11). As the domain's multi-hyphenate, the persona most likely to trip the self-dealing block by carting gear their own band entity listed (edge case: Self-Dealing). |
| Producer | Full (as buyer) | The heaviest international buyer — rare outboard is a global market. Their cart routinely holds two overseas vendors, which means **two customs entries, two brokerage fees and two independent de minimis assessments** (DT-09) that no other marketplace explains. Landed cost (13.07.02) can be 30% on top and must be a commitment before authorisation, not an estimate after it. |
| Operator | Full (as buyer) | Buys at volume for a venue/studio; the persona most likely to hit oversize freight and the 72h quote-pending path, and the one checking out from a phone at a loading dock. Wants the pedal delivered to the office and the PA to the venue — **rejected** (DT-08, D-09): one destination per checkout, because eligibility is a function of destination and two destinations means two sets of answers. |
| Fan | Full (as buyer) | **The one place in this domain where Fan has full primary access.** Buys strings or a first instrument. Zero tolerance for professional complexity — a freight quote that arrives after they've entered card details is exactly the thing they abandon on. Never sees the word CITES (13.07.03 D-04); sees "can't ship to your country". Bears the full cost of D-08's account requirement, which is this feature's sharpest conversion tension (Q-05). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The cart holds intent, **not claims** (13.03.03 D-02) — a unit in a cart is still buyable by anyone. Checkout resolves **per shipment**, not per vendor (D-06): a vendor selling a pedal and a Leslie cabinet produces two shipments with two quotes, two transit times and two sets of delivery requirements. The cart's real structure is `buyer → vendor → shipment → line`, three levels, not two.

**Checkout is two-phase** (D-05). Eligibility and quoting resolve *first*; the claim phase runs *second*; authorisation runs *last*, against a set the buyer has re-confirmed. This ordering is what dissolves the multi-vendor partial-failure problem that DT-02 identified and left unresolved: because claims precede authorisation (D-03), the fulfilable set is **known before any money moves**, so there is no such thing as "paid for a line we couldn't claim". The buyer is never charged for something they cannot get, and no unwind is required.

**Checkout may still legitimately partially fail** — a line can be ineligible, lost to another buyer, held under screening, or unquotable — and that is a designed outcome, not an error. What it may never be is silent, generic, or discovered after payment.

**Cart lines carry heterogeneous claim states** (D-11). A line added by Buy Now is unclaimed; a line arriving from an accepted offer (13.05.01) is *already* claimed with a 24h hold; an auction win (13.05.02) carries 48h. The claim phase therefore claims only the unclaimed lines and re-verifies the rest. The session deadline is the **minimum of the live holds** — in practice 15 minutes, the Buy Now hold (13.03.03 D-07).

**A cart is not a lifecycle entity** (DT-06). Checkout fans out into N orders (13.06.02) that settle independently on different clocks; the "one order" the buyer thinks they placed does not exist five seconds after authorisation. The buyer's artifact is a **checkout receipt**: a view over N orders, never a state machine.

### Product requirements (numbers, not adjectives)

| Requirement | Value | Source / rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Claim outcome per line, authoritative | **≤ 1 s**, or explicit per-line progress | Inherited from 13.03.03's claim requirement |
| Checkout session deadline | **min(live holds)** — 15 min for Buy Now lines | 13.03.03 D-07 |
| Quote validity | Honoured for the **15 min** session; re-quoted on expiry | Matches the Buy Now hold — a quote outliving its claim is a quote for nothing |
| Parcel quote resolution | **≤ 5 s** inline | Carrier API |
| Freight quote resolution | **≤ 30 s**, then explicit elapsed indicator; at **90 s** the line is offered as quote-pending and splits out (D-07) | 13.07.01: "freight quoting is slow — set the expectation, don't spin silently" |
| Human freight quote hold | **72 h** | 13.03.03 D-07, set for this feature |
| Vendors per checkout | **20** (working default) | The claim phase must resolve N units at ≤1 s each; unbounded N makes that undeliverable, and one click claiming unbounded units is the squat lever of 13.03.03 DT-04. Exact tuning → `/create-prd-compile` |
| Counted-stock quantity per line | Capped at the seller's available count; never silently reduced | 13.03.03 regime 2 |

### Happy Path

1. Buyer opens the cart. Every line **re-prices on view** (D-13); any line whose price moved since it was added shows the delta explicitly — "£2,400 → £2,600 (changed 3 days ago)". Freight-class lines already carry a coarse cost signal from add-time (D-14).
2. Buyer enters one destination (D-09). The system groups lines into shipments (D-06), quotes each shipment, and resolves **per-item eligibility** — CITES (13.07.03), landed cost quotability (13.07.02 D-02), voltage (13.01.05), and a stolen-serial re-screen (13.03.07 D-02).
3. Cart shows a **per-shipment** breakdown: item, freight, duty, import VAT, brokerage, tax — itemised, in the buyer's currency, as a commitment for the session (D-10).
4. Any ineligible line is itemised with a plain-language reason and an explicit choice: remove it and continue, or don't (D-04). Nothing is ever silently dropped.
5. Buyer commits. **Claim phase**: every unclaimed line is claimed through 13.03.03's single arbitration point; already-claimed lines (accepted offers, auction wins) are re-verified as still held by this buyer.
6. If every line claimed, the buyer is shown the confirmed set and the session countdown (15 min).
7. If any line was lost, the system **re-confirms the actual set** rather than proceeding (D-05) — "Someone bought the Jazzmaster 4 seconds before you. Continue with the other 3 items — £412?"
8. Buyer authorises **once**, for the confirmed set only.
9. System creates **one order per vendor** (13.06.02); one checkout notifies N sellers.
10. Bought lines leave the cart; ineligible and unclaimed lines **survive in it** (D-12). Checkout empties nothing it did not buy.
11. Settlement — later, per vendor, on independent clocks — writes the ownership chain (13.06.05). Not here. Checkout is payment, not settlement (CX-R03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **One item can't ship to the destination (CITES — 13.07.03)** | Cannot fail the whole cart; cannot silently drop it. Resolved at step 2, before any claim | "The rosewood-fingerboard Martin can't ship to your country — remove it and continue with 2 items (£312)?" Never the word CITES (13.07.03 D-04) |
| **Freight line needs a human quote (7ft grand, unusual route)** | **Splits out of the cart** (D-07). The unit is claimed with a 72h hold (13.03.03 D-07); the parcel lines check out now. A 72h hold cannot coexist with a 15-min session — freezing a pedal for 3 days while a freight company phones back is indefensible | "The Steinway needs a freight quote — we'll have it within 72 hours and it's held for you. Your other 2 items can check out now." |
| **Claim phase: 3 of 4 lines claim, 1 lost** | **The resolved case** (D-05, resolving DT-02). No money has moved. The set is re-confirmed, never auto-proceeded | "Someone bought the Jazzmaster 4 seconds before you. Continue with the other 3 — £412?" + the standard loss surface (13.03.03 D-01): similar units, relist alert, ISO route |
| **All lines lost in the claim phase** | Not an error. Nothing was charged | Full loss surface, cart preserved, routes onward |
| **Buyer's own cart contains a bundle and a unit inside it (13.03.05)** | Double-listing is allowed (13.03.03 D-06), so the buyer can cart both — and would then **race themselves**: the bundle claim reserves all constituents, and their own individual line fails on the same unit. Detected at **add-to-cart**, not at claim | "The Klon is already in this cart, inside the Pedalboard bundle. Add it separately anyway?" — blocked at commit if both remain |
| A unit is claimed by someone else while it sits in the cart | Expected — a cart is not a claim (13.03.03 D-02) | "Someone bought the Jazzmaster while it was in your cart" + routes onward. Never "3 people are looking at this" — that is pressure UX and it isn't true |
| **Two vendors both shipping internationally** | **Two customs entries, two brokerage fees, two independent de minimis assessments** (DT-09). The buyer cannot predict this and reads one cart as one import | Per-shipment duty breakdown with brokerage shown per shipment, not amortised into one number that hides why £22 appeared twice |
| **One vendor's lines split parcel + freight** | Two shipments from one seller (D-06) — two quotes, two transit times, two sets of delivery requirements | Grouped by shipment under the vendor: "Ships in 2 packages — pedal (3 days, £8) · combo (10–14 days, freight, £140, someone must be present" |
| **Landed cost unquotable for a route** | Per 13.07.02 D-02, the honest answer is no sale, not a hedged one. Line is ineligible | "We can't quote import charges to your country for this seller — we won't sell you a number that might be wrong." Itemised, before payment |
| **Stolen-registry hit lands between cart and capture** | Re-screened at step 2 (13.03.07 D-02). A hit **holds that line only**; a screening hold outranks a reservation (13.03.03 D-13) so it cannot be released to another buyer. Other lines proceed | "This listing is under review; your payment hasn't been taken." **Never** an accusation — the modal seller bought it innocently (13.03.07 D-01) |
| **Stolen registry unavailable at checkout** | Fail-open vs fail-closed is **13.03.07 Q-01**, unresolved and not this file's to decide. Either way the cart must be able to represent the line as held-pending-screening without failing the other lines | Under the fail-closed branch: "This listing is under review" (as above). Under fail-open: the line proceeds and the risk is 13.03.07 Q-02's |
| Buyer is in a 230V country buying a 100V amp | Warning, never a block (13.01.05 D-01). **Second and final warning point.** The acknowledgment is recorded at checkout (D-14) — this is the only moment with a timestamp, an identified buyer and a stated destination | "This amp is 100V. Your country is 230V. You'll need a transformer." Acknowledge to continue |
| **Accepted-offer line + Buy Now line in one cart** | Heterogeneous claim states (D-11). The offer line is already claimed (24h); the Buy Now line is not. Claim phase claims only the latter | One countdown, showing the binding one: "Complete within 15:00" |
| **Session expires with a pre-claimed offer line in it** | Newly-claimed lines release to `active`. The offer line **reverts to its own 24h clock** — it was the buyer's claim before checkout and checkout failing does not forfeit it | "Your checkout expired. Your accepted offer on the Klon is still yours for 19 more hours." |
| **Cart sat for a week; 2 lines repriced** | Re-price on view (D-13); the claim carries the version the buyer saw (13.03.03 D-10). A stale page is rejected, never silently repriced | Per-line delta at step 1. If a price moves between view and claim: "The price changed to £2,600 — buy at the new price?" |
| **Client submits a price or a tampered version token** | Server resolves price from the version; a version that doesn't match a price the seller actually published is rejected outright | Generic re-price prompt; no oracle for probing |
| **Same buyer, two tabs / phone and laptop** | One active checkout session per cart. The second session sees the first's claims and countdown, not a second claim set. Claiming is idempotent — **double-submitting never creates two claims or two charges** (13.03.03) | "This cart is already checking out — 12:04 left" |
| **Network drops between claim and authorise** | On reconnect the authoritative state is shown, holds intact and clocks visible. Never ambiguity about whether money moved | "Your 3 items are held — 9:12 left. Complete payment?" |
| **Authorisation fails (declined card) after claims are held** | Claims survive to hold expiry; retry needs no re-arbitration. The cart is preserved intact | "Payment declined — your items are still held for 11:40. Try another card?" |
| **Payment-provider outage mid-checkout** | Reservation clocks **pause** (13.03.03 D-15). Expiring a buyer who physically could not pay produces a cancellation nobody caused | "Checkout is paused — your hold is safe, we've stopped the clock" |
| **Buyer is capped at 1 concurrent reservation (13.03.03 D-12)** | A multi-line checkout claims N units in one action, so the abandon cap must be counted **in units**. A capped buyer cannot check out a multi-line cart at all — see Q-06, this is a real consequence of D-12 that 13.03.03 did not contemplate | "You can hold one item at a time right now. Check out the Klon, or remove the others." One-at-a-time route, not a dead end |
| **Self-dealing: a line's seller is the buyer's own account or a band entity they control** | Blocked per 13.03.03 D-14 — it manufactures a fake comp. Detected at add-to-cart. "Member of" ≠ "controls": a session player in a 6-piece is not the seller | "You can't buy your own band's listing" — and the line never enters the cart |
| **Vendor suspended (domain 24) mid-checkout** | That vendor's lines drop out of the checkout with an explanation; no capture on them; the rest proceeds | "This seller's account is under review — your payment hasn't been taken for their 2 items." Not "your order failed" |
| **Vendor has no shipping profile configured (13.12)** | Line is unquotable → ineligible, itemised like any other ineligible line. Never a parcel-rate fallback (13.07.01 D-03) | "This seller hasn't set up shipping to your country yet" |
| **Model has no dimensions → freight class underivable (13.07.01)** | Never falls back to a parcel rate — an order-of-magnitude underquote someone must eat (13.07.01 D-03). Line is quote-pending or ineligible | "We need a freight quote for this one" — the 72h path |
| **Buyer's address can't receive 200kg (3rd-floor flat, no lift gate)** | Delivery requirements stated **at checkout, not at the kerb** (13.07.01 D-04) | "Kerbside delivery — someone must be present, and it weighs 190kg. Can you receive it?" Acknowledge to continue |
| **Counted-stock line: buyer wants 4, only 3 remain** | Never silently reduce the quantity (13.03.03) | "3 available — buy 3?" Requires re-confirmation |
| **Malicious: 10,000 units of a stocked item added** | Capped at the seller's available count per line; cart capped at 20 vendors per checkout | Quantity clamps with an explicit message |
| **Buyer changes destination after quoting** | Full re-quote and full re-eligibility. Prior quotes are void — a destination change can turn a shippable line into a CITES prohibition | "Re-checking 4 items for Germany" — and any newly-ineligible line is itemised as at step 4 |
| **Buyer changes destination *after* the claim phase** | Claims survive; quotes and eligibility do not. If the new destination makes a claimed line ineligible, the claim is released rather than sold into a prohibited movement | "The Martin can't ship to Germany — released. Continue with 2 items?" |
| **Checkout succeeds; ineligible lines remain** | Checkout removes only what it bought (D-12) | Cart still holds the Martin with its reason intact — not silently emptied, not silently kept without explanation |
| **Layaway item in the cart** | Prohibited (CX-05). Layaway is a per-unit, per-seller agreement with a months-long hold and a forfeit schedule; it is initiated **from a listing**, never composed into a 15-min cart | "Layaway is arranged with the seller on the listing — it can't go through the cart" |
| **Service add-on (13.06.04) on a gear line** | Travels with its parent line: same vendor, same shipment, dispatched after the pre-dispatch service state. Cannot outlive its parent — if the gear line is lost in the claim phase, the add-on goes with it | Add-on nested under its item, never a free-floating line |
| **Local pickup line mixed with a shipped line (13.11)** | Open — but no longer on the settlement question: 13.11 D-04 (P-09) makes pickup settlement a per-listing seller choice, so the line may carry money (platform-settled) or none (off-platform), and under D-06 neither belongs to a shipment. What is unresolved is cart composition itself. See Q-04 | — |
| **Mixed cart with digital goods (domain 14)** | Undefined at domain level — D-14 separated the domains at schema level and said nothing about the cart | Q-02 |
| **Two vendors with different tax nexus** | Marketplace facilitator tax is per vendor, per destination (CX-M02). One payment, two tax treatments | Tax shown per vendor, not merged into one line that can't be reconciled |
| **FX moves between quote and capture** | The quoted total in the buyer's currency is a **commitment for the session** (D-10), consistent with 13.07.02 D-01. Drift inside 15 minutes is the platform's, not the buyer's | The number quoted is the number charged |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Quote + eligibility calculation after destination entry | Per-shipment progress, resolved independently — parcel lines settle in ≤5 s and display immediately while a freight line is still quoting. Freight shows an elapsed indicator past 30 s, never a silent spinner (13.07.01). The slowest line must not hold the fastest line's answer hostage |
| **Empty** | Nothing in cart | Empty cart with routes back to search and saved searches (CX-M28) |
| **Populated** | Lines + destination known, all eligible | Per-vendor, per-shipment breakdown: item, freight, duty, import VAT, brokerage, tax — itemised and committed for the session |
| **Error** | Authorisation fails | Cart preserved intact; **claims survive to hold expiry** with the countdown visible; retry offered. Nothing released on a declined card |
| **Partial** | Some lines ineligible, held under screening, or lost in the claim phase | Itemised per-line report with a plain reason and an explicit continue-without choice — **never** a generic "checkout unavailable". This is the state the Fan persona abandons on, and it is the state this feature exists to make survivable |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Multi-vendor checkout is a solved pattern — split the payment, create N orders | Every marketplace does this; CX-M01 already owns the money split | ❌ REJECTED: the *payment* split is solved and is CX-M01's (sub-domain D-01). The **fulfilability** split is not, and it is unique to this domain. In digital goods (14) every line delivers instantly and identically; in services (05) there is no shipping at all. Here, each line has its own freight class, its own export legality, its own voltage question, and its own possibility of being **undeliverable at any price**. A cart is therefore not "N orders with one payment" — it is N *independent feasibility questions* that must all be answered before a single payment is authorised. This is the clearest vindication of D-14 in the whole domain. |
| DT-02 | Payment should be authorised first, then claims taken | Standard checkout ordering — money first, fulfilment after | ❌ REJECTED: it inverts the risk. Authorising payment before claiming a qty=1 unit means a buyer can be charged for an amp that was sold 200ms earlier — and refunding is not a fix, because the buyer wanted *that* amp (13.03.03 D-01). Claims must be taken **before or atomically with** authorisation (D-03). This originally left the hard case open — N claims, one payment, and a claim that can fail after another succeeded. **DT-04 resolves it.** |
| DT-03 | The vendor is the unit of quoting and fulfilment — split the cart by seller | It is what "multi-vendor cart" names, and how every marketplace models it | ❌ REJECTED: the **shipment** is the unit, and a vendor can have several. A seller with a Klon and a Leslie 122 ships two things — one parcel, one palletised freight consignment with a delivery appointment and a lift gate. They have different quotes, different transit times, different delivery requirements, and different insurance. Conversely three pedals from one seller are **one box** — quoting them per line overcharges freight by 3× and is the most common way a multi-vendor cart lies to a buyer. The cart's structure is therefore `buyer → vendor → shipment → line`, three levels. Only one of those levels is what CX-M01 splits money by, and it is not the one that decides physics. |
| DT-04 | Claims-before-authorisation still leaves a post-payment partial failure to unwind — so the design must choose between per-vendor authorisation and an all-or-nothing cart | The framing DT-02 left behind, carried into Q-01 as the sub-domain's hardest question | ❌ REJECTED — **and the dilemma dissolves.** Both horns assume claims and authorisation are concurrent. They need not be. If the claim phase runs to *completion* first, the fulfilable set is fully known **while the buyer's money is still untouched**. There is no partial capture to reverse, because there was no capture. The buyer re-confirms an actual set and authorises once, for lines that are already theirs. What looked like an atomicity problem across N payment legs is a **sequencing problem inside one session**, and the sequence D-03 already mandates is the one that solves it. The genuinely irreducible cost is elsewhere: N units sit reserved for up to 15 minutes while the buyer decides, which is 13.03.03 DT-04's squat surface multiplied by cart size — a real price, paid knowingly (Q-06). Q-01 narrows from a product dilemma to a processor-mechanics question for CX-M01. |
| DT-05 | Every cart line starts unclaimed, so checkout claims all of them uniformly | The cart is where claiming happens, so claiming is the cart's job | ❌ REJECTED: gear is a **negotiated category** — offers and auctions (13.05) are the norm, not the exception, and both already produce claims. An accepted offer carries a 24h hold; an auction win carries 48h (13.03.03 D-07). Those units are *already the buyer's* when they reach the cart. So cart lines carry heterogeneous claim states with **independent expiry clocks**, and the claim phase claims some lines while merely re-verifying others. Two consequences nobody had drawn: the session deadline is the **minimum** of the live holds (15 min dominates), and a failed checkout must **not** forfeit a pre-existing 24h offer claim — that claim predates the cart and surviving checkout failure is the whole point of having won it. |
| DT-06 | One payment produces one order the buyer can track | It is the buyer's mental model, and every confirmation email in commerce reinforces it | ❌ REJECTED: there is no order for the cart, and pretending otherwise creates an entity with no lifecycle. Checkout fans out into N orders (13.06.02) that settle on **independent clocks** — vendor A's inspection window closes and pays out while vendor B's item is still in customs for 11 days. The buyer's "order" exists for about five seconds. The honest artifact is a **checkout receipt**: a view over N orders, a payment record, never a state machine. This matters because the failure it prevents is real — a cross-vendor "cancel my order" control that cannot exist, or a single status that must lie about two shipments. The Fan persona is the one who will look for that control, and the answer must be a clear list of N things, not a fake unity. |
| DT-07 | Guest checkout is right for the Fan persona — an account is friction on someone buying £12 of strings | Standard conversion wisdom, and the Fan persona's stated intolerance of complexity | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the thesis arriving at the till.** Settlement writes an ownership event to the registry (13.06.05 D-01, domain D-05). A guest has no identity to hold that record, so a guest sale produces an ownership event with **no owner** — a hole in the chain at the category's highest-volume transaction shape, which is exactly the reconstruction failure D-18 exists to end, reproduced inside the platform. The account requirement is therefore not bureaucracy; it is the precondition of the product. **But the cost is real and lands entirely on Fan**, the persona least willing to pay it, so it cannot be waved through — see Q-05. Note the platform already distinguishes anonymous read from identified transaction: 13.03.07 D-04 puts public serial lookup behind no account at all. Looking is open; owning is not. |
| DT-08 | The Operator should be able to ship different lines to different addresses — pedal to the office, PA to the venue | A real and frequent Operator need; every B2B commerce tool supports it | ⚠️ CONFIRMED as a need, ❌ REJECTED for the cart. Destination is not a delivery preference here — it is an **input to legality**. CITES determination is materials × origin × **destination** (13.07.03); landed cost is per destination (13.07.02); voltage is per destination (13.01.05). Two destinations means running the entire feasibility question twice over overlapping line sets, and presenting a buyer with a cart where the same Martin is eligible for one address and prohibited for another. That is not a harder UI; it is a different product. **One destination per checkout** (D-09); the Operator does two checkouts, which is honest and costs them one extra pass. Revisit only if Operator volume makes it a top complaint — and if so, model it as N carts, never as one cart with N addresses. |
| DT-09 | Duty and import charges are per order, so a cart quotes them once | It is how the buyer perceives it — one purchase, one import | ❌ REJECTED: customs operates on **consignments, not carts**. Two vendors shipping internationally to one buyer generate two customs entries, two brokerage fees, and two **independent de minimis assessments**. The consequences are counter-intuitive and invisible: splitting a purchase across two sellers can keep both consignments under a de minimis threshold and incur **no duty at all**, while consolidating the identical goods with one seller crosses it and incurs full duty plus brokerage. The buyer cannot predict this, and a single merged "import charges" number actively conceals the mechanism — a Producer seeing £22 brokerage twice will assume a bug. So: **duty, VAT and brokerage are quoted and displayed per shipment**, never amortised into one cart-level number. Nobody framed multi-vendor as a customs-arithmetic problem; for this domain it is one. |
| DT-10 | The quoted total is indicative — FX and duty are inherently approximate, so a disclaimer covers it | It is what most cross-border marketplaces do, and precision is genuinely hard | ❌ REJECTED, on precedent already set: 13.07.02 D-01 rules that a quoted landed cost is a **commitment, not an estimate**, because an estimate transfers risk to the buyer at the exact moment they cannot assess it, and the Fan persona reads an estimate as a price. FX is the same failure with a different mechanism — a rate moving between quote and capture is not the buyer's error to absorb, and the exposure is bounded by a **15-minute session**, which is a trivial risk window to hold. Applying a different standard to FX than to duty inside the same total would be incoherent: the buyer sees one number and cannot tell which half is a promise. The quoted total is a commitment for the session (D-10). If a border or a currency cannot be quoted to that standard, 13.07.02 D-02 already gives the answer — don't sell across it. |
| DT-11 | The freight cliff is a checkout problem — show the real cost once a destination is known | Freight needs a destination, so it looks like it can only be a checkout concern | ❌ REJECTED: by checkout the cart is already built and the buyer is already committed emotionally, so a £140 freight charge on a £180 combo reads as a bait-and-switch rather than physics. 13.07.01's own Role Lens says the Fan "needs to understand *before* the cart that big things cost real money to move." The system knows the freight class from catalog dimensions at **listing view** (13.07.01 D-01) and knows a coarse destination from the buyer's last-used or profile country. That is enough for a signal — "Freight item — typically £110–£160 to UK, quoted at checkout" — which is honest, non-blocking, and arrives while the decision is still open. Precision is a checkout concern; **the cliff is not.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A cart holds intent, never a claim | Per 13.03.03 D-02 — cart-holds on qty=1 gear let one user freeze what others want. |
| D-02 | Eligibility is resolved per item, per destination, **before** payment | Per DT-01 — a cart is N feasibility questions, not one. |
| D-03 | Claims are taken before or atomically with authorisation, never after | Per DT-02. |
| D-04 | Ineligible lines are itemised and explained, never silently dropped or generically failed | The Fan persona abandons at the first unexplained thing. |
| D-05 | **Checkout is two-phase: claim to completion, re-confirm the actual set, then authorise once.** The buyer is never charged for a line they cannot get | Per DT-04 — resolves Q-01 as posed. Sequencing, not payment atomicity, is the answer; the fulfilable set is known while the money is still untouched. |
| D-06 | **The shipment, not the vendor, is the unit of quoting, eligibility and delivery requirements** | Per DT-03 — one vendor can ship a parcel and a freight consignment; three pedals from one seller are one box. Quoting per vendor or per line both lie. |
| D-07 | **A freight line needing a human quote leaves the cart** — claimed with a 72h hold, quoted out of session, checked out separately | Per 13.03.03 D-07 and 13.07.01. Resolves Q-03. A 72h wait cannot share a session with a 15-min hold without freezing unrelated inventory for three days. |
| D-08 | **An account is required to check out** — no guest checkout | Per DT-07 — settlement writes ownership (13.06.05 D-01); a guest sale writes an ownership event with no owner. The conversion cost lands on Fan and is acknowledged, not hidden (Q-05). |
| D-09 | **One destination per checkout** | Per DT-08 — destination is an input to legality (CITES, landed cost, voltage), not a delivery preference. Multi-destination is N checkouts. |
| D-10 | **The quoted total is a commitment for the 15-minute session** — FX and duty variance inside it are not the buyer's | Per DT-10, consistent with 13.07.02 D-01. The buyer sees one number and cannot tell which half is a promise; both must be. |
| D-11 | **Cart lines carry independent claim states and expiry clocks**; the session deadline is the minimum live hold; a failed checkout never forfeits a pre-existing claim | Per DT-05 — offers and auctions deliver pre-claimed lines into a negotiated category. |
| D-12 | **Checkout removes only what it bought.** Ineligible, held and lost lines survive in the cart with their reasons intact | A cart emptied of things the buyer never bought is a second, silent failure on top of the first. |
| D-13 | **The cart re-prices on view and shows the delta**; the claim carries the version the buyer saw | Per 13.03.03 D-10 — a week-old cart is entirely stale, and rejecting six lines at claim time is worse than repricing one at view time. |
| D-14 | **The voltage acknowledgment is recorded at checkout as dispute evidence** | Answers 13.01.05 Q-03 from this side. Checkout is the only moment with a timestamp, an identified buyer and a declared destination together. Warning still informs, never blocks (13.01.05 D-01). |
