# Feature: Layaway & Staged Payment on Held Inventory

> **Number**: 13.06.03
> **Parent**: [Cart, Checkout & Orders](./13.06-cart-checkout-orders-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A buyer pays for a unit in instalments while the seller holds it off the market; the unit ships and ownership transfers on the final payment. It is the old music-shop arrangement, and it survives because the goods are non-fungible: you cannot "wait until payday" for a specific 1962 Jazzmaster, because it will be gone. Distinct from BNPL, where a lender pays the seller in full immediately and the buyer owes the lender.

**Split from the sweep's candidate #27** ("Financing, BNPL & Layaway"): BNPL and financing are payment methods serving every commerce domain and belong to CX-M01 (sub-domain D-02). Layaway is an inventory hold plus a payment schedule on a qty=1 unit — it has no analogue in digital goods or services, so it stays here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | As buyer: the persona this exists for — a portfolio of small income streams (per the persona doc) means a £3k instrument is a months-long proposition, and the unit won't wait. As seller: may offer it to close a sale they'd otherwise lose. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Buys expensive outboard on schedule; sells the same way. Less time-pressured than the Musician but higher ticket values. |
| Operator | Config | Offers layaway as a shop policy across their stock — the traditional dealer arrangement. Configures terms rather than negotiating per unit. |
| Fan | Read-only (may use as buyer) | Could buy a first instrument on layaway. The persona least able to assess whether a forfeit clause is fair — which is why forfeit terms must be stated in money, not percentages. |

## Behavior

The buyer commits; the unit leaves the market (a claim under 13.03.03, held for the schedule's duration); payments run to a schedule; on final payment the unit ships and ownership transfers (13.06.05). **The hold is the product** — the payment schedule is the easy half; taking a non-fungible unit off the market for three months is the hard, risky half.

### Happy Path

1. Seller offers layaway on a listing (or agrees it in negotiation)
2. Buyer commits with a deposit; unit → held, off market
3. Instalments run to schedule
4. Final payment lands
5. Unit ships; ownership transfers on settlement (13.06.05)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer defaults mid-schedule | The unit has been off-market for months; the seller has lost sales and market timing. What happens to money paid? | Forfeit terms stated up-front in **money, not percentages** — see DT-02 |
| Buyer cancels early and amicably | Refund minus a stated holding cost | Terms known before commitment, never discovered at cancellation |
| Seller sells the unit anyway (off-platform) | The layaway's entire premise is broken; the buyer has paid toward an object that's gone | Severe seller consequence — this is closer to fraud than to a cancellation |
| Unit damaged/destroyed while held by the seller | Who bears the risk on a partially-paid object nobody owns yet? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-01. Real and unresolved. |
| Market moves during the schedule | Price is locked at commitment; both sides carry that risk | Locked price stated |
| Schedule outlives the platform's escrow assumptions | Months of held funds is not a normal escrow shape (CX-M01) | Escrow model must support long holds — a real constraint on CX-M01 |
| Buyer wants to accelerate and pay off early | Should be trivially allowed | "Pay the balance now" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Layaway is a payment-schedule feature | It's how the sweep framed it (bundled with BNPL and financing under #27) | ❌ REJECTED: the payment schedule is the trivial half — CX-M01 can run instalments for any domain. What makes layaway *this* domain's problem is the **inventory hold on a non-fungible unit**, and it raises a question that has no answer anywhere else on the platform: for three months, a specific irreplaceable object sits with the seller, partially paid for by the buyer, owned by neither in any useful sense. **If it burns, whose loss is it?** Domain 15 tracks custody and ownership as distinct things, which is exactly the vocabulary this needs — and neither this file nor 15 currently answers it. This is what makes layaway a domain node rather than a CX-M01 configuration. |
| DT-02 | Forfeit terms are a standard clause — a percentage of the paid amount | Every layaway arrangement historically does this | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: some forfeit is *necessary* — the seller genuinely lost months of market exposure and cannot be made whole by returning the deposit. But a percentage is exactly the shape that lets a bad actor run layaway as a business: offer it widely, structure the terms so defaults are profitable, keep the unit and the money. The Fan and Musician personas are the marks. Forfeit must be stated as a **money amount at commitment**, restated at every instalment, and capped — a Fan cannot evaluate "30% of paid to date on a declining balance" but can evaluate "if you stop, you lose £240". This also puts layaway close to regulated consumer credit in several jurisdictions, which `meta/constraints.md` has not yet examined. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The inventory hold is the feature; the schedule is CX-M01's | Per DT-01. |
| D-02 | Forfeit is stated in money at commitment, restated at every instalment, and capped | Per DT-02 — a percentage on a declining balance is unevaluable by the personas most exposed. |
| D-03 | The price is locked at commitment | Both sides carry the market risk; a repricing mid-layaway would be indefensible. |
