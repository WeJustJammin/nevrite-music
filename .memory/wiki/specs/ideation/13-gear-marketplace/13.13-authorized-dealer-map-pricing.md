# Feature: Authorized Dealer Program & MAP Pricing

> **Number**: 13.13
> **Parent**: [Gear Marketplace (Physical Goods)](./gear-marketplace-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The rules of **new** gear retail: manufacturers authorise dealers, authorised dealers get warranty standing and brand support, and in exchange they agree not to *advertise* below a Minimum Advertised Price. MAP is why "add to cart to see price" exists across music retail — the dealer may sell below MAP but may not display it. It is a real, load-bearing constraint on any marketplace carrying new gear from brands.

> **⚠️ This feature has no persona.** None of the four — Musician, Producer, Operator, Fan — is an authorised dealer. A Musician selling a used pedal has no agreement with Fender; an Operator runs a venue, not a franchise. This is the sharpest evidence for `meta/personas.md` Q-01, which explicitly said to revisit the dealer persona "if the Gear Role Matrices come out thin during drilling." **It came out empty.**

## Behavior

Dealer authorisation is a **brand-scoped status**: this seller is authorised for this manufacturer, which grants warranty standing (13.08.03) and imposes MAP obligations. MAP restricts *display*, not sale — the price may be lower in the cart, in an offer (13.05.01), or in an email. Enforcement is contractual between manufacturer and dealer; the platform's role is to give the dealer the controls to comply.

### Happy Path

1. A dealer is verified as authorised for a manufacturer
2. They list new gear from that brand
3. MAP-restricted models display "add to cart to see price"
4. Actual price shows in the cart, below MAP
5. Warranty standing (13.08.03) attaches because the unit came through an authorised channel

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **MAP display rules vs. the price guide (13.04)** | The guide publishes what units actually sold for — including below MAP. Does MAP gag the guide? | See DT-02. A genuine, unresolved collision. |
| Unauthorised seller lists new gear below MAP | Not their problem — MAP binds dealers, not private sellers | No restriction; correct |
| Grey-market new gear | Genuine product, unauthorised channel, no warranty (13.08.03 Edge Cases) | Should be disclosed; buyers rarely know to ask |
| Dealer loses authorisation | Warranty standing and MAP obligations both lapse | Status change with downstream effects |
| Manufacturer demands the platform enforce MAP | The platform is not party to the agreement | A commercial pressure point, not a technical one |
| MAP applied to used gear | Doesn't apply — MAP governs new product only | Must not leak into the used listings that are 90% of this domain |
| Offer below MAP (13.05.01) | Permitted — an offer is not an advertisement | The distinction MAP actually turns on |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This domain needs an authorised dealer program because it sells new gear (D-05: "new + used music equipment") | The owner directive explicitly says new **and** used, and new gear from brands means MAP | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS A FACT, REJECTED AS A PRIORITY. MAP is real and unavoidable *if* the platform carries new gear from major brands. But **carrying new gear from major brands requires authorised dealers, and authorised dealers are not a persona this platform has** — they are Sweetwater and Thomann, businesses with wholesale accounts, credit lines and franchise agreements, who have no reason to list on a new marketplace with no traffic. The whole cluster presupposes a supply side that doesn't exist at launch and may never. Meanwhile the domain's actual thesis contribution (provenance — 13.03.06, 13.06.05) lives **entirely in used gear**, where MAP is irrelevant. D-05 says new + used; the honest reading is that **used is the wedge and new is an aspiration**, and this feature is downstream of a supply relationship nobody has. |
| DT-02 | MAP restricts listing display, so it's a rendering rule on listings | The narrow reading — hide a number on a page | ❌ REJECTED: MAP would collide with the **price guide** (13.04.01), and nobody has noticed. The guide's entire purpose is publishing what units actually sold for, drawn from immutable sold listings (13.03.02 D-01). Real transactions routinely close below MAP. So a MAP-compliant platform that also runs a transparent price guide is **publishing exactly the below-MAP prices MAP exists to suppress** — in aggregate, permanently, and indexed for search (13.04 Q-02). Either the guide is gagged for MAP-restricted models (destroying its value on new gear and creating a two-tier truth), or the platform accepts that its price guide is structurally in tension with dealer agreements. **The price guide and the dealer program may be mutually exclusive products**, and the guide is far more aligned with the platform's thesis. This is a strategic collision, not a rendering rule. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | MAP restricts display, never sale price | The distinction the whole mechanism turns on; getting it wrong would block legitimate discounting. |
| D-02 | MAP never applies to used gear | Used is ~90% of this domain; a MAP leak into used listings would be a serious defect. |
| D-03 | The platform provides compliance controls; it does not enforce MAP | The platform is not party to the dealer's agreement, and taking on enforcement takes on the liability. |
