# Feature: Stolen-Serial Screening at Listing & Checkout

> **Number**: 13.03.07
> **Parent**: [Listings & Inventory](./13.03-listings-inventory-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list); Should-have taken to Level-1 depth
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Checking a serial against the stolen-gear registry at the moment a listing is published, and again before money moves. Domain 15 owns the registry and the reporting; this owns the **enforcement point** — the gate in the listing flow. Domain 15's own rationale states that only a platform that is also the marketplace can check a serial at listing time, and it names this as "the highest-goodwill unserved need in the corpus." That check has to live where the listing is, which is here.

**Why this exists as a node**: domain 15 can hold the registry and the report; it has no listing flow to gate. The sweep's 28 candidates contain no screening step at all — the marketplace side of the platform's most goodwill-rich feature was simply missing.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | As seller: is screened, usually invisibly. As buyer: sees a "serial checked against the stolen registry" badge — and, being the persona whose gear gets stolen from vans, is the one who reports and the one this returns gear to. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, weighted to studio break-ins. Screened at listing; sees the badge when buying. |
| Operator | Read-only | Screened at fleet intake and bulk listing (13.03.04) — a rental house buying a used unit is a laundering vector, and screening at intake protects them from becoming a receiver. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the badge. The persona with no way to assess whether used gear is stolen and no idea it's a risk — the badge is doing all the work for them. |

## Behavior

Screening runs on any listing carrying a serial, at publish and again at checkout (state can change in between — a theft is often reported *after* the listing goes up). A hit does **not** silently kill the listing: theft reports are unverified third-party claims, and false positives destroy an innocent seller. The design has to protect a real victim's recovery chance and an innocent seller's sale simultaneously.

### Happy Path

1. Seller publishes a listing with a serial
2. System screens against the domain 15 registry
3. No hit: listing publishes with a "serial checked" badge
4. Re-screened at checkout before payment captures
5. Buyer completes purchase knowing the check ran

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Serial hits an active theft report | Listing held, not deleted; seller told; reporter notified; routed to domain 24 | Seller: "This serial matches a theft report — here's how to resolve it." **Never an accusation** — the seller may have bought it innocently, which is the common case |
| False positive (mistyped serial in the original report) | **DQ-MG-02:** the listing stays held, never deleted, and neither party is accused. Severity, SLA and escalation are **consumed from domain 24** (`24.01.03` — routing requires skill, severity and clock; severity sets the SLA); this feature authors no number of its own. The seller's substantive remedy is the already-locked `reported → contested` path in `15.02.04`, which is a dispute lane, not a support queue. | "Listing on hold pending review" with the contested-path affordance; both parties informed, neither accused |
| Theft reported *after* listing published | Re-screen at checkout catches it | Buyer sees the sale held before money moves |
| Theft reported after purchase completes | The buyer has bought stolen goods and may have to surrender them — the worst outcome available | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — post-sale hit handling is unresolved and legally loaded. See Q-02. |
| Malicious false theft report to sabotage a competitor's listing | The registry is open to reports; this is a live abuse vector | Reporting must carry identity and consequence — a domain 15 design constraint this feature depends on |
| No serial on the listing | No screening possible. Most listings at launch | No badge — absence of a badge is not an accusation either |
| Serial present but re-stamped/faked | Screening cannot detect this (13.01.03 Edge Cases) | Badge means "checked", never "authentic" |
| Buyer wants to check before buying | The buyer's need is identical to the platform's | Public serial lookup — arguably the single best acquisition surface this domain has. See DT-02. |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Stolen-gear screening belongs entirely to domain 15 — it owns the registry | 15's Overview claims the stolen registry outright, and duplicating it here would be a boundary violation | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: 15 owns the **registry, the report and the recovery**. But 15's own rationale says the value exists only because a serial can be checked *at the point of sale* — and 15 has no point of sale. The **gate** is a listing-flow concern and cannot live anywhere but here. This is a clean split: 15 answers "is this serial stolen?"; 13 decides what happens to the listing when the answer is yes. Neither domain can deliver the feature alone, which is the consolidation thesis (D-18) in its purest form in this domain. |
| DT-02 | Screening is a compliance/hygiene feature — a checkbox to avoid trafficking stolen goods | It reads as a defensive obligation, so it gets built as one | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **demand-side engine for the entire registry**. Nobody registers gear for fun; they register it because registration is what makes a check meaningful, and the check is what makes a theft recoverable. Screening creates the reason to register; registration creates the ownership chain; the chain is the provenance moat (D-18). Framed as compliance it gets a minimal implementation and stays invisible. Framed correctly, the **public serial lookup** — free, no account, "is this guitar stolen?" — is the highest-intent acquisition surface in the domain and possibly the platform: it reaches a musician at the exact moment they are about to hand over cash for a used instrument. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A hit holds the listing; it never accuses the seller | Most sellers of stolen gear bought it innocently. An accusation to an innocent seller is an unrecoverable trust failure. |
| D-02 | Screening runs twice — at publish and at checkout | Theft reports frequently post-date the listing. One check is a false assurance. |
| D-03 | The badge means "checked", never "authentic" or "clean" | Re-stamped serials defeat screening entirely (13.01.03). Overclaiming here would be actively harmful. |
| D-04 | Public serial lookup requires no account | Per DT-02 — the acquisition surface only works if it's reachable at the moment of a cash handover, by someone who has never heard of WeJammin. |
