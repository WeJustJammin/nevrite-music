# Feature: Inventory & Oversell Prevention

> **Number**: 13.03.03
> **Parent**: [Listings & Inventory](./13.03-listings-inventory-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Making sure the one 1962 Jazzmaster is sold exactly once. Used gear is qty=1 and non-fungible, so overselling is not a backorder inconvenience — it is a promise the seller physically cannot keep, and the loser of the race wanted *that unit*, not one like it. This feature owns the product answer to that race: **who wins, what the loser sees, how long a winner may hold, and how the seller is told**.

It carries two of the domain's three inventory regimes (domain D-06): **regime 1** — qty=1 non-fungible units, the defining case — and **regime 2** — counted fungible stock (strings, valves, patch cables). Regime 3 (pooled time-boxed rental) is 13.10-owned and out of scope here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own listings) | **As seller**: sees the unit flip to `reserved` the instant it's claimed, with the hold countdown; cannot un-reserve, cannot end a listing out from under an in-flight claim, cannot buy their own unit (D-14). Hit hardest by the band-entity duplicate-listing case (DT-05) — the drummer and the band both list the band's cymbal. **As buyer**: the persona most likely to *lose* a race on a hunted vintage piece, and the one who cares most — a saved search has been running for months. Also the persona a sniping bot beats every time (DT-08). |
| Producer | Full (own) | Loses races on rare outboard where the next unit may be years away, so the "it's back" alert (D-09) has disproportionate value to them. As seller: matched pairs are bundles, and bundle atomicity means their claim reserves N units at once (D-05/D-06) — the only persona for whom a *partial* bundle reserve would destroy the product rather than inconvenience them. |
| Operator | Full (own) | The persona that oversells hardest — fleet stock listed across multiple channels (13.03.04), sold at a shop counter, listed here, now double-promised. **Cross-channel is their oversell surface, not concurrent buyers.** The only persona for whom regime 2 (counted stock) is routine. Consumes the sold-elsewhere cancellation budget (D-11) and is the only persona who can claim that reason at all — it requires a connected channel. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees availability state as a buyer; no inventory controls, no seller side. Buys consumables (regime 2) more than any other persona, so they meet the "only 2 left" partial-quantity case (Edge Cases) most often. Most confused by losing a regime-1 race — needs "someone bought it 4 seconds before you" in plain language, never a stock error. Statistically the persona for whom this is a first-ever purchase here. |

## Behavior

Listing state (13.03.02) is authoritative; this feature defends it under concurrency. **Arbitration resolves on the physical unit, not on the listing** (D-05) — the domain reached "the unit is the identity anchor" independently five times (domain D-04), and this is the feature that spends it.

**Every path that can claim a unit funnels through one arbitration point** (CX-01 synthesis Q5): Buy Now, offer acceptance (13.05.01), auction close (13.05.02), and layaway first payment (13.06.03). A claim carries the unit ID and the **price version** the buyer saw. Multiple simultaneous claims resolve to exactly one winner. **The losing buyer's experience is a product surface, not an error path** (D-01).

**Regime 1 (qty=1)**: a claim moves the unit to `reserved` and makes *every* listing containing that unit unavailable in the same operation. **Regime 2 (counted stock)**: a claim decrements the count; oversell is a count race, not a unit race, and the loser is told what's left rather than that they lost. At count=1 regime 2 degenerates into regime 1 and inherits its loss surface at count=0.

**Hold durations are per claim type** (D-07), because the human context differs:

| Claim type | Hold | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Buy Now | **15 min** | Buyer is at the keyboard. Long enough to find a card and fix a typo; short enough that a hunted unit isn't frozen. Extended **once by 10 min** on payment-in-progress (3DS challenge). |
| Accepted offer (13.05.01) | **24 h** | Intent is proven (they offered), but acceptance may land at 03:00. Matches the offer-expiry convention already used in 13.05.01. |
| Auction win (13.05.02) | **48 h** | A binding bid; the winner has been in a multi-day process already. |
| Freight-quote-pending (13.06.01) | **72 h** | A 7ft grand piano's quote needs a human at a freight company. Gear-specific — no other marketplace's checkout waits on a person. |
| Layaway (13.06.03) | Payment schedule | Per DT-09 — `reserved` persists for months. |

Product requirement, not an engineering budget: **the claim outcome must be authoritative on screen within 1 s, or the UI must show explicit progress**. The latency budget itself is routed to `/create-prd-compile`.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer taps Buy Now on an `active` qty=1 listing at the price shown (£2,400)
2. The claim is submitted carrying the unit ID and the price version displayed
3. Arbitration resolves on the unit; outcome is authoritative within 1 s
4. Exactly one claim wins; unit → `reserved`; the 15-min Buy Now hold clock starts
5. Every other listing containing that unit — a bundle it belongs to (13.03.05) — becomes unavailable **in the same operation**; there is no interval in which the bundle is still buyable
6. Winner is routed to checkout (13.06.01) with a visible countdown
7. Losing claimants get the loss surface within the same second: "Someone bought this 4 seconds before you", similar units, "alert me if it relists" (CX-M28), and an ISO route (13.05.03)
8. Seller sees `reserved` with the countdown; they cannot un-reserve it
9. Payment captures → `sold`; settlement writes the ownership chain (13.06.05); **only now** does the sale become a comp (13.04.01, per DT-07)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two buyers race on one unit | One wins atomically on the unit | Loser: "Someone bought this 4 seconds before you" + similar units + "alert me if it relists" — never a 500, never a silent cart removal |
| Winner abandons payment | Hold expires per D-07; unit → `active`; prior losers and watchers alerted | "The Jazzmaster you missed is back" — a genuinely delightful moment, and free |
| Two accepted offers on one unit (13.05.01) | An accepted offer is a claim; the second acceptance fails | Seller: "You already accepted an offer on this unit" |
| **Seller accepts an offer while a Buy Now is in flight** | Both are claims; the Buy Now wins if it arbitrates first. The seller loses a race on their own unit | Seller: "This unit sold at the full price of £2,400 while your acceptance was in flight — no action needed." Offering buyer: standard loss surface |
| **Buy Now on an auction listing** | Buy Now is withdrawn on the first bid. Before any bid, a Buy Now claim closes the auction | "Buy Now ended when bidding started" |
| **Auction win fails arbitration** | Should be impossible — a listing is auction OR offer mode (13.05 D-03) and bid-time eligibility (13.05.02 DT-01) pre-clears the winner. If it happens it is a defect, not a race | Escalates to support; never a silent unsold auction |
| Sold on another channel, still listed here (Operator's real case) | Sync (13.03.04) is best-effort; a window always exists. Seller cancels | Cancellation reason "sold on another channel" — unscored within the D-11 budget. See DT-02. |
| **"Sold on another channel" claimed by a seller with no connected channel** | Rejected — the reason requires a connected channel (D-11) | "You have no connected channels — pick a different reason" |
| **Sold-elsewhere cancellations exceed the budget** | Beyond 2% of trailing-90-day completed orders (floor: 2), further ones score as seller-fault (D-11) | "Your sync isn't keeping up — further cancellations will affect your seller standing" |
| Unit in a cart, not yet purchased | **A cart is not a claim.** Carts hold intent; only purchase claims | "In someone's cart" is never shown — it would be pressure UX and it isn't true |
| **Bundle contains a unit also listed individually (13.03.05)** | **RESOLVED (D-05/D-06)**: double-listing is allowed; arbitration is on the unit. A bundle claim reserves all constituents atomically — if any one is already reserved, the whole bundle claim fails cleanly and reserves nothing. An individual claim on a constituent wins the unit and pushes the bundle listing to unavailable-pending-seller | Bundle buyer: "The Klon in this bundle sold separately 2 minutes ago — the bundle is no longer available." Seller: "Your pedalboard bundle is unavailable — the Klon sold separately. Re-price the bundle or end it." Auto-splitting never happens (13.03.05 D-03) |
| Seller cherry-picked: buyers take the good pedals individually, bundle left as dross | Not a system fault — a pricing consequence of the seller's own choice to double-list. The seller may set the bundle **all-or-nothing** (constituents not individually listed) at listing time | Shape picker: "Board only" vs "Board or parts" |
| **Two listings of one *unregistered* unit (band entity + member)** | On-platform oversell with **no seller at fault and no sync to blame** — nothing links the two listings. See DT-05 | Both buyers paid; one gets cancelled. Neither cancellation is scored against either seller. **Unregistered units cannot be defended.** |
| Seller has genuine multi-qty stock (new pedals, strings) | Regime 2: claims decrement the count | "Only 2 left" — never a race-loss message while stock remains |
| **Buyer wants 4, only 3 remain** | Never silently reduce the quantity | "3 available — buy 3?" — explicit, requires re-confirmation |
| Counted stock hits 0 | "Out of stock" ≠ "sold"; the listing persists and is restockable (13.03.05) | "Out of stock — tell me when it's back" |
| **Seller edits the price while a claim is in flight** | The claim carries the price version it was made against and **wins at that price** (D-10). Price *drops* are honoured in the buyer's favour | Buyer is never charged more than they saw. Seller: "Sold at £2,400 — your £2,600 edit landed after the sale" |
| **Buyer's page is stale; the price already changed before they clicked** | Claim rejected on price-version mismatch — not silently repriced | "The price changed to £2,600 — buy at the new price?" |
| Seller ends the listing while a claim is in flight | The claim wins. A unit cannot be withdrawn from under a buyer who already claimed it | Seller: "This unit was bought while you were ending the listing" |
| **Stolen-registry hit lands on a `reserved` unit (13.03.07)** | The hold **outranks** the reservation (D-13). Expiry does not release the unit to `active` while a hold is live — that would let a second buyer claim a unit under a theft report | Buyer: "This listing is under review; your payment has not been taken." Never "this is stolen" — the modal seller bought it innocently (13.03.07 D-01) |
| **Seller suspended (domain 24) while a unit is reserved** | Claim is suspended; no capture; buyer told plainly and routed onward | "This seller's account is under review — your payment hasn't been taken." Not "your order failed" |
| Network drops mid-claim; buyer doesn't know if they won | On reconnect the authoritative state is shown. **Double-tapping Buy Now never creates two claims or two charges** | "You bought this" or the loss surface — never ambiguity |
| **Payment-provider outage during a hold** | Reservation clocks **pause** during a declared outage (D-15) — expiring a buyer who physically could not pay is unfair | "Checkout is paused — your hold is safe, we've stopped the clock" |
| **Reservation squatting / griefing** | Claiming is cheap; the hold window is the attack surface (DT-04). Rivals can freeze a unit through its highest-attention first 48 h | After 1 abandoned reservation in 30 d: capped at **1 concurrent reservation**. After 2 in 90 d: **auth-before-claim** — the abuser eats the auth-hold friction. Both feed CX-M08 |
| **Sniping bot buys an underpriced vintage listing in 800 ms** | Real and endemic (DT-08). Default behaviour: strict first-claim-wins. The fair-lottery alternative is a user decision — Q-04 | The Musician with a 6-month saved search loses to a script, every time |
| **Seller claims their own listing** (own account, or a band entity they control) | Rejected (D-14) — it manufactures a fake comp, the same corruption vector as shill bidding (13.05.02) | "You can't buy your own listing" |
| Consignor sells privately while the dealer has it listed (13.09.02) | Cross-channel oversell **inside our own consignment flow** — the consignor is not the seller of record | Dealer cancels; scored to neither party; routed to 13.09.02 |
| Local pickup: deal collapses in person (13.11) | The unit was claimed and money may have moved. Post-claim collapse is 13.06.02/13.11's, not this feature's | Routed — see Q-05 |
| Malicious claim replay / rapid repeated claims | Rate-limited per account; replays are idempotent and never double-charge | Silent to honest users |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Claim submitted | "Securing this item" — must resolve authoritatively within 1 s or show explicit progress. The only moment the buyer is uncertain, and it must be short |
| Empty | Regime 2 listing at qty=0 | "Out of stock — tell me when it's back". The only genuine empty here: restockable, **not** sold, and the distinction the qty=1 lifecycle doesn't have |
| Populated | Unit `active`, no live claim | "Available" + Buy Now (regime 1) / "12 in stock" + quantity picker (regime 2) |
| Error | Claim lost, or rejected on price-version mismatch | Explicit loss message with routes onward (D-01) — never an error dialog. Price mismatch: the new price plus a re-confirm |
| Partial | Unit `reserved`, payment pending | Buyer: checkout countdown (15 min / 24 h / 48 h / 72 h per D-07). Seller: "Reserved — awaiting payment (expires in N)", no un-reserve control. Others: "Reserved — alert me if it returns" (per DT-03, shown honestly) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This domain is entirely qty=1, so inventory is a single boolean | D-14's own framing ("qty=1 non-fungible stock") and the domain index's "brutal physics" | ❌ REJECTED — and this is a real gap in D-14's reasoning. Candidate #23 (parts, consumables, bundles, B-stock) is **genuinely fungible with real stock counts**: strings, valves, patch cables, a dealer's 12 identical new pedals. The domain carries **two inventory regimes here**, not one — and a third (pooled rental) in 13.10, which the domain index rolled up as D-06. The qty=1 story is correct about what makes the domain distinct but is not complete. Flagged to the domain index; it slightly weakens the D-14 boundary argument without overturning it (13.03.05 D-01 keeps them separated). |
| DT-02 | Cross-channel sync makes cross-channel oversell solvable | Sync is listed as a candidate (#19) and reads like the fix | ❌ REJECTED: sync is best-effort over systems WeJammin does not control, with propagation measured in minutes. A unit sold in a physical shop at 14:02 cannot be un-sellable here at 14:02:30. **Cross-channel oversell is unpreventable in principle**; the honest design is cheap, fast, non-punitive cancellation *for this cause specifically* rather than pretending sync is a guarantee. Contradicts the naive reading of candidate #19 and matters for Operator adoption — a seller punished for physics stops listing. |
| DT-03 | A `reserved` unit should read as gone — showing "reserved" invites vultures and looks like pressure UX | Symmetry with D-02's rejection of "3 people are looking at this" | ❌ REJECTED: the two cases are opposites. "3 people are looking at this" pressures the *buyer* with something unverifiable; "reserved" tells a watcher something true and useful — **it is not gone forever**. For a hunted qty=1 unit the distinction between "sold" and "reserved" is the difference between grief and hope, and it is what makes the reservation-expiry alert (D-09) possible at all. Reverb shows only "Sold"; being more honest here is cheap and differentiating. |
| DT-04 | A claim is free, therefore claiming is safe | The claim-then-authorise ordering is standard and avoids authorising losers | ❌ REJECTED: if claiming is free and holds are long (24 h on an accepted offer), **reservation squatting is a cheap weapon**. A rival seller or a griefer can claim-and-abandon a unit repeatedly, freezing it through the first 48 h after publish — exactly when watchlist alerts have fired and attention peaks. eBay's unpaid-item strikes exist for this. Design answer: 1 concurrent reservation after an abandon in 30 d; **auth-before-claim** after 2 in 90 d, so the abuser (and only the abuser) eats the auth-hold friction. Claim-then-auth stays the default for everyone else. |
| DT-05 | On-platform oversell is impossible because we control both listings | The whole premise of unit-level arbitration (D-05) | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the sharpest finding here. Bands are **first-class entities** (personas.md), so a drummer and their band can *both* list the band's cymbal. Two listings, one object, two sellers, **neither at fault** — both genuinely believed they had it. Nothing links them unless the unit is registered (domain 15), and registration is optional (domain Q-04). So: **unit-anchored arbitration is only as good as registration coverage**, and on-platform oversell survives inside a system that controls both sides — the cross-channel failure (DT-02) reproduced with no channel to blame. Two consequences: (a) D-05 must be stated as conditional, not absolute; (b) **oversell protection is a third, day-one reason to register a unit** — one that works before the credit graph has any density. That directly answers the Step 5 question CX-07 poses ("is pre-fill alone enough to start the loop?"): no, but pre-fill + oversell protection might be, and both work at launch. |
| DT-06 | The losing buyer should get an exclusive second-in-line right if the winner abandons | Auctions already do second-chance offers (13.05.02); it looks like the obvious kindness | ❌ REJECTED: an *automatic system queue* is gameable — claim, abandon, and a colluding partner holds an exclusive at leisure, freezing a unit off-market for the abandon window **plus** the exclusive window. It also conflates two different things: 13.05.02's second-chance offer is **seller-initiated and optional** (the seller chooses to offer it), which is legitimate. An automatic queue removes the seller's choice and delays return-to-market on the hottest units. The open race plus an "it's back" alert (D-09) is fairer, faster, and unexploitable. |
| DT-07 | An expired reservation is market data — someone agreed to that price | Instinct from 13.03.02 D-01 ("sold listings are the comp set") | ❌ REJECTED: **nothing transacted.** Worse, if claims fed comps, accept-and-abandon would **manufacture price signals** — a seller and an accomplice agree £2,000 on a £900 pedal, abandon, and the guide records demand at £2,000. Only **settled** sales become comps (D-08). This is adjacent to 13.04.01 Q-02 (shill bidding) but not covered by it: shill bidding corrupts an auction's *price*; this corrupts the comp set with a *sale that never happened*. Sharpens 13.03.02's "sold → comp" to "settled → comp". |
| DT-08 | Sniping bots are an auction problem, not a Buy Now problem | Anti-snipe extension is discussed in 13.05.02 and nowhere else | ❌ REJECTED: **Buy Now sniping is worse.** A bot watching new listings buys an underpriced '59 Les Paul in 800 ms; the Musician with a 6-month saved search has never once been fast enough. This is endemic on Reverb and eBay and neither does anything about it. It directly harms the persona this feature's D-01 exists to protect, and first-claim-wins **is a values position** — it says the marketplace favours reaction time. The alternative (a randomised claim window on new listings, sneaker-drop style, making reaction time worthless) is real and defensible but adds a wait to every purchase, 99% of which nobody is racing for. Default stays first-claim-wins; the choice is the owner's — **Q-04**. |
| DT-09 | Layaway (13.06.03) is a payment plan — a checkout concern, not an inventory one | It sits under Cart/Checkout in the tree | ❌ REJECTED: layaway **must** claim the unit at the *first* payment, or the platform is taking a deposit on something another buyer can still buy. So layaway is a **fourth claim type with a months-long hold**, and it breaks the assumption every other part of this feature makes — that `reserved` is transient. A unit can sit `reserved` for 90 days while watchers ask why. The watcher-facing copy ("Reserved — alert me if it returns") is honest but misleading at that horizon, and 13.06.03's Q-01 (custody liability) compounds it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The losing buyer is a designed experience, not an error | The persona wanted *that* unit after months of searching. A 500 here is a lost user. |
| D-02 | A cart is not a claim; only purchase claims | Cart-holds on qty=1 gear would let one user freeze a unit others want. Also rules out "3 people are looking at this" pressure UX. |
| D-03 | Two inventory regimes live here: qty=1 non-fungible and counted-stock fungible. Regime 3 (pooled rental) is 13.10's | Per DT-01, aligned to domain D-06. |
| D-04 | Cross-channel oversell is treated as unpreventable; cancellation for that cause is cheap and non-punitive | Per DT-02 — punishing sellers for physics loses the Operator persona. |
| D-05 | **Arbitration resolves on the unit, not the listing** — conditional on the unit being registered | Resolves Q-01. Spends domain D-04's five-times-converged finding. Per DT-05 the conditional is load-bearing: unregistered units cannot be defended, and stating this absolutely would be a lie. |
| D-06 | Double-listing one unit (bundle + individually) is **allowed**, not prohibited | Sellers genuinely want "the whole board for £900, or pick pedals individually" — prohibiting it bans real behaviour. Bundle claims reserve all constituents atomically or fail reserving nothing; sellers who don't want cherry-picking choose all-or-nothing at listing time. |
| D-07 | Hold durations are per claim type: 15 min Buy Now (+10 min once on payment-in-progress), 24 h accepted offer, 48 h auction win, 72 h freight-quote-pending, layaway per schedule | One duration cannot serve a buyer at a keyboard and a buyer asleep when their offer is accepted. The 72 h freight case exists because our checkout can legitimately wait on a human at a freight company. |
| D-08 | **Only settled sales become comps.** Claims and expired reservations never do | Per DT-07 — otherwise accept-and-abandon manufactures price signals and poisons 13.04.01. |
| D-09 | No exclusive second-in-line queue; expiry returns the unit to the open market and alerts prior losers | Per DT-06 — an automatic queue is collusion-gameable. A seller-initiated second-chance offer (13.05.02) stays legitimate because the seller chooses it. |
| D-10 | A claim honours the price version the buyer saw; a stale page is rejected, never silently repriced | Charging a buyer a price they never saw is the most trust-destroying failure available here. Price *drops* are honoured in the buyer's favour. |
| D-11 | Sold-elsewhere cancellations are unscored up to **2% of trailing-90-day completed orders (floor: 2)** and require a connected channel | Makes D-04 operable without making "sold elsewhere" a universal excuse. Rate-based, not count-based, so a 400-unit Operator isn't punished for volume; the floor protects a low-volume seller's first two. Beyond the budget, sync isn't working or the reason is being abused — both are the seller's to fix. |
| D-12 | Repeat abandoners: 1 concurrent reservation after 1 abandon in 30 d; auth-before-claim after 2 in 90 d | Per DT-04 — the friction lands only on the abuser. Claim-then-auth stays the default so honest losers are never authorised. |
| D-13 | A stolen-screening hold outranks a reservation; expiry does not release a held unit to `active` | Per 13.03.07 — releasing would let a second buyer claim a unit under a theft report. |
| D-14 | Self-dealing claims are blocked — own account **or** a controlled band entity | It manufactures a fake comp; same corruption vector as shill bidding, arriving through the entity model. |
