## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Lifecycle ↔ Inventory

**Relationship**: Lifecycle owns the state machine; inventory owns the guarantee that the `active → reserved` transition happens exactly once per unit. The split matters because lifecycle is a product concern (what states exist, what edits are allowed) while the enforcement is a concurrency concern — and the *product* half of the race (what the loser sees) belongs to inventory, not to an error handler.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: loses races on hunted vintage pieces after months of saved searches; 13.03.03 D-01 exists for this persona.
- **Producer**: same, on rare outboard where the next unit may be years away.
- **Operator**: rarely races concurrent buyers; their oversell is cross-channel (CX-02).
- **Fan**: needs the loss explained in plain language — has no mental model for why an item vanished mid-click.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The listing's availability state is the shared entity. Lifecycle writes it on legitimate transitions; inventory arbitrates concurrent writes. Single owner (the listing), single writer at a time — arbitration, not merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Claim → atomic reserve → checkout (13.06.01). If payment fails or expires, reservation releases and the listing returns to `active`, which *re-triggers* watchlist alerts (CX-M28/CX-M07) to prior losers. Async, and one of the domain's few genuinely delightful moments.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — Fan can claim (buy) but cannot transition a listing any other way. Seller can end/edit but cannot un-reserve a paid unit.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Reservation expiry → "it's back" to prior losers and watchers. Bounded by watcher count, which for a hyped vintage listing could be large. Rate/batching is a CX-M07 concern.
5. **State transition conflict**: The core case. Two Buy Nows, an accepted offer (13.05.01), and an auction close (13.05.02) can all claim the same unit. All three paths must funnel through one arbitration point, or the domain oversells. **Flagged for `/create-prd-architecture` as an invariant.**

### CX-02: Bulk & Sync ↔ Inventory

**Relationship**: Sync exists to stop an Operator selling one amp twice across channels. It cannot succeed — WeJammin does not control the other systems, propagation is minutes, and a shop counter sale is instant. 13.03.03 DT-02 and 13.03.04 D-02 both concede this; the design response is to make cancellation-for-this-cause cheap rather than to promise consistency.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: the only persona materially affected. This is their adoption question.
- **Musician/Producer**: single-channel sellers; sync is a migration convenience, not a risk.
- **Fan**: buyer-side; experiences it only as a cancelled order, which is the harm being traded away.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: **No global authority exists** — this is the defining property. Each channel is authoritative for itself; WeJammin's state wins here and nowhere else. Conflicts surface to the seller rather than auto-resolving (13.03.04 Edge Cases).
2. **Trigger chain**: External sale → webhook/poll → reconcile → end listing here. Every hop adds latency; the window is irreducible. If sync *write-out* fails, we may have caused an oversell elsewhere — surfaced loudly (13.03.04 D-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Channel credentials are seller-held; the platform acts as the seller against third-party APIs. Delegated authority (CX-M09) and a real trust boundary.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Sync failure → seller. A cancelled buyer → order lifecycle (13.06.02). The buyer's harm is real and caused by a system limitation, not seller fault — which per 13.03.03 D-04 must not be scored against the seller (13.03.03 Q-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: Sold-here and sold-there within the same window = two buyers, one amp, no arbitration possible. Someone gets cancelled. The only open question is who absorbs the cost.

### CX-03: Bulk & Sync ↔ Listing Creation

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's central conflict. Single-listing creation enforces model binding (13.01.04), a condition grade (13.02.01), a category flaw checklist (13.02.02), and unit-specific media (13.03.01 D-01). A CSV row carries none of them. Either bulk listings are second-class — unmatched, undisclosed, stock-photo'd — or bulk is dramatically harder than an upload.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: the persona bulk exists for, and the persona the quality bar would exclude.
- **Producer**: batch of 20–40 heterogeneous units; hardest to template because every piece of outboard has different flaws.
- **Musician**: unaffected — lists one at a time.
- **Fan**: buyer-side. If bulk listings are second-class, the Fan is the persona least able to detect that a listing is undisclosed and stock-photo'd — they'd read absence of flaws as absence of flaws.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both write listings. Bulk must not create a *shadow* listing type with different obligations — that is precisely the failure mode.
2. **Trigger chain**: Import → match at volume → publish (matched or unmatched) → review queue. The chain is fine; the *obligations* are what break.
3. **Permission intersection**: None distinct — same seller, same rights. The asymmetry is in what the flow demands, not in who may do it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Import completion → seller, with the needs-attention count.
5. **State transition conflict**: A bulk price update racing a live offer is handled (13.03.04 Edge Cases: skip with reason). **The "does the quality bar bend" framing is resolved by DQ-MG-01 and was a false dilemma** — it does not bend. Each axis holds its own posture: model binding unrelaxed (`13.01.04` DT-11 raises it), grading bounded-and-disclosed (`13.02.01` D-11), disclosure unrelaxed with no substitute (`13.02.02` D-08), unit media unrelaxed with its capture moment moved to label print (`13.03.01` D-06). This is why bulk does **not** create the shadow listing tier this CX prohibits: there is no second obligation tier, only a different decision count and moment.

### CX-04: Stolen Screening ↔ Lifecycle

**Relationship**: A registry hit forces a listing out of `active` for reasons the seller did not cause and may not understand — the only externally-triggered lifecycle transition in the sub-domain. The design constraint (13.03.07 D-01) is that this must not read as an accusation, because the modal seller of stolen gear bought it innocently.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer/Operator**: as sellers, can be held by someone else's theft report — or someone else's typo (13.03.07 false-positive case).
- **Fan**: buyer-side; sees only the badge. Protected by a mechanism they never see operate.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Lifecycle owns listing state; screening forces a hold. The hold must be a distinct state from a seller-initiated `ended` — conflating them would let a hold look like a withdrawal and destroy the recovery trail.
2. **Trigger chain**: Publish → screen → hit → hold + notify seller + notify reporter + escalate to domain 24. Re-screen at checkout catches reports that post-date publish (13.03.07 D-02). If the registry is down: fail open at publish, contested at checkout (13.03.07 Q-01).
3. **Permission intersection**: The seller cannot un-hold their own listing — resolution routes through domain 24. Correct, and needs to be fast (13.03.07 false-positive case), since a slow resolution is indistinguishable from an accusation.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Hit → seller, reporter, moderation. The reporter notification is the recovery moment and the whole point of the registry.
5. **State transition conflict**: A hit landing *during* checkout, or after settlement (13.03.07 Q-02 — unresolved, legally loaded). Post-settlement is worst: ownership has already transferred (13.06.05) and may need reversing.

### CX-05: Parts & Bundles ↔ Inventory

**Relationship**: 13.03.05 breaks two of inventory's assumptions at once. Counted stock introduces a second regime (fungible, restockable, "out of stock" ≠ "sold"). Bundles break object↔listing 1:1 — one physical unit can sit in a bundle listing *and* an individual listing, and both must reserve together.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: bundles a pedalboard; hits the shared-unit case if any pedal is also listed alone.
- **Operator**: holds genuine counted stock; the only persona for whom regime two is routine.
- **Producer**: matched pairs where splitting destroys value — an atomicity claim, not a grouping.
- **Fan**: buys consumables (regime two) most often of any persona; never encounters regime one as a seller.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The physical **unit** is the shared entity across bundle and individual listings — not the listing. This is the same conclusion 13.03.02 DT-02 reached from relisting: **the unit, not the listing, must be the identity anchor**. Two features arriving independently at the same architectural requirement is strong signal. **Flagged for `/create-prd-architecture`.**
2. **Trigger chain**: Bundle claim → reserve all constituents atomically. If any constituent is already reserved individually, the bundle claim must fail cleanly (13.03.05 Q-01, 13.03.03 Q-01 — same unresolved question from both sides).
3. **Permission intersection**: None distinct.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A bundle sale reserves N units and may cancel N individual listings' watchers.
5. **State transition conflict**: Bundle Buy Now racing an individual Buy Now on a shared constituent. Both are legitimate; exactly one must win; the arbitration point is the unit, not either listing.

### CX-06: Provenance ↔ Stolen Screening

**Relationship**: Both read domain 15 keyed on the same serial and ask opposite questions — "what has this done?" and "is this stolen?" Kept as separate nodes because the questions, consumers and failure behaviours differ entirely. Recorded because they share a dependency and an identical failure mode: **no serial, no feature**. Medium confidence — the interaction is structural rather than behavioural.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: relies on both, understands neither, verifies nothing.
- **Musician/Producer**: supply the serial that powers both by registering the unit.
- **Operator**: screens at intake; accrues provenance on fleet gear.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. One question is worth naming now: a listing with rich provenance and a stolen-registry hit is the highest-stakes case in the domain — a valuable, historically significant, allegedly stolen instrument. Which panel wins the page? Step 5.

### CX-07: Listing Creation ↔ Provenance

**Relationship**: Registering a unit in domain 15 pays twice at listing time — it pre-fills the listing (model, serial, mods, photos) *and* unlocks the provenance panel. This is the consolidation thesis (D-18) producing a compounding loop: the registry makes listing easier, listing surfaces provenance, provenance raises price, higher prices make registration worth doing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: feels the pre-fill most (least patient with forms), and the provenance most (emotionally invested in the instrument's history).
- **Producer**: supplies the provenance data through sessions (domain 02), then benefits from it when selling.
- **Operator**: fleet registration → faster bulk listing.
- **Fan**: sees the result; contributes nothing to the loop.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. The loop is asserted from the two files' own text, not yet validated against real registration behaviour. Step 5 should test whether the pre-fill benefit alone justifies registration before the credit graph has any density — because at launch, pre-fill is the *only* half that works, and if it isn't enough on its own the loop never starts.
