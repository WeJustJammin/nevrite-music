# Feature: Listing Lifecycle & Relisting

> **Number**: 13.03.02
> **Parent**: [Listings & Inventory](./13.03-listings-inventory-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The state machine a listing moves through — `draft → active → reserved → sold`, plus `paused`, `held`, `lapsed` and `ended` — together with the rules governing what may be edited in each state, what happens when a listing goes stale, and what a relist inherits when a sale collapses.

It matters more for gear than for fungible retail because a qty=1 listing has no "back in stock": when it sells it is gone forever, and when a sale collapses the unit must return to market **carrying its history**, not as a fresh listing pretending nothing happened. Under D-18 (provenance is the wedge), a lifecycle that lets a seller launder a unit's history by relisting is not a UX flaw — it is the platform's own thesis failing inside its own marketplace.

The feature declares state. It does not enforce it under concurrency — that is 13.03.03.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own listings) | Manages 1–5 listings by hand. Edits price when nothing bites; relists after a buyer ghosts; answers a freshness challenge once a quarter. Two lifecycle events are theirs alone: **the gig pause** — they list a pedal, then it appears on a confirmed show's backline manifest (18.09) and they need it Saturday, so they pause rather than end (DT-10); and **band-owned gear**, where the listing belongs to the Band entity (domain 01) and their authority to end or edit it revokes if they leave the band (CX-M09). Wants "why isn't this selling?" answered — which is where the price guide (13.04) enters, not this feature. |
| Producer | Full (own) | The persona the staleness design is calibrated for. Niche outboard sells over 6–12 months; a 30-day hard expiry would kill listings that were going to sell (DT-05). Their second lifecycle context is that studio gear is listed **while still in the rack** — so the same unit may be out on a rental (13.10) or in a session, and the sale listing must pause rather than promise a unit that is physically 200 miles away in a hire van (DT-10). |
| Operator | Full (own) | Fleet-scale, where individual listings blur into inventory state. Drives lifecycle through bulk operations (13.03.04), not one-by-one. Owns four lifecycle cases no other persona has: **cross-channel `sold-elsewhere` ends** (13.03.03 D-04 — non-punitive by decision); **two-device edit conflicts** — the persona is defined as working from a phone at a loading dock while a desk exists (DT-13); **consignment** as consignee, where they hold custody but not the authority to end (13.09.02 D-01, DT-11); and **the same unit listed for sale and for hire**, the domain's third-regime collision (D-06). |
| Fan | None (no controls) | No lifecycle surface — cannot end, pause, edit or relist anything (per the sub-domain Role Matrix and 13.03.01's Role Lens). **But the Fan is the actor whose behaviour fires this feature's most common transition**: an abandoned checkout is what drives `reserved → active`. They are also the involuntary decision-maker in the post-claim amendment flow (D-08) — the persona least equipped to evaluate "the seller found a crack in the neck; accept or void?" That asymmetry is why the amendment's auto-resolution voids rather than accepts (D-09). |

## Behavior

Lifecycle is authoritative over availability; 13.03.03 defends that state under concurrent claims. Three principles govern the whole machine:

1. **The unit — not the listing — is the identity anchor** (domain D-04). Time-on-market, watchers, the canonical public page, and cancellation history all attach to the unit and survive across relists (DT-06, DT-07, DT-08).
2. **A claim freezes the listing** (DT-04). At `reserved`, the listing's seller-authored content snapshots. The buyer bought *that snapshot*; nothing may mutate it afterwards. This is the point of immutability — not `sold`.
3. **An offer is made against a stated listing.** Any material change to that statement voids the offer — whether the change is price (D-03) or condition (D-11). This generalises D-03 from a rule into a principle.

### Listing States

| State | Meaning | Public? |
|-------|---------|---------|
| `draft` | Created, unpublished, resumable indefinitely. Owned by 13.03.01. | No |
| `active` | Published and claimable. | Yes — indexable |
| `paused` | Seller-suspended. Not claimable, not gone. Retains watchers and time-on-market clock state. | Page resolves; delisted from search |
| `held` | Forced out of `active` by a party other than the seller — registry hit (13.03.07), moderation (24), or seller suspension. Seller cannot self-clear. | Page resolves neutrally; no reason shown |
| `reserved` | Claimed atomically via 13.03.03. **Content frozen.** Payment authorising. | Yes — shown reserved |
| `sold` | Payment captured. Terminal for the listing; 13.06.02 owns everything onward. | Yes — permanent, indexable |
| `lapsed` | Freshness challenge unanswered. Off market; one-tap relistable; watchers retained. | Page resolves; delisted from search |
| `ended` | Seller-terminated with a reason code. Terminal. | Page resolves; delisted from search |

### Transitions

| From | To | Trigger | Actor |
|------|----|---------|-------|
| `draft` | `active` | Publish (payout onboarding is the only hard prerequisite — 13.03.01 D-03) | Seller |
| `active` | `reserved` | Buy Now, accepted offer (13.05.01), or auction close (13.05.02) — all three funnel through the single arbitration point (13.03 CX-01 Q5) | Buyer / system |
| `active` | `paused` | Seller pauses; or auto-pause when the unit goes out on a rental (13.10.02) | Seller / system |
| `active` | `held` | Registry hit, moderation action, seller suspension | System / domain 24 |
| `active` | `lapsed` | Freshness challenge unanswered after 14 days | System |
| `active` | `ended` | Seller ends with a reason code | Seller |
| `paused` | `active` | Seller resumes; or rental returns | Seller / system |
| `paused` | `ended` | Seller ends | Seller |
| `reserved` | `sold` | Payment captured | System |
| `reserved` | `active` | Reservation expires, payment fails, or buyer cancels pre-capture. **Re-triggers "it's back"** to unit-watchers and prior race losers (13.03 CX-01 Q2) | System |
| `reserved` | `held` | Checkout re-screen registry hit (13.03.07 D-02) | System |
| `lapsed` | `active` | Relist — one tap, indefinitely available, no data loss | Seller |
| `held` | `active` \| `ended` | Hold cleared or upheld | Domain 24 |

**Transitions that must not exist:**

| Blocked | Why |
|---------|-----|
| `active → sold` | Skipping `reserved` bypasses the single arbitration point and oversells. |
| `reserved → ended`, `reserved → paused` | A seller must not escape a claim by withdrawing the listing. Their only exit is order cancellation (13.06.02), which costs them (CX-M08). |
| `sold → active` | A collapsed sale does **not** revive the listing. It produces a **relist** — a new listing inheriting unit identity (DT-02). Reviving would silently erase the failed sale. |
| `ended → active` | `ended` is terminal. Returning to market is a relist, and time-on-market carries (D-06). |

**Media is additive because deletion is evidence destruction.** The flaw photos that 13.02.02 D-03 makes publish-blocking cannot be removed while a listing is `active` — removing them would leave a listing that could never have been published in the state it now sits in.

### Staleness — the answer to "when does a listing expire?"

**There is no hard expiry** (DT-05). Instead:

1. **Dormancy clock**: 90 consecutive days with no seller activity on the listing *and* no buyer engagement (offer, message, watch). Any of those resets it to zero.
2. At 90 days → **freshness challenge**, one tap, via CX-M07. On `active`: "Still available?" On `paused`: "Do you still own this?" — the second answer feeds domain 15's registry accuracy.
3. Confirm → clock resets to zero. Nothing else changes.
4. No response within 14 days → `lapsed`. Watchers told: "The seller hasn't confirmed this is still available."
5. `lapsed → active` in one tap, forever, with nothing lost.

**Confirming freshness must never bump ranking** (D-07). A free ranking bump for answering a hygiene prompt would (a) turn the challenge into a bump-farming ritual every seller games, destroying the signal, and (b) cannibalise CX-M25, where bumps are the paid product.

The clock runs in `paused` too — otherwise pause is the way to dodge the challenge permanently.

**Why 90/14**: used gear's median time-to-sale is weeks; the vintage and niche-outboard tail is months. 90 days sits far beyond the median, so a normally-selling listing never sees a challenge, and the Producer's 8-month outboard sale costs one tap per quarter. 14 days covers a two-week tour. Global constants at v1 (D-15).

### What a Relist Inherits

| Property | Behaviour |
|----------|-----------|
| Unit identity (serial, domain 15 registry link) | **Inherited** — the anchor (domain D-04) |
| Time-on-market | **Inherited** if the unit was off market < 30 days; **resets** at ≥ 30 days (D-06) |
| Watchers | **Inherited** — a watch follows the unit, not the listing (DT-07), so "it's back" fires across a relist |
| Media, condition grade, disclosure | **Inherited** and pre-filled, editable — the seller may have new photos |
| Price | **Editable**, defaults to the last ask |
| Cancellation history against the seller | **Inherited** — this is the entire point of DT-02 |
| The unit's settled sale history | **Inherited** — rendered by provenance (13.03.06) |
| Prior offers | **Not** carried as live offers. Prior offerers are notified the unit relisted, with the old and new price |
| Views, engagement counts | **Reset** — per-listing and meaningless carried across |
| Public canonical URL | The **unit page** is canonical and durable (DT-08). Each listing gets its own URL; the unit page is what CX-M23 indexes |

### Happy Path

1. Seller publishes a draft → `active`. Time-on-market clock starts on the **unit**.
2. Buyer clicks Buy Now → 13.03.03 arbitrates → exactly one claim wins → `reserved`. **The listing's seller-authored content snapshots at this instant.**
3. Payment captures → `sold`. The listing is now permanent and immutable. 13.06.02 takes over the order.
4. Order proceeds: dispatch (evidence pack seals — 13.02.04), delivery, inspection window.
5. Order **settles** (13.06.02 terminal state) → three things fire: funds release (CX-M01), ownership writes to domain 15 (13.06.05), and **the sale is admitted to the comp set** (13.04.01).
6. The sold listing page persists forever, publicly, showing the settled price, the date, and comps for the same model (CX-M23).

> **Step 5 is the correction this pass made.** The comp admits at **settlement**, not at `sold` (DT-03). A price that was captured but later refunded, cancelled or returned is not evidence of anything.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer abandons payment | Reservation expires (window owned by 13.03.03 Q-03); `reserved → active`; reservation history persists internally | "The Jazzmaster you missed is back" to unit-watchers + prior race losers, urgency tier: minutes, not digest (CX-M07) |
| Payment authorises, then capture fails at settlement | Same as abandonment — `reserved → active`. Never `sold` | Buyer: "Your payment didn't complete." Seller: "Back on sale — no fault recorded" |
| Late capture lands **after** the release already committed and another buyer claimed | Release is authoritative once committed. The late capture is voided and refunded in full — never oversold | Late buyer: full refund + "someone claimed it while your payment was completing" + the watch CTA |
| Seller edits price while an offer is live | Blocked (D-03). An offer is against a stated price | "Withdraw or decline the pending offer first" |
| Seller **downgrades condition** while an offer is live | Live offers auto-void with a reason (D-11). Symmetric with D-03 — moving the condition under an offer is the same bad-faith act as moving the price | Offerer: "The seller updated this listing's condition; your offer was cancelled. Here's what changed." |
| Seller **ends** a listing that has live offers | Permitted — a seller must be able to leave the market. Offers auto-decline with reason; offerers notified | Offerer: "The seller withdrew this listing." If the unit relists within 30 days, they're told, **with the old and new price shown side by side** (D-14) |
| Seller ends and relists at a higher price to escape a low offer | Not prohibited — intent is unpoliceable. Made **visible** instead: time-on-market does not reset (< 30 days), prior offerers are notified with both prices, and the relist count sits on the unit page | Buyer: "On the market since March · relisted" — the seller's escape is legible, not blocked |
| Seller tries to end an **auction** with ≥1 bid, inside 12h of close | Blocked (D-10). A bid is a binding commitment in auction culture; letting the seller walk at hour 11 makes bidding worthless | "You can't end an auction with active bids this close to closing" |
| Seller ends an auction with bids **earlier** than 12h before close | Permitted with a reason code; all bidders notified; counted as a cancellation against the seller | "All 7 bidders will be notified and this will count against your seller record. End anyway?" |
| Seller materially edits the unit's condition **after purchase, before dispatch** | The snapshot is frozen (DT-04), so the edit cannot apply to the listing. It becomes a **disclosure amendment against the order** (D-08): dispatch clock pauses, buyer notified with a before/after diff, buyer chooses accept (optionally at a seller-offered reduction) or void with full refund and no buyer fault. No response in 72h → **auto-void** (D-09) | Buyer: "The seller found something after you bought this: [diff]. Accept, accept at £X, or cancel for a full refund." Seller: "Waiting on the buyer — dispatch is paused." |
| Seller "discovers" damage post-sale repeatedly, to escape prices they regret | Accepted amendments cost the seller nothing — that is the honest path working. Voided amendments count as cancellation-with-cause. **The rate is the signal**: 1 in 40 is an honest seller; 8 in 10 is a price-escape scam and a CX-M08 pattern-detection concern (DT-12) | Seller: nothing (rate detection is silent). Domain 24: pattern flagged |
| Seller deletes a listing to erase a bad history | **There is no delete for a published listing** (D-04). `draft` deletes outright — nothing external depends on it. Everything else exits via `ended` or `sold`, both terminal and permanent. A settled sale's comp and ownership event survive any seller action | "This listing can't be deleted. End it instead — it'll be removed from search and stay on your records." |
| Seller files a GDPR/CCPA erasure request covering a settled sale | De-identification, not deletion (D-05). A comp needs {model, condition, date, price, region}; it does not need the seller's name. The seller is de-identified from the public listing; the comp and the ownership event survive as facts about an object | "Your identity has been removed from these listings. The sale record itself is a transaction fact and can't be erased." Legal validation: Q-02 |
| Listing goes stale unsold | No hard expiry. Freshness challenge at 90d → `lapsed` at 104d → one-tap relist forever | "Still available?" → later, "Relist in one tap" — original media and disclosure intact |
| Seller lists an amp here, sells it in their shop, forgets the listing | The freshness challenge purges it at 104 days. **This is a cross-channel oversell mitigation that sync structurally cannot be** (DT-05) — sync only knows about connected channels; the challenge catches a cash sale off a shop counter | Seller at 90d: "Still available?" — one tap ends it honestly, before a buyer gets cancelled |
| Unit sold off-platform mid-listing | Seller ends with reason `sold-elsewhere`. Non-punitive by decision (13.03.03 D-04) — punishing sellers for physics loses the Operator | End-listing reason picker. Repeated pattern with prior on-platform contact = a disintermediation signal for domain 24 |
| Seller writes contact details into the end reason to poach a buyer | End reasons are a **closed picker plus an optional private note** (D-16). The note is never shown to buyers — it exists for the seller's records and for moderation | Picker only. No free-text reaches any buyer |
| Sold listing hit by an old link or a search engine crawl | Must not 404 — sold listings are the price guide's evidence and CX-M23's substance | Sold-state page with settled price, date, comps for the same model, and a canonical link to the unit page |
| Sold listing whose order has **not yet settled** is crawled | The page is public and shows "Sold", but **the price does not publish until settlement**. Publishing an unsettled price would put a refundable number into the comp set | "Sold" — no price. The price appears at settlement |
| A settled sale is **returned** post-settlement (13.08.01) | The comp is **withdrawn** from the set (D-03/DT-03); ownership reverts (13.06.05); the unit returns to the seller and relists with full inheritance | Price guide: the comp silently disappears. Seller: relist CTA with everything pre-filled |
| Buyer and seller are linked parties wash-trading to manufacture a comp | The price guide is the domain's most valuable asset and therefore its most manipulable (DT-15). Comp admission needs a self-dealing exclusion — settled money is necessary but not sufficient evidence | Nothing visible. Excluded comps never enter 13.04.01; the pattern routes to domain 24 |
| An **unmatched** listing sells before its model reaches the catalog (13.01.02) | Immutability covers **seller-authored content only** (D-12). Catalog binding is a system-owned reference that may be resolved later — otherwise the comp set permanently loses every sale that predated its model's catalog entry | Nothing. The comp appears in the model's guide once the model exists |
| Unit goes out on a rental (13.10) while its sale listing is `active` | **Auto-pause** (D-13). A sale claim on a unit that is 200 miles away in a hire van is unshippable. Rental produces no comps and no ownership transfer (13.10 D-03), so it does not touch the sale record — but it does pause the time-on-market clock | Seller: "Paused while out on hire until the 14th — it'll go back up automatically." |
| Unit appears on a confirmed show's backline manifest (18.09) while listed | **Prompt, never auto-pause** (D-13). The seller may intend to sell it *after* the show, or *at* the show. Auto-pausing would override a legitimate intent | "You've got a show Saturday and this pedal is on the stage plot. Pause the listing until Sunday?" |
| Consignor withdraws their unit mid-listing (13.09.02) | The consignee's listing ends. The end is attributed to the **consignor** — the consignee did nothing wrong and must not be scored for it (13.09.02 D-01: custody transferred, ownership did not) | Consignee: "The owner withdrew this instrument. No effect on your seller record." |
| Consignee tries to end a consigned listing on their own initiative | Blocked (DT-11). Custody is not authority — ending requires the consignor's mandate via CX-M09 | "You'll need the owner's approval to end this listing." |
| Band member who listed band-owned gear leaves the band | Their authority to edit or end the listing revokes on departure (CX-M09). The listing belongs to the Band entity (domain 01) and survives. A `reserved` listing must not be strandable by a departure mid-transaction | Departed member: listing no longer in their manage view. Band admins: unaffected |
| Seller account suspended (domain 24) with listings in every state | `active`/`paused` → `held`. `reserved`/`sold` are **not** retroactively voided — committed money completes or refunds via 13.06.02. Suspension stops new commerce; it does not strand a buyer's authorised payment | Buyer of a `reserved` unit: nothing changes. Seller: "Your listings are on hold pending review." |
| Registry hit lands at the checkout re-screen on a `reserved` listing (13.03.07 D-02) | `reserved → held`. The buyer's authorisation is voided, never captured | Buyer: "This listing is under review and your payment was cancelled" — **never** "this may be stolen". Per 13.03.07 D-01, nobody is accused |
| Watchers of a listing that goes to `held` | Told **nothing**. A hold is not public — announcing "under review" to a watcher list broadcasts exactly the accusation 13.03.07 D-01 forbids. The listing simply leaves search. Watchers are told only if the hold resolves to `ended` | Watchers: silence. Anyone holding the URL: "This listing is unavailable" — neutral, no reason |
| Domain 15 says someone else owns a unit that is currently listed | **Warns and flags for review; never auto-holds.** The registry's coverage is partial and self-asserted at the edges (that is what 13.06.05 exists to fix) — a false hold is the 13.03.07 D-01 harm, and sub-domain D-01 says the flow never stalls | Seller: "Our registry shows this unit transferred to someone else. Is that right?" Listing stays up |
| Seller edits the same listing from a phone and a desktop at once | The two edits must reconcile **per field** — the later save must never blind-overwrite the earlier one wholesale (DT-13). The Operator is defined as a two-device user, and a wholesale overwrite would silently drop a flaw disclosure — the one thing 13.02.02 D-03 makes publish-blocking | "This listing changed on your other device: [diff]. Keep yours, keep theirs, or merge." |
| Connection drops mid-transition at the loading dock | A retried transition must never double-apply. If the transition already landed, the retry reports the current state rather than repeating it or erroring | "Already ended" — not an error |
| Bulk operation (13.03.04) issues a transition blocked for some rows | Skip-with-reason; never partial application. A bulk "end all" over 50 listings that hits 3 `reserved` ends 47 and reports 3 skipped, each with its reason | "47 ended · 3 skipped — 3 have buyers mid-checkout" |
| Bundle listing ends while a constituent unit is also listed individually (13.03.05) | Ending the bundle must not end or strand the constituents' own listings — one physical unit, two listings, coupled only at claim time (13.03 CX-05) | Seller: "Ending this bundle. The 4 pedals stay listed individually." |
| A gear-sale cancellation lands on a multi-hyphenate who is also a working Producer | The cancellation scores against their **gear-seller role only**. CX-M08's reputation is per-role by design — a superb mixing engineer may be an unreliable gear seller, and the converse must also hold (DT-12) | Their Producer profile is untouched. Their seller record shows the cancellation |
| Musician's `active` listing is for a pedal the platform knows is on their own gig's stage plot **and** a buyer claims it | The claim wins — an `active` listing is a promise, and the prompt (D-13) was offered and declined. The seller plays the gig with a borrowed pedal or cancels and wears it | No special handling. The prompt existed; ignoring it has a cost |
| Seller has zero listings | Empty state, not an error | "List your first piece of gear" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing management view opens | Listing table skeleton grouped by state; never a full-page block |
| Empty | Seller has no listings | "List your first piece of gear" with a route into 13.03.01 |
| Populated | Listings exist | State-grouped table: Active · Reserved · Sold · Paused · Held · Lapsed · Drafts, each with count and the next action |
| Error | A state transition fails | Explicit reason plus retry. **State is never left ambiguous** — an unknown listing state is the source of oversell. On a retried transition, the current state is reported, not an error, and the transition never double-applies |
| Partial | `reserved`, payment pending | Seller: "Reserved — awaiting payment (expires in N)". Others: "Reserved — alert me if it returns". Also covers a `sold` listing whose order has not settled: public "Sold" with no price yet |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A sold listing can be archived or deleted — its job is done | Standard e-commerce instinct; sold items are clutter | ❌ REJECTED: the sold listing **is the comp**. Deleting it destroys the price guide's only evidence base (13.04.01) and erases the strongest SEO surface the domain has (CX-M23 — "what is my 1972 Telecaster worth" is a search with enormous volume). Sold listings must persist as immutable public records. This also means a seller cannot delete their way out of a bad price history — which is correct, and needs saying (Q-02). |
| DT-02 | Relisting after a failed sale is just creating a new listing | It looks identical from the form's perspective | ❌ REJECTED: a relist carries history a fresh listing doesn't — a prior reservation that collapsed, possibly a buyer dispute, possibly a seller cancellation. Treating relist as "new" launders that history and lets a seller cycle a problem unit indefinitely with a clean face. **The relist must inherit the unit's identity** (serial → domain 15 where registered), not just its form fields. Note this contradicts nothing but requires the unit, not the listing, to be the identity anchor — an architectural point. |
| DT-03 | A sale enters the comp set at `sold` | This file's own surface pass said so; `sold` is when the money moves | ❌ REJECTED — and this corrects our own prior text. 13.06.02's terminal state is **settlement**, not `sold`; the inspection window, disputes, cancellations and returns all sit between them. A comp admitted at `sold` is a price that may be refunded next week. **The comp admits at settlement and is withdrawn if a post-settlement return unwinds the sale** (13.08.01). Consequence: a sold page is public immediately but publishes no price until settlement. The ~2–4 week lag this introduces is irrelevant in a market where the median vintage listing takes months — and it buys a guide that reports only transactions that stuck. Reverb's guide does not make this distinction, which is why its numbers include sales that were unwound. |
| DT-04 | Immutability begins at `sold` | The natural reading of DT-01, and of "a sold listing is a record" | ❌ REJECTED: immutability must begin at **`reserved`**. The buyer clicked Buy Now against a specific price, condition grade, disclosure set and photo set. That is what they bought. If the listing is editable between `reserved` and `sold`, the seller can silently change what was purchased while the payment is authorising. The claim takes a **snapshot**; the snapshot is the contract. This single reframe resolves the file's oldest open question (post-purchase condition edits — old Q-01, now D-08) and it resolves it *better* than a bespoke rule would: the edit doesn't need to be blocked, it needs to be **rerouted** into an amendment the buyer can refuse. |
| DT-05 | A hard expiry window is needed to keep the marketplace clean | eBay expires at 30 days; stale listings waste buyer time | ❌ REJECTED as stated, with the underlying concern **confirmed**. A hard expiry is hostile to the Producer persona — CX-M28's own text says "gear buying is a long patient hunt where the buyer waits months for the right year and colour," and the supply side has the same clock. Reverb runs listings indefinitely for exactly this reason. But the concern is real: the modal failure is a buyer messaging about an amp the seller sold on Craigslist 14 months ago and forgot about. The resolution is a **freshness challenge**, not an expiry: 90 days dormant → one-tap "still available?" → 14 days → `lapsed`. Second-order finding, and the better one: **this is a cross-channel oversell mitigation that sync structurally cannot be.** 13.03.03 DT-02 concedes cross-channel oversell is unpreventable because sync only sees connected channels — but the freshness challenge catches the cash sale off a shop counter that no webhook will ever report. A hygiene feature turns out to address the sub-domain's most-conceded failure. |
| DT-06 | Relisting resets the listing's age, and that's fine | A relist is a new object, so its clock starts at zero | ❌ REJECTED: this is the single most obvious gaming vector in the feature. "On the market since March" is a negotiation signal buyers use; if a relist resets it, every seller relists monthly and the signal is dead within a quarter — Reverb sellers do exactly this today. Per domain D-04, **time-on-market is a property of the unit's continuous market presence, not the listing.** A collapsed sale doesn't reset it (the unit never left). `paused` pauses it (the unit genuinely left). A gap of ≥30 days resets it (the market has plausibly forgotten). This also closes the end-and-relist-higher loophole around D-03 without prohibiting anything. |
| DT-07 | Watchers belong to the listing | A watch is a bookmark on a page | ❌ REJECTED: if a watch belongs to the listing, every relist orphans its watchers — and the "it's back" notification (13.03 CX-01 Q2), which the sub-domain calls "one of the domain's few genuinely delightful moments," cannot fire across a relist at all. The buyer who watched the Jazzmaster that fell through is precisely the person who wants the relist. **A watch must follow the unit, not the listing.** CX-M28 already supports watching across different kinds of thing, so what is watched here is the unit. This is the third independent arrival at domain D-04 from inside this one file. |
| DT-08 | The listing URL is the durable public surface | It's the page that exists and gets shared | ❌ REJECTED: a guitar that is listed, falls through, relists, lapses and relists again mints five URLs for one object — fragmenting the CX-M23 equity that the cross-cut calls "the only channel that compounds without spend" across five pages, none of which accumulates. **The unit page is canonical; listings are events on it.** 13.03.07 D-04 already requires a public, no-account, per-serial surface — that surface is the natural canonical home, and it is where provenance (13.03.06) and lifecycle history co-render. Fourth arrival at D-04. |
| DT-09 | A sold listing is immutable in every respect | The clean reading of DT-01 | ❌ REJECTED, narrowly: **immutability covers seller-authored content** — price, condition, disclosure, description, media, fulfilment. It must **not** cover system-owned catalog binding. A listing published before its model existed in the catalog (13.01.04 D-01 permits publishing unmatched) that then sells, and whose model is contributed to the catalog a month later (13.01.02), must be bindable retroactively — otherwise the comp set permanently loses every sale that predated its own model's catalog entry, which is a systematic hole in exactly the long tail where the guide has least data and most value. Binding a catalog reference does not change what was sold. |
| DT-10 | A seller pauses a listing for reasons only they know | A pause is a manual act; the platform has no view into it | ❌ REJECTED — and this is where the consolidation thesis (D-18) pays out in a place nobody would look for it. WeJammin **knows** the unit is out on a rental (13.10.02) and **knows** it is on a confirmed show's backline manifest (18.09). No gear marketplace can know either, because no gear marketplace is also the rental system and the show production system. Rental → **auto-pause** (a claim on a unit in a hire van is unshippable; this is a fact, not a preference). Gig → **prompt, never auto** (the seller may intend to sell it after the show, or at it — an auto-pause would override a legitimate intent). The asymmetry matters: automate the physical impossibility, prompt the human judgement. |
| DT-11 | Ending a listing is always the seller's unilateral right | It's their gear and their listing | ❌ REJECTED — three exceptions, each from a different direction. (a) `reserved`/`sold`: ending would let a seller escape a claim for free; their exit is order cancellation, which costs them (CX-M08). (b) An auction with bids inside 12h of close: a bid is a binding commitment, and a seller who can walk at hour 11 makes bidding worthless — this is why eBay has the same rule. (c) A consigned listing (13.09.02 D-01): the consignee holds custody, not authority. The generalisation: **the right to end decays as other parties' commitments accumulate against the listing.** |
| DT-12 | Cancellations are the only lifecycle signal reputation needs | Cancellation is the visible harm | ❌ REJECTED on two counts. First: the honest-disclosure/seller's-remorse problem has no per-incident answer. A seller who finds a crack while packing is doing exactly what we want; a seller who "finds" a crack whenever a price disappoints is running a scam; **the two are indistinguishable at N=1 and trivially distinguishable at N=40.** The amendment *rate* is the signal, not its incidence — so amendments must be recorded even when they cost the seller nothing. Second: CX-M08 states reputation is per-role because "a superb mastering engineer may be an unreliable gear seller." The converse is the load-bearing half for D-19's multi-hyphenate: **a gear-sale cancellation must never contaminate the same human's Producer reputation.** One human, several roles, several ledgers — a lifecycle event scores exactly one of them. |
| DT-13 | Concurrent edits to one listing are a theoretical concern | Sellers edit one listing from one device | ❌ REJECTED: the personas rule it out. The Operator is defined as working "on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk" — while having a desk. The Musician "lists a pedal from their phone." Two-device editing is routine here, not exotic. And letting the later save win wholesale has a specific catastrophic case: the desktop tab, opened before a flaw was photographed, silently overwrites the flaw disclosure the phone just added — deleting the one thing 13.02.02 D-03 makes publish-blocking, producing an `active` listing that could never legally have been published. **The two edits must reconcile per field** and the seller must be shown a diff to choose between — never a silent wholesale overwrite. |
| DT-14 | The end-reason picker is a neutral admin control | It's a dropdown on an exit path | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **disintermediation surface**. If the reason carries free text and any of it reaches a buyer with a live offer, it is a channel to say "sold, but call me on 07…". Ending a listing is precisely the moment a seller has motive to take the deal off-platform — and the sub-domain already treats repeated `sold-elsewhere` ends as a disintermediation signal, which means we already believe the motive is real. **Closed picker; optional note; the note is never buyer-visible.** |
| DT-15 | The comp set is trustworthy because it records real settled money | Settlement is the strongest evidence available | ❌ REJECTED: settled money is **necessary but not sufficient**. Two accounts controlled by one human can settle a real payment at £8,000 for a guitar worth £4,000, wait out the inspection window, and mint a comp — then the "seller" relists at £7,500 against a guide their own wash trade authored. The fee is the entire cost of manufacturing market evidence. This is not hypothetical: the price guide is the domain's most valuable asset (13.04.01), and value is what attracts manipulation. **Comp admission needs a self-dealing exclusion** — linked-party detection routing through CX-M08's behavioural signals and domain 24. This file cannot own the detection, but it owns the gate, and it must not present the gate as safe when it isn't. |

Two specific handoffs this pass added: (a) the **reservation-expiry-vs-late-capture** arbitration — release is authoritative once committed, and a capture landing after it is voided and refunded, never oversold; (b) the reservation window length is **13.03.03's Q-03, not ours** — we depend on it and deliberately do not invent it.
- **13.03.04 Bulk Listing & Channel Sync** ([path](./13.03.04-bulk-listing-channel-sync.md)) — *trigger dependency.* Bulk issues lifecycle transitions at fleet scale; blocked rows skip-with-reason and never partially apply. Per DT-05, the **freshness challenge catches the off-channel sale sync structurally cannot see** — this is a mitigation for 13.03.03 DT-02's conceded failure, arriving from a feature nobody expected it from. 13.03.03 Q-02 (does CX-M08 distinguish a sync cancellation from a seller-fault one) is inherited here unchanged, not duplicated.
- **13.03.07 Stolen-Serial Screening** ([path](./13.03.07-stolen-serial-screening.md)) — *permission + externally-triggered transition.* `held` is its transition into our machine, and the checkout re-screen (13.03.07 D-02) can move `reserved → held`, voiding a live authorisation. Per 13.03.07 D-01, **watchers of a held listing are told nothing** — a hold notification is an accusation broadcast.
- **13.03.06 Provenance & Session History** ([path](./13.03.06-provenance-session-history-display.md)) — *shared entity.* Per DT-08 the **unit page is canonical**, which makes it the surface where provenance and lifecycle history co-render. This inherits CX-06's deferred question ("a valuable, historically significant, allegedly stolen instrument — which panel wins the page?"), and adds a third panel to it: lifecycle history.
- **13.03.01 Listing Creation** ([path](./13.03.01-listing-creation-media-demo.md)) — *handoff.* Owns `draft`; hands off at publish. Per 13.03 CX-R03 this is a handoff, not contention — we agree and do not contest it.
- **13.03.05 Parts, Bundles & B-Stock** ([path](./13.03.05-parts-bundles-bstock.md)) — *shared entity.* Ending a bundle must not end or strand constituents' individual listings. Coupling exists only at claim time (13.03 CX-05), not at end time.
- **13.04.01 Price Guide & Comps** ([path](../13.04-price-discovery-market-data/13.04.01-price-guide-comps.md)) — *trigger dependency, materially revised this pass.* Comp admission gates on **settlement, not `sold`** (DT-03); a post-settlement return **withdraws** the comp; and per DT-15 settled money is necessary but not sufficient — the set needs a **self-dealing exclusion** it does not currently specify. 13.04.01's D-01 ("report a distribution with its sample size") is strengthened by all three, since each removes a class of false evidence.
- **13.05.01 Offers & Counter-Offers** ([path](../13.05-offers-auctions-negotiation/13.05.01-offers-counter-offers.md)) — *permission + notification.* D-03 blocks price edits under a live offer; D-11 extends the same logic to condition. Ending a listing auto-declines live offers and notifies offerers — and per D-14, notifies them again with both prices if the unit relists within 30 days.
- **13.05.02 Auctions & Bidding** ([path](../13.05-offers-auctions-negotiation/13.05.02-auctions-bidding.md)) — *permission.* We own the block (`ended` unreachable with ≥1 bid inside 12h of close); 13.05.02 owns the semantics that justify it. The premise — that a bid binds the *seller*, not only the bidder — is Q-03 and must be answered jointly.
- **13.06.02 Order Management & Lifecycle** ([path](../13.06-cart-checkout-orders/13.06.02-order-management-lifecycle.md)) — *handoff + trigger dependency.* Hands off at `sold`. **Settlement is the gate for comp admission and for ownership.** Order cancellation is the only exit from `reserved`/`sold`. The amendment flow (D-08) pauses 13.06.02's dispatch clock and relies on its D-02 ("every stall has a bounded timeout with an automatic resolution") — our 72h auto-void is an instance of that rule.
- **13.06.05 Ownership Transfer** ([path](../13.06-cart-checkout-orders/)) — *trigger dependency.* Settlement writes the chain; a post-settlement return must unwind it. A pre-settlement collapse never wrote it, so the relist path is clean — this is why the settlement gate makes the return case tractable rather than corrupt.
- **13.08.01 Returns & Refunds** ([path](../13.08-returns-rma-warranty/13.08.01-returns-refunds.md)) — *trigger dependency.* A post-settlement return withdraws the comp, reverts ownership, and returns the unit for a relist with full inheritance.
- **13.09.02 Consignment & Custody** ([path](../13.09-tradein-consignment/13.09.02-consignment-sales-custody.md)) — *permission.* Per its D-01 (custody transfers, ownership does not), the consignee cannot end a consigned listing on their own initiative, and a consignor's withdrawal ends the listing **without scoring the consignee**.

Under D-18 this is consolidation producing a lifecycle capability that is structurally unavailable to any pure gear marketplace.

- **13.02 CX-02 synthesis Q5** ([path](../13.02-condition-originality-disclosure/13.02-condition-originality-disclosure-cx.md)) — *shared question, answered here.* Q5 asks what happens when a seller edits a disclosure between purchase and dispatch. **D-08 is offered as the answer**, and it is compatible with 13.02.04's seal-at-dispatch rule: the two seal points are different artifacts. The **snapshot freezes at `reserved`** and records what the unit was *sold against*; the **evidence pack seals at dispatch** and records what was *shipped*. An amendment writes to neither — it is a new object against the order that the buyer may refuse.

| D-06 | Time-on-market is a property of the **unit's continuous market presence**, not the listing. Resets only after ≥30 days off market | Per DT-06. Closes the relist-to-reset-age vector and the end-and-relist-higher bypass of D-03. `paused` pauses the clock; a collapsed sale does not reset it. |
| D-07 | The freshness challenge is one tap and **never bumps ranking** | Per DT-05. A ranking reward turns hygiene into bump-farming, kills the signal, and cannibalises CX-M25. |
| D-08 | A post-claim material change is an **amendment against the order**, not a listing edit | Per DT-04. The snapshot froze at `reserved`; the buyer bought it. | D-09 | An unanswered amendment **auto-voids at 72h**, favouring the buyer | Per 13.06.02 D-02, every stall needs a bounded automatic resolution. It resolves toward void because the buyer bought a thing that is no longer that thing — and because the Fan persona, least equipped to evaluate the diff, must be safe by default. 72h covers a weekend. |
| D-10 | An auction with ≥1 bid cannot be seller-ended inside 12h of close; earlier ends are permitted with a reason, notify all bidders, and count as a cancellation | Per DT-11. A seller who can walk at hour 11 makes bidding worthless. Premise (a bid binds the seller) is Q-03. |
| D-11 | A material condition downgrade auto-voids live offers | Per DT-14's principle. Symmetric with D-03: an offer is against a **stated listing**, and condition is part of the statement. |
| D-12 | Immutability covers **seller-authored content only**; system-owned catalog binding may resolve later | Per DT-09. Otherwise the comp set permanently loses every sale that predated its own model's catalog entry — precisely in the long tail where the guide is thinnest. |
| D-13 | Rental **auto-pauses** a sale listing; a gig manifest **prompts** | Per DT-10. Automate the physical impossibility (the unit is in a hire van); prompt the human judgement (they may mean to sell it after the show). |
| D-14 | Ending with live offers is permitted; the pattern is made **visible**, not prohibited | Per DT-06/D-06. Intent is unpoliceable; legibility is achievable. Offers auto-decline, offerers are notified, a relist within 30 days shows them both prices, and time-on-market does not reset. |

Derive it from the comp set once the set exists. Reversibility: high. |
| D-16 | End reasons are a **closed picker plus a private note**; no seller free-text reaches a buyer | Per DT-14. | D-17 | A registry ownership mismatch **warns and flags; it never auto-holds** | Registry coverage is partial and self-asserted at the edges (which is what 13.06.05 exists to fix). A false hold reproduces the 13.03.07 D-01 harm, and sub-domain D-01 says the flow never stalls. |
