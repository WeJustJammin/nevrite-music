# Feature: RMA & Repair Routing

> **Number**: 13.08.02
> **Parent**: [Returns, RMA & Warranty](./13.08-returns-rma-warranty-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Getting a broken thing to someone who can fix it, and tracking it while it's out of the owner's hands. Covers manufacturer RMA on new gear, dealer bench repair, and independent luthier/tech work on used gear that has no warranty. It matters here because repair is frequently the *right* remedy — a repaired original beats a refund and a destroyed instrument for everyone (13.07.04 DT-02) — and because a repair is a custody event that belongs in the instrument's permanent history.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sends an amp for repair; may also *be* the tech — the multi-hyphenate persona is both sides of this. Wants to know where their instrument is and when it's coming back. |
| Producer | Full | Routes outboard for service; the persona with the most gear needing periodic calibration rather than repair. |
| Operator | Config | Offers repair as a shop with a bench; runs an intake queue. Also the persona with a fleet needing scheduled maintenance. |
| Fan | Read-only (may request) | Requests a repair on gear they bought. No provider side. Needs "where is my guitar and when do I get it back", not an RMA workflow. |

## Behavior

An RMA is a **custody event on a specific unit**: the object leaves its owner, goes to a bench, and comes back changed. Routing depends on the regime — manufacturer (warranty, 13.08.03), dealer (in-house bench), or independent (domain 05's service providers). The work performed is a permanent fact about the instrument and belongs in the registry (domain 15), not only in an order.

### Happy Path

1. Owner reports a fault on gear they own (registered or purchased here)
2. System determines the regime: in warranty → manufacturer/dealer; out of warranty → repair options (domain 05)
3. RMA issued; custody transfers; the unit is tracked as out for repair
4. Work completed and recorded — what was done, by whom, when
5. Unit returns; the service record appends to its registry history

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Repair reveals a prior undisclosed repair | The tech finds a headstock splice under a refinish. It's now a *disclosure* fact about the unit | Feeds 13.02.03 originality; and if the unit was bought here recently, potentially 13.08.01 |
| Repair costs more than the item's worth | Common on used gear | Estimate before work; the guide (13.04) informs the decision |
| Item is damaged *at* the repairer | Custody was with a third party | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — liability during repair custody unresolved; same shape as 13.06.04 Q-02 |
| A repair *is* a modification (replacing a failed original part) | Materially affects value and originality (13.02.03) | The repair must record what was replaced — a repair and a mod are the same physical act with different intent |
| Manufacturer RMA on a used unit | Warranty likely didn't transfer | Routes to independent repair with the reason stated (13.08.03) |
| Owner isn't the buyer of record | Gear changes hands; the repair history shouldn't | Registry-keyed, not order-keyed — see DT-01 |
| Repair provider is on-platform (domain 05) vs. off-platform | Both are real; the registry should record either | Off-platform repairs need a manual record path or the history has holes |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An RMA is an order-scoped process — it attaches to the purchase | The retail model, and how the sweep implicitly framed it (bundled with returns and warranty under #14) | ❌ REJECTED: repairs are **unit-scoped, not order-scoped**, and they outlive every order. An instrument bought here in 2027 may be repaired in 2031 by an owner who bought it from someone who bought it from the original buyer. If repair history is keyed to an order, it dies with the first resale — and the whole point of a service history is that it accretes across owners. **Keyed to the unit (domain 15), it becomes exactly the Carfax pattern 15's index names as its strategic model.** This is the third feature to independently conclude that the unit, not the transaction, must be the identity anchor (with 13.03.02 DT-02 and 13.03 CX-05). |
| DT-02 | Repair routing is essentially a services-marketplace lookup with extra steps | Domain 05 has service providers; this looks like a filtered search over them | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED: the *provider matching and the job* are domain 05's, and this must not reimplement them. What is **not** 05's: the custody chain on an irreplaceable object, the regime determination (warranty vs. out of warranty vs. B-stock — 13.08.03), and the fact that the output is a **permanent modification to a registered unit's history**. Domain 05's model is scoped human output delivered to a client (ideation-index D-14's framing); it has no concept of taking physical custody of a $40k object and giving it back structurally altered. That residue is real and it lives here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Repair history is unit-scoped, keyed to the registry, never order-scoped | Per DT-01 — the third independent arrival at unit-as-identity-anchor. |
| D-02 | Provider matching and the job are domain 05's; custody, regime and the registry write are this domain's | Per DT-02 — clean seam, consistent with ideation-index D-14. |
| D-03 | A repair records what was replaced, not only that it was fixed | A repair and a modification are the same physical act; 13.02.03 needs the parts detail. |
| D-04 | A repair record is **first-party corroboration for the repair-quality severity split** (13.02.01 D-15) — pre-fill only, never auto-assert | Per owner ratification (DQ-12.O3). D-03's record already names what was replaced, by whom and when, which is what distinguishes a professionally-executed stable repair from an amateur one — and that distinction decides an Excellent-vs-Good grade ceiling. 13.02.02 D-07 governs the use: the record pre-fills the seller's severity option and surfaces contradictions; the seller signs and carries the liability. This file asserts nothing on a listing. |
