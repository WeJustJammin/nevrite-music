# Feature: Warranty Registration, Transfer & Claims

> **Number**: 13.08.03
> **Parent**: [Returns, RMA & Warranty](./13.08-returns-rma-warranty-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who will fix this for free, for how long, and does that survive a sale. Three regimes coexist here: **new** gear with a full manufacturer warranty, **B-stock** with reduced terms against factory-acknowledged defects, and **used** gear where the warranty usually died the moment the original buyer sold it. The last case is the one that matters most, because it is most of this domain — and the honest answer ("you have no warranty") is a fact buyers should know before they buy, not after something fails.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own gear) | Registers a warranty on new gear and forgets. Discovers on resale that it doesn't transfer, or on failure that they never registered it. The persona whose warranty card went in a bin in 2019. |
| Producer | Full (own) | Tracks warranty across a lot of expensive equipment; the persona for whom "is this still under warranty?" is a real, repeated question with real money attached. |
| Operator | Config (offer as a dealer) | Offers warranty terms as a shop — their own, the manufacturer's, or B-stock terms. Also holds fleet gear where warranty status affects maintenance decisions. |
| Fan | Read-only (own purchases) | Buys a used guitar assuming it has a warranty because everything else they buy does. **Needs to be told plainly, before purchase, that used gear generally has none** — the persona has no basis for knowing this. |

## Behavior

Warranty is a **property of the unit-plus-regime**, not of the order. It is derived from item condition (new / B-stock / used), the manufacturer's transfer policy, and the purchase date. Registration binds a serial to a start date; transfer (where permitted) moves it to a new owner at settlement; a claim routes through 13.08.02 to the honouring party.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer purchases new gear; warranty registers automatically from the order — serial, date, terms
2. Warranty status shows on the unit in the buyer's registry (domain 15)
3. Something fails inside the term
4. Claim routes to the manufacturer or dealer via 13.08.02
5. Repair is performed and recorded to the unit's permanent history

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Used gear: warranty didn't transfer** | The normal case, and buyers routinely assume otherwise | Stated on the listing **before purchase**: "No transferable warranty" — see DT-02 |
| Warranty transfers but only with proof of original purchase | The registry has exactly that, if the chain is intact (13.06.05) | The clearest everyday payoff of the ownership chain — see DT-01 |
| B-stock reduced terms | Third regime; neither new nor used | Terms stated on the listing (13.03.05) |
| Manufacturer refuses because the unit was modified | A mod voids most warranties; 13.02.03 records mods | Contradiction surfaced: the registry knows about the mod the claim depends on not existing |
| Manufacturer no longer exists | Common for boutique and vintage gear | Warranty is informational only; no honouring party |
| Grey-market import | Region-restricted warranties are widespread; a unit bought abroad may have none locally | Ties to 13.01.05 region attributes and 13.07.02 — same import, second consequence |
| Seller claims a transferable warranty that isn't | A price-raising claim with no proof | Claims need evidence, same principle as 13.03.06 D-01 |
| Registration never happened | Warranty may be unenforceable | Auto-registration from the order is the fix — the persona will not do it manually |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Warranty status check | Inline on the unit |
| Empty | No warranty (used gear) | **Explicit "no warranty"**, never a blank — silence reads as unknown, and unknown reads as maybe |
| Populated | In warranty | Terms, remaining period, honouring party |
| Error | Status underivable | "Unknown — check with the manufacturer", honestly |
| Partial | Warranty exists but transferability unclear | Stated as unclear, not assumed either way |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Warranty is order metadata — it attaches to the purchase | The retail model, and how the sweep framed it (#14, bundled with returns) | ❌ REJECTED, and the reasoning converges with 13.08.02 DT-01 and 13.03.02 DT-02: warranty is a property of the **unit**, and a transferable warranty is precisely a claim about the unit's *ownership chain* — "this instrument was bought new on this date by someone who sold it to me". That is the exact question 13.06.05's chain answers, and the fourth independent arrival at unit-as-identity-anchor in this domain. **A transferable warranty is a provenance claim wearing a commercial hat**, and WeJammin can substantiate it where a marketplace without a registry structurally cannot — a small, unglamorous, real expression of the D-18 thesis. |
| DT-02 | Warranty is a minor detail — most used-gear buyers know there's no warranty | True for the professional personas; the sweep gave it a third of one candidate | ⚠️ REJECTED for one persona, and that is the whole point. Musicians, Producers and Operators know. The **Fan** does not, because every other thing they buy has a warranty and nothing in their experience suggests a guitar wouldn't. Per D-11 fans are first-class users, and per the persona doc they have "no professional stake" and will not read terms. A Fan buying a £600 used guitar that fails in three weeks, having assumed it was covered, is a support case, a complaint, and a lost user — entirely preventable by one honest line before purchase. **This is a case where the correct product move is to prominently tell the buyer something that makes the purchase less attractive**, which is exactly the kind of thing a marketplace optimising for conversion will not do — and a platform whose thesis is trust must. |
