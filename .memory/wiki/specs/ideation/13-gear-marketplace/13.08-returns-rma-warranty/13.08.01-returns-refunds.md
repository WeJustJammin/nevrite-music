# Feature: Returns & Refunds

> **Number**: 13.08.01
> **Parent**: [Returns, RMA & Warranty](./13.08-returns-rma-warranty-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Sending it back and getting the money returned. Statutory in most markets the platform will operate in, and structurally harder here than in retail: the returning object is the same irreplaceable object, its condition on return is itself a contested claim, and return freight on a 60kg amp from another country can exceed what the amp is worth. A refund also has to unwind an ownership transfer (13.06.05) that has already been written.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | Returns something that isn't what was described. As a **private seller**, may not owe the statutory rights a dealer owes — which is the sub-domain's sharpest open question (Q-01). |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Returns outboard that doesn't perform as claimed. The persona whose "not as described" is usually functional rather than cosmetic — and hardest to evidence. |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Runs returns as a shop, at volume, with a real policy. Almost certainly carries full dealer obligations. |
| Fan | Full (as buyer) | **Statutory rights, not a product decision.** Distance-selling protections attach to this persona whatever the marketplace prefers. Needs a return path that requires no understanding of why they're entitled to it. |

## Behavior

Return reason drives everything: **not as described** adjudicates against the disclosure record (13.02.02) and evidence pack (13.02.04) and the seller pays; **change of mind** is a statutory right where it applies and the buyer usually pays return freight; **damaged in transit** is not a return at all and routes to 13.07.04 (D-01). A completed refund appends a compensating ownership event (13.06.05 D-03).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer receives the guitar; the neck has a repair that wasn't disclosed
2. Buyer opens a return citing "not as described", with photos
3. System assembles the pack: disclosure record, listing photos, dispatch evidence, arrival evidence
4. Return authorised; seller pays return freight; label issued
5. Item returns; refund releases from escrow; ownership reverses by compensating append

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Item returns in worse condition than it left** | The buyer played it, gigged it, or dropped it. The pack is the baseline both ways | Return condition assessed against dispatch evidence — the pack protects the seller here (13.02.02 DT-02) |
| Return freight exceeds the item's value | A £90 pedal from Japan with a £120 return | Real; sometimes the rational answer is a partial refund and the buyer keeps it — a remedy that needs to exist |
| Private seller vs. dealer obligations | Most regimes distinguish them; the marketplace may host both | Sub-domain Q-01 — **two return regimes on one platform** |
| Imported item returned | Duty reclaim is painful and slow (13.07.02 Q-03) | `[PENDING]` — who does the paperwork? |
| Buyer swaps parts before returning (returns a Strat with different pickups) | Endemic fraud in this category, and the reason originality disclosure (13.02.03) exists on both ends | Pack + serial + originality record; escalates to domain 24 |
| Change-of-mind on a bespoke or modified item | Usually exempt from statutory return rights | Exemptions stated at purchase, not at return |
| Ownership already transferred (13.06.05) | Refund must reverse it by appending, never by deleting | Registry shows the sale and its reversal — the honest history |
| Buyer returns after the inspection window | Statutory windows and platform windows differ and may not align | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A used-gear marketplace can operate a no-returns or all-sales-final policy | Common in private used-gear sales; the sweep's candidate (#14) is silent on it, and sellers would prefer it | ❌ REJECTED: not available as a choice in most of the platform's likely markets. Distance-selling regimes grant consumers a return right that a platform's terms cannot remove, and the Fan persona (D-11, first-class users) brings consumer status with them. But this surfaces something bigger the candidate list never confronted: **a marketplace hosting both private sellers and dealers is hosting two legal regimes**. A Musician selling one pedal is not a trader; an Operator's shop is. Same UI, same flow, materially different obligations — and getting it wrong exposes either the private seller to obligations they don't owe, or the platform to facilitating a dealer's evasion. **This is a live compliance question `meta/constraints.md` has flagged as `[PENDING]` and nothing has answered.** |
| DT-02 | The return's condition assessment is a seller-protection problem | The obvious framing: stop buyers abusing returns | ⚠️ REFRAMED: it is **symmetric**, and the evidence pack (13.02.04) is what makes it so. Without a pre-dispatch baseline, the seller claims the buyer damaged it and the buyer claims it arrived that way — one memory against another, which is the platform's founding failure (problem statement: "the record is always a reconstruction"). With the pack, both claims are checkable. So the pack — built for damage claims — turns out to be the load-bearing artifact for returns too. **That is the strongest argument yet that 13.02.04 (a Deep Think addition, absent from the sweep) is a `must`**: two separate sub-domains independently depend on it for their central adjudication. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Return reason drives remedy and payer | "Not as described", "change of mind" and "damaged in transit" are three different events with three different answers. |
| D-02 | Return condition is assessed against dispatch evidence | Per DT-02 — symmetric protection, and it only works because the pack exists. |
| D-03 | Partial refund with retention is a first-class remedy | When return freight exceeds the item's value, forcing a return destroys value for everyone. |
| D-04 | Statutory rights are honoured regardless of seller preference | Per DT-01 — not a policy choice. |
