# Feature: Returns & Refunds

> **Number**: 13.08.01
> **Parent**: [Returns, RMA & Warranty](./13.08-returns-rma-warranty-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Sending it back and getting the money returned. Statutory in most markets the platform will operate in, and structurally harder here than in retail: the returning object is the same irreplaceable object, its condition on return is itself a contested claim, and return freight on a 60kg amp from another country can exceed what the amp is worth. A refund also has to unwind an ownership transfer (13.06.05) that has already been written.

Three findings from this pass reshape what the feature is. **The return leg is the more dangerous leg** and the domain protects only the outbound one (DT-05). **"Full refund" is not a well-defined term here** — a gear order's money is seven components with seven different reversibilities (DT-12). And **the platform's inspection window and the statutory return window are different clocks owned by different parties**, which means a return can land after the seller has been paid — so a refund is not always an escrow reversal, and sometimes it is a debt (DT-03).

This file also takes ownership of the **remedy fork** the sub-domain CX file (CX-02, synthesis question 5) records as unowned, and which 13.07.04 Q-02 asks from the insurance side.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | **As buyer**: returns a guitar whose neck has a repair that wasn't disclosed. Knows a shipped guitar needs a setup, so is the persona *least* likely to open a return over settling (DT-08). **As seller**: a **private seller**, who in most regimes does not owe the statutory rights a dealer owes — the sub-domain's sharpest open question (Q-01) — and who is frequently the honest seller who genuinely did not know about the flaw, because they bought it that way (DT-07). Also the persona who ships a used guitar and gets it back having been gigged, which is a diminished-value deduction, not a refusal (D-02). |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | **As buyer**: returns outboard that doesn't perform as claimed. The persona whose "not as described" is functional rather than cosmetic — hum, a dead channel, a compressor that doesn't track — and therefore **the persona for whom 13.02.04 D-12 (the pack accepts audio) is load-bearing rather than nice-to-have**: a photograph of their complaint is a photograph of a knob. Also the persona most likely to prefer a **repair** over a refund (CX-02): a re-capped vintage unit is the thing they wanted; a refund is not. **The multi-hyphenate risk lives here** — the Producer selling the compressor is plausibly also the bench that would repair it, which makes their remedy offer an interested one (DT-06, 13.02.04 D-07). |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Runs returns as a shop, at volume, with a real policy. Almost certainly carries full dealer obligations (Q-01). Receives returns at a loading dock and must assess condition against dispatch evidence at volume, not per-unit in an office (13.02.04 Role Lens, CX-M32). **The only persona for whom a return rate is a conversion instrument rather than a cost** (DT-11) — a dealer's approval period is how gear has always been sold. Configures per-listing return terms; may not configure below the statutory floor for a consumer buyer (D-04). |
| Fan | Full (as buyer) | **Statutory rights, not a product decision.** Distance-selling protections attach to this persona whatever the marketplace prefers. Needs a return path that requires no understanding of why they're entitled to it. Two specific exposures nothing else in the domain covers: they read a neck that moved in transit as a defect (DT-08), and **they are the least qualified party in the transaction handling its most dangerous leg** — packing an £8,000 guitar for return freight, having never shipped an instrument (DT-05). No seller side. |

## Behavior

A return is defined by **three independent axes**. The breadth pass had one of them, which is why "full refund" appeared in it four times as though it meant something.

### Axis 1 — Reason: drives the remedy and the payer (D-01)

| Reason | Adjudicated against | Remedy | Return freight | Routes to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Not as described** | The disclosure version pinned at purchase (13.02.02 D-10) + the sealed evidence pack (13.02.04) | Return + refund, **or** an alternative the buyer may accept (Axis 4) | Seller | This file |
| **Change of mind** | Nothing — it is a right where it applies, and needs no cause | Return + refund, less any diminished-value deduction (D-02) | Buyer, **if** disclosed before purchase; otherwise seller | This file |
| **Damaged in transit** | The pack's dispatch half vs. arrival half + carrier scans | Insurance claim; possibly repair | Neither — the carrier's problem | **13.07.04** (D-01) |
| **Never arrived** | Carrier delivery scan or its absence | Non-delivery claim | N/A | **13.07.04** / CX-M14 |
| **Counterfeit / stolen-serial hit** | Serial + registry (13.03.07) | Refund; the object may not be returnable to the seller at all | Neither | **Domain 24** (Q-04) |

**The reason is not always agreed, and D-01 is circular when it isn't** (DT-10): the payer follows the reason, and the reason is exactly what's contested. Therefore **the freight decision is never a precondition for the object moving**. The label issues at authorisation; its cost is a refund component (Axis 3) settled at adjudication.

### Axis 2 — Timing: decides whether a refund is a reversal or a recovery (DT-03)

Two clocks run, and they are not the same clock:

| Clock | Owned by | Governs | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inspection window** | The platform (13.06.02 Q-01) | When escrow releases the seller's money | A product decision, sized against seller cash flow |
| **Statutory return window** | The legislature | Whether the buyer may unwind the contract | Fixed by law; **cannot be shortened by terms** (D-04) |

The inspection window will be shorter than the statutory window — because extending it to match would hold *every* seller's money for the full statutory period on *every* sale to protect against the minority that return, and seller cash flow is the Musician persona's stated pain. **So the seller is routinely paid while the return right is still live.** Two regimes follow:

- **Pre-settlement return** — escrow still holds the money. The refund is a reversal. Clean.
- **Post-settlement return** — the seller has been paid. The refund is a **recovery**, and it can fail. This is the case the breadth pass did not have, and it is not rare.

**Recovery ladder** (in order, each rung attempted before the next):
1. **Offset** against the seller's platform balance (CX-M01), immediately and in full where the balance covers it.
2. **Payout hold** — subsequent payouts to that seller are withheld and applied until covered. The seller is told the amount, the reason, and the order it relates to on the first held payout, never discovered by them from a missing deposit.
3. **Debt** — no balance, no forthcoming payouts. The buyer's refund is **not** gated on any of this (D-06); the shortfall is a platform loss position and its allocation is a financial decision (Q-05).

### Axis 3 — Component: the refund is component-wise (DT-12)

"Refund the order" is not an instruction. A landed gear order is up to seven components, each with its own reversibility:

| Component | Reversible? | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Item price | Yes, fully | Always refunds, less any diminished-value deduction (D-02) |
| Outbound freight | **No — consumed** | Refunds on *not as described* (the seller caused the wasted journey); does not refund on *change of mind* where lawfully disclosed |
| Return freight | N/A — an expense, not a payment | Settled to the payer determined by Axis 1, at adjudication |
| Transit insurance (13.07.04) | **No — consumed** | The cover was live for the outbound leg. Follows outbound freight's rule |
| Import duty | Reclaimable, **slowly, sometimes not at all** | See the re-export rule below. **The buyer's refund never waits on it** (D-06) |
| Import VAT | Reclaimable on evidenced re-export | Same |
| Platform fee | Ours to decide | A policy call, not a mechanic (Q-05) |
| POS service add-on (13.06.04) — e.g. a pre-dispatch setup | **No — delivered** | The work was performed. Refundable only if the work is itself the defect |

**Re-export and duty reclaim** — resolving the breadth pass's open duty question. Whoever *paid* the duty is who is entitled to reclaim it, so the answer falls out of 13.07.02 Q-02 rather than needing a new decision:
- **Under DDP** (the platform collected the landed cost at checkout — 13.07.02's recommended posture, D-01 there): the platform holds the reclaim. The buyer never learns import duty was a thing, in either direction. This is the honest continuation of "a quoted landed cost is a commitment".
- **Under DAP** (the buyer paid the courier at the door): the buyer holds the reclaim, because the buyer paid it. The platform's duty is then not to *do* the paperwork but to make the reclaim **possible and known** — generate the return/re-export documentation from the order it already has, and tell the buyer plainly that the reclaim exists, roughly what it's worth, and that it is theirs to make. A returns flow that silently drops a £560 duty payment on the floor is the same failure 13.07.02 DT-01 exists to prevent, arriving at the other end of the transaction.
- **In both branches**: reclaim timelines are measured in months and reclaims fail. The refund releases on condition assessment and **never on reclaim completion** (D-06).

### Axis 4 — The remedy fork (DT-06) — this file owns it

CX-02's synthesis question 5 records that nothing in the sub-domain owns the fork, and 13.07.04 Q-02 asks the same question from the insurance side. **It belongs here**, because every alternative is an alternative *to a right*, and the right lives in this file. You can only fork away from something you hold.

| Remedy | Who may propose | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Return + refund | Either | **The buyer** — it is the floor, not an option |
| Repair, then keep (13.08.02) | Seller offers; buyer may request | The buyer |
| Partial refund, buyer retains (D-03) | Either | The buyer |
| Setup / adjustment paid by the seller (DT-08) | Seller offers | The buyer |
| Replacement | **Nobody** — there is no second unit. This is what non-fungibility means | — |

Three rules make the fork safe:

1. **Accepting an alternative pauses the return right; it never extinguishes it** (D-07). A repair can run for weeks, past both windows. If the alternative fails — the repair doesn't fix it, the setup doesn't cure the buzz — the clock resumes with the time that was left on it. Otherwise choosing repair is a trap, which is CX-02's exact objection.
2. **The seller offers; the buyer chooses** (D-08). Alternatives are presented with the return right restated beside them, in the same visual weight. A seller steering a Fan away from a refund is pressure on the persona least able to recognise it (CX-02 role scoping).
3. **An interested offer is labelled as one** (13.02.04 D-07). A Producer offering to repair, at their own bench, the unit they sold is not offering a neutral remedy. The offer stands; it is not laundered.

**Escrow holds across the entire fork**, including a multi-week repair — which is precisely why Axis 2's recovery ladder exists: escrow will not always still be holding.

### Happy Path

The clean, pre-settlement, domestic case.

1. Buyer receives the guitar. Arrival capture seals into the pack (13.02.04 step 9). Playing it that evening, they find a repaired headstock under the finish that the disclosure marked `Absent`.
2. Buyer opens a return from the order, citing **not as described**, and adds two photos of the repair.
3. System assembles the pack automatically — the disclosure version pinned at purchase, listing photos, the seller's dispatch frames, carrier scans, the buyer's arrival capture. **The buyer builds no case**; they described a problem.
4. Reason triage runs. `Not as described` on a structural item is not a settling/setup signature (DT-08), so no adjustment is proposed. The seller is notified with the delta — their own `Cracks/repair: Absent` affirmation against the buyer's photo.
5. Seller does not contest. Return is authorised: seller pays freight, label issues, **valid 14 days**.
6. Return-dispatch prompt fires at label print — the same mechanism as the outbound leg (13.02.04 D-13), pointed at the buyer: *"Photos: the guitar, the guitar in its case, the case in the box."* Packing standards (13.07.01) and structural insurance (13.07.04 D-01) bind on this leg exactly as they did on the outbound one (D-09, DT-05).
7. Return-dispatch pack seals at label print + 15 minutes or carrier acceptance, whichever is first (13.02.04 D-03).
8. Item is delivered back. Seller confirms receipt and condition is assessed against the dispatch evidence — **the same baseline, read in the other direction** (D-02, DT-02). It matches. No deduction.
9. Refund releases from escrow, component-wise: item price + outbound freight (the seller caused the wasted journey). Return freight is charged to the seller.
10. Ownership reverses by **compensating append** (13.06.05 D-03) — never deletion. The registry shows the sale and its reversal, which is the honest history.
11. The buyer's evidence and the seller's own contradicted affirmation both persist (13.02.02 D-10, CX-M19). The listing may relist; the record of what was disclosed at dispatch does not change.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Item returns in worse condition than it left** | The buyer played it, gigged it, or dropped it. The pack is the baseline both ways (DT-02) and the legal expression of this is a **diminished-value deduction**, not a refusal — a consumer may handle goods as they would in a shop, and a shop does not let you gig with it | Assessed against dispatch evidence. Deduction itemised against the specific difference, with both frames shown side by side. **A return is never refused for condition** — refusal is a remedy the seller does not hold; deduction is |
| **Buyer returns after the inspection window but inside the statutory window** | The common case, not an edge case (DT-03). The seller has been paid. The return proceeds; the refund becomes a **recovery** (Axis 2) | Buyer: nothing different — the right doesn't know about our escrow design. Seller: "This order settled on the 3rd. The refund will be taken from your balance." |
| **Post-settlement return; seller has withdrawn the funds and has no balance** | Recovery ladder rung 2 → 3. Payouts held until covered; if no forthcoming payouts, a debt | Seller sees the amount, the reason and the order on the **first held payout** — never as an unexplained missing deposit. Buyer sees a refund on the normal timeline (D-06) |
| **Recovery fails entirely** — seller is gone, no balance, no activity | The buyer is refunded regardless (D-06). The shortfall is a platform loss position | Buyer: unaffected, and never told they were "lucky". Allocation of the loss → **Q-05** |
| **Return is destroyed in return transit** | The failure the domain does not currently protect against (DT-05). Seller has lost the object **and** faces a refund; buyer has no item. If the return leg carried structural insurance (D-09) this is an insurance claim; if it didn't, it is a hole | Routes to 13.07.04 with the **return-dispatch** pack as baseline. This is why D-09 exists — without it, the same £250 carrier liability cap 13.07.04 DT-01 rejects on the outbound leg silently applies to the return leg on a £8,000 guitar |
| **Return is lost in return transit** | Same structure, worse: no object to assess. Carrier acceptance scan proves the buyer shipped; delivery scan is absent | Buyer's refund releases on **proof of postage**, not on the seller's receipt — the buyer discharged their obligation at the carrier counter. The loss is the return leg's insurance problem, not the buyer's |
| **Buyer never ships the authorised return** | The label expires at 14 days. Nothing else happens — no penalty, no punishment | Return closes as `Not returned`; the order stands. Re-openable if the statutory window has time left on it. A right unexercised is not a right forfeited |
| **The reason is contested** — buyer says not-as-described, seller says change-of-mind | D-01 is circular here (DT-10), and the object cannot sit in a stranger's house while two people argue about who pays £40 of freight | **Freight is booked at authorisation regardless.** The payer is settled at adjudication as a refund component (Axis 3). Both parties are told the freight is provisional and follows the outcome. If unresolved on the evidence → domain 24 |
| **Fret buzz / high action / intonation off on arrival** | A wooden neck moves across a climate boundary. **This is physics, not a defect** — and it is a large share of gear returns (DT-08) | On a `not as described` return citing a settling signature, on a category the catalog (13.01.01) knows is climate-sensitive: *"Necks move in transit — this is normal and a setup usually fixes it. Want the seller to cover a setup near you, or return it anyway?"* **Offered, never gated** (domain D-03). The return proceeds on one tap |
| **Tube amp arrives with a microphonic or shattered tube; reverb tank rattles** | Well-known transit failure. This is **damaged in transit**, not not-as-described — a £15 valve is not a defective amp | Routes to 13.07.04 (D-01). Remedy is a valve, not an unwound sale. The distinction matters: routing it here would put the seller on the hook for a carrier's handling |
| **Change-of-mind on a hygiene-exempt category** — harmonica, mouthpiece, reed, IEM tips, earplugs | Statutorily exempt once unsealed, and in music these are **whole categories, not an edge case** (DT-04). Exemption is a **catalog category property** (13.01.01), never a seller claim | Stated at purchase: *"Once opened, this can't be returned for change of mind."* Not-as-described is unaffected — an exemption is not a shield against a defect |
| **Change-of-mind on a bespoke or built-to-order item** — custom shop, custom-wound pickups, a made-to-order boutique pedal | Same exemption, same mechanism: a catalog/listing-type property set at listing, not asserted at return (DT-04) | Exemption stated **at purchase, never at return**. A seller who discovers their exemption at return time has an argument; a buyer who is told at checkout has a decision |
| **Seller claims "bespoke" on a stock Strat to dodge a return** | Exactly why DT-04 makes exemption a catalog property. A seller cannot assert an exemption the category does not carry | Exemption unavailable; the return proceeds. Repeated attempts fan out to CX-M08 / domain 24 as a pattern, not an incident |
| **Return freight exceeds the item's value** — a £90 pedal from Japan with £120 return freight | Real. Forcing the return destroys value for everyone: buyer loses time, seller receives a pedal worth less than the freight paid to move it | **Partial refund with retention** (D-03) is offered as a first-class remedy, not a favour — but per D-08 the buyer chooses. If the buyer insists on the return, the return happens; the arithmetic is the seller's problem, not a veto |
| **Imported item returned — duty and import VAT** | Resolved: see Axis 3's re-export rule. The reclaim follows whoever paid (DDP → platform; DAP → buyer, with documentation generated and the reclaim named) | **The refund never waits on the reclaim** (D-06). Under DAP the buyer is told the reclaim exists, its rough value, and that it is theirs — silence here is the £560-at-the-door failure (13.07.02 DT-01) reflected |
| **Buyer swaps parts before returning** — returns a Strat with different pickups | Endemic fraud in this category, and the reason originality disclosure (13.02.03) exists on both ends | Pack + serial frame + originality record. Per 13.02.04 DT-06 the unit's **disclosed flaws are an identity signature** — a wear pattern is a fingerprint. Escalates to domain 24 with the frames attached |
| **Buyer returns an empty box, a brick, or a different unit** | The mirror of the outbound fraud 13.02.04 already defeats | Carrier **acceptance weight** on the return leg settles most of it (13.02.04 DT-06) — a 0.4 kg acceptance scan on a 4 kg amp is not an argument. Domain 24 with the weight record and the return-dispatch frames |
| **Ownership already transferred (13.06.05)** | The refund must reverse it by **appending**, never by deleting | Registry shows the sale and its reversal — the honest history. An append-only chain that can be rewritten is not a chain (13.06.05 D-03) |
| **Registry reversal write fails** | Symmetric with 13.06.05 D-04: a registry failure must not hold a buyer's money | Refund proceeds. The reversal is retried automatically for a bounded period, and flagged for follow-up if it still hasn't landed. The chain is briefly wrong and knows it; the buyer's money is never wrong |
| **Buyer accepts a repair; the repair fails** | Per D-07 the clock **paused**, it did not stop. The return right resumes with the time that was left on it | *"The repair didn't fix it. Your return option is still open — 6 days left."* This is the rule that makes the fork safe rather than a trap (CX-02) |
| **Buyer accepts a partial refund, then opens a return anyway** | A partial refund with retention is a **settlement of the claim**, unlike a repair which is an attempt at one. Accepting it closes the return for that reason | *"You accepted £120 and kept the pedal on the 4th — that settled this claim."* A **different, later** reason (a new fault) opens a new return if a window remains. Stated at acceptance, before the tap, not discovered after |
| **Buyer opens a return while an RMA/repair (13.08.02) has custody of the unit** | Mutually exclusive per incident — you cannot return an object that is at a bench (CX-02 synthesis 1) | The return is accepted and **queued**, not refused: it becomes the resumed right of D-07. The unit returns from the bench to its owner, then ships back. Refusing it would let a repair consume a statutory window |
| **Buyer opens both a return (this file) and a damage claim (13.07.04) for one incident** | One event, two remedies, and they'd double-pay | Deduplicated at open by cause, not by user choice: carrier scans and the arrival pack decide which path it is (D-01). The buyer describes a problem once; the platform picks the route and says which it picked and why |
| **Seller refuses the return, or won't provide a return address** | Not a veto they hold. Where the right applies, it does not need the seller's cooperation | Return authorises on the platform's own authority; the label issues to the seller's registered address. A seller who will not accept delivery has not defeated the refund — they've lost the object too |
| **Seller doesn't confirm receipt of the return** | Money plus a unique object in indefinite limbo is the worst available state (13.06.02 D-02) | **72h from the return's delivery scan** with no assessment → the return is accepted **as received**, refund releases in full, no deduction. The assessment is the seller's right, not the buyer's obligation to wait on |
| **Seller account is deleted, suspended, or banned between purchase and return** | The buyer's right does not evaporate with the counterparty's account. Mirrors 13.02.04 D-09 exactly — the pack survives de-identified | Return proceeds; refund runs the recovery ladder against whatever remains. The pack is retained de-identified for the claim window |
| **Bundle sale (13.03.05) — buyer returns one of five pedals** | One order, N units, and 13.06.05 fans out N transfers | Partial return is permitted where the bundle was **priced per unit**; where it was priced as a bundle, returning one constituent re-prices the remainder and that is a change to the contract, not a partial refund. Bundle-priced → all or nothing, stated at purchase. Ownership reverses only for the returned constituents |
| **Local pickup (13.11), cash off-platform** | No settlement event, no escrow, no order to refund. The domain's most common transaction shape has **no return mechanism at all** | Honest: the platform has nothing to reverse. This is one more cost of 13.11 DT-01's off-platform cash hole and domain Q-06. Where money *did* move through the platform, local pickup returns work normally |
| **Local pickup, platform-settled** | Works, with the handover as the custody boundary and the joint handover capture as the baseline (13.02.04) | Return is a second handover, or freight if the parties prefer. The baseline is what both parties confirmed at collection |
| **Currency moved between purchase and refund (FX)** | An international order priced in one currency, refunded weeks later at a different rate | The buyer is made whole in **the currency they paid, at the amount they paid** — not at today's rate. FX drift is a cost of running a cross-border marketplace, not a haircut on a consumer's refund (CX-M20) |
| **Buyer requests a return in a listing message rather than opening one** | The highest-volume real-world return request on any marketplace, and today it is prose in a chat log — 13.02.02 DT-12's finding, arriving in this file | Promotable in one tap: *"Open a return for this?"* An unpromoted message still counts as **notification** where the statutory clock turns on notifying the seller — the platform does not get to run out a consumer's clock because they used the wrong button (D-04) |
| **Return opened by a Band entity's member (domain 01)** | The buyer was the band, not the person who clicked buy | Authority follows CX-M09 delegated authority, not the purchasing individual. Any member with the entity's commerce authority may open, accept a remedy, or accept a partial refund. The refund lands with the entity |
| **Two Operator staff process the same return concurrently** | Letting the later action silently overwrite the earlier would attribute one person's offer to another, and could refund twice | One remedy decision holds at a time per return, each attributed to the person who made it (the shape 13.02.02 uses for concurrent disclosure edits). The second person sees the conflict and the decision already made. A return never pays out more than once, however many times issuance is attempted |
| **The multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once** — Producer sold the compressor and also offers to repair it at their own bench | Not exotic; routine for this persona. The offer is legitimate and possibly the best outcome for both — **and it is not neutral** | Offer stands, labelled *"repair offered by the seller"* (13.02.04 D-07). The platform does not refuse it and does not launder it. The buyer's return right sits beside it at equal weight (D-08) |
| **Seller and buyer are the same human** (two accounts, or selling to their own band entity) | The adversarial premise evaporates; the mechanism does not | Return processes normally. The registry's reversal is about the object and outlives both accounts (13.02.04 DT-09) |
| **Buyer is a minor (Fan)** | A contract with a minor is a different legal object, and 13.11 already puts a possibly-minor Fan in a room with a stranger and cash (domain Q-12) | The return right is, if anything, stronger. Nothing in this flow may condition a refund on capacity the buyer may not have had. Escalates to CX-M31 — and reinforces domain Q-12's request to extend CX-M31 to this domain |
| **A stolen-serial hit lands after purchase (13.03.07)** | The buyer bought stolen goods. Title may never have passed, so a "return to seller" may be legally wrong — the object may owe itself to a third party who is neither of them | Refund is not in doubt; **the destination of the object is** (Q-04). Escalates to domain 24 immediately, and lands on the same unresolved seam as 13.03.07 Q-02 and 13.06.05's edge case. **Do not route this like a return** — routing it here would have the platform ship stolen property back to the person who sold it |
| **Refund transaction fails at the payment provider** | Transient failures happen; a statutory obligation does not pause for one | Retried automatically for a bounded period, then handed to a support queue with a **stated deadline it cannot silently miss**. The obligation is dated from the qualifying event, not from our last attempt |
| **Return submission itself fails** | A statutory right cannot be lost to a technical failure on our side | Draft preserved on the buyer's device and on the platform; **the open request is timestamped at attempt, not at success**. Notification for clock purposes runs from the attempt. Resumable from any device |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Return being opened | "Pulling together what we already have" — the pack, the disclosure version, the carrier timeline assembling automatically. **The buyer is not building a case** |
| **Empty** | No returns on the account | Not shown. A returns list nobody has is a menu item advertising a problem |
| **Populated** | Return in progress | Timeline: reason, remedy, who pays, current position, what happens next and roughly when |
| **Error** | Submission or refund transaction fails | Preserved and timestamped at attempt (see edge cases). **A statutory right cannot be lost to a technical failure on our side** |
| **Triage** | Reason cited; signature check running | Only surfaces if it has something to say: a settling/setup signature (DT-08), a transit-damage signature routing to 13.07.04, or an exemption on the category |
| **Remedy offered** | Seller (or platform) proposes an alternative | The alternative **and the return right, side by side, equal weight** (D-08). Interested offers labelled (D-07 of 13.02.04) |
| **Paused** | Buyer accepted an alternative; repair or setup running | *"Return paused — 6 days left on it if this doesn't work."* The remaining clock is shown, not hidden (D-07) |
| **Authorised** | Return approved; label issued | Label + **packing instruction, not just a label** (DT-05). 14 days to ship. Freight payer shown, marked provisional if the reason is contested |
| **In return transit** | Carrier acceptance scan on the return leg | Tracking. **Refund eligibility now attaches to proof of postage**, not to the seller's receipt |
| **Partial — received, assessing** | Return delivered; seller assessing condition | "Received — checking condition against dispatch." **72h cap**, then accepted as-received |
| **Deduction proposed** | Assessment found a difference from dispatch | Both frames side by side, the specific difference named, the amount itemised. Contestable; never a refusal |
| **Recovering** | Post-settlement return; escrow no longer holds | **Buyer sees nothing different** — this is our problem, not theirs (D-06). Seller sees the offset or the held payout with its reason and its order |
| **Refunded** | Refund issued | Component-wise breakdown: what came back, what didn't, and why. Under DAP, the duty reclaim named as theirs to make |
| **Contested** | Reason or condition disputed on the evidence | Read-only exhibit view for both parties; domain 24 holds it. **Freight already moved** — the object is not hostage to the argument (DT-10) |
| **Closed — not returned** | 14-day label expiry, or buyer stood down | Order stands. Re-openable while a statutory window has time on it. No penalty language anywhere |

| DT-10 | D-01 ("the reason drives the remedy and the payer") is self-consistent and can be executed as written | It reads as a clean rule and it is the sub-domain's founding decision | ❌ REJECTED as written — **it is circular in the contested case**, which is the common case. The payer follows the reason; the reason is what the parties are arguing about. | DT-11 | Returns are a cost to minimise, and a low return rate is a healthy signal | Universal e-commerce framing, and the metric any dashboard would default to | ⚠️ REFRAMED for one half of the domain, and the split is the interesting part. **In music retail, the return *is* the fitting room.** You cannot know whether a guitar is *yours* until you have played it for a week in your own room, at your own volume, against your own hands — which is why the approval period is a century-old institution in this trade and why the serious dealers all offer one. For a dealer, a return is a **conversion instrument**: it is what makes a stranger spend £3,000 on an object they have never touched. | DT-12 | A refund is one payment reversal — refund the order | The breadth pass wrote "full refund" four times as though the phrase denoted something | ❌ REJECTED: **"full refund" is not a well-defined term in this domain.** A landed gear order is up to seven components and **each has its own reversibility, which is a property of the component and not of the return**: outbound freight is *consumed* (the journey happened); transit insurance is *consumed* (the cover was live); a pre-dispatch setup bought as a POS add-on (13.06.04) was *delivered* (the tech did the work); import duty is *reclaimable but slowly and sometimes not at all*; import VAT is reclaimable **only on evidenced re-export** — a document that does not exist unless the return flow generates it; the platform fee is *ours to decide*; only the item price refunds unconditionally, and even that is net of any diminished-value deduction. Reversing "the order" as one number is wrong in every direction at once: it either hands the buyer money for a journey that occurred, or quietly keeps money the buyer is owed. This also **resolves the duty open question without a new decision** — the reclaim follows whoever *paid*, which falls straight out of 13.07.02 Q-02's DDP/DAP fork, and the only genuinely new rule needed is that **the buyer's refund never waits on the reclaim**, because reclaim timelines are measured in months and reclaims fail. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Return reason drives remedy and payer — as an outcome, not as a sequence** | "Not as described", "change of mind" and "damaged in transit" are three different events with three different answers. **Amended by DT-10**: the rule is circular when the reason is contested, so it can never gate the object's movement. Freight is booked at authorisation; the payer settles at adjudication as a refund component. |
| D-02 | Return condition is assessed against dispatch evidence, and the remedy is a **diminished-value deduction, never a refusal** | Per DT-02 — symmetric protection, and it only works because the pack exists. Refusal is not a remedy the seller holds where a right applies; deduction is, and it is the legal expression of "you may handle it as you would in a shop, but you may not gig with it". |
| D-03 | Partial refund with retention is a first-class remedy | When return freight exceeds the item's value, forcing a return destroys value for everyone. **Bounded by D-08**: it is an *offer*, and the buyer may still insist on the return. |
| D-04 | Statutory rights are honoured regardless of seller preference, platform terms, or which button the user pressed | Per DT-01 — not a policy choice. Terms cannot shorten a statutory window; a return requested in a message still constitutes notification; a submission that fails on our side is timestamped at attempt. **The platform never runs out a consumer's clock on a technicality of its own construction.** |
| D-05 | **The inspection window and the statutory return window are separate clocks and are not aligned** | Per DT-03. Resolves Q-03. Aligning them means holding every seller's money for the full statutory period on every sale — punishing the Musician persona's stated pain to solve our accounting inconvenience. The consequence is accepted deliberately: a refund is a **reversal** pre-settlement and a **recovery** post-settlement. |
| D-06 | **The buyer's refund is never gated on the platform's recovery, on a duty reclaim, or on the seller's cooperation** | Per DT-03 and DT-12. Recovery failing is our loss position, not the buyer's problem. A customs reclaim takes months and may fail. A seller refusing to accept delivery has not defeated a refund. Inherits 13.02.04 D-06's shape — the mechanism changes weight, never eligibility. |
| D-07 | **Accepting an alternative remedy pauses the return right; it never extinguishes it** | Per DT-06. A repair can run for weeks and fail. If choosing repair burns the window, choosing repair is a trap — CX-02's exact objection. The clock resumes with the time that was left, and the remaining time is shown, not hidden. **Exception**: a partial refund with retention *settles* the claim rather than attempting it, and closes it — stated at acceptance, before the tap. |
| D-08 | **The seller offers; the buyer chooses. Replacement is not on the menu.** | Per DT-06. The remedy preference splits by persona (CX-02) — a Producer wants the repair, a Fan wants their money back — so it cannot be a platform default. Alternatives render beside the return right at equal weight. Interested offers are labelled (13.02.04 D-07), never refused and never laundered. There is no second unit; inheriting a replacement branch from generic commerce would be a category error. |
| D-09 | **The return leg inherits packing standards, structural insurance, and a dispatch evidence pack — and adds packing instruction** | Per DT-05. The domain protects only the outbound leg, and the return leg is packed by the least experienced party in the transaction. Costs nothing: 13.02.04 D-15 already scopes the pack to a custody boundary, and 13.07.04 Q-01's value threshold need only bind on both legs instead of one. |
| D-10 | Reversal is a compensating append and never blocks the refund | Ownership reverses by appending (13.06.05 D-03); a failed reversal is retried automatically while the money moves. Symmetric with 13.06.05 D-04 — the chain is briefly wrong and knows it; the buyer's money is never wrong. |
| D-11 | **Return exemptions are a catalog category property, never a seller claim, and are stated at purchase** | Per DT-04. Hygiene (harmonicas, mouthpieces, reeds, IEMs, earplugs) and bespoke (custom shop, built-to-order) are whole categories in music. Same logic as 13.07.02 D-03. A seller cannot assert an exemption the category does not carry; an exemption discovered at return time is an argument, one shown at checkout is a decision. |
| D-12 | **The refund is component-wise; each component's reversibility is a property of the component, not of the return** | Per DT-12. "Full refund" denotes nothing in a domain where an order is item + outbound freight + insurance + duty + import VAT + fee + a delivered service add-on. Reversing "the order" as one number is wrong in both directions at once. |
| D-13 | **A not-as-described return is a fact about the object, not a verdict on the seller** | Per DT-07. The private seller frequently did not know — they bought it that way. Only a contradiction between the seller's own evidence and their own affirmation is an integrity signal for CX-M08 / domain 24. Any other rule inverts 13.02.02 D-04's seller-armour framing and produces a platform of blank listings. |
| D-14 | **The platform adjudicates on the object, never on cross-domain inference about a user's intent** | Per DT-09. The gig calendar is not evidence. Generalises beyond this file: it is the first written refusal of the "but we could know" ideas a 24-domain platform will generate, and it is what keeps the lock-in earned rather than hostile (problem-statement Q-02). |
| D-15 | **Every stall in the return is bounded** | Inherits 13.06.02 D-02. Return label: 14 days, then closed-not-returned with no penalty. Seller's condition assessment: 72h from the return's delivery scan, then accepted as-received. Refund transaction: retried automatically for a bounded period, then a support queue with a stated deadline. Money plus a unique object in indefinite limbo is the worst state available. |
