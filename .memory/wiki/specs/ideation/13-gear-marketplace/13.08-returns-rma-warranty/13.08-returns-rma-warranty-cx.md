## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Warranty ↔ RMA & Repair

**Relationship**: Warranty status is the routing decision. In warranty → manufacturer or dealer, free to the owner. Out of warranty (most of this domain) → independent repair through domain 05, paid by the owner. B-stock → a third path with reduced terms. The regime determines the payer, the provider and the turnaround, so it has to be resolved before an RMA can be raised at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: discovers the regime at the worst moment — when something has failed. The persona whose warranty card went in a bin.
- **Producer**: tracks warranty deliberately across expensive equipment; the regime is a known quantity, not a surprise.
- **Operator**: configures warranty terms as a dealer *and* consumes them for fleet maintenance — both sides of the same mechanism.
- **Fan**: assumes a warranty exists (13.08.03 DT-02). The regime determination is where that assumption breaks, and it breaks after the failure rather than before.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both are keyed to the **unit** in domain 15, not to the order — 13.08.02 D-01 and 13.08.03 D-01 arrived there independently. The registry record is the shared entity; warranty writes status, RMA writes service history.
2. **Trigger chain**: Fault reported → warranty status resolved → regime chosen → RMA issued → custody transfers → work → registry append. If warranty status is *unknown* (13.08.03 States), the chain must still proceed — routing to paid repair with the ambiguity stated, never stalling.
3. **Permission intersection**: Operator is Config on both; the other three are Full or Read-only. A dealer configuring warranty terms and running the bench that honours them is one party on both sides — worth watching, and the same shape as 13.06.04 DT-01's seller-fulfilled-inspection problem.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Regime determination notifies the owner of what it will cost them — often the real news.
5. **State transition conflict**: A warranty expiring while a unit is in repair custody. Low frequency; the regime should pin at RMA issue, not re-evaluate mid-repair. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.

### CX-02: Returns ↔ RMA & Repair

**Relationship**: Two remedies for one problem, and the domain currently defaults to the wrong one. When a used amp arrives with a fault, a return unwinds everything — reverse freight on 30kg, duty reclaim if imported, refund, ownership reversal (13.06.05), and a seller left with an amp that still has the fault. A repair fixes the object and keeps the sale. On non-fungible goods where "send another" doesn't exist, repair is frequently better for both parties — and 13.07.04 Q-02 raises the same fork from the damage-claim side without either file owning it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: would often prefer £120 off and keep the guitar over shipping it back to Japan.
- **Producer**: on outboard, a repair by a competent tech is a *better* outcome than a refund — the unit was wanted for a reason.
- **Operator**: as a seller, strongly prefers repair-and-retain over an expensive return.
- **Fan**: wants their money back and does not want to manage a repair. **The remedy preference splits by persona**, which is why it cannot be a platform default.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both act on the same order and the same unit. They are mutually exclusive per incident — you cannot return an item that is at a bench. Sequencing must be explicit.
2. **Trigger chain**: Fault → remedy fork → return (13.08.01) **or** repair (13.08.02) **or** partial refund and retain (13.08.01 D-03). Escrow must hold across the fork; a repair path can run for weeks, well past the inspection window (13.06.02) and the statutory return window (13.08.01 Q-03). **If a repair fails, the return right must survive it** — otherwise choosing repair is a trap.
3. **Permission intersection**: The buyer chooses the remedy where statutory rights apply; the seller may only offer alternatives. A seller steering a Fan toward repair to avoid a return would be pressure on the persona least able to resist it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Remedy choice notifies seller, and possibly a repairer and an insurer.
5. **State transition conflict**: The unresolved one. **Nothing in the sub-domain owns the remedy fork** — 13.08.01 mentions partial refund, 13.08.02 mentions repair, 13.07.04 Q-02 asks the same question from the insurance side, and no file specifies who chooses, when, or what happens if the chosen path fails. Medium confidence because the interaction is certain but the design is absent. **Step 5 must give the fork an owner.**

### CX-03: RMA & Repair ↔ Warranty

**Relationship**: The reverse edge of CX-01, and it bites. A repair performed on a unit can void the warranty that authorised it — most manufacturer warranties are voided by modification, and 13.08.02 D-03 requires a repair to record *what was replaced*, which is precisely the evidence of modification. The platform therefore records the fact that voids the cover it is tracking.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: has a tech replace a failed original pot; discovers the warranty is now void and the instrument's originality (13.02.03) has changed too.
- **Producer**: re-capping a vintage unit is routine maintenance *and* a modification — the same act, two readings.
- **Operator**: as a dealer honouring warranties, sees claims on units their own bench modified.
- **Fan**: unlikely to reach this; would not understand it if they did.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. The interaction is real and documented in 13.08.03's Edge Cases, but its resolution is not. Step 5 should establish: does an authorised warranty repair record differently from an owner-commissioned modification, given they are the same physical act? The registry (domain 15) needs to distinguish "manufacturer replaced this under warranty" from "owner had this swapped" — the first preserves both warranty and originality standing, the second may destroy both. **That distinction is intent, not evidence**, which makes it hard to capture honestly and easy to game.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 13.08.01 Returns | 13.08.03 Warranty | Considered: a warranty failure could trigger a return. Rejected — they operate on different timescales and different causes. A return addresses "this isn't what I bought" within days; a warranty addresses "this broke" over years. Where they appear to overlap (a fault present on arrival), that is a **return** — the item was never as described — and warranty is irrelevant. Conflating them would let a seller deflect a return by pointing at a warranty. |
| R-02 | 13.08.02 RMA & Repair | 13.08.02 (self) — manufacturer RMA vs. independent repair | Considered splitting them as distinct capabilities. Rejected — same custody chain, same registry write, same tracking; only the payer and provider differ, which is the *regime* (owned by CX-01) rather than a second capability. Splitting would duplicate the custody model to express one variable. |
