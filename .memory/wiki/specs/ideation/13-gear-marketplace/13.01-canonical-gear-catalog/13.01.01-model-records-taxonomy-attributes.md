# Feature: Model Records, Taxonomy & Attribute Schemas

> **Number**: 13.01.01
> **Parent**: [Canonical Gear Catalog](./13.01-canonical-gear-catalog-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The canonical record for a gear **model** — who made it, what it is called, when it was made, what it is made of, and which category it sits in — plus the per-category attribute schema that defines what facets exist. A guitar has scale length, pickup configuration and body wood; a microphone has polar pattern and transducer type; a cymbal has diameter and alloy. One flat attribute table cannot serve all three, so the taxonomy carries the schema.

The record exists because **the model is the comp-set key**. 13.04 cannot price a thing it cannot identify, 13.03.07 cannot screen a serial it cannot attach to a scheme, and domain 15 cannot hold an ownership chain for an object with no identity. Everything in this domain that compounds is keyed on this record being right.

Two things make it harder than a product catalog. First, **the market's idea of "a model" is not the factory's**: Fender made Stratocasters continuously from 1954, but the market prices a December 1964 one and a February 1965 one at a multiple of each other, and nothing about the name changed. Second, **four of these attributes are legally consequential** — materials gate CITES export (13.07.03), category drives tariff classification (13.07.02), dimensions and weight decide freight class (13.07.01), and voltage decides whether a buyer destroys an amp (13.01.05). This is not a facet schema with some safety fields bolted on. It is a compliance schema that also does facets.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Lands on a model page while hunting or listing: spec table in their locale's units, the **spec-period timeline** (a 1965 Strat page tells them "units before ~Jan 1965 are pre-CBS — your serial or headstock decal resolves which you have"), variant pickers, active listings, price-guide link. Cannot edit the record here; if they believe it is wrong, the "something's wrong with this record" affordance routes them into 13.01.02, where they do have write access. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same page, weighted to outboard and mics where the **revision is the product**: an 1176 Rev A "bluestripe" and a Rev F are not an older and a newer 1176, they are different-sounding devices at different prices. Uses the attribute table as a purchase gate — channel count, transducer type, impedance, mains voltage decide whether the unit is usable at all, not merely nicer. Same contest route as Musician, and disproportionately the persona who fills the studio long tail no consumer dataset carries. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads model records to describe backline, PA and studio inventory accurately in venue/studio listings (domain 16) and rental rate cards (13.10). The attributes they care about are the ones nobody browses on: **weight and dimensions** (load-in, flight case, freight class per 13.07.01) and **mains voltage/market** (a fly date, or gear bought internationally). Batch-reads at fleet intake. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the model page as a browsable catalog entry when shopping for a first instrument: plain-language spec, no consequence-class badges, no facet-schema visibility, no contest affordance. The persona most exposed to a wrong safety attribute and least equipped to notice one — which is why safety attributes fail loud rather than silent (D-07). Has no catalog write path of any kind, consistent with 13.01.02 D-03: a Fan who thinks a *listing* is wrong files a listing report (domain 24), not a catalog contest. |

## Behavior

A model record is a **type, never a unit**. It carries no condition, no price, no owner, no serial. Listings (13.03) and registered instruments (domain 15) both point at it. Everything true of one physical object — condition, mods, refinish, provenance — belongs to 13.02 and domain 15 and must never leak into the catalog.

### The three-level identity model

The single most consequential behavior here is where the boundaries fall between the three levels. Getting this wrong is what produces either a catalog of 400 near-identical Stratocaster records or one Stratocaster record whose price guide is meaningless.

| Level | What it is | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model record** | The comp-set key. A distinct market. | The market prices it separately (D-03) | `Fender Stratocaster (1954–1965)` and `Fender Stratocaster (1965–1985, CBS)` are two records |
| **Variant** | An option *within* one model. Modifies price; does not create a market. | The market prices it as a modifier of the same comp set | Sunburst vs Blonde; left-handed; factory-relic'd |
| **Unit fact** | True of exactly one physical object. | It changed after the object left the factory | Refinished; recapped; rewired for 230V; fret wear |

### Variants come in two kinds, and the difference is behavioral

- **Choice variant** — offered concurrently; the original buyer *chose* it. Finish, colour, hardware, handedness. **Must always be asked**, because nothing derives it.
- **Spec period** — a time-bounded segment of the model's year range. Nobody chose it; it is a consequence of *when* the unit was made. Maple → rosewood fretboard (Fender, mid-1959). P-90 → PAF (Gibson, 1957). Brazilian → Indian rosewood (Martin, 1969). Japan → Taiwan (Boss DS-1, late 1980s). **Must be derived from the year, never asked as a choice** — a seller of a 1963 Strat must not be presented with a "maple or rosewood?" picker, because in 1963 there was no such choice.

**Country of manufacture is a spec-period attribute, not a model attribute** (DT-08). Some makers encode it in the model name ("American Professional II") and there it is *also* the model. Others do not: a Squier Standard Stratocaster was made in Japan, then Korea, then China and Indonesia, under one name, with a 3–5× price spread between the ends. A DS-1 is a $50 pedal or a $300 pedal depending on a country the name does not mention. This is the most under-modelled attribute in used gear and it is invisible until the price guide starts lying.

### Attributes carry a consequence class

Every attribute in a category's schema declares one of four classes, and the class — not the attribute — decides how it behaves when it is absent, wrong, or unverified (D-02):

| Class | Consumers | If unset | If asserted on an unmoderated model |
|---|---|---|---|
| `facet` | 13.04 comps, CX-M16 search | Excluded from value filters; counted as "not yet specified" | Trusted |
| `safety` | 13.01.05 voltage/market warnings | **Renders loud** — "voltage unverified" | May *raise* a warning; may never *suppress* one (D-07) |
| `compliance` | 13.07.03 CITES (materials), 13.07.02 tariff (category) | Treated as unknown, which 13.07.03 escalates rather than clears | May raise a restriction; may never clear one |
| `logistics` | 13.07.01 freight class (dimensions, weight) | Quote falls back to per-shipment/manual | Trusted (a wrong weight costs a re-quote, not an amp) |

Safety and compliance attributes are **priors, not authorities** (DT-04). The catalog knows models; a unit can falsify any model-level claim — a Japan-market amp can have been rewired, a 1969 D-35 can have Brazilian rosewood because the transition was not clean. Every attribute in those two classes is therefore declared unit-overridable, and unit-level disclosure (13.02.03) beats it, per 13.01.05 D-03.

### Attribute values are unit-bearing and normalised

`scale_length: 25.5 in` — typed and unit-bearing, which is what makes facets rangeable instead of string-matched. Two traps (DT-14):

- **Two measurement systems.** A buyer filtering "25–26 inch scale" must match a model whose spec was contributed in millimetres. Values normalise to a canonical unit on write and render in the viewer's locale. Gibson's own "24.75 inch" is quoted as both 24.75 in and 628 mm and has varied in fact — the plausible-range check must tolerate that without silently rounding it away.
- **Ordered dimensions.** A 14×5.5 snare and a 5.5×14 snare are the same drum; both orderings are in circulation. An ordered-dimension attribute declares its order in the schema, so a range facet on "diameter" never accidentally filters on depth.

### Happy Path

1. Musician searches "1965 Fender Stratocaster" — from a listing draft (13.01.04) or from catalog browse.
2. System resolves candidates and the user opens the model record. Page renders: maker **as of manufacture** (Fender Electric Instrument Co.), the single schema-bearing category path, year range, spec-period timeline, choice-variant pickers, the category's attribute table in the viewer's units, and links to active listings + the 13.04 price-guide entry.
3. The year 1965 straddles a spec-period boundary. The system **does not pick**: it surfaces the fork — "Units made before ~January 1965 are pre-CBS. A serial or a headstock decal resolves this. The difference is most of the price."
4. User enters the serial → 13.01.03 decodes → the spec period resolves to `1954–1965 (pre-CBS)` with the decode's confidence and its "decoded, not verified" label intact (13.01.03 D-01).
5. Page narrows to the resolved spec period; the price-guide entry narrows with it, and the comp set stops mixing two markets.
6. User selects the choice variant (Sunburst, right-handed).
7. The **(model + spec period + variant)** triple — not a model ID — is what a listing (13.03) or a registry entry (domain 15) binds to. That triple is the comp key, and it is snapshotted at bind time as a historical fact (D-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Model spans a spec change mid-year with no name change (resolves the sub-domain's open mid-year-spec-change boundary) | Spec period by default. Promoted to a distinct model record only when the market prices it separately — per D-03: ≥2× median sold-price divergence over ≥8 sales in 24 months, or a moderator assertion of a market-recognised boundary pending measurement | Spec-period timeline on the model page, not a second search result |
| A year straddles a spec-period boundary (1965 Strat; 1969 Martin D-35; 1959 maple/rosewood Strat) | **Never auto-resolved.** Serial decode (13.01.03) or seller assertion resolves it. This is the *intra*-model twin of 13.01.04 DT-02's reissue trap and must be excluded from auto-bind for the same reason | "Which is it? The serial can tell us — this decides most of the value" |
| Manufacturer reissues a model under the same name decades later | Distinct model record carrying a **typed, directional `reissue_of` + referenced era** — not a generic "related model". Matching (13.01.04) needs the *type* to know it must disambiguate; an untyped association hands it an unwinnable problem (DT-06) | Disambiguation picker: "1962 original" vs "American Vintage II '61 (2022–)" with the spec delta surfaced |
| A reissue of a reissue (a 1982 '57 reissue and a 2022 '57 reissue) | Both point `reissue_of` at the **original**, never at each other. Referenced era + own year range disambiguate. The 1982 one is now itself vintage and priced in its own right — a reissue is not permanently cheap | Three records, each with its own comps; the chain shown, not flattened |
| One model name, several countries of manufacture (Squier Standard Strat; Boss DS-1 Japan→Taiwan) | Country is a **spec-period attribute** (DT-08). Until it resolves, the comp set spans a 3–5× spread and the guide must not speak — hand to 13.04.03 (valuation confidence / thin market) rather than averaging two markets into a number that describes neither | "Made in Japan or Taiwan? This changes the value substantially" |
| Brand has since changed hands (Epiphone Kalamazoo→Japan→Korea→China; Moog→inMusic 2023) | **Make is a time-varying catalog entity, not a string** (DT-07). Record shows maker-at-manufacture; warranty (13.08.03) and MAP/authorisation (13.13) read the *current* brand owner. These are different questions and a string cannot answer both | "Epiphone (Kalamazoo, USA) — Epiphone is now a Gibson brand manufactured elsewhere" |
| Model plausibly belongs in two categories (Fender Bass VI; Line 6 HX Stomp; Fender Rhodes) | **Exactly one schema-bearing category** — deterministic schema resolution. Additional categories are **browse-only aliases with zero schema authority** (DT-05). A multi-parent schema lattice reproduces ideation-index D-14's ~40-nullable-column failure inside the catalog | Appears under both browse paths; one spec table, no conflicting attributes |
| Attribute exists for one category but is requested in another ("pickup config" on a snare drum) | Schema is per-category; the facet is simply absent | Facet not rendered |
| Attribute contributed in the wrong unit or dimension order (scale in mm; 5.5×14 vs 14×5.5) | Normalised to canonical unit on write; ordered-dimension attributes declare order in the schema; out-of-plausible-range values are held for moderation rather than stored (DT-14) | "25.5 mm? Did you mean 25.5 in / 648 mm?" |
| Safety-class attribute **unset** on a provisional (unmoderated) model | Renders **loud**, not silent. Unset voltage on a mains-powered category is "voltage unverified", never nothing (D-07) | "Mains voltage for this model hasn't been verified — check the unit before you plug it in" |
| Safety-class attribute **wrong** on a provisional model (contributor typed 230 V for a 100 V Japanese amp) | **This is CX-05's conflict, and it dissolves**: an unmoderated safety value may *raise* a warning but may never *suppress* one. Nothing is blocked; 13.01.02 D-01 and 13.01.05 D-01 both stand intact | Warning still shows, marked unverified — the wrong value cannot buy silence |
| Attribute added to a category after models exist (resolves CX-04's schema-backfill question) | `facet` class activates immediately (unset simply does not match). `safety`/`compliance` classes enter **draft** — fillable by moderators, non-rendering — and activate only at ≥80% coverage across models with ≥1 listing in the trailing 90 days (D-10). Otherwise adding `voltage` instantly paints "unverified" across an entire category | Facet appears with an honest count: "1,240 models match · 3,300 not yet specified" |
| Attribute deprecated/removed from a category | Values retained, facet hidden, comps that used it as a dimension recompute. **Never hard-deleted** — it is comp history, and a dropped value is silent corruption | Attribute vanishes from filters; historical comps still explain themselves |
| Model's category is reclassified (a Rhodes filed under `synth` moves to `electric-piano`) | Values that map, map. Values with no home in the new schema are **retained non-destructively** and surfaced to moderation. Never silently dropped | Moderator sees an orphaned-values diff; buyer sees the corrected spec table |
| Model merged while a listing is **mid-checkout** (resolves CX-03's in-flight-order question) | The bound triple is a historical fact. The in-flight order **snapshots make/model/spec-period/variant as displayed at placement**; a later merge, rename or split never mutates a placed order (D-08) | Nothing changes under them. The order record says what they bought, forever |
| Model merged while a **registry entry** (domain 15) is bound | Registry keeps the triple it bound; display resolves through the alias graph. A registry entry is an **ownership record** whose value is being stable and evidential (domain D-05) — a merge must never degrade it to "my '62/'99 Jazzmaster (merged)" (DT-12) | "Fender Jazzmaster (1962) — catalog record updated" — the instrument is unchanged |
| Bad merge of two genuinely distinct models | **13.04 is the detector** (DT-09): a model whose bound sold comps go bimodal is, with high probability, two models. Emits a split candidate into 13.01.02's queue. This is the only proposed mechanism anywhere in the sub-domain that *notices* silent catalog corruption | Nothing, until the split lands — which is the point: nobody is the injured party at the moment of injury |
| Someone opens a merged-away model's URL / bookmark | Resolves through the alias to the survivor with a note. **Never 404** — a dead catalog URL is a lost listing and a lost inbound link | "Merged into [survivor] — you're looking at the current record" |
| Two moderators edit one model record concurrently | Optimistic concurrency on the record version; the stale edit is **rejected with a diff**, never last-writer-wins. A curator's silently-discarded work is how you lose curators | "This record changed while you were editing — here's what changed" |
| Concurrent contributions of the same missing model (CX-01) | Duplicate provisionals; dedupe catches them at moderation; both listings re-point to the survivor without breaking | Both sellers keep their listings; one model survives |
| **The multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once**: a moderator with earned standing in "vintage guitars" edits a model their own live listing is bound to | **Model identity is a financial instrument** (DT-13). Year-range and spec-period edits change comp membership — this persona can move the price guide under their own listing. Action is recorded to CX-M19 with the conflict flagged, and excluded from their own auto-approval path | Moderator sees "you have an active listing bound to this record — this action will be logged and reviewed" |
| Malicious edit: widening a year range to fold a cheap model into an expensive comp set | Same detector as bad merges. Year-range edits are the **highest-risk edit in the catalog** because they silently change what a price means; on canonical models with active listings they require standing plus audit | Nothing visible — hence the detector, not the UI |
| Malicious input: 10,000-char model name; brand-stuffed name ("Strat copy — see also Gibson") | Model names are canonical identifiers, length-bounded; brand stuffing is a moderation reject and a domain-24 concern. A catalog name is not ad copy | Rejected at contribution with the reason stated |
| Factory-relic'd vs home-relic'd | Factory relic is a **model/variant** attribute — it left the factory that way. Home relic is a **unit modification** (13.02.03) that destroys value. Same photograph, opposite price sign | Variant reads "Relic (factory)"; a home-relic'd unit reads as a disclosed mod, not a variant |
| Left-handed | A choice variant — but a **hard filter**, not a preference: a lefty cannot use a righty. Must be filterable in its own right, and a unit claiming left-handed against a righty-only variant is a mismatch worth flagging | Left-handed as a first-class filter, not a finish-adjacent footnote |
| Gear sold new as a set (drum shell pack; matched mic pair with sequential serials) | The model is **the unit of manufacture as sold new** (D-12). Sold-new-as-a-kit → a model (or a "stereo pair" variant). Seller-assembled later → a **bundle** (13.03 CX-05), not a model. A matched pair prices above 2× a single; an assembled pair does not | Kit has a model page; assembled set is a bundle of model-bound items |
| Model is currently in production vs discontinued | An open-ended year range is meaningful: **MSRP/MAP (13.13) speaks for current models, comps (13.04) for discontinued**, both during the overlap. The year range decides which pricing authority owns the number | Current: "Street price £X · used comps £Y". Discontinued: comps only |
| Model has no listing and no registered unit, ever | **The catalog only grows where a real object needs it** (D-11). No standalone "add a model" destination exists — contribution is entry-pointed from listing (13.01.04) and registration only. A speculative model is unverifiable, unmaintained, and a spam surface | No bare "add model" CTA anywhere on the catalog |
| Catalog lookup fails | Listing creation must not be blocked — fall back to unmatched listing (13.01.04 D-01) | Error with retry; the listing flow continues |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Model page requested | Skeleton with a spec-table placeholder **sized from the category's schema** — the attribute count is known before the values are, so there is no layout shift when they land |
| Empty | Category exists, zero models in it | Honest statement plus the route to the flow that *needs* a model — "list one" / "register one" (D-11). **Not** a bare "add the first model" CTA: that would offer a write path the sub-domain Role Matrix gives nobody, and offer Fan an action they cannot take |
| Populated | Model resolved, spec period resolved | Spec table in the viewer's units, spec-period timeline, variant pickers, active listings, price-guide entry |
| Error | Catalog lookup fails | Error with retry; listing creation is never blocked — unmatched-listing fallback (13.01.04) |
| Partial — spec incomplete | Model canonical, some `facet` attributes unset | Spec table with a "spec incomplete — help fill it in" affordance on blank rows, routing to 13.01.02 |
| Partial — spec period unresolved | Year straddles a boundary, no serial supplied | Both periods shown side by side with the spec delta and the price consequence stated; nothing auto-picked. The price guide defers to 13.04.03 rather than averaging two markets |
| Partial — provisional model | Contributed, not yet moderated | Provisional badge. `facet` values render normally; `safety`/`compliance` values render as unverified and cannot suppress a warning (D-07) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A single flat attribute table can serve all gear categories | Naive schema instinct; the same instinct ideation-index D-14 rejected for the merged `listing` entity | ❌ REJECTED: guitars, mics, cymbals and rack gear share almost no attributes. A flat table reproduces the ~40-nullable-column failure ideation-index D-14 named. Attribute schema must be per-category. |
| DT-02 | Catalog correctness is high blast radius, not just a data-quality nicety | Reasoning about downstream consumers | ✅ CONFIRMED: model identity feeds comps (13.04), alerts, facets, and the registry's unit identity (domain 15). A wrong merge of two similar models silently poisons every price the guide quotes. Moderation (13.01.02) is therefore a **must**, not a **could**. |
| DT-03 | The attribute schema is a facet schema | Follows from DT-01 — "the taxonomy carries the schema" reads like a search concern | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the finding that reframes the feature. Auditing the actual consumers: 13.07.03 reads **materials** to gate CITES export; 13.07.02 reads **category** for tariff classification (duty depends on what the object legally *is*); 13.07.01 reads **dimensions + weight** for freight class; 13.01.05 reads **voltage/market** for a warning that prevents a destroyed amp. Four attributes carry legal or safety consequence. They cannot be optional the way "body wood" is optional, and they cannot carry the same trust as "pickup config" when a stranger contributes them. **This is a compliance schema that also does facets** — hence the consequence class (D-02), which is the mechanism the rest of this file hangs off. |
| DT-04 | A catalog attribute is an authority about the object | It is canonical, curated data, so it looks authoritative | ❌ REJECTED, generalising 13.01.05 DT-02. That file found the instance (a *unit* may have been rewired, so a model-derived voltage warning is not authoritative). The general rule nobody wrote: **every consequence-class attribute is a model-level prior that a unit can falsify.** A 1969 Martin D-35 "has" Indian rosewood — except the transition was not clean and some 1969 units are Brazilian, which is the difference between a shipment and a CITES seizure. The catalog is a prior, not an oracle. Every `safety` and `compliance` attribute is therefore declared unit-overridable in the schema itself, so 13.02.03 has something defined to override. |
| DT-05 | The taxonomy is a tree | Every product catalog is | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT — and the constraint is the whole point. A Fender Bass VI is a guitar and a bass. A Line 6 HX Stomp is a multi-effects pedal and an audio interface. A Fender Rhodes is a keyboard and an electric piano. The market genuinely browses this as a graph. But **multi-parent schema inheritance is ideation-index D-14's failure recurring inside my own feature**: two parents, two schemas, conflicting attributes, nullable soup. Resolution: **exactly one schema-bearing category per model** (deterministic, single source of attributes) **plus browse-only category aliases carrying zero schema authority.** Tree for schema, graph for browse. |
| DT-06 | The reissue problem is a matching problem | 13.01.04 DT-02 frames it that way — "reissues must be excluded from auto-bind" | ❌ REJECTED as a complete account: it is a **taxonomy** problem that 13.01.04 inherits. A reissue is a distinct model *whose identity is a reference to another model* — "American Vintage II 1961 Stratocaster" was made in 2022 and its name contains 1961. If the record carries only a year range and a generic "related model" link, then a text search for "1961 Stratocaster" hits the reissue's own name and matching is handed an unwinnable problem by the data model. The relation must be **typed and directional** (`reissue_of` + referenced era) so matching can *know* it is in the trap rather than infer it. Consequence: reissues of reissues (a 1982 '57RI and a 2022 '57RI) both point at the original, never at each other — and the 1982 one is now vintage in its own right. A reissue is not permanently cheap. |
| DT-07 | Manufacturer is a string on the model record | It is a name; names are strings | ❌ REJECTED: brands transfer, and the market prices the transfer. Gibson bought Epiphone in 1957 and built Epiphones in Kalamazoo until ~1970 — a pre-1970 Kalamazoo Casino and a later import Casino are different instruments at different prices with one name. Moog went to inMusic in 2023. Fender, Yamaha and Behringer each hold stables of transferred brands. Three different questions fall out and a string answers none of them: **who made it then** (comps, provenance), **who owns the brand now** (13.13 authorisation/MAP), **who honours the warranty** (13.08.03). Make is a catalog entity with time-varying ownership — brands have provenance too, which is quietly the same shape as domain 15's ownership chain. |
| DT-08 | Country of manufacture is a model attribute | It is in the original overview of this file, listed flat alongside make and year | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **spec-period** attribute, and this is the most under-modelled fact in used gear. Where a maker encodes it in the name ("American Professional II") it is also the model. Where they do not, one name spans continents: a Squier Standard Stratocaster is Japanese, Korean, Chinese or Indonesian depending only on when, at a 3–5× spread; a Boss DS-1 is a $50 pedal or a $300 pedal on the same evidence, with no serial to decode. The failure is silent: the price guide averages two markets and produces a number that describes neither. Falls out cleanly from the spec-period mechanism (D-03), which is corroboration that the mechanism is the right one. |
| DT-09 | Nobody can ever detect catalog corruption, because nobody is the injured party at the moment of injury | DT-02 states the blast radius and then stops. Auditing the sub-domain: every other domain has a dispute path; the catalog has none | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it has a fix nobody proposed. **The price guide is the catalog's error detector.** A model whose bound sold comps go bimodal is, with high probability, two models — price bimodality is precisely the signal that a spec period is missing or a merge was wrong. 13.04 emits a split candidate into 13.01.02's moderation queue. This inverts the assumed dependency (catalog → comps) into a loop, and it is the only mechanism in the sub-domain that *notices*. It also makes D-03's separability test measurable rather than a matter of taste. |
| DT-10 | Contributors should be able to extend a category's attribute schema | Symmetry with contribution: if they can add models, why not facets? Wikipedia/Discogs precedent argues for it | ❌ REJECTED: an attribute is a **comp-set dimension** and a **facet**, not a note. Open attribute creation yields ten near-duplicate spellings of "pickup config", a facet rail nobody can read, and comps that fragment across synonyms — corruption dressed as enthusiasm. Contribution (13.01.02) adds **models** and fills **values**; only moderation adds **attributes**. This boundary is not stated in 13.01.02 and needs to be. |
| DT-11 | The cold-catalog empty state should offer "Add the first model" | It is what the [SURFACE] draft of this file said | ❌ REJECTED — and it was a live self-contradiction in my own file. The sub-domain Role Matrix gives **all four personas Read-only on 13.01.01**, and 13.01.02's Behavior says contribution is inline in the listing flow and **"never a separate destination"**. So the draft offered a CTA nobody has the access to take, and offered it hardest to Fan, who has None. The correct behavior is the stronger principle: **the catalog only grows where a real object needs it** (D-11) — the empty state routes to listing or registration, the flows that need a model to exist. A model with no listing and no unit is unverifiable, unmaintained, and a spam surface. |
| DT-12 | A model merge is safe as long as listings re-point cleanly | 13.01.02's Edge Cases reason entirely in terms of listings | ❌ REJECTED — it ignores the higher-stakes consumer. A merge re-points listings *and* registry entries, and a listing is ephemeral while **a registry entry is an ownership record whose entire value is being stable and evidential** (domain D-05: settlement writes the ownership chain). Silently re-pointing a registered instrument degrades the evidence; an unmerge could orphan it. Resolution is the pattern this whole platform runs on and that 13.01.03 D-02 already applies to decoded-vs-asserted fields: **record the fact at the moment it was true.** The binding is a historical fact; display resolves through the alias graph; a placed order snapshots identity at placement and is never mutated by a later merge. Catalog housekeeping must not be able to rewrite what someone owns or what someone bought — which is D-18 applied to the catalog's own maintenance. |
| DT-13 | Model records are read-only for everyone, so there is no role-collision surface here | The Role Matrix says Read-only four times; it looks closed | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the multi-hyphenate landing in the catalog exactly as personas.md warns. Moderator standing is **earned and category-scoped** (13.01.02 D-02) — which means the person with standing in "vintage guitars" is, definitionally, someone who deals in vintage guitars. **They can merge, split or re-range models that their own live listings are bound to, and those edits decide their own comp set.** Model identity is a financial instrument. Year-range edits are the sharpest form: they silently change comp membership. The very mechanism that makes moderation competent (expertise is proven by trading) is what makes it conflicted. Mitigation is the minimum that does not break the earned-standing model: conflicts flagged and logged to CX-M19, self-approval excluded. Whether that is sufficient is a values call (Q-04). |
| DT-14 | Unit-bearing attributes are enough to make facets rangeable | The [SURFACE] draft asserts it and moves on | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: unit-bearing is necessary and not sufficient. Two failures survive it. **Measurement systems**: a buyer filtering "25–26 in scale" must match a model contributed in mm, so values normalise to a canonical unit on write and render in the viewer's locale — and Gibson's "24.75 in" has genuinely varied and is quoted as both 24.75 in and 628 mm, so the plausible-range check must not round the truth away. **Ordered dimensions**: 14×5.5 and 5.5×14 are the same snare, both orderings circulate, and a naive range facet on "first dimension" silently mixes diameter with depth. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attribute schema is per-category, not global | Per DT-01 — the same reasoning ideation-index D-14 used to keep 13/14/05 apart applies within the catalog. |
| D-02 | Every attribute declares a **consequence class**: `facet` \| `safety` \| `compliance` \| `logistics` | Per DT-03. Four attributes carry legal or safety weight (materials→CITES, category→tariff, dims+weight→freight, voltage→harm). The class — not the attribute — decides required-ness, trust and failure behaviour. Without this the schema treats "body wood" and "mains voltage" identically, and one of them destroys an amp. |
| D-03 | **The model/variant boundary is market pricing separability, not factory spec change.** A spec change is a **spec period** by default; promoted to a distinct model record only when the market prices it separately | Resolves Q-01. Factories change specs constantly; the market only prices some of those changes. Separability aligns the boundary with the record's actual job (ideation-index D-14: the model is the comp-set key). **Test**: ≥2× median sold-price divergence between adjacent periods over ≥8 sales in a trailing 24 months — launch defaults; 13.04.03 owns sample sufficiency. **Cold-start fallback**: a moderator asserts a market-recognised boundary (pre-CBS, PAF-era) with evidence, pending measurement — the test cannot run before comps exist, and pretending otherwise would block the catalog on the data the catalog produces. |
| D-04 | **Spec periods are derived from the year; choice variants are asked.** Never present a spec period as a picker | Nobody chose rosewood in 1963 — that is what Fender was making. Asking is both wrong and a data-quality trap: a seller confronted with a choice they never had will guess. Finish and handedness must always be asked; nothing derives them. |
| D-05 | **Exactly one schema-bearing category per model**; additional categories are browse-only aliases with zero schema authority | Per DT-05. Tree for schema (deterministic attribute resolution), graph for browse (the market really does look for a Bass VI in both places). Multi-parent inheritance is ideation-index D-14's nullable-column failure recurring inside the catalog. |
| D-06 | `reissue_of` is a **typed, directional** relation carrying a referenced era — not a generic "related model" | Per DT-06. Matching (13.01.04) needs the type to know it is in the reissue trap. Reissues of reissues point at the original, never at each other. |
| D-07 | An **unmoderated `safety`/`compliance` value may raise a warning but may never suppress one** | Resolves the CX-05 conflict the CX file flagged as needing the owner. The conflict only exists if "moderate the attribute" means "block the listing" — it does not. Nothing is blocked, 13.01.02 D-01 (provisional-publish, never blocking) stands, 13.01.05 D-01 (warnings inform, never block) stands, and a stranger's wrong 230 V cannot buy silence on a 100 V amp. Asymmetric trust, not a gate. |
| D-08 | **A binding is a historical fact; display is a resolution.** Placed orders and registry entries snapshot identity at bind time and are never mutated by a later merge, split or rename | Per DT-12. The pattern the platform runs on (D-18; 13.01.03 D-02 applies it to decoded-vs-asserted fields). Catalog housekeeping must not rewrite what someone owns or what someone bought. A merged-away identifier resolves through the alias graph and never 404s. |
| D-09 | Contributors add **models** and fill **values**; only moderators add **attributes** | Per DT-10. An attribute is a comp dimension and a facet, not a note. Open attribute creation fragments comps across synonyms and makes the facet rail unreadable. |
| D-10 | Schema additions in `safety`/`compliance` class enter **draft** (fillable, non-rendering) and activate at **≥80% coverage across models with ≥1 listing in the trailing 90 days**; `facet` class activates immediately. Backfill never overwrites an asserted value | Resolves CX-04's schema-backfill question. Activating `voltage` on day one paints "unverified" across an entire category — correct but a mass perceived regression. Coverage is measured over *actively-listed* models so the never-listed long tail cannot block activation forever. |
| D-11 | **The catalog only grows where a real object needs it.** No standalone "add a model" destination | Per DT-11. Consistent with 13.01.02 ("never a separate destination") and with the sub-domain Role Matrix, which the [SURFACE] empty state contradicted. A model with no listing and no registered unit is unverifiable, unmaintained, and a spam surface. |
| D-12 | The model is **the unit of manufacture as sold new**. Sets sold new as sets are models (or variants); seller-assembled sets are **bundles** (13.03) | Drum shell packs and sequential-serial matched mic pairs left the factory as one product and price as one — above 2× a single. An assembled pair did not and does not. |
| D-13 | **Make is a time-varying catalog entity**, not a string. The record shows maker-at-manufacture; 13.13 and 13.08.03 read the current brand owner | Per DT-07. "Who made it then", "who owns the brand now" and "who honours the warranty" are three questions with three answers and one name. |
| D-14 | **The per-category schema is one shared mechanism — attribute schemas, flaw checklists and component sets — and it also authors the flaw→grade `(axis, severity-token)` projection on each severity option. Catalog moderation holds the standing over it** | Owner ratification (DQ-12.O2), confirming what the Cross-Cut Notes flagged for Step 6 and resolving 13.02.03 Q-02 in the affirmative. The projection is a property of an **authored severity option on the checklist**, not a model attribute value, so it sits beside the materiality verdict the same author already sets (13.02.02 D-06) rather than in the consequence-class table. Moderation holds it because it **caps what every seller in the category may claim** (13.02.01's ceiling table reads it) — a real authority grant, consistent with D-09 (contributors add models and fill values; only moderation authors schema) and with the platform rule that configuration permits taxonomy selection and proposals but never vocabulary curation. **This commissions the field as new work**: the schema does not carry it today. |
