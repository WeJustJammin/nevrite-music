# Feature: Fitment, Compatibility & Region/Voltage Warnings

> **Number**: 13.01.05
> **Parent**: [Canonical Gear Catalog](./13.01-canonical-gear-catalog-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Catalog-level relationships that answer "will this actually work for me?" — does this pickup fit
that route, does this case fit that guitar, does this 9V centre-negative supply match that pedal,
and will this 100V Japanese amp survive being plugged into 230V. It reads model attributes plus
the buyer's context and warns before money moves. The two halves have asymmetric stakes and that
asymmetry is the spine of the feature: a wrong **fitment** claim costs a return; a wrong (or
unshown) **voltage** claim costs a destroyed amp and, at worst, a fire. So fitment fails silent and
voltage fails loud (D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees "this is a 100V Japan-market unit — you're in the UK (230V), it needs a step-down transformer" on the listing and again at checkout. Sees whether a part fits an instrument they own — resolved against their **registered rig's (model + spec period + variant) triples** in domain 15, not against a bare model, because fitment can differ by spec period. Cannot edit the relationship; a wrong fitment claim routes into 13.01.02 contribution, where they have write access. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same warnings, weighted to rack gear and outboard where region variants are rife and mains voltage is **not** user-switchable on older units — the exact class where the warning is load-bearing rather than informational. Uses fitment for rack-unit form factor and PSU/current matching. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads fitment when buying spares/consumables for a backline fleet (does this valve/speaker/case fit the fleet's models), and voltage warnings when sourcing gear internationally for a venue or studio — a fly-date or an eBay-Japan purchase is exactly where the destroyed-amp risk lives. |
| Fan | Read-only | The persona most likely to be harmed by an unwarned voltage mismatch and least likely to know it is a question. The warning must be plain-language and locale-rendered, never spec-shorthand: "this won't work with UK power without a converter — buying anyway?" not "100V/50Hz". Has no write path of any kind. |

> No persona is `None` here: every buyer, whatever their role, is subject to the same
> destination-derived warning — a safety warning that some personas do not see would be the
> failure this feature exists to prevent.

## Behavior

Fitment and compatibility are **relationships between catalog models** (part↔instrument,
case↔instrument, PSU↔pedal), not attributes of a listing. The relationship is expressed at the
granularity the market actually fits at: usually model, but where a spec period changes a physical
interface (a route, a mount, a rail spacing) the relationship is keyed to the **(model + spec
period)** pair from 13.01.01, not the bare model — otherwise a part "fits a Stratocaster" while not
fitting the 1962 one the buyer owns.

Region/voltage warnings are **derived**, not stored on the listing: the model's `safety`-class
market/voltage attribute (13.01.01 D-02) × the buyer's shipping destination → a warning severity.
Warnings surface at listing view and again at checkout; they **inform, they do not block** (D-01).
The buyer may legitimately want the 100V unit — they own a transformer, or they collect.

Because the catalog knows **models, not units** (13.01.01: "a model record is a type, never a
unit"), every voltage warning is a model-level *prior* that a unit can falsify. A unit that has been
rewired for another region is a **unit fact** owned by 13.02.03, and unit-level disclosure overrides
the model-derived warning — but overriding means *re-labelling*, never *silencing* (see DT-02, DT-03).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer in the UK views a listing for a Japan-market amp.
2. System reads the model's `safety`-class voltage/market attribute and compares it against the
   buyer's shipping destination (from CX-M13).
3. Listing shows a plain-language warning with severity and the remedy — what would be required to
   use it safely (a step-down transformer), rendered in the buyer's locale.
4. Warning repeats at checkout as an **acknowledgement**, not a blocker.
5. Buyer proceeds knowingly (acknowledgement recorded per Q-03), or filters it out.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Model has a user-switchable voltage selector | No hard warning, but **switchable ≠ switched** — the catalog cannot know the tap position, which is a unit fact | "Voltage-switchable — safe for your region once the selector is set to 230V. Confirm before powering on." |
| Unit was modified for another region (a Japan amp rewired for 230V) | **Catalog cannot know this** — it is a unit fact (13.02.03), not a model fact. The disclosure overrides the model-derived warning | Model warning re-labelled to the seller's disclosed claim: "Seller states rewired to 230V (unverified) — confirm before use." Not silence (DT-03) |
| Seller falsely claims a rewire to suppress the warning | The override may **re-label** but may never **remove** the safety warning (mirrors 13.01.01 D-07). The claim is an attributed seller assertion, recorded as dispute evidence | Warning still present, marked "seller-asserted, unverified"; the false claim cannot buy silence |
| Buyer's destination unknown (not logged in, no address) | Warning cannot be derived; show the model's market attribute neutrally, no personalisation | "Japan-market (100V)" stated as spec, no "you're in…" warning |
| Buyer changes shipping destination mid-flow (ships to a friend abroad) | Warning **re-derives on destination change** — a stale warning keyed to the old address is worse than none | Warning recomputes at the address step and at checkout against the *final* destination |
| Multi-item / multi-vendor cart, one item triggers a voltage warning | Warning is **per-item**, not per-cart — checkout (13.06.01) attributes it to the specific line | "1 item needs attention: the [amp] won't work with UK power without a converter" |
| Fitment relationship keyed to the wrong granularity (part fits the model but not the buyer's spec period/variant) | Relationship is at (model + spec period) where the interface changed; a bare-model claim that ignores the split is a data bug routed to 13.01.02 | "Fits 1954–1965 (pre-CBS) routing — your unit is later; check before buying" |
| Fitment data absent for a part | No claim made — silence, not a guess | Fitment section absent |
| Fitment relationship is wrong (bad data) | Buyer buys a part that doesn't fit → return under 13.08.01 | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` — whether bad fitment data is platform liability (catalog-curated) or seller liability (contribution-grown, 13.01.02) is a genuine ambiguity, see Q-01 |
| Listing unmatched to a model (13.01.04) | No model attributes → no warning derivable | Nothing shown — a real, named cost of unmatched listings |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing/checkout render | Inline with listing load; never a separate blocking fetch — the warning must not gate the page |
| Empty | No fitment/region data for this model | Section omitted entirely — no "unknown" noise on the fitment half |
| Populated | Warning derived | Plain-language warning with severity and remedy, locale-rendered |
| Error | Derivation fails | **Fail silent on fitment; fail loud on voltage** — an unshown fitment note costs a return, an unshown voltage warning costs an amp. On voltage-derivation failure, fall back to stating the raw market attribute rather than showing nothing |
| Partial | Model market known, buyer destination unknown | Neutral spec statement without personalisation |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Fitment and voltage are the same feature | They arrived as one sweep candidate (#24) and both answer "will this work for me?" | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH TENSION: kept as one node because both are *derivations from catalog model relationships* and share a surface. But they differ in stakes — a wrong fitment costs a return; a wrong voltage costs a destroyed amp. Hence the split failure policy (D-02): fail silent on fitment, fail loud on voltage. They are one feature with two severity tiers, not two features. |
| DT-02 | The model-derived voltage warning is authoritative | It comes from canonical catalog data, so it looks trustworthy | ❌ REJECTED: a *unit* may have been rewired, and the catalog only knows *models* (13.01.01: "a model record is a type, never a unit"). Unit-level mod disclosure (13.02.03) must be able to override a model-derived warning. This is the general trap of this sub-domain — model facts masquerading as unit facts. Generalised by 13.01.01 DT-04: every consequence-class attribute is a prior a unit can falsify. |
| DT-03 | If a unit disclosure can override the warning, the override can be weaponised to suppress it | Follows from DT-02 — if a seller's "rewired to 230V" claim removes the warning, a false claim removes a *safety* warning, which is the one thing that must not happen | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH RESOLUTION: the override **re-labels, never removes**. A voltage warning always renders; a unit disclosure changes it from "model is 100V" to "seller states rewired to 230V — unverified," and the unverified state is recorded as dispute evidence (Q-03). This mirrors 13.01.01 D-07 (an unmoderated `safety` value may raise a warning but never suppress one) applied to the *unit* side rather than the *contribution* side. The asymmetry is the same: safety warnings are additive-only. |
| DT-04 | All warnings should surface at equal visual weight | Symmetry instinct — a warning is a warning | ❌ REJECTED: surfacing a fitment note at the same weight as a voltage warning is **warning fatigue that dilutes the one that can start a fire**. Voltage occupies its own severity tier — it is the only warning in this feature with a physical-harm consequence, so it renders distinctly and survives the same treatment fitment does not. This is D-02's asymmetry expressed in the UI, and it depends on CX-M21 (severity cannot be carried by colour alone). |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Reads **13.01.01 Model Records** ([path](./13.01.01-model-records-taxonomy-attributes.md)) — market/voltage attributes come from the per-category `safety`-class schema; fitment relationships key to the **(model + spec period)** granularity that file's spec-period mechanism defines. 13.01.01 explicitly feeds this file via CX-04 and resolves the CX-05 raise-but-never-suppress conflict.
- Overridden by **13.02.03 Modification & Originality Disclosure** ([path](../13.02-condition-originality-disclosure/13.02.03-modification-originality-disclosure.md)) — unit facts beat model facts. See DT-02/DT-03: the override re-labels, never silences a voltage warning.
- Surfaces in **13.06.01 Cart & Multi-Vendor Checkout** ([path](../13.06-cart-checkout-orders/13.06.01-cart-multivendor-checkout.md)) — the second warning point; warnings are per-item across a multi-vendor cart, attributed to the specific line, and the acknowledgement is captured at checkout.
- Reads **Domain 15 Gear Registry** ([path](../../15-gear-registry-ownership/)) — "does this fit gear I own?" resolves against the buyer's registered rig's model triples, so fitment can respect spec-period differences the buyer never has to know about.
- Consumes **CX-M13 Geo, Location & Map Discovery** — buyer shipping destination drives the region derivation, and a destination *change* must re-fire the derivation.
- Consumes **CX-M19 Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** — an acknowledged (or unacknowledged) voltage warning at checkout is dispute evidence; the record must capture *which attribute state* produced the warning (model-canonical vs provisional-unverified vs seller-asserted-override), per Q-03 and 13.01.01's sharpening of it.
- Consumes **CX-M21 Accessibility** — a safety warning must survive screen readers and colour-blind rendering; severity (and the voltage tier's distinct weight, DT-04) cannot be carried by colour alone.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Warnings inform, never block | The buyer may legitimately want the 100V unit (they own a transformer, or they're a collector). Blocking is paternalistic and wrong. |
| D-02 | Fail silent on fitment, fail loud on voltage | Per DT-01 — asymmetric stakes justify asymmetric failure behaviour, in derivation-failure handling *and* in visual weight (DT-04). |
| D-03 | Unit-level disclosure overrides model-derived warnings by **re-labelling, never silencing** | Per DT-02/DT-03. A safety warning is additive-only; a unit claim changes what it says, not whether it shows. Mirrors 13.01.01 D-07 on the unit side. |
