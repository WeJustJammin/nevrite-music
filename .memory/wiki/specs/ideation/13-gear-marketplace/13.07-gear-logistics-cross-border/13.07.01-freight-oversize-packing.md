# Feature: Freight, Oversize Handling & Packing Standards

> **Number**: 13.07.01
> **Parent**: [Gear Logistics & Cross-Border](./13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The gear that doesn't go in a box: Hammond organs, Leslie cabinets, upright pianos, 8×10 bass rigs, drum kits, large-format consoles. Freight is a different transport regime from parcel — palletised, quoted per shipment, needing a lift gate and someone present to receive 200kg — and the packing standards for the parcel-sized stuff are what decide whether a vintage guitar arrives as a guitar or as a headstock repair.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as seller) | Discovers that the amp they've listed can't just go in a box. Needs packing guidance that is specific ("detune the strings, support the headstock, double-box with 2 inches of clearance"), because they've never shipped a guitar and the alternative is learning by breaking one. |
| Producer | Full (as seller) | Ships heavy racks and outboard. Knows the physics; needs the quoting, not the education. |
| Operator | Full (as seller) | Ships backline and PA at freight scale routinely — the persona with real palletising experience and a loading dock. Needs speed, not hand-holding. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the freight cost as a buyer, usually with shock — a £400 organ with a £300 freight quote. Needs to understand *before* the cart that big things cost real money to move. |

## Behavior

Freight class is derived from the catalog model's dimensions and weight (13.01.01's per-category attributes) — the buyer should not have to know that a Leslie 122 is freight. Quoting is per-shipment and may not resolve instantly; some routes need a human. Packing standards are **category-specific guidance surfaced at dispatch**, when the seller is holding the tape.

### Happy Path

1. Seller lists a Leslie cabinet; catalog attributes classify it as freight
2. Buyer enters a destination; system quotes freight, not parcel
3. Buyer sees the real cost before committing (13.06.01 D-02)
4. On sale, seller gets category-specific packing guidance and a freight booking
5. Delivery is scheduled — someone must be there

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Freight quote needs a human / can't resolve in a session | Real for unusual routes and oversize items | "Quote required" rather than a fake number — but this breaks checkout's flow (13.06.01 Q-03) |
| Buyer has no way to receive 200kg (a third-floor flat, no lift gate) | The shipment arrives and cannot be delivered | Delivery requirements stated **at checkout**, not at the kerb |
| Model has no dimensions in the catalog | Freight class underivable → quoted as parcel → catastrophic underquote | Seller must be able to override; unmatched listings (13.01.04) are especially exposed |
| Seller ignores packing guidance; item arrives damaged | The claim (13.07.04) turns on whether packing met the standard | Packing evidence into the pack (13.02.04) at dispatch |
| Item is genuinely unshippable to that destination | Not every route exists at any price | Stated as ineligible, itemised (13.06.01 D-04) |
| Local pickup is the only viable option | Common for large items — a piano usually shouldn't ship | Route to 13.11; the honest answer for a lot of this category |
| Buyer in an apartment buys a 7ft grand | Physically deliverable to the building, not to the flat | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — where does delivery legally end? |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Quote requested | Freight quoting is slow — set the expectation, don't spin silently |
| Empty | No destination yet | "Enter a destination for a freight quote" |
| Populated | Quote returned | Cost, transit estimate, delivery requirements |
| Error | Quote fails | Manual quote path; never a parcel-rate fallback — that underquotes by an order of magnitude |
| Partial | Quote requires a human | "We'll quote this within N hours" — breaks the checkout session (13.06.01 Q-03) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Freight is a shipping-method option — parcel or freight, a dropdown | It looks like a carrier-selection concern, which is CX-M14's | ❌ REJECTED: freight changes the *shape of the transaction*, not the carrier. It cannot be quoted instantly, needs an appointment (implicating CX-M05), needs a receiver physically present with equipment, may cost more than the item, and may not exist for a route at any price. A dropdown implies the buyer knows which to pick — but the whole point is that **the buyer should never have to know a Leslie 122 is freight**; the catalog knows its dimensions, so the system knows. Making it a user choice pushes a physics question onto the persona least equipped to answer it (Fan). |
| DT-02 | Packing standards are advisory content — a help article | They read as guidance, so they get written once and buried | ❌ REJECTED: packing standards are the **counterparty to the damage claim** (13.07.04). A claim's outcome turns on whether the item was packed to standard, which means the standard has to be (a) specific enough to be assessable — "double-boxed with 2 inches of clearance, strings detuned, headstock supported" — and (b) **evidenced at dispatch** into the pack (13.02.04), or it is unenforceable. A help article nobody reads at 11pm while taping a box is not a standard; guidance surfaced *at label print*, when the seller is holding the tape, is. Same principle as 13.02.04 DT-02: meet the user at the moment the fact becomes true. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Freight class is derived from catalog attributes, never chosen by the buyer | Per DT-01 — the buyer shouldn't have to know a Leslie is freight. |
| D-02 | Packing standards are specific, assessable, and surfaced at dispatch | Per DT-02 — a standard that isn't evidenced is unenforceable. |
| D-03 | Never fall back to a parcel rate when a freight quote fails | An order-of-magnitude underquote that someone must then eat. |
| D-04 | Delivery requirements are stated at checkout, not at the kerb | "Someone must be present with a lift gate" is not a surprise to spring on delivery day. |
