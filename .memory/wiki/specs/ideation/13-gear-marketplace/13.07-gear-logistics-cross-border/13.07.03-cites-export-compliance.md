# Feature: Restricted Materials (CITES) & Export Compliance

> **Number**: 13.07.03
> **Parent**: [Gear Logistics & Cross-Border](./13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Musical instruments are full of endangered species. Brazilian rosewood, ivory nuts and inlays, tortoiseshell picks and binding, abalone, certain ebonies — all CITES-listed, all common in exactly the vintage instruments this domain is built around. Moving them across borders requires permits, and getting it wrong means the instrument is **seized and destroyed**, not returned. This is the only feature in the domain where the failure mode is the physical annihilation of an irreplaceable object.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (as seller) | Declares materials on a listing. **Does not know** their 1965 guitar has a Brazilian rosewood fingerboard, or that this matters. The persona is not being evasive — they genuinely don't know, and nothing has ever told them. |
| Producer | Config (as seller) | Same, but materials questions are rarer in outboard — mostly relevant when selling instruments from a studio's collection. |
| Operator | Config (as seller) | Ships internationally at volume; more likely to know CITES exists and to have hit it. Still needs per-unit determination, which is a per-instrument fact. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees only whether an item can ship to them. Should never encounter the word CITES — the persona will not read a permit explanation and doesn't need to. |

## Behavior

Material composition is a **model-level attribute** (13.01.01) with **unit-level override** (a specific year's production may differ, and a mod may have replaced the part). Restriction is derived: materials × origin × destination → permitted / permit-required / prohibited. Determination gates bid (13.05.02 D-01) and checkout (13.06.01 D-02), because a binding sale of a prohibited movement is worse than no sale.

### Happy Path

1. Seller lists a 1965 guitar; catalog attributes flag a probable Brazilian rosewood fingerboard for that model-year
2. System asks the seller to confirm materials rather than assuming
3. Buyer in another country views it; system derives permit-required for that route
4. Requirement is stated before commitment, with what it means in time and money
5. Buyer proceeds knowingly, or the listing shows as domestic-only for them

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller genuinely doesn't know the materials | The common case, not the exception | "Unknown" must be a valid answer — and it forces conservative treatment, per DT-02 |
| Model-level attribute wrong for this unit | Production varied year to year and within years | Unit-level override, same pattern as 13.02.03 (unit facts beat model facts) |
| **Auction won by an ineligible bidder** | A binding sale of an unmovable object | Bid-time gate (13.05.02 D-01) — this feature is why that rule exists |
| Pre-Convention instrument with documentation | Legally movable with the right paperwork — and that paperwork is itself valuable | Documentation is a registry artifact (domain 15); see DT-01 |
| Instrument seized in transit | **Destroyed, not returned.** No insurance makes this whole | The nightmare scenario; everything here exists to prevent it |
| Permit obtained by the seller | Weeks of lead time; the buyer is waiting | Order state must tolerate a multi-week permit wait |
| Rules change (rosewood rules have changed repeatedly) | Determinations go stale | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02 |
| Item is a bundle (13.03.05) with one restricted constituent | The bundle is only as shippable as its most restricted item | Per 13.03.05 D-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Eligibility check | Inline at bid/checkout |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | CITES is a compliance checkbox — ask the seller, block if restricted | The sweep's framing (#12), and how a compliance feature usually gets built | ❌ REJECTED — and this reveals something the candidate list missed entirely. **CITES documentation is provenance.** A pre-Convention certificate proves the instrument's materials predate the restriction, which requires proving *when the instrument was made and what it's made of* — and that documentation is permanently valuable, travels with the instrument, materially affects its price, and is catastrophic to lose. It is a **registry artifact** (domain 15), not an order attachment. Treating it as per-transaction paperwork means the next owner starts from scratch on an instrument that already has the certificate — which is the reconstruction failure this platform exists to end, in its purest form. Nobody framed CITES as a provenance feature; it is one. |
| DT-02 | Unknown materials should default to permitted — most instruments aren't restricted | Optimistic default; avoids blocking honest sellers over a question they can't answer | ❌ REJECTED: the asymmetry is extreme and one-directional. Wrongly permitting → the instrument is **seized and destroyed**, irreversibly, and no insurance restores a 1962 Jazzmaster. Wrongly restricting → a sale doesn't happen. These are not comparable costs, and unknown is the *modal* answer (the Musician persona genuinely does not know). So: **unknown means conservative** — treat as restricted for international movement, and give the seller a real path to determine it (model-year guidance from the catalog, photos, a determination service). This is the only place in the domain where blocking a transaction on missing data is right, and it directly overrides the domain-wide "never stall the flow" instinct for cross-border only. |
| DT-03 | The determination can be computed once at listing time and reused at bid/checkout | Cheaper; the materials don't change, so why recompute a fixed fact | ✅ CONFIRMED that materials are fixed, but the *determination* is not: it is materials × origin × **destination** × **current rules**, and three of those four vary after listing. Destination is unknown until a specific buyer appears (a domestic-only listing to one buyer is permit-required to another), and rosewood rules have changed repeatedly (Q-02). So the fixed input is cached (materials, origin), but the **determination is recomputed live at each commitment gate** — bid (13.05.02) and checkout (13.06.01) — never served from a listing-time snapshot. A stale "permitted" cached against changed rules is precisely the destroy-the-instrument case; the fail-closed rule (D-02) only protects a determination that was actually run at the moment of commitment. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Unknown materials are treated conservatively for international movement | Per DT-02 — seizure is irreversible; a blocked sale is not. |
| D-02 | Fail closed on international determination failure | The only correct fail-closed in the domain besides 13.04.03. |
| D-03 | CITES documentation is a registry artifact, not an order attachment | Per DT-01 — it travels with the instrument, permanently. |
| D-04 | The Fan persona never sees the word CITES | They see "ships to you" or "doesn't". The mechanism is ours to carry. |
| D-05 | Determination is recomputed live at each commitment gate, never served from a listing-time snapshot | Per DT-03 — destination and rules vary after listing; a cached "permitted" against changed rules is the destroy-the-instrument case. |
