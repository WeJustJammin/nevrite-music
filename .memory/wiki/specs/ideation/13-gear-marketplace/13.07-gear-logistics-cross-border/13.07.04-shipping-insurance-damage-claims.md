# Feature: Shipping Insurance & High-Value Damage Claims

> **Number**: 13.07.04
> **Parent**: [Gear Logistics & Cross-Border](./13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Insuring an irreplaceable object in transit and resolving it when it arrives broken. Carrier default liability is trivial — often a few hundred pounds regardless of what was inside — so a $40k vintage instrument shipped on default terms is effectively uninsured. And when it does break, the money is the smaller half of the problem: the object is gone, and no payout produces another 1959 Les Paul.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | Ships a guitar and discovers carrier liability caps at £250. As a buyer, opens the claim when it arrives with a cracked headstock. The persona least likely to have insured correctly and most likely to be ruined by it. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Ships heavy, expensive, fragile outboard. Understands declared value; needs the process to not eat a week. |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Ships constantly; may hold their own transit insurance, in which case platform insurance is a duplicate cost. |
| Fan | Full (as buyer) | May claim. Has no idea insurance was a decision that was made, possibly by someone else, possibly badly. Needs a claim path that doesn't require understanding declared value or carrier liability tiers. |

## Behavior

Declared value drives the insurance regime, and the value is knowable: the sale price, corroborated by the price guide (13.04). Claims are adjudicated against the sealed evidence pack (13.02.04) plus packing-standard compliance (13.07.01). **The pack is what makes a claim resolvable at all** — without a pre-dispatch baseline, a damage claim is one memory against another, which is the platform's founding failure mode.

A resolved claim is not a single outcome — it is a **remedy fork**, and the platform owns the fork rather than defaulting to a payout:

- **Repair** — the object is repairable and repair restores more value than a refund destroys. The platform routes to a luthier/tech via Services (domain 05); the repaired original returns to the buyer, and the repair enters the unit's registry history (domain 15). This is the *preferred* remedy for non-fungible objects and resolves Q-02: repair is first-class, offered ahead of refund wherever the object is repairable.
- **Refund** — the object is destroyed, unrepairable, or the buyer declines repair. Escrow (CX-M01) releases the payout to the buyer; the seller's instrument is gone. Money is the consolation, never the restoration (DT-02).
- **Partial** — cosmetic or minor damage the buyer accepts with a price adjustment; the object stays, the sale stands, the delta settles.

The remedy is proposed by the system from the pack evidence and the repairability of the fault, then **agreed by both parties**; a contested fork escalates to domain 24, never to a unilateral platform decision.

### Happy Path

1. Sale completes at £8,000; system sets declared value from the sale price
2. Seller packs to standard; packing evidence seals into the pack
3. Item arrives damaged; buyer photographs on arrival
4. Claim received before the inspection deadline atomically opens `claim-open` with the pack as baseline: state at dispatch vs. state on arrival; auto-settlement and ownership transfer suspend
5. System proposes a remedy from the evidence — repair (routed to Services), refund, or partial with price adjustment — with repair offered first where the fault is repairable
6. Both parties agree the remedy; escrow holds throughout and releases on resolution
7. Claim resolves; the damage — and any repair — enters the object's registry history (domain 15) as a permanent fact about the unit, not just the order

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Carrier liability caps far below the item's value | The default case for anything valuable | Insurance to declared value is not optional above a threshold — see DT-01 |
| Damage was pre-existing and disclosed (13.02.02) | The pack proves it shipped that way | Claim declined on evidence, not on argument — the honest seller's protection (13.02.02 DT-02) |
| Seller packed badly | Carrier and insurer both decline; the standard was the term (13.07.01 D-02) | Seller bears it — which is why the standard must be specific and surfaced at dispatch |
| No dispatch evidence (seller skipped it) | Weaker claim, both directions | 13.02.04 D-02 — prompted, not blocked, and this is the cost of skipping |
| Object is unrepairable / destroyed | **Money doesn't fix this.** There is no replacement | See DT-02 — the domain's hardest truth |
| Object is repairable | Repair may be better than a refund for both sides — and cheaper | Repair-vs-refund is a real remedy fork nothing in the domain currently owns |
| Buyer damages it and claims transit damage | Arrival evidence timing is the counterweight | Pack + arrival timestamp; escalates to domain 24 |
| Insurer requires the price guide's valuation and it's low-confidence | 13.04.03's thin markets are exactly where valuable gear lives | Confidence must travel with the valuation (13.04.03 Cross-Cut Notes) |
| Operator suppressed platform insurance (DT-03) but their own policy declines | The account-level opt-out shifted risk to them; the log shows they bore it | Claim adjudicates against their policy; platform surfaces the logged opt-out, escalates to 24 if the Operator contests |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Insurance is a seller's choice — offer it as a checkbox at dispatch | It's how every carrier does it, and it respects seller autonomy | ❌ REJECTED above a value threshold: the choice is made by the party who **doesn't bear the loss**. The seller has the money once it settles; the buyer has a claim against a carrier whose liability caps at £250 on an £8,000 instrument. The seller's rational choice (skip the £90 insurance) is the buyer's catastrophe, and the Musician persona will not read the small print on either side. Above a threshold, insurance to declared value must be **structural, priced into the transaction**, not an opt-out — and the declared value is knowable from the sale price, so the usual excuse for making it manual doesn't apply. |
| DT-02 | A damage claim is a money problem — value it, pay it, done | Insurance thinking: the payout is the remedy | ❌ REJECTED: this is where non-fungibility hurts most. A destroyed 1959 Les Paul is not restored by £40,000 — the buyer wanted **that object**, the seller's instrument is gone from the world, and if it carried provenance (13.03.06) the record it played on can never be re-linked to a surviving instrument. The domain has no answer to this and cannot have one; the honest conclusion is that **prevention is the whole feature** — packing standards (13.07.01), evidence packs (13.02.04), and steering irreplaceable objects toward local pickup (13.11) rather than transit. It also argues that **repair should be a first-class remedy**, not a fallback: a repaired original is worth more to both parties than a payout and a destroyed instrument, and nothing in the domain currently owns that fork (Q-02, now resolved in Behavior). |
| DT-03 | Insurance is always the platform's to arrange — charge it on every shipment | Structural insurance (D-01) sounds like it should be universal | ❌ REJECTED for the Operator: an Operator who ships constantly often carries their **own** transit policy, so platform-arranged insurance is a duplicate cost on every shipment. Insurance-to-declared-value must be **structural but suppressible** — an Operator can attest existing coverage and opt their account out above the threshold, accepting that a claim then adjudicates against *their* policy, not the platform's. The suppression is per-account and logged, so a later dispute (domain 24) can see who bore the risk. Naively charging everyone would tax the highest-volume sellers to insure risk they already cover. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Above a value threshold, insurance to declared value is structural, not optional | Per DT-01 — the party choosing doesn't bear the loss. |
| D-02 | Declared value derives from the sale price, corroborated by the guide | Removes the manual step that makes under-insurance the default. |
| D-03 | Claims adjudicate on evidence, not argument | The pack and the packing standard are the terms; that's what makes them worth having. |
| D-04 | Prevention is the feature; the payout is the consolation | Per DT-02 — no payout replaces the object. |
| D-05 | Resolution is a remedy fork — repair (preferred, routed to Services 05) / refund / partial — proposed by system, agreed by both, escalated to 24 if contested | Per DT-02 and resolving Q-02; a repaired original beats a payout plus a destroyed object. |
| D-06 | Structural insurance is suppressible for Operators who attest their own coverage; suppression is per-account and logged | Per DT-03 — don't tax high-volume sellers to insure risk they already carry. |
| D-07 | A timely damage claim atomically suspends settlement and title transfer | The claim receives the promised inspection window; later settlement follows the withdrawal, rejection, or resolved financial outcome, never the expired pre-claim timer |
