# Feature: Shipping Insurance & High-Value Damage Claims

> **Number**: 13.07.04
> **Parent**: [Gear Logistics & Cross-Border](./13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Insuring an irreplaceable object in transit and resolving it when it arrives broken. Carrier default liability is trivial — often a few hundred pounds regardless of what was inside — so a $40k vintage instrument shipped on default terms is effectively uninsured. And when it does break, the money is the smaller half of the problem: the object is gone, and no payout produces another 1959 Les Paul.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | Ships a guitar and discovers carrier liability caps at £250. As a buyer, opens the claim when it arrives with a cracked headstock. The persona least likely to have insured correctly and most likely to be ruined by it. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Ships heavy, expensive, fragile outboard. Understands declared value; needs the process to not eat a week. |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Ships constantly; may hold their own transit insurance, in which case platform insurance is a duplicate cost. |
| Fan | Full (as buyer) | May claim. Has no idea insurance was a decision that was made, possibly by someone else, possibly badly. Needs a claim path that doesn't require understanding declared value or carrier liability tiers. |

## Behavior

Declared value drives the insurance regime, and the value is knowable: the sale price, corroborated by the price guide (13.04). Claims are adjudicated against the sealed evidence pack (13.02.04) plus packing-standard compliance (13.07.01). **The pack is what makes a claim resolvable at all** — without a pre-dispatch baseline, a damage claim is one memory against another, which is the platform's founding failure mode.

### Happy Path

1. Sale completes at £8,000; system sets declared value from the sale price
2. Seller packs to standard; packing evidence seals into the pack
3. Item arrives damaged; buyer photographs on arrival
4. Claim opens with the pack as baseline: state at dispatch vs. state on arrival
5. Claim resolves; buyer is refunded; the object's damage enters its registry history (domain 15)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Carrier liability caps far below the item's value | The default case for anything valuable | Insurance to declared value is not optional above a threshold — see DT-01 |
| Damage was pre-existing and disclosed (13.02.02) | The pack proves it shipped that way | Claim declined on evidence, not on argument — the honest seller's protection (13.02.02 DT-02) |
| Seller packed badly | Carrier and insurer both decline; the standard was the term (13.07.01 D-02) | Seller bears it — which is why the standard must be specific and surfaced at dispatch |
| No dispatch evidence (seller skipped it) | Weaker claim, both directions | 13.02.04 D-02 — prompted, not blocked, and this is the cost of skipping |
| Object is unrepairable / destroyed | **Money doesn't fix this.** There is no replacement | See DT-02 — the domain's hardest truth |
| Object is repairable | Repair may be better than a refund for both sides — and cheaper | Repair-vs-refund is a real remedy fork nothing in the domain currently owns |
| Buyer damages it and claims transit damage | Arrival evidence timing is the counterweight | Pack + arrival timestamp; escalates to domain 24 |
| Insurer requires the price guide's valuation and it's low-confidence | 13.04.03's thin markets are exactly where valuable gear lives | Confidence must travel with the valuation (13.04.03 Cross-Cut Notes) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Claim submitting | Pack assembling automatically — the buyer isn't building a case |
| Empty | No claims | Not shown |
| Populated | Claim open | Timeline, evidence, current position |
| Error | Claim submission fails | Preserved; never lose a claim |
| Partial | Claim under review | Honest expectation-setting — insurance claims are slow and pretending otherwise breeds anger |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Insurance is a seller's choice — offer it as a checkbox at dispatch | It's how every carrier does it, and it respects seller autonomy | ❌ REJECTED above a value threshold: the choice is made by the party who **doesn't bear the loss**. The seller has the money once it settles; the buyer has a claim against a carrier whose liability caps at £250 on an £8,000 instrument. The seller's rational choice (skip the £90 insurance) is the buyer's catastrophe, and the Musician persona will not read the small print on either side. Above a threshold, insurance to declared value must be **structural, priced into the transaction**, not an opt-out — and the declared value is knowable from the sale price, so the usual excuse for making it manual doesn't apply. |
| DT-02 | A damage claim is a money problem — value it, pay it, done | Insurance thinking: the payout is the remedy | ❌ REJECTED: this is where non-fungibility hurts most. A destroyed 1959 Les Paul is not restored by £40,000 — the buyer wanted **that object**, the seller's instrument is gone from the world, and if it carried provenance (13.03.06) the record it played on can never be re-linked to a surviving instrument. The domain has no answer to this and cannot have one; the honest conclusion is that **prevention is the whole feature** — packing standards (13.07.01), evidence packs (13.02.04), and steering irreplaceable objects toward local pickup (13.11) rather than transit. It also argues that **repair should be a first-class remedy**, not a fallback: a repaired original is worth more to both parties than a payout and a destroyed instrument, and nothing in the domain currently owns that fork (Q-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Above a value threshold, insurance to declared value is structural, not optional | Per DT-01 — the party choosing doesn't bear the loss. |
| D-02 | Declared value derives from the sale price, corroborated by the guide | Removes the manual step that makes under-insurance the default. |
| D-03 | Claims adjudicate on evidence, not argument | The pack and the packing standard are the terms; that's what makes them worth having. |
| D-04 | Prevention is the feature; the payout is the consolation | Per DT-02 — no payout replaces the object. |
