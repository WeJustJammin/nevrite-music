# Feature: International Shipping, Customs, Duties & Landed Cost

> **Number**: 13.07.02
> **Parent**: [Gear Logistics & Cross-Border](./13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What the thing actually costs to land in the buyer's country — item, freight, duty, import VAT, brokerage — quoted before commitment rather than demanded by a courier at the door. Gear is a global market by nature: the rare unit is rarely in your country, and the price difference across borders is often what makes a purchase worth making. It's also where the worst buyer experience in the category lives: a £2,000 preamp followed three weeks later by a £560 bill nobody mentioned.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (buyer) / Config (seller declarations) | Buys from another country because that's where the pedal is. Needs the total, not the item price. As seller: supplies declarations, and is the persona most likely to get them wrong — or to be asked by a buyer to mark it as a gift. |
| Producer | Read-only / Config | The heaviest international buyer — rare outboard is a global market. A 30% landed-cost surprise on a £4k unit is the difference between a good deal and a bad one, and they need to know at checkout. |
| Operator | Config | Imports at volume for a venue/studio; may have a broker, a deferment account and a VAT registration that changes the arithmetic entirely. |
| Fan | Read-only | Least aware that import duty exists. Will read the item price as the price. **The persona the "no surprises" rule exists for** — a customs bill after the fact is exactly the failure the persona doc's "money reaches the artist rather than a reseller" simplicity expectation cannot absorb. |

## Behavior

Landed cost is quoted **at checkout, before authorisation** (13.06.01 D-02): item + freight + duty + import tax + brokerage, with the tariff classification driven by the catalog category. Whether the platform *collects* import charges up-front (DDP) or leaves the buyer to pay the courier (DDU/DAP) is the decision that determines whether the surprise exists at all.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer in the EU views a listing in the US
2. System classifies the item, computes duty and import VAT for the destination
3. Checkout shows the landed total, itemised
4. Buyer pays the full landed cost up-front (DDP)
5. It arrives with nothing further to pay

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Classification is wrong → duty under-quoted | Someone eats the difference: platform, seller, or a surprised buyer | See DT-01 — a quote is a commitment |
| Buyer asks the seller to under-declare or mark as gift | Endemic in this category, and it is customs fraud | Must be refused; the ask itself may be a domain 24 signal |
| Item is a returning instrument (a musician's own gear coming home) | Different regime entirely — not a commercial import | Out of scope here; touches carnets in domain 18 |
| Country has no viable route | Ineligible line (13.06.01 D-04) | Itemised ineligibility |
| Duty rates change between listing and sale | Quote is time-bounded | Quoted at checkout, honoured for the order |
| **Vintage instrument with rosewood** | Duty is the *easy* part; CITES may prohibit the movement outright | 13.07.03 gates this before landed cost is even relevant |
| Order value under the destination de-minimis threshold | Below the threshold, duty and/or import tax do not apply — but the threshold is per-country and moving (US $800; EU removed the €22 VAT de-minimis in 2021, so import VAT applies from €0 via IOSS). Getting this wrong over-quotes and loses the sale, or under-quotes and recreates the surprise | Line resolves to zero duty where the threshold clears it; the quote is still itemised so the buyer sees *why* it is zero |
| Buyer is VAT-registered and can reclaim (Operator) | The landed cost is quoted **gross** — the platform's job is an accurate total including import VAT, not to pre-net a reclaim it cannot verify. A VAT-registered buyer reclaims through their own filing downstream; the import VAT line is shown separately precisely so it is reclaimable | Itemised import VAT line, labelled reclaimable-by-buyer; net-of-reclaim arithmetic is the buyer's, not the platform's |
| Return of an imported item | Duty paid on import is reclaimable in principle but the paperwork is real and painful, and it is a returns-flow concern, not a checkout one — checkout must not promise a refund it does not own | Returns handled in 13.08.01; the landed-cost breakdown is the record that flow reads. Long-tail reclaim mechanics: `[PENDING — /write-be-spec, with 13.08.01]`; see Q-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An estimated landed cost is good enough — buyers understand duty is approximate | It's what most cross-border marketplaces do, and precision is genuinely hard | ❌ REJECTED: an estimate transfers the risk to the buyer at exactly the moment they cannot assess it, and the Fan persona reads an estimate as a price. The failure is not a small variance — it is a courier at the door demanding £560 before handing over a package, weeks after the purchase, with no recourse. That single experience destroys the buyer relationship more thoroughly than almost anything else in the domain. **A quoted landed cost must be a commitment** (DDP), with the platform or seller absorbing classification variance. If that is commercially unacceptable, the honest alternative is to **not sell across that border** — not to quote a number that might be a lie. This directly informs sub-domain Q-01. |
| DT-02 | Landed cost is a tax problem, so it belongs to CX-M02 | CX-M02 (Tax & Fiscal Compliance) serves gear-marketplace and owns marketplace facilitator tax, VAT and GST | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED: the **tax arithmetic** is CX-M02's — VAT registration, marketplace facilitator obligations, and remittance are identical mechanisms across gear, digital goods, services and ticketing. But **tariff classification is not tax, it is product identity**: the duty rate depends on what the object *is* (a musical instrument, a piece of electronic equipment, an item containing endangered species), which only the catalog (13.01.01) knows. Digital goods have no tariff code; services have no customs. The classification half stays here and reads the catalog; the arithmetic half is consumed from CX-M02. |
| DT-03 | Landed cost is a flat "item value × duty rate + VAT" formula the platform can compute once and cache | It looks like simple arithmetic once classification is known | ❌ REJECTED: the destination's **de-minimis threshold** breaks the flat model. Below the threshold, duty and/or import tax vanish; the threshold is per-country and politically volatile (the EU abolished its €22 VAT de-minimis in 2021 and a duty de-minimis reform is in progress; the US $800 threshold is itself contested). So the landed-cost engine cannot be a static rate table — it must be a **per-destination, time-bounded ruleset** that knows current thresholds and the collection regime (IOSS for EU low-value, standard clearance above). This is why D-01's "a quote is a commitment" needs D-02's "if a border can't be quoted reliably, don't sell across it": a border whose de-minimis rules the platform cannot keep current is a border it should not quote. Consumed from CX-M02, keyed by the classification produced here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A quoted landed cost is a commitment, not an estimate | Per DT-01 — an estimate is a lie with a disclaimer. |
| D-02 | If a border can't be quoted reliably, don't sell across it | Per DT-01 — the honest alternative to a bad quote is no sale, not a hedged one. |
| D-03 | Tariff classification stays here; tax arithmetic is CX-M02's | Per DT-02 — classification is product identity, which only the catalog knows. |
| D-04 | Under-declaration requests are refused, and may be a trust signal | Customs fraud, endemic in the category, and the ask reveals intent. |
| D-05 | Import VAT is quoted **gross** and itemised as a separate line, never pre-netted for reclaim | Per the Operator edge case — the platform cannot verify a buyer's reclaim entitlement, so it quotes the true total and surfaces the reclaimable line; net-of-reclaim arithmetic is the buyer's. |
| D-06 | The landed-cost engine is a per-destination, time-bounded ruleset, not a static rate table | Per DT-03 — de-minimis thresholds and collection regimes (e.g. IOSS) are per-country and volatile; a static table silently drifts into wrong quotes. |
