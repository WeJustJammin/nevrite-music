# Feature: Condition Grading Scale

> **Number**: 13.02.01
> **Parent**: [Condition, Originality & Disclosure](./13.02-condition-originality-disclosure-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

A standardised, enumerated scale describing the physical and functional state of a unit — Brand New / Mint / Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair / Poor / Non-Functioning. It is the single most price-relevant attribute after the model itself, and it must be an enum rather than prose, because the price guide (13.04) needs to compare like with like and buyers need to filter.

The definitional line that makes the scale coherent: **a grade measures deviation from the bound catalog model's as-shipped state — not from an abstract ideal.** A factory-relic'd Custom Shop reissue left the factory looking beaten; it is Brand New, not Poor. A 1966 amp that has been on the road for 58 years has deviated a long way from as-shipped; it is Good, and it is worth more than the Mint reissue. This one rule resolves relic'd finishes, B-stock, vintage patina and "wear is desirable" without a special case for any of them, and it is why model binding (13.01.04) is load-bearing for grading rather than decorative.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (grade as seller, read as buyer) | Picks one of 8 grades at the condition step, with the full definition rendered inline at the point of choice — never behind a help link, never hover-only. As a buyer, filters `Good & up`. The persona most likely to over-grade their own gear — not dishonestly, but because they're fond of it — so they are the persona the flaw/grade cross-check (CX-01) exists to catch, and the one who most often meets the "A repaired headstock isn't Mint" challenge. |
| Producer | Full (grade as seller, read as buyer) | Grades studio outboard where function dominates cosmetics — a scuffed rack unit that works perfectly is Good with `Functions as designed` asserted; a mint-faced unit that drifts is Fair or Non-Functioning. Consumes the derived cosmetic descriptor on Fair-and-below listings when hunting restoration stock ("Non-Functioning · cosmetically Excellent" is a *buy signal* for this persona and nobody else). |
| Operator | Full (grade as seller, read as buyer) | Grades ex-rental and ex-backline stock, which is systematically road-worn, at volume — usually on a phone at a loading dock. Needs Good and Fair to read as "heavily used, professionally maintained", not "broken", which is exactly what the `Functions as designed` assertion at Good-and-above delivers. The only persona that hits bulk-defaulted grading (D-11). |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads grades as a buyer; may offer/bid but has no seller side (domain Role Matrix), so never grades. Sees the grade badge plus a plain-language *consequence* line ("Fair: works, but with faults the seller has listed"), tap-to-expand on touch. The persona with zero calibration for what "Very Good" means in gear, and the one a merged condition/originality score would most successfully mislead (CX-04). Gets a safety interstitial when buying Non-Functioning mains-powered gear (E-19). |

## Behavior

Grade is a **required enum on every listing** — 8 values, no default, no "not specified". Each grade carries a **published, versioned definition** rendered inline at selection. Three rules govern it:

1. **Grade is bounded by disclosed flaws (13.02.02), never free.** The flaw set produces a *ceiling*; the seller picks at or below it. The cross-check challenges impossible combinations at listing time rather than silently accepting or silently rejecting them (D-02).
2. **Grade is independent of originality (13.02.03)** per sub-domain D-01. A refinished instrument can be Mint. The two axes render together and never merge (CX-04).
3. **Grade measures deviation from the bound model's as-shipped state** (D-04), and **excludes consumables** (D-06).

### The Scale

Working enum. Exact labels are Q-02 (adopt the de-facto market scale vs. bespoke) — the *semantics* below are settled regardless of what the labels end up being called.

| # | Grade | Definition | Function | Requires |
|---|-------|-----------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Brand New** | Unused and unopened, exactly as it left the factory, with full manufacturer warranty and original packaging/accessories. | Asserted | New-stock listing type; no prior custody transfer in the registry (E-16) |
| 2 | **Mint** | Indistinguishable from as-shipped. Opened, possibly played briefly, no wear detectable. | Asserted | **Zero disclosed flaws on any axis** |
| 3 | **Excellent** | Very light cosmetic deviation only — micro-scratches, faint playwear. No finish loss. | Asserted | Cosmetic flaws ≤ `light`; at most one professionally-executed, stable, disclosed structural repair |
| 4 | **Very Good** | Moderate, obvious cosmetic wear — dings, finish checking, buckle rash, wear-through at contact points. Non-essential parts may be missing or replaced. | Asserted | Cosmetic flaws ≤ `moderate` |
| 5 | **Good** | Heavy cosmetic wear, hardware corrosion or pitting, or an unrepaired non-structural issue. Fully usable. | Asserted | Cosmetic flaws ≤ `heavy` |
| 6 | **Fair** | Works, but with disclosed functional impairment (intermittent, noisy, out of spec, one channel down) and/or stable unrepaired structural damage. | **Not asserted** | ≥1 functional flaw at `impaired`, or structural `unrepaired` |
| 7 | **Poor** | Works only partially, or is missing components essential to operation. Requires work before normal use. | **Not asserted** | Missing-part flaw at `essential` |
| 8 | **Non-Functioning / For Parts** | Does not perform its primary function. Sold for parts, restoration or repair. No representation of playability. | **Denied** | ≥1 functional flaw at `fails` |

**The asymmetry is the design, and it is the answer to DT-02.** Grades 1–5 are a *functional assertion with a cosmetic gradient*; grades 6–8 are a *functional statement that is cosmetically blind*. A single seller-facing enum therefore carries both dimensions without a second scale — because the two dimensions only ever conflict at the two ends, and each end is handled by a derived render rather than a new field (D-05):

- **"Ugly but flawless"** (Producer's rack unit) → **Good**, and the listing renders `Functions as designed` as an explicit assertion, because Good asserts it. The Producer's information need is met by an assertion the grade already makes.
- **"Mint-looking but broken"** → **Non-Functioning**, and because grades 6–8 are cosmetically blind, the listing renders a **derived cosmetic descriptor** from the cosmetic flaw set: `Non-Functioning · cosmetically Excellent`. Derived, never seller-authored — it cannot contradict the flaws because it *is* the flaws.

Result: one enum, one comp key, comp portability preserved (Q-01), and neither failure case lies.

### Grade Ceilings

The cross-check is a pure function of the disclosed flaw set (13.02.02) → a ceiling. It never picks the grade; it bounds it.

| Flaw axis | Severity | Effect on grade |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| any | none disclosed | No ceiling (Mint available) |
| cosmetic | `light` | Ceiling: Excellent |
| cosmetic | `moderate` | Ceiling: Very Good |
| cosmetic | `heavy` | Ceiling: Good |
| structural | `repaired` (professional, stable) | Ceiling: Excellent |
| structural | `repaired` (amateur or unstable) | Ceiling: Good |
| structural | `unrepaired` | Ceiling: Fair |
| functional | `impaired` (intermittent / out of spec / partial) | Ceiling: Fair |
| functional | `fails` | **Forced: Non-Functioning** (not a ceiling — a value) |
| missing part | `non-essential` | Ceiling: Very Good |
| missing part | `essential` | **Ceiling: Poor** |

The lowest applicable ceiling wins. `functional: fails` is the only rule that *sets* rather than *caps* — a unit that does not work is Non-Functioning regardless of how pretty it is, and there is no seller judgement left to exercise.

> **How a disclosed flaw reaches this table** — **resolved by owner ratification (DQ-12.O1 / DQ-12.O2)**, superseding this note's earlier "flagged for Step 6" status. Each **severity option** authored in 13.02.02's per-item severity enumeration carries one authored **`(axis, severity-token)` pair** drawn from this table's vocabulary — axis `cosmetic` | `functional` | `structural` | `missing-part`, severity-token from the Severity column above — alongside the materiality verdict that option already produces (13.02.02 D-06). The pair is authored in the **shared per-category structured-attribute schema** (13.01.01 D-14), catalog moderation holds the standing over it, and it is **never seller-facing**: the seller continues to pick per-item plain language only. This stays the **single** ceiling table — it is not copied or re-authored per category — and because no grade's meaning changes, the versioned definitions below need **no edit and no version bump** (D-07 intact). See D-14.

### Definition Versioning

Grade definitions are **immutable once published and versioned** (`v1`, `v2`, …). Every listing stores `(grade, definition_version)` at the moment of grading. Rewording "Very Good" in 2027 does not silently re-grade 40,000 live listings or re-key three years of comps — it publishes `v2` and leaves `v1` listings meaning what they meant. 13.04 buckets comps by `(model, grade, definition_version, originality)` and may only merge versions across an explicit, published mapping. Version changes are audit-logged (CX-M19).

### Happy Path

1. Seller reaches the condition step of listing creation, having already bound a catalog model (13.01.04) and completed the flaw checklist (13.02.02)
2. System computes the ceiling from the disclosed flaw set and renders all 8 grades with full definitions inline — grades above the ceiling are shown but disabled, each annotated with the specific flaw that blocks it ("Mint unavailable — you disclosed a headstock repair")
3. Seller selects a grade at or below the ceiling
4. System re-runs the cross-check against the current flaw set and accepts, storing `(grade, definition_version, ceiling_at_time_of_grading)`
5. Listing publishes; the Condition panel renders **Grade + function assertion + derived cosmetic descriptor (if grade ≤ Fair)**, alongside — never merged into — the Originality panel (13.02.03)
6. Grade becomes a buyer-facing facet (CX-M16), inclusive-upward by default, and a comp-set dimension (13.04.01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| E-01 | Grade contradicts disclosed flaws (Mint + "headstock repair") | Grade is above the ceiling → challenged at selection, not silently accepted. Cannot be chosen | "A repaired headstock isn't Mint — did you mean Excellent?" with Excellent pre-selected |
| E-02 | **Cosmetically mint, functionally broken** | `functional: fails` **forces** Non-Functioning regardless of cosmetics. The cosmetic state is not lost — it is rendered as a derived descriptor from the cosmetic flaw set | "Non-Functioning / For Parts · cosmetically Excellent" — the exact render the restoration market needs |
| E-03 | **Cosmetically wrecked, functionally perfect** (Producer's rack unit) | Cosmetic `heavy` → ceiling Good. Good asserts function, so no information is lost | "Good · Functions as designed" — the assertion is the feature |
| E-04 | Seller declines to grade | Not permitted — required field, no default, no "unspecified" | Cannot publish; step cannot be skipped |
| E-05 | Systematic over-grading by a seller | Buyer disputes accumulate against them; feeds seller performance (CX-M08). Never surfaced on the individual listing — a scarlet letter on one listing punishes the wrong transaction | Pattern surfaces in the seller scorecard |
| E-06 | Vintage gear where wear is desirable (road-worn 1966 amp) | Grade is deviation-from-as-shipped, not desirability. It is Good and worth 5× a Mint reissue | Grade stated flatly; the market prices it (13.04). No "worse" framing |
| E-07 | **Factory-relic'd gear** (Custom Shop Heavy Relic, Murphy Lab, Nash) | The wear *is* the as-shipped state, encoded in the bound model (13.01.01). Deviation is measured from that. Unrelic'd grading logic would grade a brand-new £6k guitar as Poor | "Brand New" on a guitar covered in factory wear — correct, and only correct because the model is bound |
| E-08 | Listing is **unmatched** to a catalog model (13.01.04) | No model → no as-shipped baseline → grade is a bare enum with weakened semantics. **Does not block** (domain D-03) | Grade accepted; listing carries a "condition unverified against model" marker; comp weight reduced (13.04.03) |
| E-09 | Consumables are worn (dead strings, spent tubes, thin drum heads, flat battery) | Excluded from grading entirely (D-06). A guitar with dead strings is not Very Good | Consumables listed as "not graded — replace on arrival"; they never touch the ceiling |
| E-10 | The unit is Mint; the included case is destroyed | Grade scopes to the **unit bound to the catalog model**, not the accessories. Accessory condition is 13.03.01's "what's included" | "Mint · case: heavily worn (not graded)" — stated separately, never averaged in |
| E-11 | **Seller edits a flaw on a live listing, invalidating the published grade** (CX-01's open item) | Grade and flaws must never come to rest in a mutually contradictory state on a live listing. The edit is blocked until the seller resolves it — the *listing* never stalls and never auto-downgrades. Silently re-grading a seller's unit is the platform asserting a fact the seller is liable for | Modal: "This flaw makes Mint unavailable. Change the grade to Excellent, or remove the flaw." The old, consistent grade-and-flaws state stays live until the seller replaces it |
| E-12 | **Downward regrade with live offers** (13.05.01) | All open offers **auto-void** on any downward grade change, with the reason stated. A buyer's offer was made against a material fact that has changed; binding them to it silently is the trap | Offeror: "Seller changed condition Excellent → Good. Your £900 offer was withdrawn — re-offer?" |
| E-13 | **Upward regrade with live offers** | Offers survive — the buyer is not disadvantaged — but the offeror is notified. An upward regrade under a live offer is a soft price-raising move and must not be invisible | Offeror: "Seller changed condition Good → Excellent." Offer stands |
| E-14 | **Downward regrade on an auction with ≥1 bid** | Grade cannot be edited downward. A bid was placed against a material fact; changing it under a live bid is not a correction, it is a bait-and-switch. The auction must be cancelled (13.05 owns cancellation) | "You can't lower the condition on a live auction with bids. Cancel the auction to regrade." |
| E-15 | **Post-purchase, pre-dispatch downward regrade** | The sold fact is frozen. Seller may record the correction; buyer gets an immediate notification and a **one-click cancel with full refund**. The seller cannot unilaterally alter what was bought | Buyer: "The seller has corrected this unit's condition to Good. Cancel for a full refund, or keep the order." |
| E-16 | **"Brand New" on a unit the registry (15) shows a prior owner for** | Pre-fill-and-flag, mirroring 13.02.03 D-03 — the registry is evidence, not an authority over the seller's own signed claim. Grade challenged, never auto-set | "Our records show this unit was transferred to another owner in 2024. Brand New means unused — is this right?" |
| E-17 | **Concurrent grade edit by two members of one entity account** (studio/band/Operator staff) | A second save that lands on top of an unseen first must not silently overwrite it — last-write-wins is unacceptable on a price-relevant fact. The second save is refused and the conflicting value the other member set is named | "Sam changed this to Good 2 minutes ago. Review before saving." |
| E-18 | **Cross-check service unavailable** | Distinguish *contradiction* from *unavailability*. Unavailable → **save and publish** (D-03), then re-run the cross-check once the service is reachable again. If the deferred re-check finds a contradiction, notify the seller — never auto-change the grade | Publishes normally. If a contradiction is found later: "Your Mint grade conflicts with a disclosed repair — please review." |
| E-19 | **Fan buys Non-Functioning mains-powered gear** | Non-Functioning is a real, large segment and stays first-class — but "for parts" on a vintage valve amp means death caps and two-prong plugs, and the Fan persona has no professional stake and no calibration | Interstitial before purchase: "This item does not work and is sold for parts. It may be unsafe to power on. Have it checked by a technician." Acknowledgement required |
| E-20 | **Non-visual flaw** — smoke odour, high noise floor, hum, intonation drift | Caps the grade normally (odour = cosmetic `moderate`; noise floor = functional `impaired`), but **no photo can corroborate it**, so 13.02.02 D-03's photo rule is unenforceable and the evidence pack (13.02.04) has a blind spot. The cap still applies; the *evidence* does not exist for part of the class. **Now closed per Q-03**: the medium is authored per item — hum and noise floor are `audible` and evidenced by clip (13.02.02 D-03; the pack accepts audio, 13.02.04 D-12); odour is `undemonstrable`, disclosed and never gated | Flaw disclosed and grade capped; audible flaws corroborated by clip, `undemonstrable` ones stated as unevidenceable. See Q-03 |
| E-21 | **Bulk listing** (13.03.04) — 200 rows, per-row grading | Grade stays required — no bulk exemption, because an ungraded listing is unfilterable and uncomparable. A per-upload seller-set default **is** permitted, recorded as `bulk_defaulted: true`, disclosed on the listing, and carrying reduced comp weight (13.04) | Listing shows "Graded in bulk". Does **not** resolve domain Q-13 — resolves only its grading slice |
| E-22 | **Bundle listing** (13.03.05) — one grade over many units | Out of scope for this file: a bundle is not a unit, and D-04's anchor is the unit. Grading a bundle as one thing is 13.03.05 Q-01 | Routed, not decided here |
| E-23 | **B-Stock / open-box** | **Not a grade.** B-stock describes the *provenance of the defect* (factory-graded), not the state — a B-stock unit still has a physical condition, usually Mint or Excellent. Reverb collapses it into the condition enum; that is the same category error as merging condition and originality (D-01) | "B-Stock · Excellent" — a listing-type flag (13.03.05) plus a grade, never one value |
| E-24 | **Malicious input** — seller free-texts "MINT!!! 10/10" into the description while grading Fair | The enum is the machine-readable fact; prose never overrides it. Facets, comps and disputes read the enum. A description contradicting the enum is a "not as described" input for domain 24 | Buyer sees the enum badge with definition; contradicting prose is a dispute signal, not a grade |
| E-25 | **Wash trading to manipulate the price guide** | Seller self-deals a Mint '59 at £40k to themselves to manufacture a comp. Grade × price is the comp key, so **the grade is an attack surface on 13.04**. This file cannot detect it — arm's-length detection is 13.04's exclusion rule and 24's fraud surface | Nothing in this feature. Requirement stated to 13.04 — see DT-07 |
| E-26 | **Grade migration from another marketplace** | A seller importing from Reverb brings their calibration, not ours. Definitions are inline at the point of choice precisely so an incoming seller re-reads them; there is no auto-mapping of an imported condition string to a grade | Grade must be selected, never inherited from an import |
| E-27 | **Professionally-repaired structural flaw with no platform record** — a headstock repair a luthier did in 1988 | The severity option splits where the category admits it, and the two halves project to different ceilings: professional-and-stable caps at **Excellent**, amateur-or-unstable caps at **Good** (Grade Ceilings, D-15). The seller picks, and the pick is a **signed claim carrying their liability** — the platform holds no first-party record for a repair that predates it, which is the common vintage case. Where a first-party record for this unit *does* exist (13.08.02 repair, 13.09.03 intake, 13.10 rental return), the option is pre-filled and corroborated and any contradiction is **surfaced, never auto-asserted** (13.02.02 D-07) | With a record: *"Our repair record for this unit says professional bench, 2025-09 — confirm it still holds."* Without one: the plain-language option and the seller's name on it. No corroboration badge is shown and none is implied |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Condition step opened; flaw set fetched to compute the ceiling | Grade list skeleton, 8 rows. Definitions render as soon as the list does — they are static content and must not wait on the ceiling |
| Empty | Step reached, no grade selected | All 8 grades enabled, definitions inline, required-field marker. No default selection — a pre-selected grade is a grade the seller did not choose |
| Populated | Grade selected and saved | Condition panel: grade badge + definition (tap-to-expand on touch, not hover-only) + `Functions as designed` assertion (grades 1–5) or derived cosmetic descriptor (grades 6–8) |
| Error | Cross-check service unavailable (distinct from a contradiction) | All 8 grades enabled, no ceiling shown, "we'll check this against your disclosures shortly". Publishes normally (D-03); async re-check per E-18 |
| Partial | Grade set, flaw checklist incomplete | Grade held **provisional** — stored, shown to the seller, not yet a comp input. Ceiling re-computes and the cross-check re-runs when 13.02.02 completes; if the completed set invalidates the provisional grade, E-11's challenge fires before publish |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Grading should be free-text so sellers can describe nuance | Sellers genuinely want to explain; gear condition is nuanced | ❌ REJECTED: prose is unfilterable, uncomparable and unenforceable. The price guide cannot build a comp set from "some honest wear, plays great". Prose belongs *alongside* the enum as a description, never *instead of* it. Sub-domain D-02. |
| DT-02 | One scale can carry cosmetic and functional condition together | Reverb's scale does exactly this and buyers read it fluently | ✅ **CONFIRMED — tension resolved (supersedes the Step 3 "confirmed with tension" verdict).** The tension was real but rested on an assumption that never held: that a second dimension had to be a second *seller-authored enum*. It doesn't. The scale is asymmetric — grades 1–5 assert function with a cosmetic gradient; grades 6–8 state function and are cosmetically blind — so the two failure cases sit at opposite ends and each is closed by a **derived render** off the flaw set 13.02.02 already collects: `Functions as designed` at Good-and-above, and a derived cosmetic descriptor at Fair-and-below ("Non-Functioning · cosmetically Excellent"). Comp portability is preserved because the comp key never changes. The residual owner decision is much narrower than "one scale or two" — see Q-01. |
| DT-03 | A grade measures deviation from a pristine ideal | The instinctive reading of any condition scale | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the file's load-bearing correction. It grades a £6k factory-relic'd Custom Shop as Poor, and it has no answer for B-stock or for why a road-worn 1966 amp outvalues a Mint reissue. The correct baseline is **the bound catalog model's as-shipped state**. One rule, and relic'd finishes, B-stock, vintage patina and desirable wear all stop being special cases. Consequence: model binding (13.01.04) is a **semantic prerequisite** for grading, not a nice-to-have — which strengthens 13.01.04's own carrot-not-gate argument, since an unmatched listing now demonstrably degrades a second thing. |
| DT-04 | Grade definitions can be edited freely — they're just copy | Copy is soft; nobody versions help text | ❌ REJECTED: the definition **is** the grade. Reword "Very Good" and every live listing carrying it silently changes meaning, and every historical comp keyed to it now describes a scale that no longer exists — 13.04's entire time series is retroactively corrupted by a copy edit, invisibly, with no error anywhere. Definitions are immutable once published and versioned; listings store `(grade, definition_version)`. **This is the failure mode nobody designs for**: it has no symptom until the comps are already wrong. |
| DT-05 | The fix for the functional case is a second seller-set enum | The obvious reading of DT-02's tension | ❌ REJECTED: it costs comp portability (the thing Q-01 says argues hardest for the de-facto scale), adds a seller decision to a step already carrying one, and — decisively — **it can contradict the flaw set**. A seller-set `cosmetic: Mint` alongside a disclosed heavy finish crack is a new lie surface requiring a second cross-check. A *derived* descriptor cannot contradict the flaws because it is computed from them. Derive, don't ask. |
| DT-06 | The platform should compute the grade from the flaw set instead of asking the seller | It's a pure function of disclosed data — why make a human guess? Removes over-grading (the Musician's failure mode) at a stroke | ❌ REJECTED — three independent reasons, any one sufficient. (1) The flaw checklist **is not exhaustive by design** (13.02.02's "anything else?" free-text exists precisely because it can't be), so a computed grade would assert completeness the data never had. (2) A computed grade **launders seller liability onto the platform** — it converts sub-domain Q-02 ("is a mis-graded listing a platform obligation or a seller liability?") from an open question into a settled *yes, ours*, by accident, in a feature file. (3) The flaw set yields a **ceiling, not a value**: two units with identical disclosed flaws can honestly differ, and only the person holding the unit knows. Compute the ceiling, let the human choose beneath it, keep the liability where the knowledge is. |
| DT-07 | Grade is a buyer-facing attribute | The obvious framing — it exists so buyers can filter | ⚠️ **REFRAMED**: grade is also an **input to the price guide**, and therefore an **attack surface**. 13.04 keys comps on model × grade × originality, so a fabricated sale at a fabricated grade injects a fabricated comp — wash-trade a Mint '59 to yourself at £40k and you have moved the published value of every '59 on the platform. The multi-hyphenate makes this cheap: one human legitimately holds several accounts and entity memberships (band, studio), so "buyer and seller are the same person" is not even anomalous here. This file cannot fix it — **the arm's-length exclusion rule belongs to 13.04's comp selection** — but the requirement has to be raised from the side that creates the exposure, and no swept candidate raised it. |
| DT-08 | The grade facet is a neutral read surface | Facets are just filters | ❌ REJECTED — **the facet is the grade-inflation engine.** A buyer ticking only `Excellent` silently deletes every honest seller who graded conservatively, so conservative grading costs reach and inflation is *structurally rewarded*, not merely tempting. E-05's dispute-based correction fires far too late — the honest seller has already lost the sale, learned the lesson, and started grading Excellent. The mitigation this file owns: the grade facet is **inclusive-upward by default** (`Good & up`), never an exact-match multi-select default (CX-M16). The transparency mitigation — publishing a seller's grade-vs-dispute distribution — is a values decision and mirrors 13.09.03 DT-02's structural-conflict finding almost exactly. See Q-04. |
| DT-09 | Consumables are part of a unit's condition | They're physically part of the object | ❌ REJECTED: strings, tubes, drum heads, felts, batteries and cables are **expected to be replaced on arrival** and every experienced buyer already prices them at zero. Grading them would make a £4k guitar with dead strings Very Good, which is nonsense a domain expert would spot instantly and which would poison the comp set with a variable that costs £8 to eliminate. Explicitly excluded (D-06). The corollary matters and is easy to miss: a *worn* consumable is excluded, but a **missing essential** one is not — no tubes in a valve amp is a `missing-part: essential` flaw capping at Poor, because the amp does not work. |
| DT-10 | Non-visual flaws are covered because they're on the checklist | 13.02.02 collects them; the cap table applies to them | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP, not a solution.** Smoke odour, high noise floor, hum, drifting intonation — all cap the grade correctly, and **none can be photographed**. 13.02.02 D-03 (material flaws require a photo) is unenforceable for them, and 13.02.04's evidence pack — the artifact that makes every damage dispute adjudicable — is blind to the entire class. A smoke-smelling amp is the textbook "not as described" claim and the pack has literally nothing to say about it. Recording it honestly rather than pretending the cap table closes it. **The gap has since been closed by the sibling files, not by this one** — 13.02.02 D-03 authors the evidence medium per item (`audible` → clip, `undemonstrable` → text, never gated) and 13.02.04 D-12 makes the pack accept audio, so only the genuinely uncapturable residue (odour, monthly intermittents) stays unevidenced, and it is disclosed as such. See Q-03. |
| DT-11 | B-Stock belongs in the condition enum because Reverb puts it there | De-facto standard alignment (Q-02) argues for copying it | ❌ REJECTED: it is a category error, and it is *the same one* sub-domain D-01 exists to prevent. B-stock describes the **provenance of a defect** (factory-graded, distinct warranty per 13.03.05), not the **state** of the unit — a B-stock unit is still cosmetically Mint or Excellent, and collapsing it into the grade destroys that fact and makes the B-stock bucket uncomparable with itself. Adopting the de-facto scale (Q-02) should **not** extend to adopting its errors. Grade + listing-type flag, never one value. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

**Within 13.02 (sub-domain):**
- Constrained by **13.02.02 Mandatory Flaw Disclosure** ([path](./13.02.02-mandatory-flaw-disclosure.md)) — **trigger dependency + hard data requirement, now satisfied**. The Grade Ceilings table is a pure function of the flaw set and needs an axis and a severity token to execute. **Resolved by owner ratification (DQ-12.O1)**: the carrier is an authored **`(axis, severity-token)` pair on each severity option** (13.02.02 D-13), sitting beside the materiality verdict the same author already sets, never seller-facing. This file keeps one ceiling table and needs no per-category copy of it; 13.02.02 keeps its per-item severity language (their D-05) untouched.

- Feeds **13.02.04 Condition Evidence Pack** ([path](./13.02.04-condition-evidence-pack.md)) — **shared entity**. The grade and its `definition_version` seal into the pack; a dispute argues "was it Excellent on 4 March" against the definition in force *then*, not today's wording. This file owns the ceiling table and the grade semantics; it does not own — and must not copy — the per-category mapping.

Downward regrade auto-voids open offers (E-12); upward regrade notifies but preserves them (E-13); downward regrade on an auction with bids is refused and routes to cancellation (E-14). This extends 13.05.01's existing "live offers restrict the seller's price edits" to condition edits, which are materially the same act.
- Constrains **13.06.02 Order Lifecycle** — **state race**. Post-purchase downward regrade freezes the sold fact and grants the buyer one-click cancel with full refund (E-15). This is the grade-side answer to CX-02's open synthesis item on post-purchase disclosure edits.

Also a **corroboration source for D-15**: an intake grading is a professional bench assessment of the same repair, so it pre-fills and corroborates the repair-quality option (their D-05) without replacing the owner's claim.

A repair record already carries what was replaced, by whom and when (their D-03), which is exactly what distinguishes a professionally-executed stable repair from an amateur one. - Shares its scale with **13.10 Gear Rental & Backline** — condition at hire-out and return uses this scale; the *unit* is the anchor (domain D-04), not the rental.

- Reads **Domain 15 Gear Registry** ([path](../../15-gear-registry-ownership/)) — **shared entity**. A registry custody record contradicting a "Brand New" claim is challenged, never auto-corrected (E-16) — the same pre-fill-and-flag pattern as 13.02.03 D-03, and structurally impossible for Reverb (no registry).

This is a ranking-policy-adjacent decision: the default distributes reach across the seller base and, chosen wrongly, actively rewards dishonesty.
- Feeds **CX-M08 Reviews, Ratings & Portable Reputation** — **notification fan-out**. Systematic over-grading is a seller-performance signal, aggregated in the scorecard, never on the individual listing (E-05).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Grade is a required enum with inline published definitions | Per DT-01 — definitions must be at the point of choice or sellers guess. No default value: a pre-selected grade is a grade nobody chose. |
| D-02 | Impossible grade/flaw combinations are challenged at listing time, not silently accepted | Cheapest possible dispute prevention: one prompt now vs. an adjudication later. |
| D-03 | Grade cross-check **failure** never blocks publish; a cross-check **contradiction** does block the *edit* | Domain D-03 protects sellers from the platform's uncertainty (service down → publish, flag async, E-18). It does not license publishing a contradiction the seller just asserted — and E-11 blocks the edit, not the listing, so nothing stalls either way. |
| D-04 | **Grade measures deviation from the bound model's as-shipped state**, not from a pristine ideal | Per DT-03. Resolves relic'd gear, B-stock, vintage patina and desirable wear with one rule instead of four special cases. Makes 13.01.04 model binding a semantic prerequisite. |
| D-05 | **One seller-authored enum; the second dimension is derived, never asked** | Per DT-02/DT-05. `Functions as designed` at Good-and-above; derived cosmetic descriptor at Fair-and-below. Preserves comp portability (Q-01) and cannot contradict the flaw set. |
| D-06 | **Consumables are excluded from grading**; missing *essential* components are not | Per DT-09. Dead strings don't lower a grade; a valve amp with no tubes caps at Poor because it does not work. |
| D-07 | **Grade definitions are immutable once published and versioned**; listings store `(grade, definition_version)` | Per DT-04. A copy edit must not retroactively re-grade live listings or corrupt 13.04's time series. Symptomless failure mode — versioning is the only defence. |
| D-08 | **`functional: fails` forces Non-Functioning; every other flaw sets a ceiling** | The one case with no seller judgement left: a unit that does not work is Non-Functioning however it looks. The cosmetic truth is preserved by the derived descriptor (D-05), not by the grade. |
| D-09 | **Grade and flaws must stay mutually consistent on a live listing** | Per E-11. A live listing must never come to rest in a state where its own two fields contradict each other — without auto-downgrading (which asserts a fact on the seller's behalf) and without stalling (which violates domain D-03). |
| D-10 | **Downward regrade voids open offers; upward regrade notifies and preserves them** | Per E-12/E-13. Asymmetric because the harm is asymmetric — a buyer bound to a worse item is trapped; a buyer holding an offer on a better item is not. |
| D-11 | **Bulk grading gets a seller-set per-upload default, flagged and comp-weighted down** — never an exemption | Per E-21. An ungraded listing is unfilterable and uncomparable, which is the whole point of the enum. Resolves only the grading slice of domain Q-13. |
| D-12 | **B-Stock is a listing-type flag, not a grade** | Per DT-11. Adopting the de-facto scale (Q-02) must not extend to adopting its category error. |
| D-13 | **The grade facet defaults to inclusive-upward** (`Good & up`), never exact-match multi-select | Per DT-08. An exact-match default structurally rewards over-grading by deleting conservative sellers from the result set — the facet would otherwise *be* the inflation engine. |
| D-14 | **The flaw→ceiling projection is an authored `(axis, severity-token)` pair on each severity option** — authored in the shared per-category structured-attribute schema (13.01.01 D-14), held by catalog moderation, never seller-facing | Owner ratification (DQ-12.O1 / DQ-12.O2), resolving the hard data requirement this file raised on 13.02.02. It is the only carrier that leaves every locked decision standing: 13.02.02 D-05's per-item severity language survives because the pair is never shown to a seller; their D-06 survives because the checklist author sets the pair once per category exactly as they already set materiality; and this table still **caps** rather than sets. The consequence that matters most downstream: the Scale table and the versioned grade definitions need **no edit and no version bump** (D-07 intact), so 13.04's comp key is undisturbed. Authoring the ceiling grade directly on the option was rejected (it rewrites six Requires cells); keying the ceiling off the materiality verdict was rejected (it deletes three). |
| D-15 | **The repair-quality split is kept and is seller-asserted**: professionally-executed and stable caps at **Excellent**; amateur or unstable caps at **Good** | Owner ratification (DQ-12.O3). This axis changes what a grade *means*, which is versioned content under D-07, so it was decided on its own rather than as a side effect of D-14. Collapsing both rows to Good was rejected: it deletes a live Scale-table allowance on the false premise that no source supplies repair quality, when three do for on-platform repairs (13.08.02 records the repairer and what was replaced; 13.09.03 is a professional bench assessment; 13.02.02 DT-04 models a repair as a fact). The seller picks; the pick is a signed claim carrying their liability, and it is a description of the work rather than an assessment of its seriousness, so 13.02.02 D-06 is not violated. Where the platform holds a first-party record for the unit, the option is **pre-filled and corroborated** and contradictions are **surfaced, never auto-asserted** (13.02.02 D-07). No grade's meaning moves in either direction, so **no version bump** (D-07 intact). Corroboration degrades to seller-asserted-only until 13.08.02 Q-02 resolves — see Q-06 — which is also the common case, because most vintage repairs predate the platform. |
