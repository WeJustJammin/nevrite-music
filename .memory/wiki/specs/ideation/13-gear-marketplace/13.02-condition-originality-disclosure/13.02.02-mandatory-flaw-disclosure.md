# Feature: Mandatory Flaw Disclosure

> **Number**: 13.02.02
> **Parent**: [Condition, Originality & Disclosure](./13.02-condition-originality-disclosure-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

A structured, category-aware checklist of the specific defects that matter for this kind of gear, which the seller completes before publishing. Not a free-text "anything else?" box — an enumerated set: neck cracks and headstock repairs for guitars, tube socket and transformer condition for amps, keybed and edge-chip status for cymbals. It exists because "not as described" is the most common gear dispute, and almost all of it is preventable by asking the right question at listing time.

Its second, larger purpose is **seller armour** (DT-02): a defect declared through a structured field, evidenced, and timestamped before dispatch is a defect the seller can never be blamed for later. The feature is framed and worded throughout as protection for the person filling it in, because a feature framed as buyer protection is a feature sellers resist.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as seller) | Works a 6–12 item checklist for the category while listing — headstock/neck integrity, fret wear, electronics function, structural cracks. Pre-filled from any prior condition record the platform holds for this unit (DT-05), and must re-affirm every pre-filled answer rather than accept it silently. As buyer: reads material flaws first, cosmetic collapsed, never a flaw count (DT-08). The persona most likely to under-disclose a flaw they stopped noticing years ago. |
| Producer | Full (as seller) | Discloses on the **functional** axis — noisy pots, drifting calibration, dead channels, hum, intermittent jacks — which is where studio-gear defects live and where a cosmetic checklist discloses nothing. The persona who most uses the audio-clip evidence path (DT-10): their flaws are audible, not photographable. Also the persona with the most `Not tested` legitimacy (no bias probe, no calibration rig). |
| Operator | Full (as seller) | Discloses road wear and repair history on ex-rental/backline stock at volume. Never itemises 400 units at import — publishes `Not itemised` and completes disclosure at label print, holding the unit at the loading dock (D-08). Their fleet units frequently already have platform-held condition records from rental returns (13.10) and repairs (13.08.02), so pre-fill does the most work here. Needs draft persistence on a bad dock connection. |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads flaws as a buyer; has no seller surface anywhere in 13.03 and therefore never discloses. The persona least able to interpret "hairline check in the finish" — so every flaw item renders a plain-language **consequence** string ("cosmetic only — does not affect sound or playability"), never a definition and never a valuation claim (DT-09). A Fan who starts selling is a Musician-lens user, not a new persona. |

## Behavior

The checklist is **bound to the category of the catalog model the listing is matched to** (13.01.04), using the same per-category schema mechanism as 13.01.01's attributes and 13.02.03's component sets — **ratified as one shared mechanism, not three parallel ones** (DQ-12.O2; 13.01.01 D-14, 13.02.03 D-07). Each checklist item is authored with the category, not composed at runtime, and carries five properties:

| Item property | Values | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Answer set** | `Present` / `Absent`, plus `Not tested` **only** where `assessability = test-required` | The seller is holding the unit; blanket "Unknown" manufactures deniability (DT-13) |
| **Assessability** | `observable` (hand + eyes) / `test-required` (needs equipment or disassembly) | An amp seller genuinely cannot assess bias drift without a probe; forcing `Absent` fabricates a claim |
| **Severity enumeration** | Per-item, authored with the item — never a generic Light/Moderate/Severe scale (DT-11) | "Moderate crack" is meaningless. `Crack: finish-only / through-timber-stable / through-timber-repaired / active` is how a tech speaks |
| **Materiality** | Computed from the `(item, severity)` pair, not from the item alone | "Crack — finish only" is cosmetic; "Crack — through timber" is material. The seller never adjudicates materiality (D-06) |
| **Grade projection** | An authored `(axis, severity-token)` pair on **each severity option** — axis `cosmetic` \| `functional` \| `structural` \| `missing-part`, severity-token from 13.02.01's Grade Ceilings vocabulary | 13.02.01's ceiling table is a pure function of the flaw set and cannot execute without it. Set once per category by the same author who sets materiality, and **never seller-facing** (D-13) |

**Grade projection and repair quality** (D-13, D-14). The `(axis, severity-token)` pair is authored in the **shared per-category structured-attribute schema** and **catalog moderation holds the standing over it**, because it caps what every seller in that category may claim (13.01.01 D-14). It rides beside the materiality verdict the same author already sets; the seller never sees it and never picks it — they see per-item plain language, exactly as before. Where a category admits the distinction, a **repair** severity option **splits by repair quality** — professionally executed and stable versus amateur or unstable — because 13.02.01's ceiling table treats them differently (Excellent vs Good, their D-15). The seller's pick there is a **description of the work**, not an assessment of how serious it is, so D-06 stands: they are still never asked to adjudicate their own liability. Where the platform holds a first-party record for the unit, the option is pre-filled and corroborated per D-07; where it does not — the common case, because most vintage repairs predate the platform — the pick stands alone as a signed claim.

**Materiality test** — an `(item, severity)` pair is **material** if it satisfies **any** of three conditions:

1. **Structural** — affects, or has previously affected, the load-bearing integrity of the unit (headstock break or repair, neck reset, body/shell crack through material, cracked cymbal, cracked speaker cone or frame).
2. **Functional** — the unit does not fully perform its primary function, or performs it intermittently (dead pickup, dead channel, seized piston, drifting calibration, intermittent jack).
3. **Concealed** — not visible in a standard listing photo set and not implied by the grade (hidden repair under finish, water/smoke/mould/pest damage, internal corrosion, tube socket burn).

Everything else — buckle rash, finish checking, dings, hardware pitting, scratches — is **cosmetic**: implied by the grade (13.02.01), disclosed for the record, and **never photo-gated**. Widening the photo gate past the material set turns the only publish-blocking rule in the listing flow into noise, and noise gets clicked through.

**Evidence requirement** (D-03, made precise): every **material** flaw requires **one capture that shows the flaw itself** — not the unit generally — before publish. The capture medium follows the item's authored `evidence` property:

| Evidence property | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `photographable` | ≥1 photo framing the flaw | Headstock repair, body crack, cracked cone, corroded contacts |
| `audible` | ≥1 audio clip demonstrating it (CX-M10) | Hum, scratchy pot, tube rattle, drifting oscillator, dead channel (DT-10) |
| `undemonstrable` | Structured text only; **not publish-blocking** | Smoke odour, mould smell, "intermittent, once a month" |

**Disclosure is versioned and dated.** Every affirmation carries an `as-of` date and the account that made it. An order **pins the disclosure version the buyer bought against** — later edits never mutate what the buyer saw, and never mutate the sealed evidence pack (13.02.04 D-03). A pre-filled answer is not an answer until the seller re-affirms it (DT-06).

A completed disclosure earns a **`Condition itemised`** trust marker and eligibility for flaw-free buyer facets. An absent disclosure earns **`Condition not itemised`** and neither. The listing still publishes (domain D-03 — the flow never stalls); it simply carries none of the protection.

### Happy Path

1. Seller reaches the disclosure step; system resolves the bound model's category (13.01.04) and loads that category's checklist — 6–12 items, each with its severity enumeration and plain-language consequence string visible at the point of answering.
2. System pre-fills any item for which the platform already holds a first-party condition record for **this unit** (D-04 unit anchor): a rental return inspection (13.10), a repair record (13.08.02), an intake grading (13.09.03), or a prior sealed pack from an earlier sale on-platform. Each pre-filled item is marked `From your last record — 2025-11-04. Confirm it still holds.` and is **unanswered** until touched (DT-05, D-07).
3. Seller answers each item `Present` / `Absent`, or `Not tested` where the item is `test-required`.
4. For each `Present`, seller picks a severity from that item's own enumeration. System computes materiality — the seller is never asked "is this serious?".
5. For each material flaw, system requests the item's authored evidence medium: a photo of the flaw, an audio clip demonstrating it, or (for `undemonstrable` items) structured text only.
6. Seller adds free-text under **"Anything the checklist didn't ask about?"** — in addition to the enum, never instead of it (D-02).
7. System re-runs the grade cross-check (13.02.01, CX-01) and challenges contradictions inline: *"A repaired headstock isn't Mint — did you mean Excellent?"*. If the platform's own record contradicts the answer, the contradiction is surfaced to the seller before publish (D-07).
8. Seller affirms. The disclosure is stamped with an `as-of` date and the affirming account; evidence writes into the pack (13.02.04, CX-02).
9. Listing publishes with `Condition itemised`, material flaws rendered first and expanded, cosmetic collapsed behind "and 6 cosmetic notes" — **never a flaw count badge** (DT-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Listing unmatched to a catalog model (13.01.04) | No category → no category checklist. Falls back to the **generic 6**: cosmetic wear · structural damage · functional fault · electrical safety · missing/damaged parts of the unit · smoke/water/mould/pest damage. All free-text severity. A real cost of staying unmatched | Generic checklist + "Link a model to get the proper checklist for this gear" |
| Seller marks every item `Absent` on a 60-year-old instrument | Not blocked — implausible is not impossible, and a genuinely mint survivor exists. But the claim raises its own evidentiary bar | Soft challenge once: *"No flaws at all on a 1962 instrument is unusual. Sure? Buyers scrutinise mint-vintage claims hardest."* Dismissible; not repeated |
| Flaw exists but isn't on the checklist | The checklist is deliberately bounded (DT-03) and can never be exhaustive. Free-text carries the tail. Recurring free-text themes are the input that earns a category a new item (D-09) | "Anything the checklist didn't ask about?" — always present, never a substitute for the enum |
| Seller omits a flaw that surfaces after delivery | This is the dispute. The pinned disclosure version + the pack (13.02.04) is the baseline domain 24 adjudicates against | Buyer opens a not-as-described claim; the disclosure as-of dispatch is the fixed record both sides argue from |
| Category checklist missing for a niche category (e.g. a theremin, a hurdy-gurdy) | Generic 6 + free-text; flagged to catalog work. Never invent items at runtime — an unauthored item has no severity enumeration and no materiality, so it cannot be evidence-gated | Generic checklist; no visible failure |
| **Bulk import of 200–400 units (13.03.04)** | Per-unit checklists at import are unusable, and **templating answers across units is prohibited** — a templated `Absent` is a fabricated signed claim at volume, worse than silence (DT-14, D-08). What *is* templatable: category binding, checklist selection, `Not tested` defaults. Import publishes every row as **`Condition not itemised`** — honest, unprotected, excluded from flaw-free facets. Disclosure is then re-prompted at label print, where the seller is physically holding the unit | Review-queue pattern matching 13.03.04's unmatched-model queue: *"400 published · 400 not itemised. Itemise now, or we'll ask when each one ships."* |
| New/counted-stock fungible inventory (domain D-06, regime 2) | **The checklist is a qty=1 artifact.** A sealed new unit has no unit-specific flaw set; grade `New` implies the material set is absent by definition. Requiring 200 checklists for 200 identical new pedals is the same category error as unit-grading fungible stock | No checklist step at all for `New` counted-stock listings |
| Not-itemised listing sells, seller itemises at label print, reveals a **material** flaw | The buyer bought unknown condition and is now told a fact they never saw. **Dispatch pauses**; buyer must re-confirm. No response in **72h** → auto-cancel, full refund, no seller penalty (they did the right thing late). This is the mechanism that stops "not itemised" being a gate-dodge | Buyer: *"The seller has itemised this before shipping: repaired headstock (structural). Confirm, or cancel for a full refund."* |
| Seller edits disclosure on a **live listing with pending offers** (13.03.02 D-03 restricts edits) | Asymmetric, in the buyer's favour. **Material flaw added** → all pending offers void + offerers notified; the offer was priced against a different fact. **Material flaw removed/downgraded** → offers stand, offerers notified and may withdraw. Cosmetic edits → no offer effect | Offerer: *"The seller disclosed a new material flaw. Your offer was withdrawn — review and re-offer if you still want it."* |
| Seller edits disclosure **after purchase, before dispatch** | Same 72h re-confirm mechanism as the label-print case. Material change → dispatch pauses, buyer confirms or cancels penalty-free. Non-material → notify only, no pause. A material post-purchase disclosure change that the buyer never sees would void the entire point of the pack | Buyer notified with the delta, not the whole disclosure |
| Seller edits disclosure **after dispatch / after the pack seals** | The edit is permitted on the listing (it may be relisted) but **cannot mutate the sealed pack or the order's pinned version** (13.02.04 D-03). The order forever shows what was disclosed at dispatch | Seller: *"This won't change what your buyer bought against — that record is sealed."* |
| **Retraction** of a material flaw ("I was wrong, it isn't cracked") | Permitted, versioned, and the prior affirmation is retained forever — a retraction never deletes history. Retraction inside an active order is treated as a material change (72h re-confirm) because it is price-raising and reverses a signed claim | Seller: *"Your earlier disclosure stays on record. Retraction is logged, not erased."* |
| **The platform's own record contradicts the seller** — rental return logged a cracked cone in March; seller marks it Absent | The strongest signal in this file, and structurally impossible for a marketplace without a registry, a repair record and a rental fleet in one product (D-18). Behaviour mirrors 13.02.03 D-03: **surface the contradiction to the seller, never auto-assert.** The seller signs the disclosure and carries the liability | Seller: *"Your rental return on 2026-03-11 recorded a cracked cone. You've marked it Absent — has it been repaired, or is this a correction?"* Answering "repaired" reclassifies it, it does not erase it |
| Seller resolves the contradiction dishonestly and publishes anyway | Publishes (domain D-03). The contradiction and its resolution are logged to the pack and the audit ledger (CX-M19). Whether the platform *acts* on knowingly-contradicted disclosure is a policy call, not an agent's | See Q-04 |
| **Relisting a stale disclosure** (13.03.02) — unit was listed 14 months ago | Gear deteriorates in storage. Prior disclosure pre-fills but every item reverts to unanswered when the `as-of` date is **older than 90 days**; the seller re-affirms as-of today (DT-06). Under 90 days, pre-filled items are shown affirmed with their date and can be bulk-confirmed | *"This disclosure is from 2025-05-02. Gear changes in a year — confirm each item as it is today."* |
| **Checklist schema changes after publication** — a new item is added to the guitar checklist | Live listings do **not** retroactively become non-compliant. A disclosure is a claim against the checklist version in force on its `as-of` date. The new item appears unanswered on the next edit or relist | No change to the live listing; nothing accuses the seller of an omission they were never asked about |
| Media capture unavailable at the moment of publish (our outage) | **The photo gate is waived when the failure is ours.** Blocking a seller's publish because our media service is down is punishing them for our fault — and it is the only blocking rule in the flow, so it must be exquisitely narrow | Publishes with `Evidence pending — headstock repair`. Seller re-prompted on next visit and at label print. Never supplied → the flaw stands disclosed but unevidenced, and that is the seller's exposure, stated plainly |
| Photo supplied is blurry, dark, or doesn't show the flaw | Accepted. The platform makes **no claim to evaluate photo quality** — asserting a computer-vision capability here would be fabricating a mechanism. Its evidentiary weight is whatever it is, and that is the seller's risk | No quality gate; the consequence line already says what a good photo is for |
| Photo shows a crack the checklist marks `Absent` | **Not detectable at listing time.** No CV claim (see above). It surfaces in the dispute, where it is devastating for the seller — a contradiction between their own evidence and their own claim | Nothing at listing time. Domain 24 has both artifacts |
| Grade `Non-functioning / for parts` selected (13.02.01) | An itemised checklist on a for-parts unit is absurd — the answer to most items is "it's broken". Checklist collapses to a **required free-text "what's wrong with it"** + all `test-required` items default to `Not tested`. Materiality gating is suspended: the entire listing is a disclosed defect | *"For-parts listings just need the story. What's wrong with it?"* |
| Free-text contains a phone number, email, or off-platform solicitation | Free-text is UGC. Routes to moderation (CX-M11/domain 24). Directly serves the Musician anti-persona ("takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees") and the Operator's storefront policies (13.12) | Standard moderation surface — not a disclosure-specific behaviour |
| Free-text contains a slur or abusive content | Same UGC path. Noted because disclosure free-text is an *unmoderated-looking* field on a commerce form, which is exactly where this gets missed | Standard moderation surface |
| **Two staff edit the same Operator listing's disclosure concurrently** | Last-write-wins is wrong for a signed claim — it would attribute one person's affirmation to another. The second writer is shown the conflict and must re-affirm the contested items under their own account. Attribution is per-item, per-account | *"Someone else updated this disclosure 40 seconds ago. Review the 3 changed items and confirm."* Mechanism routes to `/create-prd-architecture` (domain-index concurrency routing) |
| Draft lost mid-checklist — Operator on a bad connection at a loading dock | The persona's stated context. Answers persist per item as they're made, not on submit; resumable from any device | Resume banner: *"7 of 11 answered — pick up where you left off"* |
| **Multi-hyphenate, two roles at once**: Operator sells an ex-rental unit that is currently out on a live rental booking (13.10) | The unit's condition is not knowable right now — it's in a van in another city, and its condition record is the *last return*, not today. Disclosure of an out-on-hire unit is inherently `as-of` the last return | Pre-fill from last return + hard notice: *"This unit is out on hire until 2026-08-02. You're disclosing its condition as of its last return — confirm on return before it ships."* Re-prompt fires on return |
| **Repair quality on a unit the platform holds a repair record for** (13.08.02) — the seller picks the amateur/unstable option on a repair our own bench record describes as professional, or the reverse | Pre-fill and corroborate, never auto-assert (D-07, D-14). The record fills the option, the seller re-affirms it, and a contradiction is surfaced before publish and logged however the seller resolves it. The pair behind whichever option is affirmed projects to 13.02.01's ceiling either way — Excellent for professional/stable, Good for amateur/unstable | Seller: *"Our repair record for this unit (2025-09-14, bench) describes a professional, stable repair. You've marked it amateur — is this a correction?"* |
| **Repair predates the platform** — a headstock repair done in 1988 | The common case on vintage, and there is nothing to corroborate against. The seller's pick stands alone as a signed claim. Corroboration for **off-platform** repairs is blocked on 13.08.02 Q-02 and degrades to nothing rather than to a guess | No pre-fill, no record reference, no inferred quality — the plain-language option and the seller's name on it |
| **Multi-hyphenate, two roles at once**: Producer sells outboard they logged a repair against (13.08.02) as the repairer | They are both the person who fixed it and the person selling it. Their repair record is first-party and *stronger* evidence than a disclosure — pre-fill carries it and the mod side (a re-cap) lands on 13.02.03's axis simultaneously (DT-04) | *"You re-capped this in 2025-09. That's a mod (originality) and a resolved fault (flaw) — both pre-filled."* |
| Buyer asks about a flaw in a listing message; seller answers "no cracks anywhere" | The most common real condition interaction on any gear marketplace, and today it lives as prose in a chat log. **Promotable**: the answer can be one-tapped into the structured disclosure, timestamped and pinned to the pack (DT-12, D-10). Unpromoted, it remains adjudicable prose — worse for everyone, including the seller | Seller: *"Add 'Cracks: Absent' to your disclosure? It'll protect you if this comes up later."* Buyer sees the disclosure update, not just a reply |
| Unit has a genuine **safety** defect — original two-prong mains lead and a death cap on a vintage amp, frayed cord, a recalled unit | Material and concealed under the test, so it discloses and evidence-gates like any other flaw. But a domain expert would ask whether *disclosure is sufficient* for a unit that can electrocute someone, or whether the platform has a duty beyond it. Selling these is routine and legal in most markets; the platform's own obligation is not an agent's call | Disclosed as material with a safety consequence string. Whether sale is also gated → **Q-03** |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Category resolved from the bound model; checklist + prior condition records being fetched | Checklist skeleton with the item count already known ("11 items for Electric Guitar") — never an indeterminate spinner on a form the seller is about to fill in |
| **Empty** | No category resolvable — listing unmatched (13.01.04) | Generic 6 + "Link a model to get the proper checklist for this gear" CTA. Free-text severity throughout |
| **Populated** | All items answered, all material flaws evidenced, affirmed with an `as-of` date | `Condition itemised` marker; material flaws rendered first and expanded with consequence strings; cosmetic collapsed behind a summary line; no count badge |
| **Error** | Checklist lookup fails, or media capture is unavailable | Checklist down → free-text fallback, publish proceeds, never stall (domain D-03). Media down → photo gate **waived**, `Evidence pending` on the listing, re-prompt at next visit and label print |
| **Partial — in progress** | Some items answered, evidence outstanding | Per-item progress; publish blocked **only** on missing evidence for material flaws where evidence is capturable: *"Photo required for: headstock repair (structural)"*. Everything else publishes |
| **Partial — not itemised** | Bulk import, or seller skipped the step entirely | Listing is live with `Condition not itemised`: excluded from flaw-free facets, no trust marker, no seller-side evidentiary protection. Consequence stated once, choice left. Re-prompted at label print |
| **Partial — stale** | Pre-filled disclosure with an `as-of` date older than 90 days (relist) | Items shown but unanswered: *"This is from 2025-05-02. Confirm each item as it is today."* Listing publishes as not-itemised until re-affirmed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A single generic flaw checklist works across all gear | Simplicity instinct | ❌ REJECTED: the same reasoning as 13.01.01 DT-01. "Fret wear" is meaningless on a microphone; "diaphragm condition" is meaningless on a cymbal. A generic checklist asks wrong questions, gets ignored, and therefore discloses nothing. Checklists must be category-derived. |
| DT-02 | Mandatory disclosure primarily protects the buyer | The obvious framing — it's a buyer-protection feature | ⚠️ REFRAMED (partially rejected): its **larger** value is protecting the *honest seller*. A seller who disclosed a repair via a structured field, with a photo, timestamped before sale, is immune to a later "it arrived damaged" claim — the evidence pack (13.02.04) proves the state at dispatch. Framed as buyer protection, sellers experience disclosure as friction and resist it. Framed as seller armour, they complete it willingly. **This reframe drives the UX copy** — the same insight as the Producer persona's "make the lazy path the correct path". |
| DT-03 | A category checklist should be exhaustive — ask everything that could be wrong | Completeness instinct; a defect not asked about is a defect not disclosed | ❌ REJECTED: an exhaustive guitar checklist is 50+ items, and **every additional item lowers the completion quality of the items that matter**. A 50-item checklist produces click-through, and click-through produces *signed false claims at scale* — strictly worse than no checklist, because a fabricated "Absent" is evidence against the seller and misleads the buyer. The checklist is bounded to the **dispute-generating set** — the ~6–12 flaws that actually produce not-as-described claims for that category. Free-text carries the tail (D-02), and recurring free-text themes are what earn a category a new item (D-09). Length is a cost paid in honesty. |
| DT-04 | A repair belongs on the flaw axis; a mod belongs on the originality axis; they are cleanly separable | CX R-02's rule — "a flaw is unintended deterioration, a mod is an intentional change" | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: the rule holds, but a **repair is one event that produces a fact on both axes**, and R-02's clean line hides it. A repaired headstock is (a) a flaw — a break happened, severity `repaired` — and (b) an originality fact if the repair replaced original parts. A refret is (a) fret wear reaching `needs refret` and (b) non-original fret wire. Neither axis alone tells the truth, and forcing a choice makes the unit un-findable: a buyer filtering "no headstock breaks" must find it on the **flaw** axis, because they are asking about the break, not the glue. **So: a repair discloses here as the flaw's remediation severity, and simultaneously on 13.02.03 as an originality declaration. Same event, two axes, never merged** — which is exactly sub-domain D-01 holding under pressure rather than breaking. This is the case that would have been mis-modelled. |
| DT-05 | The platform already knows about flaws it is asking the seller to remember | 13.02.03 DT-02 (registry pre-fill) applied to flaws — and the D-04 unit anchor | ✅ CONFIRMED: and **stronger here than in 13.02.03**. There, pre-fill comes from the registry — what a *previous custodian* recorded, a second-hand claim. Here, four sources are **first-party platform records about this unit**: a rental return inspection (13.10), a repair record (13.08.02), an intake grading (13.09.03), and a prior sealed pack from an earlier sale on-platform. The platform *observed* these. Asking a seller to recall from memory what our own record already states is the reconstruction failure the problem statement names as the root cause, committed by the platform that exists to end it. **Constraint, inherited from 13.02.03 D-03: pre-fill, never auto-assert.** The seller signs and carries the liability. This is structurally impossible for Reverb — it requires a marketplace, a rental fleet, a repair record and a registry in one product, which is D-18's consolidation-enables-provenance claim producing a concrete feature. |
| DT-06 | A disclosure, once made, remains true | Implicit in treating disclosure as a listing attribute rather than a dated claim | ❌ REJECTED: **gear deteriorates in storage.** An amp listed 14 months ago and relisted today has a scratchy pot it didn't have then; a guitar that sat in an unheated garage has finish checking and a bowed neck. A pre-filled disclosure the seller glanced past is a **signed claim they never actually made** — the worst artifact this feature can produce, because it looks like diligence. So: every disclosure carries an `as-of` date and an affirming account; pre-fill older than 90 days reverts every item to unanswered. The 90-day line is the seasonal-change boundary for wood and electrolytics — a guitar surviving one winter is the smallest interval where the answer genuinely changes. This is "capture at the moment it's true" applied to a fact with a shelf life. |
| DT-07 | Completeness — "no case", "missing footswitch", "no power supply" — is a flaw and belongs on this checklist | A missing footswitch on a Twin Reverb is a ~$150 fact and one of the most common not-as-described gripes after cosmetics; it *feels* like a defect | ❌ REJECTED as a category error, with a caveat that matters. **Absence is not deterioration.** A missing case is not a flaw of the unit — the unit is fine; the *package* is smaller. Folding it in would corrupt the axis exactly the way DT-01 corrupts the checklist, and it would put "no case" on the same list as "cracked neck" for a buyer scanning materiality. It belongs to the listing's **"what's included"** set (13.03.01), and "original case included" is already adjacent to 13.02.03's "originals included" concept. **The caveat**: the *disclosure obligation* is identical in kind and the dispute rate is comparable, so "what's included" needs the same structured, enumerated, per-category treatment this feature gets — flagged to 13.03.01 rather than absorbed. **The boundary**: a part missing *from the unit* (no power tubes, a missing key, no tremolo arm where it's integral) is a **functional flaw** and stays here; an accessory, case, or box is "includes" and leaves. |
| DT-08 | More disclosure is always better, so the UI should encourage and display volume | The honest instinct — surface everything the seller said | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the sharpest UX consequence in the file. Two failures. (1) **Defensive over-disclosure**: sellers who disclose 43 trivia items to look thorough drown the one headstock repair; the buyer's eye glazes and the material fact is functionally concealed *by* disclosure. (2) **A flaw count punishes honesty**: a "12 flaws" badge makes the diligent seller's listing look worse than the lazy seller's blank one — which collapses DT-02's seller-armour framing completely and teaches sellers that disclosure costs them. So: **never render a flaw count.** Material flaws render first and expanded; cosmetic collapses behind a summary line. A listing with completed disclosure shows `Condition itemised` — a **trust marker, not a scarlet letter** — and a blank one shows `Condition not itemised`. The diligent listing must look *better* than the silent one or the whole mechanism inverts. |
| DT-09 | Each flaw item needs a definition so buyers know what it means | Reasonable — 13.02.01 D-01 does exactly this for grades | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: not a **definition** — a **consequence**. "Finish checking = lacquer crazing from thermal cycling" tells the Fan nothing they can act on; "cosmetic only — does not affect sound or playability" tells them everything. The Fan persona explicitly "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" and has no calibration for gear vocabulary. **The constraint: a consequence string states functional and structural impact only, never a valuation claim.** "This reduces value by about half on a vintage instrument" is (a) 13.04's job, (b) frequently wrong, and (c) makes the platform the author of a price opinion on a specific unit — an obligation nobody voted for. State what it *does*; let 13.04 and the market state what it's *worth*. |
| DT-10 | A photo is the evidence medium for a disclosed flaw | 13.02.02 D-03 and 13.02.04 both assume photos throughout | ❌ REJECTED as insufficient — **and this is where a music platform diverges from a generic marketplace.** The Producer persona's flaws are overwhelmingly *not photographable*: hum, a scratchy pot, tube rattle, a drifting oscillator, a dead channel. A photo of a noisy potentiometer is a photo of a knob. But **the flaw is audible, and this is a platform with audio playback as a cross-cut (CX-M10) and demo clips already in the listing flow (13.03.01)** — a 10-second clip of the hum is better evidence than any photo could be, and the seller is holding an instrument and a phone. So evidence medium is authored per item: `photographable` → photo; `audible` → clip; `undemonstrable` → text, not publish-blocking. **The third bucket is the one that would have been missed**: smoke odour, mould smell, an intermittent monthly fault. These are material by the concealment test, are top-tier dispute generators, and **cannot be captured in any medium**. A rule of "material ⇒ photo" would have either blocked those listings forever or forced a fake photo. Evidence is required *where evidence exists*. |
| DT-11 | Severity is a shared scale — Light / Moderate / Severe — reused across all items | Schema economy; one enum is cheaper than 40 | ❌ REJECTED: "moderate crack" is not a thing anyone has ever said. A generic scale is unanswerable by the seller, uninterpretable by the buyer, and — fatally — **materiality cannot be computed from it**, because "moderate" means cosmetic on finish checking and structural on a body crack. Severity options are authored **per item**, in the language a tech actually uses: `Fret wear: light / grooved, needs level / needs refret`. `Crack: finish-only / through-timber, stable / through-timber, repaired / active`. This is D-01's category-derivation logic applied one level down, and it is what makes the `(item, severity) → materiality` computation possible at all, which is what lets D-06 keep the seller out of the materiality decision. |
| DT-12 | Disclosure happens on the listing form | The form is where the fields are | ❌ REJECTED as incomplete: **the highest-volume condition disclosure on any gear marketplace happens in messages, and it is entirely unstructured.** "Any cracks?" → "No, none anywhere, it's clean" is a claim with the full weight of a checklist answer, made by the seller, relied on by the buyer, priced into the sale — and it lives as prose in a chat log that domain 24 must later read and interpret. This is precisely the platform's founding failure mode: *the fact was true, someone said it, and no system captured it as a fact.* So: **a condition claim made in a listing message is promotable into the structured disclosure in one tap** — timestamped, pinned to the pack, protecting the seller who said it. Unpromoted, it stays adjudicable prose, which is what everyone else offers. Capture-at-source applied to the messaging surface. |
| DT-13 | "Unknown" should be a valid flaw answer, mirroring 13.02.03 D-02's first-class Unknown | Symmetry with the sibling feature; both are seller declarations on the same form | ❌ REJECTED as a blanket rule — and CX R-02 predicted exactly this asymmetry ("'Unknown' is valid for one and not the other"). Originality earns Unknown because it is genuinely unknowable without disassembly and a provenance chain: nobody knows if the pickups are original without pulling them. **A flaw is different: the seller is physically holding the unit and can look at it.** "Unknown: is there a crack?" is not honesty, it is manufactured deniability, and it would become the universal default within a week. **But the blanket rule fails too**: some flaws genuinely require equipment or disassembly — bias drift, calibration, internal corrosion, transformer condition. A Producer without a bias probe truly cannot assess it, and forcing `Absent` fabricates a claim while forcing `Present` is a lie in the other direction. **Resolution: no blanket Unknown; `Not tested` is a third answer available only on items the checklist authors as `test-required`.** And `Not tested` on a functional item is itself an honest price signal that protects the seller — the DT-02 framing again. |
| DT-14 | Bulk sellers should get templated flaw sets so disclosure scales — set the defaults once, apply to 400 rows | The obvious answer to Q-01, and the only one that makes bulk feel solved | ❌ REJECTED, and it is the most dangerous idea in this file precisely because it looks like the solution. **A templated `Absent` is a fabricated signed claim, replicated 400 times.** It converts a workload problem into a liability event: the seller has now personally attested to the condition of 400 units they never inspected, each attestation is evidence in a future dispute, and each is false. The failure is worse than no disclosure, because silence misleads nobody. What is legitimately templatable is everything *except the answers*: category binding, checklist selection, `Not tested` defaults on `test-required` items. **The resolution is to stop treating "unanswered" as a failure**: bulk publishes as `Condition not itemised` — honest, live, and simply carrying none of the protection — and re-prompts at label print, where the seller is holding the actual unit at the loading dock. This is the Operator's own stated context, it is where 13.02.04 already prompts, and it converts an impossible bulk task into 400 easy 30-second ones spread across months. **Don't gate the absence — price it.** |
| DT-15 | An electrical-safety defect is just another material flaw | The test classifies it cleanly: concealed + functional + structural risk | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH TENSION — the classification is right and probably insufficient. A vintage amp with an original two-prong cord and an intact death cap is (a) extremely common, (b) legal to sell in most markets, (c) desirable to collectors *in original condition*, and (d) able to kill the buyer. Disclosure handles the information asymmetry perfectly and does nothing about the hazard. Whether a marketplace that **knows** a unit is unsafe — because we asked, and the seller told us — may simply publish it with a warning is a product, legal and values question, not an agent's call. Note the trap: the platform is in a *worse* position than one that never asked, because it has documented knowledge. **The safety consequence string is specified here; the sale gate is not.** See Q-03. |
