# Feature: Condition Evidence Pack & Dispute Baseline

> **Number**: 13.02.04
> **Parent**: [Condition, Originality & Disclosure](./13.02-condition-originality-disclosure-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list)
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The timestamped, sealed record of what this unit demonstrably looked like — and sounded like — at listing and at dispatch, so that when a buyer says it arrived damaged there is a factual baseline instead of two memories. Disclosure (13.02.02/03) records what the seller *claimed*; the evidence pack records what was *shown*, when, by whom, and who had custody of the object at that moment.

Without it, every damage dispute is one memory against another — which is the exact failure the platform's whole thesis exists to prevent (D-18), applied to chattel instead of splits. **Six independent consumers depend on it** (13.07.04, 13.08.01, 13.08.02, 13.09.03, 13.10, domain 24), which makes it the most depended-upon node in this domain.

**Why this exists as a node**: the sweep listed disclosure and it listed damage claims (#13), but nothing connecting them. Damage claims without a pre-dispatch baseline are unresolvable — the claims process presupposes an artifact the candidate list never created.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | **As seller**: their listing photos and required flaw photos (13.02.02 D-03) seal into the pack without them knowing the pack exists; at label print they are asked for 3 dispatch frames including the packed box. The persona most likely to ship a guitar in its gig bag inside a thin carton and discover at claim time that the packing *was* the argument. **As buyer**: prompted to film the sealed box before cutting it open — the single act that protects them, and the one their instinct fights. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Ships high-value outboard where the claim turns on whether the transformer was already loose or the channel already dead. **The persona for whom a photo-only pack is useless** (DT-03): their disclosures are functional — noisy pots, drifting calibration, failed channels — none of which a photograph can carry. They capture a 20-second audio/video pass of the unit at level, which is the only baseline that can adjudicate "it arrived buzzing". Also the persona most likely to be the *interested inspector* under 13.06.04 (DT-08). |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Ships and receives fleet gear constantly; the pack is the only practical way to run condition accountability at volume. **Dispatch capture happens on a phone, at a loading dock, on bad signal** — the persona's stated constraint — so capture must work offline and reconcile later (CX-M32), with capture time and upload time recorded separately (D-08). Also the persona whose gear crosses custody boundaries that are not sales at all: rental out, rental return, trade-in intake (DT-09). |
| Fan | Read-only (own orders; may append arrival evidence) | Sees the seller's sealed pack for gear they bought — **but only after their own arrival capture seals or is declined** (DT-07). Cannot see any other party's pack, cannot see packs for orders they are not a party to, and has no seller side. The persona least likely to photograph a box before opening it and most likely to be the one holding the loss when they didn't. Arrival prompt copy states the consequence in plain language, never in evidentiary jargon. |

## Behavior

The pack accumulates automatically from artifacts the flow already produces — listing photos, required flaw photos (13.02.02 D-03), mod photos (13.02.03), dispatch capture, carrier scan events, arrival capture — each timestamped and immutable once sealed. **It is a byproduct, not a chore**: the seller is never asked to "build an evidence pack", they are asked for media at moments they were already holding a phone.

Four properties define it:

1. **Byproduct, never assembled** (D-01) — evidence gathered at dispute time is reconstruction, which is the failure being fixed.
2. **Sealed at dispatch, append-only after** (D-03) — an editable baseline is not a baseline. Post-seal media attaches as a labelled addendum and never overwrites.
3. **Every frame carries provenance** (D-07) — capturer identity, role-at-capture, whether that party had an interest, device capture time, server receipt time, and which of those two the platform can vouch for.
4. **Weight, never eligibility** (D-06) — missing evidence changes how strong a claim is, never whether it can be made.

The pack accepts **photo, video and audio** (CX-M10 already carries audio playback). It ingests **carrier scan events automatically** (CX-M14): acceptance weight, transit scans, damage/exception scans, delivery timestamp. That third-party evidence costs neither party an action and neither party can contest it, which makes it the most valuable material in the pack (DT-06).

The pack is scoped to a **custody boundary**, not to a sale (DT-09). A sale is one kind of boundary; rental dispatch, rental return, consignment intake and repair intake are others, and they need the identical artifact.

### Happy Path

1. Seller lists an amp. Listing photos and the required flaw photo for the disclosed torn grille cloth (13.02.02 D-03) write into the pack, timestamped at upload. Seller is shown no "evidence pack" UI at all.
2. Seller is prompted once, inline in the listing flow: *"Add 20 seconds of it running — this is what protects you if a buyer says it arrived buzzing."* Seller records an audio/video pass at level. It writes to the pack.
3. Buyer purchases. The pack snapshots the disclosure state **as it stood at purchase** — this is the state the buyer bought against (D-05).
4. Seller prints a shipping label (CX-M14). The dispatch prompt fires at label print — the moment the seller is already at the object with a phone: *"3 photos: the unit, the unit packed, the sealed box."*
5. Seller captures 3 frames. Upload begins. The label prints regardless — printing is never blocked on media (D-02).
6. Pack **seals** at label print + 15 minutes, or on carrier acceptance scan, whichever is first. In-flight uploads that land inside the window seal with the pack; anything later attaches as a post-seal addendum with its capture time preserved.
7. Carrier accepts the parcel. The acceptance scan and the weighed weight ingest into the pack automatically (CX-M14). Nobody was asked for them.
8. Delivery scan fires. Buyer is prompted within the notification itself (CX-M07): *"Before you open it — film the box. 30 seconds. If it's damaged inside, this is the difference between a refund and an argument."* **The seller's dispatch half is not shown to the buyer at this point** (DT-07).
9. Buyer films the sealed box, then unpacks and films the unit. Arrival evidence seals. The **full pack now opens to both parties**, dispatch half included.
10. No dispute: the pack is inert and nobody ever looks at it. Dispute: 13.07.04 / 13.08.01 / domain 24 read the pack as the baseline — state at dispatch vs. state at arrival, with the carrier's own scans between them.
11. On settlement, the sealed pack accrues to the unit's registry record (13.06.05), subject to Q-03's visibility decision.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller skips dispatch capture | Pack seals with listing evidence only — weaker but valid. **Not blocking** (D-02) | "Without dispatch photos, a damage claim is harder to defend." Consequence stated, choice left. Pack renders `Partial`. |
| Buyer opens a damage claim with no arrival evidence | Claim proceeds. Evidentiary weight is lower; eligibility is untouched (D-06) | At claim open: "Do you have anything from when it arrived? Even a phone photo helps." Never a gate. |
| **Listing uses stock/catalog photos on a used unit** | Detected by comparison against the bound model's canonical reference media (13.01.01) — the platform owns both sides, so it can compare. **Publish is not blocked** (domain D-03). The listing is labelled, and the pack renders `No unit-specific evidence` | Seller: "These look like catalog photos. A pack with no photos of *your* unit can't defend you." Buyer: a listing-level badge, "No photos of this specific unit." In a "not as described" dispute the seller has no baseline and the dispute resolves against them (D-04). Repeat offences fan out to CX-M08 and domain 24. |
| Stock photos on **new/sealed** stock | **Legitimate.** You cannot photograph a sealed pedal without unsealing it. The rule is condition-conditional: it binds on used grades only (13.02.01) | No label, no penalty. The pack's baseline for sealed stock is the unopened box at dispatch. |
| Photos are of a different unit entirely (deliberate swap fraud) | The pack cannot detect this from imagery alone. Three counterweights: the required serial frame (D-04), the carrier's acceptance weight (a Tele is not a Twin Reverb), and the fact that **used gear's disclosed flaws are an identity signature** — a wear pattern is a fingerprint no two units share (DT-06) | Escalates to domain 24 with the serial frame, the weight record and the flaw imagery attached. |
| Buyer photographs a *different, already-broken* unit as arrival evidence | Same counterweights, inverted. The dispatch flaw imagery is compared against the arrival imagery — the wear pattern either matches or it doesn't | Domain 24 adjudicates on the signature, not on the assertion. |
| **Buyer opens the box before filming it** | The single most common failure and the one nobody designs for. The packing evidence is destroyed with the box; the carrier's liability argument dies with it (13.07.01, 13.07.04) | Arrival prompt fires **on the delivery scan**, phrased as "before you open it", not after. If the box is already open, capture still proceeds and is labelled `post-unpacking` — weaker, still evidence. |
| Arrival capture happens 6 hours after delivery | Between delivery and capture, the buyer had custody. Evidence taken inside **60 minutes of the carrier's delivery scan** is labelled `at-delivery`; later is `post-delivery` (DT-04). Not a block — a labelled fact adjudicators weigh | Buyer sees the label on their own evidence and why it is there. |
| Carrier delivers to a neighbour / reception; buyer collects that evening | The 60-minute clock is measured from the **delivery scan**, so this buyer is structurally `post-delivery` through no fault of their own | The label carries the reason ("delivered to a third party at 09:14, collected by you at 18:40") so weight is assessed honestly rather than mechanically. |
| Carrier records a damage/exception scan in transit | **Third-party evidence neither party produced or can contest.** Ingests automatically; frequently decisive | Both parties see it in the pack timeline. Routes the claim toward carrier/insurance (13.07.04) rather than toward blame. |
| "Empty box arrived" | The carrier's **acceptance weight** settles it against the dispatch imagery | Weight at acceptance shown against declared item weight. A 0.4 kg acceptance scan on a 4 kg amp is not an argument. |
| Damage occurred in transit, genuinely nobody's fault | Exactly what the pack exists to establish. Routes to insurance (13.07.04), not to blame | Claim routed to the carrier/insurance path; neither party is flagged. |
| Seller edits a disclosure between purchase and dispatch | The pack snapshotted disclosure **at purchase** (step 3) and snapshots again **at seal**. A post-purchase change is visible as a diff, not as a silent overwrite (D-05) | Buyer is notified of any material change to a disclosure on an order they have already paid for. Whether that voids the sale belongs to order lifecycle (13.06.02) and the parent CX file's CX-02 — **the pack's job is that the change cannot be invisible.** |
| Seller edits a disclosure *after* seal | Sealed evidence never mutates. The listing's disclosure may change; the pack's record of what was disclosed at dispatch does not | Pack shows disclosure-at-dispatch. Rewriting history destroys the pack's only property. |
| Seller deletes the listing after dispatch | The pack survives listing deletion. It is attached to the order and the unit, not to the listing | Both parties retain access. A seller cannot delete the baseline by deleting the ad. |
| Media upload fails at dispatch (dock wifi) | Label printing is never blocked (D-02). Capture is held locally and retried; on continued failure it is queued for reconciliation on next connectivity (CX-M32) | "Saved — will upload when you have signal." Capture time is preserved and recorded separately from upload time (D-08). |
| Uploads land after the seal window | Attach as a **post-seal addendum**, labelled, with both timestamps. Never merged into the sealed set | "Added after dispatch" on the addendum. Adjudicators weigh it accordingly. |
| Device capture timestamp is forged | Device clocks are user-controlled; server receipt time is not. Both are recorded and **the pack states which one it can vouch for** (D-08) | Sealed frames show a vouched server time. Addenda show "captured (device): 14:02 · received: 17:31". No frame ever presents a device time as though the platform verified it. |
| Label printed twice / dispatch prompt fires twice | Seal is idempotent — **first seal wins**. The second capture appends as an addendum | Seller sees the pack already sealed; new media is labelled as added after. |
| Two people on one seller account capture simultaneously (Operator, two staff) | Both writes land pre-seal; both are kept. Frames are not a single-writer field — the pack is an append-only set, so there is nothing to merge | Both sets appear, each attributed to its capturer (D-07). |
| Order is for a **Band entity** (domain 01), not a person | Pack visibility follows delegated authority (CX-M09), not the purchasing individual. Any member with the entity's commerce authority sees it; the member who happened to click buy holds no special access | Band members with authority see the pack under the band's orders. |
| **The multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once** — Producer is both the seller and the third-party inspector under 13.06.04 | 13.06.04 DT-01 already establishes that a seller-fulfilled inspection is not an independent one. The pack does not resolve this; it **refuses to launder it**. Every frame carries role-at-capture and an interested-party flag (D-07) | Inspection evidence captured by the seller is labelled "captured by the seller" and never rendered as independent verification. |
| Seller and buyer are the same human (own two accounts, or sells to their own band entity) | The pack still records — the adversarial premise evaporates but the unit's condition history does not, and that history outlives both accounts (DT-09) | No prompts are suppressed. The record is about the object. |
| **Local pickup** (13.11) — no dispatch, no transit, no carrier scans | Not moot — **relocated**. The custody boundary is the handover, and it is the strongest possible evidence moment: both parties are present and can attest simultaneously. If money moves through the platform, a joint handover capture is prompted at confirm-collection. If cash changed hands off-platform, no event fires and the pack ends at listing evidence | Pickup pack: listing evidence + (where platform-settled) a joint handover capture both parties confirm. Otherwise `Partial`, permanently. This is one more cost of 13.11 DT-01's off-platform cash hole. |
| Unit has no serial (most launch listings) | The pack still exists and still works for the order. It **cannot accrue to the unit** (13.06.05: no serial, no registry record), so it dies with the order rather than becoming condition history | Pack functions; the registry link is absent, silently — no error, nothing to fix. |
| **Pack contents requested after account deletion** | The pack is evidence **about an object**, not about a person. Account deletion removes the personal linkage; it does not remove the object's condition record, because the counterparty's right to defend a live claim does not evaporate when the other side deletes their account. The pack is retained in **de-identified form** — parties become role references bound to the order — for the duration of the longest applicable claim window, then persists de-identified against the unit (CX-M18) | Deleting party: "Your account is deleted. Evidence attached to completed orders is kept without your name while claim windows are open." **Exact retention durations are statutory and belong to `/create-prd-security` (Q-02) — the *shape* is settled here, the *clock* is not.** |
| Erasure requested while a claim is open | Erasure does not defeat a live claim. The pack is de-identified but retained until the claim closes | Stated at request time. Not a refusal — a scope. |
| Pack requested under data portability (CX-M18) | Both parties can export their own pack for their own orders, including media | Standard export. What the *other* party captured is included — they are a party to the same order, and a one-sided baseline is not a baseline. |
| Buyer declines arrival capture entirely | The decline is recorded with a timestamp, and the full pack opens immediately (DT-07's withholding has nothing left to protect) | "Skipped" recorded on the pack. No penalty, no re-prompt loop — one prompt, one decline. |
| Buyer never confirms delivery and the carrier has no delivery scan | No trigger for the arrival prompt. Falls back to the order's auto-settle timer (13.06.02) | Arrival prompt fires on auto-settle instead, labelled `post-delivery` by definition. |
| Pack is requested by an insurer (13.07.04) | Insurers need the packing evidence and the carrier scans specifically, not the listing gallery | Scoped view. The pack is not a blob; consumers read the slice they need. |
| Category has no photographable defect surface (a cable, a set of strings) | Requiring 4 unit frames on a £9 patch cable is friction with no dispute to prevent | Unit-media minimum binds by value and category, not universally. Below the threshold, listing evidence alone is sufficient and no dispatch prompt fires. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An evidence pack is a separate artifact the seller assembles when needed | The naive framing — "upload your evidence" at dispute time | ❌ REJECTED: evidence assembled *at dispute time* is exactly the reconstruction failure the problem statement names as the root cause ("no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction"). By then the unit is in another country and the photos were never taken. The pack must accumulate **as a byproduct of the flow**, at the moment the fact is true. This is the platform's founding thesis (D-18) applied to physical goods — and the strongest argument that this node belongs in the domain at all. |
| DT-02 | Blocking dispatch until photos are captured would make the pack reliable | Reliability instinct — a mandatory pack is a complete pack | ❌ REJECTED: it inverts the incentive and copies the failure the Producer persona is defined by ("closes a project without capturing splits"). The fix there was **make the lazy path the correct path**, not add a gate. Here: prompt at label-print (already in the flow, already holding a phone), state the consequence, never block. A blocked dispatch is an angry seller; a defended seller who skipped photos once and lost a claim is a seller who photographs forever. |
| DT-03 | The pack is photographs | Every marketplace's evidence flow is a photo uploader, so this is the shape the feature defaults into | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient — ✅ CONFIRMED as *partial*. **A large share of gear's defects are audible or electrical, not visual.** A photo of an amp proves nothing about a hum, a noisy pot, a drifting compressor, an intermittent channel — and those are precisely the disclosures the Producer persona is defined by making (13.02.02 Role Lens: "noisy pots, drifting calibration, failed channels — which is where the real defects live in studio gear"). A photo-only pack is structurally blind to exactly the defect class its most valuable seller discloses, and every "it arrived buzzing" claim becomes unadjudicable. The pack must accept **audio and video**. This costs nothing: CX-M10 is *Media Handling **& Audio Playback*** — the platform is already an audio platform, which is the one thing a generic marketplace cannot cheaply become. **A domain expert would consider a photo-only condition record for amplifiers and outboard amateurish.** |
| DT-04 | Arrival evidence and dispatch evidence are symmetrical halves of one baseline | The natural framing — before and after, two ends of a journey | ❌ REJECTED: they are **not symmetrical, because of custody**. Dispatch evidence is captured while the seller still holds the object and before it leaves — the seller cannot have caused arrival damage, so the evidence's timing needs no defence. Arrival evidence has an **open window**: between the delivery scan and the capture, the *buyer* had the object. Photos taken 4 minutes after delivery and photos taken 6 hours after are not the same evidence, and treating them identically hands a dishonest buyer a free afternoon. So arrival evidence carries a property dispatch evidence does not: **latency-to-delivery**. Specified as: inside 60 minutes of the carrier's delivery scan = `at-delivery`; beyond = `post-delivery`. Labelled, never blocked — and the label carries its reason, because a parcel left with a neighbour makes an honest buyer structurally late. |
| DT-05 | The dispatch capture should photograph the unit | Obvious — the dispute is about the unit's condition | ⚠️ REFRAMED, and this is the sharpest finding here. **The most contested question in a gear damage claim is not the object's condition — it is whether it was packed to standard.** 13.07.01 makes packing the term; 13.07.04 records that "carrier and insurer both decline" on bad packing, and the seller bears it. So a fourth photo of the guitar adds nothing an eight-photo listing gallery didn't; **a photo of the guitar in the case, the case in the box, and the void fill is the evidence that decides the money.** The dispatch prompt asks for the *packed* states, not another beauty shot. Its mirror on the buyer's side is worse: **the buyer's instinct is to tear the box open**, and the packing evidence is destroyed with the box — permanently, in the first thirty seconds, by the party the evidence would have protected. This is the failure mode nobody designs for. The arrival prompt must therefore fire **on the delivery scan** and lead with "before you open it". |
| DT-06 | Evidence has to be captured by somebody, so the pack's completeness is bounded by how diligent the parties are | Follows from DT-01 — a byproduct is still a byproduct of *someone's* action | ❌ REJECTED as a ceiling. **The best evidence in the pack is produced by neither party and requested from nobody.** The carrier already weighs the parcel at acceptance, scans it through transit, records damage exceptions, and timestamps delivery — and CX-M14 is already in this flow to print the label. That material ingests automatically, costs zero user actions, and has a property no user-captured frame can have: **neither party produced it, so neither party can contest it.** It settles the cases user evidence cannot — "the box arrived empty" dies against a 0.4 kg acceptance weight; "nobody's fault" is proven by an exception scan. Second-order finding on the same axis: **a used instrument's disclosed flaws are an identity signature.** Every used guitar's wear pattern is a fingerprint no two units share, so 13.02.02's required flaw photos do double duty — they are the counterweight to *both* swap frauds (seller ships a different unit; buyer photographs a different broken one). Neither use was the reason those photos were required. |
| DT-07 | Both parties should see the whole pack as soon as it seals — transparency is the point | Instinct: the pack exists to be a shared baseline, so share it | ❌ REJECTED for the buyer's pre-capture window. Showing the seller's sealed dispatch evidence to the buyer **before** the buyer captures theirs **contaminates the arrival evidence**: it tells an honest buyer what to look for, and it tells a dishonest one exactly what to claim and precisely which angle to avoid. Loss adjusters have known this for a century — you take the claimant's account *before* you show them the other side's. So: on the delivery scan, the buyer is prompted to capture while the dispatch half stays **withheld**; once arrival evidence seals — or the buyer declines, timestamped — the full pack opens to both. Nothing is ever withheld from the party who captured it. The withholding is narrow, ends within minutes, and is the difference between a baseline and a script. |
| DT-08 | Every frame in the pack is equally credible because the platform timestamped it | The seal implies uniform trustworthiness | ❌ REJECTED twice over. **(a) Interest**: 13.06.04 DT-01 establishes that a seller-fulfilled inspection is not an independent one — and the multi-hyphenate makes this routine, not exotic, because the Producer selling the compressor is plausibly also the bench that inspects it. If the pack renders interested evidence as neutral evidence, it *launders* the conflict 13.06.04 went to the trouble of identifying. Every frame therefore carries capturer identity, role-at-capture, and an interested-party flag. **(b) Time**: a device clock is user-controlled and a server receipt clock is not. An Operator's genuine dock capture uploads three hours late (CX-M32) and must still count as dispatch evidence — so both times are recorded, and the pack **states which one it can vouch for**. Presenting an unverifiable device timestamp with the same confidence as a sealed server timestamp is the pack quietly lying about its own reliability, which is worse than having no pack. |
| DT-09 | The pack is an artifact of a sale, scoped to an order | Where it was found — the sweep's gap sat between disclosure and damage claims, both sale-shaped | ❌ REJECTED: the pack is an artifact of a **custody boundary**, and a sale is only one kind. Rental dispatch and rental return (13.10, twice per cycle, same owner throughout), consignment intake (13.09.02), inspection/intake grading (13.09.03) and repair intake (13.08.02) are all moments where an irreplaceable object changes hands and someone will later argue about what state it was in. They need this identical artifact, and scoping it to `order` denies it to all of them. **This lands directly on domain Q-09** — "an irreplaceable object is held by someone who doesn't own it; if it's destroyed, whose loss is it?" — which three features hit independently and which the domain records as unowned. Q-09 is looking for a custody vocabulary; the pack is the evidentiary half of one, and it converges with D-04's five-times-independent finding that **the unit, not the order, is the identity anchor**. |
| DT-10 | Arrival evidence should be required to open a damage claim — no photos, no claim | Follows seductively from the pack's whole purpose, and would fix the evidence-quality problem overnight | ❌ REJECTED, and the reason is not softness. It would mean a Fan who unpacked their first guitar the way every human unpacks a parcel has **no remedy** — and 13.07.04's Role Lens is explicit that this persona "has no idea insurance was a decision that was made, possibly by someone else, possibly badly" and "needs a claim path that doesn't require understanding declared value". Converting a design ideal into an eligibility gate turns the platform's cleverness into a consumer-protection failure, and it would collide with statutory rights the platform does not get to condition on photo compliance (13.08.01's two-regimes finding; `meta/constraints.md` consumer protection). **Evidence changes weight, never eligibility** — the same logic as D-02, applied to the other party. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- **Seals evidence from** 13.02.02 Mandatory Flaw Disclosure ([path](./13.02.02-mandatory-flaw-disclosure.md)) — *shared entity*: required flaw photos are the pre-dispatch half of the baseline (CX-02). Second, unplanned dependency per DT-06: those same photos are the **unit's identity signature**, which is what defeats swap fraud in both directions.
- **Seals evidence from** 13.02.03 Mod & Originality Disclosure ([path](./13.02.03-modification-originality-disclosure.md)) — *shared entity*: mod declarations and their photos seal identically to flaws. Resolves the parent CX file's CX-03 medium-confidence gap **from this side**: the pack treats a photographed mod declaration exactly as it treats a photographed flaw. Whether 13.02.03 *mandates* the photo is that file's decision, not this one's.
- **Snapshots** the disclosure state at purchase and at seal — *state race*: this is the pack's answer to the parent CX file's CX-02 unresolved item ("seller edits a disclosure between purchase and dispatch"). The pack cannot decide whether the sale voids — that is 13.06.02's — but it guarantees the change **cannot be invisible** (D-05).
- **Is the baseline for** 13.07.04 Shipping Insurance & High-Value Damage Claims ([path](../13.07-gear-logistics-cross-border/13.07.04-shipping-insurance-damage-claims.md)) — *trigger dependency*: a claim on a $40k instrument without a pre-dispatch baseline is unadjudicable. That file's happy path step 2 ("packing evidence seals into the pack") is produced here.
- **Is the baseline for** 13.07.01 Packing Standards — *trigger dependency*: per DT-05 the packed-state frames are what prove standard compliance, and the standard is the insurance term. Without this capture the packing standard is unenforceable and therefore decorative.
- **Ingests from** CX-M14 Shipping, Fulfilment & Logistics — *trigger dependency*: acceptance weight, transit scans, damage/exception scans, delivery timestamp ingest automatically (DT-06). Also the **dispatch prompt's trigger** is CX-M14's label-print event; without that hook the byproduct model has no moment to attach to.
- **Is the baseline for** 13.08.01 Returns & Refunds ([path](../13.08-returns-rma-warranty/13.08.01-returns-refunds.md)) — *trigger dependency*: "not as described" is judged against it.
- **Serves** 13.08.02 Repair & Custody and 13.09.03 Inspection & Intake Grading ([path](../13.09-tradein-consignment/13.09.03-inspection-intake-grading.md)) — *shared entity* per DT-09: intake is a custody boundary and needs this identical artifact. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The pack accumulates as a byproduct; it is never separately assembled | Per DT-01 — the founding thesis (D-18) applied to gear. |
| D-02 | Dispatch capture is prompted with stated consequence, never blocking | Per DT-02 — make the lazy path the correct path. Consistent with domain D-03 (the listing flow never stalls). |
| D-03 | Sealed at dispatch; arrival evidence and late uploads append, never overwrite | An editable baseline is not a baseline. Seal fires at label print + 15 min or carrier acceptance scan, whichever is first; seal is idempotent, first seal wins. |
| D-04 | **Unit-specific media is required on used grades; stock/catalog imagery is detected and labelled, not blocked** | Resolves Q-01. Detection is by comparison against the bound model's canonical reference media (13.01.01) — the platform owns both sides. Blocking would add a third exception to domain D-03, which D-03 deliberately holds to two. The consequence that bites is evidentiary: no unit media → no baseline → a "not as described" dispute resolves against the seller. **Minimum on used grades above the value threshold: 4 unit frames, one showing the serial where the category has one** — a serial frame is inherently unit-specific and cannot be a catalog image, and it is already required by 13.03.07 and 13.06.05, so it costs the seller nothing new. **Legitimate on new/sealed stock** — you cannot photograph a sealed pedal without unsealing it — so the rule binds on used grades only (13.02.01). |
| D-05 | The pack snapshots disclosure state at purchase and at seal | Per the CX-02 gap. The pack does not adjudicate a post-purchase disclosure change; it makes it impossible to hide. |
| D-06 | **Evidence changes weight, never eligibility** | Per DT-10 and D-02, applied symmetrically. Missing arrival evidence weakens a claim; it never bars one. Any other rule denies a remedy to the Fan persona and collides with statutory rights the platform cannot condition on photo compliance. |
| D-07 | Every frame carries capturer identity, role-at-capture, and an interested-party flag | Per DT-08(a). 13.06.04 identified that a seller-fulfilled inspection is not independent; rendering it as neutral would launder that. The multi-hyphenate makes this routine, not exotic. |
| D-08 | Capture time and receipt time are recorded separately, and the pack states which it vouches for | Per DT-08(b). Device clocks are user-controlled; server clocks are not. An offline dock capture (CX-M32) uploaded hours later is still dispatch evidence — but the pack must not present an unverifiable device timestamp with the confidence of a sealed one. |
| D-09 | The pack survives account deletion in **de-identified** form for the duration of open claim windows | The pack is evidence about an object, not about a person. The counterparty's right to defend a live claim does not evaporate because the other side deleted their account. Parties become role references bound to the order. **The clock is statutory and belongs to `/create-prd-security` (Q-02); the shape is decided here.** |
| D-10 | The pack survives listing deletion and is attached to the order and the unit, never to the listing | A seller cannot delete the baseline by deleting the ad. |
| D-11 | Arrival capture is prompted **before** the dispatch half is revealed to the buyer | Per DT-07 — showing the seller's evidence first contaminates the buyer's. The withholding is narrow (minutes), ends on capture or on a timestamped decline, and never applies to the party who captured the evidence. |
| D-12 | The pack accepts photo, video **and audio** | Per DT-03 — a photo-only pack is blind to the entire functional defect class the Producer persona discloses. CX-M10 already carries audio; this is free and a generic marketplace cannot cheaply copy it. |
| D-13 | The dispatch prompt asks for the **packed** states, not more shots of the unit | Per DT-05 — packing is the insurance term (13.07.01, 13.07.04) and is what the money turns on. A ninth photo of the guitar adds nothing the listing gallery did not. |
| D-14 | Carrier scan events ingest automatically and are first-class pack evidence | Per DT-06 — neither party produced them, so neither party can contest them. Zero user actions, highest evidentiary value. |
| D-15 | The pack is scoped to a **custody boundary**, not to a sale | Per DT-09 — rental out/return, consignment intake, repair intake all need the identical artifact. Converges with domain D-04 (the unit is the identity anchor) and feeds domain Q-09. |

| Q-02 | How long is a sealed pack retained? D-09 settles the **shape** — de-identified survival of account deletion, retention bounded by open claim windows — but the **durations** are statutory (return window vs. insurance claim window vs. dispute window vs. GDPR erasure), they will not naturally align (13.08.01 Q-03 hits the identical seam), and `meta/constraints.md` has GDPR/CCPA and consumer protection both `[PENDING]`. An agent cannot pick a retention period that is simultaneously a legal defence and a privacy commitment. | User | `/create-prd-security` |

The former is far more valuable and far more privacy-loaded: it means a future buyer sees what a *previous* owner's counterparty photographed. This is the same values question as domain Q-04 (the chain always records; the owner controls visibility) and `problem-statement.md` Q-02 (earned vs. hostile lock-in). **Cannot be defaulted by an agent** — it decides what one person's evidence reveals about another person's gear. **Still open**: [15.04](../../15-gear-registry-ownership/15.04-gear-collection-visibility.md) D-01/D-02 settle *collection* visibility ("per-item and private by default"; aggregate value has no public form) but say nothing about a third party's sealed evidence accruing to someone else's unit, and domain Q-04 is itself still open and already re-pointed to `/create-prd`. Re-pointed here to the same stage, so the two can be ratified together. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-04 | Above what **value threshold** do the unit-media minimum (D-04) and the dispatch prompt bind? Four frames on a £9 patch cable is friction with no dispute to prevent; four frames on a £4,000 guitar is trivially worth it. The shape is decided (value- and category-conditional, not universal); the number is a product decision that trades seller friction against dispute cost, and it interacts with 13.06.02 Q-01's inspection window, which is being sized on the same axis. | User | `/create-prd` |
