## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Flaw Disclosure ↔ Grading Scale

**Relationship**: Grade is not free — it is bounded by what the seller disclosed. A disclosed structural repair makes Mint unavailable. The cross-check runs at listing time and challenges the seller rather than silently rejecting or silently accepting.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona most likely to trigger the challenge — over-grades gear they're fond of, not dishonestly.
- **Producer**: triggers it on the functional axis, where the scale is weakest (13.02.01 DT-02).
- **Operator**: triggers it at bulk volume, where a per-item challenge is friction (13.02.02 Q-01).
- **Fan**: buyer-side only. Experiences the *result* — grades that mean something.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The listing owns both fields. No concurrent writers — one seller, one form. The constraint is a validation rule, not a merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Flaw marked → grade cross-check → challenge if impossible. Cross-check failure does **not** block publish (13.02.01 D-03); it flags for async review. Order matters: grade may be set before flaws, so the check must re-run on disclosure completion (13.02.01 Partial state).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, cleanly — Fan has no access to either write path, so the entire cross-check is invisible to that persona.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None at listing time. Systematic over-grading fans out later to CX-M08 seller performance, not to any individual listing.
5. **State transition conflict**: A seller editing flaws on a live listing can invalidate the published grade. `[PENDING — Step 5]`: does the listing auto-downgrade, or is publish held until the seller re-confirms? Auto-downgrade changes a price-relevant fact under active offers (13.05.01) — non-trivial.

### CX-02: Flaw Disclosure ↔ Evidence Pack

**Relationship**: The pack's pre-dispatch half is produced entirely by disclosure. 13.02.02 D-03 requires photos for material flaws; those photos, timestamped at listing, are what make a later "it arrived cracked" claim adjudicable. Neither feature works without the other: disclosure without evidence is an unverifiable claim; a pack without disclosure has photos of nothing in particular.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer/Operator**: as sellers, produce the evidence while doing something else (13.02.04 D-01). As buyers, consume it.
- **Fan**: read-only on their own orders. Prompted for arrival evidence — the only capture the persona ever performs.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pack is append-only and seals at dispatch (13.02.04 D-03). Disclosure photos are written *before* seal, so no conflict. Editing a disclosure after seal must not mutate sealed evidence — the pack records what was shown *at the time*, and rewriting history destroys its only property.
2. **Trigger chain**: Material flaw marked → photo required → photo enters pack → pack seals at dispatch. This is the **only publish-blocking rule in the listing flow** (13.02.02 D-03), deliberately narrow.
3. **Permission intersection**: Buyer sees the pack for their own order only. Pre-purchase, they see listing photos but not the (not-yet-existing) dispatch evidence.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Dispatch prompt fires at label print (CX-M07 + CX-M14). Arrival prompt fires on delivery confirmation.
5. **State transition conflict**: Seller edits a disclosure between purchase and dispatch — the buyer bought against the old disclosure. `[PENDING — Step 5]`, and it interacts with order lifecycle (13.06.02). A material post-purchase disclosure change should arguably void the sale.

### CX-03: Originality ↔ Evidence Pack

**Relationship**: Mod declarations should seal into the pack the same way flaw disclosures do — a "replaced pickups, originals included" claim is exactly as disputable as a crack, and exactly as cheap to photograph. Medium confidence because 13.02.03 does not currently mandate photos the way 13.02.02 D-03 does, so the evidence path is asserted rather than specified.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: highest value here — a re-capped unit's mod photos are the only proof of service quality.
- **Musician**: moderate — matters intensely on vintage, negligibly on a used pedal.
- **Fan**: read-only; the persona least equipped to evaluate mod photos even when present.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. Step 5 must decide whether originality declarations carry a photo requirement mirroring 13.02.02 D-03, and whether "originals included" specifically requires evidence (it is a price-raising claim, which is precisely when claims need proof).

### CX-04: Grading Scale ↔ Originality — a deliberate non-interaction

**Relationship**: These two must be **rendered together and never merged**. This is recorded as a cross-cut rather than a rejected pair because the *absence* of interaction is itself the design constraint — the failure mode is an implementer "helpfully" combining them into a single quality score, which is the error sub-domain D-01 and 13.02.03 DT-01 exist to prevent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: the reason this matters. A merged score would read as authoritative to the persona least able to detect that it is nonsense — "Mint" on a refinished '59 Les Paul.
- **Musician (collector context)**: filters on originality independently of grade.
- **Producer**: needs the axes *inverted* in effect — a mod raises studio-gear value — which is only possible if they are separate.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None, and that is the point. Two independent fields, two independent renderings.
2. **Trigger chain**: None. Neither constrains the other. Contrast CX-01, where flaws *do* constrain grade.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical scoping (seller writes, all read), which is part of why merging them looks tempting.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None. The risk is architectural, not runtime: any downstream consumer (especially 13.04 price guide) that reduces both to one dimension reintroduces the error. **Flagged for `/write-architecture-spec` as an invariant.**

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 13.02.01 Grading Scale | 13.02.04 Evidence Pack | Considered: the grade could be evidenced. Rejected — a grade is an *opinion* derived from flaws, not a fact that can be photographed. The evidence attaches to the underlying flaws (CX-02); evidencing the opinion is a category error. |
| R-02 | 13.02.02 Flaw Disclosure | 13.02.03 Originality | Considered: both are seller declarations on the same form, so they look like one thing — the sweep bundled them (#04). Rejected — a flaw is unintended deterioration, a mod is an intentional change; they price in opposite directions in studio gear, and "Unknown" is valid for one and not the other. Shared form ≠ shared semantics. |
