## Overview

What state this specific unit is in, what has been done to it, and what the seller is obliged to say about both. Condition is roughly 40% of a used instrument's price — but **condition and originality are orthogonal axes**, and conflating them is the classic error: a mint-condition 1959 Les Paul with a refinish is worth a fraction of a beaten all-original one. This sub-domain owns both axes, the disclosure obligations attached to them, and the evidence trail that makes a dispute resolvable.

**Why sub-domain, not feature**: grading, flaw disclosure, mod/originality disclosure and the evidence pack are four distinct capabilities that interact — the grade is constrained by disclosed flaws, originality prices independently of grade, and both feed the same dispute baseline.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Condition and originality are separate axes, not one scale | A refinished mint vintage instrument and an all-original player-grade one cannot occupy the same scale. Collapsing them makes the price guide (13.04) systematically wrong on exactly the units where the money is. | Node Classification Gate + domain-expert reasoning, this pass |
| D-02 | Disclosure is structured data, not prose | Free-text "some wear" is unsearchable, uncomparable and unenforceable. Structured flaws feed comps, facets and dispute adjudication. | This pass |
