# Feature: Modification & Originality Disclosure

> **Number**: 13.02.03
> **Parent**: [Condition, Originality & Disclosure](./13.02-condition-originality-disclosure-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What has been changed on this unit since it left the factory — refinishes, replaced pickups, non-original tuners, re-frets, re-capped amps, swapped transformers — and what remains original. For vintage gear this is the dominant price variable, frequently exceeding condition in magnitude: an all-original instrument in poor condition routinely outvalues a mint one that has been refinished. It is a distinct axis from condition (sub-domain D-01) and it must be disclosed unit-by-unit because the catalog only knows models.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as seller) | Declares what's been swapped on the guitar they're selling — often gear they modified themselves years ago and half-remember. As a buyer, filters for all-original when collecting, or *for* mods when they want the hot-rodded version. |
| Producer | Full (as seller) | Declares service history on outboard — re-capped power supplies, replaced op-amps, modded units. In studio gear a mod is often an *upgrade* that raises value, inverting the vintage-instrument logic. |
| Operator | Full (as seller) | Declares maintenance-driven modifications on fleet gear (replaced speakers, re-tubed amps, upgraded jacks) — routine and value-neutral in a rental context, material in a sale context. |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads originality claims without the background to weigh them. Most exposed to buying a modified unit at all-original prices; needs consequence stated plainly, not a parts list. |

## Behavior

Originality is captured as a **structured component-level declaration** derived from the model's category: for a guitar, the component set is finish / neck / pickups / tuners / bridge / electronics / frets; for an amp, transformers / speakers / caps / tubes. Each component is Original / Replaced / Unknown, with a note and photo where material. **"Unknown" is a first-class answer** — most sellers genuinely do not know, and forcing a binary manufactures false claims.

### Happy Path

1. Seller reaches the originality step; system loads the component set for that category
2. Seller marks each component Original / Replaced / Unknown
3. For each Replaced, seller notes what it was replaced with (and whether the original part is included)
4. System renders an originality summary on the listing alongside, not merged into, the condition grade
5. Buyer filters on all-original, or reads the exact deviation set

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller genuinely doesn't know (bought it used, no history) | "Unknown" is valid and must not be penalised into a false "Original" | Unknown rendered honestly; buyer prices the uncertainty |
| Original parts included with a modified unit | Materially raises value — a reversible mod is not a permanent one | "Original pickups included" as a distinct, price-relevant fact |
| Mod is an upgrade that raises value (studio gear) | Originality is **not** a quality scale — it is a factual axis. The market prices it, per direction of category | Neutral rendering; no "worse" framing |
| Unit registered in domain 15 with a service/mod history that contradicts the listing | The registry knows what the seller is claiming not to know. Real, high-value contradiction | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether registry history auto-populates or merely flags is unresolved. See DT-02. |
| Refinished vintage instrument | Condition can still be Mint (D-01, orthogonal axes); originality carries the truth | Grade: Mint · Originality: refinished — both visible, neither hidden |
| Model-derived voltage warning contradicted by a rewire mod | Unit fact beats model fact | Overrides 13.01.05 warning per that file's DT-02 |
| Seller marks every component "Original" to inflate value on a unit registry history contradicts | Not silently trusted — where registry (domain 15) holds a conflicting record, the contradiction surfaces per D-03; where no registry exists, the claim stands but is the seller's signed liability | Buyer sees the all-original claim; where a registry contradiction exists, sees the flag alongside it |
| Component marked "Replaced" with no note and no photo | The declaration is accepted but incomplete; a note is required for Replaced, a photo prompted where material (finish, pickups, electronics) | Inline prompt "describe the replacement"; listing shows "Replaced — details not provided" rather than a bare flag |
| Fan (read-only) attempts to edit the originality declaration | Blocked — Fan has no seller surface here; disclosure is authored only by the selling persona | Read-only summary; no edit affordance rendered |
| Seller edits originality after the unit has an active offer or sale in progress | Late edit to a price-material disclosure must not silently alter a deal in flight — the change is versioned and the counterparty notified `[PENDING — /write-be-spec: whether an in-flight offer is invalidated or merely re-surfaced]` | Buyer with an active offer sees "seller updated the originality disclosure" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Originality is a sub-property of condition and can fold into the grade | The sweep's candidate list bundled them as one item (#04, "Condition Grading & Mandatory Flaw/Mod Disclosure") | ❌ REJECTED — this is the sub-domain's founding decision (D-01). They are orthogonal: a refinish is not *damage*, it is a *change*, and it can coexist with mint condition while destroying 70% of the value. Folding them produces a price guide (13.04) that is systematically wrong on precisely the high-value units where being wrong is expensive. A domain expert would consider conflating them disqualifying. |
| DT-02 | Domain 15's registry service history should auto-populate this disclosure | The registry holds mod/service history; re-typing it is waste, and WeJammin's whole thesis is capture-once | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT: auto-populate is exactly the thesis applied here (D-18 — the record survives, the seller doesn't reconstruct it from memory). **But it must not auto-*assert*.** Registry history is what *previous* custodians recorded; the seller is the one signing the disclosure and bearing the liability. So: pre-fill and *surface contradictions*, never silently assert on the seller's behalf. The contradiction case ("registry says re-fretted in 2019; you said original") is a genuine trust signal and possibly the most valuable thing in this file. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Originality is component-level structured data, not prose | Same reasoning as 13.02.02 D-02 — prose can't be filtered, compared or adjudicated. |
| D-02 | "Unknown" is a first-class answer | Forcing a binary manufactures false claims from honest sellers. Most used gear has unknown history. |
| D-03 | Registry history pre-fills but never auto-asserts | Per DT-02 — the seller signs the disclosure and carries the liability. |
| D-04 | Originality is a factual axis, not a quality scale | A mod is an upgrade in studio gear and a devaluation in vintage instruments. The UI must not editorialise. |
