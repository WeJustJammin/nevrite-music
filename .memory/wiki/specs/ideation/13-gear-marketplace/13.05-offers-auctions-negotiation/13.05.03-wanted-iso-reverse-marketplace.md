# Feature: Wanted/ISO Reverse Marketplace

> **Number**: 13.05.03
> **Parent**: [Offers, Auctions & Negotiation](./13.05-offers-auctions-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A buyer posts what they are looking for — model, condition, budget, region — and sellers come to them. It inverts the marketplace for the case the forward marketplace handles worst: gear that isn't currently listed anywhere. For genuinely rare units, the buyer's demand is the only signal that exists, and an ISO post is what surfaces the unit sitting in someone's attic who was never going to list it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Posts "ISO: 1962 Jazzmaster, any condition, up to £4k" and waits. The persona who has hunted one instrument for years; this is the feature that ends that hunt. Also browses ISOs to find buyers for gear they hadn't thought to sell. |
| Producer | Full | Posts ISOs for rare outboard — the segment where nothing is ever listed and the market is entirely word-of-mouth. This is the persona's current workaround (asking around) made into a surface. |
| Operator | Full | Posts ISOs for fleet gaps and backline requirements; browses ISOs as a sourcing channel for stock they hold. The persona most likely to *answer* ISOs at volume. |
| Fan | Config | May post an ISO — it is a buying act. Cannot respond as a seller. Low usage expected; the persona rarely wants something specific enough to name it. |

## Behavior

An ISO is a demand-side listing: a catalog model (or a description, if unmatched), acceptable condition range, budget, region and an expiry. Sellers with matching stock — or matching gear in their registry (domain 15) — are surfaced the ISO and respond with an **offer** (13.05.01), which is why this sits in this sub-domain rather than under Listings.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer posts an ISO bound to a catalog model with a budget and condition range
2. System matches it against existing listings first — the want may already be satisfied
3. If not, the ISO publishes and is surfaced to plausible sellers
4. A seller responds with an offer on a unit they hold
5. Buyer accepts; a normal order follows

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Matching listing already exists | Don't publish an ISO for something already for sale | "3 of these are listed now" — check first |
| **ISO matches gear in a seller's registry that isn't for sale** | The highest-value and most dangerous case. Domain 15 knows who owns one. See DT-02. | Consent-gated; opt-in only |
| Nobody responds | Silence is the norm for genuinely rare gear | Expiry with a relist prompt; alert on a future matching listing (CX-M28) |
| Seller spam-responds to every ISO with irrelevant stock | Attention tax on the buyer | Response gating by match quality and seller standing |
| ISO budget is unrealistic (£500 for a £4k instrument) | Wastes everyone's time | Price guide (13.04) can inform *if* the model has confidence; often it won't (13.04.03 DT-02) |
| ISO for an uncatalogued model | Matching fails (13.01.04); free-text ISO with weaker reach | Publishes unmatched, like a listing |
| ISO fulfilled off-platform | Buyer got it elsewhere; ISO must be closable | "Found it" close reason — feeds disintermediation signal |
| ISO reveals a buyer's intent to sellers who then price against them | A public "I'll pay up to £4k" is a stated maximum | See DT-01 — budget visibility is a real design question |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An ISO should state the buyer's budget — sellers need to know if it's worth responding | Obvious efficiency argument, and it's how classified "wanted" ads have always worked | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH TENSION: a stated budget is a **stated maximum**, and every responding seller will price at it. The buyer has pre-conceded their negotiating position — inverting 13.05.01, where the *seller* opens and the buyer negotiates down. In thin markets (13.04.03 DT-02: most of this catalog) there is no comp to anchor against, so the ISO budget becomes the only number in the room and therefore the price. **Budget visibility is a design decision with a real cost**, not a form field. A range, or a "serious offers" mode with no stated figure, may serve buyers better — and no marketplace does this well today. |
| DT-02 | ISOs should match against the gear registry (domain 15), not only against listings | This is the consolidation thesis's most seductive move in this domain — WeJammin *knows who owns one*, and Reverb structurally cannot | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH HARD CONSTRAINT: it is the single most powerful capability the 13↔15 seam enables, and it is also **the fastest way to destroy the registry**. If registering gear means strangers get told you own something valuable and start asking to buy it, registration stops — and the registry is the provenance moat (D-18) and the theft-recovery mechanism (13.03.07). The personas doc calls van and venue theft endemic; an ISO-to-registry match is, functionally, a targeting query with a friendly interface. **It can only exist as strict per-unit opt-in ("I'd consider offers on this"), never as a default, and never as a "3 owners nearby" count** — an aggregate count leaks the fact that units exist near a location, which is most of the harm with none of the value. Even opt-in, this needs the owner's explicit sign-off before it is designed further. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Check existing listings before publishing an ISO | Creating demand for something already supplied wastes both sides. |
| D-02 | Registry matching is strict per-unit opt-in; aggregate counts are prohibited | Per DT-02 — this is a safety control, not a privacy preference. |
| D-03 | Seller responses are gated by match quality and standing | Without it, ISO becomes a spam surface and the buyer stops reading. |
