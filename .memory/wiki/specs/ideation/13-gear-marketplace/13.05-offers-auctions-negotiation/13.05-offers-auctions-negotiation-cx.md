## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Wanted/ISO ↔ Offers

**Relationship**: ISO is demand without a listing; offers are the instrument that turns it into a transaction. A seller seeing an ISO responds with an offer against a unit they hold — which may be a live listing, unlisted stock, or (per 13.05.03 DT-02, opt-in only) a registered unit that was never for sale. The offer's existing state machine (13.05.01 D-01) is what makes an ISO response claimable rather than a conversation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: posts the ISO that ends a years-long hunt; also answers ISOs for gear they hadn't thought to sell.
- **Producer**: the ISO's natural constituency — rare outboard is never listed, so demand-side posting is the only mechanism that works.
- **Operator**: answers ISOs at volume as a sourcing/sales channel.
- **Fan**: may post an ISO, cannot answer one. Low expected usage — the persona rarely wants something specific enough to name.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ISO and the offer are separate entities; the ISO is the *context*, the offer is the *claim*. An ISO answered by three sellers produces three independent offers — the buyer accepts at most one, and acceptance claims that seller's unit through 13.03.03.
2. **Trigger chain**: ISO published → matched to sellers → seller offers → buyer accepts → claim → checkout. If the ISO expires with a live offer outstanding, the offer must survive its parent context — an offer's lifetime is its own (13.05.01), not the ISO's. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the asymmetry that defines the pair — Fan may post (Config) but not respond, so the demand side is open to all four personas and the supply side to three.
4. **Notification fan-out**: ISO → plausible sellers. Bounded by match gating (13.05.03 D-03); ungated, this is a spam surface. Registry-matched ISO (13.05.03 DT-02) would fan out to *owners*, which is the safety problem.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two sellers offer against one ISO; the buyer accepts one. The other offer is on a *different unit*, so no inventory race — unlike CX-02, this pair has no arbitration problem. The ISO simply closes.

### CX-02: Offers ↔ Auctions — a designed mutual exclusion

**Relationship**: Recorded as a cross-cut rather than a rejected pair because the *prohibition* is the design. Both mechanisms terminate in a claim on a qty=1 unit (sub-domain D-01). A listing running both would have two independent claim paths racing each other with no rule for which wins — an accepted offer at £900 and a live auction at £950 with an hour to run is unanswerable without inventing arbitrary precedence. Sub-domain D-03 forbids the configuration rather than adjudicating it.

**Role scoping**:
- **All personas** equally, as buyers — the cost of the exclusion is that a buyer must first work out which game a listing is playing.
- **Fan**: bears that cost worst (13.05.02 DT-02) — already the least calibrated, now asked to distinguish two market cultures.
- **Musician/Producer/Operator**: as sellers, must choose the mode at listing time and cannot change it once bids or offers exist.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The unit is the contested entity. The exclusion means only one claim path can ever be live per listing, so 13.03.03's single arbitration point never has to rank an offer against a bid.
2. **Trigger chain**: None between them — that is the point. The mode is chosen at listing creation (13.03.01) and locked once the first bid or offer lands.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical scoping across both (Fan buys, doesn't sell), which is part of why stacking them looks harmless.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Independent per mode.
5. **State transition conflict**: The conflict this pair exists to *prevent*. **Flagged for `/write-architecture-spec` as an invariant**: listing mode is immutable once a claim path is active. Note this is the second invariant this domain has surfaced for architecture, alongside the single-arbitration-point rule (13.03 CX-01) and the unit-as-identity-anchor rule (13.03 CX-05).

### CX-03: Auctions ↔ Offers — the second-chance crossing

**Relationship**: The one sanctioned crossing of CX-02's line. When an auction winner ghosts, the seller is left with an unsold unit and a dispersed audience — worse than a ghosted offer, because the auction's moment has passed. A second-chance offer to the underbidder recovers it. This is offers-machinery invoked from an auction's failure path, which does not violate D-03 because the auction is over: only one claim path is live at any instant.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer/Operator**: as sellers, recover a failed auction. As underbidders, receive an unexpected second chance.
- **Fan**: could receive a second-chance offer as an underbidder — and is the persona most likely to have bid emotionally and least likely to still want it at the price they reached. See the deferred question below.

**Synthesis questions deferred** — Medium confidence. The mechanism is standard (eBay has run it for two decades) but its interaction with sub-domain D-03 needs Step 5 to confirm the sequencing is airtight: the auction's claim must be fully released before an offer claim can open, or the domain oversells via the recovery path — an ironic failure. Also open: is a second-chance offer binding on the underbidder (it wasn't a bid), and does the Fan persona need a cooling-off treatment here given 13.05.02 DT-02's overpaying dynamics?

## Rejected Pairs

| R-01 | 13.05.03 Wanted/ISO | 13.05.02 Auctions | Considered: an ISO could trigger an auction — several owners surface, let them compete, buyer wins the best price.
