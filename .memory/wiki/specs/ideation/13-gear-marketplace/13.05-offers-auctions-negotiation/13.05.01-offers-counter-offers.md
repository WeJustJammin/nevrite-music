# Feature: Offers & Counter-Offers

> **Number**: 13.05.01
> **Parent**: [Offers, Auctions & Negotiation](./13.05-offers-auctions-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A buyer proposes a price below asking; the seller accepts, declines or counters. This is how used gear actually trades — the asking price is an opening position, and a marketplace that only supports Buy Now is fighting the category's culture. Acceptance is a binding claim on the unit, which is what separates this from chat.

It is also the domain's **price-formation instrument where the price guide is mute**. Most of this catalog is permanently thin (13.04.03 DT-02), so for the gear people most want valued, an offer is the only number in the room that came from a real buyer with real money.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | Offers on gear they want; fields offers on gear they're selling. The persona's whole relationship with the category — nobody pays sticker for a used pedal. Wants offers in one place, not scattered across DMs. Runs both sides simultaneously (buyer on a Jazzmaster, seller of a cymbal) and must never see those two lists merged — see D-11. Typically ≤5 live listings, so per-offer manual review is the norm; auto-rules (D-03) are available but not their default. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Same, with longer, more considered negotiations on expensive outboard where a 10% move is real money and the buyer may be a stranger in another country. The persona that makes the **48h default expiry** load-bearing: a 24h window across a 9-hour timezone offset can close while one party sleeps (D-06). Also the persona whose listings sit for months (13.03.02 Q-03), so a live offer must be able to outlive its listing's calendar expiry (D-10). |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Fields offers at volume across fleet stock; needs bulk rules (auto-accept above X, auto-decline below Y) or it becomes a full-time job. **The persona whose fit here is doubtful** — the Operator sells time and space, not guitars, and "fleet stock of gear with bulk offer rules" is dealer behaviour. See DT-10; this is the fourth independent arrival of the domain's dealer-persona gap (domain Q-03). |
| Fan | Read-only (buyer side only) | May make an offer — it's a buying act. "Read-only" scopes the **seller** surface: no listings, no offer inbox, no auto-rules (13.03.01 Role Lens; sub-domain Role Matrix note). Least likely to know that offering is normal and expected; the affordance must invite it, not hide it behind "contact seller" (D-04). Also the persona least able to infer that acceptance obligates them, so the offer form states it in one plain sentence before submit (D-12) rather than in a checkbox nobody reads. |

## Behavior

An offer is a **price proposal with a lifetime**. The seller may accept (claiming the unit — 13.03.03), decline, or counter (which transfers the decision back). Offers are **structured**, not messages: they carry an amount, an expiry and a state, so acceptance can be atomic (D-01). Live offers restrict the seller's price edits (13.03.02 D-03).

The state machine per thread: `pending` → (`accepted` | `declined` | `countered` | `withdrawn` | `expired` | `voided`). A counter closes the current move and opens a new `pending` move in the opposite direction — same object, same claim semantics, opposite party (sub-domain R-02). Only `accepted` claims inventory.

**Four properties fix the object's shape, and each is derived, not chosen:**

1. **An offer does not reserve the unit.** Only acceptance claims (D-05, from 13.03.03 D-02). Without this, spraying offers across five sellers is an inventory denial-of-service.
2. **An offer is cash-only, in the listing's currency, on the item price.** Not landed cost, not part-exchange (D-07, D-08, D-09). Each non-cash or variable component destroys atomic acceptability.
3. **An offer's lifetime is its own.** It does not inherit the expiry of the listing, the ISO, or any other context that produced it (D-10). This resolves the sub-domain CX-01 synthesis Q2 gap from this side.
4. **Offers are private.** Buyer sees only their own thread; seller sees all (D-13). Public offers would reconstruct an auction on an offer listing — the exact collision sub-domain D-03 exists to forbid.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer opens a £1,000 listing, sees the estimated delivered total (item + freight to their address, 13.07.01) **before** the offer control, and taps "Make an offer"
2. Buyer enters £900. The form states, in one sentence: *"If the seller accepts, this Fender Jazzmaster is yours to check out. Freight to SE15 is about £45 on top. Your offer expires Sat 18 Jul, 14:20."* Buyer submits
3. System validates against the seller's minimum threshold (D-03), eligibility (region/voltage/CITES — D-14), and the self-dealing check (D-04a); the offer publishes as `pending` with a 48h clock
4. Seller is notified within 60s (P95, D-15); reviews with the price guide (13.04) alongside — which, on this model, may be mute (13.04.03 DT-02), leaving the offer itself as the best evidence
5. Seller counters £950. The buyer's move closes; a new `pending` move opens toward the buyer with a fresh 48h clock. Move 2 of 6 (D-06)
6. Buyer accepts the counter
7. Acceptance is arbitrated at 13.03.03's single arbitration point against any concurrent Buy Now or auto-rule; it wins
8. Unit → `reserved`; every other live offer on the unit is auto-`voided` in the same atomic step, with a stated reason (D-16)
9. Buyer proceeds to checkout (13.06.01), where the £950 item price is joined by freight, duties and landed cost (13.07) — none of which moved the negotiated number

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Multiple live offers on one unit** | Seller may only accept one — the second acceptance fails atomically (13.03.03 Edge Cases). All other live offers `voided` in the same step as the winning claim | Seller: "You already accepted an offer on this unit". Losers: "This unit was claimed" + similar listings + "alert me if it relists" — never silence |
| **Acceptance races a Buy Now** | Single arbitration point (13.03.03); exactly one wins | Seller (if Buy Now won): "This sold at full price while you were reviewing — the offer has been voided". Never a bare error |
| **Seller taps Accept 100 ms before the offer expires; request lands 50 ms after** | Acceptance is evaluated against the offer's state **at the arbitration point**, not at tap time. Expired ⇒ fails | "That offer expired 3 seconds ago" + one-tap **"Counter at £900"** — the race loss becomes a recovery path, per 13.03.03 D-01's philosophy |
| **Buyer withdraws at the same instant the seller accepts** | Withdrawal is only effective before arbitration; acceptance wins the race | Buyer: "Too late — the seller accepted your offer" (never a silent withdrawal that leaves a claim standing) |
| Buyer offers, then buys via Buy Now | Offer becomes moot; auto-withdrawn atomically with the purchase | "Offer withdrawn — you bought it" |
| **Buyer sprays offers on 5 units of the same model across 5 sellers** | Rational buyer behaviour; the offer equivalent of overbooking. Bounded **because offers don't reserve** (D-05) — sellers keep selling. Residual harm is the D-03 price freeze; the seller's escape is a one-tap decline, which unfreezes instantly (D-17). No cap on concurrent offers | Nothing special to the buyer. Ghosting after acceptance costs them under CX-M08 |
| **Seller wants to reprice with offers live** | Blocked (13.03.02 D-03) — and blocked while **any** offer is live, so a listing with 5 live offers cannot be repriced until all 5 resolve | "Decline all 5 offers to change the price" — one tap, instant unfreeze (D-17). Never a trap |
| **Price *drop* while offers are live** | Also blocked. Considered and rejected as an exception — see DT-08 | Same as above |
| **Offer above asking price** | Allowed — real behaviour on rare units where a buyer pre-empts competition. But strictly worse for the buyer than Buy Now, if Buy Now exists | Warned before submit: "You can buy this now for £1,000 — your offer is £1,050." Letting it through silently is a dark pattern by omission |
| Offer of £0, negative, or non-numeric | Rejected at validation; never reaches the seller | Inline error on blur |
| **Absurd high offer (£99,999,999) then ghost** | Costs the buyer nothing today; wastes seller attention; and **would poison any price guide fed by offer data** — see DT-03 | Seller sees buyer standing (CX-M08) alongside the amount. Amount capped at 10× asking at validation |
| **Buyer grinds: declined at £850, re-offers £855, £860, £865…** | Re-offer after a decline requires **+≥5% of asking** over the declined amount, else a 24h cooldown (D-18). Currency-neutral by construction | "Your last offer of £850 was declined. A new offer must be at least £900, or you can try again tomorrow" |
| **Buyer binary-searches the seller's auto-accept threshold** | Real attack on D-03's rules: repeated offers reveal X and Y, then the buyer offers exactly X. Mitigated by D-18's cooldown/step **and** by D-19 — auto-declines notify on a randomised 30–120 min delay so a rule-driven decline is not distinguishable from a human one | Buyer sees an ordinary decline. Honest buyers pay a latency cost — a real, accepted tension (DT-09) |
| **Endless counters** — thread used as a chat channel | Capped at 6 moves per thread (1 offer + 5 counters), per D-06. Real negotiations converge in ≤3 | On move 6: "This is the last counter on this thread." After: "Start a new offer, or message the seller" (CX-M06) |
| **Seller lists "£1,000 FIRM" but D-04 makes offers prominent** | D-04 as originally written had no off switch and was therefore coercive. Sellers may disable offers per listing (Buy Now only); the minimum threshold (D-03) is the softer version (D-20, DT-06) | Buyer sees no offer control; "This seller isn't taking offers" — stated, not merely absent |
| **Offer on a `reserved` unit** (someone else's Buy Now is mid-payment) | Permitted as a **queued offer**. Does not compete with the live reservation; becomes actionable only if the unit returns to `active`. Clock starts on return, not on creation. Auto-`voided` if the unit → `sold`, or if it hasn't returned within 14 days (D-21) | "Reserved — your offer is queued. If this falls through, the seller sees it first." Converts 13.03.03's "The Jazzmaster you missed is back" moment instantly instead of restarting a race |
| **Part-exchange: "£600 + my Blues Junior"** | **Not an offer.** Non-cash components are not atomically acceptable — accepting creates an obligation on a unit the platform doesn't control and can't value at the arbitration point. Routed to 13.09 (D-07, DT-04) | "Trades are handled through part-exchange" → 13.09 entry point. The single most-expected gear behaviour the offer object cannot carry |
| **Buyer means £900 all-in; seller means £900 + £120 freight** | The category's most common offer ambiguity — gear freight is material (amps, cabs, pianos), unlike flat-rate small goods. Resolved: the offer is on **item price only** (D-08), with the delivered estimate shown at make- and review-time so neither party is surprised at checkout | Both sides see "£900 item + ~£120 delivery = ~£1,020" on the same screen as the number they're agreeing |
| **Buyer offers in USD on a GBP listing** | Offers are always denominated in the **listing's** currency (D-09). Any conversion is indicative and labelled | "≈ $1,150 — you'll be charged £900 plus delivery." Prevents FX drift between acceptance and checkout becoming a dispute |
| **Local pickup changes the number's meaning** (13.11) | No conflict — the offer is item price; fulfilment (ship vs pickup) is chosen at checkout | Delivered estimate shows both options where pickup is offered |
| **Offer on a bundle** (13.03.05) | Bundle atomicity means an offer is on the whole bundle, never a constituent — bundle splitting is a negotiation, not a system operation (13.03.05 D-03). Acceptance claims **every** constituent through the arbitration point; if any constituent cannot be claimed, the whole acceptance fails atomically | "Offers are on the full bundle." Depends on 13.03.03 Q-01 (shared-unit reservation across listings) — flagged, not resolved here |
| Lowball spam | High-volume derisory offers waste seller attention | Seller-set minimum offer threshold; auto-decline below it (D-03), notified on D-19's delay |
| **Seller offers on their own listing** (shill / price-anchoring) | Blocked. **Identity-level blocking is insufficient** — bands, labels and studios are first-class entities (personas.md), so a Musician can offer on their own band's storefront listing without sharing an identity. Blocked when the offering identity **is** the seller **or holds sell permission on the selling entity** (D-4a, DT-11) | "You can't make an offer on your own listing" — including where "your own" means your band's |
| **Offer from a user the seller has blocked** (domain 24) | Never delivered; not silently dropped for the sender either | Offer control unavailable: "You can't offer on this seller's listings" |
| **Seller suspended or deactivated mid-negotiation** (domain 24) | Live offers `voided`, not left pending against an account that cannot respond | "This listing is unavailable" — never a countdown against a party that no longer exists |
| **Stolen-registry hit lands while an offer is live** (13.03.07) | Screening runs at publish and at checkout (13.03.07 D-02), so a hit typically surfaces **after** acceptance, at checkout, before money moves. Listing held; live offers **freeze** (clock paused), not expire — expiring a buyer's offer for a hold they didn't cause is punishing the wrong party (D-22). No new offers accepted while held | Buyer: "This listing is on hold while we check something" — never an accusation, per 13.03.07 D-01. Escalates to domain 24 |
| **Offer accepted, then the unit gets a stolen-registry hit at checkout** | Claim exists; listing held; money has not moved | Escalates to domain 24 before settlement (13.03.07) |
| Buyer ghosts after acceptance | Depends on whether acceptance binds (Q-01) — the reservation expires either way; its length is 13.03.03 Q-03's, not this file's | Unit returns to `active`; ghosting feeds buyer reputation (CX-M08) |
| Seller accepts, then can't ship | Seller cancellation; costs them under CX-M08 — **except** the cross-channel-sync cause, which is non-punitive (13.03.03 D-04) | Cancellation reason captured |
| **Listing expires unsold with a live offer** (13.03.02 Q-03) | The listing's calendar expiry does **not** kill a live negotiation. The listing is extended to the offer's expiry (D-10) | Seller: "Extended — you have a live offer" |
| **ISO expires with a live offer outstanding** (CX-01) | The offer survives its parent context — its lifetime is its own (D-10). Resolves the sub-domain CX-01 synthesis Q2 gap from this side | ISO closes; the offer thread continues untouched |
| **Second-chance offer to an auction underbidder** (CX-03) | Offers machinery invoked from an auction's failure path. Permitted only once the auction's claim is **fully released** — otherwise the domain oversells via its own recovery path. Not stackable with a live auction (sub-domain D-03) | Underbidder: "The winner didn't complete — you can have it at your last bid of £940." Whether it binds the underbidder is Q-05 |
| Buyer accepts a counter after the seller's unit was claimed elsewhere | Same arbitration; acceptance fails | "This unit was claimed" + routes onward — never an ambiguous failure |
| Notification arrives 6 h late on a 48 h offer | Eats 12.5% of the window. Latency is the feature's usability, not a nice-to-have | Expiry clock is always visible **in-product**, never only in an email (D-15) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Offer submitting / accepting | Inline spinner. Acceptance shows "Securing this item" — must be fast enough to be invisible (13.03.03) |
| **Empty** | No offers on a listing | Seller: "No offers yet" + the minimum threshold if set. Buyer: prominent "Make an offer" affordance (D-04), or "This seller isn't taking offers" if disabled (D-20) |
| **Populated** | Offers exist | Seller: offer list with amount, delivered estimate, expiry countdown, buyer standing (CX-M08), move count. Buyer: their own thread only — never other buyers' offers, never a count of them (D-13) |
| **Error** | Accept fails (raced, expired, or ineligible) | Named cause + a route onward: "This unit was claimed" / "That offer expired 3 seconds ago → Counter at £900". Never a 500, never a bare failure |
| **Partial — countered** | Countered, awaiting the other party | "Countered at £950 — awaiting response (expires in 47h)" + move N of 6 |
| **Partial — queued** | Offer on a `reserved` unit (D-21) | "Queued — clock starts if this becomes available again. Expires if not available within 14 days" |
| **Partial — frozen** | Listing held by a stolen-registry check (D-22) | "On hold while we check something — your offer is paused, not expired" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Offers can be handled through messaging (CX-M06) — it's a conversation about price | Haggling is social, and the platform already has a contextual inbox serving this domain | ❌ REJECTED: an offer must be **atomically acceptable**, because acceptance claims a qty=1 unit (sub-domain D-01). A message saying "ok, £900 then" cannot be arbitrated against a simultaneous Buy Now — there is no state to lock. Messaging *around* an offer is right and expected (CX-M06); the offer itself must be a structured object with a state machine. This distinction is what makes 13.03.03's single-arbitration-point invariant implementable at all. |
| DT-02 | Offers are a buyer-side feature — a way to pay less | The obvious framing | ⚠️ REFRAMED: offers are the domain's **price-discovery instrument in thin markets** — and they run exactly where the price guide is mute (13.04.03 DT-02: thin is permanent for most of this catalog). On a model with two comps in six years, the guide cannot say what it's worth; a live offer *can*, because a real buyer with real money has stated a number. **Declined offers are therefore evidence too** — a pattern of offers clustering at £900 against a £1,400 asking price is market information the comp set will never contain. Whether that becomes an input to 13.04 is a real question (Q-03), and it partially answers 13.04.03 Q-03's "is the guide worth building if it's mute on the rare?" — the mechanism that speaks where the guide can't already exists in this file. |
| DT-03 | Q-03 (do declined offers feed the price guide?) is independently answerable | It was filed as its own question, and DT-02 makes the data look valuable | ❌ REJECTED — **Q-03's answer is a function of Q-01's, and the two must be decided together.** If acceptance does not bind (Q-01's "invitation to checkout" branch), an offer is a **costless assertion**: anyone with an account can state any number, at any volume, for free. A price guide fed by costless assertions is corruptible by whoever wants to move it — including the seller's own sockpuppet placing offers at £1,400 to lift the guide on their own model. That is **shill bidding (13.05.02 Edge Cases) relocated to a surface with no auction to hide in, and no reserve or bid history to audit it against**. Offer data can only inform 13.04 if offers carry a cost — payment authorisation, reputation stake, or binding acceptance. So the sequence is forced: decide Q-01 first; Q-03 is unanswerable before it. Two questions filed independently are one question. |
| DT-04 | An offer can carry a part-exchange component — "£600 + my Blues Junior" | **What a gear-trade domain expert asks about immediately.** An enormous share of real used-gear offers are part-cash-part-gear; Reverb runs a whole trade-in flow beside its offer flow, and every shop counter in the category works this way | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real expectation, ❌ REJECTED as an offer feature. A non-cash component destroys atomic acceptability: accepting "£600 + your Blues Junior" simultaneously claims *my* unit and creates an obligation on *your* unit — which is not listed, not graded (13.02), not screened (13.03.07), and not valuable at the arbitration point, because valuing it requires an inspection (13.09.03) that cannot happen inside a tap. The claim would be half-atomic, which is worse than not atomic. **Part-exchange belongs to 13.09, initiated there, never smuggled into an offer.** This is a genuine boundary, and it means the domain must route the expectation explicitly — a buyer who types "£600 + my Blues Junior" into a message (CX-M06) and gets a "yeah alright" has created exactly the unarbitrable agreement DT-01 rejects. |
| DT-05 | The offer is on the delivered/landed price — that's the number the buyer actually pays | The buyer's mental model, and it's what they care about | ❌ REJECTED: freight is destination-dependent (13.07.01) and not knowable until an address exists; duties and landed cost shift it again on international (13.07.02, Q-05 domain). An offer on a landed number would be an agreement to an amount that **changes after acceptance** — non-atomic by construction, and a dispute generator. The offer is on **item price** (D-08). **But the buyer's mental model is not wrong, it is unserved**: "£900 accepted" becoming "£1,180 at checkout" is a bait-and-switch by omission, and on this catalog — where a 4×12 cab's freight is a third of its value — it would be the offer flow's single biggest complaint. Hence D-08's other half: the delivered estimate is shown **on the same screen as the number being agreed**, at both make-time and review-time. The number is item-only; the disclosure is total. |
| DT-06 | D-04 ("the offer affordance is prominent") is free — it costs nothing to invite offers | It reads as pure UX generosity toward the Fan persona | ❌ REJECTED — **D-04 as originally written is coercive, because it had no off switch.** "£1,000 FIRM" is a real, common and legitimate seller position in this category. A prominent, always-present offer control tells every buyer that every price is negotiable, which forces the firm-price seller either to haggle or to field offers they will always decline. The fix is small and required: offers are disablable per listing (D-20), and the state is **stated, not merely absent** — "This seller isn't taking offers" — because an absent control reads as a bug. D-03's minimum threshold is the soft version of the same control. D-04 survives, scoped: prominent **where offers are enabled**. |
| DT-07 | A pending offer should reserve the unit — it's a serious expression of intent | It feels fair to the buyer, and symmetrical with the seller's frozen price | ❌ REJECTED, directly from 13.03.03 D-02 ("a cart is not a claim; only purchase claims"). The same logic binds harder here: if a pending offer reserved the unit, one buyer could freeze five sellers' inventory for 48h at zero cost, and a malicious one could freeze a whole model's supply. **Offer-spraying would become an inventory denial-of-service** — the qty=1 regime's worst failure, executed for free. Only acceptance claims (D-05). The residual asymmetry is real: the seller's price *is* frozen by a live offer (13.03.02 D-03) while their inventory is not. That asymmetry is acceptable only because declining is one tap and unfreezes instantly (D-17) — so the constraint is never a trap. Without D-17, D-03's price freeze would reintroduce the DoS through the pricing surface instead of the inventory one. |
| DT-08 | Price *drops* should be exempt from 13.03.02 D-03's edit block — a drop can only help the offering buyer | Symmetry argument: D-03 exists to stop bad faith, and a drop is not bad faith | ❌ REJECTED: a drop below a live offer creates an absurdity — a buyer's £900 offer pending on a now-£850 listing, where the buyer is offering more than the stated price and the seller has an incentive to accept it. The exception designed to protect the buyer harms them. Keep D-03 flat: **all price edits blocked while any offer is live**, no special cases, unblocked by declining (D-17). Recorded because the exception is intuitive and someone will propose it again downstream. |
| DT-09 | The seller's auto-rules (D-03) are private configuration | They live in the seller's settings, so they look private | ❌ REJECTED — **auto-rules are probeable, and a probed rule is a published rule.** With auto-decline below Y and auto-accept above X, a buyer making repeated offers binary-searches both thresholds in a handful of moves, then offers exactly X and takes the unit at the seller's true floor. Worse, the rules **self-identify by latency**: an auto-decline fires in 200 ms; a human decline arrives hours later. The timing alone tells the buyer a rule exists and roughly where it sits. Two controls, both required: D-18's +5%-or-cooldown limits the probe rate, and D-19 notifies auto-declines on a randomised 30–120 min delay so rule-driven and human declines are indistinguishable. ⚠️ **Accepted tension**: honest buyers wait up to 2h for a decline the system decided instantly. That is a real cost paid by the many to protect the few, and the alternative — a seller's floor being trivially discoverable — makes D-03's rules actively harmful to the persona they exist to serve. Flagged for `/create-prd` to revisit if the latency cost proves worse than modelled. |
| DT-10 | The Operator persona justifies D-03's bulk auto-rules | D-03's stated rationale: "the Operator persona cannot absorb the attention tax at fleet volume" | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **and this is the domain's dealer-persona gap arriving from a fourth direction.** `meta/personas.md` defines the Operator as selling **time and space** — a rehearsal room, a studio, a venue. A rehearsal-room operator does not hold "fleet stock" of guitars, and does not need bulk offer rules to survive an offer inbox. The persona that does — high-volume gear stock, thin margins, auto-accept floors, offer volume measured in hundreds per week — is a **dealer**, and no persona in the ratified set is one. Domain Q-03 flagged this in 13.13, 13.09 and 13.12; `13.08.01 DT-01` flagged it as a compliance question. This is the fourth arrival, and the first from the *negotiation* surface. D-03's mechanism is right; its rationale is hanging on a persona that may not exist. Does not block D-03 — a Musician with 20 listings wants a minimum threshold too — but the *bulk* half needs an owner. See Q-04. |
| DT-11 | Blocking self-offers is a simple identity check — is the offerer the seller? | The obvious implementation, and it's what every marketplace does | ❌ REJECTED as insufficient, because **this platform's entity model is not every marketplace's**. `meta/personas.md`: bands, labels, studios and venues are **first-class entities** that own and get paid independently of their members. So a Musician can offer on a listing sold by *their own band's* storefront, or a Producer on their *own studio's* — different identity, same interest, and the offer anchors a public price on their own unit for free. An identity-equality check passes it. The check must be **permission-scoped**: blocked when the offering identity is the seller **or holds sell permission on the selling entity** (D-4a). This is the multi-hyphenate structural fact biting a surface nobody expected it to touch, and it will recur anywhere the platform asks "is this the same party?" — flagged to `/create-prd-architecture` alongside personas.md Q-04. |
| DT-12 | An offer on a `reserved` unit is meaningless — the unit is spoken for, so hide the control | The state says unavailable; showing an offer control looks like a bug | ❌ REJECTED, and inverting it produces a genuinely good feature the sweep never surfaced. Reservations **do** collapse — 13.03.02's happy path names abandoned payment explicitly, and 13.03.03 calls the return "a genuinely delightful moment, and free". Today that moment restarts a race: the unit returns to `active` and whoever refreshes fastest wins, which re-runs the exact experience 13.03.03 D-01 designed away. A **queued offer** (D-21) converts it instead — the buyer who missed states their number once, and if the reservation collapses the seller has a real offer waiting rather than a repopulated listing page. Clock starts on return, not creation, or a 48h offer dies inside a 72h reservation. 14-day ceiling so a queue is not an indefinite obligation. This is the cheapest win in the file: it costs one state and it makes 13.03.03's best moment convert. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An offer is a structured object with a state machine, never a message | Per DT-01 — atomicity requires state. |
| D-02 | Acceptance claims the unit atomically through the single arbitration point | Per sub-domain D-01 and 13.03.03 CX-01 synthesis Q5. |
| D-03 | Sellers may set a minimum offer threshold with auto-decline, and an auto-accept floor | Lowball spam is an attention tax at volume. **Rationale amended**: the persona this serves at *bulk* scale may not exist (DT-10, Q-04). The threshold half serves every seller; the bulk half awaits a dealer persona. |
| D-04 | The offer affordance is prominent **where offers are enabled** | Offering is the category norm; the Fan persona won't know that unless the UI says so. **Scoped by D-20** — the original unscoped form was coercive (DT-06). |
| D-4a | Self-dealing is blocked by **permission**, not identity — the offerer may not be the seller *or* hold sell permission on the selling entity | Per DT-11 — bands/studios/labels are first-class entities (personas.md), so an identity check passes a Musician offering on their own band's listing. |
| D-05 | **A pending offer never reserves the unit.** Only acceptance claims | Per DT-07 and 13.03.03 D-02. Without this, offer-spraying is a free inventory DoS on qty=1 stock. |
| D-06 | Default expiry **48h**, seller-configurable **24h–7d** per listing. A counter resets the clock to the same window. Max **6 moves** per thread (1 offer + 5 counters) | 48h guarantees both parties a full waking cycle regardless of timezone offset — the Producer negotiates with strangers across continents, and a 24h default can close while one side sleeps. 6 moves is 2× the ≤3 rounds real negotiations converge in: generous enough never to bind an honest deal, tight enough that the object cannot be used as a chat channel (which CX-M06 already serves better, per DT-01). |
| D-07 | **Offers are cash-only.** Part-exchange is 13.09's mechanism, never a component of an offer | Per DT-04 — a non-cash component makes acceptance half-atomic, which is worse than not atomic. |
| D-08 | **The offer is on the item price**, in isolation from freight, duties and landed cost — **and** the delivered estimate is shown on the same screen as the number being agreed, at make- and review-time | Per DT-05 — a landed-cost offer is an agreement to an amount that changes after acceptance. But the buyer's all-in mental model is unserved, not wrong: on this catalog freight is material, and "£900 accepted → £1,180 at checkout" is a bait-and-switch by omission. |
| D-09 | Offers are always denominated in the **listing's** currency; conversions are indicative and labelled | Prevents FX drift between acceptance and checkout becoming a dispute over an amount both parties thought was fixed. |
| D-10 | **An offer's lifetime is its own.** It does not inherit the expiry of its listing, its ISO, or any other originating context; a listing with a live offer extends to the offer's expiry | Resolves sub-domain CX-01 synthesis Q2 and the 13.03.02 Q-03 interaction. Killing a live negotiation for a calendar reason destroys value for both parties and no rule requires it. |
| D-11 | The offers surface is **role-scoped**: "Offers I've made" and "Offers on my listings" are never one merged list | The multi-hyphenate runs both sides simultaneously (personas.md). A merged list makes the most common persona's most common state incomprehensible. |
| D-12 | The offer form states what acceptance means, in one plain sentence, before submit — not a checkbox | The Fan persona has no category fluency and no reason to infer that acceptance obligates them. A checkbox is not read; a sentence beside the number is. |
| D-13 | **Offers are private.** The buyer sees only their own thread; the seller sees all. No pending-offer count is shown to buyers | Derived from sub-domain D-03, not chosen: public offers — or even a count — reconstruct an ascending auction on an offer listing, which is the exact two-claim-path collision D-03 forbids. The count leaks the competitive signal with none of the auction's structure (reserve, bid history, close time), mirroring 13.05.03 D-02's prohibition on aggregate registry counts. Industry precedent agrees (Reverb, eBay Best Offer). |
| D-14 | Eligibility (region, voltage, CITES) is checked at **offer time** | Weaker than 13.05.02 D-01's bid-time rule because an offer may not bind — but an accepted offer on an undeliverable unit still costs the seller a cancellation under CX-M08 for a defect the platform could have caught. |
| D-15 | Offer / counter / accept / decline notifications dispatch within **60s P95**; the expiry clock is always visible in-product, never only in an email | A 6h notification delay on a 48h offer silently consumes 12.5% of the window. Time-boxing without visible time is a trap. |
| D-16 | When a claim lands, every other live offer on that unit is `voided` **in the same atomic step**, with a stated reason and routes onward | Per 13.03.03 D-01 — the losing buyer is a designed experience, not an error path. A silently vanishing offer is indistinguishable from being ignored. |
| D-17 | The seller may decline any offer at any time, for any reason, instantly unfreezing price edits | Makes 13.03.02 D-03's price freeze survivable at volume (a listing with 5 live offers is frozen until all 5 resolve) and closes the DoS that D-05 would otherwise reopen through the pricing surface (DT-07). |
| D-18 | After a decline, a new offer on the same listing must exceed the declined amount by **≥5% of asking**, or wait **24h** | Blocks £5-increment grinding and rate-limits threshold probing (DT-09). Expressed as a percentage of asking to stay currency-neutral. |
| D-19 | Auto-declines notify the buyer on a **randomised 30–120 min delay** | Per DT-09 — an instant decline self-identifies as a rule and reveals roughly where the floor sits. ⚠️ Accepted tension: honest buyers wait for a decision made instantly. |
| D-20 | Offers are **disablable per listing**; the state is stated, not merely absent | Per DT-06 — "£1,000 FIRM" is legitimate and common. An absent control reads as a bug; "This seller isn't taking offers" reads as a position. |
| D-21 | Offers may be made on `reserved` units as **queued offers** — clock starts if the unit returns to `active`, voided on `sold` or after 14 days unreturned | Per DT-12 — converts 13.03.03's "The Jazzmaster you missed is back" moment instead of restarting a race. Cheapest win in the file. |
| D-22 | A stolen-registry hold **freezes** live offers (clock paused); it never expires them, and no new offers are accepted while held | Per 13.03.07 D-01/D-02 — expiring a buyer's offer for a hold they didn't cause punishes the wrong party, and the hold is not an accusation against anyone. |
