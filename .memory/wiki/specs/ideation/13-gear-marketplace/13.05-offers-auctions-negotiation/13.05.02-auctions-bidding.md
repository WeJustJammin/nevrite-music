# Feature: Auctions & Bidding

> **Number**: 13.05.02
> **Parent**: [Offers, Auctions & Negotiation](./13.05-offers-auctions-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A timed, ascending-price sale where the highest bid at close wins. It suits the cases the offer model handles badly: genuinely rare units where nobody knows the price, estate/liquidation sales where the seller wants a clean exit at whatever the market says, and units with contested value. It is a different market culture from offers — and running both may be a mistake worth naming (sub-domain Q-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (both sides) | Bids on rare pieces; occasionally auctions something they can't price. The persona most likely to get emotionally caught in a bidding war on an instrument they've hunted for years. |
| Producer | Full (both sides) | Auctions studio clear-outs where the goal is *sold by Friday*, not *maximum price*. Bids on rare outboard where an auction is the only place it surfaces. |
| Operator | Full (both sides) | Liquidates fleet stock in batches. The persona most likely to use auctions as a business tool rather than an event. |
| Fan | Read-only (bidder side only) | May bid. No seller side. **Most at risk**: auction dynamics are designed to induce overpaying, and this persona has no calibration for what the thing is worth, no price guide fluency, and the least ability to walk away. See DT-02. |

## Behavior

A timed listing with a start price, optional reserve, and a close time. Bids are ascending and binding. At close, the highest bid above reserve **claims the unit** (sub-domain D-01) through the same arbitration point as Buy Now and offer acceptance. A listing is either an auction or an offer listing, never both (sub-domain D-03).

### Happy Path

1. Seller lists as an auction with start price, reserve and duration
2. Buyers bid; each bid is binding
3. Auction closes; highest bid above reserve wins
4. Unit claimed atomically; winner proceeds to checkout (13.06.01)
5. Sale becomes a comp (13.04.01) — and an auction comp is arguably the truest price signal the guide can have

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reserve not met | No sale; no claim | Seller may relist (13.03.02) |
| Winner ghosts | Unit unsold, seller has lost the auction's momentum — worse than an offer ghost because the audience has dispersed | Second-chance offer to the underbidder; ghosting hits buyer reputation |
| Bid sniping in the final seconds | Endemic to auctions; anti-snipe extension is the standard answer | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether to extend on late bids |
| **Winner is in a country the item cannot ship to (CITES — 13.07.03)** | A won auction that cannot be delivered. The seller has a binding sale and an undeliverable object | **Bid-time eligibility check required** — see DT-01. This is the sub-domain's sharpest defect. |
| Shill bidding by the seller | Inflates price with fake demand; corrupts the comp (13.04.01 Q-02) | Detection is domain 24; the comp damage is 13.04's |
| Auction closes while the unit has a stolen-registry hit pending | Claim exists on a held listing | Escalates before money moves (13.03.07) |
| Auction and a pending offer on the same unit | Prevented structurally — a listing is one mode or the other (sub-domain D-03) | Mode chosen at listing time |
| Seller cancels mid-auction | Bidders have relied on it; cancellation is not free | Cancellation reason + CX-M08 consequence |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An auction is just a listing with a timer and a price that goes up | The standard mental model, and how it looks from the listing form | ❌ REJECTED — and this exposes a real defect the candidate list never surfaced. A bid is **binding**, so eligibility must be checked at **bid time**, not at checkout. This domain has hard shipping constraints no other marketplace has: a rosewood-fingerboard guitar cannot cross certain borders without CITES paperwork (13.07.03), a 100V unit is useless in a 230V country (13.01.05), and a 7ft grand piano cannot be freighted to some destinations at any price (13.07.01). In an offer flow, an ineligible buyer is a declined offer — no harm. **In an auction, an ineligible winner is a binding sale of an undeliverable object.** Every other marketplace's auction can ignore this because their goods ship anywhere. Ours cannot. Bid-time eligibility is a hard requirement, and it did not exist anywhere in the sweep. |
| DT-02 | Offering both auctions and offers gives sellers useful choice | More options, more flexibility — the obvious read, and why sub-domain Q-02 is open | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH TENSION: they are different *market cultures*, not different settings. Reverb's audience expects to negotiate; eBay's expects to bid. Running both means every buyer must first work out which game this listing is playing, and the Fan persona — already the least calibrated — bears that cost. There is also a thesis argument against: auctions optimise for **price extraction**, while this platform's thesis (D-18) is that consolidation earns trust and provenance keeps it. An auction-heavy marketplace is a different relationship with the user than a negotiation-heavy one. Not an agent call — but the trade-off should be explicit at MoSCoW rather than defaulted into. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Eligibility (shipping, CITES, region) is checked at **bid time**, not checkout | Per DT-01 — a binding bid from an ineligible bidder is an undeliverable sale. |
