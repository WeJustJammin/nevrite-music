# Feature: Gear Seller Storefront & Commerce Policies

> **Number**: 13.12
> **Parent**: [Gear Marketplace (Physical Goods)](./gear-marketplace-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A seller's shopfront and the rules they trade under: return policy, handling time, which regions they ship to, whether they accept offers, whether they offer local pickup. These are the settings that make a seller's listings behave consistently, and they are gear-specific because the policies that matter here — freight willingness, international shipping, offer acceptance, restocking on an irreplaceable object — don't exist in the same shape for digital goods (14) or services (05). A licence key has no handling time; a mix has no return freight.

The storefront is also the **seller-side public surface** of the domain: the page a buyer lands on from search (CX-M16), from a listing, or from Google (CX-M23). Per DT-07 it is the one place in the domain where a seller's *accumulated* provenance contribution can be rendered — which is the thesis (D-18) appearing on a page the sweep filed as vendor admin.

**Split from sweep candidate #17** ("Vendor Onboarding, Storefronts, Tiers & Scorecards"): onboarding is CX-M15, seller performance scorecards and tiers are CX-M08, and carrier rate configuration is CX-M14. What remains — and what this file owns — is the **storefront presentation** and the **gear-specific commerce policy set**.

### The policy set — the domain's commerce terms

Policies are **structured fields, never free text** (D-09). Every policy is a storefront-level default inherited by every listing at publish; the listing may override it only in the direction D-06 allows.

> **Calibration seeds.** Following the precedent set at 13.03.01 (media caps) and 13.01.04 Q-01: the *behaviour* is specified and locked; the *constants* below marked "seed" are first-fit values awaiting real marketplace data. A seed is a decision, not a placeholder — it ships as written until data moves it.

| Policy | Form | Storefront default | Listing override | Statute / eligibility interaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Return window** | integer days, `[statutory floor .. 365]` | the buyer's statutory floor (D-02) | **extend only** (D-06) | Floor resolves per **buyer's** jurisdiction, not the seller's. |
| **Who pays return freight** | enum per reason `{seller, buyer}` | per 13.08.01 D-01 | extend toward buyer only | Not-as-described → **seller always**, not configurable. |
| **Restocking fee** | percent `0–15` (seed) | `0` | **reduce only** | Never applies to a statutory change-of-mind return or a not-as-described return. Where it cannot lawfully apply, it is not displayed. |
| **Handling time** | business days **per freight class**, `1–30` (seed) | `3` parcel · `10` freight/oversize (seeds — DT-04) | either direction | A promise; feeds CX-M08 (D-12). |
| **Ship-to regions** | structured region list (CX-M20 vocabulary) | seller's own region only | **narrow only** | A **willingness, not a capability** (D-07). CITES and freight feasibility overrule it downward at checkout (13.06.01 D-02) and at bid time (13.05.02 D-01). |
| **Local pickup** | boolean + area | `off` | either direction | Area, never an address (13.11 D-01). |
| **Offers** | boolean + auto-decline **percent of asking** (D-13) | `on`, no auto-decline | either direction; listing sets an **absolute** floor (13.05.01 D-03) | MAP never restricts offers (13.13 D-01). |
| **Layaway** | boolean | `off` | either direction | Terms are 13.06.03's. |
| **Trade-in accepted** | boolean | `off` | either direction | 13.09.01. |
| **Consignment accepted** | boolean | `off` | n/a (storefront-level only) | 13.09.02. |
| **Warranty beyond statutory** | structured (13.08.03) | none — stated explicitly, never blank (13.08.03 D-02) | extend only | **In addition to** statutory rights, never instead of. |
| **Combined shipping** | boolean | `on` | either direction | Multi-line from one vendor (13.06.01). |
| **Away mode** | boolean + return date, ≤ 90 days (seed) | `off` | n/a | Suspends new claims; **never** suspends obligations on existing orders (D-14). |

### The three rules that govern the set

1. **Statute beats policy, and the policy must not claim otherwise** (D-02). Where a term is unenforceable against a given buyer, it is **not rendered to that buyer as if valid** — because per DT-02 displaying it is worse than omitting it.
2. **Inheritance is forward-only, and the claim snapshot is the contract** (D-03, D-04). Per 13.03.02 DT-04 the listing snapshot freezes at `reserved`; **the policy set is part of that snapshot**. A policy edit therefore cannot reach a claimed order at all — which makes propagation latency structurally harmless (D-04). This is stronger than a fairness rule; it is why no fairness rule is needed.
3. **Overrides move toward the buyer on rights, either way on operations** (D-06). A listing may take longer to dispatch than the storefront promises (a 60kg cab honestly does). A listing may **not** carry a shorter return window than the storefront advertises — that is how a shop advertises "30-day returns" storefront-wide and quietly excepts the expensive stock.

### Happy Path A — the seller configures once (the spine)

1. Seller completes CX-M15 activation; the storefront is created with **D-05's defaults already set** — no unconfigured state ever exists (D-05)
2. System shows the policy set as a single reviewable panel, with the return window pre-filled at the **statutory floor for each region the seller ships to** (D-02), and states in plain language which fields it derived rather than asked for
3. Seller adjusts what they care about — typically ship-to regions and local pickup; typically nothing else (Musician per Role Lens)
4. On save, system **validates against statute before persisting** — a return window below the floor is rejected inline, on blur, naming the region ("Your 7-day window is below the 14-day minimum for buyers in the EU"), not on submit
5. Seller publishes their first listing (13.03.01); the listing inherits the full set at publish and displays the inherited values inline in the listing form, editable within D-06's direction
6. A buyer opens the listing → sees the applicable policies **before** purchase; the terms in D-10's itemised set are rendered at checkout as line items, not as a link
7. Buyer purchases → the policy set **snapshots into the order** at `reserved` (D-04)
8. Months later the seller narrows their return window; **future listings inherit it; live listings hold their published terms; claimed orders are untouched** (D-03, D-04)

### Happy Path B — a buyer reads a storefront

1. Buyer lands on the storefront from search, a listing, or an indexed page (CX-M23)
2. Sees, in fixed order: shop identity + standing (01, CX-M08) → **policy panel, rendered identically to every other storefront's** (D-08) → provenance contribution, if any (DT-07) → listing grid
3. Policy panel shows only terms **valid for this buyer** — their statutory floor is applied to the displayed return window, and terms unenforceable against them are absent, not struck through (D-02)
4. Buyer filters the grid (CX-M16), follows the shop (CX-M28), or messages the seller (CX-M06)
5. Buyer purchases from within the storefront; the terms they read are the terms that snapshot

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Policy contradicts a statutory right** | Statute wins; the term is unenforceable **and not displayed to the buyer it cannot bind** (D-02) | Buyer in a protected market sees their real window. Seller sees why their entry was overridden, per region. Per DT-02 a "no returns" shown to a consumer is worse than no policy at all. |
| Seller enters a return window below the statutory floor | Rejected on blur, per region, before save | "7 days is below the 14-day minimum for buyers in the EU. Your window there will be 14 days." Not a submit-time failure. |
| **Policy edited while N listings are live** | Forward-only (D-03). Live listings hold published terms until relisted or edited | Seller: "Applies to new listings. Your 46 live listings keep the terms they were published under." Buyers: no change. |
| **Policy edited after a claim, before dispatch** | **Structurally impossible to harm the buyer** — the snapshot froze at `reserved` (13.03.02 DT-04, D-04 here). Not an amendment (13.03.02 D-08), because the seller is not changing the goods | Nothing. Silence is correct. Contrast: this is *not* the 13.03.02 Q-01 shape the [SURFACE] draft assumed — the snapshot already answered it. |
| **Two-device policy edit conflict** (Operator: phone at the dock, desk session open) | Last-write-wins is wrong for terms that bind strangers. The second save is rejected against a stale read; the seller re-reads before overwriting | "Your policies changed on another device 4 minutes ago. Here's the difference — keep yours or theirs?" Same shape as 13.03.02 DT-13. |
| **Private seller with a dealer-shaped storefront** | The regime question, unanswerable today | Q-01 — and per DT-05 the answer may attach to the *storefront*, not the person. |
| Storefront vs. the seller's identity profile (domain 01) | A shop is an organisation; 01 owns organisations | D-01 — 01 owns the entity; this file owns the trading terms. |
| **One human, two capacities** — a Producer's private pedal sale and their studio's ex-rental stock | Per DT-05 the trader determination may differ **between two storefronts of one person**, which is lawful and is why "is this seller a trader?" may be the wrong question | Q-01, reframed. |
| Seller declares "no international"; buyer is abroad | Eligibility resolves per item, per destination, **before payment** | Itemised and explained, never silently dropped (13.06.01 D-04). |
| **Seller declares "ships worldwide"; the item is a rosewood-board Martin** | The policy is a **willingness**; CITES is a **fact**. The fact wins (D-07) | Buyer: "This one can't ship to your country — remove it and continue?" (13.06.01). Seller: warned at listing time, not at the buyer's checkout. |
| Handling time promised vs. actual | A promise is a performance metric | Feeds CX-M08 (D-12). |
| **One storefront handling time across a pedal and a piano** | Wrong in both directions — a lie for freight, a drag on parcel | Per-freight-class, defaulted from 13.07.01's classification (DT-04, D-15). |
| **Seller goes on tour with the gear they're selling** | The platform *knows* — domain 17 holds their dates. It proposes away mode rather than waiting to be told (DT-03) | "You have 12 dates from Friday. Pause dispatch until the 30th?" One tap. The consolidation dividend, on a vendor-admin page. |
| **Seller forgets to turn away mode ON** (the real failure — not forgetting to turn it off) | Late dispatch → CX-M08 damage → a scorecard hit for being on tour | D-16: the proposal in DT-03 is the mitigation; there is no punitive mechanism here. |
| Away mode activated with orders already `reserved` | Away mode gates **new** claims only. Existing obligations stand | Seller: "Away mode is on. You still have 2 orders to dispatch — away mode doesn't pause those." (D-14) |
| Away mode never ended; return date passed | At `return date + 14 days` (seed) the storefront prompts; listings do **not** auto-relist | "Your shop has been away since March. Reopen, or extend?" Never silent reopening — a silent reopen is a dispatch promise the seller never made. |
| **Seller configured nothing** | Cannot happen (D-05). Defaults are set at creation, not left null | There is no "unconfigured seller" state to render. |
| **Storefront with zero active listings but real history** | Not empty — per DT-07 the sold record and the chain contribution survive | "No listings right now. 47 instruments sold. 12 with a complete ownership record." Reverb renders a feedback number here; this is the thing it structurally cannot render. |
| Every listing lapsed via the freshness challenge (13.03.02 DT-05) | Same as above; the shop reads as dormant, not deleted | "This shop hasn't listed since 2024." Honest, not hidden. |
| **Per-unit provenance on the storefront without the owner's consent** | Forbidden. The chain's visibility is the **current owner's**, not the seller's (13.06.05 D-02, pending domain Q-04). Only the **aggregate count** is the seller's own fact | D-17. A shop cannot advertise using a buyer's instrument. |
| **Seller suspended or banned** (domain 24) | The storefront unpublishes; **live orders' obligations do not vanish**; the indexed page (CX-M23) tombstones rather than 404s | Anyone holding the URL: "This shop is unavailable." Never a reason — same principle as 13.03.02's `held` (13.03.07 D-01: nobody is accused). Buyers with open orders keep their order, their terms and their return rights. |
| **Seller requests erasure** (CX-M18) while sold listings are permanent (13.03.02 D-01) and the chain is append-only (13.06.05) | The storefront **page** unpublishes; the sold record persists de-identified as comp data; the chain is not rewritable | Q-04 — a real collision between a statutory right and two locked immutability decisions, arriving from the public-page angle. |
| **Free text in a policy field** — "no returns, all sales final, WhatsApp me on 07…" | Impossible by construction (D-09). Structured fields only | Free text is simultaneously where the unenforceable claim hides *and* the off-platform leak lives — the `meta/personas.md` Musician anti-persona ("takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees"). One decision closes both. |
| Staff member edits the return policy (CX-M09) | Rights-affecting terms require the storefront's **owning principal**; operational terms are delegable (D-11) | Assistant: "Handling time is yours to change. Return terms need the shop owner." |
| **Consignee's storefront selling a consignor's unit** (13.09.02) | The **consignee's** policy set applies — they are the seller of record — but a return unwinds to the **consignor**, who never chose those terms and owns the object (13.09.02 D-01) | Q-05. The consignor is bound by terms set by someone who does not own their asset. |
| Rental stock (13.10) under a sale storefront | The sale policy set does not fit — a return window is meaningless against a hire; deposits and damage have no field here | Reinforces domain Q-07 (promote rental): the policy set is a **fifth** structure rental cannot reuse, alongside its order, comp, ownership and condition models. |
| **Cross-channel policy drift** (13.03.04) — their eBay terms differ from ours | Policies are **not** synced by CX-M29/13.03.04; only inventory is | Correct and deliberate: a policy is a promise to *this* platform's buyers under *their* statutory floor. Syncing terms would import another marketplace's regime. |
| Seller narrows the return window; a buyer opens a return the next day under the old one | The order's snapshot governs. The new window is irrelevant to them | Buyer: their original window, unchanged, unmentioned. |
| Buyer reads the panel in another language/currency (CX-M20) | Terms are structured (D-09), so they translate as data rather than as prose | A machine-translated free-text policy would be a liability; a structured one is not. Second dividend of D-09. |
| **Policy panel buried under seller branding** | Impossible (D-08). The panel's position, order and styling are fixed and identical on every storefront | Per DT-08 — branding a policy panel is how you hide it, and buyers cannot compare shops whose terms render differently. |
| **A cloned storefront impersonating a real shop** (indexable via CX-M23; the Operator's asset) | Identity and verification are domain 01's; the harm lands here because this is the public surface | Cross-cut note to 01 + domain 24. Not this file's mechanism. |
| Storefront exists; seller never completed payout onboarding | The storefront exists with policies and cannot publish a listing (13.03.01 D-03: no payout method is the flow's second and only other publish blocker) | Seller: "Add a payout method to publish." Policies configured; shop empty. |
| **Generous return policy → more returns → worse scorecard** | The seller with the best buyer terms is punished for honouring them. Per D-12 a **policy-honoured return is not a seller fault** | See DT-06. The metric is CX-M08's; the semantic is this file's. |
| MAP-restricted listing on a storefront (13.13) | MAP is a **display** rule on a listing, not a storefront policy (13.13 D-01) | Never appears in the policy panel. Also: 13.13's Role Lens is empty, so this row may be moot (13.13 DT-01, domain Q-03). |
| Buyer's jurisdiction is ambiguous (VPN, forwarding address, expat) | The floor resolves on the **delivery destination**, not IP or account country | Deterministic and checkable — and it is the address the goods actually go to that carries the regime. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Storefront route requested | Skeleton in fixed order: identity → **policy panel** → listing grid. The panel's slot is reserved in the skeleton — it never arrives late and reflows the page under a reading buyer (D-08). |
| **Empty** | Storefront exists; zero active listings; **zero** sold history | "This seller has no active listings." Follow (CX-M28) offered — a hunting buyer (13.03.02 DT-05: "a long patient hunt") wants to know when this shop lists again. |
| **Populated** | ≥1 active listing | Identity, standing, policy panel valid for *this* buyer, provenance contribution if any, listing grid + facets. |
| **Error** | Policy set unreadable for the viewing buyer (e.g. their statutory floor cannot be resolved) | **The listing grid does not render.** A shop whose terms cannot be stated must not sell — this is the one place the domain's "never stall" rule (D-03) correctly inverts, because the failure isn't a worse listing, it's an unstated contract. Seller sees a full-width alert; buyers see "Terms unavailable — this shop can't take orders right now." |
| **Partial — away** | Away mode active (DT-03) | Banner: "Away until 30 May — orders welcome, dispatch resumes then." Listings stay browsable; new claims gated per D-14. |
| **Partial — dormant** | Zero active listings, non-zero sold history (DT-07) | "No listings right now. 47 instruments sold. 12 with a complete ownership record." Not the Empty state — the shop has a past. |
| **Partial — regionally unavailable** | The buyer's destination is outside every ship-to region and pickup is off | Grid renders read-only with a banner, **before** any add-to-cart: "This shop doesn't ship to Australia." ## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The storefront is a domain 01 concern — a shop is an organisation, and domain 01 owns organisations and profiles | Domain 01 is "Identity, Profiles & Organizations" and explicitly models bands, labels, studios and venues as first-class entities | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and the split is clean. Domain 01 owns the **entity**: who this shop is, its members, its delegated authority (CX-M09), its verification. This file owns the **commerce policy set**, which is gear-specific and has no meaning in domain 01's terms: return policy, handling time, freight willingness, offer acceptance, region restrictions. A shop's *identity* is 01's; a shop's *trading terms* are 13's. Notably the same shop selling plugins (domain 14) would need a different policy set entirely — no returns on a licence key, no handling time, no freight — which confirms the policy set belongs to the commerce domain rather than the identity domain. This is D-14's logic applied one level down. |
| DT-02 | A seller's return policy is their choice | It's their business, their terms — that's what a policy is | ❌ REJECTED, with a real consequence: in most of the platform's likely markets a consumer's statutory return right **cannot be contracted away by a trader** (13.08.01 DT-01). So a dealer's "no returns" policy is not merely unenforceable — **displaying it is arguably a dark pattern**, because it tells a consumer they have no right they in fact have, and most will believe it and not ask. Meanwhile a *private* seller (a Musician selling one pedal) may genuinely owe no such right, and their "no returns" is honest. **The same words are lawful from one seller and misleading from another, and the platform renders both identically.** Nothing in the candidate list noticed this. It cannot be fixed at the storefront — it requires knowing which sellers are traders, which is 13.08.01 Q-01 and `meta/personas.md` Q-01 arriving from a third direction. |
| DT-03 | Away/vacation mode is a checkbox the seller sets before a holiday | Every marketplace has one; it is the least interesting field on the page | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS NECESSARY, REFRAMED ON THE FAILURE MODE. **The failure is not forgetting to turn it off — it is forgetting to turn it on.** A Musician does not decide to be unreachable; they get on a tour bus. A Producer does not schedule a residency around their pedal sale. By the time the missed handling time has damaged their CX-M08 standing, the harm is done and no UI on this page prevented it. But: **this platform knows.** Domain 17 holds their tour dates; domain 16 holds the studio block; domain 07 holds the project. A storefront that says *"you have 12 dates starting Friday — pause dispatch until the 30th?"* is a thing Reverb cannot build, because Reverb does not know the seller is a touring musician — it knows they are a seller. This is D-18's consolidation dividend landing on the most boring page in the domain, and it arrives for free: the data is already there, the storefront just has to ask. The general form is worth flagging beyond this file: **wherever a policy is a promise about the seller's future availability, the platform's calendar knowledge should propose the policy rather than wait to be told.** |
| DT-04 | Handling time is one number per seller | It is one number on every marketplace, because on every other marketplace the goods are broadly alike | ❌ REJECTED — a single storefront handling time is **wrong in both directions on this catalog**. Putting a pedal in a padded envelope is a 24-hour job. Dispatching a 60kg 4×12 cab means sourcing a double-wall box, foam, a pallet, and a freight collection slot that may be four days out (13.07.01). A seller who sets `2 days` lies about the cab; a seller who sets `10 days` makes their pedals look abandoned and loses the sale to a faster listing. So handling time must be **per freight class**, and — the useful part — the class is **already computed** by 13.07.01 for freight quoting. The seller does not enter a matrix; they confirm two numbers the system proposes from stock they have already classified. Nothing in domain 14 or 05 has this shape: a licence key has no handling time and a mix has revision rounds, which is the D-14 test passing again at field level. |
| DT-05 | The trader-vs-private determination (Q-01) is a property of the **person** — a seller either is a trader or isn't | The natural reading, and how DT-02, 13.08.01 Q-01 and `meta/personas.md` Q-01 all implicitly framed it | ❌ REJECTED, and this **advances Q-01 rather than restating it**. The multi-hyphenate breaks the framing: one human is simultaneously a private individual selling their own spare pedal *and* the operator of a studio liquidating ex-rental stock. In most consumer-protection regimes trader status attaches to acting **in the course of a business** — which is a property of the *capacity being exercised*, not of the human. The same person genuinely can be a trader in one capacity and a private seller in another, on the same afternoon. So "is this seller a trader?" may be the wrong question, and the right one is **"which storefront is this listing under, and is *that* storefront trading?"** Two consequences. (1) The **storefront is the natural carrier of the regime determination** — which is a strong argument for it being a first-class object rather than a view of a profile, and it answers Q-02 from an unexpected direction (D-18's persona model forces it: `meta/personas.md` says the personas are "lenses on behaviour, not account types", and a regime that attaches to accounts contradicts that directly). (2) The platform must then resist the obvious shortcut of letting sellers self-declare per storefront, since the cheap answer to "are you a trader?" is always "no". **Caveat, stated honestly**: whether a regulator accepts a per-storefront determination is a legal question this file cannot answer, and a seller running one storefront cannot escape trader status by renaming it. The finding is that the *platform's* model must be able to express per-capacity trading; it is not a claim that the model settles the law. |
| DT-06 | The storefront's standing panel (CX-M08) and its policy panel are independent — one measures performance, the other declares terms | They are rendered side by side and nothing connects them | ❌ REJECTED — they are **coupled, and the coupling punishes exactly the wrong sellers**. A shop offering a 60-day return window takes more returns than one offering the statutory 14. If CX-M08's scorecard counts returns as a negative signal — and every marketplace's does, because a return usually *is* a signal of a bad listing — then **the platform systematically degrades the standing of the sellers whose terms are best for buyers**, and the rational seller response is to cut their return window to the statutory floor. The platform would have built a machine that competes its own buyer protections downward. The fix is a semantic distinction the metric must respect and cannot derive on its own: a **policy-honoured return** (buyer used a window the seller voluntarily offered) is not a fault, whereas a **not-as-described return** (13.08.01 D-01) is the seller's fault and should score. Only this file knows which returns were policy-honoured, because only this file knows what the policy was — so the distinction has to originate here and be *consumed* by CX-M08. Precedent exists: 13.09.02's consignor withdrawal is explicitly non-scoring against the consignee, and 13.03.03 D-04 makes `sold-elsewhere` non-punitive. This is the third instance of the same principle, which suggests CX-M08 needs a general notion of a **non-fault event** rather than three domain-specific exceptions. |
| DT-07 | An empty storefront is an empty page — a seller with no active listings has nothing to show | The obvious reading of the Empty state, and what every marketplace renders | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection lands on the thesis. A gear seller's shop is dormant most of the time — a Musician lists 2–5 units a year (13.03.01 Role Lens), so their storefront is empty ~90% of the days it exists. Reverb fills that page with a feedback number and a join date. **This platform has something else in the drawer**: 13.06.05 wrote an ownership-chain event on every settled sale this seller ever made. So the dormant storefront can say *"47 instruments sold, 12 with a complete ownership record"* — a claim no incumbent can render at any price, because they never captured the transfers. It is 13.03.06 (provenance on a listing) rewritten for the seller side, and it is the answer to the domain index's central Deep Think finding: *"every one of the sweep's 28 candidates exists at Reverb today."* This one does not. **Two constraints keep it honest.** (a) Per 13.06.05 D-02 (pending ratification at domain Q-04) chain visibility belongs to the **current owner**, not the seller — so per-unit display requires that owner's consent, and only the **aggregate count** is the seller's own fact to state (D-17). A shop advertising with a stranger's guitar is exactly the harm Q-04 is about. (b) The count is only worth showing because it is **not self-asserted** — it counts settlement events (13.06.05), not claims. The moment it counts anything a seller can type, it becomes the reconstruction problem the platform exists to end, reproduced on a shop page. |
| DT-08 | Storefront customisation — themes, custom banners, custom domains — is a differentiating feature and a plausible paid tier (CX-M24) | It is a standard marketplace upsell, and the Operator's storefront is a business asset they will want to brand | ❌ REJECTED in its maximal form, and the rejection converts into a hard constraint. Custom themes and custom CSS are CX-M23/CX-M24's problem if anyone's, but the specific proposal — *let sellers style their storefront* — collides head-on with DT-02. **The policy panel is the one region of the page that must be comparable across shops**, because a buyer deciding between two sellers of the same amp needs the terms to render the same way, and because a seller whose "no returns" is misleading has every incentive to make it small, grey and below the fold. Branding a policy panel is how you hide it. So: identity, banner and copy are brandable (domain 01's); **the policy panel's position, field order, and styling are fixed and identical on every storefront in the marketplace** (D-08), non-suppressible, and not purchasable away by any CX-M24 tier. Uniformity here is not a design limitation — it is the mechanism. Second-order: this is also why D-09's structured-fields rule matters more than it looks. A free-text policy box is a themeable policy panel by another route. |
| DT-09 | The policy panel is where the buyer learns the terms | It is literally the page region labelled "Policies", so this is what it is for | ❌ REJECTED — **nobody reads it, and the ones who do read it after the problem.** This is the single most consequential fact about this feature and it is easy to miss because the panel is the visible deliverable. The Fan persona "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" and "will never read a rider" (`meta/personas.md`); the Musician will not read a stranger's returns policy before buying a £60 pedal. So a panel is necessary and is **not** the mechanism, exactly as 13.11 DT-02 found for meetup safety guidance ("guidance nobody reads is guidance nobody has") — the same failure shape, arriving twice in one domain from two directions. The mechanism is **inline itemisation at the decision point**, and the precedent is already locked: 13.06.01 D-04 requires ineligible lines to be itemised and explained before payment, and 13.06.03 D-02 requires layaway forfeit to be stated **in money** at commitment and restated at every instalment. Apply the same rule here (D-10): the policy terms that cost the buyer **money or rights** — restocking fee, buyer-pays-return-freight, a return window at or below the statutory floor, handling time beyond the storefront default, no-international — render as **line items at checkout**, in money and days, not as a link to a page. The reframe is worth stating plainly: **this feature's real output is not a panel, it is a set of terms that other features are obliged to surface at the moments those terms bite.** That obligation is the deliverable; the panel is the reference copy. |
| DT-10 | A restocking fee is a normal, neutral seller protection — every retailer has one | It is a standard field and sellers expect it | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS A FIELD, REJECTED AS NEUTRAL. It is the policy set's sharpest edge, for a reason specific to this catalog: on a £4,000 vintage guitar, a 15% restocking fee is **£600** — more than most of the marketplace's items are worth. A percentage is unevaluable at the moment of purchase (13.06.03 D-02 found exactly this for layaway forfeit and required it be stated **in money**), and it is legally void against precisely the returns buyers most need — statutory change-of-mind and not-as-described (13.08.01 D-01). So the field survives, tightly bounded (D-18): capped at 15% (seed), **never** applicable to a statutory or not-as-described return, **stated in money at checkout** on any order where it could apply, and where it cannot lawfully apply to that buyer it is **not displayed at all** rather than displayed with an asterisk. An asterisked fee is DT-02's dark pattern with extra steps. |
