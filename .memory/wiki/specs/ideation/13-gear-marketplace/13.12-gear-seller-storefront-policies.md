# Feature: Gear Seller Storefront & Commerce Policies

> **Number**: 13.12
> **Parent**: [Gear Marketplace (Physical Goods)](./gear-marketplace-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A seller's shopfront and the rules they trade under: return policy, handling time, which regions they ship to, whether they accept offers, whether they offer local pickup. These are the settings that make a seller's listings behave consistently, and they are gear-specific because the policies that matter here — freight willingness, international shipping, offer acceptance — don't exist in the same shape for digital goods or services.

**Split from sweep candidate #17** ("Vendor Onboarding, Storefronts, Tiers & Scorecards"): onboarding is CX-M15, seller performance scorecards and tiers are CX-M08, and carrier rate configuration is CX-M14. What remains is the storefront presentation and the gear-specific commerce policy set.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own storefront) | Sets a simple policy set once — "I ship UK only, no returns beyond statutory, offers welcome" — and forgets. The persona wants defaults that are sane, not a configuration surface. |
| Producer | Full (own) | Similar, plus international willingness, since their buyers are global (13.07.02 Role Lens). |
| Operator | Full (own) | **The persona with a real storefront**: branding, a policy set, a returns process, an audience. The shop page is a business asset, and its policies are a competitive position. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No storefront — cannot sell (13.03.01 Role Lens). Sees other people's storefronts as a browsing surface only. |

## Behavior

Policies set at the storefront level are **defaults inherited by every listing**, overridable per listing where it makes sense. The storefront itself is a public page: the seller's listings, their policies, their standing (CX-M08). Where a policy conflicts with statutory obligation (13.08.01 D-04), the statute wins and the policy is not honoured.

### Happy Path

1. Seller configures their policy set once at onboarding (CX-M15)
2. Every listing inherits it
3. A buyer viewing any listing sees the applicable policies before purchase
4. A buyer viewing the storefront sees all listings plus the policy set
5. The seller adjusts a policy; future listings inherit it

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Policy contradicts statutory rights | The statute wins; the policy is unenforceable | See DT-02 — and a "no returns" policy shown to a consumer is worse than no policy |
| Policy changed while listings are live | Live listings should hold the terms they were published under | Change applies forward; buyers get what they saw |
| Policy changed after a sale, before dispatch | The buyer bought under the old terms | Order holds its terms — same shape as 13.03.02 Q-01 |
| Private seller with a dealer-shaped storefront | The regime question again | 13.08.01 Q-01 — **two legal regimes, one storefront model** |
| Storefront vs. the seller's identity profile (domain 01) | A shop is an organisation; domain 01 owns organisations | Boundary — see DT-01 |
| Seller declares "no international" but a buyer is abroad | Eligibility resolves at checkout | Itemised (13.06.01 D-04) |
| Handling time promised vs. actual | A promise is a performance metric | Feeds CX-M08 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Storefront render | Listing grid + policy panel |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The storefront is a domain 01 concern — a shop is an organisation, and domain 01 owns organisations and profiles | Domain 01 is "Identity, Profiles & Organizations" and explicitly models bands, labels, studios and venues as first-class entities | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and the split is clean. Domain 01 owns the **entity**: who this shop is, its members, its delegated authority (CX-M09), its verification. This file owns the **commerce policy set**, which is gear-specific and has no meaning in domain 01's terms: return policy, handling time, freight willingness, offer acceptance, region restrictions. A shop's *identity* is 01's; a shop's *trading terms* are 13's. Notably the same shop selling plugins (domain 14) would need a different policy set entirely — no returns on a licence key, no handling time, no freight — which confirms the policy set belongs to the commerce domain rather than the identity domain. This is D-14's logic applied one level down. |
| DT-02 | A seller's return policy is their choice | It's their business, their terms — that's what a policy is | ❌ REJECTED, with a real consequence: in most of the platform's likely markets a consumer's statutory return right **cannot be contracted away by a trader** (13.08.01 DT-01). So a dealer's "no returns" policy is not merely unenforceable — **displaying it is arguably a dark pattern**, because it tells a consumer they have no right they in fact have, and most will believe it and not ask. Meanwhile a *private* seller (a Musician selling one pedal) may genuinely owe no such right, and their "no returns" is honest. **The same words are lawful from one seller and misleading from another, and the platform renders both identically.** Nothing in the candidate list noticed this. It cannot be fixed at the storefront — it requires knowing which sellers are traders, which is 13.08.01 Q-01 and `meta/personas.md` Q-01 arriving from a third direction. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The storefront owns the gear commerce policy set; domain 01 owns the entity | Per DT-01 — clean seam, and the same shop would need different policies in domain 14. |
| D-02 | Statutory rights override seller policy, and the policy must not claim otherwise | Per DT-02 — displaying an unenforceable "no returns" to a consumer misleads them. |
| D-03 | Policies are defaults inherited forward; live listings hold their published terms | Retroactive policy changes rewrite deals buyers already assessed. |
| D-04 | No seller trades with undefined terms | Sane defaults at onboarding; an unconfigured policy set is a dispute waiting. |
