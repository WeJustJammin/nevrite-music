## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Intake Grading ↔ Trade-In

**Relationship**: The provisional offer is made on the owner's description; the grade is made on the object. When they disagree the offer re-opens, and that moment is the sub-domain's entire trust surface. 13.09.03 D-01 (photographic evidence, always) exists because an unevidenced downgrade is indistinguishable from haggling — and the owner's gear is already in the dealer's hands when it happens.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona feeling this most. Their guitar is at the dealer, the number just dropped £300, and they must judge whether that's fair from a photo.
- **Producer**: will dispute on technical grounds and can.
- **Operator**: performs the grading — and per sub-domain D-03 is a poor fit for that role.
- **Fan**: maximum asymmetry. Cannot evaluate the grade, cannot evaluate the offer, cannot evaluate the evidence.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The owner's claim and the intake grade are **both recorded, neither overwrites the other** (13.09.03 D-02). The conflict is the product — the record shows two positions and the evidence arbitrates.
2. **Trigger chain**: Description → provisional offer → arrival → inspection → grade → confirm or re-offer. **If the owner rejects the re-offer, their gear is already in someone else's custody** — and who pays to return it is unresolved (13.09.01 Q-02). That is a trap the chain creates and no file closes.
3. **Permission intersection**: The grader is the payer. Structural conflict of interest (13.09.03 DT-02), unmitigated by reputation for the two personas least able to detect it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Grade → owner, with the re-offer if any. This is the notification that matters most in the sub-domain.
5. **State transition conflict**: A stolen-registry hit at intake (13.09.03 D-03) while a provisional offer is live — the dealer now holds stolen goods offered against a deal. Escalates to domain 24 mid-transaction, and 13.03.07 Q-02's post-sale question arrives here in a new shape.

### CX-02: Intake Grading ↔ Consignment

**Relationship**: For consignment the grade does double duty: it is the evidentiary record of what condition the object was in when custody transferred (protecting both parties against later "it was fine when I gave it to you"), and it sets the condition the consignee will list it at — on an object they don't own.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer**: as consignors, need the intake evidence precisely because they are handing an irreplaceable object to someone else for months.
- **Operator**: as consignee, needs it for the same reason in reverse.
- **Fan**: None on consignment.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The intake grade enters the evidence pack (13.02.04) — the pack's **fifth** independent consumer, after disclosure, damage claims, returns and this. That count is itself the argument for the pack being a `must`.
2. **Trigger chain**: Arrival → inspection → grade + custody evidence → listing published by consignee. If the grade contradicts the consignor's account, the consignment terms may need renegotiating before listing — the minimum price was agreed against a condition that turned out to be optimistic.
3. **Permission intersection**: The seam DT-01 in 13.09.02 names — the consignee lists and discloses, but the consignor knows and is liable. The intake grade is the consignee's *only* first-hand knowledge, which is exactly why it cannot substitute for the consignor's disclosure.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Grade → consignor. Custody evidence → both.
5. **State transition conflict**: Damage discovered at intake that neither party caused (it happened in transit to the consignee) is a damage claim (13.07.04) inside a consignment intake — two custody transfers deep, with the object owned by a third party. Rare, and genuinely hard.

### CX-03: Trade-In ↔ Consignment

**Relationship**: The same person, the same object, the same moment, two offers: cash now at a discount, or more money later minus a commission and a wait. Presenting them together is honest and is why these two features share a sub-domain rather than sitting apart. Both then depend on the same inspection (CX-01, CX-02), so the choice is made before the grade and may be regretted after it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona facing the choice. Cash-poor, gear-rich, and the trade-in's convenience is worth real money to them (13.09.01 Role Lens).
- **Producer**: consigns rare outboard because reaching the right buyer matters more than the percentage (13.09.02 Role Lens) — the opposite calculus, same fork.
- **Operator**: offers both, and profits differently from each.
- **Fan**: may trade in, cannot consign — so for this persona the fork has one branch, presented by a professional.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One object, two possible paths. Mutually exclusive per unit at a time; a consignment that doesn't sell may become a trade-in later, which is a legitimate sequence.
2. **Trigger chain**: Decision → intake → grade → path executes. **The grade lands *after* the choice**, which means a lowered grade changes the economics of a decision already made. Whether the owner may switch paths post-grade is unspecified and probably should be permitted.
3. **Permission intersection**: Fan's access differs across the two (Read-only vs. None), which makes the "here are your options" presentation persona-dependent — the Fan sees one option and cannot know a better one existed for someone else.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None beyond each path's own.
5. **State transition conflict**: The ownership chain diverges sharply. Trade-in writes **two** transfers (13.09.01 DT-01); consignment writes **one**, consignor → buyer, skipping the consignee (13.09.02 D-02). Same object, same moment, structurally different provenance outcomes — worth naming for `/write-architecture-spec`.
