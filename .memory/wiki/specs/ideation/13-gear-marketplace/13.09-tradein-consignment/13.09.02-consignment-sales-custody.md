# Feature: Consignment Sales & Custody

> **Number**: 13.09.02
> **Parent**: [Trade-In, Part-Exchange & Consignment](./13.09-tradein-consignment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Handing your instrument to someone who will sell it for you and keep a percentage. You still own it; they hold it, photograph it, list it, and answer the questions. It suits people who have valuable gear and no appetite for the selling process — and it creates a state the rest of the domain doesn't have: a specific object, owned by one party, held and marketed by another, for months.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as consignor) | Hands a vintage guitar to a shop rather than dealing with photographing it, disclosing it, packing it and arguing about it. Gets more than a trade-in and less than a private sale. Wants to know it's safe and what it's doing. |
| Producer | Full (as consignor) | Consigns rare outboard to someone with the audience for it — the persona's gear is thin-market (13.04.03 DT-02), so reaching the right buyer matters more than the percentage. |
| Operator | Config (act as consignee) | The counterparty who takes custody and sells. **A poor persona fit** (sub-domain D-03/Q-01) — a venue operator is not a consignment shop. |
| Fan | None | No consignment. Requires a payout relationship, a tax identity and a custody agreement the persona has no context for. Also has nothing worth consigning, almost by definition. |

## Behavior

Custody transfers; **ownership does not** (sub-domain D-01). The consignee lists, discloses (13.02 — on someone else's object), holds, and ships. On sale, proceeds split per the agreement and ownership transfers **from the consignor directly to the buyer** — the consignee never owned it and must never appear in the chain as an owner.

### Happy Path

1. Consignor agrees terms with a consignee: percentage, minimum price, duration
2. Unit is inspected at intake (13.09.03) and custody transfers — evidenced
3. Consignee lists it, handles buyers, negotiates within the agreed floor
4. It sells; proceeds split; consignee's cut is paid
5. Ownership transfers consignor → buyer (13.06.05); consignee never enters the chain as owner

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Unit is damaged or stolen while in the consignee's custody** | The consignor owns an object they don't hold, damaged by someone else | The same custody-liability gap as 13.06.03 Q-01 and 13.08.02 Q-01 — **the third occurrence.** See DT-02. |
| Consignee goes insolvent while holding it | The consignor's object may be treated as the consignee's asset by a liquidator — a real, catastrophic legal risk | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the chain (13.06.05) is the evidence that it was never theirs |
| Consignor wants it back mid-term | It's theirs; but the consignee has invested effort | Early-termination terms; recall right must exist |
| Consignee discloses inaccurately (13.02.02) | The consignor bears the liability for a disclosure they didn't make | Who signs the disclosure? See DT-01 |
| Consignee sells below the agreed floor | Breach | Floor enforced by the system, not by trust |
| It doesn't sell | Returns to the consignor at whose cost? | Terms |
| Consignee lists it on another channel too (13.03.04) | Cross-channel oversell on someone else's object | 13.03.03 DT-02's problem, with a third party's property |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The consignee lists it, so the consignee is the seller | It's how it looks — their storefront, their photos, their buyer conversation | ❌ REJECTED, and it breaks something load-bearing. This domain has made disclosure a **liability-bearing personal assertion**: 13.02.03 D-03 says registry history may pre-fill but "never auto-assert" because "the seller signs the disclosure and carries the liability". In consignment the person signing is not the person who knows — the consignee has held the guitar for a week; the consignor owned it for thirty years and knows about the headstock repair in 1998. **The knowledge and the liability separate**, which no other selling path in the domain permits. Either the consignor must sign the disclosure (and the consignee merely transcribes), or the domain's disclosure liability model has an exception it hasn't acknowledged. The first is correct and needs designing. |
| DT-02 | Consignment custody risk is a contract detail — the terms will handle it | It reads as boilerplate, which is why it gets deferred | ❌ REJECTED, and this is now a **pattern, not an edge case**. Three separate features have independently hit the identical gap: layaway (13.06.03 Q-01 — seller holds a partly-paid object), repair custody (13.08.02 Q-01 — tech holds someone's amp), and consignment (here — consignee holds an owner's instrument). Each time the question is the same: *an irreplaceable object is held by someone who doesn't own it; if it's destroyed, whose loss is it?* Domain 15 already models custody and ownership as distinct concepts — **the domain has the vocabulary and is not using it.** This is not three edge cases; it is one unowned model. It should be resolved once, at architecture, not three times in three files. |
