# Feature: Inspection & Intake Grading

> **Number**: 13.09.03
> **Parent**: [Trade-In, Part-Exchange & Consignment](./13.09-tradein-consignment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE] — **Deep Think addition** (not in the sweep's candidate list)
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The physical examination performed when a unit arrives in someone else's hands — for trade-in, for consignment, or at any custodial intake. Someone opens the case, looks at the truss rod, plugs it in, and forms an independent view of what this thing is. That view is frequently not the one the owner gave, and the difference is money.

**Why this exists as a node**: the sweep listed trade-in and consignment (#21) but nothing between "owner describes it" and "dealer offers a number". That gap is a physical act with its own evidence, its own disputes and its own re-offer loop — and both parent features depend on it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (sees the grade given to their gear) | Learns their guitar is Very Good, not Excellent, and the offer just dropped £300. Needs the evidence — photos of what was found — or it reads as a haggling tactic. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees a bench assessment of their outboard, which they will read critically and may dispute on technical grounds. |
| Operator | Full (performs it) | Does the inspecting at intake — **and the persona fit is poor** (sub-domain D-03/Q-01): the persona is a venue/studio operator, not a shop with a bench and a grader. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees a grade they cannot evaluate, from a professional, determining what they're offered. Maximum asymmetry — the whole transaction hinges on an assessment they must simply trust. |

## Behavior

Intake grading applies the same scale as self-grading (13.02.01) and the same flaw/originality vocabulary (13.02.02/03) — but performed by the receiving party, with evidence, and recorded as an **independent assessment** rather than replacing the owner's claim. Where the two disagree, the offer re-opens (13.09.01) or the consignment listing prices differently (13.09.02).

### Happy Path

1. Unit arrives for trade-in or consignment
2. Inspector works the same category checklist the owner completed
3. Findings are recorded with photo evidence
4. Grade matches the owner's account; the provisional offer stands
5. Custody is evidenced; the transaction proceeds

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Grade is lower than the owner claimed** | The offer drops. This is the feature's whole reason to exist and its biggest trust risk | Findings with photos, itemised — never just a lower number |
| Grade is *higher* than the owner claimed | Does the offer go up? A dealer has no incentive; a trustworthy platform might insist | See DT-02 — the asymmetry test |
| Inspector finds an undisclosed structural repair | Materially changes value; may also mean the owner was hiding it, or genuinely never knew | Evidence recorded; distinguishing ignorance from concealment is not possible and should not be attempted |
| Owner disputes the findings | Their gear is in someone else's hands | Return path must exist; cost is 13.09.01 Q-02 |
| Inspection damages the unit | Rare but real — a truss rod checked too enthusiastically | The custody-liability gap again (13.09.02 DT-02) |
| Inspector is the same party who profits from a low grade | Structural conflict of interest | See DT-02 |
| Unit is registered (domain 15) with a service history | The registry may already answer what the inspection is asking | Pre-fill and cross-check, per 13.02.03 D-03 |
| Findings contradict a previous owner's disclosure on the chain | The instrument's recorded history is wrong somewhere | Genuinely valuable signal; `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Intake grading is just 13.02.01 grading performed by a different person | Same scale, same vocabulary, same checklist — it looks like a re-run | ❌ REJECTED: the *scale* is shared; the **epistemics** are not. A self-grade is a claim by an owner with an interest in it being high; an intake grade is an assessment by a receiver with an interest in it being **low**. Both are interested parties pointing in opposite directions, and the object is the only neutral thing in the room. That is why the evidence requirement is stricter here than anywhere else in the domain — an intake grade that lowers an offer without photographic findings is indistinguishable from haggling, and the Musician persona will read it as exactly that. Recording it as an *independent assessment alongside* the owner's claim (not replacing it) is what keeps both accountable. |
| DT-02 | The inspector will grade honestly because their reputation depends on it | Reputation (CX-M08) is the platform's standard answer to bad behaviour | ⚠️ REJECTED as sufficient. The conflict is **structural, not behavioural**: the party grading is the party paying, and a systematically pessimistic grader makes more money on every intake while remaining plausibly within a subjective range. Reputation only catches this if graded owners can tell they were shortchanged — and the two personas most exposed (Fan, who cannot evaluate anything, and Musician, who is accepting a discount for convenience anyway) are precisely the ones who **cannot detect it**. The honest tests: does the offer ever go **up** when the grade comes back higher than claimed? Is the grader's distribution of grades visible? A dealer whose intake grades skew a full band below every owner's account is running a business model, not a bench. **Whether the platform surfaces that is a values decision** — the same one 13.09.01 DT-02 raises about exposing the trade-in margin. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Intake findings require photographic evidence — always | Per DT-01 — an unevidenced downgrade is indistinguishable from haggling. |
| D-02 | The intake grade is recorded *alongside* the owner's claim, never replacing it | Both parties are interested; the record should show both positions and let the evidence arbitrate. |
| D-03 | Stolen-serial screening runs at every custodial intake | A stranger handing over an object is exactly the risk profile screening exists for. |
| D-04 | Ignorance and concealment are not distinguished | The system cannot know intent and should not pretend to. Record the finding, not a motive. |
