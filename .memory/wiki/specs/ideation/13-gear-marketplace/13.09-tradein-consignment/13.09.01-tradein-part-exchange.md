# Feature: Trade-In & Part-Exchange

> **Number**: 13.09.01
> **Parent**: [Trade-In, Part-Exchange & Consignment](./13.09-tradein-consignment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Giving a dealer your Strat plus cash and walking out with a Les Paul. The trade-in value is always below market — that gap is the dealer's margin and the price of not having to sell it yourself — and the whole transaction turns on a valuation made by the party who benefits from it being low.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (as the one trading in) | Trades a guitar toward another. The persona this exists for: a portfolio of small income streams means cash is scarce but gear is the currency they have. Accepts a lower number for the convenience, knowingly. |
| Producer | Full | Trades studio gear toward an upgrade. Higher values, and more able to assess whether the offer is fair. |
| Operator | Config (offer trade-in as a dealer) | The counterparty — **and a poor persona fit**, per sub-domain D-03/Q-01. A rehearsal space operator is not a guitar shop. |
| Fan | Read-only (may trade in) | Trades a first guitar toward a better one. **Cannot evaluate the offer at all** — no market sense, no comp fluency, and facing a professional. The persona most likely to accept a bad number without knowing it. |

## Behavior

A two-sided transaction: the outbound unit (what the dealer sells) and the inbound unit (what the owner gives up). The inbound unit needs a valuation *before* the deal and a physical inspection (13.09.03) *at* the deal — and the two can disagree, which re-opens the offer. Net cash settles the difference.

### Happy Path

1. Owner selects gear they want and offers gear in part-exchange
2. Owner describes the inbound unit (or it's already in their registry — domain 15)
3. Dealer makes a provisional trade-in offer against the price guide (13.04)
4. Inbound unit is inspected on arrival (13.09.03); the grade is confirmed
5. Deal completes; net cash settles; **both** ownership transfers write to the chain (13.06.05)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Inspection contradicts the owner's description** | The provisional offer was based on the owner's account; the grade is now lower | Re-offer with the reason and photo evidence — the owner must be able to walk away and get their gear back |
| Owner rejects the re-offer | Their gear is already at the dealer | Return at whose cost? `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a real trap |
| Trade-in value quoted with no comps (thin market — 13.04.03) | Most interesting gear has no confident valuation | The dealer's number is the only number in the room. See DT-02. |
| Inbound unit turns out to be stolen (13.03.07) | The dealer has taken custody of stolen goods | Screening must run on trade-in intake, not only on listings |
| Owner trades in gear with an outstanding layaway or lien | They don't fully own it | `[PENDING]` — the chain (13.06.05) would know |
| Two ownership transfers in one transaction | Outbound and inbound both move | Both write to the chain atomically |
| Trade-in of unregistered gear with no serial | No chain entry possible | Same gap as 13.06.05 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Valuation running | Provisional offer calculating |
| Empty | Nothing offered in part-exchange | Standard purchase |
| Populated | Offer made | Provisional value, net cash, and **explicitly** what it's provisional on |
| Error | Valuation fails | Manual dealer quote |
| Partial | Awaiting inspection | "Provisional — confirmed on inspection" stated up-front, not discovered later |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A trade-in is a discount code — reduce the price by the trade value | The simplest model, and how it renders on a checkout page | ❌ REJECTED: it is **two sales in one transaction**, and both are real. The inbound unit changes owner (dealer acquires it), the outbound unit changes owner (customer acquires it), and both must write to the ownership chain (13.06.05). Modelling the inbound as a discount erases it from the chain entirely — the dealer now holds a guitar with no recorded provenance event, and when they resell it the chain has a hole exactly where a transfer happened. Given 13.06.05 D-01 (settlement writes the event), a trade-in that doesn't write is a provenance leak, and trade-ins are one of the highest-volume ways used gear changes hands. |
| DT-02 | Trade-in valuation is just the price guide minus a margin | The obvious construction, and 13.04 exists to supply the number | ❌ REJECTED: the price guide is **mute on most of the gear worth trading** (13.04.03 DT-02 — thin is permanent for the rare and interesting). So for the gear where the stakes are highest, the dealer's offer is the only number in the room, made by the party who profits from it being low, to a persona who cannot check it (Fan) or who is accepting a discount for convenience anyway (Musician). **The information asymmetry is the business model here, not a bug** — and a platform whose thesis is trust has to decide how much of it to expose. Showing the guide's number alongside the trade offer (where confidence permits) would make the margin visible, which dealers would hate and users would value. That is a real product/values decision, not an agent call. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A trade-in is two ownership transfers, not a discount | Per DT-01 — anything else leaks provenance at one of the highest-volume transfer points in the category. |
| D-02 | The offer is explicitly provisional until inspection, stated up-front | Discovering provisionality at the re-offer is how this feature generates disputes. |
| D-03 | Stolen-serial screening runs at trade-in intake | Intake is a custody transfer from a stranger — the same risk as a listing, with less scrutiny. |
