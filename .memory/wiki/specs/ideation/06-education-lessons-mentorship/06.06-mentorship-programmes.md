# Feature: Mentorship Programmes

> **Number**: 06.06
> **Parent**: [Education, Lessons & Mentorship](./education-lessons-mentorship-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A structured relationship in which an established musician helps a less-established one build a
**career**, not a technique. The distinction from tuition is the whole feature: a teacher improves
your playing; a mentor tells you what to charge, which manager to avoid, whether that publishing
deal is normal, and how they handled the year they nearly quit. Nobody teaches that, and it is
transmitted almost exclusively through personal relationships — which is precisely why access to it
is so unequal.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Mentor**: offers mentorship (paid, free, or through a programme), sets capacity — this is time they are giving away, so capacity is 2, not 20 — agrees goals, meets on a cadence, and mostly answers questions nobody else will answer honestly. **As Mentee**: applies or is matched, sets career goals, meets, gets told things that cost years to learn otherwise. |
| Producer | Full | A Producer mentoring a younger engineer is **the canonical form of this feature**. Engineering has been transmitted by apprenticeship for its entire history — the assistant-engineer path *was* the industry's training system, and its collapse is why this feature has a market. |
| Operator | Config | As an academy, label, or organisation: **runs a programme** — a cohort, a duration, a set of pairings, an outcome to report. This is the one shape mentorship takes that has money and structure behind it (label development schemes, PRS/PPL-style funded programmes, conservatoire alumni schemes). |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Mentorship is a professional relationship between people with careers; a Fan has no professional stake by definition (`meta/personas.md`). |

## Behavior

Two shapes, sharing a spine:

- **Open mentorship** — a mentor offers, a mentee finds them, they agree a cadence. Lightweight, informal, usually unpaid or cheap.
- **Programme mentorship** — an Operator runs a cohort: applications, curation, pairings, a fixed duration, milestones, and an outcome report to whoever funded it.

The spine is: a pairing, goals, a cadence of conversations, and an ending. **The ending matters and
is usually missing** — mentorships that don't end formally just decay into guilt on both sides
(DT-02).

What this feature is *not*: it is not lesson booking with a different label. There is no credit
ledger, no cancellation policy, no curriculum, no practice. It reuses scheduling (cross-cut) and,
where paid, payments (cross-cut). Almost everything else in domain 06 is inapplicable.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: pairing mechanics. Whether mentors are searched (like teachers,
06.02.03), applied to, or matched by a programme administrator is unresolved and depends on which
shape dominates.

### Happy Path

1. Established musician offers mentorship: 2 mentees, monthly, 6 months, focus areas
2. Mentee applies with context and what they want out of it
3. Mentor accepts (they are choosing a person, not filling a slot)
4. Goals agreed; cadence set
5. They meet; conversations happen; goals are revisited
6. **The programme ends deliberately** — with an outcome, not a fade

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Mentorship fades out | **The base rate.** Both parties feel vaguely guilty for a year | The reason for a **fixed duration by default** (D-02). An ending that was always scheduled is not a rejection |
| Mentor is overwhelmed by applications | A well-known mentor gets 200 applications for 2 places, which is a bad experience for 200 people | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — application caps, or closing when full |
| Mentee wants a job, not mentorship | Common and corrosive. The relationship's value is candour, which dies the moment it becomes a job application | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — expectation-setting is a design problem here |
| Mentor gives bad or self-serving advice | Unlike tuition, there is no artifact to check. Nobody can audit "you should sign that deal" | No product answer. Genuinely outside what the platform can adjudicate |
| Power imbalance is abused | An established figure with career power over a young musician, meeting privately, is a **structurally elevated risk** — and the mentee's incentive to report is negative | Trust & Safety (24) + Safeguarding. See DT-03 |
| Mentee is a minor | Full safeguarding; a career mentorship for a 16-year-old is real (young artists get signed) and carries every 1:1 control | Safeguarding cross-cut |
| Paid mentorship at £200/hr | Legitimate — and then it is arguably a Services (05) engagement, not this | Q-02 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Mentorship is 1:1 lessons with a different name and should be folded into 06.01 | Strong surface similarity: one person, one person, recurring meetings, a relationship | ❌ REJECTED — **the domain's structure is the argument**. 06.01's entire content is inapplicable: no credit ledger (mentorship is often unpaid, and where paid it's not a pack), no cancellation policy (you don't burn a credit for missing a coffee with your mentor), no curriculum, no practice tracking, no assignments, no progress reports. What remains shared is "two people meet on a cadence", which is the Scheduling cross-cut, not 06.01. It also transmits **career knowledge, not technique**, which means none of 06.03's loop applies either. Folding it in would import a ledger it has no use for and a policy it would have to disable. |
| DT-02 | Mentorship needs a **built-in ending**, and this is the feature's most important design decision | Domain expertise: informal mentorships overwhelmingly fade rather than conclude, leaving both parties feeling they failed at something neither agreed to | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it inverts the obvious design. The instinct is to build for **duration** (a long relationship = success) and to nudge lapsed pairs back into contact. That is exactly wrong: an open-ended mentorship makes every un-replied message a small failure, and the mentor — who is giving time away — eventually avoids the app. A **fixed term with a deliberate ending** ("6 months, monthly, then we're done") makes the ask small enough that busy people say yes, makes ending honourable rather than a rejection, and makes renewal a positive act rather than the absence of an exit. The programme shape gets this right by accident; open mentorship must get it right by design. |
| DT-03 | Mentorship carries a **structurally elevated abuse risk** that ordinary tuition does not, and it is not primarily about minors | Reasoning from the relationship's shape: an established industry figure holds real career power over a young musician, meets them privately, and the mentee's incentive to report anything is strongly negative | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is uncomfortable, so it is stated plainly. Music has a well-documented history of exactly this dynamic, and the mentee is usually an **adult**, which means the Safeguarding cross-cut (built around minors) does not cover it. The mentee's silence is rationally motivated: the mentor is a gatekeeper to their career. A platform that **facilitates and endorses** these pairings — that is what a mentorship product does — inherits some responsibility for them. This is not solvable in this file; it routes to domain 24 as a distinct risk class, and it is a reason the programme shape (with an Operator accountable for the cohort) is safer than open pairing. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Mentorship is a **feature**, not a sub-domain, and is a sibling of 06.01 rather than a child | Per DT-01 — it shares almost nothing with tuition beyond scheduling. It is one flow (offer → pair → meet → end); promotion candidate only if programme administration deepens (Q-03). |
| D-02 | **Fixed term with a deliberate ending** is the default | Per DT-02 — the single most important design decision here. Open-ended mentorship decays into mutual guilt and drives mentors off the platform. |
| D-03 | Mentor capacity is expected to be **very small** (1–3), and the UI must assume scarcity | A mentor gives time away. Designing for supply abundance produces 200 rejected applicants per mentor. |
| D-04 | No credit ledger, no cancellation policy, no curriculum | Per DT-01 — importing 06.01's machinery would mean disabling most of it. |
