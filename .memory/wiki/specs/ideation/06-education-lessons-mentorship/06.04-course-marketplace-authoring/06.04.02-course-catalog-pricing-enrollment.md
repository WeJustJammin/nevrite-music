# Feature: Course Catalog, Pricing & Enrollment

> **Number**: 06.04.02
> **Parent**: [Course Marketplace & Authoring](./06.04-course-marketplace-authoring-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The shop. How a course is priced, found, bought and entered. This is the sub-domain's commercial
surface and its widest persona surface — a Fan can buy a course with no lesson relationship, no
teacher and no ongoing commitment, which makes this the only place in domain 06 where money moves
from someone who is not a student.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As author**: prices the course (one-off, subscription, or free), bundles, discounts, sees conversion. **As learner**: browses, previews, buys, enrolls. |
| Producer | Full | Same, as author. Their credits are what convert a stranger (06.04.01 DT-02). |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: house pricing, and bundling courses with tuition — the offer that wires this ledger to 06.01's credit ledger (index Q-02). |
| Fan | Full | **Buys courses outright.** No lessons, no teacher, no relationship — "I want to learn to play like {artist}" is a fan impulse with a credit card attached. `meta/personas.md` says the fan/hobbyist boundary "is porous"; this is where the porousness pays. |

## Behavior

A course is listed with a price, a preview, its author's credits, and enough detail that a buyer knows
what they are getting. Purchase grants an **entitlement** — perpetual access to the product, or
subscription-bound access if that's the model. The entitlement is emphatically **not a lesson credit**
(index D-04): different ledger, different semantics, different lifetime.

The pricing models available are genuinely different products:

- **One-off** — buy the course, own it. What buyers understand and trust
- **Subscription** — all-you-can-eat across the catalog. Better platform economics, worse for individual authors, and it needs a catalogue that does not exist on day one
- **Free** — a funnel into 1:1 lessons or a paid course. Given 06.02.04 DT-03's rejection of mandatory free trials, this must be the **author's** choice

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: which models ship, and whether the platform runs a catalog
subscription at all. That decision has more consequences than any other in the sub-domain and it is
a business-model call, not a feature.

### Happy Path

1. Learner (or Fan) finds a course — search, an author's profile, or the catalog
2. Previews a free lesson and reads the author's verified credits
3. Buys at a one-off price
4. Entitlement granted; the course appears in their library
5. They start consuming (06.04.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer wants a refund after consuming 80% | The classic digital-goods refund problem, in a domain where "I watched it all and didn't like it" is indistinguishable from "it was bad" | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — consumption-based refund policy, routed to Payments + domain 24 |
| Course is withdrawn after purchase | Buyer keeps access (06.04.01 D-03) | "This course is no longer sold. Your access continues" |
| Cross-border VAT on a digital course | Education is **VAT-exempt in some regimes and not others**, and the generic marketplace tax engine will not assume that. Same trap as 06.01.02's tuition VAT | Tax cross-cut. **Flagged explicitly** — this is not a generic digital-goods sale |
| Minor buys a course | Payment by a minor + a UGC video product. Age assurance and guardian consent apply | Safeguarding cross-cut |
| Course bundled with lesson credits | Wires two ledgers that D-04 separates deliberately | Index Q-02 — unresolved and structurally consequential |
| Author drops the price after someone paid full | Standard marketplace grievance | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Free course used purely as lead-gen | Legitimate and the author's choice (per 06.02.04 D-02's principle) | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Catalog fetch | Skeleton cards |
| Empty | No courses in the catalog | **The cold-start reality.** Honest emptiness beats a fake catalog |
| Populated | Courses listed | Cards: author, credits, price, preview |
| Error | Purchase failed | Retry; nothing charged |
| Partial | Purchased, entitlement pending | "Unlocking your course…" |
| Owned | Entitlement held | "In your library" — never a repeat buy prompt |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A course entitlement can share the lesson-credit ledger — both are prepaid learning | Surface similarity; the temptation to have one "learning wallet" | ❌ REJECTED — this is **D-14's exact argument, recurring inside a single domain**. D-14 rejected merging the marketplaces because "everything that differs is irreconcilable at schema level" and a merge "yields a `listing` entity with ~40 nullable columns". The same holds here: a lesson credit is **teacher-scoped, burns per occurrence, expires, and is governed by a cancellation policy**; a course entitlement is **product-scoped, perpetual, never burns, and has no policy**. They share only the word "prepaid". Merging them produces a nullable-column mess and a cancellation policy that has to explain why it doesn't apply to half its rows. |
| DT-02 | Courses need the lesson business to exist first — they are an add-on to tuition | Sub-domain ordering instinct: 06.04 comes after 06.01–06.03, so it must depend on them | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection carries real strategic weight. Courses have **no dependency on any other node in domain 06**: no booking, no scheduling cross-cut, no safeguarding gate (an adult buying a video needs no vetting), no Real-Time Rooms — which means **none of the map's hard architecture warnings on the locked stack apply to the transaction**, only to video delivery. Meanwhile it activates all four personas and monetises the credits wedge at full strength (06.04.01 DT-02). It is plausibly the domain's **best standalone bet and its natural first slice** — the reverse of what the numbering implies. Flagged for MoSCoW rather than asserted; the counter is index Q-04 (YouTube), which is a real and unanswered threat. |
| DT-03 | A catalog subscription (all-you-can-eat) is the better model, as it is everywhere else | Fender Play, Tonebase, Pickup Music, Netflix-for-everything — the whole category has converged on it | ❌ REJECTED **for launch**, on a cold-start argument rather than a business one. A subscription needs a **catalogue**; on day one there is no catalogue, and "£15/month for four courses" is an insult. Worse, subscription revenue-sharing across a thin catalogue pays individual authors close to nothing, which repels exactly the authors needed to build the catalogue — a loop that fails closed. One-off purchases pay authors properly from course one and give them a reason to publish here rather than on Teachable. Subscription is a **later** model that a catalogue earns; treating it as the target model at launch inverts the dependency. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A course entitlement and a lesson credit are **separate ledgers** | Per DT-01 — D-14's argument, recurring inside one domain. They share only the word "prepaid". |
| D-02 | **One-off purchase** is the launch model; subscription is later | Per DT-03 — a subscription needs a catalogue, and thin-catalogue subscription economics repel the authors who would build it. |
| D-03 | Free-as-funnel is the **author's** choice, never the platform's mandate | Same principle as 06.02.04 D-02 — the platform does not conscript its poorest persona's work for acquisition. |
| D-04 | Education **VAT treatment is explicitly flagged**, not inherited from digital goods | Tuition and instructional content are exempt in some regimes; a generic engine will get this silently wrong at scale. |
