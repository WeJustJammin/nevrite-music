# Feature: Course Catalog, Pricing & Enrollment

> **Number**: 06.04.02
> **Parent**: [Course Marketplace & Authoring](./06.04-course-marketplace-authoring-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The shop. How a course is priced, found, bought and entered. This is the sub-domain's commercial
surface and its widest persona surface — a Fan can buy a course with no lesson relationship, no
teacher and no ongoing commitment, which makes this the only place in domain 06 where money moves
from someone who is not a student. Purchase grants a perpetual (or subscription-bound) **entitlement**
to a product — deliberately a different thing from the teacher-scoped, burn-per-occurrence lesson
credit it sits next to (D-01).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As author**: prices the course (one-off, subscription, or free), builds bundles, sets discounts, sees conversion. **As learner**: browses, previews, buys, enrolls. The multi-hyphenate occupies both sides here, often in the same week. |
| Producer | Full | Same, as author. Their verified credits are what convert a stranger (06.04.01 DT-02) — "mixed by {credit}" is the price justification, not the price tag. |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: sets house pricing, and bundles courses with tuition — the offer that wires this entitlement ledger to 06.01's credit ledger (index Q-02, resolved in DT-04). |
| Fan | Full | **Buys courses outright.** No lessons, no teacher, no relationship — "I want to learn to play like {artist}" is a fan impulse with a credit card attached. `meta/personas.md` says the fan/hobbyist boundary "is porous"; this is where the porousness pays. |

> No persona is **None** here — this is the domain's only genuinely all-four-persona surface, and that
> breadth is a finding (parent index Role Matrix note), not an oversight.

## Behavior

A course is listed with a price, a preview, its author's credits, and enough detail that a buyer knows
what they are getting. Purchase grants an **entitlement** — perpetual access to the product, or
subscription-bound access if that's the model. The entitlement is emphatically **not a lesson credit**
(index D-04): different ledger, different semantics, different lifetime.

The pricing models available are genuinely different products:

- **One-off** — buy the course, own it. What buyers understand and trust. **The launch model** (D-02).
- **Subscription** — all-you-can-eat across the catalog. Better platform economics, worse for individual
  authors, and it needs a catalogue that does not exist on day one. A later model a catalogue earns.
- **Free** — a funnel into 1:1 lessons or a paid course. Per 06.02.04 DT-03's rejection of mandatory
  free trials, this is the **author's** choice, never a platform mandate (D-03).

**Bundling (resolved this step — index Q-02).** An author or academy may compose an offer that pairs a
course entitlement with lesson credits ("buy the beginner course, get 4 lessons"). This does **not**
merge the ledgers (D-01 stands): a single checkout writes **two distinct grants atomically** — one
entitlement row (perpetual, product-scoped) and one credit grant (teacher-scoped, burns per occurrence,
governed by 06.01.02's cancellation policy). If either grant fails, the whole purchase rolls back; the
buyer is never charged for half a bundle. The bundle is a **pricing/checkout composition**, not a third
ledger type. See DT-04.

`[PENDING — /create-prd]`: whether the platform ever runs a *catalog* subscription (all-you-can-eat)
at all. That is a business-model call with more consequences than any feature decision here; launch is
one-off only (D-02), so it does not block this feature.

### Happy Path

1. Learner (or Fan) finds a course — search, an author's profile, or the catalog
2. Previews a free lesson and reads the author's verified credits
3. Buys at a one-off price
4. Payment clears; entitlement granted; the course appears in their library
5. They start consuming (06.04.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Course is withdrawn after purchase | Buyer keeps access — an entitlement already granted is not revoked by de-listing (06.04.01 D-03) | "This course is no longer sold. Your access continues" |
| Buyer already owns the course | Purchase is blocked before payment — an entitlement is idempotent, you cannot buy the same product twice | "In your library" — Buy button replaced by "Open" |
| Bundle purchase where the credit grant fails but the course grant would succeed | Atomic rollback (DT-04) — no entitlement, no credit, no charge | "We couldn't complete your purchase. Nothing was charged. Try again" |
| Two buyers race the last seat of a limited/cohort-capped course | First committed checkout wins the seat; the second is rejected at the payment-commit boundary, not at add-to-cart | "This cohort just filled. You haven't been charged — join the next one?" |
| Buyer requests a refund after consuming 80% | The classic digital-goods problem: "watched it all and didn't like it" is indistinguishable from "it was bad" | `[PENDING — /create-prd]` — consumption-thresholded refund policy, routed to Payments + domain 24 |
| Cross-border VAT on a digital course | Education is **VAT-exempt in some regimes and not others**; the generic marketplace tax engine must not assume digital-goods treatment (D-04) | Tax cross-cut. **Flagged explicitly** — not a generic digital-goods sale |
| Minor buys a course | Payment by a minor against a UGC video product — age assurance and guardian consent apply | Safeguarding cross-cut (D-13 statutory duties) |
| Author drops the price after someone paid full | Standard marketplace grievance; no retroactive adjustment at launch | `[PENDING — /create-prd]` — price-drop credit is a policy call, not a feature |
| Free course used purely as lead-gen | Legitimate and the author's choice (per 06.02.04 D-02's principle) | — |
| Buyer charges back after downloading offline video | Provenance-of-consumption dispute; entitlement suspended pending resolution | Abuse path — routed to domain 24 disputes + Payments chargeback flow |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Catalog fetch | Skeleton cards |
| Empty | No courses in the catalog | **The cold-start reality.** Honest emptiness beats a fake catalog — a "be the first author here" CTA for authors |
| Populated | Courses listed | Cards: author, credits, price, preview |
| Error | Purchase failed | Retry; nothing charged |
| Partial | Purchased, entitlement granted but delivery/library sync pending | "Unlocking your course…" |
| Owned | Entitlement held | "In your library" — never a repeat buy prompt |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A course entitlement can share the lesson-credit ledger — both are prepaid learning | Surface similarity; the temptation to have one "learning wallet" | ❌ REJECTED — this is **D-14's exact argument, recurring inside a single domain**. D-14 rejected merging the marketplaces because "everything that differs is irreconcilable at schema level" and a merge "yields a `listing` entity with ~40 nullable columns". The same holds here: a lesson credit is **teacher-scoped, burns per occurrence, expires, and is governed by a cancellation policy**; a course entitlement is **product-scoped, perpetual, never burns, and has no policy**. They share only the word "prepaid". Merging them produces a nullable-column mess and a cancellation policy that has to explain why it doesn't apply to half its rows. |
| DT-02 | Courses need the lesson business to exist first — they are an add-on to tuition | Sub-domain ordering instinct: 06.04 comes after 06.01–06.03, so it must depend on them | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection carries real strategic weight. Courses have **no dependency on any other node in domain 06**: no booking, no scheduling cross-cut, no safeguarding gate (an adult buying a video needs no vetting), no Real-Time Rooms — which means **none of the map's hard architecture warnings on the locked stack apply to the transaction**, only to video delivery. Meanwhile it activates all four personas and monetises the credits wedge at full strength (06.04.01 DT-02). It is plausibly the domain's **best standalone bet and its natural first slice** — the reverse of what the numbering implies. Flagged for MoSCoW rather than asserted; the counter is index Q-04 (YouTube), which is a real and unanswered threat. |
| DT-03 | A catalog subscription (all-you-can-eat) is the better model, as it is everywhere else | Fender Play, Tonebase, Pickup Music, Netflix-for-everything — the whole category has converged on it | ❌ REJECTED **for launch**, on a cold-start argument rather than a business one. A subscription needs a **catalogue**; on day one there is no catalogue, and "£15/month for four courses" is an insult. Worse, subscription revenue-sharing across a thin catalogue pays individual authors close to nothing, which repels exactly the authors needed to build the catalogue — a loop that fails closed. One-off purchases pay authors properly from course one and give them a reason to publish here rather than on Teachable. Subscription is a **later** model that a catalogue earns; treating it as the target model at launch inverts the dependency. |
| DT-04 | Bundling a course with lesson credits (index Q-02) forces the two ledgers D-01 separates back together | The bundle offer "buy the course, get 4 lessons" looks like one product spanning both ledgers, which seems to contradict D-01 | ❌ REJECTED — bundling is a **checkout composition, not a ledger merge**. The correct model: one purchase, two atomic grants — an entitlement row (perpetual, product-scoped) *and* a credit grant (teacher-scoped, burn-per-occurrence, under 06.01.02's cancellation policy) — committed or rolled back together. Neither ledger learns about the other; the Payments/Entitlements cross-cut owns the atomic transaction, and each grant lands in its own ledger with its own lifetime. This is precisely what D-01 permits: separate ledgers, joined only at the point of sale. The trap DT-04 guards against is a "bundle entitlement" third type that tries to hold both semantics — that recreates DT-01's nullable-column mess. Reject the third type; keep the two-grant transaction. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A course entitlement and a lesson credit are **separate ledgers** | Per DT-01 — D-14's argument, recurring inside one domain. They share only the word "prepaid". |
| D-02 | **One-off purchase** is the launch model; subscription is later | Per DT-03 — a subscription needs a catalogue, and thin-catalogue subscription economics repel the authors who would build it. |
| D-03 | Free-as-funnel is the **author's** choice, never the platform's mandate | Same principle as 06.02.04 D-02 — the platform does not conscript its poorest persona's work for acquisition. |
| D-04 | Education **VAT treatment is explicitly flagged**, not inherited from digital goods | Tuition and instructional content are exempt in some regimes; a generic engine will get this silently wrong at scale. |
| D-05 | Bundling course + lesson credits is a **two-grant atomic checkout**, not a merged ledger | Per DT-04 — resolves index Q-02. One purchase writes an entitlement row and a credit grant, committed/rolled back together; neither ledger merges. |
