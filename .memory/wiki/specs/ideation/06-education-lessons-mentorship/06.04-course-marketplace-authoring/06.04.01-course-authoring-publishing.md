# Feature: Course Authoring & Publishing

> **Number**: 06.04.01
> **Parent**: [Course Marketplace & Authoring](./06.04-course-marketplace-authoring-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Where a musician turns what they know into something they can sell more than once. A course is
lessons, video, audio examples, tabs/charts, and practice assignments, sequenced and published. The
authoring problem is not the tooling — it is that **a course is a production, and musicians
systematically underestimate that**: they film four videos, stall, and leave a half-course sitting in
drafts forever. Design accordingly.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher/author**: structures the course, uploads video and audio examples, attaches charts and reference tracks, sets practice tasks per lesson, previews it as a learner, publishes. **As Student**: never authors — a learner has no editing surface here at all. |
| Producer | Full | **The one place in domain 06 where a Producer is a first-class actor.** "How I mix drums", "my vocal chain", "mixing in the box" — this is a Producer's course, and their verified credits (06.02.02) are exactly what makes a stranger buy it. A Producer will never take a weekly beginner; they will absolutely film a masterclass. |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: publishes house courses, and owns the rights question when a staff teacher authors on academy time — see Q-02, which is a real contract problem and not a settings toggle. |
| Fan | None | Cannot author. Can buy (06.04.02) and consume (06.04.03). |

## Behavior

A course is a tree: sections containing lessons, each lesson carrying video, audio examples,
attachments and an optional practice task. The unit of authoring is the lesson; the unit of sale is
the course (06.04.02) — there is no per-lesson purchase at this level.

**The publish gate is concrete, not vibes.** A course cannot go live until it clears four hard
requirements: (1) a price is set (06.04.02); (2) at least one lesson is marked as the free preview,
so a buyer sees before they pay; (3) every section contains at least one lesson with a processed,
playable video — no empty sections, no lessons stuck mid-transcode; (4) title, instrument, and level
are set. The credits block is *not* a gate — a course with no attached credits publishes fine
(D-02 renders it automatically when credits exist; absence is never a demerit, per 06.02.02 D-03).
Whether a stricter completeness bar applies on top of this — a minimum lesson count, a "no in-progress
courses" rule — is the open-vs-curated question (Q-01), and it is the one gate that changes with the
marketplace's governance model rather than with the course.

The differentiator available here — and it is a real one — is that **a WeJammin course arrives with
its author's credits attached**. Every other course platform sells a stranger's claim to expertise;
here, "mixed by the person who mixed {record}, confirmed by {artist}" is checkable. That is the same
wedge 06.02.02 uses for teachers, pointed at a product instead of a relationship, and it is the
strongest argument this sub-domain has against YouTube (index Q-04).

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: the authoring tool's actual shape. Whether it is a
video-first LMS builder, a lightweight "record from the practice room" flow, or an import from
existing YouTube content, is a real product decision that depends on Q-01 (open vs curated) and on
who the target author is.

### Happy Path

1. Musician/Producer starts a course; names it, sets instrument, level, and who it's for
2. Structures sections and lessons before filming — the outline is the production plan
3. Uploads video per lesson, attaches audio examples, charts, and reference tracks
4. Sets a practice task per lesson (06.04.03)
5. Previews as a learner; the credits block renders automatically from domain 02 — nothing to write
6. Sets a price (06.04.02) and publishes

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Author stalls at 4 of 20 lessons | **The base rate.** Most courses die here | `[PENDING — Step 5]`. Options: publish incrementally, drip-release, or sell a "in progress" course honestly. All have consequences for the buyer |
| Author publishes a thin course | Buyers pay for a promise; refunds and reputational damage follow. Index Q-01 (open vs curated) is exactly this question | `[PENDING]` — completeness bar vs open marketplace |
| Course uses copyrighted material — the recording being taught | **Structural, not incidental.** Teaching a song requires the song. This is 06.03.02 DT-04 at commercial scale: private teaching infringement is quiet, a *published, sold* course containing a copyrighted recording is loud | Routed to domain 24 + DMCA. See DT-03 |
| Author uploads 4K video of a 90-minute masterclass | Transcode cost and time; the map already flags heavy transcode as a hard stack constraint (index D-03) | `[PENDING — /create-prd-architecture]` |
| Academy staff authors on academy time, then leaves | Rights conflict — a real contract problem with money attached | Q-02. Not resolvable as a settings toggle |
| Author wants to update a published course | A course is a **living entitlement**, not a snapshot: edits, re-recorded lessons, and added lessons push to existing buyers automatically at no extra charge (Udemy/Teachable norm). Buyers are not re-charged for a course improving. Whether an author can also cut a *new, separately-priced* course from the same material is a pricing question for 06.04.02, not a versioning one | Existing buyers see "updated" and the new lessons appear in their library (06.04.03). `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`: whether a buyer can still reach the exact version they bought if the author later removes a lesson |
| Two co-authors edit the same lesson at once | Academy/co-taught courses have multiple authors; simultaneous edits to one lesson's metadata or ordering can clobber | Last-write-wins on distinct fields; a same-field conflict shows "changed by {other author} — reload". `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`: whether lesson-level locking is worth it |
| Author has no credits to attach | Course publishes fine; it just lacks the wedge | Same principle as 06.02.02 D-03 — absence is never rendered as a demerit |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Editor open | Skeleton outline |
| Empty | No courses | "Turn what you teach into a course" |
| Draft | Authoring | Outline + per-lesson completion. **The state most courses die in** |
| Populated | Published | Live course + sales |
| Error | Upload/transcode failed | Retry, **draft never lost** |
| Partial | Published, incomplete | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — depends on the drip/incremental decision |
| Unpublished | Author withdrew | Existing buyers retain access — an entitlement is a sale, not a subscription to the author's goodwill |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Courses belong in Digital Goods (14) — they are a digital product sold to musicians | D-14's own reasoning about marketplace separation; a course really is a digital good by any plain reading | ❌ REJECTED, using the ratified map's own principle rather than a fresh argument. The map states: "Digital music sales to fans stay in Fanbase and reuse Digital Goods' download/library delivery — split by destination." Domain 14 is "creative raw materials sold to **makers**" — samples and plugins that become inputs to new work. A course is **instruction consumed by a learner**; its output is a changed person, not a new track. It also shares nothing with 14's structure: no licence keys, no format×OS×DAW matrix, no download-and-own. Split by destination; share the delivery layer. |
| DT-02 | The author's verified credits are the differentiator versus every other course platform | 06.02.02 DT-01's flywheel, applied to a product instead of a relationship; domain rationale's credentialing claim | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is **stronger here than for teachers**, which is a genuinely surprising inversion. 06.02.02 DT-02 had to reject credits as a teacher-ranking signal because playing ability ≠ teaching ability, and the domain's best teachers often have no discography. **That objection largely evaporates for courses**: a course buyer is not buying patience with a 9-year-old, they are buying "show me how you got that snare sound" — where the credit is *exactly* the relevant qualification. The wedge that must be handled delicately in 06.02 can be used at full strength here. This is also what makes the Producer a first-class author, and it is the best answer available to index Q-04 (why not YouTube): YouTube cannot verify that the person teaching you actually made the record. |
| DT-03 | Course copyright exposure is the same problem as assignment attachments (06.03.02 DT-04), at the same severity | Both are teachers uploading copyrighted music to teach with | ❌ REJECTED — **it is categorically worse**, and the difference is commercial. A teacher scanning a page for one student is quiet infringement with a takedown as its worst outcome. A **published course, sold for money, containing a copyrighted recording** is commercial infringement of the loud, litigable kind — and teaching a song structurally requires the song. Worse, the platform takes a cut, which makes it a beneficiary rather than a passive host, weakening safe-harbour posture. This is a materially higher-severity route into domain 24 than anything in 06.03, and it argues that the licensed-repertoire question (06.03's Q-01) is not merely a nice-to-have for this sub-domain but a precondition for it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Courses stay in Education, split by **destination** | Per DT-01 — the ratified map's own principle. Learners, not makers. |
| D-02 | The credits block renders **automatically** from domain 02 | Per DT-02 — the author does no work to produce their own differentiator, exactly as in 06.02.02. |
| D-03 | An unpublished course **retains existing buyers' access** | An entitlement is a sale. Revoking on withdrawal makes every purchase conditional on the author's continued goodwill. |
| D-04 | Drafts are never lost on upload/transcode failure | Per the Draft state — it is where most courses die, and losing one kills the course permanently. |
| D-05 | A published course is a **living entitlement** — updates push to existing buyers free | Resolves Q-03 at Level 1. Snapshotting per-purchase would fossilise courses and punish authors for improving; the industry norm (Udemy/Teachable) is a moving course, and it aligns with D-03's "a sale is not conditional on the author's goodwill" — a buyer's access only ever grows. Whether a removed lesson stays reachable to prior buyers is a BE-spec detail, not a product one. |
| D-06 | Publish gate is four hard requirements: price, one preview lesson, no empty/mid-transcode sections, core metadata | Turns "enough completeness" (an adjective) into a checkable rule. Any stricter bar is governed by Q-01, not by the course. |
