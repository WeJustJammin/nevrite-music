# Feature: Course Consumption & Completion

> **Number**: 06.04.03
> **Parent**: [Course Marketplace & Authoring](./06.04-course-marketplace-authoring-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Watching the course and — very rarely — finishing it. Consumption is where the sub-domain's honesty
problem lives: **most people who buy a course never complete it**, and this is not a bug the product
can design away. What the feature can do is make the buyer's abandonment less likely and less
shameful, and give the author the only real signal they'll ever get about their own work — the
timestamp at which learners quit.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As learner**: watches lessons, plays along, marks progress, uses the practice tools (06.03.04) against the course's material, resumes where they left off across devices. **As author**: sees where learners drop off — lesson 3, minute 4 — which is the only feedback loop courses have. |
| Producer | Reports | As author: the same drop-off data. A Producer selling a mixing course learns their bus-compression section loses everyone, and re-shoots it. |
| Operator | Reports | As academy: cohort completion across house courses. |
| Fan | Full | Consumes what they bought, exactly as any learner. No distinction, and no reason for one. |

## Behavior

Playback with progress tracking, resume-across-devices, and per-lesson practice tasks. The
differentiator available is **the practice room** (06.03.04): a course lesson that says "play these
four bars at 80bpm" can hand the learner a metronome pre-set to 80, the reference recording loopable
at 70%, and a record button — inside the same surface. That is not something YouTube can do, and it is
one of the better answers this sub-domain has to index Q-04.

Completion is tracked but **not celebrated as the measure**. In music, completion is close to
meaningless: a learner who watched three lessons and can now play the thing has succeeded; one who
watched all twenty and never picked up the instrument has not. The product should not congratulate the
second and shame the first (DT-02).

**Directional call (Level 1, DT-04)**: course practice tasks **do** write to the practice log
(06.03.03) — it is the obvious integration and it wires a teacher-less product into a teacher-oriented
loop, so a Musician's course-driven practice accretes to the same identity as their lesson-driven
practice. The exact write contract (does a course task create a `practice_session` row, and does it
carry a `source: course/{id}/lesson/{n}` provenance tag) is a cross-cut detail resolved at Step 6 and
`/write-be-spec` — see Cross-Cut Notes.

### Happy Path

1. Learner opens a course from their library
2. Watches lesson 1; progress saves continuously
3. Lesson 1 sets a practice task; the practice room opens with the tempo and loop points pre-set
4. Learner practises, optionally records
5. Resumes on their phone the next day, exactly where they stopped
6. Reaches the end — or doesn't, which is normal and unpunished

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Learner abandons at lesson 3 | **The base rate across the entire category.** Not a failure state | No shame UI, no "you're falling behind" email. A quiet, easy return path |
| Learner watches everything, plays nothing | Completion says success; reality says otherwise (DT-02) | Completion is not celebrated as the measure |
| Learner opens a course they never bought | Consumption is gated on the entitlement (06.04.02); no entitlement, no playback | Playback surface is replaced by the catalog/purchase CTA, not an error |
| Entitlement revoked mid-consumption (refund, chargeback, author unpublishes) | Access ends at the entitlement boundary; already-recorded progress and any practice-log rows persist under the learner's identity | Course drops out of the library; a quiet "no longer available" state, not a data-loss event |
| Two devices play the same course at once | Progress is last-write-wins per lesson-position; both sessions are valid — one human, two screens is normal (Musician multi-device) | Resume point converges to the furthest confirmed position; no lock-out, no "already streaming elsewhere" wall |
| Offline consumption (a train, a practice room) | Download for offline is a real expectation of a paid product | `[PENDING — MoSCoW / /create-prd-security]` (Q-02) — download implies DRM the sub-domain has not touched |
| Content ripped and redistributed | The whole category's problem; a paid video product is copyable | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` |
| Learner asks the author a question | Courses are asynchronous and author-less by design — but buyers ask anyway | `[PENDING — /ideate-validate]` (Q-03) — Q&A re-introduces the unpaid-labour problem 06.03.02 Q-01 raises for lesson feedback |
| Bad connection mid-lesson | Media cross-cut; progress must not be lost | Resume from the exact point |
| Course updated while a learner is mid-way | Progress must map to the new version | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` — 06.04.01 Q-03 versioning: position-mapping across an edited lesson list |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Course open | Skeleton + resume point resolved first |
| Empty | Nothing bought | "Your library is empty" + catalog |
| Populated | Course in progress | Lesson list, progress, resume CTA |
| Error | Playback failed | Retry; progress preserved |
| Partial | Abandoned mid-course | **Normal.** A quiet resume prompt that decays and then stops |
| Complete | All lessons watched | Marked, not celebrated (DT-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The practice room (06.03.04) is what differentiates a WeJammin course from a YouTube video | 06.03.04 DT-02 — the room works with no teacher, which means it works for a teacher-less product; index Q-04 needs an answer | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the sub-domain's second real differentiator after credits. A YouTube lesson says "practise this at 80bpm" and the viewer must go find a metronome, a way to slow the recording down, and a way to record themselves — three apps and a decision to quit. A course lesson here hands them a room with the tempo set, the reference loopable, and a record button. **The two differentiators compose**: verified credits make a stranger buy it (06.04.01 DT-02); the practice room makes them able to use it. Neither is available to YouTube, and neither requires the lesson business to exist (06.04.02 DT-02). |
| DT-02 | Completion is the success metric, as it is in every LMS | Standard e-learning practice; it is the number every course platform reports | ❌ REJECTED — it is the wrong measure and an actively harmful one here. In music, **the goal is playing, not watching**. A learner who watched three lessons and can now play the riff succeeded; one who completed all twenty without touching the instrument did not. Optimising for completion optimises for watching, which is the failure mode the practice room exists to escape (DT-01). Worse, completion pressure imports the same shame dynamics 06.03.02 DT-03 and 06.03.03 DT-02 already rejected for assignments and streaks — a third instance of the same pattern in this domain, which suggests it is a domain-level design law rather than three coincidences. **Track completion; never celebrate it as the measure.** |
| DT-03 | Drop-off analytics are the author's most valuable feedback, and this is the one metric worth optimising | Reasoning from the author's position — a course author is blind after publishing; there is no room, no student, no feedback | ✅ CONFIRMED, with a caution. "68% of learners quit at lesson 3, minute 4" is genuinely actionable and no other node in the domain offers its actor anything comparable. The caution: drop-off measures **engagement**, and per DT-02 engagement is not the goal — an author optimising drop-off will make lessons shorter and more entertaining, which is how instructional content becomes content. The signal is diagnostic ("something is wrong at 3:04"), not a target. Same distinction 06.03.03 D-06 draws for the teacher's practice-gap view — the third time this domain has had to separate diagnosis from enforcement. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Completion is **tracked, not celebrated as the measure** | Per DT-02 — in music the goal is playing, not watching, and completion pressure imports the shame dynamics this domain has now rejected three separate times. |
| D-02 | Abandonment is a **normal state**, not a failure UI | It is the category's base rate. A shame prompt loses the return visit as well as the course. |
| D-03 | Course practice tasks hand the learner the **practice room**, pre-configured | Per DT-01 — the composable differentiator, and the answer to "why not YouTube". |
| D-04 | Drop-off is **diagnostic**, not a target | Per DT-03 — optimising engagement turns instruction into content. Same law as 06.03.03 D-06. |
| D-05 | Consumption is gated on the **entitlement**; losing the entitlement ends **access** but never deletes **recorded progress or practice rows** | The learner's accumulated work accrues to their identity (the provenance thesis applied to learning). A refund revokes the video, not the fact that the practice happened. Access boundary lives in 06.04.02; the identity record is domain 01. |
| D-06 | Multi-device playback is **concurrent-allowed, last-write-wins** on lesson position | The Musician persona is defined by using many surfaces in one day; a single-stream lock ("already playing elsewhere") is a DRM posture that punishes the normal case. Resume converges to the furthest confirmed position. |
