## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Practice Room Tools ↔ Practice Logging

**Relationship**: The load-bearing seam of the sub-domain, and the reason 06.03.04 was added to the
candidate list at all. A student opens the room **to practise**; the session is observed as a
byproduct; the log writes itself. Invert the dependency — a log the student opens to *report* — and it
decays within weeks (06.03.03 DT-01), taking the domain's daily-habit claim (06.03.03 DT-04) with it.

This is the platform's own thesis with the nouns swapped. `meta/problem-statement.md` argues WeJammin
can capture splits where Jaxsta cannot **because it is in the room where the work happens**. Practice
is work; it happens in a room; a product that asks a child "how long did you practise?" is asking
someone to remember, which is the exact failure the whole platform exists to reject.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: practises; never fills in a form. The log costs one tap or nothing
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: receives an honest record instead of a child's answer to "did you practise?"
- **Operator**: aggregates only — never a named student's session data (sub-domain D-05)
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected — hard absence

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The room **emits** sessions; the log **owns** entries. Single writer. Manual back-fill (06.03.03 D-03) is a second, human-initiated source — and it must be indistinguishable in the UI, or back-filled entries read as second-class and the student stops making them.
2. **Trigger chain**: Session ends → log entry drafted → one-tap confirm. **The chain must survive offline** (both features consume the Offline cross-cut): practice happens in bedrooms and garages, a dropped entry breaks a streak, and per 06.03.03 DT-02 a broken streak is a quit trigger. Async with local queue.
3. **Permission intersection**: 06.03.04 D-03 scopes observation to the *session*, not the microphone. This bounds how honest the log can be — and it is the right bound given the user is a child in their bedroom. 06.03.03 Q-01 may reopen it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The teacher sees the week's pattern before the lesson. Practice reminders are one design mistake from nagging a child (06.03.03 DT-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: A session running while a manual entry is added for the same window — double-counting. Must dedupe, and must dedupe in the student's favour.

### CX-02: Assignments ↔ Practice Logging

**Relationship**: The teacher's single most valuable view, and the sub-domain's sharpest ethical edge.
The gap between *assigned* (bars 12–20 at 80bpm) and *practised* (4×15min on the piece they already
liked) converts a weekly guess into a diagnostic. It is also, in one keystroke, a surveillance record
of a child's compliance placed in the hands of an adult with authority over them.

06.03.03 D-06 resolves the tension by constraining **framing**: diagnostic ("here's what happened"),
never evidential ("here's the proof you didn't"). That is a design constraint, not a preference, and
it is the difference between a teaching tool and a compliance tool.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: sees the gap; is trusted to teach with it, not prosecute with it
- **Musician (as Student)**: their practice is visible to their teacher — which is legitimate and expected. It is **not** visible to an academy (sub-domain D-05) or guardian; D-07 limits guardian access to lesson feedback and billing/entitlement facts.
- **Operator**: aggregate engagement only
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — assignments and logs are independent records; the gap is a derived view.
2. **Trigger chain**: No trigger. Read-side join. Which is exactly why it must not acquire one — an automated "student didn't practise" alert is the compliance tool 06.03.03 D-06 forbids.
3. **Permission intersection**: The teacher's visibility follows the teaching relationship and nothing else. Guardian report readership never implies diary access (06.03.03 D-07); former-teacher wind-down remains governed by 06.03.01 Q-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: **Deliberately none** for practice data. Feedback/report issuance follows its own safeguarded route and never creates diary access.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-03: Curriculum ↔ Assignments

**Relationship**: An assignment draws from the current curriculum unit when one exists, and works fine
when none does. The optionality is the point: most private teachers teach from experience and a
notebook (06.03.01 DT-02), and a product that requires a written curriculum before it will accept an
assignment loses them on day one. The curriculum earns its place when a teacher has 20 students and
cannot remember who is where — not before.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: assigns with or without a plan; no nag either way
- **Musician (as Student)**: sees the assignment; the plan behind it is optional context
- **Operator**: a house syllabus frames what teachers assign — the academy's actual product
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The path owns position; assignments reference a unit optionally. Nullable link, deliberately.
2. **Trigger chain**: Advancing the path suggests the next assignment. Suggestion only — a trigger that auto-assigns would make the template's pace into the student's obligation, which is 06.03.01 D-02's whole rejection.
3. **Permission intersection**: An academy syllabus constrains teacher-authored units (06.03.01), not the assignment itself.
4. **Notification fan-out**: New assignment notifies the student.
5. **State transition conflict**: Teacher edits a template while an assignment references a live unit — resolved by 06.03.01 D-04 (instances don't retroactively change).

### CX-04: Practice Room Tools ↔ Assignments

**Relationship**: The assignment renders inside the practice room, and the submission is recorded from
it. **No export step, no file picker, no upload flow.** This matters more than it sounds: every extra
step between "I played it well" and "the teacher has it" is a step at which a 12-year-old stops, and a
submission not made is feedback not given, which is the loop breaking.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: practise, record, send — one surface
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: configures the room per assignment (target tempo, loop points), so practice is specified rather than guessed at
- **Operator / Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The assignment is read into the room; the submission is written back. Disjoint.
2. **Trigger chain**: Record → submit → notify teacher. **Failure must preserve the take locally** (06.03.02 D-04): losing a student's recording of themselves being bad at something ends submissions permanently.
3. **Permission intersection**: Submitting a minor's recording to an adult is a Safeguarding-monitored surface, not a neutral file transfer.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Teacher notified of the submission.
5. **State transition conflict**: Assignment closed/rolled forward while a student is mid-take. Must accept the late take — rejecting it punishes the student for the teacher's timing.

### CX-05: Practice Logging ↔ Progress Reports

**Relationship**: Practice data is the most *available* input to a report and one of the worst
**measures** in it. Hours measure effort, not ability; the hardest-working student may be the least
improved — that is what a plateau is. 06.03.05 DT-02 rejects proxying ability from platform data
entirely, which makes this seam primarily a **constraint**: the data flows, and it must not become the
verdict.

The privacy half is equally load-bearing. The log is the student's daily diary; the report goes to a
third party (usually a parent). Separating them (sub-domain D-03) is what keeps the diary out of the
report's reader's hands.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: their diary informs a report about them; it is not handed over wholesale
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: may draw on the pattern; owns the judgement
- **Operator**: cohort outcomes only
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected — though 06.03.05 DT-01 notes the report's real reader has no persona at all

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — the report reads the log at draft time and snapshots. A report must not mutate when the log later changes; it is a statement made on a date.
2. **Trigger chain**: Term end → draft → **teacher approval** → issue. Approval is mandatory (06.03.05 D-02); nothing reaches a parent unread.
3. **Permission intersection**: The critical one. Report readership ≠ log readership. A parent may receive a report (unresolved — 06.03.03 Q-02 / 06.03.05 Q-01) and that must not imply diary access.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Report issued → student, and → whoever else per the unresolved guardian question.
5. **State transition conflict**: Log edited after a report is issued — the report stands as a dated statement, per Q1.
