# Feature: Curriculum & Lesson Plans

> **Number**: 06.03.01
> **Parent**: [Curriculum, Assignments & Practice](./06.03-curriculum-assignments-practice-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The teacher's plan for where a student is going and how they get there. In practice this is two
things that look alike and behave differently: a **reusable curriculum** (the teacher's method,
refined over years, applied to every beginner they take) and a **student's actual path** (which
diverges from the method in week three, because students are people). The feature's job is to let a
teacher carry their method between students without pretending any two students follow it identically.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: authors reusable curriculum templates (their beginner-guitar method, their grade-5 prep), instantiates one per student, adapts it as reality intervenes, reorders, and sees at a glance where each student is on their path. **As Student**: sees what's next and roughly what's ahead — enough to feel a direction, not so much that week 40 is a promise the teacher can't keep. |
| Producer | None | Not visible — a teacher's pedagogy is not a Producer's business (see the sub-domain index note on this absence). |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: publishes a **house syllabus** teachers adapt rather than replace. A school's pedagogical consistency is the thing parents are buying, and this is where it lives. Cannot rewrite an individual teacher's plan for a specific student. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A curriculum is a sequence of **units** (a technique, a piece, a concept) with prerequisites,
optional exam-board mapping (06.09), and suggested practice. It is a template. Instantiating it for a
student produces that student's path — a living object that gets reordered, skipped, repeated, and
abandoned in ways the template never anticipated.

The critical design property is that **divergence must not feel like failure**. A student who spends
six weeks on a piece the template allotted two is normal; a UI that shows them "behind schedule" is
describing the template's error as the student's. This is the difference between a teaching tool and
a compliance tool, and it is the whole reason the template/instance split exists rather than one
shared object.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: whether curriculum templates are **shareable or sellable**
between teachers. It is an obvious marketplace idea (a teacher's method is genuinely valuable IP), it
collides directly with 06.04 (Course Marketplace), and inventing that seam at breadth would be
guesswork.

### Happy Path

1. Teacher authors a curriculum template once — units, order, prerequisites, suggested practice
2. Teacher takes on a student and instantiates the template as that student's path
3. Each lesson (06.01.04) advances the path; the session record notes what was actually covered
4. Assignment for the week is drawn from the current unit (06.03.02)
5. Teacher reorders or inserts as the student's reality demands — no ceremony
6. Student sees what's next; teacher sees every student's position across their whole book

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Teacher has no curriculum and teaches by instinct | Fully supported — an assignment can exist with no plan behind it. **Most private teachers work this way and a product that requires a curriculum will lose them** | Teacher: no nagging. The plan is optional scaffolding, not a precondition |
| Student diverges hard from the template | Normal. Path is a living object; the template is untouched | Student: no "behind schedule" framing, ever (see Behavior) |
| Teacher edits a template while 20 students are on it | Instances do **not** retroactively change — a student's path is theirs | Teacher: "Applies to new students. Push to existing?" as an explicit, per-student choice |
| Student switches teachers | `[PENDING — Step 5]`. Does the path transfer? It contains the new teacher's competitor's IP, and it also contains the student's own history — which are different things tangled in one object |
| Academy syllabus vs teacher's own method | Syllabus is the frame; teacher adapts within it. Conflict is a human problem, not a system one | Teacher: house units marked as house |
| Student is taught by two teachers (piano and theory) | Two paths, one student — normal and must not be forced into one plan | Student: both, clearly attributed |
| Curriculum maps to an exam syllabus that changes | Exam boards revise syllabi periodically; mapped units go stale | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — see 06.09 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Path fetch | Skeleton unit list |
| Empty | No curriculum (common and fine) | Teacher: "Add a plan" as a quiet option, **not** an empty-state demand. Student: sees assignments only |
| Populated | Path instantiated | Unit list with the current position marked |
| Error | Save failed | Inline, draft retained |
| Partial | Path started, later units unplanned | Near-term detailed, later loose — which is how real teaching works |
| Diverged | Actual progress ≠ template pace | **No special state.** Deliberately absent — this is the feature's central design decision (D-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A curriculum is a course, so this duplicates 06.04 (Course Marketplace & Authoring) | Both are ordered sequences of instructional units — structurally near-identical | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is sharp. A course is a **product**: authored once, sold to strangers, consumed without the author present, identical for every buyer. A curriculum is a **plan**: private to one teaching relationship, adapted continuously, meaningless without the teacher. The tell is the divergence property — a course that changes per buyer is broken; a curriculum that *doesn't* change per student is a teacher not paying attention. They may share a unit primitive; they are not the same feature. The seam is recorded in the domain CX. |
| DT-02 | Most private teachers do not use a written curriculum at all, and the product must not require one | Domain expertise: independent teachers teach from experience and a notebook; structured syllabi are an academy and exam-prep phenomenon | ✅ CONFIRMED — with a real consequence for the whole sub-domain. If a curriculum is a **precondition** for assignments, the domain loses most private teachers on day one. So the loop must run without it: assignment → practice → feedback works with no plan behind it, and the curriculum is optional scaffolding that pays for itself when the teacher has 20 students and can't remember who's where. This is why the Empty state is explicitly not a demand. |
| DT-03 | The template/instance split is a data-modelling detail that can be deferred to `/create-prd` | It reads like a technical normalization question | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **product** decision with a visible surface, and getting it wrong produces the domain's worst UX. One shared object means either the teacher can't reuse their method (they rewrite it per student) or a student's divergence corrupts the template for everyone. Worse, a merged model naturally surfaces "student is behind" — reframing the template's estimate as the student's failure. A 9-year-old told they are behind schedule at the piano quits. The split is what makes divergence normal rather than a status. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Template and instance are separate objects** | Per DT-03. Lets a teacher carry their method between students without any student's divergence corrupting it — and without the teacher rewriting it 20 times. |
| D-02 | There is **no "behind schedule" state** | Per DT-03. The template's pace is an estimate; rendering divergence as a deficit blames the student for the estimate's error, and students who feel behind quit. |
| D-03 | A curriculum is **optional** — the assignment loop runs without one | Per DT-02. Requiring a plan loses most private teachers, who are the domain's supply. |
| D-04 | Template edits do **not** retroactively alter live student paths | A student's path is a record of their teaching relationship, not a view onto the teacher's current method. |
