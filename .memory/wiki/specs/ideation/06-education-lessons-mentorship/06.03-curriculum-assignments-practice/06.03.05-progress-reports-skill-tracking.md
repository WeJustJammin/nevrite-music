# Feature: Progress Reports & Skill Tracking

> **Number**: 06.03.05
> **Parent**: [Curriculum, Assignments & Practice](./06.03-curriculum-assignments-practice-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The periodic answer to "is this working?" — a question asked most often by the person paying, who is
usually not the person learning. A progress report is an artifact written **for someone else**: it
summarises what a student has covered, what they can now do that they couldn't, and what's next.
Separated from the practice log (06.03.03) because it has a different reader, a different cadence and
a different privacy class — merging them would hand the report's reader the student's diary.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: writes or approves a periodic report per student, drawing on path position (06.03.01), submissions (06.03.02) and practice patterns (06.03.03) — with the boring parts pre-drafted, because a teacher with 25 students will not write 25 reports by hand. **As Student**: sees their own trajectory — what they can play now that they couldn't in January, which is the only motivation that has ever worked. |
| Producer | None | Not visible (see the sub-domain index note). |
| Operator | Reports | As an academy: cohort-level outcomes — are our students progressing, which teachers' students progress. Legitimate quality assurance. Not a named child's diary. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. But see DT-01 — the report's **actual primary reader is the fee-paying parent, who is not in the persona set at all**. |

## Behavior

A report is generated on a cadence (per term, per N lessons) or on demand. Its content is a summary
of covered units, notable submissions, practice pattern, and the teacher's own words — which are the
only part anyone reads, and the only part that requires the teacher's time.

**Skill tracking** is the more dangerous half: representing what a student can *do* rather than what
they've *covered*. Coverage is objective and near-worthless ("we did scales"); ability is what the
report is asked for and is genuinely hard to measure. The temptation is to proxy it with what's
measurable — practice hours, submission counts, units completed — and every one of those proxies is
wrong (DT-02).

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: the skill model itself. Whether ability is representable at
all beyond exam-board grades (06.09) and a teacher's prose is genuinely unresolved, and inventing a
skill taxonomy at breadth would be fabrication.

### Happy Path

1. Term ends; the system drafts a report from path, submissions and practice data
2. Teacher reads the draft, deletes the parts that are noise, and writes two sentences that mean something
3. Teacher approves; the report is issued
4. Student sees their trajectory; the fee-payer sees what they bought
5. Next term's plan is framed by it

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Student made no progress this term | Happens constantly and is often nobody's fault (a hard piece, a hard year, a plateau). The report must be able to **say so honestly** without indicting either party | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — an honest plateau report is hard to write and the product must not force a positive one |
| Teacher never writes reports | The base rate for private teachers. Reports are an academy/parent expectation, not a private-teacher habit | Optional. **Never a nag** — see 06.03.01 DT-02 for the same principle |
| Auto-generated report is bland and wrong | Data-driven prose about a child's musicianship will be both. This is the feature's default failure | Teacher approval is mandatory; **nothing is issued unread** (D-02) |
| Practice data contradicts the report | Teacher says "great progress", log says 20 minutes all term. Both can be true — some students are efficient, some plateau while practising hard | Do **not** auto-flag the contradiction; it is a diagnostic for the teacher, not a compliance check on them |
| Parent reads a bad report and pressures the child | The foreseeable harm. A report is a document handed to an authority figure about a child's hobby | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — framing constraint, and it is the reason Q-01 exists |
| Adult self-directed learner | Wants a trajectory, not a report — no third party to satisfy | Trajectory view without the report artifact |
| Student switches teachers mid-term | Report covers what the reporting teacher saw; history persists | Attributed per teacher |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The report's primary reader is the **fee-paying parent**, not the student — and that reader is not in the ratified persona set | Domain expertise: teaching is "overwhelmingly a minors market" (Safeguarding cross-cut); reports are what parents ask for and what academies exist to provide; 06.02.01 DT-03 found the same gap from the discovery side | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the second independent arrival at the same finding from a different feature, which strengthens it considerably. This feature exists **because someone who is not the user is paying**, and that person has no persona (D-19). Domain 06 answers "what is a guardian?" for *consent* (a delegated authority — domain D-03), but a parent **commissioning, paying for and evaluating** tuition is a **buyer**, and buyers are personas. Escalated to the domain index (Q-01), which now defers to `/create-prd` — `/ideate-validate` has already run (2026-07-18) and closed without recording an answer, and D-71 explicitly declines to close the fee-paying parent ([meta/counterparties.md](../../meta/counterparties.md)). Note the honest counter: the owner chose 4 personas over 5 deliberately, and `meta/personas.md` Q-01 already anticipates exactly this kind of "revisit if a Role Matrix comes out thin" test. |
| DT-02 | Skill/ability can be tracked from platform data (practice hours, submissions, units completed) | The obvious build — the data exists, so measure with it | ❌ REJECTED — every available proxy is wrong, and confidently wrong. **Practice hours measure effort, not ability** (the hardest-working student may be the least improved — that's what a plateau is). **Units covered measure the teacher's pace, not the student's skill.** **Submission count measures compliance and confidence**, and the shyest student submits least. A report built on these would be a plausible, data-backed, fluent lie about a child's musicianship, handed to their parent. The only honest instruments available are an **external graded standard** (06.09 — which is exactly why exam boards exist and endure) and **a teacher's own ears in prose**. The platform can measure coverage; it cannot measure musicianship, and it must not pretend otherwise. |
| DT-03 | Auto-generating the report is a labour-saving win for teachers | A teacher with 25 students faces 25 reports a term — real, hated, unpaid work | ✅ CONFIRMED, **narrowly**, and the boundary matters. Generation is legitimate for the mechanical half: units covered, attendance, practice pattern, submission history. It is illegitimate for the judgement half (DT-02), and the failure mode is that generated prose is *fluent* — it reads like a considered assessment while being a summary of coverage statistics. That is worse than a blank page, because a parent can't tell. So: draft the boring parts, mandate teacher approval, and never issue anything unread (D-02). The two sentences that matter are the two the teacher writes. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Progress reports are **separate** from the practice log | Different reader, cadence and privacy class. Merging hands the report's reader the student's diary (sub-domain D-03). |
| D-02 | **Nothing is issued unread** — teacher approval is mandatory | Per DT-03 — generated prose is fluent and therefore indistinguishable from judgement to the parent reading it. |
| D-03 | The platform measures **coverage**, never **musicianship** | Per DT-02 — every available proxy is confidently wrong, and a data-backed lie about a child's ability is worse than an honest gap. |
| D-04 | Reports are **optional** for private teachers | Per the second edge case — reports are an academy/parent norm, and nagging private teachers with paperwork loses the domain's supply (same principle as 06.03.01 D-03). |
