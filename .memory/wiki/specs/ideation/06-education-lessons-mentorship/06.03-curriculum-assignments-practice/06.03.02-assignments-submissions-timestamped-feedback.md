# Feature: Assignments, Submissions & Timestamped Feedback

> **Number**: 06.03.02
> **Parent**: [Curriculum, Assignments & Practice](./06.03-curriculum-assignments-practice-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

"Practise these four bars at 80bpm, record it, send it to me." The assignment is what carries a
lesson into the week; the submission is the student's evidence; the feedback is the teacher's response
to what they actually hear. **Timestamped feedback is the load-bearing part** — music is temporal, and
"your timing slips" is useless where "listen at 0:47, you rush the third beat" is a lesson. Written
comments on a recording, anchored to the moment, are how remote teaching works between live lessons —
and they turn a weekly 30-minute relationship into a continuous one.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: assigns from the current unit or freehand (tempo target, bars, piece, a reference recording), reviews submissions, drops timestamped annotations on the audio/video, records a short play-it-like-this reply, marks the assignment done or rolls it forward. **As Student**: sees what to do this week, records and submits from inside the practice room (06.03.04), reads/hears feedback anchored to the exact moment, resubmits. |
| Producer | None | Not visible (see the sub-domain index note on this absence). |
| Operator | Read-only | As an academy: may review assignments and feedback to **quality-assure their teachers**. This is supervision of staff, not surveillance of students — a narrow, justified read. They do not assign, do not annotate, and cannot see the student's practice diary (06.03.03). |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

An assignment is small, specific and time-boxed: what to play, how fast, how many times, by when.
Vagueness is the failure mode — "practise the piece" produces no practice and no evidence. The
platform's leverage is that assignments are **created in the room** (06.01.04 D-01), while the teacher
has just heard the problem, rather than reconstructed from memory later.

A submission is audio or video. Feedback is timestamped annotation on that submission — text, a drawn
mark, or the teacher's own recorded reply from the same moment. The asymmetry that matters: a
submission costs the student courage (they are sending a recording of themselves being bad at
something), and feedback costs the teacher unpaid time. Both frictions are real and shape everything
below.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: whether feedback between lessons is **paid**. It is real work
outside the lesson hour, and expecting it free is how teachers come to resent the product. But metering
it kills the loop the sub-domain depends on. This is unresolved and consequential.

### Happy Path

1. In the lesson (06.01.04), teacher assigns: "bars 12–20, 80bpm, record it by Thursday"
2. Student practises (06.03.03/06.03.04); the assignment is right there in the practice room
3. Student records and submits from inside the practice room — no export, no upload flow, no file picker
4. Teacher gets it, listens, drops three timestamped notes and a 20-second sung reply
5. Student hears exactly where and what — anchored, not described
6. Next lesson opens where the feedback left off; the assignment closes or rolls forward

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Student never submits | Extremely common — the base rate, not an edge case. No punishment, no red badge | Teacher: sees it, raises it in the lesson like a human. Student: **no shame UI** (DT-03) |
| Teacher never responds to a submission | The worse failure: the student took a risk and got silence, and will not submit again | Teacher: reminder. `[PENDING — Step 5]` on whether an SLA exists |
| Student submits at 2am before the lesson | Fine. Timestamped, not judged | — |
| Submission is a 40-minute unedited video | Storage and attention cost; teachers won't watch it | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — length guidance, and the practice room capturing short takes by default |
| Teacher uploads a scan of a copyrighted method book | **UGC infringement, identical in kind to any other.** A private teaching attachment feels different and is not | Routed to domain 24 + DMCA. See DT-04 |
| Student submits audio containing a copyrighted backing track | Same class; lower salience, higher volume | Routed to domain 24 |
| Minor submits video of themselves | Every submission in a minors market is a **child's likeness** — retention, access and deletion are governed by the Privacy + Safeguarding cross-cuts, not by this feature's convenience | Guardian: visibility per the delegated-authority model (domain D-03) |
| Student has no way to record (no phone, bad room) | Assignment must survive with no submission — the loop degrades to "we'll hear it next lesson" | Both: submission is optional, not a gate |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Assignment/submission fetch | Skeleton assignment card |
| Empty | No assignments | Student: "Nothing set this week" — neutral, not an accusation of either party |
| Populated | Assignment live | What/how fast/by when + a record button, right there |
| Error | Upload failed | **Retry with the take preserved locally** — losing a student's recording of themselves being bad at something is the surest way to end submissions forever |
| Partial | Submitted, awaiting feedback | "Sent to {teacher}" — no promised time unless an SLA exists |
| Reviewed | Feedback attached | Waveform with annotation markers |
| Overdue | Past the due date | `[PENDING — Step 5]`. Leaning: **no overdue state at all** (DT-03) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Timestamped feedback on audio is the feature that makes remote teaching work, not the video call | Domain expertise: this is the async half of teaching, and it is where competitors are weakest — a Zoom lesson plus a WhatsApp voice note is the actual state of the art | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a stronger claim than it first appears. The **live lesson is the commodity** (anyone can run a video call; the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut carries a hard architecture warning and won't run on the locked stack). The **async annotated feedback loop is differentiated, runs fine on the locked stack, and is where 167 of the week's 168 hours are.** If the domain had to ship one half first, the evidence points at the async half — which inverts the obvious build order. Flagged for MoSCoW rather than decided here. |
| DT-02 | This is a generic LMS assignment feature, cheaply borrowed from an education-software pattern | It looks exactly like one — assign, submit, grade | ❌ REJECTED — the primitive is wrong in three ways. An LMS submission is a **document**, graded on **correctness**, **once**. A music submission is a **performance**, assessed on **execution over time**, and **iterated** — the same 8 bars resubmitted weekly for a month is success, not failure. Correctness grading has no meaning here; there is no mark. Building on an LMS's spine would import a grade concept that actively misdescribes the domain. |
| DT-03 | Assignments need due dates, completion tracking and overdue states — the standard scaffolding | Every task product ever built; parents and academies will ask for it | ❌ REJECTED as default behaviour, and this is the feature's most contested call. **The base rate of not-submitting is enormous**, and a red overdue badge on a child's hobby converts it into homework — which is precisely how children come to quit instruments. The relationship is the enforcement mechanism (the teacher will ask on Tuesday); the product should not be a second, colder one. A due date is a *suggestion*; the overdue state is the parent's demand and the student's exit. Recording the rejection because this will be re-requested with confidence. |
| DT-04 | Assignment attachments are a private teacher–student matter and carry no copyright exposure | The intuition that private = safe; a scan sent to one child feels unlike publishing | ❌ REJECTED — a scan of a copyrighted method book uploaded to the platform is **hosting infringing material**, and "it was for one student" is not a defence that has ever worked. Music education runs almost entirely on copyrighted material (method books, graded repertoire, charts), which makes this domain's per-user infringement rate structurally higher than any other UGC surface on the platform, and its content the most reflexively shared. It routes to domain 24 and the DMCA line in `meta/constraints.md`; it is also the strongest argument for the licensed repertoire library the sub-domain's Q-01 raises — a legitimate library is the only thing that gives teachers an alternative to scanning. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Feedback is **timestamped annotation on the recording**, not detached comments | Per DT-01 — music is temporal. "Your timing slips" is not feedback; "0:47, you rush" is. |
| D-02 | **No overdue state, no completion pressure** by default | Per DT-03 — the base rate of not-submitting is enormous, and a red badge turns a hobby into homework. The relationship enforces; the product must not. |
| D-03 | Submission is **optional** — the loop degrades gracefully to "play it next lesson" | Not every student can record. A gate here excludes the poorest students first. |
| D-04 | Failed uploads **preserve the take locally** and retry | Per the Error state — losing a student's recording of themselves being bad at something ends submissions permanently. |
| D-05 | There is **no grade** | Per DT-02 — resubmitting the same 8 bars for a month is success. A mark misdescribes the domain. |
