# Feature: Assignments, Submissions & Timestamped Feedback

> **Number**: 06.03.02
> **Parent**: [Curriculum, Assignments & Practice](./06.03-curriculum-assignments-practice-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

"Practise these four bars at 80bpm, record it, send it to me." The assignment is what carries a
lesson into the week; the submission is the student's evidence; the feedback is the teacher's response
to what they actually hear. **Timestamped feedback is the load-bearing part** — music is temporal, and
"your timing slips" is useless where "listen at 0:47, you rush the third beat" is a lesson. Anchored
comments on a recording are how remote teaching works between live lessons — and they turn a weekly
30-minute relationship into a continuous one.

The loop is **call and response**: the teacher demonstrates (a reference recording captured in the
room), the student answers (a take), the teacher responds to the answer at the moment it went wrong
(an annotated reply). That is how music has been taught for four hundred years. This feature is that
exchange, instrumented — and it is the only part of the domain that operates during the 167 hours a
week the student is not in a lesson.

## Behavior

An assignment is small, specific and time-boxed: what to play, how fast, how many bars, by when.
Vagueness is the failure mode — "practise the piece" produces no practice and no evidence. The
platform's leverage is that assignments are **created in the room** (06.01.04 D-01), while the teacher
has just heard the problem, rather than reconstructed from memory later. The reference recording costs
the teacher nothing there: they demonstrated it live thirty seconds earlier (D-18).

A submission is audio or video — **declared by the assignment, not chosen by the student** (D-13),
because a teacher fixing a collapsing third finger cannot diagnose from sound. Feedback is timestamped
annotation on that take: text, a mark, or the teacher's own recorded reply anchored to the same
moment. There is no grade (D-05) and no automated assessment (D-14).

The asymmetry that governs everything below: a submission costs the student **courage** — they are
sending a recording of themselves being bad at something to an adult whose opinion they care about —
and feedback costs the teacher **unpaid time**, five minutes at a time, between back-to-back lessons,
on a phone (DT-10). Both frictions are real, and every decision in this file is an attempt to spend
neither carelessly.

### Economics — the resolved half

Between-lesson feedback is real work outside the paid hour. Two failure modes bound the design:
free-at-unbounded-volume makes teachers resent the product and stop (25 students × 1 submission/week ×
6 minutes ≈ 2.5 unpaid hours weekly); metered-per-interaction makes the student hesitate at the exact
moment courage is scarcest, and the loop dies. The resolution is to separate **the student's
experience** from **the teacher's load**:

- **The student never sees a price, a meter, a counter or a quota at submission time** (D-06). Not a
  soft one, not a friendly one. A twelve-year-old deciding whether their take is worth a credit is a
  loop that has already broken.
- **The teacher bounds their own load by declaring a feedback stance** on the relationship (D-07):
  `included` (default), `in-lesson-only` ("bring it to your lesson — I don't do notes between"), or
  `included, up to N submissions per week`. Beyond the bound, the submission is still **accepted** and
  queues to the next lesson (D-08). It is never rejected — rejection is the one outcome that ends
  submissions permanently.

What remains genuinely open is **monetization structure**, not behavior: whether async feedback is
priced into lesson packages (06.01.02), sold as a distinct product, or carried as the platform's
relationship glue. That is the owner's call and it is Q-01. The behavior above holds under any answer.

### Happy Path

1. In the lesson room (06.01.04), the teacher hears the student rush bars 12–20 and demonstrates it correctly at 80bpm.
2. Still in the room, the teacher assigns: bars 12–20, 80bpm, **audio**, by Thursday — and taps once to attach the demonstration they just played as the **reference recording** (D-18). Total teacher cost: one tap plus a due date.
3. The student's practice room (06.03.04) now opens on the assignment: the bar range, the target tempo pre-loaded into the metronome, the teacher's reference recording, and a record button. No file picker, no upload flow, no export (CX-04).
4. The student practises against the click. The session becomes a practice log entry as a byproduct (CX-01) — they do not report anything.
5. The student records a take. They record four more. Takes are **local and private** until one is chosen — a student who cannot discard a bad take will not record at all.
6. The student picks take 3 and submits. The system shows "Sent to {teacher}" and, if the teacher has declared one, their stance ("{Teacher} usually replies within a few days") — an expectation the teacher set, never a promise the platform made (D-08).
7. The take carries its **capture timestamp**, not its upload timestamp — an offline student submitting on Friday for a Thursday take is not late (edge case 11).
8. The teacher gets one notification. Between lessons, on their phone, they scrub the take. The waveform shows the click grid the student recorded against — a ruler, not a verdict (D-14).
9. The teacher drops three annotations: text at 0:12, a mark at 0:31, and at 0:47 they hold the record button and sing the phrase correctly for twenty seconds. Because the take was recorded to a known click at a declared tempo, each annotation is **labelled with its bar number** as well as its time (D-12) — the teacher writes "bar 14", which is the only unit they think in.
10. The student opens it and hears exactly where and what — anchored, not described. The reply plays from the moment it refers to.
11. The student resubmits on Saturday. Take 3 and its annotations are **retained, not replaced** (D-16) — the teacher's work is never destroyed, and the pair of takes is the actual evidence of progress.
12. Next lesson opens on the exchange. The assignment closes, or rolls forward with the same eight bars, which is success and not failure (DT-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| 1 | Student never submits | **The base rate, not an edge case.** No punishment, no red badge, no state change, no reordering | Teacher: sees it, raises it on Tuesday like a human. Student: **no shame UI** (DT-03, D-02) |

| 15 | Teacher closes or rolls the assignment while the student is mid-take | **The late take is accepted** and attaches to the closed assignment (CX-04). Rejecting it punishes the student for the teacher's timing | Student: submits normally. Teacher: "A take arrived for an assignment you closed" |
| 16 | Teacher edits the assignment after a submission (80bpm → 70bpm) | The submission stays bound to the assignment **as it was when submitted** — same principle as 06.03.01 D-04. | 17 | Two teachers annotate one submission (cover teacher, academy second opinion) | Both allowed concurrently. | 18 | Annotation anchored past the end of the media (clock drift, truncated take) | Clamped to the final frame and flagged, never dropped. Losing a teacher's note to arithmetic is the same class of loss as D-04 | Teacher: "This note was moved to the end of the take" |

| 21 | Teacher's reply is longer than the student's take | Allowed and expected — teachers demonstrate, and a 90-second demo answering a 20-second take is a good lesson | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Assignment/submission fetch | Skeleton assignment card — the card's shape, not a spinner |
| Empty | No assignments set | Student: "Nothing set this week" — neutral, an accusation of neither party. Teacher: their student list, one tap from assigning |
| Populated | Assignment live | What / how fast / which bars / by when, the teacher's reference recording, and a record button — in the practice room, not a separate destination |
| Recording | Student holds record | Elapsed time against the assignment's target duration; the click if the assignment set a tempo. Auto-stops at 10:00 (edge 4) |
| Queued | Take submitted, transfer pending or offline | "Not sent yet — will send when you're back online." The take is visibly present and cannot be lost (D-04, edge 10) |
| Error | Upload failed after 3 retries | **Retry with the take preserved locally** — losing a student's recording of themselves being bad at something is the surest way to end submissions forever. The take stays in the outbox until it sends |
| Partial | Submitted, awaiting feedback | "Sent to {teacher}" + the teacher's declared stance if they set one. **No platform-promised time** — the platform does not make commitments on a human's unpaid evening (D-08) |
| Processing | Media received, not yet playable | Teacher only: "Still processing — we'll ping you." Annotation disabled with a reason, not a dead spinner (edge 19) |
| Reviewed | Feedback attached | Waveform with annotation markers; the click grid if one was recorded; bar labels where the tempo is known (D-12) |
| Closed | Teacher marked done or rolled forward | The exchange stays readable to both. Rolling forward reopens the same bars — presented as continuation, never as a repeat or a retry |
| Gated | Teacher's safeguarding check lapsed | Held, not deleted. Specific reason to the teacher; a neutral one to the student and guardian (edge 23, D-09) |
| **Overdue** | **Does not exist** | The due date renders as "by Thursday" before Thursday and **as "by Thursday" after Thursday** — same words, same colour, same position, same sort order, no badge, no count. The assignment stays open until the teacher closes or rolls it. This is a specific, testable non-behavior (D-02, domain D-04) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Timestamped feedback on audio is the feature that makes remote teaching work, not the video call | Domain expertise: this is the async half of teaching, and it is where competitors are weakest — a Zoom lesson plus a WhatsApp voice note is the actual state of the art | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a stronger claim than it first appears. The **live lesson is the commodity** (anyone can run a video call; the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut carries a hard architecture warning and won't run on the locked stack). The **async annotated feedback loop is differentiated, runs fine on the locked stack, and is where 167 of the week's 168 hours are.** If the domain had to ship one half first, the evidence points at the async half — which inverts the obvious build order. Flagged for MoSCoW rather than decided here (Q-02). |
| DT-02 | This is a generic LMS assignment feature, cheaply borrowed from an education-software pattern | It looks exactly like one — assign, submit, grade | ❌ REJECTED — the primitive is wrong in three ways. An LMS submission is a **document**, graded on **correctness**, **once**. A music submission is a **performance**, assessed on **execution over time**, and **iterated** — the same 8 bars resubmitted weekly for a month is success, not failure. Correctness grading has no meaning here; there is no mark. Building on an LMS's spine would import a grade concept that actively misdescribes the domain. |
| DT-03 | Assignments need due dates, completion tracking and overdue states — the standard scaffolding | Every task product ever built; parents and academies will ask for it | ❌ REJECTED as default behaviour, and this is the feature's most contested call. **The base rate of not-submitting is enormous**, and a red overdue badge on a child's hobby converts it into homework — which is precisely how children come to quit instruments. The relationship is the enforcement mechanism (the teacher will ask on Tuesday); the product should not be a second, colder one. A due date is a *suggestion*; the overdue state is the parent's demand and the student's exit. Recording the rejection because this will be re-requested with confidence. Ratified domain-wide as D-04. |
| DT-04 | Assignment attachments are a private teacher–student matter and carry no copyright exposure | The intuition that private = safe; a scan sent to one child feels unlike publishing | ❌ REJECTED — a scan of a copyrighted method book uploaded to the platform is **hosting infringing material**, and "it was for one student" is not a defence that has ever worked. Music education runs almost entirely on copyrighted material (method books, graded repertoire, charts), which makes this domain's per-user infringement rate structurally higher than any other UGC surface on the platform, and its content the most reflexively shared. It routes to domain 24 and the DMCA line in `meta/constraints.md`; it is also the strongest argument for the licensed repertoire library the sub-domain's Q-01 raises — a legitimate library is the only thing that gives teachers an alternative to scanning. |
| DT-05 | The async feedback channel inherits the lesson room's safeguarding protections | The reasonable assumption that a gate applied to the domain's flagship surface covers the domain | ✅ CONFIRMED AS A GAP — **it inherits nothing, and this is the failure mode nobody designed for.** 06.01.04 D-02 makes the *room* fail closed. This feature is an adult recording their voice, addressed to a named child, delivered privately, at 11pm, with no chaperone, no session boundary, no scheduled window and no recording norm — every property that makes the room governable is absent here. The cross-cut's own remit explicitly names "chaperone/recording norms for **1:1 lessons**"; an async voice note to a child is a 1:1 lesson artifact that is not a lesson. The domain has four named enforcement points (domain D-05) and **this channel is not one of them** — it should be a fifth. Resolved as D-09; escalated in Q-05 because adding an enforcement point is a cross-cut owner's call, not a feature's. |
| DT-06 | Guardian visibility of this feature follows the same rule as the practice diary (06.03.03) — one privacy posture for the sub-domain | Symmetry; both concern a minor's activity, both were drilled together | ❌ REJECTED — they are opposite, and the reason is the sharpest privacy distinction in the sub-domain. The **practice diary is the child's own record of their own effort**; an adult reading it is surveillance, which is why 06.03.03 D-05 walls it off. The **feedback exchange is a communication channel between an adult and a child**, and an adult↔minor channel that a guardian cannot see is the definition of what safeguarding exists to prevent. The identical-looking inference "protect the child's privacy" points in opposite directions depending on whether the artifact is *the child's* or *between the child and an adult*. Resolved as D-11. |
| DT-07 | Annotating at a clock timestamp is sufficient — "0:47" locates the problem | Every media-annotation tool ever built anchors to time; it is the obvious primitive | ✅ CONFIRMED BUT INSUFFICIENT — **the timestamp is what the platform can compute; the bar number is the only unit the domain speaks.** No music teacher has ever said "at forty-seven seconds you rush." They say "bar 14." Seconds are a transport artifact; bars are the music. But the mapping time→bar is only sound when the take was played to a known click at a declared tempo — which is exactly what the practice room's metronome supplies when the assignment sets a target (CX-04). Otherwise it is guesswork, and a confidently wrong bar number is worse than an honest timestamp. Resolved as D-12: anchor to time always, label with bars only when the tempo is known and the take was recorded to the click. This makes 06.03.04's metronome **load-bearing for this feature's feedback quality**, not merely a practice aid. |
| DT-08 | Audio is the natural medium for a music submission; video is a heavier nice-to-have | The feature's own framing led with audio, and audio is cheaper in every dimension | ✅ CONFIRMED AS AN INVERSION — **for the largest teaching instruments the diagnosis is visual, and the default is backwards.** A piano teacher fixing a collapsing third finger, a guitar teacher fixing a fretting-hand wrist, a violin teacher fixing a bow arm, a drum teacher fixing grip — none of these can be heard. They are seen. Audio is sufficient for timing, intonation, dynamics and phrasing; it is *diagnostically blind* to technique, which is the majority of what a beginner needs fixed and the entirety of what causes injury. The honest specification is not a global default but a per-assignment declaration: the teacher knows whether this week is a timing problem or a hands problem, and they are deciding it in the room anyway. Resolved as D-13. Cost acknowledged: it raises the volume of a child's video on the platform, which sharpens Q-03 rather than answering it. |
| DT-09 | The platform should analyse the submission and pre-annotate wrong notes, timing and pitch — the teacher arrives at a marked-up take | Technically real (Yousician, Skoove, Smart Music all do it); it directly attacks the teacher's unpaid-time problem, which is the feature's binding constraint; it will be requested with total confidence | ❌ REJECTED — on **three independent grounds**, any one of which is sufficient. (a) **Accuracy**: it works on a solo, clean, in-tune, single-line instrument in a quiet room, and a twelve-year-old's phone in a room with a TV on is the actual input. Wrong the first time it matters is worse than absent. (b) **It is a grade wearing a lab coat**: "73% of notes correct" is a mark, and D-05 says there is no mark; DT-02 established that correctness grading misdescribes performance. (c) **It contradicts a settled sibling**: 06.03.05 D-03 says the platform measures **coverage, never musicianship** — and auto-assessing a performance is measuring musicianship, at the one place the sub-domain is least defended. **The nuance that survives**: a *ruler* is not a *verdict*. Showing the waveform against the click grid the student actually recorded to is non-judgemental instrumentation — it helps the teacher see where the rush is and asserts nothing about whether it was bad. Visualization allowed; assessment rejected (D-14). |
| DT-10 | The loop's fragile side is the student's — courage, effort, a twelve-year-old's attention | The Behavior section's own asymmetry; it is the intuitive read | ✅ CONFIRMED, BUT INVERTED IN PRIORITY — **the loop breaks at the teacher's phone, and that failure is 25× larger.** A private music teacher teaches 5–8 back-to-back hours, in a studio or in students' homes, with five minutes between lessons. They review submissions standing up, one-handed, on a phone, in a corridor. A feedback surface that assumes a keyboard, a mouse and a desk will not be used — and when one teacher stops, twenty-five students stop, where a student quitting costs one. **The core teacher action — hold a button, sing the phrase correctly, release, it lands at 0:47 — must work one-handed on a phone, interruptible by an incoming call, with mic permission surviving the app being backgrounded.** This collides directly with a locked constraint: `meta/constraints.md` declares responsive web as the **only** surface, with native/PWA an explicit open question. This feature is a concrete, load-bearing argument in that question, and it is on the supply side of the domain's economics. Raised as Q-06. |
| DT-11 | Timestamped annotation on media is an Education primitive, owned by this feature | It is this feature's headline capability and its name is in the title | ❌ REJECTED as ownership — **it is the same mechanism as a producer's revision notes on a mix (07), a client's notes on a service deliverable (05), and a bandmate's comment on a demo (07/08).** SoundCloud comments, Frame.io and every mix-review tool in existence are this exact object. This is not aspirational: the teacher demonstrated it live thirty seconds earlier in the room (06.01.04 D-01), so capturing it costs one tap at the moment of zero marginal effort — which is the platform's own capture-at-source thesis (`meta/problem-statement.md`) applied to pedagogy rather than to splits. Resolved as D-18, and deliberately **offered, never required** (edge 35) — demanding it would lose the notebook teachers who are the domain's supply, which is 06.03.01 DT-02's whole finding. |
| DT-13 | Students will pass off others' recordings as their own, and the platform needs a check | The standard integrity reflex; every education product has an academic-honesty story | ❌ REJECTED as a problem worth solving — **the victim is the only detector, and they detect it perfectly, for free, in four bars.** The teacher has heard this specific student play every week for a year; a stranger's recording is not a subtle forgery to them, it is instantly obvious. More fundamentally, DT-02 established there is no grade — so there is nothing to cheat *for*. Academic dishonesty requires a mark to steal; this feature deliberately has none, which is the cleanest structural defence available and it was already built. Distinguish sharply from DT-04, which is a real and unrelated exposure: **the platform's risk in a submission is copyright, not authenticity.** |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **[06.01.04 Lesson Delivery & Session Record](../06.01-lesson-booking-packages-delivery/)** — assignments **and their reference recordings** are created in the room (06.01.04 D-01), the capture-at-source principle the platform's thesis rests on. Trigger dependency both ways: the room creates the assignment, and the room is the **backstop for unreviewed submissions** (D-08) — an unreviewed take is the first item at the next lesson.
- Touches **06.03.01 Curriculum** — assignments draw from the current unit but never require one (06.03.01 DT-02, CX-03). Shared principle: an edited template does not retroactively alter a live instance (06.03.01 D-04), which this feature mirrors for a submitted take against an edited assignment (edge 16).
- Touches **06.03.03 Practice Logging** — the assignment is what the student practises; the log is what they did. The gap is the teacher's core diagnostic (CX-02) and must stay diagnostic, never evidential (06.03.03 D-06). **Opposite privacy postures on guardian access** — see DT-06/D-11; a spec writer reading both files must not unify them.
- Touches **06.03.04 Practice Room Tools** — submission happens **from inside** the room, with no export step (CX-04). **New dependency found at depth**: the room's metronome is load-bearing for this feature's feedback quality, because bar labelling on annotations is only sound when the take was recorded to a known click (DT-07, D-12). The room's tools are optional (06.03.04 D-02) — so bar labels degrade to plain timestamps, never to guesses.
- Touches **06.03.05 Progress Reports** — submission history is a report input that proxies **confidence, not ability** (CX-07); the shyest student submits least. D-14's rejection of auto-assessment is the same argument as 06.03.05 D-03's coverage-not-musicianship rule, arriving independently at a second surface.
- Touches **[06.02 Teacher Discovery, Profiles & Trials](../06.02-teacher-discovery-profiles-trials/)** — the teacher's **feedback stance** (D-07) is a supply-side commitment a prospective student/parent would reasonably want visible before booking. Whether it belongs on the public teacher profile is 06.02's call, not this feature's.
- Touches **[06.01.02 Lesson Packages & Credits](../06.01-lesson-booking-packages-delivery/)** — the monetization half of Q-01. If async feedback is priced into a package, the package is where it is priced; if it is a separate product, this is where that product's boundary is drawn. Note Q-07 of the domain index (are credits money or entitlements?) applies to any answer that makes feedback consume one.
- Touches **[06.10 Academy & Multi-Teacher Operations](../06.10-academy-multi-teacher-operations.md)** — permission boundary. The Operator's QA read is scoped to lessons delivered under the academy and **survives the teacher leaving** (edge 25); it never follows the teacher to their private students, whose identity stays theirs.
- Touches **[07 Music Projects & Collaboration](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)** and **[05 Services Marketplace](../../05-services-marketplace/)** — **shared mechanism, not shared feature**: timestamped annotation on media is a producer's revision note and a client's deliverable note, identically shaped (DT-11). Returned for global CX synthesis (Q-07). Building it inside Education means building it three more times.
- Touches **[24 Trust, Safety & Disputes](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)** — three distinct routes: DMCA on attachments (edge 6) and takes (edge 7); minor→adult abusive content (edge 30); disintermediation attempts in feedback text (edge 31).
- Consumes **Safeguarding & Minor Protection** (global cross-cut) — **and asks it for a new enforcement point.** DT-05 finds the async channel inherits none of the room's protections; D-09 gates it at the same strength and fails closed. The domain's four enforcement points (domain D-05) should be five. Escalated as Q-05 — a cross-cut's remit is not a feature's to extend.
- Consumes **Roles, Permissions & Delegated Authority (Enforcement)** (global cross-cut) — the guardian's full visibility of the exchange (D-11) is delegated authority, per domain D-03. Also owns the 90-day access wind-down when a teaching relationship ends (edge 24) and the two-direction multi-hyphenate inbox (edge 27).
- Consumes **Privacy, Consent & Data Portability** (global cross-cut) — submissions are recordings of children at volume; retention and deletion are governed there (Q-03). D-13's video default raises that volume materially.
- Consumes **Media Handling & Audio Playback** (global cross-cut) — take capture, storage, waveform, click-grid overlay, playback, processing states (edge 19). The ratified map routes heavy transcode/waveform/DSP to `/create-prd-architecture` as a **hard stack constraint**; D-13 (video-by-default for technique instruments) makes that constraint materially heavier and should be carried forward as a sizing input, not a footnote.
- Consumes **Offline & Local Queue** (global cross-cut) — takes queue locally and survive offline (D-04, edge 10); capture time is authoritative over upload time (edge 11); **the teacher's annotations get the same guarantee** (edge 34), which is less obvious and equally load-bearing.
- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** (global cross-cut) — new submission (coalesced to one per assignment per 6h, edge 33), new feedback, and exactly one teacher nudge at 72h that never repeats and never escalates (edge 2). **Deliberately absent**: any notification to the student about a teacher's lateness, and any "student didn't practise" alert (sub-domain R-04).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Feedback is **timestamped annotation on the recording**, not detached comments | Per DT-01 — music is temporal. "Your timing slips" is not feedback; "0:47, you rush" is. |
| D-02 | **No overdue state, no completion pressure** by default | Per DT-03 — the base rate of not-submitting is enormous, and a red badge turns a hobby into homework. The relationship enforces; the product must not. Concretely: after the due date, nothing changes — same words, colour, position, sort order (States). Ratified domain-wide as D-04. |
| D-03 | Submission is **optional** — the loop degrades gracefully to "play it next lesson" | Not every student can record. A gate here excludes the poorest students first. |
| D-04 | Failed uploads **preserve the take locally** and retry — 3 attempts at 2s / 8s / 32s, then the room's outbox, retried on next open, never expired | Per the Error state — losing a student's recording of themselves being bad at something ends submissions permanently. Scope: this protects takes **the product created and now holds**. A file still on the student's device is not at risk, which is why edge 5 may reject pre-upload. |
| D-05 | There is **no grade** | Per DT-02 — resubmitting the same 8 bars for a month is success. A mark misdescribes the domain. It is also, per DT-13, the structural defence against submission fraud: nothing to cheat for. |
| D-06 | **The student never sees a price, meter, counter or quota at submission time** | Resolves the paid-feedback question at the behavior layer. A twelve-year-old weighing whether their take is worth a credit is a loop that has already broken — and courage is scarcest at exactly that moment. Holds under any monetization structure the owner picks (Q-01). |
| D-07 | The **teacher declares a feedback stance** per teaching relationship: `included` (default), `in-lesson-only`, or `included, up to N per week` | The teacher's load is bounded by the teacher, not by the platform metering the student. 25 students × 1 submission × 6 min ≈ 2.5 unpaid hours/week is a real number, and unbounded free work is how the supply side quits. |
| D-08 | **No platform SLA on feedback.** Expectation comes from the teacher's stance; the backstop is the next lesson | A platform promise on a human's unpaid evening is unenforceable and manufactures failure states. Beyond a teacher's declared bound, a submission is **accepted and queued to the lesson** — never rejected. One private teacher nudge at 72h, never repeated, never escalated, never shown to the student (edge 2). |
| D-09 | The async feedback channel is **safeguarding-gated at the same strength as the lesson room, and fails closed** | Per DT-05 — the channel is an adult recording their voice for a named child, privately, at 11pm, with none of the room's chaperone/session/scheduling properties. 06.01.04 D-02 gated the room; nobody gated this. Failure holds pending items, never deletes; specific reason to the teacher, neutral to student and guardian. |
| D-10 | **Submissions cannot be forwarded** | Per D-09's logic. A child's recording leaving the relationship it was made in is a safeguarding failure regardless of intent. The legitimate second-opinion need is served by the academy's scoped QA read (edge 25). |
| D-11 | A guardian sees the **feedback exchange in full**; a guardian sees **none of the practice diary** | Per DT-06 — the diary is the child's own record (surveillance to read); the exchange is an adult↔minor channel (unsafe *not* to be visible). The opposite posture to 06.03.03 D-05, deliberately. Where this stops as the minor approaches 18 is Q-04. |
| D-12 | Annotations anchor to **time always**; **bar labels only** when the take was recorded to the room's click at a declared tempo | Per DT-07 — bars are the domain's unit, seconds are the transport's. Inferring a bar without a known click is guesswork, and a confident wrong bar number is worse than an honest timestamp. Degrades cleanly to plain timestamps (06.03.04 D-02 makes the tools optional). |
| D-13 | The **assignment declares its medium** — audio or video — defaulting by instrument and by what is being fixed | Per DT-08 — technique is diagnosed visually and cannot be heard. The teacher knows in the room whether this week is a timing problem or a hands problem. Cost: more child video, which sharpens Q-03 and the Media cross-cut's sizing. |
| D-14 | **No automated assessment of a submission.** Non-judgemental visualization (waveform, the click grid actually recorded to) is allowed and useful | Per DT-09 — rejected on accuracy, on D-05's no-grade rule, and on 06.03.05 D-03's coverage-not-musicianship rule, independently. A ruler is not a verdict. |
| D-15 | **Length caps**: in-room takes auto-stop at 10:00 with the take preserved; out-of-room uploads over 20:00 are rejected **at selection, before transfer**, with an actionable message | Teachers will not watch 40 minutes, and storing it costs real money. Rejecting before transfer loses the student nothing — the file is still theirs. Rejecting *after* would be D-04's failure. |
| D-16 | A resubmission creates a **new take**; prior takes and their annotations are **retained, never replaced** | The teacher's annotation work is never destroyed by the student's next attempt (edge 13), and the pair of takes is the actual evidence of progress — which is what DT-02 says this domain measures. Resubmission is unlimited; the teacher's notification is coalesced (edge 33). |
| D-17 | Teacher access to a student's submissions ends **90 days after the last delivered lesson**; the student keeps theirs | Resolves the consequence CX-02 left open ("the teacher's visibility ends when the relationship does — an unresolved consequence"). 90 days covers a summer break (~10 weeks), so a returning student's teacher has not lost the thread. The work is the student's; the access was the relationship's. |
| D-18 | The **reference recording is a first-class assignment field**, captured in the room — **offered, never required** | Per DT-12 — students imitate, they do not read. It costs one tap at the moment of zero marginal effort (the teacher just played it). Required, it would lose the notebook teachers who are the domain's supply (06.03.01 DT-02). |
| D-19 | The teacher's review-and-annotate surface is **phone-first, one-handed, interruptible** | Per DT-10 — the loop breaks at the teacher's phone in a corridor between lessons, and one teacher lost is 25 students lost. This is a load-bearing input to the open surface question in `meta/constraints.md`, not a styling preference (Q-06). |
