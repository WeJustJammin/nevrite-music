## Overview

Running a music school: a roster of teachers, a term calendar, rooms, students who belong to the
school rather than to a teacher, house rates and revenue share, and a credit liability that survives
any individual teacher leaving. Music academies are ubiquitous — most towns have one, most have
between 2 and 20 teachers, and all of them run on spreadsheets and a wall calendar.

**The strategic case is supply-side cold start**: signing one academy brings twelve teachers and two
hundred students in a single motion, in one city — which is exactly the local density the ratified map
says the platform needs ("200 users in one city feels alive, 20,000 scattered globally feels dead").
No other node in this domain acquires more than one person at a time.

## Behavior

An academy is an **organisation entity** (domain 01 owns the entity model and membership graph) with
a teaching-operations layer on top. That layer is:

- **Roster** — which teachers teach here, on what terms, under which house rates
- **Term calendar** — the hard bound on what teachers can publish (06.01.01's term bounds exist for this)
- **Rooms** — allocation to lesson blocks; the Operator's actual inventory
- **House rate card & revenue share** — what students pay, what the teacher keeps
- **Credit liability** — the number that matters when a teacher leaves mid-pack (06.01.02)
- **Cohort reporting** — are our students progressing, which teachers' students progress

The seam that must be right: **the student belongs to the academy; the teacher's identity belongs to
the teacher**. Get this backwards and either the academy loses its business when a teacher leaves, or
the teacher loses their career when they change job — and the second is a direct violation of the
platform's central promise (D-03).

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: nearly all of the operational detail. This is a `[SURFACE]`
node with a real strategic case and a genuine persona problem, and elaborating it before that problem
is resolved would be building on sand.

### Happy Path

1. Academy registers as an organisation (domain 01) and adds a teaching-operations layer
2. Invites its teachers; each keeps their own WeJammin identity and joins the roster
3. Sets a term calendar, house rates, revenue share and a floor cancellation policy (06.01.03)
4. Students enrol **with the academy**; packs are academy-scoped (06.01.02 DT-03's resolution)
5. Teachers teach; the academy sees occupancy, liability and outcomes
6. A teacher leaves; their students' credits and continuity survive, because the pack was the academy's

| Academy is one person | Extremely common. The whole layer must be optional and invisible | No academy layer unless asked for |
| Academy runs group classes | Their best product; fills perishable off-peak inventory (06.05) | 06.05 |

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |

| DT-01 | An academy is a **persona gap** — the sweep named "school/academy" as one of the domain's five provisional personas, and the ratified 4 (D-19) has no slot for it | `meta/personas.md` D-19; the sweep's provisional persona list for this domain; the domain index's original Role Matrix, which had a `school/academy` column | ✅ CONFIRMED, ❌ **and it resolves without a new persona** — which is the useful part. An academy is an **Operator**: `meta/personas.md` defines that persona as one who "sells time and space rather than creative output", whose inventory is "perishable and physically constrained (an unsold Tuesday is gone forever)" and who is "frequently *both* buyer and seller". This is the domain's answer to its own share of the persona-coverage question, and it is a *negative* finding (nothing needs to change) — which is worth stating explicitly, because the two *positive* persona findings in this domain (the parent, 06.02.01 DT-03 / 06.03.05 DT-01) do need something to change. |
| DT-02 | The academy's real requirement is **teacher lock-in**, and the platform must refuse it | Reasoning from the persona's fear: an academy's entire commercial anxiety is a teacher leaving with a book of students. Any product they adopt, they will want to adopt *for that* | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the feature's defining tension. What an academy would most value is a system where the teacher's students, profile and reputation are the academy's property. **The platform cannot give them that** without destroying its own central promise: `meta/personas.md` says a musician's pain is that "nothing accumulates, and every new client requires re-proving who they are". An academy-owned teacher profile is that failure, re-created, by us. So the platform gives the academy the things it can legitimately hold — the **student relationship**, the **credit liability**, the **room**, the **brand** — and refuses the thing it wants most. That refusal may cost adoption, and it is the right call. It also means the pitch to an academy must be about **operations** (the spreadsheet dies), never about control. |
| DT-03 | This is a low-priority admin feature — an individual-teacher product is the domain's real market | The candidate list's implicit view: nine capabilities, all for individuals | ❌ REJECTED on **cold-start grounds**, which inverts its apparent priority. Every other node in domain 06 acquires **one person at a time**: one teacher publishes a page, one student books a trial. An academy is a single sale that brings **twelve teachers and two hundred students in one city** — the exact local density the ratified map calls "the only tractable cold-start strategy". It also brings the domain's most reliable supply (professional teachers with existing books, who are the patient beginner-teachers 06.02.02 DT-02 says the domain most needs and most struggles to attract). The honest counter: academies are conservative, slow-moving, run on habit, and — per DT-02 — will be told no on the thing they want most. Long sales cycle, high value. That is a MoSCoW argument, not a reason to have omitted it from the map. |
