# Feature: Exam Board Alignment

> **Number**: 06.09
> **Parent**: [Education, Lessons & Mentorship](./education-lessons-mentorship-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Mapping teaching to the graded exam syllabi that structure a large share of music tuition worldwide —
ABRSM, Trinity, Rockschool, RCM, AMEB. In the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Hong Kong and
much of the Commonwealth, **grades are the spine of instrumental teaching**: parents buy "Grade 5",
teachers plan terms around exam dates, and progress is measured in grades because there is nothing
else to measure it with.

This is the domain's most **geographically lopsided** feature. It is close to essential in the UK and
close to meaningless in the US, where the graded-exam tradition never took hold. That asymmetry is
the feature's defining fact, and it is a MoSCoW argument rather than a design one.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: maps curriculum units (06.03.01) to a graded syllabus, tracks exam readiness across the required components (pieces, scales, sight-reading, aural — a candidate fails on the component they ignored), plans terms around entry deadlines. **As Student**: sees what the grade requires and how much of it they have, which is the only progress measure in this domain that is external and objective. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. Grades are irrelevant to production work and always have been. |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: **exam prep is often the academy's core product**, and the entry logistics (registering candidates, deadlines, fees, sessions) are real administrative work they do today in spreadsheets. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The feature is, honestly, **a taxonomy and a calendar**:

- **The syllabus** — per board, per instrument, per grade: required components, repertoire lists, scale requirements, technical work
- **Mapping** — curriculum units and repertoire tagged against syllabus requirements
- **Readiness** — which components are covered, which are not, and how close the candidate is
- **The calendar** — entry deadlines and exam sessions, which are hard external dates that reorganise a whole term

Entering a candidate is done **with the board**, not with WeJammin. Whether the platform integrates
with entry systems or merely tracks the dates is Q-02 — and the honest expectation is that these are
institutions with limited public APIs, so "tracks the dates" may be the ceiling.

`[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]`: syllabus content sourcing. Boards publish syllabi
and revise them periodically; whether the platform licenses, transcribes or links to them is
unresolved and carries a copyright question (DT-03). D-05 fixes the ceiling — reference, never host —
so the open corner is *how* references are stored and refreshed, not *whether* to host.

### Happy Path

1. Teacher sets a student's goal: "ABRSM Grade 5 Piano, summer session"
2. System loads the syllabus: 3 pieces from set lists, scales, sight-reading, aural
3. Curriculum units map to requirements; the gaps are visible
4. Readiness view shows what's covered and what has been quietly avoided for six months
5. Entry deadline appears in the term plan, well before it is a crisis
6. Student sits the exam with the board; the result is recorded

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Teacher doesn't use grades | The majority worldwide, and nearly all of the US. Feature is simply absent, not "unconfigured" | Nothing. **No prompt, no nag** — a US rock guitar teacher must never see this |
| Board revises the syllabus | Happens on a cycle (typically every few years); mapped units go stale mid-teaching | Versioned syllabi (D-04); a student mid-grade stays on the syllabus they started, new students get the new version. `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth]`: the migration UX for a teacher who wants to move a candidate forward voluntarily |
| Student misses the entry deadline | A hard external date with real consequences — a term of work lands in the next session | Deadline warnings well ahead. **This is the feature's single most useful function** |
| Candidate is ready on pieces, not on scales | The classic failure. Everyone practises pieces and avoids scales; the exam does not care | Readiness **by component**, never a single percentage |
| Student takes an exam elsewhere | Result recorded, not verified — the platform has no relationship with the board | Self-reported, and honestly labelled as such |
| Different boards, same instrument | Genuinely incompatible taxonomies. Trinity Grade 5 ≠ ABRSM Grade 5 | Per-board, never normalized. See DT-02 |
| Syllabus repertoire is copyrighted | Set lists reference published editions the student must buy. **This is the licensed-repertoire gap again** (06.03 Q-01) | Reference the requirement; do not host the music |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the only **external, objective** progress standard the domain has access to — which makes it far more structurally important than "a nice-to-have taxonomy" | 06.03.05 DT-02 (no honest skill model exists; every platform-derived proxy is wrong) + 06.08 DT-02 (the platform cannot certify ability) | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it reframes the feature entirely. Two separate features independently concluded the platform **cannot measure musicianship**: practice hours measure effort, submissions measure confidence, completion measures watching. An exam grade is the one measure that is *external, examined by a human, and universally understood by the parent paying the bill*. **This is why exam boards have survived for over a century and why a platform cannot displace them** — and it means this feature is not "exam-board support", it is the domain's **only working answer to "is my child getting better?"**. That is a much stronger justification than the candidate list implies, and it is exactly the question 06.03.05 exists to answer and 06.08 fails to. |
| DT-02 | Grades can be normalized across boards into a common level scale, so the domain has one "level" concept | The tidy engineering instinct, and 06.02.03's matching genuinely wants a level taxonomy | ❌ REJECTED — the boards are **not commensurable** and pretending otherwise is worse than having no scale. Trinity Grade 5 and ABRSM Grade 5 differ in what they require and how they weight it; Rockschool tests different things entirely; the traditions are pedagogically distinct, not differently-calibrated versions of one thing. A normalized "level 5" would be a fabrication that misinforms matching (06.02.03), misinforms parents, and would be resented by teachers who know the difference. Per-board, always. **Consequence for 06.02.03**: its "level is not a scalar" problem (06.02.03 DT-02) does not get solved here — exam boards give a rigorous handle only *within* a tradition, and only for the traditions that have one. |
| DT-03 | The feature is cheap because syllabus data is public | Boards publish their syllabi openly; it looks like a scraping-and-tagging job | ❌ REJECTED, on two counts. (1) **Copyright**: a syllabus is a published work, and its set lists reference specific copyrighted editions. Reproducing it wholesale is infringement; linking to it is legal and much less useful. This is the third distinct route the licensed-repertoire gap (06.03 Q-01) has entered this domain — assignments (06.03.02 DT-04), courses (06.04.01 DT-03), and now syllabi. **Three independent arrivals means it is not a feature-level concern; it is a domain-level precondition.** (2) **Maintenance**: syllabi are revised on a cycle, per board, per instrument, per grade. That is a permanent content-operations cost, not a one-off import, and nobody budgets for it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Per-board, never normalized** | Per DT-02 — the boards are not commensurable, and a synthetic common scale would be a fabrication that misinforms matching and parents alike. |
| D-02 | Readiness is reported **by component**, never as one percentage | Candidates fail on the component they avoided. A single number hides the only thing the view exists to reveal. |
| D-03 | The feature is **absent**, not empty, for teachers who don't use grades | Most of the world, and nearly all of the US. A prompt here is noise in someone's daily tool. |
| D-04 | Syllabi are **versioned**; a student mid-grade stays on the syllabus they started | Boards revise on a cycle, and moving a candidate's goalposts mid-term is unacceptable. |
| D-05 | The platform **references** syllabus repertoire; it does not host it | Per DT-03 — set lists point at copyrighted published editions. |
