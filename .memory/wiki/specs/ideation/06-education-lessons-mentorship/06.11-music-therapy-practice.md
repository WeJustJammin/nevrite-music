## Overview

Practice-management tooling for **licensed music therapists**: client caseloads, session plans,
clinical notes, outcome measures, treatment goals, and insurance/health-system billing. Music therapy
is a real, credentialed clinical profession (HCPC-registered in the UK; board-certified MT-BC in the
US) working in hospitals, hospices, schools and dementia care.

It is adjacent to this domain by the word "music" and by nothing else. **This file recommends `wont`**,
and sets out why in enough detail that the recommendation can be checked rather than trusted.

## Role Lens

Elaborating this would be malpractice in both senses. Clinical documentation has requirements set by
regulators and professional bodies, not by product design, and a plausible-looking spec written by an
agent with no clinical qualification is worse than an honest blank — someone downstream might build it.

What is known and sufficient for the MoSCoW decision:

- Caseload and client records — **health data**
- Session plans and clinical notes — **health data, and legally privileged in some jurisdictions**
- Outcome measures against treatment goals — **clinical instruments, often themselves licensed**
- Billing to insurers or health systems — **HIPAA-covered transactions in the US**

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Music therapy belongs in Education because both involve teaching music to someone | The sweep's placement; surface similarity — both are 1:1, recurring, relational, progress-tracked | ❌ REJECTED — **therapy is not teaching**. The goal of a lesson is musical skill; the goal of music therapy is a *clinical outcome* (speech recovery after stroke, agitation reduction in dementia, motor rehabilitation) for which music is the **instrument, not the subject**. The client is not learning music and may never play a note. Sharing "1:1, recurring, relational" with tuition is a coincidence of shape, and shape has been an unreliable guide throughout this domain — the same trap as curriculum-vs-course (06.03.01 DT-01) and path-vs-curriculum (06.07 DT-01), where near-identical structures had opposite semantics. |
| DT-02 | The regulatory cost lands on this feature, so it can be built later without affecting anything else | Standard scope reasoning — defer the expensive thing | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the decisive objection. **Health-grade data contaminates the platform's data classification globally.** One PHI-bearing feature means: HIPAA business-associate obligations, GDPR Art. 9 special-category processing, a data-classification scheme every other domain must then comply with, encryption/audit/retention regimes that reach the whole database, and a breach-notification posture across the estate. `meta/constraints.md` lists eight compliance areas and **health data is not among them** — because nothing else in 24 domains produces any. This feature would add a ninth that is heavier than the other eight combined, and it would land on the security architecture of *everything*, not on itself. It is not deferrable; it is a decision about what kind of company this is. |
| DT-03 | The persona test settles it independently of the regulatory argument | `meta/personas.md` D-19's four ratified personas; the Role Lens above | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is worth having a **second, independent** disqualification, because a scope argument alone can be overruled by ambition (D-03 says coverage is preferred and cuts happen at MoSCoW). The persona test is not about ambition: **no persona in the ratified set is a music therapist**, and the fit is not close. Note `meta/personas.md` Q-01 explicitly anticipates one persona-gap test ("is a professional dealer a distinct persona?") and resolves toward *not* adding a fifth — a therapist is much further from the four than a dealer is. Serving this feature would require a fifth persona whose problem, workflow, regulator and buyer share nothing with the other four. That is a different product with a shared brand. |
