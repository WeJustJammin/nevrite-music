# Feature: Certificates, Badges & Verification

> **Number**: 06.08
> **Parent**: [Education, Lessons & Mentorship](./education-lessons-mentorship-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Split from a bundled candidate** ("Learning Paths, Playbooks & Certifications") — see 06.07. A
> path is a route; a certificate is an artifact issued at its end. Different objects, different
> questions, and — as it turns out — very different levels of justification.

## Overview

A platform-issued artifact asserting that someone completed something: a course, a path, a level. It
is the standard closing move of every education product ever built.

**This file argues against its own feature.** The domain-expert reading is that music has almost no
credentialism outside exam boards — nobody has ever asked a guitarist for a certificate, and nobody
ever will — and that a WeJammin certificate would assert something the platform cannot actually
verify. It is written honestly rather than enthusiastically, because a `[SURFACE]` node whose Deep
Think concludes "probably don't" is more useful to MoSCoW than one that pretends.

| Operator | Config | **The only persona with real demand.** An academy issuing termly certificates to children is a genuine, long-standing product — a certificate on a nine-year-old's bedroom wall is a real artifact with real motivational value, and it is the parent's evidence that the money bought something. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees a badge on a profile if displayed. Cares approximately not at all. |

## Behavior

Issuance on a trigger (course completion, path completion, an academy's term), producing a verifiable
artifact: who, what, when, issued by whom, checkable at a URL.

The verification half is the only part with integrity: a certificate nobody can check is a JPEG. But
verification only matters if **someone checks**, and the honest answer in music is that nobody does.
The market signal is the reverse of most industries: a musician is hired on what they've played, who
vouches for them, and how they sound in the room — which is exactly what domain 02's credits already
capture, verifiably, from actual work.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`: everything about the certificate's content and design. It is
deliberately not elaborated, because elaborating a feature this weakly justified is how a `wont`
becomes a `should` by accident.

### Happy Path

1. Learner completes a course or path
2. Certificate issues automatically in the author's or academy's name
3. Learner may display it on their profile
4. A third party could verify it at a URL
5. Nobody does (DT-01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Certificate asserts skill the holder lacks | The core integrity problem. Completion ≠ ability (06.04.03 DT-02) — someone can watch every lesson and play nothing | See DT-02. This is the reason the feature is `wont` |
| Learner displays it beside verified credits (06.02.02) | **Actively harmful.** A platform-issued completion badge sitting next to counter-attested credits invites the reader to treat them as the same kind of thing | 06.02 CX R-02 rejects the co-render for exactly this reason |
| Academy issues to a child | The one legitimate case. Real motivational value; low integrity stakes because everyone understands what it means | Supported |
| Exam board grade | **Not this feature.** An ABRSM certificate is issued by ABRSM (06.09) — the platform records it, never issues it | 06.09 |
| Certificate used to claim teaching credentials | A "WeJammin Advanced Guitar" certificate as evidence for a teaching listing (06.02.02) — circular and self-serving | Must not feed the credential block |
| Someone forges one | Verification exists for this. Nobody checks, so nobody forges. The absence of forgery is itself evidence of the absence of demand | — |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Music has essentially no credentialism, so a platform-issued certificate has no demand | Domain expertise: nobody asks a session player for a certificate. Hiring runs on credits, referrals, and how you sound. The exceptions prove the rule — **exam boards** (06.09) have centuries of institutional weight and a *syllabus and an examiner*, and **conservatoire degrees** matter for orchestral and academic work. Neither is something a platform can issue | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the reason this feature exists mainly to be cut. The one real exception is the **academy issuing to children** (a certificate on a bedroom wall genuinely motivates a nine-year-old and evidences the fee), which is a narrow, Operator-scoped, low-stakes case — not a platform credentialing system. Everything else is an LMS reflex: certificates exist in e-learning products because e-learning inherited them from professional training, where employers actually check. In music, nobody checks. |
| DT-02 | The platform can only certify **attendance**, not ability — which makes the certificate a claim it cannot support | 06.04.03 DT-02 (completion ≠ playing) + 06.03.05 DT-02 (no honest skill model exists beyond exam grades and a teacher's ears) | ✅ CONFIRMED, and this is the stronger objection of the two — it is an **integrity** problem rather than a demand problem, and it would matter even if demand existed. A certificate reading "completed Advanced Jazz Guitar" is *read* as "can play advanced jazz guitar" and *means* "watched the videos". The platform would be issuing an artifact whose plain reading it cannot support. Worse, it lands on the same profile as **verified credits** — the platform's most valuable asset, whose entire value is that it asserts only what was counter-attested by people who were there. Putting a completion badge beside a counter-attested credit **debases the credit by association**, and the reader cannot tell them apart. This is the same debasement 06.01.04 DT-02 rejected when it declined to mint credits for lessons, arriving from the opposite direction: there, we refused to call a lesson a credit; here, we refuse to let a badge look like one. |
| DT-03 | Badges/gamification would drive engagement even if certificates have no market value | The reflexive fallback when credentialism fails — "make them fun instead" | ❌ REJECTED, and consistently with the rest of the domain. This is the **fourth** time this domain has had to reject a motivation-by-scaffolding pattern: overdue states (06.03.02 DT-03), streaks (06.03.03 DT-02), completion-as-measure (06.04.03 DT-02), and now badges. Same argument each time: the student is often a child under existing pressure, and the product adding a second, colder source of approval-and-failure is how a hobby becomes a chore and a child quits an instrument. Four independent arrivals at one conclusion is not a coincidence — it is a **domain design law**, and it belongs in the domain index rather than buried here (see domain D-04). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | This feature is written **honestly against itself** and proposed as `wont` | Per DT-01 + DT-02. A `[SURFACE]` node that argues for its own exclusion serves MoSCoW better than one that performs enthusiasm. The candidate was ratified in the sweep; deleting it silently would hide the reasoning. |
| D-02 | If anything ships, it is the **academy-issued certificate for children** — narrow, Operator-scoped, low-stakes | The one real demand (DT-01). Not a platform credentialing system. |
| D-03 | Certificates must **never** render beside verified credits, nor feed the credential block | Per DT-02 — it debases the platform's most valuable asset by association, and the reader cannot tell them apart. |
| D-04 | **No badges, no gamification** | Per DT-03 — the domain's fourth rejection of the same pattern. Escalated to the domain index as a design law (domain D-04). |
| D-05 | The platform **records** third-party credentials (06.09); it does not **issue** its own | Recording an ABRSM grade is honest and useful. Issuing a WeJammin grade is asserting something we cannot verify. |
