## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Slot Booking ↔ Credits

**Relationship**: Confirming an occurrence places a **reservation** on the credit ledger; delivering
it converts the reservation to a **consumption**. The intermediate state matters: a student with 3
credits and 3 booked lessons has an available balance of 0, not 3, or they will over-book and
discover the shortfall at the worst moment. Money flows once (at pack purchase); the ledger moves
twice per lesson.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: sees available vs reserved as distinct numbers; is blocked from booking beyond balance without an explicit top-up
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: sees credits outstanding per student — their forward book, and their exposure if they stop teaching
- **Operator**: sees aggregate credit liability across their teachers; this is the number that matters when a teacher leaves mid-pack
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected — no visibility

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: 06.01.02 **owns** the credit ledger; 06.01.01 only reserves against it. Single writer for balance mutation, so no merge strategy is needed — this is why they are one sub-domain.
2. **Trigger chain**: Booking confirmation → reserve. If the reserve fails (insufficient balance), the **booking must fail** — fail closed, no negative balances. Sync at confirmation; the pack purchase that precedes it is async (payment settles first).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — no credits means no booking, unless pay-per-lesson is enabled. The rate card is therefore a hard precondition for the calendar (06.01.02's Empty state blocks booking entirely).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Balance-low fires to the Student; the Teacher is notified of the forward-book risk to their standing slot only if Q-02 below resolves to release.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes, and it is real — two occurrences confirming simultaneously against one remaining credit. The ledger must serialize reservation. Routed to `/create-prd-architecture`.

### CX-02: Cancellation Policy ↔ Credits

**Relationship**: The policy is a **pure function over the credit ledger**: `(occurrence, cancel
time, policy) → burn | return | make-up`. It is the ledger's primary writer and the only place
credits are destroyed. Every dispute in this sub-domain is ultimately a dispute about one of these
writes, which is why each must be an audit-log event (Audit Log cross-cut) and not just a balance
delta.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: sees the consequence in credits **before** confirming a cancellation (06.01.03 D-04); can dispute a burn
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: never manually enforces (06.01.03 D-01) but can always override in the student's favour
- **Operator**: sets the floor policy; handles escalated parent disputes
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: 06.01.02 owns the ledger; 06.01.03 writes through it. A disputed burn holds the credit in limbo — neither returned nor spent — until domain 24 resolves. That limbo state belongs to the ledger.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cancel → evaluate → write. **Fail closed**: if the write fails, the cancellation does not commit and the lesson stays booked (06.01.03's Error state). A silently-failed cancel that still burns a credit is the worst outcome available.
3. **Permission intersection**: The teacher's override is a permission that alters a financial outcome — audit-logged, one direction only (student-favouring).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both parties on every ledger effect, with the effect stated in credits, not in status codes.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — student cancels at the same moment the no-show grace period fires. Cancellation must win; a race that marks a cancelling student as a no-show is unfair and will be disputed.

### CX-03: Delivery ↔ Cancellation Policy

**Relationship**: The no-show rule is unevaluable without evidence, and delivery is where evidence
exists. Join logs from the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut make remote no-shows self-resolving — student
present, teacher absent → credit returns automatically with no dispute. **The in-person case has no
evidence at all** (06.01.03 DT-02), which means the rule needs two implementations: evidence-based
for remote, assertion-plus-dispute for in-person.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: remote no-shows resolve in their favour automatically; in-person requires them to assert and possibly dispute
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: same asymmetry inverted — an in-person student no-show is the teacher's word against the student's
- **Operator**: an academy's room-occupancy record is the **only** third-party evidence available for an in-person lesson in a room they own — potentially significant, and a reason Operator read-only occupancy access exists
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — delivery writes attendance facts; the policy reads them. Read-only relationship.
2. **Trigger chain**: Scheduled start + grace → evaluate attendance → write ledger effect. Async (a timer fires). If evidence is absent (in-person), it falls back to assertion and the timer must **not** auto-burn — an auto-burn on no evidence is a guess with money attached.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — attendance evidence is what gives the student standing to dispute.
4. **Notification fan-out**: No-show marking notifies both parties with the evidence (or the absence of it) attached.
5. **State transition conflict**: See CX-02 Q5 — the cancel-vs-grace-period race.

### CX-04: Slot Booking ↔ Cancellation Policy

**Relationship**: Two invariants that are the same fact from two sides. (a) Cancelling an occurrence
must not dissolve the series — the student keeps their Tuesday 5pm. (b) The policy attached at
booking is the one that governs, forever, for that series. Both exist because the **series is the
entity** (06.01.01 D-01) and the occurrence is derived.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Student)**: cancels one lesson, keeps the standing slot; is governed by the policy they agreed to, not a later one
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: policy changes apply to new bookings only — so a long-running series may sit under a years-old policy
- **Operator / Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The series owns the policy reference (pinned at booking); occurrences inherit it. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cancel occurrence → release slot for that date only → series continues. Sync.
3. **Permission intersection**: An Operator floor policy bounds what a teacher may pin — evaluated at booking time, which means an academy tightening its floor does **not** retroactively bind existing series.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Slot release may promote a waitlist (06.01.01 Q-02, unresolved).
5. **State transition conflict**: A suspended series (teacher on tour) receiving a cancellation for an occurrence that no longer exists. Should be a no-op; flagged for Step 5.

### CX-05: Delivery ↔ Credits

**Relationship**: Delivery converts a reserved credit into a consumed one and accrues the teacher's
payout. Deliberately **loosely coupled**: 06.01.04 D-04 forbids the session record from gating credit
consumption. The teacher taught; they get paid. The unwritten-record problem is solved by capture
design (in-room), not by holding money hostage.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (as Teacher)**: payout accrues on delivery, independent of paperwork
- **Musician (as Student)**: credit consumed on delivery — and if the safeguarding gate blocked the lesson, explicitly **not** consumed
- **Operator**: revenue share accrues on the same event
- **Producer / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — delivery emits an event, the ledger consumes it. Single writer preserved.
2. **Trigger chain**: Lesson end → consume + accrue. If the room failed (06.01.04 Error), the credit must **not** consume, and this must be visible ("your credit hasn't been used") because a student who thinks a broken room cost them a lesson will not book another.
3. **Permission intersection**: The safeguarding gate is upstream of both — a gated lesson consumes nothing and is not a no-show.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Consumption reflected in the balance; teacher's earning appears in domain 23.
5. **State transition conflict**: A partial lesson (dropped mid-way) is genuinely unresolved — `[PENDING — Step 5]` in 06.01.04.

### CX-06: Credits ↔ Slot Booking (the standing-slot risk)

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — a zero balance threatens the series, which is the student's real
asset. Whether a zero balance **suspends** (slot held, no lessons) or **releases** (slot returns to
the market) is unresolved, and it is a genuine product/fairness call: holding a slot for a
non-paying student costs the teacher money, and releasing it punishes a student who is one late
top-up away.

**Role scoping**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5]`, pending Q-02.
