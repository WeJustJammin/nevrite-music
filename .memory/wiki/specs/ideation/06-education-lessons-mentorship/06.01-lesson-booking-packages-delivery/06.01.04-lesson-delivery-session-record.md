# Feature: Lesson Delivery & Session Record

> **Number**: 06.01.04
> **Parent**: [Lesson Booking, Packages & Delivery](./06.01-lesson-booking-packages-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The lesson itself, and the artifact it leaves behind. The live room is consumed wholesale from the
`Real-Time Rooms, Presence & Audio Transport` cross-cut — this feature does not own transport. What
it owns is everything *around* the room that makes a lesson a lesson: the safeguarding gate that
must pass before anyone joins, the attendance evidence that settles a no-show, the recording under a
chaperone policy, and the **session record** — what we covered, what to practise, what to fix — which
is the seam where a delivered lesson becomes an assignment (06.03) and the credit becomes earned.

Two delivery modes are specified: **remote** (a room, a join log, a recording) and **in-person** (no
room, no join log, no recording — and therefore a materially different set of safeguarding controls,
not a lighter one). Whether in-person ships is a scope call the owner still holds (Q-03); its
behavior is specified here so that the call can be made with the cost visible rather than guessed.

This feature owns three verbs and nothing else: **gate**, **evidence**, **record**. It decides
*whether the lesson may happen*, it emits *the facts about whether it did*, and it captures *what
happened in it*. It never decides a ledger effect — 06.01.03 does that, reading the facts this
feature emits (D-06).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: passes the join gate; runs the room; shares audio/screen/score; switches to a hands camera (DT-07); records if policy permits; sees the record panel from minute 0 with a skeleton assembled from what they actually did in the room (DT-11); assigns practice from inside the lesson; sees attendance accrue live; declares a guardian observer for a minor (D-10); hard-stops at the next commitment (D-05). **As Student**: passes the same gate; joins; plays; receives record + assignment + recording; re-watches; leaves the room knowing what to practise. **As guardian** (delegated authority over a minor's Musician account, per domain D-03 — *not* a persona): consents to recording; joins as a declared observer; reads the session record by delegation (DT-09); revokes consent, which for a minor's 1:1 revokes the lesson, not merely the recording (D-12). |
| Producer | None | Not visible. A Producer running a paid remote session uses Services (05) + Real-Time Rooms directly; a lesson is not a session and the two must not share a room type — the safeguarding gate is the reason (DT-03). The room they get is also tuned differently: a Producer's remote session and a lesson want opposite points on the fidelity↔latency trade (DT-05, DT-06). |
| Operator | Read-only | As an academy or studio: sees **room occupancy and attendance** for rooms they own — never lesson content, never the recording, never the session record. For an in-person lesson in a room they own, their occupancy record is the only third-party attendance evidence that exists (CX-03), which is the whole reason this read exists. **The Operator role never grants content access, at any tier.** An academy head who wants to sit in on a teacher's lesson does so as a **consented, declared, per-occurrence observer** visible to both parties — a participant by invitation, not a permission (D-13, DT-12). |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no fan-facing entry point. A Fan who books a lesson is a Musician (student) from that moment and is lensed as one (06.01.01 Role Lens). |

## Behavior

Delivery is **gated**, then **run**, then **recorded** — in that order, and the gate is not skippable.

### The gate

Before a join is permitted for any lesson involving a minor, the `Safeguarding & Minor Protection`
cross-cut must have satisfied, as of *now* and not as of booking time:

1. **Teacher vetting current** (DBS / state background check, per the cross-cut)
2. **Guardian consent on file for this pairing** — consent is per teacher↔student pair, not per account
3. **The chaperone norm for this 1:1 established** — which in practice means recording enabled, or a
   declared present adult (DT-04, D-11)

Only then does the room open. The gate **fails closed** with a specific, actionable reason (D-02).
It emits `blocked` with a **cause attributed to a party**; attribution is evidence and belongs to
this feature, while the resulting ledger effect belongs to 06.01.03 (D-06).

Between booking and join, the gate is evaluated **twice** — booking-time (06.01.01) and join-time
(here), as defence in depth (CX-07). The join-time evaluation is authoritative for remote delivery
because it is the last moment before an adult and a child share a room. **For in-person delivery it
is not authoritative and cannot be**, because the platform is not at the door — see below, which is
the single most important structural fact about in-person mode.

### The room (remote mode)

The room is the cross-cut's. **Access is identity-bound** (D-08): only the occurrence's two parties
and a declared observer may join, authenticated. There is no link join, no anonymous join, no
dial-in code. A link-shareable room with a child in it is the worst object this platform could ship.

Layered on the cross-cut's pipe:

- **A music-fidelity audio profile, not a speech profile** (D-14, DT-05). Speech DSP — noise gates,
  AGC, echo cancellation — destroys the thing the lesson exists to hear. This is a hard requirement
  from this caller and it points the opposite way from domain 08's (DT-06).
- **Attendance evidence** — server-stamped join/leave, concurrent-presence accrual (D-09). This is
  what 06.01.03 reads to settle a no-show.
- **Optional recording under policy** — never ad hoc, never a button (DT-04).
- **The session record panel**, present from minute 0, pre-populated (DT-11).
- **The trial-conversion surface** — a converted trial is created *in this room* (domain CX-01). This
  feature owns the surface; 06.02.04 owns the conversion logic and the trial cap.

Room opens at **T−10 min** with a device check (audio input level + round-trip). Presence accrues
only while both parties are connected.

### The delivery predicate — what makes a lesson "delivered"

The single most consequential thing this feature computes, and the one the sub-domain left open
(CX-05 Q5). It is a **fact**, not a policy:

| Outcome | Condition | Emitted |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivered** | Concurrent server-stamped presence ≥ **50%** of the occurrence's scheduled duration | `delivered` — 06.01.02 consumes the reserved credit, teacher's payout accrues |
| **Interrupted** | Concurrent presence < 50% **and** a transport fault evidenced by the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut | `interrupted` + fault evidence — 06.01.03 decides the ledger effect; the credit does **not** consume by default |
| **Abandoned** | Concurrent presence < 50% **and** no transport fault — a party simply left | `abandoned` + attribution + presence log — 06.01.03 decides, with evidence attached |
| **Blocked** | Gate failed | `blocked` + cause + attribution — not a no-show, not a delivery |

**Why 50%, and why one number**: an arbitrary threshold is unavoidable; a *judgement call* is not.
A teacher who taught 26 of 30 minutes and lost the last four to a dropout taught the lesson, and
refunding it is absurd. A student who got 6 of 30 minutes did not have a lesson. Somewhere between
those a line exists, and the only defensible design is that the line is **one published number,
stated before booking**, not a support ticket. This is the same principle as 06.01.03 D-04
(consequences shown before confirming, never after).

Over-run does **not** move this line, because a credit is an occurrence, not a duration (06.01.02
D-02, and D-05 below).

### The session record

The highest-value artifact in the domain and the one most likely not to get written — which is a
**design problem, not a discipline problem** (DT-01). Three mechanisms, in order of importance:

1. **It is assembled, not authored** (DT-11). Everything the teacher did in the room that the
   platform already witnessed — files shared, bars looped, tempo set, score marked up, timestamps
   they flagged — becomes the record's skeleton. The teacher confirms and adds a sentence; they do
   not face a blank page. This is the direct analogue of the Producer's split capture being a
   byproduct of running the session (`meta/personas.md`).
2. **It is captured in the room** (D-01), not afterwards. The panel is present from minute 0.
3. **It is never a payment gate** (D-04, sub-domain CX R-03). At lesson end, if the record is empty,
   the room holds for **90 seconds** with the skeleton on screen and nothing else — skippable in one
   tap ("Skip — no notes"). A skip is honest, not hidden: the student sees "No notes from this
   lesson."

**Escalation, and its hard ceiling** (D-07): after **3 consecutive** skipped records for the same
student, the teacher gets a **private** nudge. A sustained pattern is a supply-quality signal —
visible to the Operator for academy staff (06.10's QA read) and to the platform. It never becomes a
public signal, never a pay gate, and never a streak. Domain law D-04 bans motivation-by-scaffolding;
its stated rationale is child-specific, so a private supply-quality signal aimed at a professional
adult is outside it — but a teacher-facing streak, badge, or "you're on a 12-lesson run!" would be
inside it in spirit and is banned here explicitly so nobody reintroduces it.

### In-person delivery (second mode — behavior specified; scope is Q-03)

Not a variant. A second implementation, and the parent's Q-03 is a real question because of what
follows — not because the work is large.

**The structural fact**: an in-person lesson **cannot fail closed at the door**, because the platform
is not at the door. D-02's gate is a remote-mode control. In-person, the platform's entire arsenal is:

1. **Block the booking** (06.01.01's booking-time gate) — the only genuinely preventive control
2. **Notify at T−24h and T−2h** if the gate has failed since booking — teacher, student, and guardian
3. **Mark the occurrence `blocked`** and refuse to record attendance or consume a credit

It cannot prevent two people meeting. **This must be stated plainly to whoever decides Q-03**: for
in-person, the safeguarding gate is a strong *administrative* control and a **zero-strength physical
one**.

| Concern | Remote | In-person |
|---|---|---|
| Join evidence | Server-stamped join log — automatic, strong | **Two-party mutual confirmation** (below) |
| Chaperone | The recording is the chaperone (DT-04) | **No recording exists** — the chaperone must be a physically present adult (D-15) |
| Address | Not disclosed | **Disclosed at confirmation** — for a minor, a child's home address is released to an adult (Q-07) |
| Record capture | Skeleton from in-room actions (DT-11) | **Almost no skeleton** — see below |
| Gate enforcement | Authoritative at the door | Administrative only |

**Attendance without a join log.** 06.01.03 DT-02 states the in-person case has "no evidence at all."
That is very nearly right and worth refining by one notch: it has no *automatic* evidence, but a
three-tier ladder is available, and mutual confirmation is materially stronger than assertion:

| Tier | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mutual confirmation** — teacher marks present in-app; student or guardian confirms within the occurrence window | Two-party, timestamped, hard to fake unilaterally |
| 2 | **Operator room occupancy** — an academy room's own record (CX-03) | Third-party, but only where an Operator owns the room |
| 3 | **Bare assertion** — one party's word | Weakest; 06.01.03 falls back to dispute |

The delivery predicate does not apply in-person (there is no presence to measure). `delivered` is
emitted on Tier-1 confirmation. Tier-2/3 emit `asserted` with the tier attached, and 06.01.03
decides — the timer must **not** auto-burn on Tier-3, per CX-03's own rule that an auto-burn on no
evidence is a guess with money attached.

**A minor's in-person 1:1 requires a declared present adult, or an academy room** (D-15). This
follows necessarily from DT-04: if the recording is the chaperone, then no recording means no
chaperone, and a lone adult with a child in a private home with zero platform presence is the
highest-severity configuration this platform can produce. The two acceptable configurations are a
declared guardian/chaperone present, or a supervised setting (an academy room, which is also
conveniently the only place Tier-2 evidence exists).

**The record gets *worse*, not better, in person** — the reverse of the intuition. DT-11's skeleton
has nothing to work from: no shared files, no loops, no marked-up score, no flagged timestamps. The
in-person record is structurally the most likely to go unwritten, in the mode where the platform has
the least reason to be trusted. Whoever answers Q-03 should know that in-person weakens the domain's
highest-value artifact rather than merely duplicating it.

### Happy Path (remote)

1. **T−24h**: both parties get a reminder; the gate is pre-evaluated and any failure surfaces now,
   while it is still fixable, rather than at the door
2. **T−10 min**: the room opens; both get a join prompt; device check runs (audio input level +
   round-trip)
3. **Join**: the gate re-evaluates against *current* state — vetting current, consent on file,
   chaperone norm established — and passes
4. Both join; **server-stamped** join times recorded; recording starts if policy permits; the
   guardian, if present, is declared as an observer and appears in the presence list to both parties
5. The room applies the **music-fidelity audio profile**; the teacher pins their hands camera
6. Teacher teaches: shares audio/screen/score, loops bars 12–20 at 80bpm, marks up the score, flags
   two timestamps in the recording. **Each of these actions writes a line into the record skeleton**
7. Concurrent presence crosses **50%** of the scheduled duration → the occurrence is `delivered`
   regardless of what happens next
8. **T−5 min from scheduled end**: teacher-only nudge — "5 minutes left; the record panel has 6 items
   ready"
9. Teacher confirms the skeleton, adds one sentence, and assigns practice **inside the room** — one
   action, creating the assignment in 06.03 (domain CX-02)
10. Lesson ends. Credit consumes (06.01.02); teacher's payout accrues (domain 23)
11. Student receives record + assignment + recording on one card; the assignment is already waiting
    in the practice room (06.03.04)

### Happy Path (in-person)

1. **T−24h**: reminder to both, plus the guardian; the gate is evaluated — **this is the last
   enforceable moment**, and the notice says so
2. **T−2h**: address/location reconfirmed to both parties
3. Teacher opens the occurrence in-app on arrival and marks **present**
4. Student or guardian confirms in-app within the occurrence window → **Tier-1 mutual confirmation**
5. Lesson happens. The platform is not in it and does not pretend to be
6. Teacher writes the session record **before leaving** — from a near-empty skeleton, which is the
   mode's structural weakness (above)
7. `delivered` emitted on Tier-1 confirmation; credit consumes; payout accrues
8. Student receives record + assignment. **No recording exists**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Teacher's vetting expired this morning** | Gate **fails closed** — the room does not open, no exception path. Emits `blocked`, cause `teacher_vetting_expired`, attributed to the teacher. Not a student no-show; the credit does not consume. 06.01.03 reads the attribution and treats it as teacher-side | Both: "This lesson can't start — {teacher}'s check needs renewing." Teacher: renewal flow + "your student has been told this is on us, not them" |
| **Vetting expires *between* T−24h and the lesson** | The T−24h pre-evaluation passed; the join-time evaluation fails. This is exactly why the gate is evaluated twice (CX-07) and why join-time is authoritative for remote | Both: same as above, at the door. Teacher additionally: "This was current at your reminder yesterday" |
| **Guardian consent not on file for a minor** | Gate fails closed. Booking should have caught this (06.01.01), so this is a defence-in-depth catch, not the primary control | Guardian: consent request. Lesson postponed; credit intact |
| **Guardian revokes recording consent mid-series** | For a minor's 1:1 where recording is the chaperone, this **revokes the lesson, not just the recording** (D-12) — a consequence that follows necessarily from DT-04 and would otherwise silently strip the only control | Guardian: stated at revocation time, before confirming — "This will also stop {student}'s lessons with {teacher} unless you'll be present." Never discovered at the door |
| **Guardian revokes recording consent *mid-lesson*** | Recording stops immediately (consent is immediate). The chaperone is now gone → the D-11 path fires: 60s to declare the guardian as a present observer, else the room ends. Presence already ≥50% → still `delivered`; credit consumes | Both: "Recording has stopped. {Guardian} needs to join as an observer, or this lesson will end in 60 seconds." |
| **Recording fails ≥30s into a minor's 1:1** | **The chaperone left.** D-02 says the gate fails closed; a control that can be lost mid-lesson but is only enforced at the door is theatre (DT-08). Banner at 30s; 60s countdown; auto-end unless a guardian observer is declared. Logged as a safeguarding event either way | Both: "Recording has stopped — this lesson can't continue without it. Ending in 60s." Teacher: "Declare a guardian present" |
| **Recording upload fails *after* the lesson** | The lesson happened; presence, not recording, is the delivery predicate → `delivered`, credit consumes. But the chaperone evidence is **lost**, which for a minor's lesson is a safeguarding record gap and is escalated as an incident (domain 24) — never silently swallowed | Both: "The recording of this lesson couldn't be saved." Teacher additionally: incident acknowledgement |
| **Student joins, teacher never does** | Join log evidences it; concurrent presence = 0 → `abandoned`, attributed to the teacher. 06.01.03 resolves it in the student's favour automatically, no dispute needed | Student: "{Teacher} didn't join — credit returned" |
| **Network dies mid-lesson, unrecoverable** | Cross-cut owns reconnection. If concurrent presence already ≥50% → `delivered`, credit consumes, record still capturable from the skeleton. If <50% and the cross-cut evidences a transport fault → `interrupted`: credit does **not** consume, no reliability signal against either party, one-tap re-book into the teacher's next free slot | Both: reconnect banner → if unrecoverable, "This lesson was cut short by a connection fault — your credit hasn't been used" + re-book action. Teacher: see Q-04 (compensation) |
| **Network dies, one party never returns, no transport fault** | `abandoned` + attribution + the presence log. This is not a partial lesson; it is somebody leaving. 06.01.03 decides with the evidence attached | Both: "Marked as ended early at 17:22" + the log |
| **Audio quality falls below the music-fidelity floor for >60s** | The lesson is no longer teachable — a teacher cannot hear tone through a noise gate (DT-05). Both are told plainly and offered: continue at speech quality (their choice, stated as a downgrade), or end with **no credit consumed** regardless of the 50% rule | Both: "Audio has dropped below what's usable for teaching." Two buttons: "Carry on anyway" / "End — no credit used". Never a silent downgrade |
| **Lesson runs long** | **Free** (D-05). A credit is an occurrence, not a duration (06.01.02 D-02). The room stays open until the earlier of (a) the teacher's next occurrence start minus their buffer (06.01.01 owns the buffer), (b) scheduled end + 30 min. Warnings: teacher-only at end−5 min; both at end+0; both at hard-close−2 min with a 120s countdown. No automatic second charge, ever | Both at end+0: "Scheduled time is up — the room stays open until 18:00." At hard close: named reason — "{Teacher} has a lesson at 18:00", not a generic timeout |
| **Teacher writes no session record** | Lesson completes; credit consumes (D-04 — the record must never be a payment gate). Student gets the recording and an honest empty state. Escalation is private and capped (D-07): 3 consecutive skips for the same student → private nudge; sustained pattern → supply-quality signal to Operator/platform. Never public, never a streak, never a pay gate | Teacher: 90s hold with the skeleton pre-loaded, one-tap skip. Student: "No notes from this lesson" — honest, not hidden |
| **Recording contains a copyrighted backing track** | **Not scanned; no proactive takedown** (D-16). Music education runs almost entirely on copyrighted material (domain Q-02 — three independent routes to the same finding), and a lesson recording is not published: it is visible to exactly two parties plus a guardian, and is never distributed. Content-ID on it would break every lesson on the platform to protect nobody. The recording remains reachable by an incident/legal process via domain 24 | Nothing. Silence is the correct behavior. **The legal validation of this position is Q-05, not the behavior itself** |
| **Student screen-records the lesson** | Unpreventable — no technical control exists on any platform, and pretending otherwise is worse than admitting it. The student already has the recording. The teacher's method as IP is Q-01 | Nothing at the time. Addressed by terms + the fact the leak surface already exists by design |
| **A stranger gets the room link** | There is no link (D-08). Access is identity-bound: only the occurrence's two parties and a declared observer, authenticated. An unauthenticated join is not refused — it is not expressible | Stranger: no surface exists |
| **Two devices, same user, join the same room** | Second device takes over; the first is dropped with a stated reason. Presence must **not** double-count — attendance is financial evidence (D-09) and a double-counted presence corrupts a no-show settlement | Dropped device: "You joined this lesson on another device" |
| **Teacher's device clock is wrong (or is set wrong deliberately)** | Irrelevant — join/leave/presence are **server-stamped** (D-09). A client-stamped join log decides who eats a credit and is forgeable by changing a system clock. This is the difference between evidence and a claim | Nothing. The user never sees this; that is the point |
| **Multi-hyphenate: Amy teaches Ben guitar; Ben teaches Amy production** | Two series, two rate cards, two ledgers, opposite directions. "Who is the teacher" is a property of **the occurrence**, never of the person (personas.md's central structural fact; domain D-02). Record-write permission, recording control, and the assign action all derive from the occurrence's teacher role. Amy does not see Ben's record about her, and vice versa — record visibility follows the occurrence's relationship, not the pair | Each sees the correct room for the lesson they are in. No mode switch, no "are you teaching or learning?" prompt — that prompt is the failure `meta/personas.md` names explicitly |
| **Multi-hyphenate: a teacher joins their own lesson (as student) while a lesson they teach is live** | **Presence is exclusive.** Joining room B drops them from room A with a stated reason. Room A must not keep accruing their presence — that would fabricate attendance evidence for a lesson they had left, and 06.01.03 would settle a no-show on a fiction | Both rooms: "You're now in another lesson." Room A's student: the standard party-left path |
| **Student cancels at the exact moment the teacher joins** | Cancellation wins **until concurrent presence begins**. From the moment both are present, cancellation is unavailable — you cannot cancel a lesson you are in (D-17). This is the delivery-side counterpart of CX-02 Q5's cancel-vs-grace race | Student, post-presence: the cancel action is gone, replaced by "You're in this lesson" |
| **Occurrence was cancelled; the teacher opens the bookmarked room anyway** | Room refuses with the cancellation's own facts | Teacher: "This lesson was cancelled on Sunday at 14:12 by {student} — 1 credit returned" |
| **Academy head wants to observe a teacher's lesson for QA** | Not an Operator permission at any tier (D-13). They join as a **declared, consented, per-occurrence observer**, visible to both parties in the presence list and logged. Silent observation of a child's lesson by a third adult is the precise thing safeguarding exists to forbid; building it as a role permission would make the platform the threat | Both: "{Name} (Academy) has joined to observe" — before they can see or hear anything. Either party may decline; the observation does not happen |
| **Teacher joins a minor's lesson from a café** | Not automatically preventable — the platform cannot see the room the teacher is in. It **is** in the recording, which is what makes it accountable after the fact. This is DT-04's argument restated: the recording is the control, and the control is retrospective | Nothing at the time. Policy violation, reachable by incident escalation (domain 24) |
| **In-person: student never confirms attendance** | No Tier-1 evidence. Falls to Tier 2 (Operator occupancy, if an academy room) or Tier 3 (assertion). Emits `asserted` with the tier attached. **The grace timer must not auto-burn on Tier 3** (CX-03) | Teacher: "{Student} hasn't confirmed — this will need agreeing between you." Student: confirm/dispute prompt |
| **In-person: gate fails between booking and the lesson** | The platform blocks the occurrence, notifies all three parties at T−24h and T−2h, refuses attendance and credit consumption — **and cannot stop the meeting**. Stated honestly rather than papered over | All three: "This lesson must not go ahead — {reason}." No pretence that the platform enforced anything |
| **In-person with a minor, no declared adult, not an academy room** | Booking is refused (D-15). The chaperone control does not exist in this configuration and there is no substitute | Guardian/teacher: "In-person lessons with under-18s need a parent present or an academy room" |
| **Both parties no-show (remote)** | Concurrent presence = 0, both absent, no gate failure → `abandoned`, attributed to **both**. This feature emits the symmetric fact; the ledger effect is 06.01.03's open Q-03 and is deliberately not decided here | Both: "Neither of you joined" + the log. Ledger effect per 06.01.03 |
| **Room opens; the occurrence has no reserved credit** (ledger drift) | Fail closed **against the platform, never against the student**: the room opens, the lesson runs, and the discrepancy is reconciled afterwards. Refusing a paid-for lesson at the door over an internal accounting fault is the single worst outcome available — a student who was locked out of a lesson they paid for does not book another | Nobody sees anything. Reconciled silently; logged loudly |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Room joining | "Connecting…" with device check — audio input level meter + round-trip indicator, before join, not after |
| **Gated** | Safeguarding gate failed | The **specific** reason + the **specific** action that clears it, named party, named remedy. Never a generic "unavailable" (D-02) — a generic failure here is a support ticket and an angry parent |
| **Waiting** | Room open (T−10), one party present | Present party: "Waiting for {name} — the room opened at 16:50." Grace and no-show marking belong to 06.01.03; this state only shows the fact |
| **Live** | Both present | In-room UI (cross-cut owned) + record/assign panel + presence list including any declared observer + concurrent-presence indicator |
| **Degraded** | Audio below the music-fidelity floor >60s | "Audio has dropped below what's usable for teaching" + the two-button choice. Never a silent downgrade to speech DSP |
| **Chaperone-lost** | Recording failed ≥30s in a minor's 1:1 | 60s countdown + "Declare a guardian present" action (D-11) |
| **Over-running** | Past scheduled end | "Scheduled time is up — the room stays open until {hard close}." At −2 min: 120s countdown with the named reason (D-05) |
| **Interrupted** | <50% presence + evidenced transport fault | "This lesson was cut short by a connection fault — your credit hasn't been used" + one-tap re-book |
| **Ended — record pending** | Lesson over, record empty | Teacher: 90s hold, skeleton pre-loaded, one-tap skip. Student: "Notes coming" for 90s, then the honest empty state |
| **Empty** | No session record written | Student: "No notes from this lesson" — honest, never hidden. Teacher: the private nudge if this is the 3rd consecutive skip for this student |
| **Populated** | Delivered, record written | Session record + recording + assignment, one card. The assignment is already in the practice room (06.03.04) |
| **Error** | Room failed to open | Cross-cut's failure UI + "your credit hasn't been used" — explicit, because a student who believes a broken room cost them a lesson will not book another (CX-05 Q2) |
| **Partial** | <50% presence, cause not yet classified | "Ended early — working out what happened." Resolves to Interrupted or Abandoned within the classification window; never sits here indefinitely |
| **Awaiting confirmation** | In-person, teacher marked present, student has not | Teacher: "{Student} hasn't confirmed yet." Student/guardian: one-tap confirm |

| DT-01 | The session record will not get written unless it is captured **in the room, while the lesson is happening** — and this is structurally the same problem as the Producer's split capture | `meta/personas.md` (Producer's "worst accidental": closes a project without capturing splits — "the design must make the lazy path the correct path"); `meta/problem-statement.md` root cause: "no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the reason this feature exists as more than a link to a video room. The teacher who writes notes "later that evening" writes them never; a lesson reconstructed from memory a week on is as lossy as a split reconstructed after three years. **The platform's thesis applies to teaching without modification**, which is a genuinely useful finding: the same in-the-room capture discipline that domain 07 uses for splits is what makes 06.03's practice tracking have anything to track. |
| DT-02 | A delivered lesson should generate a **verified credit** in domain 02, since the platform is already in the room and credits are the wedge | Thesis (D-18) — provenance is the wedge; the temptation to apply it everywhere is strong | ❌ REJECTED — a credit is proof you played on a *record*. "Took 40 piano lessons" is not a credit; it is a progress history, and it belongs in 06.03. Minting credits for lesson attendance would debase the credit graph, which is the platform's single most valuable asset, in exchange for a vanity metric. Logging this rejection explicitly because the pull toward it is strong and a later reader will re-propose it. **Where the wedge legitimately applies to teaching is the opposite direction**: verified credits earned elsewhere are what credential the teacher (06.02.02). |
| DT-03 | A lesson room is just the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut with a different label | The cross-cut list explicitly names "Education's live lesson room" as a caller | ❌ REJECTED — the transport is identical, but a lesson room has a **precondition no other caller has**: the Safeguarding gate. A Services remote session between two adults opens on click; a lesson may be an adult alone with a child, and must not open until vetting and consent are proven current. The cross-cut owns the pipe; this feature owns the lock on the door. **DT-05 and DT-06 later found the transport is *not* identical either** — which strengthens this rejection from a different direction than expected. |
| DT-04 | Recording is a nice-to-have convenience for students who want to re-watch | Naive product read — re-watching is the obvious student benefit | ❌ REJECTED as the primary driver. Recording's first function is **safeguarding** — the cross-cut lists "chaperone/recording norms for 1:1 lessons" as a control, i.e. the recording is the chaperone when no third adult is present. That inverts the design: recording defaults and consent flows are set by policy for child protection, and student convenience is the secondary benefit. It also means "let me turn recording off" is not a preference toggle — it is a safeguarding decision. **This hypothesis turned out to be the most load-bearing in the file**: DT-08, D-11, D-12 and D-15 are all consequences of it that nobody had drawn. |
| DT-05 | A music lesson room can use the same audio path as a video call | The cross-cut is one pipe serving six callers; reusing it is the obvious economy | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the domain expert's **first** objection, which nothing in this domain had recorded. Every meeting-grade audio path applies speech DSP: noise gates, automatic gain control, echo cancellation. Those stages destroy music specifically — a gate cuts a piano's decay, AGC flattens a crescendo, echo cancellation eats a guitar's sustain the moment both parties play. Every music teacher discovered this on Zoom in 2020, and the industry's answer is a buried "original sound" toggle, which tells you it was never designed for. **A room that defaults to speech DSP cannot do the one thing a lesson is for: let a teacher hear whether a student's tone is good.** This is a hard behavioral requirement on the cross-cut from this caller (D-14) and it is not a preference. |
| DT-06 | Latency is the lesson room's key metric, as it is for domain 08 | D-15 makes the ~25–30 ms desync ceiling the defining constraint of Real-Time Jamming; a lesson is also two musicians in a room | ❌ REJECTED, and the inversion is the finding. **Remote teacher and student cannot play together at all** — not at 25 ms, and certainly not over ordinary domestic internet. So the fundamental pedagogic act "play along with me" is *unavailable remotely*, and this is a constraint to design around, not a bug to fix. Remote teaching is **call-and-response, not unison**: I play, you listen; you play, I listen. Two consequences. (1) The room needs a turn-taking affordance and must **not** inherit domain 08's jam-room UX, which is built for the thing this room cannot do. (2) Combined with DT-05, this caller wants **fidelity over latency** while domain 08 wants **latency over fidelity** — *opposite* optimization targets on one cross-cut, which the cross-cut's own hard architecture warning does not currently mention. Routed as Q-06. **This also gives Q-03 a pedagogic argument it did not have**: some teaching (ensemble, duets, rhythm-section work) genuinely cannot be done remotely, which is a substantive reason in-person exists — not a commercial shrug. |
| DT-07 | One camera per participant, pointed at a face, is the right shot | Inherited from every meeting product ever built | ❌ REJECTED — a lesson's subject is **hands**, not faces. The student needs the teacher's hands on the fretboard or keys; the teacher needs the student's hands, posture, *and* the sheet on the stand. Real teachers solve this today with a phone on a gooseneck as a second angle — universally, and with no help from any platform. So a second camera angle and a pin/switch affordance are **table stakes for this domain**, not a premium tier, and the room must not assume one stream per participant. Behavioral requirement on the cross-cut. |
| DT-08 | If the recording is the chaperone (DT-04), then losing the recording mid-lesson loses the chaperone — and nothing in this domain designs for that | Follows necessarily from DT-04, which four documents cite and none extend | ✅ CONFIRMED — **the failure mode nobody designed for**. D-02 makes the gate fail closed, but a gate that only fails closed *at the door* while its control can silently evaporate at minute 20 is theatre. The naive behavior (recording drops, lesson continues) leaves an adult alone with a child and no control at all, which is precisely the configuration the cross-cut calls "not a bug class but an existential one". Resolved as D-11: 30s detection, 60s countdown, an escape hatch that is *humane and closed* (declare a guardian observer), auto-end otherwise, safeguarding event logged either way. The room does not slam shut; it also does not pretend. |
| DT-09 | The guardian sitting off-camera in the child's room is the real chaperone in real music teaching — and the platform models them as nothing at all | Domain expertise: a parent in the room is the single most common safeguarding control in private music tuition, and it long predates any platform | ✅ CONFIRMED — a genuine gap sitting in the intersection of four documents that each assume another handles it. That person is **present, unconsented, unlogged, and in the recording**. Three consequences nobody had drawn: (a) the recording captures a third party who never consented to being recorded; (b) the teacher may not know they are there, which changes what an adult says to a child — and is precisely what the control is *for*; (c) it is the most-used control in the market and the platform cannot see it. Resolved as D-10: the guardian is a **declared observer** — in the presence list, visible to both parties, logged, no audio/video by default. This also makes them the escape hatch in D-11 and D-12, so one modelling decision closes three failure modes. |
| DT-10 | The record's second reader is the fee-paying parent, and a teacher who knows that writes a different document | D-07 (the parent-as-buyer persona gap, found independently by two features); the guardian holds delegated authority over the minor's account (domain D-03), so they can read the record whether or not it was written for them | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it resolves in an unobvious direction. The tension looks like a privacy problem: a child might not want "she hasn't practised in a month" going to a parent, and a teacher who knows the parent reads everything will self-censor. The resolution is that **there is no private teacher↔child channel, and that is a safeguarding feature, not a privacy failure** — the cross-cut restricts adult↔minor messaging for exactly this reason. An unobservable channel between an adult and a child is the thing being prevented, so "the parent can read it" is the intended state, not a leak. What must change is only that the teacher is told at write time, so it is never a surprise. The boundary with 06.03.05 stays clean: the session record is written **to the student** and read by the guardian by delegation; a progress report is written **to the parent** and is 06.03.05's. |
| DT-11 | The record should be **assembled** from what the platform already witnessed in the room, not **authored** from a blank page | DT-01 says capture must be in-room, but in-room capture still faces a blank field — and a blank field at the end of a tiring lesson is the thing that does not get filled | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the concrete mechanism DT-01 was missing. Every in-room action is already a fact the platform holds: files shared, bars looped, tempo set, score marked up, timestamps flagged. Those *are* the record's spine. The teacher's job becomes confirm + one sentence, not compose. This is the exact analogue of `meta/personas.md`'s "make the lazy path the correct path" — the Producer's split is captured as a byproduct of running the session, and the teacher's record is captured as a byproduct of teaching. **It also predicts, correctly, that the in-person record is the weakest one in the product** (no in-room actions to harvest ⇒ no skeleton), which nothing in the domain had noticed and which materially raises the cost of answering Q-03 "yes". |
| DT-12 | An academy needs to observe its teachers' lessons for QA, and this is an Operator permission | 06.10 puts QA of staff teaching in scope; the domain Role Matrix gives Operator a QA read; a music school head genuinely does sit in on lessons | ❌ REJECTED **as a permission**, ✅ CONFIRMED **as an act**. The real-world behavior is legitimate and must be supported; modelling it as a *role permission* is not, because a permission is silent, standing, and retrospective — an Operator with a content read could watch a child's lesson without either party knowing, which makes the platform the threat it exists to guard against. Resolved as D-13: observation is a **per-occurrence, declared, consented participation**, announced before anything is seen or heard, refusable by either party, logged. The Operator **role** grants occupancy and attendance and never content, at any tier. The academy loses nothing real — a head who must ask is exactly the norm a school should have anyway. |
| DT-13 | The session record's non-existence should eventually harden into a payment gate if prompting fails | The domain's whole value proposition depends on the record, and D-04 only says the *first* answer is not a gate — leaving "does it harden later?" open | ❌ REJECTED, and closed permanently (D-07). The sub-domain already rejected this twice (D-04, CX R-03) and it keeps returning because the record genuinely matters. It stays rejected: withholding a teacher's pay over paperwork pushes the lesson to WhatsApp, which destroys the habit surface and the record along with it — the enforcement mechanism eats its own objective. The escape is that hardening was aimed at the wrong target. **The right escalation is supply-quality, not payment**: private nudge at 3 consecutive skips, then a signal to the Operator/platform. Explicitly banned in the same breath: any teacher-facing streak, badge or run counter. Domain law D-04's rationale is child-specific, so it does not formally cover a professional adult — but reintroducing scaffolding through that gap is exactly how the law gets hollowed out, so the ban is restated here rather than left to inference. |
| DT-14 | An over-run should burn a second credit, or at least a fraction of one | The naive fairness read — the teacher gave more time, so more should be paid; and real teachers over-run constantly | ❌ REJECTED (D-05). A credit is **an occurrence at the rate paid** (06.01.02 D-02), not a unit of time — so there is nothing for an extra minute to draw against without turning a credit into currency, which 06.01.02 DT-03 rejected for making the platform a bank. Worse, an automatic second charge is a financial surprise delivered *after* the fact, which violates 06.01.03 D-04's core principle (consequences before confirming) and inverts DT-03's finding there: enforcement exists to keep the teacher out of the money conversation, and a surprise over-run charge drops them straight back into it, in the worst possible moment — the student's parent's inbox. The real constraint on over-run is not money, it is **the next student**, so the hard stop is the next commitment, and it is named rather than generic. A teacher who wants to sell 45 minutes sells a 45-minute slot. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Consumes **Real-Time Rooms, Presence & Audio Transport** (global cross-cut) — the entire live room.
  **Trigger dependency + hard requirement.** Inherits its architecture warning (**will not run on
  Cloudflare Workers + Supabase realtime**; needs dedicated media infrastructure). **This caller adds
  three requirements the cross-cut does not currently carry, and Step 6 must reconcile them**:
  (1) a **music-fidelity audio profile** — speech DSP is disqualifying, not suboptimal (DT-05, D-14);
  (2) **more than one stream per participant** — the subject is hands, not faces (DT-07);
  (3) this caller optimizes **fidelity over latency**, the *opposite* of domain 08's target on the
  same pipe (DT-06). One pipe, two opposite optima ⇒ the cross-cut needs per-caller profiles. Q-06.
- Consumes **Safeguarding & Minor Protection** (global cross-cut) — the join gate, chaperone/recording
  norms, restricted adult↔minor contact. **Permission + trigger dependency.** This feature is the
  gate's **enforcement point**, not its owner (DT-03). Four findings go back to the cross-cut's owner:
  the **declared guardian observer** (D-10, DT-09) is unmodelled and is the market's most-used control;
  **chaperone loss mid-lesson** (D-11, DT-08) has no defined behavior; **consent revocation cascades to
  the lesson, not just the recording** (D-12); and **in-person mode has no chaperone-by-recording at
  all** (D-15), which is a different control set, not a lighter one.
- Consumes **Media Handling & Audio Playback** (global cross-cut) — recording storage, playback,
  retention. **Shared entity.** Note the failure path: a lost upload is a **safeguarding record gap**
  for a minor's lesson, not a media inconvenience — it must escalate, not retry-and-forget.
- Consumes **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** (global cross-cut) — **evidential dependency.** Join,
  leave, concurrent-presence, gate outcome, observer declaration and recording start/stop are
  **server-stamped** (D-09) because they are financial and safeguarding evidence, not telemetry. A
  client-stamped join log is forgeable by changing a system clock and decides who eats a credit.
- Consumes **Roles, Permissions & Delegated Authority** (global cross-cut) — **permission.** The
  guardian's read of the session record and their observer standing are delegated authority over the
  minor's account (domain D-03), not a persona (DT-10).
- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** (global cross-cut) — T−24h/T−2h gate pre-evaluation notices; the
  in-person path depends on these as its **only** enforceable control (D-15) and its fan-out includes
  the guardian as a third recipient, which the remote path does not require.
- Touches **06.01.03 Cancellation & No-Show** — **trigger dependency, one direction only.** Supplies
  the delivery predicate's output (`delivered` / `interrupted` / `abandoned` / `blocked` / `asserted`)
  plus attribution and evidence tier. This feature emits facts; 06.01.03 owns every ledger effect
  (D-06). Two new inputs for it: the **evidence ladder** refines its DT-02 ("no evidence at all" for
  in-person → *no automatic* evidence, with mutual confirmation available and materially stronger than
  assertion); and **cancellation is unavailable from the moment of concurrent presence** (D-17), the
  delivery-side counterpart of its cancel-vs-grace race.
- Touches **06.01.02 Credits** — **shared entity (the ledger).** Delivery is the event that consumes a
  reserved credit and accrues payout. `delivered` is the only trigger; a written record is not (D-04).
  Ledger drift at the door resolves **in the student's favour** and is reconciled afterwards.
- Touches **06.01.01 Slot Booking** — **state dependency.** The hard-stop boundary for an over-run is
  the teacher's next occurrence minus their buffer, both of which 06.01.01 owns (D-05). Also the
  booking-time half of the double gate (CX-07) — and for in-person, the booking-time gate is the
  **only** preventive control that exists.
- Touches **[06.03 Curriculum, Assignments & Practice](../06.03-curriculum-assignments-practice/)** —
  **the domain's most important seam** (domain CX-02). The session record is where a delivered lesson
  becomes an assignment, created in-room (D-01). DT-11 sharpens the seam: the record's skeleton is
  assembled from in-room actions, so **the quality of 06.03's input is a function of what this room
  witnesses** — which is why in-person delivery starves it (D-15).
- Touches **[06.02 Teacher Discovery, Profiles & Trials](../06.02-teacher-discovery-profiles-trials/)**
  — **surface dependency.** The trial→series conversion happens *in this room* (domain CX-01, 06.02.04
  DT-02). This feature owns the surface; 06.02.04 owns the conversion logic and the trial cap. A trial
  carries full safeguarding protections (domain D-05), so the gate does not soften for a first lesson.
- Touches **[06.10 Academy & Multi-Teacher Operations](../06.10-academy-multi-teacher-operations.md)**
  — **permission boundary.** 06.10's QA of staff teaching must not become an Operator content read
  (D-13, DT-12). QA observation is a consented per-occurrence participation. 06.10 also owns the
  academy room, which is the only place Tier-2 in-person attendance evidence exists.
- Touches **[24 Trust, Safety & Disputes](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)** — **escalation +
  notification fan-out.** Three distinct classes route here: (a) 1:1 adult↔minor contact incidents,
  the platform's highest-severity class; (b) safeguarding record gaps (lost recording, chaperone loss);
  (c) legal reach into an unscanned recording (D-16). Lesson recordings are **not** content-scanned —
  a deliberate divergence from the platform's general UGC posture, argued in D-16 and validated at Q-05.
- Touches **[23 Career, Finance & Business](../../23-career-finance-business/)** — payout accrues on
  `delivered`. Q-04 (does the platform compensate an `interrupted` lesson?) lands here as a cost line.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The session record and next assignment are captured **in the room**, not afterwards | Per DT-01 — the platform's own thesis, applied to teaching. Capture-at-source or the record is a reconstruction. |
| D-02 | The safeguarding gate **fails closed** with a specific, actionable reason | Per DT-03. An education product that opens an adult↔minor room on a stale check is an existential failure, not a bug (the cross-cut's own framing). A generic "unavailable" is a support ticket and an angry parent. |
| D-03 | A lesson does **not** mint a verified credit in domain 02 | Per DT-02 — it would debase the credit graph, the platform's most valuable asset. |
| D-04 | Failure to write a session record does **not** block credit consumption | The teacher taught; withholding their pay over paperwork is hostile and would push lessons off-platform. The fix for an unwritten record is capture design (D-01, D-07), not a payment gate. |
| D-05 | **Over-run is free**, and is hard-stopped by the next commitment — never by a charge | Per DT-14, resolving Q-04. A credit is an occurrence, not a duration (06.01.02 D-02). Room stays open until the earlier of (a) the teacher's next occurrence start minus their buffer, (b) scheduled end + 30 min. Warnings at end−5 (teacher), end+0 (both), hard-close−2 min (120s countdown, **named** reason). No automatic second charge, ever. |
| D-06 | **This feature emits facts; 06.01.03 decides ledger effects.** The delivery predicate is `concurrent server-stamped presence ≥ 50% of scheduled duration` | Resolves the sub-domain's open partial-credit question (CX-05 Q5). Outcomes: `delivered` / `interrupted` (fault evidenced) / `abandoned` (no fault) / `blocked` (gate) / `asserted` (in-person, with evidence tier). One published number, stated before booking — because an arbitrary threshold is unavoidable but a judgement call is not, and a support ticket is not a policy. |
| D-07 | An unwritten record escalates **privately, as a supply-quality signal** — never to a payment gate, never to a public signal, never to a streak | Per DT-13. 3 consecutive skips for the same student → private nudge; sustained pattern → Operator/platform signal. Domain law D-04's rationale is child-specific and so does not formally cover a professional adult — the ban on teacher-facing scaffolding is restated here so the gap is not used to reintroduce it. |
| D-08 | **Room access is identity-bound.** No link join, no anonymous join, no dial-in code | An unauthenticated join is not refused — it is not expressible. A link-shareable room with a child in it is the worst object this platform could ship. The cross-cut owns the pipe; this is the lock on the door (DT-03). |
| D-09 | Join, leave and presence are **server-stamped** | They are financial evidence (06.01.03 settles credits from them) and safeguarding evidence. A client-stamped log is forgeable by changing a system clock. Evidence, not telemetry. |
| D-10 | A guardian at a minor's lesson is a **declared observer** — in the presence list, visible to both parties, logged, no audio/video by default | Per DT-09. The market's most-used safeguarding control, modelled as nothing until now. One modelling decision closes three failure modes: it is also the escape hatch for D-11 and D-12. |
| D-11 | **Losing the recording during a minor's 1:1 loses the chaperone**, and the lesson ends unless a guardian observer is declared within 60s | Per DT-08. Follows necessarily from DT-04 + D-02: a gate that fails closed only at the door, while its control can evaporate at minute 20, is theatre. 30s detection → banner → 60s countdown → auto-end. Safeguarding event logged either way. |
| D-12 | **Revoking recording consent for a minor's 1:1 revokes the lesson**, not merely the recording | Follows necessarily from DT-04. Stated to the guardian **at revocation time, before confirming** — never discovered at the door. |
| D-13 | An Operator observing a lesson is a **consented, declared, per-occurrence participant** — never a role permission | Per DT-12. The Operator role grants occupancy and attendance, never content, at any tier. A standing silent content read over a child's lesson would make the platform the threat. |
| D-14 | The lesson room carries a **music-fidelity audio profile**, not a speech profile | Per DT-05. Speech DSP is disqualifying, not suboptimal — a teacher cannot hear tone through a noise gate, and hearing tone is the job. Hard requirement on the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut. |
| D-15 | **In-person is a second implementation with a different control set, not a lighter one.** A minor's in-person 1:1 requires a declared present adult **or** an academy room | Follows from DT-04: no recording ⇒ no chaperone. Also records the honest limit — an in-person lesson **cannot fail closed at the door** because the platform is not at the door. Its only preventive control is refusing the booking; everything after that is notification. Scope remains the owner's (Q-03), now costed rather than guessed. |
| D-16 | **Lesson recordings are not content-scanned** and carry no proactive takedown | Music education runs almost entirely on copyrighted material (domain Q-02 — three independent routes to the same finding), and a lesson recording is not published: two parties plus a guardian, never distributed. Content-ID here would break every lesson on the platform to protect nobody. Deliberate divergence from the general UGC posture; recordings stay reachable by incident/legal process (domain 24). **Legal validation is Q-05** — the behavior is decided, its defensibility is not this file's to certify. |
| D-17 | **Cancellation is unavailable from the moment of concurrent presence** | You cannot cancel a lesson you are in. The delivery-side counterpart of 06.01.03's cancel-vs-grace race (CX-02 Q5). Before concurrent presence, cancellation wins. |
