# Feature: Cancellation, Make-Up & No-Show Policy

> **Number**: 06.01.03
> **Parent**: [Lesson Booking, Packages & Delivery](./06.01-lesson-booking-packages-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The rule that decides who eats the cost of a lesson that didn't happen. A teacher selects a policy
(notice window, make-up entitlement, no-show treatment) from a bounded set of platform presets; the
platform enforces it automatically against the credit ledger owned by 06.01.02. This exists as its
own feature because it is the single most relationship-damaging conversation in tuition — "you
cancelled at 4pm, I'd already turned down a session for that slot" — and the entire point of
encoding it is that the platform, not the teacher, delivers the bad news.

It is not a settings screen (DT-01). It is a **state machine over the credit ledger with an evidence
model and a dispute path**, and its evidence model has two implementations, because a lesson at the
teacher's kitchen table leaves no join log (DT-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As Teacher**: selects a policy preset per rate-card mode (remote / their room / student's home — DT-11), tunes the bounded dimensions inside it (notice window within the preset's range, make-up cap, refill-reversal toggle), designates **make-up capacity** (specific slots or a make-up week) so entitlements are redeemable (DT-05), sees it enforced without ever asking, waives it for a single occurrence or a date range with one tap (goodwill is a feature, not a bug), sees the make-up ledger per student, sees their own private late-cancel rate. Cancelling a lesson they teach forces an **attribution choice** — "I can't make it" vs "my student told me they can't" — and the second is a contestable assertion, not a fact (D-09). **As Student**: sees the policy preset *before* booking and compares it across teachers because presets are comparable (DT-04); sees it again at cancellation time with the consequence priced in credits *before* confirming; cancels, requests a make-up, redeems against designated capacity, sees make-ups remaining and their expiry; disputes a burn. **Never** sees an attendance record or reliability score about themselves — deliberate, per domain D-04 (DT-12). |
| Producer | None | Not visible, and specifically **must not become visible by inference**. A Producer sharing a studio calendar with a teacher (06.01.01 Role Lens) sees a room block, not a lesson — and per R-05 the room hire is the teacher's separate transaction with the Operator, so cancelling a lesson does **not** release the room and does not leak a cancellation to the shared calendar (DT-11). |
| Operator | Config | As an academy: sets a **floor policy** that bounds how harsh their teachers may be (the direction is explicit — see D-06; a school's reputation is set by its worst teacher's cancellation behaviour), declares a **no-fault closure** that cascades to every lesson in the affected rooms (D-11), sees late-cancel and no-show rates for their staff, sees the make-up liability outstanding across the academy, and handles disputes escalated between a teacher and a parent before they reach domain 24. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no fan-facing entry point. A Fan who wants lessons is acting as a Musician (student) and is lensed as one from that moment (per 06.01.01). |

## Behavior

### The policy object

A policy is **a preset plus a small set of bounded dimensions** — never free-form prose (DT-04).

| Dimension | Setting | Behavior |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Preset | Flexible, Standard, or Strict | Platform-defined and fully specified. **Standard** is the platform default (Q-01). |
| Notice window | A number of hours within the preset's range | Flexible 6–24; Standard 12–36; Strict 24–48. Hard platform ceiling: **48h** — no policy may exceed it. |
| Make-up allowance | A whole number at or above the preset's minimum | Entitlements per pack (or per term for term-billed series). |
| Refill reversal | On or off; **on by default** | If the vacated slot is refilled *and delivered*, the original burned credit returns (DT-08). The replacement delivery has its own earning event; 06.01.02 Q-10 owns the disposition of any original accrual already in payout processing. |
| Mode | Remote, teacher's room, student's home, or academy | Policy is dimensioned per mode exactly as the rate card is (DT-11). `academy` is distinct because its delivery and safeguarding configuration differs from a teacher's room (06.01.04 D-15). Defaults to the same preset across all modes. |
| Teacher note | Free text, ≤ 500 chars | **Displayed, never executed.** A teacher may explain; the preset is what runs. |

Preset semantics:

| Preset | Notice window default | Cancel inside window | No-show | Availability |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|---------|--------------|
| `Flexible` | 12h | → make-up (uncapped within the pack) | → make-up | Any teacher |
| `Standard` (default) | 24h | → make-up if cap remains, else burn | → burn | Any teacher |
| `Strict` | 48h | → burn | → burn | **Gated: ≥ 20 lessons delivered on-platform** (D-08) |

Non-configurable in every preset — these are platform invariants, not defaults:

- A teacher-attributed cancellation **always** returns the credit, at any notice, and never consumes a make-up.
- A no-fault cancellation **always** returns the credit and consumes no make-up.
- A no-show is **never** auto-burned without evidence (D-05).
- A burn transfers the credit's value to the teacher as if delivered (D-12) — the policy's entire purpose is compensating a lost slot.

### The three-layer clamp

Generosity flows one way. Each layer may only be **more generous** than the one above it:

```
Platform bound   (ceiling: 48h notice; Strict gated; teacher-cancel always returns)
      ≥
Academy floor    (Operator-set; bounds their teachers' maximum harshness — D-06)
      ≥
Teacher policy   (preset + dimensions, clamped at authoring AND at booking)
```

A teacher may always be kinder than the floor. A teacher may never be harsher than it. The clamp is
evaluated **at booking**, so an academy tightening its floor does not retroactively bind live series
(CX-04 Q3), and the policy in force is the one pinned at booking, forever, for that series (D-02).

### Evaluation

The policy is deterministic: given the occurrence, the acting party, the moment it happened, whatever evidence exists, and the pinned policy, it settles the occurrence to exactly one outcome — **return** (credit back), **burn** (credit spent), **make-up** (credit preserved as a reschedule right), **no-fault** (nobody's fault, credit back, no signal), or **unsettled** (held pending assertion or dispute). Identical inputs always settle the same way — the outcome is never a judgement call.

It fires at exactly three moments:

1. **On cancellation** — synchronously, before the cancellation commits. Fail closed (D-07).
2. **At `scheduled_start + grace`** — the no-show evaluation. `grace = min(15 min, 50% × duration)`, floor 5 min. A 20-minute beginner's lesson gets 10 minutes, not 15.
3. **At `scheduled_end`** — the no-show mark is **provisional until then**. Joint presence of ≥ 5 continuous minutes before `scheduled_end` vacates any no-show mark and the occurrence settles as a normal (possibly partial) delivery (D-10).

The notice deadline is computed against the occurrence's **absolute instant (UTC)**, never wall-clock
arithmetic — 06.01.01 anchors occurrences to the teacher's local wall clock, so across a DST boundary
"24 hours before" is 23 or 25 real hours and a naive implementation silently moves the deadline under
the student's feet. The boundary instant itself is **inclusive and student-favourable**: cancelling
*at* the deadline is outside the window. The authoritative timestamp is server receipt, shown to both
parties in the audit log.

### Evidence — two implementations

| Mode | Evidence | Settlement |
|------|----------|-----------|
| **Remote** | Join logs from the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut, surfaced by 06.01.04 | Automatic. Student present + teacher absent resolves in the student's favour with no dispute needed. |
| **In-person** | **None exists** (DT-02) | Assertion + contest. The timer must not auto-burn — an auto-burn on no evidence is a guess with money attached (CX-03). Either party asserts; the counterpart has **72h** to contest; silence settles the assertion; contest escalates to domain 24. If **neither** party asserts anything within **7 days**, the credit **returns** (D-05) — the platform will not charge for a lesson it cannot evidence. |

The in-person default is deliberately teacher-adverse, and that is the incentive design: it makes the
teacher mark delivery, which is the record the platform wants anyway. This is **not** a contradiction
of R-03 / 06.01.04 D-04 (an unwritten session record must not gate pay) — see D-04 below for the
distinction between an attendance mark and paperwork.

### Make-ups

A make-up entitlement is a **capped right to reschedule a late-cancelled lesson without burning a
credit**. Its hard problem is that it is a claim on capacity that does not exist (DT-05): the
teacher's calendar is full of standing slots owned indefinitely by other students (06.01.01 D-01), so
the only redeemable slots are the ones nobody wanted.

- Entitlements redeem **only against designated make-up capacity** — slots or a make-up week the teacher explicitly marks. Music schools have run term-end make-up weeks for a century; this is the convention, not an invention.
- Validity: **60 days from grant, or end of current term + 30 days, whichever is later.** It **outlives the pack** (D-13) — the credit it replaced was already paid for.
- **A make-up that was never redeemable converts back to a returned credit at expiry** rather than lapsing (D-13). Forfeiting value the seller made unredeemable is indefensible on fairness and probably on consumer-protection grounds.
- **A make-up cannot generate a further make-up.** Late-cancelling a make-up burns it. Otherwise a student chains make-ups indefinitely.
- A teacher cancelling a make-up restores the entitlement and consumes no cap.

### Override

The teacher override always exists, is one tap, and moves in the **student's favour only**. It may be
applied prospectively to a date range ("my student has flu all week — waive the next three") or
retrospectively within **30 days** of a burn. Once granted it is **not revocable** — a teacher who
claws back a waiver is worse than one who never gave it. Every override is audit-logged as a
permission that altered a financial outcome (CX-02).

### Happy Path

1. Teacher publishes their rate card (06.01.02) and selects a policy preset per mode; `Standard` is pre-selected; the platform clamps it against any academy floor and confirms the effective policy in plain language
2. Student opens the teacher's profile and sees the policy card — comparable against other teachers because it is a named preset, not prose
3. Student books a Tuesday 17:00 recurring series (06.01.01); the policy is **pinned to the series at confirmation** and the student accepts it as part of confirming
4. Sunday: student opens Tuesday's occurrence and taps Cancel
5. System evaluates against the absolute instant — 49h notice, outside the 24h window — and shows the consequence **before** the confirm button: "Free cancellation. 1 credit returns — you'll have 8."
6. Student confirms; the cancellation commits, the credit returns, the occurrence is released and the slot reopens for that date only — **the series survives** (CX-04)
7. Both parties are notified with the ledger effect stated in credits, not status codes: "Tue 17:00 cancelled — 1 credit returned, 8 remaining"
8. The event is written to the Audit Log cross-cut as evidence, not merely as a balance delta

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Student cancels 2h before (inside window) | Policy fires: make-up if cap remains, else burn. Credit value transfers to teacher (D-12) | Student: consequence shown **before** confirming — "This uses your last make-up" / "This will use 1 credit". Never after |
| Teacher cancels late | Credit **always** returns; no make-up cap consumed; private reliability signal recorded | Student: "{Teacher} cancelled — your credit is safe, 8 remaining." Teacher: the reliability consequence, stated |
| Teacher cancels the entire series (quits) | Every future occurrence's reservation releases and credits return to balance. The **pack refund** question is 06.01.02's, not this feature's | Student: "{Teacher} has ended this series — 8 credits returned to your balance". Teacher: confirmation of the ledger effect across all affected students |
| Nobody joins a remote lesson | Grace elapses; **both-no-show** rule fires (D-03) — credit returns, no signal either side | Both: "Neither of you joined. Did this lesson move? Your credit is safe." |
| Student joined, teacher didn't (remote) | Join log evidences it; auto-resolves in the student's favour, no dispute needed | Student: "{Teacher} didn't join — credit returned" |
| Teacher joined, student didn't (remote) | Policy's no-show rule fires at grace | Both: "Marked as no-show at 17:15 — 1 credit used. Dispute this" |
| Teacher joins at +20 min after being marked a no-show, then teaches 40 min | The mark is **provisional until `scheduled_end`** (D-10). Joint presence ≥ 5 min vacates it; the occurrence re-settles as a delivery | Student: "{Teacher} joined — this lesson went ahead. Your credit was used after all." The reversal is stated, never silent |
| Nobody asserts anything on an in-person lesson | At 7 days the credit **returns** (D-05) — no evidence, no charge | Teacher: escalating prompts at 24h/72h/6d: "Did Tuesday's lesson happen? Confirm to get paid." Student: nothing until settlement |
| Teacher asserts an in-person student no-show | Contestable assertion. Credit is **held**, not burned, for a 72h contest window | Student/guardian: "{Teacher} says you missed Tuesday. That's right / That's wrong". Silence settles it |
| Student contests within 72h | Escalates to domain 24 with the (absent) evidence attached; credit held in limbo — neither returned nor spent | Both: dispute thread stating plainly that no join log exists for in-person lessons |
| Teacher cancels "on the student's behalf" (student texted them off-platform) | Attribution choice is forced (D-09). Recorded as a **student cancellation asserted by the teacher** — contestable, 72h window, student notified | Teacher: "Who's cancelling?" — never a single ambiguous Cancel. Student: "{Teacher} recorded that you cancelled Tuesday. That's right / That's wrong" |
| Student cancels at +14 min, inside the 15-min grace | **Cancellation wins the race** (D-14). Treated as a late cancel, not a no-show — telling us beats silence. Self-limiting: once the make-up cap is spent, a late cancel burns exactly like a no-show | Student: consequence priced before confirming. Teacher: "Amy cancelled at 17:14 — late cancel" |
| Student cancels and teacher cancels within the same second | First **server-received** cancellation wins; the second is a no-op, not an error. If the teacher's landed first, no student consequence and no reliability hit for the student's redundant tap | Loser: "{Teacher} had already cancelled this lesson — your credit is safe" |
| Student's slot is refilled and delivered after a late-cancel burn | Burn **auto-reverses** (DT-08). Fires on the replacement's *delivery*, not its booking — a replacement that itself cancels reverses nothing | Student: "Your slot was refilled — the credit you used has been returned." Teacher: "Tue 17:00 was refilled, so Amy's credit went back" |
| Teacher turns refill reversal off and double-collects | Permitted but bounded by the academy floor and surfaced. Flagged for `/create-prd-security` — a charge that survives mitigation of the loss looks like a penalty, not a genuine pre-estimate of loss | Student: the policy card states it explicitly at booking — "your credit is used even if the slot is refilled" |
| DST boundary between cancellation and lesson | Deadline computed on the **absolute instant**, so the window is exactly 24 real hours; only the *displayed* local time shifts | Student: deadline shown as an absolute local time with a live countdown, plus 06.01.01's DST notice |
| Cancellation submitted exactly at the deadline instant | Boundary is **inclusive and student-favourable** — this is outside the window, free | Student: "Free cancellation — just in time" |
| Client retries a cancellation after a timeout | Idempotent on the **occurrence**, not the request. Two submissions = one cancellation. Never double-burns, never double-returns | Student: the same confirmation, once |
| Cancelling an occurrence on a **suspended** series (teacher on tour) | **No-op** (D-15, resolving CX-04 Q5). Suspension already released the reservation; there is no credit to move | Student: "Your Tuesday slot is held until 12 Sep — there's nothing to cancel" |
| Cancelling an occurrence past a term bound | No-op — the occurrence was never materialized (06.01.01) | Student: "This series ends 18 Dec" |
| Academy closes the building (snow, flood, power) | Operator declares a **no-fault closure**; every lesson in the affected rooms cascades to no-fault automatically — credits return, no signals, no make-up caps consumed (D-11) | Both: "{Academy} is closed Tuesday — credit returned, nobody's fault" |
| Either party requests no-fault for a single lesson (bereavement, hospital) | Counterpart confirms → no-fault. Contested → domain 24. Not collusion-prone: the teacher forfeits income to grant it | Requester: one tap. Counterpart: "{X} has asked to treat Tuesday as no-fault. Agree?" |
| Make-up granted but the teacher has designated zero capacity | Entitlement is granted and **immediately shown as unredeemable**; at expiry it converts back to a returned credit (D-13) | Student: "You have 1 make-up — {Teacher} hasn't opened any make-up slots yet. If none open by 14 Sep it becomes a credit." Teacher: "Amy has a make-up she can't book — open some slots" |
| Student late-cancels a make-up lesson | The make-up **burns**. A make-up cannot generate a make-up | Student: shown before confirming — "This uses up your make-up for good" |
| Teacher cancels a make-up lesson | Entitlement restored, cap not consumed, validity window extended by the delay | Student: "{Teacher} cancelled — your make-up is back" |
| Pack expires while a make-up is outstanding | Make-up **outlives the pack** (D-13) — it repairs a lesson already paid for | Student: "Your pack has expired but your make-up is valid until 14 Sep" |
| Teacher's vetting expires; the safeguarding gate blocks the room | **Not a no-show, not a cancellation.** Gate failure is upstream of this feature; credit is not consumed (06.01.04 D-02, CX-05) | Both: 06.01.04's gated reason. This feature stays silent — a safeguarding block must never read as a policy consequence |
| Room/transport failure kills the lesson (06.01.04 Error) | Platform-caused → **no-fault**. Credit not consumed | Both: "your credit hasn't been used" — because a student who thinks a broken room cost them a lesson never books another |
| Lesson drops out mid-way (partial delivery) | 06.01.04 classifies the partial (**whose fault**); this feature prices it (D-16). Platform-caused → no-fault. Party-caused below 50% of duration → that party's late cancel. ≥ 50% delivered → full consumption | Both: the classification and the price, stated together |
| Minor cancels; guardian didn't want that | Cancellation is valid (D-03 delegated authority), guardian is notified of **every** ledger effect on a minor's account, and may **reverse** the minor's cancellation while the occurrence is still future and the slot not yet rebooked | Guardian: "Tom cancelled Thursday's lesson. Undo?" |
| Guardian cancels; the minor turns up anyway | The occurrence no longer exists; the room does not open. Not a teacher no-show | Student: "Your guardian cancelled this lesson" — never a generic "unavailable" |
| Teacher sets a 30-day notice window to farm burns | **Impossible.** The 48h platform ceiling and the preset system are the bound (DT-04) | Teacher: the field will not accept it — "48 hours is the maximum on WeJammin" |
| Brand-new account sets `Strict` and farms no-shows | **Gated** — `Strict` requires ≥ 20 delivered lessons on-platform (D-08). A fraudulent teacher has no delivery history | Teacher: "Strict unlocks after 20 delivered lessons. You're on 3." |
| Academy tightens its floor mid-term | Live series keep their pinned policy (D-02, CX-04 Q3); new bookings clamp | Teacher: "Your 48h window exceeds {Academy}'s 24h limit — new bookings will use 24h. Existing students are unaffected" |
| Teacher's policy is *more* generous than the academy floor | Permitted — the floor is a **ceiling on harshness**, not a mandate (D-06) | Teacher: no warning. Being kinder is never an error |
| Multi-hyphenate: teacher cancels a lesson they *teach* and one they *take*, same hour, same calendar | Two opposite consequences from one calendar. **The UI must never offer a single ambiguous Cancel** — the stance determines attribution (D-17) | "You're cancelling **the lesson you teach Amy** — her credit returns and this counts as a late cancellation by you" vs "You're cancelling **your lesson with Dave** — Dave's policy applies and this uses 1 of your credits" |
| Multi-hyphenate: A teaches B, and B teaches A; A late-cancels their lesson *with* B | A's teacher-side reliability is **untouched** — the signal attaches to the teaching stance, not the human (D-17) | A: student-side consequence only. A's own students see nothing |
| Serial late-canceller | Each occurrence settles independently. The **pattern** is 06.01.01's problem, not this feature's — it feeds 06.01.01 Q-01 (does the teacher reclaim a standing slot after N misses?) | Teacher: the make-up ledger tells the story without this feature judging it |
| Repeated both-no-shows on one series (≥ 2 in a rolling 30 days) | Series flagged as **possibly moved off-platform** (D-03). Two people independently forgetting the same standing appointment twice is not negligence — it is evidence our record is wrong | Both: "Has your Tuesday lesson moved? Your calendar here says 17:00." Not an accusation |
| Trial lesson cancelled (06.02.04) | Burns nothing — no credit exists to burn. The policy is inert | Both: plain cancellation, no ledger language |
| Group lesson cancelled (06.05) | **Out of scope.** The class runs regardless, so no slot is lost and the notice window is meaningless. 06.05 needs its own policy | — |
| Chargeback on a pack containing burned credits | Routed to the Payments cross-cut + domain 24 with the burn's audit-log evidence attached. This feature supplies evidence; it does not adjudicate | Both: domain 24's dispute surface |
| Cancellation fails to record (network/write failure) | **Fail closed** — the lesson stays booked (D-07). A silently-failed cancel that still burns a credit is the worst outcome available | Student: "Not cancelled — your lesson is still booked" + retry, with the notice deadline still counting down |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Policy fetch at cancel time | Skeleton with the occurrence's time and the notice countdown already fixed — the two facts money depends on never flicker |
| Empty | Teacher selected no policy | Platform **default preset** (`Standard`) applies and is **labelled as such** (D-03-original / D-03 below): "{Teacher} uses WeJammin's standard policy". A teacher with no policy must never mean "no rules" |
| Populated | Policy pinned to the series | Plain-language policy card (preset name + the three numbers that matter) + make-up counter + expiry |
| Error | Cancellation failed to record | "Not cancelled — your lesson is still booked" with retry and the live deadline. Fail closed |
| Partial | Cancelled; ledger effect not yet committed | "Cancelled — credit return processing". Resolves within the notification, not by the user refreshing |
| Disputed | Student contests a burn, or an in-person assertion is contested | Occurrence flagged; credit held in **limbo** — neither returned nor spent — pending domain 24 (CX-02) |
| Pending assertion | In-person lesson past `scheduled_end + grace`, nobody has asserted | Teacher: escalating "Did this happen? Confirm to get paid" at 24h/72h/6d. Student: silence until settlement or an assertion arrives |
| Unredeemable | Make-up granted, zero designated capacity in its window | Amber: "1 make-up — no slots open yet. Becomes a credit on 14 Sep if none appear" |
| Reversed | Refill delivered, or a provisional no-show vacated by late joint presence | The reversal stated explicitly with its reason. A silent reversal is as corrosive as a silent burn |
| Waived | Teacher override applied | "{Teacher} waived the policy for this lesson" — attributed to the teacher, because this is the one moment their generosity should be visible |
| No-fault | Closure, platform failure, or an agreed no-fault request | "Credit returned — nobody's fault". No signal on either party |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a settings screen and belongs folded into the rate card | Its apparent modesty — "a notice window" is one number | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **state machine over the credit ledger with a dispute path**, and it is where the money and the relationship both break. Folding it into a rate card hides an automatic financial consequence inside a pricing form. The evidence it needs (join logs) also crosses to 06.01.04, which a settings field cannot express. |
| DT-02 | A no-show can be determined from the room join log alone | Real-Time Rooms cross-cut records joins, so absence looks trivially detectable | ❌ REJECTED for the in-person case, which the sub-domain's Q-03 says is in scope. There is no join log for a lesson at the teacher's house — the platform has **no evidence** and must fall back on assertion + dispute. This means the no-show rule needs two implementations, not one, and a remote-only design silently assumes away half the market. |
| DT-03 | The policy's real function is emotional, not financial — it exists so the teacher never has to chase a student for money | Domain expertise: private teachers overwhelmingly under-enforce their own policies because enforcement damages the relationship they depend on | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it sets the design constraint: enforcement must be **automatic and attributed to the platform**, with the teacher's override in the student's favour always one tap away. A policy the teacher must manually invoke reproduces the exact problem it was meant to solve. This is the same "make the lazy path the correct path" principle `meta/personas.md` identifies for the Producer's split capture. |
| DT-04 | The teacher should **author** a policy freely; the platform executes whatever they write | The obvious reading of "a teacher sets their own terms", and how every teacher's website works today | ❌ REJECTED — three independent failures. (1) **Unenforceable**: a free-text policy ("please give me a heads up") cannot be executed by a machine, so the platform would be back to the teacher manually enforcing, which DT-03 identifies as the exact problem. (2) **Incomparable**: a student choosing between four teachers cannot compare four prose paragraphs, and the policy is a real purchase criterion. (3) **Unbounded**: free authoring permits "30 days' notice, no make-ups, no-show burns", which is a licence to collect for lessons never taught. The answer is **presets** — the same structure Airbnb converged on (Flexible/Moderate/Firm/Strict) for the same three reasons. A teacher may attach prose that is *displayed and never executed*. This reshapes Q-01 productively: the "default policy" is not a values essay, it is **which preset is pre-selected**. |
| DT-05 | A make-up entitlement is a promise the teacher frequently **cannot physically keep** | Domain expertise: the teacher's calendar is full of standing slots owned indefinitely (06.01.01 D-01), so the only slots free for a make-up are the ones nobody wanted. Real teachers' make-up credits pile up unredeemable and then expire | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the failure mode nobody designs for. An entitlement granted against capacity that does not exist is **worse than no entitlement**, because it was promised, and its expiry is experienced as a cheat. Two consequences, both concrete: (a) entitlements redeem only against **designated make-up capacity** — the term-end make-up week music schools have used for a century, made a first-class object rather than a convention; (b) a make-up that was never redeemable **converts back to a returned credit** at expiry rather than lapsing. Forfeiting value the seller made unredeemable is indefensible. This resolves Q-04. |
| DT-06 | Because the platform consolidates gigs (17) and teaching (06) onto one calendar, it is **the only party in the world that knows *why* a teacher cancelled** | The thesis (D-18): consolidation puts the platform in the room. 06.01.01 DT-03 confirmed that a working musician's teaching is structurally wrecked by gigs — so teacher-side late cancellation is not an edge case here, it is the **supply side's norm** | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a genuine payoff of the consolidation half of the thesis — but the value is in **prevention, not attribution**. The platform can see the collision at the moment the teacher accepts the gig in 17, which is hours or weeks before the student is let down. The correct product is the conflict warning at gig-acceptance time (already the CX 06.01.01 DT-03 identified), not a better post-hoc cancellation flow. Fix it upstream; the cancellation policy is the thing that runs when prevention has already failed. |
| DT-07 | …and therefore the platform should **tell the student the reason**: "your teacher cancelled because they took a gig" | Follows directly from DT-06, and it is honest — arguably the student is entitled to know | ❌ REJECTED, firmly. Three reasons. (1) It punishes precisely the multi-hyphenate behaviour the platform exists to serve — `meta/personas.md`'s central structural fact is that this human holds several roles simultaneously, and surfacing the trade-off between two of them weaponises consolidation against the user who consolidated. (2) It leaks a booking in domain 17 into domain 06 across a boundary the user never consented to cross. (3) It is relationship-destroying at exactly the moment DT-03 says the platform's job is to *absorb* the damage. Logged as a rejection because the inference is easy, the data is sitting right there, and a later reader will re-propose it. The teacher's reason, if any, is theirs to give. |
| DT-08 | If the teacher **refills** the vacated slot, the late-cancel burn should reverse | Nobody does this today because no teacher can be bothered to track it — but WeJammin owns the calendar and knows automatically | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is only implementable *because* of consolidation, which makes it a second, sharper payoff of the thesis than DT-06. The reasoning is not merely generosity: the policy's stated purpose is compensating a **lost slot**, and if the slot was not lost the charge is a windfall, not compensation. In several consumer-protection regimes a cancellation charge must approximate actual loss rather than operate as a penalty, and a charge that survives full mitigation of the loss is precisely a penalty — routed to `/create-prd-security` to confirm per jurisdiction rather than asserted here. Default **on**, per DT-03's principle: the platform delivers the fairness so the teacher does not have to be the generous one. Fires on the replacement's **delivery**, not its booking. |
| DT-09 | Both parties failing to show is a symmetric negligence case, and the hard part is deciding who to punish | The framing inherited from the breadth pass's unresolved marker on this file — "the naive rule (burn the credit) is wrong when the teacher was equally absent" | ✅ CONFIRMED that the naive rule is wrong, but the **framing was wrong too**, which is the finding. Two people independently forgetting the same standing weekly appointment, at the same time, is vanishingly rare. In practice both-no-show is a **miscommunication artifact** — a reschedule agreed over WhatsApp, a school holiday the platform never knew about, a term break, or a cancellation this feature's own Error state failed to record. So both-no-show is not evidence that two people were negligent; it is **evidence that the platform's record is wrong**. It should therefore be treated as a data-quality signal, not a punishment problem: credit returns, no signal on either party, and both are asked "did this lesson move?". Repeats (≥ 2 in a rolling 30 days on one series) flag the series as possibly off-platform — which per the thesis is churn, and the one thing the platform most needs to know. Resolves Q-03. |
| DT-10 | The in-person evidence gap (DT-02) is solved by **GPS check-in** — have both parties confirm arrival on their phones | The obvious engineering answer, and every field-service product does it | ❌ REJECTED, and it should be rejected loudly. It points a **location-tracking surface at a child in a stranger's house** to settle a £30 credit. The Safeguarding cross-cut would never sanction it, `meta/constraints.md` has no lawful basis for it, and the proportionality is grotesque — a location trail on minors as the price of arbitrating a small consumer dispute. The honest answer is that the evidence **does not exist**, and the design must be built on that fact rather than manufacture surveillance to escape it: assert, contest, and default to returning the credit when nobody asserts (D-05). A platform that cannot prove a lesson happened must not charge for it. |
| DT-11 | The notice window should be a single number per teacher | Every teacher's policy in the wild is one number, so it looks like the right shape | ❌ REJECTED — it under-compensates the in-person teacher and over-compensates the remote one, because their actual losses differ by an order of magnitude. A remote teacher who is late-cancelled loses an hour of income. An in-person teacher loses the income **plus** the room — and per R-05 the room hire is a **separate transaction** with the Operator that a lesson cancellation does not release, so the teacher is genuinely out of pocket — **plus** the travel, plus (for a student's-home lesson) the journey already begun. Policy must therefore be dimensioned by **mode**, exactly as 06.01.02's rate card already is, which keeps the two objects aligned rather than inventing a new axis. R-05 also incidentally closes a privacy question it was never written for: because the room stays booked, a cancelled lesson does **not** free the room, so a Producer sharing that studio calendar cannot infer a student's cancellation from a block disappearing. |
| DT-12 | Students should have an attendance record or reliability score, symmetric with the teacher's | Symmetry looks like fairness, and every two-sided marketplace rates both sides | ❌ REJECTED — it violates the domain's own law. Domain D-04 ("no motivation-by-scaffolding") was arrived at independently by four features on the grounds that **the student is frequently a child under existing parental pressure**, and a product adding a second, colder source of approval-and-failure is how a hobby becomes a chore and a child quits an instrument. An accumulating "you have missed 3 lessons" record is exactly that pattern wearing a marketplace costume. The asymmetry with the teacher is **deliberate and defensible**: the teacher is a supplier whose reliability is a market fact; the student is often an eleven-year-old. The policy's consequences are financial and immediate, not reputational and accumulating. Per the multi-hyphenate fact, the signal attaches to the **teaching stance**, not the human — the same person carries a reliability signal as a teacher and none as a student. |
| DT-13 | A teacher recording "my student cancelled — they texted me" is a fact the platform can act on | Teachers get told by text constantly; the flow must exist or the platform's record diverges from reality on day one | ❌ REJECTED **as a fact** — accepted as an **assertion**. The flow is necessary and must exist (without it, every off-platform cancellation is recorded as a *teacher* cancellation, which is both wrong and unfairly penalises the teacher). But a one-tap button that lets one party burn the other party's money on their unverified say-so is an obvious abuse surface and would be the first thing a bad actor found. So: every cancellation carries an **attributed actor**; a teacher cancelling on a student's behalf notifies the student, holds the credit rather than burning it, and settles only after a **72h** silent-contest window. Silence settles; contest escalates. This is the same shape as the in-person no-show assertion, which is not a coincidence — both are one party asserting a fact about another with money attached, and that shape should have one implementation. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** (global cross-cut) — cancellation confirmations, consequence-before-confirm, no-show marking, in-person assertion prompts at 24h/72h/6d, make-up expiry at 30/7/1 days, guardian notification of every ledger effect on a minor's account. Fan-out is **both parties on every ledger effect**, stated in credits, never in status codes (CX-02).
- Consumes **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** (global cross-cut) — every burn, return, conversion, override and reversal is an **evidential** event, not a balance delta, because every dispute in this sub-domain is ultimately a dispute about one of these writes. The override is specifically a *permission that altered a financial outcome* and must be logged as such.
- Consumes **Safeguarding & Minor Protection** (global cross-cut) — a gate failure is **upstream** of this feature and must never read as a policy consequence (a blocked room is not a no-show, and consumes nothing). Also the reason DT-10 is rejected.
- Consumes **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** (global cross-cut) — a burn **transfers value to the teacher** (D-12), which is a payout event this feature triggers but does not own. Note the interaction with 06.01.02's escrow-vs-pay-on-purchase question (its Q-02): if money was released at pack purchase, a burn moves nothing and a *reversal* (DT-08) has nothing to claw back.
- Consumes **Localization, Currency & Timezone** (global cross-cut) — the notice deadline is an absolute instant rendered per viewer; the DST hazard is this feature's sharpest silent bug.
- Touches **06.01.02 Packages, Credits & Rate Cards** — *shared entity*. This feature is the **primary writer** to that credit ledger and the only place credits are destroyed. It also borrows the rate card's **mode dimension** for its own (DT-11) and depends on its term/expiry model for make-up validity.
- Touches **06.01.01 Slot Booking & Recurring Series** — *trigger dependency, both ways*. Cancellation releases the occurrence and reopens the slot **without dissolving the series** (CX-04); the policy is pinned at booking from the series; a suspended series makes cancellation a no-op (D-15); the **refill reversal** (DT-08) depends on 06.01.01's slot-refill event; and repeated late cancels feed 06.01.01 Q-01 (slot reclaim), which this feature deliberately does **not** answer.
- Touches **06.01.04 Lesson Delivery & Session Record** — *evidence dependency*. Join logs settle contested remote no-shows (DT-02); 06.01.04 **classifies** a partial delivery and this feature **prices** it (D-16); the provisional-no-show vacation (D-10) reads its presence data. The seam is strict: 06.01.04 owns the fact, this feature owns the cost.
- Touches **[24 Trust, Safety & Disputes](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)** — *escalation*. A contested burn, a contested in-person assertion, and a contested no-fault request all escalate out of this domain with the audit trail (or its documented absence) attached.
- Touches **Reviews, Ratings & Portable Reputation** (global cross-cut) — *notification/permission*. Teacher-side late cancellation is a reliability signal; whether it surfaces **publicly** is Q-02. Student-side reliability is **rejected outright** (DT-12) — a hard boundary this cross-cut must respect.
- Touches **[17 Live Booking & Settlement](../../17-live-booking-settlement/)** — *trigger dependency, and the domain's most interesting one*. The teacher is a gigging musician; a gig accepted in 17 is the **structural cause** of teacher-side cancellation here (DT-06). The correct integration is a conflict warning at gig-acceptance time, **not** a reason surfaced at cancellation time (DT-07).
- Touches **[16 Venues, Studios & Spaces](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)** — *shared entity, negative*. Per R-05 the room hire is the teacher's separate transaction; a lesson cancellation does **not** release the room. This is why in-person losses are larger (DT-11) and why no cancellation leaks to a shared studio calendar.
- Touches **[06.05 Group Lessons, Workshops & Masterclasses](../06.05-group-lessons-workshops-masterclasses.md)** — *boundary*. This policy is **1:1-scoped**. A cancelled group lesson loses no slot (the class runs), so the notice window is meaningless and 06.05 needs its own model.
- Touches **[06.02 Teacher Discovery, Profiles & Trials](../06.02-teacher-discovery-profiles-trials/)** — *permission/display*. The policy preset is a **purchase criterion** shown on the teacher's profile and comparable across teachers (DT-04). A cancelled **trial** burns nothing — no credit exists (06.02.04).
- Touches **[06.10 Academy & Multi-Teacher Operations](../06.10-academy-multi-teacher-operations.md)** — *permission*. The academy floor policy (D-06) and the no-fault closure cascade (D-11) are Operator powers exercised here but owned there.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Enforcement is **automatic and platform-attributed**; the override is teacher-initiated and student-favouring only | Per DT-03 — the feature's purpose is removing the teacher from the enforcement conversation. |
| D-02 | The policy in force is the one **attached at booking time**, not the teacher's current policy | A retroactively-changed policy is a trust catastrophe and probably unenforceable anyway. |
| D-03 | A teacher with no policy gets the **platform default preset**, labelled as such | "No policy" must never mean "no rules" — the ambiguity always resolves against the student at the worst moment. |
| D-04 | Consequences are shown **before** confirming a cancellation, priced in credits | Per DT-03 — surprise is the thing that damages the relationship. |
| D-05 | **No auto-burn without evidence.** An unasserted in-person lesson returns the credit at 7 days | Per DT-02/DT-10 and CX-03: an auto-burn on no evidence is a guess with money attached. The teacher-adverse default is the incentive design — it makes the teacher mark delivery, which is the record the platform wants. **This is not R-03/06.01.04 D-04 in disguise**: those forbid gating pay on *paperwork* (the session record — content). An attendance mark is not paperwork, it is the delivery event itself. For remote lessons the platform observes it and asks nobody; for in-person it cannot observe it, so someone must assert it, and absent any assertion there is no delivery event to pay against. |
| D-06 | The academy floor is a **ceiling on harshness**, not a mandate — a teacher may always be more generous | The direction was ambiguous in the original text ("cannot undercut"). Resolved explicitly: the floor protects **students** from a harsh teacher, per the domain index's own rationale that a school's reputation is set by its worst teacher's cancellation behaviour. Generosity flows one way through the three-layer clamp. |
| D-07 | Cancellation **fails closed** — a write failure leaves the lesson booked | A silently-failed cancel that still burns a credit is the worst outcome available (CX-02). |
| D-08 | The `Strict` preset is **gated behind ≥ 20 delivered lessons** on-platform | Closes the abuse vector DT-04 identifies: a harsh policy plus no intention to teach is a licence to collect. A fraudulent account has no delivery history. Falls out of the presets design for free. |
| D-09 | Every cancellation carries an **attributed actor**, and a teacher cancelling on a student's behalf is a **contestable assertion**, not a fact | Per DT-13. The flow must exist (teachers get texts) but must not be a one-tap button to burn someone else's money on an unverified say-so. Same shape as the in-person assertion — deliberately one implementation. |
| D-10 | A no-show mark is **provisional until `scheduled_end`**; ≥ 5 min of joint presence vacates it | A lesson that actually happened must not be settled as one that didn't, merely because it started 20 minutes late. The reversal is always stated, never silent. |
| D-11 | **No-fault** is a first-class settlement class, not an override | Closures, platform failures and agreed exceptional circumstances are neither party's fault, and forcing them through "teacher cancelled" would record a reliability signal that is a lie. Operator closure cascades automatically to every lesson in the affected rooms. |
| D-12 | A burned credit **transfers its value to the teacher** as if the lesson were delivered | The policy's entire purpose is compensating a lost slot; a burn that compensates nobody is a forfeiture with no beneficiary and no defence. Makes Q-05 (does the platform take its cut of a lesson that didn't happen?) live and unavoidable. |
| D-13 | Make-ups **outlive the pack**, carry their own validity window (60 days from grant, or term end + 30 days, whichever is later), and **convert back to a returned credit if they were never redeemable** | Per DT-05. Resolves Q-04. A make-up repairs a lesson already paid for, so killing it with the pack takes value twice; an indefinite liability is unacceptable, so it gets its own bounded window; and value the seller made unredeemable must not be forfeited. |
| D-14 | **Cancellation beats the grace timer.** A cancellation received before the no-show evaluation commits is a late cancel, not a no-show | Resolves CX-02 Q5. Telling us beats silence — a student who bothered to say something at +14 min behaved better than one who vanished, and the rule should say so. Not exploitable: the make-up cap self-limits it, and once spent a late cancel burns exactly like a no-show. |
| D-15 | Cancelling an occurrence on a **suspended series** is an idempotent **no-op**, not an error | Resolves CX-04 Q5. Suspension already released the reservation; a user cancelling something already gone has achieved their intent. Idempotency keys on the **occurrence**, not the request, so retries never double-burn or double-return. |
| D-16 | 06.01.04 **classifies** a partial delivery; this feature **prices** it | The seam: they own the fact (what happened, whose fault), we own the cost. Mapping: platform-caused → no-fault; party-caused below 50% of duration → that party's late cancel; ≥ 50% delivered → full consumption. Stated here so 06.01.04's unresolved partial-credit gap has a rule to point at without this feature reaching into their file. |
| D-17 | Reliability signals attach to the **teaching stance**, not the human | The multi-hyphenate fact (`meta/personas.md`) taken seriously: the same person teaches on Tuesday and takes a lesson on Thursday. Late-cancelling as a student must not touch their teacher-side signal. Consequently a single ambiguous "Cancel" is forbidden where both stances are on one calendar — the UI must name the stance and its consequence. |
| D-18 | Both modes (remote **and** in-person) are specified here, ahead of the scope decision | Q-06 at domain level / Q-03 at parent level is a live scope question owned by the User. Specifying both makes that decision a **cut** rather than a **write** — deleting the in-person path later costs nothing; discovering it was never designed costs a re-spec. Per `boundary-not-placeholder.md`, "this is a lot of work" is not a reason to defer what can be specified. |
