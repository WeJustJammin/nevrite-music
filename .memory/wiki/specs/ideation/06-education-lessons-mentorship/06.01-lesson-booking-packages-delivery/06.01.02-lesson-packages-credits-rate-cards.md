# Feature: Lesson Packages, Credits & Rate Cards

> **Number**: 06.01.02
> **Parent**: [Lesson Booking, Packages & Delivery](./06.01-lesson-booking-packages-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How tuition is priced and paid for. Teachers publish a rate card (per-instrument, per-duration,
per-mode, per-level) and sell **packages** — "10 lessons for £350" — which the student draws down as
credits, one per delivered occurrence. This is the single sharpest divergence from Services
Marketplace (05): a service is escrowed once against a deliverable and released on acceptance,
whereas a lesson pack is money paid up front against a *relationship*, spent gradually, and governed
by expiry and refund rules that a one-off escrow has no concept of.

**This feature owns the credit ledger** — the shared state of the whole sub-domain. 06.01.01 reserves
against it, 06.01.03 burns or returns against it, 06.01.04's delivery record is what proves a draw was
earned. The invariant this file exists to protect is that every credit has exactly one authoritative
balance that no operation can drive negative or double-count — one lesson's credit is spent once and
only once. That guarantee is why these four features are one sub-domain (parent D-01): they all read
and mutate the same balance, so they must agree on what a mutation means.

## Behavior

### The rate card

A rate card is per-teacher (or per-academy) and dimensioned by the things that actually change a
lesson's price: **instrument × duration × mode × level**. Each combination is a **line**. A line has a
list price in the teacher's declared currency. A teacher who teaches guitar and piano but has priced
only guitar cannot be booked for piano — the rate card is a hard precondition for the calendar
(edge case 18), which is why this feature's Empty state blocks 06.01.01 entirely.

Publish-time validation (all fail closed, all with the offending value echoed back):

- List price > 0, and within the Operator's floor/ceiling if the teacher publishes under an academy
- A package's effective per-lesson price (`pack price ÷ credits`) must be **≤ the line's list price**
  and > 0 — a "discount" that costs more than buying singly is blocked (edge case 19)
- Package expiry window ≥ `max(90 days, credits × 14 days)` and ≤ 24 months. The floor stops a
  teacher selling 10 lessons that expire in a fortnight; the ceiling stops an unmanageable forward
  liability (edge case 20)

### What a credit is

A credit is **one lesson on one rate-card line, at the price paid** (D-02). It is not a sum of money
and not fungible across lines (D-07): 30-minute credits do not merge into a 60-minute lesson, because
two 30-minute slots are not one 60-minute slot on the teacher's calendar once buffers are counted
(06.01.01 D-02's availability rule owns the buffer). Credits are teacher-scoped or academy-scoped,
never platform-wide (D-01).

**Credit lifecycle** — the ledger's state machine. Every transition is an Audit Log event with the
actor, the trigger and the resulting balance, not merely a balance delta:

```
granted ──reserve (06.01.01 confirm)──> reserved
reserved ──deliver (06.01.04)─────────> consumed   [EARNING EVENT]
reserved ──policy burn (06.01.03)─────> burned     [EARNING EVENT]
reserved ──policy return (06.01.03)───> granted
reserved ──gate fail / room fail──────> granted    (never an earning event, never a no-show)
granted  ──expiry window elapses──────> lapsed ──> residual (D-04)
granted  ──chargeback / dispute───────> frozen ──> granted | destroyed
reserved ──chargeback / dispute───────> frozen     (occurrence suspended, not cancelled)
granted  ──refund──────────────────────> refunded (to the PURCHASER — D-08)
lapsed   ──redeem at current list──────> consumed  (student tops up the delta)
```

There is no `half-consumed`. A credit is atomic; an over-running lesson (06.01.04 Q-04) burns zero or
one additional credit, never a fraction — the ledger cannot express anything else, and that constraint
is this feature's contribution to their open question.

### The two earning events, and the thing that is not one

The teacher's payout accrues on **delivery** (06.01.04) *or* on a **policy burn** (06.01.03), never on
purchase (D-06). A burn is an earning event because the teacher sold the *slot*, held it, and could
not resell it — that is the entire reason a notice window exists (DT-04). Money moves once (purchase);
the ledger moves twice per lesson; the teacher earns on the second move.

An **expiry lapse is not an earning event** (D-05). Nothing was scheduled, no slot was held, no
teacher time was consumed. Conflating burn and lapse — both of which look like "the student didn't
take the lesson" — would pay a teacher for time they never set aside and would let the passage of time
transfer money. They are distinct ledger events with distinct audit entries and distinct user copy.

### Expiry: what actually expires

**Expiry expires the rate lock, not the value** (D-04). At the expiry instant an unspent credit does
not vanish. It becomes `lapsed` and carries a **residual**: the cash paid for it (`pack price ÷
credits`), denominated in the **purchase currency at the purchase-time FX rate**, still scoped to that
teacher. A lapsed credit remains redeemable against the teacher's *current* list price for the line;
if the rate has risen, the student tops up the delta before booking.

This is the honest reading of what the expiry was ever for: it bounds the teacher's obligation to
honour a 2026 price in 2029. It was never for confiscating a parent's money. The design is deliberately
written to survive Q-01 in either direction — under "money", nothing was destroyed; under
"entitlement", the teacher's forward exposure at a stale rate is bounded, which was the legitimate
need. **What Q-01 still decides is narrowed to one thing: whether residual is withdrawable as cash or
redeemable-only.** Everything else is settled here.

Warnings fire at **30 / 7 / 1 days** before expiry, to both the student and the purchaser when they
differ.

### Terms, subscriptions and packs are the same thing (resolves Q-03)

A **monthly plan** ("4 lessons/month") is a pack with `credits = 4` and `expiry = billing period + 7
days' grace`, granted on each billing date by the Subscriptions cross-cut. A **term fee** — how UK and
European private teaching and academies overwhelmingly actually bill (DT-07) — is a pack with
`credits = weeks in term` and `expiry = the term bound the Operator publishes` (06.01.01 owns term
bounds). No second ledger, no second cancellation policy, no second dispute path. The seam: the
Subscriptions cross-cut owns billing, dunning and the grant event; this feature owns what the granted
credit *means*, when it burns, and what its lapse does.

The expiry floor above (`≥ 90 days`) is waived for subscription-shaped packs — a monthly grant is by
construction a 1-month pack — precisely *because* D-04 means a monthly credit that goes unused
degrades to residual rather than evaporating. A use-it-or-lose-it monthly plan would be indefensible;
this one is not.

### Purchaser ≠ student

The pack records a **purchaser** and a **student**, which are the same party for an adult buying their
own lessons and different for every minor in the domain. Refunds, residual and price-change notices
route to the **purchaser** (D-08). The student sees the balance and the expiry; the purchaser sees the
balance, the expiry, the money and the refund. Two purchasers (separated guardians) buying for one
student produce two packs with two residuals that never commingle (edge case 26).

### Balance pressure on the standing slot

When available credits reach **≤ 2**, or when reserved occurrences would exhaust the balance within
**21 days** at the series' cadence — whichever fires first — the ledger raises an **at-risk** signal on
the series and prompts a top-up. This feature owns the signal and its timing. Whether a zero balance
**suspends** or **releases** the standing slot is 06.01.01's call (its Q-01, CX-06) and is not decided
here; the ledger's contribution is that the teacher's forward book and the student's slot risk are both
visible before the balance hits zero, not after.

### Teacher departure

- **Voluntary** — signalling "stop teaching" halts new pack sales for that teacher immediately.
  Existing series run to the earlier of credits-exhausted or **30 days**. Remaining credits enter the
  remedy flow.
- **Involuntary** — a domain-24 ban, a lapsed vetting check, or a death — **no notice**, remedy flow
  opens the same day. There is no actor to serve a notice period to.
- **Remedy flow** — the student (or purchaser) chooses within 30 days: (a) reassignment to another
  teacher under the same academy (academy-scoped packs only), (b) refund of the unearned residual to
  the purchaser, or (c) platform-mediated transfer to a chosen teacher, re-priced to that teacher's
  list. No response within 30 days defaults to **(b) refund** — the only option that is safe under
  both readings of Q-01. Already-delivered lessons are never clawed back from the teacher; they taught.

Accrual-on-delivery (D-06) is what makes all of this survivable: the money for undelivered lessons has
not left the platform, so the student can be made whole without recovering anything from a teacher who
has gone.

### Happy Path

1. Teacher publishes a rate card: `guitar × 60min × remote × any level = £45`, plus a package
   `10 credits, £380 (£38/lesson, 16% off), expires in 6 months`. The pack builder shows them the
   liability they are creating: **10 hours committed, effective £38/hr against a £45 list, last
   redeemable 16 Jan 2027** (DT-08)
2. Student opens the teacher's profile, sees the line and the pack side by side with both per-lesson
   prices, and the expiry stated as a date
3. Student buys the pack; the Payments cross-cut captures the payment, deduplicated so a repeated tap
   cannot charge twice
4. Credits — all 10, scoped to that teacher on that line — appear the moment capture succeeds; the
   student never waits on a later batch. The grant pins the pack's price, tax treatment, currency, FX
   rate and cancellation policy, and confirms to student **and** purchaser
5. Student books "Tuesdays 17:00, weekly" (06.01.01); confirming each occurrence **reserves** one
   credit — the balance chip reads "9 available · 1 reserved for Tue 5pm", never "10"
6. Lesson is delivered (06.01.04); the reserved credit becomes **consumed**, the teacher's earning
   accrues, and the amount appears in their income picture (domain 23)
7. Balance falls to 2 available; the at-risk signal fires with a top-up prompt naming the date the
   standing slot's next unfunded occurrence falls

| 28 | A teacher holds student credits with their own mentor and liability to their own students | **Two ledgers, never netted.** Their teaching earnings do not auto-fund their own lessons; their outstanding liability is not offset against their income | Two distinct balances, two distinct surfaces, no combined figure anywhere. ### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Balance / rate card fetch | Skeleton rate table, with the line count fixed so the layout doesn't jump |
| Empty | Teacher has no rate card | Teacher: "Set your rates to accept bookings" — **blocks booking entirely**, this is a hard precondition. Student: "This teacher hasn't published rates" |
| Populated | Rate card live, credits held | Rate table + balance chip: "7 available · 1 reserved for Tue 5pm · expires 4 Mar" |
| Pending grant | Payment captured, credits not yet granted | "Payment received — your credits are arriving." Retry state, never a failure state (edge case 3) |
| Error | Payment declined | "Payment didn't go through — your slot is held for 15 minutes" with retry and the reason from the payment cross-cut |
| Partial | Credits reserved, not yet consumed | "7 available · 1 reserved for Tue 5pm" — available and reserved are always two numbers, never one |
| Expiring | Pack within the 30/7/1-day warning window | Amber chip: "3 lessons expire 4 Mar — use them, or they drop to £114 credit at current rates". Fires to student and purchaser |
| Lapsed | Expiry window elapsed with credits unspent | "3 lessons' credit (£114) is still yours with {teacher}. The pack price no longer applies — book at £45" (D-04). **Never** "your credits have expired" |
| Frozen | Chargeback or dispute open on the pack | "This pack is on hold while your bank's dispute is reviewed." Unspent credits unbookable; reserved occurrences suspended, not cancelled |
| At risk | Available ≤ 2, or reserved occurrences exhaust the balance within 21 days | "Your Tuesday slot is funded to 26 Aug — top up to keep it" with the specific date, not a percentage |
| Winding down | Teacher signalled departure | Student: "{Teacher} is stopping on 15 Aug — 6 lessons remain." Teacher: no new pack sales, liability visible |
| Remedy | Departure notice elapsed, or involuntary departure | Purchaser: three named options (reassign / refund / transfer) with a 30-day deadline and the default (refund) stated up front |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Lesson packs can reuse the Services Marketplace (05) escrow model | The two domains are adjacent by design and both involve a musician being paid | ❌ REJECTED — decisively, and this is the strongest structural evidence for the domain's independent existence (it is exactly the contrast the domain rationale says "must be legible"). Escrow releases against **acceptance of a deliverable**. A lesson has no deliverable and no acceptance event — nobody "accepts" a piano lesson. Forcing tuition through escrow yields either a hostage release step nobody performs or an auto-release that makes escrow decorative. Packs need a **credit ledger with expiry**, which escrow does not have. |
| DT-02 | Prepaid lesson credits are a financial-regulatory question, not a UX one | Domain expertise: prepaid stored value is regulated in several jurisdictions; music schools have been caught by exactly this on closure | ✅ CONFIRMED — and escalated. If credits are refundable stored value the platform holds a liability on its balance sheet and may touch e-money/prepaid rules; if they are expiring entitlements, consumer-protection law limits forfeiture. Either way this is a `/create-prd-security` compliance input, and `meta/constraints.md` currently has no line for it. Raised as Q-01 on the parent index. **Narrowed by D-04**: since expiry no longer destroys value, Q-01 now decides only whether residual is withdrawable as cash. |
| DT-03 | Credits should be platform-wide (spendable with any teacher), because that's better for students | Agent's own first instinct — a portable wallet is obviously friendlier | ❌ REJECTED — it cannot price. Teacher A charges £30 and teacher B £70; a portable "credit" would either be a currency (making the platform a bank, per DT-02) or would silently transfer value between teachers. Credits are teacher-scoped. The genuinely hard case this leaves — an academy where students rotate between teachers — is handled by scoping the pack to the **academy** rather than the individual, which is why the academy rate card exists. |
| DT-04 | A cancellation **burn** and an expiry **lapse** are the same thing to the ledger — both are "the student didn't take the lesson" | The naive read; both reduce the balance without a lesson happening, so both look like one event with two triggers | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is load-bearing. A burn is an **earning event**: the teacher sold the *slot*, held it, turned down a session for it, and could not resell it at 4pm — that is the entire reason a notice window exists (06.01.03 DT-03). A lapse is **not**: nothing was scheduled, no time was set aside, no teacher cost was incurred. Merging them pays a teacher for time they never reserved and lets the passage of time transfer money — which is precisely the behaviour DT-02 says a regulator will look at. **The domain expert's framing that makes this obvious: teachers sell the slot, not the lesson.** Everything in 06.01.03 follows from that, and so does the fact that CX-05's "delivery is what earns the credit" is *incomplete* — there are two earning events, and the CX names one. Recorded as D-05/D-06. |
| DT-05 | The expiry window exists to let the teacher keep unearned money | Reading the mechanism at face value — "use it or lose it" is how the market words it, and forfeiture is the obvious beneficiary | ❌ REJECTED — the mechanism is real, the stated purpose is a rationalisation. What a teacher genuinely needs bounding is **the obligation to honour a 2026 price in 2029**: a student who buys 10 lessons, vanishes for three years, and returns expecting their old Tuesday at the old rate is the actual harm. That is a **rate-lock** problem, and rate-lock is all the expiry needs to solve. Forfeiture is a windfall the mechanism happens to permit, not the need it answers — and it is the half that attracts consumer-protection law and produces the "music school closed and kept our money" headline that DT-02 flags. So: **expiry expires the rate lock, not the value** (D-04). This is the file's most consequential finding — it dissolves most of Q-01 by making the answer not matter, which is strictly better than guessing at it. |
| DT-06 | Accrual-on-delivery is a student-protection measure | The obvious read — it protects the student against the teacher who vanishes mid-pack (parent Q-02's framing) | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is **also** the platform's anti-money-laundering property, which nobody has mentioned and which is the stronger argument. A marketplace that pays a vendor on purchase for an intangible, unverifiable, unshippable "service" is a laundering primitive: two colluding accounts, £5k of lessons, zero delivered, clean money out. Accrual-on-delivery starves it — money that never funds a delivered lesson never reaches the teacher. `meta/constraints.md`'s compliance table carries an unresolved KYC/AML line for exactly this class of exposure, and tuition is its most abusable instance because the "goods" are invisible. **This reframes parent Q-02 from a teacher-preference question ("paying up front is what teachers actually want") into a risk-control question with a right answer.** The genuinely open half is narrower: whether any portion is advanced, at what cadence the accrued balance pays out, and whether unearned funds must be segregated (Q-08). |
| DT-07 | The pack ("10 lessons for £350") is the market's pricing unit | The candidate name says "packages", and it is how US/online teacher marketplaces bill | ❌ REJECTED as *the* unit, ✅ CONFIRMED as *a* unit — and the gap it exposes is the thing a music-education domain expert would ask first. UK and European private teaching, and essentially every academy, bills **by the term**: a fixed number of weeks, invoiced in advance, aligned to the school calendar, with the summer unbilled. A product that only knows packs cannot express "autumn term, 12 weeks, £540, ends 18 December" and will simply not be adopted by the academies that 06.10 DT-03 identifies as the domain's only tractable cold-start strategy. The finding is that **a term is a pack whose expiry is pinned to the Operator's term bound** (06.01.01 already owns term bounds) — so the ledger needs no new concept, but the pack builder needs to know terms exist and offer them, and the expiry floor must not block a 12-week term. Same resolution absorbs the monthly subscription (Q-03). D-09. |
| DT-08 | The pack discount is a pricing choice the teacher makes competently | Standard marketplace assumption — the seller sets the price, the market corrects them | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the failure mode nobody designs for. A 12%-off 10-pack with a 12-month expiry is **selling next winter's Tuesday at last spring's price**, in a job where the seller's costs rise and their time is the only inventory. Private music teachers are, as a population, not financially sophisticated — the persona is a working musician assembling income from fragments, not a revenue manager — and the product will cheerfully let them commit a year of Tuesdays at a discount and then discover it. Every marketplace shows the seller the *revenue*; almost none shows them the **forward liability**. So the pack builder must state, before publish: **hours committed, effective hourly rate against list, and the last date the final credit could be redeemed.** This is also the number the Operator needs in aggregate (their liability report) and the number that makes edge case 8 non-catastrophic — a teacher who can see their liability doesn't accidentally sell a year of it. Cheap to build, and it is the difference between a rate card and a pricing tool. |
| DT-09 | The in-person scope question (parent Q-03) is a safeguarding question | It is framed that way everywhere it appears — a physical adult↔minor meeting is a heavier surface than a recorded room | ✅ CONFIRMED but materially incomplete — **it is also a financial question, and that half is undefended.** 06.01.03 DT-02 establishes that in-person lessons have no join log and therefore no evidence. Follow the money: a chargeback on a partly-spent in-person pack is **undefendable** — the platform cannot produce evidence that a single lesson happened, so friendly fraud ("I never got the lessons") wins by default, and it wins for the *whole pack*, including the eight lessons the student demonstrably took. The exposure scales with pack size, which is the product's own recommended purchase. This turns parent Q-03 from a scope-and-safeguarding decision into one with a loss line attached, and suggests in-person needs either a countersigned attendance mechanism (student confirms in-app; teacher's assertion alone is not evidence), a lower pack ceiling, or a different fee. **Neither this file nor 06.01.03 can decide it — but nobody had priced it.** Q-05. |
| DT-10 | Since the departure remedy proves credit transfer between teachers is mechanically possible, it should be offered as a general feature | The mechanism exists once built; withholding it looks like artificial friction, and students would obviously want it | ❌ REJECTED — the mechanism is a **remedy**, not a product, and generalising it walks straight back into DT-03. A student-initiated transfer at will means shopping with a specific teacher's money: teacher A's rate card priced the pack, teacher B never agreed to it, and re-pricing at every hop makes the credit a denominated bearer instrument — a currency — which DT-02 says makes the platform a bank. The remedy survives that objection only because it is involuntary (the student did not choose to lose their teacher), one-time, platform-mediated, and re-priced at the destination. Logged because the pull toward it is strong and a later reader **will** re-propose it on exactly the reasoning above. D-12. |
| DT-11 | Packs should auto-renew by default, to protect the teacher's forward book and stop the standing slot lapsing over an admin failure | Genuinely good for retention, genuinely good for teachers, and it is what every subscription product does | ❌ REJECTED as a default, ✅ permitted as explicit opt-in. Auto-renewing prepaid tuition on a parent's card, by default, in a domain whose customers are disproportionately parents of minors, is the exact pattern that produces the chargebacks DT-09 says we cannot defend, and the regulator attention DT-02 flags. It also compounds badly with DT-08: a teacher who cannot see their forward liability, silently renewing it. Opt-in, with pre-charge notice, and D-04's lapse rule means the downside of *not* renewing is now mild — the credits degrade to residual instead of evaporating, so the dark pattern buys little. D-13. |
| DT-12 | Domain D-03's "a guardian is delegated authority, not a persona" is sufficient for this feature | D-03 resolves the guardian cleanly for consent, and consent is the domain's dominant guardian interaction | ❌ REJECTED for this feature specifically, and it is the second independent arrival at domain D-07 from a new direction. Delegated authority explains a guardian **acting as** the student. It does not explain a guardian **acting as themselves**: the ledger has a `purchaser` — a party with their own money, their own refund rights, their own payment instrument, and their own claim in an insolvency — who is *not* the student and cannot be modelled as the student's proxy without absurdity. The proof is edge case 26: two separated guardians buying for one child produce two purchasers with two residuals that must never commingle. A delegated-authority model has no way to express that, because both are "the student". D-07 arrived here from 06.02.01 and 06.03.05 (the parent as *reader* and as *payer*); this is the parent as **counterparty**, which is stronger — and the ledger cannot be specified without a purchaser field that no ratified persona owns. Not resolved here (D-19 is ratified and the owner chose 4 over 5 deliberately); escalated with new evidence to domain Q-01. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Consumes **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** (global cross-cut) — pack purchase, teacher payout, refunds, chargeback representment. This feature deliberately uses the *payout* half and **not** the escrow half (DT-01). It needs from that cross-cut: idempotent capture, purchase-currency refunds to the original instrument, and dispute notifications that carry the network's evidence deadline.
- Consumes **Subscriptions & Entitlements** (global cross-cut) — the seam, now sharp (D-09): the cross-cut owns billing, dunning and the **grant event**; this feature owns what the granted credit *means*, when it burns, and what its lapse produces. A monthly plan and a term fee are both packs; neither is a second ledger.
- Consumes **Tax & Fiscal Compliance** (global cross-cut) — tuition VAT is jurisdictionally weird (private tuition by an individual is exempt in some regimes, not when delivered through a company), which the generic marketplace tax engine will not assume. Tax character is **pinned at purchase** (D-11); a teacher crossing a registration threshold mid-pack does not re-characterise sold packs.
- Consumes **Localization, Currency & Timezone** (global cross-cut) — cross-border remote tuition is normal, so a rate card is multi-currency. FX pinned at purchase; the credit is denominated in *lessons*, so FX touches only residual and refunds.
- Consumes **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** (global cross-cut) — every grant, reserve, consume, burn, return, lapse, freeze and refund is an evidential event with actor and trigger, not a balance delta. This is what represents a chargeback (edge case 13) and what settles a dispute.
- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** (global cross-cut) — expiry at 30/7/1 days, at-risk signal, dunning, departure notice, remedy deadline. **Fan-out is to student AND purchaser, and they are different people** (D-08) — a notification model that assumes one recipient per pack is wrong for this domain.
- Consumes **Roles, Permissions & Delegated Authority** (global cross-cut) — the guardian's delegated authority over the student's account. Insufficient for the purchaser (DT-12).
- Touches **[06.01.01 Slot Booking](./06.01.01-lesson-slot-booking-recurring-series.md)** — confirmation reserves a credit (single writer: this file owns the mutation, 06.01.01 requests it). Also supplies the **at-risk signal**; whether zero balance suspends or releases the slot is 06.01.01's call (its Q-01, CX-06), not decided here.
- Touches **[06.01.03 Cancellation Policy](./06.01.03-cancellation-makeup-no-show-policy.md)** — the policy is the ledger's primary writer. **New constraint from DT-04**: a policy burn is an earning event and a lapse is not, so the two must be distinct ledger events. Their Q-04 (do make-ups outlive the pack?) resolves against this ledger — a make-up is tracked as a credit with its own expiry, and the ledger can express either answer.
- Touches **[06.01.04 Lesson Delivery](./06.01.04-lesson-delivery-session-record.md)** — delivery earns the credit. Its join logs are also this feature's **only chargeback evidence**, which is why DT-09 matters. Its Q-04 (over-run) is bounded here: credits are atomic, so the answer is zero or one, never a fraction.
- Touches **[06.10 Academy & Multi-Teacher Operations](../06.10-academy-multi-teacher-operations.md)** — academy-scoped packs are DT-03's resolution of the portable-credit problem; the academy's liability split (unearned vs earned-unpaid) is specified here and reported there. Their Q-03 (academy closes with credits outstanding) is answered behaviourally in edge case 11 and depends on Q-08 (segregation).
- Touches **[06.02 Teacher Discovery, Profiles & Trials](../06.02-teacher-discovery-profiles-trials/)** — the rate card is what the profile displays, and a **trial is not a credit**: it is a rate-card line of its own (often free or reduced) drawing against no pack, because no pack exists yet. Conversion creates the pack (domain CX-01).
- Touches **[06.04 Course Marketplace](../06.04-course-marketplace-authoring/)** and **[06.05 Group Lessons](../06.05-group-lessons-workshops-masterclasses.md)** — the boundary, both directions. A course is a product sale (no ledger, no relationship, Fan and Producer both `Full`). A group lesson is per-head with a viability threshold (CX R-02), which this ledger cannot express. Neither draws credits.
- Touches **[05 Services Marketplace](../../05-services-marketplace/)** — the deliberate contrast (DT-01). One human may hold service escrow and lesson credits simultaneously; the wallets must not merge, and must not merge *in the UI* either.
- Touches **[23 Career, Finance & Business](../../23-career-finance-business/)** — tuition is a large share of a working musician's income. Feeds it two distinct figures: **earned** (delivered + burned) and **forward committed liability** (DT-08). A teacher whose income picture shows unearned pack money as income has been misinformed.
- Touches **[24 Trust, Safety & Disputes](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)** — disputed burns (via 06.01.03), chargebacks, involuntary teacher departure, and collusive-purchase volume anomalies (DT-06).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Credits are **teacher-scoped** (or academy-scoped), never platform-wide | Per DT-03 — a portable credit cannot price against heterogeneous rate cards without becoming currency. |
| D-02 | A credit represents **a lesson at the rate paid**, not a sum of money | Makes mid-pack rate rises fair automatically and avoids re-pricing a balance the student already bought. Also why FX movement is irrelevant to redemption (edge case 24). |
| D-03 | Tuition does **not** use escrow | Per DT-01 — there is no deliverable and no acceptance event to release against. |
| D-04 | **Expiry expires the rate lock, not the value.** A lapsed credit is never destroyed by the passage of time — it degrades to a **residual** (cash paid, purchase currency, pinned FX, still teacher-scoped) redeemable against the teacher's current list price with the student topping up any delta | Per DT-05. The expiry's legitimate purpose is bounding a stale-rate obligation, and rate-lock expiry achieves that completely. Forfeiture is a windfall, not a need — and it is the half that attracts consumer-protection law (DT-02) and produces the "school closed and kept our money" outcome (06.10 Q-03). **This narrows Q-01 to a single question** — is residual withdrawable as cash, or redeemable-only — and makes every other behaviour in this file valid under either answer. |
| D-05 | **A policy burn and an expiry lapse are distinct ledger events.** A burn is an earning event; a lapse is not | Per DT-04 — teachers sell the *slot*, not the lesson. A burn compensates a slot held and lost; a lapse compensates nothing because nothing was scheduled. Merging them pays for time never set aside and lets time transfer money. |
| D-06 | **Teacher earnings accrue on delivery or on a policy burn — never on purchase.** Money for undelivered lessons stays with the platform | Per DT-06. Protects the student against a teacher who vanishes (edge cases 8–11), and is the platform's structural AML control for an intangible, unverifiable good. **Narrows parent Q-02** from "escrow vs pay-on-purchase" to: is any portion advanced, at what cadence does the accrued balance pay out, and must unearned funds be segregated (Q-08). |
| D-07 | A credit is **pinned to a rate-card line** (instrument × duration × mode × level) and is not fungible across lines | Two 30-minute slots are not one 60-minute slot once 06.01.01's buffers are counted. The real case — a student needing remote this week — is handled by the one-off **mode swap**: swap down is free with the delta disclosed before confirming, swap up requires a top-up, teacher may waive either in the student's favour. |
| D-08 | **Refunds, residual and money notices route to the PURCHASER**, never the student, when they differ | The domain is minors-heavy and the purchaser is usually a parent. A minor is not paid out. Two purchasers for one student produce two non-commingled residuals (edge case 26) — which delegated authority (domain D-03) cannot express, hence DT-12. |
| D-09 | **A term fee and a monthly subscription are packs**, not separate products — the expiry parameter does all the work. **Resolves Q-03** | Per DT-07. A term = `credits = weeks in term, expiry = the Operator's term bound`. A month = `credits = N, expiry = billing period + 7 days`. A second ledger would mean a second cancellation policy and a second dispute path, breaking parent D-01's single-writer property. The expiry floor is waived for subscription-shaped packs, which is only defensible *because* D-04 means an unused month degrades rather than evaporates. |
| D-10 | **Concession and sliding-scale rates are teacher-granted to a named student**, never student-self-selected | A public concession checkbox is either abused or forces the platform to verify a student's income, which it will not do. Keeps the teacher's discretion where it belongs and out of a means test we have no business running. |
| D-11 | **Price, tax character, currency, FX rate, revenue share and cancellation policy are all pinned at purchase** | Mirrors 06.01.03 D-02 (policy pinned at booking) and extends it to every commercial term. A retroactively re-characterised pack is a trust catastrophe and probably unenforceable. |
| D-12 | **Credit transfer between teachers is a departure remedy, not a feature** | Per DT-10. It survives DT-03's currency objection only because it is involuntary, one-time, platform-mediated and re-priced at the destination. Offered at will, it becomes shopping with a specific teacher's money. |
| D-13 | **Auto-renew is opt-in with pre-charge notice, never a default** | Per DT-11. Default-on prepaid tuition billed to a parent's card is the pattern that manufactures the chargebacks DT-09 says we cannot defend. D-04 removes most of the reason to want it. |
| D-14 | **The pack builder must show the teacher their forward liability before publish**: hours committed, effective hourly rate against list, and the last date the final credit could be redeemed | Per DT-08. Every marketplace shows the seller their revenue; almost none shows them what they just promised. This is the cheapest intervention in the file and it is what makes a teacher's departure (edge case 8) an inconvenience rather than a catastrophe. |
