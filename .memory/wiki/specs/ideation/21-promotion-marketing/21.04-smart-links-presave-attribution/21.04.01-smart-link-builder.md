# Feature: Smart Link Builder & DSP Routing

> **Number**: 21.04.01
> **Parent**: [Smart Links, Pre-Save & Attribution](./21.04-smart-links-presave-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

One URL that sends every listener to the service they actually use. The artist shares one link;
the Spotify listener lands on Spotify, the Apple listener on Apple, the Bandcamp buyer on
Bandcamp. It is the most-used artefact in music marketing and the most commodity thing in this
entire domain — every artist already has one from an incumbent, which is exactly why its absence
is noticed on day one.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates a link against a release; the DSP destinations populate from the release record (12) rather than being pasted by hand; customises the slug, the ordering, and the page's look; shares it everywhere; sees click data honest to the click (21.04.03). |
| Producer | None | Not a marketing artefact they touch. Their name may appear on the landing page as a verified credit (02), which is a small but real dividend of the provenance data — a credit that is cited rather than claimed. |
| Operator | None | Release links are the artist's. Event/ticket links are 21.08's, where the Operator is a participant. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The recipient.** They click, they land, they go. Their experience is the product: a page that is fast, that remembers their service so they are not asked twice, and that does not harvest them on the way through. Everything the artist wants to add to this page makes the fan's experience worse. |

## Behavior

Destinations come from the release record in domain 12, not from the artist pasting URLs. This is
a small consolidation dividend with an outsized effect: the pasted-URL model is why so many smart
links point at the wrong region's store or a dead pre-release page months later.

Routing is by the listener's revealed or remembered preference. The honest hierarchy is: remember
what they chose last time, then offer the plausible options, then let them pick. Aggressive
device-based inference (iOS ⇒ Apple Music) is wrong often enough to be annoying — plenty of iPhone
users are Spotify users.

**The restraint principle**: every element the artist adds to this page — an email capture, a
follow prompt, an interstitial — reduces the probability that the fan reaches the music. The page
has one job.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates a smart link against a release in 12.
2. DSP destinations populate automatically from the release record.
3. Musician sets a slug and ordering; page renders with artwork and verified credits from 02.
4. Link is shared — announce, pitch, socials, EPK.
5. Fan clicks; the page loads fast; their service is remembered or offered.
6. Fan lands on the track in their own app.
7. Click is recorded, attributed to a campaign if inside a window (21.04.03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Release is not live on a DSP yet (staggered availability) | A link to a dead store page is the worst outcome — the fan came and found nothing | Destination hidden until live, rather than shown and broken |
| Region has no availability | Territory-dependent; the fan is somewhere the release is not | Regional fallback; the fan gets *something* rather than a dead end |
| Fan has no DSP account at all | Nothing to route to | Fallback list; do not assume every visitor is a streaming subscriber |
| Release is taken down / delisted later | The link rots silently and keeps being shared | Lifecycle (21.04.04) must detect dead destinations — a link that rots without telling anyone is the category's most common failure |
| Artist pastes a manual override URL that later breaks | Manual overrides bypass the consolidation dividend and rot the way incumbents' links rot | Permitted, but the record shows it is unmanaged |
| Link shared before release (pre-release state) | Same URL, different meaning — mutation is handled by CX-01 | Fan sees pre-save (21.04.02) |
| Bot/scraper traffic inflates clicks | Click data is the only first-party truth here, and unfiltered it is not true | Filtering is required for the number to mean anything; the machinery is architecture (routed to `/create-prd`) |
| Fan on a slow connection at a venue | A heavy landing page loses the fan entirely | Performance is the feature. This page is the most latency-sensitive surface in the domain |

### States

Two distinct state dimensions live here: the **lifecycle** of the link as an artefact (owned by
21.04.04, summarised here) and the **render states** of the two surfaces the feature actually
presents — the Musician's builder and the Fan's landing page.

**Lifecycle states** (the link over its lifespan):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Created, not published | Preview; not yet shareable |
| Pre-release | Live, release in future | Fan sees pre-save (21.04.02) |
| Live | Release out | Fan sees routing destinations |
| Partial | Some DSPs live, some not | Live destinations only; the rest hidden, not broken |
| Rotting | A destination has died | Surfaced to the artist — this is the state incumbents never show and it is where links go to lie |
| Retired | Lifecycle ended | Behaviour per 21.04.04 — a decision, not a 404 |

**Render states — Builder (Musician)**:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Release record (12) being fetched to populate destinations | Skeleton of the destination list while the derived DSP set resolves |
| Empty | No release selected yet, or the release has no distributed DSPs | Prompt to pick a release; if the release exists but has zero live destinations, an explicit "nothing to route to yet — this link will go live when {release} distributes" message, not a blank builder |
| Populated | Destinations resolved from the release | Editable destination list, slug field, ordering, artwork + verified credits (02) preview |
| Error | Release lookup fails, or slug collides | Inline error naming the cause ("that slug is taken", "couldn't reach the release record — retry"); the builder never silently discards edits |

**Render states — Landing page (Fan)**:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Page requested | The page is the most latency-sensitive surface in the domain — target is content-visible fast enough that a venue-Wi-Fi fan does not bounce; a heavy spinner is itself a failure (precise budget → `/create-prd`) |
| Populated | Destinations resolved | Remembered service surfaced first, then the plausible options, then the full list — the honest routing hierarchy |
| Error | No destination resolvable (all dead / region-blocked / no DSP account) | A fallback list rather than a dead end — the fan always gets *something* clickable, never a 404 or a spinner that never resolves |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Destinations should be **derived from the release record (12)**, not pasted — turning the most commodity feature in the domain into a small demonstration of why consolidation is worth something | Reasoning about why smart links rot. The incumbent model is paste-and-forget: the artist pastes URLs at campaign time and nothing ever revisits them, so links point at dead pre-release pages and wrong-region stores forever. A platform that already holds the release record does not have to ask | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the sub-domain's clearest argument against the "this is commodity, why build it" objection. The link is commodity; a link that *cannot* rot because the platform owns the release record is not. It is a small instance of D-18's thesis: being where the work already lives is what makes the derived artefact better, not cleverness at the artefact. |
| DT-02 | The landing page should capture the fan — email, follow prompt, data — since the traffic is already there and it is the artist's own audience | Growth instinct, and it is what the category has converged on: pre-save-as-data-harvest | ❌ REJECTED for the smart link specifically. Every element added to this page lowers the chance the fan reaches the music, which is the one thing the page exists for and the one thing the fan came for. The category's convergence on harvesting is why fans have learned to distrust these links — and per D-11 the Fan is a first-class user here whose experience we are accountable for, not a resource. The artist's owned-audience ambition is legitimate and it has a home: the permanent microsite in domain 20, where the fan arrived intending a relationship. Routing the ambition to the right surface (D-01 on the sub-domain index) is what lets this page stay fast and honest. |
| DT-03 | Once a fan's service is remembered, the page should **silently redirect** straight to their DSP — skip the chooser entirely — because the fastest path to the music is no page at all | The restraint principle taken to its logical end: if the page's one job is to get the fan to their app, the ideal page is invisible | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH A GUARD, not wholesale. Silent redirect on a remembered preference is the right default — it honours D-03 (remembered preference beats device inference) and it is genuinely faster. But an *unconditional* silent redirect traps three real fans: the one on a borrowed/shared device whose remembered service is not theirs, the one who switched services since last visit, and the one who wants to buy on Bandcamp this time though they usually stream. The behaviour: redirect on remembered preference, but always land through a page that offers a visible "not your service? / other options" escape within the first interaction — never a redirect the fan cannot get back from. The long-tail specifics of the remember mechanism (cookie vs. account-linked, TTL, cross-device) are `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`; the behaviour rule (redirect-with-escape, never a trap) is settled here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Destinations derive from the release record; manual override is possible but marked unmanaged | See DT-01. The derived link is the one that cannot rot. |
| D-02 | No audience capture on the smart link page | See DT-02. The page has one job. Capture belongs to 20. |
| D-03 | Remembered preference beats device inference | iOS does not imply Apple Music. Confident wrong routing is worse than asking. |
| D-04 | Dead destinations are surfaced, not silently served | Link rot that nobody is told about is the category's defining failure. |
