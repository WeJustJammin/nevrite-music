# Feature: Pre-Save / Pre-Add Authorization

> **Number**: 21.04.02
> **Parent**: [Smart Links, Pre-Save & Attribution](./21.04-smart-links-presave-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A fan says yes to a record that does not exist yet, and on release day it appears in their
library. Mechanically it is an OAuth grant: the fan authorises a third party to write to their
music library at a future moment. That makes this the only feature in domain 21 where a Fan hands
over real agency — and the category's history of quietly widening that grant into follows and
email harvest is why it needs to be designed carefully rather than copied.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Enables pre-save on a release; sees authorisation counts and — honestly — sees that the count is not a conversion (21.04.03); chooses what is asked of the fan, within limits the platform sets rather than leaves open. |
| Producer | None | Not their artefact. |
| Operator | None | Not their artefact. |
| Fan | **Full** | The actor, not the audience. They see plainly what they are granting *before* they grant it — "we will add this track to your library on 4 September; we will not follow anyone on your behalf; we will not read your listening history"; they authorise; they can see every pre-save they have outstanding and **revoke any of them at any time** without hunting through the DSP's settings. Per D-11 they are a first-class user, and this is the one place in domain 21 where that is more than a classification. |

## Behavior

The fan authorises via the DSP's OAuth flow. The platform holds the grant until the release
moment, then fires the library write for every outstanding authorisation.

**The scope question is the whole feature.** The minimum honest ask is library-write for this
release. The category norm has been to bundle: follow the artist, add to a playlist, hand over an
email address — all framed as "pre-save". That converts better and it is why fans have learned
that a pre-save link is a trap. What WeJammin asks is a product decision (Q-03 on the sub-domain
index), and it should be made deliberately by the owner rather than inherited from the category by
default.

**Release day is the largest fan-out in the domain** and the most likely to fail: tokens expire,
fans revoke at the DSP, DSPs rate-limit, and the whole population fires within one window. A
pre-save that silently fails is worse than no pre-save — the fan believes they have the record and
does not go looking for it.

### Happy Path

1. Musician enables pre-save on a release; the smart link (21.04.01) enters pre-release state.
2. Fan clicks; sees plainly what is being asked and what is not.
3. Fan authorises via the DSP's OAuth flow.
4. Grant is held; fan sees it in their own list of outstanding pre-saves.
5. Release moment: library write fires for every outstanding grant.
6. Fan opens their app; the record is there.
7. Link mutates to live routing (CX-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Token expired between authorisation and release day | The write fails silently at the DSP; the fan never learns they did not get the record | The category's most common invisible failure. Failures must be counted and told to the artist honestly rather than absorbed into a success number — see DT-02 |
| Fan revoked at the DSP | Grant is void; must not be retried | Respected silently; a revocation is an answer |
| Release date moves (21.01.04) | The grant fires on the **release event**, not the originally-stated date — but the fan authorised against a stated expectation | `[PENDING]` — whether fans are told is a consent question, not a UX one. See Q-01 and 21.01.04 Q-01 |
| Release is cancelled | Grants never fire and are held indefinitely against nothing | Grants must expire. Holding library-write authorisation forever for a record that will never exist is not defensible |
| Fan pre-saves, then returns to the link on release day | They already have it | Must not re-ask. A fan asked to pre-save something already in their library learns the page does not know them |
| DSP rate-limits the release-day fan-out | Partial delivery, and the failures are invisible to everyone | Retry with backoff; **failures counted and surfaced**. Scale makes this the feature's real engineering risk |
| Fan is a minor | Age assurance intersects a consent grant, and per D-13 statutory duties scale with fan reach | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` → `/create-prd-security` |
| Artist wants the fan's email as a condition of pre-save | The category norm; also the reason the category is distrusted | See Q-03 on the sub-domain index — owner's decision, not a default |

### States

Two orthogonal state models. The **interaction states** are what the Fan sees on the two surfaces
this feature owns — the ask, and their outstanding-pre-saves list (D-01). The **grant lifecycle**
is the server-side status of a single authorisation, independent of any screen.

#### Interaction states — the ask and the outstanding list

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | OAuth callback in flight, or the outstanding list is fetching | Spinner on the authorise button; skeleton rows on the list |
| Empty | Fan has no outstanding pre-saves | "You have no pre-saves waiting" — not an error, a resting state |
| Populated | Fan has ≥1 outstanding grant | Each record with its stated release date and a revoke control that works from here, not the DSP |
| Error | List fetch fails, or the OAuth flow fails/returns denied | Retry on the list; a failed or denied authorisation returns the fan to the ask stated plainly, never a dead end — the grant did not happen and the page says so |

#### Grant lifecycle — server-side status of one authorisation

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available | Pre-release, pre-save enabled | Fan sees the ask, stated plainly |
| Authorised | Fan granted | Fan sees it in their outstanding list, revocable |
| Revoked | Fan withdrew | Void; no retry |
| Expired | Token died before release | **Counted and surfaced** — never absorbed into the success number |
| Fired | Release moment, write succeeded | Fan has the record |
| Failed | Write failed at the DSP | Counted, surfaced, retried where retryable |
| Void | Release cancelled / grant aged out | Expired rather than held forever |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A pre-save is a **consent grant**, not a marketing widget — so the Fan is an actor with rights here, not an audience | Reasoning from the mechanics: the fan authorises a third party to write to their personal library at a future date. D-11 makes fans first-class users; `meta/personas.md` says the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" but says nothing about them tolerating being harvested — and names "worst accidental" fan behaviour as being a minor hitting an age-gated surface | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it produces the requirement no incumbent offers: **the fan can see and revoke every outstanding pre-save from our side**, without hunting through a DSP settings page they will never find. That is the concrete difference between "fans are users" (D-11) and "fans are a CRM record", and it is cheap to build. If D-11 means anything operationally, it means this. |
| DT-02 | Pre-save failures can be absorbed — a token that expired is nobody's fault and reporting it only makes the numbers look worse | Convenient instinct, and evidently the category norm given how invisible these failures are | ❌ REJECTED — a silently-failed pre-save is a *fan who believes they have the record and does not go looking for it*. That is a lost listener plus a broken promise, and the artist made real decisions on a success number that included them. Absorbing the failure means the two parties who care both get lied to, in opposite directions, to protect a metric. Failures are counted, surfaced to the artist, and where possible the fan is told. This is the same discipline as 21.03.03 DT-02: an honest gap beats a flattering fiction. |
| DT-03 | Bundling follows and email capture into the pre-save ask is fine — it is standard, it converts better, and the fan clicked the button | Category norm; nearly every pre-save service does it | ❌ REJECTED as a **default**, escalated to the owner (Q-03, sub-domain index). "The fan clicked the button" is doing enormous work in that sentence: they clicked a button labelled *pre-save*, and the follow they also granted was not what they came for. It is dark-pattern consent, and it is precisely why the category is distrusted. But pricing conversion against trust is a product decision (`decision-classification.md`), so it is presented with the trade-off stated: the narrow ask converts worse and is the reason a fan would trust the next one. I would recommend the narrow ask. I do not get to choose. |
