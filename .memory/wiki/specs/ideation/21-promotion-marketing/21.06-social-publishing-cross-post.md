# Feature: Social Publishing & Cross-Post

> **Number**: 21.06
> **Parent**: [Promotion & Marketing](./promotion-marketing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Actually pushing a planned beat out to Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube and the rest — compose once,
adapt per network, schedule, publish, confirm. This is the publishing half of sweep candidate 09
(the calendar half became 21.01.03 per that sub-domain's D-03). It is the most commodity feature
in the domain, the most API-fragile, and the one with the weakest connection to the platform's
thesis.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Connects social accounts; takes a beat from the calendar (21.01.03) and publishes or schedules it; adapts copy per network; sees what actually went out and what failed. For a band, publishing on behalf of the band entity is an authority question, not a convenience one. |
| Producer | None | Not a marketing function. They are, however, **bound by any active embargo** (21.02.05) — and the constraint reaches them there, not here, because they will never open this surface. |
| Operator | None | A venue posting its own event content is real behaviour and lives in 21.08. Whether it should reuse this machinery is Q-03. |
| Fan | None | The audience of the output. They see the post on the network, not here — and they should not be able to tell it was scheduled. |

## Behavior

Compose → adapt per network → schedule → publish → confirm. The mechanics are well understood and
entirely unoriginal; every product in this space does them, several do them well, and they are
cheap for anyone to build.

**The API reality is the feature's defining constraint.** Publishing APIs across the major social
networks are restrictive, inconsistent, permissioned, and change without warning. Some content
types cannot be published programmatically at all. Access can be revoked. A feature built entirely
on other companies' publishing APIs is a feature those companies can break on any given Tuesday,
and historically they do.

This is exactly why 21.01.03 D-02 decoupled planning from publishing: the campaign calendar must
remain fully valuable with zero integrations connected. This feature is a convenience layer on
top, and if it breaks, the plan survives.

**The honest position on scope**: this has no connection to D-18's thesis. It captures no
provenance, creates no fact worth preserving, and puts us in direct competition with mature
dedicated tools. It is pure consolidation with nothing compounding underneath — which is precisely
the "consolidation alone is copyable" failure `meta/problem-statement.md` warns about.

### Happy Path

1. Musician connects social accounts (OAuth; cross-cut).
2. Opens a planned beat from the calendar (21.01.03).
3. Composes; adapts per network — length, aspect ratio, tone.
4. Embargo check (21.02.05) — blocked if the content is time-locked.
5. Schedules for the beat's resolved time.
6. Publishes; confirmation flows back to the calendar.
7. Failures surface **on the calendar**, where the artist is actually looking.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Network API cannot publish this content type | Common and varies by network and account type | Told at compose time, not at publish time. Discovering it at the scheduled moment means the beat is simply missed |
| Publish fails at the scheduled moment | The artist is asleep; the beat is missed; nobody is watching | Failure surfaced on the calendar and pushed to the artist. A silent failure is a lost beat, and scheduling exists precisely so nobody is watching |
| Account disconnects (token expiry, password change, revocation) | Every scheduled beat on that network silently dies | Must be detected *before* the scheduled beat, not at publish. A queue of doomed posts is the worst state this feature has |
| Content is under an active embargo | Blocked — this is a genuine job (21.02.05 DT-01) | Blocked with the embargo named and its lift time |
| Network changes its API / revokes access | Permanent recurring risk | The calendar survives (21.01.03 D-02). That decoupling is what makes this feature's fragility tolerable |
| Band entity: who can post as the band? | Authority question, not convenience | `[PENDING]` — ties to Q-04 in `meta/personas.md` and the band-entity model in 01 |
| Post published, then the release date moves | The post is **escaped** — public and stating a date now wrong | 21.01.04 classifies; correction is a human action |
| Artist posts directly on their phone instead | Extremely likely — most artists post from the app, in the moment | The calendar diverges from reality and nobody reconciles it. See Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not connected | No accounts linked | Connect prompt; the calendar still works fully |
| Draft | Composing | Per-network previews |
| Blocked | Embargo active | Named embargo, lift time |
| Scheduled | Queued for a time | Scheduled marker on the calendar |
| At risk | Account disconnected while beats are queued | Escalated **before** the scheduled time |
| Published | Success | Confirmed on the calendar |
| Failed | Publish failed | Surfaced on the calendar and pushed to the artist |

| DT-01 | This feature has **no connection to the platform thesis** and should be honestly proposed as low-priority despite being the most-requested-looking thing in the domain | Direct test against `meta/problem-statement.md` D-18: consolidation is the platform *because* it puts us in the room where provenance is capturable. Social publishing captures nothing — no credit, no split, no fact that accretes to anyone's identity. It is the one feature in domain 21 where the answer to "what does being here let us capture?" is genuinely nothing | ✅ CONFIRMED — and the problem statement names this failure mode precisely: "Consolidation alone is copyable, and means fighting six incumbents simultaneously, each better than you at their one thing, with nothing that compounds." | DT-02 | Planning and publishing should be one feature, since the artist experiences them as one motion | Product coherence instinct; it is how every competitor packages it | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the decision that saves the valuable half. Publishing depends entirely on third-party APIs that are restrictive, permissioned and revocable; the calendar depends on nothing but our own data. Merging them makes the valuable, durable half hostage to the fragile, commodity half — one API revocation and the campaign plan dies with the posting tool. Decoupled (21.01.03 D-02), the calendar is fully useful with zero integrations, and this feature degrades to a manual copy-paste workflow instead of taking the plan down with it. The artist experiencing them as one motion is a UI concern, not an architecture one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Publishing is decoupled from planning | See DT-02. The valuable half must not be hostage to the fragile half. |
| D-02 | Failures surface on the calendar, not in a publishing log | The calendar is where the artist looks. A failure in a log nobody opens is a silent failure. |
| D-03 | Disconnected accounts are detected before the scheduled beat, not at publish | A queue of doomed posts that fail at 3am is the worst outcome this feature can produce. |
| D-04 | Proposed `could`, honestly | See DT-01. No thesis connection, strong incumbents, permanent API tax. |
