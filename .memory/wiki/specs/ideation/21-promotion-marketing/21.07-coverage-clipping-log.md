# Feature: Coverage & Clipping Log

> **Number**: 21.07
> **Parent**: [Promotion & Marketing](./promotion-marketing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Every time someone writes about you, plays you, or puts you on a list — captured, dated, linked
and kept. It closes the pitch loop (21.02.03), feeds the press quotes on the EPK (21.09), and
does something no other feature in this domain does: it turns a marketing artefact into a
**verified fact**. A press quote on an EPK is normally a claim. Here it can be evidence.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Logs coverage (or has it captured for them); links it to the pitch that produced it and the campaign it belongs to; pulls quotes into the EPK; sees their coverage history accumulate across years rather than evaporating between campaigns. |
| Producer | Read-only | **Coverage that names them.** A review crediting them as producer is a reputational asset they currently have no way to collect — and one that a mis-credit damages with no correction path. This is a genuine Producer stake in domain 21 and it is the only feature here where they have one. Correction runs through domain 02. |
| Operator | Read-only | **Coverage of shows at their space.** A live review naming the venue is the Operator's equivalent asset, and per `meta/personas.md` their reputation "accrues from shows that went smoothly" — this is where that becomes visible rather than merely felt. Scoped to events at their room; no access to an artist's release coverage. |
| Fan | None | No tool access. Fans see the resulting quotes on public surfaces (the EPK, the artist's profile in 01/20) — which is exactly why those quotes being *verified* rather than claimed matters to somebody other than the artist. |

## Behavior

A coverage item: URL, outlet, author, date, type (review / feature / interview / premiere /
playlist add / radio add), the pulled quote, and its link to a pitch (21.03.03) and a campaign.

**The verification insight.** Fake and embellished press quotes on EPKs are endemic — a quote from
a review that does not exist, a line lifted from a comment section and attributed to a magazine, a
"BBC Introducing" claim that means something much smaller than it sounds. Nobody checks, because
checking is tedious and the person receiving the EPK has thirty of them. But a coverage item
captured with a live URL, an outlet, a date and a retrievable page is *checkable* — and the
platform can mark the difference between a quote it can verify and a quote the artist typed in.

That is precisely the provenance wedge (D-18), showing up in the acquisition domain: verified fact
versus asserted claim, rendered differently, at the moment it matters. It is also the strongest
argument that this domain is not merely an adjacency.

**Coverage without a pitch is normal.** The best coverage is often organic — someone just wrote
about you. The log must accept coverage with no pitch parent (21.03.03), and treating attribution
as mandatory would exclude the happiest case.

### Happy Path

1. Musician pitches press (21.02.03).
2. A piece is published.
3. Coverage is captured — manually, or detected by monitoring (Q-01).
4. Item links to the pitch that produced it; the pitch's outcome becomes `covered`; the CRM records that this journalist covers this artist.
5. Musician pulls the quote.
6. Quote flows to the EPK (21.09) **marked verified**, with a live link back to the source.
7. Next campaign, the EPK is stronger and the CRM knows who to pitch. The loop closes (21.02.03 DT-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Link rots — the outlet folds, the article vanishes | Music journalism dies constantly; a verified quote whose source is a 404 is unverifiable again | The verification claim decays with the link. Whether we archive the page ourselves is Q-02, and it is a copyright question, not only a storage one |
| Artist types a quote that has no source | Allowed — it is their EPK and they may have a legitimate offline source | But rendered **differently**: claimed, not verified. The distinction is the entire feature |
| Quote is real but selectively edited ("a triumph" from "far from a triumph") | Verification proves the source exists, not that the quote is honest | An honest limit worth stating: we verify the link, not the reading. Overclaiming here would undermine the whole point |
| Coverage is negative | It is still coverage and still real | The log is a record, not a highlight reel. The EPK selection is a separate choice the artist makes |
| Coverage names the Producer incorrectly | A provenance failure in public | Producer sees it (Role Lens) — currently they never would. Correction runs through 02 |
| Paid coverage / sponsored post | It is an advertisement wearing a review's clothes | Must be marked. Ties to the disclosure cross-cut and 21.05.03 |
| Automated monitoring produces false positives (name collisions) | Band names collide constantly — this is not an edge case, it is the normal state of any band called something ordinary | Detection must be suggestion-with-confirmation, never auto-insertion |
| Playlist/radio adds as coverage | Same shape, different artefact — a **second verification class** with weaker guarantees (DT-03). An editorial playlist has a live URL that can be checked *now*, but a playlist is mutable: the track can be removed, so the add is a point-in-time fact, not the fixed artefact an article is. A radio add has no public page at all — only a station's airplay log, which the artist rarely holds | Rendered as **verified-at-timestamp**, not verified-permanent — a distinct third badge from verified (article) and claimed (typed). The badge states when the add was confirmed and by what evidence (playlist URL snapshot / airplay report / self-attested), so the EPK reader sees the strength of the claim, not a false equivalence with a fixed press quote |
| Curator removes the track after the add is logged | The historical fact is unchanged — it *was* added — but the live URL no longer proves it | Add stays in the log, badge decays toward claimed unless a snapshot was captured (ties to Q-02 archiving). The log records what happened; it does not pretend the source is still live |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No coverage | Empty state; the pitch log is where it will come from |
| Detected | Monitoring found a candidate | **Suggested**, never auto-added — name collisions make certainty impossible |
| Verified | Live URL, retrievable, fixed source confirmed (article) | Marked verified; usable as a verified EPK quote |
| Verified-at-timestamp | Live but mutable source confirmed (playlist add) | Marked verified as of a date, with the evidence named; weaker than a fixed article, stronger than a typed claim (DT-03) |
| Claimed | Artist-entered, no verifiable source (or self-attested radio add) | Usable, marked claimed. Not hidden — distinguished |
| Decayed | Source link rotted | Verification lapses; the artist is told before the EPK quietly weakens |
| Attributed | Linked to a pitch | Closes the loop; feeds the CRM and the directory aggregate |
| Organic | No pitch parent | Fully valid — often the best coverage there is |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Verified coverage is a provenance fact**, structurally identical to a verified credit — and this is where domain 21 connects to the platform's thesis rather than merely sitting beside it | Reasoning about what an EPK press quote actually is: an assertion, unchecked, at the exact moment it is most valuable and most self-interested. That is `meta/problem-statement.md`'s diagnosis word for word — "they are asking people to remember, and memory is contested, lossy, and self-interested." Fake press quotes are endemic for precisely the reason fake credits are: nobody is present when the fact becomes true, so the record is a reconstruction by the party who benefits | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the single most important finding in this domain. It means 21 is not merely an acquisition adjacency bolted onto a rights platform: it contains a genuine instance of the wedge. The platform is present when coverage happens (it sent the pitch, it holds the link), so it can capture the fact at source rather than accept a claim later. Verified-vs-claimed rendering is the same mechanism as verified-vs-claimed credits, applied to a different artefact — and it is why this feature is proposed `should` while its flashier neighbours are `could`. |
| DT-02 | Automated coverage monitoring should auto-populate the log — we can detect mentions, so we should insert them | Efficiency instinct; it is what media-monitoring tools do | ❌ REJECTED — band-name collisions are not an edge case, they are the default condition for any band whose name is a normal English word, and an auto-inserted false positive corrupts a record whose entire value is that it is trustworthy. Worse, it would corrupt it *in the direction of flattery* — a false positive adds coverage that does not exist, which is exactly the failure DT-01 says this feature exists to prevent. Building an auto-inserting monitor would mean fabricating provenance in a provenance feature. Suggestion-with-confirmation is slower, and it is the only option consistent with the feature's reason to exist. |
| DT-03 | Playlist and radio adds are coverage, but they cannot carry the same verification badge as an article, and pretending they can would break DT-01 | Resolving Q-03. An article is a *fixed* artefact: once published at a URL, the page either exists or 404s, and its text does not change under the artist's feet. A playlist is *mutable* — the track is present until a curator removes it — and a radio add leaves no public page at all, only a station airplay log the artist usually cannot produce. Collapsing all three into one "verified" badge would let the weakest-evidenced artefact borrow the credibility the article earns, which is exactly the false-equivalence DT-01 says this feature exists to defeat | ✅ CONFIRMED — coverage is **not one class**. Three verification strengths, rendered distinctly: **verified** (article, fixed live source), **verified-at-timestamp** (playlist add, live-but-mutable, badge names the confirmation date and evidence), and **claimed** (typed / radio-self-attested, no retrievable source). The verified-at-timestamp badge decays toward claimed if the source mutates and no snapshot exists (ties to Q-02). This keeps the log honest about *how strong* each fact is, not merely whether it is present — which is the whole feature at a finer grain. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Verified and claimed coverage are rendered **differently**, never identically | See DT-01. This is the domain's thesis connection; collapsing the distinction discards it. |
| D-02 | Monitoring suggests; it never auto-inserts | See DT-02. Auto-insertion would fabricate provenance inside a provenance feature. |
| D-03 | Coverage with no pitch parent is fully valid | Organic coverage is often the best coverage; requiring attribution would exclude the happiest case. |
| D-04 | We verify the link, not the reading | Selective quotation is real and outside what verification can honestly claim. Overclaiming here would undermine D-01. |
| D-05 | Coverage carries **three** verification strengths, not two — verified (fixed article), verified-at-timestamp (mutable playlist add), claimed (typed / self-attested) | Resolves Q-03. See DT-03. A playlist add is real coverage but a mutable source; one badge for all three would let the weakest borrow the strongest's credibility, the exact failure D-01 exists to prevent. |
