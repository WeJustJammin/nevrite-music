# Feature: Playlist & Curator Pitching

> **Number**: 21.02.02
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Pitching independent and third-party playlist curators — the ones who are not the DSP's editorial
team and who can, in principle, be paid. This is the most dangerous feature in the domain: paid
placement violates DSP terms, gets tracks removed and artists' profiles sanctioned, and the entire
category around it (pay-for-placement playlist services, stream farms) is a fraud economy the
platform must not become a distribution channel for.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds relevant curators via the directory (21.03.01); sends a pitch within rate limits (21.02.06); tracks responses in the CRM (21.03.03). Sees, structurally and unavoidably, that this path is **not** the editorial path and that nothing here can be bought (21.05.03). Sees a curator's real response rate before spending a pitch on them. |
| Producer | None | Not a pitching function. Their session facts feed the pitch's credibility (the credits) but they neither send nor see these. |
| Operator | None | No relationship to playlist pitching. |
| Fan | None | Not a participant. Note the structural fact that a playlist curator may *be* a Fan in persona terms — an enthusiast with a following — which is precisely why Q-01 on the sub-domain index matters: the gatekeeper here is not cleanly any of our four personas. |

## Behavior

A pitch is composed against a target from the directory, sent within limits, and tracked. The
mechanics are ordinary. Everything that matters is the guardrails around them.

**The payola line.** Paying for placement on DSP playlists violates DSP terms; the sanction lands
on the *artist's* track, not the seller's business. Pay-for-consideration (paying for a listen and
a response, with no placement promised) is the model that platforms in this space have used to
survive — and even that has drawn DSP sanction against specific services. WeJammin's stated
position (domain index, D-01) is pay-for-consideration only, never pay-for-placement. This feature
is where that line is drawn or crossed.

**Why the honesty is a feature, not a tax.** The artist cannot tell the difference between a
legitimate curator and a stream farm — the pitch looks identical, the promises are better on the
fraudulent side, and the harm is deferred and lands on them. A platform that shows real response
rates and refuses to broker placement is doing the one thing the artist cannot do for themselves.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens playlist pitching for a track; the feature makes explicit that editorial (21.02.01) is a different, free, unbuyable path.
2. Directory (21.03.01) surfaces curators relevant to the track's genre — each showing real response rate and typical turnaround, not a marketing blurb.
3. Musician selects targets; rate limits (21.02.06) cap the selection and say why.
4. Composes a pitch; credits and track data pre-fill from 02/12; the artist writes the pitch itself.
5. Sends. Each send is logged to the CRM (21.03.03) against that target.
6. Responses (or, mostly, silence) are tracked; silence after N days is itself recorded as an outcome, because it is the most common one.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Curator's listing offers guaranteed placement for money | The listing is a policy violation and must be removed, not merely labelled | Blocked at directory level (21.03.01 / 21.05.03). A "guaranteed placement" offer visible in our directory is us brokering payola |
| Artist wants to pay a curator for consideration | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether WeJammin brokers *any* payment on this path is an unresolved product decision with real consequences either way. See Q-01 | — |
| Curator is a stream farm (fake playlist, bot listeners) | Placement actively harms the artist — DSPs detect artificial streams and sanction the track. The harm is deferred and invisible at pitch time | Detection is a 24 problem; the *policy* consequence is that the directory must have a removal path and the artist must not have been encouraged toward them |
| Artist blasts every curator in a genre | Rate limits block it and explain why | "You can pitch 8 more curators this week. Curators who receive bulk pitches respond to none of them." — the constraint must read as strategy, because it is |
| Curator has opted out of receiving pitches | Hard block; opt-out beats our queue | Target shown as not accepting pitches, with no send affordance |
| Track is already released | Allowed — unlike editorial, third-party playlisting has no release-date window. This asymmetry with 21.02.01 confuses users constantly | The absence of urgency is worth stating explicitly, since the neighbouring feature is all urgency |
| Placement happens, then the playlist is purged by the DSP for selling placement | The artist's track is caught in the sanction through no fault of their own | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a real and nasty scenario; unclear what recourse exists |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No pitches sent for this track | Empty state with the editorial-vs-independent distinction made structurally, before any pitch is composed |
| Composing | Pitch in progress | Composer with pre-filled credits/track data and remaining rate-limit budget visible up front, not on submit |
| Queued | Sent, awaiting delivery | Queued marker; may be held by a date-change cascade (21.01.04) |
| Sent | Delivered | Logged to CRM against the target |
| Responded | Curator replied | Response recorded; outcome captured |
| Silent | N days elapsed, no response | Recorded as an outcome — silence is the modal result and pretending it is a pending state is a lie |
| Blocked | Rate limit / opt-out / policy | Named reason and, where applicable, reset time |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The artist cannot distinguish a legitimate curator from a stream farm, and the platform's genuine value here is that **it can** — so honest response-rate data is the product, not the send mechanism | Reasoning about the actual information asymmetry. The fraudulent side always presents better: guaranteed numbers, fast turnaround, confident claims. The legitimate side offers "I'll listen." The harm from choosing wrong is deferred (a DSP sanction weeks later) and lands entirely on the artist | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it inverts the feature's centre of gravity. Sending an email is not a product; knowing which of 400 curators actually respond, from aggregate evidence no individual artist could ever assemble, is. It also makes 21.03.01's data quality the real dependency, and reframes the rate limit as a quality signal rather than a tax. |
| DT-02 | A "guaranteed placement" offer can be safely listed as long as it carries a warning label | Marketplace neutrality instinct — let the artist decide, we just surface options | ❌ REJECTED — listing it *is* brokering it, and a warning label does not change what we are doing; it documents that we knew. The sanction lands on the artist's track, not on the seller and not on us, which makes "let the user decide" a transfer of our risk onto the least-informed party. `meta/personas.md` names the Musician's worst-accidental behaviour as signing something they don't understand; this would be us handing them the pen. Remove the listing. |
| DT-03 | This feature's payola exposure is not confined to domain 21 — the same offer will appear as a **service listing in domain 05** ("playlist promotion, 100k streams guaranteed"), where 21's policy does not reach | Reasoning about what breaks at the domain intersection. D-14 established that 05 is a separate services marketplace with its own listing flow; nothing in that flow knows about DSP terms | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the most important cross-domain finding in this sub-domain. The payola policy must bind **listing creation in 05**, not just pitching in 21, or the guardrail is a front door with an open back door. Recorded as a high-confidence cross-domain touchpoint and as the reason 21.05.03 is proposed `must` despite sitting inside a `could` sub-domain. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Pay-for-consideration only; never pay-for-placement | Domain index D-01. Placement purchase violates DSP terms and the sanction lands on the artist. |
| D-02 | Guaranteed-placement listings are **removed**, not labelled | See DT-02. A warning label documents our knowledge without changing our conduct. |
| D-03 | Honest response-rate data is the feature's core, not the send path | See DT-01. The send is commodity; the asymmetry-correction is not. |
| D-04 | Silence is recorded as an outcome, not a pending state | It is the modal result. A UI that shows 40 pitches "pending" forever is lying about what happened. |
