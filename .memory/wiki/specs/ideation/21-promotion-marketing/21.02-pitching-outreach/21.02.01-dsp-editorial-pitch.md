# Feature: DSP Editorial Pitch

> **Number**: 21.02.01
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The one-shot pitch to a streaming platform's editorial team, submitted through the DSP's own tool
(Spotify for Artists, Apple Music for Artists, and equivalents) before the track is released. It
is the highest-stakes item in the whole domain: the window is hard, the pitch cannot be repeated,
and once the track releases the opportunity is gone permanently. WeJammin cannot submit it for
the artist — but it can guarantee they never miss it, and that they arrive at the form prepared.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the window countdown as the campaign's hardest deadline; assembles the pitch content (genre, mood, instrumentation, culture/context, the story) inside WeJammin where the credits and track data already live; is handed a prepared, copy-ready pitch and a deep link to the DSP's own tool; records that the pitch was submitted and what was said, so the next release's pitch is not written from a blank page. |
| Producer | None | Does not pitch DSP editorial — this is the artist's account and the artist's one shot. Their contribution to it is indirect and important: the credits and instrumentation the pitch describes come from the session facts they captured (domain 02). They are not shown the pitch. |
| Operator | None | No relationship to release-cycle DSP editorial. Operators sell time and space, not recorded output; nothing in a venue/studio booking flow touches a streaming platform's editorial window, so this feature never surfaces for them. |
| Fan | None | Invisible and must stay invisible. A pitch is confidential, implies nothing about placement, and leaking "we pitched Spotify" to fans sets an expectation that editorial will usually disappoint. |

## Behavior

The platform's honest position is that **it is not in this loop**. There is no route by which
WeJammin submits an editorial pitch on the artist's behalf; the DSPs deliberately gate this to
their own tools, tied to the artist's own verified account. Any design that implies otherwise is
a lie the artist discovers at the worst possible moment.

So the feature does the three things that are actually available:

1. **Never miss the window.** Derive the deadline from the grid (21.01.01) and escalate as it
   approaches. This is the whole justification for the domain per its own index.
2. **Arrive prepared.** The pitch form asks for genre, mood, instrumentation, and context — all
   of which WeJammin already knows from the release metadata (12) and the verified credit graph
   (02). Assembling that into copy-ready pitch text is real work eliminated.
3. **Remember what was said.** The pitch is submitted elsewhere, but the *record* of it — what
   was claimed, when, for which track — lives here and is the input to the next cycle.

The prerequisite chain is the part artists get wrong: the track must be delivered by the
distributor and present in the DSP's system before it can be pitched at all. The pitch deadline
is therefore downstream of the distribution deadline, which is downstream of the master
(21.01.01 DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. Grid derives the DSP editorial window from the release date and the channel rules.
2. As the window opens, the platform confirms the prerequisite: has the distributor delivered? If not, this is a *distribution* problem surfaced as a *pitch* alarm.
3. Musician opens the pitch assembler; genre, credits, instrumentation and personnel are pre-filled from 02 and 12. They write the story — the part only they have.
4. Platform produces copy-ready pitch text plus a deep link to the DSP's own submission tool.
5. Musician submits in the DSP's tool.
6. Musician marks it submitted in WeJammin; the content is archived against the track.
7. The grid's hard row clears. If it never clears, escalation continues until the window closes.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | WeJammin cannot submit the pitch — the DSPs gate this to their own tools and their own account verification — so the feature must be an **assembler and an alarm**, not a submitter | Reasoning about what is actually integrable. DSP editorial pitching is deliberately not an open API surface; it is a first-party tool tied to a verified artist profile | ✅ CONFIRMED — and this is a *strengthening* constraint, not a weakening one. It forces the feature onto the two things that are genuinely defensible: the deadline nobody else computes, and the pre-fill from a credit graph nobody else has. A "submit to Spotify" button would be a lie; an alarm that fires because we know the master, the delivery and the window is real. |
| DT-02 | The most valuable pre-fill is not the metadata — it is the **verified credits and instrumentation from domain 02** | The editorial pitch form asks who played what, what the instrumentation is, what the context is. This is exactly the data the platform's whole thesis is built on capturing at source (`meta/problem-statement.md`: "who played on it, who wrote what") | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the clearest expression of the thesis in this entire domain. Provenance was captured for rights reasons, and here it pays a marketing dividend nobody planned: the pitch writes itself from facts that are *verified* rather than remembered. This is what "the two halves are causally linked" (D-18) looks like at feature level, and it is an argument for this feature being higher-priority than its channel siblings. |
| DT-03 | We should let artists record the pitch outcome (playlisted / not) to build a success model | Analytics instinct; it would make a nice dataset | ❌ REJECTED at this level — the DSPs do not report a pitch *outcome*; a track either appears on editorial playlists or does not, and attributing that to the pitch is unfalsifiable. Recording "did it work?" would manufacture a causal story from a coincidence, and artists would optimise against noise. What we *can* honestly record is what was submitted and when. Placement observation belongs to 22, correlational and labelled as such. This is the same discipline as 21.04.03's attribution honesty. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |

| D-01 | The feature is an **assembler + alarm + archive**, never a submitter | We cannot submit. Implying we can is a lie discovered at the moment of unrecoverable loss. |
