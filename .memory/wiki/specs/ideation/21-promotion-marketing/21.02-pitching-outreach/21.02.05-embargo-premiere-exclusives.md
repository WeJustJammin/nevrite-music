# Feature: Embargo, Premiere & Exclusive Windows

> **Number**: 21.02.05
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

An outlet gets the story first, in exchange for covering it — and everyone else, including the
artist, must stay silent until the agreed moment. The embargo is a promise to a third party, and
its enforcement spans every publication surface the platform has. The breach vector is almost
never the outlet; it is somebody on the artist's own side posting a studio photo three days early.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Negotiates and records the exclusive: which outlet, what content, lift time, what was promised in return. Sees everything the embargo binds — beats, links, pitches, their own posts — and gets stopped before breaking it. On lift, sees the fan-out fire. |
| Producer | Read-only | **Bound, not privileged.** They can destroy an exclusive with one studio photo, and today nobody ever tells them one exists — this is the gap the feature closes. They see the constraint ("do not post about this project before 2026-09-04 09:00 BST") and nothing else: not the outlet, not the campaign, not the plan. Constraint without plan is the exact shape of their access. |
| Operator | None | Release embargoes do not reach venues. A tour-announce embargo does, and lives in 21.08 — where the venue is a genuine breach risk, because venues announce shows on their own calendars. |
| Fan | None | By definition. An embargo a fan can see is not an embargo. |

## Behavior

An embargo is an object: bound content, lift timestamp, bound parties, the outlet holding the
exclusive, and the terms. It is referenced by every channel and owned by none of them (see
21.02 CX-01).

**Exclusivity is a constraint, not a note.** Two outlets promised the same premiere is a
contradiction the system must catch when the second promise is made — not at publication, when it
is a burned relationship and possibly a pulled piece. That means exclusivity has to be modelled
structurally, not typed into a free-text field.

**Lift is a wall-clock fact.** When lift time arrives, it has arrived. Downstream actions (smart
link goes live, beats unblock, coverage capture starts watching) fan out from it, and if one of
them fails, the embargo has still lifted. Rolling back a lift is not a coherent operation —
nothing can un-publish the outlet's piece.

**The date-change collision.** A negotiated lift time is an *external commitment* (21.01.04). If
the release moves, the lift must not silently follow, because a human agreed that time with an
outlet. This is the sharpest interaction in the sub-domain.

### Happy Path

1. Press outreach (21.02.03) produces interest; an outlet wants the premiere.
2. Musician records the exclusive: outlet, content scope, lift time, what the outlet promised.
3. System registers the embargo and computes everything it binds.
4. Bound parties are told — including the Producer, who receives the constraint in project context without the campaign.
5. Beats scheduled inside the window (21.01.03) are blocked; the smart link's live date (21.04.04) aligns to lift.
6. Lift time arrives. Fan-out: link live, beats unblock, coverage capture (21.07) begins watching for the piece.
7. The premiere URL becomes the coverage log's first entry and the quote flows to the EPK.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A second outlet is promised the same exclusive | Caught at set time as a contradiction | "This content is already exclusively promised to [outlet] until [time]." Structural, not advisory |
| Artist or Producer posts inside the window via 21.06 | Blocked before publication — this is the feature's primary job | Blocked with the embargo named and its lift time. The block *is* the value |
| Artist posts on Instagram directly, outside WeJammin | We cannot prevent it. Pretending otherwise is dishonest | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — can we *detect* a breach fast enough to matter? The first minutes are when an exclusive is still salvageable |
| Release date moves; lift time was negotiated with the outlet | Lift does **not** auto-follow — it is an external commitment requiring renegotiation | Cascade preview (21.01.04) flags it as externally committed and names the counterparty |
| Outlet breaks its own embargo (publishes early) | Everyone else's embargo is now pointless; the artist needs to release the others immediately | Breach detection should offer immediate lift-for-all. Speed is the only remedy |
| Outlet takes the exclusive and never publishes | The window burned for nothing, and the artist has no recourse | Recorded against the outlet in the CRM (21.03.02) — this is exactly the fact that should inform the next cycle and that a spreadsheet always forgets |
| Two embargoes on one asset (print exclusive + radio first-play) | Not merged — separate objects, separate lift times, both binding | Both shown; the earliest lift governs what is actually public |
| Lift fires but a downstream action fails (link doesn't go live) | Embargo has still lifted. No rollback | Downstream failure surfaced independently; the lift is not reversed |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | No embargo on this content | Content publishes freely |
| Negotiating | Outlet interested, terms not agreed | Provisional; binds nothing yet |
| Active | Embargo set, lift in the future | Countdown; everything bound is visibly bound; bound parties notified |
| Breach risk | A blocked publish attempt occurred | Surfaced to the artist — somebody just tried, and will probably try again outside the platform |
| Breached | Content is public before lift | High severity; salvage window is minutes |
| Lifted | Lift time reached | Fan-out fired; content free |
| Void | Outlet never published / exclusive abandoned | Recorded against the outlet for the next cycle |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The breach vector is **the artist's own side**, not the outlet — and the person most likely to break an embargo is the one nobody ever tells it exists | Reasoning about who actually leaks. Outlets have a professional interest in honouring embargoes; they trade on it. The artist's Producer, bandmate or manager has no idea an exclusive was negotiated and posts a studio photo. `meta/personas.md` records the Producer's worst *accidental* behaviour as exactly this class — harming the project through absence of information rather than intent | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it defines the Producer's Role Lens here as the sub-domain's one non-None access. The design principle that falls out is unusual and worth stating: **the constraint must propagate further than the plan does.** Everyone who can break it must know it exists; almost none of them should see the campaign. Most access models cannot express that shape, which is probably why no existing tool does this. |
| DT-02 | The embargo is really a 21-local concern, since it originates in press outreach | Initial framing — it arrived as part of sweep candidate 03 | ❌ REJECTED — the *mechanism* (content that must not surface before timestamp T, enforced across every publication surface, binding parties who cannot see why) is not press-specific at all. It is identical to a scheduled release in 12, a tour announce in 17/19, an unlisted product drop in 13/14, and a fan-only reveal in 20. Building it inside press outreach would mean rebuilding it four more times, inconsistently, and the inconsistency is where breaches live. **Routed as the `Timed Reveal / Embargo Lock` cross-cut**; what stays here is the *premiere negotiation* — who gets the exclusive, what they promised, what happens when they don't deliver. That part is genuinely press-domain. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Exclusivity is modelled structurally, not as free text | A double-promised premiere must be caught when the second promise is made, not at publication. |
| D-02 | The constraint propagates further than the plan | See DT-01. Everyone who can breach must know; almost none of them should see the campaign. |
| D-03 | Lift is a wall-clock fact with no rollback | Nothing can un-publish the outlet's piece. Modelling lift as a transaction would be modelling a fiction. |
| D-04 | The generic time-lock mechanism is routed to a cross-cut; premiere negotiation stays here | See DT-02. |
