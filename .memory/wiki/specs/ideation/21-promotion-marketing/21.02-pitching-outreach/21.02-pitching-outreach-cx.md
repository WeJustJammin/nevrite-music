## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Embargo ↔ every channel

**Relationship**: An embargo is a timestamp before which specified content must not become
public, plus the set of parties bound by it. It is not a property of the press pitch that created
it — it binds the DSP pitch (confidentiality), the playlist pitch (asset sharing), the radio
service copy, the content calendar (21.01.03), the smart link's live date (21.04.04), and the
artist's and Producer's own social posting. The breach vector is almost never the outlet; it is
someone on the artist's own side posting a studio photo.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets the embargo, sees what it binds, and is the party who most often breaks it.
- **Producer**: **bound but not privileged** — they can break an embargo with one studio photo and today nobody ever tells them one exists. They need to know the constraint without seeing the campaign.
- **Operator**: not affected. A tour-announce embargo is different and lives in 21.08.
- **Fan**: no visibility by definition — an embargo they can see is not an embargo.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The embargo is owned by 21.02.05 as a single object. Channels reference it; none of them owns or mutates it. Two embargoes on one asset (a print exclusive and a radio first-play) must be modelled as separate objects with separate lift times, not merged.
2. **Trigger chain**: Embargo lift is the trigger of a fan-out — smart link goes live, beats unblock, the premiere URL becomes the coverage log's first entry. If lift fires and a downstream action fails, the embargo has still lifted: it is a wall-clock fact, not a transaction. Downstream failures are independently recoverable and must not roll the lift back.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and inverted from the usual direction. Being bound by an embargo requires *no* access to the campaign — the Producer must receive the constraint without receiving the plan.
4. **Notification fan-out**: On set, everyone bound must be told. On lift, everyone waiting must be released. On breach, the artist must know immediately — the exclusive may still be salvageable in the first minutes.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — the embargo can race the date-change cascade (21.01.04). If the release moves, the embargo lift attached to the old date must not silently follow, because the lift time was *negotiated with an outlet* and is an external commitment, not a derived offset.

### CX-02: Rate Limits ↔ Playlist / Press

**Relationship**: 21.02.06 sits above the human-relationship channels as a constraint layer,
capping how much any sender can push at any target and how much any target receives in total
across all senders. It does not apply to 21.02.01 (the DSP's own form has its own one-shot rule)
and applies weakly to 21.02.04 (radio pitching is low-volume and relationship-gated by nature).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: experiences it as friction, and will resent it. The design problem is making the constraint feel like quality rather than punishment.
- **Producer / Operator / Fan**: not affected — they do not send pitches.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The limit counter is per (sender, target) and per target globally. The global counter is the one that protects the gatekeeper and it is shared across every sender — meaning one target's inbox budget is a commons, and commons get tragedied.
2. **Trigger chain**: Send → check limits → allow / hold / block. Synchronous and blocking; a rate limit checked after sending is not a rate limit.
3. **Permission intersection**: Possibly — a paid tier that buys higher limits is the obvious monetisation and it is also exactly how a pitching platform becomes the spam cannon it promised not to be. Flagged as a product decision, not ours (see Q-02 on the feature).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A blocked send must tell the sender *why*, specifically, with the reset time. A silent drop is worse than a rejection.
5. **State transition conflict**: A target can opt out entirely (see 21.02.06) while sends are queued against them. Opt-out must win over queued state — the queue is our convenience, their opt-out is their right.

### CX-03: DSP Editorial ↔ Playlist & Curator

**Relationship**: Both aim at playlists; they are legally and contractually opposite. DSP
editorial is pitched free through the DSP's own tool and cannot be bought. Third-party/independent
playlists are where paid placement is offered — and paying for placement on them violates DSP
terms and can get the *artist's* track removed. The user does not reliably know these are
different things, and the platform's obligation is to make the distinction structural rather than
explanatory.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: must never be able to confuse the two paths. The confusion is the harm.
- **Producer / Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — different target types, different pitch objects. The shared object is the *track*, which both reference read-only.
2. **Trigger chain**: None directly. Both read the grid's deadlines.
3. **Permission intersection**: The payola guardrail (21.05.03) gates 21.02.02 and does not gate 21.02.01 — because there is nothing to buy on the editorial path.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Independent.
5. **State transition conflict**: None. Recorded because the *absence* of interaction is the point: the design must keep them visibly separate, and the temptation to unify them into one "playlist pitching" surface is exactly the trap.
