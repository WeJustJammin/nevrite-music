# Feature: Pitch Rate Limits & Curator Inbox Protection

> **Number**: 21.02.06
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A directory of gatekeepers plus a send button is a spam cannon aimed at the exact people the
product depends on. This feature is the constraint layer that stops WeJammin from destroying the
resource it monetises: caps per sender, caps per target across all senders, opt-out that beats
everything, and quality gates that make the limit read as strategy rather than punishment. Nobody
asked for this feature. Every platform that skipped it was sanctioned by the gatekeepers it
needed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Cannot configure limits — that is the point. Sees their remaining budget *before* composing, not on submit; sees why a target is unavailable (their opt-out, a global cap, a quality gate); sees the reasoning framed as effectiveness, because it is: bulk pitching does not work, and the limit is the platform declining to help them do the thing that fails. |
| Producer | None | Not a sender. No exposure. |
| Operator | None | Not a sender in this sub-domain. Event outreach in 21.08 needs its own answer to this same problem — see Q-03. |
| Fan | None | Not a sender. Note again that the *protected party* — the curator or journalist — is not one of the four personas at all (Q-01, sub-domain index). We are building protection for people who have no seat in our persona model, which is itself the finding. |

## Behavior

Three mechanisms, in increasing order of authority:

1. **Per-sender caps** — how many pitches one artist may send in a window. Protects targets from
   any single enthusiast.
2. **Per-target global caps** — how much any one target receives from *all* WeJammin senders
   combined. This is the one that actually protects the gatekeeper, and it is a commons: every
   sender draws from the same finite inbox budget. Commons get tragedied, which is why this
   cannot be a per-user setting.
3. **Target opt-out** — absolute. Beats queues, beats paid tiers, beats everything. A gatekeeper
   who does not want WeJammin pitches does not receive them, and that must be true without them
   holding an account (Q-01 again: they are off-platform by assumption D-03).

Plus **quality gates**: near-identical content across targets, no personalisation, obvious genre
mismatch. These are the difference between a rate limit (crude, resented) and a relevance
constraint (still resented, but correct).

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens a pitch channel; remaining budget is visible before they invest any effort.
2. Selects targets; each shows its own availability state and why.
3. Composes; quality gates evaluate before send, not after.
4. Sends within budget; counters decrement — both theirs and the target's global counter.
5. Budget exhausted: blocked, with the reset time and the reason, framed as effectiveness.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Target's global cap is exhausted by *other* artists' pitches | This artist is blocked through no fault of their own | The honest message is uncomfortable: "This curator has received their maximum pitches this week." It reveals that our users compete for a commons. Hiding it would be worse |
| Artist pays for a higher tier and expects more sends | **The central design trap.** Selling higher limits converts the protection into a toll and turns us into the spam cannon we refused to be — with a revenue incentive to be worse | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02. This is a product decision with a values component and it should be made deliberately, not discovered |
| Target opts out while pitches are queued to them | Opt-out wins; queue is purged | Artist told the target opted out; the queued effort is lost, correctly |
| Quality gate fires a false positive on a genuinely personalised pitch | Artist is blocked from sending something good | Gates must be appealable/overridable with friction, not absolute — a false positive that cannot be overridden is worse than a marginal spam leak |
| Artist works around limits by sending from their own email client | Entirely possible and we cannot stop it | If sending is *as the artist* (Q-02, sub-domain index), our limits are advisory and the protection is theatre. If sending is *as WeJammin*, they are real and existential. The sender-identity decision determines whether this feature works at all |
| A target is also a WeJammin user (a curator who is also a musician) | Their opt-out and their account are different objects; conflating them means a musician who opts out of pitches loses their own sending | Must be separable |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available | Budget remaining, target accepting | Send affordance live, remaining budget shown |
| Constrained | Budget low | Warning framed as prioritisation: "3 pitches left this week — spend them on the best fits" |
| Blocked — sender cap | Personal budget exhausted | Reset time + the effectiveness rationale |
| Blocked — target cap | Target's global budget exhausted | Honest explanation of the commons |
| Blocked — opt-out | Target opted out | Permanent for that target; no appeal, no affordance |
| Blocked — quality gate | Content failed relevance/duplication checks | Specific reason + override path with friction |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A directory plus a send button is a **spam cannon**, and every platform in this category has had to solve it — by charging the sender, capping volume, or compensating the gatekeeper for attention | Reasoning about what a pitching marketplace does to its supply side. The gatekeeper's attention is the scarce resource; the platform's growth incentive is to sell access to it; those two facts are in direct opposition and the platform always wins in the short run and dies in the long run | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is why this feature exists at all despite appearing on no candidate list and being wanted by no user. The domain index's payola concern is downstream of this same structure: once gatekeeper attention is scarce and monetisable, someone will sell it. This node is the domain's immune system, and it was missing entirely from a 12-candidate sweep — which is itself evidence that sweeps optimise for what users ask for, not for what keeps the product alive. |
| DT-02 | Rate limits should be a paid tier — higher limits for paying artists | Obvious monetisation; every outreach tool does it; it would work | ❌ REJECTED as a default, escalated to the owner (Q-02). Selling limits inverts the feature: the protection becomes a toll, and the platform acquires a direct financial incentive to make its own users spam harder. The gatekeepers respond by opting out en masse, and the directory — the domain's actual asset — becomes worthless. This is not a moral objection; it is that the revenue destroys the asset it is extracted from. But pricing is a **product decision** and `.claude/rules/decision-classification.md` puts it squarely with the owner, so it is presented, not decided. |
| DT-03 | The protected party is **not one of the four personas** — we are building protection for people with no seat in our model, whose interests therefore have no natural advocate in any downstream spec | Reasoning about Role Lens completeness. Musician, Producer, Operator, Fan — a Pitchfork editor is none of them. `meta/personas.md` Q-01 already flags an analogous gap for the dealer persona in 13/14/15 | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the sub-domain's most important structural finding, escalated to Q-01 on the sub-domain index. It has a concrete consequence: every downstream spec writer will read the Role Lens, see four personas none of whom is the curator, and optimise for the sender. The curator's interests are represented in this system only by this feature. That is a fragile place to put the thing the whole domain depends on. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Limits are not user-configurable | A protection the protected-from party can adjust is not a protection. |
| D-02 | Target opt-out is absolute and works without an account | Assumption D-03 puts targets off-platform. An opt-out that requires signing up is not an opt-out. |
| D-03 | Quality gates are overridable with friction, never absolute | A false positive that cannot be overridden costs a real opportunity; a marginal spam leak costs less. |
| D-04 | Budget is shown before composition, not on submit | Discovering the limit after writing the pitch is a hostile design that teaches users to resent the constraint. |
