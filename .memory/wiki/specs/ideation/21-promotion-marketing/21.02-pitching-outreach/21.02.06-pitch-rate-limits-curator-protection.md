# Feature: Pitch Rate Limits & Curator Inbox Protection

> **Number**: 21.02.06
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A directory of gatekeepers plus a send button is a spam cannon aimed at the exact people the
product depends on. This feature is the constraint layer that stops WeJammin from destroying the
resource it monetises: caps per sender, caps per target across all senders, opt-out that beats
everything, and quality gates that make the limit read as strategy rather than punishment. Nobody
asked for this feature. Every platform that skipped it was sanctioned by the gatekeepers it
needed — deliverability collapses, the domain lands on a blocklist, and the directory (the
sub-domain's actual asset) becomes worthless. This node is the domain's immune system.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Cannot configure limits — that is the point (D-01). Sees their remaining per-channel budget *before* composing, not on submit (D-04); sees why any target is unavailable (their opt-out, an exhausted global cap, a quality gate), each with a distinct named reason and, where applicable, a reset time. Sees the constraint framed as effectiveness because it is: bulk pitching converts to nothing, and the cap is the platform declining to help them do the thing that fails. Can request a quality-gate override with friction (D-03) but cannot raise, reset, or bypass any numeric cap. |
| Producer | None | Not a sender in this sub-domain. Their session facts feed a pitch's credibility (credits) but they neither send, see, nor are governed by these limits. No exposure. |
| Operator | None | Not a sender here. Event/tour outreach in 21.08 is the same shape — a venue pitching local press — with the Operator as sender, and needs its own instance of this protection (Q-03). It is not inherited automatically. |
| Fan | None | Not a sender. Note again that the *protected party* — the curator, journalist, radio programmer, or DSP editor — is **not one of the four personas** (Q-01, sub-domain index; Q-04 below). We are building protection for people who have no seat in the persona model, whose interests therefore have no natural advocate in any downstream spec except this feature. |

## Behavior

Three enforcement mechanisms, in increasing order of authority, plus a relevance layer. All
numeric values below are **design defaults** — behavioural starting points, not architecture —
and their exact tuning is a product/operations decision (Q-05).

1. **Per-sender caps** — how many pitches one sender account may send in a rolling window, counted
   **per channel** (playlist, press/blog, radio are separate budgets so exhausting one does not
   block another). Default: **10 pitches per channel per rolling 7 days**, where a consumed pitch
   returns to the budget 7 days after it was sent — not a fixed weekly calendar reset. Protects
   targets from any
   single enthusiast. This cap is a floor of protection, not the real backstop (see mechanism 2
   and DT-04).
2. **Per-target global caps** — how many pitches any one target receives from **all** WeJammin
   senders combined. Default: a single target receives at most **15 WeJammin-originated pitches
   per rolling 7 days** across every sender. This is the mechanism that actually protects the
   gatekeeper, and it is a **commons**: every sender draws from the same finite inbox budget.
   Commons get tragedied, which is exactly why this cannot be a per-user setting and cannot be
   sold per-sender (D-01, DT-02). The global count is authoritative and must never overshoot the
   cap — two senders competing for the last slot is a real race the system has to resolve so the
   target never receives a 16th pitch (see Edge Cases).
3. **Target opt-out** — absolute. Beats queues, beats any paid tier, beats every other rule. A
   gatekeeper who does not want WeJammin pitches does not receive them, and that is true **without
   them holding a WeJammin account** (D-02; D-03 sub-domain index puts targets off-platform).
   Opt-out is keyed to the target's contact identifier (email address / submission handle), is
   **global** (suppresses every WeJammin sender, not just the one who triggered it), and persists
   until the address owner reverses it.

Plus **quality gates** — the relevance layer that turns a crude rate limit into a correctness
constraint:

- **Templated-content gate**: near-identical pitch body reused across targets. Default trigger:
  text similarity ≥ **85%** across ≥ **3** targets in one send batch → flagged as templated.
- **No-personalisation gate**: pitch contains zero target-specific tokens (curator name,
  playlist/outlet name, or a track-to-target rationale) → surfaced as a **warning**, not a hard
  block.
- **Genre-mismatch gate**: the track's primary genre is not in the target's declared
  accepted-genre set → flagged. Fails **open** (does not block) when the target record has no
  accepted-genre data, because blocking on data we do not have punishes the sender for our gap.

All quality gates are **overridable with friction** (D-03), never absolute: an override requires
an explicit typed confirmation, is logged against the sender, and is capped at **2 overrides per
rolling 7 days** before the account is escalated to Trust & Safety (domain 24) for review.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens a pitch channel for a track. Remaining budget for that channel (e.g. "7 of 10
   pitches left this week") renders **before** they invest any composing effort (D-04).
2. Selects targets from the directory (21.03.01). Each target shows its own availability state and
   the reason for it: available, near its global cap, at its global cap, or opted out.
3. Composes the pitch. Quality gates evaluate the draft **before** send, inline, not after.
4. Sends within budget. On success **two** budgets change together — the sender's per-channel
   budget and the target's global count — and the user never observes one moving without the
   other: either the send counts against both, or (on any failure) against neither. The send is
   logged to the CRM (21.03.03).
5. Continues until budget is exhausted, at which point the send affordance is blocked with the
   next-pitch-returns time ("your next pitch frees up in 2 days") and the effectiveness framing.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Target's global cap exhausted by *other* senders' pitches | This sender is blocked through no fault of their own; the global cap (mechanism 2) is authoritative and does not care who consumed it | The honest, uncomfortable message: "This curator has received their maximum pitches this week." It reveals that senders compete for a commons. Hiding it would be worse — it would teach the sender the cap is arbitrary rather than shared |
| Sender on a higher paid tier expects more sends | **The central design trap, now resolved for the default.** Selling higher caps converts protection into a toll and gives the platform a revenue incentive to make its own users spam harder (DT-02). **Default behaviour: tier does NOT raise any cap.** The per-target global cap is absolute regardless of any tier (selling it would sell the commons). The per-sender cap is also not raised by default. Whether *any* higher limit is ever sellable is a live owner decision (Q-02) — but until the owner rules otherwise, the enforced behaviour is: paid tier grants zero additional pitch budget | If a paid user hits the cap, the block message is identical to a free user's and includes: "Pitch limits protect the curators you're trying to reach — they don't scale with your plan." No upsell affordance appears on the block |
| Two senders send to the same target at its 15th/last global slot simultaneously | The global cap holds: exactly one send succeeds, the other is rejected. The count never overshoots to 16, regardless of timing | Winner: normal sent confirmation. Loser: "This curator reached their limit while you were composing" — pitch is **not** sent, is preserved as a draft, and no per-sender budget is consumed (a rejected send must not cost the sender a pitch) |
| Target opts out while pitches are already queued to them | Opt-out wins and purges the queue; any queued-but-unsent pitch to that target is cancelled before delivery | Sender told the target opted out; the queued effort is lost, correctly. Per-sender budget consumed by a purged queued pitch is **refunded** (the send never reached anyone) |
| Opt-out lookup service is unreachable at send time | **Fail closed.** If the system cannot confirm the target has *not* opted out, it does not send — sending to someone who may have opted out is the one failure this feature exists to prevent | "Can't confirm this curator's contact preferences right now — try again shortly." Pitch held as draft; no budget consumed |
| Quality gate false-positive on a genuinely personalised pitch | Sender is blocked from sending something good; an unappeallable gate that costs a real opportunity is worse than a marginal spam leak (D-03) | Specific reason ("this looks templated — 3 of your targets got near-identical text") plus the friction override path; override is logged and counts toward the 2-per-week ceiling |
| Sender forges / triggers an opt-out on a target to deny competitors | Because opt-out is **global** (suppresses every WeJammin sender including the attacker), sabotaging a target removes it from the attacker's own reach too — the incentive to weaponise opt-out is removed by design. An opt-out request also notifies the target's contact address so the real owner is always informed and can reverse it | Attacker gains nothing; the target and all senders see the same "not accepting pitches" state |
| Sybil evasion: one human spins up N accounts to multiply per-sender budget | Per-sender caps become meaningless against multi-account abuse — which is precisely why the **per-target global cap is the real backstop**: it aggregates across all senders regardless of how many accounts exist, so 5 sock-puppets still cannot push a target past 15. Account-farm *detection* is a domain-24 concern; the *containment* is structural and lives here | Each sock-puppet hits its own per-sender cap; the target is protected by the global cap they collectively share |
| Malicious pitch content (script/markup injected to break the quality-gate check or the CRM render) | Pitch body is normalised and sanitised before both gate evaluation and any downstream render; gates operate on stripped text, and no sender-supplied markup is executed anywhere | No visible difference for a legitimate sender; the injected payload is inert |
| Target record is partial — has a contact identifier but no accepted-genre set | Genre-mismatch gate cannot evaluate, so it **fails open** (does not block); the other two gates and both caps still apply | No genre warning shown; sender is not penalised for our missing data |
| Sender works around limits by pitching from their own email client instead of WeJammin | Entirely possible and unstoppable **if** pitches send *as the artist* (their address). If pitches send *as WeJammin* (our domain, our deliverability), the limits are real and existential because the abuse lands on *our* reputation. The sender-identity decision (Q-02, sub-domain index) determines whether this whole feature is enforcement or theatre | Nothing in-product; this is the reason Q-01 below flags the feature's viability as conditional |
| Target is also a WeJammin user (a curator who is also a Musician) | Their two roles are governed independently: opting their curator-inbox out of received pitches must not reduce or disable their own sending budget, and exhausting their sending budget must not change what their curator-inbox receives. One person, two capacities, neither leaks into the other | Both behaviours work independently; a musician who opts their curator-inbox out can still pitch their own releases |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Budget, opt-out status, and per-target availability being resolved on channel open or target select | Budget meter and per-target availability shown in a loading state; send affordance disabled until all three resolve |
| Empty | New sender, no pitches sent on this channel yet | Full budget shown ("10 of 10 pitches this week") with the effectiveness framing surfaced *before* first send, not after |
| Available (Populated) | Budget remaining and target accepting | Send affordance live; remaining budget and the target's own remaining headroom both shown |
| Constrained | Personal budget low (≤ 3 remaining) | Warning framed as prioritisation: "3 pitches left this week — spend them on the best fits", not as punishment |
| Blocked — sender cap | Personal per-channel budget exhausted | Next-pitch-returns time ("next pitch frees up in 2 days") + the effectiveness rationale; no upsell |
| Blocked — target cap | Target's global budget exhausted by any combination of senders | Honest explanation of the shared commons; suggestion to try a different, available target |
| Blocked — opt-out | Target opted out | Permanent for that target until the target reverses it; no appeal, no send affordance, no override |
| Blocked — quality gate | Draft failed templated/genre check | Specific named reason + friction override path (counts toward 2/week ceiling) |
| Partial | Some availability data resolved, some pending or unknown (e.g. global count read but opt-out lookup degraded) | Per-target availability resolves individually; targets whose opt-out status is unconfirmed are shown as "checking…" and are **not** sendable until confirmed (fail-closed) — the sendable targets remain usable |
| Error | Availability/opt-out service unreachable or send rejected by a last-slot race | Named error with retry; drafts preserved, no budget consumed on failure |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A directory plus a send button is a **spam cannon**, and every platform in this category has had to solve it — by charging the sender, capping volume, or compensating the gatekeeper for attention | Reasoning about what a pitching marketplace does to its supply side. Gatekeeper attention is the scarce resource; the platform's growth incentive is to sell access to it; those two facts are in direct opposition, and the platform always wins short-term and dies long-term | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is why this feature exists despite appearing on no candidate list and being wanted by no user. The domain index's payola concern is downstream of the same structure: once gatekeeper attention is scarce and monetisable, someone will sell it. This node is the domain's immune system, and it was missing from a 12-candidate sweep — evidence that sweeps optimise for what users ask for, not for what keeps the product alive. |
| DT-02 | Rate limits should be a paid tier — higher limits for paying senders | Obvious monetisation; every outreach tool does it; it would work commercially | ❌ REJECTED as the default, escalated to the owner (Q-02). Selling limits inverts the feature: protection becomes a toll and the platform acquires a direct financial incentive to make its users spam harder. Gatekeepers respond by opting out en masse and the directory — the domain's actual asset — becomes worthless. This is not a moral objection; it is that the revenue destroys the asset it is extracted from. Pricing is a **product decision** per `decision-classification.md`, so it is presented, not decided — but the *default enforced behaviour* (tier grants zero extra budget) is specified now so the feature is complete without waiting on the pricing call. |
| DT-03 | The protected party is **not one of the four personas** — we are building protection for people with no seat in our model, whose interests have no natural advocate in any downstream spec | Reasoning about Role Lens completeness. Musician, Producer, Operator, Fan — a Pitchfork editor is none of them. `meta/personas.md` Q-01 already flags an analogous gap for the dealer persona | ✅ CONFIRMED — the sub-domain's most important structural finding (Q-01 sub-domain index, Q-04 here). Concrete consequence: every downstream spec writer reads the Role Lens, sees four personas none of whom is the curator, and optimises for the sender. The curator's interests are represented in this system *only* by this feature — a fragile place to put the thing the whole domain depends on. |
| DT-04 | The per-**sender** cap is the primary protection | Intuition — "limit how much each person can send" reads like the obvious lever | ❌ REJECTED — the per-sender cap is trivially defeated by multiple accounts (sybil), so it protects nothing against a determined abuser. The **per-target global cap** is the real backstop precisely because it aggregates across all senders and is therefore account-count-invariant: 5 sock-puppets share one target budget. This reframes the whole feature — the commons cap is load-bearing and the per-sender cap is merely a first, softer courtesy limit. It also means the global cap's **never-overshoot** guarantee is the single most safety-critical behaviour in the feature (see the last-slot race). |
| DT-05 | An opt-out that anyone can trigger for any off-platform address is a sabotage vector | Adversarial review: targets are off-platform (D-03), so we cannot cryptographically prove the person opting out owns the address | ✅ CONFIRMED as a risk, ❌ REJECTED as a blocker — because opt-out is **global**, sabotaging a target suppresses it for the attacker too, which removes the competitive incentive to forge one. Combined with notifying the target's own address on every opt-out (so the real owner is always informed and can reverse), the residual harm collapses to "someone can stop *everyone* including themselves from pitching a curator", which no rational competitor wants. The feature fails **safe** (toward not-sending), which is the correct direction for a protection layer. |
| DT-06 | When the opt-out lookup is unavailable, the send should proceed (fail-open) so the sender is not blocked by our outage | Availability-first instinct — don't let our infra problems break the user's workflow | ❌ REJECTED — a protection layer must fail **closed**. Sending to a target whose opt-out status we cannot confirm risks committing the exact violation the feature exists to prevent, and the cost (a missed spam-law/deliverability incident, our whole domain blocklisted) vastly exceeds the cost of a delayed pitch. When in doubt, do not send. The draft is preserved and no budget is consumed, so the sender loses nothing but time. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Limits are not user-configurable | A protection the protected-from party can adjust is not a protection. |
| D-02 | Target opt-out is absolute, global across all senders, and works without an account | Assumption D-03 puts targets off-platform. An opt-out requiring signup is not an opt-out; a per-sender opt-out is not protection. Global scope also removes the sabotage incentive (DT-05). |
| D-03 | Quality gates are overridable with friction (typed confirmation, logged, 2/week ceiling → 24), never absolute | A false positive that cannot be overridden costs a real opportunity; a marginal spam leak costs less. |
| D-04 | Budget is shown before composition, not on submit | Discovering the limit after writing the pitch is hostile design that teaches users to resent the constraint. |
| D-05 | The per-target **global** cap is the load-bearing protection; the per-sender cap is a softer courtesy limit | Per-sender caps are sybil-defeatable (DT-04); the global cap is account-count-invariant and is the real backstop. |
| D-06 | The global cap never overshoots under concurrent sends; a rejected send consumes no sender budget and preserves the draft | Two senders racing for the last slot must not overshoot the cap, and losing a race must cost the sender nothing but a retry. |
| D-07 | The feature **fails closed**: unconfirmed opt-out status blocks the send | A protection layer that fails open can commit the exact violation it exists to prevent (DT-06). |
| D-08 | Default enforced behaviour: a paid tier grants **zero** additional pitch budget, and no upsell appears on any cap block | Selling caps inverts the feature (DT-02); the default holds until/unless the owner rules otherwise (Q-02). |
