## Overview

Asking a gatekeeper to care: DSP editorial, playlist curators, press and blogs, radio. The domain
index observed that three roles — publicist, plugger, playlist pitcher — share one shape (a
target, a pitch, an embargo, a result), which is why they are one sub-domain rather than three.
What differs is the per-channel rules, and the rules are where all the danger lives: DSP editorial
is one-shot inside a hard window, playlists are a payola minefield, press runs on exclusivity and
embargo, radio runs on add dates and formats.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: the channels interact and compete. A press premiere's
embargo constrains the announce, which constrains the DSP pitch's confidentiality. Channels
contend for the same finite asset and the same timeline. And the rate-limit/curator-protection
capability is a platform-wide constraint on all of them at once. Multiple distinct capabilities,
genuinely interacting.

**The merge**: sweep candidates 02 (Playlist & Curator Pitching), 03 (Press, Blog & Media Outreach
+ Premiere/Embargo) and 04 (Radio & DSP Editorial Pitching) are folded here as channel features.
Candidate 04 is further **split**, because DSP editorial and terrestrial radio share nothing but
the word "pitch" — one is a web form with a hard algorithmic consequence, the other is a human
relationship with add dates and chart panels.

> **Note on the uniform Producer/Operator/Fan absence**: this is a real finding, not laziness.
> Release-cycle pitching is a single-persona sub-domain. Per `meta/personas.md`, personas are
> lenses on behaviour — a Producer pitching their own record is acting in the Musician lens. The
> Producer's only genuine stake is (a) appearing correctly credited *inside* a pitch, and (b)
> being bound by an embargo they could otherwise break by posting a studio photo. The Operator's
> marketing life is entirely in 21.08. The Fan is the audience the gatekeeper serves, never a
> participant here. Where a persona has real presence it is granted — see 21.02.05.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |

| D-01 | Candidates 02, 03, 04 **merged** into one sub-domain as channel features | The domain index itself identified the shared shape: "a contact database, a pitch, an embargo, a result". Three sibling sub-domains would triplicate the pitch object, the target reference, the send path and the response log to model rule differences that are better expressed as per-channel behaviour on one object. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | Candidate 04 **split**: DSP editorial ≠ radio | They share only the word "pitch". DSP editorial is a one-shot web form inside a hard window with an algorithmic consequence and no human relationship. Radio is a human relationship with add dates, format panels and statutory payola exposure under 47 U.S.C. § 317. Modelling them as one feature would produce a union of two disjoint rule sets. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-03 | **Pitch targets are assumed OFF-platform by default** | A Pitchfork editor or a Spotify editorial curator will not hold a WeJammin account on day one, and the product cannot be predicated on them doing so. Therefore a pitch is *outbound* (email / the DSP's own form / the outlet's submission form) with tracking on our side — not an inbox item on theirs. This is the single most consequential structural call in the sub-domain and it is recorded as an assumption to be challenged, not a conclusion. See Q-01 — **which DQ-10 explicitly declines to close** (11 D-10; domain 21 D-03). | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-04 | Payola guardrails are enforced here but **owned** by 21.05.03 | Playlist pitching is the primary payola vector, but the policy also binds paid promotion (21.05), the services marketplace (05) and coverage (21.07). One owner, many enforcement points. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
