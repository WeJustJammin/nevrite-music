# Feature: Press, Blog & Media Outreach

> **Number**: 21.02.03
> **Parent**: [Pitching & Outreach](./21.02-pitching-outreach-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Pitching journalists, blogs, magazines and podcasts — the publicist's job, done by the artist
because the artist cannot afford a publicist. A pitch is a targeted email with an EPK attached, a
story, an asset, and usually an embargo. The outcome, when it lands, is coverage (21.07), which
becomes a press quote, which becomes the credibility that wins the next pitch.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Builds a target list from the directory (21.03.01) and their own CRM (21.03.02); writes and personalises pitches; attaches the campaign EPK (21.09); sets embargoes and negotiates premieres (21.02.05); sends within rate limits; tracks responses; logs resulting coverage (21.07). Sees which journalists have historically covered artists like them — the thing a real publicist sells. |
| Producer | None | Does not pitch press. Their stake is indirect and real: the pitch and the resulting coverage name them as a credit, and a piece that mis-credits them is a reputational fact they cannot easily correct. Correction runs through domain 02, not here. |
| Operator | None | Release press is the artist's. A venue pitching local press about a show at their room does exist — and it lives in 21.08, where the Operator has full access scoped to their own events. See Q-02 on the domain index. |
| Fan | None | Not a participant. Fans encounter the *output* — the published article — through 21.07 and the public profile, never the pitch. |

## Behavior

Outbound, personalised, low-volume, relationship-driven. The mechanics are email; the value is
targeting and memory.

**Why the CRM matters more than the composer.** A press pitch's success is almost entirely a
function of whether the journalist covers this kind of music and whether there is any prior
relationship. The email itself is the least important part. This is why 21.03 is a sibling
sub-domain rather than a helper: the target data and the relationship history *are* the feature,
and the composer is a text box.

**Personalisation is not a template variable.** A merge-field pitch ("Hi {FIRST_NAME}, I loved
your piece on {RECENT_ARTICLE}") reads exactly as what it is and is the fastest way to burn a
target permanently. The product tension is that the platform's leverage is scale and the channel's
requirement is the opposite of scale. Rate limits (21.02.06) are the resolution, and they will
feel like an obstacle to every user.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens press outreach for the campaign; the grid (21.01.01) has already established the press lead-time deadline.
2. Builds a target list — directory suggestions filtered by genre and by who has actually covered comparable artists, plus their own prior contacts from 21.03.02 with the history attached ("last pitched 2025-11, replied, covered").
3. Writes the pitch; the EPK (21.09) attaches with verified credits; the story is theirs.
4. Sets an embargo if the release is unannounced (21.02.05).
5. Sends within rate limits; each send logs to the CRM (21.03.03).
6. Responses tracked. Interest may convert into a premiere negotiation (21.02.05).
7. Published coverage is captured into 21.07 and the quote flows back into the EPK for the next cycle.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Release date moves after pitches are sent | Pitches stated the old date and are now wrong. Cascade (21.01.04) classifies them **escaped** and drafts corrections the artist sends | Correction tasks per recipient; never auto-sent (21.01.04 DT-02) |
| Journalist has left the outlet (contact rotted) | Bounce or silence. Contact rot is the directory's central data-quality problem — music journalism has extreme churn | Bounce feeds directory freshness (21.03.01); the artist is told the contact is stale rather than left assuming rejection |
| Journalist opts out of pitches | Hard block across all senders | No send affordance; the target shows as not accepting |
| Artist sends a near-identical pitch to 200 targets | Rate limits block it | "Personalised pitches to 10 relevant journalists outperform 200 identical ones." — the constraint is the advice |
| Embargo broken by the artist's own social post | The exclusive collapses; the outlet may pull the piece and the relationship is damaged | Handled by 21.02.05; the breach vector is nearly always our own side |
| Two outlets both promised the same exclusive | Contradiction that must be caught at set time, not discovered at publication | 21.02.05 must model exclusivity as a constraint, not as a note in a text field |
| Pitch sent from our domain gets us marked as spam | If we send as WeJammin, one user's behaviour damages every user's deliverability | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on Q-02 on the sub-domain index. This is why sender identity is an architecture-shaping decision, not a preference |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The press pitch loop **closes on itself across campaigns**: coverage becomes a quote, the quote becomes EPK credibility, the credibility wins the next pitch — so the durable asset is the accumulated record, not any single send | Reasoning about why publicists are valuable and why their value walks out the door with them (domain index: "a personal spreadsheet that walks out the door when the person leaves"). Mapping that against `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis that the platform's job is making work *accumulate* rather than evaporate | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is this feature's connection to the platform thesis. Consolidation is why the artist is here sending the pitch; the accumulated relationship-and-coverage record is why they cannot leave. It also has a structural consequence: the pitch object must be durable and cross-campaign, not scoped to one release. A pitch tool that forgets last cycle rebuilds the exact spreadsheet we are replacing. |
| DT-02 | Merge-field personalisation is a reasonable compromise between scale and relevance | Efficiency instinct — it is what every outreach tool ships | ❌ REJECTED — a merge-field pitch is recognisable as one, and being recognised as a mass sender burns a music journalist permanently in a small industry with long memories. The channel's requirement (genuine specificity) is the exact opposite of the platform's natural leverage (scale). Shipping merge fields would let users destroy their most valuable relationships efficiently, which is a worse product than one that makes it slow. The honest resolution is to make the *targeting* smart and leave the writing to the human — which is also why rate limits are a feature and not a limitation. |
| DT-03 | Podcasts are a distinct channel and should be split out of press outreach (their own feature), the way DSP editorial was split from radio (D-02 on the sub-domain index) | Resolving this file's own Q-02: podcasts have a different lead time, a different ask (an on-air interview, not a "please listen and cover"), and a different asset (talking points, not a written EPK story) | ❌ REJECTED at Level 1 — the shape is identical to press: a target, a pitch, an asset, a result, and the same off-platform-outbound-with-tracking mechanics (sub-domain D-03). The differences are per-channel *behaviour*, not a different capability — exactly the test D-01 used to keep press/playlist/radio as channel features on one object rather than triplicating it. Podcasts fold in here as a channel variant. **Residual**: two variant needs are real and carried forward — (a) the ask is a booking not a placement, so the composer must offer a "pitch as guest" variant whose asset is a talking-points/availability one-sheet rather than the press EPK; (b) if podcast booking later grows interview-scheduling and prep needs of its own, that is the promotion trigger to split it into its own feature. Not fabricating that scheduling detail now — `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No merge-field mass personalisation | See DT-02. Efficient relationship destruction is not a feature. |
| D-02 | The pitch object is **durable and cross-campaign** | See DT-01. Scoping pitches to a release rebuilds the amnesia we exist to fix. |
| D-03 | Bounces are surfaced as contact rot, not as rejection | Music journalism churns hard. An artist who reads a bounce as "they hated it" learns the wrong lesson from a data-quality event. |
| D-04 | Podcasts fold in here as a **channel variant**, not a separate feature | See DT-03. Same shape and same outbound mechanics as press; the differences (interview-not-placement ask, talking-points-not-EPK asset) are per-channel behaviour on one pitch object. Splits out only if podcast booking grows its own scheduling/prep needs. |
