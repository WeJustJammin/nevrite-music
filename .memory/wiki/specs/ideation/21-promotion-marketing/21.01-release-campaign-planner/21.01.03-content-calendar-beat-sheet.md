# Feature: Content Calendar & Beat Sheet

> **Number**: 21.01.03
> **Parent**: [Release Campaign Planner](./21.01-release-campaign-planner-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The campaign's marketing beats — announce, teaser, single drop, video, release day, post-release
push — planned as offsets from the release anchor rather than absolute dates, so the whole plan
reflows for free when the date moves. This is the planning half of sweep candidate 09; the
actually-posting half is feature 21.06 (see D-03 on the sub-domain index). It is the artist's
day-to-day working view of the campaign: what goes out, when, on which channel, with what draft
attached.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Authors beats, sets each one's offset (R−28, R−7, R+3) or pins it to an absolute date, attaches draft content and assets, reorders, and reads the calendar as the day-to-day working view of the campaign. Sees beats collide with other campaigns and, when an availability signal exists, with tour dates. On a band campaign, edit rights follow the band-entity role (domain 01) — a member without campaign-edit rights reads but cannot author. |
| Producer | None | Marketing beat planning is not session work and per `meta/personas.md` the Producer will not visit a marketing surface. Their only campaign obligation — the master deadline — reaches them via 21.01.02 in project context. A Producer promoting their *own* release is acting in the Musician lens. |
| Operator | None | Release beats are the artist's. A venue planning its own event content works in 21.08, where the Operator has full access to event marketing scoped to their space. |
| Fan | None | The calendar is unannounced plan data. Fans see beats only once they publish — and a leaked calendar is a spoiled announce, which is a real and irreversible campaign loss. The plan surface is never exposed to a Fan session under any state. |

## Behavior

A beat is a planned piece of content with a position, a channel intent, and attached draft
material. Position is either an **offset** from the release anchor (the default, and the reason
the calendar survives a date change) or an **absolute pin** (a fixed festival slot, a magazine's
print date). The distinction must be explicit at authoring time, because inferring it is
guaranteed to be wrong in both directions.

Beats are plan objects. Publishing them is 21.06's job and is deliberately decoupled — the
calendar remains fully useful with zero social integrations connected, which matters given how
fragile those integrations are (see 21.06).

The calendar renders every beat against the *current* resolved anchor date, alongside the grid's
hard deadlines (21.01.02), any active embargo windows (21.02.05), and the beats of the artist's
*other* live campaigns. Where an artist-availability signal is exposed by 17/18, the calendar
reads it and warns — softly, never blocking — when a beat lands on a day the artist is on stage
or in transit. The warning is advisory because posting from the road is normal and only the
artist knows whether a given beat needs their live attention; the exact shape of that availability
signal is 17/18's to define (see Q-02, Cross-Cut Notes).

### Happy Path

1. Grid exists with a release anchor.
2. Musician adds a beat: "Announce + pre-save live", offset R−28, channels: social + email.
3. Adds further beats at R−14, R−7, R (release day), R+7.
4. Attaches draft copy and an asset to each; assets that don't exist yet appear on the gate (21.01.02).
5. Calendar renders beats against the current anchor alongside the grid's hard deadlines, so the artist can see that the announce sits *after* the DSP pitch — as it must, since pitching is confidential and announcing is not.
6. Beats with a connected channel can be handed to 21.06 to schedule; beats without one are still a plan.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Anchor moves such that an offset-anchored beat lands in the past | Beat is **not** silently rescheduled; it is flagged as stranded and requires a decision | "3 beats now fall before today. Review." — silently moving a plan item to a past date loses the artist's intent |
| Anchor moves and a beat has already published | Cascade (21.01.04) classifies it as **escaped** — it cannot be replanned, only corrected | The published beat is frozen on the calendar with its real publish date and an "already public" marker |
| Beat pinned absolute, anchor moves | Pinned beat does not reflow — by design — but its relationship to the campaign may now be nonsense (a "release day" post 4 weeks before release) | Pinned beats that lost coherence with the anchor are flagged, not moved |
| Beat scheduled inside an active embargo window | Blocked — publishing during an embargo breaks the exclusive and burns the outlet relationship | Blocked with the embargo named and its lift time shown (see 21.02.05) |
| Two campaigns' beats collide on the same day/channel | Surfaced as a collision, not prevented | Collision indicator; the artist may genuinely want a double-post day |
| Beat conflicts with a tour date (artist on stage / travelling) and 17/18 expose an availability signal | Soft warning, never a block — the calendar advises but does not decide; posting from the road is normal | "This beat lands on a show day (Manchester, R−9). Still want it here?" — advisory badge on the beat card |
| Beat conflicts with a tour date but 17/18 expose **no** availability signal | No warning is possible; the calendar cannot invent availability it cannot read | No badge — the absence is honest; the collision surfaces only if the artist also has a same-day beat on another campaign |
| Beat saved with neither an offset nor an absolute pin | Rejected at authoring — position is mandatory and its type must be explicit (D-01) | Inline validation: "Anchor this beat to the release (offset) or to a fixed date." |
| Two co-managers of the same band campaign edit the beat set at once (domain 01 multi-member entity) | Last-write-wins is unacceptable for a plan the whole band relies on; concurrent edits must not silently clobber | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec: concurrency model — optimistic-lock conflict vs field-level merge for the campaign plan]` |
| Band member without campaign-edit rights attempts to author or reorder a beat | Blocked at the permission boundary; edit rights derive from the band-entity role in domain 01, not from campaign membership alone | Read-only calendar with authoring controls hidden and a "you have view access to this campaign" note |
| Empty channel intent (beat authored with no channel selected) | Allowed as a pure plan item — a beat can exist as intent before its channel is chosen — but it cannot be handed to 21.06 until a channel is set | Beat card shows "no channel yet"; the hand-to-21.06 action is disabled with that reason |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Calendar opening, anchor and beats resolving | Skeleton grid with the anchor column marked; beats fade in as they resolve |
| Empty | Grid exists, no beats | Empty state offering a starter beat set derived from the campaign type, as a suggestion rather than an imposition |
| Planned | Beat authored, not scheduled | Beat card with offset, channel intent, draft content, asset link |
| Scheduled | Handed to 21.06 with a connected channel | Scheduled marker with the resolved absolute time |
| Published | 21.06 confirmed publication | Frozen on the calendar with real publish time; becomes an escaped item for cascade purposes |
| Stranded | Reflow put it in the past | Flagged, requires decision — never auto-resolved |
| Blocked | Embargo conflict | Blocked with the named embargo and lift time |
| Read-only | Viewer lacks campaign-edit rights on the band entity | Full calendar visible, all authoring/reorder controls hidden |
| Error | Publish attempt failed in 21.06 | Failure surfaced here (this is where the artist is looking) with the reason from 21.06 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Beats must be stored as **release-relative offsets**, not absolute dates, because the release date always moves | Reasoning about how campaigns are actually described in the industry — "announce at four weeks out, single two at two weeks" is the native vocabulary. Absolute dates are a rendering, not the data | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is what makes the date-change cascade tractable for this half of the campaign. But it forced a second realisation: absolute pins are also real (a print magazine's on-sale date does not move because your album did), so the model needs both, explicitly distinguished. A single-representation model is wrong either way. |
| DT-02 | A stranded beat (reflowed into the past) should be auto-rescheduled to keep the plan coherent | Product instinct — the reflow should "just work" | ❌ REJECTED — auto-rescheduling silently discards the artist's intent about *sequence*. If the announce was R−28 and the date pulled forward to R−10, the correct answer is not "announce yesterday" and it is not "announce today" — it is a strategic decision only the artist can make (compress the campaign, or move the date back). Automating it would produce a confidently wrong plan, which is worse than a visibly broken one. Flag and require a decision. |
| DT-03 | A tour-date conflict should *block* a beat from being placed on a show day | Symmetry with the embargo block — both are calendar conflicts, so treat them the same | ❌ REJECTED — embargo and tour-conflict are not the same class of event. An embargo is a *contractual* hard constraint: publishing inside it breaks an exclusive and burns an outlet relationship, so blocking is correct. A show day is only a *soft* signal: artists routinely post from the road, tour crews and co-managers exist precisely so content still ships while the artist is on stage, and the calendar cannot know whether a given beat needs the artist's live hands. Blocking here would obstruct a legitimate, common workflow. Warn, do not block. The block/warn distinction is the load-bearing decision for how the calendar reads external availability. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Beats are offset-anchored by default, absolute-pinned by explicit choice; position is mandatory and its type explicit | Offsets make reflow free; pins are real. Inferring which one the user meant would be wrong roughly half the time, in both directions. A beat with no position is rejected. |
| D-02 | Planning is fully decoupled from publishing | 21.06 depends on third-party APIs that are restrictive and change without notice. The valuable half must not be hostage to the fragile half. |
| D-03 | Stranded beats are flagged, never auto-moved | Sequence is strategy. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | Tour-date conflicts warn softly; only embargoes block | A contractual constraint blocks; an availability signal advises. See DT-03. Resolves Q-02 at behavior level — the *presence* of the warning is decided here; the *shape* of the availability signal remains 17/18's to define. |
