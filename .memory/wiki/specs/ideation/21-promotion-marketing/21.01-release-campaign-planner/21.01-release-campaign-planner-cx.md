## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Grid ↔ Asset Readiness Gate

**Relationship**: The grid emits dated obligations; the gate tracks whether the artefact each
obligation depends on exists and is final. The gate does not invent deadlines — it attaches state
to the grid's rows. A grid row without a gate status is a date nobody is accountable for.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees both — the date and whether they are going to make it. This is the whole product.
- **Producer**: sees the gate rows they own (master, stems, alternate mixes) and the dates attached. Does not see the marketing rows.
- **Operator**: not affected — release-cycle assets are not their business.
- **Fan**: no visibility. Campaign planning is invisible until it produces something public.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The grid owns the date; the gate owns the readiness state. Neither writes the other's field. An asset marked ready does not move a date — it clears a blocker on one.
2. **Trigger chain**: Gate → grid is advisory (a red asset flags the dependent deadline at risk). Grid → gate is derivational (a new deadline creates the gate rows it needs). Async both ways; no rollback needed because neither mutates the other.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the interesting one. A Producer can mark *their* asset ready but cannot see or affect the marketing deadline that consumes it. Write access to a gate row does not confer read access to the grid row it feeds.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An asset going red near its deadline must notify the Musician; an asset going green must notify whoever is blocked downstream. The Producer marking a master final is the event that unblocks distributor delivery in domain 12.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — an asset can be marked final and then revised (a recall). The gate must model "was final, no longer is", because the downstream action (distributor delivery) may already have fired on the first signal.

### CX-02: Grid ↔ Content Calendar

**Relationship**: Beats are stored as offsets from the release anchor, and the calendar renders
them against the current anchor. This is why a date move reflows the calendar for free — there is
no second source of truth to reconcile.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: authors and reorders beats; sees the calendar as the day-to-day working view.
- **Producer**: no visibility — this is marketing planning, not session work.
- **Operator**: no visibility.
- **Fan**: no visibility; sees only the beats that eventually publish.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The grid owns the anchor date. The calendar owns beat content and offsets. Only the offset is shared, and only the calendar writes it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Anchor change → recompute every beat's absolute date. Synchronous, deterministic, no failure mode — *unless* a beat has already published, at which point CX-03 takes over.
3. **Permission intersection**: Anyone who can edit the grid can edit the calendar. No divergence at this level. Band-entity approval (see domain CX) applies to publishing, not planning.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A reflow that moves a beat into the past must alert the Musician — silently rescheduling a post to a date that has already passed is a data-loss-shaped bug.
5. **State transition conflict**: A beat can be dragged to an absolute date the user intends to pin (e.g. a fixed festival slot). Pinned beats must not reflow. The model needs both offset-anchored and absolute-pinned beats, and the distinction has to be explicit rather than inferred.

### CX-03: Date-Change Cascade ↔ everything

**Relationship**: The anchor moves and three classes of consequence fall out — recomputable
(deadlines, unpublished beats), escaped (pitches sent, posts published, links live), and
externally committed (distributor delivery scheduled, premiere promised to an outlet on a date).
The cascade's job is to sort consequences into those three buckets and only automate the first.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: owns the decision to move, and receives the bill — the list of humans who must now be told.
- **Producer**: sees their deadlines move; is a beneficiary of a slip and a victim of a pull-forward.
- **Operator**: not affected unless the campaign is tour-linked (see domain CX).
- **Fan**: only affected if something already public changes — a pre-save link whose release date shifted.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The cascade is the only writer during a move. Concurrent edits to the grid during a cascade must be blocked or the recompute races the human.
2. **Trigger chain**: Anchor change → recompute → diff → classify → present. Escaped items produce *tasks*, never automated messages. Auto-emailing a journalist "the date changed" without the artist reading it first is a relationship-destroying action taken on their behalf.
3. **Permission intersection**: Moving the anchor is a high-privilege act — for a band entity it should require the same authority as approving a release, not the authority to edit a post.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Large. Producers (deadlines), pitch recipients (stated date now wrong), pre-savers (domain 20/21.04), distributor (domain 12). Fan-out is exactly why this is a feature and not a field update.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes, the sharpest in the sub-domain — a move can race a pitch send. If a pitch is in the send queue stating R = 2026-09-04 and the anchor becomes 2026-10-02 mid-flight, the queue must be held and re-confirmed. Sent is irreversible.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 21.01.02 Asset Readiness Gate | 21.01.03 Content Calendar & Beat Sheet | Considered because both are "things that must be ready by a date". Rejected: no shared state and no trigger dependency. Asset readiness concerns deliverables that gate *distribution* (master, artwork, metadata); calendar beats are marketing content that gates nothing. A late master stops the release; a late Instagram post does not. Both relate to the grid, but not to each other — the appearance of a connection is the grid showing through. |
| R-02 | 21.01.03 Content Calendar & Beat Sheet | 21.01.04 Date-Change Cascade | Partially rejected. The cascade reflows the calendar (covered in CX-02/CX-03 via the grid), but there is no *direct* calendar↔cascade relationship independent of the anchor. Recording it as its own pair would double-count the anchor dependency. |
