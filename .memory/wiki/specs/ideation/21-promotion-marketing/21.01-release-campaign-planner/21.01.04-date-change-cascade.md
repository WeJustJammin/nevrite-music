# Feature: Date-Change Cascade

> **Number**: 21.01.04
> **Parent**: [Release Campaign Planner](./21.01-release-campaign-planner-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The release date moves — it always moves — and everything hanging off it must be re-derived.
The cascade recomputes what can be recomputed, and refuses to automate what has already escaped
into the world: pitches already sent stating the old date, posts already published, links already
live, promises already made to an outlet. Labels call this "date change hell" and it is the
single most destructive routine event in a campaign.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Initiates the move; sees the impact preview *before* committing — what recomputes silently, what has escaped and needs a human correction, what is externally committed and may not be movable at all. Receives the resulting task list: the humans who must now be told. Owns every outbound correction. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees their deadlines move. A slip is relief; a pull-forward is a crisis, and it must reach them as an explicit change notice in project context — not as a silently-updated date they never notice until it is missed. |
| Operator | None | Not affected by release-cycle date changes. A *tour* date change is a different and far worse event, owned by 17/18 with marketing consequences in 21.08. |
| Fan | None | No access — but a date change can reach fans as a consequence: a pre-save already authorised now fires on a different day (21.04.02), and an announce already published now states a date that is wrong. |

## Behavior

The cascade's whole design is a three-way classification of consequences:

| Class | Definition | Cascade's action |
|---|---|---|
| **Recomputable** | Derived state nobody outside has seen — future deadlines, unpublished beats, gate due dates | Recompute silently. Free. |
| **Escaped** | Already public or already sent — pitches, published posts, live links, announced dates | Never automate. Generate a task naming the specific correction and the specific human. |
| **Externally committed** | A promise a third party is relying on — a premiere date given to an outlet, a scheduled distributor delivery, a confirmed DSP pitch | Flag as possibly immovable. May require renegotiation, and may be the reason the move cannot happen. |

The move is previewed before it is committed. A cascade that mutates first and reports second is
unusable, because the classification is exactly the information the artist needs to decide
whether to move at all.

### Happy Path

1. Musician changes the release anchor from 2026-09-04 to 2026-10-02.
2. Cascade computes the diff **without committing** and classifies every consequence.
3. Preview shows: 14 deadlines recompute silently; 3 pitches already sent state the old date; 1 announce post is published and public; 1 premiere is promised to an outlet for the old date; the distributor delivery is already scheduled.
4. Musician reviews. The premiere promise is the expensive one — they decide it is worth it.
5. Musician commits.
6. Recomputable items update. Escaped items become tasks with drafted (not sent) corrections. The Producer's deadline change is pushed to them as an explicit notice.
7. Externally-committed items are surfaced as required renegotiations, each linked to the counterparty and the original commitment.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A pitch is mid-flight in the send queue when the anchor moves | Queue is **held**; the queued pitch states a date that is now false | "3 pitches held — they state the old release date. Review before sending." Sending a knowingly-wrong date is worse than sending late |
| The new date forfeits the DSP editorial window (moved too close, or pulled forward inside the minimum) | Cascade surfaces the forfeit in the **preview**, before commit — this is the highest-value moment in the feature | "Moving to 2026-08-12 forfeits DSP editorial pitching (window closes 2026-08-05). This cannot be recovered." |
| The new date *recovers* a previously-forfeit window (moving later) | Also surfaced — a date slip is sometimes a rescue, and nobody realises it | "Moving to 2026-10-02 re-opens DSP editorial pitching. Pitch by 2026-09-04." |
| Distributor delivery already sent for the old date | Externally committed; may require a takedown/redelivery through domain 12, which has its own cost and lead time | Flagged with a link to 12; not automated under any circumstances |
| Concurrent edit — someone edits the grid while a cascade preview is open | Preview is stale; commit must not apply a diff computed against a changed base | Preview invalidated with a recompute action. The alternative — applying a stale diff — corrupts the grid silently |
| Anchor moved repeatedly in quick succession | Each move cascades from the *current* state; escaped-item tasks must not duplicate across moves | Task dedupe: one correction task per counterparty per campaign, updated rather than duplicated |
| Pre-saves already authorised for the old date | The pre-save fires on the release, not the date, so it survives — but the fan authorised on a stated expectation | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether fans are notified of a date change is partly a consent question, not a UX one. See 21.04.02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | No move in progress | Anchor is editable from the grid |
| Previewing | Anchor change entered | Classified impact preview; nothing has been mutated |
| Preview stale | Underlying grid changed during preview | Invalidated with recompute action — never a silent apply |
| Committing | Musician confirmed | Recomputation runs; escaped items become tasks |
| Committed with debts | Cascade done, corrections outstanding | Campaign header carries the outstanding-correction count until cleared |
| Blocked | An externally-committed item cannot move | The move can still proceed, but the conflict is named and persistent |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The cascade must classify consequences into recomputable / escaped / externally-committed, and only automate the first | Reasoning about what a date change actually breaks. A deadline is a number in our database; a pitch sent to a journalist is a fact in someone else's inbox. Treating them as the same object is what makes date changes so destructive today — the spreadsheet updates and nobody tells the journalist | ✅ CONFIRMED — this classification is the feature. Without it the cascade is a `UPDATE ... SET date = date + interval`, which is exactly the spreadsheet the industry already has and which is precisely why date changes go wrong. |
| DT-02 | The cascade should auto-notify pitch recipients that the date changed, since we know who they are and what we told them | Efficiency instinct — we have the data, the send is trivial | ❌ REJECTED — auto-emailing a journalist on the artist's behalf, in the artist's name, about a change the artist may not have decided how to frame, is a relationship-destroying action taken without consent. `meta/personas.md` records that the Musician's currency is relationships they cannot re-prove. We draft the correction; the human sends it. This also aligns with `decision-classification.md`: what to tell a counterparty is a product/relationship decision, never ours. |
| DT-03 | The preview's most valuable output is not the task list — it is telling the artist that the proposed date **forfeits or recovers a hard window before they commit to it** | Reasoning from 21.01.01 DT-01: hard windows are the only unrecoverable thing in the campaign. The moment a date is being chosen is the only moment that information can prevent the loss rather than describe it | ✅ CONFIRMED — this makes the cascade a *decision-support* feature, not a cleanup feature, and it inverts the framing. Post-hoc it is damage control; pre-commit it is the reason the damage never happens. It also means the preview must run on a hypothetical date the user is merely considering, which is a materially different requirement from reacting to a committed change. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The cascade previews before it commits | The classification is the information needed to decide whether to move. Computing it after the decision inverts the feature's value. |
| D-02 | Escaped items produce drafted tasks, never automated sends | See DT-02. The artist owns every word that reaches a counterparty in their name. |
| D-03 | Moving the anchor is a high-privilege action for a band entity | Per `meta/personas.md`, bands are first-class entities. The authority to move a release date is not the authority to edit a post, and conflating them lets one member torch a campaign. |
