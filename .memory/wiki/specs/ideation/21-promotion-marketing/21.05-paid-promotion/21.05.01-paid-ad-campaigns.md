# Feature: Paid Ad Campaigns

> **Number**: 21.05.01
> **Parent**: [Paid Promotion](./21.05-paid-promotion-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Buying attention on the platforms where it lives — Meta, TikTok, YouTube, Google. Ads are the
clean case in this sub-domain: an impression is not an endorsement, nobody is being paid to say
they like the record, and the guardrail (21.05.03) barely touches it. The hard questions here are
not ethical, they are about how deep the platform should go: planner, integration, or agency.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Plans spend against the campaign grid (21.01.01); connects ad accounts; builds or picks creative; sets budget and audience; sees results — honestly, with modelled numbers labelled as modelled (21.04.03 D-04). Sees ad spend as a business expense against this release (23), which is the thing artists never actually track and which decides whether the campaign made money. |
| Producer | None | Not a marketing function. |
| Operator | None | Boosting an event post is real Operator behaviour and lives in 21.08, where their spend is tied to a show rather than a release. |
| Fan | None | No tool access — **and a real stake**. If ad audiences are built from WeJammin's fan graph (Q-02, sub-domain index), the Fan is a data subject with objection rights under D-13, not a resource. Their absence from this Role Lens is an access fact, not an interest fact. See DT-03. |

## Behavior

Depends entirely on Q-01 (sub-domain index) — planner, integrated, or agency. Written here at the
level that survives all three: the artist decides what to spend, on what, aimed at whom, and finds
out what happened.

**What the platform genuinely knows that an ad tool does not**: the release date and the campaign
grid (so spend can be timed to the beats that matter rather than smeared), the fan graph (audience
building — which is Q-02's problem), the finance ledger in 23 (so spend lands against the release's
actual P&L), and the honest attribution boundary (21.04.03). None of that is ad expertise; all of
it is context an ad tool cannot have.

**What the platform must not pretend**: that it can tell the artist whether the ad worked. Ad
platforms report modelled conversions post-ATT and post-cookie; those are the ad platform's model
output, not measurements, and the label must survive the import into our UI.

### Happy Path

1. Musician plans spend against grid beats — announce, release day, the post-release push.
2. Connects ad accounts (OAuth; cross-cut).
3. Builds a campaign: budget, audience, creative (often a seeded creator's post — CX-02).
4. Launches; spend runs.
5. Results import — **with their modelled/measured provenance intact**.
6. Spend posts to the release's P&L in 23, which is where the artist learns whether any of this made sense.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Ad account banned or restricted | Music accounts get flagged routinely — copyright automation, unusual spend patterns, new accounts — and it can happen mid-campaign at the worst moment | The failure is the ad platform's and the artist experiences it as ours. If we are integrated (Q-01b) we own the support burden for a decision we did not make and cannot appeal |
| Ad rejected for music copyright — on the artist's own music | The classic self-own: their own track triggers an automated claim | Named specifically, because "ad rejected" sends the artist looking for the wrong problem. Ties to 09/11 |
| Ad platform API breaks or changes | Permanent, recurring, unannounced | Architecture risk routed to `/create-prd` (D-04). It is the main argument for Q-01a (planner, not integration) |
| Attribution asked to prove the ad drove streams | Cannot be answered by anyone | 21.04.03 D-01 governs; correlation labelled as correlation |
| Fan-graph audience built without a lawful basis | Using the fan list to build custom/lookalike audiences is the most obvious value of holding it and the most direct route to breaking it | See Q-02 on the sub-domain index and DT-03 |
| Artist spends into a forfeit campaign | The DSP window is already lost; the spend cannot recover it | The grid knows. Warning before spend, not after — this is the planner's dividend |
| Budget outruns the release's revenue | The thing artists never notice until the accounting | 23 makes it visible; nothing else in the artist's stack ever does |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not connected | No ad account linked | Connect prompt (or, under Q-01a, deep links out and nothing to connect) |
| Planned | Spend allocated to beats, not launched | Plan against the grid; useful with zero integrations |
| Live | Campaign running | Spend and results with provenance labels |
| Rejected | Ad platform refused the creative | Specific reason surfaced, especially the copyright self-own |
| Restricted | Account actioned by the platform | Our worst support state — a failure we did not cause and cannot fix |
| Complete | Campaign ended | Results with the observability boundary stated; spend posted to 23 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform's contribution here is **timing and consequence**, not ad expertise — spend aligned to the grid's beats and landed against the release's P&L in 23 | Reasoning about what we know that Meta Ads Manager cannot: the release date, the campaign structure, the forfeit windows, the revenue. An ad tool knows how to buy impressions far better than we ever will; it has no idea the artist is buying them against a window they already forfeited, or that the spend exceeded what the release earned | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it argues for Q-01a (planner) over Q-01b (integration). The valuable half needs no API at all: knowing that this spend lands two days after the pitch window closed is a *grid* fact. The API-integrated half is the fragile, commodity, support-heavy part where we compete with the ad platforms' own tools and lose. |
| DT-02 | We should integrate the ad platform APIs so artists can run everything from WeJammin — that is what consolidation means | Direct reading of D-18's consolidation thesis | ❌ REJECTED as stated — this misreads the thesis. D-18 says consolidation is the platform *because it puts us in the room where provenance is capturable*. Ad buying captures no provenance and generates no fact worth preserving; it is the one activity in this domain with no thesis connection whatsoever. Meanwhile it imports permanent API-breakage risk (D-04), a support burden for account bans we cannot appeal, and direct competition with Meta's own free tooling. Consolidation is a means, not a virtue — where it buys no provenance and costs a permanent maintenance tax, it is just scope. Escalated as Q-01 because the shape of the feature is the owner's call, but the recommendation is planner. |
| DT-03 | Building ad audiences from WeJammin's fan graph is the obvious play — it is the most valuable thing about holding a fan graph | Reasoning about what the fan data is *for*, commercially | ✅ CONFIRMED as valuable, ❌ **unresolved** as acceptable — escalated to Q-02. Exporting fan data to Meta to build custom and lookalike audiences means the fan's relationship with the artist becomes targeting data at a third party. Per D-11 fans are first-class users, and D-13 attaches statutory duties that scale with fan reach. `meta/personas.md` gives the Fan no professional stake — they are here because they like a band, and they did not sign up to be an audience segment. There is a legitimate version (explicit, granular, revocable consent) and a lucrative version (bury it in the ToS). The distance between them is the whole question, and it is the owner's. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Recommendation is **planner, not integration** | See DT-01/DT-02. The valuable half needs no API; the API half is fragile, commodity, and captures no provenance. Escalated as Q-01 — the owner decides. |
| D-02 | Imported ad-platform numbers keep their modelled/measured provenance | 21.04.03 D-04. A model's output re-rendered as measurement is a lie we did not even author. |
