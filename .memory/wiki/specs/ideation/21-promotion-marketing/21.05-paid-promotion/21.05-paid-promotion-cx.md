## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Guardrail ↔ Ads / Seeding

**Relationship**: The guardrail is a gate above both spending paths, classifying what is being
bought. The distinction is not intuitive and the penalty for getting it wrong lands on the artist,
not the seller — which is why it must be structural rather than educational.

| What is bought | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Attention (an ad impression) | Allowed | Nobody is being paid to endorse; it is a paid ad and it looks like one |
| Attention + endorsement (a paid creator post) | Allowed **with disclosure** | Material connection exists; the audience must be told |
| Placement (a playlist slot, airplay) | **Blocked** | Violates DSP terms; radio adds statutory exposure. The sanction hits the artist's track |

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the party who gets sanctioned, and the party least able to tell the three apart.
- **Producer / Operator**: bound by disclosure if they are ever compensated to promote, which they can be.
- **Fan**: the party disclosure exists to protect — they are entitled to know a post is paid.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The guardrail owns the policy; the features own their spend records. The guardrail writes a verdict, never a budget.
2. **Trigger chain**: Spend action → classify → allow / require disclosure / block. Synchronous and blocking. A guardrail evaluated after the money moves is a report, not a guardrail.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — and it is the reason for the read-only Role Matrix row. No user tier, no payment, no seniority can lift a payola block. A guardrail with an override is an override with a guardrail-shaped label.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A block must explain itself concretely ("this is pay-for-placement; DSPs remove tracks for this") — a bare denial reads as arbitrary and sends the artist to buy it elsewhere, which is the exact harm.
5. **State transition conflict**: A campaign compliant when created can become non-compliant if terms change. Whether we re-evaluate running campaigns against changed rules is unresolved and matters — see 21.05.03.

### CX-02: Seeding → Ads (the creative loop)

**Relationship**: Creator seeding produces native content; ads amplify it. The seeded post is
usually better creative than anything the artist would make, which is why the industry converged
on this pattern. The two features are one workflow in practice and modelling them as unrelated
spends would miss the loop entirely.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: runs both; experiences them as one motion.
- **Fan**: sees an amplified creator post — where the disclosure obligation compounds, because it is now paid content *and* paid distribution.
- **Producer / Operator**: not involved.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The creator's content is the creator's. Amplification rights are a term of the seeding deal, not a platform assumption — and assuming them is how creators get burned.
2. **Trigger chain**: Seed → creator posts → artist amplifies. Amplification without the creator's permission is a rights problem in 09 and a relationship problem everywhere.
3. **Permission intersection**: Amplifying content requires both the creator's grant and — separately — music rights clearance, which is where 21.05.02 DT-02's Content ID self-own lives.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The creator should know their post is being amplified. It changes what they made into an ad.
5. **State transition conflict**: A creator deleting their post while it is being amplified as an ad. The ad may outlive the organic post, which is a strange and real state.
