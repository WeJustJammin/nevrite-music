## Overview

Spending money to reach people: ads on the platforms where attention is, creators paid to make
content with the music, and the line that must not be crossed — paying for *placement* rather than
*attention*. The domain index was explicit: "paid guaranteed placement violates DSP terms and gets
tracks pulled, so this must be pay-for-consideration, never pay-for-placement." This sub-domain
owns that line for the whole platform.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: ad campaigns and creator seeding compete for one
budget and feed each other (a seeded creator's post is the best ad creative the artist will get),
and the payola guardrail gates what may be bought on either. Three capabilities in genuine tension.

**The merge**: sweep candidates 08 (Paid Ads & Creator Seeding) and 12 (Paid Placement & Payola
Disclosure) are folded here. Candidate 12 was the guardrail for candidate 08 and for playlist
pitching (21.02.02) — a policy needs one owner and many enforcement points, not a node of its own
floating beside the thing it governs.

> **Two notes on this matrix, both real findings.**
>
> **1. The Fan is `❌ None` on ad campaigns but is the *target*.** Building ad audiences from
> WeJammin's fan data — custom audiences, lookalikes — makes the Fan a data subject with objection
> rights under D-13's statutory duties, not a neutral resource. "No access" understates their
> stake; they have no *tool* access and a real interest. See 21.05.01 DT-03.
>
> **2. 21.05.03 is `👁️ Read-only` for everyone** — including the Musician. A guardrail its
> subject can configure is not a guardrail. It is universally visible because the policy binds
> everyone and because a rule nobody can read cannot be complied with. The Operator and Fan rows
> are not padding: an Operator boosting an event post is bound by disclosure law, and a Fan
> deserves to know that a post they are seeing was paid for.
