# Feature: Payola Guardrail & Paid-Consideration Policy

> **Number**: 21.05.03
> **Parent**: [Paid Promotion](./21.05-paid-promotion-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The line between buying attention and buying placement, enforced structurally rather than
explained. The domain index made this a condition of the domain's existence: "paid guaranteed
placement violates DSP terms and gets tracks pulled, so this must be pay-for-consideration, never
pay-for-placement." The penalty for crossing it lands on the artist's track, not on the seller and
not on us — which is why this cannot be left to the artist's judgement. The guardrail is a
**synchronous, fail-closed classifier** that sits in front of every spend and every promotion
listing, rules on what is being bought, and — when it blocks — explains the block in concrete
terms, because the explanation is the only part that actually protects the artist.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | **Cannot configure it — that is the definition of a guardrail (D-02).** When a spend is classified, they see one of four verdicts with a plain-language reason: *allowed* (no friction), *allowed with disclosure* (a non-removable label is attached), *blocked* (a concrete explanation naming the exact rule, the DSP that enforces it, and the consequence to *their own track*), or *held* (a consideration-shaped offer the platform has not resolved — Q-01). They can read the full policy verbatim, and can request a re-classification with corrected metadata, but cannot lift, appeal, or override a block at any tier. |
| Producer | Read-only | Bound whenever they are compensated to promote something — a plugin demo, a gear endorsement — and the classifier fires on *their* action regardless of which persona lens they entered through (multi-hyphenate rule, see Edge Cases). The policy is fully visible to them because a rule nobody can read cannot be complied with. They do not see other users' verdicts. |
| Operator | Read-only | Bound too. A venue boosting an event post, or paid to feature a promoter's show, is inside disclosure law; the classifier attaches the same non-removable disclosure obligation to Operator-originated paid endorsements. Their exposure is real and, without this feature, invisible to them. They see the policy and their own verdicts only. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The party the disclosure half exists to protect (D-11).** They configure nothing and see no verdict UI — what reaches them is the *outcome*: every paid endorsement they view carries a visible, non-removable "Paid partnership"/"Sponsored" label rendered at the point of consumption. They can read the public policy statement (a guardrail whose beneficiary cannot read it is one we are merely asking to be trusted on). They can report a suspected undisclosed paid post through domain 24. None of the four personas — Musician included — can configure the classifier; the absence of a "Full/Config" row anywhere in this table is the feature. |

## Behavior

The guardrail runs **before money moves** and **before a promotion listing publishes**. It takes the
structured intent of a spend or listing (what is being paid for, to whom, in exchange for what
promised outcome) and returns exactly one verdict. It never runs asynchronously after the fact, and
it never fails open: if it cannot classify, it blocks.

### The classification table

| What is bought | Verdict | Code | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention (ad impression, boosted reach) | **Allowed** | `PAYOLA_ALLOWED` | Nobody is paid to endorse; it looks like an ad because it is one |
| Attention + endorsement (paid creator post, sponsored review, paid testimonial) | **Allowed with mandatory disclosure** | `PAYOLA_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` | A material connection exists; the audience must be told. Obligation attached, non-removable |
| Placement on a DSP playlist / guaranteed adds | **Blocked** | `PAYOLA_PLACEMENT_BLOCKED` | Violates DSP terms; playlists selling placement get purged and the *artist's* track can be caught in it |
| Airplay / broadcast for payment | **Blocked — hardest line** | `PAYOLA_AIRPLAY_BLOCKED` | US law (47 U.S.C. §317 sponsorship-identification) requires disclosure of consideration for broadcast; undisclosed pay-for-airplay carries statutory exposure, not merely contractual |
| Consideration (paying a curator to *listen*, no placement promised) | **Held — default deny** | `PAYOLA_CONSIDERATION_HELD` | The model comparable platforms have used to survive; also the model that has drawn DSP sanction. Until Q-01 is resolved by the owner, the classifier holds it as blocked-with-explanation, never silently allows it |
| Anything the classifier cannot resolve (missing/partial intent fields, obfuscated listing text) | **Blocked — fail closed** | `PAYOLA_UNCLASSIFIABLE` | A guardrail that passes what it cannot understand is not a guardrail |

**No override at any tier (D-02).** No payment, no seniority, no support ticket, no admin flag lifts a
payola block. An override capability would be an override with a guardrail-shaped label, so the
capability does not exist. An attempt to mutate a verdict is rejected as `PAYOLA_IMMUTABLE`.

**A block must explain itself concretely (D-03).** A bare denial reads as arbitrary and sends the
artist to buy the same thing off-platform in thirty seconds — which is the exact harm, achieved by
our own opacity. Every block response carries: (a) what the classifier decided this is, (b) which
rule/regulator/DSP enforces it, (c) the specific consequence to the artist's *own* track (removal,
claim, statutory exposure). The explanation is the intervention.

**One policy, many enforcement points, stricter verdict wins (D-05).** The same offer can be
submitted as a spend in domain 21 or as a service listing in domain 05. Both call the same
classifier. If two enforcement points ever produce different verdicts for one underlying offer
(e.g. because 05's listing text was richer than 21's spend metadata), the **stricter** verdict is
authoritative — the offer resolves to the most-restrictive classification any surface produced.

**The disclosure obligation is a property of the promoted content, not a checkbox.** When
`PAYOLA_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` fires, a disclosure label is bound to the promoted item for the life
of that content, rendered to the Fan audience at the point of consumption, and cannot be toggled
off by any role. A tamper attempt (mutation, listing edit that strips the label) is rejected as
`DISCLOSURE_IMMUTABLE` and leaves the label intact.

### Happy Path

1. Musician (or Producer/Operator) initiates a spend or creates a promotion listing; the client
   submits the structured intent (target, payee, promised outcome).
2. The classifier runs **synchronously**, before any funds are authorized and before the listing
   publishes, and returns exactly one verdict with a `code`.
3. **Attention** → `PAYOLA_ALLOWED`; the action proceeds with no friction.
4. **Endorsement** → `PAYOLA_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`; a non-removable disclosure label is attached to
   the promoted content and the action proceeds.
5. **Placement / airplay / consideration / unclassifiable** → the corresponding block code; no
   funds are authorized, the listing does not publish, and the user is shown the concrete
   three-part explanation (what this is, who enforces it, what it costs *their* track).
6. Allowed actions carry their obligations forward; blocked actions offer exactly one legitimate
   next step — read the policy, or resubmit with corrected metadata — never an appeal or override.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Multi-hyphenate acting in two roles** — one human is both a Musician (buying promotion for their own track) and an Operator (paid to feature a promoter's show) in the same session | The classifier binds to the **action**, not the account's persona. Each spend/listing is classified on its own intent; the user's other role is irrelevant to the verdict. There is no "Operators are exempt" path because there are no per-role exemptions | Two independent verdicts, each explained on its own terms. The disclosure obligation on the Operator-side endorsement is not waived by the Musician-side allowed ad |
| **Concurrency** — two spends for the same track submitted within the same instant | Classification is per-action and idempotent on the action's intent hash; each is classified independently and neither observes the other's in-flight state. No shared mutable verdict exists to race on | Both get their own verdict; identical intents get identical verdicts |
| **Race: terms version changes between classify and pay** | The verdict is bound to the DSP-terms version it was computed against. If that version changes before funds are authorized, the pending spend is invalidated and must be re-classified | `PAYOLA_TERMS_STALE` — "the platform's compliance rules updated between when this was checked and now; re-checking." The re-check runs automatically; the user re-confirms |
| **Empty / partial intent** — a promotion listing in 05 is half-filled and the fields that decide placement-vs-attention are absent | The classifier cannot determine what is being bought, so it fails closed | `PAYOLA_UNCLASSIFIABLE` with the specific missing fields named ("we can't tell whether this promises placement; specify what the buyer receives"). Not a silent pass |
| **Permission** — any role attempts to configure, appeal, or override the classifier | No such capability exists for any persona (all rows are Read-only). A direct attempt is rejected | `PAYOLA_IMMUTABLE` — "the payola policy cannot be configured or overridden by any account" |
| **Evaluation dependency unavailable** — the terms/rules store the classifier reads is unreachable | The classifier fails closed; it never authorizes a spend it could not evaluate | `PAYOLA_EVAL_UNAVAILABLE` — "we couldn't verify this against current compliance rules right now; nothing was charged. Try again shortly." The spend is held, never let through |
| **Malicious input** — a listing crafted to read as "attention" in its structured fields while the free-text body promises "guaranteed #1 playlist placement" | The classifier scores structured intent *and* scans free text for placement-promise signals; a conflict between the two escalates to the stricter verdict and holds | The offer resolves to `PAYOLA_PLACEMENT_BLOCKED` (or `_HELD`), with the explanation citing the conflicting free-text promise. Structured-field gaming does not defeat it |
| **"Consideration" that is placement with extra steps** — pay for a listen, placement mysteriously always follows | The hardest, adversarial-by-construction case. Held as `PAYOLA_CONSIDERATION_HELD` under the default-deny stance until Q-01 sets an owner policy; the platform does not broker it in the interim | "Paying for consideration is not currently offered on WeJammin. Here's why the line matters and where DSPs have drawn it." Never a silent allow |
| **The same offer appears as a service listing in domain 05** | The classifier binds to 05's listing-creation flow, not only to 21's spend flow (D-05). 05's listing calls the same policy; nothing in 05 needs to know DSP terms because it defers to the one classifier | A `PAYOLA_PLACEMENT_BLOCKED` listing cannot publish in 05 either — the front door and the back door (D-03) enforce identically. See 21.02.02 DT-03 |
| **Terms change; a running campaign becomes non-compliant** | New spends against the now-non-compliant intent are blocked immediately on the next classification cycle (a terms-version change invalidates cached verdicts). What happens to *already-committed* spend — halt-and-refund vs grandfather-to-completion — is a product decision, not a mechanism gap; the mechanism (re-classify on terms change, flag the affected campaign, notify the owner of the spend) is defined and unconditional | The campaign owner is notified that the rules changed and their running campaign is now flagged; whether it is halted mid-flight follows the owner-set policy in Q-02. No campaign silently continues while newly non-compliant |
| **An artist is sanctioned by a DSP for something bought off-platform** | We cannot detect off-platform buys (we don't see them), so there is no proactive interception. If the sanction surfaces to us — reported by the user or via domain 24 — we open a support path and re-serve the concrete explanation of what pay-for-placement was and why the track was pulled. Whether we proactively warn users we *know* previously received a blocked-offer explanation is a product/legal decision (Q-05) | If they report it: a support acknowledgement, the educational explanation of the sanction, and a link to disputes (24). Not silence, and not a claim of fault we cannot substantiate |
| **Our own platform features drift toward selling placement** | The endogenous risk (DT-03): a "featured artist" slot or promoted-listing revenue is placement-for-money on *our* surface, and nothing external would block it. Not enforced by this classifier today — it fires on user spends, not on WeJammin's own product surfaces — and recorded as Q-03 so the contradiction is on the record before someone reasonable proposes the feature | Not user-visible; this is an internal governance flag, deliberately preserved so the contradiction must be argued past rather than around |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Spend/listing submitted, classifier running synchronously | A brief "checking compliance" indicator; no funds authorized yet |
| Empty | No classifiable target yet (form incomplete) | The classifier is not invoked; the user completes the required intent fields first |
| Populated — Allowed | Attention verdict | No friction; the clean case (`PAYOLA_ALLOWED`) |
| Populated — Allowed with obligation | Endorsement verdict | Disclosure label attached and non-removable (`PAYOLA_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`) |
| Populated — Blocked | Placement / airplay / consideration / unclassifiable verdict | Concrete three-part explanation; no funds moved; one legitimate next step (read policy or correct metadata) |
| Error | Evaluation dependency unreachable | `PAYOLA_EVAL_UNAVAILABLE`; spend held, nothing charged, retry offered — fail closed |
| Partial | Terms version changed mid-flight, or partial intent data | `PAYOLA_TERMS_STALE` (auto re-check) or `PAYOLA_UNCLASSIFIABLE` (named missing fields); never a silent pass |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The guardrail's **explanation is the product**, not the block — because the artist can buy the forbidden thing elsewhere in thirty seconds and will, unless they understand what it is | Reasoning about the actual threat model. We are not a gate on the world; we are one of many places to buy playlist promotion. A bare "not permitted" teaches the artist that WeJammin is restrictive, not that pay-for-placement is dangerous — so they buy it on the open web and get their track pulled anyway, having learned only to resent us. `meta/personas.md` names the Musician's worst *accidental* behaviour as signing something they don't understand | ✅ CONFIRMED — the block prevents nothing on its own; the explanation prevents the harm. This inverts the feature: it is educational infrastructure with an enforcement mechanism, not the reverse. And it is why 21.05.03 is proposed `must` despite sitting inside a `could` sub-domain — the explanation has value even if we never build a single paid-promotion feature. |
| DT-02 | The disclosure mechanism belongs to domain 21 | Initial framing — it arrived bundled with sweep candidate 12, next to paid promotion | ❌ REJECTED for the *generic* half. Material-connection disclosure applies wherever compensated endorsement happens on the platform: a paid gear review (13), a sponsored plugin demo (14), a paid service testimonial (05), a paid fan post (20). Building it inside 21 means four inconsistent reimplementations, and the inconsistency is where the violations live. **Routed as the `Sponsorship & Paid-Endorsement Disclosure` cross-cut.** What stays here is the music-specific payola policy — DSP terms, radio statute — which is real domain knowledge and belongs nowhere else. Note the shape: this is the second time in this domain that a mechanism arrived dressed as a feature (cf. 21.02.05 DT-02, the embargo). |
| DT-03 | The most dangerous payola risk is our users buying it elsewhere | The obvious threat model, and the one the domain index framed | ❌ REJECTED as *the most dangerous* — it is the most likely, but not the worst. The worst is **endogenous**: WeJammin itself selling placement on its own surfaces. A "featured artist" slot, a promoted listing, a paid boost in discovery — every marketplace builds these, they are the obvious revenue, and they are pay-for-placement with our own name on it. We would not be violating a DSP's terms, so nothing would block it; we would simply be doing on our surface the exact thing this feature exists to say is wrong. And the argument for it will be made by someone reasonable, in a growth meeting, in eighteen months. Recorded as Q-03 so that when it is proposed, the contradiction is already on the record and has to be argued past rather than around. |
| DT-04 | The classifier should fail **open** on ambiguity or dependency outage — so it never blocks a legitimate spend by accident | The availability-first instinct: a compliance check that goes down shouldn't halt the business | ❌ REJECTED, and it is the defining design choice of the feature. A guardrail that lets through what it cannot evaluate is decoration — the one spend it waves through on an outage is the one that gets the artist's track pulled, and the penalty is irreversible and lands on the user, not on us. The correct failure mode is **fail closed**: `PAYOLA_UNCLASSIFIABLE`, `PAYOLA_EVAL_UNAVAILABLE`, and the default-deny stance on `PAYOLA_CONSIDERATION_HELD` are all the same decision applied three ways. The cost — occasionally holding a legitimate spend during an outage, nothing charged — is trivially reversible; the cost of failing open is not. This is what makes it a guardrail rather than an advisory. |
| DT-05 | Per-role exemptions are needed — an Operator boosting their own event post is different from an artist buying playlist placement | The intuition that context should soften the rule for some personas | ❌ REJECTED. The classifier binds to the **action's intent**, not the actor's persona, because payola law binds the transaction, not the profession — an Operator paid to feature a promoter's show is inside the same disclosure statute as a creator paid to post a track. Per-role exemptions would immediately be gamed by the multi-hyphenate: a user who is both Musician and Operator would route placement buys through whichever lens carried the exemption. A single actor-agnostic policy is the only one that survives the structural fact that one human holds several roles at once (`meta/personas.md`). This is why every row in the Role Lens is Read-only and none is exempt. |
