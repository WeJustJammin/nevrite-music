# Feature: Creator & Influencer Seeding

> **Number**: 21.05.02
> **Parent**: [Paid Promotion](./21.05-paid-promotion-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Paying creators to make content using the track — the dominant destination of music marketing
budget in the short-video era, and a swamp. Every deal is a compensated endorsement requiring
disclosure; the fraud rate is high; the metrics are gameable; and the operational burden of
briefing, paying and verifying dozens of individuals is real work that does not look like software.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds creators; briefs them; agrees terms; pays; verifies the post went up, stayed up, and carried its disclosure; amplifies the good ones as ad creative (CX-02). Sees which creators actually delivered — the fact the whole category obscures. |
| Producer | None | Not a marketing function. Note: a Producer *could* be a paid endorser in a different context (a plugin demo in 14), which is exactly why the disclosure mechanism belongs to a cross-cut rather than to this feature (D-02, sub-domain index). |
| Operator | None | Not their function here. |
| Fan | None | No tool access — **and disclosure exists entirely for them**. A fan watching a paid post is entitled to know it was paid for. Their `None` here is an access fact; the whole regulatory apparatus around this feature exists to protect the party with no seat at the table. |

## Behavior

A seeding deal is: find a creator, brief them, agree terms and payment, they post, you verify, you
pay, you maybe amplify.

**Every part of this is a marketplace problem wearing a marketing costume** — discovery, briefing,
escrow, delivery verification, dispute. Which means it collides with domain 05 (Services
Marketplace): a creator selling "I'll post your track to my 200k followers" is a *service listing*,
and the platform already has a services marketplace with escrow, reviews and disputes (D-14).
Building a second, parallel, worse marketplace inside a marketing domain is the failure mode here.

**Disclosure is mandatory and it is the creator's legal obligation as much as the artist's**;
material connections must be disclosed to the audience. The platform's role is making the
disclosure the path of least resistance, and verifying it happened — which nobody does, which is
why disclosure compliance in music seeding is poor.

**The fraud floor**: bought followers, engagement pods, posts deleted the day after payment,
metrics that mean nothing. Verification is the actual product, and it is unglamorous.

### Happy Path

1. Musician allocates seeding budget against the grid.
2. Finds creators — plausibly *through domain 05*, not a bespoke directory here (DT-03).
3. Briefs: the track, the moment, the ask, the disclosure requirement — non-negotiable and pre-filled.
4. Terms and payment agreed; escrow via 05's existing machinery.
5. Creator posts with disclosure.
6. Platform verifies: posted, disclosed, still up after N days.
7. Payment releases; good posts get amplified as ad creative (CX-02), with the creator's permission.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Creator posts without disclosure | Legal exposure for both parties; the artist is often the deeper pocket and the more findable one | Verification must catch it before payment releases. Post-hoc disclosure is worth little |
| Creator deletes the post after payment | The classic seeding fraud | Verification must persist past payment — "still up after N days" as an escrow condition, not a checkbox at payout |
| Creator's followers are bought | The artist pays for an audience that does not exist | The category's floor. Detection is hard, weak, and adversarial; honest uncertainty beats a fake authenticity score |
| Creator's post is muted/claimed for using the track | Automated rights systems claim the audio — including the artist's own | Ties to 09/11. The self-own: your seeding campaign is blocked by your own copyright registration |
| Artist amplifies the creator's post without asking | It converts what they made into an ad | Amplification rights must be an explicit term, never assumed (CX-02) |
| Creator is a minor | Payment, contract capacity, and endorsement law all land at once | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` → `/create-prd-security` |
| Deal happens on-platform, payment goes off-platform | `meta/personas.md` names this as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour ("takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees") — here it also evades disclosure verification | The disclosure guarantee and the fee are structurally coupled: escape one and you escape both |
| Payment disputes | 05 already has escrow and dispute machinery (D-14) | Reuse it. Do not build a second one (DT-03) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The product here is **verification**, not discovery — because the fraud floor is the category's defining fact and the artist cannot verify anything themselves | Reasoning about where the artist actually loses money. Finding creators is easy and getting easier. Knowing whether the post went up, carried its disclosure, stayed up, and reached humans rather than a pod is the part they cannot do, and it is the part every seeding marketplace is structurally disincentivised to do well — their revenue is the transaction, not its integrity | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it echoes 21.02.02 DT-01 exactly: in both features the platform's value is correcting an information asymmetry that the incumbents profit from preserving. That is now a pattern across the domain rather than a one-off, and it is worth naming: **wherever this domain intermediates a paid relationship, the honest half is the product and the transaction is the commodity.** |
| DT-02 | Seeding is a rights problem as much as a marketing one — the artist's own copyright registration will block their own seeding campaign | Reasoning about what breaks at the 21↔09/11 intersection. Automated rights systems claim audio in UGC. If the artist has registered their work (which 09/12 will encourage), the creator's post gets claimed or muted — by the artist's own registration | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a genuine cross-domain failure that only shows up because the platform holds both sides. A standalone seeding tool never sees it; a standalone rights tool never sees it. WeJammin holds the registration *and* runs the campaign, so it can pre-empt the collision — which is a small, concrete instance of the consolidation thesis producing something neither half could produce alone. |
| DT-03 | Creator seeding needs its own creator directory, briefing flow, escrow and dispute resolution inside domain 21 | The obvious build, and what the sweep candidate implied | ❌ REJECTED — every one of those is a services-marketplace primitive that D-14 already established in domain 05, complete with escrow, reviews and disputes. A creator selling a post *is* a service provider selling scoped human output — 05's exact definition. Building a parallel marketplace inside a marketing domain would produce a second, worse, unmaintained copy, and would split the fraud signal across two systems so that neither sees the whole picture of a bad actor. What belongs to 21 is the *campaign* half: budget against grid beats, the disclosure requirement, verification, amplification. The transaction belongs to 05. This materially shrinks the feature, which is the correct outcome. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The transaction belongs to domain 05; 21 owns the campaign half | See DT-03. A second marketplace would be worse and would split the fraud signal. |
| D-02 | Disclosure is pre-filled and non-removable in the brief | It is a legal obligation for both parties. Making it the path of least resistance is the only intervention that reliably works. |
| D-03 | Verification persists past payment ("still up after N days") | Post-deletion is the category's classic fraud; a payout-time checkbox catches none of it. |
| D-04 | Amplification rights are an explicit term, never assumed | Turning a creator's post into an ad without asking is how creators stop working with you. |
