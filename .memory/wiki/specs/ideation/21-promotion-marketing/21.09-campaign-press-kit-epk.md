# Feature: Campaign Press Kit (EPK) & Asset Pack

## Overview

The thing every pitch attaches and every gatekeeper asks for: bio, photos, the music, verified
credits, press quotes, contact. "Send me the EPK" is the standard first reply to any press pitch,
and building one is the tedious, recurring, always-out-of-date task that every artist does badly.
**This appeared on no candidate list** — a Deep Think addition — which is remarkable given the
domain's own rationale ends with "coverage capture → EPK".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Builds a campaign EPK — mostly by *not* building it, since the credits (02), the release (12), the coverage quotes (21.07) and the assets (21.01.02) are already in the platform. Chooses what to include, shares a link, sees who opened it. Keeps versions, because the EPK for the album is not the EPK for the tour. |
| Producer | Read-only | **Verifies their own credit on it.** An EPK is where a mis-credit gets propagated to press at scale — the journalist copies it, and the wrong producer name enters the public record permanently. They can see EPKs naming them and dispute through 02. This is the second of only two genuine Producer stakes in domain 21 (with 21.07). |
| Operator | Read-only | **Venues request EPKs when booking.** This is real Operator behaviour that surprised me: the press kit is not only a press artefact — it is a booking artefact, and the Operator is a consumer of it via a shared link. See DT-03. |
| Fan | None | Not fan-facing. The public artist page (01/20) is the fan's surface; an EPK is a professional document with contact details and unreleased assets in it, and confusing the two is how unreleased music leaks. |

## Behavior

An EPK is a **campaign artefact**, not a profile — versioned, scoped to a release or a tour,
shared by link, with a lifespan. It sits on the same side of the domain index's boundary as the
smart link: campaign instrument, not permanent home. The permanent artist page belongs to 01/20.

**It writes itself, and that is the point.** Every component already exists in the platform: the
bio and photos (01), the release and the music (12), the credits (02 — *verified*, not typed), the
press quotes (21.07 — verified, with live sources), the assets (21.01.02). The artist's job
collapses from assembly to selection.

This is the domain's densest expression of the consolidation thesis. Not because an EPK builder is
clever — several exist — but because ours is populated by facts the platform captured at source
rather than fields the artist retyped from memory into a template. The credits are right because
the Producer confirmed them in the session. The quotes are checkable because the platform holds a
retrievable link to a fixed artefact and performs the verification act on it (21.07 D-04, D-06) —
*not* because we sent the pitch, which never affects a quote's strength.

**One badge per quote, and it says nothing about sourcing.** The EPK renders the strength 21.07
computed — `verified` / `verified-at-timestamp` / `claimed` — and renders no provenance: whether a
WeJammin pitch produced the coverage stays a log state in 21.07 and never reaches this surface
(D-05). The accepted consequence is stated plainly because it is the cost of the choice: a
journalist reading the EPK cannot tell a platform-observed capture from an artist-self-logged one.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates an EPK for the campaign.
2. It populates: bio and photos from 01, release and audio from 12, credits from 02 marked verified, press quotes from 21.07 marked verified, assets from 21.01.02.
3. Musician selects and orders — the work is curation, not data entry.
4. Shares a link with a press pitch (21.02.03) or a booking enquiry (17).
5. Recipient opens it; sees the music, the story, and credits/quotes that are checkable rather than asserted.
6. Musician sees it was opened — a weak but real signal that the pitch landed somewhere.
7. Next campaign: a new version. The old one still resolves, because it is in a journalist's inbox forever.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The EPK is a campaign artefact and stays in 21 | See DT-02. Versioned, scoped, link-shared, has a lifespan, contains unreleased material, not fan-facing. |
| D-02 | It auto-populates from platform data; the artist's work is curation | See DT-01. The dividend of having been in the room. |
| D-03 | Unverified credits are blocked or marked claimed, never rendered as verified | Manufacturing verification inside the verification feature would be self-defeating. |
| D-04 | Old versions resolve rather than 404 | They live in journalists' inboxes forever. Same logic as 21.04.04 D-01. |
| D-05 | **The EPK renders one strength badge per item and nothing about sourcing** — pitch provenance (`Attributed` / `Organic`) stays a 21.07 log state and does not reach this surface | Owner ratification of DQ-20.3. The badge surface is **unchanged** by this decision, which is the point: a compound provenance+strength label is the only option in the pass requiring spec no source has written (no provenance vocabulary, no compound-label layout), and it would land a new rendering surface on a file that is still `[PARTIAL]`, with `[PENDING]` pin/retention mechanics (Edge Cases) and an open leak-control question (Q-02). Nothing is lost by deferring, because the fact that would render is already captured as a log state in 21.07. A single label also matches 21.07's reason for badges at all — "nobody checks, because checking is tedious" — and D-03's block-or-mark-claimed rule for credits is the same one-label shape. **Accepted**: the journalist cannot distinguish a platform-observed capture from an artist-self-logged one. Rendering provenance later is **additive** to this choice; unwinding a compound badge would not be. See 21.07 D-06/D-07 and Q-04. *(User ratification, DQ-20.3.)* |
