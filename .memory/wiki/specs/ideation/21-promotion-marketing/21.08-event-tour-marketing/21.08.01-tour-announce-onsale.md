# Feature: Tour Announce & On-Sale Campaign

> **Number**: 21.08.01
> **Parent**: [Event & Tour Marketing](./21.08-event-tour-marketing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Revealing a run of dates at once, then opening sales — coordinated across every city, every venue,
every ticketing link, at a single moment. The announce is the tour's highest-leverage marketing
event: reach peaks once and never returns. It is also uniquely fragile, because it depends on a
dozen venues each staying quiet until the same minute.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Plans the announce and on-sale; sees every date's readiness (does it have a confirmed venue, a ticket link, a capacity, an on-sale time?); fires the coordinated reveal; sees first-week sales per market — which is when a weak city is still fixable. |
| Producer | None | No involvement in live marketing. |
| Operator | Config | **Holds the facts the announce depends on** — on-sale time, ticket link, capacity, and whether the date is actually confirmed. They configure their own date's announce readiness and are bound by the announce embargo. They are also the most common leak vector: a venue publishing the show on its own calendar before the announce is a routine, unforced disaster. |
| Fan | None | The audience. They receive the announce (via 20) and act on it — and per D-11 they are a user with alert preferences, not a broadcast target. Their reaction *is* the baseline the rest of this sub-domain measures against. |

## Behavior

An announce is a **multi-party timed reveal**, which is what makes it different from a release
announce. A release depends on one distributor. A tour depends on twelve venues, each with its own
calendar, its own website, and its own staff who were told about the show weeks ago.

**Readiness is per-date and gating.** A date with no ticket link cannot be announced — announcing
a show fans cannot buy is worse than not announcing it, because it spends the reach and returns
nothing. This is the asset-readiness pattern (21.01.02) with different deliverables and a
different owner: the Operator holds most of them.

**Tagged links must be created at announce time or attribution never exists** (CX-03). This is an
unrecoverable data loss of the same class as the DSP pitch window, and the answer is the same: the
system creates the links rather than hoping the artist remembers.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates a tour campaign against dates from 17.
2. Each date's readiness populates from 17/19 — venue confirmed, ticket link live, capacity, on-sale time. Operators fill their own gaps.
3. Announce embargo is set; every venue is bound and told.
4. Per-date, per-channel tagged links are created automatically (CX-03).
5. Announce fires — socials, fan email (20), press (21.02.03), venue channels.
6. On-sale opens at the agreed moment.
7. First-week sales per market establish the baseline (CX-02) and the health loop (21.08.02) begins.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A venue publishes the show before the announce | The reveal is spoiled for everyone; reach is spent at random | The tour-specific version of 21.02.05 DT-01 — the breach comes from your own side, from a party nobody told. Venues must be *bound* by the announce embargo, which today they simply are not |
| A date has no ticket link at announce | Announcing an unbuyable show spends the peak and returns nothing | Gated. Announce blocked for that date, or the date is held back |
| One date is not confirmed yet | Announcing an unconfirmed date is how "date TBC" and then "date cancelled" happens | Held back rather than announced provisionally |
| Dates added after announce | No baseline, no comparable history, and the reach is gone | Cannot be assessed by the same rule (CX-02); needs its own tagged links or it becomes the one date with no data |
| On-sale times differ per venue | Normal — venues have their own systems and their own hours | The announce is one moment; on-sales may not be. The model must not assume a single on-sale |
| Ticketing is off-platform (venue uses its own ticketer) | Extremely common, and it breaks the closed loop entirely | Attribution degrades to click-only, i.e. back to 21.04.03's honest-but-limited position. See 21.08.03 |
| Announce fires while a date is being renegotiated | The reveal is a commitment; renegotiating an announced date is a public failure | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — ties to 17's booking states |
| Fan alert fatigue — twelve dates, twelve notifications | The fan follows the artist, not the tour | Fan-out belongs to 20 and must be one announce, not twelve. Getting this wrong costs the artist a follower per city |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The announce is a **multi-party timed reveal**, and the failure mode is a venue leaking it — a party the release-cycle embargo model never contemplated | Applying 21.02.05 DT-01 ("the breach vector is your own side") to live music. A release announce has one upstream party; a tour has twelve venues, each with a website, a Facebook page, and a box office staffer who was told in a booking email six weeks ago | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it generalises the embargo cross-cut in a way the release-cycle features could not have surfaced. The `Timed Reveal / Embargo Lock` mechanism must bind **counterparty organisations**, not just the artist's own collaborators. That is a materially larger claim than "tell the Producer not to post": it means the embargo has to reach an Operator's staff at a business we do not control, through a relationship (the booking) that lives in a different domain. Strong evidence the mechanism belongs at cross-cut level (21.02.05 DT-02) rather than inside press outreach. |
| DT-02 | Announce readiness is the asset-readiness gate (21.01.02) again, but **the deliverables belong to someone else** | Reasoning about what actually blocks a tour announce: ticket link, capacity, confirmed status, on-sale time. In a release campaign the artist or their Producer owns every gate row. Here the Operator owns most of them, and they have no incentive to hurry | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it inverts the gate's politics. 21.01.02's gate is the artist chasing themselves and their Producer; this gate is the artist chasing twelve businesses. That makes visibility the whole product: an artist who can see which venue has not supplied a ticket link has something they have never had. It also means the gate cannot *enforce* — it can only reveal. Enforcement over a counterparty is a booking-contract matter in 17, not a marketing feature. |
| DT-03 | A tour announce is just the release campaign grid with different deadlines, so 21.01 should own it | The polymorphism hypothesis (D-04, sub-domain index), taken to its conclusion | ❌ REJECTED as *complete* — partially true and misleading. The backward-planned shape genuinely is the same (anchor = on-sale, offsets = announce, press, ads), and if the campaign object is polymorphic (Q-01) the grid should generalise. But three things here have no release-cycle equivalent: the **multi-party** reveal (DT-01), the **counterparty-owned** readiness gate (DT-02), and the **closed measurement loop** (CX-01) that exists only because we own the box office. Folding this into 21.01 would import a single-party model into a twelve-party problem. The honest answer is that the *grid* generalises and this sub-domain keeps the loop, the Operator, and attribution — which is what Q-01 should resolve. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Venues are bound by the announce embargo | See DT-01. They are the most common leak vector and today nobody tells them an embargo exists. |
| D-02 | Per-date readiness gates the announce; it reveals rather than enforces | See DT-02. Enforcement over a counterparty is a booking-contract matter in 17. |
| D-03 | Tagged per-date links are created automatically at announce | See CX-03. Unrecoverable if missed, same class as the DSP pitch window. |
| D-04 | The model does not assume a single on-sale moment | Venues have their own systems and hours; staggered on-sales are normal. |
