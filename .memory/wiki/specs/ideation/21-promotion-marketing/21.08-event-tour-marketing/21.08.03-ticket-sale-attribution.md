# Feature: Ticket-Sale Attribution

> **Number**: 21.08.03
> **Parent**: [Event & Tour Marketing](./21.08-event-tour-marketing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Which channel sold this ticket. It is the same mechanism as campaign attribution (21.04.03) and
the opposite epistemic situation: the conversion is a row in domain 19's database, not an event
inside Spotify. This is the one place in the platform where "did the marketing work?" has a true
answer — and the answer is only available because the platform owns the box office.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees tickets sold by source — fan email, the announce, a geo-targeted ad, a local press piece, the venue's own channels. Sees cost per ticket sold, which is the number that decides where the next tour's money goes and which they have never had. |
| Producer | None | No involvement. |
| Operator | Reports | **Reports on their own date.** They spend on their own shows and are entitled to know what their spend sold. Not full access — the artist's channel mix across the run is the artist's business, and a venue does not need to see how the Leeds date is doing. Per-date reporting is the honest scope. |
| Fan | None | Not a participant, and the tracked party. A ticket purchase attributed to a click is a join between a marketing event and a *transaction* — which is more personal than a click log and carries the D-13 duties that scale with fan reach. |

## Behavior

Tagged links (created at announce — CX-03) carry a source; the click is recorded; the purchase in
19 carries the source through to the transaction. The join exists because both ends are ours.

**Why this is genuinely different from 21.04.03.** There, the chain breaks at the click: the
stream happens inside a DSP that returns nothing, so every claim past the click is inference, and
the honest product refuses to draw the funnel. Here the chain completes: click → checkout →
purchase, all on our surfaces. Cost per ticket sold is a real number, not a modelled one.

**The dependency is total.** If ticketing is off-platform — the venue uses its own ticketer, which
is common — the chain breaks at exactly the same place it breaks for streams, and this feature
degrades to 21.04.03's honest-but-limited click reporting. That is not a bug; it is the same
honesty applied when the data genuinely is not there. But it means this feature's value is
proportional to 19's adoption, which is an assumption worth stating loudly (Q-01).

**What stays honest even here**: attribution is still last-touch unless we model it, and we do not
(21.04.03 D-02). A fan who saw the ad, forgot, then bought from the fan email is credited to the
email. Showing the touches without inventing a credit-allocation model is the same discipline —
the conversion being observable does not make multi-touch modelling any less of a fabrication.

### Happy Path

1. Announce creates per-date, per-channel tagged links (CX-03).
2. Fan clicks the geo-targeted ad for the Manchester date.
3. Lands on the ticket page (19); buys.
4. Purchase carries the source; the join completes.
5. Musician sees: 40 tickets from the ad, cost per ticket sold £4.20, versus 120 from the fan email at zero marginal cost.
6. Operator sees the same for their date.
7. The loop closes (CX-01) and the next spending decision is informed by a fact rather than a feeling.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Off-platform ticketing | The chain breaks at the click; the loop does not close | Degrades to click-only reporting, labelled honestly. Same discipline as 21.04.03 — say what is not known |
| Fan clicks the ad, buys a week later from a search | Last-touch credits the wrong channel; multi-touch would fabricate a different wrong answer | Show the touches; do not allocate credit (21.04.03 D-02). The conversion being observable does not license inventing a model |
| Fan buys for a friend / buys four tickets | One click, four tickets, one attribution | Fine — tickets are the unit, not people. Worth stating because it is the rare case where the coarser measure is the right one |
| Untagged announce | No attribution for the whole tour, unrecoverable | CX-03. The system creates the links; hoping the artist remembers is how the data never exists |
| Venue's own channels sold the tickets | Real and common — the room has regulars who buy anything on that stage | Must be attributable, or the venue's contribution is invisible and the artist under-credits them. This matters for the co-op conversation |
| Refunds / cancellations | Attributed sales that were not sales | Attribution must net refunds or the cost-per-ticket is flattering nonsense |
| Fan buys at the door | The most honest ticket in music has no attribution at all | Untracked. Say so rather than let the online number masquerade as the whole picture |
| Scalper bulk-buys via a tagged link | Attributed as a marketing success; it is a fraud event | `meta/personas.md` names bulk-buying-to-scalp as the Fan's worst intentional behaviour. Ties to 19/24 — and it would flatter the very metric the artist trusts most |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No data | Pre-on-sale | Empty |
| Collecting | Sales running | Tickets by source, live |
| Attributed | Purchases joined to sources | Cost per ticket sold — the real number |
| Degraded | Off-platform ticketing | Click-only, labelled; the gap stated rather than hidden |
| Partial | Some dates on-platform, some not | Mixed; the difference must be visible or the comparison lies |
| Netted | Refunds processed | Adjusted; the honest figure |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the **only true conversion attribution in the platform**, and it exists solely because we own both ends of the chain | Comparing directly against 21.04.03 DT-01. There, the conversion is inside a DSP and no join key exists for anyone. Here, click and purchase are both ours, so the join is a database operation rather than an inference | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it justifies D-03 (keeping this here rather than folding into 21.04.03). Two features, one mechanism, opposite truth status: merging them would force an honest feature and a necessarily-limited one to share a UI, and the shared UI would inevitably imply the DSP side knows what the ticket side knows. That implication is precisely the lie 21.04.03 exists to refuse. Separation is what keeps both honest. |
| DT-02 | Since the conversion is observable here, multi-touch attribution becomes legitimate — the objection in 21.04.03 DT-02 was about unobservable conversions | Tempting and wrong; observability seems to unlock the modelling | ❌ REJECTED — observing the *conversion* does not mean observing the *cause*. Multi-touch attribution is a credit-allocation model regardless of how well you can see the endpoint: it decides, by assumption, how much credit the ad gets versus the email, and no data anywhere resolves that. Better endpoint data makes the model's output more precise and no more true. The discipline from 21.04.03 D-02 survives intact: show the touches, decline to allocate. It is worth recording because this is exactly where the argument for modelling would feel strongest and be no better founded. |
| DT-03 | The number that matters is **cost per ticket sold**, and no artist currently has it | Reasoning about the decision this feature serves. Artists decide tour marketing spend on a promoter's phone call and a feeling. Cost per ticket sold is the single number that makes the decision rational, it requires joining marketing spend (21.05.01/23) to ticket revenue (19), and no tool in an artist's stack holds both | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a consolidation dividend that could not be manufactured by any point solution: a ticketing company knows sales but not spend; an ad platform knows spend but not sales; a marketing tool knows neither. It also lands squarely in domain 23's territory, which is the right home for the conclusion — this feature produces the number, 23 is where the artist learns whether the tour made money. That handoff should be explicit rather than left for someone to notice later. |
