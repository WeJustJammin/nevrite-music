## Overview

Selling tickets to shows: the coordinated announce across a run of dates, the on-sale, the
per-date health loop that spots a city underselling and spends against it, and the attribution
that — uniquely in this platform — actually works, because we own the transaction. This is also
the **only** place in domain 21 where the Operator is a full participant: venues market their own
calendars, and today they do it in a different silo from the artists playing them.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: the announce, the per-date health loop, and
attribution are distinct capabilities that feed each other. Soft-seat detection reads ticket
sales, triggers targeted spend, and measures the result — a closed loop that the release-cycle
features cannot close because DSPs report nothing back. It is a genuinely different machine, not
the release campaign with different nouns.

> **Note on the Operator — the reason this sub-domain exists separately.** Everywhere else in
> domain 21 the Operator is `❌ None`, because release-cycle marketing is the artist's job. Here
> they are a marketer in their own right: they promote their room's calendar, they spend their own
> money on their own shows, and a show underselling is *their* revenue problem as much as the
> artist's. The `⚙️ Config` on announce is deliberate — the artist owns the announce (it is their
> tour), but the venue holds the on-sale time, the ticket link and the capacity, and announcing
> before the venue is ready is a real and common failure. On soft-seat response they are `✅ Full`
> **scoped to their own events**: it is their money and their Tuesday. Per `meta/personas.md`,
> "an unsold Tuesday is gone forever" — that perishability is precisely what makes them a
> first-class marketer here and nowhere else in this domain.
