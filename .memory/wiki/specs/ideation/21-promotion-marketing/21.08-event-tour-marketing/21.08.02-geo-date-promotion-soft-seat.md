# Feature: Geo-Targeted Date Promotion & Soft-Seat Response

> **Number**: 21.08.02
> **Parent**: [Event & Tour Marketing](./21.08-event-tour-marketing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Noticing that Thursday in Manchester is selling badly while everything else is fine, and doing
something about it while there is still time. This is the tour marketer's actual job — the
date-level loop of watch, diagnose, spend, measure — and it is the one place in the entire
platform where that loop can genuinely close, because we own the box office.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees every date's sales trajectory against its baseline; sees which markets are soft early enough to act; targets spend at a city; sees whether it worked (21.08.03). Decides where the tour's finite marketing budget goes — which is a real decision with real money, made today on a promoter's phone call and a gut feeling. |
| Producer | None | No involvement. |
| Operator | **Full** — scoped to their own events | Their date, their revenue, their perishable Tuesday. Per `meta/personas.md`, "an unsold Tuesday is gone forever" — an Operator watching their own show undersell has both the strongest incentive and the best local knowledge to fix it, and currently no tooling and no visibility into what the artist is doing about it. This is the single strongest Operator stake in domain 21. |
| Fan | None | No tool access — **and they are the retargeted party**. Geo-targeting fans near a soft date means using their location and their follow relationship as ad targeting data. Per D-11 they are users; per D-13 statutory duties scale with fan reach. Their `None` is an access fact concealing a real interest. See 21.05.01 DT-03. |

## Behavior

Three steps, and only the first two are novel:

1. **Detect.** Sales per date, against that date's baseline (CX-02), trending. "Soft" is relative —
   400 sold in Manchester might be triumphant or alarming depending on capacity, market history and
   time to show.
2. **Diagnose.** Weak announce reach in that market? No local press? A competing show? A bad night?
   This is where the platform's cross-domain data could actually say something, and where it could
   also confidently say something stupid.
3. **Spend.** Geo-targeted ads, local press, fan email to that city, a support-act announcement.

**Timing is the whole feature.** Soft-seat detection at week one is actionable; at week six it is a
post-mortem. The value is entirely in the earliness, and earliness is exactly when the signal is
weakest and most likely to be noise.

**It must never auto-spend.** A detection rule firing money at a false positive is a machine
spending an artist's rent on a problem that did not exist.

### Happy Path

1. Tour announced; baselines established from first-week sales (CX-02).
2. Manchester trends below its baseline in week two.
3. Flagged to **both** the artist and the venue — which is the thing that does not happen today.
4. Diagnosis surfaces what is knowable: announce reach in that market was low; no local press coverage.
5. Musician (or Operator, for their date) chooses a response: geo-targeted spend, a local press push, a fan email to that city.
6. Spend runs; attribution (21.08.03) measures ticket sales against it — a real answer, uniquely.
7. The loop informs the next date and the next tour.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| False positive — a date is "soft" but sells out in week five anyway | Slow-burn markets are real; some cities buy late by culture | Spending against a non-problem. The cost of a false positive is real money, which is why detection advises and never acts (DT-02) |
| Detection too late to matter | Week six is a post-mortem, not a response | Earliness is the value and earliness is when the signal is worst. This tension does not resolve; it is managed |
| Ticketing is off-platform | No sales data, no detection, no loop | The loop simply does not exist for that date. This is the feature's hard dependency on 19 owning the box office, and it is a big assumption |
| Artist and venue both spend on the same date, unaware | Duplicate spend, or worse, competing ads bidding against each other for the same audience | The co-op problem (Q-04, sub-domain index). Both parties are in our system; not showing each other is a choice we would be making |
| Date sells out during the spend | Money burning on a solved problem | Spend must stop on a sell-out signal. Sales data lag makes this a real window of waste |
| Fan geo-targeting without a lawful basis | Using follower location for ad targeting | See 21.05.01 DT-03 and Q-02 on that sub-domain |
| The diagnosis is wrong | "Low announce reach" when the truth is the support act is unpopular locally, or it is a bad room | A confident wrong diagnosis directs real money badly. Diagnosis must present evidence, not conclusions |
| Support act announcement is the real fix | Frequently true and entirely outside marketing's control | Worth surfacing as an option even though 21 cannot execute it — the honest answer is sometimes "this is not a marketing problem" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No baseline | Pre-announce or date added late | Cannot assess; said plainly rather than assessed badly |
| Healthy | Tracking at or above baseline | Quiet. No alarm is a feature |
| Soft — early | Below baseline, time to act | Flagged to artist **and** venue, with evidence |
| Soft — late | Below baseline, too late for spend to work | Flagged honestly as a post-mortem, not as an opportunity to sell them ads |
| Responding | Spend running | Spend visible to both parties (Q-04 permitting) |
| Recovered | Trajectory improved | Attribution says whether the spend did it (21.08.03) |
| Sold out | Capacity reached | Spend stops immediately |
| Not assessable | Off-platform ticketing | No data, no loop — stated, not faked |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **This is the only closed marketing loop in the platform**, because ticket conversion is observable while stream conversion is not | Reasoning about the epistemics. 21.04.03 DT-01 establishes that nobody can honestly attribute a stream to an ad — the conversion happens inside a DSP that returns nothing. Here the conversion is a ticket sale in domain 19's own database. Same mechanism, entirely different truth status | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the strongest argument for this sub-domain's existence and for D-03 (keeping attribution here rather than folding it into 21.04.03). It also inverts the usual assumption about where the platform's marketing value is: everyone assumes it is in the release cycle, because that is where the glamour is. But the release cycle is where measurement is impossible. The unglamorous live side is where the platform can actually tell an artist the truth about their money — and it can do that *only* because it owns the box office, which is a consolidation dividend of exactly the kind D-18 predicts. |
| DT-02 | Detection should trigger spend automatically — the loop is closed, so let it run | The natural conclusion of DT-01, and where every performance-marketing product ends up | ❌ REJECTED — the loop being closed makes it *measurable*, not *correct*. Detection's earliest and most valuable signal is also its weakest and noisiest (slow-burn markets are a real cultural phenomenon, not a data error), and a rule firing on a false positive spends an artist's rent on a problem that did not exist. `meta/personas.md` describes a musician living on "a portfolio of small income streams" — this is not a population whose budget should be handed to a heuristic. And `decision-classification.md` puts spend allocation with the user. The system advises; the human spends. |
| DT-03 | The venue should see this too — and the fact that they currently do not is a whole class of waste | Reasoning about the Operator's stake. Both parties want the same show sold; both have budget; both have information the other lacks (the venue knows the local market and the room; the artist knows the fanbase). Today they are in separate silos and coordinate by phone, badly, or not at all | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is D-02 on the sub-domain index made concrete. Both parties are *already in our system*, so not showing them to each other is an active choice rather than a limitation. It also creates the co-op question (Q-04) with no obvious answer: if both spend on the same date, they may bid against each other for the same audience — which is worse than either spending alone, and is a failure only visible from a platform that sees both. Discovering the failure is progress; the mechanism to resolve it is not designed. |

## Decisions
