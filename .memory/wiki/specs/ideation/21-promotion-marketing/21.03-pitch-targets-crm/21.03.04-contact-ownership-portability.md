# Feature: Contact Ownership & Portability

> **Number**: 21.03.04
> **Parent**: [Pitch Targets & Relationship CRM](./21.03-pitch-targets-crm-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Who owns the contact list, and what leaves with you. The domain exists because a publicist's
contacts "walk out the door when the person leaves" — but the platform's answer to that must not
be "so we'll keep them hostage instead." This feature is where `meta/problem-statement.md`'s Q-02
— is the lock-in earned or hostile? — stops being philosophy and becomes a schema.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Exports their entire CRM — contacts, notes, pitch history, outcomes — in a form usable elsewhere, without asking, without a retention interception, without a downgrade. Sees clearly what is theirs (their record) and what is not (the directory). For a band, sees and settles who owns the list. |
| Producer | None | Not a CRM owner in this sub-domain. |
| Operator | None | Not a CRM owner in this sub-domain. |
| Fan | None | No access. Fan-list portability is a different and equally live question in domain 20 — the same values decision, a different asset. |

## Behavior

Three questions, in increasing difficulty:

1. **Can the artist take their CRM?** Yes, in full, unconditionally. Contacts, notes, history,
   outcomes. This is the easy one and it is only easy because D-02 on the sub-domain index kept
   the private record separate from the directory.
2. **Can they take the directory?** No. They did not create it; it is platform reference data
   assembled at platform cost, and it is exactly the asset `meta/problem-statement.md` calls "the
   value they cannot take with them." Refusing this is not hostile — it is the difference between
   keeping your own work and taking ours.
3. **Who owns a band's list?** The genuinely hard one, and the one that reproduces the domain's
   founding problem inside our own product.

**The line is drawn by authorship**, and D-02's entity separation is what makes that line
computable rather than rhetorical. Everything the artist wrote or generated is theirs. Everything
we assembled is ours. The uncomfortable middle — a contact the artist *contributed to the
directory* — is DT-02 below.

### Happy Path

1. Musician requests an export.
2. Export assembles their full private record — every contact, note, pitch event, outcome — as a snapshot.
3. Delivered in an open, usable format. Not a PDF. Not a screenshot. A file another tool can read.
4. No retention interception, no "are you sure?", no downgrade prompt, no notification to anyone.
5. The artist keeps their relationships. What they lose by leaving is the directory, the aggregate response data, and the accumulation — which is what earned lock-in actually means.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Empty CRM** — artist with zero contacts requests an export | Export still succeeds; a portability right that only works once you have data is not a right | A valid, empty-but-well-formed file (headers, no rows) and a note that the CRM is empty — never an error |
| **Permission** — a departed band member (or any non-owner) requests export of a CRM that is not theirs | Denied at the ownership boundary; export is scoped to the requester's own private record (D-02 entity separation makes "own" computable) | They can export only what authorship assigns to them; the contested band list is Q-01, not a silent grab |
| Artist exports a contact they originally contributed to the directory | Authorship is genuinely split — they supplied it, we verified and enriched it, others have used it | They take their version. See DT-02 |
| Band member leaves; who keeps the CRM? | The fight. Q-04 on the sub-domain index | `[PENDING]` — needs the band-entity model from 01 and Q-04 in `meta/personas.md` before this is designable |
| Artist exports and the platform offers a discount to stay | This is the hostile-lock-in tell, and it is what everyone does | Explicitly not built. See DT-03 |
| Export includes notes about identifiable third parties | It is their data about their relationships and they are entitled to it | Fine on portability grounds; the third-party-data exposure is 21.03.02 Q-01's problem, not this feature's |
| Artist imports a list from elsewhere | Portability must be bidirectional to be honest — but the import path is also how scraped lists enter (21.03.02 Q-03) | Genuine tension; the honest position is that a portability claim with no import path is half a claim |
| Export requested during active campaign writes | Snapshot is fine — the artist needs their contacts, not transactional perfection | No blocking |
| **Abuse / concurrency** — rapid repeated export requests (scripted scraping, or a second request while one is still assembling) | Exports are de-duplicated per owner: an in-flight assembly is reused rather than re-run, and repeated requests are rate-limited, not refused | The existing job's progress, or the ready file — never a hard block that reads as a portability denial. Rate-limit copy makes clear it is a throttle, not a policy |
| Account deleted — what happens to their aggregate contributions? | The aggregate is anonymised and already published | See CX-01 synthesis Q1; GDPR question |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available (Populated) | CRM has contacts | Export is a normal, always-available action, not buried in settings |
| Empty | CRM has no contacts yet | Export still offered; produces a valid empty file, not an error or a disabled button |
| Assembling (Loading) | Export requested | Progress; large CRMs take time |
| Ready | Assembled | Download in an open format |
| Failed (Error) | Assembly error | Retry — and a failed export must never be allowed to look like a policy refusal |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Full unconditional CRM export is **the point, not a concession** — because the platform's stated lock-in thesis only works if the lock-in is earned | `meta/problem-statement.md` D-18: provenance is "the value they cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture", and Q-02 explicitly asks whether the lock-in is earned or hostile. Reasoning: if we keep the artist by holding their address book, we are not executing the thesis — we are substituting a cheap hostage for the expensive asset the thesis actually calls for | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it clarifies what earned lock-in *is* here. What the leaver loses is not their contacts; it is the directory, the aggregate response data no competitor has, and the accumulated history that made the next pitch better. Those are things we built and they never owned. That is a defensible moat. Holding their contacts would be an indefensible one, and — worse — an admission that the real moat isn't strong enough. |
| DT-02 | A contact the artist contributed to the directory is cleanly theirs to take | Authorship instinct — they supplied it | ❌ REJECTED as *clean*, though the practical answer is still "they take their version." Authorship is genuinely split: they supplied the raw contact, we verified it, enriched it, tracked its freshness (21.03.01 DT-01), and other artists have since acted on it. They take their copy; the directory keeps its record. What they cannot take is the *enrichment* — and that asymmetry is exactly DT-01's earned lock-in showing up at the level of a single row. The reason to reject the clean framing is that pretending it is simple would hide a real design decision behind an obvious-sounding word. |
| DT-03 | Export should trigger a retention flow — a discount, a "what went wrong?", a pause offer | Universal industry practice; it demonstrably works | ❌ REJECTED — intercepting an export converts portability from a right into a negotiation, and it is the precise moment a product reveals whether its lock-in is earned. It also fails on its own terms: a user who exports has already decided, and the interception mostly harms the users who were merely backing up their data. `meta/personas.md` says the Musician's switching trigger is "losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove" — this is a population with a lived, specific reason to distrust systems that hold their information. An export dark pattern would be read exactly as what it is. Record it as an anti-pattern so nobody adds it later as a growth experiment. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Full unconditional CRM export, open format, no interception | See DT-01 and DT-03. The thesis requires earned lock-in; this is where "earned" is proved or disproved. |
| D-02 | The directory is not exportable | Platform-assembled reference data the artist never created. This is the legitimate moat. |
| D-03 | Export triggers no retention flow and no notification | See DT-03. Logged as an anti-pattern so it is not reintroduced as a growth experiment. |
