## Overview

Who to pitch, and what happened last time. The domain index named the wound precisely: a contact
database "currently living in a personal spreadsheet that walks out the door when the person
leaves." This sub-domain is the platform's answer — a shared directory of gatekeepers whose data
quality no individual could sustain, plus a private relationship record that accumulates instead
of evaporating.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: the shared directory and the private CRM are
different objects with opposite ownership, and they interact constantly. Your private entry points
at a public target; the public target's response-rate statistic is aggregated from many private
records; a directory rule change invalidates your queued pitch; a bounce in your CRM is a
freshness signal for everyone's directory. Add pitch tracking and portability and there are four
interacting capabilities.

**The merge**: sweep candidates 05 (Media & Pitch Target Directory) and 06 (Press Contact CRM &
Pitch Tracking) are folded here. Keeping them as sibling sub-domains would have split an object
from its own history.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Candidates 05 and 06 **merged** into one sub-domain | A contact and its history are one object viewed two ways. Splitting them across sub-domains would have put the directory entry in one place and the memory of pitching it in another — recreating the fragmentation `meta/problem-statement.md` says the platform exists to end. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | The shared directory and the private CRM are **separate entities**, not one entity with a visibility flag | Opposite ownership and opposite lifecycles. The directory is platform-owned reference data about a business; the CRM is the artist's private record of a relationship. A visibility flag on one table would put "Pitchfork's submission address" and "Dave said he'd cover us if we got a better single" in the same object, and the second must never leak. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-03 | Aggregate response-rate data flows **from** private CRMs **to** the public directory, anonymised | This is the sub-domain's core value: no individual artist can know that a curator answers 4% of pitches; the platform can, from the same data the artists are already generating as a byproduct. It is also the sub-domain's sharpest privacy boundary — see 21.03.01 Q-01. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-04 | Contact portability is treated as a **first-class feature**, not an export button | `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02 asks whether the platform's lock-in is earned or hostile, and names data portability as a values decision. The contact list is the exact asset where that question becomes concrete. | Agent, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | **Where does the directory's initial data come from?** A directory of gatekeepers is a cold-start problem with no organic solution: it is worthless until it is comprehensive, and it becomes comprehensive only through sustained editorial effort, licensing, or scraping. Scraping contact details of journalists who never consented is a GDPR exposure and a relationship disaster. This is the single largest cost and risk in the sub-domain and it determines whether 21.03.01 is buildable at all. | User | `/ideate-validate` |
| Q-02 | Are aggregate response rates published **per named target** ("Dave at Outlet answers 4%") or only in aggregate bands? Per-target is far more useful and is a published performance claim about an identifiable individual's professional conduct. | User | `/ideate-validate` |
| Q-03 | Do Operators need their own contact/CRM surface for local press and promoters? Real behaviour, currently homeless — would land in 21.08. | Agent | `/ideate-discover` Step 5 |
| Q-04 | For a band entity, is the CRM owned by the band or the member who built it? This is D-04's hard case and it is exactly how relationships get lost when a member leaves. | User | `/create-prd-architecture` |
