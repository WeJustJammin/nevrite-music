# Feature: Pitch Tracking & Response Log

> **Number**: 21.03.03
> **Parent**: [Pitch Targets & Relationship CRM](./21.03-pitch-targets-crm-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What was sent, to whom, when, and what came back. Every pitch across every channel lands here as a
durable event, which makes it three things at once: the artist's memory across campaigns, the
contact timeline in the CRM, and — anonymised and aggregated — the only sustainable source of the
directory's response-rate data.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees every pitch they have sent, its state, and what came back — across channels and across campaigns, not scoped to one release. Records outcomes the system cannot observe (a reply that came by DM, a "yes" over a phone call). Sees the pattern that matters: which targets ever answer them, which never have, and what they were pitching when it worked. |
| Producer | None | Not a sender; no visibility into any artist's pitch history. |
| Operator | None | Not a sender in this sub-domain. |
| Fan | None | No access. |

## Behavior

A pitch event is durable and cross-campaign (21.02.03 D-02). It records: target, channel, when,
what was sent (including the EPK version), and outcome.

**Outcome states must include silence as a terminal outcome**, not as a permanent "pending".
Silence is the modal result of pitching — most pitches are never answered — and a UI that shows
forty pitches "awaiting response" forever is lying about what happened and denying the artist the
one lesson available.

**Observability is limited and must be admitted.** We can observe a send and a bounce. We can
sometimes observe an open, unreliably and increasingly not at all. We generally *cannot* observe a
reply that went to the artist's own inbox, a DM, or a phone call — so outcome recording is partly
manual, and the design must make that one click rather than a form.

**Upward aggregation** (CX-01) is where these events stop being a diary and become the platform's
compounding asset: no individual can know a curator's true response rate; we can, from events
artists generate for their own reasons anyway.

### Happy Path

1. Musician sends a pitch (21.02.02 / 21.02.03 / 21.02.04); an event is created automatically.
2. Event appears on the campaign view and on the contact's CRM timeline (21.03.02).
3. A reply arrives in the artist's own inbox; they mark the outcome in one click.
4. Coverage follows; it links to the pitch event and to 21.07 — the loop closes.
5. Anonymised outcome flows to the directory's aggregate.
6. Next cycle, the target surfaces with "you pitched them twice, they covered you once."

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No response ever | Terminal **silent** state after N days, not perpetual pending | "No response (silent)" — a real outcome, and the most common one |
| Reply arrives off-platform and is never recorded | The log is silently wrong and the aggregate inherits the error | This is the feature's core data-quality risk. One-click recording is a mitigation, not a solution — see Q-01 |
| Release date moves after the pitch was sent | The pitch stated a date now false; cascade (21.01.04) classifies it as escaped | Correction task; the pitch event records that a correction was needed |
| Open tracking is blocked (privacy proxies, image blocking) | Open data is largely unavailable and getting worse | Do not display open rates as if meaningful. A metric that is 40% observable is not a metric — it is a source of confident wrong conclusions |
| Artist deletes a contact | The pitch events are theirs; the aggregate already flowed | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether deletion retracts the aggregate contribution is a GDPR question (CX-01 synthesis Q1) |
| Same target pitched for two campaigns simultaneously | Both events on one timeline — and it is probably a mistake the artist wants stopped | Surfaced; rate limits (21.02.06) may block the second anyway |
| Coverage happens without any pitch (organic) | The loop's happiest case has no pitch event to attach to | 21.07 must accept coverage with no pitch parent — attribution here is optional, not required |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Queued | Send initiated | May be held by a cascade |
| Sent | Delivered | On the campaign view and the contact timeline |
| Bounced | Delivery failed | Feeds directory freshness; shown as rot, not rejection |
| Responded | Reply observed or recorded | Outcome captured |
| Silent | N days elapsed | **Terminal** — the modal outcome, named honestly |
| Covered | Coverage linked | Loop closed; quote flows to the EPK |
| Declined | Explicit no | Recorded — a "no" is more valuable than silence and should be visibly distinct |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Silence must be a terminal outcome**, not a pending state — because it is what usually happens, and modelling it as "still waiting" makes the log lie about the artist's own history | Reasoning about the real distribution of pitch outcomes. Most pitches are never answered. Every CRM-shaped tool models the unanswered case as an open state because the sales metaphor it inherited assumes a pipeline that closes. Music pitching is not a pipeline; it is mostly a void | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it has teeth beyond UI copy. If silence is pending, the response-rate aggregate (CX-01) cannot be computed at all, because no denominator ever closes. The directory's entire value proposition (21.03.01 DT-01) depends on this feature being willing to say "nobody answered." The honest state model is a precondition for the domain's best asset. |
| DT-02 | We can rely on open/click tracking to determine outcomes automatically | Standard email-tooling assumption | ❌ REJECTED — open tracking is unreliable and degrading (image proxies, privacy protections, blocked remote content), and replies mostly arrive in the artist's own inbox where we cannot see them at all. Building the outcome model on observable signals would produce a log that is confidently wrong: a journalist who read on their phone with images off and replied directly would register as "never opened, no response." That is worse than an honest gap, because the artist would act on it — and the error would propagate into the directory aggregate that other artists rely on. Manual outcome recording, made trivial, is the correct answer even though it is the less impressive one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Silence is a terminal outcome | See DT-01. Also a hard precondition for computing any response rate. |
| D-02 | Outcome recording is manual and one-click; observable signals are supplementary | See DT-02. An honest gap beats a confidently wrong inference that propagates into shared data. |
| D-03 | Open rates are not displayed as a metric | Partially-observable data rendered as a number produces confident wrong conclusions. |
| D-04 | Pitch events are durable and cross-campaign | The cross-campaign pattern is the value; per-release scoping rebuilds amnesia. |
