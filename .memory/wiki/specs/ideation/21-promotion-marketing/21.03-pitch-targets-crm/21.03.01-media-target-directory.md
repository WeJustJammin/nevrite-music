# Feature: Media & Pitch Target Directory

> **Number**: 21.03.01
> **Parent**: [Pitch Targets & Relationship CRM](./21.03-pitch-targets-crm-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A shared, platform-maintained database of gatekeepers — outlets, journalists, playlist curators,
stations, shows — with their submission rules, formats, coverage history and real response rates.
It is the asset no individual artist can build and no spreadsheet can keep current, and it is the
thing that stops the publicist's knowledge from walking out the door. It is also the single
largest cost and legal risk in the domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Searches targets by genre, format, territory, outlet type; sees each one's submission rules, what they have actually covered, typical turnaround, and real response rate; sees when a target is not accepting pitches at all. Contributes contacts and corrections back. Uses this to spend a scarce pitch budget (21.02.06) on the targets that might actually answer. |
| Producer | None | Not a pitching function. A Producer looking up press targets for their own release is in the Musician lens. |
| Operator | None | Local press and promoter contacts are real Operator behaviour and this directory is not shaped for them (it is release-cycle media). The gap is genuine — see Q-03 on the sub-domain index — and would land in 21.08. |
| Fan | None | Not a participant. Note the awkward structural fact that many playlist curators and bloggers *are* Fans in persona terms — enthusiasts with an audience — which is why the gatekeeper's absence from the persona model (Q-01 on 21.02) keeps surfacing. |

## Behavior

A target record holds: identity (person and/or outlet), what they cover, how to reach them, their
submission rules, their opt-out state, and their aggregate response rate (computed from
21.03.03 via CX-01, anonymised).

**The core problem is not the schema — it is freshness.** Music journalism churns viciously:
people move outlets, outlets fold, formats change, submission addresses die. A directory is
worthless the moment it is stale, and staleness is invisible until an artist has already spent a
pitch from a scarce budget on a dead contact. So the directory's real design problem is a
**decay model**: every fact needs a confidence that degrades with age and is refreshed by
evidence — a bounce (21.02.03) is evidence of rot; a response is evidence of life.

**The cold start has no organic solution.** The directory is worthless until comprehensive and
becomes comprehensive only through sustained editorial effort, licensing, or scraping. Scraping
journalists' contact details without consent is a GDPR exposure and a relationship disaster with
the exact people the domain depends on. This is Q-01 on the sub-domain index and it determines
whether this feature is buildable at all.

### Happy Path

1. Musician searching for press targets for an indie-rock single.
2. Filters by genre, territory, outlet type.
3. Results ranked by relevance and by evidence of actual coverage of comparable artists — not by a self-described blurb.
4. Each result shows: what they have covered, response rate, typical turnaround, submission rules, freshness ("verified 3 weeks ago").
5. Musician adds targets to their list; private history from 21.03.02 overlays where it exists.
6. Pitches sent (21.02.03); outcomes flow back and refresh both the freshness signal and the aggregate.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Contact has rotted (person left the outlet) | Bounce evidence degrades the record's confidence | "Last verified 8 months ago — 3 recent bounces" rather than a confident-looking dead entry. Displaying stale data as fresh is the failure mode that costs artists their pitch budget |
| Target opts out | Record remains visible (the outlet exists) but shows as not accepting; no send affordance | Opt-out must work without the target holding an account (21.02.06 D-02) |
| Target's listing offers guaranteed placement for payment | Removed, not labelled | 21.02.02 D-02 / 21.05.03. A guaranteed-placement offer visible here is us brokering payola |
| Small-n response rate (target received 3 pitches total) | The statistic is noise and, worse, re-identifiable | Suppressed below a threshold. "Not enough data" beats a 33% that is one artist's outcome exposed (see 21.03 CX-01 synthesis Q3) |
| Duplicate records for one person (moved outlets, entered twice) | Must merge — while private CRM entries reference both | Private history survives the merge; the relationship is with the person, not our row |
| Artist contributes a contact that is wrong or malicious | A poisoned directory harms every user and burns a real person | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — contribution needs verification; an open write path into a contact database is an abuse vector |
| Journalist demands removal of their data (GDPR erasure) | Must be honoured, fully and quickly | This is why Q-01 matters. If the record exists because we scraped it, erasure is not an edge case — it is the expected outcome at scale, plus a complaint |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No results for the filter | Empty state — honest about coverage gaps rather than padding with irrelevant targets |
| Populated | Results found | Ranked list with freshness and response rate on every row |
| Stale | Confidence decayed past threshold | Explicitly marked; still shown, because a stale contact beats no contact if the artist knows |
| Not accepting | Target opted out | Visible, no send affordance |
| Suppressed stat | Small-n | "Not enough data" instead of a misleading number |
| Removed | GDPR erasure / policy violation | Gone; private CRM references must degrade gracefully rather than dangle |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The directory's hard problem is **freshness, not coverage** — so every fact needs a decaying confidence refreshed by evidence, and staleness must be visible rather than inferred | Reasoning about music-journalism churn and about what actually harms the artist. A missing contact costs an opportunity the artist never knew existed; a *stale* contact costs a pitch from a budget of 8 per week (21.02.06), plus the false belief that they were rejected when in fact nobody read it | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it produces the design principle that the platform's own bounce and response data (CX-01) is the only sustainable freshness source. It also creates a virtuous dependency: the directory gets more accurate the more the platform is used, which is a real compounding asset of the kind `meta/problem-statement.md` says the product needs — "the value they cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture." |
| DT-02 | The directory can be bootstrapped by scraping publicly-available journalist contact details | The obvious cold-start answer, and what several products in this space evidently did | ❌ REJECTED as a default, escalated to the owner (Q-01, sub-domain index). Three reasons compound: (a) GDPR — a journalist's work email is personal data and "publicly available" is not a lawful basis; (b) the erasure requests would arrive at scale and each is a complaint from someone the domain depends on; (c) it poisons the well with the exact population whose goodwill is the asset. A directory built by burning gatekeepers optimises for launch day and destroys the thing being launched. But the alternative (licensing or editorial effort) is a real cost, and the cost is the owner's to accept or decline. |
| DT-03 | Publishing a **per-named-target response rate** is the directory's most valuable and most dangerous output | Reasoning about what the artist actually needs (which of 400 curators answers) versus what we would be doing (publishing a performance statistic about an identifiable individual's professional conduct, computed from data they never gave us, with no consent flow and no seat in our persona model) | ✅ CONFIRMED as valuable, ❌ **unresolved** as acceptable — escalated to Q-02 on the sub-domain index. The tension is real and does not dissolve: aggregate bands ("responds sometimes") are safe and nearly useless; per-target rates are the product. A middle path — targets can see and contest their own statistic — requires them to have accounts, which contradicts assumption D-03 on 21.02 that they are off-platform. This is a genuine knot, and I do not think it is mine to untie. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every fact carries a decaying confidence; staleness is displayed, never hidden | See DT-01. A confident-looking dead contact costs a scarce pitch and teaches a false lesson. |
| D-02 | Small-n statistics are suppressed | Below threshold a response rate is both noise and re-identifiable. |
| D-03 | Ranking is by evidence of actual coverage, not by self-description | Self-described blurbs are how the fraudulent side outcompetes the legitimate side (21.02.02 DT-01). |
| D-04 | Private CRM references must survive directory merges and removals | The relationship belongs to the artist, not to our row. |
