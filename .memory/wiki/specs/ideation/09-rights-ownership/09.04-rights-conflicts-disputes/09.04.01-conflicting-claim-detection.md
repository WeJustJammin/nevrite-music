# Feature: Conflicting Claim Detection

> **Number**: 09.04.01
> **Parent**: [Rights Conflicts & Disputes](./09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Automatic detection of claims that cannot all be true: two people claiming the same 25%, a share
assigned twice, a work registered by two different sets of writers, a total that exceeds 100% of a
right. The system notices before anyone does.

This is where WeJammin's structural advantage is most concrete. Every incumbent detects conflicts
*forensically* — someone reads a royalty statement, it looks wrong, and by then the facts are years old
and the relationships are gone. WeJammin holds consented records from the moment of creation, so
detection fires at **claim time**. A duplicate claim caught in week one is a text message. The same
claim caught in year four is a lawsuit.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees conflicts affecting anything they are attached to, with the specific overlap named ("your 25% and X's 25% both claim the same writer share"). Corrects if it is their error; asserts a dispute if it is not. Cannot suppress a detection — a flag someone can hide is worthless to the person it protects. |
| Producer | Full | Same visibility, and frequently the fastest resolution path — they were in the room and can say what was agreed in one message. Their attestation is worth more than either claimant's assertion, which is exactly the structural value `meta/personas.md` assigns them. |
| Operator | None | No claims, no conflicts. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. Conflicts are commercially sensitive and reputationally loaded; surfacing "this song's ownership is contested" to fans would be a defamation-adjacent product. |

## Behavior

Detection runs on **claim events**, not on a schedule — the moment a share is proposed, an assignment
recorded, or a work registered, the system checks it against everything already known. The classes it
catches:

| Class | Example | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Arithmetic overlap** | Claims summing over 100% of one right | Certain — it is arithmetic |
| **Double assignment** | The same share transferred to two grantees | Certain — the chain says so |
| **Duplicate registration** | Two works registered that are plausibly the same song | Probabilistic — needs human confirmation |
| **Territory collision** | A worldwide grant and a Japan grant of the same right | Certain, if territory is modelled ([09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md) DT-01) |
| **External conflict** | A society or DSP reports a competing claim from off-platform | Certain that a conflict exists; the platform sees only one side |
| **Declared-status contradiction** | A composition declared public domain against a determinate in-copyright term status in a supported jurisdiction | Certain that the two records disagree; certain about neither of them |

Detection **notifies**; it does not open a case (see
[09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-cx.md](./09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-cx.md) CX-01) and it does not
freeze money (CX-04).

### Happy Path

1. A writer claims 30% of a work whose ledger already allocates 100%.
2. Detection fires immediately, at claim time — not at the next royalty run.
3. Both parties are notified with the overlap named precisely.
4. The likely truth surfaces in a message: someone mistyped, or two people recorded the same agreement from different ends.
5. A ledger amendment ([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) corrects it with consent from all parties.
6. No case, no freeze, no lawyers. This is the feature succeeding.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| False positive on duplicate-work detection (two genuinely different songs with the same title) | Common — song titles are not unique. Flagged as probabilistic, dismissible by either party. Dismissal and remembered dismissal apply only to this probabilistic flag, never to a pending/completed deterministic `09.01.01` unclaimed-stub merge lifecycle. | "These may be the same song" — a question, never an assertion. |
| `09.01.01` finds equal canonical asserted-name sets for two eligible unclaimed stubs | It records a deterministic non-mutating merge candidate; `09.01.01` alone owns atomic recheck/action. This is not this feature’s probabilistic duplicate flag. | Candidate provenance is visible to attached parties; no person identity, entitlement, or ledger fact is asserted. |
| `09.01.01` skips a candidate after its atomic recheck fails | The skipped result retains the precise failed eligibility predicate and its source assertions. Detection does not retry, reconcile, or force a merge. | "This duplicate merge was not completed because the records changed" with retained candidate provenance. |
| `09.01.01` merge action fails | Failure is surfaced loudly with retained candidate/provenance context; no partial merge is treated as complete. | "We could not merge these duplicate records. Nothing changed." |
| A completed `09.01.01` duplicate merge is viewed | This is a completed record-consolidation fact, not a dismissible probabilistic duplicate flag. Its retained lineage remains available to claim-time detection. | "Merged duplicate record" with source-to-survivor provenance; no statement that writer identities were resolved. |
| Both parties believe they are right | Detection has done its job; the rest is a dispute ([09.04.02](./09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md)). | "Assert a dispute" available to either. |
| Conflict detected on a **released, earning** work | The expensive case: money is already flowing to someone. | Detection notifies; freeze happens only if a dispute is asserted (CX-04). |
| External conflict from a society | The platform sees only its own side; the counterclaim is opaque. | "PRS reports a competing claim on this work" — surfaced honestly as a partial view. |
| Nobody responds to the notification | Conflict persists as a visible, permanent flag. It does not expire. | Flag stays on the work for every party. Unresolved and honest about it beats silently resolved. |
| Detection fires on a work mid-capture (`proposed`, not yet consented) | Suppressed until the ledger is consented. | Nothing — a sheet in progress is *supposed* to be incomplete; flagging it would train users to ignore flags. |
| Two parties "correct" simultaneously in opposite directions | Overlap persists; detection re-fires. Corrections are consent-gated amendments, not unilateral fixes. | Both see the overlap standing. |
| A bad actor files a claim against a work they had no part in, to manufacture a conflict as harassment | A claim is a real assertion by an accountable identity — detection surfaces it, but the target holds the existing consented ledger and the burden sits with the newcomer. Repeat abusive claiming is a Trust & Safety signal, not a rights outcome. | "X has asserted a claim on this work" — named, attributable, never anonymous. Resolvable as a dispute the target is positioned to win. |
| Duplicate-work detection given only a shared title and no other overlap (writer, ISWC, audio) | Below the match threshold — suppressed, not surfaced. Title-only coincidence is the dominant false-positive source and flagging it trains users to ignore flags (D-06). | Nothing. |
| A work's `public-domain` declaration ([09.01.01 D-13](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)) is contradicted by a **determinate in-copyright** term status from [09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) | Detection fires and notifies both the declarant and any party attached to the work. It does **not** clear the declaration, change the publishing ledger's state, block or retract a release already made, or decide which record is right — the declaration is the user's assertion and the term status is source-attributed facts under one jurisdiction's rule, and choosing between them is adjudication ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)). A case opens only if a party asserts a dispute (D-01); money freezes only then (D-02). The flag is **persistent and not dismissible** (D-07). | "This song is declared public domain. Term status says in copyright in `DE` until 2031." Both records with their sources and dates, standing side by side on the work. "Assert a dispute" available to either side. |
| The contradiction is against a work released **months ago** | Same handling — the platform does not reach backwards. The release stands, the declaration stands, the flag appears now and stays. Detection is claim-time (D-03); a term status arriving late is a new claim event, not a retroactive judgement on an old one. | The flag appears on the work with its date, and states plainly that nothing was withdrawn. Anyone evaluating the work sees both records; nobody's release was undone by a lookup. |
| Later claim or incompatible authorship assertion involves a work previously auto-merged under `09.01.01`'s unclaimed-stub exception | Detection reads the retained per-source assertions at ordinary **claim time** and notifies affected parties. It does **not** auto-unmerge the records, auto-open a case, freeze money, or adjudicate writer identity/alias status; a person may use the existing dispute action if they assert a dispute. | "A new claim conflicts with an earlier duplicate merge" with the claim named and ordinary dispute-routing action. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Conflicts panel requested | Skeleton |
| Empty | No conflicts | "No conflicting claims" — a genuinely reassuring state, because detection is continuous |
| Populated | Conflicts detected | Per-conflict class, parties, the specific overlap, and available actions |
| Partial | Probabilistic conflict (possible duplicate) | Flagged with uncertainty stated; dismissible |
| Candidate | `09.01.01` reported exact canonical asserted-name-set equality for otherwise eligible stubs | Non-mutating deterministic candidate with profile, source assertions, and eligibility provenance; not a person-identity fact and not dismissible as a probabilistic flag |
| Completed | `09.01.01` atomically consolidated two eligible stubs | Source-to-survivor lineage and recorded action result; never says writers’ identities were resolved |
| Skipped | `09.01.01` atomic recheck failed | Precise failed predicate and retained candidate provenance; nothing moved or reconciled |
| Error | Detection or a `09.01.01` merge action failed | **Surfaced, never silent.** A detection system that fails quietly is worse than none — it manufactures false confidence; a failed merge states that nothing changed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A detected conflict should **automatically open a dispute case** — that is what detection is *for* | The obvious pipeline: detect → escalate → resolve | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's defining call. Nearly every detected overlap is a **typo**, not a conflict. Two people recorded the same agreement from opposite ends; someone fat-fingered 30 instead of 20. Auto-opening a case takes two collaborators who are still friendly and hands them a docket, with all the adversarial framing that implies — and the platform did it *to* them, unprompted. That is the reconstruction-era dynamic (contested, lawyered, expensive) reproduced by the very system meant to prevent it. Detection notifies; humans decide whether a disagreement exists. Recorded as CX-01. |
| DT-02 | Detection should **freeze the money** as soon as a conflict is found — otherwise the wrong person gets paid while everyone talks | Protective instinct; and it is true that paid-out money is hard to recover | ❌ REJECTED — the asymmetry runs the other way. A false positive that freezes an artist's income is the platform **harming an innocent party on its own initiative**, on the strength of its own arithmetic, with no human having alleged anything. Detection is high-volume and includes probabilistic classes; freezing on all of it would make income unpredictable, which for a working musician is the difference between a tool and a hazard. Freeze requires a human assertion (CX-04). Same principle as [09.03.02 D-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md): notify, don't execute. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect detection against **off-platform** claims, and the candidate list implies only internal checks | Conflicts do not respect the platform boundary. The MLC, PROs and DSPs all hold competing claims, and a work can be registered twice — once on WeJammin, once directly with a society by a co-writer who never joined. Internal-only detection would give users **false confidence**: a clean WeJammin ledger says nothing about a conflicting PRS registration. | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary, ⚠️ flagged as dependent. It requires society/DSP integration ([domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)), which is a large and slow surface, and the platform will only ever see one side of the counterclaim. Recorded as a cross-domain touchpoint with the honest caveat: until that integration exists, "no conflicts" means "no conflicts *we can see*" — and the empty state must not overclaim. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **09.01.01 Work & Recording Records** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)) — its narrow unclaimed-stub merge retains raw per-source assertions and provenance. A later incompatible assertion is an ordinary claim-time detection input, never an automatic undo, case, freeze, or identity/alias ruling.
- Touches **09.01.02 Ownership Ledger** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — the ledger is the object detection reads; the validator catches within-ledger errors, detection catches across-claim ones.
- Touches **09.03.01 Chain of Title** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) — double assignments are chain conflicts.
- Touches **09.03.05 Copyright Term & Public Domain** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md)) — *escalation source*. A determinate in-copyright status contradicting a `public-domain` declaration ([09.01.01 D-13](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)) is the declared-status contradiction class. Both records survive detection intact — that is the two-truths posture of [09.01.04 D-09](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.04-publishing-rights.md), not a precedence rule, and this feature invents no precedence rule to settle it. 09.03.05 D-06 stands: a term status authorizes nothing, so it cannot revoke a state it was never able to write.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — society/DSP counterclaims are the off-platform detection source (DT-03).
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — case machinery is a cross-cut mechanism; and repeat abusive/harassing claiming (the manufactured-conflict abuse case) is a T&S signal, not a rights outcome. See [../rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md).
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([path](../../12-release-distribution/)) — D-06's precision-first duplicate detection depends on identifiers (ISWC/ISRC) and audio fingerprinting that originate at registration/release, so the corroborating signals detection reads are produced upstream in domain 12.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Detection **notifies**; it never auto-opens a case | Most overlaps are typos. Auto-escalation hands friendly collaborators a docket and reproduces the adversarial dynamic the platform exists to prevent (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | Detection **never freezes money** on its own | A false positive stopping an artist's income is the platform harming an innocent party unprompted (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | Detection runs at **claim time**, not on a schedule | Claim-time detection is the whole advantage over forensic, statement-driven discovery. |
| D-04 | Detection failures are **surfaced loudly** | A detection system that fails silently manufactures false confidence — worse than having none. |
| D-05 | Arithmetic-overlap, double-assignment, territory-collision, and external-conflict detections are **not suppressible** by the parties; a probabilistic duplicate-work flag is dismissible by either party, with dismissal remembered permanently for that pair | A flag someone can hide is worthless to the person it protects. The duplicate-work exception is a question rather than an assertion (D-06); its remembered dismissal prevents repeated false-positive prompts for the same pair. |
| D-06 | Duplicate-work detection is **precision-first**: a probabilistic flag requires a corroborating signal beyond title (shared writer, ISWC/ISRC, or audio-fingerprint match). Title-only coincidence never fires. | Song titles are not unique; recall-first fuzzy matching produces constant false positives that teach users to dismiss every flag, which destroys the arithmetic/chain classes too. Better to miss a title-only near-duplicate than to erode trust in the certain classes. The exact match weights/thresholds are a `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` tuning detail; the behavioral stance (never fire on title alone; probabilistic flags are dismissible questions, never assertions) is locked here. `09.01.01`'s separately deterministic unclaimed-stub candidate/action lifecycle is neither a probabilistic flag nor dismissible: it uses its own exact canonical asserted-name-set predicate and atomic recheck. Resolves Q-02. |
| D-07 | A **declared-status contradiction** — a composition declared public domain ([09.01.01 D-13](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)) against a determinate in-copyright term status ([09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md)) — is a claim-conflict trigger that **persists visibly and is not dismissible**, and never alters the declaration, the ledger state, or a release already made | Two records disagree and both are real: one is the user's attributed assertion, the other is source-attributed facts under one jurisdiction's rule, and neither was ever able to write the other (09.03.05 D-06). Detection's whole job here is to keep both visible — this is D-01 and D-02 applied to a new class, not an exception to them. Dismissal is wrong for the same reason it is wrong on the arithmetic classes (D-05): it would delete the only standing signal that a release rests on a contested premise, and the party it protects is often not the party holding the dismiss button. Clearing the declaration instead would make the platform the adjudicator [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md) forbids, and retracting a release would punish a user for a lookup that did not exist when they released. |
