## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Conflicting Claim Detection ↔ Dispute Cases

**Relationship**: Detection is arithmetic; a dispute is a human position. The gap between them is the
most important design space in the sub-domain. When the system notices that two claims over one right
sum to 130%, the overwhelmingly likely explanation is that someone mistyped, or that two people
recorded the same agreement from different ends. The correct response is to tell both parties and let
them fix it in an afternoon. The incorrect response — opening a formal case — takes two collaborators
who are still friendly and hands them a docket.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Notified of the overlap; can correct, or assert a dispute if the other party disagrees.
- **Producer**: Same. Frequently the one who can resolve it instantly, because they were in the room.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ledger is the contested object. Detection reads it; a case attaches to it. Neither owns it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Detection → notify all parties → *wait*. A case opens only on an explicit human assertion. If nobody asserts and nobody corrects, the conflict persists as a visible flag — surfaced, unresolved, and honest about it.
3. **Permission intersection**: Anyone party to either claim may assert a dispute. Nobody can suppress a detection.
4. **Notification fan-out**: All parties on both claims. Not the wider work — a conflict between two of five co-writers is not the other three's business until it escalates.
5. **State transition conflict**: If both parties correct simultaneously in opposite directions, the overlap persists and detection re-fires. Corrections are ledger amendments ([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) and inherit their consent rules — so a "correction" is not a unilateral fix.

### CX-02: Dispute Cases ↔ Rights Freeze & Escrow

**Relationship**: Opening a dispute over a share freezes distribution of **that share only**. The
uncontested 75% keeps flowing to the people who are not arguing — punishing bystanders would be both
unjust and an obvious lever for a bad actor (dispute a work to strangle a co-writer's income until
they settle).

The freeze is the dispute's only clock. In the current industry, whoever holds the money holds the
leverage and can stall for years; escrow inverts that by making stalling cost *both* parties equally.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Sees their share frozen, with the amount accumulating visible. Read-only on the escrow itself — nobody can release their own escrow.
- **Producer**: Same.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Escrow is owned by the platform's financial layer, not by either party. This is precisely why neither can touch it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Case opens → contested share flagged → next distribution routes that share to escrow → uncontested shares distribute normally. If escrow fails to hold, distribution of the contested share must **halt**, never fall back to paying someone.
3. **Permission intersection**: No party can release escrow. Release requires resolution ([09.04.02](./09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md)) or, per [09.04 D-04](./09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md), an outcome the platform records rather than decides.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Contested parties, plus [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) which must alter its distribution.
5. **State transition conflict**: A dispute opening mid-royalty-run — same version-binding discipline as [09.02.04 CX-03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md). The run binds to the state at its start.

### CX-04: Conflicting Claim Detection ↔ Rights Freeze & Escrow

**Relationship**: Detection creates a visible conflict flag and notifies the parties; it has no authority to alter distribution. It never opens a case or freezes money. An explicit human dispute assertion is the required state-changing event: the assertion opens a dispute, and CX-02 then routes only the contested share to escrow from the next distribution.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared-state boundary**: Detection owns the conflict flag; the dispute owns the contested-share status. A detected flag cannot transition a share to frozen.
2. **Trigger chain**: Detection → notify all parties to both claims → *wait*. No assertion means the flag remains visible and no money freezes. An assertion by a party to either claim → dispute opens → CX-02's next-distribution escrow routing for that share only.
3. **Permission intersection**: Any party to either claim may assert the dispute; detection itself cannot assert one. Neither party may release escrow once the dispute has opened (CX-02).

> **CX-03 deferred implementation detail**: sources lock only stale escrow as a detection signal and escalating prompts to both parties; they do not lock a stale-escrow threshold or escalation-prompt cadence. `/write-be-spec` must specify that threshold and cadence without changing those locked behaviors.
