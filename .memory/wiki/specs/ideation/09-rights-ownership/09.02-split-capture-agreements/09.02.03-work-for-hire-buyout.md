# Feature: Work-for-Hire & Buyout Designation

> **Number**: 09.02.03
> **Parent**: [Split Capture & Agreements](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The designation that says: *this person did the work, took a fee, and owns none of it.* Work-for-hire
(and its cousin, the buyout) is **the most common arrangement in music** — the overwhelming majority of
session players, engineers, and hired guns are paid a fee and hold no share. If the capture flow cannot
express that, it cannot record the majority case correctly: it will invent a phantom co-owner, or
capture nothing at all.

It is also the most dangerous designation available, because it permanently extinguishes an ownership
claim. Everything about this feature's design follows from that asymmetry: it is routine, it is
correct, and it must never be applied to someone without their knowledge.

The feature's hardest structural finding is that **"work-for-hire" names two different operations**
wearing one word (DT-05). Getting them confused is how a platform either invents phantom owners or
silently hands a bought share to the wrong party.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The person being designated — the session drummer taking £200 and no share. Consents explicitly; sees in plain language what they are **paid** (£200, and whether it is already agreed at booking or newly proposed), what they **give up** ("you will own no part of this recording and earn no royalties from it"), and what they **keep**: their **credit**, their **performer/neighbouring-rights eligibility**, and any non-waivable attribution right — none of which the fee buys (see [09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) R-02, D-03). Can decline and counter-propose a share. Also the **payer** in the common indie case where the artist, not a label, is buying the contribution — in which case they consent to the *obligation* as well (D-06). Sees the consent channel recorded against their own consent (D-11). |
| Producer | Full | Proposes the designation during capture, usually correctly — this *is* the deal they struck verbally. Cannot apply it unilaterally: their assertion that someone is WFH is not evidence, and a platform that accepted it would be automating the exact rights-stripping it exists to prevent. Routinely the designee too — a producer WFH on the engineering while taking points on the production (CX-05) — in which case they consent to their own extinguishment, which is permitted because it is their own stake to give up, but is recorded as self-consent rather than hidden inside the proposal. |
| Operator | Config | The one place in this domain an Operator has a real stake. A studio that supplies its staff engineer under a WFH holds the resulting rights **as an entity** — the engineer's contribution vests in the studio, not the engineer (the **vesting** shape, DT-05). The Operator configures their studio's standard WFH terms once (label, perpetuity, beneficiary = the studio entity); those terms then **pre-fill** whenever the studio's staff work a session. They do not edit individual ledgers, cannot consent for their engineer, and their configured terms are snapshotted at designation time — changing the studio default never reaches back into an agreed designation (D-18). |
| Fan | None | No visibility. Buyout fees and ownership terms are commercially sensitive, and the Fan has no stake in either side of the transaction. The only thing that surfaces to a Fan is the **credit** — which is exactly the thing the buyout does not touch (D-03), so the Fan's view is identical whether a contributor was bought out or holds 50%. That invariance is the point. |

## Behavior

A WFH designation is scoped to a **contribution**, not to a person (see
[09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) CX-05) — a producer can
be WFH on engineering while taking points on production, and a person-scoped model would force a false
choice. The contribution is a **required term**: a designation naming no contribution is blocked, not
defaulted to "everything" (D-15).

### The two shapes — the feature's central distinction

"Work-for-hire" is used colloquially for two operations with **opposite ledger consequences**. The
designation records which one it is; there is no default (DT-05, D-07).

| Shape | What it means | Ledger consequence | Typical case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extinguishment** (no beneficiary) | The person never held a share and never will. The fee is the whole of their consideration. | Nothing moves. They are absent from the ledger *by agreement*, and the record says so. | The session drummer. The majority case. |
| **Vesting** (named beneficiary) | The contribution's share **exists** and belongs to someone else — an entity or a person named at designation time. | A share vests in the named acquirer. This is a chain-of-title event ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)), not a redistribution. | The studio holding its staff engineer's contribution. A co-writer selling their 25% to the artist. |

A platform that models only extinguishment cannot express the studio case at all. A platform that
models only vesting invents a phantom owner for every session drummer. A platform that models neither
and instead **redistributes the vacated share pro rata to the remaining parties** does the worst thing
available: it silently gives the drummer's percentage to people who did not buy it (DT-05).

### What is recorded

| Term | Required? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Contribution scope** | Required, never defaulted | Person-scoping forces the false either/or CX-05 rejects (D-15). |
| **Consideration** | Required — a fee amount, or an affirmed **zero-consideration** state | £0 and an unfilled field are indistinguishable, and this feature exists to distinguish agreement from absence (DT-06, D-09). |
| **Beneficiary** | Required — a named acquirer, or an affirmed **none** | The two shapes above. There is no safe default (D-07). |
| **Perpetuity** | Required — perpetual, or a stated term | Media and library buyouts are routinely term-limited. Defaulting to perpetual is the platform inventing the most extinguishing term available (DT-07, D-10). |
| **Label** | Optional, recorded as a stated term | "Work made for hire" / "assignment" / neither, as the parties describe it. Recorded as what the parties *called* it, never as a status the platform certifies (D-05, D-17). |
| **Union agreement** | Optional flag + named agreement | A union session's fee is not one number (DT-08). Flagging it is honest; computing it is Q-04. |
| **Consent channel** | Recorded automatically per consenter | An extinguishment consented via an emailed link is weaker evidence than one consented from a verified account. Hiding the difference makes a weak record look strong (DT-09, D-11). |

### What it does

1. **Records the party's absence from — or another party's presence on — the ownership ledger.** They are not a 0% row *by accident*; they are absent *by agreement*, or the share sits with the named acquirer, and the record must say which of the three it is.
2. **Attaches a consideration record**, referencing an existing engagement in [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) where the session was booked and priced on-platform, or creating a new obligation where it was not.
3. **Preserves** the credit, the performer record, and neighbouring-rights eligibility. The fee buys the copyright, not the fact.

The atomicity in (1)+(2) is load-bearing: a WFH whose consideration record failed to write is an
uncompensated rights transfer — a rights grab with a UI (D-01).

### Who consents

Two consents, not one (DT-04, D-06):

- **The designee** — because the designation extinguishes or moves *their* stake. Always required. Never inferable from the Producer's assertion, an Operator's studio policy, or an engagement's terms.
- **The payer** — because the designation creates an obligation against *them*. **Waived only when the consideration references an existing [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement**, where the payer already agreed the amount at booking. This is consolidation buying provenance: the platform booked the session, so it already holds the payer's consent to the price and does not need to ask twice.

Where the designee is an **entity** (a band, a studio), consent comes from that entity's authorised
signatory per [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/). Where no signatory is configured,
the designation cannot complete — the platform does not guess who may sign away an entity's rights.

> **Legal note**: "work made for hire" is a US statutory doctrine with specific requirements (employment,
> or a signed writing within enumerated categories). Most of the world uses assignment instead, and in
> some territories certain rights — moral rights, equitable remuneration — cannot be assigned at all.
> **The platform does not assert that a designation achieves WFH status in any jurisdiction** (D-05).
>
> **Resolved for v1** (this was the breadth pass's open marker): the designation is stored as a **territory-neutral instrument**
> — "fee, no share", with the parties' own label recorded as a term rather than as a certified status —
> and the consequence copy carries a **single territory-neutral notice** naming the three residuals that
> survive in enough territories that claiming otherwise is the dangerous error: the **credit**, the
> **performer record and neighbouring-rights eligibility** ([09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)),
> and **moral rights / attribution** where non-waivable ([09.03.06](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md)).
> The parties' stated governing law is captured as a term for downstream use; it is **never** used to
> validate, block, or vary what the platform claims the agreement achieves. Whether v1 goes further and
> models the *real distinct instruments* per territory is a schema-shaping product decision — Q-01, and
> a dependency of the domain's territory question ([09 Q-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)).

### Happy Path

The majority case: a paid session drummer, booked and priced on-platform.

1. Producer closes the session in [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/). Capture ([09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)) presents the session's participant list and asks **share or fee?** per contributor — before it asks for any percentage (CX-01).
2. Producer marks the drummer **fee, no share**, scoped to a named contribution ("drums — tracking, 14 Mar"). A designation with no named contribution is blocked inline: *"Work-for-hire on what?"* (D-15).
3. The platform finds the [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement that booked and priced this drummer — £200, agreed at booking — and **references** it as the consideration. Because the payer agreed £200 at booking, the platform does not ask them to consent again (D-06). Had the session been booked off-platform, the payer would enter the amount and consent to the obligation here.
4. Producer affirms the remaining required terms: **beneficiary — none** (the drummer was never proposed a share, so nothing vests anywhere), **perpetuity — perpetual**, label — *"work for hire"* as the parties call it. None of these is pre-selected (D-07, D-09, D-10).
5. Drummer receives the designation on their phone, in the room. The copy states, in this order: **what they are paid** ("£200 — already agreed when this session was booked"); **what they give up** ("You will own no part of this recording and earn no royalties from it"); **what they keep** ("Your credit, your performer record, and any neighbouring-rights income from it — the fee does not buy these"); and **what WeJammin is doing** ("We record what you agreed. Whether it achieves a transfer under your local law is a question for a lawyer.").
6. Drummer consents in one tap. The designation records the consent, its timestamp, and the **channel** — account, verified — against their name (D-11).
7. The designation and the consideration record **commit atomically** (D-01). If the consideration record fails, the designation does not stand — never the reverse order.
8. The drummer appears on the capture view as **"Fee, no share — consented, £200"** — visible in the room, and structurally distinct in the record from a 0% row nobody filled in (DT-01, D-02). Their credit publishes via [domain 02](../../02-credits-attribution/); their performer record confirms via [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md).
9. No ledger notification fires, because nothing shifted — the drummer was never proposed a share. **Where a proposed share is switched to a fee**, the ledger is now short of 100%: the platform shows the gap ("75% allocated — 25% unassigned") and requires the remaining parties to allocate it explicitly. It never redistributes pro rata, because auto-redistribution is the platform inventing shares — the same error [09.02.01 D-02](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) rejects (D-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Designee declines | Designation fails; they remain a ledger party (or an unallocated contributor); sheet stays `proposed`. | Routed to the Producer with the objection. A cheap disagreement, surfaced in the room. |
| Consideration record fails to write | **Designation does not stand.** Atomic with the consideration (D-01). | "Couldn't record the fee — the buyout hasn't been applied." Never the reverse order. |
| Designee is retroactively designated after consenting to a share | The most abusable operation in the sub-domain. Routes through [09.02.04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) as a rights transfer requiring explicit consent — never an administrative correction. **A beneficiary is mandatory here**: an agreed share does not evaporate, it moves to whoever bought it (DT-05). | Full amendment flow, framed as what it is: "This removes your ownership of the recording and transfers your 25% to [named acquirer]." See CX-04. |
| Designation proposed with **no contribution named** | Blocked. A person-scoped buyout is the false either/or CX-05 exists to prevent (D-15). | "Work-for-hire on what?" — required, unfilled. Mirrors [09.02.02](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md)'s required points base. |
| Designation proposed with **no beneficiary decision** | Blocked. Extinguishment and vesting have opposite ledger outcomes; a default picks one silently (D-07). | "Does this share go to someone, or does it not exist? Pick one." |
| **Fee of £0** entered | Not a fee. Blocked as a fee-bearing designation, and offered as a distinct **affirmed zero-consideration** state requiring the designee's separate affirmation ("I am taking no fee and no share"). Recorded as its own thing, never as a £0 buyout (DT-06, D-09). | "£0 isn't a fee. If they're doing this for nothing, say so explicitly — we'll record that, and they'll be asked to confirm it." |
| Negative or non-numeric fee | Rejected inline; entered terms preserved. | Inline error; nothing else on the sheet is lost (mirrors [09.02.01 D-05](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). |
| **Perpetuity not stated** | Blocked. Defaulting to perpetual is the platform silently choosing the most extinguishing term available (DT-07, D-10). | "Forever, or for a term?" — required, unfilled. |
| **Term-limited buyout reaches its expiry** | The share's return is a **reversion**, not this feature's job. Routes to [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md), which per [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) **notifies rather than executes** on a conditional reversion. | "The buyout on X's contribution ends 14 Mar 2031" on the rights view, well before it lands. |
| **Studio's standard terms pre-fill for a session the studio did not staff** | Pre-fill is scoped to sessions where the studio's staff are participants. | Terms shown as pre-filled and editable, never silently applied. |
| **Operator changes studio default terms after a designation used the old ones** | Configured terms are **snapshotted at designation time**. A live reference would let an Operator rewrite an agreed deal by editing a settings page (D-18). | Nothing changes on the existing designation. New sessions pre-fill from the new default. |
| **Studio's staff engineer refuses the studio's own standard WFH** | Designation fails. The Operator's config **pre-fills**; it does not consent. Whether that refusal is an employment problem is off-platform and none of the platform's business. | "X declined." Recorded as a fact, not escalated to the Operator as an enforcement action. |
| **Staff engineer both engineers (studio WFH) and plays bass (their own contribution) on the same session** | Contribution scoping handles it (D-04, D-15). The studio's WFH reaches the engineering; the bass performance and any share in it are the engineer's. The multi-hyphenate case, working correctly. | Two rows for one person: "Engineering — WFH to [Studio]", "Bass — 10% share". |
| **Producer designates themselves WFH on one contribution while taking points on another** | Allowed and routine (CX-05). Self-consent is permitted — it is their own stake to give up — but is recorded **as self-consent**, not folded invisibly into the proposal. | "You are buying out your own engineering contribution. Confirm." Both stakes shown separately on the sheet everyone consents to. |
| **Producer designates themselves WFH and names themselves the payer** | Allowed; it is coherent (a producer waiving a share for a fee they invoice). Both consents collapse onto one person and are recorded as such. | Sheet shows a single party in both positions — visible to every consenter, which is the disclosure mechanism. |
| **WFH applied to a co-writer** | Allowed but flagged hard — buying out a composition share is legitimate and rare, and is frequently a mistake. Fires only when the designation's **scope includes composition**; a designation scoped to a performance never touches a writer share. | "This removes X from the *composition*, not just the recording. Is that intended?" Confirm, don't block. |
| Designee holds a **points** arrangement on the same contribution | Blocked — mutually exclusive per contribution ([09.02.02 D-04](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md), CX-05). Combinable across *different* contributions. | "Points and a buyout fee for the same work — pick one." |
| Designee is **added back as a ledger party for the same contribution** while the designation stands | Blocked as a contradiction. The designation must be superseded via [09.02.04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) first — you cannot hold a share and a buyout of that same share simultaneously. | "X was bought out of this contribution. Amend the buyout before giving them a share of it." |
| **The domain-05 engagement's terms contradict the designation** — booking said "fee for service, no buyout", designation transfers ownership | Surfaced, never silently accepted. The platform holds **both** records and must not let them disagree quietly (DT-11). It does not rule which is right (D-04 at [09](../rights-ownership-index.md)); it notifies (D-05). | "The engagement you booked says fee-for-service without a buyout. This designation transfers ownership. Which is right?" — to both parties. |
| **Fee amount changes in domain 05 after the designee consented** (engagement re-priced) | The consent was bound to the terms shown. Consent binds to a **designation version**; changing the consideration invalidates it and everyone re-consents (mirrors CX-01 synthesis #5). | "The fee changed from £200 to £150. Please review this again." Prior consent retained as a historical fact, not carried forward. |
| **Fee obligation is created but never paid** | Designation stands; the platform does **not** auto-revert (DT-10). Auto-reverting means executing on an inference and letting a payment glitch mutate a ledger — the exact thing [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) forbids. The unpaid state is surfaced on the recording's rights view and on both parties' dashboards. Contested → [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md). | "Bought out — fee unpaid since 14 Mar." Prominent on the rights view. The record of the deal is not the record of the payment, and conflating them hides one or the other. |
| **Engagement is refunded or cancelled after the designation consented** | Same as unpaid: the consideration is now reversed, the designation still stands as a consented fact, and the discrepancy is surfaced rather than resolved. | "The fee for this buyout was refunded. The buyout still stands as agreed — if that's wrong, raise it." Routes to [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md). |
| **Designation applied while the ledger is mid-consent** | In-flight consents are **invalidated** — the sheet those parties were approving has changed (CX-01 synthesis #5). Consents already given are retained as historical facts on the prior version but do not carry forward. Everyone re-consents. | "The sheet changed — X was moved to a fee. Please review again." Never a silent carry-forward. |
| **Two co-producers designate the same person on the same contribution simultaneously** | Second proposal rejected. Designations **queue, never merge** — merging two proposed designations produces a designation nobody proposed ([09.02.04 D-04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). | "A designation for this contribution is already pending X's consent." |
| **One party proposes a share for someone while another designates them fee-not-share** | Same conflict, opposite directions. First write wins the pending slot; the second is shown the pending one rather than overwriting it. | "There's already a pending proposal for X's drums — a share of 20%. Review it before proposing a buyout." |
| **Designee's contribution is cut from the final recording after the buyout** | Designation and consideration **stand** — they did the work and were paid for it. The performer record is a separate question: they are no longer on the released master, and removing a **confirmed** performer is a contested edit per [09.01.05 D-01](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md), routing to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/). Not decided here. | "Your take was cut. The £200 stands. Your performer record on this master is under review." |
| **Territory does not permit the assignment** (moral rights, equitable remuneration, non-assignable residuals) | Platform records the agreement; does not assert enforceability; does not block. Blocking would mean the platform ruling on foreign law, which is the D-04 violation the whole domain is built to avoid. | The territory-neutral notice, always shown, never conditional: *"WeJammin records what you agreed. Some rights — your credit, your performer record, and moral rights in some countries — may not be transferable at all, and this agreement does not attempt to take them. Whether it transfers what it says it transfers under your local law is a question for a lawyer."* |
| **Designee is a minor** (identity record shows under-18) | The designation **cannot complete on the minor's unaccompanied consent**. It requires a second consent from a guardian/representative recorded against that identity in [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/). If none is identified, the designation does not stand — and **the safe failure is that the minor keeps their share** (or remains an unallocated contributor, and the sheet stays `proposed` until the parties agree a share). This is not the platform ruling on capacity to contract (which is jurisdictional, and D-04 territory); it is the platform declining to record a minor's solo tap as an extinguishment. | "X is under 18. A parent or guardian needs to agree to this too." Then, if unresolved: "This buyout hasn't been applied. X still holds their share." |
| **Designee's age is unknown** (no age signal — age assurance is still unresolved in [meta/constraints.md](../../meta/constraints.md)) | Proceeds normally. The platform does not gate on data it does not have, and does not ask the Producer to attest to someone else's age — an attestation whose only function would be to shift liability onto the person least able to know. The dependency is Q-02. | Nothing special. The consent record captures whatever identity signal existed at consent time, including its absence. |
| **Ensemble session — 14 players, one contractor** | The platform can bulk-**propose** (one term-set, one action, 14 notifications) but cannot bulk-**consent**: the extinguishment is individual, and 14 consents manufactured from one contractor's tap are exactly the evidentiary garbage this domain rejects (D-16). Whether a contractor's authority to sign for the players is modelled as an agency relationship in [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) is Q-06. Capture's own scaling problem is [09.02.01 Q-03](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) — not answered here. | "Buyout proposed to 14 players — waiting on 11." Chase from one screen. |
| **Designee is an entity** (a band, a studio, a production company) | Consent comes from the entity's authorised signatory per [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/). No signatory configured → designation cannot complete. | "[Band] has nobody authorised to sign for it. Set that up in the band's settings." |
| **Designee consents via signed link with no account** | Permitted — [09.02.01 D-04](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) makes account-free consent structural, and requiring an account here would make the majority case depend on universal adoption. But the **channel is recorded and displayed**, because a link-consented extinguishment is weaker evidence than an account-consented one and hiding that difference makes a weak record look strong (DT-09, D-11). Whether a buyout should demand *more* identity rigour than an ordinary share consent is Q-05. | On the designation, permanently: "Consented via emailed link, 14 Mar 2026, 19:42." Stated as a fact, not as a warning. |
| **Union session** (AFM or equivalent scale agreement) | Flag and named agreement recorded as stated terms. The platform shows a notice that the agreement may impose obligations — scale, pension and health contributions, recurring new-use payments — that WeJammin does not track, and does not compute any of them (DT-08). | "This is recorded as an AFM session. Scale, pension/health and new-use payments under that agreement aren't tracked here." Honest about the hole rather than papering it. |
| **Designee's account is compromised and the attacker consents** | Out of scope here — account security is [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/). The designation binds the consent to the identity and channel at consent time (D-11), which is what makes a repudiation investigable at all. Repudiation routes to [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md). | "Dispute this consent" is reachable from the designation itself. |
| **Network failure mid-consent** | Consent is **not** recorded. No optimistic state — showing "consented" for an extinguishment that did not commit is the single worst lie this feature could tell. Retry preserves everything entered. | "Couldn't record your agreement — nothing has changed. Try again." |
| **Designation left `proposed` for months** | Does not expire. A stale proposal is still evidence of what was proposed and when ([09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). The sheet stays `proposed`, which carries [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)'s `no split agreed` consequence into [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/)'s release gate. | "Waiting on X since March" on every party's dashboard, and at the release gate. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Designation panel opened | Skeleton. Never a blank form — the participant list and any [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement terms are already known and pre-fill. |
| **Empty** | No WFH designations on this work | "All contributors hold shares." A positive statement of fact, not an empty tray with a CTA — there is nothing wrong with a work that has no buyouts on it. |
| **Populated** | Designation consented | Party · contribution scope · shape (extinguished / vested in [acquirer]) · consideration (£ or affirmed zero) · perpetuity or term · label as stated · consent channel and timestamp · and — given equal weight — **what was retained**: credit, performer record, neighbouring-rights eligibility. |
| **Partial** | Proposed, awaiting consent | "Waiting on X" — naming *which* consent is outstanding (designee, payer, guardian, entity signatory), because "pending" alone tells nobody what to chase. Ledger not locked; existing arrangement unchanged. |
| **Error — consideration failed** | Consideration record failed to write | Designation rolled back; explicit message: "Couldn't record the fee — the buyout hasn't been applied." Entered terms preserved. |
| **Error — contradiction** | Designation disagrees with the [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement | Both records shown side by side, neither presented as authoritative. "Which is right?" — to both parties. Not blocking; not silently resolved. |
| **Partial — unpaid** | Designation consented; fee obligation outstanding | "Bought out — fee unpaid since 14 Mar." The designation is live and displayed as such; the payment gap is displayed next to it, not instead of it. |
| **Superseded** | Designation amended or reversed via [09.02.04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) | Prior designation retained permanently and retrievably, with what changed, who proposed it, and who consented. The history is the evidence ([09.02.04 D-01](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A WFH party should be a **0% row** in the ledger rather than absent from it | Data-model tidiness — one list, everyone on it | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with the important distinction. They should be *visible* in the capture view (invisible contributors are how people get forgotten), but the record must distinguish **"0% by agreement"** from **"0% because nobody said"** — and a bare 0% row cannot. The first is a consented buyout; the second is an unfinished sheet. Same number, opposite meanings, and conflating them means the registry cannot tell a completed deal from an abandoned one. Resolution: present in the view, structurally distinct in the record. |
| DT-02 | A fee buys the **copyright**, so it buys everything | The colloquial understanding of a buyout, and the one most producers operate on | ❌ REJECTED — a buyout transfers the copyright. It does not transfer the **fact of performance**, and in most territories it does not extinguish **neighbouring-rights eligibility** ([09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)), which arises from having played rather than from owning. It also does not remove the **credit**, and in moral-rights territories the right of attribution may not be waivable at all ([09.03.06](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md)). Modelling WFH as "delete this person" would silently strip a session musician of an income stream the fee-payer never bought — from the persona the platform is centred on. Recorded as R-02. |
| DT-03 | This is an enhancement — Should, not Must | It sounds like an edge designation; the split sheet is the headline | ❌ REJECTED — inverted. Fee-not-share is the **majority** of paid music work. A capture flow that cannot express it does not have a gap at the edges; it has a gap in the middle, and it will mis-record the most common session on the platform. This is a correctness dependency of the wedge, not an addition to it — hence the Must proposal ([09.02 D-03](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)). |
| DT-04 | Only the **designee** needs to consent — they are the one giving something up | The obvious reading of "the designation extinguishes their claim, so it is their call" | ❌ REJECTED — a designation is a two-sided instrument and the file's own D-01 proves it: it **creates a fee obligation**, and an obligation binds *somebody*. If a Producer can unilaterally designate a drummer at £5,000, the Producer has just committed the artist to £5,000 — the drummer consented enthusiastically, and the person who has to pay was never asked. That is the Producer anti-persona ("pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention") aimed at the payer instead of the designee, and the file's guard against it only covered one direction. Resolution: **two consents — designee and payer**. The elegant part is the exception: where the consideration references an existing [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement, the payer already agreed the price at booking, so the platform does not ask twice. Consolidation is not decorating provenance here; it is *removing a consent step* that would otherwise make the majority case slower. Recorded as D-06. |
| DT-05 | The vacated share **redistributes pro rata** to the remaining parties, whose relative shares rise | The `[SURFACE]` draft's own Happy Path said exactly this, and it reads as obviously correct | ❌ REJECTED — **and this was a live defect in the draft.** It conflates two operations that share a word and have opposite consequences. If a co-writer sells their 25% for £5,000, that 25% goes **to the person who bought it** — not sprinkled pro rata across the drummer, the engineer and the bassist, none of whom paid for it. Pro-rata redistribution silently gifts a purchased asset to bystanders. Conversely, for the session drummer, *nothing* redistributes, because they never held a share to vacate — the sheet was authored with them outside it. Resolution: the designation records a **beneficiary — a named acquirer, or an affirmed none** (D-07), giving two shapes: **vesting** (share moves to the acquirer; a chain-of-title event, [09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) and **extinguishment** (nothing moves). And the third case — a *proposed* share switched to a fee — leaves the ledger short of 100%, which the platform **shows** and asks the parties to allocate, rather than filling in itself: auto-redistribution is the platform inventing shares, which [09.02.01 D-02](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) already rejects (D-08). |
| DT-06 | A fee of **£0** is just a fee of £0 — allow it and move on | Friction-reduction; the "mate plays on the track for free" case is real and common | ❌ REJECTED as stated, ✅ CONFIRMED as a distinct state. The gratuitous case is real — people do play for free — but £0 in a fee field is **indistinguishable from an unfilled fee field**, and this feature's entire reason for existing (DT-01) is distinguishing agreement from absence. A silent £0 buyout is the most dangerous record on the platform: it looks like a completed deal and is actually an uncompensated rights transfer, which is precisely what D-01's atomicity exists to prevent — defeated by a default. Resolution: zero-consideration is a **separate, affirmed state** requiring the designee's own affirmation ("I am taking no fee and no share"), recorded distinctly from a fee-bearing buyout. This mirrors [09.02.02 DT-01](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md) exactly: the one place friction-reduction must yield is where a default would invent a financial term. A favour today is a lawsuit in three years, and the only defence is that they said so, on the day, on the record. |
| DT-07 | A buyout is by definition **perpetual and total**; a term field is over-engineering | The word "buyout" in session work colloquially means "all of it, forever" | ❌ REJECTED — true for session work, false for the two places buyouts are most formalised. **Media and library buyouts are routinely term-limited** ("all media, 2 years, UK") — that is the entire commercial structure of the advertising and production-music businesses, and the term is what is actually being negotiated. Recording a 2-year buyout as perpetual silently extinguishes a reversion the person bargained and priced for, and does it invisibly, and forever. Defaulting to perpetual is the platform picking the **most extinguishing available term** on the user's behalf — the same class of error as defaulting the points base ([09.02.02 DT-01](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md)), except the loss is total rather than arithmetic. Resolution: perpetuity is an **affirmed term, never a default** (D-10). Expiry of a term-limited buyout is a reversion and belongs to [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md), which notifies rather than executes per [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md). |
| DT-08 | A session fee is **a single number** | Every design in this feature assumes "the fee" is one amount with one payer | ❌ REJECTED — and this is what a domain expert would ask first that nobody here has mentioned. **A US union session is not a naked buyout.** Under an AFM scale agreement the deal is scale + pension and health contributions + **recurring new-use payments** when the recording is used in a new medium — and the agreement frequently does not permit the player's rights to be bought out at all. Recording that as "£200, bought out, perpetual" misstates the deal, hides a recurring obligation from the payer, and hides a recurring income stream from the player — the same class of harm as DT-02, and against the same persona. ⚠️ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap. **Resolved for v1 as far as honesty allows**: a union flag and the named agreement are recorded as stated terms, and a notice states plainly that WeJammin does not track scale, pension/health or new-use under it. Whether the platform ever *implements* union session handling is a large scope decision with a real integration surface — Q-04, not a guess. Flagging the hole is defensible; computing a scale rate the platform does not know is not. |
| DT-09 | A consent is a consent — the **channel** it arrived through is an implementation detail | [09.02.01 D-04](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) makes account-free consent structural, so treating channels differently looks like relitigating a settled decision | ❌ REJECTED — the decision stands; the **silence about it** does not. An extinguishment consented by tapping an emailed link is materially weaker evidence than one consented from a verified account, and this feature produces the platform's most consequential record. If the record does not say which happened, every designation looks equally strong, and the weakest ones look strongest of all — precisely because nobody can tell. That is an evidentiary product misrepresenting its own evidence. Resolution: **record and display the consent channel** (D-11). Note what this does *not* do: it does not require an account, does not warn, does not score. It states a fact and lets whoever is weighing the record weigh it. Whether a buyout should *demand* more identity rigour than an ordinary share consent depends on the enforceable-vs-evidentiary decision ([09 Q-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)) and is Q-05 — not fabricated here. |
| DT-10 | An **unpaid fee** should auto-revert the designation — no money, no transfer | Morally satisfying, and matches how many real assignments are drafted (conditional on payment) | ❌ REJECTED — the instinct is right and the mechanism is wrong. Auto-reverting means the platform mutates a ledger on the strength of its own reading of a payment state, which is exactly what [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) ("notify, don't execute") forbids and for the reason it forbids it: a failed card, a refund hold, or a domain-05 timing quirk would silently rewrite ownership, and the platform would have harmed someone on an inference. It is also adjudication ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)) — whether non-payment voids *this* agreement is a legal question about *their* contract. Resolution: **surface prominently, never auto-revert** (D-13). The record of the deal and the record of the payment are different records; conflating them means losing one. A designee whose fee never arrived has a dispute ([09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md)), and — critically — the platform's visible "unpaid since March" is the evidence they need to win it, which they would not have had if the platform had "helpfully" reverted the ledger and erased the discrepancy. |
| DT-11 | The [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement terms and the designation **cannot disagree** — 05 pre-fills 09.02.03, so they are the same fact | The pre-fill relationship in the draft's own Cross-Cut Notes implies one flows from the other | ❌ REJECTED — pre-fill is editable (it must be; deals change in the room), so the two records will routinely diverge, and the divergences that matter are exactly the abusive ones. "Booked as fee-for-service, no buyout" followed by "designated as a perpetual buyout" is not a data-entry drift; it is the deal being changed after it was struck, by the party running the flow, against the party who is about to tap consent on their phone. **The platform holds both records — it is the only actor that can see the contradiction at all**, which is a consolidation payoff nobody asked for and the sharpest one in this feature. Resolution: **surface the contradiction to both parties; rule on neither** (D-14). Not a block — the deal genuinely may have changed, and blocking would make the platform the arbiter of which record is true ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Notify, don't execute ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **05 Services Marketplace** ([path](../../05-services-marketplace/)) — **two distinct relationships, both load-bearing.** (a) *Trigger dependency*: a paid session's engagement terms determine and pre-fill the designation, and — because the payer agreed the price at booking — an engagement-backed consideration **removes the payer's consent step entirely** (DT-04, D-06). (b) *Contradiction surface*: pre-fill is editable, so the engagement and the designation will diverge; the platform holds both and must surface the disagreement without ruling on it (DT-11, D-14). Refunds and re-pricing after consent both propagate here.
- Touches **09.01.02 Ownership Ledger & Validation** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — *shared entity + state race*. Switching a **proposed share** to a fee leaves the ledger short of 100%; validation must render the gap and require explicit reallocation, never auto-fill it (D-08). Designating mid-consent invalidates in-flight consents (CX-01 synthesis #5).
- Touches **09.01.05 Performer & Neighbouring Rights** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)) — *shared entity*. Explicitly **not** extinguished by a buyout (R-02, D-03). A cut contribution's performer record is a contested edit, routed to 09.04, not decided here.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — *permission / payout logic*. **The place R-02 actually fails if nobody is looking.** A bought-out drummer is absent from the master ledger but may still be owed neighbouring-rights money. If domain 10 reads "not on the ledger" as "owed nothing", the buyout deletes an income stream the fee-payer never bought — at the payout layer, silently, years later, to the platform's structural-centre persona. Domain 10 must read the performer record, not the ownership ledger, for this class of payment.
- Touches **09.01.03 Master Rights Ownership** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md)) — *shared entity*. The **vesting** shape puts a named acquirer (often a studio entity) onto the master ledger. Extinguishment does not.
- Touches **09.03.01 Chain of Title** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) — *trigger dependency*. A buyout of an **agreed** share is a transfer to a named acquirer, not a redistribution (DT-05) — it is a chain-of-title event and must land there. This sharpens [09.02.04 Q-04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)'s amendment-vs-transfer boundary: a buyout with a beneficiary is a transfer; one without is not.
- Touches **09.03.02 Term, Territory & Reversion** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md)) — *trigger dependency*. A term-limited buyout's expiry is a reversion and belongs there (DT-07, D-10). Also the landing point for Q-01's territory modelling.
- Touches **09.03.06 Moral Rights & Attribution** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md)) — *shared entity*. Attribution may be non-waivable regardless of what the buyout says; named in the territory-neutral notice.
- Touches **09.02.04 Split Amendment & Re-Consent** ([path](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) — *trigger dependency*. Retroactive designation routes there as a rights transfer (CX-04), and — per DT-05 — must name a beneficiary, because an agreed share does not evaporate.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — *shared entity*. The credit survives the buyout. The Fan-facing view is identical whether a contributor was bought out or holds 50%, which is the point.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — *permission*. Three separate dependencies: the **studio-as-entity** holding WFH over staff (the Operator's only real stake in this domain); the **authorised signatory** required whenever a designee or acquirer is an entity; and the **age signal** the minor rule fires on (D-12), which depends on the age-assurance item still unresolved in [meta/constraints.md](../../meta/constraints.md).
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([path](../../12-release-distribution/)) — *trigger dependency*. An unresolved designation leaves the sheet `proposed`, which carries [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)'s `no split agreed` release block. This feature does not add a second gate; it feeds the existing one.
- Touches **23 Career, Finance & Business** ([path](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — *other*. A fee and a royalty are taxed differently and forecast differently. The zero-consideration state (D-09) is a third case again: no income, and a permanent one-way disposal of an asset.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — *other*. Repudiated consents and compromised-account consents; the consent-channel record (D-11) is what makes a repudiation investigable at all.
- Touches **09.04.02 Split & Ownership Dispute Cases** ([path](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md)) — *trigger dependency*. Unpaid fees, refunded engagements, and contested designations all land here rather than being auto-resolved (DT-10, D-13).

> For detailed cross-cut analysis, see [09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) (CX-01, CX-04, CX-05, R-02).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The designation and the consideration record are **atomic** | A WFH without consideration is an uncompensated rights transfer. Order matters: consideration first, or nothing. |
| D-02 | "0% by agreement" is **structurally distinct** from "0% because nobody said" | Same number, opposite meanings. Without the distinction the registry cannot tell a closed deal from an abandoned sheet (see DT-01). |
| D-03 | A buyout **does not** extinguish the performer record, the credit, or neighbouring-rights eligibility | The fee buys the copyright, not the fact of having played (see DT-02). |
| D-04 | Designation is scoped to a **contribution**, not a person | A person can be WFH on one contribution and take points on another; person-scoping forces a false either/or. |
| D-05 | The platform **records** the agreement; it does not **assert** that WFH status is achieved in any jurisdiction | WFH is a US statutory doctrine; most of the world assigns; some rights cannot be assigned. Asserting enforceability is a legal conclusion the platform has no standing to make ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |
| D-06 | **Two consents — designee and payer** — with the payer's waived when the consideration references an existing [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement | The designation binds two people: it extinguishes one party's stake and creates an obligation against another. Only asking one of them lets a Producer commit an artist to a fee they never agreed. The engagement exception is a consolidation payoff — it removes a consent step from the majority case (see DT-04). |
| D-07 | The designation records a **beneficiary** — a named acquirer, or an affirmed none. No default. | Extinguishment and vesting are opposite ledger operations sharing one word. A default silently picks one (see DT-05). |
| D-08 | A ledger shortfall from a share→fee switch is **shown, never auto-filled** | Auto-redistributing pro rata gifts a vacated share to bystanders and is the platform inventing shares — already rejected at [09.02.01 D-02](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md). |
| D-09 | **Zero consideration is a distinct affirmed state**, never a £0 fee | £0 and an unfilled field are indistinguishable, and distinguishing agreement from absence is this feature's whole job (see DT-06). |
| D-10 | **Perpetuity is an affirmed term**, never a default | Media and library buyouts are routinely term-limited. Defaulting to perpetual is the platform choosing the most extinguishing term available (see DT-07). Expiry is a reversion → [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md). |
| D-11 | The **consent channel** is recorded and displayed against every consent | An account-free consent to an extinguishment is weaker evidence than a verified one. An evidentiary product that hides the strength of its own evidence makes the weakest records look strongest (see DT-09). |
| D-12 | A **known minor's unaccompanied consent does not complete a designation**; a guardian/representative consent is required, and the safe failure is that **the minor keeps their share** | Not a ruling on capacity to contract (jurisdictional, and D-05 territory) — a product policy about what the platform will record as an extinguishment. Letting a 15-year-old tap away a master for £200 is the rights-stripping the platform exists to prevent, aimed at the person least able to see it. |
| D-13 | An **unpaid or refunded fee is surfaced, never auto-reverting** the designation | Auto-reverting mutates a ledger on the platform's own reading of a payment state — forbidden by [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md), and it erases the very discrepancy the designee needs as evidence (see DT-10). |
| D-14 | A **contradiction with the [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) engagement is surfaced to both parties, ruled on by neither** | The platform is the only actor that can see it. Blocking would make it the arbiter of which record is true ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)); silence would let a deal be rewritten after it was struck (see DT-11). |
| D-15 | **Contribution scope is a required term** — no person-scoped designation, no default to "everything" | The false either/or CX-05 exists to prevent, and a defaulted "everything" is the most extinguishing default available. |
| D-16 | **Bulk propose, never bulk consent** | One term-set to N designees is efficiency. One tap standing in for N extinguishments is manufactured consent — the evidentiary garbage the domain exists to replace. |
| D-17 | The parties' **label is recorded as a stated term**, never as a certified status; v1 shows a **single territory-neutral notice** | "Work made for hire" means something specific in the US and something different or nothing elsewhere. Recording what the parties called it is a fact; certifying that it worked is a legal conclusion (see the Legal note, D-05). |
| D-18 | Operator-configured studio terms are **snapshotted at designation time**, never live-referenced | A live reference would let an Operator rewrite an agreed deal by editing a settings page. |
