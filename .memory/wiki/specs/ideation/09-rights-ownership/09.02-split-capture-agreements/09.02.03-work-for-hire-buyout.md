# Feature: Work-for-Hire & Buyout Designation

> **Number**: 09.02.03
> **Parent**: [Split Capture & Agreements](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The designation that says: *this person did the work, took a fee, and owns none of it.* Work-for-hire
(and its cousin, the buyout) is **the most common arrangement in music** — the overwhelming majority of
session players, engineers, and hired guns are paid a fee and hold no share. If the capture flow cannot
express that, it cannot record the majority case correctly: it will invent a phantom co-owner, or
capture nothing at all.

It is also the most dangerous designation available, because it permanently extinguishes an ownership
claim. Everything about this feature's design follows from that asymmetry: it is routine, it is
correct, and it must never be applied to someone without their knowledge.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The person being designated — the session drummer taking £200 and no share. Consents explicitly; sees in plain language what they are giving up ("you will own no part of this recording and earn no royalties from it") and what they are keeping (their **credit**, and their **performer/neighbouring-rights eligibility**, neither of which the fee buys — see [09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) R-02). Can decline and counter-propose a share. |
| Producer | Full | Proposes the designation during capture, usually correctly — this *is* the deal they struck verbally. Cannot apply it unilaterally: their assertion that someone is WFH is not evidence, and a platform that accepted it would be automating the exact rights-stripping it exists to prevent. |
| Operator | Config | The one place in this domain an Operator has a real stake. A studio that supplies its staff engineer under a WFH holds the resulting rights **as an entity** — the engineer's contribution belongs to the studio, not the engineer. The Operator configures their studio's standard WFH terms once; those terms then pre-fill whenever the studio's staff work a session. They do not edit individual ledgers. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

A WFH designation is scoped to a **contribution**, not to a person (see
[09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) CX-05) — a producer can
be WFH on engineering while taking points on production, and a person-scoped model would force a false
choice.

The designation:

1. **Removes** the party from the ownership ledger (they are not a 0% row *by accident* — they are absent *by agreement*, and the record must say which).
2. **Creates a fee obligation**, routing to [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) if the session was a paid engagement.
3. **Preserves** the credit and the performer record. The fee buys the copyright, not the fact.

The atomicity in (1)+(2) is load-bearing: a WFH whose fee obligation failed to create is an
uncompensated rights transfer — a rights grab with a UI.

> **Legal note**: "work made for hire" is a US statutory doctrine with specific requirements (employment,
> or a signed writing within enumerated categories). Most of the world uses assignment instead, and in
> some territories certain rights cannot be assigned at all. The platform must not assert that a
> designation *achieves* WFH status in a given jurisdiction. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
> on how territory-specific this gets. See Q-01.

### Happy Path

1. During capture, Producer marks the session drummer as fee-not-share.
2. Fee amount is entered (or already known, because the platform booked and priced the engagement — consolidation buying provenance again).
3. Drummer receives the designation with plain-language consequences: what they lose, what they keep, what they are paid.
4. Drummer consents. Their ledger row is removed; the fee obligation is created; their credit and performer record stand.
5. Ledger totals shift to the remaining parties, who are notified — their relative share just rose and they should know why.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Designee declines | Designation fails; they remain a ledger party; sheet stays `proposed`. | Routed to the Producer with the objection. A cheap disagreement, surfaced in the room. |
| Fee obligation fails to create | **Designation does not stand.** Atomic with the fee. | "Couldn't record the fee — the buyout hasn't been applied." Never the reverse order. |
| Designee is retroactively designated after consenting to a share | The most abusable operation in the sub-domain. Routes through [09.02.04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) as a rights transfer requiring explicit consent — never an administrative correction. | Full amendment flow with the consequence stated. See CX-04. |
| Studio's standard terms pre-fill for a session the studio did not staff | Pre-fill is scoped to sessions where the studio's staff are participants. | Terms shown as pre-filled and editable, never silently applied. |
| WFH applied to a **co-writer** | Allowed but flagged hard — buying out a composition share is legitimate and rare, and is frequently a mistake. | "This removes X from the *composition*, not just the recording. Is that intended?" |
| Territory does not permit the assignment | Platform records the agreement; does not assert enforceability. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Designee is a minor | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — capacity to contract is jurisdiction-specific and the platform has fans, hence minors, on it (D-13). | `[PENDING]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A WFH party should be a **0% row** in the ledger rather than absent from it | Data-model tidiness — one list, everyone on it | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with the important distinction. They should be *visible* in the capture view (invisible contributors are how people get forgotten), but the record must distinguish **"0% by agreement"** from **"0% because nobody said"** — and a bare 0% row cannot. The first is a consented buyout; the second is an unfinished sheet. Same number, opposite meanings, and conflating them means the registry cannot tell a completed deal from an abandoned one. Resolution: present in the view, structurally distinct in the record. |
| DT-02 | A fee buys the **copyright**, so it buys everything | The colloquial understanding of a buyout, and the one most producers operate on | ❌ REJECTED — a buyout transfers the copyright. It does not transfer the **fact of performance**, and in most territories it does not extinguish **neighbouring-rights eligibility** ([09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)), which arises from having played rather than from owning. It also does not remove the **credit**, and in moral-rights territories the right of attribution may not be waivable at all ([09.03.06](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md)). Modelling WFH as "delete this person" would silently strip a session musician of an income stream the fee-payer never bought — from the persona the platform is centred on. Recorded as R-02. |
| DT-03 | This is an enhancement — Should, not Must | It sounds like an edge designation; the split sheet is the headline | ❌ REJECTED — inverted. Fee-not-share is the **majority** of paid music work. A capture flow that cannot express it does not have a gap at the edges; it has a gap in the middle, and it will mis-record the most common session on the platform. This is a correctness dependency of the wedge, not an addition to it — hence the Must proposal ([09.02 D-03](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The designation and the fee obligation are **atomic** | A WFH without a fee is an uncompensated rights transfer. Order matters: fee first, or nothing. |
| D-02 | "0% by agreement" is **structurally distinct** from "0% because nobody said" | Same number, opposite meanings. Without the distinction the registry cannot tell a closed deal from an abandoned sheet (see DT-01). |
| D-03 | A buyout **does not** extinguish the performer record, the credit, or neighbouring-rights eligibility | The fee buys the copyright, not the fact of having played (see DT-02). |
| D-04 | Designation is scoped to a **contribution**, not a person | A person can be WFH on one contribution and take points on another; person-scoping forces a false either/or. |
| D-05 | The platform **records** the agreement; it does not **assert** that WFH status is achieved in any jurisdiction | WFH is a US statutory doctrine; most of the world assigns; some rights cannot be assigned. Asserting enforceability is a legal conclusion the platform has no standing to make. |
