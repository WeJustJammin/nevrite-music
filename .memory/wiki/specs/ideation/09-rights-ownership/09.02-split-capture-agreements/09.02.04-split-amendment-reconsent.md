# Feature: Split Amendment & Re-Consent

> **Number**: 09.02.04
> **Parent**: [Split Capture & Agreements](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The sanctioned path to change a split that has already been signed. Splits do not stay still: a
contributor is added months later, a percentage was entered wrong, a sample gets declared and dilutes
everyone, a writer signs a publisher, someone dies. A ledger that cannot be corrected is not immutable
— it is **wrong forever**, and worse than no ledger at all, because people trust it.

Amendment's central property is **supersession, not mutation**: the original consented sheet remains a
permanent, retrievable fact and the amendment is a new version pointing at it. The product is a record
whose value rests entirely on it not being quietly rewritable; a mutable ledger is worth exactly as much
as the memory it was built to replace.

The feature's real difficulty is not the versioning. It is that **an amendment is the only operation in
the domain that takes something away from someone who already has it** — which makes it the operation
every bad actor reaches for, and the one every good actor avoids because its true cost is not the
consent but the downstream wreckage (see DT-08). Both facts drive the design: unanimity with no
override, and honesty about what an amendment breaks before anyone signs it.

**This is a Deep Think addition — it was not on the candidate list.** The candidates model capture as a
one-shot event. Real splits are amended constantly, and the amendment is where the abuse lives.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Proposes amendments (adding a forgotten contributor, correcting their own share) and consents to others' proposals. Sees the **whole** proposed ledger, not their own row ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-01), with their before → after delta stated in figures. Where their share **decreases**, consent is not a one-tap: they must confirm the decreased figure itself (D-09). Sees every historical version of any sheet they are on — what changed, who proposed it, who consented, when. Can refuse; refusal is a first-class state that leaves the current version governing, not a veto to be worn down. Retains standing to propose on any sheet they appear on **in any version**, including versions that removed them (D-08). |
| Producer | Full | Usually the proposer, because they are usually the one who notices. Sending a proposal records their own consent to it explicitly and at that timestamp — proposing is not consenting, and the record must show every party signed. Cannot amend unilaterally, and cannot amend *around* a party by removing them first: removal is itself an amendment requiring that party's consent. Sees, before sending, exactly what the amendment will break downstream — active licences, filed registrations, in-flight royalty runs (D-11). |
| Operator | None | No amendment surface. A venue or studio operator is not a party to a split by virtue of operating the room, and has no stake in one. The one adjacent path is not an Operator path: where a studio **entity** holds a ledger share via a work-for-hire it holds ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), the entity is a party like any other and consents through its authorised signatory ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — as a rights-holding entity, not as an Operator. Explicit absence; matches the sub-domain Role Matrix. |
| Fan | None | No visibility, in any version. Splits are commercially sensitive and a fan has no stake. A superseded version is *more* sensitive than the current one, not less — it is a record of what people used to accept. |

## Behavior

### What an amendment is

An amendment produces a **new version** of the ledger. The prior version becomes `superseded` and is
retained permanently and retrievably. The chain of versions *is* the evidentiary record — a split sheet
that shows it was 25/25/25/25 in March, became 30/30/20/20 in June with everyone's consent, and who
asked for that, is far stronger evidence than one that only shows today's numbers.

Proposals are always computed against the version that is `current` at the moment of composition. A
superseded version cannot be amended; it is a closed fact.

**Critical timing property: the current version keeps governing while an amendment is pending.** Money
must not stop because someone opened a proposal. Distribution binds to the version that was `current`
at the start of the royalty run (see
[09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) CX-03).

**What is versioned here is the allocation, not the payout basis.** What a percentage is applied to on a
name-your-price sale — paid-net — is a platform-wide constant printed at capture, **not a field on any
ledger version** ([09.02.01 D-16](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). An amendment
therefore cannot change it, no proposal may offer to trade on it, and there is no payout-basis row to
dilute, lock or carry forward. The constant is versioned separately, on its own effective dates, as a **platform
term** ([09.02.01 D-17](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)) — which means a settled sheet
now sits under two independent version dimensions: the ledger version it agreed, and the basis terms
version its agreement was made under. Which basis terms version governs a sheet **after** it is amended
— the one the original agreement bound, or the one in force when the amended version was consented — is
not settled by the ratified rule and is **Q-07**. It is not guessed here: it is a money question with a
different answer for every party on a diluted sheet.

### The consent set

- **Every party on the current version** must consent, plus any party being **added**, plus any party being **removed**.
- Consent is to the **whole ledger** ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-01) and binds to the exact version and exact values shown ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-09). A consent to a sheet the party never saw is not a consent.
- Consent is counted **per row, not per human**. One person routinely holds two rows — their writer share and their self-held publisher share ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-03), or a personal row and their loan-out company's row. Those capacities can have different beneficial owners and must be signed separately. The interface shows the human once with two items; the counter says rows.
- **Entity parties sign through their authorised signatory** ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)). A band is an entity, not a persona (`meta/personas.md`), and its membership changing does not re-open a consented sheet ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-13).
- **No admin override.** If the platform could force an amendment through, that capability would be the attack surface for the entire domain — and the first thing an attacker, a hostile lawyer, or a subpoena would reach for. This is the source of the domain's spine ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)): the platform holds facts and routes judgements; it never adjudicates.
- Concurrent proposals **queue**; they do not merge. Merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed.

### Proposal lifecycle

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Draft | Saved always, never gated ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-05). A draft collects no consent and notifies nobody. |
| Send | Exactly **one** proposal per ledger may be `open` at a time. A second proposal is `queued` and cannot collect consent until the first resolves. |
| Consent | Recorded per row: `pending` / `consented` / `refused` ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-08). A consent is never displayed as recorded until the server acknowledges it. |
| Edit | Any change to any row resets **every** consent to `pending` ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-06). The cost is stated before the change commits. Edit-and-resend fans out notifications at most **3 times per rolling 7 days per proposal**; beyond that the edit still saves and still resets consents, but parties learn from their dashboard rather than a fourth notification (D-10). |
| Withdraw | Available to the proposer while the proposal is `open`. Once the last required consent is recorded, the version is `consented` and irrevocable — a later change of mind is a new amendment. |
| Refuse | Not terminal and not an error. The proposed version stays `proposed` carrying a `refused` row and never becomes current. The proposer may withdraw, edit, or leave it standing. |
| Expiry | **None.** A proposal open for three years is still evidence of what was proposed and when — mirroring [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)'s non-expiring sheet. |

### Amendment vs transfer — the boundary (resolves Q-04)

The two operations have **different consent sets**, which is what makes the line load-bearing. Dress a
transfer as an amendment and you hand every co-owner a veto over what someone else does with their own
share. Dress an amendment as a transfer and you strip everyone of a consent right they hold.

The test is **"was the sheet wrong, or did the world change?"**

| | **Amendment** (this feature) | **Transfer** ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) |
|---|---|---|
| What is untrue | The original allocation was an inaccurate statement of who created the work or what was agreed. | Nothing. The original allocation stays true; an accurately-established share changed hands under a new deal. |
| Consent set | Every party on the current version, plus additions, plus removals. | Transferor and transferee only. Co-owners hold no veto over a share that is not theirs. |
| Where it lands | A new ledger version here. | A chain-of-title event in 09.03.01. |
| Worked example | The keys player who came in for one afternoon was never on the sheet. The sheet was wrong. → amendment. | A writer signs a publisher: their **writer share** is untouched (they still wrote it); their self-held **publisher share** ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-03) moves to the publisher. The sheet was right. → transfer, no co-writer consent. |

What the parties see reflects the same split: the sheet shows both **who established a share** and
**who currently holds it**. Amendment changes the former. Transfer changes the latter.

**When an operation has both shapes, it requires the union of both consent sets — never the lesser.**
Retroactive work-for-hire is the case (CX-04): it extinguishes an established share (transfer-shaped)
while every remaining party's percentage rises (amendment-shaped). So it runs the amendment flow, needs
unanimity, *and* needs the designee's explicit transfer-grade consent in the plain language of
[09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) — never framed as an administrative correction.

### Scope: one bargain, one proposal

An amendment is scoped to a ledger, but a single proposal **may span the master and publishing ledgers,
and when it does it is atomic** — both pass or neither. A party accepting a 5% publishing dilution in
exchange for a master share is consenting to a package; letting one half pass alone hands them a deal
they refused. The consent set is then the union of both ledgers' parties.

### Retroactivity

**Settlement is forward-only by default.** The new version governs from the next royalty run; money
already distributed under the old version stays distributed.

A proposal may additionally request a **true-up** — netting the historical difference against future
royalties. The true-up is a **separate consent item on the same proposal**, because agreeing to a new
split going forward is not agreeing to repay money you have already spent. The allocation change can
pass while the true-up fails.

The platform **computes** the true-up figure ("you were paid £X under v1; under v2 you would have been
paid £Y; delta £Z") and this does not contradict [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)
D-02's ban on suggesting shares: distributions already paid are **arithmetic over facts the platform
holds**, not a judgement about who deserves what. What the platform will not do is compel it — there is
no override (D-02), so an unconsented true-up simply does not happen, and pursuing it is a debt matter
between the parties. Recovery routes to [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/); the platform does
not reach into anyone's bank account.

### Sourcing a new share

Making room for an added party requires taking percentage from someone. The proposer chooses the
**dilution basis** — pro rata across all parties, from named parties, or equal — and **the record names
who chose it**. (A dilution basis is a choice about *whose percentage moves*; it is not the payout basis,
which is a platform constant nobody on a sheet chooses — see above.) Pro rata is offered as the neutral
transform because it is the only one that preserves the parties' own
agreed proportions without the platform judging contribution; it is never a silent default and never the
only option (see DT-07). Shares are exact rationals ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)
D-02), so pro-rata dilution of a three-way split introduces no rounding and no crumb for the platform to
quietly assign.

An amendment **cannot produce a 0% row.** Zeroing a party is expressed as removal-by-agreement or as a
work-for-hire designation, because "0% by agreement" and "0% because nobody said" are the same number
with opposite meanings ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) D-02).

### Happy Path

1. A contributor is remembered — the keys player who came in for one afternoon.
2. Any party opens a proposal against the current version. Draft saves immediately; nothing is sent and nobody is notified yet.
3. Proposer adds the keys player at 10% and picks the dilution basis: pro rata across the existing four. The dilution basis, and who chose it, go into the record. The payout basis is not on this screen and is not theirs to pick — it is the platform constant, unchanged by the amendment ([09.02.01 D-16](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)).
4. Before send, the platform states what this amendment touches: 2 active sync licences granted by the v1 parties, 1 filed PRO registration that will diverge, and the next royalty run's date. It notifies; it does not act ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)).
5. Proposer sends. Their own consent is recorded at that timestamp. Every existing party plus the added party is notified.
6. Each party sees the **whole** proposed ledger with their before → after delta: `25% → 22.5%`. The delta is the thing being consented to.
7. The four diluted parties each confirm the decreased figure explicitly. The added party consents in one tap — their share only rises.
8. Throughout, v1 stays `current`. A royalty run that starts on day 3 distributes on v1 and its statement says so.
9. Last consent lands. v2 becomes `current`; v1 becomes `superseded` and stays visible to every party forever.
10. [Domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) distributes on v2 from the next run. The parties are shown that the PRO registration is now stale and that WeJammin cannot refile it for them.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One party refuses | Proposed version stays `proposed` carrying a `refused` row; never becomes current; current version keeps governing. Not terminal — the proposer may withdraw, edit, or leave it standing. | "X declined — the existing split still applies." Not an error state, and copy must not frame it as one. |
| Refusal hardens; proposer keeps resending to wear them down | Edit-and-resend notifies at most 3 times per rolling 7 days per proposal. Further edits save and still reset consents, but do not fan out. | "You've resent this 3 times this week — further changes won't notify again until {date}." Refusers see changes on their dashboard. Route offered: [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md). |
| A party is **unreachable** (moved on, ghosted, left the industry) | No consent → no amendment. Notices at day 0, day 7, day 21 on every channel on file. At day 45 with zero interaction the proposal is badged `blocked`. **The proposal never expires and silence is never converted into consent** ([09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Escalation to [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md) is offered, not automatic. | "Blocked — waiting on X, no response since {date}. The existing split still applies." Unreachability is displayed as a **status**, never as an answer. Whether any escape hatch exists at all is Q-01. |
| A party has **died** | Their estate holds the consent right ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)). Estate verification runs at the higher bar with a notice period ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md) D-02). | "Waiting on the estate of X — probate pending since {date}." Proposal does not expire. |
| Party consented, then died before the others | The consent stands. A consent is a timestamped fact; death does not retract it. | Their row reads `consented {date}` with the death recorded separately. |
| Proposer edits the sheet **after** a consenting party has died | [09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-06 resets every consent — including the dead party's, which can now only be re-given by a verified estate. This is stated **before** the edit commits. | "Editing will clear X's consent. X is deceased — only a verified estate can consent again, which may take months. Continue?" |
| Party died and **no estate** is identified | Collapses into the unreachable case. | Same `blocked` badge. Q-01 / [09 Q-07](../rights-ownership-index.md). |
| Amendment lands mid-royalty-run | Run binds to the version `current` at its start. The new version becomes current immediately for future runs. | Distribution statement names the ledger version and the timestamp it was bound at — otherwise a period gets computed against two splits and nobody can reconcile it. |
| **A sheet agreed under an earlier basis terms version is amended** | The amendment changes the allocation only. It cannot change the payout basis — there is no payout-basis field on a ledger version to amend ([09.02.01 D-16](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)) — and any proposal implying otherwise is describing a platform terms change, which is not an amendment and not in any party's gift. Whether post-amendment sales settle on the terms version the original agreement bound or the one in force at the amended version's consent is **open (Q-07)**; the parties must not be shown a guess. | Deferred — see Q-07. The proposal flow states the allocation delta as usual and asserts nothing about the basis. |
| **A party proposes an amendment to get a different payout basis** — "amend me onto ask-basis" | Not an amendment and not refusable-by-consent either: the payout basis is one platform constant for every sheet on the platform, so there is nothing here to propose against ([09.02.01 D-16](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). The lever the parties actually hold is the percentages. | "The payout basis is a platform rule, the same on every split — it isn't part of this sheet and can't be amended." |
| Retroactive amendment after money has been paid on the old split | Forward-only. A true-up is a **separate consent item**; the platform computes the delta but can never compel it. | "Going forward: v2. Already paid under v1: £X. Request a true-up?" — an explicit second ask. |
| True-up requested; the allocation is consented but one party refuses the true-up | The allocation amendment passes; the true-up does not. Two consents, two independent outcomes. | "New split active. True-up declined by X — settlement is forward-only." |
| Party consents, then withdraws before others do | Withdrawal allowed while the proposal is `open`. | Consent shown as revocable-until-complete. |
| Withdrawal races the final consent | Once the last required consent is recorded the version is `consented` and irrevocable. A withdrawal arriving after that loses. | "This amendment completed before your withdrawal was recorded — propose an amendment to change it." |
| Two parties propose at once | First to send is `open`; the second is `queued` and collects no consent. No merging (D-04). | "A proposal from Y is already open — yours is queued behind it." |
| The open proposal passes; a queued proposal was computed against the old base | The queued proposal is recomputed against the new current version and any consents on it are void. "Dilute pro rata" against 25/25/25/25 is a different sheet from the same instruction against 30/30/20/20. | "The sheet changed underneath this proposal. Here's what your change now does — re-send to collect consent." |
| A party submits against a **stale view** (page open while another proposal landed) | Rejected at submit; the divergence is shown. | "The sheet changed while you were editing" with a v-old → v-new diff. |
| A **transfer** lands mid-proposal ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) | A transfer needs only two parties' consent, so it can complete while an amendment is pending. The share's holder changed: the transferee must now consent, and the transferor's consent **or** refusal does not carry to them. | "X's share transferred to Y. Y hasn't consented yet." |
| Identity merge lands mid-proposal ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — same human, two accounts | Two rows collapse into one; the base version changed; the proposal is invalidated and recomputed. | "Two of these parties turned out to be the same person. Here's the corrected sheet." |
| A party is a **band or studio entity** | The entity consents through its authorised signatory ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)). Membership changes do not re-open the sheet ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-13). | Signatory named on the consent row — not "the band consented", but who signed for it. |
| **The multi-hyphenate holds two rows** — writer share and self-held publisher share, or personal plus loan-out | Two consent items, signed separately, because the capacities can have different beneficial owners. Counter says rows, not humans. | The person appears once with two things to sign: "Sign as writer" and "Sign as {Publisher}". "Waiting on 3 of 5" where 5 is rows. |
| Proposer is also a party | Sending records their consent explicitly at the send timestamp. Editing resets it exactly like everyone else's. | Their own row shows `consented — proposed and signed {timestamp}`. |
| A party's share **decreases** | Their consent requires confirming the decreased figure itself, not a generic tap. Parties whose share is unchanged or rises consent in one action. | "Your share goes 25% → 22.5%. Confirm 22.5%." Friction sits exactly where the harm is. |
| Amendment would set a party to 0% | Not permitted as a 0% row. Expressed as removal-by-agreement (consent required) or as a work-for-hire designation. | "This removes X from the ledger" — not "X: 0%". See [09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) D-02. |
| Someone proposes amending a party **into** work-for-hire | Rights transfer, not a correction. Union of both consent sets (see the boundary table); designee's explicit consent in plain language. | "This removes your ownership of the recording" — never "administrative update". See CX-04. |
| Amendment **adds a party** to a work with active licences ([domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/)) | The added party did not grant those licences. In some territories joint owners may license non-exclusively and merely account to each other; in others the licence is defective without them. The platform **flags the exposure** to the added party and to the licensees and adjudicates nothing ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)). | To the added party: "This work has 2 active licences you did not grant." To the licensee: "An owner was added to a work you licensed." |
| Work is already registered externally (PRO / MLC / Copyright Office — [09.06.03](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.03-copyright-office-registration.md)) | Registrations are now stale. The platform surfaces which ones will diverge **before** send and notifies after; it never silently refiles ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Registries also accept coarser precision than an exact rational, so a registrable restatement may differ from the agreed figure. | "3 external registrations will no longer match this sheet. WeJammin can't update them for you." Stated before anyone signs, because this is the amendment's real cost. |
| A share is **frozen / escrowed** ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)) | Freeze is share-scoped, never work-scoped ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md) D-01). An amendment **not touching** the frozen share proceeds normally. An amendment **touching** it needs consent from **every claimant** to that share, not only the current record holder. | "This share is contested — both X and Y must consent to change it." |
| Amendment is the outcome of a resolved dispute | Applied as a consented amendment, never a unilateral write (CX-06). There is no dispute-resolution path into the ledger that bypasses consent. | Standard flow; the case reference is attached to the version. |
| A **court order** directs a change to the ledger | The platform records the order as evidence and notifies; it does not execute (see DT-04). Where the order names **WeJammin itself**, compelled compliance is recorded as a `court-ordered` version with the order attached — never as consent, never silently. | "A court order affecting this sheet has been filed by X." If the parties do not comply, the record shows the order **and** the ledger contradicting it — which is evidence of non-compliance, not a broken product. |
| A **former** party (removed by an earlier amendment) wants to propose | Standing to propose extends to any party on **any version in the chain**, not only the current one — otherwise a wrongly-removed party is locked out of the exact mechanism that would restore them (DT-06). | Their proposal is ordinary and unanimity still applies. |
| A former party proposes repeatedly | The same 3-notifications-per-7-days cap. Unanimous refusal routes to [09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md), not to re-proposal. | "X's proposal was declined by all parties." |
| An **added party refuses** the share offered to them | Nothing changes. The refusal is retained as a fact: someone proposed X was an owner and X said no. | "X declined a proposed 10% share on {date}" — kept permanently. It is evidence, and it protects the remaining parties later. |
| Amendment changes labels/roles only — zero percentage delta (writer → writer/producer) | Still a new version, still full consent: role affects points, credits, and standing. | "No percentage changes" stated explicitly, so nobody signs fearing they are losing money — or signs carelessly because they aren't. |
| Amendment proposed to a sheet that never locked (`proposed`, not `consented`) | Not an amendment — that is still capture. Routes back to [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md). | Edit in place; no version created. |
| Network failure while consenting | The consent is **never** shown as recorded until acknowledged. An optimistic success state here would fabricate a signature. | "Not recorded — retry." Never a green tick. |
| Duplicate submit (double-tap, retry after timeout) | Idempotent — one consent, one timestamp. | Unchanged state; no second row. |
| Objection / reason text is abusive | It is user content on a channel the recipient cannot block. Capped at 1,000 characters; reportable to [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/); it cannot be **deleted** (it is part of the record) but it can be **hidden pending review**, and the fact of the hiding is recorded. | "This message is hidden pending review." The record shows something was said and was hidden — not that nothing was said. |
| A party deletes their account (GDPR erasure) | The rights record survives — a split sheet is the establishment and defence of legal claims, which is the exemption that applies. Deletion offers conversion to a **rights-only record**: no profile, no discovery, still a consenting party with a contact channel. Declining a channel puts them in the unreachable case. | "You'll stay on 4 split sheets. We need a way to reach you when they change." Routes to [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) / [24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/); the GDPR/CCPA compliance item is still uninterviewed in [meta/constraints.md](../../meta/constraints.md). |
| Ensemble-scale sheet (14 parties — orchestra, choir) | Unanimity across 14 parties is a practical freeze; amendment inherits capture's scaling problem wholesale. Not solved here, because the bulk path must be designed at capture or the sheet is unamendable by construction. | Tracked at [09.02.01 Q-03](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md), which owns the ensemble path. |
| Proposal sits open for years | Does not expire. A stale proposal is still evidence of what was proposed and when. | "Proposed by Y in March 2026 — waiting on 2 of 5." Visible on every party's dashboard indefinitely. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Version history or a proposal requested | Skeleton of the version timeline |
| Empty | No amendments; the original stands | "No changes since March 2026" — a positive signal, not an absence. The sheet has held. |
| Populated | One or more superseded versions | Version timeline: each version's date, what changed per party (before → after), who proposed, who consented, when, and the basis they chose for any dilution |
| Partial | Proposal `open`, awaiting consent | "Amendment pending — waiting on 2 of 5. Current split still applies." Named rows, per-row state (`pending` / `consented` / `refused`), chase action, and the proposer's own signature timestamp |
| Queued | A second proposal sent while one is `open` | "Queued behind Y's proposal — this collects no signatures yet, and will be recomputed if Y's passes." |
| Blocked | 45 days with zero interaction from a required consenter, or an estate awaiting probate | "Blocked — waiting on X, no response since {date}. The existing split still applies." Escalation offered, never automatic. Never resolves itself into consent. |
| Error | Write failed / consent not acknowledged / stale base version | Inline retry; the proposal and any entered figures are preserved (capture data is unrepeatable — [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) D-05). A stale base shows the diff rather than a generic failure. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A consented split should be **immutable** — that is the whole value proposition | The evidentiary framing taken to its logical end | ❌ REJECTED — immutability of the *record* is the value; immutability of the *agreement* is a bug. Splits legitimately change: late contributors, honest errors, declared samples, publisher assignments, deaths. A system that cannot represent change forces the change to happen **off-platform**, in a side letter or a verbal understanding — which is precisely the reconstruction-from-memory failure the domain exists to eliminate, now reintroduced by the platform's own rigidity. The resolution is versioning: the *history* is immutable, the *current agreement* is not. Get this wrong and users route around the product at the exact moment it matters most. |
| DT-02 | A platform admin should be able to **force** an amendment to unstick a frozen sheet | Operational pragmatism — support tickets will demand it, and the unreachable-party case is genuinely painful | ❌ REJECTED — an override capability is the single highest-value attack surface in the domain. It is what a social-engineering attack targets, what a hostile party's lawyer subpoenas, and what an insider abuses. Its existence also silently changes the *meaning* of every sheet on the platform: a record that the operator can rewrite is not evidence of what the parties agreed, it is evidence of what the operator currently says they agreed. No override. The frozen-sheet problem must be solved by a mechanism that does not require the platform to be trusted with unilateral power over ownership (see Q-01). *This annotation is now the cited source of the domain's spine, [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md).* |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect **"what happens when a party is unreachable"** to be answered, and no candidate touches it | The candidate list has no amendment concept at all, so it certainly has no dead-party concept. But this is not exotic: musicians leave the industry, change names, ghost, and die. The industry's existing answer is "you can't fix it, so the catalogue stays broken" — which is a large part of why the black box exists at all. | ⚠️ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap, **still unsolved but now narrowed from three options to two.** Deemed-consent-after-notice is **foreclosed**, not merely distasteful: it renders silence as an answer, which [09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md) prohibits on ratified grounds — and it would manufacture the exact evidentiary garbage the domain exists to replace, against the party least able to object. That leaves arbitration via [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/) (legitimate, slow, expensive, and the strongest live challenge to [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)) or permanent freeze ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)) (honest, and it helps nobody get paid). The **default behaviour is specified** (indefinite `blocked`, never deemed consent); what remains open is whether an escape hatch exists at all. Raised as Q-01 rather than guessed — a fabricated answer here would propagate into the spec as though it were a decision. |
| DT-04 | A **court order** should be executed against the ledger — the platform reads the order and writes the corrected split | The obvious operational reading: a court has spoken, the record should reflect it, and refusing looks like the platform obstructing justice | ❌ REJECTED for orders directed at **parties**, ✅ CONFIRMED narrowly for orders directed at **WeJammin**. Reading an order and deciding what it means for a ledger is *interpretation*, and [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) is explicit: arithmetic executes, interpretation notifies. An order against a party is enforceable by the court against that party — they execute the amendment. If they refuse, the honest record is the ledger **plus the attached order contradicting it plus the names of who refused**, which is *stronger* evidence than a rewritten sheet: it is proof of non-compliance. A platform that quietly rewrites splits on receipt of paperwork it read itself has rebuilt DT-02's override and wrapped it in a legal-looking envelope — and forged orders become the attack. Where an order names WeJammin itself, compliance is compelled, and the version is recorded as `court-ordered`, never as `consented`, never silently. The distinction is not pedantry: it is the difference between "the platform never chooses to override" and "the platform overrides when it decides the paperwork is good enough". See Q-05. |
| DT-05 | Consent, once given, survives edits to the proposal — otherwise every typo restarts the whole collection | Efficiency; the collection is the slow part and the friction will be complained about loudly | ❌ REJECTED, and the sibling already settled it ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-06/D-09). Carrying a signature across a changed sheet is a forgery with good intentions. The *specific* consequence this feature must own is the one the general rule does not name: **a consent that resets may be unobtainable**. If a consenting party has died, or gone unreachable, a one-character edit converts a nearly-complete amendment into a permanently blocked one. The rule stands; the design obligation is to state that cost **before** the edit commits, naming the party and the reason, not to weaken the rule. |
| DT-06 | Only parties on the **current** version may propose an amendment | It follows from CX-03's "any party to the ledger may propose", and it is the natural reading | ❌ REJECTED — this reading contains a designed-in trap nobody would notice until it hurt someone. If standing is scoped to the current version, then a party **removed by an amendment is locked out of the mechanism that would restore them**. A wrongly-removed contributor — removed by error, or removed by a retroactive WFH they consented to under a misunderstanding — has exactly one sanctioned route back, and this reading closes it, leaving them only the dispute path, which is slower and adversarial by construction. Standing must extend to any party appearing on **any version in the chain**. The griefing risk this opens (a bought-out session player proposing forever) is handled by the notification cap and by unanimity, both of which cost the proposer far more than they cost the refusers. |
| DT-07 | Pro-rata dilution is the obvious neutral default and should simply be applied | It preserves everyone's relative proportions and requires no judgement | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with the correction that matters. Pro rata is the right *offer* — it is the only allocation that changes nobody's agreed proportions relative to each other. But applying it as a silent default would breach [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) D-02 in spirit: it puts the platform's authority behind a number and anchors the negotiation. And it is frequently **wrong on the facts** — when the keys player's 10% was omitted because one person took a share that was not theirs, pro rata makes the innocent parties pay for it equally. The resolution is that pro rata is offered as an arithmetic transform, the proposer picks the dilution basis, and **the record names who chose it**. A human owns the choice; the platform owns the arithmetic. |
| DT-08 | The expensive part of an amendment is collecting the consent | The whole feature looks like a signature-collection problem | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the industry expectation nobody in the candidate list has: **the expensive part is everything downstream of the signature.** A real amendment invalidates filed PRO/MLC registrations, contradicts sync licences already granted, and desynchronises every distributor and administrator holding the old split. This is the actual reason splits go unamended in the real world — not that people can't be reached, but that the person who noticed the error knows fixing it means twenty emails to entities that may not answer. A tool that makes signatures easy and hides the wreckage produces a platform whose ledger is beautifully correct and matches nothing that pays anybody. Hence D-11: the downstream damage is enumerated **before** the proposal is sent, not discovered after. It also means the industry's real amendment instrument — the letter of direction — is a live gap (Q-06). |
| DT-09 | An amendment that adds a party is a clean, additive operation | Adding someone who was always a contributor feels like pure correction — nobody is harmed except by dilution, which they consent to | ❌ REJECTED — it is the operation with the largest blast radius outside the domain. **The added party did not grant the licences the previous owners granted.** A sync licence cleared from four owners now has a fifth who never signed it, and whether that licence is merely accountable-to or outright defective is territory-dependent. So an "additive" amendment can retroactively break a licensee's clearance and expose the parties to a claim from the person they just did the right thing by. The platform cannot resolve this ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)) but concealing it would be worse than useless: it must tell the added party what they are joining and tell the licensees what changed under them. Routed to [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/). |
| DT-10 | The abuse to design against is a **forced** amendment | DT-02's framing — the override, the coerced signature | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — force is covered, but the attack that actually works against a unanimity model is **attrition**. There is no override to exploit, so the rational bad actor edits and resends until the holdout signs to make it stop, which is coercion assembled entirely from legitimate operations. Notification is the weapon, so notification is what is rate-limited (3 notified resends per rolling 7 days per proposal) — while [09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-05's "save is never gated" is preserved, because blocking the edit itself would be the wrong lever and would break the drafting path. Refusal must also stay costless and non-terminal: a refusal that closes the proposal would teach parties that refusing is the aggressive act. It isn't. |
| DT-11 | A retroactive settlement is the platform's job once the split is corrected | It is arithmetic the platform can do, and the money flowed through the platform | ❌ REJECTED as an obligation, ✅ CONFIRMED as a computation. Consenting to a new split going forward and consenting to repay money you have already spent are **two different asks**, and bundling them is how a fair correction becomes a bill nobody expected. Hence: separate consent item, independent outcome. The platform computes the delta — that is arithmetic over distributions it already holds, not a judgement of desert, so [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) D-02 is not engaged — but it cannot compel it, because there is no override (D-02 here). An unconsented true-up simply does not happen and becomes a debt between the parties. See Q-02. |
| DT-12 | Cross-ledger amendments can be two independent proposals — one for master, one for publishing | Ledgers are separate ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-10 is explicit that "200%" is two pools), so the amendments should be too | ❌ REJECTED — separate pools, but not necessarily separate *bargains*. Real amendments trade across the two: "take 5% of my publishing and I'll give you a point of the master" is an ordinary conversation. Two independent proposals means one half can pass while the other fails, leaving a party bound to a deal they explicitly did not agree to — and the platform would have constructed that outcome itself. So a proposal may span both ledgers and is **atomic** when it does, with the consent set as the union. This does not contradict [09.02 D-04](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md) (capture must write both, never one): capture's rule prevents deferral; this rule prevents a package being torn in half. |
| DT-13 | Consent is per human — one person, one signature | The natural reading of "every party must consent", and simpler to explain | ❌ REJECTED — it breaks on `meta/personas.md`'s declared structural fact, the multi-hyphenate. One human routinely holds two rows on the same sheet: their writer share and their self-held publisher share ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-03), or a personal row and their loan-out company's. Those capacities can have **different beneficial owners** — sign away the publisher row and you may be signing something your publisher, not you, controls. Collapsing them into one signature is a per-row consent failure wearing per-human clothing. Consent is per row; the interface shows the human once with two things to sign; the counter counts rows. The same logic makes proposing distinct from consenting — the proposer's own row still signs, explicitly and timestamped. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — **binding**: distribution must bind to a ledger *version* at run start, or a period is computed against two splits and is unreconcilable. It must also resolve a **second, independent version dimension** — the platform basis terms version the split agreement was made under ([09.02.01 D-17](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)) — and which of the two an amended sheet reads is Q-07. **Trigger dependency**: a consented true-up is netted against future royalties by 10, which is also where any recovery attempt lives. The platform never reaches into a bank account.
- Touches **20 Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan** ([path](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)) — **constraint inherited, nothing authored.** The payout basis a D2F sale applies these percentages to is a platform-wide constant, not a property of any ledger version ([20.04.04 D-10 / D-11](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md)), so amendment neither versions it nor can be used to vary it. The versioned platform-terms record it depends on is unmodelled anywhere in the tree — [20.04.04 Q-08](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md), at `/create-prd-architecture`.
- Touches **11 Music Licensing** ([path](../../11-music-licensing/)) — **permission/standing**: an added party did not grant the licences the prior owners granted (DT-09). An amendment can retroactively hole a licensee's clearance. Fan-out is bidirectional: notify the added party and every active licensee. Whether the licence survives is territory-dependent and the platform does not rule on it ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)).
- Touches **09.06.03 Copyright Office Registration** ([path](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.03-copyright-office-registration.md)) and the wider **09.06** public record — **stale-state dependency**: an amendment desynchronises every external filing. Registries also accept coarser precision than the ledger's exact rationals ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-02), so the registrable restatement may not equal the agreed figure. Notify, never refile silently ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). This is the amendment's real cost (DT-08).
- Touches **09.03.01 Chain of Title** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) — **boundary + race**: the amendment/transfer line is resolved in the Behavior section (different consent sets, different homes). The race is live: a transfer needs only two parties, so it can complete while an amendment is pending and silently change who must consent.
- Touches **09.03.04 Estate & Succession** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)) — **shared entity**: a dead party's consent right passes to a verified estate. That feature names this one's central problem as the thing it unblocks; the reverse dependency is that with no estate, amendment has no consenter at all.
- Touches **09.04 Rights Conflicts & Disputes** ([path](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)) — **bidirectional**: a hardened refusal becomes a dispute (09.04.02); a resolved dispute returns as a consented amendment and never as a unilateral write ([rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md) CX-06). **State conflict**: a frozen share (09.04.03) needs every *claimant's* consent, not the record holder's, and freeze is share-scoped so an amendment elsewhere on the sheet proceeds.
- Touches **09.02.03 Work-for-Hire** ([path](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)) — **union-of-consent-sets**: retroactive WFH is transfer-shaped and amendment-shaped at once and takes both consent requirements (CX-04). Also supplies the "0% by agreement vs 0% because nobody said" rule that forbids a 0% row here.
- Touches **09.01.02 Ownership Ledger Validation** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — **substrate**: whole-ledger consent (D-01), exact rationals (D-02), self-held publisher shares (D-03), edit-resets-consent (D-06), consent-binds-to-version (D-09), entities never flattened (D-13). Amendment is that model's second act; it invents nothing and inherits everything.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity + race**: entity parties sign via an authorised signatory; an identity merge mid-proposal collapses two rows and invalidates the proposal; an account deletion must leave a rights-only record with a contact channel or create an unreachable party.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **moderation + arbitration**: objection text is unblockable user content on a channel the recipient did not choose (hide-pending-review, never delete); and 24 holds the only live candidate escape hatch for Q-01.
- Touches **09.01.06 Sample & Interpolation Provenance** ([path](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)) — **trigger dependency**: a declared interpolation dilutes the ledger and fires this flow, and it is the canonical source of the retroactivity question shared with Q-02.
- Touches **meta/constraints.md** ([path](../../meta/constraints.md)) — the GDPR/CCPA compliance line is still uninterviewed, and the erasure-vs-rights-record tension resolved here depends on it.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Amendment **supersedes**, never mutates | The history is the evidence. A rewritable ledger is worth what the memory it replaced was worth (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | **No admin override**, ever | An override is the domain's highest-value attack surface, and its existence changes what every sheet on the platform *means* (see DT-02). Cited as the source of [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md). |
| D-03 | The **current version keeps governing** while an amendment is pending | Money must not stop because someone opened a proposal. |
| D-04 | Concurrent proposals **queue**, never merge; exactly one may be `open` and collect consent | Merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed. A queued proposal is recomputed against the new base when the open one passes, because the same instruction means a different sheet against a different base. |
| D-05 | Amendments show **before/after deltas** per party, not just new numbers | The delta is what is being consented to; new numbers alone hide the ask. |
| D-06 | **Amendment ≠ transfer**: "was the sheet wrong, or did the world change?" — different consent sets, different homes | Resolves Q-04. A transfer dressed as an amendment gives co-owners a veto over a share that is not theirs; an amendment dressed as a transfer strips everyone's consent right. Where an operation has both shapes it takes the **union** of both consent sets, never the lesser (CX-04). |
| D-07 | **Silence is never consent.** An unreachable party leaves the proposal `blocked` indefinitely; unreachability is a status, never an answer | Deemed consent is foreclosed by [09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md), not merely disliked. It manufactures a statement nobody made, against the party least able to object (see DT-03). |
| D-08 | Standing to propose extends to any party on **any version in the chain**, not only the current one | Otherwise a wrongly-removed party is locked out of the only mechanism that could restore them (see DT-06). |
| D-09 | Consent is **per row, not per human**, and a party whose share **decreases** must confirm the decreased figure explicitly | The multi-hyphenate holds two rows with potentially different beneficial owners (DT-13). Friction belongs exactly where the harm is — one tap where nothing is lost, deliberate confirmation where something is. |
| D-10 | The abuse model is **attrition, not force**; notification is rate-limited (3 notified resends per rolling 7 days per proposal), saving never is | With no override to exploit, the working attack is resending until the holdout signs to make it stop. Rate-limit the weapon, not the draft ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-05) — and keep refusal costless and non-terminal (see DT-10). |
| D-11 | **Downstream damage is enumerated before the proposal is sent** — active licences, filed registrations, in-flight runs | The signature is the cheap part; the wreckage is the reason real splits go unamended (see DT-08). Notify, never execute ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |
| D-12 | Settlement is **forward-only**; a true-up is a **separate consent item** the platform computes but can never compel | Consenting to a new split going forward is not consenting to repay spent money (see DT-11). Computing a delta over distributions already made is arithmetic, not a judgement of desert. |
| D-13 | A cross-ledger proposal is **atomic** — master and publishing pass together or not at all | Amendments trade across the two pools. Splitting a package hands a party a deal they refused, constructed by the platform (see DT-12). |
| D-14 | An amendment **cannot produce a 0% row**; zeroing a party is removal-by-agreement or a WFH designation | Same number, opposite meanings ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) D-02). A 0% row would be the removal-without-consent loophole. |
| D-15 | A **court order** is recorded and notified, never executed against the ledger by the platform — except where the order names WeJammin itself, which is compelled compliance recorded as `court-ordered`, never as consent | Reading an order and deciding what it means is interpretation, and interpretation notifies ([09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). A platform that rewrites splits on paperwork it graded itself has rebuilt the override (see DT-04). |
| D-16 | An amendment **versions the allocation, never the payout basis** — the basis is a platform-wide constant, not a field on any ledger version, so no proposal can change it and none may offer to | Owner ratification of DQ-19.1 / DQ-19.2 / DQ-19.3, 2026-07-22 ([09.02.01 D-16 / D-17](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). The basis is disclosed by being **printed at capture** rather than captured as a field, which is what makes the pre-agreement disclosure obligation true for sheets agreed years before any listing exists. The consequence for this feature is narrow and almost entirely a removal: no basis row to dilute, no basis lock to trigger, no cross-basis package to make atomic (D-13 is unaffected — it governs master-vs-publishing, not basis). What it does add is a **second version dimension** over a settled sheet — ledger version and basis terms version — whose interaction with supersession is Q-07. |
