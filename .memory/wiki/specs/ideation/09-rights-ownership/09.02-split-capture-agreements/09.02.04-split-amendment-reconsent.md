# Feature: Split Amendment & Re-Consent

> **Number**: 09.02.04
> **Parent**: [Split Capture & Agreements](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The sanctioned path to change a split that has already been signed. Splits do not stay still: a
contributor is added months later, a percentage was entered wrong, a sample gets declared and dilutes
everyone, a writer signs a publisher, someone dies. A ledger that cannot be corrected is not immutable
— it is **wrong forever**, and worse than no ledger at all, because people trust it.

Amendment's central property is **supersession, not mutation**: the original consented sheet remains a
permanent, retrievable fact and the amendment is a new version pointing at it. The product is a record
whose value rests entirely on it not being quietly rewritable; a mutable ledger is worth exactly as much
as the memory it was built to replace.

**This is a Deep Think addition — it was not on the candidate list.** The candidates model capture as a
one-shot event. Real splits are amended constantly, and the amendment is where the abuse lives.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Proposes amendments (adding a forgotten contributor, correcting their own share) and consents to others' proposals. Sees every historical version of any sheet they are on — who proposed each change, when, and what it did to their share. Can refuse; refusal is not a veto to be worn down but a first-class outcome that leaves the existing sheet governing. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the proposer, because they are usually the one who notices. Cannot amend unilaterally, and cannot amend *around* a party by removing them first — removal is itself an amendment requiring that party's consent. |
| Operator | None | No stake in a split, therefore no stake in amending one. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

Amendment produces a **new version** of the ledger. The prior version becomes `superseded` and is
retained permanently and retrievably. The chain of versions *is* the evidentiary record — a split sheet
that shows it was 25/25/25/25 in March, became 30/30/20/20 in June with everyone's consent, and who
asked for that, is far stronger evidence than one that only shows today's numbers.

Critical timing property: **the current version keeps governing while an amendment is pending.** Money
must not stop because someone opened a proposal. Distribution binds to the version that was `current`
at the start of the royalty run (see
[09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) CX-03).

Consent rules:

- **Every party on the current version** must consent, plus any party being added.
- **No admin override.** If the platform could force an amendment through, that capability would be the attack surface for the entire domain — and the first thing an attacker or a subpoena would reach for.
- Concurrent proposals **queue**; they do not merge. Merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed.

### Happy Path

1. A contributor is remembered — the keys player who came in for one afternoon.
2. Any party proposes an amendment: add them, and dilute everyone pro rata to make room.
3. The proposal shows every party their *before* and *after* share, not just the new numbers. The delta is the thing being consented to.
4. Every existing party plus the new one consents.
5. New version becomes `current`; prior becomes `superseded` and stays visible forever.
6. [Domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) distributes on the new version from the next run.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One party refuses | Amendment fails; current version stands and keeps governing. | "X declined — the existing split is unchanged." Not an error state. |
| A party is **unreachable** (moved on, ghosted, no longer in the industry) | The hardest case. No consent → no amendment → the sheet is frozen, possibly forever, possibly wrongly. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. See Q-01 — this is the feature's central unsolved problem. |
| A party has **died** | Their estate holds the consent right ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)). | Routed to the estate; if no estate is identified, this collapses into the unreachable case. |
| Amendment lands mid-royalty-run | Run binds to the version current at its start. | Distribution statement names the sheet version it used — otherwise a period gets computed against two splits and nobody can reconcile it. |
| Retroactive amendment after money has already been paid on the old split | Forward-only by default; clawback is a separate, explicit decision. | `[PENDING]` — see [09.01.06 Q-02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md). |
| Someone proposes amending a party **into** work-for-hire | Rights transfer, not a correction. Explicit consent from that party; framed as what it is. | "This removes your ownership of the recording" — never "administrative update". See CX-04. |
| Party consents, then withdraws before others do | Withdrawal allowed while the version is `proposed`. Once `current`, it is an amendment like any other. | Consent shown as revocable-until-complete. |
| Amendment proposed to a sheet that never locked (`proposed`, not `consented`) | Not an amendment — that is still capture. Routes back to [09.02.01](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md). | Edit in place; no version created. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A consented split should be **immutable** — that is the whole value proposition | The evidentiary framing taken to its logical end | ❌ REJECTED — immutability of the *record* is the value; immutability of the *agreement* is a bug. Splits legitimately change: late contributors, honest errors, declared samples, publisher assignments, deaths. A system that cannot represent change forces the change to happen **off-platform**, in a side letter or a verbal understanding — which is precisely the reconstruction-from-memory failure the domain exists to eliminate, now reintroduced by the platform's own rigidity. The resolution is versioning: the *history* is immutable, the *current agreement* is not. Get this wrong and users route around the product at the exact moment it matters most. |
| DT-02 | A platform admin should be able to **force** an amendment to unstick a frozen sheet | Operational pragmatism — support tickets will demand it, and the unreachable-party case is genuinely painful | ❌ REJECTED — an override capability is the single highest-value attack surface in the domain. It is what a social-engineering attack targets, what a hostile party's lawyer subpoenas, and what an insider abuses. Its existence also silently changes the *meaning* of every sheet on the platform: a record that the operator can rewrite is not evidence of what the parties agreed, it is evidence of what the operator currently says they agreed. No override. The frozen-sheet problem must be solved by a mechanism that does not require the platform to be trusted with unilateral power over ownership (see Q-01). |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect **"what happens when a party is unreachable"** to be answered, and no candidate touches it | The candidate list has no amendment concept at all, so it certainly has no dead-party concept. But this is not exotic: musicians leave the industry, change names, ghost, and die. The industry's existing answer is "you can't fix it, so the catalogue stays broken" — which is a large part of why the black box exists at all. | ⚠️ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap, **unsolved and honestly flagged**. Candidate directions (all with real problems): a notice-period-plus-deemed-consent model (fast, and it manufactures consent that was never given — exactly the evidentiary garbage we reject); arbitration via [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/) (legitimate, slow, expensive); or freeze-and-escrow ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)) (honest, and it helps nobody get paid). Raised as Q-01 rather than guessed — a fabricated answer here would propagate into the spec as though it were a decision. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Amendment **supersedes**, never mutates | The history is the evidence. A rewritable ledger is worth what the memory it replaced was worth (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | **No admin override**, ever | An override is the domain's highest-value attack surface, and its existence changes what every sheet on the platform *means* (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | The **current version keeps governing** while an amendment is pending | Money must not stop because someone opened a proposal. |
| D-04 | Concurrent proposals **queue**, never merge | Merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed. |
| D-05 | Amendments show **before/after deltas** per party, not just new numbers | The delta is what is being consented to; new numbers alone hide the ask. |
