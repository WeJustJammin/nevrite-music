# Feature: Moment-of-Creation Split Capture

> **Number**: 09.02.01
> **Parent**: [Split Capture & Agreements](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

**The thesis feature.** Everything in D-18 reduces to this screen: the platform is in the room because
of consolidation, and being in the room is what lets it ask "who did what, and what's the split?" while
everyone is present, friendly, and remembers. That question costs one signature today and a lawsuit in
three years. No competitor can retroactively manufacture the answer, because none of them were there.

The feature captures both ledgers (master and publishing), routes each contributor to
share / fee / present-not-a-party, collects consent from every ledger party, and locks the result. Its
design constraint is unusual and absolute: **it must be faster than not doing it.** `meta/personas.md`
names the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as closing a project without capturing splits —
committed by the persona best placed to prevent it — and concludes: *the design must make the lazy path
the correct path.*

**Design budget** (the constraint made testable): a 4-contributor sheet must be completable by the
Producer in **≤ 8 interactions and ≤ 90 s** from prompt to sent, and consent must cost each contributor
**≤ 2 interactions** (open → confirm). The comparison is not against doing nothing — it is against the
honest alternative (a PDF split sheet plus weeks of chasing signatures), which costs hours. Any
proposed addition to this flow is measured against the 8/90 budget; if it does not fit, it is not
capture, it is paperwork, and paperwork is what this feature exists to defeat.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The contributor. Two distinct positions, and the difference is load-bearing. **As a ledger party**: receives the sheet on their phone while still in the room, sees the **whole** ledger — every party, every percentage, both ledgers, not just their own line ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) D-01) — and consents or objects in one tap. **As a fee party** ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)): sees the ledger's *shape* (who the parties are, that they are not one of them, what they are paid, what they keep) but not the parties' percentages — they are consenting to a fee, not to a share (D-09). Objecting is a first-class action, not a hidden one: an objection while everyone is present is cheap; the same objection in three years is litigation. Can also initiate capture themselves — the Producer is the *usual* driver, not the required one. |
| Producer | Full | The capture point. Prompted at session close from the session's participant list ([domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)) — never a blank form. Routes each participant to share / fee / present-not-a-party, enters percentages, sends for consent. Sees per-party consent state and can chase from the same screen. **Cannot consent on anyone's behalf**, cannot mark the sheet done while it is `proposed`, and cannot see who has *opened* the sheet without acting (a read receipt turns a chase into a pressure tactic). Their own proposal records their consent to the version they proposed — and is invalidated by any subsequent edit, including their own (D-07). |
| Operator | None | A venue or studio operator is not a party to a split by virtue of operating the room. The studio's engineer may be captured (as a Musician or Producer); the studio entity enters only via a work-for-hire it holds ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), which is a different feature with its own Config-level Operator surface. Explicit absence — **build no Operator surface here**. Note the domain Role Matrix grants Operator ⚙️ Config at 09.02 level; that grant is entirely spent on 09.02.03 and reaches zero at this feature. |
| Fan | None | No visibility, including for works they own or have paid for. Splits are commercially sensitive and fans have no stake. This is the single clearest "different product wearing the same brand" boundary in the domain. A Fan who is also a hobbyist musician sees this feature only when they are a participant in a session — i.e. through the Musician lens, never the Fan one. |

> **The multi-hyphenate case is the normal case here, not an edge.** One human at one session is
> routinely the producer, a performer, and a co-writer. They appear on the sheet **once as a person and
> once per contribution** — production (points, [09.02.02](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md)),
> performance (performer record, [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)),
> writing (publishing ledger share). Designations scope to a **contribution**, never a person
> ([09.02.02 D-04](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md), [09.02.03 D-04](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)),
> so the capture surface groups rows by person but designates per contribution. A UI presenting one row
> per human cannot express the most common person in the room.

## Behavior

Capture is triggered by an event in the work — a session closing, a stem accepted, a project marked
complete — not by a user deciding to go and file paperwork. **Nobody opens an app to file a split
sheet** (D-18); the flow must arrive at them.

The flow, per contributor: *were you here?* → *share, fee, or neither?* → *how much?* → *consent from
every ledger party*. Both ledgers are prompted in one pass ([09.02 D-04](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md))
— letting the Producer complete the master and defer publishing would let the flow report success while
producing the exact failure the domain exists to prevent.

**The subject is the work, not the session.** A session is the trigger; a work is what gets a sheet. A
four-song session prompts four sheets, because four songs have four splits and collapsing them into one
would fabricate an agreement nobody made. The friction is mitigated by carry-forward (D-06), not by
merging.

**Two triggers, two subjects** (DT-08). "The moment of creation" is not a moment for asynchronously
assembled work:

| Trigger | Subject | What is captured | Consent required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution delivered / stem accepted | The contribution | Who, what, when — facts the platform already holds | No — a fact, not a claim |
| Work marked complete / session closed | The work | The negotiated ledger, pre-populated from the accumulated contribution record | Yes — every ledger party |

Facts accrete continuously and cost nothing. The ledger locks once. This is the same line DT-02 draws
between *presence* (a fact the platform holds) and *share* (a judgement it does not).

The output is a `consented` ledger in [09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md),
plus a performer record in [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md),
plus credits in [domain 02](../../02-credits-attribution/) — three records, one prompt. That ratio is
the feature's whole design goal.

### The consent chase — concretely

This feature's requirement on the **Consent Chase & Escalating Notification** cross-cut
([../rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md)). The sheet **never expires** — a stale proposal
is still evidence of what was proposed and when.

| Elapsed | Action |
|---|---|
| T+0 | Push/SMS/email to every ledger party, in the room |
| T+1d, T+3d, T+7d, T+14d, T+30d | Escalating reminder to non-responders only; the proposer is shown who has not responded |
| Months 2–12 | Monthly |
| Year 2+ | Quarterly, indefinitely |
| Always | Visible on every party's dashboard as "Waiting on N of M since {date}" |

Any party may **mute their own chase**. Muting is not consenting, and not muting is not consenting —
but muting **is recorded on the sheet**, because "chased nine times, muted at reminder four" and "never
received the message" are materially different evidence, and the difference is exactly what gets argued
about later.

### Happy Path

1. Producer marks the session complete in [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/). The
   session delivered one candidate recording, so one sheet is prompted. (A session that delivered no
   artefact prompts nothing — see edge cases.)
2. The platform presents the session's participants — already known, because the platform booked the
   room, paid the engineer, and hosted the stems. **This is consolidation buying provenance.** Every
   participant arrives **undesignated**; nobody is pre-placed in the ledger.
3. Producer routes each participant to **share** / **fee** / **present-not-a-party** — a positive act
   for each, three named outcomes, no default. Suggested designations come from the session's known
   facts (who was booked as what, who is listed as a writer, what the engagement's terms said in
   [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/)) — never from an algorithm guessing at creative
   contribution.
4. Producer enters percentages for the share parties, on both ledgers. `Split equally` is available as
   a one-tap tool the Producer chooses; it is never pre-applied (DT-09). The arithmetic verdict comes
   from [09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) — this feature
   surfaces it, does not own it.
5. Producer sends. Sending records the Producer's own consent to the version they proposed (D-07).
6. Every ledger party gets a notification on their phone, in the room. Each sees the full sheet — both
   ledgers, every party, every number. Fee parties get the fee-consent flow instead
   ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), showing the ledger's shape without its percentages.
7. Each consents in one tap, or objects with a reason.
8. All ledger parties consented → ledger locks, credits publish, performer records confirm, and the
   sheet becomes exportable as a signed human-readable document (DT-11). The record is complete on
   day one.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A contributor objects | Sheet stays `proposed`; nothing locks. Objection routed to the Producer with the reason. | "X objected: *reason*." A **success** state for the platform — the disagreement surfaced while it was cheap. Copy must not frame it as an error, must not use error styling, and must not offer "dismiss". |
| An objection reason is abusive or defamatory | The **objection is a fact and stands**; the **reason text is content** and is reportable to [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety/). Removing the text never removes the objection, and never unblocks the lock. | Objection reasons are visible to ledger parties only, with a report action. If removed: "X objected. Reason removed following a report." The sheet stays `proposed`. |
| Contributor has no WeJammin account | Invited via email/SMS; consents via a signed link without a full account (D-04), against an identity stub in [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/). If they later create an account with that address, the consent attaches to the claimed identity — it is not re-collected. | "Waiting on 1 of 4 — invite sent." |
| **The proposer controls the invite address** — self-consent via a link they receive themselves | The forgery vector opened by D-04. The platform cannot prevent it, so it must **not flatten it**: the consent record permanently carries how the consent arrived (in-account authenticated / signed link to `j***@gmail.com`), and every party sees that distinction on the sheet, forever (D-08). Strength of consent is a fact the sheet carries, not a thing the platform launders. | Each consent row is badged "consented in-account" or "consented via emailed link to j***@gmail.com". Never a bare green tick. |
| Producer closes the session and skips capture | The flow is skippable — a modal nobody can dismiss trains people to dismiss modals (DT-01). The recording is permanently badged `no split agreed`, visible to every collaborator, and it release-blocks in [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/). | "No split agreed" on the work, on every contributor's view, and at the release gate. Consequence, not obstruction. |
| Session delivered no artefact (rehearsal, failed session, writing session with nothing kept) | **No prompt.** The trigger is scoped to sessions that produced a candidate work or recording. Prompting for a sheet on a work that does not exist is noise, and noise trains the dismissal reflex DT-01 exists to protect against. | Nothing. Capture is available manually if the Producer disagrees. |
| Nobody consents; sheet sits `proposed` for months | Escalating, decaying chase per the schedule above. The sheet does not expire. | "Waiting on 3 of 4 since March." Visible on every party's dashboard. |
| **Session had 14 people (orchestra, choir, horn section)** | **Not a split problem — an engagement-shape problem** (DT-06, resolving Q-03). If the session was booked as an ensemble engagement (one contractor/fixer, one fee, N players — [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) knows this because the platform booked it), the default path is: **one fee designation consented by the contractor**, who *attests* they hold the engagement and the authority to bind it — the platform records the attestation and asserts nothing about it (D-04, [09.02.03 D-05](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)). The 14 players get **14 performer rows** ([09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)) and 14 credits ([domain 02](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — because a buyout buys the copyright, not the fact of having played ([09.02.03 D-03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md), CX rejected pair R-02) — and per [09.01.05 D-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md) those rows **do not block the ledger**. Consent load: 3 ledger parties + 1 contractor, not 14. | Producer sees "Horn section (4) — engaged via {contractor}, fee". Players see their performer row and credit to confirm at their leisure; confirming is not a gate. |
| An ensemble member is **not just a player** — the horn player wrote the arrangement | First-class escape hatch, never buried. Any person on a bulk designation can say "I contributed more than performance", which pulls them out of the bulk path into an individual designation and returns the sheet to `proposed`. Same logic as objection-as-success: cheap now, litigation later. | "I did more than play on this" as a primary action on the bulk row, not in a menu. Producer is notified with the claim. |
| 14 people were **individually invited to a project** — a writing camp, a large collective | Then there genuinely are 14 ledger parties, and the sheet has 14 rows and needs 14 consents. **No cap, no bulk path, no degradation** (DT-05). The pain is chase, not modelling. | Full 14-row sheet. "Waiting on 9 of 14." |
| Split proposed while a sample is undeclared | Capture proceeds; the sample flag blocks release separately ([09.01.06](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)). | Both facts shown; neither blocks the other's capture. |
| Producer proposes themselves a larger share than agreed | The named Producer anti-persona. Whole-ledger consent means every ledger party sees the Producer's line, not just their own. | The sheet is the disclosure mechanism. This is why per-row consent was rejected ([09.01.02 D-01](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)). |
| **Producer edits the sheet while a contributor is consenting** | Consent binds to a **ledger version** (CX-01 synthesis Q5). The consent lands against version N and is permanently recorded as a fact about version N. The edit creates version N+1. Consent to N **does not count** toward N+1 — including the proposer's own. All consents re-request with a visible before/after delta ([09.02.04 D-05](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). | Consenter: "The sheet changed after you agreed — here's what changed." Proposer: "Editing will re-request all 3 consents." Never a silent carry-forward. |
| Two producers both trigger capture on the same work | **One sheet per work.** The second trigger joins the existing `proposed` sheet as a co-proposer rather than creating a rival. Two rival sheets on one work is precisely the conflict [09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md) exists to detect — cheaper to prevent at capture than to detect afterwards. | "A sheet already exists for this work, proposed by X. Open it." |
| One session yields four songs | Four sheets. **Parties and designations carry forward; percentages do not** (D-06, DT-13). Who was in the room is a fact about the session and reusing it is not an anchor; what each song is worth to each person is a per-work judgement, and pre-filling it would be the platform inventing a number under cover of convenience (DT-02). | Song 2 arrives with the same four people already routed share/fee/neither, and every percentage field empty. |
| Contributor's share is fixed by a **pre-existing agreement** (beat licence, publishing deal, band agreement) | Capture records the number **and** the assertion that it is contractually fixed rather than negotiated in the room. The platform does not read, verify, or enforce the underlying contract (D-04). Other parties still consent to the whole ledger — a prior agreement between two parties does not bind a third. | "X's 50% is stated as fixed by a prior agreement." Other parties consent to the ledger including that row, or object. |
| Party is a **band or other entity**, not a person | The entity is the ledger party. Consent routes to whoever [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)'s Band & Ensemble Governance says can bind it. If no governance record exists, capture **does not block and does not guess**: it names the individual human consenting and records that they **asserted** authority to bind the entity. Recording the assertion is the domain's whole posture ([09.02.03 D-05](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md), [09.01.05 D-02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)). | "Consented for {band} by {person}, who states they are authorised to sign for the band." Permanently on the sheet. |
| A ledger party is **known to be a minor** | The sheet records the consent **and** records that the consenter was under 18 at the time of consent, where age is known ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/); D-13 puts minors on the platform). The platform does not assert enforceability (D-04) and does not silently treat a minor's tap as an adult's signature. Whether a guardian co-consent is *required* is a legal/product call — see Q-05. | Consent row badged "consenter was 16 at the time of signing". Visible to all ledger parties before they consent to the ledger. |
| Party has **no PRO affiliation or IPI on file** | Capture proceeds (DT-07). A sheet with percentages but no society identifiers is evidence but is **not operational** — the society cannot match the registration and the money goes to black box, which is the exact pathology this domain exists to end. So the gap is **visible and chaseable, never blocking**: same logic as [09.01.05 D-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md) — blocking capture on an identifier taxes the moment the thesis says must stay cheap. | A second, separate badge on the locked sheet: **"not society-ready — 2 of 4 writers have no PRO on file"**, with a chase action. Distinct from `no split agreed`; distinct colour, distinct meaning. |
| Sheet totals do not validate (97%, or 210% on publishing) | The sheet **does not send**. Arithmetic is [09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)'s ([09.01.02 D-04](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)); this feature surfaces the verdict and names the gap. | "Publishing is 3% short. Add it to someone or the sheet can't be sent." Inline, on the offending ledger, on blur. |
| Network drops mid-entry; send fails | **Never discards entered data** (D-05). Entered state survives, inline retry. This data is unrepeatable and re-entry means it will not happen. | "Couldn't send — retry." The sheet is exactly as it was. |
| Contributor consents, then says they misunderstood | Routes to [09.02.04](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) — amendment, not deletion. | "Propose an amendment" — the consent is a fact; the disagreement is a new one. |
| A consented party requests **GDPR erasure** | The consent survives as a fact; the personal data does not. Routed to `/create-prd-security` per [../rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md) ("PII isolation for rights parties") and [09.01.02 Q-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md). Capture's requirement: a sheet must remain a valid, readable agreement with a party's PII redacted. | Party rendered by their durable identifier and role, not their legal name. The percentages and the consent stand. |
| Non-party tries to view the sheet — a label, a manager, a curious collaborator on another work | **None.** Session participation does not confer sheet access; only ledger party, fee party, or proposer does. A label that has signed the work reads the ledger via [09.01](../09.01-rights-registry/), not via this sheet. | 404, not 403 — an existence disclosure on a commercially sensitive document is itself a leak. |
| A ledger party deactivates their account before consenting | The sheet stands `proposed`; the chase continues to their last known contact. Deactivation is not objection and is not consent. | "Waiting on 1 of 4 — X's account is inactive." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Capture flow opened | Participant list skeleton — **never a blank form**. If the participant list cannot be fetched within 3 s, the flow degrades to manual add rather than spinning; a spinner at session close is a dismissal. |
| Empty (no prompt) | Session produced no candidate work | Nothing is shown. Capture remains manually available. |
| Empty (degraded) | Session produced a work but no contributors could be identified | Should be unreachable from a session close. Fallback is manual add, **explicitly badged as the degraded path** — the sheet records that its parties were typed, not derived, because a typed sheet has weaker provenance and the record should say so. |
| Populated (`draft`) | Producer is routing/entering, not yet sent | Live arithmetic verdict from 09.01.02; `Split equally` tool available; nothing is a claim yet, nothing is notified. |
| Populated (`proposed`) | Sheet sent | Full ledger, both sides, per-party consent state with arrival-method badges, mute state, chase action. The proposer's own consent shown as consented-at-proposal. |
| Populated (`consented`) | Every ledger party consented to the current version | Locked sheet, signed badge, timestamp per consent, arrival method per consent, participant list, export action. Plus, independently, the `not society-ready` badge if PRO/IPI gaps remain. |
| Partial (awaiting consent) | Some consented | "Waiting on N of M" with names and elapsed time. **The sheet governs nothing yet** — and says so, in those words. |
| Partial (superseded consent) | Sheet edited after consents arrived | Consents to the prior version shown as historical facts, greyed, with "consented to a previous version"; current version shows them as outstanding. |
| Error (send) | Send failed | Inline retry. **Never discards entered data.** |
| Error (invalid) | Totals do not validate | Send disabled with the specific gap named on the offending ledger. Not a toast — an inline, persistent, ledger-anchored message. |
| Objected | Any ledger party objected | Not an error state. Sheet stays `proposed`, the objection and its reason are shown to ledger parties, the Producer is routed to respond. Neutral styling, no dismiss. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Capture should be **mandatory** at session close — an undismissable gate | It is the thesis feature; the strongest possible enforcement seems obviously right | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the most important call in the feature. A gate you cannot dismiss trains users to route around the event that triggers it: producers will simply stop marking sessions complete, and the platform loses its single point of presence in the room. Worse, a forced sheet is a *coerced* sheet — signatures collected to escape a modal are exactly the evidentiary garbage the domain exists to replace. The correct design is **consequence, not obstruction**: skipping is allowed, permanently visible to every collaborator, and blocks release. Make the lazy path correct; do not make the correct path compulsory. |
| DT-02 | The platform can suggest splits from session data | Consolidation gives us who was present, for how long, on what | ❌ REJECTED for *creative* shares. Time in the room does not measure authorship — the person who wrote the hook in ten minutes did not contribute less than the engineer who sat for nine hours. An algorithmic suggestion would carry the platform's authority into a judgement it has no basis for, and would anchor negotiations toward a number the platform invented. Suggesting *who was present* is confirmed and valuable; suggesting *what they deserve* is rejected. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect the capture surface to be **where the work is**, and the declared surface model may not reach it | `meta/personas.md`, Producer's unique constraint: *"Their work happens inside a DAW, not a browser. Any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and go fill in a web form will not be used."* The project is declared `single-surface` responsive web (D-02). | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as an unresolved structural tension — escalated.** The thesis depends on being in the room; the persona file states the room is a DAW; the surface model contains only a browser. The mitigation drafted here (trigger from session close, consent on the contributor's phone) narrows the gap but does not close it for the Producer, who is the capture point. Raised as Q-01 / [09.02 Q-02](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-index.md) — this is a surface-model question, not a feature question, and it is the highest-stakes open item in the domain. |
| DT-04 | The dangerous failure is a **wrong** split | Natural framing — disputes are about wrong numbers | ❌ REJECTED — the dangerous failure is **no** split. A wrong split is visible, disputable, and fixable while people are reachable. An absent split is silent: the record ships, earns, and only becomes a problem once the money is worth fighting over and the relationships have soured. Every design trade in this feature resolves in favour of *getting something captured* over *getting it perfect* — hence skippable-but-consequential, hence objection-as-success, hence never discarding entered data on error. |
| DT-05 | Above N parties the flow should cap or degrade to a bulk path | Per-person consent obviously does not scale; a limit seems prudent | ❌ REJECTED, firmly. A writing camp genuinely has twelve writers; a large collective genuinely has fourteen. A cap does not simplify the sheet — it **corrupts** it, by forcing a real party out of a ledger they belong in, and it does so silently, in the one record whose entire value is that it is true. The scaling pain is **chase**, not modelling, and chase is a cross-cut with its own answer. There is no headcount at which a ledger party stops being a ledger party. |
| DT-06 | The 14-person ensemble is a split-capture scaling problem | The obvious reading of the orchestra/choir case | ❌ REJECTED — it is an **engagement-shape** problem, and reading it as a scaling problem produces the wrong feature. Orchestral and session players are not split parties in practice: they are hired under a session agreement through a contractor (a fixer), paid scale, and bought out. So the ensemble resolves through machinery that already exists: the **fee branch** ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)) designated once by the contractor, whose authority the platform **records rather than asserts** (D-04); **14 performer rows** ([09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)) that survive the buyout ([09.02.03 D-03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)) and **do not block** the ledger ([09.01.05 D-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)); and **14 credits** ([domain 02](../../02-credits-attribution/)). Consent load is 4, not 14. The one thing that must be built is the **escape hatch** — an ensemble member who wrote the arrangement must be able to leave the bulk path in one action, prominently. **This resolves Q-03.** |
| DT-07 | The sheet is percentages plus signatures | The famous part of a split sheet, and what every mock-up shows | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient — and this is what a music-industry expert would notice first and nobody here has mentioned. A real split sheet carries legal name (not stage name), **PRO affiliation** (ASCAP/BMI/SESAC/PRS/GEMA…), **IPI/CAE number**, publisher and publisher's PRO, alongside the percentage. The percentages are what people argue about; the **PRO and IPI are what make the sheet operational**. Without them the society cannot match the registration and the money lands in black box — the pathology in `meta/problem-statement.md`. A platform that captures a perfect percentage split with no society identifiers has produced a *better memory*, not a *working registration*. Per the domain's boundary fix, **IPI is an Interested Party identifier owned by [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)** — so capture does not own it, it **requires** it and must surface its absence: a second badge, `not society-ready`, non-blocking, chaseable. Non-blocking because blocking capture on an identifier taxes the moment the thesis says must stay cheap ([09.01.05 D-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)). |
| DT-08 | "The moment of creation" is a moment | The feature's own name asserts it | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the failure mode nobody designs for. `meta/problem-statement.md` names remote collaboration as a "Why Now": the modern session is routinely asynchronous, assembled over six weeks from five contributors in four timezones. There is no room, no session close, and no moment. Capture-early produces an incomplete ledger that needs amending; capture-late is the thesis's own failure mode wearing a schedule. The resolution splits the problem along the line DT-02 already drew: **facts accrete continuously** (contribution delivered → who/what/when recorded, no consent, no cost) and **the ledger locks once** (work complete → negotiated sheet, pre-populated from the accumulated facts). If the async trigger is not built, the feature captures only the shrinking subset of music made by people in one room at one time. |
| DT-09 | The platform should default to an equal split | It is an industry convention (the "Nashville rule"), not a platform invention, so DT-02's objection may not apply | ⚠️ **SPLIT — confirmed as a tool, rejected as a default.** The distinction is **who authored the number**. A pre-filled equal split is the platform asserting a division nobody stated, and it lands with the platform's authority behind it at the precise moment the parties should be talking to each other. A `Split equally` button the Producer presses is a keyboard shortcut for a decision the Producer made. Same digits, opposite provenance — and provenance is the product. Ship the button; never pre-fill it. |
| DT-10 | The per-contributor question is binary: share or fee | [CX-01](./09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md) states it as "share or fee?" before "how much?" | ❌ REJECTED as incomplete — it is **ternary**. The participant list from [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/) contains everyone who was in the room, including the runner, the label A&R who dropped in, and the friend on the sofa. A binary question forces the Producer to either invent a fee for a non-contributor or leave them permanently unresolved on the sheet — and an unresolved person on a rights record is exactly the ambiguity that becomes a claim later. So the third branch is **present-not-a-party**, and it must be **recorded, not omitted**, on the same reasoning as [09.02.03 D-02](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md): "present and agreed to be no party" and "present and nobody ever said" are the same absence and opposite facts. This extends CX-01 rather than contradicting it — flagged to Step 6 in Cross-Cut Notes. |
| DT-11 | A consented sheet is the finished product | The feature's success state, and the whole point of the wedge | ❌ REJECTED — and this one indicts the thesis if unaddressed. The sheet is captured perfectly, and then the work is released through a label's distributor, registered by the label's publisher, and paid by a society — through channels WeJammin is not in. The perfect sheet then sits in a system no PRO, label, or lawyer will ever query. **A captured split with no way out is not an asset; it is a hostage** — which is precisely the "hostile vs earned lock-in" question `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02 asks about the whole product, arriving here first and concretely. So: a `consented` sheet must be exportable **from this feature** as a signed, human-readable document a lawyer or a label will accept, with every consent, timestamp, arrival method, and version. Machine interchange (CWR / DDEX RIN → a society) is [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)'s and is routed to `/create-prd` as an integration contract — but the human-readable export is capture's own, because a wedge whose output cannot leave is a trap, and the users this product is for have been trapped before. |
| DT-12 | The no-account signed-link path (D-04) is safe because the link is signed | Signing the link is the obvious security control, and it is the one that gets built | ❌ REJECTED — it secures the wrong thing. The link's integrity is not in doubt; the **address it was sent to** is. The Producer both authors the sheet and types the invite addresses, so the honest threat is a Producer who consents as a party who never saw the sheet, or as one who does not exist. The platform cannot prevent this without destroying D-04 (and D-04 is load-bearing: requiring accounts makes capture depend on universal adoption at the one moment it cannot). So the platform must **refuse to launder it**: consent arrival method is a permanent, visible property of every consent row — "in-account" or "via emailed link to j***@gmail.com" — shown to every party, on the sheet, forever. This is the domain posture applied to the domain's own weakest link: **hold the fact, do not assert the conclusion** (D-04). It also directly serves Q-02 (enforceable vs evidentiary), because the strength of each consent is precisely what that question turns on. |
| DT-13 | A four-song session should carry the first sheet forward to the next three | It is the obvious friction fix, and friction is the enemy per the 8/90 budget | ⚠️ **SPLIT — confirmed for parties and designations, rejected for percentages.** Who was in the room and how each was routed (share/fee/neither) are **facts about the session**; reusing them saves real work and anchors nothing. What each person is worth **on song two** is a per-work judgement, and pre-filling it from song one is DT-02's forbidden move smuggled in as convenience — the platform inventing a number, now with the false authority of "you already agreed to this". Carry the people; empty the percentages. |
| DT-14 | Objection reasons are ordinary free text | It is a text field on a form between four people who know each other | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **harassment surface** that the platform *mandates* every ledger party read, in a document they cannot leave and cannot make governing without engaging with. Standard content moderation applies ([domain 24](../../24-trust-safety/)), with one non-obvious constraint: **removing the text must never remove the objection**. If reporting a reason cleared the block, the reporting flow would become the override that [09.02.04 D-02](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) refuses to build, reachable by anyone with an offensive-sounding counterparty. The objection is a fact; the reason is content; they have different lifecycles. |
| DT-15 | The platform should determine who can consent on behalf of a band | Bands are first-class entities that sign independently of members (`meta/personas.md`) — someone must be able to bind them | ❌ REJECTED — it is D-04 in a costume. Deciding which member can bind a band is reading a partnership agreement and reaching a legal conclusion, and the moment WeJammin does that, a band's split sheet stops being evidence of what the band agreed and becomes evidence of what WeJammin decided the band agreed. Where [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) governance exists, capture routes to it. Where it does not, capture **records the assertion** — "{person} states they are authorised to sign for {band}" — permanently, on the sheet, visible to every party before they consent. A recorded assertion is defensible; an inferred authority is a liability the platform manufactured for itself. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **07 Music Projects & Collaboration** ([path](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)) — **trigger dependency, and the hard one.** Session close is the capture moment; without 07 this feature has no event to hang on. Two requirements on 07 that Step 6 must reconcile: (a) the trigger must be scoped to sessions that **delivered a candidate work** (no artefact → no prompt, per DT-01's dismissal-reflex logic); (b) 07 must also emit a **contribution-delivered / stem-accepted** event, not only session-close, or the async case (DT-08) never fires and capture serves only same-room work.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **shared entity + trigger dependency.** The same prompt produces credits. One question, three records. For the ensemble case, 02 receives 14 credit rows from a single fee designation (DT-06) — 02 must not assume a credit implies a ledger row.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity, three distinct dependencies.** (a) Non-account consent needs an **identity stub** that later merges when the address is claimed, carrying the consent with it (D-04). (b) **PRO affiliation and IPI live here** per the domain's boundary fix; capture reads them and badges their absence `not society-ready` (DT-07) — 01 must expose them as a queryable property of a party, not a profile decoration. (c) **Band & Ensemble Governance** decides who can bind an entity; where it is absent, capture records an asserted authority instead (DT-15) — 01 should expect that assertion as an input, since it is evidence about the band.
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([path](../../12-release-distribution/)) — **trigger dependency (blocking).** `no split agreed` is a release block. This is where skipping gets its consequence, and it is the entire enforcement mechanism behind DT-01. Note for Step 6: `not society-ready` (DT-07) is a **second, independent** badge and must be decided separately — it is plausibly a warning at the release gate rather than a block, since a work can legitimately release before its writers sort their PRO admin.
- Touches **05 Services Marketplace** ([path](../../05-services-marketplace/)) — **trigger dependency + pre-fill.** A paid engagement implies fee-or-share, and the engagement's terms should pre-fill the designation (never the percentage — DT-02/DT-09). Also the ensemble path: 05 is what makes DT-06 work, because 05 knows the session was booked as one contractor engagement with N players rather than N individual invitations. **If 05 does not model an ensemble engagement, DT-06 has no signal to key on and the orchestra case regresses to DT-05's rejected cap.**
- Touches **09.02.03 Work-for-Hire** ([path](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)) — **extends CX-01.** CX-01 states the per-contributor question as binary ("share or fee?"). DT-10 finds it **ternary**: share / fee / present-not-a-party. Step 6 should update CX-01 accordingly — the third branch is not a WFH with a zero fee (that would fabricate a fee obligation for a coffee runner), it is a separate recorded outcome.
- Touches **09.02.04 Split Amendment** ([path](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) — **state-race, per CX-03.** Capture produces the locked ledger amendment re-opens. Capture's edit-invalidates-consent rule (D-07) is the same mechanism as amendment's re-consent, applied pre-lock; Step 6 should confirm they are one mechanism with two entry points rather than two implementations that will drift.
- Touches **09.04.01 Conflicting Claim Detection** ([path](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md)) — **state-race, prevented at source.** One sheet per work, second proposer joins rather than forks. Rival sheets on one work are 09.04.01's cause of action; capture should not manufacture them.
- Touches **24 Trust & Safety** ([path](../../24-trust-safety/)) — **content moderation on a rights document.** Objection reason text is reportable (DT-14). The constraint 24 must honour: removing the reason never removes the objection and never unblocks the lock, or the report flow becomes the admin override [09.02.04 D-02](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) refuses to build.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — **hand-off.** The human-readable signed export is capture's (DT-11); machine interchange (CWR / DDEX RIN to a society) is 10's. The seam matters: if neither builds an exit, the wedge produces a hostage (`meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02).
- Cross-cut mechanism: **E-Signature & Consent Capture** — the pen, not the sheet. This feature's non-negotiable requirement on it: **consent arrival method (in-account vs signed link, with the redacted address) is a permanent, visible property of every consent** (DT-12). A pen that renders all signatures identically destroys the only defence the no-account path has.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Consent Chase & Escalating Notification** — this feature's requirements: the schedule above (T+0/1d/3d/7d/14d/30d → monthly to 12mo → quarterly forever), **no expiry**, per-party **mute that is recorded on the sheet**, and **no read receipts to the proposer** (a read receipt converts a chase into a pressure tactic against a party who has not agreed).
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Territory Model** — capture is where a split first enters the system, so if territory is a dimension of the ownership model ([domain Q-05](../rights-ownership-index.md), D-08), **this is the flow that must ask for it or permanently lose it**. Flagged, not designed here: the 8/90 budget cannot absorb a territory question per row, and the overwhelming majority of sheets are worldwide. Step 6 / `/create-prd-architecture` must decide whether capture defaults to worldwide with an override, or the schema carries territory that capture never populates.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Capture is **skippable, with consequences** — not mandatory | An undismissable gate makes producers stop marking sessions complete, costing the platform its presence in the room, and produces coerced signatures (DT-01). |
| D-02 | The platform suggests **who was present**, never **what they deserve** | Presence is a fact the platform holds. Creative contribution is a judgement it has no basis for, and an anchor it should not set (DT-02). `Split equally` ships as a Producer-pressed tool, never a pre-fill (DT-09). |
| D-03 | Objection is a **first-class success state** | A disagreement surfaced in the room is the product working. Framing it as an error teaches people to avoid the flow. The objection is a fact; its reason is content, separately moderated, and removing the text never clears the objection (DT-14). |
| D-04 | Consent is available **without an account**, via signed link | Requiring accounts makes capture depend on universal adoption at the one moment it cannot afford to. |
| D-05 | Errors **never discard entered data** | Capture data is unrepeatable; forcing re-entry means it does not happen. |
| D-06 | The subject is the **work**, not the session. Across works from one session, **parties and designations carry forward; percentages never do** | Four songs have four splits. Who was in the room is a fact about the session; what each song is worth to each person is a per-work judgement, and pre-filling it is DT-02's forbidden move dressed as convenience (DT-13). |
| D-07 | Consent binds to a **ledger version**. Proposing records the proposer's consent to the version they proposed; **any** edit invalidates **every** consent, including the proposer's, and re-requests with a visible delta | Requiring the proposer to separately consent to what they just typed is theatre, and theatre trains click-through. But a consent that survives an edit is a signature on a document that changed after it was signed. Version-binding gives both (CX-01 synthesis Q5, [09.02.04 D-05](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). |
| D-08 | **Consent arrival method is a permanent, visible property of every consent** — "in-account" vs "via emailed link to j***@gmail.com" | D-04's cost is that the proposer may control the invite address. The platform cannot prevent that without abandoning D-04, so it must refuse to launder it. The strength of a consent is a fact the sheet carries, not one it flattens (DT-12). |
| D-09 | **Ledger parties see the whole ledger; fee parties see its shape, not its percentages** | Whole-ledger consent is a protection for people *in* the ledger ([09.01.02 D-01](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — it is how the Producer's own line gets disclosed. A bought-out drummer is not a ledger party ([09.02.03](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)) and is consenting to a fee, not a share; showing them commercially sensitive percentages protects nobody and leaks to everybody. They do see that they are **not** a party, who the parties are, and what they are giving up — which is the consequence-framing that matters, and it lands at the exact moment the "designate them fee to hide the ledger" exploit would have to be attempted. Derived from ratified decisions rather than owner-stated; see Q-06. |
| D-10 | The per-contributor question is **ternary**: share / fee / present-not-a-party — and the third is **recorded**, never omitted | The participant list contains non-contributors. "Present and agreed to be no party" and "present and nobody ever said" are the same absence and opposite facts — the same distinction [09.02.03 D-02](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) draws for 0% (DT-10). |
| D-11 | Missing PRO/IPI **badges, never blocks** — `not society-ready`, distinct from `no split agreed` | A sheet without society identifiers is evidence but not a working registration, and unmatched registrations are the black-box pathology this domain exists to end (DT-07). But blocking capture on an identifier taxes the moment the thesis says must stay cheap ([09.01.05 D-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)). Visible, chaseable, non-obstructive. |
| D-12 | The ensemble resolves through the **fee branch plus non-blocking performer rows**, with a prominent escape hatch — not through a party cap | An orchestra is an engagement shape, not a scaling problem (DT-06). There is no headcount at which a ledger party stops being a ledger party (DT-05). **Resolves Q-03.** |
| D-13 | A `consented` sheet is **exportable from this feature** as a signed, human-readable document | A wedge whose output cannot leave the platform is hostile lock-in, not earned lock-in (DT-11; `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02). Machine interchange to societies is [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)'s. |
| D-14 | The platform **records asserted authority** to bind an entity; it never infers it | Reading a partnership agreement is a legal conclusion. The moment WeJammin reaches one, every sheet stops being evidence of what parties agreed and becomes evidence of what WeJammin says they agreed — which is the domain's whole asset (D-04 at [domain level](../rights-ownership-index.md); DT-15). |
| D-15 | **No read receipts to the proposer.** Consent state is shown; "opened but did not act" is not | The proposer already sees who has not consented — that is the chase. Surfacing that a specific party read the sheet and declined to sign converts a chase into a pressure tactic aimed at exactly the person the whole-ledger-consent design is protecting. |
