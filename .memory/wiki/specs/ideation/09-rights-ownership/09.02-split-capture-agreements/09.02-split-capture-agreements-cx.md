## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Work-for-Hire ↔ Split Capture

**Relationship**: The capture flow's first question per contributor is not "what percentage?" — it is
"is this person taking a share or a fee?". WFH is the fee branch. Getting this order wrong produces the
two worst outcomes available: a session player recorded as a phantom co-owner (which the artist will
discover at the worst moment), or a real co-writer paid a fee and silently stripped of a share they
never agreed to give up.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The session player being designated. This is the one designation that permanently removes their stake — so it requires their affirmative consent, not the Producer's assertion.
- **Producer**: Makes the designation during capture, usually correctly and in good faith; must not be able to make it unilaterally.
- **Operator**: Config-only, and only via the studio-holds-WFH-over-its-engineer case — the studio entity becomes the owner of the engineer's contribution.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ledger is owned by the rights object. A WFH designation *removes* a party from it. Both the designation and the ledger row are consent-gated, so no silent merge is possible.
2. **Trigger chain**: Designate WFH → party's ledger row is removed → fee obligation created (routing to [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) if the session was a paid engagement, or [domain 23](../../23-career-finance-business/) if not). If the fee obligation fails to create, the designation must **not** stand — a WFH with no fee is an uncompensated rights grab.
3. **Permission intersection**: A Producer's write access to the recording does not confer the right to designate someone else WFH. The designee consents.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Notifies the designee (consent request) and every other ledger party (their relative share just rose — they should know why).
5. **State transition conflict**: Designating WFH while the ledger is mid-consent invalidates in-flight consents, because the sheet those parties are approving has changed. Consent must be bound to a ledger version.

### CX-03: Split Amendment ↔ Split Capture

**Relationship**: Capture's output is a locked, consented ledger. Amendment is the sanctioned — and
only — path back. The critical property is **supersession, not mutation**: the original consented
ledger remains a permanent, retrievable fact, and the amendment is a new version referencing it. This
is not an engineering nicety. The product is a record whose value is that it cannot be quietly changed
after the fact; a mutable ledger is worth exactly as much as the memory it was supposed to replace.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Proposes or consents to amendments; can see every historical version of any sheet they are on, including who proposed each change and when.
- **Producer**: Same; typically the proposer.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Only one version is `current`. Concurrent amendment proposals queue; they do not merge — merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed.
2. **Trigger chain**: Propose amendment → current version stays `current` and live → all parties consent → new version becomes `current`, prior becomes `superseded`. **The old split keeps governing while the new one is pending** — money must not stop flowing because someone opened a proposal.
3. **Permission intersection**: Any party to the ledger may propose. All parties must consent. There is no admin override; if there were, it would be the attack surface for the entire domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every party on the current version, plus any party being added.
5. **State transition conflict**: An amendment landing mid-royalty-run is a real race. The distribution must bind to the version `current` at the run's start, or a single payout period gets computed against two different splits.

> CX-02, CX-04 and CX-05 synthesis questions are deferred to `/ideate-discover` Step 5, per the CX gate.
