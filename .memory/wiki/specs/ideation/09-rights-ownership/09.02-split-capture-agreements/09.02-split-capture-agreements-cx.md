## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Work-for-Hire ↔ Split Capture

**Relationship**: The capture flow's first question per contributor is not "what percentage?" — it is
"is this person taking a share or a fee?". WFH is the fee branch. Getting this order wrong produces the
two worst outcomes available: a session player recorded as a phantom co-owner (which the artist will
discover at the worst moment), or a real co-writer paid a fee and silently stripped of a share they
never agreed to give up.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The session player being designated. This is the one designation that permanently removes their stake — so it requires their affirmative consent, not the Producer's assertion.
- **Producer**: Makes the designation during capture, usually correctly and in good faith; must not be able to make it unilaterally.
- **Operator**: Config-only, and only via the studio-holds-WFH-over-its-engineer case — the studio entity becomes the owner of the engineer's contribution.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ledger is owned by the rights object. A WFH designation *removes* a party from it. Both the designation and the ledger row are consent-gated, so no silent merge is possible.
2. **Trigger chain**: Designate WFH → party's ledger row is removed → fee obligation created (routing to [domain 05](../../05-services-marketplace/) if the session was a paid engagement, or [domain 23](../../23-career-finance-business/) if not). If the fee obligation fails to create, the designation must **not** stand — a WFH with no fee is an uncompensated rights grab.
3. **Permission intersection**: A Producer's write access to the recording does not confer the right to designate someone else WFH. The designee consents.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Notifies the designee (consent request) and every other ledger party (their relative share just rose — they should know why).
5. **State transition conflict**: Designating WFH while the ledger is mid-consent invalidates in-flight consents, because the sheet those parties are approving has changed. Consent must be bound to a ledger version.

### CX-02: Producer Points ↔ Split Capture

**Relationship**: Points are captured with the split sheet but remain a master-side encumbrance, not
an ownership-ledger row. They must therefore be recorded in the same consented agreement without
changing any ledger percentage or giving the points holder ownership standing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The party paying the points sees what the arrangement costs them and consents to it as part of the whole sheet.
- **Producer**: The points holder may propose their own points, but that proposal is not a substitute for whole-sheet consent.
- **Operator**: No access; operating a room does not earn points.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Points attach alongside the master ledger as an encumbrance and remain outside that ledger. They do not consume ownership percentage, grant licensing or blocking standing, or enter chain of title.
2. **Trigger chain**: During capture, the arrangement records a point percentage, named base, and recoupability. After the sheet is consented, the encumbrance attaches to the master and [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) honours it in distribution order.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Producer may propose points, including their own compensation, but points appear on the sheet that every ledger party consents to. The full-sheet consent is the disclosure control for a Producer's self-proposed arrangement.
4. **Notification fan-out**: No separate points-only fan-out is specified. Points are present on the same sheet for which capture notifies every ledger party, so every required consenter receives the disclosed arrangement through the existing capture flow.
5. **State transition conflict**: A points arrangement with no named base remains partial and cannot proceed; a consented arrangement attaches as an encumbrance. No ownership-ledger transition follows from that attachment.

**Sources**: [09.02.02 Producer Points & Producer Agreements](./09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md) — **Behavior**, **Happy Path**, **States**, and **D-01–D-02**; [09.02.01 Moment-of-Creation Split Capture](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) — **Happy Path**.

### CX-03: Split Amendment ↔ Split Capture

**Relationship**: Capture's output is a locked, consented ledger. Amendment is the sanctioned — and
only — path back. The critical property is **supersession, not mutation**: the original consented
ledger remains a permanent, retrievable fact, and the amendment is a new version referencing it. This
is not an engineering nicety. The product is a record whose value is that it cannot be quietly changed
after the fact; a mutable ledger is worth exactly as much as the memory it was supposed to replace.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Proposes or consents to amendments; can see every historical version of any sheet they are on, including who proposed each change and when.
- **Producer**: Same; typically the proposer.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Only one version is `current`. Concurrent amendment proposals queue; they do not merge — merging two proposed splits produces a split nobody proposed.
2. **Trigger chain**: Propose amendment → current version stays `current` and live → all parties consent → new version becomes `current`, prior becomes `superseded`. **The old split keeps governing while the new one is pending** — money must not stop flowing because someone opened a proposal.
3. **Permission intersection**: Any party to the ledger may propose. All parties must consent. There is no admin override; if there were, it would be the attack surface for the entire domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every party on the current version, plus any party being added.
5. **State transition conflict**: An amendment landing mid-royalty-run is a real race. The distribution must bind to the version `current` at the run's start, or a single payout period gets computed against two different splits. Distribution must also resolve a **second, independent version dimension**: the payout basis is a versioned *platform term* printed at capture rather than a field either child owns ([09.02.01 D-16 / D-17](./09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)), so a settled sheet carries both the ledger version it agreed and the basis terms version it was agreed under. Amendment re-versions the first and cannot touch the second ([09.02.04 D-16](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)); which basis terms version an amended sheet then reads is [09.02.04 Q-07](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md).

### CX-04: Split Amendment ↔ Work-for-Hire

**Relationship**: Amending an already-agreed party into work-for-hire is a rights transfer, never
an administrative correction. It removes established ownership and must name the beneficiary to whom
that established share moves; an agreed share does not evaporate.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The designee sees the transfer plainly, gives explicit transfer-grade consent, and retains their existing share if they do not consent.
- **Producer**: May propose the operation but cannot make it unilaterally or frame it as a correction.
- **Operator**: No amendment surface. A studio entity that holds a share consents as an entity through its authorised signatory, not through Operator access.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The operation changes both the current consented ledger and the work-for-hire designation. It is transfer-shaped because it extinguishes an established share and amendment-shaped because it changes remaining parties' percentages; its beneficiary is mandatory.
2. **Trigger chain**: Proposal runs through the amendment flow → every required party consents → a new ledger version supersedes the current version. Until completion, the current ledger continues to govern. The retroactive designation is recorded as the rights transfer it is, not as an administrative correction.
3. **Permission intersection**: The operation takes the union of both consent sets, never the lesser: amendment unanimity plus the designee's explicit transfer-grade consent in Work-for-Hire's plain language, plus the payer's consent to the consideration. Payer consent is waived only when that consideration references an existing Domain 05 engagement **whose amount the payer agreed at booking**. No Producer or administrator override exists.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The amendment flow notifies every party on the current version and any party added; the designee receives the transfer-grade request. The producer's proposal is not notification or consent on another party's behalf.
5. **State transition conflict**: An open amendment leaves the current version governing. Concurrent amendment proposals queue rather than merge; a completed amendment creates a new version and keeps the prior version as retrievable evidence.

**Sources**: [09.02.04 Split Amendment & Re-Consent](./09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) — **Behavior: Amendment vs transfer — the boundary**, **D-03–D-06**, and **Edge Cases: “Someone proposes amending a party into work-for-hire”**; [09.02.03 Work-for-Hire & Buyout Designation](./09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) — **Edge Cases: “Designee is retroactively designated after consenting to a share”**, **D-07**, and **D-15**.
