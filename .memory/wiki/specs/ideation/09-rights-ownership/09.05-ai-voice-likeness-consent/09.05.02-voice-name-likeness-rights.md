# Feature: Voice, Name & Likeness (NIL) Rights

> **Number**: 09.05.02
> **Parent**: [AI, Voice & Likeness Consent](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Your voice is not a recording. A recording is property — owned, sold, inherited. A voice is a person,
protected by rights of publicity that stay with the human and cannot be transferred with a master. A
label that buys your catalogue does not buy your voice, and this is the distinction the industry is
currently litigating in real time.

This feature records who holds NIL rights, what they have granted, and to whom. It is the only right in
domain 09 that **no ledger can allocate**, because it was never a share of anything.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The person. Holds their own NIL absolutely — the platform cannot let anyone else grant it, however many masters they own. Grants specific, scoped permissions (a named project, a term, a purpose) or refuses. Sees every request for their voice or likeness, including from parties who own recordings of them. Notably: a work-for-hire session singer who owns **nothing** of a master holds full NIL over their voice on it. |
| Producer | Config | Surfaces the question during capture and records the position; holds no authority over anyone's likeness. Where their own voice or name is involved, they are a Musician in that capacity. |
| Operator | None | No NIL stake in anyone's work. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility of the grant. The **consequence** — a disclosure that a voice is synthetic — is fan-facing via [09.05.03](./09.05.03-ai-generated-content-disclosure.md), which is a different feature for a different reason (CX-02). |

## Behavior

NIL attaches to a **person**, not to a work. It is asserted once and referenced by every recording that
person appears on. Grants are scoped:

- **What** — voice, name, image, or a distinctive style/persona?
- **Purpose** — a specific track, a project, a campaign, or open-ended?
- **Term** — bounded or perpetual?
- **Withdrawal** — can it be revoked, and what happens to work already made under it?

The load-bearing rule: **master ownership confers zero NIL authority** (see
[09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-cx.md](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-cx.md) CX-01). Training a
voice model on a recording requires the owners' consent to use the recording *and* the performer's
consent to be modelled — different rights, different holders, usually different people.

**The law here is unsettled and moving** (state right-of-publicity statutes, the ELVIS Act, NO FAKES,
the EU AI Act). Post-mortem rights vary wildly by territory. The platform records positions; it does not
assert what is enforceable where.

### Happy Path

1. Musician asserts NIL over their identity as part of their profile ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)).
2. A producer wants to build a vocal model from a session they own the master to.
3. The request routes to the singer — **not** to the master owner, even though the master owner is the one who asked.
4. Singer grants: this project, two years, no sublicensing.
5. Grant recorded and scoped; outputs must disclose ([09.05.03](./09.05.03-ai-generated-content-disclosure.md)).
6. Term expires; the grant lapses. Further use needs a new one.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Master owner claims their ownership includes the voice | Refused. The rule exists precisely for this. | "Master ownership does not include NIL — the performer must consent." |
| WFH session singer, owns 0% of the master | Holds full NIL regardless. | The buyout bought the copyright, not the person. Same shape as [09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) DT-02 and [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md). |
| Grant withdrawn after a model is trained | The model does not untrain. Withdrawal stops future use under the grant. | "Withdrawn — does not recall models already built." Say it plainly; the alternative is a promise nobody can keep. |
| Performer is dead | Post-mortem publicity rights vary enormously by territory — decades in some US states, nonexistent elsewhere. | Estate asserts where applicable ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)); territory-scoped. |
| Someone clones a voice with no grant at all | The platform has no detection and no enforcement power. | Routes to [domain 24](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/) as a claim, with the recorded absence-of-grant as evidence. |
| Style imitation without voice cloning ("in the style of X") | Genuinely unsettled law; style is not generally protected. | "Style is not covered" — do not imply a right that may not exist. |
| A band's collective identity vs individual NIL | Two different things. The band name is [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)'s (trademark); each member's voice is their own. | Both surfaced; no inference between them. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | NIL should be a **property of the recording** — it is about a voice on a master, so store it there | Data-locality instinct; it is where the use happens | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's defining call. NIL attaches to the **person**, and the difference is not filing-cabinet pedantry — it determines who is asked. Store it on the recording and it inherits the recording's ownership, which means the master owner becomes the party who grants it. That is exactly the harm: a label ticking a box to license a singer's voice with a right that was never the label's. Storing it on the person means the request routes to the human every time, regardless of who owns the tape. The platform's model must make the wrong grant **structurally impossible**, not merely disallowed by a rule someone could relax later. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **WFH session singer** case to be the hard one, and no candidate addresses it | The candidate list said "Voice, Name & Likeness (NIL) Rights" as a bare noun. But the sharpest case is a singer who was paid £200, signed a buyout, owns nothing — and whose voice is now the most valuable trainable asset on the master. Every instinct in the platform's ownership model says they have no stake in that recording, and every instinct is wrong. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and this is the third feature where the same structural bias surfaces (with [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md) and [09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) DT-02). The pattern is now unmistakable: **the sweep's candidate list consistently sees rights through the owner's eye**, and repeatedly loses the rights of people who own nothing. That population is `meta/personas.md`'s declared structural centre. Worth carrying into `/ideate-validate` as a systematic finding, not three coincidences. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | NIL attaches to the **person**, never to the recording | Storing it on the recording makes the master owner the granting party — the exact harm. The model must make the wrong grant structurally impossible (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | **Master ownership confers zero NIL authority** | The load-bearing rule of the sub-domain. A catalogue sale does not transfer a voice. |
| D-03 | A WFH buyout **does not** extinguish NIL | The fee bought the copyright, not the person. Consistent with [09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md) D-03. |
| D-04 | The platform records positions; it does **not** assert enforceability | The law is moving and varies by territory. Asserting a right that may not exist is worse than recording a position. |
