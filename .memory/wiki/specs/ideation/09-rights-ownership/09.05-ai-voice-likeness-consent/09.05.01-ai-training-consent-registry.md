# Feature: AI Training Consent Registry

> **Number**: 09.05.01
> **Parent**: [AI, Voice & Likeness Consent](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A declaration of whether a work or recording may be used to train an AI model, granted by the people who
actually own it. Every standalone AI-consent registry has the same structural defect: it collects
assertions from parties whose authority to grant is unverified — a lead singer opting in a master owned
half by a label, a writer opting out a song they hold 25% of.

WeJammin's consent is **anchored** ([09.05 D-01](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)). Behind
every grant sits a consented ownership ledger ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md))
built from multi-party agreement at the moment of creation. That is what turns a database of preferences
into a permission someone could rely on.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The owner. Sets a default position across their catalogue (most will opt out once and never think about it again), and overrides per work. Sees which of their works have a **complete** consent position and which are unresolvable because a co-owner has not answered — the honest state, and the common one. Sees any grants made and to whom. |
| Producer | Config | Surfaces the question at capture and records positions; holds no consent authority over a work they do not own. Where they own master shares, they are an owner in a Musician capacity. Config-only is the right level: they prompt, they do not grant. |
| Operator | None | No ownership, no consent authority. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. A consent position is a commercial decision about a work, not consumer information. What a fan is entitled to know is whether a track they are hearing is AI-generated — [09.05.03](./09.05.03-ai-generated-content-disclosure.md), a different feature with a different audience. |

## Behavior

A consent position attaches to a rights object and requires **all owners of that right**
([09.05 D-02](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)). One writer of four cannot opt a song in or
out; the position is the ledger's, not a person's.

Positions carry more than a boolean, because "no AI training" is not one preference:

- **Scope** — any training, or only specified purposes? (A composer may accept training for stem separation and refuse it for generative composition.)
- **Grantee** — everyone, or a named licensee?
- **Term** — perpetual, or bounded?
- **Compensation** — free, or licensed? (Which is [09.05 Q-02](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md).)

Training on a **vocal** recording additionally requires the performer's NIL grant, which is a different
right held by a different person (see [09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-cx.md](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-cx.md) CX-01).

### Happy Path

1. Musician sets a catalogue-wide default: no training without a licence.
2. Position propagates to every work where they are the sole owner — resolved instantly.
3. For co-owned works, the position is `pending` until every co-owner answers.
4. Co-owners are asked once, in context, not via a campaign.
5. All answer; the work has a complete, anchored consent position, machine-readable and attributable to people who demonstrably hold the right.
6. An AI company seeking a licensed corpus can see who to ask and that the answer is real.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Co-owners disagree — one opts in, one out | **The most restrictive position governs.** No consent is the default; a grant requires unanimity. | "X has not consented — this work is not available for training." Disagreement is a valid resting state, not an error. |
| A co-owner never answers | Position stays `pending`; no grant is possible. | "Waiting on 1 of 3." Silence is not consent, and never becomes it. |
| Work already scraped before any position existed | The platform cannot undo it. | "Recorded from 12 March 2026" — the position's start date is visible, so nobody mistakes it for retroactive protection. |
| Model trained in breach of a recorded position | The platform has evidence, not enforcement. | Position + timestamp available as evidence for a claim the owner brings. See DT-01. |
| Owner opts in, then changes their mind | Withdrawal recorded; the already-trained model does not untrain. | "Withdrawn — does not affect models already trained." Managing this expectation is essential; the alternative is a promise nobody can keep. |
| Vocal recording; performer is not an owner (WFH session singer) | Master owners' consent is insufficient. NIL is the singer's alone. | Both grants required; missing either → refused (CX-01). |
| Work contains an uncleared sample | A grant cannot be trusted over an object whose ownership is contested — the ledger is incomplete, so the "all owners" test cannot be satisfied. The position stays blocked, not `pending`: the blocker is provenance, not a missing answer. | "Ownership unresolved — a consent position cannot be set until the sample at [09.01.06](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md) is cleared." Exact clearance-state coupling: `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |
| Position set, then a co-owner is added later (ledger correction) | A new owner invalidates a previously-unanimous grant — the grant was over a smaller owner set than the true one. The position reverts to `pending` and the new owner is asked. | "Ownership changed — this position needs 1 new confirmation before it is valid again." |
| Named-grantee position; the grantee entity is later acquired/renamed | The grant was to a specific licensee, not to whoever now holds that name. The grant does not silently transfer. | "Granted to [original entity] — does not extend to successors unless re-granted." Assignment rules: `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Consent panel requested | Skeleton |
| Empty | No position set | "No position recorded" — explicitly **not** "no consent given". The distinction is the feature |
| Populated | Position complete | Position, scope, grantee, term, date set, and who consented |
| Partial | Some co-owners answered | "Waiting on N" — no grant possible |
| Blocked | Underlying ownership is contested (uncleared sample, disputed share) | "Ownership unresolved" — the position is neither set nor pending; the ledger must resolve first |
| Error | Write failed | Inline retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A consent registry is pointless because it is **unenforceable** — a scraper does not check your database | The strongest available objection, and it is largely true today | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and it reframes what the feature is *for*. It cannot stop anyone. Its value is (a) **evidentiary** — a timestamped, multi-party-consented refusal is the foundation of a later claim, and "I never agreed" is much stronger with a date on it; (b) **normative** — the actors who *do* ask (licensed model developers, and increasingly anyone facing the EU AI Act's transparency obligations) need a corpus with clean permissions, and there is currently nowhere to get one; (c) **commercial** — an anchored corpus is licensable, which is [09.05 Q-02](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md). Framing it as protection would be a lie users would discover at the worst moment. Framing it as evidence and as a market is honest and still valuable. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect consent to be **per-owner**, since it is each person's decision about their own work | It sounds like autonomy, and it is how most registries behave | ❌ REJECTED — a co-owned work with three writers where one opts in is not "one-third trainable". Training is not divisible; the model ingests the whole recording or none of it. Per-owner consent would produce grants that cannot be honoured and would let one co-owner license an asset that is two-thirds someone else's. Most restrictive governs, unanimity to grant. This is the rule most likely to make WeJammin's consent **slower and less complete** than a competitor's — and it is precisely what makes a WeJammin grant worth relying on, which is the only reason to build it (see [09.05 D-02](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)). A registry optimised for coverage over authority is the thing that already exists and does not work. |
| DT-03 | "No position recorded" should default to **opt-out** — the protective default | Protective instinct, and it is where the norms are heading | ❌ REJECTED as a *record*, though it may be right as a *policy*. The registry's product is a statement of what the owners actually said. Recording an absence as a refusal fabricates a position nobody took — and it is the mirror image of the error in [09.01.06](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md) DT-02, where an absence was going to be presented as clearance. Both cases: **silence must read as silence.** The platform may separately adopt a *policy* that unrecorded works are not licensed through it (a defensible operational stance), but that is the platform's decision, not the owner's declaration, and the two must not be conflated in the record. Hence the empty state says "no position recorded", never "no consent given". |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Unanimity to grant; most restrictive position governs** | Training is not divisible. Per-owner consent produces grants nobody can honour (see DT-02). |
| D-02 | "No position recorded" is **never** rendered as "no consent given" | The record states what owners said. Fabricating a refusal is the same error as presenting silence as clearance (see DT-03). |
| D-03 | The feature is **evidentiary and commercial**, not protective | It cannot stop a scraper. Claiming otherwise is a promise users would discover is empty (see DT-01). |
| D-04 | Consent carries **scope, grantee, term and compensation** — not a boolean | "No AI training" is not one preference; stem separation and generative composition are different asks. |
