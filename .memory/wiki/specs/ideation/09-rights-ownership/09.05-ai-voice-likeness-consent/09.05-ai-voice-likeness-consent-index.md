## Overview

The rights that are not copyright: whether your work may be used to **train** a model, whether your
**voice and likeness** may be synthesised, and whether a track discloses that a machine made part of
it. These are the newest rights in music and the least settled — the law is moving, the norms are not
formed, and the tooling is nonexistent.

The sub-domain's claim to be here rather than somewhere else is specific: a consent registry is only
worth anything if the consent comes from **someone who actually holds the right**. Every standalone
AI-consent registry has the same defect — it collects assertions from people whose authority to grant
is unverified. WeJammin has an ownership ledger with multi-party consent behind it
([09.01](../09.01-rights-registry/), [09.02](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/)), so a consent granted
here is *anchored*. That is not an incremental improvement; it is the difference between a database and
a permission.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: training consent, NIL rights, and AI disclosure interact
directly — a voice model is trained on recordings (training consent) to synthesise a performer (NIL) and
produces output that must be declared (disclosure). Each is the precondition or consequence of another.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | **Consent granted here is anchored to the ownership ledger** | The defining design decision, and the sub-domain's entire justification for existing inside domain 09 rather than as a standalone product. Every independent AI-consent registry collects assertions from parties whose authority to grant is unverified — a lead singer "consenting" to training on a master owned 50% by a label. Anchoring to a consented ledger means a grant here is made by people who demonstrably hold the right. | Deep Think; D-18 thesis |
| D-02 | **Consent requires all owners of the right, not one** | Follows from D-01 and from [09.01.02 D-01](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) (consent is to the whole ledger). A training grant over a jointly-owned master is not one person's to give. This is the rule most likely to make WeJammin's consent *slower* than a competitor's — and the only thing that makes it mean anything. | Node Classification Gate; Deep Think |
| D-03 | The platform records consent and disclosure; it **does not detect** AI use | Consistent with the domain pattern ([09.03.06 D-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md), [09.04 D-04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md)): hold the facts, route the judgements. AI detection is unreliable and a false accusation of synthetic authorship is defamatory. | Deep Think |
| D-04 | **NIL kept separate from training consent** despite both being "AI consent" | They are different rights over different objects with different holders. Training consent is over a *work or recording* and is held by its owners. NIL is over a *person* and is held only by that person — it cannot be sold with the master, and a label that buys your catalogue does not buy your voice. Merging them would imply otherwise, which is the exact error the industry is currently litigating. | Node Classification Gate |
