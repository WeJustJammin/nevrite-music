# Feature: AI-Generated Content Disclosure & Provenance

> **Number**: 09.05.03
> **Parent**: [AI, Voice & Likeness Consent](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A declaration of what a machine contributed to a track — a fully generated instrumental, a synthesised
vocal, an AI-assisted master, an AI-written lyric. The disclosure exists for the **listener**: someone
hearing a voice is entitled to know whether a human sang it.

It is also a rights fact with teeth. In the US, purely AI-generated material has **no human authorship
and therefore no copyright** — which means an undeclared AI contribution may render part of a work
unprotectable, and neither the writer nor anyone who licenses it from them knows.

**This is the only Fan-visible feature in domain 09** besides the public lookup
([09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md)). A disclosure
nobody outside the rights team can see is not a disclosure.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares AI contributions to their own work; sees declarations on anything they are attached to — because an undeclared AI element in a collaborator's stem affects the copyright status of *their* song too. Sees the copyright consequence stated: which parts may be unprotectable, and therefore what they cannot license. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the only person who knows — they ran the tools. Declares at session close, when the fact is fresh and the tool is still open, rather than at release when nobody remembers which plugin did what. Sees declarations on stems they receive, which is the guard against inheriting an unprotectable element from a client. |
| Operator | None | No stake in a track's AI provenance. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The audience this feature exists for.** Sees a plain, honest label on a released track — "synthesised vocal", "AI-generated instrumental". Not a rights panel, not percentages: one legible fact. Per `meta/personas.md`, the Fan will not tolerate professional-tool complexity, so the disclosure must be a label, not a document. |

## Behavior

A declaration is typed and scoped to what the machine actually did — a single "contains AI" flag is
useless, because an AI-mastered human performance and a fully generated track are not the same thing and
do not have the same copyright status.

| Type | What it means | Copyright consequence (US) |
|---|---|---|
| **AI-generated composition** | Melody/lyric produced by a model | No human authorship → likely unprotectable |
| **AI-synthesised vocal** | A voice that nobody sang | Also engages NIL if it resembles a real person ([09.05.02](./09.05.02-voice-name-likeness-rights.md)) |
| **AI-assisted production** | Human work, machine tools (stem separation, mastering, pitch correction) | Ordinary human authorship — protectable. **The overwhelming majority of real use.** |
| **AI-generated instrumental** | A produced bed nobody played | Likely unprotectable |

The consequence is compositional: a work with a human lyric over an AI-generated instrumental is
**partly protectable**. That is a genuinely awkward record — and it is true, so the model must hold it.

Per [09.05 D-03](./09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent-index.md), the platform **records declarations; it
does not detect AI use.**

### Happy Path

1. Producer uses a generative tool for a pad; at session close the platform asks what tools contributed.
2. Producer declares: AI-generated instrumental element, named tool.
3. The work shows the declaration and the consequence: "the pad may not be protectable — the topline and vocal are."
4. On release, the disclosure surfaces to listeners as a plain label ([domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/), [domain 20](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)).
5. Downstream licensing ([domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/)) knows which elements can actually be licensed.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| AI-assisted production (autotune, stem separation, AI mastering) | Declared as assisted; **no** copyright consequence, and no scarlet letter. This is most music now. | "AI-assisted production" recorded, not surfaced to fans as a warning. Over-labelling ordinary tools is how a disclosure regime becomes noise. |
| Partly AI work | Partial protectability — real and awkward. | "Vocal protectable; instrumental may not be." The model must hold a work that is copyright in one half and not the other. |
| Nobody declares | The platform cannot detect it. | Empty reads as **undeclared**, never as "no AI" — same principle as [09.01.06](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md) DT-02 and [09.05.01](./09.05.01-ai-training-consent-registry.md) DT-03. Silence is silence. |
| Synthesised vocal resembling a real person | Engages NIL as well as disclosure. | Both required; missing NIL blocks (CX-02). |
| Fan sees "AI" and assumes the whole track is fake | The real risk of a crude label. | Which is why types are distinct and assisted production is not fan-surfaced. |
| Territory requires disclosure by law (EU AI Act) | Obligation, not a choice. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — territory-varying statutory duty. |
| Declared AI work later enters a training corpus | The recursive case: can consent be granted over an output whose inputs are unknown? | `[PENDING]` — CX-04, low confidence, unresolved. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should **detect** AI-generated content rather than rely on self-declaration — declarations will be ignored by exactly the people who should make them | The obvious objection to any honour system, and it is a fair one | ❌ REJECTED — detection is unreliable in both directions, and the two errors are not symmetric. A false negative is a missing label. A false positive is the platform **publicly accusing a musician of faking their own performance** — defamatory, unfalsifiable (how do you prove you sang?), and aimed at the one thing an artist cannot afford to have doubted. Detectors are also trivially defeated by re-recording the output. Consistent with the domain's settled pattern ([09.03.06 D-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md), [09.04 D-04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md)): **hold the facts, route the judgements.** |
| DT-02 | A single "contains AI" flag is enough for listeners — they want a yes or no | Simplicity, and it is what most platforms have shipped | ❌ REJECTED — it would label essentially all modern music, since almost every record touches AI mastering, stem separation or pitch correction. A label that applies to everything communicates nothing, and worse, it would tar a human performance that used an AI-assisted master with the same mark as a fully synthetic track. The types must be distinct, and **AI-assisted production must not be fan-surfaced at all** — surfacing it is how a disclosure regime becomes noise and then gets ignored, taking the disclosures that matter with it. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect the **copyright consequence** to be the headline, and the candidate list frames this as pure disclosure | The candidate was "AI-Generated Content Disclosure & Provenance" — a labelling feature. But the US Copyright Office position is that AI-generated material has no human author and therefore no copyright. That means an undeclared AI element does not just mislead a listener; it silently makes part of a work **unprotectable and unlicensable** — and the writer will discover it when a sync buyer's lawyer asks. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is a rights feature that happens to have a labelling surface, not a labelling feature. It also explains why it sits in domain 09 rather than in [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/) with the other delivery metadata: the declaration determines what the work *is*, legally, and only a rights domain can hold that. The partial-protectability case (human topline, AI bed) is awkward precisely because it is true. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform **records** declarations; it never **detects** AI use | A false positive is a public accusation that an artist faked their own performance — defamatory and unfalsifiable (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | Declaration types are **distinct**; AI-assisted production is not fan-surfaced | A flag that applies to all modern music communicates nothing and drowns the disclosures that matter (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | The **copyright consequence** is surfaced alongside the declaration | An undeclared AI element silently makes part of a work unprotectable; the writer learns this from a buyer's lawyer (see DT-03). |
| D-04 | Empty state reads as **undeclared**, never as "no AI" | Third instance of the same rule in this domain: silence is silence. |
