## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: AI Training Consent ↔ NIL Rights

**Relationship**: The single most important distinction in the sub-domain, and the one the industry is
currently litigating. A vocal recording is **property** — owned, transferable, and its owners may
consent to training on it. A voice is a **person** — a right of publicity that stays with the human,
cannot be sold with the master, and does not pass to whoever buys the catalogue.

So training a voice model on a recording needs *both*: the master owners' consent to use the recording,
and the performer's consent to be modelled. These are different people in the ordinary case. A session
singer who was work-for-hire owns none of the master — and still holds their own voice. If the platform
modelled these as one consent, a label could tick a box and grant away a singer's voice with a right
that was never theirs, which is precisely the failure everyone is worried about.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Holds the NIL right personally and inalienably; may or may not hold master shares. The WFH session singer is in one and not the other — the same asymmetry as [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md).
- **Producer**: Config — surfaces and records both grants during capture; holds neither by default.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility of consent; sees only the resulting disclosure (CX-02).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Two records over one recording, owned by different parties. Neither can write the other. This is structurally the same shape as master-ownership vs performer-eligibility ([09.01 CX-04](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01-rights-registry-cx.md)) — and for the same reason: personhood facts are not property facts.
2. **Trigger chain**: A training request over a vocal recording must resolve **both** grants. Either missing → refused. There is no partial grant, because a voice model trained with one and not the other is exactly the harm.
3. **Permission intersection**: Master ownership confers **zero** NIL authority. This is the load-bearing rule of the sub-domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A training request notifies master owners **and** every identified performer — including those who own nothing and would otherwise never hear about it.
5. **State transition conflict**: A master sold mid-request carries the master consent question to the new owner; the NIL grant is untouched and stays with the performer. Rights that travel and rights that do not, diverging in real time.

### CX-02: NIL Rights ↔ AI Disclosure

**Relationship**: Consent between the parties does not cure the harm to the listener. If a singer
licenses their voice and the resulting track ships undisclosed, a fan hears a performance and attributes
it to a human who never sang it. The listener was not a party to the consent and cannot be bound by it.
Disclosure is therefore not a courtesy attached to a NIL grant — it is a distinct obligation running to
a third party, which is why [09.05.03](./09.05.03-ai-generated-content-disclosure.md) is the only
Fan-visible feature in the sub-domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The voice holder — may require disclosure as a condition of their grant.
- **Producer**: Makes the disclosure at the point of delivery, being the one who knows what is in the track.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: **The whole point.** The disclosure exists for them, and a disclosure they cannot see is not one.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The NIL grant and the disclosure are independent records over different objects (a person; a track). No conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: NIL grant used → synthetic vocal in a track → disclosure required at release ([domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/)). If disclosure fails, the release should block — the grant's condition is unmet.
3. **Permission intersection**: A NIL grant may be **conditional** on disclosure. Withdrawing disclosure would breach the grant, not merely the labelling rule.
4. **Notification fan-out**: To [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/) (release gating) and [domain 20](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/) (the fan-facing surface where the label appears).
5. **State transition conflict**: A NIL grant withdrawn after release, with the track live and disclosed — does the disclosure become a defence or an admission? `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 09.05.02 NIL Rights | [09.03.06 Moral Rights](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.06-moral-rights-attribution.md) | Superficially similar — both are personal, both survive transfers, both are non-economic. But moral rights attach to **authorship** of a work (you wrote it, so you are named); NIL attaches to **personhood** (your voice is yours whether or not you wrote anything). A session singer who wrote nothing has full NIL and no moral rights. Merging would tie a personhood right to an authorship record and quietly disenfranchise every non-writing performer. |
| R-02 | 09.05.01 AI Training Consent | [09.01.06 Sample & Interpolation Provenance](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md) | Both concern "your music inside someone else's output", which invites a merge. But a sample is a **copy** of an identifiable recording, settled with a licence and a share; training is **statistical ingestion** producing no copy, with no share to settle and, at present, no settled law. Modelling training as a very small sample would import a clearance framework built for copying into a problem that is not copying — and would imply a per-output royalty nobody can compute. |
| R-03 | 09.05.03 AI Disclosure | [09.02.01 Split Capture](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) | Tempting: both are captured at session close, so why not one prompt? Rejected because an AI contribution has **no split to capture** — a model is not a party, holds no share, and consents to nothing. Routing disclosure through the split flow would put a non-person in a ledger of persons and force the question "what percentage does the AI get?", which is not a question. Disclosure is a property of the work, not a row in its ownership. |
