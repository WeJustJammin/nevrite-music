# Feature: Public Rights Lookup ("Who owns this?")

> **Number**: 09.06.04
> **Parent**: [Rights Evidence & Public Record](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The public face of the whole domain: ask "who owns this?" and get an answer. It is the question the
industry cannot answer — the MLC launched holding hundreds of millions in unmatched mechanicals for
exactly this reason — and it is the question Jaxsta, Muso.AI and Sound Credit all exist to answer and
all struggle with, because they are reconstructing from liner notes, label submissions, and self-claims.

WeJammin builds the same surface on a different substrate: **records consented at creation by the people
in the room**. Same product, different truth.

It is also the **demand side of the thesis** ([09.06 D-04](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)).
D-18 says provenance is why users cannot leave — but a record's worth to its owner is proportional to how
much other people rely on it. `meta/personas.md` states the Musician's success criterion precisely:
*credits are cited rather than claimed.* Cited by whom, if nobody can look them up?

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two sides. As a **subject**: sees how their own record appears publicly, and controls what is exposed within the platform's policy — this is their professional reputation rendered by someone else's system. As a **searcher**: looks up a work before covering it, sampling it, or pitching for a session, and can see who they would be dealing with. |
| Producer | Full | Heavy searcher. Resolving "who owns this master?" before clearing a sample is currently days of email; here it is a query. Also sees their own production catalogue as others see it — which is the portfolio they are actually judged on. |
| Operator | Read-only | Thin but real: a venue or studio resolving a recording to check who to license for a room playlist, or checking an act's catalogue when booking. Read-only — an Operator has no rights record of their own to expose here. This is one of only two features in domain 09 where an Operator sees anything. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The credits-curiosity case**, and one of only two Fan-visible features in the domain (with [09.05.03](../09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent/09.05.03-ai-generated-content-disclosure.md)). "Who wrote this? Who played that solo?" — a legitimate, high-volume question that today sends people to Wikipedia and Discogs. Per `meta/personas.md`, the Fan will not tolerate professional complexity: they get names and roles, never percentages, never deal terms. A different product wearing the same brand. |

## Behavior

The lookup resolves a work or recording — by identifier ([09.06.01](./09.06.01-identifier-issuance-reconciliation.md),
CX-01) or by search — and returns a **publication-safe projection** of the rights record. Never the
ledger itself (see [09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-cx.md](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-cx.md) R-02):
the lookup reads a deliberately constructed public view, so that adding a private field to the schema
later cannot leak it to the internet by default.

**What is public is the sub-domain's largest open product decision** ([09.06 Q-01](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)).
The tiers, roughly:

| Tier | Examples | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| **Already public** | Writer names, performer names, roles, publisher names | On every released record's liner notes |
| **Arguably public** | Registration status, AI disclosure, identifier values | Facts, no commercial exposure |
| **Sensitive** | Percentages, deal terms, points, fee amounts | Commercially damaging if exposed |
| **Never** | Disputes, escrow, personal data, unreleased works | Reputational and legal harm |

Every record shows its **trust level**, and the badge it renders is the record-level **provenance
class** — `captured` / `asserted` / `imported` ([rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md) CX-07,
[09.01.03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md) §6 / DT-08). A record consented
by four parties in 2026 renders differently from one asserted by a single user about 1998, and both
render differently from one ingested from a distributor feed that nobody on the platform attested to.
That distinction *is* the product; without it WeJammin is Jaxsta with better fonts.

**This is not the same attribute as the chain's per-event trust level**, and conflating the two is the
easiest way to get the badge wrong. The chain of title carries `platform-witnessed` / `evidence-attached`
/ `asserted` on **each event** ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)
D-01) — those are the underlying *facts*. The provenance class is the **derived, record-level badge**
computed from them, and per CX-07 it "cannot be set independently of the chain": nobody hand-sets
`captured` without the underlying signed consent existing. Two vocabularies, two layers, one direction of
travel — facts up from the chain, badge down to the lookup. The residual derivation gap is Q-05.

### Happy Path

1. A supervisor hears a track and needs to clear it for a sync.
2. Looks it up by ISRC; gets the writers, the publishers, the master owners, the registration status, and the trust level.
3. Sees "consented split, 4 parties, March 2026" rather than "source: label submission".
4. Contacts the right people the first time — instead of a three-week title search that ends in a guess.
5. The Musician's record was cited, not claimed. That is the wedge paying out.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Work not on WeJammin | No answer. Coverage is the platform's binding constraint — a lookup nobody can rely on for arbitrary works is not a reference. | "Not found on WeJammin" — never a guess, and never an implication that not-found means unowned. |
| Ownership contested | Disputes are **never** public ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)). | Shows the last consented state, or "ownership not currently confirmed" — never "disputed", which would be a reputational weapon. |
| Ledger incomplete (`proposed`, not consented) | Not published. A sheet in progress is not a record. | "Not confirmed." |
| Owner wants their record hidden | Tension: hiding defeats the purpose; forcing exposure is coercive. Undecided — D-01/D-05 govern *what* projects, never *whether* a given owner's record projects at all. | `[PENDING — owner call, Q-02 → /create-prd]` — see Q-02. |
| Fan sees a percentage | Should be structurally impossible, not policy-prevented. | Percentages are outside the fan projection entirely; the Fan view is a different projection, not a filtered one. |
| Someone scrapes the entire lookup | An open lookup is scrapeable by construction — including by the AI companies [09.05.01](../09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent/09.05.01-ai-training-consent-registry.md) is trying to hold to account. | The irony is real; see [09.06 Q-03](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md). |
| Pre-platform work with asserted history | Rendered at a visibly lower trust level. | "Asserted by owner, not verified" — the honest boundary between what we witnessed and what we were told. |
| Record is correct but unflattering (a writer with 5%) | It is true. | The reason percentages are probably not public. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Lookup requested | Skeleton |
| Empty | No results | "Not found on WeJammin" — a bounded claim, not a verdict on the work |
| Populated | Record resolved | Parties, roles, identifiers, registration status, trust level |
| Partial | Some layers known (recording yes, composition no) | Known layers shown; missing layers explicitly named as unknown, not omitted |
| Error | Lookup failed | Retry; never a fallback guess |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The lookup should show **percentages** — that is what "who owns this?" really means, and half an answer is not an answer | The literal reading of the feature's own name | ❌ REJECTED as a default, ⚠️ flagged as the owner's call ([09.06 Q-01](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)). Splits are commercially sensitive in ways that are asymmetric and hard to undo: a co-writer discovers their 15% is worse than a peer's 25% on a different record and the friendship is over; a label sees an artist's publishing position before a negotiation; a session player's fee becomes their public price ceiling forever. The lookup's *purpose* — letting a licensor find the right people to call — is fully served by names, roles and identifiers. Percentages add nothing to that use case and add real harm. **Serve the use case, not the literal question.** |
| DT-02 | This is the Jaxsta play, and Jaxsta struggled, so this is a weak feature | `meta/problem-statement.md` names Jaxsta/Muso.AI as the failure mode: "Nobody wakes up wanting a credits database" | ❌ REJECTED — the argument confuses the *product* with the *business*. Jaxsta's failure was that a credits database has no daily hook, so it could never acquire the data cheaply and had to buy or scrape it; it was a database *first*. Here the lookup is a **projection of records the platform already holds** for other reasons — captured because people were booking sessions, paying engineers, and sharing stems. It costs almost nothing to publish and it makes every record more valuable to its owner. This is the exact structure of D-18: consolidation is the daily hook, provenance is the byproduct, and the lookup is the byproduct becoming visible. Building it *first* would be the Jaxsta failure; building it *from* the capture is the thesis. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect **trust level** to be the differentiator, and the candidate list treats this as a search feature | The candidate said "Public Rights Lookup ('who owns this?')" — a query surface. But every incumbent already has a query surface; they lose on *reliability*, because they cannot tell you whether an entry came from four people agreeing in a room or one person filling in a form. A lookup that renders both identically has thrown away its only advantage and is competing on UI. | ✅ CONFIRMED — trust level is the first-class differentiator. The badge rendered is the record-level **provenance class** (`captured` / `asserted` / `imported`, [rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md) CX-07), derived from — not identical to — the per-event trust levels the chain carries ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) D-01). It also makes the platform's honesty legible at exactly the moment it is tempting to fudge: WeJammin's own imported catalogue data ([09.03.01 Q-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) will render at a *lower* trust level than its native records, publicly. That is uncomfortable and correct, and it is the strongest possible demonstration that the trust level means something. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The lookup reads a **publication-safe projection**, never the ledger | Privacy must be opt-in by construction. A direct read makes every future schema field public unless someone remembers to exclude it (R-02). |
| D-02 | **Percentages are not public** by default | The use case (find who to call) is served by names and roles; percentages add only harm (see DT-01). Owner's call to revisit. |
| D-03 | **Trust level is a first-class, visible attribute**, and the value rendered is the record-level **provenance class** — `captured` / `asserted` / `imported` (CX-07) | It is the only thing distinguishing this from the incumbents — including, publicly and uncomfortably, for WeJammin's own imported data (see DT-03). The chain's per-event `platform-witnessed` / `evidence-attached` / `asserted` ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) D-01) are the facts the class is derived from, not the badge itself; the derivation is Q-05. |
| D-04 | Disputes are **never** exposed publicly | "Ownership disputed" on a public record is a reputational weapon; the platform would be supplying it. |
| D-05 | The Fan view is a **separate projection**, not a filtered one | Structural impossibility beats policy prevention for the highest-volume, lowest-trust audience. |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | **[OWNER]** **What exactly is public?** The tier table above is a proposal, not a decision. The sub-domain's largest product call; see [09.06 Q-01](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md) and [domain 09 Q-06](../rights-ownership-index.md), which are the same call. Partially fenced already — D-02 keeps percentages out, D-04 keeps disputes out, and [rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md) CX-05 states "percentages and deal terms have no code path to the lookup" — but the *positive* line (what does project) is undecided. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-02 | **[OWNER]** Can an owner **opt out** of the public record? Hiding defeats the purpose; forcing exposure is coercive; and a lookup with holes is less reliable for everyone. No sibling decision settles it: D-01/D-05 govern *what* projects, never *whether* a given owner's record projects at all. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-03 | **[OWNER]** Is the lookup **open to the internet** or gated to users? Open maximises reliance (and therefore the record's value to its owner); it also feeds the scrapers. See [09.06 Q-03](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md). A competitive-position call (D-04 makes the lookup the demand side of the thesis); the access model and scraping posture are downstream of the answer, not a substitute for it. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-04 | Does the lookup **federate** to external sources (the MLC, societies, Discogs) when WeJammin has no record? It fixes the coverage problem and imports data whose trust level we cannot vouch for. An external-source integration and data-flow question — the trust-tier consequence is already governed by CX-07's provenance class (imported never renders as captured), so what is left is where the boundary sits. Same shape as [15.02 Q-02](../../15-gear-registry-ownership/15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery/15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md), already routed to integration architecture. | User | `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Q-05 | **How does a record's provenance class derive from the chain's per-event trust levels?** The layering is settled (CX-07: chain owns the facts, lookup owns the derived badge, D-03 above), and two of three cases are obvious — an establishing event that is `platform-witnessed` yields `captured`, one that is `asserted` yields `asserted`. Two edges are not: (a) an off-platform transfer recorded with a paper contract attached is `evidence-attached` at the event level ([09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) Edge Cases) and has **no** counterpart class — does the record render `captured` (the platform did see a signed instrument) or `asserted` (the platform was not in the room)? (b) `imported` is a class with **no** counterpart event trust level — the chain would record a feed ingest as `asserted`, which loses the "nobody on the platform attested" distinction CX-07 depends on for WeJammin's own bulk data. Both edges change what a licensor sees on the badge the domain calls its differentiator, so neither is safe to infer. Blocks nothing in v1 (09.03 is `SHOULD`/`COULD` throughout per [moscow-ledger.md](../../moscow-ledger.md)); needed before the lookup renders a badge over any non-captured record. | Agent | `/write-architecture-spec` (09.06 shard; reconcile with the 09.03 shard's event model) |
