## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Identifier Issuance ↔ Public Rights Lookup

**Relationship**: The lookup is only as good as its keys. "Who owns *Waiting Room* by *The Colours*?" is
an unanswerable question — there are four songs with that title and two bands with that name. "Who owns
`GBAHT2600123`?" has exactly one answer. Identifiers are what make the public record *resolvable* rather
than *searchable*, and the difference is the entire gap between a database and a reference.

This is also why identifier coverage bounds the lookup's usefulness: a work with no ISWC can be found
by a human browsing but cannot be *cited* by a machine, which is what a DSP, a society or a licensing
system actually needs.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Wants their work resolvable so their credits can be cited rather than claimed — `meta/personas.md`'s stated success criterion.
- **Producer**: Same, plus needs to resolve *other* recordings when clearing samples or tracing a master.
- **Operator**: Read-only — a studio might resolve a recording to check who to license for a room playlist. Thin, but real.
- **Fan**: Read-only — resolves a track they are listening to. The credits-curiosity case, and the only reason a Fan is here at all.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The rights object owns its identifiers; the lookup only reads. No write path from the lookup, ever — a public surface that could mutate the registry would be the domain's largest attack surface.
2. **Trigger chain**: Identifier issued → object becomes resolvable → lookup can answer by key. If no identifier exists, the lookup degrades to fuzzy title matching, which must be visibly marked as a guess rather than an answer.
3. **Permission intersection**: Issuing requires ownership; resolving requires nothing. The asymmetry is the design — anyone may ask, only owners may assert.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None. Lookups are not events, and telling an artist "someone looked you up" would be a surveillance product nobody asked for.
5. **State transition conflict**: An identifier reassigned or reconciled while cached externally means a stale key resolving to a moved target. ISRCs must never be reused, which is the standard's own answer to exactly this.

### CX-02: Proof-of-Creation Timestamping ↔ Copyright Office Registration

**Relationship**: Two rungs on the same ladder. Timestamping is free, instant, and automatic — it says
*this existed, in this form, on this date, and these people were attached to it*. Registration is paid,
slow, and statutory — in the US it says *you may now sue, and you may claim statutory damages*.

The relationship is that registration should be **assembly, not recollection**. Filing requires the
creation date, the authors, the ownership, and a deposit copy — and a platform that was in the room
already holds every one of them. Today an independent musician who wants to register faces a form
demanding facts they must reconstruct from memory and a session folder. If WeJammin has been
timestamping since the session closed, the form is pre-filled from records made when they were true.

That is D-18's structure again: consolidation put the platform in the room, being in the room produced
the evidence, and the evidence makes the statutory step cheap.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The registrant. Registration is an author's act; the timestamp is their evidence.
- **Producer**: Full on timestamping (they are in the room and their attestation is what makes it credible), **none** on registration — they are not the author of the composition and cannot file for someone else's work.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The timestamp is immutable evidence; the registration is a filing referencing it. Neither mutates the other.
2. **Trigger chain**: Session closes → timestamp created automatically → (optionally, later) registration assembled from it. If registration fails at the office, the timestamp is unaffected — the evidence stands regardless of the filing.
3. **Permission intersection**: Anyone in the session can be attached to a timestamp; only an author can register.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Registration outcomes notify the registrant and the ledger parties; timestamps notify nobody (they are ambient).
5. **State transition conflict**: A work amended ([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) after registration means the filing describes a superseded ledger. Whether that requires a supplementary registration is a real question — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 09.06.02 Proof-of-Creation Timestamping | [09.03.05 Copyright Term & Public Domain](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) | Tempting — both concern dates and copyright duration. But copyright term runs from the **author's death** (life+70) in most territories, not from creation or fixation. A timestamp establishes *that* a work existed on a date, which is an evidentiary fact for authorship disputes; it contributes nothing to a term calculation. Linking them would suggest a creation date drives expiry, which is the wrong mental model and would mislead every user who saw it. |
| R-02 | 09.06.04 Public Rights Lookup | [09.01.02 Ownership Ledger](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) | Rejected as a **direct read**, deliberately and importantly. The lookup must consume a *projection* — a filtered, publication-safe view — never the ledger itself. A direct coupling would make every future ledger field public by default, so adding a private commercial term to the schema would leak it to the internet unless someone remembered to exclude it. Privacy must be opt-in by construction, not by vigilance. See [09.06 Q-01](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md). |
| R-03 | 09.06.03 Copyright Office Registration | 09.06.01 Identifier Issuance | A copyright registration number is not an identifier in the ISRC/ISWC sense — it identifies a *filing*, not a *work*, and it is jurisdiction-specific. Two registrations of one work in two countries are two numbers for one thing; two ISRCs on one recording is an error. Merging the concepts would corrupt the identifier model, which depends on one-key-one-object. |
