# Feature: Proof-of-Creation Timestamping

> **Number**: 09.06.02
> **Parent**: [Rights Evidence & Public Record](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A permanent, tamper-evident record that **this work existed in this form on this date, with these people
attached to it**. Musicians have improvised versions of this forever — the "poor man's copyright" of
posting yourself a sealed envelope, which does almost nothing legally and is the clearest possible signal
of an unmet need.

The feature is a **byproduct**, not a task. If the platform is in the room when the session closes, the
timestamp costs the user nothing: no form, no fee, no decision. It is D-18's structure applied to
evidence — consolidation put us there, being there makes the record free.

> **Split from sweep candidate 21** (Proof-of-Creation Timestamping & Copyright Office Registration).
> Timestamping is free, instant, automatic, and universal; registration is paid, slow, external, and
> jurisdiction-specific ([09.06.03](./09.06.03-copyright-office-registration.md)). One node could not be
> both.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The author. Gets timestamps automatically on everything they touch — the demo at 2am, the session, the stem. Sees a chronology of their own creative work that is *evidentially useful*, which is the thing the sealed envelope was always trying to be. Cites it in an authorship dispute ([09.04.02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.02-split-ownership-dispute-cases.md)). |
| Producer | Full | Present at fixation, and their attachment to a timestamp is what makes it credible — a self-timestamped file proves you had a file; a timestamp with an independent witness attached proves something much harder to argue with. `meta/personas.md` makes this their defining structural value; here it is nearly free. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator does not attest to authorship of the work made in their room. Explicit absence — though see Q-03, where a studio's booking record is arguably corroboration. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. Timestamps are private evidence, not public record. |

## Behavior

A timestamp captures: a **content hash** of the fixed material, the **datetime**, the **parties
attached**, and the **context** (which session, which project). It is created automatically at fixation
events — session close, stem upload, work creation — and it is immutable thereafter.

What it proves and does not prove is the whole design constraint:

- ✅ **Proves**: this exact content existed on this date, and these people were attached to it here.
- ❌ **Does not prove**: that they wrote it, that it is original, that nobody else made it first.

The strength does not come from cryptography. It comes from **independent parties being attached at the
time** — a hash proves a file has not changed; a multi-party session record proves several people who
had no reason to collude were present at a moment.

### Happy Path

1. Producer closes a session; stems and a mix are fixed.
2. Platform creates a timestamp automatically. Nobody does anything.
3. Every session participant is attached; each can see it on their own profile.
4. The timestamp is permanent and cannot be backdated or altered by anyone, including the platform.
5. Years later, an authorship dispute arises; the timestamp is cited with the session record and the attached parties.
6. If the user chooses, it is also the source material for a statutory registration ([09.06.03](./09.06.03-copyright-office-registration.md), CX-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User wants to timestamp something made off-platform | Allowed — upload and timestamp. But the evidence is much weaker: it proves the file existed on the upload date, not the creation date. | "Timestamped 14 June — this proves the file existed then, not when it was made." Say the limit plainly. |
| Someone timestamps material they did not write | The platform cannot know. It proves possession, not authorship. | The limit must be stated in the UI, or users will believe they hold proof they do not have. |
| Two people timestamp the same material | Both timestamps are valid facts. Whoever is earlier had it earlier — which is evidence, not a verdict. | Both shown in a dispute; the platform does not rule ([09.04 D-04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md)). |
| Content changes after timestamping | New content, new timestamp. The old one still attests to the old form — that chronology is itself valuable. | Version chronology; nothing is overwritten. |
| User deletes the file | The hash and the record survive; the content may not. | "Timestamp retained; content removed." Interacts with GDPR erasure ([09.01.02 Q-03](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)). |
| Platform is accused of backdating | The platform's own attestation is worth little if the platform could have written it. | The reason for external anchoring — see Q-01 and DT-03. |
| Session with one participant (a solo demo at 2am) | Timestamped, and weak — a self-attestation with no witness. | Still worth having; the honest framing is "better than nothing", not "proof". |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Timeline requested | Skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing timestamped | Should be rare — timestamping is automatic. If empty, the automation is not firing |
| Populated | Timestamps exist | Chronology: what, when, who was attached, which session |
| Partial | Timestamped, content deleted | Record shown, content marked removed |
| Error | Creation failed | **Surfaced loudly.** A silently missing timestamp is a silently missing defence |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Timestamping proves **authorship** — that is what users want it for, and it is how "poor man's copyright" is understood | The user's mental model, and the reason they would use the feature | ❌ REJECTED, and the gap must be closed with copy rather than exploited. A timestamp proves **possession at a time**, not creation. Someone who steals a demo and timestamps it has produced a perfectly valid timestamp of stolen material. The temptation is to let users believe the stronger claim — it makes the feature sound better and nobody finds out until a dispute. That is precisely the "asking people to remember" failure inverted: a false sense of proof is worse than no proof, because it stops people from getting real protection. What genuinely strengthens it is **multi-party attachment**: several independent people present at a moment is hard to fake, and that is a byproduct of the platform being in the room, not of the hash. |
| DT-02 | This should be **opt-in** — users should choose what to timestamp | Autonomy instinct; also it seems presumptuous to hash everything | ❌ REJECTED — opt-in evidence is evidence you have when you thought you needed it, which is never the moment you actually need it. Nobody at 2am on a demo knows that this is the one that gets contested in 2031. The value is in **automatic, universal, ambient** capture: the timestamp exists because you used the platform, not because you predicted a dispute. This is the same structural argument as [09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) DT-04 (the dangerous failure is the *absent* record, not the wrong one) and it is why timestamping is a byproduct rather than a task. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would ask **"why would anyone believe WeJammin's timestamp?"** — and no candidate addresses it | Self-attestation is weak by construction: a platform saying "we recorded this on the 3rd" is only as good as the platform's own integrity and incentives. In a dispute, opposing counsel's first move is to ask whether WeJammin could have written that record later. | ⚠️ CONFIRMED as a real gap with a **known and cheap** fix: anchor the hashes externally — an RFC 3161 trusted timestamping authority, or periodic publication of a Merkle root to a public medium. This is the one place in the domain where cryptographic anchoring earns its keep, and it is worth contrasting with [09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) DT-01, where blockchain was rejected outright. The difference is precise: there, the claim was "X owns 25%" and cryptography cannot make a false claim true (the oracle problem). Here, the claim is "this hash existed by this date" — a statement about the *record itself*, which is exactly what tamper-evidence can establish. **Anchor the timestamp; do not anchor the ownership.** Routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Timestamping is **automatic and universal**, never opt-in | Nobody knows at 2am which demo gets contested in 2031. Opt-in evidence is absent exactly when it is needed (see DT-02). |
| D-02 | The UI states plainly that it proves **possession, not authorship** | A false sense of proof stops people seeking real protection — worse than no proof (see DT-01). |
| D-03 | Strength comes from **multi-party attachment**, not from the hash | Independent people present at a moment is hard to fake; a hash only proves a file did not change. |
| D-04 | Timestamp hashes are **externally anchored** | A self-attested timestamp is only as good as the platform's own integrity, which opposing counsel will attack first (see DT-03). |
| D-05 | Timestamp creation failures are **surfaced loudly** | A silently missing timestamp is a silently missing defence. |
