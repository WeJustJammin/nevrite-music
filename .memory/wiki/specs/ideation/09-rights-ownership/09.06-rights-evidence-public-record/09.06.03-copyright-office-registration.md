# Feature: Copyright Office Registration Filing

> **Number**: 09.06.03
> **Parent**: [Rights Evidence & Public Record](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Filing a work with a national copyright office. Copyright exists automatically on fixation — registration
does not create it — but in the US registration is what makes it **enforceable**: you cannot sue for
infringement of an unregistered work, and registering before infringement (or within three months of
publication) is what unlocks statutory damages and attorney's fees. Without it, an independent artist
whose song is stolen has a right they cannot afford to enforce.

The platform's contribution is that filing becomes **assembly rather than recollection**. Registration
demands the creation date, the authors, the ownership, and a deposit copy — every one of which the
platform already holds if it was in the room (see
[09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-cx.md](./09.06-rights-evidence-public-record-cx.md) CX-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The author and registrant. Sees which of their works are registered and which are exposed; files with a pre-filled form assembled from records made when they were true rather than reconstructed from a session folder. Sees the three-month post-publication window that determines whether statutory damages are available — the deadline nobody knows about until they need it and it has passed. |
| Producer | None | Registration is an **author's** act. A producer is not the author of the composition, and cannot file for someone else's work. Where they authored something themselves, they file as a Musician. Explicit absence — a deliberate scoping call, matching [09.03.03](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.03-termination-rights-notice-windows.md). |
| Operator | None | No authorship, no filing. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility of filing. Registration **status** is a fact the public lookup may surface ([09.06.04](./09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), CX-03) — registered works carry a legal presumption a self-claim does not. |

## Behavior

Registration is an **external, paid, slow** transaction with a government office (US eCO; other national
offices differ or do not exist). Its shape is unlike anything else in the domain: a fee, a queue measured
in months, a deposit copy, and an outcome the platform does not control.

The platform assembles from what it holds: authors ([09.01](../09.01-rights-registry/)), creation date
([09.06.02](./09.06.02-proof-of-creation-timestamping.md)), ownership ([09.01.02](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)),
and the deposit material. It submits, tracks, and records the outcome.

Two decisions are the user's, and the platform should surface both rather than assume: **what** to
register (a single work, or a group registration of up to 20 unpublished works in one fee — dramatically
cheaper for a prolific writer) and **when** (before publication, or within three months of it, to
preserve statutory damages).

### Happy Path

1. Musician finishes a work; the platform already holds authors, creation date, ledger and audio.
2. Platform notes the work is unregistered and surfaces the three-month window if it has been published.
3. Musician chooses to register — individually, or as a group with nine other unpublished works in one fee.
4. Form is pre-filled from the platform's records; the user reviews rather than reconstructs.
5. Filed; fee paid; status tracked through the office's queue.
6. Registration issues months later; the number is recorded and surfaces in the lookup as a presumption of validity.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User is not in the US | The US Copyright Office is the one with real enforcement consequences; many territories have no registry at all. | "Registration is a US mechanism" — stated, not silently absent. A non-US user still benefits if they might ever enforce in the US. |
| Work has multiple authors | All must be named; the ledger already knows them. | Pre-filled from the consented split. This is the assembly-not-recollection payoff in one screen. |
| Three-month window missed | Statutory damages unavailable. The right survives; the ability to afford enforcing it does not. | The window must be surfaced **before** it passes; after is a condolence. |
| Office rejects the filing | Fee may be lost; the reason may be technical. | Reason surfaced; refile path. The platform does not control the outcome and should not imply it does. |
| Work amended after registration | The filing describes a superseded ledger; a supplementary registration may be needed. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — CX-02. |
| Group registration rules change | The office's rules move (they have, repeatedly). | The platform's assembly logic must track them or it silently files invalid applications. |
| User expects registration to *create* copyright | Extremely common misunderstanding. | "You already own this. Registration is what lets you enforce it." The distinction is the whole feature. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert would expect **group registration** to be the headline, and no candidate mentions it | The candidate said "Copyright Office Registration" — one work, one filing. But the US office allows up to 20 unpublished works in a single application for one fee, and for a working songwriter that is the difference between registering everything and registering nothing: individually it is ~$45 a song, which nobody with 60 demos a year will pay. The platform holds the whole catalogue and knows exactly which works are unregistered and unpublished — it can assemble the optimal group automatically, which is a service no individual can perform for themselves without a spreadsheet and a rules lawyer. | ✅ CONFIRMED — group registration is the actual product here. The single-work filing is the special case, not the default. This inverts the candidate's framing entirely, and it is a clean example of consolidation buying provenance: only a platform holding the whole catalogue can optimise across it. |
| DT-02 | The platform should **auto-register** everything — it has the data, it knows the windows, it maximises protection | The strongest form of the value proposition, and it removes the decision nobody remembers to make | ❌ REJECTED — it **spends the user's money** without asking. Registration carries a real per-filing fee, and auto-registering every 2am demo would bill a musician for protecting material they will never release. It also makes irreversible choices on their behalf: registering a work *published* the wrong way, or filing a group that forecloses a better grouping later. And it puts the platform in the position of having made a legal filing in a user's name — with their signature attesting to facts the platform assembled. The right shape is the domain's settled pattern once more (**notify, don't execute**, per [09.03.02 D-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md) and [09.04.01 D-02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md)): tell them the window is closing, assemble the optimal group, pre-fill the form, and let them press the button. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Group registration is the default path**; single-work filing is the special case | ~$45/song individually means a prolific writer registers nothing. Only a platform holding the whole catalogue can assemble the optimal group (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | The platform **never auto-registers** | It spends the user's money, makes irreversible filing choices, and puts their signature on facts the platform assembled (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | The three-month post-publication window is surfaced **before** it closes | After it passes, the information is a condolence. |
| D-04 | The UI states that registration **enables enforcement**, it does not create copyright | The most common misunderstanding, and correcting it is the feature's honest framing. |
