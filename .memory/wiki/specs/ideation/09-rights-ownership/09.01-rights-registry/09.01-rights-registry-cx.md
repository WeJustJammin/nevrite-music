## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Work & Recording Records ↔ Ownership Ledger

**Relationship**: Every ledger is attached to exactly one rights object, and the object's type determines
the validation rule set. A ledger on a recording sums to 100%. A ledger on a work sums to 200% in the
conventional writer/publisher expression (or 100% if expressed as writer-share-only — the registry must
pick one canonical expression and convert at the edges, or it will silently double or halve people's
income). This is the single most consequential coupling in the domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Sees two ledgers side by side on one song; edits both; is the party harmed if the wrong invariant is applied.
- **Producer**: Sees both while running capture; typically drives the master ledger and prompts for the publishing one.
- **Operator**: No visibility — a studio is not a party to the ledger unless it holds a work-for-hire.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: `09.01.01` alone owns duplicate candidate handling. Its only automatic path is two current unclaimed, unconsented, conflict-free stubs whose `writer-name-canonical-v1` sets are exactly equal and whose identity/alias evidence does not establish distinct or unresolved writers. Before commit it rechecks every guard atomically; an ineligible/stale/competing candidate aborts with **no** link movement, ledger rewrite, concatenation, reconciliation, or partial mutation. A valid merge retains all source assertions, IDs, provenance, and merge lineage; consented/proposed paths remain human-consent flows. Canonical text equality is never person-identity resolution.
2. **Trigger chain**: Creating a rights object creates an empty ledger in `unallocated` state. It does not fail; it waits. Nothing downstream (release, licence, payout) may proceed against an unallocated ledger.
3. **Permission intersection**: Write access to the object does not imply write access to the ledger — a Producer may create the recording record while the ledger requires consent from every named party (see 09.02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A ledger change notifies every party named in it, including those whose share did not change (their *relative* position did).
5. **State transition conflict**: Two parties editing shares concurrently can produce a ledger that totals correctly but that neither agreed to. Ledger writes must be proposals requiring consent, never last-write-wins.

### CX-02: Sample & Interpolation Provenance ↔ Ownership Ledger

**Relationship**: The naive model treats a sample declaration as metadata (a flag: "contains a sample").
That is wrong for interpolations. When a work interpolates another work, the original's writers become
writers of the new work — commonly 15–50% of the publishing, settled by negotiation. So declaring an
interpolation is a **ledger-mutating event**: everyone already on the sheet gets diluted pro rata, and
because dilution changes their agreed share, everyone must re-consent (see 09.02.04).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The writer declaring the sample; also every co-writer whose share is about to shrink.
- **Producer**: Usually the person who knows a sample is in there — often the only person who knows.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility (the derived fact may surface in 09.06.04 public lookup).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ledger is owned by the rights object; the sample declaration proposes a mutation to it. The declaration must not be able to write shares directly.
2. **Trigger chain**: Declare sample → propose ledger dilution → re-consent from all existing parties → if any party refuses, the sample declaration stands (the fact is true regardless) but the ledger enters `disputed` and the work is release-blocked. **The fact and the settlement are separate records** — a declaration must never be reversible just because the split negotiation failed.
3. **Permission intersection**: Anyone with write access to the recording may declare a sample; nobody may unilaterally settle it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Fans out to every party on both ledgers of the sampling work, and — once the clearance is pursued via domain 11 — to the sampled work's owners.
5. **State transition conflict**: A sample declared *after* release is the expensive case. The work is already earning; the dilution is retroactive. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

### CX-03: Publishing Rights ↔ Ownership Ledger

**Relationship**: Co-publishing and administration deal terms are not additional free-standing ownership
claims. The ledger holds each nominal writer/publisher row; the publishing-rights record holds the deal
terms that derive the writer's effective share. Every publisher row is anchored to exactly one writer row,
so the ledger can prove the arithmetic boundary without interpreting the deal. The data representation of
that link — nested ownership versus flattened effective percentages — remains [09.01 Q-03](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md#open-questions), explicitly owned by `/create-prd-architecture`; none of the rules below selects it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Creates or changes a publishing relationship only for their own writer row; sees nominal and effective share as distinct values.
- **Producer**: May capture the ledger, but cannot create or modify a publisher relationship over a writer's share.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ownership ledger owns nominal rows and their invariant; [09.01.04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md) owns the co-pub/admin terms and derives effective share. A publisher row must remain anchored to one writer row, rather than becoming a free-standing claim ([09.01.02 Cross-Cut Notes](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#cross-cut-notes); [09.01.04 The invariant this feature contributes to the validator](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#the-invariant-this-feature-contributes-to-the-validator)).
2. **Trigger chain**: A writer declares or modifies their own publisher relationship. A co-pub assignment writes a transfer to chain of title; an admin appointment writes no transfer. A post-lock publisher signing is a transfer of that writer's own share, not a co-writer ledger amendment ([09.01.04 D-04 and D-06](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#decisions); [09.01.04 Cross-Cut Notes](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#cross-cut-notes)).
3. **Permission intersection**: Only the writer may create or modify their publisher relation. A named party receives read access to the complete ledger, not project or catalogue access; consent remains to the whole ledger ([09.01.04 D-06](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#decisions); [09.01.02 D-01 and D-07](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#decisions)).
4. **Notification fan-out**: When the nominal ledger changes, [CX-01](#cx-01-work--recording-records--ownership-ledger) notifies every named party, including those whose own nominal row did not change. The publisher relationship does not permit a change to another writer's row ([09.01.04 D-06](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#decisions)).
5. **State transition conflict**: A publisher transfer and a ledger amendment are distinct transitions. The former is limited to the writer's own share and recorded in chain of title; the latter invalidates the prior ledger version and follows the ledger's re-consent lifecycle ([09.01.04 D-04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#decisions); [09.01.02 D-06 and D-09](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#decisions)).

### CX-04: Master Rights Ownership ↔ Performer & Neighbouring Rights

**Relationship**: Two disjoint records over one recording. Master ownership answers "who owns this
master and gets paid when it is exploited". Performer eligibility answers "who played on this, at what
level (featured / non-featured), and is therefore entitled to equitable remuneration collected by a
neighbouring-rights society". The overlap is partial and the platform must not conflate them: a
featured artist is usually in both; a session drummer is usually in the performer record only; a label
or an investor may be in the master record only, having never been in the room.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The session player whose entire stake in this recording is the performer record — invisible if the platform only models ownership.
- **Producer**: Attests the performer record; their attestation is what makes it worth more than a self-claim.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both hang off the recording; neither owns the other. No merge conflict — they are independent lists.
2. **Trigger chain**: Capture (09.02) writes both from one session prompt. If the master ledger completes but the performer record does not, the recording is still valid — but a Musician silently loses an income stream. This asymmetry should be surfaced, not tolerated.
3. **Permission intersection**: A person can be listed as a performer with no ledger permission at all. Performer presence must not imply ownership rights, and ownership must not imply performer presence.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The performer record feeds domain 02 (credits) and domain 10 (society registration) — a change notifies both.
5. **State transition conflict**: Low risk; independent lifecycles.

### CX-06: Master Rights Ownership ↔ Publishing Rights

**Relationship**: Master and publishing are independent rights pools with different validation rules and
separate ledgers. They must open and be displayed together during capture, while remaining separately
editable. Their adjacent presentation labels their 100% master and 200% publishing totals, so a change to
one cannot be mistaken for a change to the other ([09.01.03 Happy Path](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md#happy-path); [CX-01](#cx-01-work--recording-records--ownership-ledger)).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Reviews and consents to each named ledger independently; sees master ownership/control and publishing nominal/effective shares as distinct rights positions.
- **Producer**: Creates the record and drives capture with both ledgers present; sees pair completeness rather than treating one consented ledger as complete capture.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pools are separate records with separate invariants: the master ledger totals 100%; the publishing ledger retains its writer/publisher expression. Neither pool owns or mutates the other ([CX-01](#cx-01-work--recording-records--ownership-ledger); [09.01.03 D-02](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md#decisions)).
2. **Trigger chain**: Creating the recording opens both ledgers `unallocated`; capture presents them side by side and names their distinct totals. The Producer sees pair completeness when one ledger is consented and the other untouched, rather than a single completion state ([09.01.03 Happy Path](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md#happy-path); [09.01.02 Role Lens](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#role-lens)).
3. **Permission intersection**: A party's rights are pool-specific. Master ownership/control does not create authority over a writer's publishing relationship, which only that writer may create or modify; named-party read remains scoped to the ledger in which they are named ([09.01.04 D-06](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md#decisions); [09.01.02 D-07](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#decisions)).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Each ledger change fans out to every party named in that ledger, including parties whose own share did not change. Separate pools therefore notify their own named parties; they do not imply a write to, or notification fan-out from, the other pool ([CX-01](#cx-01-work--recording-records--ownership-ledger)).
5. **State transition conflict**: Each ledger advances through its own proposal and consent lifecycle. A change invalidates consent for the changed ledger version; downstream release, licensing, and payout remain blocked against an `unallocated` ledger. Pair presentation exposes the mismatch without collapsing independent states into one ([09.01.02 D-06 and D-09](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md#decisions); [CX-01](#cx-01-work--recording-records--ownership-ledger)).
