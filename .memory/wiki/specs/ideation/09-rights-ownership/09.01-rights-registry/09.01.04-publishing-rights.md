# Feature: Publishing Rights & Deal Structures

> **Number**: 09.01.04
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The composition side of the duality: who wrote it, who publishes it, and what deal sits between them.
Publishing is where the arithmetic gets strange — the same 100% of a song is conventionally expressed
as 200%, split into a **writer's share** (100%) and a **publisher's share** (100%) — and where the deal
structures (self-published, co-publishing, administration, sub-publishing by territory) determine the
gap between what a writer nominally owns and what they actually receive.

This feature holds the *structures*. The arithmetic that validates them is
[09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The songwriter. Declares their writer share; declares whether they self-publish (the independent default) or have a publisher, and on what deal. Sees their **effective** share — a writer with 25% on a 50/50 co-pub deal receives on 18.75% of the song, and that is the number that predicts their bank balance. Sees which territories are sub-published to whom, if any. |
| Producer | Config | A producer is frequently *also* a co-writer — the multi-hyphenate fact — in which case they hold a writer share and this is Full for them in that capacity. In their capacity as *producer*, they configure: they prompt for and enter writer names during capture, but cannot allocate publishing shares on a work they did not write. Their production contribution is compensated on the master side ([09.01.03](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md)), not here. |
| Operator | None | A venue or studio operator has no publishing interest. There is no arrangement in normal practice where operating a room confers a share of a composition. Explicit absence — do not build an Operator surface here. |
| Fan | None | No access. Writer *identity* may surface publicly via [09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md); writer *percentages* and deal terms are commercially sensitive and do not. |

## Behavior

A publishing ledger holds writer rows and publisher rows over a work. The deal structures it must
express:

| Structure | What it means | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-published** | The writer holds their own publisher share | The independent default and the platform's most common case. Must be the zero-effort path — and the row must exist rather than be blank ([09.01.02 D-03](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)). |
| **Co-publishing** | The publisher takes a portion of the publisher share, writer keeps the rest | The standard signed-writer deal. Creates a *share of a share* — nested, not flat. |
| **Administration** | The admin collects and takes a fee; ownership does not move | Frequently confused with co-pub by writers. An admin deal is not an assignment; conflating them mis-states the chain of title. |
| **Sub-publishing** | A local publisher handles a territory | Makes shares territory-varying, which is the model's hardest question (see [09.01 Q-01](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)). |

### Happy Path

1. Work record exists; publishing ledger opens `unallocated`.
2. During capture, writers are named and given writer shares totalling 100%.
3. Each writer declares their publishing position — self-published (default, one tap) or publisher-held.
4. Publisher rows are created accordingly; the ledger totals 200%; the validator passes.
5. All writers consent to the whole ledger; it locks.
6. Each writer's view shows nominal share, effective share, and who controls the difference.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Writer has no publisher | Self-held publisher row auto-created. | "You publish yourself — you hold both shares" rather than an empty field. Blank is what creates unmatched money. |
| Writer signs a publisher *after* the ledger locks | Not an amendment to the split — a **transfer** of their publisher share, recorded in [09.03.01 Chain of Title](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md). Does not require the other writers' consent, because their shares are untouched. | Their row shows the new publisher; co-writers are notified but not asked to approve. |
| Writer thinks an admin deal transferred ownership | The model distinguishes them; the UI must too. | Admin shown as "collects for you — you still own it"; co-pub shown as "owns part of your publisher share". |
| Sub-publishing splits a share by territory | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` pending the territory model decision. | `[PENDING]` |
| Writer is dead; estate holds the share | Estate is the party ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)). | Row shows the estate entity. |
| Publisher claims a share the writer never agreed to | Ledger enters `disputed` ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)). | Dispute banner; collections frozen. |
| Work has a writer who is not on WeJammin | Invited stub; ledger stays `proposed`. | "Waiting on 1 of 3" with chase. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The 200% convention is a legacy quirk we should normalise away internally and present as 100% | Engineering simplification instinct — 200% is genuinely confusing | ❌ REJECTED, with reservation. Every external counterparty — societies (CWR registrations), DSPs, publishers — speaks in writer-share/publisher-share terms. Normalising internally means converting at every boundary, and each conversion is a chance to halve or double someone's income. The convention is load-bearing, not decorative. **However**, which expression is canonical remains genuinely open (see Q-01) — this rejects only the assumption that it is a free internal choice. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **admin vs co-pub** to be distinguished, and the candidate list does not distinguish them | The sweep gave "Publishing Rights (writer/publisher shares, co-pub, admin)" as one bullet. But an admin deal moves no ownership while a co-pub deal does. If both are stored as "publisher row", the chain of title is wrong for every administered work — and the chain of title is the thing this domain sells. | ✅ CONFIRMED — deal type is a first-class attribute, and it determines whether a transfer is written to 09.03.01. This is a small modelling decision with a large truth consequence. |
| DT-03 | **Effective share** should be computed on demand rather than stored | Normalisation instinct | ⏳ DEFERRED to `/create-prd-architecture` — computing it is correct in principle but it is the number every user actually cares about and every forecast in [domain 23](../../23-career-finance-business/) depends on, across nested deals and territory overlays. Whether that computation is cheap enough to be live is an architecture question, not a product one. Flagged rather than guessed. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Admin and co-pub are structurally different**, not two labels on one row | Admin moves no ownership; co-pub does. Conflating them corrupts the chain of title for every administered work (see DT-02). |
| D-02 | Self-publishing is the **default and the one-tap path** | It is the most common case among the platform's users, and forcing a decision here taxes the capture moment. |
| D-03 | The 200% writer/publisher expression is **retained**, not normalised away | It is the language of every external counterparty; conversion at boundaries is where income gets halved (see DT-01). |
| D-04 | Signing a publisher post-lock is a **transfer, not an amendment** | It touches only that writer's own share, so it needs no co-writer consent — but it must appear in the chain of title. |
