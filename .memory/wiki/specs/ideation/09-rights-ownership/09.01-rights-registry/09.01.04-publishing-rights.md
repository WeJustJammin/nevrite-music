# Feature: Publishing Rights & Deal Structures

> **Number**: 09.01.04
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The composition side of the duality: who wrote it, who publishes it, and what deal sits between them.
Publishing is where the arithmetic gets strange — the same 100% of a song is conventionally expressed
as 200%, split into a **writer's share** (100%) and a **publisher's share** (100%) — and where the deal
structures (self-published, co-publishing, administration, sub-publishing by territory) determine the
gap between what a writer nominally owns and what they actually receive.

That gap is the feature's reason to exist. A writer who "owns 25% of the song" may receive on 18.75%
of it, only part of which is money that ever reaches them, and only in territories where nobody else
is collecting first. **No existing tool tells a working writer that number.** Because WeJammin holds
the deal structure at the moment the writer declares it — not reconstructed from a contract they
cannot find — it can.

This feature holds the *structures*. The arithmetic that validates them is
[09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md); this feature contributes one invariant to that
validator (the anchoring rule, D-05) that arithmetic alone cannot see.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The songwriter. Declares their writer share; declares whether they self-publish (the independent default, one tap) or have a publisher, and on which deal type. **Names the publisher on their own writer row and only their own** (D-06) — no co-writer, and no Producer, may name a publisher over a share they did not write. Sees their **effective share** broken out by right type and territory rather than as one number (D-07): a writer with 25% on a 50/50 co-pub deal receives on 18.75% of the song worldwide, of which 12.5 points arrive direct from their PRO and 6.25 points route through the publisher and are exposed to recoupment. Sees which territories are sub-published to whom, and what that costs them there. Sees the three truths kept apart (D-09): consented on WeJammin, registered at the society, actually collecting. |
| Producer | Config | A producer is frequently *also* a co-writer — the multi-hyphenate fact — in which case they hold a writer row and are Full **in that capacity, over that row only**. In their capacity as *producer* they configure the capture: they prompt for and enter writer names and prompt each writer to declare a publishing position, but they cannot create, edit, or anchor a publisher row on a work they did not write, and cannot name their own publishing company on a co-writer's share (D-06 — this is the specific attack anchoring exists to close). A producer-writer whose company genuinely is offering to administer a co-writer's share may do so, but it renders as a separate, plain-language assignment consent addressed to that co-writer — never folded into ledger consent (D-12). Explicit absence: do not build an Operator surface here. |
| Fan | None | No access, and no filtered projection of one. ## Behavior

A publishing ledger holds **writer rows** and **publisher rows** over a work, in the conventional
200% expression: each writer's contribution generates writer points *and* publisher points in equal
measure, and the two pools each total 100%.

The structural rule that makes this a feature rather than a second column of numbers: **a publisher
row is never a free-standing percentage of the work — it is a share of a named writer's share**
(D-05). Every publisher row is anchored to exactly one writer row. The industry standard agrees so
literally that CWR has a record type for it: **PWR — "Publisher For Writer"** exists because the link
is the fact, not an implementation detail.

### The invariant this feature contributes to the validator

For a publishing ledger to be valid:

1. `Σ writer_share(W) over all writers W = 100%` — exactly, as exact rationals ([09.01.02 D-02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)).
2. **For every writer W**: `Σ (publisher rows anchored to W) = writer_share(W)` — exactly.
3. The 100% publisher pool is therefore a **derived consequence** of rule 2, not an independent check.

Rule 2 is the one arithmetic alone cannot see. A ledger where Publisher X holds 60 publisher points
while representing writers who wrote 25% between them totals 200 and is *arithmetically valid and
semantically impossible*. Only the anchoring rule catches it.

*Worked example — the ledger the Overview describes:*

| Row | Anchor | Points (of 200) |
|---|---|---|
| Writer A | — | 25 (writer) |
| A's own publishing company | Writer A | 12.5 (publisher) |
| Publisher X (50/50 co-pub with A) | Writer A | 12.5 (publisher) |
| Writer B | — | 75 (writer) |
| B — self-held publisher row | Writer B | 75 (publisher) |
| **Total** | | **200** ✓ — and A's publisher pool (12.5+12.5) = A's writer share (25) ✓ |

A's effective share = 25 + 12.5 = 37.5 / 200 = **18.75%**.

### The deal structures it must express

| Structure | What it means | Conventional shape | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-published** | The writer holds their own publisher share | 100% of their own publisher points | The independent default and the platform's most common case. Must be the zero-effort path — and the row must exist rather than be blank ([09.01.02 D-03](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)). |
| **Co-publishing** | The publisher takes a portion of the writer's publisher share; ownership moves | Conventionally 50% of that writer's publisher points | The standard signed-writer deal. Creates a *share of a share* — nested, not flat. Writes a transfer to [09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md). |
| **Administration** | The admin collects and retains a fee; **ownership does not move** | Conventionally a 10–15% fee | Frequently confused with co-pub by writers. An admin deal is not an assignment; conflating them mis-states the chain of title. Writes **no** transfer. |
| **Sub-publishing** | A local publisher collects in a territory for a term, retaining a fee | Conventionally 10–25%, at source or at receipt | Makes **collection** territory-varying while ownership stays put. Recorded as a grant via [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md). |

**Conventional rates are shown as reference text beside the field, never prefilled into it.** A
prefilled 50/50 that the writer never confirmed is the platform fabricating a deal term — precisely
what [domain D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md) forbids. Deal type ≠ self-published requires an
explicit rate; silence is not a default.

### Ownership share vs collection share

Two different numbers, and the industry has always known it: CWR declares **ownership shares** on the
publisher/writer records and **collection shares** on the territory records, separately and per right
type. They routinely differ — an administrator may **own 0% and collect 100%** in a territory,
accounting to the owners afterward. Collection share is therefore a **declarable value, not a
derivation of ownership share** (D-08). A model that derives collection from ownership cannot express
an ordinary admin deal, and will file wrong.

### Effective share is a matrix, not a number

The single headline percentage is the number every user wants and the number that is most often a lie
(D-07). It varies along three axes:

| Axis | Why it varies |
|---|---|
| **Right type** | Publishing shares can be carved by right — performance, mechanical, sync, print — and commonly are (a writer keeping sync while a publisher takes mechanical). CWR carries PR / MR / SR ownership shares as three distinct fields for exactly this reason. |
| **Territory** | Sub-publishing takes a fee in-territory. The same writer's collection is one number worldwide and a smaller one in Japan. |
| **Channel** | In the US and most PRO territories the **writer's share of performance income is paid direct by the PRO and never touches the publisher**. The publisher's half routes through the publisher and is exposed to recoupment. For an unrecouped writer the direct-paid half is the *only* money that arrives — 12.5 points of the worked example's 18.75, not 18.75. |

So the display is a right-type × territory breakdown with a qualified headline, and the number is
labelled **"share of income"** — never a forecast. Recoupment and advances live in
[domain 23](../../23-career-finance-business/) and [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/); this
feature must not claim to predict a bank balance it has no data for.

### Territory, and why ownership does not need it asked

**Ownership is worldwide by nature.** A writer owns their share everywhere a copyright subsists; that
is not a defaulted-in assumption, it is what the right is. Territory enters the model only through a
**grant** — a sub-publishing deal carving collection in a country set (D-10). This is what reconciles
[domain D-08](../rights-ownership-index.md) ("territory is a dimension of the ownership model") with
[domain D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md) ("silence must read as silence"): the dimension exists in
the model from day one, and capture does not tax the writer with a territory question they have no
answer to. Consistent with [09.03.02 D-03](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md),
a *grant* with no recorded territory is flagged incomplete — never silently read as worldwide.

Territory sets are **time-versioned**, because countries are. A sub-publishing grant made for a
territory that has since split, merged, or left an economic grouping is flagged for human review, never
silently remapped. CISAC's TIS vocabulary is the industry's territory language; which vocabulary the
model adopts is [/create-prd-architecture](../rights-ownership-index.md)'s call, not this feature's.

### Three truths, never rendered as one

A ledger has three independent states that the platform must never collapse (D-09):

| Truth | What it means | Who decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **Consented** | The parties agree this is the split | The parties, on WeJammin |
| **Registered** | A society/DSP holds a matching registration | The society, on its own systems |
| **Collecting** | Money is actually being matched and paid against it | Reality |

A work can be perfectly consented, never registered, and earning into the black box — the exact
outcome this domain exists to prevent, reached with a flawless internal record. The gap is detectable
and must be surfaced.

### Happy Path

1. Work record exists ([09.01.01](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)); its publishing ledger opens `unallocated`.
2. During capture, the Producer (or a writer) names the writers and enters writer shares; the running total and remainder are visible on every keystroke ([09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)).
3. Writer shares total 100%; the writer pool validates.
4. Each writer is prompted — individually, on their own row — to declare their publishing position. **Self-published is one tap** (D-02); it creates a self-held publisher row anchored to them for their full publisher points.
5. A writer declaring a publisher picks the **deal type** (co-pub / admin / sub-pub is a later grant), names the publisher entity, and enters the rate. Publisher rows are created anchored to that writer.
6. The anchoring invariant validates per writer: each writer's publisher pool equals their writer share. The ledger totals 200%; the validator passes; the ledger moves to `proposed`.
7. Every named party — writers **and** anchored publishers — consents to the whole ledger's *shares, publisher identities and anchoring*. Deal economics are not in the consent surface (D-12).
8. All parties consent; the ledger moves to `consented` and locks. Co-pub rows write an assignment to [09.03.01 Chain of Title](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md); admin rows write none.
9. Each writer's view shows nominal share, effective share broken out by right type and territory, and who controls the difference.
10. The work shows its **one-stop status**: "Licensable with 1 approval" or "Needs 3 approvals — A's publisher, B, C's admin" (D-14).
11. Registration proceeds via [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/); the ledger's `registered` truth updates independently of its `consented` truth, and the two are shown separately.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Writer has no publisher | Self-held publisher row auto-created, anchored to them, for their full publisher points. | "You publish yourself — you hold both shares" rather than an empty field. Blank is what creates unmatched money. |
| **Self-published writer has no publisher entity / IPI at their society** | Declaration is **accepted and consent is never blocked** — the fact is true. But the self-held publisher share is uncollectable until a publisher entity with an IPI exists at their PRO, and the platform knows it. Flagged at declaration; hard-blocked only at society registration ([domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)). | "You hold your publisher share — but nobody can pay it to you yet. You need a publishing entity registered with [PRO]." One-tap route into [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) setup. The most common silent black-box generator on the platform, caught at source. |
| Publisher row entered as a free-standing % of the work | Rejected at entry — there is no such object (D-05). | The field asks "publisher for which writer?" before it asks "how much?". Anchoring is the first question, not a validation afterthought. |
| Publisher rows anchored to W total ≠ W's writer share | Save blocked, with the specific remainder named per writer — not a global "doesn't total 200". | "Publisher X holds 12.5 of your 25 points — 12.5 unaccounted. Who has the rest?" |
| Ledger totals 200% but a publisher's share exceeds its anchor writers' contributions | Caught by the anchoring invariant, which pure arithmetic passes. | The exact class of error the 200% check cannot see, named plainly rather than surfaced as a total mismatch. |
| **Producer (also a co-writer) names their own publishing company on a co-writer's row** | Blocked. Only the writer may anchor a publisher to their own share (D-06). | "Only [co-writer] can name their publisher." No override, no admin path. This is the persona anti-pattern in `meta/personas.md` — a Producer pushing a share through "while contributors aren't paying attention" — closed structurally rather than by policy. |
| Producer-writer's company genuinely is administering a co-writer's share | Allowed — it is an ordinary deal. But it is surfaced to that co-writer as a **distinct assignment consent** in plain language, addressed to them, never folded into ledger consent (D-12). | "You are giving [Producer]'s company the right to collect your publisher share and keep 15%." Separate tap. The platform does not judge the deal; it refuses to let it be invisible. |
| Writer thinks an admin deal transferred ownership | The model distinguishes them (D-01); the UI must too. No transfer is written for admin. | Admin: "collects for you — **you still own it**". Co-pub: "**owns part** of your publisher share". |
| Writer signs a publisher *after* the ledger locks | Not an amendment to the split — a **transfer** of their own publisher share (D-04), recorded in [09.03.01](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md). No co-writer consent, because their shares are untouched. | Their row shows the new publisher; co-writers are notified but not asked to approve. |
| …and then the writers want to correct the split | The new publisher is now an **anchored party** and therefore a consent-holder on any amendment that dilutes their anchor (D-11). They may refuse. | "Amending this needs 4 consents — including Publisher X, who wasn't here when you wrote it." The platform does **not** adjudicate ([domain D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)); the amendment fails or the ledger enters `disputed` ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)). The consequence of signing a publisher is that your co-writers' ability to fix a mistake now depends on a party they never met. It is true; it is said out loud. |
| **Sub-publishing splits collection by territory** | The grant is recorded via [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md): grantor = the publisher row (which may be a self-held one), grantee = the sub-publisher, right = collection, territory = a country set, term, fee, at-source or at-receipt. **Ownership rows do not move.** A territory-scoped collection share is created, anchored to the granting publisher row — second-order anchoring: writer → publisher → sub-publisher(territory). Within a territory and right type, collection shares total 100%. | Territory breakdown per writer: "Worldwide 18.75% · Japan 15.9% after [Sub-pub]'s 15%". At-receipt deals are labelled as such — money passing through two hands is charged twice, and the writer sees it before they sign, not on a statement three years later. |
| Two sub-publishers granted overlapping territory | Conflict — both cannot be valid. Detected **at recording time**, when the second grant is entered, not at collection time. Consistent with [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md). | "[Sub-pub A] already collects in Japan until 2029." Blocked before it becomes unmatched money. |
| Sub-publishing term expires but a **retention period** runs | Collection continues for the sub-publisher on income *earned during the term*, for the retention period, on income *earned after* it does not. The two income vintages route differently through the same territory. | "Your Japan rights are back — but [Sub-pub] still collects on plays from before June 2029 until 2031." Without this, a writer sees money still going to a publisher whose deal they know has ended, and concludes they are being stolen from. |
| Grant made for a territory that no longer exists | Territory sets are time-versioned. The grant is flagged for human review; the platform **never silently remaps** it. Interpretation is not the platform's ([domain D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). | "This grant covers Yugoslavia. That territory no longer exists — what does the contract say now?" |
| Writer share carved by right type (writer keeps sync, publisher takes mechanical) | Supported — right type is a dimension, not a flag. Ownership and collection shares are declared per right type. | Per-right breakdown: "Sync: you, 100% of your share. Mechanical: Publisher X collects." A sync-carve-out is one of the few pieces of leverage a signed writer retains; a model that flattens it erases it. |
| **A consented ledger is never registered** | Detected: `consented` ≠ `registered` (D-09). The work releases, streams, earns, and the money black-boxes. | "This split is agreed but not registered with [PRO/MLC]. Nobody can pay it." Escalating, because this is the failure the domain exists to prevent, and it is silent by construction. |
| **The society's registration disagrees with the ledger** | Neither record is overwritten. A registration conflict is raised — the platform's record can be *right* and *ignored*, and the publisher may have filed first and differently. Routes to [09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md) and [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/). | "The MLC shows Publisher Y at 50%. Your ledger shows 25%. Here's both." Notify, don't execute ([domain D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Conflicting registrations are the single largest cause of unmatched money; a perfect internal record that never checks the outside is a diary, not a registry. |
| Off-platform publisher entity named | The row is marked **asserted**, not verified. It is never filed as controlled — the CWR SPU/OPU distinction ("publisher I represent" vs "publisher someone says exists") is a real standard and the model mirrors it. | "Publisher X — named by [writer], not confirmed by them." An asserted row is a claim, rendered as a claim. |
| Malicious: user names a major publisher who is not theirs | Asserted-only; never controlled; never filed as though WeJammin represents them. If the named entity later claims their profile they see every assertion made about them and can dispute ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)). | The claim is visibly one person's assertion, so it neither cashes nor defames quietly. |
| Interpolation declared, diluting writer shares | Writer shares shrink pro rata (CX-02); **every anchored publisher row shrinks with its anchor**, because it is a share of a share. All writers *and* all anchored publishers re-consent (D-11). | "Declaring this sample reduces 6 parties' shares, including 2 publishers. All 6 must agree." The fact stands regardless; only the settlement is negotiable ([09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)). |
| Work-for-hire composition — a company is the **author** | Supported: the writer row is held by an entity, with no natural-person writer behind it. There is no reversion and no termination right ([09.03.03](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.03-termination-rights-notice-windows.md)) because there is no author to revert to. | "Author: [Company] (work made for hire)". Odd-looking and correct — the credit/performer record survives it regardless ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)). |
| Writer is dead; estate holds the share | Estate is the party ([09.03.04](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)). Anchored publisher rows are unaffected — the deal survives the writer. | Row shows the estate entity; the publisher anchor points at it. |
| Publisher claims a share the writer never agreed to | Ledger enters `disputed` ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)). | Dispute banner; collections frozen ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)). |
| Work has a writer who is not on WeJammin | Invited stub; ledger stays `proposed`; no publisher row can be anchored to them until they declare (only the writer names their publisher, D-06). | "Waiting on 1 of 3" with chase. Their publishing position is shown as **undeclared**, not as self-published — silence reads as silence. |
| Two writers declare their publishing positions concurrently | No conflict. Anchoring partitions the ledger by writer, so independent anchors are independently writable; the invariant is checked per writer at proposal. **A whole class of concurrency conflict is eliminated by the modelling decision** rather than by locking. | Both see the other's row appear. Nothing is lost, nothing is blocked. |
| Race: a transfer lands while an amendment to the same writer's share is in flight | Both target the same anchor. The second write **rebases and re-presents** rather than applying to a base that changed underneath it — a publisher who agreed to half of a 25% share has not agreed to half of a 20% share. | "This changed while you were signing: [writer]'s share is now 20%, so your share is 10 points, not 12.5. Confirm again?" Never last-write-wins ([09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) CX-01 §5). |
| Capture on a phone in a studio with no signal | Writer names, shares and publishing positions are draftable offline. **Consent is not.** A locally-timestamped consent is exactly the reconstructable, self-asserted evidence the platform exists to replace. | Draft saved; "Consent needs a connection — 3 people still to sign." Refused honestly rather than faked and reconciled. |
| Publishing ledger complete, master ledger untouched | Warned — the classic "we did publishing, we'll do the master later" abandonment (CX-06). | Both ledgers shown side by side with the untouched one called out. Editing one believing you edited the other is the domain's most common user error. |
| Writer changes PRO | Their anchored publisher must have a matching affiliation at the new society — a BMI writer's publisher needs a BMI-affiliated entity. The existing anchor may need a differently-affiliated entity behind the same commercial deal. | "Your publisher collects at ASCAP; you're now at BMI. They'll need a BMI publisher entity or your publisher share won't reach them." Reference data from [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/). |
| Operator or Fan reaches a publishing URL | No surface exists for these personas — not a filtered view, not a permission error on a rendered page. | Nothing. The absence is the design ([09.01 Role Matrix](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)). |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Publishing view requested | Skeleton preserving the writer/publisher two-column shape and the anchor indentation — so the nesting is legible before the numbers land |
| Empty (`unallocated`) | Work created, no writers | "Who wrote this?" — the single most valuable unanswered question in the industry. Deliberately alarming copy: an absent ledger is the dangerous state, not an invalid one ([09.01.02 DT-03](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) |
| Partial | Writers set, one or more publishing positions **undeclared** | Writer shares shown; "Publishing position not declared" per row — **never** rendered as self-published. Ledger cannot leave `proposed` |
| Partial | All positions declared, one writer's publisher pool ≠ their writer share | Per-writer remainder named; save disabled with the reason stated as a question, not a code |
| Populated (`consented`) | All writers and all anchored publishers consented | Writer rows + anchored publisher rows + per-writer effective-share matrix + one-stop status + signed badge and timestamp |
| Populated (`consented`, **not registered**) | Consent locked; no matching society registration | Everything above, plus a persistent registration warning. Consent and registration shown as two facts, never one (D-09) |
| Registered / collecting | Society registration confirmed; matching income observed ([domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)) | Effective share shown alongside what is actually arriving — the only view in the product where the promise and the payment sit together |
| Disputed | Conflicting claim or refused amendment ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)) | Dispute banner; collections frozen ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)); the ledger stays readable — a dispute hides nothing |
| Error | Invariant violated | Remainder surfaced live via [09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md); anchoring violations named per writer, not as a global total failure |
| Error | Write failed | Inline retry; entered data preserved. Nothing partially written — a half-written ledger is a wrong ledger |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The 200% convention is a legacy quirk we should normalise away internally and present as 100% | Engineering simplification instinct — 200% is genuinely confusing | ❌ REJECTED, with reservation. Every external counterparty — societies (CWR registrations), DSPs, publishers — speaks in writer-share/publisher-share terms. Normalising internally means converting at every boundary, and each conversion is a chance to halve or double someone's income. The convention is load-bearing, not decorative. **However**, which expression is canonical remains genuinely open (see Q-01) — this rejects only the assumption that it is a free internal choice. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **admin vs co-pub** to be distinguished, and the candidate list does not distinguish them | The sweep gave "Publishing Rights (writer/publisher shares, co-pub, admin)" as one bullet. But an admin deal moves no ownership while a co-pub deal does. If both are stored as "publisher row", the chain of title is wrong for every administered work — and the chain of title is the thing this domain sells. | ✅ CONFIRMED — deal type is a first-class attribute, and it determines whether a transfer is written to 09.03.01. This is a small modelling decision with a large truth consequence. |
| DT-03 | **Effective share** should be computed on demand rather than stored | Normalisation instinct | ⏳ SUPERSEDED by DT-06 — the premise was wrong, not just the cost. "Compute vs store" assumes effective share is *derivable*. For an ordinary admin deal it is not: collection share is a negotiated value, declared independently of ownership. The remaining question is representation, narrowed in Q-03. |
| DT-04 | A publisher row can be a free-standing percentage of the work — "Publisher X holds 30% of the publishing" | It is how publishing is colloquially discussed, and it makes the 200% validation a simple two-pool sum | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the feature's core structural finding. A publisher's interest is always *a share of a named writer's share*, acquired from that writer and from nobody else. Free-standing rows permit a ledger that totals 200 and is impossible: Publisher X holding 60 publisher points while representing writers who wrote 25% between them passes every arithmetic check 09.01.02 can run. **Industry precedent is decisive**: CWR has a dedicated record type, **PWR — "Publisher For Writer"** — whose entire purpose is carrying this link. A standard does not add a record type for an implementation detail. Anchoring also closes the Producer self-dealing path (D-06) and eliminates a whole class of concurrency conflict for free, because it partitions the ledger by writer. |
| DT-05 | **Effective share is a single number** — "you receive on 18.75%" | It is the number every user asks for, and the file's own Overview presented it as a scalar | ❌ REJECTED — and the scalar is most misleading for exactly the writers who most need it. It varies along three axes: **right type** (a writer keeping sync while a publisher takes mechanical is ordinary; CWR carries PR/MR/SR ownership shares as three separate fields), **territory** (sub-publishing takes a fee in-territory), and **channel** — in the US and most PRO territories the writer's share of performance income is **paid direct by the PRO and never touches the publisher**, while the publisher's half routes through the publisher and is exposed to recoupment. For an unrecouped signed writer, of the worked example's 18.75 points, **12.5 arrive and 6.25 do not** — and the honest effective share this year is 12.5%. Rendering one number would state, confidently, the opposite of the writer's lived experience. |
| DT-06 | Collection share is **derivable** from ownership share + the deal rate | Normalisation instinct; it would make effective share a pure function | ❌ REJECTED — this is why DT-03's framing was wrong. CWR declares ownership shares and **collection shares separately**, per territory and per right type, because they are negotiated separately: an administrator commonly **owns 0% and collects 100%** in a territory, accounting to the owners afterward. A derived model cannot express the most common administration deal in music, and would file CWR that is wrong in a way societies reject. Collection must be **declarable** (D-08). This is the difference between a model that reflects deals and one that reflects an engineer's idea of deals. |
| DT-07 | A **consented** ledger means the money will flow | The whole platform is built on capture-at-source; consent feels like the finish line | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the failure mode nobody designs for. Consent is an agreement *between the parties*. Societies act on **registrations**, and money follows **matching**. Three independent truths (D-09). A work can be flawlessly consented on WeJammin, never registered, and earn straight into the black box — the exact outcome this domain exists to prevent, achieved with a *perfect* internal record. Worse: the publisher may have registered something *different*, and registered first. The platform's record can be right and ignored. A registry that never checks what the outside world holds is a diary. The gap must be modelled, detected, and surfaced — not assumed away by the elegance of the capture flow. |
| DT-08 | **Self-publishing is zero work** — one tap and you are done | D-02, and it is true of the ledger | ⚠️ CONFIRMED WITH A CONDITION. The ledger row is one tap and D-02 stands. But a self-held publisher share is **uncollectable** until the writer has a publishing *entity* with an IPI affiliated to their PRO — societies pay publisher shares to publishers, and a writer without a publisher entity has their publisher half sit unclaimed. So the platform's cheerful "you hold both shares" is, for a writer with no pub entity, a statement of intent the platform knows cannot be cashed. That is a black box generated *by the default*, in the feature whose reason for existing is preventing black boxes. Resolution: the one tap still completes (the fact is true, and blocking consent over a registration prerequisite would be the tail wagging the dog); it raises a flag and a one-tap route into domain 01 setup, and hard-blocks only at registration in domain 10. |
| DT-09 | A domain expert nobody has mentioned — the **music supervisor** — would ask one question of this ledger, and it is not "who owns it?" | Sync licensing is where publishing splits become operationally fatal. A supervisor with a Thursday deadline asks **"is it one-stop?"** — can I clear this with one signature, or do I need five? Works die in sync not because someone refuses but because nobody could reach all the publishers in time. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a **one-stop indicator** is derivable from data this ledger already holds (count of distinct approval-holders across publisher rows and their carve-outs, plus the master side via CX-06), costs nothing, and is invisible to every competitor because none of them hold both ledgers. It is [D-18](../../ideation-index.md)'s consolidation payoff in one line of UI: only a platform that is also where the master lives can answer it. Surfaced in the happy path (step 10) and routed to [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/). |
| DT-10 | Signing a publisher post-lock affects only that writer — the existing D-04 is complete | D-04's reasoning is sound: the transfer touches only that writer's own share, so co-writers' shares are untouched and their consent is not required | ⚠️ CONFIRMED BUT INCOMPLETE — D-04 is right about the transfer and missed its consequence. The new publisher becomes an **anchored party to the ledger**, and per [09.01.02 D-01](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) every named party consents to the whole ledger — so they are now a consent-holder on **every future amendment** that dilutes their anchor. One writer's private deal has permanently enlarged the consent set for everyone. This is legally correct (you cannot renegotiate away a share you already assigned) and operationally brutal: the co-writers' ability to fix a split they got wrong now depends on a publisher who was not in the room. The platform does not adjudicate ([domain D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)) — so it must at minimum **say this out loud at signing time**, not discover it during the amendment. Recorded as D-11. |
| DT-11 | Territory must be asked at capture, since [domain D-08](../rights-ownership-index.md) makes it a dimension of the model | Reading D-08 as "territory is required everywhere" | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection resolves an apparent conflict between two ratified decisions. **Ownership is worldwide by nature**: a writer owns their share everywhere a copyright subsists, so "worldwide" is not a default filling a silence, it is the extent of the right. Territory enters only via a **grant** (sub-publishing). So D-08's dimension exists in the model from day one *without* capture asking a question the writer cannot answer — and [domain D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md) (silence reads as silence) is honoured, because the thing that *can* be silent — a grant's territory — is flagged incomplete, exactly as [09.03.02 D-03](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md) flags a grant with no term. The asymmetry is principled: assuming a *grant* is worldwide gifts the grantee; assuming *ownership* is worldwide states the law. |
| DT-12 | Because consent is to the whole ledger ([09.01.02 D-01](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)), co-writers must see each other's deal terms | Consistent-sounding application of a good rule | ❌ REJECTED — the rule is about *shares*, not economics. A co-writer must see every writer share, every publisher identity and every anchor, because their own share is meaningless without them. They have no interest whatsoever in another writer's admin rate, advance, or term — and exposing it hands a publisher the other side's negotiating position before a renegotiation. Consent surface = shares + publisher identity + anchoring. Deal economics are private to the writer and their publisher (D-12). Over-applying a rule is how a privacy leak gets built with a clear conscience. |
| DT-13 | The ledger should model **controlled composition clauses**, since they reduce what a signed writer actually receives | They are a publishing-economics term and DT-05 already says the headline number must be honest | ❌ REJECTED — a controlled composition clause (reducing mechanicals to a fraction of statutory, capped per album) is a term of a **record deal**, not a publishing deal. It belongs to [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/). Modelling it here would put record-deal terms inside the publishing registry and make the ledger a place where any income-affecting clause could plausibly live — which is how a registry becomes a contract database nobody can reason about. It is, however, one more reason the effective-share figure is labelled a **share**, not a forecast (D-07). |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — *trigger dependency + shared entity*. CWR registration is generated from this ledger, and the ownership/collection share distinction (D-08) is the field-level shape of that file. Errors here become unmatched money there. Critically, the **reconciliation direction reverses**: 10 must feed society-held registration data *back* so `registered` and `collecting` can be told apart from `consented` (D-09, DT-07).
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — *shared entity + hard prerequisite*. IPI/CAE numbers are Interested Party identifiers living there, per the domain's boundary fix. **A self-held publisher row is uncollectable without a publisher entity + IPI affiliated to the writer's PRO** (DT-08) — this is not a soft link, it is the difference between the default working and the default generating a black box. PRO affiliation matching (a BMI writer needs a BMI-affiliated publisher entity) is reference data owned there.
- Touches **09.03.01 Chain of Title** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)) — *trigger dependency*. Co-pub assignments write a transfer; **admin appointments write nothing** (D-01). The distinction is set here and consumed there; get it wrong and every administered work has a fictional chain of title.
- Touches **09.03.02 Term, Territory & Reversion** ([path](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md)) — *shared entity*. **Sub-publishing IS a grant in that feature's model** — this feature does not own a second territory mechanism (D-10). Retention periods and territory-overlap detection are that feature's machinery; the deal structure and the resulting collection picture are this one's.
- Touches **09.02.04 Split Amendment & Re-Consent** ([path](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) — *permission*. **Anchored publishers are consent-holders on amendment** (D-11, DT-10). The consent set for a work grows every time any writer signs a deal — which means 09.02.04's "who must re-consent?" is not a fixed list derived from the original session.
- Touches **09.01.02 Ownership Ledger & Validation** ([path](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — *shared entity*. The **anchoring invariant (D-05) is a new rule that validator must enforce** and cannot derive: `∀W: Σ(publisher rows anchored to W) = writer_share(W)`. It is structural, not arithmetic — which sits exactly on that feature's D-04 boundary ("arithmetic only, deal semantics live in siblings") and needs an explicit hand-off rather than an assumption. See CX-03.
- Touches **09.01.06 Sample & Interpolation Provenance** ([path](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)) — *trigger dependency + notification fan-out*. Interpolation dilutes writer shares; because publisher rows are shares of shares, **every anchored publisher shrinks with its anchor and must re-consent**. The fan-out from one sample declaration is larger than CX-02 assumes: it is writers **plus their publishers**.
- Touches **09.04.01 Conflicting Claim Detection** ([path](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md)) — *trigger dependency*. A **society registration that disagrees with the ledger** is a conflict class that originates outside the platform entirely — unlike every other conflict in 09.04, which originates between users on it.
- Touches **11 Music Licensing** ([path](../../11-music-licensing/)) — *trigger dependency*. The **one-stop indicator** (D-14, DT-09) is derived here and consumed there; a sync licence needs every publisher's approval within their carve-outs, and the count is the first question a supervisor asks.
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([path](../../12-release-distribution/)) — *trigger dependency*. Release makes mechanical liability real, which is when an incomplete publishing ledger stops being untidy and starts being money in a black box. **Controlled composition clauses live there** (DT-13) and distort effective receipts without touching a share.
- Touches **23 Career, Finance & Business** ([path](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — *shared entity*. Effective shares are the basis of income forecasting — **as a matrix, not a scalar** (D-07). **Recoupment lives there**, and it is why this feature's number is labelled a share and not a forecast.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — *shared entity*. Writer credit and writer share are different facts: the credit is public and permanent; the percentage is neither.
- Touches **09.06.04 Public Rights Lookup** ([path](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md)) — *permission*. Writer identity may be public; percentages, deal types and rates are not. See [domain Q-06](../rights-ownership-index.md).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Admin and co-pub are structurally different**, not two labels on one row | Admin moves no ownership; co-pub does. Conflating them corrupts the chain of title for every administered work (see DT-02). |
| D-02 | Self-publishing is the **default and the one-tap path** | It is the most common case among the platform's users, and forcing a decision here taxes the capture moment. Qualified by D-15. |
| D-03 | The 200% writer/publisher expression is **retained**, not normalised away | It is the language of every external counterparty; conversion at boundaries is where income gets halved (see DT-01). |
| D-04 | Signing a publisher post-lock is a **transfer, not an amendment** | It touches only that writer's own share, so it needs no co-writer consent — but it must appear in the chain of title. Consequence recorded separately in D-11. |
| D-05 | **Every publisher row is anchored to exactly one writer row.** Invariant: `∀W: Σ(publisher rows anchored to W) = writer_share(W)`; the 100% publisher pool is a derived consequence, never an independent check | A publisher's interest is always a share of a named writer's share. Free-standing rows admit ledgers that total 200 and are impossible. CWR's `PWR` ("Publisher For Writer") record type exists for precisely this link (see DT-04). |
| D-06 | **Only the writer may create or modify the publisher rows anchored to their own share** — no co-writer, no Producer, no admin override | Closes the persona anti-pattern structurally rather than by policy: a Producer naming their own company on a co-writer's share is the highest-value attack available in this feature, and anchoring makes it unrepresentable. |
| D-07 | **Effective share is presented as a right-type × territory matrix with a qualified headline**, labelled "share of income", never as a scalar and never as a forecast | The scalar is most wrong for unrecouped signed writers — the population it most needs to serve (see DT-05). Recoupment lives in domain 23; controlled-composition in domain 12 (DT-13). |
| D-08 | **Collection share is a declarable value, not a derivation of ownership share** | An administrator owning 0% and collecting 100% in a territory is an ordinary deal that a derived model cannot express. CWR declares the two separately for this reason (see DT-06). |
| D-09 | The ledger models **three independent truths — consented / registered / collecting** — and never renders one as another | A perfectly consented, never-registered work earns into the black box the domain exists to prevent. Collapsing them means the platform's best-case record is indistinguishable from its worst-case outcome (see DT-07). |
| D-10 | **Ownership rows are worldwide by nature; territory enters only through a grant.** Sub-publishing is a territory-scoped collection grant anchored to a publisher row, recorded via [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md); a grant with no recorded territory is flagged incomplete | Reconciles domain D-08 (territory is a model dimension) with domain D-06 (silence reads as silence). Worldwide ownership states the law; a worldwide *grant* assumption would gift the grantee (see DT-11). |
| D-11 | **An anchored publisher is a consent-holder on every subsequent amendment that changes its anchor writer's share**, and this is disclosed to the writer at signing, not discovered at amendment | Follows inescapably from D-05 + [09.01.02 D-01](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md). Legally correct and operationally brutal; the platform does not adjudicate, so its only honest move is to say it early (see DT-10). |
| D-12 | **Deal economics (rate, term, advance) are private to the writer and their publisher.** Ledger consent covers shares, publisher identity and anchoring only. A publisher administering a co-writer's share renders as a distinct, plain-language assignment consent addressed to that co-writer | Whole-ledger consent is about shares, not economics. Exposing a writer's admin rate to a co-writer hands the other side a negotiating position (see DT-12). |
| D-13 | **Publisher rows naming an off-platform entity are marked `asserted`, never `verified`, and are never filed as controlled** | Mirrors CWR's own SPU/OPU distinction. An assertion about a third party must render as an assertion, or a false claim cashes quietly. |
| D-14 | The work surfaces a **one-stop indicator** — how many approvals a licence requires and whose | Derivable from data already held, invisible to every competitor because none hold both ledgers, and it is the first question a music supervisor asks (see DT-09). Consumed by [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/). |
| D-15 | The self-published one-tap default **completes and never blocks consent**, but raises an uncollectable-share flag and routes to publisher-entity setup; the hard block lives at society registration ([domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)) | A self-held publisher share with no publisher entity/IPI is a black box created by the platform's own default. Blocking consent over a downstream registration prerequisite would tax the capture moment D-02 exists to protect (see DT-08). |
| D-16 | **Conventional rates are shown as reference text, never prefilled.** Any deal type other than self-published requires an explicitly entered rate | A prefilled 50/50 the writer never confirmed is the platform fabricating a deal term — exactly what domain D-06 forbids. |
