# Feature: Work & Recording Records (composition/master duality)

> **Number**: 09.01.01
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-20

## Overview

Every piece of music on WeJammin is **two separate copyrights**: the *work* (the composition — melody
and lyrics, owned by writers and publishers) and the *recording* (the master — a specific fixed
performance of that work, owned by whoever paid for or made it). This feature is where those records
are created, linked, versioned, and viewed: one work can carry many recordings (the original, a live
version, a cover by a stranger), and a recording normally points at exactly one work — but not always.

Users do not think in this model — they think "my song". The feature's job is to make the duality
**visible without being a lecture**, because the single most expensive mistake in the domain is a
musician who edits the master split believing they edited the publishing split.

This feature owns the **objects and the links between them**. It does not own the percentages inside
them ([09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)), what those percentages mean
([09.01.03](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md), [09.01.04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md)), how
consent is collected ([09.02](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/)), or how the codes that name these
objects are issued ([09.06.01](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.01-identifier-issuance-reconciliation.md)).

> **Merged from sweep candidates**: 01 (Work vs Recording Duality) + 08 (Songwriter vs Performer Share
> Distinction). Candidate 08 was 01 restated in persona terms; its performer-side consequence is carried
> by [09.01.05](./09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md).

## Role Lens

> **Persona access is a lens, not an account type.** `meta/personas.md` states the multi-hyphenate is
> the structural fact of this industry and that the Fan boundary is "porous". `None` below means *this
> lens has no surface here* — never *this human is locked out*. A Fan who writes a song is acting as a
> Musician at that moment and gets the Musician surface; a venue Operator who engineers a session is
> acting as a Producer. Nothing in this feature keys off an account role.

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates a work when they write something; creates a recording when they record it. Sees "My Songs" **work-first** — a work row expands to its recordings. On a song detail screen sees both layers on one screen: *Composition* (who wrote it) and *Recording* (who made this version), never merged into one number. Marks a recording as a cover of an existing work, which attaches them to **zero** of that work's publishing and blocks them from proposing a writer row on it. Sees every recording that exists of a work they wrote — including covers by people they have never met — and is notified when a new one appears. Declares a work as `derived-from` another (a translation, a rewrite, an arrangement of a traditional tune). Declares a composition **public domain** — an assertion the platform records and never makes on their behalf (D-13). Names writers on a work they created; naming a person is an **invitation to confirm**, never an assignment. |
| Producer | Full | Typically the one who creates the *recording* record, because they are the one in the room when it is fixed. Their library is **recording-first** — the mirror of the Musician's — because a producer routinely has 200 recordings and 0 works. Links a recording to a work, which may not exist yet, so they can create a stub work from the recording flow without leaving the session. Declares lineage (this is a remix of that master; this is a radio edit of that master). **Not barred from a writer row by role** — a producer who wrote is a co-writer, and `meta/personas.md` forbids any UX that makes someone choose one hyphen. The constraint is consent-based, not role-based: nobody, in any lens, may assign *themselves* a writer share without the other named writers' consent ([09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). |
| Operator | None | A venue/studio operator is not a party to a composition or a master, and there is **no Operator surface in this feature** — do not build one. Studios appear here only indirectly: if the studio entity holds a work-for-hire over its engineer's contribution ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), it appears as an owner *in the ledger* ([09.01.03](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md)), never as a creator of these records. The domain index records this absence as a finding, not an omission. |
| Fan | None | Fans never create, edit, or read rights records through this feature. The read-only, public-safe projection of this data — "who wrote this song, who played on this version" — is [09.06.04 Public Rights Lookup](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), a different feature with a different privacy posture. It is a **separate projection, not a filtered professional view** (`meta/personas.md`: the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"). This feature renders no Fan-facing surface at all. |

## Behavior

The registry holds two object types with a link between them. Both are creatable independently: a song
can be written and never recorded; a recording can exist with no identified work (a field recording, an
improvisation not yet claimed as a composition, a drum stem someone has asserted as a rights object in
order to license it). The link is therefore **optional at creation and required before release** —
deferring the constraint to the point where it actually matters rather than blocking capture, which is
the moment the platform must never make expensive (D-18).

### What is a "recording" here — the boundary with domain 07

A session in [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/) produces dozens of takes, comps and
stems. **None of them are recordings in this registry until someone asserts one as a rights object.**
The registry holds recordings that are candidates for exploitation; domain 07 holds session artifacts.
Without this boundary every take of every session arrives here demanding a ledger, and the registry
becomes a file browser with a legal opinion attached. Assertion is a single explicit act at session
close ([Happy Path](#happy-path) step 2), not a background sync.

### Two classes of link — they are not the same relationship

The naive model puts one `relationship type` on a recording. That field is overloaded: some of its
values describe the recording's relationship to a **work** and others to a prior **recording**
(DT-04). The registry therefore holds two distinct, independently-optional link classes.

**1. Recording → Work** — `0..N` links, each carrying a **type** and a **weight**.

| Type | Meaning | Publishing consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `original` | This recording is the first fixation of this work | None — the work's ledger stands as captured |
| `cover` | Someone else's work, performed as written | The performer attaches to **0%** of the work's publishing |
| `live` | A live performance of this work | **None of its own** — the work's ledger is untouched by the fixation. Liveness is a *manner of fixation*, so it never adds or removes a publishing consequence (see the note below) |
| `re-recording` | A new master of a work the performer already has | None — the work is untouched; this is the point of it |
| `remix` | A reworking whose relationship to the *source master* is carried by a lineage link | None **unless** new composition was added, which makes it a `derived-from` work, not a remix |

Normal case: **exactly one link, type `original`, weight 100%**. Everything below is the exception.

**`live` and `cover` are not alternatives — a live cover is both, and it is a cover.** Whose work it is
and how it was fixed are independent facts, and the table above reads as one enum only because the
common cases happen to pick one value each. A band playing someone else's song at a gig is the ordinary
case, not an edge: `live` contributes **no** publishing consequence of its own ("untouched by the
fixation"), so it cannot displace one either. Both cover rules fire in full on a live rendition of
someone else's work — the writer-row block (a performer who did not write it does not acquire a writer
row by playing it in a room), and the mechanical-licence prompt where the composition is owned
off-platform. Reading a live cover as merely `live` would silently drop a licence obligation on the most
common recording a working band makes, and it would do it to the person who is *complying* by declaring
the source. Where the two facts genuinely disagree with the single-type shape, cover-ness governs the
publishing consequence and liveness governs the presentation. **How the two are represented — a second
field on the link, a compound type, or a recording-level fixation attribute — is Q-09**; the behaviour
above holds under all three and does not wait on it.

**2. Recording → Recording (lineage)** — `0..N` links, each carrying a **kind**.

| Kind | Meaning | Inherits the parent's master ledger by default? |
|---|---|---|
| `edit-of` | Radio edit, extended mix, instrumental, clean version | **Yes** — same fixation, topped and tailed; the parties are unchanged by construction |
| `remaster-of` | A new master from the same fixation | **Yes** — same reasoning |
| `alternate-take-of` | Another take from the same session | **Yes** — different fixation, same parties, same day |
| `remix-of` | A new master built from the source master | **No** — a remixer is commonly a new party (fee or master points) |
| `re-recording-of` | A wholly new fixation replacing a prior master | **No** — new session, potentially entirely new parties |

Inheritance is **by reference, not by copy**: an `edit-of` shares its parent's consented master ledger
until someone forks it, at which point it becomes an independent ledger requiring its own consent. The
default is presented and overridable in both directions. Rationale: requiring five people to re-sign
for a radio edit is exactly the friction that kills capture, and an edit genuinely has no new party to
consent. See [Cross-Cut Notes](#cross-cut-notes) — this obliges [09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md).

### Multi-work recordings (medleys, live sets, mashups)

A recording may name more than one work. **The works are never merged** — each keeps its own publishing
ledger, untouched. What the recording holds is an **allocation across them**: each Recording→Work link
carries a positive exact-rational weight, and the weights must total **exactly 100%** per
[09.01.02 D-02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) (a three-work medley is thirds, which is
unrepresentable in decimal).

For declared **disjoint** work spans — a medley or continuous live set where one work ends before the
next begins — duration proration is an editable proposed weight: each work's share of the recording's
running time. For a mashup whose embodied works overlap in time, duration supplies no non-arbitrary
allocation. **CQ-07 Option B:** the declarant must enter every positive exact weight totaling 100%; the
system offers no duration calculation, normalization, equal-share fallback, inferred remainder, or
display-rounded validation. Missing, zero, negative, or non-100%-total overlap weights remain incomplete
rather than being repaired.

This resolves Q-01 without a many-to-many ledger: downstream royalty allocation
([domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)) consumes the valid, as-of Recording→Work weights; it never
authors, normalizes, or infers them. Nothing about any work's own ledger changes. A work you are not
actually performing is not a component. A two-second quotation is a **sample**, not a medley component,
and belongs to [09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md) (DT-05).

A **mashup** is both cases at once and must be declared as both: it combines two existing *masters*
(a lineage/sample matter — [09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)) and therefore
embodies two *works* (two weighted Recording→Work links). Declaring only one half clears one right and
leaves the other exposed — the exact failure [CX-05](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md) names.

### Work versions and derived works

A work may link to another work as `derived-from`. This resolves Q-02, and the resolution is: **the
platform never decides which path applies.** Whether a rewritten lyric created a new work or merely
changed an existing one is a legal judgement about originality, and D-04 (domain) is absolute — the
platform holds facts and routes judgements, it never adjudicates. Both paths exist; the user declares.

| The user's situation | Path | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| "We corrected the lyric / added Sam as a co-writer on our song" | **Amend the existing work** — the default | Ledger amendment via [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md): re-consent from every party, supersession not mutation, existing recordings stay linked, already-accrued earnings are not redistributed |
| "This is a new song built on that one" (translation, adaptation, arrangement, substantial rewrite) | **New work with a `derived-from` link** | A new work, a new ledger, its own recordings |

A `derived-from` link is **not a clean slate**. The original work's writers retain their copyright in
the underlying work, so a derived work carries a publishing encumbrance to its parent — structurally
identical to the interpolation case [CX-02](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md) describes. This registry
owns the **link**; the ledger consequence of one is
[09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)'s and
[09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)'s mechanism, and must not be re-implemented here.

The case that makes `derived-from` load-bearing rather than tidy: a **public-domain parent**. A
traditional tune arranged in 2026 is a copyrighted arrangement of an uncopyrighted work — the whole of
trad-folk and classical depends on this. The derived work's ledger is fully real even though the
parent's ledger is `public-domain` and empty (DT-13).

### Declaring a composition public domain

`public-domain` is a **declared** state, not a computed one. A user declares "this composition is in the
public domain" on the work — exactly as they declare a cover, a sample, or a work-for-hire — and that
declaration puts the work's publishing ledger into `public-domain`: a terminal state with **zero rows**
that satisfies the **composition side of the release gate only** (D-13). The master side is untouched;
the song is free and the recording of it is not
([09.03.05 D-02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) — the
answer is always two answers).

**The platform never asserts it.** The declaration is treated like every other unverifiable rights fact
here: attributed, timestamped, visible to anyone who looks, and disputable via
[09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md). An asserted row
is a claim, rendered as a claim ([09.01.04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md)).

[09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md), when it is
built, is a **lookup that informs the declaration** — it pre-fills it, shows its inputs and its
jurisdictions, and warns when it returns `in copyright` or `unknown`. It never writes the state and
never blocks. Once it exists, its per-jurisdiction results attach to the declaration as **corroboration
or contradiction**, never as an override. This is the two-truths shape the domain has already ratified
at [09.01.04 D-09](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md) — `consented` ≠ `registered`, two truths kept apart
and both shown — reused rather than reinvented: the declaration and the term status are separate
records, and neither is rendered as the other. A contradiction ("you declared public domain; term status
says in copyright in `DE` until 2031") surfaces **persistently** on the work and routes to
[09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md) if a party
objects. **A release already made is not retracted by the platform.**

A v1 declaration is **worldwide**, matching the ledger's single `World` territory
([09.01.02 D-15](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)). Where 09.03.05's four jurisdictions
disagree with each other or with the declaration, the disagreement is **surfaced, not modelled** — no
partial, per-jurisdiction ledger state exists in v1, and nothing here anticipates
[09 Q-05](../rights-ownership-index.md), which is where territory becomes a real dimension.

### Creation, naming and confirmation

- A work requires **≥ 1 named writer at creation**. A name string is sufficient — a WeJammin identity
  is not required, because the person may not be on the platform and demanding one would tax the
  capture moment. Title is required, trimmed, 1–200 characters.
- **Duplicate titles are allowed and are not a signal.** Many songs share a title; a title is not an
  identity.
- **Naming a person never assigns them anything.** A named writer who has not confirmed renders as
  *named, not confirmed* and is never rendered as confirmed — D-06 (domain): silence must read as
  silence. This is also the entire defence against poisoning: creating a work asserting a famous
  writer's name attaches nothing to that person and grants the creator nothing.
- **Fields commit on confirmation, not on a save button.** An inline work created during recording
  creation is written as each field is confirmed. Capture data is unrepeatable; a client-side draft
  pending an explicit save is a data-loss surface at the exact moment the platform exists to protect.

### Deletion and withdrawal

| Object condition | Deletable? |
|---|---|
| Created in error, no consented ledger, no links, by its creator, within **15 minutes** of creation | Yes — hard delete |
| Anything else (any consented ledger row, any link in either direction, any recording attached, any earnings) | **Never.** Transitions to `retracted` and is retained, unresolvable in lookup but intact as evidence |

A rights record with a consented ledger is evidence of what parties agreed. Deleting one destroys the
asset. The 15-minute window covers the only genuine case — a fat-fingered duplicate noticed immediately.

### Duplicate detection and merging

Duplicate candidates are **flagged, never auto-merged** (D-02) — with one deliberately narrow
unclaimed-stub exception. A duplicate candidate never establishes that two asserted writer names are one
person, or that two claimed rights records are one work. Merging two works merges two ledgers, which is a
rights event requiring consent from every party holding a **consented share** on either side. Where zero
such parties exist, there is nobody to harm and nobody to consent:

| Both records | Merge behavior |
|---|---|
| Have consented ledger parties | Proposed merge; requires consent from **every** consented party on both sides; no admin override (D-04) |
| One has consented parties, one is an unclaimed stub | Proposed merge; requires consent from the consented side only; notifies every recording owner attached to the stub |
| **Both are distinct current unclaimed, unconsented stubs**; neither has a claim, consented ledger row, consent, conflict, or identity/alias evidence that its asserted writers are distinct or unresolved; and their canonical writer-name sets are exactly equal | **Auto-merge atomically**, with notification to every attached recording owner. No consent exists to violate (DT-09); canonical text equality does not resolve writer identity. |
| Both unclaimed stubs have a strict-subset, canonical-unequal, or otherwise non-equal writer-name set; or any eligibility guard is absent | **Flagged, never merged.** Merging would mean the platform choosing between competing assertions or identity evidence — that is adjudication (D-04). |

**Canonical writer-name set equality is one source-owned predicate, `writer-name-canonical-v1`.** For
each original asserted writer string, the profile uses pinned Unicode 15.1 data in this exact order:

1. Normalize to Unicode NFC.
2. Apply Unicode Default Case Folding.
3. Trim Unicode `White_Space` at both ends and replace each non-empty internal `White_Space` run with one
   U+0020 SPACE.
4. Diacritic-fold: normalize to Unicode NFD, remove each code point in Unicode General_Category `M`
   (`Mn`, `Mc`, or `Me`), then normalize to Unicode NFC.

The resulting keys form an order-independent set; the two sets must be exactly equal. Repeated equal
keys collapse **only for this set-equality predicate** — every original asserted writer string and its
source-record membership remain cardinality-preserving display and evidence data and are never replaced
by a canonical key. The profile is locale-independent: database collation, display formatting, and
runtime locale cannot alter its result. A canonical-equivalent string is **not** an identity key: two
distinct people may have the same canonical text. Token order, punctuation, transliteration,
abbreviations, aliases, legal/stage identity, fuzzy similarity, and title similarity are excluded from
this equality predicate. A strict subset never passes it.

ISWC/ISRC values, audio fingerprints, fuzzy titles, fuzzy writer names, and overlapping writer sets may
nominate a duplicate candidate only. No candidate signal — including a matched identifier — bypasses
exact canonical-set equality or the current eligibility checks. **Two records sharing an ISRC with
different consented ledgers is a conflict, not a duplicate** — it routes to
[09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md).

The system rechecks every eligibility atomically at merge commit in one serializable transaction over
both records: both must still exist, remain distinct current unclaimed, unconsented, conflict-free stubs,
and have no claim, consented ledger row, consent, distinct-person evidence, unresolved identity/alias
evidence, or won/entered competing merge; their canonical sets must still be equal. Any failed recheck
records the precise failed predicate as a stale/ineligible candidate outcome and aborts with no
recording-link movement, ledger rewrite, or partial record change. A successful merge selects the
survivor deterministically by earlier immutable creation timestamp, then lexical immutable work ID; it
retains the other record as a consolidated evidence redirect, never a hard deletion. Before moving
attached recording links, it retains every original writer string and source-record membership,
per-source record ID, canonical profile/version and set, candidate evidence/time, recheck time,
pre-action state, exact eligibility results, system actor, source provenance, merge lineage, and
reassignment/notification delivery result. Recording owners receive the existing before/after
notification, which never says writer identities were resolved. Consented/proposed merge flows remain
human-consent flows.

A dismissed duplicate flag is **remembered permanently for that pair** and never re-raised — two
different songs called *Home* must not nag their writers forever.

### The core UX rule

Each ledger is labelled by **what it pays**, not by its legal name: *"Recording — paid when this version
is streamed or sold"* beats *"Master"*. The users who most need this distinction are the ones least
fluent in the vocabulary.

And the corollary that only bites the multi-hyphenate: a person who is 50% writer and 25% master owner
has **no single "share of this song"**. Any surface that computes one has invented a number that
corresponds to no money anywhere. **The two percentages are never summed, averaged, or unified — in any
view, export, notification, or summary tile** (DT-08).

### Happy Path

1. Producer closes a session in [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/). The platform asks what, if anything, the session produced that should exist as a rights object — an explicit assertion, not an automatic sweep of every take.
2. Producer selects one comp and names it. A **recording** record is created and committed on that keystroke's confirmation.
3. System asks: *"Is this a recording of a song already on WeJammin?"* — with three answers: pick an existing work, create one now, or *not yet*.
4. Producer picks *create one now*, types the title and the names of the writers present. A **work** record is created inline, without leaving the session.
5. System creates the Recording→Work link, type `original`, weight 100%, and shows the two layers side by side — *Composition* and *Recording* — each with an empty ledger and a visible `unallocated` badge.
6. Named writers who are not the creator render as **named, not confirmed**, each with an invitation pending.
7. Producer proceeds to split capture ([09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). **Both ledgers are prompted in one flow, never one silently** — the recording ledger and the composition ledger, each labelled by what it pays.
8. Each named writer confirms; their row flips from *named* to *confirmed*.
9. Musician later opens "My Songs", sees the work work-first, expands it, and sees its one recording and that recording's type. The Producer opens the same data recording-first and sees the same facts in the opposite order.
10. Months later the band records a live version. It is a **new recording**, linked to the **same work**, type `live`. The work's publishing ledger is not touched, not re-consented, and not re-prompted — which is the duality doing its job, silently, for the first time the user can feel it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recording created with no work identified | Allowed. Recording enters `work-unlinked`; it can be shared and paid for as a master but not released or licensed. | Amber badge "No composition linked — required before release", with a one-click link/create action. |

| Two **unclaimed stubs** of the same work with exactly equal `writer-name-canonical-v1` sets | Auto-merged only when both are still distinct current unclaimed, unconsented, conflict-free stubs and no distinct-person or unresolved identity/alias evidence exists. All guards are rechecked atomically at commit; originals, source IDs, provenance, and merge lineage remain retained. | Attached recording owners are notified: "*Yesterday* was merged with a duplicate. Your recording now points at the surviving record." The notice never says the writers’ identities were resolved. |
| Two **unclaimed stubs** with a strict-subset, canonical-unequal, token-order, punctuation, abbreviation, transliteration, fuzzy-only, or identifier-only apparent match | Flagged only. Candidate signals cannot bypass exact canonical-set equality; a merge would otherwise select between competing assertions or identities — adjudication (D-04). | Both stubs persist and both surface in the candidate list; the recordings stay where they are. |

| Later incompatible claim or authorship assertion against a previously auto-merged stub | Retained per-source assertions enter ordinary claim-time detection; the platform does not auto-unmerge, auto-open a case, freeze funds, or adjudicate identity. | Existing detection notification and dispute-routing actions, with the new assertion described as a claim rather than a resolved identity fact. |
| 500 people cover the same famous song, creating 500 stubs | Stub proliferation is contained only by the narrow exact-canonical-set rule above, not by blocking stub creation or fuzzy/identifier auto-merge. | Coverer #500 is offered a candidate in search-ahead before they type a new one; candidate selection remains their assertion. |
| Cover recording of a work whose owners are not on WeJammin | Allowed — the work exists as an unclaimed stub. | Coverer tries to put themselves on the covered work's publishing ledger | **Blocked.** A `cover` link means the work is performed as written; a writer row on it is a contradiction. | "You've marked this a cover — covering a song earns you the recording, never the song. If you added new writing, this is a **new work based on that one**" → routes to `derived-from`. |
| User edits the master ledger believing it is publishing | The core UX failure this feature exists to prevent. | Each ledger is labelled by *what it pays*; a confirmation on first edit in a session names which copyright is being changed and who it pays. |
| A person is on both ledgers (the writer-producer — the multi-hyphenate) | Both percentages render, separately, always. **No summed, averaged or unified "your share" is rendered anywhere** (DT-08). | "You: 50% of the composition · 25% of this recording" — two numbers, two labels, never one. |
| **Medley** / recording incorporating multiple works with disjoint spans | Allowed. N positive exact-rational Recording→Work links sum to exactly 100%; duration proration is an editable proposal only because the declared spans do not overlap. Works are never merged (DT-05). | Composition panel becomes a list: "*Work A* 40% · *Work B* 35% · *Work C* 25%", each expandable to its own untouched ledger. |
| **Mashup** whose embodied works overlap in time | Allowed only after the declarant supplies every positive exact-rational Recording→Work weight totaling exactly 100%. No duration-based suggestion, normalization, equal split, inferred remainder, or rounded-display calculation exists for overlap. Master lineage/sample declarations remain a separate required list. | "These songs overlap. Enter the allocation each song represents; totals must equal 100%." No allocation or royalty preview is shown until every required weight is valid. |
| Medley weights do not total 100% | Rejected. Not saved, nothing silently normalised — normalising means the platform choosing someone's share. | "These add up to 97%. 3% is unallocated — which work is it?" with the shortfall named. |
| A linked work with weight 0 | Rejected at entry. | "A work at 0% isn't part of this recording. If you quoted a few seconds of it, that's a **sample**" → routes to [09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md). |
| Medley of works owned by others | Allowed to capture; **release-blocked with a different reason than a cover**. A medley combines works and alters their character — it is outside what a compulsory mechanical licence permits, so each publisher's negotiated permission is required (DT-06). | "A medley needs each publisher's permission — a standard mechanical licence doesn't cover combining songs" → [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/), one row per work. |
| Live set recorded as one continuous file containing 12 songs | Supported as a 12-work medley, but the system suggests the better shape first. | Above 12 linked works: "This looks like a set, not a medley. Splitting it into one recording per song will pay everyone more accurately." Suggestion only — never forced. |
| **Mashup** of two existing masters | Requires both declarations: a lineage/sample declaration against each source master, and weighted work links for each embodied work. Declaring one half clears one right and exposes the other ([CX-05](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md)). | Two checklists, side by side: "Masters used" and "Songs performed" — neither satisfies the other. |
| **Re-recording** where an old contract restricts re-recording | **Notified, never blocked** (D-05). Re-recording restrictions are standard in label deals; WeJammin is not a party to that contract and cannot read it. Blocking on a contract it has never seen means acting on its own guess (DT-07). | "Re-recording restrictions may apply under a prior agreement. WeJammin doesn't hold that contract — check before you release." Dismissible, recorded as shown. |
| Work created but never recorded | Fully supported. Work sits with a publishing ledger and no recordings. | "No recordings yet" empty state with a CTA to start a project. |
| Work created inline, writers named, nobody has confirmed | Work is `writers-unconfirmed`. Release-blocked. Not an error — the normal state for the hours between a session and everyone opening their phone. | Composition panel shows each name greyed with "invited — not confirmed", and a nudge action. |
| A stranger claims an unclaimed stub | If the stub **names them**, they confirm their own row — trivial. If it does not, they are asserting authorship of a work someone else described → a conflicting claim. | Named party: "Confirm you wrote *X*". Unnamed party: claim opened, routed to [09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md). |
| Recording linked to a work its owners dispute | The link is allowed (that is what a cover is) and **notifies the work's owners**. Notification is the defence, not prevention — preventing it would require the platform to rule on whether the link is true. | Work owners: "Sam released a recording of *X*." with a dispute action. |
| Relinking a recording from work A to work B, **pre-release, unconsented ledger** | Allowed. Notifies the writers of the work being detached — their expected income just vanished and silence would be indefensible. | Detached work's writers: "*Recording Y* is no longer listed as a recording of *X*." |
| Relinking a recording from work A to work B, **post-release or post-consent** | **Blocked.** It moves publishing income from one set of writers to another. Routed to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/) as a claim conflict. | "This recording has been released as a recording of *X*. Changing that moves money — it needs to go through a claim." |
| **Race**: two users link the same recording to different works within seconds | The link is written against the version the user was shown. The second write is **rejected — nothing is overwritten, nothing merged**. Last-write-wins here silently moves publishing. | "Sam linked this to *X* 4 seconds ago. You were linking it to *Y*." Both options shown; the user re-decides against current state. |
| **Race**: Producer and Musician both create a work from the same session close | Creation from a session is idempotent per session — the session holds one "what did this produce" record, and the second creator joins the existing draft rather than forking it. | The second user lands in the first user's in-progress record, with the first user's presence visible. |
| Two records share an **ISRC** with different consented ledgers | Not a duplicate — a conflict. An ISRC *is* identity; two ledgers under one ISRC means two parties assert incompatible ownership of one recording. | Routed to [09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md), which **flags and notifies both sides and executes nothing** — detection "has no authority to alter distribution… an explicit human dispute assertion is the required state-changing event" ([09.04 CX-04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-cx.md)), and [domain D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) reserves execution for arithmetic while interpretation notifies. A collision is an inference, so this feature does **not** freeze either record on its own reading. Whether an *asserted* dispute then blocks release, or only escrows payment, is an open owner call — [09.04 Q-02](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes-index.md) and [09.04.03 Q-04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md) — and this row must not pre-empt it. |
| Malicious: creating a work asserting a famous writer's name | Attaches nothing to that person and grants the creator nothing. Named ≠ confirmed (D-06). | The stub reads "named, not confirmed" to everyone, forever, until that person confirms. It is inert. |
| Duplicate-detection **false positive** (two real songs called *Home*) | Flag dismissible by either party; dismissal is remembered permanently for that pair. | "Not the same song" — one click, never asked again. |
| A work in the **public domain** | The user **declares** it (D-13). Its publishing ledger becomes `public-domain`, a terminal state with zero rows — **not** `unallocated`. It satisfies the **composition side** of the release gate; the master side is unaffected. Without a user-writable path, no PD work could ever be released until [09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) ships with historical reference data the platform does not have — which is absurd. 09.03.05 informs and corroborates the declaration; it never writes it. | "Composition is in the public domain — no publishing owners. You own your recording of it." The declarant and the date are shown beside it: this is your assertion, on the record. |
| Declared public domain; [09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) later returns a **determinate in-copyright** status in a supported jurisdiction | The declaration stands, the ledger state stands, and a release already made is **not** retracted (D-13). The term status attaches to the declaration as a contradiction, surfaces persistently on the work, and routes to [09.04.01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md) as a claim conflict if a party objects. The platform picks no winner — that is adjudication (D-04 domain). | "You declared this public domain on 12 Mar. Term status says in copyright in `DE` until 2031." Both records, side by side, each with its source. Persistent, not dismissible, never one silently replacing the other. |
| An arrangement of a public-domain work | A new work, `derived-from` the PD work, with a real ledger of its own. The parent's emptiness does not empty the child (DT-13). | Two composition rows: "*Trad. — public domain*" and "*Arrangement — you, 100%*". |
| A recording is a cover **and** contains a sample | Orthogonal. Both declared; neither substitutes for the other. | Both surfaces render; the release gate lists both requirements. |
| Deleting a work that has recordings | Refused. It would orphan every recording pointing at it. | "*X* has 3 recordings. Songs with recordings can't be deleted." |
| Deleting a record created 20 seconds ago in error | Hard-deleted — inside the 15-minute window, no consented ledger, no links. | Gone, with an undo toast. |
| Network failure mid-create | Nothing typed is lost. Confirmed fields are already committed; the in-flight field retries. **Names are never discarded** — capture data is unrepeatable. | Inline "Saving…" → "Couldn't save that — retrying" with a manual retry. The typed text stays on screen. |
| A surviving work absorbs a merged duplicate that had recordings | Recordings follow the survivor automatically; their links are rewritten, not dropped. | Recording owners notified with the before/after work named. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Song list or song detail requested | Skeleton rows preserving the **two-layer shape**, so the duality is legible before data arrives — the model is taught by the skeleton, not by a tooltip |
| Empty | User has no works or recordings | Musician: "Your songs live here. Start a project or add a song you've already written." Producer: "Recordings you've made live here." — the empty state is lens-specific because their libraries genuinely differ |
| Populated | Both records exist and are linked | Two-layer song view: Composition panel + Recording panel, each with its ledger summary, each labelled by what it pays, never summed |
| Partial | Recording exists with no work linked; or work exists with no recordings; or writers named but unconfirmed; or a multi-work recording whose weights are incomplete | The present layer renders fully; the absent one shows an inline "Link a composition" card — **not an error**. Unconfirmed writers grey with "invited". Incomplete disjoint-span weights show the named shortfall; temporal-overlap mashups name every missing/invalid declared weight and offer no automatic allocation. Partial is the normal state of a freshly-captured song, and must never look like a failure |
| Error | Write failed, or a concurrent write was rejected | Inline error with retry; **never** discards entered names. A concurrency rejection names who did what, when, and re-presents the decision against current state — it does not merge, retry silently, or overwrite |

Banned in every view, export, notification and tile. |
| DT-09 | D-02's "never auto-merge" must apply to unclaimed stubs too | Consistency instinct | ❌ REJECTED as absolute, ✅ REFINED — D-02's *reason* is that merging ledgers moves consented shares. **Two unclaimed stubs have no consented parties**: there is nobody to harm and nobody to consent. Canonical text equality is not person identity; fuzzy, identifier, audio, title, or legal/stage identity evidence may nominate a candidate but never authorize the merge. Auto-promoting them floods the registry with objects that each demand a ledger, a link and consent, and the Producer's "My Recordings" becomes an unusable file browser with a legal opinion attached. ## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **[09.01.02 Ownership Ledger & Validation](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)** — *shared entity + state race*. The object type selects the invariant ([CX-01](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md)). Two new obligations land on 09.01.02 from this file and must be reconciled at Step 6: (a) **medley weights** are exact rationals summing to 100% — a second arithmetic surface alongside the 100%/200% invariants, on the *link* rather than the ledger; (b) **ledger inheritance by reference** for `edit-of` / `remaster-of` / `alternate-take-of` lineage, where a child recording shares its parent's consented ledger until forked. (b) is **now settled, and this file's position held**: 09.01.02 D-01 says consent is to the whole ledger, and inheritance means a ledger consented once governs a second object. The parties are unchanged by construction, so no new consent is owed — and [09.01.02 D-17](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) records the mechanism that makes this consistent rather than merely convenient: the child opens **no ledger of its own**, so there is no second ledger reaching `consented` without a consent act; there is one ledger, referenced twice. It forks to an independent copy on first divergence, and D-06 then resets every consent on the copy. The radio edit does not cost five signatures. (a) medley weights remains open.
- Touches **[09.01.06 Sample & Interpolation Provenance](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)** — *shared mechanism*. Three of this file's cases route into 09.01.06's ledger-mutation machinery rather than duplicating it: a `derived-from` work link (the parent's writers take a share of the child's publishing), a mashup's master-side declarations, and a sub-threshold "medley component" that is really a quotation. **This file must not re-implement dilution + re-consent.** Boundary: this file owns the link; 09.01.06 owns what the link does to shares.

The consent-based (not role-based) writer-row rule in the Role Lens is enforced here, not in this feature.

The **declaration writes it** (D-13); 09.03.05 pre-fills, warns, and — once built — attaches its four-jurisdiction results as corroboration or contradiction, never as an override or a block. 09.03.05 D-06 is *confirmed* by this arrangement, not amended: a term status still authorizes nothing, and the declaration does. DT-13's arrangement case therefore works on day one, without 09.03.05 and without any death-date reference dataset — the parent is declared PD while the child's ledger stays live.

This file consumes codes and never issues them; identifier assignment never resolves writer identity or overrides the atomic predicate.
- Touches **[09.06.04 Public Rights Lookup](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md)** — *permission*. - Touches **[07 Music Projects & Collaboration](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)** — *trigger dependency + boundary*. The session close is where these records are born; this is the wedge in action. **Boundary (DT-12)**: session artifacts are not rights objects. - Touches **[11 Music Licensing](../../11-music-licensing/)** — *trigger dependency*. A sync licence needs *both* a master licence and a publishing licence; a licensor who owns one and not the other cannot grant a sync alone. **New from this file**: a medley's release path is materially harder than a cover's — per-publisher negotiated permission, refusable, one row per linked work (DT-06). 11 must model that as a distinct clearance shape, not as N covers.
- Touches **[12 Release & Distribution](../../12-release-distribution/)** — *trigger dependency*. Release requires both records complete and linked. Reading the state as clearing both sides is the single most expensive misreading available here.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Work↔recording link is **optional at creation, required at release** | Protects the capture moment (unrepeatable) at the cost of a deferred constraint (cheap, enforced where the user has the information). |
| D-02 | Duplicate works are **flagged, never auto-merged** — except where **no consented party exists on either side** and the asserted facts do not conflict | Merging works merges ledgers: a rights event requiring consent from every consented party. Where that set is empty there is nobody to harm and nobody to consent, and auto-merge is the only thing preventing unbounded stub proliferation on famous works (DT-09). The exception is a refinement of D-02's reasoning, not an exception to it. |
| D-03 | Ledgers are labelled by **what they pay**, not by their legal name — and the two percentages are **never unified into one number** | "Recording — paid when this version is streamed" beats "Master". The users who most need this distinction are the ones least fluent in the vocabulary. The corollary (DT-08) is the same rule defended at the summary tile instead of the edit screen. |
| D-04 | **Two link classes**, not one overloaded type: Recording→Work (typed, weighted) and Recording→Recording lineage (kinded) | `remix` and `re-recording` describe a relationship to a prior master; `cover` and `live` describe a relationship to a work. One field carrying both means a remix cannot name what it remixed, so the master owner's permission has nowhere to live (DT-04). |
| D-05 | A recording may link to **many works, each with a positive exact-rational weight** summing to exactly 100%; **works are never merged**. Duration proration is an editable proposal only for disjoint spans; overlapping mashups require declarant-entered weights with no automatic allocation. | Resolves Q-01 and CQ-07 Option B. A merged "medley work" would invent a rights object nobody owns and destroy each component's independent ledger, term, and territory. Duration describes disjoint span, not simultaneous creative or legal contribution; Domain 10 consumes valid as-of link weights and never repairs or invents allocation (DT-05). |
| D-06 | Work versioning offers **both paths — amend, or new work with `derived-from`** — and the platform never decides which applies | Whether a rewrite created a new work is a judgement about originality. D-04 (domain) is absolute: the platform holds facts and routes judgements. Defaulting to amendment is a UX default, not a ruling; the `derived-from` alternative is always one click away and never inferred. Resolves Q-02. |
| D-07 | Lineage kinds that add **no new party** (`edit-of`, `remaster-of`, `alternate-take-of`) inherit the parent's consented master ledger **by reference**; kinds that add one (`remix-of`, `re-recording-of`) do not | Five signatures for a radio edit is the friction that kills capture, and an edit has genuinely no new party to consent. Inheritance is a default, overridable in both directions, and forks into an independent ledger the moment anyone changes it. **Obligation discharged** — [09.01.02 D-17](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) carries it: a no-new-party child opens no ledger, holds a reference, and forks to an independent copy on first divergence, at which point 09.01.02 D-06 resets every consent. The opt-*in* direction of "overridable in both directions" is not symmetric and is tracked at [09.01.02 Q-08](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md). |
| D-08 | **This feature blocks nothing at capture.** It records, flags, and lets the release gate refuse | The pattern under D-01, DT-03 and DT-11 is one rule. Every blocking instinct — force a work, force a licence, force a linked composition — taxes the one moment the platform exists to protect. Enforcement is [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/)'s, where the user has motive, time, and information. |
| D-09 | A recording exists here only by **explicit assertion**, never by promotion of a session artifact | Auto-promoting takes and stems floods the registry with objects demanding ledgers, and destroys the signal that makes an assertion meaningful (DT-12). | D-11 | Records with any consented ledger row, link, or earnings are **never deletable**; hard delete exists only within **15 minutes** of creation for an unlinked, unconsented record, by its creator | A rights record with a consented ledger is evidence of what parties agreed — deleting one destroys the asset the platform is for. | D-13 | **The user's declaration is the writer of `public-domain`, and the platform never asserts it.** A declared public-domain composition puts the work's publishing ledger into `public-domain` — terminal, zero rows, invariants not run ([09.01.02 D-16](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)) — which satisfies the **composition side of the release gate only**. [09.03.05](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) informs the declaration (pre-fill, inputs, jurisdictions, a warning on `in copyright` or `unknown`) and, once built, attaches its per-jurisdiction results as **corroboration or contradiction** — never an override, never a block. A v1 declaration is **worldwide** ([09.01.02 D-15](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md)); jurisdictional disagreement is surfaced, not modelled. | Every alternative that makes 09.03.05 the writer blocks trad-folk and classical release until a `COULD`-bucket feature ships with historical death-date reference data WeJammin does not have — the outcome the public-domain edge case already calls absurd, and the genre DT-13 says the registry cannot declare out of scope. The declaration route is this file's own posture, not an exception to it: D-08 blocks nothing at capture and lets the release gate refuse; [09 D-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) is interpretation notifies, arithmetic executes; an asserted row is a claim rendered as a claim ([09.01.04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md)). A user can release an in-copyright song by ticking a box — the mitigation is the tree's standing one (attributed, timestamped, visible, disputable), which is a mitigation and not a prevention, and it is the trade the domain already makes everywhere else.
