# Feature: Work & Recording Records (composition/master duality)

> **Number**: 09.01.01
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Every piece of music on WeJammin is **two separate copyrights**: the *work* (the composition — melody
and lyrics, owned by writers and publishers) and the *recording* (the master — a specific fixed
performance of that work, owned by whoever paid for or made it). This feature is where those records
are created, linked, and viewed: one work can carry many recordings (the original, a live version, a
cover by a stranger), and a recording always points at exactly one work.

Users do not think in this model — they think "my song". The feature's job is to make the duality
**visible without being a lecture**, because the single most expensive mistake in the domain is a
musician who edits the master split believing they edited the publishing split.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates a work when they write something; creates a recording when they record it. Sees "My Songs" with both layers on one screen — *Composition* (who wrote it) and *Recording* (who made this version) — clearly separated. Marks a recording as a cover of an existing work, which attaches them to *zero* of that work's publishing. Sees every recording that exists of a work they wrote, including covers by people they have never met. |
| Producer | Full | Typically the one who creates the *recording* record, because they are the one in the room when it is fixed. Links it to a work — which may not exist yet, so they can create a stub work from the recording flow without leaving the session. Cannot create publishing shares on a work they did not write, but can name the writers for their confirmation. |
| Operator | None | A venue/studio operator is not a party to a composition or a master. Studios appear here only indirectly — if the studio entity holds a work-for-hire over its engineer's contribution ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), it appears as an owner *in the ledger*, never as a creator of these records. |
| Fan | None | Fans never create or edit rights records. The read-only, public-safe projection of this data — "who wrote this song, who played on this version" — is [09.06.04 Public Rights Lookup](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), which is a different feature with a different privacy posture. |

## Behavior

The registry holds two object types with a one-to-many link (work → recordings). Both are creatable
independently: a song can be written and never recorded; a recording can exist with no identified work
(a field recording, a drum-only stem, an improvisation not yet claimed as a composition). The link is
therefore **optional at creation and required before release** — deferring the constraint to the point
where it actually matters rather than blocking capture, which is the moment the platform must never
make expensive.

Recordings carry a **relationship type** to their work: *original*, *cover*, *live*, *remix*,
*re-recording*. The type is not decoration — a re-recording is the mechanism by which an artist escapes
a master they no longer control while the underlying work is untouched, and the model must express that
without contortion.

### Happy Path

1. Producer closes a session in [domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/) and the platform asks what the session produced.
2. Producer names the recording; the system asks "is this a recording of a song already on WeJammin?"
3. Producer picks an existing work, or creates a new one inline by naming it and the writers present.
4. The system shows the two layers side by side, each with an empty ledger and a visible "unallocated" badge.
5. Producer proceeds to split capture ([09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)); both ledgers are prompted in one flow, never one silently.
6. Musician later opens "My Songs" and sees the work, its recordings, and each recording's relationship type.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recording created with no work identified | Allowed. Recording enters `work-unlinked` state; it can be shared and paid for but not released or licensed. | Amber badge "No composition linked — required before release", with a one-click link/create action. |
| Two users independently create the same work (same song, entered twice) | Duplicate detection flags the candidate pair; **no automatic merge** — merging two works merges two ledgers, which is a rights event, not a housekeeping one. | "This may be the same song as *X*" prompt to both parties, with a proposed merge requiring consent from every party on both ledgers. |
| Cover recording of a work whose owners are not on WeJammin | Allowed — the work exists as an unclaimed stub. The coverer owns their master; the publishing points at an off-platform work. | Recording shows "Composition owned off-platform — mechanical licence required" linking to [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/). |
| User edits the master ledger believing it is publishing | The core UX failure this feature exists to prevent. | Each ledger is labelled with *what it pays* ("Recording — paid when this version is streamed/sold") rather than jargon; a confirmation on first edit names which copyright is being changed. |
| Medley / work incorporating multiple works | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a recording pointing at many works breaks the one-to-many model. | `[PENDING]` |
| Work created but never recorded | Fully supported. Work sits with a publishing ledger and no recordings. | "No recordings yet" empty state with a CTA to start a project. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Song list or song detail requested | Skeleton rows preserving the two-layer shape, so the duality is legible before data arrives |
| Empty | User has no works or recordings | "Your songs live here. Start a project or add a song you've already written." |
| Populated | Both records exist and are linked | Two-layer song view: Composition panel + Recording panel, each with its ledger summary |
| Partial | Recording exists, no work linked (or vice versa) | The present layer renders; the absent one shows an inline "Link a composition" card, not an error |
| Error | Write failed | Inline error with retry; **never** discards entered names — capture data is unrepeatable |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The duality is a data-model concern and therefore not-product | Node Classification Gate: "architecture wearing a product costume?" | ❌ REJECTED — the *axiom* is a cross-cut every domain inherits (recorded as such in [../rights-ownership-cx.md](../rights-ownership-cx.md)), but the *surface* is unavoidably product: users must create, link, and distinguish these records, and the whole domain's value collapses if they mislabel which one they are editing. Both were recorded rather than choosing one. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **re-recording** to be a first-class relationship type, not a generic "version" | The Taylor Swift re-record precedent made this a mainstream artist strategy, not an edge case. A re-recording is a *new master of an existing work* — precisely what the duality enables — and it is the clearest demonstration to a user of why the two objects are separate. | ✅ CONFIRMED — added to relationship types. It doubles as the best available explanation of the duality: "you can re-record it because you never lost the song, only the tape." |
| DT-03 | The model should force every recording to have a work at creation | Data-integrity instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a constraint at creation time taxes the capture moment, which the thesis (D-18) says must be frictionless because it is unrepeatable. The constraint belongs at the *release* gate ([domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/)), where the user has time, motive, and the information. Deferring the constraint to where it bites is the correct trade. |

- Touches **11 Music Licensing** ([path](../../11-music-licensing/)) — a sync licence needs *both* a master licence and a publishing licence; a licensor who owns one and not the other cannot grant a sync alone.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Work↔recording link is **optional at creation, required at release** | Protects the capture moment (unrepeatable) at the cost of a deferred constraint (cheap, enforced where the user has the information). |
| D-02 | Duplicate works are **flagged, never auto-merged** | Merging works merges ledgers. That is a rights event requiring consent from every party on both sides, not a de-duplication job. |
| D-03 | Ledgers are labelled by **what they pay**, not by their legal name | "Recording — paid when this version is streamed" beats "Master". The users who most need this distinction are the ones least fluent in the vocabulary. |
