# Feature: Master Rights Ownership

> **Number**: 09.01.03
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The recording side of the duality: who owns the master, in what proportion, and what that ownership
actually confers. Master ownership is a single 100% pool — simpler arithmetic than publishing, and
more contested in practice, because the master is the asset labels acquire and artists spend careers
trying to get back.

The feature also carries the distinction that catches everyone out: **owning a master is not the same
as controlling its distribution**. An artist can own 100% of a master and still be unable to release it
because a distribution agreement grants exclusive rights for a term.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Names the master owners and their percentages; sees whether they own it outright (the independent default), share it with a band entity, or have assigned it to a label. Sees the encumbrances stacked on top — a distribution exclusive, a producer's points, a sample obligation — and therefore sees the gap between "I own 100%" and "I can do what I like with it". |
| Producer | Full | Co-owner in many independent arrangements and the party who created the recording record. Enters the master ledger during capture. Their own stake is usually **points** ([09.02.02](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md)) rather than ownership — a distinction this feature must keep sharp, since a producer with 3 points owns 0% of the master. |
| Operator | Read-only | A studio operator sees master ownership **only** where the studio entity is itself a named party — the "studio takes points/ownership in lieu of a fee" arrangement, still common in independent recording. Read-only because the studio's stake originates in an agreement ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), not in an edit to this ledger. Where no such agreement exists, the Operator sees nothing. |
| Fan | None | No access. Master ownership percentages are commercially sensitive and have no fan-facing use. |

## Behavior

Master ownership is a 100% pool of `(party, share)` rows over a recording, validated by
[09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md). The parties are people or entities from
[domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) — the band-as-entity case matters here, because
a band owning a master collectively is different from four members owning 25% each, and the difference
only becomes visible when the band dissolves.

Layered on the ownership pool are **encumbrances** — things that constrain what the owner may do
without changing who owns it:

- a **distribution/licence exclusive** for a term and territory (see [09.03.02](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.02-term-territory-reversion.md))
- **producer points** — a royalty off the top, not an ownership share
- a **sample obligation** — a master-use royalty owed to a sampled recording's owner ([09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md))

### Happy Path

1. Recording record exists ([09.01.01](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)).
2. During capture, the master ledger is prompted alongside the publishing ledger — never alone.
3. Parties and shares are entered; default for an independent session is 100% to the artist or band entity.
4. Producer points, if any, are recorded as an encumbrance rather than as a share.
5. All parties consent; the ledger locks.
6. The master detail view shows ownership **and** "what you can do with it" — the encumbrances, plainly.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Band owns the master as an entity | Supported — the band entity is the single 100% party. Member-level entitlement is governed by the band agreement in [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/), not here. | "Owned by *The Band* (entity)" with a link to the band's governance, so the member/entity boundary is explicit rather than assumed. |
| Band dissolves | Rights registry does **not** decide the outcome. The band agreement does. | Master shows the band entity with a "governance in flux" pointer to domain 01. Deliberately no opinion — a rights registry that adjudicates partnership dissolution is out of its lane. |
| Artist owns 100% but cannot release | Correct and intended: an exclusive distribution encumbrance blocks it. | "You own 100%. *Distribution exclusive to X until 2029* — this is why you can't release it here." Naming the reason is the whole point. |
| Master assigned to a label | Ownership rows change; the transfer is recorded in [09.03.01 Chain of Title](../09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md). | Transfer requires consent from current owners; history is visible forever. |
| Re-recording made to escape an owned-elsewhere master | Fully supported — new recording, same work, new 100% pool. | The new master's ledger is independent; the work's publishing ledger is untouched. This is the duality paying off. |
| Producer claims ownership, artist believes it was points | Ledger enters `disputed` via [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/). | Dispute banner; payouts frozen. This is the most common master-side conflict and the reason points and shares must be structurally different objects. |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Producer points can be modelled as a small ownership share (e.g. 3 points = 3%) | It is how they are colloquially discussed, and it would simplify the model to one pool | ❌ REJECTED — points are a **royalty off the top**, not an ownership interest. A producer with 3 points cannot license the master, cannot block a sync, does not vote on a sale, and is not in the chain of title. Modelling them as ownership grants them all four by accident. This conflation is one of the most common real-world master disputes, so a platform whose thesis is provenance cannot afford to reproduce it in its schema. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **ownership vs control** gap to be surfaced explicitly — and nothing in the candidate list covers it | Artists routinely discover they own a master they cannot exploit. The candidate list had "Master Rights Ownership" as a bare noun; ownership without control is a half-truth that reads as a lie the first time it bites. | ✅ CONFIRMED — encumbrances are first-class here, and the master view answers "what can I actually do with this?" rather than only "whose is it?". This is a consolidation payoff: only a platform that also holds the distribution and licence records can answer that question at all. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Points are encumbrances, not shares** | Modelling points as ownership silently grants licensing, blocking, and chain-of-title standing (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | The master view surfaces **ownership and control together** | Ownership alone is a half-truth. Only a consolidated platform can answer the control half — this is the thesis in miniature. |
| D-03 | Band dissolution outcomes are **out of scope here** — deferred to domain 01 governance | A rights registry adjudicating partnership law is out of its lane; it records the outcome, it does not determine it. |
