# Feature: Performer & Neighbouring Rights Eligibility

> **Number**: 09.01.05
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The record of **who played on a recording and at what level** — featured or non-featured — which is a
different record from who owns it. In most of the world, performers on a master are entitled to
equitable remuneration when it is broadcast or publicly performed, collected by neighbouring-rights
societies, *regardless of whether they own any of it*. A session drummer owns 0% of the master and 0%
of the work, yet has a real, collectable income stream from every play — and almost never collects it,
because nobody wrote down that they were there.

**This is a Deep Think addition — it was not on the candidate list.** Its absence would have meant the
registry could not express the primary income stream of the platform's structural centre persona.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The session player. Confirms they performed on a recording and at what level (featured / non-featured), and on what instrument. Sees every recording they have performed on accumulating in one place — which is precisely the "proof of work that accretes to identity" the persona's success criteria describe. Can use that record to register with a neighbouring-rights society via [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) and collect money they currently never see. |
| Producer | Config | The attester. They were in the room and they know who played — their confirmation is what makes this record worth more than a self-claim, which `meta/personas.md` identifies as their defining structural value. They configure the performer list during capture; they do not own it, and a performer's own confirmation is still required. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator is not a performer by virtue of providing the room. Where a studio's engineer performs (rare but real), that person is a Musician in that capacity, not an Operator. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No access to the eligibility record. The *credit* derived from it — "drums: X" — is fan-facing, but that surfaces via [domain 02](../../02-credits-attribution/) and [09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), not here. |

## Behavior

A list of `(performer, level, instrument/role, confirmation-state)` rows attached to a recording,
disjoint from the master ownership ledger. A person may appear in both, either, or neither.

The **level** distinction is the load-bearing part:

- **Featured performer** — the named artist. Typically also a master owner. Higher remuneration share.
- **Non-featured performer** — session players, backing vocalists, orchestra. Usually 0% owners. Lower
  per-play share, but across a working session musician's catalogue it is a meaningful income stream —
  and it is the one most reliably lost, because the paperwork that proves presence was never filed.

Eligibility is **territory-dependent and unusually so**: the US has no general public-performance right
in sound recordings for terrestrial radio, so a US-only performer's picture differs sharply from a
European one. The platform records the *fact of performance*; it must not assert entitlement, which is
a legal conclusion that varies by territory and by society.

### Happy Path

1. Producer closes a session; the platform asks who played, prompting from the session's participant list ([domain 07](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)) rather than a blank field.
2. Producer marks each person's level and instrument. This is one screen, in the room, while everyone is present.
3. Each named performer receives a confirmation request; confirming is one tap.
4. The recording's performer record locks alongside the master ledger.
5. Musician's profile shows the performance; it is now citable rather than claimable.
6. Musician (or the platform, via [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)) uses the record to register the performance with a neighbouring-rights society.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Performer owns 0% of the master | Normal and expected — the whole point. | "You own 0% of this master. You are a non-featured performer — this may still earn you neighbouring rights." Naming the income stream is the value. |
| Performer is also a master owner | Both records exist independently; no inference either way. | Both shown, clearly as separate facts. |
| Producer lists someone who was not there | Performer declines the confirmation request. | Row stays unconfirmed; never counts. Counter-attestation is the guard against the "inflates credits" anti-persona behaviour. |
| Performer confirms; Producer later removes them | Removing a confirmed performer is a **contested edit**, not a delete. | Routes to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/). A confirmed fact is not unilaterally erasable. |
| Territory has no neighbouring right for this use | Fact of performance still recorded; entitlement not asserted. | "Recorded. Entitlement varies by territory" — the platform states facts, not legal conclusions. |
| Sampled performers on a source recording | Not performers on the new recording. | Excluded by design — see [09.01-rights-registry-cx.md](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md) R-02. |
| Performer never confirms | Row stays `unconfirmed` indefinitely; does not block the master ledger. | "Waiting on N" — visible but not obstructive. Blocking the ledger on a performer confirmation would tax capture for a non-ownership fact. |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |

| DT-01 | Performer presence is already covered by [domain 02 Credits](../../02-credits-attribution/) — this is a duplicate node | Node Classification Gate: is this a thing in this domain, or someone else's? | ❌ REJECTED — a credit is an *attribution* claim ("drums: X", displayed to humans); neighbouring-rights eligibility is a *rights* record ("X is a non-featured performer on this master, potentially entitled to equitable remuneration, in these territories"). They derive from the same moment and must not diverge, which makes them a cross-cut — but they answer different questions to different consumers, and only one of them is an input to a collection society. Recorded as CX to domain 02 rather than merged. |
| DT-02 | This node should not exist, because the sweep's 23 candidates did not include it and the sweep was exhaustive | Deference to the candidate list | ❌ REJECTED — and the omission is instructive. The candidate list covered ownership exhaustively (master, publishing, points, WFH, chain of title) and missed the one rights layer that belongs to people who own **nothing**. That is a systematic bias: the sweep saw rights through the owner's eye. The persona most affected — the session musician who plays on other people's records — is `meta/personas.md`'s explicitly declared structural centre, and their most-lost income stream had no home in the registry. This is the clearest evidence in the domain that the candidate list was a floor, not a ceiling. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Performer record is **disjoint** from the master ownership ledger | A person may be in either, both, or neither. Inferring one from the other erases the session musician, who is in one and not the other. |
| D-02 | The platform records the **fact of performance**, never asserts entitlement | Entitlement is a territory- and society-varying legal conclusion. Recording facts is defensible; asserting conclusions is not. |
| D-03 | An unconfirmed performer row **does not block** the master ledger | Blocking capture on a non-ownership confirmation taxes the moment the thesis says must stay cheap. Visible, not obstructive. |
| D-04 | Producer attestation is **required but not sufficient** — the performer confirms too | Counter-attestation is the structural guard against the credit-inflation anti-persona behaviour, and it is what makes the record worth more than a self-claim. |
