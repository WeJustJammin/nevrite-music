# Feature: Sample & Interpolation Provenance

> **Number**: 09.01.06
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The declaration that a recording or work incorporates someone else's — a sampled master, a replayed
interpolation, a sample-pack loop — and the clearance status of that incorporation. Uncleared samples
are the industry's most efficient way to convert a successful record into a liability, and the exposure
is **not limited to the person who did it**: `meta/personas.md` names it as a Musician's worst
accidental behaviour precisely because it reaches every collaborator downstream.

This feature owns the **fact** ("this contains that") and the **status flag** ("cleared / uncleared /
pending"). It does not own the clearing — obtaining the licence is [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares a sample when they use one; sees the clearance status on everything they are attached to, including works they did not build — because their exposure is not limited to their own choices. Sees, before release, exactly which uncleared element is blocking and whose problem it is. Consents (or refuses) when someone else's sample declaration dilutes their publishing share. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the only person who knows a sample is in there — they built the track. Declares it at session close, when the fact is fresh and the source is still identifiable, rather than at release when nobody can remember where the loop came from. Sees which stems carry declared samples before delivering them, which is the guard against exporting the liability to a client. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator has no interest in a track's sample provenance. Explicit absence — no Operator surface here. |
| Fan | None | No access to the internal flag. The *derived* fact ("interpolates *X* by *Y*") may surface publicly via [09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md) once settled, since sampled-writer credits are already public information on released records. |

## Behavior

A sample declaration is a typed link from this rights object to a source, plus a status. The **type**
determines which right is implicated, and this is the distinction that gets people sued:

| Type | What was taken | Right implicated | Typical settlement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Master sample** | The actual audio of a recording | The source **master** *and* the underlying **work** — both | A master-use royalty or buyout, plus a publishing share |
| **Interpolation** | The melody/lyric, replayed by you | The source **work** only | A share of the new work's publishing (commonly 15–50%) |
| **Sample-pack / licensed loop** | Audio you hold a licence for | Governed by that licence's terms | Usually already cleared — but only within the licence's scope |

An interpolation is **not metadata** — it mutates the ledger (see
[09.01-rights-registry-cx.md](./09.01-rights-registry-cx.md) CX-02). Sampled writers become writers of
the new work, diluting everyone already on it, which requires their re-consent
([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)).

Critically: **the declaration and the settlement are separate records**. The fact that a sample is in
there stays true whether or not the negotiation succeeds. A declaration must never be reversible
because clearance failed — that would make the honest path the risky one.

### Happy Path

1. Producer builds a track containing a loop; at session close the platform asks "does this contain anyone else's music?"
2. Producer declares it, picks the type, and identifies the source — searching WeJammin's registry first, then external, then free text as a last resort.
3. If the source is a sample pack bought on [domain 14](../../14-digital-goods-marketplace/), the licence terms are already known and the status resolves to `cleared-by-licence` automatically. This is a consolidation payoff no point solution can offer.
4. Otherwise, status is `uncleared`; the recording carries a visible flag and is release-blocked.
5. Clearance is pursued via [domain 11](../../11-music-licensing/); on success the status flips and, for an interpolation, the ledger dilution is proposed and re-consented.
6. Release unblocks.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Sample declared, clearance never obtained | Fact stands; status stays `uncleared`; release stays blocked. | "Blocked: uncleared sample from *X*" — naming the blocker and the owner of the problem, on every collaborator's view. |
| Source cannot be identified ("I don't remember where this came from") | Declaration accepted with `source-unknown`. Honesty must be cheaper than silence. | "Declared, source unidentified — this cannot be cleared until identified." Still release-blocking, but recorded. |
| Sample declared **after** release | The expensive case — and the mechanics are settled; only the recovery *policy* is still open (Q-02). The declaration stands and is not reversible (D-01). The dilution arrives as a **proposed amendment, never a write**, and the `consented` version keeps governing payouts while it is pending ([09.02.04 D-03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)); a refusal leaves the fact intact and moves the ledger to `disputed` ([09.01.02](./09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md), same edge case). Any correction to already-paid distributions is a **restatement** — transitive across calculations, recoupment, statements and payouts ([10.03.04 D-05](../../10-royalties-collections/10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.04-restatement-adjustment-overpayment.md)) — and overpayment recovery is **never silent** ([10.03.04 D-03](../../10-royalties-collections/10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.04-restatement-adjustment-overpayment.md)). | Dilution proposal with per-party before/after deltas ([09.02.04 D-05](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)), and the downstream damage — active licences, filed registrations, in-flight runs — enumerated **before** it is sent ([09.02.04 D-11](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). Money already distributed appears as a separate, named line, never adjusted silently. |
| Sample-pack licence does not cover this use (e.g. exclusivity, or a sync) | Status is `cleared-by-licence` **within scope** only; scope is checked at the point of use. | "Cleared for release. Not cleared for sync — this pack's licence excludes it." |
| Interpolation dilutes a co-writer who refuses to consent | Declaration stands; ledger enters `disputed`; release blocked. | Dispute banner. The refusal does not undo the fact. |
| Collaborator discovers an undeclared sample in a delivered stem | Routes to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/) — this is the persona's named nightmare. | Flag-raising action available to anyone attached to the work, not only its author. |
| AI-generated element trained on unlicensed material | Out of scope here; routes to [09.05](../09.05-ai-voice-likeness-consent/). | Pointer, not a duplicate flow. |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|

| Partial | Declared, source unidentified | Row shown with `source-unknown` and what is needed to progress |
| Error | Write failed | Inline retry; declaration text preserved |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a *flag* — metadata on a recording — and therefore trivially a feature | The sweep's own framing: "Sample Provenance & Clearance **Status Flags**" | ✅ CONFIRMED as a feature, ❌ REJECTED as a flag. It survives the sub-domain test (one capability: declare-and-track an incorporation) so it stays a file. But the "flag" framing understates it: an interpolation declaration **mutates the ownership ledger** and triggers re-consent from every existing writer. A flag does not do that. The sweep's own noun was misleading about the feature's blast radius. |
| DT-02 | An empty provenance panel should read as a clean bill of health | Natural UX instinct — green means good | ❌ REJECTED — this is the most dangerous available design. "No samples declared" means nobody said anything, which is indistinguishable from "nobody looked". Presenting absence as clearance manufactures false confidence in exactly the population (`meta/personas.md`: collaborators downstream of an uncleared stem) that gets hurt. Empty state must read as *unasked*, never as *clear*. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would expect the **sample-pack licence chain** to auto-resolve, and no candidate connects it | Nobody in the candidate list linked domain 14 (Digital Goods) to sample clearance. But a producer buying a loop pack on WeJammin means the platform *already holds the licence terms* — it can answer "is this cleared?" without asking. | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a cross-domain touchpoint to 14. This is the thesis in miniature: the consolidation (selling the sample pack) is what makes the provenance (knowing the loop is cleared) capturable at zero user effort. No point solution can do this, because none of them sold you the pack. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The **declaration** and the **settlement** are separate records | A declaration must never be reversible because clearance failed, or the honest path becomes the risky one. |
| D-02 | Empty state reads as **unasked**, never as **clear** | Presenting silence as clearance manufactures false confidence in the exact population that gets hurt (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | Anyone attached to a work may **raise** a provenance flag, not only its author | The exposure is collective, so the alarm must be too. |
| D-04 | `source-unknown` is an **accepted** declaration state | Honesty must be cheaper than silence, even when it is incomplete. |
