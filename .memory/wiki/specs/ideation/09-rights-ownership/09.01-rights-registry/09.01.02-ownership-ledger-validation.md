# Feature: Ownership Percentage Ledger & Share Validation

> **Number**: 09.01.02
> **Parent**: [Rights Registry](./09.01-rights-registry-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The ledger is the list of who owns what percentage of a rights object, and the validator is the engine
that refuses to let it be arithmetically wrong. It enforces the invariants of music ownership — a master
ledger totals 100%; a publishing ledger totals 200% in the conventional writer/publisher expression —
and it holds the exactness, nesting, and effective-share rules that every downstream payout depends on.

This feature is deliberately *only* arithmetic and structure. What the shares **mean** (a co-pub deal,
producer points, a buyout) lives in the sibling features. A validator that knows about deal semantics
becomes a business-rules engine nobody can reason about (D-04).

**The validator's guarantee is soundness, never truth.** A ledger that balances perfectly can allocate
100% to four people who were never in the room. The platform can prove the arithmetic and can prove who
consented; it cannot prove the facts, and per [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md) it must never
imply that it has. This constrains the *language* of the feature, not just its logic — see DT-12.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Enters or accepts their own percentage; sees a live running total with the remainder called out ("15% unallocated") rather than a silent invalid state. Sees their *effective* share when a publisher controls part of it — the number that predicts their income — displayed beside the nominal one, each labelled, never silently substituted. Sees **who typed every row**, not only its value (DT-06). Consents to the whole ledger or not at all (D-01); may explicitly refuse with a reason, which is a different state from not having answered (D-08). Cannot change a share anyone has consented to — that routes to [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md). Being named on a ledger grants read on **that whole ledger and nothing else** (D-07) — a session player consenting to a split does not thereby gain access to the project, the stems, or the other party's catalogue. |
| Producer | Full | Drives the ledger during capture — usually typing other people's percentages, which is exactly why every entry requires that person's consent rather than the Producer's word, and why their authorship of each row is recorded and shown to everyone (DT-06). Can always save an unbalanced draft; what is gated is *proposing* it (D-05) — the capture moment must never be lost to a validation error. Sees both ledgers (master and publishing) with their different invariants, and sees a pair-completeness indicator ("1 of 2 ledgers consented") when one is done and the other untouched — the classic "we did the master, we'll do publishing later" abandonment, which is the Producer's declared worst accidental behaviour in [meta/personas.md](../../meta/personas.md). |
| Operator | None | A venue or studio operator has no share in a ledger by virtue of being an Operator. If a studio entity holds an ownership stake (via work-for-hire or a points-for-time arrangement), it appears here as a **named party** — an entity from [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) — and is then treated exactly like any other party: it consents, it is bound, it is visible. The persona grants nothing; the party row does. |
| Fan | None | No access. Percentage ownership is commercially sensitive and asymmetrically damaging to disclose; even the public lookup ([09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md)) is expected to expose *who* without exposing *how much* — see [09 Q-06](../rights-ownership-index.md). Nothing in this feature has a Fan-facing projection. |

## Behavior

A ledger is a set of `(party, pool, role, contribution-basis, share, consent-state, entered-by)` rows
attached to exactly one rights object. It has a lifecycle of its own —
`unallocated` → `proposed` → `consented` → (`disputed` | `superseded`) — and downstream domains gate on
that **state**, not on the numbers.

### The invariants

The sweep candidate that produced this feature was named *"Ownership Percentage Ledger & 200%
Validation"*. That name encodes a bug: **200% is not one invariant, it is two, and checking the sum
alone lets a corrupt ledger pass** (DT-04).

| # | Invariant | Applies to | Rule |
|---|-----------|-----------|------|
| INV-1 | Pool totals | Master ledger | The single ownership pool sums to exactly 100%. |
| INV-2 | Pool totals, independently | Publishing ledger | The **writer pool** sums to exactly 100% **and** the **publisher pool** sums to exactly 100%. A ledger of 150 writer + 50 publisher totals 200% and is invalid. The two pools are checked separately and reported separately. |
| INV-3 | Per-writer publisher derivation | Publishing ledger | For each writer W holding writer share `w`, the publisher-pool rows attributable to W sum to exactly `w`. A publisher cannot hold publisher points that no writer's share generated. This is what makes a 50/50 co-pub of a 25% writer expressible (writer keeps 12.5 publisher points, publisher takes 12.5 — attributable sum 25) and a publisher claiming 30 points off a 25% writer rejectable. |
| INV-4 | Range | All | Every share is in `[0, 100]`. 0% is valid and meaningful (INV-6). Negative shares are rejected at entry. |
| INV-5 | Exactness | All | Shares are exact rational values. The pool total is computed on the exact values, never on their display rounding (D-02, DT-01). |
| INV-6 | Zero rows are rows | All | A 0% party (work-for-hire contributor, non-featured performer) is a valid row and is not pruned. Absence and zero are different statements (per [09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |
| INV-7 | No blank publisher rows | Publishing ledger | Where a writer is unpublished, their publisher share is auto-created as self-held (D-03). Absent rows are the origin of black-box money. |

INV-1..INV-3 are evaluated **per territory**. Until [Q-04](#open-questions) resolves, every ledger has
exactly one territory — `World` — and the invariants collapse to the single-territory case. This is
stated so the model is not *written* territory-blind even while it *runs* territory-free (see
[09 D-08](../rights-ownership-index.md)).

### Exactness and the display

Thirds are the common case — three co-writers — and 33.33 × 3 = 99.99, which either fails INV-1 forever
or silently loses a hundredth of every royalty cheque, compounding across a catalogue (DT-01).

- **Input** is accepted in three forms: a decimal (validated on blur), a fraction (`1/3`), or the
  **equal-split control**, which is the intended path for thirds, sevenths, and every other
  non-terminating case and is one tap.
- Any decimal the user types is taken as **exactly that decimal** — `33.33` means 33.33, not "about a
  third". The system never infers that a user meant a fraction. If the resulting pool does not total,
  the remainder is shown and the equal-split control is offered; nothing is silently corrected.
- **Display** rounds to 2 decimal places, and any row whose exact value is not representable at 2dp is
  marked as rounded (`33.33%*` with the exact value on hover/expand). A consent binds to the **exact**
  value, so the exact value must be reachable from the consent surface — otherwise a party consented to
  a number they were never shown (DT-05).
- **The display is never the source of a write.** Editing one row must not resubmit the rendered values
  of untouched rows, or a single edit silently converts every exact third into 33.33 and breaks the
  pool (DT-07).
- **The ledger never produces a remainder — money does.** Because shares are exact and total exactly
  100%, there is no residue in the ledger. Residue appears only when domain 10 divides an integer number
  of cents by an exact rational. That is domain 10's allocation, and the ledger's obligation to it is to
  expose exact rationals plus a **deterministic canonical row order** so that cent allocation is
  reproducible and auditable across runs (DT-08). **The canonical order is the ascending lexicographic
  ordering of each row's identity key `(pool, party-id, role, contribution-basis)`** — the same tuple
  that already makes every row unique (see the multi-hyphenate edge case), so the order is total and has
  no ties. It is a function of the ledger's *content*, never of retrieval order, insertion sequence, or
  `entered-by`, so the same consented ledger sorts identically on every run and in every store. Pools
  order before parties so a payout can walk writer rows and publisher rows in stable blocks. This resolves
  the representation question at the behavioural boundary; only the storage *type* of the rational remains
  `/create-prd-architecture`'s (Q-02). **`party-id` collation is fixed: compare immutable canonical
  serialized internal party IDs as unsigned UTF-8 byte sequences in lexicographic order. The comparator has
  no locale, display-name, case-folding, Unicode-normalization, or database-default behaviour; SQL, Workers,
  exports, and replay jobs must use this same semantic comparator (or a persisted equivalent binary sort key).**

### Nesting and effective share

A publisher controlling half of a writer's 25% is **not** a 12.5% row. It is a claim over a share
(CX-03). The ledger holds the **nominal** row and a link to the encumbrance defined in
[09.01.04](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md); the **effective** figure is derived from that deal's terms.
Neither is a lossy computation of the other, and both are displayed, each labelled:

- **Nominal** is what every external counterparty needs — societies, DSPs, registration forms.
- **Effective** is what the Musician needs — it is the number that predicts income, and it is the number
  a nominal-only ledger lies about (this is why domain 23's forecasting reads effective, not nominal).

An **entity party is never flattened**. A band holding 40% is one row holding 40%. The band's internal
distribution is a separate ledger under [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)
governance. Flattening four members into the song ledger would make the song's ownership churn every
time the band's membership changed — an event with no bearing on who owns this song (DT-09).

### Consent

Consent is to the **whole ledger**, never to a row (D-01). Consequences:

- Every named party must see every row, every value, and every row's author before consenting (D-07).
- Each row carries one of exactly three consent states: `pending`, `consented`, `refused`. `pending` is
  rendered as pending — never as assent, never as refusal (per [09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)).
- **Any change to any row resets every consent to `pending`** (D-06). This is expensive and it is the
  point: the ledger four people agreed to no longer exists once a fifth is added, so their agreement to
  it cannot be carried forward.
- Consent is submitted against a specific ledger version. A consent arriving against a superseded
  version is rejected as stale and the party is re-shown the current ledger with a delta (D-09).

### Alarm, not default

The dangerous state is not an invalid ledger — it is an **absent** one (DT-03). An invalid ledger is
loud and blocked; an unallocated ledger is silent, and the music still gets released, streamed and paid,
into a black box. Therefore `unallocated` is an alarm state, and this feature's job is to make it a
**queryable, gating, visibly alarming state** — the chasing and prompting belongs to
[09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md), which owns
the write path. Nothing downstream (release, licence, payout) may proceed against `unallocated`.

Note that a partially-allocated draft totalling 97% **is** `unallocated` — it is not yet a complete
proposal. This is deliberate: it keeps the ratified lifecycle at four states, and it makes the alarm
state carry the exact population it should — every ledger that is not yet a finished offer.

### Happy Path

1. A rights object is created ([09.01.01](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)); its ledger opens in
   `unallocated` with zero rows and copy that reads as an alarm, not a placeholder.
2. During capture, the Producer names parties and enters percentages. The running total and the
   remainder recompute on every keystroke and are always on screen — the remainder is a live figure
   ("15% unallocated"), never a validation error delivered after the fact.
3. Each row records who entered it. Alice's 25% typed by the Producer is shown to everyone as
   "25% — entered by [Producer]", distinct from a share Alice entered herself.
4. Three co-writers split equally: the Producer taps **equal split**; the ledger writes exact thirds and
   displays `33.33%*` on each row, with the exact value one tap away.
5. The Producer saves and closes the session. The draft persists regardless of whether it balances —
   saving is never gated (D-05).
6. When the pools satisfy INV-1..INV-7, the **Propose** action unlocks. The Producer proposes; the
   ledger moves to `proposed` and every named party's rows go to `pending`.
7. Each party opens the ledger and sees **the whole thing** — all rows, all values, each row's author,
   the exact values behind any rounded display, and their own nominal *and* effective shares.
8. Each party consents, or refuses with a reason. Refusal is recorded and visible; it does not delete
   the proposal and does not adjudicate anything.
9. All parties consent → the ledger moves to `consented` and locks, with a timestamp and the version it
   consented to. Further change requires amendment
   ([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)).
10. Downstream gates open: release ([12](../../12-release-distribution/)), licensing
    ([11](../../11-music-licensing/)) and payout ([10](../../10-royalties-collections/)) may now resolve
    against this ledger.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Shares total 97% | Draft **saves**. Ledger stays `unallocated`. **Propose** is disabled. | "3% unallocated — who owns it?" as a live remainder figure beside the total, with the equal-split control offered. Save is never blocked; the Propose button carries the reason. |
| Shares total 105% | Draft saves; Propose disabled. System highlights **nothing** automatically — it must not choose whose share comes down. | "You've allocated 105%. Someone's share needs to come down." No row is pre-selected or auto-adjusted. |
| Publishing ledger: writer pool 150%, publisher pool 50%, total 200% | **Rejected** by INV-2, which a naive 200%-sum check would pass. Pools are validated and reported independently. | "Writer shares total 150% — should be 100%. Publisher shares total 50% — should be 100%." Two separate remainders, two separate fixes. |
| Publisher claims 30 publisher points against a 25% writer | Rejected by INV-3. | "This publisher holds 30 points from [Writer]'s 25% share. A publisher can't hold more than the writer's share generates." Names the writer whose share is over-drawn. |
| Three-way equal split | Exact thirds retained; display rounds to 2dp and marks the rounding. | "33.33%*" on each row, total shows exactly 100%. Exact value on expand. The total never displays 99.99%. |
| User re-types `33.33` over a row whose exact value is 1/3 | Taken literally — that row becomes exactly 33.33 and the pool falls to 99.99%. Not silently re-inferred as a third. | Live remainder appears: "0.01% unallocated". Equal-split control offered as the fix. |
| User edits row 1; rows 2–3 hold exact thirds | Rows 2–3 are untouched and keep their exact values. The rendered 33.33 is never written back (DT-07). | Nothing — which is the correct outcome. The silent corruption this prevents is invisible when it works. |
| Input `1/0`, `-5`, `101`, or a decimal beyond representable precision | Rejected **at entry**, on blur, before it reaches the pool. Never coerced or rounded into the ledger. | Inline error on that field naming the specific problem ("Share can't be negative", "Can't divide by zero"). Other rows unaffected. |
| Party listed with 0% | Valid and important (INV-6) — a work-for-hire contributor or non-featured performer is a real 0% party. | Row shown with "0% — paid by fee, not by share", linking to the WFH designation ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)). Not pruned, not hidden. |
| Publishing ledger with no publisher rows | Valid — unpublished writers self-hold their publisher share. Rows auto-created as self-held (D-03, INV-7). | Rows appear pre-filled with an explanation ("You hold your own publishing"), never left blank. Blank is what creates unmatched money. |
| Same human is a writer **and** the master owner **and** a performer | Three rows across two ledgers plus a performer record ([09.01.05](./09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)). Never deduplicated — the row key is `(party, pool, role, basis)`, not `party`. | One person shown in multiple places, each labelled by what it pays. This is the multi-hyphenate case and it is the norm, not an anomaly. |
| Same party twice in one pool with different contribution bases (music 15% / lyrics 10%) | Valid — split sheets itemise contribution. Rows kept distinct; the party's total is shown aggregated with the breakdown expandable. | "Alice — 25% (music 15%, lyrics 10%)". |
| Producer enters a share for themselves | Permitted — they are frequently a legitimate party. The row records `entered-by = self` and is displayed to every other party as authored by the Producer. | Every party sees "[Producer] — 20% — entered by [Producer]" alongside "Alice — 25% — entered by [Producer]". Authorship is the disclosure; whole-ledger consent is the defence (DT-06). |
| A band entity is a party holding 40% | One row, 40%, party = the band entity. Members are **not** flattened into this ledger. | "The Band — 40%". Internal distribution links out to domain 01 governance and is not this ledger's arithmetic (DT-09). |
| Band's membership changes after the ledger consents | This ledger does not change. The band still holds 40%. | Nothing on this ledger. The consequence lands in domain 01, where it belongs. |
| A new party is added after 2 of 4 have consented | **All** consents reset to `pending` — the ledger they agreed to no longer exists (D-06). | "Adding [name] changes everyone's share. All 4 will need to confirm again." Stated **before** the add commits, so the cost is a decision, not a surprise. |
| Two Producers edit the same draft simultaneously | Field-level last-write-wins **within `unallocated` only** — nobody has consented, so there is nothing to protect — with every change attributed in a visible edit trail. | Live "[Producer B] changed Alice 25% → 30%" in the trail. At Propose, the whole ledger plus its trail is what parties review. |
| Two parties hit **Propose** at the same moment | First lands; second is rejected as operating on a stale version. Proposals queue, never merge — merging two proposals produces a split nobody proposed ([09.02.04 D-04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). | "This was proposed a moment ago by [name]. Here's what changed." Delta shown; no silent overwrite. |
| A party consents against a version that changed underneath them | Consent rejected as **stale**. Never applied to the new version. | "This split changed while you were reviewing it. Here's what's different." Before/after delta per [09.02.04 D-05](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md), then re-consent. |
| Network fails mid-consent; user retries | Consent is idempotent on `(party, ledger version)`. A retry cannot produce two consents or a partial state. | Either "Confirmed" or an unchanged pending row with a retry. Never an ambiguous outcome on a legal act. |
| Party is unregistered (no account) | Accepted as an invited stub. Ledger stays `proposed` until they consent; the stub is a real party, not a placeholder. | "Waiting on 1 of 4" with a chase action (chase belongs to [09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)). |
| Party explicitly **refuses** | Row → `refused` with the stated reason. Ledger stays `proposed`; it does **not** move to `disputed` — a refusal is a negotiation, not yet a conflict. Nothing is adjudicated. | "[Name] didn't agree: '[reason]'." Capture reopens for a new proposal. |
| Party is silent for 30 days | Row stays `pending`. Silence is **never** upgraded to consent or downgraded to refusal ([09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)). | "Waiting on [name] — 30 days" with escalating prominence, never an inferred answer. Terminal handling is [09 Q-07](../rights-ownership-index.md). |
| A consented party later deletes their account (GDPR erasure) | The **consent survives**; the personal data does not. The row is retained under the legal-claims ground for continued processing, and pseudonymised in place: share, pool, role, consent fact and timestamp intact; name and contact reduced to a stable opaque handle. The ledger does not revert, does not revalidate, and does not reopen — an erasure request is not an amendment, and treating it as one would let anyone unpick a signed split by deleting their account. Payouts to that party route per domain 10. | Other parties see "Erased party — 25% — consented 2024-03-11". The share is still theirs; the person is no longer named. Exact tombstone model is Q-03. |
| A consented ledger is diluted by a sample declaration ([09.01.06](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)) | Arrives as a **proposed amendment**, not a write. The `consented` version keeps governing payouts throughout ([09.02.04 D-03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). If any party refuses, the declaration still stands — the fact is true regardless — and the ledger enters `disputed` (CX-02). | Consented ledger with a banner: "A sample declaration would change these shares. 4 people need to confirm." Money keeps flowing on the current version. |
| Master ledger consented; publishing ledger never started | Both are valid states — but the pair is incomplete, and this is the Producer's declared worst accidental behaviour. Surfaced, never tolerated. | "1 of 2 ledgers consented — publishing hasn't been started." Persistent, on the object, not a dismissible toast. |
| Recording exists with no linked work | Valid — the link is optional at creation, required at release ([09.01.01 D-01](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)). The recording has a master ledger and no publishing ledger yet. | One ledger shown, with the publishing ledger's absence stated rather than implied by a missing tab. |
| Two duplicate works are found, each with its own ledger | **Flagged, never auto-merged** ([09.01.01 D-02](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)). Merging works merges ledgers, which is a rights event needing consent from every party on both sides — not a de-duplication job. | "These look like the same song. Merging them would merge two splits — everyone on both would need to confirm." |
| Ledger enters `disputed` ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)) | The **contested share** is frozen, not the work ([09.04.03 D-01](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)) — work-scoping would turn a dispute into a weapon against uninvolved co-owners. Uncontested shares keep paying. | Dispute banner naming the contested rows only. Other parties' rows show as still paying. |
| A payout arrives against an `unallocated` ledger | Distribution **halts**; it never falls back to a best guess or a pro-rata default ([09.04.03 D-03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)). | "Money arrived for this song and nobody owns it yet." The alarm state finally has money attached — which is exactly the black-box mechanism this domain exists to prevent (DT-03). |
| A licence was granted against a ledger that later amends | Out of scope here — the ledger records the amendment and the version each licence resolved against; whether the licence follows is domain 11's. | Nothing on this surface beyond version history. |
| Territory-varying shares (sub-publishing) | v1 has exactly one territory (`World`) and the invariants run per territory against that single value. | No territory UI in v1. See Q-04 — the model is written territory-aware, not territory-blind ([09 D-08](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |
| A named party is a minor | The ledger accepts the row; capacity to consent is not this feature's judgement to make ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Routed to [09 Q-04](../rights-ownership-index.md) (enforceable vs evidentiary) and domain 24. | Nothing distinguishing on this surface. Flagged as Q-05 — an honest gap. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Ledger requested | Skeleton rows matching the known row count — never a spinner that hides whether the ledger is empty, because empty is the alarm. |
| Empty (`unallocated`, 0 rows) | Object created, no shares entered | "Nobody owns this yet" + CTA into capture. Deliberately alarming copy (DT-03). |
| Partial (`unallocated`, rows present, pools don't satisfy INV-1..3) | Draft in progress | All rows, live total, live remainder, equal-split control, **Propose disabled with the reason on the button**. Save always available (D-05). |
| Populated (`proposed`) | Pools valid; proposed for consent | Full ledger, per-row consent status (`pending`/`consented`/`refused`), per-row author, "Waiting on N of M". |
| Populated (`consented`) | All parties consented | Locked ledger, signed badge, timestamp, consented version id. Nominal and effective both shown. Edits route to amendment. |
| Error (invariant violated) | INV-1..INV-7 unsatisfied | Inline remainder indicator per pool; the blocked action states the reason **as a question, not a code** ("3% unallocated — who owns it?"). Never a toast; never after the fact. |
| Error (write failed) | Network/service failure on save or consent | The draft is preserved locally and the failure is explicit. A consent never shows as succeeded unless it did — silence on a legal act is the one thing this surface must never do. |
| Partial (`disputed`) | Conflict raised ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)) | Ledger with a dispute banner scoped to the contested rows; those shares frozen to escrow ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)); uncontested rows still paying. |
| Superseded | An amendment consented ([09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)) | Read-only historical version, reachable from the current one. Never deleted — the history is the evidence ([09.02.04 D-01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)). |
| Pair-incomplete | One of a work/recording ledger pair consented, the other `unallocated` | Persistent "1 of 2 ledgers consented" on the object. Not dismissible. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Percentages can be stored as decimals like every other money-adjacent number | Default engineering instinct | ❌ REJECTED — three co-writers is the most common configuration in music and it is exactly the case decimals cannot represent. A 33.33% × 3 ledger totals 99.99% and either fails validation forever or silently loses a hundredth of every royalty cheque, compounding across a catalogue. Exact rational shares are required. Trivial now, unfixable after a million rows. |
| DT-02 | Consent should be per-row — you approve your own share | Reasonable-sounding permission model | ❌ REJECTED — the key insight of the feature. Your 25% is only meaningful relative to the other 75%. Per-row consent lets a Producer show each contributor only their own line, and every contributor cheerfully approves a sheet that does not total what they think it totals. Consent is to the **whole ledger**, which matches the persona anti-pattern in `meta/personas.md` (a Producer pushing through a larger share "while contributors aren't paying attention"). |
| DT-03 | The dangerous state is an invalid ledger | Validation-centric framing | ❌ REJECTED — the dangerous state is an **absent** one. An invalid ledger is loud and blocked; an unallocated ledger is silent, and the music still gets released, streamed and paid — into a black box. The MLC's hundreds of millions in unmatched mechanicals is made of absent rows, not wrong ones. `unallocated` must be an alarm state, not a neutral default. |
| DT-04 | "200% validation" is one invariant: the publishing pool sums to 200 | The sweep candidate's own name (*"Ownership Percentage Ledger & 200% Validation"*) | ❌ REJECTED — **and the candidate name encodes the bug**. 200% is two independent invariants: writer pool = 100% *and* publisher pool = 100%. A ledger of 150 writer + 50 publisher sums to 200 and passes a naive check while being catastrophically wrong — the writers are over-allocated by half and the publishers under-allocated by half, and every mechanical and performance payment splits on the wrong denominator. A third invariant (INV-3) follows: each writer's publisher points must derive from that writer's own share, or a publisher holds points no writer generated. The single most valuable finding in this file. |
| DT-05 | Displaying a rounded share is a cosmetic concern | Presentation/logic separation instinct | ❌ REJECTED — consent is a legal act and it binds to what the party agreed to. If a party is shown "33.33%" and the exact value is 1/3, either the consent binds to a number they never saw or it binds to a number the ledger doesn't hold. The exact value must be reachable from the consent surface, and rounded rows must be marked as rounded. Rounding stops being cosmetic the moment a signature attaches to it. |
| DT-06 | Recording each row's **author** (who typed it) is redundant once whole-ledger consent exists | Consent-is-sufficient framing | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary — whole-ledger consent (D-01) defeats the Producer anti-pattern *at the moment of signing*; row authorship defeats it *at the moment of reading*. "Alice — 25%" and "Alice — 25% — entered by [Producer]" are different disclosures: the second tells Alice that the number she is being asked to bless is the Producer's assertion about her, not her own statement. In a domain whose product is evidence, the provenance of each assertion is part of the record — a ledger that stores values but not their authorship has thrown away half of what it witnessed. This is the thesis (`capture at source`) applied to the ledger's own rows. |
| DT-07 | Round-tripping displayed values through the edit surface is harmless | Standard form-binding pattern | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real corruption path — if editing row 1 resubmits the rendered values of rows 2–3, one edit converts two exact thirds into 33.33 each and drops the pool to 99.99% with **no user action on those rows**. The user's own edit appears to break rows they never touched, and the corruption is invisible until validation fails or a payout goes short. The display must never be the source of a write for untouched rows. This is the specific mechanism by which DT-01's exactness gets destroyed in practice. |
| DT-08 | The ledger must decide the sub-cent rounding policy for payouts | Follows naturally from owning the arithmetic | ❌ REJECTED — the ledger produces **no** remainder. Shares are exact and total exactly 100%; residue exists only when an integer number of cents is divided, which happens in domain 10, against a distribution amount this feature never sees. Putting the policy here would put a money-allocation rule inside a validator (violating D-04) and would bind every distribution to a choice made without the distribution's context. What the ledger **does** owe domain 10 is a deterministic **canonical row order**, so cent allocation is reproducible and auditable across runs rather than dependent on retrieval order — otherwise two runs of the same distribution can pay different people the odd cent, and neither is defensible. |
| DT-09 | A band party should be expanded into its members so the ledger shows real humans | "Show the actual people" instinct; supported by the Musician being the structural centre | ❌ REJECTED — a band is a first-class entity (`meta/personas.md`), and flattening it makes the song's ownership a function of the band's current membership. A member leaving in 2029 would rewrite a 2026 song's consented ledger — an event with no bearing on who owns that song, and it would require re-consent from every party (D-06) for a change none of them made. The band holds 40% as one row; its internal distribution is a separate ledger under domain 01 governance. Correct nesting is via the entity, not via expansion. |
| DT-10 | Saving an unbalanced ledger should be blocked, since it is invalid | Validation-centric framing (and the language this file itself used at `[SURFACE]`) | ❌ REJECTED — **and it contradicts the thesis**. The capture moment is unrepeatable; a Producer mid-session typing shares who cannot save loses the work, and the fallback is the WhatsApp thread the platform exists to replace. Blocking save converts a validation rule into a data-loss path aimed precisely at the persona whose lazy path must be the correct path. Save always succeeds; **Propose** is what the invariants gate. The unbalanced draft stays `unallocated` — the alarm state — which is exactly the right population for it. |
| DT-11 | Being named on a ledger should grant project access, since you're a collaborator | Reasonable permission-inheritance instinct | ❌ REJECTED — whole-ledger consent (D-01) requires that a named party read the **whole ledger**, and nothing more. A session player asked to bless a split does not thereby earn access to the stems, the project, the other parties' catalogues, or the client relationship. Deriving broad access from a ledger row would make naming someone on a split sheet a security decision, and would give Producers a reason not to name people — inverting the incentive the whole domain depends on. The row grants exactly the visibility consent requires. |
| DT-12 | A validated ledger should be badged "Valid" | Obvious UI affordance | ❌ REJECTED — the validator proves arithmetic, never truth. A ledger allocating 100% to four people who were never in the room passes every invariant. Badging that "Valid" is the platform making a claim it cannot support, and per [09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md) the moment WeJammin appears to certify content, every record on the platform stops being evidence of what parties agreed and becomes evidence of what WeJammin says they agreed — which is the entire asset. The badge says **"Balances"**; the strong claim available is **"Signed by all 4 parties on [date]"**, which is a fact the platform genuinely witnessed. Language is load-bearing here, not decoration. |
| DT-13 | A refusal should move the ledger to `disputed` | Symmetry with the consent path | ❌ REJECTED — a refusal is a negotiation, a dispute is a conflict, and collapsing them makes the first person to say "I think I wrote more than that" the trigger for a freeze and escrow ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)). That penalises exactly the honest disagreement the consent gate is designed to elicit, and teaches parties that the safe move is silence — which is the failure mode ([09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)) the domain most needs to avoid. Refusal keeps the ledger `proposed` and reopens capture. `disputed` is reached from [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/), not from here. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **[09.01.01 Work & Recording Records](./09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)** — **shared entity + invariant selection**: the object type selects which invariant set runs (master → INV-1; work → INV-2/INV-3). The ledger cannot exist without an object; the object creates an `unallocated` ledger on creation. Duplicate-work merges are ledger merges and are gated on consent from every party on both sides (CX-01).
- Touches **[09.01.04 Publishing Rights](./09.01.04-publishing-rights.md)** — **nested claim + derivation**: co-pub creates shares-of-shares. The ledger holds the nominal row plus a link to the encumbrance; 09.01.04's deal terms are what derive the effective figure. INV-3 is the arithmetic boundary between the two features — the ledger enforces that publisher points derive from a writer's share; 09.01.04 decides what the deal does with them (CX-03).
- Touches **[09.01.06 Sample & Interpolation Provenance](./09.01.06-sample-interpolation-provenance.md)** — **trigger dependency, ledger-mutating**: an interpolation declaration dilutes every existing writer pro rata and therefore proposes an amendment requiring re-consent from all. The declaration must never write shares directly, and the fact survives even if the settlement fails (CX-02).
- Touches **[09.01.03 Master Rights Ownership](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md)** — **encumbrance vs share**: producer points are encumbrances, not ledger rows ([09.01.03 D-01](./09.01.03-master-rights-ownership.md)). The ledger must not accept a points arrangement as a share, or it silently grants licensing and chain-of-title standing to a royalty participant.
- Touches **[09.01.05 Performer & Neighbouring Rights](./09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md)** — **disjoint records, shared human**: a session drummer is a 0% ledger row (or absent entirely) and a full performer record. Ledger presence must not imply performer presence, and vice versa (CX-04).
- Touches **[09.02.01 Moment-of-Creation Split Capture](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)** — **write path**: capture is the only way rows get into this ledger; this feature owns the invariants and the state, capture owns the prompting and the chase. The `unallocated` alarm is exposed here and acted on there.
- Touches **[09.02.04 Split Amendment & Re-Consent](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)** — **state boundary**: `consented` is the handoff. Every post-lock change is an amendment that supersedes rather than mutates, and the consented version keeps governing while a proposal is pending.
- Touches **[09.02.03 Work-for-Hire & Buyout](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)** — **the 0% row's meaning**: WFH is why INV-6 exists. A 0% row that says "paid by fee" is the difference between a contributor who was paid and a contributor who was forgotten.
- Touches **[09.04.01 Conflicting Claim Detection](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.01-conflicting-claim-detection.md)** / **[09.04.03 Rights Freeze & Royalty Escrow](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)** — **state-race + freeze scope**: a ledger is the object a conflict is *about*. Freeze is share-scoped, so the ledger must support a partially-frozen state where uncontested rows keep paying.
- Touches **[10 Royalties & Collections](../../10-royalties-collections/)** — **payout instruction + determinism contract**: this ledger *is* the instruction every distribution resolves against. The ledger owes domain 10 exact rationals and a deterministic canonical row order; domain 10 owns cent allocation and the sub-cent remainder policy (DT-08).
- Touches **[01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)** — **entity party, not flattened**: a band/label/studio party is one row. Its internal distribution is domain 01's governance ledger, and its membership changes must never propagate into a consented song ledger (DT-09).
- Touches **[12 Release & Distribution](../../12-release-distribution/)** and **[11 Music Licensing](../../11-music-licensing/)** — **gating dependency**: both gate on `consented`, never on the numbers. `unallocated` blocks release; licences record which ledger version they resolved against.
- Touches **[23 Career, Finance & Business](../../23-career-finance-business/)** — **derived-value dependency**: forecasting reads **effective** shares. Forecasting on nominal shares over-states income for every writer in a co-pub deal — the population most likely to be relying on the forecast.
- Touches **[02 Credits & Attribution](../../02-credits-attribution/)** — **explicit non-dependency**: a credit is not a share and a share is not a credit. The 0% WFH contributor has a full credit; a label with no presence in the room has a master share and no credit. The ledger must never be used as the credit source.
- Touches **[24 Trust & Safety](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)** — **permission boundary**: DT-11 forbids deriving project access from a ledger row. Ledger visibility is scoped to the ledger.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Consent is to the **whole ledger**, never to a single row | Per-row consent is exploitable by exactly the persona anti-pattern the domain exists to prevent (DT-02). |
| D-02 | Shares stored as **exact rationals**, not decimals | Three-way splits are the common case and are unrepresentable in decimal (DT-01). Cheap now, unfixable later. |
| D-03 | Unpublished writers' publisher shares are **auto-created as self-held**, never left blank | Blank publisher rows are the mechanical origin of black-box money. The default must be "you hold it", not "unknown". |
| D-04 | The validator holds **arithmetic only**; deal semantics live in sibling features | Prevents the validator degenerating into a business-rules engine nobody can reason about. |
| D-05 | **Save is never gated; Propose is.** An unbalanced draft persists as `unallocated` | The capture moment is unrepeatable. Blocking save is a data-loss path aimed at the persona whose lazy path must be the correct path (DT-10). |
| D-06 | **Any change to any row resets every consent to `pending`** | The ledger they agreed to no longer exists. Carrying consent forward across a changed ledger would make D-01 decorative. The cost is stated before the change commits, so it is a decision rather than a surprise. |
| D-07 | Being named on a ledger grants read on **that ledger only** | D-01 requires whole-ledger visibility; nothing requires more. Deriving project access from a row would make naming someone a security decision and give Producers a reason not to name people (DT-11). |
| D-08 | Three consent states per row — `pending`, `consented`, `refused` — and **refusal keeps the ledger `proposed`** | Silence must read as silence ([09 D-06](../rights-ownership-index.md)). Collapsing refusal into `disputed` would punish honest disagreement and teach parties that silence is safer (DT-13). |
| D-09 | Consent binds to a **ledger version** and to **exact** values; stale consents are rejected, not applied | A consent applied to a version the party never saw is not a consent. A consent to a rounded display is a consent to a different ledger (DT-05). |
| D-10 | **"200%" is validated as two pools plus a per-writer derivation rule** (INV-2, INV-3), never as one sum | A 150/50 ledger sums to 200 and is catastrophically wrong. The candidate's own name encoded the bug (DT-04). |
| D-11 | Every row records **who entered it**, and that authorship is shown to every party | In a domain whose product is evidence, the provenance of an assertion is part of the record. The value alone throws away half of what the platform witnessed (DT-06). |
| D-12 | The badge says **"Balances"**, never "Valid" | The validator proves arithmetic, never truth. A platform that appears to certify content stops being a witness — which is the whole asset ([09 D-04](../rights-ownership-index.md), DT-12). |
| D-13 | Entity parties are **never flattened** into member rows | A band's membership change must not rewrite a consented song ledger (DT-09). |
| D-14 | The ledger produces no remainder; it owes domain 10 **exact rationals + a deterministic canonical row order** | Residue is created by dividing cents, not by the ledger. But without a canonical order, two runs of the same distribution pay the odd cent to different people, and neither is defensible (DT-08). |
| D-15 | Invariants are evaluated **per territory**; v1 has exactly one territory (`World`) | Writes the model territory-aware while running it territory-free, so [09 Q-05](../rights-ownership-index.md) resolves as a scope decision rather than a migration from hell ([09 D-08](../rights-ownership-index.md)). |
