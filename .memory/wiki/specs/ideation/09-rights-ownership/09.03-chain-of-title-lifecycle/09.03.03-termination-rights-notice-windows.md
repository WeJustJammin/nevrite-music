# Feature: Copyright Termination Rights & Notice Windows

> **Number**: 09.03.03
> **Parent**: [Chain of Title & Rights Lifecycle](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

US copyright law lets authors **undo a grant they made decades ago** — roughly 35 years later, within a
narrow statutory window, with advance notice. It cannot be contracted away: a deal saying "you waive
termination" does not remove the right. It is the mechanism behind the wave of artists reclaiming
catalogues they signed away as unknowns.

The window is the whole feature. Miss it and the right evaporates permanently, and the window opens
decades after anyone was thinking about the deal. A platform holding the grant dates can compute the
window and tell the author while they can still act — which is a service no one currently provides, for
a right most authors do not know they have.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The author, or their statutory heirs. Sees which of their grants are eligible for termination and when each window opens and closes. Receives notice well before the window, because a notification arriving inside it is nearly useless — the notice period itself is years long. Initiates the notice process. |
| Producer | None | Termination belongs to **authors** and their statutory heirs. A producer is not an author of the composition, and a producer's grant of their own points or master share is not the kind of grant the statute reaches in the same way. Explicit absence — this is a deliberate scoping call, not an oversight. |
| Operator | None | No authorship, no termination interest. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

Termination eligibility is computed from facts the platform can hold: the grant date, the grant type,
the work's creation and publication dates, and whether the work was made for hire (**work-for-hire is
not terminable** — the author was never the author for statutory purposes, which links this feature
directly to [09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)).

The mechanics that make it hard:

- **Two regimes** — §203 for post-1978 grants, §304 for pre-1978, with different windows and different eligible parties.
- **A long fuse** — notice must be served 2–10 years before the effective date, and the effective date is itself decades out.
- **Statutory heirs, not testamentary ones** — who may terminate is fixed by statute (widow/widower, children, grandchildren, in shares) and may be different people from whoever inherited the copyright (see [09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-03).
- **Derivative works survive** — a remix released under the terminated grant stays licensed. Termination is prospective, not a rewind.
- **US only** — this is a US statutory right with no general equivalent elsewhere.

### Happy Path

1. Musician records a 2027 publishing assignment as a chain event ([09.03.01](./09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)).
2. Platform computes the §203 window — roughly 2062–2067, notice servable from about 2052.
3. The grant carries a quiet, non-urgent marker: "terminable from 2062".
4. Around 2050, escalating notification: "You can begin reclaiming this in two years."
5. Musician (or heirs) initiates; the platform assembles the notice's required content from the chain.
6. Notice served, recorded, effective date set; on that date the grant becomes prospectively void.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Grant was work-for-hire | Not terminable. | "Not terminable — recorded as work-for-hire in 2027." Naming *why* matters: this is exactly why labels wanted WFH designations, and the author deserves to see the connection. |
| Author dead; heirs hold the right | Statutory hierarchy determines who, in what shares — possibly not the people named in the will. | Routed to the estate ([09.03.04](./09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md)) with the divergence flagged explicitly. |
| Window missed | Right is gone permanently. | This is the failure the feature exists to prevent. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` on how aggressively to escalate. |
| Grant is non-US or work has no US nexus | No termination right. | "Termination is a US right — not applicable." State the limit; do not imply a right that does not exist. |
| Derivative works exist under the grant | They survive termination. | "Terminating does not recall the 2031 remix." Managing the expectation is the point. |
| Grant date unknown (pre-platform, asserted) | Window cannot be computed reliably. | "Grant date not verified — window is an estimate." Trust level propagates from the chain (see [09.03.01](./09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) D-01). |
| Platform computes the window wrong | The author relies on it, misses the window, loses the right permanently and irreversibly. | The risk driving DT-02. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a **Must** — it is the mechanism behind artists reclaiming their catalogues, and it is the most emotionally resonant right in the domain | The cultural salience of high-profile catalogue reclamations | ❌ REJECTED → proposed **Won't (now)**. The clock is ~35 years. Every grant *made on WeJammin* becomes terminable in the 2060s; nobody signing up in 2026 has a window opening for decades. The only users it serves today are those arriving with 1990s catalogues — a real segment, but not the beachhead, and their grant dates are pre-platform and unverified anyway ([09.03.01](./09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)), so the platform would be computing a legal deadline from data it cannot stand behind. The genuine v1 obligation is narrow and cheap: **record grant dates and WFH designations precisely enough that the window is computable later**. That is a schema requirement, not a feature. Building the notice machinery now would ship a service whose first real user arrives after the founder has retired. |
| DT-02 | The platform should **compute and assert** the termination window, since it holds the dates | It is the obvious value: nobody knows their windows, and we have the inputs | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — compute, but frame carefully, and this generalises past this feature. A missed termination window is **permanent and unremediable** — unlike almost every other error in the domain, there is no correction path. If the platform displays "your window opens 2062" and the computation is wrong (two statutory regimes, derivative-work carve-outs, heir hierarchies, WFH characterisation — none trivial), the author relies on it and loses the right forever. The resolution is to present computed windows as **prompts to get advice**, with the inputs shown ("based on: grant dated 12/03/2027, not work-for-hire, published 2028"), never as a bare authoritative date. Showing the inputs is what lets a lawyer check the platform's work — and is the same posture as [09.01.05](../09.01-rights-registry/09.01.05-performer-neighbouring-rights.md) D-02: state the facts, don't assert the legal conclusion. Escalated to [09.03 Q-03](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md). |

| D-01 | Proposed **Won't (now)** — but the **schema obligation is v1** | The clock is 35 years; no 2026 signup has a window this decade. Grant dates and WFH designations must be recorded precisely enough to compute the window later — that is a data requirement, not a feature (see DT-01). |
