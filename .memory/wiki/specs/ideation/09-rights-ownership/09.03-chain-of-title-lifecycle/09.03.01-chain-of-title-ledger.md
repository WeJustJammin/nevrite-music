# Feature: Chain of Title Ledger

> **Number**: 09.03.01
> **Parent**: [Chain of Title & Rights Lifecycle](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The unbroken history of every transfer of every right in a work or recording — who owned it, who they
gave it to, when, on what terms, and what came back. Chain of title is what a buyer's lawyer asks for,
what a sync licensor demands before clearing, and what nobody can produce, because the history lives in
a filing cabinet, three email accounts, and a dead manager's memory.

WeJammin's structural advantage here is that it does not have to *reconstruct* the chain. If the
platform was present when ownership was first established ([09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md))
and present for each transfer since, the chain is a **byproduct** rather than a research project. That
is the thesis applied to history rather than to a single moment.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The rights owner. Sees the complete history of everything they own or have owned — the original split, the publisher they signed in 2027, the reversion in 2032. Initiates transfers (assigning a share, selling a master). Generates a chain-of-title document for a counterparty, which today takes weeks of lawyer time and here is a button. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the chain for works they are a party to — as an owner, a points holder, or a WFH counterparty. Read-only because a producer's presence in a chain is a *consequence* of transfers others made; they do not administer it. Where a producer owns a master share, they act as a Musician in that capacity. |
| Operator | None | A venue or studio operator has no chain-of-title interest. Where a studio entity holds rights via WFH ([09.02.03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.03-work-for-hire-buyout.md)), the *entity* appears in the chain as a party — but that entity is administered through [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/), not through an Operator surface here. |
| Fan | None | No access. Chain of title is commercially sensitive; the public-safe projection of "who owns this now" is [09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), which deliberately shows the current state without the history. |

## Behavior

The chain is an **append-only sequence of rights events** over an object: initial establishment,
assignment, licence grant, reversion, termination, inheritance, security interest. Each event names the
right affected (master? publishing? which share? which territory?), the parties, the date, the terms,
and the evidence.

The chain's integrity property: **every current ownership row must be traceable to an unbroken sequence
back to establishment.** A share that appears with no event that put it there is a break, and a break is
what makes a catalogue unsellable.

Not everything is an append. Termination unwinds a historical link (see
[09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-02), and reversion
appends an event nobody triggered (CX-01). The model must accommodate both without becoming mutable.

### Happy Path

1. Split captured; chain begins with an `established` event citing the consented ledger.
2. Writer signs a publisher; assignment event appended, citing the agreement and its terms.
3. Grant's term expires; reversion event appended automatically; ownership returns.
4. Owner requests a chain-of-title document for a sync licensor; the platform generates it from events, complete, with gaps (if any) named rather than hidden.
5. Licensor clears the sync without a three-week title search.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Work predates WeJammin — chain begins mid-story | The honest case, and the common one for any user with a catalogue. Chain starts at `asserted-prior-ownership` with the user's own claim, explicitly marked as unverified. | "History before 2026 is asserted, not verified" — the boundary between what the platform witnessed and what it was told must be visible, always. |
| Break in the chain (a share with no originating event) | Surfaced, never papered over. | "Gap: X's 25% has no recorded origin." A chain with a named gap is more useful than one that pretends to be complete. |
| Transfer recorded off-platform (side letter, paper contract) | Recordable as an event with attached evidence, marked `evidence-attached` rather than `platform-witnessed`. | Two visibly different trust levels. |
| Termination unwinds a 35-year-old link | Not a deletion. The link stays historically valid and becomes prospectively void. | Timeline shows the grant, and shows it terminated — both true, in sequence. |
| Reversion nobody noticed | Event appends automatically on term expiry; both parties notified. | "Your masters reverted on 12 March." Many users will not know until told, which is the opportunity. |
| Two conflicting transfers of the same share (double assignment) | Chain enters `disputed`; routes to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/). | Both events shown; neither silently wins. Last-write-wins here would be the platform picking a side in a fraud. |
| Reversion lands mid-royalty-period | The chain records the reversion at its true date; the period splits between the departing and returning payee (see [09.03-cx](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-01). The chain does not round the date to a period boundary — that would misstate ownership to save arithmetic. | Timeline shows the reversion on its actual day; downstream [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) splits the money. |
| Termination unwinds a grant that spawned a derivative work | The link goes prospectively void, but the remix released under it survives (CX-02). "Void" is not "never happened". | Grant shown terminated; the derivative still shown as validly made under it. Both true. |
| Moral rights asserted after a full assignment | Chain expresses "everything economic moved, this did not" (CX-05). A chain silent on non-travelling rights falsely asserts completeness. | Assignment event carries an explicit "moral rights retained by author" annotation where the territory grants them. |
| Security interest recorded (catalogue pledged against a loan) | Recordable as an encumbrance event — ownership-adjacent, does not transfer title but constrains it. Base recording is in scope; the enforcement/foreclosure model is deferred. | Timeline shows the lien and its holder. `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` on foreclosure/release mechanics. |
| Owner wants history hidden from a counterparty | Chain generation is scoped to a right and a period; it is not an all-or-nothing dump. The generated document names the scope on its face so a counterparty cannot mistake a partial disclosure for a complete one. | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` on the full disclosure-control model (Q-01). |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Chain requested | Timeline skeleton |
| Empty | Object established on-platform, no transfers | "Owned since establishment, never transferred" — a complete chain, not an empty one. Copy must say so. |
| Populated | Events exist | Chronological timeline with parties, terms, and trust level per event |
| Partial | Pre-platform history asserted | Timeline with an explicit "asserted history" boundary marker |
| Error | Generation failed | Inline retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An append-only cryptographically-verifiable chain (blockchain/DLT) is the obvious fit — this is literally a ledger of transfers | It is the canonical proposed application, and the shape genuinely matches | ❌ REJECTED **as product**; routed to `/create-prd-architecture` as a storage option with prejudice. The oracle problem is fatal to the value proposition: a chain can prove *a claim was recorded at time T and not altered since*. It cannot prove *the claim was true*. Someone entering "I own 100%" onto an immutable ledger has produced an immutable false statement — and the immutability now makes it harder to fix, which is worse than a database. What actually makes WeJammin's chain trustworthy is not tamper-evidence, it is **multi-party consent at the moment of establishment** ([09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md)) — being in the room, not being on a chain. The cryptography secures a record whose truth comes from somewhere else entirely. Recorded in `notProduct`. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the chain to distinguish **what the platform witnessed** from **what it was told**, and no candidate mentions this | The candidate list said "Chain of Title" as a bare noun. But WeJammin's entire claim is *we were there*. A chain that renders a user's unverified assertion about 1998 identically to a multi-party consented split from 2026 has thrown away the only thing that makes it better than the filing cabinet — and has quietly lent the platform's credibility to a self-claim. | ✅ CONFIRMED — trust level is a first-class attribute of every event: `platform-witnessed`, `evidence-attached`, `asserted`. This is also the honest answer to the catalogue-import problem: WeJammin cannot verify history it was absent for, and should say so rather than launder it. |
| DT-03 | A gap in the chain should block the object from being licensed or sold | Correctness instinct — an unverifiable chain is a liability | ❌ REJECTED — blocking would make the platform useless to anyone with a pre-existing catalogue, which is most professionals with anything worth selling. Nearly every real chain has gaps; the industry transacts anyway, with lawyers pricing the risk. The platform's job is to **make the gap visible and precise** ("X's 25% has no recorded origin") so a counterparty can price it, not to refuse to transact. A named gap is a service; a block is an insult. Same principle as [09.02.01](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) DT-01: consequence, not obstruction. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every event carries a **trust level**: `platform-witnessed` / `evidence-attached` / `asserted` | The platform's claim is "we were there". Rendering an unverified assertion identically to a consented split throws that away and launders a self-claim (see DT-02). |
| D-02 | Gaps are **named and precise**, never blocking | Every real catalogue has gaps; the industry transacts anyway. Making the gap priceable is the service (see DT-03). |
| D-03 | Chain trustworthiness comes from **multi-party consent at establishment**, not from tamper-evident storage | Cryptography secures a record whose truth originates elsewhere; the oracle problem means an immutable ledger can only guarantee an immutable lie (see DT-01). |
| D-04 | Conflicting transfers **both appear**; neither silently wins | Last-write-wins on a double assignment is the platform picking a side in a possible fraud. |
| D-05 | A **generated chain document names its own scope on its face** (this right, this period) | A scoped disclosure that looks complete is a trap; the counterparty must be able to see it is partial. Disclosure control is a feature (Q-01), not an excuse to hide the boundary. |
| D-06 | **Security interests are recordable as encumbrance events** — ownership-adjacent, non-transferring | Catalogue financing is common; a lien that constrains title but is invisible to a buyer is exactly the kind of hidden fact the chain exists to surface (Q-03). Foreclosure mechanics deferred. |
