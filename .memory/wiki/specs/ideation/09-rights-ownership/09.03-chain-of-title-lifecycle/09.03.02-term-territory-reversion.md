# Feature: Term, Territory & Reversion

> **Number**: 09.03.02
> **Parent**: [Chain of Title & Rights Lifecycle](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Every grant of rights has boundaries: **how long** (term), **where** (territory), and **what happens at
the end** (reversion). A publishing deal for 10 years worldwide, a distribution exclusive for 3 years in
North America, a licence for the life of copyright — these are the shapes of real music deals, and the
gap between "I own it" and "I control it" is made entirely of them.

Reversion is the payoff: when a term ends, rights return automatically. Artists routinely do not know
this has happened. A platform holding the terms can tell them — which converts a passive record into an
event, and is one of the clearest "the platform knew something I didn't" moments available in the domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The grantor. Records the term and territory when they sign a deal; sees countdowns on every grant they have made; gets told when rights come back — often the first they hear of it. Sees, per work, the plain answer to "what can I actually do with this, where, and until when?", which today requires reading a contract they may not have a copy of. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees terms on grants they are party to — a distribution deal that constrains a master they part-own, or a term on their own points ([09.02.02](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.02-producer-points-agreements.md)). Read-only: they see the constraint, they do not set it, unless they are the grantor in a Musician capacity. |
| Operator | None | No stake in a grant's term or territory. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

A grant carries: the right granted (master? publishing? which share?), the grantee, the **term** (fixed
years, life of copyright, perpetual, or conditional), the **territory** (worldwide, a country set, a
region), and the **reversion condition** (automatic on expiry, or triggered by an event such as
non-exploitation or a sales threshold).

Term expiry produces a **reversion event** with no actor — the passage of time writes to the chain (see
[09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-01). The chain update
and the registry ownership update must be **atomic**; if they diverge, the platform's two sources of
truth disagree about who owns the work, which is the worst inconsistency available here.

**Conditional reversion** is the hard part and the valuable part. "Rights revert if the label does not
release within 18 months" or "if royalties fall below £X for two consecutive years" are common clauses
whose conditions the platform can *actually evaluate*, because [domains 10](../../10-royalties-collections/)
and [12](../../12-release-distribution/) hold the data. Nobody can currently check these clauses without
an audit, so in practice they go unenforced. This is a consolidation payoff of unusual size.

### Happy Path

1. Musician signs a publishing deal; records the grant with term (10 years), territory (worldwide), reversion (automatic).
2. The grant appears on the work as a constraint, not just a document — "controlled by X until 2036, worldwide".
3. Countdown visible throughout; reminders escalate as expiry approaches.
4. Term expires; reversion event appends; ownership returns in the registry; both parties notified.
5. Musician sees their rights back without having had to remember, chase, or hire anyone.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Grantee disputes the reversion | Reversion stands (time passed); grantee's objection routes to [09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/). | Rights shown as reverted with a dispute banner. A grantee cannot veto arithmetic. |
| Conditional reversion condition met | Platform detects it from data it already holds — no release in 18 months; royalties below threshold. | "Your reversion condition appears to be met" — **notify, don't auto-execute**. See DT-02. |
| Territory overlaps (one grant worldwide, another for Japan) | Conflict; both cannot be valid. | Flagged at recording time, not at expiry. Territory conflicts are detectable the moment the second grant is entered. |
| Grant with no term recorded | Perpetual by default is dangerous; unknown is honest. | "Term not recorded" — flagged as incomplete, never assumed perpetual. Assuming perpetuity silently gifts the grantee everything. |
| Term expires while a distribution is live | Rights revert; distribution rights end; live release may need takedown. | Escalated to [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/) — a reversion with a live release is an operational event, not just a record change. |
| Reversion lands mid-royalty-period | Period splits between two payees. | Statement names the effective date; same versioning discipline as [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md). |
| Automatic reversion the platform executes wrongly | Registry now names the wrong owner on the platform's own initiative. | This is the risk that drives D-02 below. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Territory can be deferred — model term now, add territory later | Scope-reduction instinct; territory is genuinely expensive | ❌ REJECTED, emphatically. Territory is not an attribute of a grant, it is a **dimension of the ownership model itself**: the same share can be held by different parties in different territories simultaneously (sub-publishing is exactly this, and it is ordinary). A model without territory does not lose a field — it loses the ability to represent a share that is one thing in Japan and another in the US. Retrofitting a dimension into a live ownership ledger means rewriting every row, every validation, and every downstream royalty calculation, against production data, with money attached. This is the sub-domain's structural risk ([09.03 Q-01](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)) and it must be decided before the schema, not after. |
| DT-02 | The platform should **auto-execute** conditional reversions it can detect | It holds the data; detection is the hard part and it is solved; executing seems like the obvious payoff | ❌ REJECTED — detection and execution are different risks. Evaluating "did the label release within 18 months?" requires interpreting a *contract clause*, and the platform holds a structured summary of that clause, not the clause. If the summary is wrong, auto-execution means WeJammin unilaterally stripped a grantee's rights based on its own misreading — a live legal exposure, and precisely the unilateral power over ownership that [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md) DT-02 refuses on principle. **Notify, don't execute**: telling an artist "your reversion condition appears to be met, here's the evidence" delivers nearly all the value (nobody can check these clauses today) at none of the liability, and leaves the decision with the party whose right it is. Fixed-term reversion is different — a date passing is arithmetic, not interpretation — and does execute automatically. |
| DT-03 | The empty state (a work with no grants) should be framed as a **positive ownership statement**, not a blank "no data" panel | Absence of any grant is the *strongest* ownership position a work can have — the owner controls everything, everywhere, forever — yet the naive rendering of "0 rows" is an empty screen that reads as a gap | ✅ CONFIRMED. This is the same "the platform knew something I didn't" reframe the Overview identifies for reversion, applied to stasis instead of change. "You control this outright, worldwide, with no time limit" turns the absence of grants into reassurance and, critically, contrasts against the [PARTIAL] state (grant recorded, term unknown) so the two are never confused: outright control and unrecorded-term control look nothing alike and must not render alike. Confirms the two-tier empty/partial distinction in the States table rather than collapsing both into one "nothing here" view. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Territory is a **dimension of the ownership model**, not an attribute of a grant | The same share can be held by different parties in different territories at once. Retrofitting this into a live ledger is unsurvivable (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | Conditional reversion: **notify, never auto-execute**. Fixed-term reversion: **auto-execute** | Interpreting a clause is a judgement the platform can get wrong; a date passing is arithmetic. Different risks, different behaviours (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | A grant with no recorded term is **flagged incomplete**, never assumed perpetual | Assuming perpetuity silently gifts the grantee everything the grantor never agreed to give. |
| D-04 | Reversion writes to the chain and the registry **atomically** | Divergence means the platform's two sources of truth name different owners — the worst inconsistency in the domain. |
