# Feature: Estate, Succession & Catalog Legacy

> **Number**: 09.03.04
> **Parent**: [Chain of Title & Rights Lifecycle](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Copyright outlives its author by 70 years in most of the world. Rights therefore pass to estates, and
the estate then has to run a catalogue it may not understand, using records the author never left
behind. This feature holds who inherits what, who may act for a deceased owner, and how a catalogue
keeps functioning when the person who made it is gone.

It is also the feature that quietly unblocks others. [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)'s
central unsolved problem is the unreachable consenter; death is the most permanent version of
unreachable, and an identified estate is the only clean answer. And **the date of death is a rights-critical
fact** — life+70 is measured from it — which makes this the input to [09.03.05](./09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two capacities. As the **owner planning ahead**: nominates who acts for their catalogue, records intentions, and — the highest-value action here — leaves a catalogue whose splits, chain, and terms are already recorded, which is the difference between an inheritance and a puzzle. As an **heir**: administers an inherited catalogue, consents to amendments on the deceased's behalf, collects royalties. |
| Producer | None | A producer has no succession interest in someone else's catalogue. Where a producer's own points or master share pass to their estate, they are the owner in a Musician capacity. Explicit absence — no Producer surface here. |
| Operator | None | No succession interest. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

The feature holds three things:

1. **Succession nomination** (pre-death) — who the owner says should act. Recorded intent, not a will; the platform is not a probate service and must not present itself as one.
2. **Estate representation** (post-death) — a verified party empowered to act for the deceased owner. Verification is the hard part: acting on a false claim of death or authority hands a catalogue to a stranger.
3. **Statutory heir divergence** — for termination rights ([09.03.03](./09.03.03-termination-rights-notice-windows.md)), the eligible parties are fixed by statute and may not be the people who inherited the copyright (see [09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-03). The platform must be able to represent two different sets of people with two different powers over the same work.

Succession is recorded as a **chain of title event** — inheritance is a transfer, and it must be
traceable like every other.

### Happy Path

1. Musician nominates an estate contact and records their intentions for the catalogue.
2. On death, the nominated party notifies the platform and provides verification.
3. Platform verifies and grants estate representation over the catalogue.
4. Inheritance events append to the chain for each affected work.
5. Estate administers: consents to amendments, collects royalties, licenses works. The catalogue keeps earning without a gap.
6. Date of death recorded with source/provenance; [09.03.05](./09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md) may consume it only for its supported `US`/`FR`/`DE`/`GB` jurisdictional term result. Absent or insufficiently sourced death evidence remains `unknown`, never an inferred date or term result.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No nomination made; owner dies | The common case — most people do not plan. Falls back to an external verification path (probate documents, legal representation). | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` on the fallback. |
| Contested estate; two claimants | Platform does **not** adjudicate. Catalogue enters a protective state; royalties escrow ([09.04.03](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/09.04.03-rights-freeze-royalty-escrow.md)). | "Estate contested — collections held." Probate is a court's job, not a platform's. |
| False death claim | The attack. Someone claims a living artist died to seize a catalogue. | Verification requirements are the whole defence; see DT-02. |
| Statutory heirs ≠ testamentary heirs (termination) | Both sets represented, with different powers over the same works. | Explicitly surfaced: "X inherited this copyright; Y holds the termination right." Confusing, and true. |
| Estate wants to sell the catalogue | Ordinary transfer via [09.03.01](./09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md), and a clean chain is exactly what makes it saleable. | Chain document generated; this is where the domain's whole thesis pays out financially. |
| Owner nominated someone who predeceased them | Nomination stale; falls back to external verification. | Periodic re-confirmation prompt — a nomination made once and never revisited decays silently. |
| Heir has no idea what they inherited | Extremely common. | Catalogue overview with what it earns, who controls it, and what is coming back and when — the orientation nobody currently gets. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert would expect this to be about *dead famous people's estates* — a niche for heirs of the notable | The candidate's own framing: "Estate, Succession & **Catalog Legacy**" reads as a prestige-catalogue concern | ❌ REJECTED, and the reframing matters. Its live function is **structural, not commemorative**: (a) death is the terminal case of [09.02.04](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.04-split-amendment-reconsent.md)'s unreachable-consenter problem, which blocks amendments on *ordinary* catalogues — a dead co-writer freezes a split sheet permanently; (b) date of death drives every life+70 term calculation ([09.03.05](./09.03.05-copyright-term-public-domain.md)); (c) it is where the platform's clean-chain value converts to cash, since a catalogue sale needs exactly the document [09.03.01](./09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md) produces. Framing this as a feature for heirs of the famous would have hidden that it unblocks three other features for everyone. |
| DT-02 | Estate verification can reuse the platform's standard identity verification | It is an identity problem; [domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/) already solves identity | ❌ REJECTED — the threat model is inverted. Ordinary identity verification asks *are you who you say you are?*, and the subject can participate in proving it. Estate verification asks *is someone else dead, and are you entitled to act for them?* — and the one person who could refute the claim is, by hypothesis, unavailable. The failure mode is not an impersonated account; it is a **living artist's catalogue handed to a stranger who filed a death claim**, quite possibly a real relative in a genuine dispute. This needs a materially different bar: documentary evidence, a notice period to the account holder (a living artist will object loudly), and no automated grant. Recorded as a `/create-prd-security` escalation. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform records **intent**, never administers probate | Contested estates go to a court. A platform adjudicating inheritance is far outside its competence and its standing. |
| D-02 | Estate verification requires a **materially higher bar** than identity verification, including a notice period to the account holder | The subject cannot participate in disproving the claim; a living artist given notice will object loudly, which is the cheapest available defence (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | Inheritance is a **chain of title event** | A transfer is a transfer; succession must be as traceable as a sale. |
| D-04 | Statutory and testamentary heirs are **both representable** over the same work | Termination interests are set by statute, not by the will. One set of heirs cannot represent both powers. |
