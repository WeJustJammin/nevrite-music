# Feature: Copyright Term & Public Domain Determination

> **Number**: 09.03.05
> **Parent**: [Chain of Title & Rights Lifecycle](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-21

## Overview

How long a copyright lasts, and what it means when it stops. Most territories use life of the author
plus 70 years for compositions; sound recordings follow different rules again (and differ sharply
between the US and the EU). When the term ends the work enters the **public domain** — it belongs to
nobody, and anyone may use it without permission or payment.

The practical value is answering two questions the platform is uniquely placed to answer: *is this old
song free to record?* and *when does my copyright expire?* The first is a live question for anyone
arranging traditional material; the second matters to estates and catalogue buyers.

> **Merged from sweep candidate 23** (Public Domain & Copyright Term Determination). Public domain is
> not an independent capability — it is the terminal state of a term calculation. A separate node would
> be a feature whose entire behaviour is "the term calculation returned zero".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|

Sees the expiry horizon on their own catalogue (distant, and it matters to their estate). Read-only because term is **computed from facts, not declared**: nobody chooses when their copyright expires, and a user-editable term field would be a fiction with legal consequences. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same lookup, same reason — usually asking "can we cut this traditional song without clearing anything?" before booking the session rather than after. |

| Fan | None | No access here. Arguably a fan might want to know a song is public domain, but that is a curiosity served — if at all — by [09.06.04](../09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md), not by this feature. |

## Behavior

Term is computed from: the work type (composition vs sound recording), the author's death date (from
[09.03.04](./09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md) — see
[09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md](./09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle-cx.md) CX-04), creation and
publication dates, authorship type (individual, joint, corporate/anonymous), and **territory**.

Territory is not a refinement here — it is decisive. The same work is routinely in the public domain in
one country and in copyright in another, because term rules, war-time extensions, and rule-of-the-shorter-term
differ. A single global "is it public domain?" answer is not a simplification; it is **wrong**.

**CQ-09 Option B — v1 supports exactly `US`, `FR`, `DE`, and `GB` for term-status determination.**
Territory remains an explicit model dimension, not a global boolean. For one of those jurisdictions, the
platform can show a determinate status only when it holds source-attributed facts required by that
jurisdiction's applicable rule: work object/type, authorship type, all required author death dates,
creation/publication facts where applicable, and the rule/version used. Every displayed result includes
its jurisdiction, inputs, and effective rule. Any other territory, missing death date or historical fact,
unsupported work/category rule, or insufficient source evidence is explicitly **`unknown` / `not
determined`** — never omitted, extrapolated from another jurisdiction, or guessed.

A determinate status is not legal advice, a clearance opinion, a licence, an ownership determination, or
a release-gate override. It describes the recorded facts under one supported jurisdictional rule; Domain
11 licensing and Domain 12 release controls remain independent. The modern-arrangement and
composition-versus-recording distinctions still apply separately in every jurisdiction.

Joint works compound this: term runs from the **last surviving author's** death, so a work by two writers
has a term nobody can compute without both death dates.

### Happy Path

1. Musician wants to record an arrangement of a traditional song.
2. Looks it up; the platform shows only determinate status within `US`, `FR`, `DE`, and `GB` — for example, one supported jurisdiction may be public domain while another remains in copyright.
3. Each result shows its source-attributed inputs and effective rule: for example, every required author death date and a life-plus-term result where that rule applies.
4. Missing facts or an unsupported territory render `unknown` / `not determined`; the platform directs the Musician to the independent licensing/release evidence path rather than declaring the use safe.
5. Their recording is a new copyright regardless — the arrangement and the master are theirs.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Public domain in one territory, not another | The normal case, not an edge. | Per-territory status. A single global answer would be actively wrong. |
| Death date unknown | Term is not computable. | "Can't determine — author's death date unknown." Never a guess; a guessed public-domain determination is an invitation to infringe. |
| Joint work; one author living | Term runs from the last survivor. | "In copyright until at least 2095 — X is still living." |
| Traditional / anonymous work | Different rules (often from publication); frequently indeterminate. | "Cannot determine in this jurisdiction — required historical facts are unavailable." Never a `likely public domain` shortcut. |
| Territory outside `US` / `FR` / `DE` / `GB` | Out of v1 term-status scope. A status in another territory cannot be inferred from a supported one. | "Term status is not determined for this territory in v1." |
| Source facts are incomplete, unverified, or do not support the applicable jurisdictional rule | Fail closed as `unknown` / `not determined`; neither a public-domain nor in-copyright result is fabricated. | Jurisdiction, missing fact/category, and available source inputs shown. |
| Sound recording of a public-domain composition | Two answers. The composition is free; **the recording is not**. This is the most common and most expensive misunderstanding here. | Both stated explicitly, side by side, with each result's jurisdiction and inputs. |
| Work in public domain, but a modern **arrangement** is not | The arrangement is a separate copyright. | "The song is public domain in [jurisdiction]. This arrangement is not." |
| User treats a determinate term status as clearance, licence, ownership decision, or release approval | Block the inference, not the work. Licensing and release retain their independent evidence/permission gates. | "This is a term status from recorded facts, not clearance to use or release the work." |
| Platform says public domain; it is wrong | User records and releases, and infringes on the platform's advice. | The risk driving D-01: inputs, rule, jurisdiction, and unknown state remain visible. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Term status is shown **with its inputs and territory scope**, never as a bare verdict | A wrong determination causes reliance-based infringement with the platform's fingerprints on it. Showing the working makes it checkable (see DT-01). |
| D-02 | The answer is **always two answers** — composition and recording | The most common real-world error is assuming a PD song means a free recording (see DT-02). |
| D-03 | Term is **computed, never declared** — read-only to all personas | Nobody chooses when their copyright expires. An editable term field is a fiction with legal consequences. |
| D-04 | Unknown inputs produce **"cannot determine"**, never a guess | A guessed public-domain determination is an invitation to infringe. |
| D-05 | V1 term status covers exactly **`US`, `FR`, `DE`, and `GB`** | This bounded legal-data exception to the US-first market supports the material moral-rights regimes without claiming global coverage. Every other jurisdiction is explicitly `unknown` / out of v1 scope, never a global default. |
| D-06 | A determinate term status is **not clearance, legal advice, ownership, or release authorization** | It states source-attributed facts and one jurisdictional rule; licensing and release have independent rights/evidence gates. |
