## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Term, Territory & Reversion ↔ Chain of Title

**Relationship**: A grant with a term is a transfer with an expiry date built in. When the term ends,
the right returns automatically — no transaction, no counterparty action, sometimes no notification
from anyone. The chain of title must therefore contain events that nobody triggered: a reversion is an
event caused by the *passage of time*, not by an actor. A chain that only records things people did
will drift out of truth the moment a term lapses, and will keep confidently naming the wrong owner.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The reverting owner. Frequently does not know their rights have come back — which is itself the product opportunity.
- **Producer**: Read-only, and only where they are a party to the grant.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The chain owns the transfer history; the grant owns its own term. The reversion event is written by the term's expiry, not by either party — so neither can suppress it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Term expires → reversion event appended to chain → ownership rows in [09.01](../09.01-rights-registry/) updated → both parties notified. If the ledger update fails, the chain and the registry disagree about who owns the work — the worst available inconsistency in the domain. This must be atomic.
3. **Permission intersection**: Neither party can prevent a reversion. A grantee "declining" to release the right is a dispute ([09.04](../09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)), not a veto.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both parties, plus [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/) (payee changes) and [domain 12](../../12-release-distribution/) (distribution rights may have just ended).
5. **State transition conflict**: A reversion landing mid-royalty-period splits the period between two payees. Same versioning problem as [09.02.04 CX-03](../09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02-split-capture-agreements-cx.md).

### CX-02: Termination Rights ↔ Chain of Title

**Relationship**: Every other event in this sub-domain **appends** to the chain. Termination is the
exception: it reaches back thirty-five years and unwinds a link that was validly forged at the time.
The chain cannot model this as a correction (the grant was not a mistake) nor as a new transfer (no
consideration moved, and it was not consensual). It is a third thing: a valid transfer, statutorily
undone, with the original link remaining historically true and prospectively void.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: The author or their statutory heirs — and notably *not* necessarily the person who currently owns the copyright.
- **Producer**: No access. Termination belongs to authors and their statutory heirs, not to producers.
- **Operator**: No visibility.
- **Fan**: No visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The chain owns the link. Termination changes the link's *prospective* effect without erasing its *historical* validity. A model that only stores current state cannot express this at all.
2. **Trigger chain**: Notice served within the statutory window → effective date arrives (years later) → grant becomes prospectively void → ownership returns. The long gap between notice and effect is unlike anything else in the domain.
3. **Permission intersection**: Termination cannot be contracted away — a grant saying "you waive termination" does not remove the right. The platform must not let a recorded agreement suppress it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The current owner (who is losing rights they lawfully bought), the terminating parties, and [domain 10](../../10-royalties-collections/).
5. **State transition conflict**: Derivative works made under the grant survive termination. A terminated grant does not unmake the remix released under it — so "void" is not "never happened", and the chain must say so.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 09.03.05 Copyright Term & Public Domain | 09.03.02 Term, Territory & Reversion | Rejected as a *state-sharing* pair — recorded in CX-06 only as a **confusion risk**, not a data dependency. A grant's term and a copyright's term are unrelated clocks: a 5-year licence on a work with 60 years of copyright left share nothing but a countdown widget. Modelling them as one mechanism because they look alike in the UI would be a category error with real consequences (a user believing their song entered the public domain because a licence lapsed). |
| R-02 | 09.03.06 Moral Rights | 09.03.03 Termination Rights | Both are author-protective and non-waivable, which invites a merge. But they are unconnected: moral rights persist *through* every transfer and are never the subject of termination; termination undoes an economic grant and has no bearing on attribution. Merging would imply terminating a grant restores moral rights that were never lost. |
| R-03 | 09.03.04 Estate & Succession | 09.03.02 Term, Territory & Reversion | Death does not trigger reversion. A grant survives the grantor's death and continues under the estate on its original terms. Linking them would invent a reversion-on-death rule that does not exist and would hand estates rights that are still validly licensed. |
