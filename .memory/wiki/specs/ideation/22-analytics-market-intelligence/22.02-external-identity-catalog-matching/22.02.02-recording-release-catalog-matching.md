# Feature: Recording & Release Catalog Matching

> **Number**: 22.02.02
> **Parent**: [External Identity & Catalog Matching](./22.02-external-identity-catalog-matching-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Binding external recordings and releases — identified by ISRC, UPC, or a DSP's own track URI — to
WeJammin catalog entities, so that per-track numbers land on the right record. This is the feature
that makes 22.08 (Credit-Linked Performance) computable: a credit says "I played drums on this
recording", and only a catalog match turns that into "this recording did 4M streams". A match here
is a **claim with evidence and a confidence** (inheriting 22.02.01 D-03), never a bare database join.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Reviews and confirms which external recordings correspond to records they're credited on; resolves rows that matched nothing; corrects a wrong proposal; requests creation of a missing catalog entity. For the session player this is the moment their scattered work becomes a catalog they can actually see — most have never had one. A Musician may confirm a binding on their **own** behalf but cannot unbind another contributor's binding on the same recording (see Edge Cases → permission); they can only dispute it into 22.02.03. |
| Producer | Full | Same, at project granularity: confirms a project's deliverables map to the released recordings. Frequently holds the most reliable knowledge of what a track actually *is* — they were in the room when it was made (personas.md: "they are the person who knows"). Where a band/label entity has delegated catalog-match scope (domain 01), a Producer may match on behalf of that entity; a DSP **read** grant (a 22.01 connection) does **not** by itself confer match authority (index D-01). |
| Operator | None | Venues/studios/rehearsal spaces have no catalog of recordings — their inventory is time and space (personas.md). Not rendered anywhere in this feature. Operators read *aggregate* market data in 22.04, which is never bound to a recording they own. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. A Fan cannot bind a recording — binding is a provenance action reserved to credited parties, and exposing it to fans would open an impersonation surface (personas.md worst-intentional: scalping/impersonation profile). The recording-level stats a Fan eventually sees on a public artist profile (domains 01/20) are downstream of a match a Fan can neither see nor influence. |

## Behavior

Matching is scoped by the artist match above it (CX-01) **or** entered directly through the credit
graph (D-04), and is driven by identifiers, strongest first:

- **ISRC** — the recording identifier. Authoritative *in principle*, unreliable in practice: the same
  recording is routinely issued under multiple ISRCs by successive distributors, and ISRCs are
  re-used and mis-keyed constantly. It is the best key available and it is not a primary key. Every
  ISRC is validated against the format `CC-XXX-YY-NNNNN` (2 alpha, 3 alphanumeric, 2 digit year,
  5 digit designation; 12 chars ex-hyphens) before any lookup; a malformed value never reaches the
  match engine.
- **UPC** — the release identifier (12–13 digit GTIN). Same caveats, one level up.
- **DSP track URI** — reliable within one DSP, meaningless across DSPs; used only to disambiguate
  within a single connected source, never as a cross-source key.
- **Fuzzy (title + artist + duration ±2s + release date ±14d)** — last resort. **Proposes; never
  binds** (D-03).

The result is deliberately **many-to-many**: one WeJammin recording may legitimately correspond to
several external recordings (an original, a remaster, a territory-specific re-issue), and the
numbers of all of them belong to it. Binding uniqueness is enforced on the **pair**
`(external_recording_id, wejammin_recording_id)` — the same external recording may **not** bind to
two different WeJammin recordings without producing a conflict (routed to 22.02.03).

**Version identity vs. career rollup (D-05)**: distinct recordings — original, remaster, radio edit,
live take — are **never silently merged** at recording granularity. Career-level headline totals
group versions of the same underlying **work** (via ISWC where available, else an explicit
user-confirmed "same work" link) and sum them, with the component recordings always inspectable.
The default headline is the grouped-by-work sum; the per-recording distinct breakdown is one click
away and never hidden.

### Happy Path

1. Artist match confirmed (22.02.01) **or** a verified credit selected (D-04); system enumerates the
   candidate external recordings in scope.
2. System matches them against WeJammin catalog entities by ISRC (with corroboration), then UPC,
   then DSP URI, then fuzzy.
3. User sees a review list grouped by confidence tier: **auto-matched** (corroborated ISRC/UPC,
   ≥ 0.9 confidence), **proposed** (single-signal or fuzzy, 0.5–0.9), **unmatched** (< 0.5, no
   candidate).
4. User confirms the proposals, corrects the wrong ones, and dispositions the unmatched (bind
   manually, request catalog-entity creation, or leave unmatched).
5. Per-recording numbers land against the bound catalog entity; 22.08's credit rollup becomes
   computable for the newly-matched recordings, and any live shared report (22.05.03) recomputes.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Same recording under 2 ISRCs (re-distributed) | Both external recordings bind to one catalog entity; per-recording numbers **sum** at the catalog entity | "2 versions of this recording found — combined" with the components inspectable |
| ISRC re-used for a different recording (real distributor error) | ISRC alone is insufficient; corroboration (duration ±2s, title, release date ±14d) required before binding. Fails corroboration → not bound | Flagged to 22.02.03 as an ambiguous identifier, never silently bound |
| Remaster / radio edit / live version | Distinct recordings — **must not** silently merge into the original at recording granularity (D-05). Grouped under the work for the career total only | Proposed as *related versions of the same work*, requiring an explicit "same work or different work?" confirmation |
| Compilation appearances (one recording on many releases) | UPC-level match multiplies; recording-level match stays singular | Release-level rollup keeps releases distinct; recording-level does not double-count the recording's streams |
| **Concurrency** — two co-credited contributors confirm a match on the same recording at the same time | Idempotent on the pair key: both resolve to the one binding; the second confirmation is recognized as already-bound and succeeds without creating a duplicate row | Both see "matched" — no error, no duplicate |
| **Race** — one external ISRC bound to WeJammin recording A while a second user concurrently binds it to recording B | Pair-key uniqueness holds per pair, but one external recording fanning out to 2 distinct WeJammin recordings trips a fan-out guard → both bindings frozen | "This external recording is being matched to two different WeJammin recordings — frozen for review"; routed to 22.02.03 |
| **Race** — user confirms a match while a co-credited party concurrently disputes it | The dispute wins (a contested recording blocks binding, D-02 of 22.02.03); a confirmation issued against the now-superseded pre-dispute state is rejected as stale rather than silently overriding the dispute | "Someone disputed this while you were reviewing" — the stale confirm does not apply |
| **Permission** — delegated manager matches on an artist's behalf without catalog-match scope | Rejected; a DSP read/connection grant is not a match grant (index D-01) | "Match scope not delegated" — the action is refused |
| **Permission** — contributor B tries to unbind a match contributor A created | Not permitted; B may bind on their own behalf or dispute A's binding, but cannot delete it | "You can't unbind another party's match" → offered the dispute path |
| **Network failure** — DSP enumeration API times out mid-fetch | Matching runs on the partial set actually fetched; unfetched sources retried with exponential backoff (3 attempts, 1s/4s/16s). Partial set is never presented as complete | Partial state banner: "showing 2 of 3 sources — 1 still loading" with a manual retry |
| **Malicious input** — user manually enters an ISRC for a famous recording they didn't play on | Manual/self-asserted bind is allowed but stamped evidence-strength `self-asserted`; it **cannot** mint verified proof (22.08.03) and never auto-binds | Low-confidence badge; "self-asserted — not verifiable" |
| **Malicious input** — malformed / injection-laden ISRC or UPC | Rejected at format validation before any lookup or query | "Invalid identifier format" — the value never reaches the match engine |
| **Malicious input** — scripted mass-confirmation to inflate a catalog | Confirmations rate-limited to 100/min/user; a batch above 500 in a rolling hour is held for review rather than applied | "Confirmation rate exceeded" on the throttled action; held batch shows "pending review" |
| **Partial data** — recording has an ISRC but no duration or release date to corroborate | ISRC match proposed at reduced confidence (capped 0.7); requires explicit confirm, never auto-bound (D-02) | "Matched by ISRC only — confirm this is the right recording" |
| **Partial data** — external row missing ISRC entirely | Drops to fuzzy tier only; proposes, never binds (D-03) | Appears in "proposed", never in "auto-matched" |
| Track has 6 credited contributors | All 6 legitimately claim the same recording. No division occurs — one binding, full numbers to each contributor's view | Each sees the recording's full numbers; see 22.08 D-01 |
| **Multi-hyphenate in two roles** — one human credited on the recording as *both* performer and producer | The recording binds **once**, not twice; both credits attach in domain 02 against the single binding; the person's rollup counts the recording exactly once | One recording, one match; two credits listed under it — no double-count |
| A recording the user is credited on but the artist match doesn't cover | The session player's default case — routed to evidence-based matching via the credit graph (D-04), not via an artist profile the user controls | Unmatched-by-profile rows surface a "match via your verified credit" path |
| Fuzzy match on a covers/tribute act | High collision risk (same title, same duration, different everything) | Never auto-bound; fuzzy proposes only |
| Catalog entity doesn't exist in WeJammin yet | Nothing to bind to | Offer to create it from the external metadata — with imported provenance flagged (22.01.04 D-02); see Q-03 |
| Parent artist match revoked while catalog matches exist beneath it (CX-01) | Every dependent catalog match invalidates — loudly, cascading, never silently orphaned | "12 recordings unlinked because the artist match was withdrawn" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Enumeration/matching in flight | Progress with running counts ("matched 40 / scanning 120") |
| Empty | No external recordings under the profile/credit scope | "No recordings found for this profile" with a route to connect a DSP or add credits |
| Populated | Matches proposed | Confidence-grouped review list (auto-matched / proposed / unmatched) |
| Partial | Some matched, some not — or a source failed to enumerate | Matched set live and usable; unmatched queue and any failed source shown separately with retry |
| Contested | Ambiguous/conflicting identifiers or a fan-out/race conflict | Affected rows frozen; routed to 22.02.03; the rest of the list stays usable |
| Error | Matching engine failed | Retry; manual ISRC/UPC entry as fallback (still format-validated) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | ISRC is a reliable primary key and matching is therefore trivial | The naive assumption the sweep's candidate list implicitly makes | ❌ REJECTED: ISRCs are re-issued per distributor, re-used in error, and frequently absent or wrong on older catalog. Any design treating ISRC as a primary key will silently merge distinct recordings and split identical ones. It is the best *hint* available, not a key — hence corroboration (duration ±2s, title, date ±14d) gates every ISRC bind. This single misconception is the most likely cause of a wrong number in this domain. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **many-to-many, not one-to-one**, and nobody said so | Catalog knowledge; the candidate list implies a simple join | ✅ CONFIRMED: one WeJammin recording ↔ many external recordings (re-issues, remasters, territory variants) is the normal case, not an exception. A one-to-one model undercounts a career by exactly the amount that matters most — the long-lived catalog that got re-released, which is the work with the most accumulated streams. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with 22.08 (Credit-Linked Performance)? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the domain's most consequential coupling. 22.08 rolls up "the records I played on" **across releases the Musician does not own and cannot connect** (22.01 Q-03). So the rollup's coverage is capped by this feature's ability to match recordings via the *credit graph* rather than via an artist profile the user controls. If matching only works downward from a claimed artist profile, 22.08 works only for artists — and fails precisely the session-player persona it exists to serve. **The credit graph must be a first-class matching entry point, not a fallback.** That is a structural requirement discovered here, not in 22.08. |
| DT-04 | Resolving "does a remaster count toward the career total?" (old Q-01) needs only a display toggle | Framing the version-rollup question as cosmetic | ❌ REJECTED: answering it correctly forces a **work-level identity (ISWC)** above the recording, because summing an original + remaster into one career headline requires knowing they are the *same work* — a fact recording-level ISRC matching cannot supply. So Q-01 is not a display preference; it exposes a missing entity tier. D-05 adopts work-grouping-by-ISWC as the default and keeps recordings distinct beneath it, which also cleanly explains why compilations (one recording, many releases) and versions (one work, many recordings) are *different* multiplicities, not the same bug. The version question could not be answered without introducing the work tier — that is the discovery. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Matching is **many-to-many** | DT-02. One-to-one undercounts exactly the catalog that matters most. Binding uniqueness is on the pair `(external_recording_id, wejammin_recording_id)`, not on either side alone. |
| D-02 | ISRC is a hint requiring corroboration, **not a key** | DT-01. Trusting it produces silent, undiagnosable errors in a number users quote commercially. Corroboration window: duration ±2s, release date ±14d, title match. |
| D-03 | Fuzzy matching **proposes only** — it never binds | The covers/tribute collision is unresolvable by heuristic, and a wrong bind is worse than an unmatched row. |
| D-04 | The **credit graph is a first-class matching entry point** | DT-03. Without it, 22.08 serves only artists and fails the session player — inverting the domain's thesis alignment. |
| D-05 | **Recordings stay distinct; career totals group by work (ISWC).** Default headline = grouped-by-work sum; per-recording breakdown always one click away | DT-04 + old Q-01. Silently merging a remaster into the original loses provenance; never grouping them makes the headline career number understate a re-released catalog. Grouping at the *work* tier while keeping recordings distinct satisfies both, and requires the ISWC tier of the identifier registry. This is a **default** the user may override (see Q-04). |
| D-06 | A binding is **owned per contributor and cannot be deleted by another contributor** | Permission edge cases. A co-credited party may bind on their own behalf or dispute an existing binding into 22.02.03, but must not be able to unbind work another party attested — that would let one contributor erase another's provenance. |
