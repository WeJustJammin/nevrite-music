# Feature: External Artist Profile Matching & Claiming

> **Number**: 22.02.01
> **Parent**: [External Identity & Catalog Matching](./22.02-external-identity-catalog-matching-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Binding an external artist profile — a Spotify artist URI, an Apple Music artist ID, a TikTok
handle — to a WeJammin identity or band entity, so that ingested numbers have a legitimate owner.
The hard part is not the search box; it is that artist names collide constantly, one human belongs
to several artist entities at once, and WeJammin cannot independently verify that a stranger's claim
on a Spotify profile is true.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Searches for and claims the external profiles that are theirs — personal artist name plus every band they're in (personas.md: roles are simultaneous, not sequential). Sees the confidence and evidence behind each match; contests a profile someone else has claimed. |
| Producer | Full | Claims their own producer/artist profiles, and — where a band entity has delegated it (domain 01) — matches on behalf of a project. Most likely persona to hold a legitimate relationship to a profile they do **not** own, which is the situation that generates most real conflicts. |
| Operator | None | Venues have no external artist profile. Not rendered. The Operator reads *aggregate* market data in 22.04, which is never bound to their own identity. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. A fan cannot claim an artist profile — this is the boundary that keeps impersonation out of the domain. Public artist stats a fan sees (domain 01/20) are downstream of a match a fan can neither see nor influence. |

## Behavior

A match is a **claim with evidence and a confidence**, never a bare join (D-03). The system proposes;
the user confirms; the platform records *why* it believed the binding — and the claim remains
contestable afterwards.

Evidence available, strongest first:
- **An OAuth grant on the DSP's artist account (22.01.01)** — near-conclusive. The DSP has already
  done the verification we cannot do.
- **Distribution through WeJammin (domain 12)** — conclusive for releases we distributed; the
  platform minted the identifiers itself and needs no external verification.
- **A verified credit on a recording that profile released (domain 02)** — this is the interesting
  one, and the platform's structural advantage: the credit graph is counter-attested at source
  (personas.md: the Producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim). Nobody else has it.
- **Self-assertion alone** — weakest, and never sufficient for anything that gets exported as proof.

### Happy Path

1. User opens Analytics → Identities; system proposes candidate external profiles derived from their name, credits and connections.
2. Each candidate shows evidence and confidence ("Spotify artist — matched via your connected account", "matched via 3 verified credits").
3. User confirms the ones that are theirs, rejects the rest.
4. System binds the profile and unlocks catalog matching (22.02.02) beneath it.
5. Ingested numbers begin landing against the bound identity.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two artists share a name ("Nova") | Candidates are disambiguated by evidence, never auto-bound | Both shown with distinguishing evidence; confirmation required |
| Two WeJammin identities claim one profile | Match → `contested`; neither binds | Hand-off to 22.02.03; both parties notified |
| One human, several artist entities (solo + 2 bands) | All bind; each is a separate match | A list, not a single "your artist profile" — the singular framing fails the primary persona |
| Band splits; who keeps the profile? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a domain 01 entity-governance question that lands here as a symptom | `[PENDING]` |
| Profile claimed via self-assertion only, then used for 22.08.03 proof | Binding allowed; export of *verified* proof is not | Badge reflects evidence strength; a self-asserted match cannot mint verified proof |
| Artist changes name on the DSP | External ID is the join key, so the match survives | Display name updates; no action needed |
| DSP merges/splits artist profiles (real and common) | Match breaks or silently re-points | `[PENDING — Step 5]`. Flagged: this is an externally-triggered mutation of a fact we depend on and cannot control |
| A user claims a profile that isn't theirs and nobody contests it | It binds. We cannot detect it. | Nothing — see DT-03 and Q-01. Honest gap. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Candidate generation in flight | Skeleton candidate list |
| Empty | No candidates found | "We can't find your artist profiles. Connect a DSP or add credits." — routes to the two evidence sources |
| Populated | Candidates proposed | Candidate list with evidence + confidence |
| Confirmed | User bound a profile | Bound identity with evidence badge |
| Contested | Two claimants | Frozen; adjudication pending (22.02.03) |
| Error | Search/candidate generation failed | Retry; manual search by URL as fallback |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **verified credit graph is match evidence no competitor can obtain** | Reasoning from D-18 + personas.md (the Producer as counter-attesting trust broker) | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the domain's quiet structural advantage. Chartmetric et al. match by name, popularity and fuzzy heuristics, because that is all they have. WeJammin can match by *counter-attested credits captured at source* — a fact the platform witnessed rather than inferred. This makes matching both more accurate and self-reinforcing: better provenance → better matching → better analytics → more reason to capture provenance. It is the flywheel the thesis predicts, appearing in an unglamorous place. |
| DT-02 | What breaks at the intersection with domain 01 (Identity)? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — a real collision. Domain 01 owns bands as **entities** with membership (D-19). A band's Spotify profile belongs to the *band entity*, not to any member, but any member may be the one who connects it. So: when a member leaves, do their analytics rights leave with them? Can a departed drummer still see the band's streams for records they played on? This is unanswerable in 22 alone — it is entity governance — but 22 is where the pain is *felt*. Escalated to Step 5 and to domain 01. |
| DT-03 | Self-assertion is sufficient because impersonation is rare | The comfortable assumption | ❌ REJECTED: impersonation is *not* rare in this exact niche — claiming another artist's profile is a known attack on every credits/analytics platform, and personas.md names it explicitly as the Musician's worst intentional behavior ("inflates credits to claim work they didn't do; games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation"). But the honest conclusion is uncomfortable: **we cannot prevent it**, because we cannot verify an external fact. What we *can* do is make the evidence strength visible and refuse to launder a self-claim into verified proof (22.08.03). Detection depends on someone contesting. That is a real, unresolved weakness, recorded rather than papered over. See Q-01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Matches carry **evidence strength**, and strength gates downstream use | A self-asserted match may power a private dashboard but must never mint verified proof (22.08.03). One binary "matched" flag would collapse the distinction the platform sells. |
| D-02 | Candidates are **proposed, never auto-bound** | Name collision is the norm in music. Auto-binding mis-attributes a discography in one silent action, and the error surfaces months later inside a commercial claim. |
| D-03 | The **external ID** is the join key, not the display name | Artists rename constantly; a name-keyed match breaks on a rename and re-binds on a collision. |
