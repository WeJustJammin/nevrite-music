# Feature: External Artist Profile Matching & Claiming

> **Number**: 22.02.01
> **Parent**: [External Identity & Catalog Matching](./22.02-external-identity-catalog-matching-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Binding an external artist profile — a Spotify artist URI, an Apple Music artist ID, a TikTok
handle — to a WeJammin identity or band entity, so that ingested numbers have a legitimate owner.
The hard part is not the search box; it is that artist names collide constantly, one human belongs
to several artist entities at once, and WeJammin cannot independently verify that a stranger's claim
on a Spotify profile is true.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Searches for and claims the external profiles that are theirs — personal artist name plus every band they're in (personas.md: roles are simultaneous, not sequential). Sees the confidence and evidence behind each match; contests a profile someone else has claimed. |
| Producer | Full | Claims their own producer/artist profiles, and — where a band entity has delegated it (domain 01) — matches on behalf of a project. Most likely persona to hold a legitimate relationship to a profile they do **not** own, which is the situation that generates most real conflicts. |
| Operator | None | Venues have no external artist profile. Not rendered. The Operator reads *aggregate* market data in 22.04, which is never bound to their own identity. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. A fan cannot claim an artist profile — this is the boundary that keeps impersonation out of the domain. Public artist stats a fan sees (domain 01/20) are downstream of a match a fan can neither see nor influence. |

## Behavior

A match is a **claim with evidence and a confidence**, never a bare join (D-03). The system proposes;
the user confirms; the platform records *why* it believed the binding — and the claim remains
contestable afterwards.

Evidence available, strongest first (each tier sets a confidence floor consumed downstream by
22.08.03's proof export):

- **An OAuth grant on the DSP's artist account (22.01.01)** — near-conclusive. The DSP has already
  done the verification we cannot do. Confidence tier: `dsp-grant`.
- **Distribution through WeJammin (domain 12)** — conclusive for releases we distributed; the
  platform minted the identifiers itself and needs no external verification. Confidence tier:
  `first-party`.
- **A verified credit on a recording that profile released (domain 02)** — this is the interesting
  one, and the platform's structural advantage: the credit graph is counter-attested at source
  (personas.md: the Producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim). Nobody else has it.
  Confidence tier: `credit-graph`, scaled by the number of corroborating verified credits.
- **Self-assertion alone** — weakest, and never sufficient for anything that gets exported as proof.
  Confidence tier: `self-asserted`.

**The binding is to an identity or an entity, not always to a person (D-04).** A solo artist profile
binds to a Musician/Producer identity; a band's profile binds to the **band entity** (domain 01,
D-19), and any member with the entity's delegated matching right may perform the bind. The binding
survives the member; it does not travel with them when they leave.

**Uniqueness is enforced at the binding, not in the UI (CX-02)**: at most one *confirmed subject*
may exist per (external-profile-ID) at a time. A member using a delegated matching right acts **for**
the band entity; that action binds the entity, not a second personal subject. A second subject claim
does not overwrite the first — it freezes binding. An unrelated claimant opens a contested claim
(22.02.03); a band-entity/member representation collision routes to domain 01. Neither binds until
one subject is selected.

### Happy Path

1. User opens Analytics → Identities; the system proposes candidate external profiles derived from
   their display name, verified credits (domain 02), connected DSP accounts (22.01.01) and social
   connections (22.01.02). Candidate generation is capped at the top 20 by descending confidence to
   keep the confirm surface reviewable.
2. Each candidate shows its evidence tier and a plain-language reason ("Spotify artist — matched via
   your connected account", "matched via 3 verified credits") plus the confidence tier badge.
3. User confirms the ones that are theirs and rejects the rest. Confirming a `credit-graph` or
   `self-asserted` candidate shows the strength badge so the user knows the binding's ceiling before
   they rely on it.
4. On confirm, the system checks binding uniqueness. If the profile is free, it binds the profile to
   the chosen identity/entity, stamps the evidence tier, and unlocks catalog matching (22.02.02)
   beneath it.
5. Ingested numbers (22.01) begin landing against the bound identity; the bound profile appears in
   the user's identity list with its evidence badge and a "contest / unlink" affordance.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Name collision** — two artists share a name ("Nova") | Candidates are disambiguated by evidence, never auto-bound (D-02). Each carries its own external ID and evidence set. | Both shown with distinguishing evidence (follower count, top track, connected-account flag); explicit confirmation required per candidate |
| **Concurrency** — two WeJammin identities confirm one profile within the same window | Binding-uniqueness check fails the second write. First confirm binds; second returns `409 {error:"profile_already_claimed", code:"MATCH_CONFLICT"}` and the profile moves to `contested`; neither binding is treated as settled once contested — the first is frozen too. Hand-off to 22.02.03. | Both parties notified; profile shows "contested — resolution pending", binding frozen |
| **Band entity and member both seek one artist profile** — the same human is a member of a band and also has a personal Musician identity | Never two bindings. A member may confirm **for** the band under delegated authority, which binds the band entity only. If the profile's represented subject is genuinely unclear, binding freezes and domain 01 governance selects either the band entity or the personal identity; 22 then binds that one subject. | "Representation pending — is this profile the band or you?" No catalog match unlocks until the governance outcome selects one subject. |
| **One human, several artist entities** (solo + 2 bands) | All bind; each is a separate match with its own evidence. | A list, not a single "your artist profile" — the singular framing fails the primary persona |
| **Band splits; who keeps the profile?** | The profile stays bound to the **band entity** (D-04, D-19). It never transfers to a departing member — the entity, not the person, is the match subject. Entity control on dissolution is domain 01's governance. A departed member's continued analytics access to records they personally hold verified credits on is a *rights* question, not a *binding* question — resolved by Q-02, defaulting to retain read access to own-credit records until Q-02 lands. | Departing member: the band profile leaves their claimable list; records they hold verified credits on stay visible per Q-02's default. The band entity keeps the binding. Remaining members: no change |
| **Self-asserted match reused for 22.08.03 proof** | Binding is allowed at `self-asserted`; *export of verified proof* is refused at that tier. The proof exporter reads the evidence tier and rejects anything below its floor (see Q-01). | Badge reflects evidence strength; attempting a verified-proof export on a `self-asserted` match is blocked with "this match is self-asserted — connect the DSP or add a verified credit to export it as proof" |
| **Malicious claim** — a user claims a profile that is not theirs and nobody contests it | It binds at `self-asserted`. **We cannot detect it** — we cannot verify an external fact (DT-03). Detection depends on someone contesting. The only structural defense is that the low tier caps what the false binding can *do*: it can populate a private dashboard but cannot mint verified proof. | Nothing at claim time — an honest, recorded gap. See DT-03 and Q-01 |
| **Malformed manual search** — user falls back to pasting a profile URL and pastes a non-DSP or malformed URL | The URL is validated against the DSP allowlist (Spotify/Apple Music/TikTok/etc. host + ID pattern) before any lookup. A non-matching or malformed URL is rejected client- and server-side; no external call is made. | `422 {error:"invalid_profile_url", code:"INVALID_PROFILE_URL"}` → "That doesn't look like a supported artist profile link" |
| **Artist renames on the DSP** | The external ID is the join key (D-03), so the match survives a display-name change. | Display name updates on next sync; no user action needed |
| **DSP merges or splits artist profiles** (real and common) | Externally-triggered mutation of a fact we depend on and cannot control. On the next sync the external ID either stops resolving (split: one ID → two) or now maps to a different roster (merge: two IDs → one). The match is **not** silently re-pointed — that would violate D-02. Instead it transitions to `needs-reconfirmation`, numbers arriving under the affected ID are **quarantined** (not dropped, not guessed — mirrors 22.01.04 and 22.02.03), and the user is prompted to re-confirm against the new candidate set. | "Spotify changed this artist profile — please re-confirm the match. 2 weeks of streams are held pending your confirmation." Quarantined numbers excluded from totals until re-confirmed |
| **Partial evidence during confirm** — candidate generation completes but the credit-graph service is unreachable, so only DSP/social evidence loaded | Candidate is still bindable on whatever evidence *did* load, at that evidence's tier; the missing credit evidence is flagged, not silently omitted. Re-running candidate generation later can raise the tier. | Candidate shows "credit evidence unavailable — matched on connected account only"; a "recheck evidence" affordance |
| **Permission** — Fan or Operator reaches the endpoint directly | Rejected by role gate (Role Lens: both `None`). No candidates generated, no binding possible. | `403 {error:"not_permitted", code:"ROLE_FORBIDDEN"}` |
| **Delegated match without right** — a Producer with roster *read* access (22.01) but no delegated *matching* right tries to bind a client's profile | Refused. Connecting (read) and matching (ownership) are separate (index D-01); read access must not silently become a binding right. | `403 {error:"delegation_required", code:"NO_MATCH_DELEGATION"}` → "You can view this roster but the artist must grant matching rights" |
| **WeJammin-distributed release** — profile already first-party via domain 12 | No matching needed; the binding is first-party by construction and pre-populated. Confirm is a formality, at `first-party` tier. | Profile shown pre-bound with "verified via WeJammin distribution" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Candidate generation in flight | Skeleton candidate list |
| Empty | No candidates found (no name match, no credits, no connected DSP) | "We can't find your artist profiles. Connect a DSP or add credits." — routes to the two evidence sources (22.01.01 / domain 02) |
| Populated | Candidates proposed | Candidate list with evidence tier + confidence per candidate |
| Confirmed | User bound a profile | Bound identity with evidence badge and contest/unlink affordance |
| Partial | Some evidence sources loaded, others unreachable; or some candidates bound while others still pending | Bound + pending candidates side by side; unreachable-evidence flag with a "recheck" affordance — never a silent omission |
| Contested | Two unrelated claimants on one profile | Frozen; binding suspended; adjudication pending (22.02.03) |
| Governance pending | Band entity and member seek the same profile as different subjects | Frozen; domain 01 selects the one represented subject. A delegated member action binds the entity and does not enter this state. |
| Needs-reconfirmation | External ID merged/split by the DSP; numbers quarantined | "Re-confirm this match" prompt with the held-stream count |
| Error | Search / candidate generation failed | Retry; manual search by profile URL as fallback (validated against the DSP allowlist) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **verified credit graph is match evidence no competitor can obtain** | Reasoning from D-18 + personas.md (the Producer as counter-attesting trust broker) | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the domain's quiet structural advantage. Chartmetric et al. match by name, popularity and fuzzy heuristics, because that is all they have. WeJammin can match by *counter-attested credits captured at source* — a fact the platform witnessed rather than inferred. This makes matching both more accurate and self-reinforcing: better provenance → better matching → better analytics → more reason to capture provenance. It is the flywheel the thesis predicts, appearing in an unglamorous place. |
| DT-02 | What breaks at the intersection with domain 01 (Identity)? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — a real collision, now partly resolved at the *binding* layer. Domain 01 owns bands as **entities** with membership (D-19). A band's Spotify profile binds to the band *entity*, not to any member, but any member with the delegated right may perform the bind — so the binding is stable across a lineup change (resolved: it stays with the entity). What is **not** resolvable in 22 alone is the *rights* consequence: when a member leaves, does their analytics access to records they played on leave too? That is entity governance, felt here but decided in domain 01. Escalated as Q-02; the binding-layer half is now decided (profile stays with the entity). |
| DT-03 | Self-assertion is sufficient because impersonation is rare | The comfortable assumption | ❌ REJECTED: impersonation is *not* rare in this exact niche — claiming another artist's profile is a known attack on every credits/analytics platform, and personas.md names it explicitly as the Musician's worst intentional behavior ("inflates credits to claim work they didn't do; games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation"). But the honest conclusion is uncomfortable: **we cannot prevent it**, because we cannot verify an external fact. What we *can* do is make evidence strength visible and refuse to launder a self-claim into verified proof (22.08.03). Detection depends on someone contesting. That is a real, unresolved weakness, recorded rather than papered over. See Q-01. |
| DT-04 | A DSP-side profile merge/split can be treated like a rename — just re-point the join key | The convenient generalisation, since D-03 already makes external ID the join key | ❌ REJECTED. A rename keeps one identity behind one ID; a merge/split *changes the set of identities behind the ID*, which is a different fact. Re-pointing automatically would re-attribute a discography with no human in the loop — exactly the silent mis-attribution D-02 exists to prevent, except now triggered by an external system rather than a name collision. So the merge/split path must **quarantine and re-confirm**, not auto-follow. This is why "Artist renames" and "DSP merges/splits" are two different rows with two different behaviors, not one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Matches carry **evidence strength**, and strength gates downstream use | A self-asserted match may power a private dashboard but must never mint verified proof (22.08.03). One binary "matched" flag would collapse the distinction the platform sells. |
| D-02 | Candidates are **proposed, never auto-bound** | Name collision is the norm in music. Auto-binding mis-attributes a discography in one silent action, and the error surfaces months later inside a commercial claim. |
| D-03 | The **external ID** is the join key, not the display name | Artists rename constantly; a name-keyed match breaks on a rename and re-binds on a collision. |
| D-04 | The match subject is an **identity or an entity**, not always a person | A band's profile binds to the band entity (domain 01, D-19); the binding stays with the entity across lineup changes and never transfers to a departing member. This is what makes the "band splits" case answerable at the binding layer. |
| D-05 | Binding **uniqueness** is enforced at the store: one external profile has one confirmed subject. A delegated member action binds its band entity; a second subject freezes binding and routes either to 22.02.03 adjudication or domain 01 representation governance. | Enforcing uniqueness in the UI would let a race bind twice. The band/member collision is not permission to bind both; it needs the entity model to select what the external profile represents. |
| D-06 | A DSP-side merge/split **quarantines and re-confirms**; it never auto-re-points | DT-04. An external mutation of the identity-set behind an ID is not a rename; following it silently would re-attribute a catalog with no human in the loop — the exact failure D-02 bans, arriving from outside. |
