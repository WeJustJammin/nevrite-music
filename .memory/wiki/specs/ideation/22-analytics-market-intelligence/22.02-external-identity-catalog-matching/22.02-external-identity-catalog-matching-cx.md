## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Artist Matching ↔ Catalog Matching

**Relationship**: Artist matching binds an external profile (Spotify artist URI) to a WeJammin
identity. Catalog matching binds external recordings (ISRC) to catalog entities — and its candidate
set is scoped by the artist match. Data flows downward; errors cascade downward with amplification:
one wrong artist match silently mis-attributes an entire discography, and every number downstream
(22.05's dashboard, 22.08's credit rollup, 22.08.03's exported proof) inherits the error wearing a
confidence badge.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees the cascade most sharply — a wrong artist match makes someone else's streams appear in *their* career total, which they may quote commercially.
- **Producer**: same, plus the roster case: a mis-match on one client's artist corrupts a deliverable to that client.
- **Operator**: not affected — no catalog.
- **Fan**: no visibility, but is a downstream victim: public stats on an artist profile (domain 01/20) would be wrong.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the artist match is the parent; catalog matches reference it. The artist match is owned by 22.02.01, and 22.02.02 must never mutate it. Merge strategy: none needed — single writer.
2. **Trigger chain**: artist match confirmed → catalog match candidates generated → user confirms. If the artist match is later **revoked**, every dependent catalog match must be invalidated, not orphaned. This is the sharp edge: silent orphaning leaves the numbers in place with no owner, which renders as "still yours". Invalidation must be cascading and loud. Sync-vs-async is `[PENDING — Step 5]`, but the cascade itself is non-negotiable.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — whoever can confirm an artist match implicitly gains the power to bind a catalog. That is a privilege escalation path if delegation (domain 01) permits a manager to match on an artist's behalf.
4. **Notification fan-out**: a cascading invalidation must notify the identity whose numbers just changed, and anyone holding a shared report (22.05.03) built on the invalidated match.
5. **State transition conflict**: yes — a catalog match confirmed while its parent artist match is being contested. Candidate: freeze dependent confirmations while a parent is contested. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.

### CX-02: Artist Matching ↔ Conflict Resolution

**Relationship**: Matching generates claims; resolution adjudicates the contested subset. The two
are a loop rather than a pipeline — a resolution outcome rewrites the match, which may generate a
new claim.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: raises and defends claims on their own profiles.
- **Producer**: same; also the most common *counterparty* in a legitimate dispute (producer and artist both claiming an artist profile they each have a real relationship to).
- **Operator / Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the match record is shared. Resolution owns the contested flag; matching owns the binding. Both write — this is the one genuine two-writer surface in the sub-domain, and it needs an explicit state machine at `/write-architecture-spec`.
2. **Trigger chain**: collision detected → match held in `contested` → adjudication → bind or reject. A failed adjudication leaves the match unbound, never speculatively bound.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — see Q-02 in the index. WeJammin cannot verify an external fact; the adjudicator may have to be the DSP, not us.
4. **Notification fan-out**: both claimants must be notified. A silent loss is worse than a loss.
5. **State transition conflict**: two identities confirming the same profile concurrently is the whole point of this pair. Uniqueness must be enforced at the binding, not in the UI.

### CX-03: Catalog Matching ↔ Conflict Resolution

**Relationship**: Ambiguous recordings (re-issued ISRCs, compilations, multi-artist tracks, the same
recording released under two ISRCs by two distributors) produce matches that cannot be resolved by
rule. Resolution provides the human decision and records it as precedent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the session player is disproportionately affected — their contribution to a track is real, but "whose stream is it?" has no clean answer.
- **Producer**: same.
- **Operator / Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: as CX-02, at recording granularity.
2. **Trigger chain**: ambiguity → quarantine → adjudication → bind. Quarantine (not drop, not guess) is the rule; see 22.01.04's row quarantine.
3. **Permission intersection**: a contributor confirming "this recording is mine" must not thereby claim exclusive ownership of the recording's numbers — 22.08 deliberately does **not** divide streams between contributors (see 22.08 D-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
