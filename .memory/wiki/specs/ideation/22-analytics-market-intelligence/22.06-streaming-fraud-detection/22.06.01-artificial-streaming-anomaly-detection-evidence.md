# Feature: Artificial-Streaming Anomaly Detection & Evidence

> **Number**: 22.06.01
> **Parent**: [Streaming Fraud & Fake Engagement Detection](./22.06-streaming-fraud-detection-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Detecting the shapes artificial streaming makes in ingested data — implausible geography, mechanical
consistency, listener/stream ratios that no human population produces — and packaging the observation
as an evidence dossier the artist can act on **before** their distributor acts on them. The point is
timing: a warning at week one is a decision; the same information at week twelve is a post-mortem on
a catalog that has already been removed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees flagged anomalies on their own tracks with the observed pattern explained in plain language, the coinciding events (a campaign they bought, a playlist that added them), and their options: cancel the campaign, disavow to the distributor, preserve the record. Disputes a false positive. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees flags on projects they're credited on. Their real stake: a record they made getting taken down damages their credit and their client relationship, and they usually have no idea it's happening. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. Streaming fraud has no bearing on a room's calendar. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Worth stating precisely: the fake listeners are **not** Fans — they are not people. No Fan-persona surface exists here and none should. |

## Behavior

Detection observes shapes in aggregate data. The honest limits, stated first because they determine
everything else:

- **We see daily aggregates, not streams.** DSPs do not expose per-stream, per-listener or per-source
  detail to artists. The signals that actually identify bot traffic — device fingerprints, session
  patterns, account age — are precisely the ones we cannot see (parent Q-01).
- **What remains observable**: implausible geography (a spike in a market with no other footprint),
  mechanical regularity (curves without human weekly rhythm), impossible ratios (streams per listener
  far outside plausible bounds), and coincidence with a purchased campaign.
- **This is anomaly detection, not fraud detection.** The output is "this looks unlike organic
  growth", never "this is fraud". We cannot know the latter, and D-03 forbids claiming it.

The dossier is the deliverable: what was observed, when, what else was true at the time, and what the
artist can do. It exists to be **handed to a distributor by the artist**, which is why it must be
readable by a human who is not us.

### Happy Path

1. Ingested data shows a pattern inconsistent with organic growth.
2. Detection flags it; a dossier is assembled with the observation, the window and coinciding events.
3. Artist is warned promptly — this is the entire value (parent D-01).
4. Dossier explains in plain language: "3,400 streams from a market where you have no other listeners, at a constant rate, starting the day your campaign with {vendor} began."
5. Artist acts: cancels, disavows, or preserves the record. **They** decide; we never report to anyone.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Genuine viral spike | Looks anomalous — sudden, geographically concentrated, mechanical-ish | The false-positive case that matters most. Hedged framing, never an accusation; a real breakout must not be told it's fake. See DT-02 |
| Sync placement / TV use | Produces a sharp unfamiliar-geography spike | Same class of false positive; coinciding-events context is what distinguishes it |
| Artist bought the fraud knowingly | Warning is unwelcome, not exculpatory | The same warning. We do not adjudicate intent — D-03 |
| Fraud bought by a third party to sabotage an artist | Real and rare | Dossier is the artist's only defence to a distributor; strengthens the case for evidence over accusation |
| Anomaly on a record with 6 credited contributors | All are exposed; only one may have bought the campaign | `[PENDING — Step 5]`. Who gets warned, and does naming the buyer to co-contributors accuse them? Uncomfortable and unresolved |
| Detection fires after the takedown | Too late — the product has failed | The failure mode parent D-01 exists to prevent |
| False positive disputed and confirmed | Dossier retracted; vendor score decremented | Retraction must propagate — see `#CX-01` Q5, or the score is a ratchet |
| Data too coarse to distinguish anything | Detection declines rather than guesses | Nothing shown. Parent Q-01 — this may be the *normal* case |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Clear | No anomalies | Nothing. Silence is the healthy state; a "you're clean" badge invites complacency |
| Flagged | Anomaly detected | Prominent warning ranked **above** the numbers (22.05.01 D-03) |
| Dossier | Evidence assembled | Plain-language dossier, exportable |
| Disputed | Artist contests | Under review; flag remains visible with the dispute noted |
| Retracted | False positive confirmed | Flag removed; dossier retained as a record of the error |
| Unable | Data too coarse | Nothing — never a fabricated all-clear |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The valuable thing is detection | The obvious framing, and what the candidate name says | ❌ REJECTED: detection is a means; **the warning's timing is the product** (parent D-01). Artificial streaming's cost is not the fake streams — it is the DSP fine passed through by the distributor, and ultimately the takedown of the artist's catalog. Every option the artist has (cancel the campaign, disavow with evidence, stop paying the vendor) expires with time. A perfect detector reporting at week twelve has produced a post-mortem. A crude detector reporting at week one has saved a catalog. **This inverts the usual accuracy/latency trade-off**: for this feature, early and hedged beats late and certain — which is a genuinely unusual design conclusion and should survive into the spec. |
| DT-02 | The false-positive cost is symmetric with the false-negative cost | The standard assumption behind any detector | ❌ REJECTED, and the asymmetry runs in a direction that should shape the whole feature. A **false negative** costs the artist a catalog. A **false positive** tells an artist their real, hard-won breakout is fake — and given personas.md's Musician is "not a star; makes a living from a portfolio of small income streams", a genuine viral moment may be the most important event of their career. Being told by their own platform that it looks fraudulent, on their best week, is a uniquely cruel product failure and a plausible reason to leave. So the resolution is not "tune the threshold" — it is **never accuse, always describe** (parent D-03): "this pattern is unusual, here's what we observed, here's what else was happening" is survivable in both error directions. An accusation is not. The framing *is* the mitigation. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with 22.03.01 (Placement) and 22.05.01 (Dashboard)? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — a direct contradiction between two features that are each individually right. 22.03.01 wants to alert on playlist adds (a win). 22.05.01 wants to render a streaming spike (a win). **Both are the fraud's signature.** Without this feature gating them, the platform enthusiastically congratulates the user on the exact event that is about to get their catalog removed — and the congratulation comes from the system they trusted to tell them the truth. This makes 22.06 a **hard upstream dependency of the celebration path**, not an optional analysis bolted on later: placement quality must be classified before alerting (22.03.01 D-04), and fraud warnings must outrank numbers in the dashboard (22.05.01 D-03). Both of those decisions exist because of this feature, which is an argument that 22.06 is more load-bearing than its `could` rating suggests. See Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Describe, never accuse** | DT-02. The false-positive harm is uniquely cruel and the false-negative harm is catastrophic; descriptive framing is survivable in both directions, an accusation in neither. |
| D-02 | **Early and hedged beats late and certain** | DT-01. Every option the artist has expires with time. |
| D-03 | The dossier is written for a **human outside WeJammin** | Its purpose is to be handed to a distributor by the artist. Internal jargon makes it useless at the only moment it matters. |
| D-04 | We **never report to a DSP or distributor** ourselves | We cannot verify fraud, and a platform that reports its own users to the party that can delete their catalog is not on their side. The artist owns their evidence. |
| D-05 | "Clear" renders as **silence**, not a badge | An all-clear on data too coarse to detect anything is a fabricated reassurance. |
