## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Anomaly Detection ↔ Vendor Risk Scoring

**Relationship**: The one-way half of the loop, and the sound half. When a detection fires, the
dossier records what else was true at the time — including any promo campaign the artist bought.
Across many artists, a vendor whose campaigns repeatedly coincide with anomalies becomes visible as a
pattern that no single artist could ever see. **This cross-artist visibility is the entire value**: an
individual artist buying a "playlist promotion" package has no way to know it's a bot farm, and the
vendor depends on that ignorance. A platform that sees many artists' outcomes can break it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the buyer, and the victim. Contributes dossiers (usually unknowingly) and consumes scores.
- **Producer**: read-only. Relevant when advising an artist on a release campaign.
- **Operator**: no visibility. Streaming fraud has no bearing on a room.
- **Fan**: no visibility. Worth noting: the fake "listeners" are not Fans — they are not people.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: dossiers are owned by 22.06.01; the vendor score is derived and recomputable. Single writer, no merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: anomaly detected → dossier written → vendor aggregate recomputed → future campaigns annotated. Async and idempotent; a failed aggregation loses annotation, not evidence. Correct failure direction.
3. **Permission intersection**: the sharp edge. A vendor's score is computed from **many artists' private dossiers**. The score is public-ish; its inputs are not, and must never be reconstructible from it. Same k-anonymity requirement as 22.04.01 D-05 and 22.05.04 D-03, with a higher stakes floor — here the derived claim is reputationally damaging to a named business.
4. **Notification fan-out**: an anomaly notifies the artist. A vendor's score changing notifies nobody — but an artist with an **active campaign** from a vendor whose score just deteriorated is arguably owed a warning, and that is a genuinely new alert class. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: a dossier retracted (false positive confirmed) must decrement the vendor score. If retractions do not propagate, the score is a ratchet that only ever worsens — which given D-03's "never accuse" stance would be an accusation accumulating by neglect.

### CX-02: Vendor Risk Scoring ↔ Anomaly Detection (feedback)

**Relationship**: A known-bad vendor's campaign raises the prior on an anomaly being artificial. This
is reasonable and it is also where the loop can go wrong: if a high vendor score makes detection more
likely to fire, and firing raises the vendor score, the system **confirms its own beliefs**. A vendor
scored badly early would accumulate evidence generated by the scoring itself.

The commitment made now: **the score annotates a dossier; it never triggers or strengthens a
detection**. Detection must stand on the data alone. This costs some sensitivity and buys the only
thing that matters here — the score remains an observation rather than a self-fulfilling prophecy.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees the annotation, not the mechanism.
- **Producer**: read-only.
- **Operator / Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Medium
confidence; synthesis deferred per the CX template's rule. The one commitment above is not deferred:
it is a design constraint, and it should be tested for at `/write-architecture-spec` rather than
rediscovered when a vendor asks why their score only ever goes down.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |

| R-01 | 22.06.01 Anomaly Detection | 22.04.02 Routing Insight | Rejected as a direct pair — the same reasoning as `22.04-audience-geography-routing-insight-cx.md#R-02`, recorded from this side. Bot streams have locations and could route a real tour to fake demand, which is an expensive failure. But the fix belongs upstream: 22.06 flags, 22.04.01 excludes flagged streams from the map, and every consumer inherits the correction. Wiring detection directly into routing would create a second filter that will drift from the first, and two filters that disagree are worse than one that is merely imperfect. |
| R-02 | 22.06.02 Vendor Risk Scoring | 22.03.03 Curator Intelligence | Rejected as a *pair*, though they share machinery — bot-playlist identification serves both. They are not an interaction: a curator is a person who chooses tracks; a promo vendor is a business selling a service. The shared component (playlist quality classification) is a **mechanism serving both**, which makes it a cross-cut recorded at domain level, not an interaction between these two features. Modelling it as a pair would put the same classifier in two places with two owners. |
