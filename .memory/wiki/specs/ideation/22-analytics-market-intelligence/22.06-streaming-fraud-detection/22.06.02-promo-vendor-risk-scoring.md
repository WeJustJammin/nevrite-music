# Feature: Promo Vendor Risk Scoring

> **Number**: 22.06.02
> **Parent**: [Streaming Fraud & Fake Engagement Detection](./22.06-streaming-fraud-detection-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Which "playlist promotion" services actually deliver artificial streams. An individual artist buying a
promo package cannot possibly know — the vendor's whole business model depends on that ignorance, and
the harm surfaces months later as a distributor strike. A platform observing many artists' outcomes
can see the pattern no single buyer ever will. That cross-artist view is the only reason this feature
is possible, and the only reason it is worth building.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees an observed-risk indication on a promo vendor **before** buying, and a warning if a vendor they've used deteriorates. They consume the score; they never set it, and they contribute to it (via 22.06.01 dossiers) usually without knowing. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same. Relevant when advising on a release campaign — a producer recommending a vendor who gets the record taken down has damaged their own client relationship and credit. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. |

## Behavior

A vendor's score is **derived entirely from observed outcomes**: across artists who bought from vendor
X, how often did an anomaly (22.06.01) follow the campaign? It is a coincidence rate with a sample
size — not an investigation, not a verdict, and not a claim about the vendor's intent.

The commitments that keep this defensible:
- **Observed coincidence only.** Never "this vendor sells bots" — always "of 34 campaigns we observed,
  22 were followed by anomalous streaming".
- **Sample size always shown.** A score from n=3 is noise wearing a number's clothes.
- **The score never triggers detection** (CX-02). Otherwise the loop confirms its own beliefs and a
  vendor scored badly early accumulates evidence the scoring itself generated.
- **Retractions propagate.** A confirmed false positive decrements the score, or it is a ratchet that
  only worsens — an accusation accumulating by neglect, which D-03 forbids.

### Happy Path

1. Artist considers a promo vendor (encountered on- or off-platform).
2. System shows what has been observed: campaign count, coincidence rate, period.
3. Artist decides with information they could not otherwise have had.
4. If they buy and an anomaly follows, a dossier (22.06.01) is written and the vendor's aggregate updates.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Too few observations | No score computed | "Not enough observations" — never a score from n=3 |
| Legitimate vendor, one bad coincidence | Rate reflects it honestly with n | Rate + sample size; a single event cannot condemn |
| Vendor rebrands to escape a score | Score does not follow | The obvious evasion. We cannot track corporate identity — an honest, unfixable limit. See Q-02 |
| Vendor disputes their score | Real business, real reputational harm | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — is there a right of reply? A vendor is not our user and has no account. See Q-01 |
| Artist bought off-platform, we never know | No observation, no data | Coverage gap: the score only sees campaigns we know about, which may be a small and unrepresentative slice |
| WeJammin (domain 21) is itself the vendor | We would be scoring ourselves | Parent Q-03 — decide before 21 has revenue depending on the answer |
| Score deteriorates while an artist's campaign is live | Arguably owed a warning | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a new alert class. See `#CX-01` Q4 |
| Score is wrong and an artist skips a good vendor | Invisible harm to a third party | Nothing. Named here because it is the cost nobody measures |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unknown | Insufficient observations | "Not enough observations" — the honest and probably common default |
| Observed | Sufficient sample | Coincidence rate + n + period |
| Deteriorating | Recent rate worse than historic | Trend shown; recency matters more than lifetime average |
| Disputed | Vendor contests | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

| DT-01 | This is the natural sibling of anomaly detection and should ship with it | The loop framing in the parent index | ❌ REJECTED on sequencing, not on merit — which is why it is the domain's only `wont` proposal. The score requires **many artists' campaign outcomes to be observable**, which requires: (a) 22.06.01 shipped and accurate on data that may be too coarse (parent Q-01), (b) enough WeJammin artists buying promo, (c) us **knowing** they bought it — which only happens reliably if domain 21 brokers the purchase. On day one there is no data; with few users there is noise; and noise here is a reputational claim about a real business with a real lawyer. **The feature's quality is a function of platform scale**, and shipping it early produces its worst version at the moment we can least defend it. | DT-02 | What breaks that nobody mentioned? **The vendor is a business we are publicly rating, and they are not our user.** | Reasoning about who is on the other side | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the same structural problem as 22.03.03 DT-02 (curator dossiers), one notch more dangerous. There, the subject is a hobbyist with no lawyer. Here it is a company whose revenue we are directly attacking, who never consented to be measured, who has every incentive to litigate, and who can — unlike a curator — afford to. **This is the domain's highest legal-exposure feature.** The mitigations are real but partial: observed coincidence rather than characterisation, sample sizes always attached, retractions propagating, no intent claimed. They reduce exposure; they do not eliminate it, because a true statement that destroys a business still attracts a lawsuit. Escalated to `/create-prd-security`, and it materially reinforces the `wont`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Observed coincidence only** — never characterisation or intent | DT-02. "22 of 34 campaigns were followed by anomalies" is a defensible fact; "this vendor sells bots" is a lawsuit. |
| D-02 | Sample size is **always** shown, and no score exists below a floor | A rate without n is noise wearing a number's clothes, and it is the form most likely to be screenshotted. |
| D-03 | The score **never triggers or strengthens detection** | CX-02. Otherwise the loop confirms its own beliefs and manufactures the evidence for its own conclusions. |
| D-04 | Retractions **propagate** and decrement | Otherwise the score is a ratchet — an accusation accumulating by neglect, which parent D-03 forbids. |
