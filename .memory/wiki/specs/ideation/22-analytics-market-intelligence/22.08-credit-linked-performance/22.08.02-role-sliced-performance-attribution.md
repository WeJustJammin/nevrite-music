# Feature: Role-Sliced Performance Attribution

> **Number**: 22.08.02
> **Parent**: [Credit-Linked Performance](./22.08-credit-linked-performance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18
> **Origin**: **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list.

## Overview

Partitioning the contribution catalog by what you actually did: your drumming work, your mixing work,
your writing. The multi-hyphenate is not one career with several labels — it is **several careers
sharing one human**, and they perform differently. A drummer whose mixing work outperforms their
drumming has learned something that changes what they sell, what they charge, and what they spend
their week doing.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their catalog partitioned by role — drums, mixing, writing, band membership — each with its own performance and trajectory. The persona's defining trait made legible: personas.md's "session drummer, occasional mixer, member of two bands" is three careers, and no tool in existence separates them. |
| Producer | Full | Their slices: production, mixing, engineering, writing. The distinction matters commercially — a producer who mixes is selling two services at two rates on domain 05, and this is the evidence for both. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. No credits. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. |

## Behavior

A lens over 22.08.01's rollup, never a second computation (CX-01). The partition key is the **credit
role** recorded in domain 02 at capture time — which makes this feature entirely dependent on domain
02 recording *what someone did*, not merely *that they were there*. If 02 records "contributor", this
feature is unbuildable (Q-01).

The comparison is where the insight lives: not "your drumming did 4M streams" in isolation, but "your
mixing work consistently outperforms your drumming work" — a fact about how to spend a career, which
is the sort of thing this persona currently learns by accident over a decade, or never.

Index D-01 still holds: no division. Each slice reports the full numbers of the records in it, and the
slices overlap — a record you both drummed on and mixed appears in both, at full value. **Slices do
not sum to the career total**, and that must be stated rather than quietly true, because a user who
adds them up and gets a bigger number will assume a bug.

### Happy Path

1. User opens their catalog and switches to By Role.
2. System partitions by credit role; each slice shows its records, performance and trajectory.
3. Comparison view: how the slices differ over time.
4. User acts — repositions a listing (domain 05), adjusts rates, or simply learns something true about their own career.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Both drummed and mixed one record | Appears in both slices at full value | Overlap stated; slices deliberately do not sum |
| Credit role is vague ("contributor") | Cannot slice | Unsliced bucket, visible. A silent "other" pile would hide the real coverage problem |
| Role disputed (co-writer vs producer) | That slice's numbers move; others stand | Cascade per `22.08-credit-linked-performance-cx.md#CX-03` Q2 |
| One role has 2 records | Slice is noise | Floors; "not enough work in this role to compare" |
| Role taxonomy differs across records | Domain 02's problem, and 02 has already solved it | Slices key on 02's **canonical** role, not on the record's literal — 02.06 D-05 (02 owns the vocabulary), D-01 (canonical core + alias layer), D-11 (resolution happens once, at assertion). "Drums" and "drum kit" land in one slice. See Q-02 |
| Slices don't sum to the total | Correct, and confusing | Stated explicitly, not discovered by arithmetic |
| Session player, one role only | Partition is trivial | Feature adds nothing — and that is fine. It exists for the multi-hyphenate, who is the majority of this persona but not all of it |
| Operator or Fan reaches this view (shared deep link, misrouted nav) | No credits → nothing to partition; the feature has no surface for them | Absent from their navigation entirely; a shared link resolves to their own (empty) role context, never another user's slices |
| Credit revoked mid-view (attestation withdrawn, dispute upheld) while user is reading | Snapshot-at-render — no mid-scroll mutation; the affected slice shrinks on next render. If that credit was a slice's only high-value record, the slice may drop below the floor | Slice restates on reload, not underfoot; and any 22.08.03 proof scoped to that role invalidates downward, per `22.08-credit-linked-performance-cx.md#CX-03` |
| User tries to inflate a role slice by relabelling their own credit role | Cannot — the partition key is the **counter-attested** role from domain 02 (index D-02), not a value this feature lets the user set | Slice reflects 02's attested role; there is no self-relabel control to abuse here |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Switched to By Role; rollup recomputing / partition in progress | Per-slice skeletons — never a partial-slice flash that reads as "this role has less work than it does" |
| Empty | No credits | Routes to domain 02 |
| Single-role | One role only | Partition suppressed — a "By Role" view with one row is noise |
| Populated | Multi-role | Slices with per-slice coverage |
| Partial | Some credits unroled | Unsliced bucket, sized and visible |
| Error | Rollup unavailable (22.08.01 fails, or domain 02 unreachable) | "Can't load your role breakdown right now" with retry — **never** a zero-filled slice, which would falsely read as "no work in this role" and misrepresent a career (coverage honesty, index D-03) |
| Unsliceable | 02 records no roles | Feature unavailable, with the reason named. See Q-01 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Role slicing is a filter — a UI convenience over the catalog | The obvious framing | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **primary persona's defining structure**, not a view option. personas.md is explicit — the multi-hyphenate's "roles are simultaneous, not sequential", and "any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer OR a seller?' fails them immediately". A career total that blends drumming and mixing answers a question nobody has: nobody's next job is "musician". Their next job is a drumming session or a mix, and it is won by evidence about **that role**. The role slice is therefore how this data becomes actionable at all, and a "filter" framing would deprioritise it into a dropdown. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect the **comparison across roles** to be the insight, and nobody mentioned it | Reasoning about what the persona actually needs to decide | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the feature's real output. The persona's genuine strategic question is not "how am I doing?" but **"which of my three careers should I feed?"** — a question they currently answer by vibes, and answer badly, because the feedback loop takes a decade and is confounded by everything. "Your mixing credits consistently outperform your drumming credits, and the gap is widening" is a career-changing sentence that **only this platform can produce**, and it falls out of data we already have. It is also the input to a real economic decision: what to list on domain 05, at what rate, and what to stop doing. This is the strongest specific insight found anywhere in domain 22. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with domain 02 and 05? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — a **hard upstream dependency that is easy to miss and expensive to discover late**. This feature lives or dies on domain 02 recording **role granularity at capture time**. If the split sheet flow records "contributor: 25%" without "drums", the data is unsliceable forever — and it is *unrecoverable*, because you cannot reconstruct who played what three years later (which is the reconstruction failure problem-statement names as the root cause). So 22.08.02's viability is decided **inside domain 02's capture UX**, by a design choice made long before anyone builds this feature, in a flow whose designers are optimising for speed at the session and have every incentive to ask fewer questions. The second-order point matters more than the first: **the capture flow must be told what the analytics will need**, or it will helpfully omit it. Escalated to domain 02 drilling and to `/write-architecture-spec`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Role slicing is **structural**, not a filter | DT-01. It is the primary persona's shape; a dropdown framing buries the feature's whole point. |
| D-02 | Slices **overlap and do not sum** — stated explicitly | Index D-01's no-division rule makes this correct and counterintuitive. A user who sums them and finds a discrepancy will assume a bug and lose trust in the number. |
| D-03 | Unroled credits go to a **visible** bucket, never a silent "other" | The bucket's size *is* the measure of domain 02's capture quality, and hiding it hides the problem. |
| D-04 | The **cross-role comparison** is the headline output | DT-02. "How am I doing?" is the question asked; "which career should I feed?" is the one worth answering. |
