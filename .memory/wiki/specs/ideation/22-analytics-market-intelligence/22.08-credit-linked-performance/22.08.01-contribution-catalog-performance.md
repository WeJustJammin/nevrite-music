# Feature: Contribution Catalog Performance

> **Number**: 22.08.01
> **Parent**: [Credit-Linked Performance](./22.08-credit-linked-performance-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list.

## Overview

The records you played on, and how they're doing. For a session musician this is the first time their
work has ever existed as a **catalog** rather than as a scattered memory — forty records across a
decade, most of which they have no idea about since the week they tracked them. It is the direct
payoff of provenance: the credits captured at the session (domain 02) become a portfolio that
accumulates, which is precisely D-18's "the reason they cannot leave".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Every record they hold a counter-attested credit on, with that record's performance, on one timeline. Sorts by recency, momentum, size — user-chosen, never an algorithmic "importance" rank (DT-04). Sees their catalog **grow as a body of work** rather than as a list of forgotten sessions. This is the persona the feature exists for and the only tool in the world that could show them this. |
| Producer | Full | Same, for records they produced, mixed or engineered. Their catalog is usually larger and older than a session player's, which makes the rollup more valuable and the coverage problem worse — a producer's 200-record back catalog spans distributors that no longer exist. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. No credits, no catalog. An Operator (venue/studio) sells time and space, holds no counter-attested creative credits, and therefore has an empty subject set. Access is **denied because they hold no credit subject** — the surface must not render an empty catalog, because an empty catalog would imply "you have zero records" to a persona for whom the concept does not apply at all. The denial is a distinct outcome from "you have credits but no data". |
| Fan | None | Never rendered here. A fan may see a *proof* on a public listing (22.08.03) — a total, never the catalog, because the catalog reveals relationships that are sometimes confidential. Direct access is **denied for the same reason — no credit subject** — never a rendered-but-empty catalog. |

## Behavior

The rollup joins the counter-attested credit graph (domain 02) to matched catalog performance
(22.02.02), producing: the set of recordings you are credited on, each with its performance series,
and aggregate views over the set.

Two rules define it:

- **Streams are never divided** (index D-01). A drummer on a 4M-stream record played on a 4M-stream
  record. Dividing by split would conflate performance with money — domain 10's question, not this
  one — and would produce the absurdity of a drummer "having" 400k streams.
- **Coverage honesty is structural** (index D-03). This rollup spans releases the user does not
  control, assembled from sources with independent failure states. It is patchy **by construction**,
  and the patchiness must degrade the claim rather than hide behind a clean total.

### Coverage Measures and Authority

This feature's displayed `N of M records (P%)` is **record coverage**: `M` is every
counter-attested credited record in the catalog subject set; `N` is each such record for which the
catalog renders a performance datum, including a stale-as-of-date datum. A record rendered
"performance unknown" or "performance unobtainable" remains in `M` and is not in `N`. Record
coverage is always disclosed before the aggregate under [D-01](#decisions) and
[D-02](#decisions); it describes the catalog's body-of-work completeness and does **not** calculate,
replace, or relax the source gate.

[22.01.03's **Coverage Rule — when a rollup may refuse to state a number**](../22.01-source-connections-ingestion/22.01.03-ingestion-health-gaps-freshness.md#the-coverage-rule--when-a-rollup-may-refuse-to-state-a-number-resolves-q-01)
is the sole authority for whether this aggregate may state a number. It applies its own source-level
dual gate — source coverage ratio `c` and `dominant-loss` — independently of record coverage. If
that rule refuses a single figure, retain the record-coverage disclosure but render the source rule's
"at least X across N of M sources" outcome with its named gaps; do not render this feature's
aggregate total. The `0.80` and `0.20` defaults, their upward-only tuning rule, and their pending
calibration are owned only by 22.01.03's Coverage Rule, [D-06](../22.01-source-connections-ingestion/22.01.03-ingestion-health-gaps-freshness.md#decisions), and
[Q-03](../22.01-source-connections-ingestion/22.01.03-ingestion-health-gaps-freshness.md#open-questions); this feature does not define a record-coverage refusal threshold.

The catalog behaves as a **consistent, recomputable view**: a single open of the catalog always shows
an internally consistent set — a credit added or a source refreshed while the view is being assembled
never produces a half-updated result. The same inputs always yield the same catalog, so re-opening or
retrying is safe and never produces a torn or duplicated view. This is a **behavioral guarantee**
(consistent in-flight view, safe idempotent retry); the concurrency strategy that delivers it is an
architecture decision (Q-04, deferred to `/create-prd-architecture`).

### Happy Path

1. User opens "My Work".
2. System resolves their counter-attested credits (domain 02) to matched recordings (22.02.02) as one
   internally consistent set (no half-updated view while resolving).
3. Catalog renders: each record, its performance, its trajectory — with per-record coverage stated
   (data present / data stale-as-of-date / data unobtainable).
4. Aggregate view: the body of work, over time, with an explicit coverage figure stated **before** the
   total — "we have data for 12 of your 41 credited records (29%)".
5. User drills into any record; sees who else was on it (subject to domain 02's visibility rules), and
   which of their own roles applied to that record (subject to 22.08.02's role slice).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | Category | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Credited on a record we have no data for | Partial data (dominant case) | Record listed, performance unknown — **not omitted**. Omitting it would silently shrink their career | Record row with "performance unknown", excluded from the coverage numerator, still counted in the denominator |
| Coverage is 30% of their credits | Partial data | The aggregate is meaningless as a total | Coverage stated **before** the number: "we have data for 12 of your 41 credited records (29%)" — never a bare total |
| Credit is self-claimed, not counter-attested | Malicious / integrity input | Included in the private catalog, excluded from any proof (D-04); visibly badged as unverified | Row tagged "self-claimed — not verified"; index D-02 |
| User fabricates credits to inflate the catalog | Malicious input | Self-claims can never enter the counter-attested subject set; only domain 02 attestation admits a record. Bulk self-claims are still private-only and carry a fraud signal to 22.06 | Rows appear only in the private, unverified section; no proof can cite them |
| A credit is revoked / disputed mid-view | Race / state transition | The in-flight view stays internally consistent (revocation does not tear the open view); on next open the rollup shrinks. Any already-published proof (22.08.03) is invalidated via `22.08-credit-linked-performance-cx.md#CX-02` | On refresh: record drops out; a one-time notice "your catalog changed — a credit was revoked" |
| Two credits added concurrently while the rollup is assembled | Concurrency | Neither appears in the view already being assembled; both appear on the next open. No partial/torn view — the view is internally consistent and re-openable | Current view unchanged; next open shows both new records |
| Same record, two of the user's own roles (drummer **and** mixer) | Multi-hyphenate in two roles | Record appears **once** in the catalog; both roles listed on the row; streams counted once for the record, never doubled per role (streams-never-divided applies to counting too) | One record row, roles "Drums · Mix"; the split into per-role views is 22.08.02's job (CX-01) |
| DSP/source fetch times out or errors for some records | Network failure | Per-record fetch is isolated; a failed source marks only its records stale-as-of-last-success, never fails the whole catalog. Retried on the ingestion schedule (22.01.03) | Affected rows show "data as of {date} — refresh failed"; catalog still renders for everything else |
| Every source is unreachable at render | Network failure (total) | The credit set (domain 02, first-party) still resolves; only performance data is missing | Catalog renders as a **list** with no numbers (Unknown state) plus a "performance data temporarily unavailable" banner — the list is still their work |
| Record has 6 contributors | Concurrency of view / shared entity | All six see the full numbers | No division; index D-01 |
| Ghost production / NDA'd work | Permission | The credit may exist privately but not publicly | Domain 02's visibility model governs; the record shows in the private catalog, never in proof |
| Record performs badly | Neutral-framing | The honest answer is unflattering | Neutral framing, no editorial verdict; pull-only (22.05.02 D-04) |
| Old catalog, distributor defunct | Permanent data gap | No data obtainable at any price | Listed, "performance unobtainable" — permanently. An honest gap that will never close |
| Rollup computation itself fails | Error | Distinguished from "no data": the credit set could not be assembled at all, so this is a hard failure of the view, not a coverage gap | Full-screen error state with retry; re-opening/retrying is safe and cannot produce a duplicated or torn catalog (the view is recomputable) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rollup query dispatched | Skeleton rows sized to the known credit count from domain 02 (e.g. 41 placeholder rows), with the coverage figure shown as "computing"; the list scaffold appears before performance data resolves, so the size of the body of work is visible immediately |
| Empty | No credits in domain 02 | Routes to credits — the honest message is that the catalog is downstream of capture: "your catalog fills as your credits are captured" with a link to domain 02 |
| Populated | Credits + data for all resolvable records | Catalog with per-record coverage; aggregate with coverage figure stated first |
| Partial | Some records lack data (the normal state) | Coverage figure stated prominently, not buried; known records show numbers, unknown records show "performance unknown" |
| Unknown | Credits exist, no data at all (or total source outage) | Catalog rendered as a list with no numbers — still valuable; it is their work. Distinguished from Empty (which has no credits) |
| Error | Rollup could not be assembled | Retry affordance; re-opening is safe, with no risk of a duplicate or torn catalog on retry (the view is recomputable) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the domain's only **uncopyable** node, and its reason to exist | Reasoning from D-18 against the incumbent set | ✅ CONFIRMED. Every incumbent — Chartmetric, Soundcharts, Viberate, Songstats — models **one artist entity** and ingests the same purchasable DSP data. None can answer "how are the records I played on doing?", because none holds a counter-attested credit graph; and they cannot retroactively manufacture one, because it only exists if you were **in the room** when the credit was captured (D-18). Jaxsta and Muso.AI hold credits but not the session, and no daily hook — the failure mode problem-statement names explicitly. This feature is the intersection those two failures leave empty, and it is the reason domain 22 is more than a Chartmetric clone. Everything else here is table stakes; this is the wedge. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **coverage, not accuracy, to be the binding constraint** — and nobody said so | Reasoning about where the rollup's data actually comes from | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the feature's central risk. The rollup spans records **the user does not own**: a session drummer cannot connect a DSP account for someone else's release. So coverage depends entirely on (a) 22.02.02 matching via the credit graph rather than via a claimed artist profile (22.02.02 D-04), and (b) obtaining performance data for releases we hold no grant on — which may simply be unobtainable (22.01 Q-01, Q-03). **The honest expectation is that coverage is low and permanently incomplete**, especially for older catalog. That is survivable *if* the feature is honest about it — a list of 41 records with data for 12 is still the only such list in existence, and still valuable. It is fatal if presented as a total. Hence index D-03 and the "listed, unknown — not omitted" rule. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with domain 02 and 05? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — a **circular cold-start**, and it is the platform's hardest sequencing problem, not just this feature's. The chain: this feature needs counter-attested credits at volume → credits are captured because Producers run sessions on WeJammin (personas.md: the Producer is "the capture point") → Producers come because the platform is where the work happens → the work is there because consolidation drew people in → and one of the strongest reasons to bother capturing a credit is *this feature*, which shows the credit paying off. So the payoff that motivates capture requires the capture to already exist. This is exactly D-18's claim ("consolidation is the precondition for provenance") arriving as a concrete ordering constraint: **22.08 cannot be the beachhead alone — it must ride on a consolidation surface that brings people in for another reason.** That is a real answer to problem-statement Q-03 and it comes from this feature, which the sweep never proposed. |
| DT-04 | The catalog should **fill data gaps with modeled/estimated streams** (extrapolate from comparable records) so the aggregate reads as complete | Product temptation: a patchy catalog looks worse than a full one, so estimate the rest | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection is load-bearing. Modeled numbers manufacture the exact thing this feature exists to prevent — an unverifiable claim about work. An estimate exported through 22.08.03 as "verified proof" would make WeJammin a credibility launderer (index D-02), inverting D-18. A gap is honest; an estimate presented as fact is a lie of convenience wearing the platform's brand. Coverage is stated and the gap stays visible (D-01, D-02). If estimation is ever wanted it must be a **separate, explicitly-labelled "estimated" surface** that can never feed proof — not a silent fill of this rollup. |
| DT-05 | The catalog should be **algorithmically ranked** by an "importance score" (streams × recency × split size) as the default order | Product temptation: surface the "best" records first | ❌ REJECTED. A split-size weighting smuggles division back in through the sort (index D-01 forbids it in the numbers; a ranked order re-imports it as editorial). More fundamentally, ranking editorialises *the user's own career* — deciding for a session drummer that their 4M-stream pop record "matters more" than the indie record they're proudest of. Order is **user-chosen** (recency, momentum, size) with recency as the neutral default; the platform lists, it does not judge. This is the same neutral-framing discipline as 22.05.02 D-04. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Records with **no data are listed, never omitted** | Omission silently shrinks a career. An unknown is honest; an absence is a lie of convenience — and this is the number they will quote to a client. Unknown records count in the coverage denominator, never in the numerator. |
| D-02 | Coverage is stated **before** the aggregate, as "N of M records (P%)" | DT-02. A total computed over 30% coverage is not a total, and the user must know that before they read the number, not after. |
| D-03 | Streams are **never divided** by split, and never doubled by role | Index D-01. Performance ≠ money; conflating them makes both wrong. A record the user held two roles on still counts its streams once. |
| D-04 | Self-claimed credits are in the private catalog, **never** in proof, and always visibly badged | Index D-02. The badge's value is that it cannot be faked; an unbadged self-claim would erode it silently. |
| D-05 | Data gaps are **never filled with estimates**; order is **never algorithmically ranked** | DT-04, DT-05. Both re-introduce unverifiable or editorial content into a feature whose entire value is verifiable, neutral fact. Estimation, if ever built, is a separate labelled surface that cannot feed proof. |
| D-06 | The catalog is a **consistent, recomputable view** — behavioral guarantee only | Every open shows an internally consistent set, and re-opening/retrying never produces a torn or duplicated catalog. This makes concurrency, retry, and total-source-outage safe. The *mechanism* that delivers this (read-timestamp/MVCC/snapshotting) is deliberately not decided here — it is an architecture concern (Q-04). |
