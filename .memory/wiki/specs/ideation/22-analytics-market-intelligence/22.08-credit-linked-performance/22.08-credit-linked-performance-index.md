## Overview

*"How are the records I played on doing?"* — a question that has never had an answer, for anyone, ever.
A session drummer with credits on forty records has no dashboard of their own catalog, because no
system knows what they played on. Every analytics product in existence models **one artist**; the
working musician is a body of contributions attached to other people's releases, and is invisible to
all of them.

WeJammin can answer it, and **only WeJammin can**, because answering it requires a counter-attested
credit graph captured at the session (domain 02) joined to catalog performance data (22.01–22.02).
Nobody else holds both. This is not an adjacent nice-to-have: it is D-18's thesis rendered as a
screen — the provenance captured at source becomes the portfolio the musician's career accrues into,
and the reason they cannot leave.

**Why this is a sub-domain (2+ interacting capabilities)**: contribution rollup, role-sliced
attribution, and verified proof export are distinct capabilities that interact strictly — the rollup
produces the numbers, the role slice reinterprets them per contribution type, and the proof export
converts either into a commercial asset whose integrity depends on both. The export is what makes the
other two consequential, and what makes their honesty non-negotiable.

**Why it was missed**: the sweep looked at what analytics products *do* (aggregate artist metrics) and
enumerated the category's furniture. It never asked what **this platform's data** makes newly possible.
Every candidate in the list serves an artist with a Spotify profile; none serves the multi-hyphenate
session player who is the ratified primary persona (D-19).

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Streams are **never divided** between contributors | A record's numbers belong wholly to every contributor's view of it. A drummer on a 4M-stream record played on a 4M-stream record — full stop. Dividing by split percentage would conflate *performance* with *money* (domain 10's question), and would produce the absurdity of a drummer "having" 400k streams. Rollups are therefore **not sums of ownership**; they are catalogs of participation. | Agent, Deep Think, 2026-07-16 |
| D-02 | Verified proof requires **counter-attested** credits, never self-claims | The entire value is that it cannot be faked. A self-asserted credit exported as verified proof would make WeJammin a credibility launderer — the exact inversion of D-18. See 22.01.04 D-02, 22.02.01 D-01. | Agent, thesis-derived, 2026-07-16 |
| D-03 | Coverage honesty is **structural**, not decorative | This rollup spans releases the user does not control, assembled from sources with independent failure states. One dead token silently shrinks a session player's apparent career — and 22.08.03 exports that number as *proof*. Gaps must degrade the claim, not the presentation. | Agent, Deep Think, 2026-07-16 (see 22.01.03 DT-02) |
