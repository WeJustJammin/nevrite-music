# Feature: Show Impact Attribution

> **Number**: 22.04.03
> **Parent**: [Audience Geography & Tour Routing Insight](./22.04-audience-geography-routing-insight-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Did playing that city actually grow it? Measuring the change in a city's audience after a show —
listeners, followers, ticket buyers, merch — against what the city was doing before. This is the
loop-closing feature and the domain's highest-novelty node: **no incumbent can build it**, because
answering it requires holding the booking record and the streaming record in the same system, and
nobody else holds both.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees, per show, what happened to that city afterward: audience lift, follower gain, ticket-buyer retention, decay curve. Over a career this is the answer to the question that governs their entire touring economics — *does touring actually build anything, or am I just breaking even for the fun of it?* Nobody has ever been able to tell them. |
| Producer | None | Not rendered. No standing in live performance. |
| Operator | Reports | Aggregate only: "shows in this room lift artists' local audience by X% on average" — a genuine and honest sales asset for a venue, and one no venue can currently claim with evidence. Never an individual artist's lift, which is their negotiating position (parent D-02). |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. The fan who came to the show *is* the measurement — invisibly so. |

## Behavior

For each show (from domain 17's booking record), an attribution window opens. The system compares the
city's audience trajectory before and after, against the artist's non-played cities as a control, and
reports a lift with an explicit confidence.

This is **quasi-experimental measurement on small numbers**, and the honesty burden is severe:
- A single show in a mid-size city moves numbers that are mostly noise.
- Confounders are everywhere and mostly unobservable — a release, a playlist add, a support slot on
  someone else's tour, a local radio play, a season.
- The control (other cities) is imperfect, because a tour is not randomised.

Which is exactly why this must report **ranges with confidence and confounders named**, never a
headline percentage. The temptation to ship "Manchester +40%!" is strong and it would be a fabricated
fact with a decimal point on it.

### Happy Path

1. A show completes (domain 17 booking record).
2. Attribution window opens; the city's pre-show baseline is already recorded.
3. After the window, the system compares post-show trajectory against baseline and control cities.
4. Result: "Manchester audience up 12–30% over 8 weeks (moderate confidence). A playlist add on 4 March also occurred."
5. The result annotates the map (22.04.01) and informs future routing (22.04.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show coincides with a release | The confounder swamps the signal | "Can't separate this show from your 2 March release" — an honest refusal is the correct output |
| Small city, tiny numbers | Noise dominates | "Too small to measure" — never a precise-looking number computed from nothing |
| Show cancelled/rescheduled after the window opened | Window invalidated, not measured | Nothing measured; a show that didn't happen has no impact. See `#CX-02` synthesis Q5 |
| Booked off-platform | No record, no attribution | "We can only measure shows booked here" — **D-18 in miniature**: being in the room is the precondition for the insight |
| Support slot on a bigger tour | Attribution attributes the headliner's draw to the support act | Confounder named; this is a very common case for the primary persona and would systematically over-credit them if unflagged |
| Festival appearance | City-level attribution is close to meaningless | Flagged as a weak case; measured with wide bands or declined |
| Multiple shows in one city in the window | Attributed to the run, not to a show | Run-level result; per-show splitting would be invented precision |
| Genuine null result (nothing happened) | Reported plainly | "No measurable change" — and this is a *valuable* answer, arguably the most valuable, since it may save the artist a whole tour's losses |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Show played, window open | "Measuring — results in ~6 weeks" |
| Populated | Window closed, signal sufficient | Lift range + confidence + confounders |
| Null | Window closed, no measurable change | "No measurable change" — presented neutrally, not as a failure |
| Declined | Confounders or noise too severe | Explicit refusal with the reason named |
| Empty | No shows in the booking record | Routes to domain 17 |
| Invalidated | Show cancelled | Removed silently — nothing to report |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the **only genuinely un-copyable feature in domain 22** | Competitive reasoning against the whole incumbent set | ✅ CONFIRMED. Chartmetric, Soundcharts, Viberate and Songstats all ingest the same DSP and social data — the audience half is a commodity available to anyone who pays for the pipes. What none of them holds is **the artist's booking record**, because they are not where the booking happens. WeJammin holds domain 17, so it alone can join "you played Manchester on 3 March" to "Manchester's audience did this afterward". This is D-18's thesis in its purest observable form: *consolidation is what makes the insight possible*, and the insight is a reason the artist cannot leave. It is the single strongest argument in this domain's favour. |
| DT-02 | Therefore it should be built early | The natural conclusion from DT-01, and the trap | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the most important finding in this sub-domain. The feature **cannot exist** until domains 17/18 hold real booking history *and* enough time has passed to measure anything. It requires: bookings on-platform, at volume, with 6–12 months of post-show data, plus connected DSP sources predating the shows. On day one it measures nothing; in year one it measures noise. **Novelty and buildability are inversely ordered here** — the most differentiating feature in the domain is the last one that can work. Proposed `could` for that reason alone, not because it is unimportant. This is the concrete form of parent Q-01, and it is a real constraint on any "differentiate first" instinct. |
| DT-03 | What would a domain expert expect that nobody mentioned? **The null result is the valuable one.** | Reasoning about the artist's actual economics | ✅ CONFIRMED. The industry's unexamined article of faith is that touring builds an audience. For most working musicians at this level, a given show may build nothing — they lose money and go home. A platform that could honestly say "these four cities never grow when you play them" would save an artist thousands of pounds and weeks of their life, which is worth more than any positive number this feature could report. But it is a deeply unwelcome message, and the product temptation will be to bury nulls or dress them up. **Nulls must be first-class and neutrally framed** (see States). This also means the feature's honesty is its whole value — a version that only celebrates wins is strictly worse than nothing, because it launders a losing strategy. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Results are **ranges with confidence and named confounders**, never a headline percentage | This is quasi-experimental inference on small numbers. A precise figure would be a fabricated fact — the exact disease D-18 says this platform treats. |
| D-02 | **Null results are first-class** and neutrally framed | DT-03. The null may be the most economically valuable output in the domain, and it is the one the product will be tempted to hide. |
| D-03 | The system **declines to measure** when confounders swamp the signal | A measurement that cannot separate a show from a release is not a weak measurement; it is not a measurement. |
| D-04 | Attribution only exists for shows **booked on-platform** | Not a limitation to apologise for — it is the mechanism. Being in the room is what makes the record exist (D-18). |
