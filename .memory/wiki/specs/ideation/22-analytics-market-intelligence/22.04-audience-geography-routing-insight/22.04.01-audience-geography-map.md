# Feature: Audience Geography Map

> **Number**: 22.04.01
> **Parent**: [Audience Geography & Tour Routing Insight](./22.04-audience-geography-routing-insight-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Where the listeners are, at city granularity, across every connected source and over time. The bare
listener map is a commodity — Spotify for Artists hands one out free, and Chartmetric/Soundcharts/
Viberate all ship it — so it is not included for its own sake. Two things make WeJammin's version
non-commodity: it renders **two layers, rented reach and owned audience** (D-02) where every
incumbent has only the rented one, and it foregrounds **engagement depth over raw listener count**
(D-01) because listener ≠ attendee is the domain's most dangerous unstated assumption. Beyond that
it earns its place instrumentally: it is the substrate routing (22.04.02) consumes and the canvas
that show-impact attribution (22.04.03) writes its findings back onto.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their audience by city and territory, over time, per source; sees which cities are growing, which are decaying, and — once 22.04.03 exists — which they built by playing there. This is the map they take into a booking conversation as evidence of a draw. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees geography for projects they're credited on. Context, not a lever: a producer does not route tours. Genuinely useful in one case — knowing a record broke in a territory tells them something real about the work. |
| Operator | Reports | **Aggregate market data for their catchment only** — "how many artists in this genre have a following within 50km", never "this artist has 4,200 listeners here". The boundary is load-bearing (parent D-02): an artist's city-level count is their negotiating position on a guarantee, and handing it to the counterparty who sets that guarantee inverts the relationship. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Worth stating plainly: fans **generate** this data by listening and never see it. That asymmetry is inherent to the domain and is not a gap to be closed. |

## Behavior

Per (city, period, source) audience measures — listeners, streams, engagement depth — normalised
across sources that define their geography differently and report at different granularities and
lags. Rendered as a map and, more usefully, as per-city trend series.

The honest caveats, which are not footnotes:
- **DSP geography is coarse and lagged** — often metro-level, often a 28-day rolling window, often
  inferred from IP or account registration rather than observed presence.
- **Listener ≠ potential attendee.** A passive algorithmic listener in a city is not a person who
  will buy a ticket. Conflating them is how a band ends up playing to nine people in a city the
  map called strong. This is the feature's core epistemic risk and the reason 22.04.03 matters.
- **Fraudulent streams have locations too.** Flagged streams (22.06) must be excluded from the map,
  or bots route real tours (see `#R-02` in the sub-domain CX).

### Happy Path

1. User opens Audience → Geography.
2. Map renders per-city audience for the selected period and sources, with freshness stated.
3. User drills into a city: trend over time, source breakdown, engagement depth.
4. User compares cities, or compares a city against their booking history (once 22.04.03 exists).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Source reports metro-level, not city | Rolled up to the coarsest common granularity; not downscaled | Granularity stated; a fabricated city split would be an invented fact |
| Sources disagree on a city's size | Both shown; not averaged | Per-source breakdown. Averaging incomparable definitions produces a number nobody measured |
| Rolling 28-day window only | No true history from that source | History accrues from connection date; see 22.01.02 DT-02 |
| Gap in a source (22.01.03) | City series breaks; not interpolated | Gap marker; a smoothed map is a fabricated map |
| Bot streams inflate a city | Excluded once flagged by 22.06 | Retroactive correction, disclosed as a restatement (22.01.03 D-04) |
| Small city, few listeners | Rendered, but noise dominates | Low-confidence framing; ranges over points |
| Operator's catchment has one artist | Aggregate = that artist's private data | **Suppressed.** k-anonymity floor required — see parent Q-03 and CX-01 Q3 |
| VPN / diaspora listeners | Geography is wrong in ways we cannot detect | Nothing — an honest, unfixable limit worth stating once in the UI rather than never |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aggregation in flight | Map skeleton |
| Empty | No geography-capable source connected | "Connect a DSP to see where you're being heard" |
| Populated | Data present | Map + per-city trends, freshness stated |
| Partial | Some sources missing/gapped | Coverage stated per source; never silent omission |
| Suppressed | Below the anonymity floor (Operator view) | "Not enough artists in this market to report" |
| Error | Aggregation failed | Retry; last-good with its age stated |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Engagement depth is first-class**, not a drill-down | DT-02. Listener count alone routes bands into empty rooms. |
| D-02 | The map renders **two layers**: rented reach (DSP) and owned audience (WeJammin followers/ticket buyers) | DT-03(b). The owned layer is better evidence and is unavailable to every incumbent. |
| D-03 | Sources are **never averaged** into a single city number | They define and infer geography differently. An average is a number nobody measured. |
| D-04 | Flagged fraudulent streams are excluded **in the map**, not at each consumer | Single filter, single truth; duplicated filters drift apart. |
| D-05 | Operator aggregates enforce a **k-anonymity floor** | Below it, "aggregate market data" is one artist's private negotiating position with a hat on. |
