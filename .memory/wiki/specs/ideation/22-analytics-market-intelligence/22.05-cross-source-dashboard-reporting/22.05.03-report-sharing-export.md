# Feature: Report Sharing & Export

> **Number**: 22.05.03
> **Parent**: [Cross-Source Dashboard & Reporting](./22.05-cross-source-dashboard-reporting-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Showing someone else, and taking it with you: permissioned share links and snapshots for the people
who ask for numbers (a label, a manager, a booking agent, a client), plus bulk export of the user's
own data. Export is not a checkbox — it is the platform's answer to problem-statement Q-02, *"is the
lock-in earned or hostile?"*

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Shares a snapshot or live link with a label, agent or venue they're negotiating with; controls what it contains and how long it lives; exports everything they own. The negotiating case is the real one — "here are my numbers in Manchester" is evidence in a conversation about a guarantee. |
| Producer | Full | The routine professional deliverable: a client report on the record they made, which today is a manual PDF assembled from three dashboards. This is the highest-frequency use of the feature and the least glamorous. |
| Operator | Read-only | Receives shares far more than they send — an artist's pitch for a booking. Their own aggregate market data is not theirs to redistribute (22.04.01 D-05), so they cannot re-share it onward. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Public artist stats a fan sees are a domain 01/20 surface, not a share. |

## Behavior

Two distinct things wearing one name, and the distinction is load-bearing:

- **Share** — a render of a report definition for another party. Permissioned, revocable, and either
  a snapshot (frozen) or live (reflects later edits and corrections).
- **Export** — the user's own data, in bulk, in a format they can use elsewhere. No permission
  question: it is theirs.

Honesty travels (parent D-03). A shared report carries its gaps, estimate labels and imported
provenance, because otherwise sharing is a **laundering mechanism** — the artist sees the hole, the
label sees a clean chart, and WeJammin is the instrument that cleaned it.

Cohort comparisons (22.05.04) **never** travel into a share or export (CX `#R-02`): they are computed
from third parties' private data, and exporting them leaks a claim about people who never agreed to be
a denominator.

### Happy Path

1. User picks a report and chooses Share.
2. Chooses snapshot (frozen, dated) or live (updates), sets scope and expiry.
3. Link issued; recipient opens it — no account required for a link share.
4. Recipient sees the report with its honesty markers intact, and nothing outside the shared scope.
5. Separately: user chooses Export, gets their own data in a portable format, no questions asked.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Live share, data restated after sending | Recipient sees a different report than was sent | Restatement disclosed in the share (22.01.03 D-04). A decisive argument for snapshot-as-default |
| Live share, a match is contested (22.02.03) | Affected numbers suspend to unknown | Recipient sees the same suspension the owner does — never a stale confident value |
| Shared report contains a gap | Gap travels | Recipient sees the hole. Non-negotiable — parent D-03 |
| Link forwarded to a third party | It works — a link is a bearer token | Expiry and scope are the only real controls; stated plainly at share time rather than implied |
| Recipient screenshots a snapshot | Nothing we can do | Nothing. Worth naming: shares are advisory, not enforceable |
| DSP terms forbid redistribution | The whole feature may be unlawful for DSP-sourced series | `[PENDING]` — parent Q-01. This is the feature's existential risk, not an edge case |
| Export requested on account deletion | Portability obligation (GDPR) | Must work even mid-deletion; overlaps a platform-wide DSAR cross-cut |
| Export of ingested third-party data | May be forbidden even though the user considers it theirs | `[PENDING]` — the uncomfortable case: our *own* honesty about earned lock-in may be constrained by a DSP's terms. See Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Nothing shared | Share CTA on any report |
| Active | Shares exist | List with scope, type, expiry, last viewed |
| Expired | Past expiry | Recipient sees an expiry notice, not a 404 — a 404 reads as deception |
| Revoked | Owner revoked | Same treatment |
| Suspended | Underlying data contested | Recipient sees the suspension, not stale numbers |
| Exporting | Bulk export running | Progress; delivered async |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Export is a compliance checkbox | The default framing of data portability | ❌ REJECTED: export is **the platform's answer to problem-statement Q-02** — is the lock-in earned or hostile? D-18 claims provenance is *"the value they cannot take with them"*. That claim is only honourable if everything else **can** be taken. The distinction: an artist can export their numbers, their catalog and their credits — but they cannot export **the counter-attestations** (personas.md: the Producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim), because those are relationships, not records; they exist between parties and are meaningless outside the graph that holds them. That is earned lock-in: the thing you'd miss is a network of verified agreement, not a file we're holding hostage. Making export excellent *strengthens* the thesis rather than weakening it — and a platform confident in its moat has no reason to hoard data. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **snapshot vs live** to be the central decision, and nobody mentioned it | Reasoning about what a shared report is actually *for* | ✅ CONFIRMED. The use case is almost always a negotiation or a deliverable — a pitch to a venue, a report to a label, a client update. In every one, the sender needs the recipient to see **what was sent**, not a document that silently changes afterward. A live share that restates downward mid-negotiation (22.01.03 D-04 guarantees this will happen) actively damages the sender. So **snapshot is the correct default** and live is the specialist case, which is the opposite of how dashboards normally do sharing. Nobody in the candidate list distinguished them at all. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with 22.01.04 (Manual Import) and 22.08.03 (Verified Proof)? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED: this is the **laundering surface**. Sharing is where a self-claimed number (a hand-edited CSV, 22.01.04) could acquire institutional authority simply by arriving inside a WeJammin-branded report sent to a label. The recipient reads the brand, not the provenance. If import provenance does not survive into the share, WeJammin becomes a credibility launderer — the precise inversion of D-18, using the platform's trust to certify an unverified claim. Provenance markers in shares are therefore a **thesis requirement**, not a UI detail. This is the same mechanism 22.08.03 depends on, viewed from the other end. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Snapshot is the default**; live is the specialist case | DT-02. The dominant use is a negotiation or deliverable, where a silently-changing document damages the sender. |
| D-02 | Honesty markers and provenance **always travel** into shares and exports | DT-03 + parent D-03. Otherwise WeJammin launders self-claims into institutional facts. |
| D-03 | Cohort comparisons **never** travel | CX `#R-02`. They are third parties' data and were never consented to leave. |
| D-04 | Export is **excellent by design**, not minimal-compliant | DT-01. Earned lock-in requires that leaving be possible; the moat is the counter-attested graph, which cannot be exported because it is a relationship. |
| D-05 | Expired/revoked links say so; they never 404 | A 404 reads as deception to a recipient who was legitimately sent something. |
