## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Dashboard ↔ Alerts & Digests

**Relationship**: One report definition, two deliveries — pull (dashboard) and push (digest). The
digest must be the *same computation*, not a parallel one; two implementations of "your week" will
disagree eventually, and the moment they do, both numbers become untrustworthy and the user has no
way to tell which lied.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: both, fully. The digest is likely their primary contact with this domain — most will never open the dashboard weekly, but they will read an email.
- **Producer**: both, scoped to their projects.
- **Operator**: dashboard read-only, digest config only — they configure what their venue's report says, but the underlying market data is not theirs.
- **Fan**: neither.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the report definition is the shared entity. The dashboard owns editing it; digests reference it. If a definition is deleted while a digest references it, the digest must disable rather than silently send an empty or stale report.
2. **Trigger chain**: schedule fires → report renders → delivery. If rendering fails, **no digest is sent** — an empty or partial digest is worse than silence, because it reads as "nothing happened". If delivery fails, retry; the render is idempotent.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — a digest to a delegated viewer must render at *their* permission level, not the owner's. Rendering at the owner's level and mailing it out is a data leak with a schedule attached. This is the highest-risk item in the sub-domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: this pair *is* the fan-out. The noise budget (22.05.02 D-01) is enforced here, at the composition point, not per-rule.
5. **State transition conflict**: a definition edited mid-render. Snapshot-at-render-start is the obvious resolution; flagged for `/write-be-spec`.

### CX-02: Dashboard ↔ Sharing & Export

**Relationship**: Sharing renders the same definition for a different viewer. The differences are
permission (what may they see) and honesty (do gaps travel — yes, D-03).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: shares with a manager, label, agent, or a venue they are negotiating with.
- **Producer**: shares a project's performance with a client — a routine professional deliverable.
- **Operator**: read-only; receives shares more than they send.
- **Fan**: never.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: a **live** share reflects definition edits after sharing — including edits that add data the recipient was never meant to see. A **snapshot** share does not. These are different products with different risks, and the choice must be explicit at share time rather than defaulted. See 22.05.03 D-01.
2. **Trigger chain**: share created → link issued → renders on view. A revoked match (22.02.03) or a newly-discovered gap changes what an already-shared link shows *after the fact* — the recipient may see a different report than the sender sent. Unavoidable for live shares; a decisive argument for snapshots being the default.
3. **Permission intersection**: the whole feature. An unauthenticated link-holder has no identity to scope against, which is why link-shares must be scoped at creation, not at view.
4. **Notification fan-out**: does the owner learn their link was viewed? Useful (a label opened your report) and mildly surveillant. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: revoking a share while it is being viewed. Standard; low stakes.

### CX-03: Dashboard ↔ Benchmarking

**Relationship**: Benchmarking is a lens over the dashboard's own numbers, not a separate report. It
adds one thing: a cohort. The cohort is where every hard problem in this pair lives — how it is
constituted, whether it is anonymous, and whether we may lawfully compute it at all (index Q-02).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: full — "compared to what?" is their question.
- **Producer**: read-only. A producer benchmarking themselves against other producers is a plausible future, and is deliberately not proposed now — see 22.05.04 Q-03.
- **Operator / Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none. The cohort is derived; the dashboard is unaffected.
2. **Trigger chain**: cohort computed → comparison rendered. If the cohort is too small, comparison is **suppressed**, not widened — silently widening a cohort to make a comparison possible produces a meaningless comparison the user will read as meaningful.
3. **Permission intersection**: the cohort is computed from **other users' private data**. Aggregation and a k-anonymity floor are doing all the privacy work here, exactly as in 22.04.01 D-05. Same mechanism, same risk, same escalation.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none. "You fell behind your cohort" as an alert would be actively harmful to the persona and is not proposed.
5. **State transition conflict**: none — read-only derivation.

### CX-04: Alerts & Digests ↔ Sharing & Export

**Relationship**: The two compose — a scheduled digest sent to a shared recipient is a manager's
weekly report. Composition is the point at which two independently-correct permission models produce
one incorrect outcome.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets up a manager's weekly digest.
- **Producer**: sends a client a monthly project report.
- **Operator / Fan**: not applicable.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Medium
confidence; synthesis deferred per the CX template's rule. One commitment made now: **the digest
renders at the recipient's permission level** (CX-01 Q3), and that rule is not restated in the
sharing feature — it is enforced once, here, at the composition.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 22.05.04 Benchmarking | 22.05.02 Alerts & Digests | Rejected deliberately, as a product judgement rather than a technical one. "You fell behind your cohort this week" is mechanically trivial and would be genuinely harmful: an unsolicited push telling a working musician they are losing to their peers, on a platform whose thesis is that their work should accrue value. Benchmarking is a question the user asks when they choose to; it must never be a message they receive. The pair is technically easy and is rejected on values. |
| R-02 | 22.05.03 Sharing & Export | 22.05.04 Benchmarking | Rejected: a shared or exported report must **not** carry cohort comparisons. The cohort is computed from third parties' private data; exporting it hands that derived data to an uncontrolled recipient, and a shared "you're in the top 10% of your cohort" leaks a claim about people who never consented to be a denominator. The features may sit side by side in the UI; their outputs must not travel together. |
