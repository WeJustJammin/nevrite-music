# Feature: Peer Benchmarking & Cohort Comparison

> **Number**: 22.05.04
> **Parent**: [Cross-Source Dashboard & Reporting](./22.05-cross-source-dashboard-reporting-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

"Compared to what?" — the question that makes a number mean something. 40,000 monthly listeners is
good or bad only against a reference, and every artist supplies one anyway, badly, by comparing
themselves to whoever they happened to think of. This feature offers a defensible cohort instead of
an anxious guess.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their own numbers against an anonymised cohort of comparable artists — comparable by genre, career stage, catalog size and release cadence. Chooses when to look. Never told unprompted (CX `#R-01`). |
| Producer | Read-only | Can see a project's benchmark as context for a client conversation. **Producer-vs-producer benchmarking is deliberately not offered** — see Q-03; it is a different and more fraught product. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. A venue benchmarking against other venues is a real want and is domain 16/17's question, not this domain's — it is about rooms and bookings, not about audience data. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. |

## Behavior

A cohort is constituted from artists comparable on **stage and shape**, not on fame: genre, catalog
size, years active, release cadence, audience scale band. The comparison renders as a distribution the
user sits inside — a band, not a rank.

The privacy mechanism is doing all the work here and must be stated plainly: **the cohort is computed
from other users' private data**. Aggregation plus a k-anonymity floor is what makes that acceptable,
exactly as in 22.04.01 D-05. Below the floor, comparison is **suppressed, never widened** — silently
broadening a cohort to make a comparison possible produces a meaningless number the user will read as
meaningful.

Named-peer comparison ("you vs. that band") is **not offered**. It is what users would most want, and
it is computing and publishing a claim about identifiable third parties from data they gave us for a
different purpose.

### Happy Path

1. User opens their dashboard and chooses Compare.
2. System constitutes an anonymised cohort and states its basis and size: "142 artists, similar genre and catalog size, 2–5 years active".
3. Comparison renders as a distribution with the user's position inside it.
4. User adjusts the cohort basis within permitted dimensions, or closes it and never looks again.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cohort below the anonymity floor | **Suppressed**, not widened | "Not enough comparable artists to compare privately" |
| User is a genuine outlier (very niche genre) | No meaningful cohort exists | Suppressed, framed neutrally — not as a deficiency |
| User asks to compare with a named artist | Not offered | Explicit: "we don't compare you to named artists" — the refusal is the feature working |
| Cohort contains mostly inactive artists | Comparison flatters, meaninglessly | Cohort basis must include activity; otherwise "you're above average" means "you're above the dead" |
| User's numbers are far below cohort | The honest answer is discouraging | Neutral framing, pull-only, no push (22.05.02 DT-03) |
| Multi-hyphenate: which cohort? | Their solo work, their bands and their session work belong to different cohorts | Per-context comparison; a single cohort for a multi-hyphenate is incoherent |
| Session player with no artist identity | Cohort of *what*? | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the interesting case: 22.08's credit-linked cohort ("drummers with comparable credit graphs") may be the only defensible benchmark for the primary persona. See DT-03 |
| DSP terms forbid using their data to compute cross-user aggregates | Feature may be unlawful | `[PENDING]` — parent Q-01/Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Not yet used | Compare CTA on the dashboard |
| Loading | Cohort computing | Skeleton distribution |
| Populated | Cohort sufficient | Distribution + user's position + cohort basis and size |
| Suppressed | Below anonymity floor | Neutral suppression notice |
| Declined | No coherent cohort | Explicit, non-judgmental |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Benchmarking against **named peers** is the valuable version | What users actually ask for, and what incumbents partly offer | ❌ REJECTED: it means computing and publishing a claim about identifiable third parties from data they provided for a different purpose. Under GDPR that is a purpose-limitation and legitimate-interest problem with an unhappy answer, and under DSP terms it is likely a redistribution breach. It is also *culturally* corrosive in a way worth naming: WeJammin's thesis (D-18) is that a musician's work should accrue verifiable value. A feature whose core loop is "here's how you rank against people you know" converts that into a status game, and the platform's own data becomes the scoreboard. Anonymised cohorts keep the informational value and drop both the legal and the cultural liability. |
| DT-02 | The cohort must be constituted on **stage and shape, not fame** | Reasoning about what makes a comparison actionable | ✅ CONFIRMED. Comparing a working session musician to any recognisable artist produces despair and no information. The useful comparison is against artists at the same point doing the same kind of thing — same genre, same catalog size, same years active, same release cadence. That is also the comparison that yields an *action* ("artists like you release every 6 weeks; you release every 9 months") rather than a verdict. The distinction between an informative comparison and a demoralising one is entirely in cohort construction, which makes cohort construction the whole feature. |
| DT-03 | What would a domain expert expect that nobody mentioned? **The primary persona has no artist identity to benchmark.** | personas.md + reasoning from the domain's own structure | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it reframes the feature. The multi-hyphenate session drummer is not an artist with monthly listeners — they are a body of credits attached to other people's records. Conventional artist benchmarking simply **has no subject for them**, which means this feature as normally conceived serves everyone except the persona the platform is built for. The version that *would* serve them is credit-graph-native: *"drummers with comparable credit graphs work 3 sessions a month; you work 1"* or *"their credited catalog is growing faster than yours"*. That benchmark is computable **only** on WeJammin, because only WeJammin holds counter-attested credits (D-18) — and it is the same structural advantage 22.02.01 DT-01 found in matching. It also depends entirely on 22.08 existing. Recorded as the strongest future direction for this feature and a reason its `could` rating is about sequencing, not about worth. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Anonymised cohorts only**; named-peer comparison is not offered | DT-01. Legal exposure plus a status-game dynamic that contradicts the platform's thesis. |
| D-02 | Cohorts are constituted on **stage and shape, not fame** | DT-02. Fame-based comparison is demoralising and uninformative; stage-based comparison yields actions. |
| D-03 | Below the anonymity floor, comparison is **suppressed, never widened** | A widened cohort is a meaningless comparison the user will act on as if it were meaningful. |
| D-04 | Comparison is **pull-only** — never pushed | CX `#R-01` + 22.05.02 DT-03. An unsolicited "you're behind your peers" is harm with a schedule. |
| D-05 | Cohort output **never leaves** the platform | It is third parties' data; it never consented to travel. |
