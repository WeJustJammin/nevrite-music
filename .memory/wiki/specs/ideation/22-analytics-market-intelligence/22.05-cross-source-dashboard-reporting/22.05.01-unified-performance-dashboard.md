# Feature: Unified Performance Dashboard

> **Number**: 22.05.01
> **Parent**: [Cross-Source Dashboard & Reporting](./22.05-cross-source-dashboard-reporting-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

One timeline where every source sits on the same axis — streams, followers, views, placements, shows,
ticket sales — so the question the domain index names as the concrete pain becomes answerable:
*"is my career growing?"* Six dashboards cannot answer it, not because each is bad, but because the
answer only exists in the comparison none of them can make.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their whole picture on one axis: every connected source, every placement, every show, over any period. Configures what the view contains; drills into any series. For the multi-hyphenate this must span *all* their contexts at once — solo, two bands, session work — because "their career" is the union, and every existing tool models only one of them. |
| Producer | Full | Same machinery, scoped to their projects and their own releases. The client-facing angle matters: this is where they answer "how is the record doing?" without three logins and a spreadsheet. |
| Operator | Read-only | A cut-down view: their venue's own social reach (22.01.02) and aggregate market data for their catchment (22.04.01). They cannot configure the underlying series because most of it isn't theirs. The venue's booking and box-office numbers live in domains 17/19 — **not duplicated here**, because a second copy of an authoritative number is a second number. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Public artist stats a fan sees (monthly listeners on a profile) are a domain 01/20 surface reading this domain's data — the dashboard itself is a professional instrument. |

## Behavior

The dashboard's real work is **making incomparable things comparable without lying about it**. Streams,
followers, views and ticket sales have different units, cadences, lags and reliabilities. Putting them
on one axis is the value; pretending they are the same quantity is the trap.

Hard rules inherited and enforced here:
- Gaps are gaps, never zeros, never interpolated (22.01.03 D-01).
- Estimates are labelled estimates (22.03.01 D-03).
- Imported provenance is visible (22.01.04 D-02).
- Co-occurrence is described; causation is never asserted (parent D-02).

The last one has teeth. When a TikTok spike precedes a streaming spike by four days, the dashboard
draws both and says they happened. It does not say one caused the other, because nothing in the data
supports that — and the domain index's own framing ("the TikTok-spike-to-streaming-spike correlation
is only computable when both sit on one timeline") is a claim about **computability of correlation**,
not about causation. Sliding from one to the other is the easiest fabrication in this domain to commit
by accident.

### Happy Path

1. User opens Analytics.
2. Dashboard renders every connected source on one timeline for the default period, freshness stated.
3. Placements (22.03.01) and shows (17) appear as annotations against the curves.
4. User adjusts period, adds/removes series, drills into any point.
5. The view persists as a report definition — reused by digests (22.05.02) and shares (22.05.03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nothing connected | No data at all | Empty state routing to 22.01 — naming the loss, not the task: "see how the records you played on are doing" |
| One source gapped | That series breaks; others unaffected | Gap marker on the series, not a whole-dashboard error |
| All sources gapped | Dashboard is unknown, not zero | Whole-view banner; curves suppressed rather than drawn at zero |
| Sources with different lags | Rendered at their own recency | Per-series freshness; a common "as of" date across sources with 1-day and 45-day lags would be false |
| Multi-hyphenate with 3 contexts | All rendered; switchable and unionable | Context switcher — a single-artist model fails the primary persona outright |
| **Same person is Musician _and_ Producer on one release** | The release's streams count **once** in the union, not once per role | Release attributed to both credits in the drill-in, but its curve is added to the total a single time — a union is a set, not a sum. Double-counting a multi-hyphenate's own work inflates the exact number the platform exists to make trustworthy |
| Backfill in progress | Boundary drawn | "History loading to {date}" — not a flat line |
| Restated history | Series corrected, restatement disclosed | "Spotify revised 3–9 March down 4%" (22.01.03 D-04) |
| **Two series with wildly different scales** (e.g. 4M streams vs 800 ticket sales) | Co-plotted **rebased to indexed-100 at period start**; raw twin-axis co-plot is refused (D-06) | Both curves show % change from the period's first datapoint (=100), with a badge: "indexed to {start date} = 100". Absolute figures are read on drill-in, one series at a time on its own axis. Twin raw-value axes are never offered — sliding two axes is the easiest way to manufacture the visual correlation D-02 forbids |
| Fraudulent streams present | Shown until flagged, corrected after | Restatement, disclosed — and a fraud warning outranks the numbers (22.06) |
| Operator requests a series that is not theirs | Scoped out before the query, never rendered-then-hidden | Their read-only view simply omits it; a "hidden" row that leaks the metric's existence and shape is still a leak (D-05, 22.04.01 D-05) |
| Query times out / source API unreachable mid-load | Per-series failure isolated; the dashboard does not blank | Failed series shows retry + its last-good value **with the age stated** ("as of 6 days ago"); other series render normally. Never a silent stale number presented as current |
| Report definition edited on one device while rendering on another | Snapshot-at-render-start; the in-flight render completes against the definition it began with (CX-01 Q5) | The edit applies to the next render, not the current one — no half-old-half-new chart. On reopen, the newer definition wins (last-write) with no merge prompt, because a saved view is single-owner |
| Malicious / absurd input — 50-year date range, injection in a saved-view name, hand-crafted definition referencing a series the user cannot see | Bounded and validated at definition-save time | Date range clamped to the retention window with a notice; view names escaped and length-capped; a definition referencing an unauthorised series is rejected at save, not silently trimmed at render — so the scope is decided once, where it is auditable |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Query in flight | Skeleton chart, not a spinner over a blank page |
| Empty | No sources connected | Loss-framed empty state + connect CTA |
| Populated | Data present | Unified timeline with annotations and freshness |
| Partial | Some sources gapped/loading | Per-series honesty markers |
| Unknown | All sources dark | Suppressed curves + banner — explicitly not zero |
| Error | Query failed | Retry; last-good with its age stated |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The dashboard is the domain's `must` — it is the articulated pain | The domain index's own framing ("an artist logs into six dashboards and can never answer 'is my career growing?'") | ✅ CONFIRMED as `must`, but with a sharp caveat that changes what gets built. It is the **consolidation** half of D-18 — the daily reason to show up, the thing users can articulate wanting. It is also **entirely copyable**: Chartmetric, Soundcharts, Viberate and Songstats all ship exactly this, and several do it well. So the dashboard wins the user and does nothing to keep them. It is a `must` because without it the domain has no front door, *not* because it is defensible. The defensible content is 22.08's credit-linked view rendered inside this frame — which is D-18's structure exactly: the copyable thing is the platform, the uncopyable thing is the wedge, and they must ship together or neither works. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **the multi-hyphenate to break every existing dashboard**, and nobody said so | personas.md: "Any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer OR a seller?' fails them immediately" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a genuine differentiator hiding in a boring feature. Every incumbent dashboard models **one artist entity**. The primary persona is a session drummer in two bands who also mixes — their "career" is the *union* of several artist identities plus a body of session credits attached to none of them. No existing tool can render that; not because it's hard, but because their data model has one artist at the root. WeJammin's identity model (domain 01, multi-hyphenate-native per D-19 and Q-04) makes it natural. **This is a case where the persona model, not the analytics, is the moat** — and it should shape the dashboard's root object from day one, because retrofitting a union view onto a single-artist root is a rewrite. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with 22.06 and 22.01.03? | Cross-domain failure reasoning | ✅ CONFIRMED — an ordering problem that is easy to get wrong and expensive to fix. The dashboard is where good news and bad news collide: a streaming spike (good) may *be* artificial streaming (catastrophic — distributor takedown). If the dashboard renders the spike as a win and the fraud warning as a side note, the user celebrates the event that is about to remove their catalog. Likewise a gap: a flatline reads as failure and may be a dead token. **Honesty markers and fraud warnings must outrank the numbers in visual hierarchy, not annotate them** — which is a layout consequence of a data-integrity principle, and exactly the kind of thing that gets lost between an ideation doc and an FE spec. Recorded here so it survives the trip. |
| DT-04 | Co-plotting series of wildly different magnitude needs an honest default — dual axes, indexed-to-100, or refusal | Q-01; honest-dataviz reasoning against D-02 | ✅ CONFIRMED as **indexed-to-100 at period start**, with dual raw-value axes explicitly banned (→ D-06). The dashboard's question — *"is my career growing?"* — is intrinsically a rate-of-change question, and "% change since the period start" is a **genuine shared unit** across streams, followers, views and tickets, not a manufactured one. Twin raw-value axes are the classic lie: the analyst can slide two independent scales until any two unrelated curves appear to track, which is precisely the visual causation D-02 forbids. Refusing to co-plot at all was rejected — it kills the one comparison the feature exists to enable. Absolute values remain reachable on single-series drill-in, where one axis cannot lie about a relationship because there is no second series to relate it to. |
| DT-05 | The dashboard should ship an AI-written "insight of the week" / natural-language summary of what changed | Obvious product-analytics convention (every incumbent teases this) | ❌ REJECTED. A generated narrative over co-occurring series will, to be readable, assert relationships the data does not support — "your TikTok push drove streams up 12%." That is causation-from-co-occurrence (D-02) generated at scale and stated in confident prose, i.e. the platform manufacturing the exact fabrication D-18 says it exists to end, in its own flagship view. The honest version of "what changed" is the annotated timeline the feature already draws; a paragraph that reads well is worth less than a chart that cannot lie. If a summary ships later it may only restate observed magnitudes and freshness ("streams +12% w/w; TikTok +40% w/w; both on 8 Mar"), never a connective verb between two series. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The root object is the **multi-hyphenate identity**, not an artist entity | DT-02. A single-artist root fails the primary persona and cannot be retrofitted without a rewrite. |
| D-02 | **Co-occurrence is described; causation is never asserted** | Nothing in the data supports causal claims. Asserting one would fabricate the exact class of fact D-18 says the platform exists to end — in our own flagship view. |
| D-03 | Honesty markers and fraud warnings **outrank numbers** in visual hierarchy | DT-03. A win rendered above the warning that it is fraud is worse than no dashboard. |
| D-04 | Per-series freshness, **never a single "as of" date** | Sources lag from 1 to 45 days. One timestamp across them is false for all but one. |
| D-05 | Venue booking/box-office numbers are **not duplicated** into the Operator's view | Domains 17/19 are authoritative. A second copy is a second number that will disagree. |
| D-06 | Series of different magnitude are co-plotted **indexed-to-100 at period start**; **dual raw-value axes are banned** | DT-04. "% change since period start" is a genuine shared unit for a growth question; twin axes let scale manufacture a correlation, which is D-02's fabrication in chart form. Absolute values live on single-series drill-in. |
| D-07 | The union across a multi-hyphenate's contexts is a **set, not a sum** — a work they hold in two roles counts once | Edge case. Double-counting a person's own release across their Musician and Producer credits inflates the very number this feature exists to make trustworthy. |
| D-08 | **No generated causal narrative / "insight of the week"** — summaries may only restate observed magnitudes, never a connective verb between two series | DT-05. Prose is where causation-from-co-occurrence sneaks back in at scale; the annotated timeline is the honest form of "what changed". |
