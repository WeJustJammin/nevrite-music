# Feature: Alert Rules & Scheduled Digests

> **Number**: 22.05.02
> **Parent**: [Cross-Source Dashboard & Reporting](./22.05-cross-source-dashboard-reporting-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Being told without looking: event-triggered alerts (a playlist add, a removal, a chart entry, a broken
connection) and scheduled digests (your week, your month). For most users this will be their **only**
regular contact with domain 22 — nobody opens an analytics dashboard weekly, but everyone reads an
email — which makes the digest, not the dashboard, the domain's real interface.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets rules on their own series; receives digests on a cadence they choose; mutes or tunes anything noisy. The high-value alerts are narrow: playlist add/removal, a broken connection, a chart entry, a fraud warning. |
| Producer | Full | Same, scoped to projects. Their distinctive case is client-facing: a monthly digest to a client about the record they made, which is professional deliverable rather than self-tracking. |
| Operator | Config | Configures digests for their venue's own reach and catchment market data; cannot set rules on data that isn't theirs. Realistically a low-volume user here — a venue's operational alerts (bookings, deposits) live in domains 16/17, and duplicating them would be worse than useless. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Fans do receive gig alerts (D-11, domain 20) — a different mechanism for a different purpose, and deliberately not this one. Confusing the two would put professional analytics plumbing on a consumer surface. |

## Behavior

Alerts and digests are **one feature** (parent D-01) because they share the only resource that matters
here: the user's tolerance. Every message spends from a finite budget, and once it is spent the user
mutes the channel — at which point the fraud warning that would have saved their catalog goes unread
too. The noise budget is therefore not a nice-to-have; it is the feature's central constraint, and it
can only be enforced where all messages are composed together.

Rules inherited and enforced:
- Transient failures never alert (22.01.02 D-01).
- Placement quality is classified **before** alerting — never celebrate the bot-playlist add that is
  about to get the user taken down (22.03.01 D-04).
- A digest is the **same computation** as the dashboard, not a parallel one (CX-01).
- A digest renders at the **recipient's** permission level, not the owner's (CX-01 Q3, CX-04).
- Gaps travel into the digest (parent D-03) — an incomplete week says so in its header.

### Happy Path

1. User sets a rule ("tell me about playlist adds and removals") or accepts sensible defaults.
2. A qualifying event occurs; quality/fraud classification passes.
3. Alert delivers via the platform's notification cross-cut: "Added to Fresh Finds — 412k followers".
4. Separately, a weekly digest renders the user's report definition and delivers it with gaps stated.
5. Anything noisy is muted or tuned from inside the message itself, not from a settings page.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nothing happened this week | Digest still sends? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. A weekly "nothing happened" is honest and demoralising for the primary persona; suppressing it hides genuine stagnation. A real product tension, flagged not guessed |
| Rule matches 200 times in a day | Batched, never fired 200 times | One roll-up; per-rule rate limiting is mandatory |
| Bot-playlist add | **Not** alerted as a win | Fraud warning instead (22.03.01 D-04) |
| Broken connection | Alerted — user-fixable and consequential | "Spotify disconnected — 11 days missing" + reconnect |
| Rate-limited source | **Not** alerted | Silence. Transient, self-healing, not the user's problem |
| Digest render fails | **No digest sent** | Nothing. An empty digest reads as "nothing happened" — a lie by omission |
| Report definition deleted, digest references it | Digest disabled, not silently broken | "Your weekly digest was turned off — the report it used was deleted" |
| Delegated recipient's permissions shrink | Digest re-renders at the new lower level | Recipient sees less; no leak. The alternative is a data leak with a schedule |
| Bad news only (numbers falling) | Reported plainly | Neutral framing. See DT-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No rules set | Defaults offered, not an empty configuration screen |
| Active | Rules set, digest scheduled | Rule list + next digest date |
| Suppressed | Rule over budget | "This rule fired 40 times — batching it" |
| Disabled | Underlying definition gone | Explicit, with the reason |
| Failed | Delivery failed | Retry; visible in-app regardless — the in-app record never depends on email |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Alerts and digests are two features | The sweep's implied split ("alerting" and "automated digests" as separate capabilities) | ❌ REJECTED: they are two deliveries of one thing, and — decisively — they share a **noise budget**. Specified separately, each is individually reasonable and collectively fatal: five sensible alert rules plus a weekly digest plus a monthly digest is enough to get the whole channel muted. Once muted, the fraud warning (22.06) and the broken-connection alert (22.01.03) — the two messages with real money attached — go unread. The budget is only enforceable where every message is composed in one place. This is the clearest case in the domain where the correct classification is driven by a shared *constraint* rather than shared data. |
| DT-02 | The digest is the domain's **actual interface**, not the dashboard | Reasoning about real user behavior vs. designed intent | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it reorders the domain's priorities. The persona is a working musician juggling three roles and a portfolio of small income streams (personas.md) — they will not habitually open an analytics dashboard, and the honest expectation is that most never open it twice. They will read an email. So the digest is where this domain lives or dies, and 22.05.01's dashboard is mostly the place you go **after** a digest told you something worth investigating. That argues the digest deserves at least equal design investment, and that a digest which is merely a link to the dashboard has failed — the answer must be *in* the message. |
| DT-03 | What breaks at the intersection with the persona? **This feature is emotionally loaded in a way the others aren't.** | personas.md + reasoning about push vs pull | ✅ CONFIRMED: pull surfaces are asked for; push surfaces arrive uninvited. A weekly email telling a working musician their numbers fell — unprompted, on a Monday — is a materially different act from that same fact sitting on a dashboard they chose to open. This is why `#R-01` in the CX file rejects benchmark alerts on values grounds, why 22.04.03 Q-03 hesitates over unprompted null results, and why "nothing happened this week" is an unresolved product tension above. The general principle: **push amplifies whatever it carries, including harm**, and this domain's data is frequently unflattering. Restraint here is a product requirement, not a preference. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | One feature, one **noise budget** | DT-01. Independently-reasonable rules collectively mute the channel, taking the fraud warning with them. |
| D-02 | A failed render sends **nothing** | An empty digest reads as "nothing happened" — a lie by omission. |
| D-03 | The answer is **in the message**, not behind a link | DT-02. A digest that requires a click has failed the persona it exists for. |
| D-04 | Push is **restrained by default**; unflattering facts are pull-first | DT-03. Push amplifies whatever it carries, and this domain's data is often unwelcome. |
| D-05 | Digests render at the **recipient's** permission level | Otherwise it is a data leak on a schedule. |
