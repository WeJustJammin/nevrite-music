# Feature: Scouted-Artist Visibility & Consent

> **Number**: 22.07.04
> **Parent**: [A&R Scouting Signals & Watchlists](./22.07-ar-scouting-watchlists-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The subject's end of scouting: whether you can be found, watched and signalled on at all, what a scout
sees when they find you, and whether you're told any of it is happening. This is not a settings page
bolted onto three finished features — it is a **gate on all of them** (parent D-02). If any scouting
ships without it, WeJammin has built surveillance of its own primary persona and handed it to their
counterparties.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Controls whether they appear in scouting search, what a scout sees (which metrics, which credits), and whether they're notified when watched. **This is the persona the feature exists to protect** — they are the most-scouted and the least powerful party in every scouting relationship. |
| Producer | Config | Same controls, same exposure. A producer is scouted for their work as often as they scout for artists — the symmetry is real here. |
| Operator | Config | Their venue's visibility to artists prospecting rooms (22.04.02's inverse). A different exposure — a business's, not a person's — and correspondingly lower stakes, but the same mechanism. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Fans are not scoutable, are not in the professional index, and have no exposure here. Explicit absence, and an important one: this is the boundary that stops professional evaluation machinery from reaching a consumer population. |

## Behavior

Consent is a **gate evaluated at query and fire time** (CX-03), never cached, and revocation
**cascades**: removed from search indexes, watchlist entries degrade to tombstones (22.07.01 D-03),
signals suppress (22.07.02 D-02). A revocation that only stops future indexing while existing
watchlists keep working is not a revocation — it is a settings toggle that does nothing.

The unresolved design fork, stated honestly because it is genuinely hard:

**Default on or default off?**
- *Default off* is the privacy-respecting choice and makes scouting nearly empty — which may be the
  correct outcome (see 22.07.03 DT-02: the consented version already exists in domain 05).
- *Default on* makes scouting work and makes our primary persona surveillable by default, on data they
  gave us to protect themselves.

There is a third framing that may dissolve the fork: **scouting visibility could be an opt-in that
users have an active reason to want**. A session player who is invisible to every A&R tool in
existence (22.07.02 DT-02) may *want* to be findable — findability is how they get the next job (D-18:
accumulated proof wins the next job). If so, the honest design is opt-in with a clear benefit
statement, not a default at all. That is the recommendation, and it is Q-01.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User revokes | Cascade: search index, watchlists, signals | Confirmation naming what actually stops — vague reassurance here is worthless |
| Non-user artist | No subject to ask | `[PENDING]` — 22.07.01 Q-02. If we scout non-users on external data, consent is a fiction for everyone outside the platform, and that undermines the feature's whole claim |
| User asks "who is watching me?" | The sharpest question in the sub-domain | `[PENDING]` — see DT-02 |
| Consent off, but the artist lists on domain 05 | They *want* to be found — for hire | Not a contradiction: 05's listing is consent for a specific purpose. Consent is per-purpose, not global. See D-03 |
| Org-shared watchlist, user revokes | Must cascade to every org member | Revocation cannot be per-scout; it is per-subject |
| Consent off makes user invisible to a collaborator | Collateral cost of the gate | Purpose-scoping (D-03) is what prevents "hide from labels" from meaning "hide from friends" |
| Minor / under-18 user | Scouting a child | `[PENDING]` — D-13 flags age assurance as a live statutory duty. Default-off for minors is the only defensible position, and it may be legally required |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not set | New user | Depends on Q-01 — the default *is* the decision |
| Visible | Consented | What a scout sees, previewable from their side |
| Limited | Partial consent | Per-purpose scope shown |
| Hidden | Revoked | Confirmation of what stopped |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a privacy settings page and belongs with account settings (domain 01) | The obvious classification, and it is wrong in a way that matters | ❌ REJECTED: a settings page is a **record of a preference**; this is a **gate in a query path**. Its correctness lives in 22.07.03's index scoping and 22.07.02's fire-time evaluation — it is enforcement, not configuration. Modelling it as a settings page in domain 01 would produce the classic failure: a toggle that writes a flag nobody reads, while the search index — built before the flag existed — keeps returning the user. The *control surface* may well render in domain 01's settings; the **feature** is the enforcement, and it must be owned where the enforcement happens. |
| DT-02 | Users should be told when they're watched | The transparency instinct, and it is not obviously right | ⚠️ UNRESOLVED — argued both ways because both are strong, and this is the sub-domain's sharpest question. **For**: it is *their* data; being evaluated in secret by parties with power over your career is precisely the asymmetry a platform claiming to serve musicians should refuse to build. **Against**: it destroys the feature's utility — no scout will use a tool that announces their interest to their target, so scouting migrates off-platform where the artist gets no visibility *and* no notification, leaving them worse off. There is also a real harm in telling: "a label is watching you" creates hope that is usually unfounded, and a working musician does not need more of that. **A middle path worth testing at Step 5**: tell the subject the *aggregate* ("4 people are watching you") without identity — preserving the scout's discretion while ending the secrecy. Recorded unresolved because a confident answer here would be a fabrication, and this is a values decision the owner must make. |
| DT-03 | What breaks that nobody mentioned? **Consent is per-purpose, not global — and the platform's own structure proves it.** | Reasoning from 22.07.03 DT-02 + the 05 overlap | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it resolves an apparent contradiction. An artist listing on domain 05's services marketplace has consented to being found **for hire**. That is not consent to being watched by a label, tracked for momentum, or ranked in a scouting list. One global "discoverable" flag would conflate them and, in practice, would be set to on by the same user who would refuse each specific purpose if asked. Conversely a single "hide me" would break their livelihood on 05 — the thing they actively want. So consent must be **scoped by purpose**: findable-for-hire (05), findable-for-collaboration (03/04), watchable-for-evaluation (22.07). This is more work and it is the only coherent model, and it is also the design that lets a user hide from labels without hiding from friends. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | This is a **gate**, not a settings page | DT-01. Enforcement in the query path; a flag nobody reads is the classic failure. |
| D-02 | Revocation **cascades** — index, watchlists, signals | Anything less is a toggle that does nothing while feeling like it did something. |
| D-03 | Consent is **per-purpose**, never global | DT-03. Findable-for-hire ≠ watchable-for-evaluation. A global flag would either break livelihoods or launder consent. |
| D-04 | Consent is evaluated at **query/fire time**, never cached | A cached consent decision is a leak with a TTL. |
| D-05 | Minors are **default-off**, pending legal review | D-13's age assurance duty; scouting a child is not a thing to get wrong once. |
