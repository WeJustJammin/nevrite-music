# Feature: Artist Watchlists

> **Number**: 22.07.01
> **Parent**: [A&R Scouting Signals & Watchlists](./22.07-ar-scouting-watchlists-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Standing lists of artists you're keeping an eye on, so that momentum signals (22.07.02) have a scope
and a one-off search becomes a durable interest. Structurally simple; the difficulty is entirely at
the other end — the people on the list are our users, and they did not put themselves there.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Watches potential collaborators, peers, the band they want to tour with. The thesis-aligned use (parent D-03): a working musician tracking people they might actually work with. Subject to 22.07.04 on the other side — they are also watchable. |
| Producer | Full | Watches artists they might work with, or have worked with. In an org context this is the closest thing to the A&R case (see the parent's persona mapping and Q-01). |
| Operator | Full | Watches artists they might book — a genuinely different motive, and one the platform serves well because it holds venue and audience data together (22.04.02). "Who's growing near me" is the Operator's real query. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. Fans follow artists (domain 20) — a different mechanism with a different meaning: following is public affection, watching is private evaluation. Merging them would be a category error with a privacy consequence. |

## Behavior

A watchlist is a named set of artist identities with an optional note. Entries reference identities;
they never snapshot data. That reference-not-snapshot rule is what makes consent revocation
mechanically possible (CX-03) — a snapshot would preserve exactly the data the subject withdrew.

Every read is **consent-scoped at read time**, never cached (CX-03 Q5). An entry whose subject has
revoked degrades to a tombstone — the scout knows the artist exists and that they are no longer
visible, which is the minimum honest state. It does not silently vanish (which would be confusing)
and it does not keep working (which would be a leak).

### Happy Path

1. User finds an artist (via 22.07.03, or directly).
2. Adds them to a watchlist with an optional note ("saw them support X, great drummer").
3. Watchlist shows each artist's visible-to-them metrics, consent-scoped.
4. Momentum signals (22.07.02) fire against the list.
5. User acts — reaches out (domain 03/04), books (17), or removes them.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A watchlist is a trivial CRUD feature | Its apparent simplicity — it is a list of references | ❌ REJECTED: the CRUD is trivial and it is not the feature. The feature is a **standing claim on another person's data**, and every hard property lives at the subject's end: consent evaluated at read time, revocation cascading to a tombstone, references rather than snapshots, caps against bulk surveillance. Specified as CRUD, it would ship as a permanent snapshot cache that survives revocation — i.e. the exact thing consent is supposed to prevent, implemented by accident. The subject's rights are the spec; the list is an implementation detail. |
| DT-02 | What breaks that nobody mentioned? **Watching is not following, and conflating them would be a privacy failure.** | Reasoning against domain 20's fan-follow mechanism | ✅ CONFIRMED. Domain 20's follow is public affection — the artist wants followers, the count is a trophy, and it is mutual in spirit. A watchlist is **private professional evaluation**: the subject usually doesn't know, the watcher doesn't want them to, and the purpose is assessment rather than support. Same verb in English, opposite social contracts. If the data models merge (a plausible engineering economy — both are "user X is interested in artist Y"), then either follows become secretly private or watches become publicly visible, and both outcomes are wrong. **They must be separate entities from day one**, and that is a decision that has to be made before domain 20 or 22 is specced, not after. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Entries are **references, never snapshots** | DT-01. A snapshot preserves exactly the data a revocation withdraws, making consent theatre. |
| D-02 | Consent is evaluated at **read time**, never cached | A cached consent decision is a leak with a TTL. |
| D-03 | Revocation produces a **tombstone**, not a silent disappearance | The scout learns access ended; they learn nothing new about the subject. Silence would be confusing; continued access would be a breach. |
| D-04 | **Watching and following are separate entities** | DT-02. Merging them makes follows secretly private or watches publicly visible — both wrong, and unfixable later. |
