## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Discovery Search ↔ Watchlists

**Relationship**: Search finds candidates against criteria; adding one to a watchlist converts a
one-off query into a standing interest. Data flows one way — a watchlist entry references an artist
identity and never mutates search. The pair is what distinguishes a scouting tool from an address
book: without search, a watchlist can only contain artists you already knew, which is the population
that needs no tool.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: searching for collaborators — the thesis-aligned use (parent D-03).
- **Producer**: searching for artists to work with; in an org context, the closest thing to the A&R case.
- **Operator**: searching for artists to book — a genuinely different query ("who draws near me") that shares the machinery. See 22.04.02's Operator view, which is the same question asked geographically.
- **Fan**: no access. Fan-facing artist discovery exists (domains 20/21) and is a **different product** — consumer discovery, not professional evaluation. Merging them would put professional scouting data on a consumer surface.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none. Watchlists reference identities; search is stateless.
2. **Trigger chain**: search → add → watch. If the watched artist later revokes consent (22.07.04), the entry must **degrade or drop**, not persist as a stale snapshot of data they withdrew. This is where the gate has real mechanics.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes, and it is the whole design — every result is filtered by the subject's consent state before it is a result at all. Filtering at render is too late; the query itself must be consent-scoped.
4. **Notification fan-out**: does the scouted artist learn they were added to a watchlist? See 22.07.04 — this is the sub-domain's sharpest single question.
5. **State transition conflict**: consent revoked mid-session. The watchlist must reflect it on next read; caching consent state is a latent leak.

### CX-02: Watchlists ↔ Momentum Signals

**Relationship**: The watchlist scopes the signal. Momentum computed across every artist in the index
is a firehose nobody reads; computed across the twelve artists you actually care about, it is the
product. The watchlist is therefore not a convenience — it is the thing that makes signals tractable.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: "this collaborator is breaking out" — a prompt to reach out.
- **Operator**: "this artist is growing in my catchment" — a prompt to book. The Operator's signal is inherently geographic, which is 22.04's machinery, not 22.05.04's cohort machinery.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none. Signals are derived; watchlists are the scope parameter.
2. **Trigger chain**: signal computed → matched against watchlists → alert. Shares 22.05.02's noise budget — a scouting alert and an analytics alert spend the same user tolerance, and the budget is enforced at composition (22.05.02 D-01), not here.
3. **Permission intersection**: a signal about a non-consenting artist must not fire, even if they were watched *before* revoking. Consent is evaluated at fire time, not at watch time.
4. **Notification fan-out**: the scout is notified. The subject is not — and whether that asymmetry is acceptable is 22.07.04's core question.
5. **State transition conflict**: an artist revoking consent while a signal is in flight. Evaluate at fire time; do not pre-compute recipient lists.

### CX-03: Visibility & Consent → all three (gate)

**Relationship**: Not an interaction — a **constraint**. Consent determines whether an artist appears
in search at all, may be watched, and may generate signals. It is modelled as a child because it needs
its own Role Lens and behavior, but structurally it is a gate on the other three (parent D-02).

**Role scoping**: every persona is on **both sides**. A Musician scouting collaborators is also a
Musician who may be scouted. This symmetry is the sub-domain's saving grace — it is not a tool one
class uses on another, provided D-03's peer-facing framing holds. The moment it becomes label-facing,
the symmetry breaks and it *is* one class watching another.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: consent state is owned by the subject and read by all three. Single writer, many readers.
2. **Trigger chain**: revocation must **cascade** — remove from search, degrade watchlist entries, suppress signals. A revocation that only stops future indexing while leaving existing watchlists intact is not a revocation.
3. **Permission intersection**: this **is** the permission model for the sub-domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: does revocation notify the scouts who were watching? Telling them is itself a disclosure ("someone stopped letting you watch them"); not telling them leaves a silently-dead watchlist. `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: consent evaluated at **fire/read time**, never cached. A cached consent decision is a leak with a TTL.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 22.07.03 Discovery Search | 22.05.04 Peer Benchmarking | Rejected as a pair despite sharing a computation — this is the sub-domain's most important rejection. Both need "artists comparable to X". But cohort-for-benchmarking is **anonymised and inward** (you against a distribution, no individual named); scouting search is **identified and outward** (a list of named people). Wiring them together would let the anonymised cohort be de-anonymised by cross-referencing it against search — the classic aggregation attack, and it would silently break 22.05.04's k-anonymity floor. The **comparable-artist computation is a shared mechanism** (recorded as a cross-cut at domain level), and its two consumers must be kept apart by design, not merely by intention. |
| R-02 | 22.07.02 Momentum Signals | 22.06.01 Anomaly Detection | Rejected, though both detect unusual growth curves and the overlap is real. They differ in subject, purpose and consequence: 22.06.01 examines **your own** data to warn **you** of a risk you face; 22.07.02 examines **someone else's** data to inform **a third party** of an opportunity. Same maths, opposite ethics. Sharing an implementation is defensible; modelling them as an interacting pair would blur a boundary that must stay sharp — and would create an obvious path to "this artist's growth looks fake" being shown to the people evaluating them, which nobody has consented to and which D-03's "describe, never accuse" was written to prevent. |
