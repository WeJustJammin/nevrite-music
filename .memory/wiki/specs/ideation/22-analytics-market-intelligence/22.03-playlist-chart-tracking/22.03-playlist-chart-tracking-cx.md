## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Placement Tracking ↔ Curator Intelligence

**Relationship**: Placement events are the raw material; curator intelligence is the aggregate. For
each curator, the observable record is: what they added, when, and what happened to those tracks'
numbers afterward. That aggregate then annotates future placements ("added by a curator whose adds
typically lift streams 3–8× for two weeks"). It is a genuine feedback loop, and the loop is what
makes 22.03 a sub-domain rather than three files.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees both sides — their own placements, and who drove them.
- **Producer**: read-only. Useful context for a client's release, but not their lever to pull.
- **Operator**: no visibility. Playlists have no bearing on a room's calendar.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none. Placement events are the single source; curator aggregates are derived and recomputable. Curator intelligence never writes placements.
2. **Trigger chain**: placement observed → curator aggregate recomputed → future placements annotated. Async and idempotent. If aggregation fails, placements are still correct and complete — the loss is annotation, not fact. Correct failure direction.
3. **Permission intersection**: the sharp question is not permission but **visibility of others' data**: a curator's track record is computed from placements across many artists, most of whom are not our users. See DT-02 in 22.03.03 — this is where the privacy and ToS questions bite.
4. **Notification fan-out**: a placement event notifies the artist (22.05.02). A curator aggregate change notifies nobody — it is background context, and alerting on it would be noise.
5. **State transition conflict**: a placement removed and re-added inside one sync window may be observed as no event at all. Polling cannot see transitions it never sampled — an honest limit of the method, recorded rather than hidden. `[PENDING — Step 5]` for whether that matters.

### CX-02: Placement Tracking ↔ Chart Monitoring

**Relationship**: A big enough placement drives chart-eligible consumption, so a chart entry often
*follows* a placement by days. The correlation is real and worth surfacing on a timeline; the
causation is not provable by us, because chart methodologies are undisclosed by design and mix
sales, streams and airplay with weightings we cannot see.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees both on one timeline; draws their own conclusion.
- **Producer**: read-only.
- **Operator / Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Medium
confidence; synthesis deferred per the CX template's Medium/Low rule. The one commitment made now:
the system **describes co-occurrence and never asserts causation** (22.05.01 D-02).

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 22.03.02 Chart Monitoring | 22.03.03 Curator Intelligence | No relationship. Charts have no curators — they are computed from consumption, not chosen by a person. The instinct to connect them comes from both being "external things that rank music", which is a category error: one is a decision, the other is arithmetic. Modelling a "chart curator" would invent an actor who does not exist. |
| R-02 | 22.03.01 Placement Tracking | 22.01.02 Social Connection | Rejected: a TikTok "sound" is not a playlist placement, despite the surface similarity of "your track got picked up somewhere". No add/remove event, no curator, no position. Forcing them into one model would produce a placement record with no transitions — the exact thing D-01 says has no value. |
