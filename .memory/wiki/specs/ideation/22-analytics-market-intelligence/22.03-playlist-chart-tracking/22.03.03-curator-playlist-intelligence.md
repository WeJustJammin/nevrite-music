# Feature: Curator & Playlist Intelligence

> **Number**: 22.03.03
> **Parent**: [Playlist & Chart Tracking](./22.03-playlist-chart-tracking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Knowing *who* is behind a playlist and whether their adds actually do anything: a curator's observed
track record, a playlist's genre profile and turnover, and whether a placement is worth having at
all. This is the intelligence layer that turns a placement from an event into a judgement — and it
is also the feature that most needs restraint, because a curator is a person and our observations
about them are consequential.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Looks up a playlist or curator; sees observed track record (typical lift, retention, turnover, genre fit), reach quality, and fraud risk; uses it to decide whether a pitch is worth the effort or a placement is worth celebrating. |
| Producer | Read-only | Consumes the same intelligence as context for a client's release; not their decision to make. |
| Operator | None | Not rendered. |
| Fan | None | Never rendered. A fan is often *also* a curator — their playlist may appear in this data — but the intelligence surface is professional and one-directional. See DT-02, which is where this gets uncomfortable. |

## Behavior

Everything here is **descriptive, never predictive-as-fact** (parent D-02). The observable record:
which tracks a curator added, when, what those tracks' numbers did afterward, how long placements
survive, how much the playlist turns over, and what its genre profile looks like.

The derived judgements:
- **Reach quality** — followers are a weak proxy; a 400k-follower playlist with no listener
  engagement is worth less than a 20k one that people actually play.
- **Retention** — do adds stick, or is it a 48-hour revolving door?
- **Fraud risk** — is this a bot playlist? Hands off directly to 22.06.
- **Genre fit** — computed from the playlist's contents, not from its title.

### Happy Path

1. User is added to a playlist (22.03.01), or looks one up directly.
2. System shows the curator/playlist profile: reach quality, typical lift range, retention, turnover, fraud risk.
3. User reads the placement's likely worth in context: "adds here typically lift streams 2–4× for about 10 days".
4. User decides — celebrate, pitch again, or ignore.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Curator has too few observed adds | No track record computable | "Not enough history to say" — never a fabricated score from n=3 |
| Playlist is a bot farm | Fraud risk raised; handed to 22.06 | "This playlist shows artificial engagement patterns — a placement here may put your distribution at risk" |
| Editorial playlist (Spotify's own) | Curator is an institution, not a person | Institutional profile; no individual named. See D-03 |
| Curator's record is bad | The honest answer is unflattering to a real person | Neutral, factual framing only — observed numbers, no characterisation. See DT-02 |
| Playlist changes genre over time | Historic profile ≠ current | Profile is time-aware; a stale profile is a wrong recommendation |
| Curator asks us to remove their profile | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a GDPR/legitimate-interest question | `[PENDING]` |
| User treats "typical lift" as a promise | The known misreading | Ranges and sample sizes always shown; never a single number, which reads as a forecast |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Profile computing | Skeleton |
| Empty | Playlist not indexed | "We don't have data on this playlist" |
| Populated | Sufficient observations | Profile with ranges + sample sizes |
| Partial | Thin data | Explicit low-confidence framing, not a hedged number |
| Flagged | Fraud risk detected | Warning, prominent, ahead of any positive metric |

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |

| DT-01 | Curator intelligence is the incumbents' actual moat, and their UI is not | Competitive reasoning about Chartmetric/Soundcharts | ✅ CONFIRMED: their defensibility is an accumulated index of playlists and curators built over years, not the dashboard on top of it. A new entrant cannot replicate it quickly and probably should not try — which is why parent Q-01 (build/license/narrow) is a genuine strategic fork, not a build detail. **Note the tension with D-18**: WeJammin's thesis says its moat is provenance captured at source, not scraped external data. Competing on someone else's moat is off-thesis by construction; licensing it is on-thesis (buy the commodity, build the differentiator). This reasoning is why 22.03.03 is proposed `could` rather than `should`. |
| DT-02 | What breaks here that nobody mentioned? **The curator is a person, and this is a dossier on them.** | Reasoning about who is on the other side of the data | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the feature's real risk. "This curator's adds don't move anything" is a factual observation with reputational consequence for a named individual — frequently an unpaid hobbyist, sometimes a Fan persona (personas.md notes the boundary is porous). We would be computing a performance rating on private individuals from third-party data they never gave us, and showing it to people who might act on it. That is a GDPR question (legitimate interest, right to object), a defamation question, and a values question — and it is *not* symmetrical with the rest of the platform, where every subject of a record is a party to it. Escalated to `/create-prd-security` and recorded in `notProduct` routing. |
| DT-03 | This turns WeJammin into a pitching platform | Adjacency reasoning (parent Q-03) | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED: intelligence about curators is one short step from *contacting* curators, which is SubmitHub's business — and that business has a structural pay-to-play problem that would sit badly beside a provenance-and-honesty thesis. The step is not forced: knowing whether a placement was worth having is analytics; brokering the placement is promotion (domain 21). Recommend the boundary hold, and flag it because the pull toward monetising the index will be strong. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Ranges and sample sizes, **never a single score** | A single number reads as a forecast and invites a lawsuit-shaped misunderstanding. A range with n=7 attached is honest and self-limiting. |
| D-02 | Fraud risk is rendered **above** any positive metric | A user who reads "400k followers" before "bot farm" has already made the wrong decision. Order is the design. |
| D-03 | Individual curators are profiled **only where they are publicly acting as curators**; institutional playlists name no individuals | DT-02. Minimises the dossier-on-a-private-person problem without pretending it away. |
