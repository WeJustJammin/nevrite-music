## Overview

Watching the placements that move streams: playlist adds, positions and removals; chart entries and
positions; and the curators behind them. Placement is the dominant exogenous driver of a streaming
curve — a track's numbers usually move because a playlist moved, not because the world changed its
mind — so a timeline without placement data can show *what* happened but never *why*.

**Why this is a sub-domain (2+ interacting capabilities)**: placement tracking, chart monitoring and
curator intelligence are distinct capabilities that interact. A removal is meaningless except against
the placement history that preceded it; curator intelligence is *derived* from accumulated placement
history and then feeds back to explain the next placement. Chart monitoring is the independent-ish
sibling — kept here because a chart entry and a playlist add are the same question ("what external
thing just moved my numbers?") asked of two different systems.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Placement is modelled as an **event stream**, not a current-state list | "You are on 14 playlists" is nearly useless; "you were added to Fresh Finds on 3 March and removed on 2 April, and here is what your streams did on both days" is the product. The value is entirely in the transitions. | Agent, Deep Think, 2026-07-16 |
| D-02 | Curator intelligence is **descriptive, never predictive-as-fact** | Claiming "this curator will add you" is a promise we cannot keep and a defamation risk we cannot defend. Track record is an observation; likelihood is a guess. | Agent, 2026-07-16 |
| D-03 | Editorial, algorithmic and user playlists are **distinct classes**, never one list | They differ in how you get on them, what a removal means, and whether a human decision was involved. Merging them produces advice that is wrong for at least two of the three. | Agent, Deep Think, 2026-07-16 |
