# Feature: Claim Inbox & Suggested Claims

> **Number**: 02.03.02
> **Parent**: [Claiming & Cold-Start Seeding](./02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The surface that tells a human "we think this work is yours" and lets them take possession of it in one act. It is fed by two streams that look different but are the same thing: credits created *about* a non-user by a Producer's roll call (`02.02.01`), and credits created *about* them by someone's catalog import (`02.03.01`). Both leave a named professional with a real page and real work on it, waiting.

This is the second half of the platform's only self-reinforcing loop (`02.01.02` DT-01): capture → page → claim → the claimant captures their own sessions → more shells. Sub-domain D-03 records it as an acquisition surface, not a data chore.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own inbox) | Arrives — often *because* they were told they were credited — and finds work already attributed to them, from people they know. Claims it in one act. The strongest possible onboarding: the product has already done something for them before they have done anything for it. Rejects what is not theirs. |
| Producer | Full (own inbox) | Same, and typically has more waiting: their name is on everything they ran, and other Producers' imports name them constantly. Claiming their back catalog is the fastest route to a credible page. |
| Operator | Full (room's inbox) | Claims the room's credits — sessions recorded there that someone else captured or imported. Requires proving control of the room, which is an Identity/venue-verification question (domain 01/16), not a credits one. |
| Fan | None | No inbox. A fan has no professional credits by definition; the boundary is porous (`personas.md` — a fan may be a hobbyist musician), and the answer is that claiming a credit is a professional act that makes you a Musician in that moment, not a Fan feature. |

## Behavior

The inbox's job is **conversion, not completeness**. A shell with forty unclaimed credits presented as forty decisions is a chore; the same forty presented as "here is your discography — is this you?" is a gift. The difference is framing, and it decides whether the growth loop turns.

Claiming is not free of consequence. A claim is an assertion of identity ("I am the John Smith on that record"), and its evidence tier is *not* the credit's tier — a claimed import stays an import. What claiming changes is **who the credit is attached to**, not **how well we know it**. Two people conflate these constantly and the UI must not: accepting a suggestion does not verify the work, it verifies the person.

The reach problem is the hard part and it is not solved here. A shell has no account, so it has no notification channel. The credit sits in an inbox nobody can open until the human independently arrives. In practice the delivery mechanism is the person who created the shell — the Producer telling the drummer "you're on WeJammin, go claim it" — which makes the roll call's social moment (`02.02.01`) the actual acquisition channel, not this inbox. The inbox is the *destination*; someone else has to be the invitation.

### Happy Path

1. A Producer's session or import names a non-user; a shell is created holding their credits
2. The human arrives — invited by the Producer, or by their own search, or by a claim suggestion after signing up
3. System matches them to shells (name, co-contributors, identifiers, overlap with their stated history) and presents suggestions
4. They confirm "this is me"; shells merge into their identity (`02.01.04`), credits attach, discography populates
5. Their now-real page is the thing that makes them stay — and their own sessions become the next shells

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody ever claims a shell | The common case, indefinitely. Credits stay attached to an unclaimed shell; the discography exists but belongs to nobody | Public shell page; "claim this profile" |
| Two people claim the same credit | Contested — routes to adjudication (`02.03.03`). The credit **stays attached to the first claimant** for the duration (`02.03.03` D-05); it detaches only if the contest reaches `Unresolvable`, and then returns to the unclaimed shell it came from (`02.03.03` D-06) | "Someone else has claimed this". The first claimant's public line is unchanged throughout (`02.01.02` D-06) |
| Claimant is not that person | Identity fraud. Detection rests on the people who created the credit — the Producer knows which John Smith was in the room | Contestable; corroboration sought from the session owner |
| Suggestions are wrong (common names) | Bad suggestions are worse than none — a user shown forty strangers' credits learns the product doesn't know them | High match floor; show few, show confident |
| Shell has embargoed credits (`02.01.05`) | Claim proceeds; embargo persists. Claiming does not publish | Claimed, still unpublished |
| Person wants their shell deleted, not claimed | Real request: someone who does not want to be on the platform at all. Currently unanswered (`02.02.01` Q-02) | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` |
| Claimed shell later proves to be someone else | Unclaim reverses the merge: credits detach from the wrong identity back to an unclaimed shell, the claimant's discography loses those entries, and a correction record is written so history shows the reassignment rather than silently rewriting itself. The true owner can then claim. Triggered by contest resolution (`02.03.03`) or the claimant self-correcting | "This work was reassigned"; corrected discography, entries removed |
| **Contest reaches `Unresolvable` — where does the credit render?** | Same destination as the unclaim row above: the credit **returns to the unclaimed shell it came from** (`02.03.03` D-06). Zero new state, zero new render mode — it is the pre-claim status quo, with the claim CTA back on the surface where the true owner would find it. **What an unclaimed shell publishes is still Q-02** and is not decided by this fallback; if shells publish little, the fallback collapses toward ledger-only in practice | The shell's public page with "claim this profile", exactly as before anyone claimed it |
| User accepts every suggestion to inflate their page | Claiming attaches identity only, never raises tier (D-01), so a padded page is padded with *visibly-unverified* imports; corroboration from session owners is what confers weight, and knowingly false claims are contestable identity fraud (domain 24) | Page fills, but claimed credits read as unverified until a Producer attests |
| Operator claims a room's inbox without proving control | Blocked — room claiming is a venue-control assertion, resolved by venue verification (domains 01/16), not by the credits act; credits stay on the room shell until control is proven | "Verify you manage this space to claim its credits" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Inbox opened; match engine computing suggestions | Skeleton list — "finding work that might be yours" |
| Unclaimed | Shell created by roll call or import | Public shell page with a claim CTA |
| Suggested | System matched a user to a shell | Suggestion in their inbox |
| Claimed | User confirmed identity | Credits attached; discography populated |
| Contested | Two claimants | **Stays attached to the first claimant** while adjudication runs (`02.03.03` D-05); both told; the contested marker is a record-view affordance for the parties, and the public discography line is unchanged and never hidden (`02.01.02` D-06). Reverses this row's earlier "Held; adjudication" reading, which detached the credit on filing. On `Unresolvable` it detaches and returns to the unclaimed shell (`02.03.03` D-02, D-06) |
| Rejected | User said "not me" | Permanent negative assertion (`02.01.04` D-03) — never re-suggested |
| Empty | No shells match | No inbox surface at all — never an empty-state chore |
| Error | Claim write or match query failed | "Couldn't complete that claim — retry"; the shell stays unclaimed and nothing is half-attached (the claim/merge is atomic, per `02.01.04`) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The claim inbox is the platform's primary acquisition channel | Sub-domain D-03; `02.02.01` DT-03; `02.01.02` DT-01 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED with a fatal caveat.** The economics are excellent — every session names 3–8 professionals who each arrive to a populated page. But **a shell has no notification channel**, so the inbox cannot invite anyone. It is a destination, not a channel. The actual invitation is the Producer saying "go claim it", which means the acquisition loop depends on a *social* act the platform can prompt but never perform. Any growth model that assumes the inbox does the acquiring is wrong, and would be discovered wrong only after building it. |
| DT-02 | Claiming a credit verifies it | Conflation the UI will invite constantly — "verify it's you" reads as "verify it's true" | ❌ **REJECTED** — claiming attaches a credit to a person; it says nothing about whether the credit is accurate. A claimed Discogs import is still a Discogs import (sub-domain D-01). If claiming raised the tier, every user could launder imported credits into verified ones by clicking "yes that's me", which is a one-click hole straight through the differentiator. Identity verification and fact verification are orthogonal, and this feature only does the first. |
| DT-03 | Aggressive suggestion maximises claims and therefore growth | Growth reflex; more suggestions, more conversion | ❌ **REJECTED** — bad suggestions are worse than no suggestions here, because the suggestion *is* the product's first demonstration of competence. A drummer shown forty credits belonging to three other John Smiths learns immediately that the graph does not know who anyone is — and that is a judgement about the whole product, formed in the first thirty seconds. Precision over recall, permanently. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Claiming verifies the person, never the credit | DT-02. Otherwise imported credits launder into verified ones with one click. |
| D-02 | Precision over recall on suggestions | DT-03. The suggestion is the product's first demonstration of competence, delivered before trust exists. |
| D-03 | Rejection writes a permanent negative assertion | Consistent with `02.01.04` D-03. Re-suggesting a rejected shell after every import run trains users to ignore the inbox. |
| D-04 | Framed as "here is your discography", never as a queue of decisions | Sub-domain D-03. Conversion is the metric; a chore does not convert. |
