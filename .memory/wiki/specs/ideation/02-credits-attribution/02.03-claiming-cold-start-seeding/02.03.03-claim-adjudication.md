# Feature: Claim Adjudication

> **Number**: 02.03.03
> **Parent**: [Claiming & Cold-Start Seeding](./02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

What happens when two people say the same credit is theirs. Rare in absolute terms and unavoidable in practice: common names, band members with overlapping roles, and the occasional person who simply claims a famous record. Distinct from a credit dispute (`02.05`) — nobody here disagrees about *what happened*, only about **which human it happened to**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (respond when contested) | Told their claim is contested; supplies distinguishing evidence — who else was on the session, when, where, what they played, who can vouch. Cannot see the other claimant's identity by default; that is an unresolved question with real harassment implications (Q-02). |
| Producer | Config (respond; corroborate as session owner) | Two roles here, and the second is decisive: if the credit came from a session they ran, **they know which John Smith was in the room**. Their corroboration is the resolution mechanism, not evidence to be weighed alongside others'. |
| Operator | Config (respond) | Can corroborate that a session happened in their room and, sometimes, who came through the door. Weaker than the Producer's knowledge but independent of both claimants. |
| Fan | None | No claiming, therefore no adjudication. |

## Behavior

The resolution principle: **ask the person who was there, not an adjudicator who wasn't.** A credit created by a session (`02.02`) has an owner who knows the answer with certainty, and asking them costs one message. An admin queue weighing two strangers' evidence is guesswork with a process around it — and it is exactly the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` names, re-imported into the platform's own operations.

That principle degrades gracefully by credit origin:

- **Session-captured credit** — the Producer knows. Ask them. Resolved.
- **Imported credit** — nobody on the platform knows. The source is a database entry typed by a stranger; neither claimant can be corroborated from within. This is the hard case, and it is the majority case.
- **Self-asserted credit claimed by two people** — no evidence exists on either side, by construction.

The uncomfortable conclusion: for imported credits, adjudication has **no reliable mechanism at all**. This is not a gap to be filled with a better process — it is a direct consequence of import being reconstruction (sub-domain D-01). The honest resolution for the unresolvable case is to leave the credit unattached rather than award it, because awarding a contested credit on weak evidence gives one person the other's career, permanently, on a guess.

### What happens to the attachment while the contest runs

**The credit stays attached to the first claimant until the contest closes; it detaches only on `Unresolvable`** (D-05). This **reverses** the earlier model, which held the credit unattached for the duration of the contest.

Both models reverse something, so the choice is which exposure to accept. Detaching on filing means a free, unilateral, evidence-free second claim strips a line from a working professional's public sales page (`02.01.02` DT-01), and no source in the corpus defines an abuse control for that act. Staying attached means a possibly-wrong line stays up for one corroboration round-trip — which `02.01.02` D-06 has already priced as "the status quo ante" for exactly this shape of harm, and which is short by construction when a corroborator exists.

The objection that used to defeat this model no longer holds: D-02's terminal lock means an unresolvable contest leaves the credit **unattached**, so pendency can no longer permanently award a credit to whoever clicked first.

Three constraints this does not touch:

- The contested marker is a **record-view affordance only**. There is no public marker and no public suppression — `02.01.02` D-06 and `02.01.01` D-11 already lock that, and D-03 below is corrected to match.
- **A contest is not published.** The public discography line is unchanged and never hidden while a contest runs.
- **`Unresolvable` still leaves the credit unattached** (D-02), and the credit then returns to the unclaimed shell it came from (D-06).

The **asymmetry this creates must be stated plainly**: the true owner watches a stranger carry their credit and has no unilateral control over it, because `02.01.05`'s Unlisted/Confidential controls are available to a party **named on the record**, not to a party who claims the record should have named them. That gap is Q-04.

### Happy Path

1. Second claimant claims a credit already claimed
2. The credit **stays attached to the first claimant** while contested (D-05); both claimants are told; the contested marker is a record-view affordance for the parties, never a public page state (`02.01.02` D-06)
3. System routes to the party who knows: the session owner (captured), or nobody (imported)
4. That party identifies the correct claimant; the credit attaches to them (a no-op where the first claimant was right); the other claim is rejected with a permanent negative assertion
5. Both claimants are told the outcome

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Imported credit, no corroborator exists | The majority case, and it has no clean answer. The credit reaches `Unresolvable` and **detaches**, returning to the unclaimed shell it came from (D-02, D-06) rather than being awarded on a guess | "Cannot be resolved — this credit was imported, not captured". The line reverts to the shell's page with its claim CTA |
| Both claimants are right (two players, both on the record, source recorded one credit) | Not a contest — a data error. The source under-recorded. Resolution is two credits, not one winner | Both credited |
| Corroborator (Producer) is gone | Falls back to the unresolvable path. **Nothing bounds how long that takes** — there is no corroboration SLA anywhere in the corpus, so the pendency window under D-05 is unbounded in the worst case (Q-05) | The credit stays with the first claimant until the fallback fires, then detaches to the shell |
| Corroborator is themselves a claimant | Cannot adjudicate their own claim. Falls back, with the same unbounded-pendency exposure (Q-05) | Escalated; on `Unresolvable`, detached to the shell |
| Claimant is a serial false claimant | Pattern is a Trust & Safety signal (24), not a credits problem. Adjudicating each instance separately misses the pattern entirely | Flagged to 24 |
| Contested credit is on a public discography | **The public line is unchanged and is never hidden** (`02.01.02` D-06; `02.01.01` D-11; credits-attribution CX-16). Corrects D-03's earlier suppression rule | Visitors: an unchanged line on the first claimant's page. Parties: the contested marker on the record view |
| The true owner is the *second* claimant and the credit is on a stranger's page | The exposure D-05 accepts. | Two real people with the same name, contest reaches `Unresolvable` | The credit returns to the unclaimed shell (D-06) — the pre-claim status quo, with the claim CTA on the surface where either could still claim it. | Claim contested years after attachment | Same path; the credit has by then accumulated downstream effects (royalty registration, reputation, traversal edges) that must unwind | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncontested | One claimant | Normal claim (`02.03.02`) |
| Contested | Second claim on a claimed credit | **Stays attached to the first claimant** (D-05); both told; contested marker on the record view for the parties only; the public discography line is unchanged and never hidden |
| Awaiting corroboration | Routed to the session owner | "Waiting on <Producer>" — attachment unchanged throughout; no SLA bounds this state (Q-05) |
| Resolved | Corroborator identified the claimant | Attached to that claimant (a no-op if it was already them); loser gets a permanent negative assertion |
| Unresolvable | No corroborator exists (import) | **Detaches** (D-02): the credit returns to the unclaimed shell it came from (D-06), permanently marked contested. No new render mode — this is the pre-claim status quo |
| Escalated | Fraud pattern suspected | Routed to 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Contested claims need an admin adjudication queue, like every other marketplace dispute | Standard platform operations; disputes get adjudicators | ❌ **REJECTED** — an admin weighing two strangers' evidence about a session they did not attend is *reconstruction*, the exact failure `problem-statement.md` identifies, imported into the platform's own operations. The platform's structural advantage is that it knows who was in the room; the correct mechanism is to **ask them**. Where it does not know (imports), no admin knows either, and a process that produces a confident answer anyway is producing a fabrication with a case number. Leaving it unattached is the honest outcome. |
| DT-02 | Claim adjudication is really the same thing as credit dispute (`02.05`) and should merge | Both are contested-credit flows with evidence and resolution | ❌ **REJECTED** — different disagreements with different resolution mechanisms. A dispute argues *what happened* ("I played that solo, not him") and is resolved by evidence from the session. A claim contest argues *who this person is* ("that John Smith is me") and is resolved by identity — the credit itself is not in question. They share the contested state and the suppression behavior, which is why the plumbing overlaps and the flows must not. Merging them would force identity questions through an evidence process that cannot answer them. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Resolution is by asking the party who was present — never by admin adjudication | DT-01. The platform's advantage is presence; adjudication throws it away and guesses. |
| D-02 | Unresolvable contests leave the credit unattached | Awarding on weak evidence hands one person the other's career on a guess. Unattached is honest and reversible; wrongly awarded is neither. |
| D-03 | ~~Contested credits are suppressed from public discography pending resolution~~ — **SUPERSEDED by `02.01.02` D-06 + `02.01.01` D-11 (credits-attribution CX-16), and by D-05 below** | The rule was written before the credit-graph reconciliation. A contest is an objection, not evidence: publishing or suppressing on a free unilateral filing lets the filing itself impose the sanction. The public discography line is therefore **unchanged and never hidden** while contested; the contested marker is a record-view affordance for the parties. Row retained rather than deleted so the reversal is auditable. |
| D-04 | Claim adjudication stays separate from credit disputes | DT-02. Identity questions cannot be answered by an evidence process built for factual ones. |
| D-05 | **The credit stays attached to the first claimant for the duration of a contest; it detaches only on `Unresolvable`** | Owner ratification of DQ-04.1. Reverses this file's earlier Happy Path step 2 and `02.03.02`'s Contested row, both of which held the credit unattached during pendency. Both models reverse ratified text, so the choice is which exposure to accept: detach-on-filing lets a free, unilateral, evidence-free claim strip a line from a professional's public sales page with no abuse control defined anywhere in the specs; stay-attached leaves a possibly-wrong line up for one corroboration round-trip, which `02.01.02` D-06 already prices as "the status quo ante". D-02's terminal lock removes the only serious objection to staying attached — pendency can no longer permanently award a credit to whoever clicked first. Known asymmetry: the true owner has no unilateral control over the line (Q-04), and pendency is unbounded where no corroborator exists (Q-05). |
| D-06 | **On `Unresolvable`, the credit returns to the unclaimed shell it came from** — the pre-claim status quo, not a new render mode | Owner ratification of DQ-04.2. The destination already exists: `02.03.02`'s unclaim row names exactly this trigger, and the shell's claim CTA is the surface where the true owner would find it (`02.03.02` Unclaimed row; `02.01.02` DT-01's acquisition loop). A party-side partial render would require a rule for shell pages that no source describes. **This decision explicitly does not answer `02.03.02` Q-02** — what an unclaimed shell publishes before anyone claims it remains owner-held and deferred to `/create-prd-security`; if shells publish little, this fallback collapses toward ledger-only in practice. |
