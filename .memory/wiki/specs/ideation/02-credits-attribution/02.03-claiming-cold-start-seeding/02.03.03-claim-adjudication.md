# Feature: Claim Adjudication

> **Number**: 02.03.03
> **Parent**: [Claiming & Cold-Start Seeding](./02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens when two people say the same credit is theirs. Rare in absolute terms and unavoidable in practice: common names, band members with overlapping roles, and the occasional person who simply claims a famous record. Distinct from a credit dispute (`02.05`) — nobody here disagrees about *what happened*, only about **which human it happened to**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (respond when contested) | Told their claim is contested; supplies distinguishing evidence — who else was on the session, when, where, what they played, who can vouch. Cannot see the other claimant's identity by default; that is an unresolved question with real harassment implications (Q-02). |
| Producer | Config (respond; corroborate as session owner) | Two roles here, and the second is decisive: if the credit came from a session they ran, **they know which John Smith was in the room**. Their corroboration is the resolution mechanism, not evidence to be weighed alongside others'. |
| Operator | Config (respond) | Can corroborate that a session happened in their room and, sometimes, who came through the door. Weaker than the Producer's knowledge but independent of both claimants. |
| Fan | None | No claiming, therefore no adjudication. |

## Behavior

The resolution principle: **ask the person who was there, not an adjudicator who wasn't.** A credit created by a session (`02.02`) has an owner who knows the answer with certainty, and asking them costs one message. An admin queue weighing two strangers' evidence is guesswork with a process around it — and it is exactly the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` names, re-imported into the platform's own operations.

That principle degrades gracefully by credit origin:

- **Session-captured credit** — the Producer knows. Ask them. Resolved.
- **Imported credit** — nobody on the platform knows. The source is a database entry typed by a stranger; neither claimant can be corroborated from within. This is the hard case, and it is the majority case.
- **Self-asserted credit claimed by two people** — no evidence exists on either side, by construction.

The uncomfortable conclusion: for imported credits, adjudication has **no reliable mechanism at all**. This is not a gap to be filled with a better process — it is a direct consequence of import being reconstruction (sub-domain D-01). The honest resolution for the unresolvable case is to leave the credit unattached rather than award it, because awarding a contested credit on weak evidence gives one person the other's career, permanently, on a guess.

### Happy Path

1. Second claimant claims a credit already claimed
2. System holds the credit — neither claimant gets it while contested
3. System routes to the party who knows: the session owner (captured), or nobody (imported)
4. That party identifies the correct claimant; the credit attaches; the other claim is rejected with a permanent negative assertion
5. Both claimants are told the outcome

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Imported credit, no corroborator exists | The majority case, and it has no clean answer. Credit stays unattached rather than being awarded on a guess | "Cannot be resolved — this credit was imported, not captured" |
| Both claimants are right (two players, both on the record, source recorded one credit) | Not a contest — a data error. The source under-recorded. Resolution is two credits, not one winner | Both credited |
| Corroborator (Producer) is gone | Falls back to the unresolvable path | Unattached |
| Corroborator is themselves a claimant | Cannot adjudicate their own claim. Falls back | Unattached; escalated |
| Claimant is a serial false claimant | Pattern is a Trust & Safety signal (24), not a credits problem. Adjudicating each instance separately misses the pattern entirely | Flagged to 24 |
| Contested credit is on a public discography | Suppressed while contested — the alternative is publishing a fact the platform knows may be wrong | Removed from both pages pending resolution |
| Claim contested years after attachment | Same path; the credit has by then accumulated downstream effects (royalty registration, reputation, traversal edges) that must unwind | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncontested | One claimant | Normal claim (`02.03.02`) |
| Contested | Second claim on a claimed credit | Held; both told; suppressed from public |
| Awaiting corroboration | Routed to the session owner | "Waiting on <Producer>" |
| Resolved | Corroborator identified the claimant | Attached; loser gets a permanent negative assertion |
| Unresolvable | No corroborator exists (import) | Unattached, permanently marked contested |
| Escalated | Fraud pattern suspected | Routed to 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Contested claims need an admin adjudication queue, like every other marketplace dispute | Standard platform operations; disputes get adjudicators | ❌ **REJECTED** — an admin weighing two strangers' evidence about a session they did not attend is *reconstruction*, the exact failure `problem-statement.md` identifies, imported into the platform's own operations. The platform's structural advantage is that it knows who was in the room; the correct mechanism is to **ask them**. Where it does not know (imports), no admin knows either, and a process that produces a confident answer anyway is producing a fabrication with a case number. Leaving it unattached is the honest outcome. |
| DT-02 | Claim adjudication is really the same thing as credit dispute (`02.05`) and should merge | Both are contested-credit flows with evidence and resolution | ❌ **REJECTED** — different disagreements with different resolution mechanisms. A dispute argues *what happened* ("I played that solo, not him") and is resolved by evidence from the session. A claim contest argues *who this person is* ("that John Smith is me") and is resolved by identity — the credit itself is not in question. They share the contested state and the suppression behavior, which is why the plumbing overlaps and the flows must not. Merging them would force identity questions through an evidence process that cannot answer them. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Resolution is by asking the party who was present — never by admin adjudication | DT-01. The platform's advantage is presence; adjudication throws it away and guesses. |
| D-02 | Unresolvable contests leave the credit unattached | Awarding on weak evidence hands one person the other's career on a guess. Unattached is honest and reversible; wrongly awarded is neither. |
| D-03 | Contested credits are suppressed from public discography pending resolution | The platform should not publish a fact it knows may be misattributed. |
| D-04 | Claim adjudication stays separate from credit disputes | DT-02. Identity questions cannot be answered by an evidence process built for factual ones. |
