# Feature: External Catalog Import

> **Number**: 02.03.01
> **Parent**: [Claiming & Cold-Start Seeding](./02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Ingestion of existing credit data from the industry's reconstructed record — MusicBrainz, Discogs, DDEX deliveries, PRO/CMO registrations, and the user's own one-sheet — so that a new user's discography is not blank and the graph has enough density for traversal (`02.01.03`) to work at all. It is a cold-start solution, and it is deliberately a second-class citizen of the graph.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own catalog) | Points the platform at their existing footprint — Discogs artist page, MusicBrainz ID, PRO membership, or a pasted list — and gets a populated discography in minutes instead of typing twenty years of work. This is the moment the product stops looking empty, and for a 20-year career it is the difference between adoption and a shrug. |
| Producer | Full (own catalog) | Same, plus their catalog carries other people: importing their discography names the session players on it, creating shells that feed the claim inbox (sub-domain D-03). One Producer's import can seed dozens of professionals. |
| Operator | Full (room's catalog) | Studios have decades of discography in liner notes and on their own website. Importing it makes the room's page (`02.01.02`) real immediately, which is directly commercial for them. |
| Fan | None | No import path. A fan importing credit data would be crowd-sourcing the graph, which `02.01.06` D-04 rejects outright. |

## Behavior

Import produces **candidate credits at the lowest provenance tier**, permanently marked by source. They are visible, useful, and never confusable with captured facts (sub-domain D-01). A Discogs credit says "someone typed this into a database at some point"; that is genuinely weaker evidence than "four people in the room confirmed it on the day", and the graph must say so forever.

Import is where duplication enters the system. A pure capture graph barely duplicates — everyone in the room is a resolved identity. The moment external catalogs land, name collisions, spelling variants, and studio-name chaos ("Abbey Road" / "Abbey Rd. Studio Two") become the dominant data-quality problem, which is the entire reason `02.01.04` exists.

Volume asymmetry matters for design: one import can create thousands of credits naming hundreds of people, most of whom are not users. Every one is a claim-inbox candidate (`02.03.02`) and a merge candidate (`02.01.04`), and both of those are human-attention-bounded. **Import capacity is not the constraint; human adjudication capacity is.** An import that generates more proposals than humans will ever review has not seeded the graph, it has buried it.

### Happy Path

1. User identifies their existing footprint (Discogs/MusicBrainz ID, PRO membership, or a pasted list)
2. System fetches, normalises roles to the taxonomy (`02.06`), and resolves parties/works where identifiers permit (`02.01.04`)
3. Candidates are presented to the user for their own credits — not silently written into their page
4. User accepts; credits land at import tier, source-marked, on their discography
5. Other parties named in the import become shells; their credits queue in their claim inboxes

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Source data is wrong (common — that is the industry's actual state) | Wrong credit imports at low tier. The user corrects (`02.01.06`) or rejects at review. The tier is what makes this survivable | Reviewable before acceptance |
| Source licence forbids ingestion/redistribution | Hard stop. Sub-domain Q-01 is a legal gate — some of these sources plausibly prohibit exactly this | `[PENDING — /create-prd]` |
| Import names hundreds of non-users | Shells created; inbox candidates queued. Also the growth engine (sub-domain D-03) — and simultaneously a consent problem (`02.02.01` Q-02) at much larger scale | Producer sees unclaimed markers |
| Same credit imported from two sources | Deduplicated on identifiers where present; otherwise a merge candidate. Two sources agreeing is mild corroboration — but they routinely copy from each other, so agreement is often not independent | Single credit, multiple source marks |
| User imports credits that are not theirs | Import is self-assertion with extra steps. Nothing about Discogs data makes a claim true | Import tier only; contestable (`02.03.03`) |
| Import volume exceeds human review capacity | The realistic failure at scale (D-04). Candidates are ranked (identifier-resolved and role-mappable first; bare-name matches last) and only the top band is surfaced for review; the low-confidence tail is held, not shown, so unreviewed noise never reaches traversal. Exact ranking signals and the surfaced-vs-held cut are `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` | A ranked review queue, not a raw dump; a "held for later" count rather than thousands of low-quality rows |
| Source goes away or changes terms | The credit keeps its permanent source mark, but the mark is flagged un-re-verifiable. It stays pinned at import tier and can never be promoted on the strength of that dead source — only a fresh capture (sub-domain D-01) can raise it | Credit still shows; source badge reads "source no longer verifiable" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Seeding from Discogs/MusicBrainz contradicts the product's thesis and should be refused | `problem-statement.md`: reconstruction *is* the root failure; these sources are pure reconstruction | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a tension, REJECTED as a conclusion.** The thesis is that reconstruction is a bad way to *establish* facts — not that existing facts should be discarded. The graph needs density before traversal is useful, and a musician with twenty years of work will not adopt a blank page. The resolution is tiering: import for **gravity**, never for **evidence** (sub-domain D-01). This only holds if the tier distinction is enforced permanently and visibly — the moment an import can look like a capture, the product has quietly become Jaxsta with a nicer onboarding. |
| DT-02 | More import is better — density is the goal, so ingest everything available | Cold-start logic; competitors' databases are large | ❌ **REJECTED** — the binding constraint is human adjudication, not ingestion. Every imported credit generates claim candidates (`02.03.02`) and merge candidates (`02.01.04`), both bounded by human attention. Past the point where proposals outpace review, import *degrades* the graph: unreviewed low-tier noise dilutes traversal and buries the real signal. Import must be ranked and throttled to what humans will actually process. |
| DT-03 | Two independent sources agreeing corroborates a credit | Standard corroboration logic | ❌ **REJECTED** — Discogs, MusicBrainz and the rest copy from each other and from the same liner notes. Their agreement is usually **common-source**, not independence, so treating concurrence as corroboration would systematically inflate confidence in propagated errors. It would also give import a route to a tier it must never reach (D-01). Agreement is a weak deduplication signal, not evidence. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Imported credits are permanently source-marked and capped below captured tiers | Sub-domain D-01, DT-01. The distinction is the product. |
| D-02 | Candidates are reviewed before landing on a user's page — never auto-written | An auto-populated discography full of someone else's John Smith is a bad first impression at exactly the moment adoption is decided. |
| D-03 | Cross-source agreement is a deduplication signal, not corroboration | DT-03. These sources are not independent. |
| D-04 | Import is ranked and throttled to human review capacity | DT-02. Unreviewed candidates are noise, and noise is worse than absence. |
