# Feature: Credit Visibility & Embargo

> **Number**: 02.01.05
> **Parent**: [Credit Graph & Discography](./02.01-credit-graph-discography-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The control that lets a credit be **captured at source and still be secret**. Most professional sessions worth capturing are under NDA or are for unreleased work; a platform that publishes "you played on the new <major artist> album" three months before release does not get a second session. This feature is the reason a professional can safely let WeJammin into the room — without it, the thesis's own capture mechanism is a leak.

**Deep Think addition** — absent from the sweep's candidate list. Its absence would have been discovered in the market, expensively.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own credit) | Can hide an individual credit from their public discography without deleting the record — the fact stays true and stays evidence, it just isn't published. Cannot *unhide* a credit the Producer embargoed; a player cannot unilaterally break a session's NDA. |
| Producer | Full (sets session embargo) | Sets embargo at the session, for everyone in it, at capture time — and this must be one control at the top of the capture flow, not a per-credit chore. Sets the release trigger (date, or manual, or on-release event from domain 12). They own the client relationship and therefore the confidentiality obligation. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees that their room hosted an embargoed session; cannot see or publish who was on it. A studio's own discography (`02.01.02`) cannot list an embargoed work — a room that leaks its client list loses its clients. |
| Fan | None | Embargoed credits do not exist. Not hidden, not greyed, not "1 hidden credit" — absent from the page, absent from search, absent from the API. A visible absence is itself a leak (the count tells you something happened). |

## Behavior

Embargo is a property of the credit record enforced **at query**, not a display filter (sub-domain D-01). If it lived in the render layer, `02.01.03` traversal, the public API, and `02.08` export would each leak it independently, and each would have to remember not to.

Three visibility levels, and they are not a simple ladder — they answer different questions:

- **Public** — anyone. The default for released work.
- **Embargoed** — session participants only, until a release trigger fires. The confidentiality case.
- **Private** — the credited party chose not to publish this one. Permanent, personal, and *not* confidentiality. A player who doesn't want a particular record on their page has an aesthetic or reputational reason, not a legal one.

Embargo lifts on a trigger; private never lifts on its own.

The default matters more than the mechanism. `personas.md` records the Producer's worst accidental behavior as closing a session without capturing, and instructs that **the lazy path must be the correct path**. If the default is Public, the lazy path leaks NDA work. If the default is Embargoed, the lazy path is a graph full of invisible credits nobody ever publishes — which starves `02.01.02` and the growth loop with it. Neither default is safe, which is why D-01 below picks the trigger rather than the state.

### Happy Path

1. Producer starts a session; sets confidentiality once, at the top ("is this under wraps?")
2. Capture (`02.02`) runs normally; every credit inherits the session's embargo
3. Credits are visible to participants, invisible everywhere else — no public page, no search path, no export
4. Release trigger fires (date reached, or domain 12 reports the release, or Producer lifts manually)
5. Credits become public in one event; every participant's discography updates at once, and each is told it happened

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Release date slips (routinely) | A date-only trigger publishes early — the failure this feature exists to prevent. Date must be a *hint*, with the release event from domain 12 as the authority | Producer sees "will publish when released" not "will publish 3 Nov" |
| Work is shelved forever | Credits stay embargoed indefinitely. Correct — the record survives for evidence, disputes and eventual royalties; it just never publishes | Participants see it privately, marked "unreleased" |
| Player leaves the platform mid-embargo | Embargo persists; it is the session's property, not the player's | — |
| Player publicly claims the credit off-platform (Instagram) | Out of scope for enforcement. WeJammin does not publish it; policing the human is not this feature's job | — |
| Embargoed credit is contested (`02.05`) | Dispute proceeds privately among participants; no public contested marker, because there is no public credit | Participants only |
| Export requested during embargo (`02.08`) | Embargoed credits excluded by default; participant-scoped export may include them | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Producer sets embargo *after* capture, credit already public | Retraction from public is possible but the leak already happened; the honest answer is that this is unrecoverable | Warning that it has been public since <time> |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Public | Default for non-embargoed sessions; or trigger fired | Rendered everywhere |
| Embargoed — pending | Session marked confidential | Participants only; "unreleased" marker; countdown or "on release" |
| Embargoed — indefinite | No trigger set, or work shelved | Participants only; no date shown |
| Released | Trigger fired | Public; participants notified it went live |
| Private | Credited party opted out individually | Owner only; never publishes on its own |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A credits platform can default everything to public, because a credit is inherently a thing you want seen | The sweep's implicit model — no candidate child mentioned confidentiality at all | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the finding that justifies the feature.** The sessions most worth capturing are the ones under NDA. A public-by-default capture flow is a confidentiality breach generator aimed precisely at the professional tier the product needs. The whole thesis is *be in the room*; you are not invited into the room twice if you tweet what happened in it. |
| DT-02 | Embargo can be a display filter on the discography page | Simplest implementation | ❌ **REJECTED** — `02.01.03` traversal, the public API and `02.08` export all read the graph without going through the page. A render-layer filter leaks through every one of them, and each new reader has to independently remember the rule. Enforcement belongs at the record, checked at query. |
| DT-03 | Publishing on a stored release *date* is sufficient | Obvious mechanism; dates are easy | ❌ **REJECTED** — release dates slip constantly, and the failure is asymmetric: publishing a day late costs nothing, publishing a week early ends a client relationship. The date is a hint; the authority is the actual release event from domain 12, with manual lift as the fallback. A date-triggered publish is a scheduled leak. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Embargo is set **once per session at capture time**, defaulting to the room's/Producer's standing preference — not per credit, not per player | Per-credit confidentiality is admin the Producer will skip, and skipping it is a breach. A standing preference makes the lazy path the correct path, which `personas.md` demands explicitly. It also sidesteps the unsafe-default dilemma: the Producer states their norm once, not per session. |
| D-02 | Enforcement at the record, evaluated at query — never a render filter | DT-02. |
| D-03 | The release *event* (domain 12) is the trigger; a date is only a hint | DT-03. |
| D-04 | Embargoed credits are **absent**, not marked hidden, for non-participants | A "1 hidden credit" affordance leaks the existence of the work, which is often the confidential part. |
