# Feature: Credit Visibility & Embargo

> **Number**: 02.01.05
> **Parent**: [Credit Graph & Discography](./02.01-credit-graph-discography-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The control that lets a credit be **captured at source and still be secret**. Most professional sessions worth capturing are under NDA or are for unreleased work; a platform that publishes "you played on the new <major artist> album" three months before release does not get a second session. This feature is the reason a professional can safely let WeJammin into the room — without it, the thesis's own capture mechanism is a leak.

It exists because of the shape of `problem-statement.md`'s root cause. The fix for "absence at the point of truth" is presence in the room. Presence in the room creates a confidentiality obligation the reconstruction-era competitors never had: Jaxsta and Discogs only ever see released work, so they cannot leak an unreleased one. WeJammin sees the session on the day. **Capture-at-source and confidentiality are the same problem seen from two ends**, and this feature is the join.

**Deep Think addition** — absent from the sweep's candidate list. Its absence would have been discovered in the market, expensively.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own credit) | Always sees every credit naming them, embargoed or not — embargo never hides a credit from the person it names (D-09). Can **unlist** an individual credit from their own discography page without deleting the record — the fact stays true and stays evidence, it just isn't on their page (and, per D-06, still appears on the work's ledger, which the UI must say plainly). Can request early lift from the Producer (D-10) and can force lift on proof the work is already public (D-11) — subject to a Producer challenge to that evidence, which pauses the lift and routes to resolution rather than blocking it (D-19–D-22), and which they may answer with fresh evidence as many times as they can substantiate it (D-22). Cannot unilaterally break a session's embargo before release; cannot remove themselves from a work's ledger. |
| Producer | Full (sets session embargo) | Sets confidentiality **once, at the top of the session**, for everyone in it — one control at session start, not a per-credit chore (D-01). Chooses whether to share the release-date hint with participants (D-08). Lifts manually; approves or declines a player's early-lift request (decline is logged and shown to the requester). May **challenge** an evidence-based lift inside its 72-hour window by stating one of D-19's closed grounds — a challenge to the evidence that pauses the lift and is resolved by re-verification or the dispute engine (D-20, D-21), **never a veto they decide themselves**. On a co-produced session any Producer can *impose* embargo; only the session owner can relax it (D-12). Cannot see or set visibility on sessions they did not run. |
| Operator | Read-only (own room credit) | Sees that their room hosted a session and sees the room's **own** credit ("Recorded at X") with its embargo marker. Cannot see who else was on it — that stays the Producer's grant (`02.01.01` Role Lens). The room's discography (`02.01.02`) cannot list an embargoed work, and the room's booking/calendar surfaces (16/17) must not name the work or the parties. Note the honest limit (DT-11): the Operator was physically there and already knows. What the platform controls is not their knowledge but whether the platform is the channel that publishes it. |
| Fan | None | Embargoed credits do not exist. Not hidden, not greyed, not "1 hidden credit" — absent from the page, absent from search, absent from paths, absent from counts, absent from the API, and absent from every aggregate derived from them (D-04, D-05). A visible absence is itself a leak: the count tells you something happened, and often the *existence* of the session is the confidential part. A fetch of an embargoed credit's URL returns 404, never 403 (D-05). |

## Behavior

Embargo is a property of the credit record enforced **at query**, not a display filter (sub-domain D-01). If it lived in the render layer, `02.01.03` traversal, the public API, `02.08` export and every count on every page would each leak it independently, and each new reader would have to remember the rule unprompted. One reader forgetting once is a breach.

### The two axes — the correction this pass makes

The breadth pass modelled visibility as one three-value ladder (Public / Embargoed / Private) while noting the values "are not a simple ladder — they answer different questions." That instinct was right and the model was wrong. They are **two orthogonal axes**, and collapsing them into one enum produces a specific, shippable lie (D-06, DT-12).

**Axis A — Confidentiality.** A property of the *record*. Binds every surface. Has a basis outside the platform (an NDA, an unannounced release) and a defined end.

| Level | Scope | Who sees it | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Per credit | Everyone | — (default for work that has released) |
| **Embargoed** | Set per session, **lifts per recording** (D-07) | Session participants + the credited party | The recording is demonstrably public (D-03, D-11) |
| **Confidential** | Per credit, permanent | The credited party + the work's ledger owner | Never, unless both parties agree to lift (see Q-02) |

**Axis B — Page curation.** A property of *one party's discography page*. Binds that page only.

| Level | Scope | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Listed** | Per credit, per party | Appears on the party's discography (default) |
| **Unlisted** | Per credit, per party | Removed from that party's discography page **only** — still on the work's ledger, still on co-contributors' pages, still in the graph |

A credit therefore has one Axis-A value and one Axis-B value, and they do not interact: an Unlisted credit on a released work is still public on the work page; an Embargoed credit's Axis-B value is moot until lift, and applies the moment it lifts.

**Why the rename matters.** The breadth pass called Axis B "Private". It is not private. A player who marks a credit Private, expecting it gone, will find their name on the work's public ledger and on four co-contributors' pages — because a credit is a **joint** fact that four other people also assert (`02.01.01` DT-03, domain D-08: evidence sets the tier, it never gates the credit). Letting one party suppress the graph unilaterally would let a player scrub a credit the Producer truthfully asserted. So Axis B is a page-level opt-out and must be **labelled as one**: the UI says "Removed from your discography. The work's credit list still shows you, because <N> other people also credit you here." Calling it Private would be a truth-in-labelling failure that users discover only after they have relied on it.

The cases Axis B is *not* the answer to are routed, not fudged: erasure of a credit a party genuinely must not be linked to is de-identification (`02.01.01` Q-01, `/create-prd-security`); a credit under a former name is identity + merge (`02.01.04`, domain 01 — see Cross-Cut Notes); a credit that is factually wrong is amendment (`02.01.06`) or dispute (`02.05`); harassment-driven removal is domain 24.

### The trigger model

**The date never publishes anything.** A date is a hint that schedules a *question*, not a publish (D-03). Release dates slip constantly and the cost is asymmetric: publishing a day late costs nothing, publishing a week early ends a client relationship. Authority to lift comes from exactly three sources, all of which reduce to one predicate — **the recording is demonstrably public**:

1. **Release event from domain 12** — authoritative, on-platform, fires on confirmed store availability. Credits public within 60 seconds; participants notified within 5 minutes.
2. **Verified external evidence** (D-11) — any participant supplies a public store/DSP URL or ISRC for the recording. The platform verifies public availability, notifies the Producer, and lifts after a **72-hour objection window**. An objection inside that window is a **challenge to the evidence, not a veto** (D-19–D-22): it pauses the lift at the status quo and routes to resolution. This is not a scheduled leak: its precondition is proof the work is *already* public, so an **unobjected** window costs three days of nothing.
3. **Producer manual lift** — always available, immediate.

#### The objection is a challenge, not a veto

The 72-hour window is the compensating control against a forged store URL or a mis-supplied identifier (Q-04). It is not a Producer veto over an already-released work — a veto would reinstate exactly the critical-path dependency D-11 exists to remove.

**An objection must state a ground, drawn from a closed list** (D-19). Each enumerated ground challenges the same predicate D-03 already assigns the platform — *the recording is demonstrably public* — so no new kind of adjudication is taken on:

| # | Ground | What it asserts |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | **The evidence identifies a different recording** | The supplied URL/ISRC is not this recording |
| (ii) | **The URL or identifier is not publicly reachable** | The evidence does not in fact show public availability |
| (iii) | **Other** | Free text. Logged, shown to the resolver, routed to a human — **never auto-handled and never auto-resolved** |

Grounds (i) and (ii) supply information the platform's first automated check did not have, which is what makes the inline re-check below meaningful rather than a repeat of a pass that already succeeded.

**A timely objection pauses the lift at the status quo** (D-20). The credits stay embargoed while the objection is contested — they neither publish nor become permanently unpublishable. The record's state is `Lift pending — objection contested`.

**Resolution routes to the dispute engine** (`02.05`; D-21), with **platform re-verification as the free first rung**: the platform re-checks the supplied evidence against the stated ground before any human is involved. If the re-check settles it, the objection resolves there. Otherwise the contested objection becomes a case on the engine domain D-03 already shares, and the adjudicator reads the embargoed record under D-18's logged, non-publishing grant. Contested lifts therefore inherit `02.05`'s ladder latency and are no longer a three-day matter.

Because an embargoed status quo plus an unbounded resolver is a de facto veto, **a resolution SLA is mandatory** (D-21). The value is deferred (Q-06); the requirement is not.

**Re-submission of evidence after an objection is unbounded** (D-22). No cap, no cooldown, no escalation counter — every contested objection is already in front of an adjudicator, so the resolver bounds the loop. A repeat objector must keep winning on the merits rather than blocking for free. This matches the `Lift-stalled` path's existing shape, where the 90-day nudge repeats indefinitely and the evidence-based lift is offered again each time.

**Two notification fan-outs are new**: *objection raised* (to every participant, including the participant who supplied the evidence) and *objection resolved* (to the same set, carrying the outcome). Neither names the work to a non-participant, per D-05.

The case an objection deliberately does **not** cover: a recording that is publicly reachable but was never authorised for release — a leak or a bootleg. No enumerated ground fits it, because no source in the corpus defines an authorised-vs-unauthorised predicate and D-03's "demonstrably public" does not license one. This is a **known open gap** (Q-07), recorded rather than fudged.

Lift is **one-way** (D-13) with one narrow exception: a lift caused by an event that is itself retracted within 7 days (a store that honoured the date wrong and pulled it — DT-07) may be reverted. The revert re-embargoes the credits and **permanently logs the exposure window**; it does not un-publish what was seen. Every participant is told it happened and for how long. Honesty about an unrecoverable leak beats a silent re-hide.

### Lift is a state of the recording, not a batch job

The breadth pass modelled lift as "one event publishes every credit on the session." That is wrong for the normal case (DT-17). A session produces several recordings; two release and one is shelved. A player claims their credit six months after release. An amendment commits a successor credit post-release. If lift is a one-time sweep over a set, the shelved track's credits publish with the others and the late claimant stays invisible forever.

So: **lift sets `released` on the recording.** Credits are evaluated against their recording's state at query time. A credit committed *after* lift on a released recording is public on commit, with no further action. A session where one of three recordings released leaves the other two embargoed — because credits are per-work (`02.01.01`), and the confidential thing is the unreleased track, not the day.

Composition credits attach to the recording the session produced and lift with it. (Whether a credit anchors to recording, work, or both is sub-domain Q-03; this feature only needs the recording to be the thing that *releases*, which is not in dispute.)

### The default and the lazy path

The default matters more than the mechanism. `personas.md` records the Producer's worst accidental behavior as closing a session without capturing, and instructs that **the lazy path must be the correct path**. If the default is Public, the lazy path leaks NDA work. If the default is Embargoed, the lazy path is a graph full of invisible credits nobody ever publishes — which starves `02.01.02` and the growth loop with it. Neither default is safe.

D-01 sidesteps the dilemma by moving the decision off the session. The default resolves down a chain, first hit wins:

1. **Project setting** (domain 07), if the session belongs to a project — because a record is made over many sessions for one client, and the NDA is per client, not per day (DT-05). A 12-session album with 12 independent embargo settings leaks on the one the Producer rushed.
2. **Producer's standing preference** — stated once at onboarding ("my sessions are confidential until release, by default"), not per session.
3. **Room's standing preference** (Operator), for sessions in a room whose operator has one.
4. **Embargoed**, if none of the above exists. The unsafe direction of a wrong default is unrecoverable; the safe direction is a nudge (DT-14's re-ask).

The session-start control shows the inherited value and takes one tap to change it. The Producer states their norm once; the lazy path is then correct by construction.

### Who is a participant

The participant set is the session roll (`02.02.01`) — every party on it, including those the roll records as present-but-uncredited (a label rep on the couch is on the roll and gets no credit, but they were in the room and an NDA covers them). Plus the credited party on any credit, always, even if the roll never named them (D-09).

Participant status is **per session, not per role**: it does not follow from being a Musician or a Producer, and it does not decay when someone leaves the platform (D-14). Explicitly rejected: scoping a participant's visibility to the intervals they attended (DT-13). A dep who played 2pm–3pm sees the session's credits, not a 60-minute slice — an NDA covers the project, not your hours, and interval-scoped visibility is a model nobody in music would recognise.

An **unclaimed shell** (`02.03.02`) is a participant with no channel: the record is theirs, and they see it the moment they claim. Nothing is sent to them while the session is embargoed (D-15).

### Privileged, non-publishing access

Three readers are not participants and must nonetheless see an embargoed credit. Each is a **logged, non-publishing** grant — it never renders on a public surface and never enters an index:

| Reader | Why | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Dispute adjudicator (`02.05`, D-03 cross-cut engine) | Cannot adjudicate a credit they cannot see | Scoped to the credit under dispute; logged to the case; visible to the parties |
| Trust & Safety (domain 24) | Cannot action a report on an invisible record | Scoped to the report; logged |
| The credited party's own legal use | The record exists *because* it is evidence (`problem-statement.md`) — withholding it in the moment it is needed defeats the feature | Participant-scoped export (see below) |

## Happy Path

1. Producer starts a session. The confidentiality control at the top shows the inherited value ("Confidential until release — from your standing preference") with a one-tap override
2. Producer leaves it as inherited and gets on with the session — the lazy path
3. Capture (`02.02`) runs normally; every credit the session emits inherits the session's confidentiality at commit
4. Credits are visible to participants and to each credited party, and invisible everywhere else — no public page, no search hit, no traversal path, no count, no export, no API
5. Participants attest their credits privately during the embargo (`02.04.01`); provenance tier accrues while the credit is invisible (DT-20)
6. Domain 12 reports the recording released
7. System sets `released` on the recording; every committed credit on it becomes public within 60 seconds; search, traversal and counts pick it up on invalidation
8. Every participant is notified within 5 minutes that their credit is live, with a link to their updated discography — arriving already attested rather than as a release-day tier-zero row
9. Credits committed later on that recording (a late claim, an amendment's successor) are public on commit, with no further lift action

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |

| **Release date slips** (routinely) | Nothing publishes. The date only scheduled a question (D-03) | Producer sees "Will publish when released", never "Publishes 3 Nov" |
| **Work is shelved forever** | Credits stay embargoed indefinitely. Correct — the record survives for evidence, disputes and eventual royalties; it just never publishes | Participants see it privately, marked "Unreleased" |
| **Producer moves on; nothing ever lifts** (Q-01's rot) | At 90 days past the hint date (or 90 days post-session if none) and every 90 days after, the Producer is asked "Did <work> release?". Never auto-lifts. After 4 unanswered nudges the work is marked `Lift-stalled` and participants are offered the evidence-based lift path (D-11) | Producer: periodic one-tap prompt. Participants: "This work looks released? Show us where" |
| **Label releases it off-platform; Producer unreachable / account deleted** | Any participant supplies a public store URL or ISRC → platform verifies public availability → 72-hour objection window → lift if unobjected. Resolves Q-01 without the Producer existing | "We verified this is publicly available. Credits publish in 72 hours — <Producer> can challenge the evidence in that window" |
| **Producer objects inside the window on ground (i) or (ii)** | The lift **pauses at the status quo**: credits stay embargoed and do not publish while contested (D-20). Producer: their stated ground and its resolution state. Everyone else: the work still does not exist |

| **Objection is groundless / the evidence stands** | Re-verification or the adjudicator rejects the objection; the lift completes and the credits publish by the ordinary lift path | Participants notified of the resolution, then of the lift, in that order |
| **Participant supplies fresh evidence after an objection succeeded** | Permitted, unbounded (D-22). No cap, no cooldown, no counter — the resolver bounds the loop rather than an invented threshold. Same shape as the repeating `Lift-stalled` nudge | The evidence path is offered again, unchanged |
| **Recording is publicly reachable but was never authorised** (leak, bootleg) | **Unhandled by design.** No enumerated ground fits it and no source defines an authorised-vs-unauthorised predicate; D-03's "demonstrably public" does not license one. Recorded as an open gap rather than invented as a ground | Producer may object only under (iii) "other", which routes to a human with no test to apply — see Q-07 |
| **Store honours the date wrong; release event fires early, label pulls it** | Credits published. Revert available for 7 days: re-embargoes, permanently logs the exposure window, notifies every participant. The leak is not undone (DT-07) | "Public from 14:02–16:40 on 3 Nov (2h 38m) due to a premature store listing. Re-embargoed." |
| **Producer sets embargo *after* capture; credit already public** | Retraction from public is possible; the leak already happened. The honest answer is that this is unrecoverable | Warning stating exactly how long it was public, and that search engines and the API may hold copies |
| **Two Producers on a co-produced session disagree** | Confidentiality is a ratchet: any Producer may impose embargo, only the session owner may relax it (D-12). Under disagreement the restrictive value stands | The imposing Producer is named to the other; "Session marked confidential by <name>" |
| **Player leaves the platform mid-embargo** | Embargo persists — it is the session's property, not the player's (D-14). Their participant read access ends with their account; their credit does not | — |
| **Player publicly claims the credit off-platform (Instagram)** | Out of scope for enforcement. WeJammin does not publish it; policing the human is not this feature's job — and doing so would require surveillance the product has no right to | — |
| **Player demands early publication (wants the CV line)** | Request goes to the Producer. Approve → immediate lift. Decline → logged, shown to the requester with the decline reason if given. No arbitration: the platform does not hold the NDA and does not adjudicate (domain D-10). Post-release, the player does not need permission — D-11 applies (D-10, Q-02) | "Requested. <Producer> declined — the client's release is unannounced." |
| **Producer weaponizes embargo to withhold credits in a payment dispute** | The anti-persona behavior (`personas.md`: "withholds stems as leverage") aimed at this feature. Blocked three ways: the credited party always sees their own credit (D-09); they can export it participant-scoped; and once the work is public they lift it themselves (D-11) without the Producer. Embargo can delay publication, never conceal a credit from the person it names | The player's own copy is never withheld |
| **Embargoed credit is contested (`02.05`)** | Dispute proceeds privately among participants; adjudicator gets a logged, non-publishing grant. No public contested marker — there is no public credit to mark | Participants and the adjudicator only |
| **Dispute resolves while embargoed** | Outcome applies to the record; nothing publishes. The corrected credit publishes at lift like any other | Participants only |
| **Export requested during embargo (`02.08`)** | Resolved: a participant may export the embargoed credits of sessions they participated in, and their own credits always. A non-participant-scoped export (a Producer's whole catalog, a room's discography) excludes embargoed credits and **states the exclusion explicitly** — a silent gap makes the user discover it when the label rejects the RIN (`02.08` D-04). No embargoed credit is ever included in an automated outbound emission to a DSP, registry or partner (D-16) | "3 of 47 credits excluded — embargoed. Export as participant to include them." |
| **Automated PRO / neighbouring-rights registration of an unreleased work (domain 10)** | Registration is normal pre-release and PRO databases are public — so the platform can leak by doing the correct rights thing. No embargoed credit is auto-propagated to any external registry (D-16). The Producer may register manually; that is their disclosure to make, and they are told so | "Registering this now makes the credit public in <PRO>'s database. Continue?" |
| **Merge racing lift** (CX-01 Q5, CX-02 Q5) | Resolved: visibility travels with the **credit**, never with the party. A merge re-points a credit's party and does not touch its confidentiality. The danger is only in the implementation — merge must union **credits and re-derive the page**, never union two rendered pages (D-17) | Nothing. The survivor's page shows exactly the credits the survivor is entitled to publish |
| **Amendment in flight when lift fires** | Lift publishes the current committed record. A pending proposal does not block a release — a release will not wait, and publishing a slightly-wrong credit is a smaller harm than a missing release-day credit. The successor publishes when it commits | Participants' lift notification states "1 amendment pending on this credit" |
| **Late claim on an already-released recording** | Public on commit. Lift is a state of the recording, not a batch (DT-17) — otherwise a player who claims six months after release stays invisible forever | Credit appears immediately; no lift step |
| **One of three recordings from a session releases** | Only that recording's credits publish. The other two stay embargoed — the confidential thing is the unreleased track, not the day (D-07) | Producer's embargo view shows 1 released / 2 embargoed on one session |
| **Session still open when the recording releases** (a single dropping while album sessions continue) | Draft credits do not publish. `released` is set on the recording; credits publish as they commit at close | Producer sees "This recording is released — credits publish as you close" |
| **Lift partially fails (search index down)** | Safe direction only. Records public, index stale = credit missing from search — degraded, not leaked. The system re-attempts indexing automatically; if it still has not caught up it flags the credit for manual replay, and the credit stays visibly "retrying" until it does. The reverse — index public, record still embargoed — must be impossible by construction (CX-01 Q2) | Participants notified of lift; search catches up. No user-visible error |
| **Non-participant fetches an embargoed credit's canonical URL** | 404, never 403 (D-05). A 403 confirms the record exists, which is the disclosure | Identical to a credit that never existed |
| **Non-participant searches the embargoed party/work by name** | Same empty result as a name that isn't in the graph. No "results hidden", no result count discrepancy | "No results" |
| **Traversal path runs *through* an embargoed session** (CX-01) | The edge does not exist for a non-participant, so paths through it do not exist. Critical: both endpoints may be public while the *path* between them discloses the confidential session ("A → <session> → B") | The path is simply not among the results |
| **Aggregate/count leak** | Any count, badge, streak, leaderboard, "N sessions this year", activity feed entry or public analytic (domain 22) derived from credits must exclude embargoed credits for non-participants. The count is the leak: a session total that increments tells a watcher a session happened, for whom, and when (D-05, DT-09) | Public counts move only at lift |
| **Operator's room calendar shows a booking for the confidential session** (16/17) | The booking is the Operator's own commercial record and is theirs to hold; it must not name the work or the parties on any surface the Operator publishes. A public "now recording: <artist>" feed is the leak | Room's public page shows availability, never the client |
| **Multi-hyphenate: Operator of the room *and* a player on the session** | Access is the union and the union is correct — she was in the room (DT-16). Role-based blindness cannot be enforced against a human who holds another role with legitimate access. What is enforced is what each *surface* publishes: her Musician view shows the credits; the **room's** discography still cannot list the work | She sees it as a participant; the room's page does not |
| **Multi-hyphenate: Producer of the session *and* a credited player on it** | No conflict. She set the embargo and she can lift it. The tension Q-02 describes between player and Producer is, here, internal to one person — which is a good argument that it is a negotiation, not an adjudication | Both controls in one place |
| **Participant is told the release date they should not have** | The credit is always visible to participants; the **date hint is not** (D-08, DT-19). A release date is frequently confidential in its own right and leaks are a marketing failure. The Producer chooses whether to share it | Default: "Publishes on release". If shared: "Expected 3 Nov" |
| **Embargoed credit for an unclaimed shell — the growth loop stalls** | Real cost, accepted. No claim invitation is sent while embargoed (D-15) — "come claim your credit on <unreleased album>" is an email that names the confidential work, and email is forwarded and breached. The shell and credit persist; the invitation fires at lift. The Producer can still tell them by mouth, which is domain Q-05's answer anyway | The human sees nothing until lift or until someone tells them |
| **Import (`02.03.01`) of an unreleased work** | Cannot happen in the normal case — import sources (MusicBrainz, Discogs, PRO) only carry released work. If a source ever does carry pre-release data, the imported credit inherits `Embargoed` and its lift follows D-11, because the platform has no participant set to consult | Marked "Embargoed — imported, no session" |
| **Ghost production / contractually uncredited** | Endemic in pop and EDM, and Axis A's `Confidential` level is where it lands — per credit, permanent, requiring the assent of both the credited party and the work's ledger owner, because it changes what a joint assertion publishes. The **preferred** answer for the common case is an alias identity (domain 01) holding a public credit: the ledger stays truthful ("Produced by <alias>") and what is confidential is the alias↔human link, not the fact. Whether WeJammin facilitates a permanent non-credit at all is a product decision (Q-02) | Producer + party both confirm; ledger shows the alias, or nothing |
| **Deceased contributor's embargoed credit** | Embargo persists; the estate's control over lift is sub-domain Q-02, not this feature's to settle. The credit survives — session players' credits feed neighbouring-rights income after death, which is precisely when the record matters most | Participants only, unchanged |
| **GDPR erasure request against an embargoed credit** | Same collision as any credit (`02.01.01` DT-03/Q-01) — a joint fact, likely de-identified rather than deleted. Embargo changes nothing about it and must not be offered as a substitute: "we hid it" is not erasure, and telling a user it is would be a compliance failure dressed as a feature | Routed to the erasure flow, not to this control |

## States

> Two state sets. The first is the record's; the second is the Producer's embargo-management surface, which is where a human actually interacts with this feature.

### Credit visibility state (Axis A × Axis B)

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Public** | Recording released (any of the three D-03 triggers), or the session was never confidential | Rendered everywhere; counted in every aggregate |
| **Public — Unlisted** | Public credit, party set Axis B to Unlisted | Absent from *that party's* discography; present on the work's ledger and on co-contributors' pages, with the UI stating so |
| **Embargoed — dated hint** | Session confidential; a release date is known and the Producer chose to share it | Participants: credit + "Expected 3 Nov". Everyone else: does not exist |
| **Embargoed — on release** | Session confidential; date unknown or unshared | Participants: credit + "Publishes on release". Everyone else: does not exist |
| **Embargoed — indefinite** | No trigger, or work shelved | Participants: credit + "Unreleased". No date, no countdown |
| **Lift-stalled** | 4 × 90-day nudges unanswered, or Producer account gone | Participants: credit + "Released? Show us where" (D-11 evidence path). Everyone else: does not exist |
| **Lift pending — objection window** | Evidence-based lift accepted; 72-hour window open | Participants: "Publishes in 71h — <Producer> can challenge the evidence". Producer: challenge the evidence (stated ground, D-19) / confirm now |
| **Lift pending — objection contested** | Producer objected inside the window on a stated ground (D-19) | **Embargoed while contested** (D-20): participants see the credit + "A challenge to the evidence is being resolved", with no date and no countdown; the credit does not publish and is not permanently unpublishable. Producer sees their ground and its resolution state. Everyone else: does not exist |
| **Released** | `released` set on the recording | Public; participants notified within 5 minutes that it went live |
| **Reverted** | Premature lift revoked within 7 days | Participants: credit + permanent exposure-window record. Everyone else: does not exist again |
| **Confidential** | Per-credit permanent suppression, both parties assented | The credited party + the ledger owner only. Never publishes, ever, on any surface |

### Producer's embargo-management surface

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Surface opened | Skeleton rows grouped by work; never a spinner over a blank page |
| Empty | No embargoed sessions | "Nothing under wraps. Your standing preference is <value> — change it" — the empty state is where the standing preference (D-01) is discoverable, because the Producer only thinks about this when they are here |
| Populated | ≥1 embargoed work | Works grouped by session, each with its state, its hint date if any, participant count, and a one-tap lift |
| Partial | Some credits on a work lifted, some did not (index lag, or later-committing credits) | Per-work "8 of 9 published — 1 retrying" with the failing credit named. Never a silent partial |
| Error | Automatic re-indexing exhausted without the credit reaching search | "Lift didn't complete. 3 credits are still embargoed — retry." Explicitly states the safe direction: nothing leaked |
| Stalled | ≥1 work at `Lift-stalled` | Surfaced first, with age ("shelved or released? 14 months") — the rot is only visible if it is ranked |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A credits platform can default everything to public, because a credit is inherently a thing you want seen | The sweep's implicit model — no candidate child mentioned confidentiality at all | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the finding that justifies the feature.** The sessions most worth capturing are the ones under NDA. A public-by-default capture flow is a confidentiality breach generator aimed precisely at the professional tier the product needs. The whole thesis is *be in the room*; you are not invited into the room twice if you tweet what happened in it. |
| DT-02 | Embargo can be a display filter on the discography page | Simplest implementation | ❌ **REJECTED** — `02.01.03` traversal, the public API and `02.08` export all read the graph without going through the page. A render-layer filter leaks through every one of them, and each new reader has to independently remember the rule. Enforcement belongs at the record, checked at query. |
| DT-03 | Publishing on a stored release *date* is sufficient | Obvious mechanism; dates are easy | ❌ **REJECTED** — release dates slip constantly, and the failure is asymmetric: publishing a day late costs nothing, publishing a week early ends a client relationship. The date is a hint; the authority is the actual release event from domain 12, with manual lift as the fallback. A date-triggered publish is a scheduled leak. |
| DT-04 | Confidentiality is an unfortunate constraint bolted onto capture-at-source | Framing implied by the feature's late arrival (a Deep Think addition, not a swept candidate) | ✅ **CONFIRMED as the inverse.** They are the same problem from two ends. The reconstruction-era competitors (Jaxsta, Discogs, Muso.AI) *cannot* leak an unreleased session because they were never in the room — their defect is their protection. WeJammin's presence at the point of truth is simultaneously the differentiator and the liability, and this feature is the only thing that separates them. That reframes the file: it is not a privacy setting attached to domain 02, it is the precondition on which `02.02` is allowed to exist at all. It also explains why the sweep missed it — the sweep was cataloguing capabilities, and this is the cost of one. |
| DT-05 | The unit of confidentiality is the session | D-01's model; the session is where the control lives | ❌ **REJECTED as the unit — CONFIRMED as the control point.** An NDA is per client/project, not per day: a record is made over 12 sessions across four months and one omission leaks the album. So the *default* must inherit from the project (domain 07) rather than be re-decided per session (D-01's chain). The session remains where the control is *shown*, because that is where the Producer is. Setting and scoping are different questions and the breadth pass answered only the first. |
| DT-06 | Embargo lifts per session | Follows from DT-05's rejected framing; the surface pass's "credits become public in one event" | ❌ **REJECTED** — sessions produce multiple recordings, and recordings release independently. A session that yields three tracks where two release and one is shelved must publish two-thirds of its credits. **Setting is per session; lifting is per recording** (D-07). This asymmetry is invisible until you write the failure case, and a spec that models one enum on "the session" would ship the shelved track's credits on the album's release day. |
| DT-07 | Once lifted, always public — publication is irreversible | Reasonable: a fact, once out, is out | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH ONE EXCEPTION.** The general rule holds and revert must not be sold as an undo. But a real and routine case defeats a purely one-way model: a DSP honours the release date wrong, goes live early, the release event fires, credits publish, and the label pulls the record and re-dates it. WeJammin has now published NDA credits on a store's clerical error. A 7-day revert with a **permanent, honest exposure log** is the least-dishonest response: the credits re-embargo, and the record says "public for 2h 38m" forever. Anything that quietly re-hides them lets the Producer believe nothing happened, and they will find out from the client. |
| DT-08 | Any information leak is a credit leak | Implicit in D-04's "absent, not marked hidden" | ❌ **REJECTED as too narrow — and this is the failure mode nobody designs for.** D-04 correctly kills "1 hidden credit", then stops. The credits are only the payload; the *side channels* are where this actually leaks: a session count that increments, a "top 10 most-credited drummers of 2026" leaderboard (domain 22), an activity feed, a public API result count, a 403 that should have been a 404, a traversal path whose endpoints are both public, a room's "now recording" feed. Every one of these discloses that a session happened, when, and often with whom — which is frequently the entire confidential fact. **The rule generalises: nothing derived from an embargoed credit may vary observably for a non-participant** (D-05). This is one testable invariant instead of a dozen features remembering a rule. |
| DT-09 | Embargo protects the participants from the platform's publication | The feature's stated purpose | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH A HOLE.** It protects against WeJammin publishing. It does not — cannot — protect against WeJammin *causing* publication elsewhere. Two automated paths take an embargoed credit outside: RIN/delivery emission (`02.08`) and PRO / neighbouring-rights registration (domain 10), where registering an unreleased work pre-release is *normal practice* and PRO databases are public. The platform can leak by doing the correct rights thing, on the user's behalf, without ever rendering a page. Hence D-16: no automated outbound publication of an embargoed credit, ever. A manual registration is the Producer's own disclosure and they are told so at the point of it. |
| DT-10 | Embargo is a defensive control that only ever restricts | It is a confidentiality feature; confidentiality restricts | ❌ **REJECTED — it is also an offensive weapon, and the anti-persona says so.** `personas.md` gives the Producer's worst intentional behavior as "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute." Embargo is a better lever than stems: it withholds the player's *career evidence*, which is the thing the platform taught them to value. A naive spec hands the trust broker a weapon aimed at the persona the product is built for. Hence D-09 (the credited party always sees their own credit), participant-scoped export, and D-11 (post-release lift needs no permission). Embargo may delay publication; it may never conceal a credit from the person it names. |
| DT-11 | The Operator can be modelled as blind to the participant list | `02.01.01`'s existing boundary; the room's confidentiality obligation | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED AS POLICY, REJECTED AS FICTION.** The Operator let the band in and made the tea. Modelling them as not knowing is theatre, and specs built on comfortable fictions fail in the direction of the fiction. The accurate statement: the platform does not control the Operator's *knowledge*, only whether the platform is the *channel* through which it publishes. So the room's discography, calendar, and any "now recording" surface are the real controls — not the Operator's read access, which is a courtesy boundary the Operator can trivially route around by remembering. Stating this honestly is what makes the multi-hyphenate case (DT-16) resolvable instead of contradictory. |
| DT-12 | "Private" is the third visibility level, a personal sibling of embargo | The breadth pass's own model, which then hedged that "they are not a simple ladder" | ❌ **REJECTED — the model was a shippable lie.** A credit is a joint fact four other people also assert (`02.01.01` DT-03; domain D-08). A player marking a credit "Private" cannot remove it from the work's ledger or from co-contributors' pages without unilaterally scrubbing an assertion that is not theirs — which is exactly the erasure the domain refuses. So "Private" can only ever be a **page-level opt-out**, and calling it Private tells the user it is something it is not. They will discover the truth after relying on it, which is the worst possible time. **Two orthogonal axes** — Confidentiality (record, binds everywhere) and Page curation (one page, Listed/Unlisted) — with honest labelling on the second (D-06). The breadth pass's own hedge was the tell; it noticed the shape and kept the enum. |
| DT-13 | A participant's visibility should be scoped to the intervals they attended | The roll is temporal (`02.02.01`) — a dep who played 2pm–3pm arguably should not see the 10am tracking | ❌ **REJECTED** — no NDA in music works this way. You are covered for the project, not for your hours, and a dep who saw who was in the live room at 2pm has the information anyway. Interval-scoped confidentiality is a model no musician would recognise, produces absurd states (a player who cannot see the record they played on because it was comped after they left), and buys nothing an attacker could not get by asking the drummer. Participant status is per session, binary. The temporality of the roll matters for *attribution*; it does not matter for *confidentiality*. |
| DT-14 | Embargo without an expiry is fine — shelved work simply stays hidden | The surface pass's "Work is shelved forever" row, which is correct as far as it goes | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED, WITH THE ROT ADDRESSED.** Indefinite embargo is right for genuinely shelved work. But it is indistinguishable from the failure case — the work released, off-platform, and nobody lifted (Q-01) — and the two have opposite correct outcomes. An auto-expiry would be DT-03's scheduled leak wearing a longer clock, so that is out. The answer is to make the question cheap and repeated (90-day nudge, 4 times) and then to remove the Producer from the critical path entirely (D-11: any participant proves public availability; 72-hour objection; lift). This resolves Q-01 including its terminal form — the Producer's account is gone — because the lift predicate is "the recording is demonstrably public", which nobody's absence can falsify. **An objection inside that window is a stated-ground challenge to the evidence, resolved by the dispute engine (D-19–D-22), never a Producer veto** — which is what keeps the Producer off the critical path instead of reinstating them at the last step. |
| DT-15 | Embargo suppresses the growth loop's invitation, and that is a bug to design around | The claim inbox (`02.03.02`) is the acquisition mechanism; domain Q-05 makes it load-bearing | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A COST, REJECTED AS A BUG.** "Come claim your credit on <unreleased album>" is an email naming the confidential work, sent to an address the platform does not control, that gets forwarded and breached. The growth loop asking to be fed here is the loop asking to be the leak. Embargo taxes acquisition and that is the correct trade — the alternative is leaking to acquire users, which ends the relationship that produced the credit. The invitation fires at lift; the shell and credit persist meanwhile; the Producer can still tell the human directly, which is domain Q-05's real answer regardless. |
| DT-16 | Role-based access can express the multi-hyphenate's visibility | Standard RBAC instinct; the Role Lens is a role table | ❌ **REJECTED** — and `personas.md` predicts it: "any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer' fails them immediately." An Operator who is *also* a player on the session holds Operator (blind) and participant (full) simultaneously. Access must be the **union**, and the union is correct — she was in the room. The blindness that survives is not a read restriction on a human but a publication restriction on a **surface**: her Musician view shows the credits, the room's discography still cannot list the work. Permission here is per-session participation, not per-role, and any spec that resolves this by picking one role will lock a real person out of their own credit. |
| DT-17 | Lift is an event that publishes a set of credits | The surface pass's happy path: "credits become public in one event" | ❌ **REJECTED** — it is a **state on the recording**, evaluated at query. As an event it silently drops every credit that arrives afterwards: a player claiming their credit six months post-release (the claim inbox's whole purpose), an amendment's successor, a late attestation. Those would stay embargoed forever, on a released record, with no control anywhere in the product to fix it — a bug that surfaces months after launch as "my credit never appeared" and is unfixable without a re-model. As a state it is one predicate and the late cases are free. |
| DT-18 | Embargo blocks attestation, since participants can't act on an invisible credit | Plausible reading of "invisible everywhere else" | ❌ **REJECTED, and the inverse is a real benefit.** Attestation (`02.04.01`) is a participant act, and participants are exactly who *can* see an embargoed credit. So the tier accrues privately during the embargo and the credit publishes **already attested** — arriving on release day as a verified fact rather than a tier-zero row that then has to chase confirmations from people who have moved on. Embargo does not merely tolerate attestation; the embargo period is the ideal window for it, because everyone is still in contact. Worth stating so nobody "fixes" this by gating attestation on visibility. |
| DT-19 | If a participant can see the credit, they can see everything about it | Simplification; participants are one trust class | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED, ONE EXCEPTION.** Per-participant trust tiers are over-engineering (see DT-13). But the **release date** is a distinct confidential fact with its own leak economics — dates leak, leaked dates are a marketing failure, and the date is not something a remote string player who dialled in for 40 minutes needs. So: the credit is always visible to participants; the date hint is shared at the Producer's option (D-08). One field, one exception, no tier system. |

The roll **is** the participant set (D-14), including present-but-uncredited parties. If the roll is wrong, the permission boundary is wrong — `02.02.01`'s dominant failure ("nobody sets up the roll at all") is therefore also this feature's dominant failure, and the passive-seeding mitigation it proposes must not seed a *participant* it cannot justify.
- Touches **07 Projects** ([../../07-projects-collaboration/](../../07-projects-collaboration/)) — **shared entity.** The project is the correct scope for the confidentiality default (DT-05, D-01 chain step 1) because the NDA is per client, not per day. The subtle case is the *path*: both endpoints public, the path between them discloses the confidential session. `02.01.03` D-02 makes paths visible by design, so path rendering must be embargo-aware or the transparency feature is the leak. Any search index is a derived projection and must invalidate on lift; the stale direction must only ever be under-publishing.

Axis B (Unlisted) is a property this page renders and only this page: its Q-01 (search-engine indexability) compounds here — an indexed page that briefly showed a prematurely-lifted credit (DT-07) is cached outside the platform, which is why revert is honest about exposure rather than an undo.

This resolves the CX file's R-03 caveat: visibility is *not* an input to merge eligibility, so the pair stays rejected as a standing relationship — the race is real and the fix lives in merge's implementation, not in a new edge.
- Touches **02.01.06 Correction & Amendment** ([02.01.06-credit-correction-amendment.md](./02.01.06-credit-correction-amendment.md)) — **state race.** An amendment in flight does not block a lift; the successor publishes on commit (DT-17's state model makes this free). The lift notification must name the pending amendment.
- Touches **02.03.02 Claim Inbox** ([../02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding/](../02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding/)) — **notification suppression.** No claim invitation while embargoed (D-15); it fires at lift. - Touches **02.04.01 Attestation** ([../02.04-attestation-credit-confidence/](../02.04-attestation-credit-confidence/)) — **permission intersection, benign.** Attestation is a participant act, so it proceeds during embargo and the credit publishes already-attested (DT-18). Nothing must gate attestation on visibility.
- Touches **02.05 Credit Dispute Resolution** ([../02.05-credit-dispute-resolution.md](../02.05-credit-dispute-resolution.md)) — **permission + trigger dependency (new).** The adjudicator is not a participant but must read the credit: a logged, non-publishing grant scoped to the case (D-18). The shared dispute engine (domain D-03) serves 6 other domains and must therefore carry a general "privileged read of a non-public record" concept, not a credits-specific hack. **D-21 adds a second, new inbound edge**: a contested lift objection is routed to `02.05` after inline platform re-verification fails to settle it. That case is *not* a factual dispute about a contribution — it argues only D-03's predicate, on one of D-19's closed grounds — so it must not be conflated with a credit dispute, and it carries a mandatory resolution SLA (Q-06) that ordinary credit disputes do not.

No auto-propagation (D-16); manual registration warns at the point of it.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([../../09-rights-ownership/](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **permission + leak path.** `09.02` split capture happens in the same human moment as credit capture (domain Q-06) and must inherit the same confidentiality, or the split sheet publishes what the credit hid. `09.06` rights-evidence-public-record is a publication surface over the same session and must be embargo-aware independently. Whether WeJammin models the NDA itself or only its effect is 09's call (Q-03).
- Touches **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([../../16-venues-studios-spaces/](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) and **17 Live Booking** — **leak path.** A room's client confidentiality is an Operator's commercial asset; the booking record, the calendar and any "now recording" surface must not name an embargoed work or its parties (DT-11).
- Touches **22 Analytics & Insights** ([../../22-analytics-insights/](../../22-analytics-insights/)) — **aggregate leak (DT-08, D-05).** Any public statistic computed over the credit graph — leaderboards, "most-credited", trend counts — must exclude embargoed credits for non-participants. This load is **unbudgeted in current specs** and is recorded here so 24's capacity planning sees it rather than discovering it.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity.** The alias/pseudonym identity is 01's, and it is the preferred answer for ghost production: a public credit held by an alias, with the alias↔human link as the confidential fact (see Q-02). The former-name case (a credit under a deadname across a back catalog) is likewise 01 + `02.01.04`, **not** this feature — Axis B would leave the name on the work's ledger, which is precisely the harm. Routed deliberately rather than fudged.

| D-19 | **An objection to an evidence-based lift must state a ground, from a closed list**: (i) the evidence identifies a different recording; (ii) the URL/identifier is not publicly reachable; (iii) other — free text, logged, shown, routed to a human, never auto-handled and never auto-resolved | Both enumerated grounds challenge the predicate D-03 already assigns the platform ("demonstrably public"), so the platform takes on no new kind of adjudication. An open ground space would let the objection assert anything; a closed list restricted to D-03's own predicate keeps D-20 and D-21 decidable. An authorised-vs-unauthorised ground was explicitly declined: no source defines that predicate, and creating it would make domain 12 the authority for "authorised release" (Q-07). |
| D-20 | **A timely objection pauses the lift at the status quo** — the credits stay embargoed while the objection is contested; they neither publish nor become permanently unpublishable | An objection is a challenge to evidence, not a veto over a released work. Pausing preserves Q-04's compensating control against a forged store URL, which auto-lifting-anyway would destroy, while refusing the Producer the unilateral block that a veto would restore. Costs one new state row (`Lift pending — objection contested`) and the two copy strings rewritten above. |
| D-21 | **A contested objection resolves through the dispute engine (`02.05`), with inline platform re-verification as the free first rung. A resolution SLA is mandatory** | Only resolver that names an accountable decider without inventing one: the engine already exists as a domain cross-cut (domain D-03), the adjudicator already has a privileged non-publishing read of an embargoed record (D-18), and the routing habit already exists (`02.02.01` D-10 — an unresolved objection becomes a dispute). Re-verification alone cannot resolve what the first automated pass already passed, and resolution-by-silence is refused by D-03/DT-03. The SLA is not optional: an embargoed status quo plus an unbounded resolver is a de facto veto. Value deferred to Q-06. Per-objection operational load lands on domain 24 and is unbudgeted in current specs (see Cross-Cut Notes). |
| D-22 | **Re-submission of evidence after an objection is unbounded** — no cap, no cooldown, no escalation counter | Every contested objection is already in front of an adjudicator (D-21), so the resolver bounds the loop and no counting rule needs inventing. The indefinite-veto hazard applied only to the veto option D-20 declined. A threshold N would be unsourced and would create a new domain-24 abuse pattern; a "first one free" rule is composed from nothing and is redundant once every contested objection is on the engine. **If D-20 is ever revisited to a true veto, this decision must be revisited with it** — veto + unbounded re-submission is the one combination that leaves a permanent costless weapon. |
