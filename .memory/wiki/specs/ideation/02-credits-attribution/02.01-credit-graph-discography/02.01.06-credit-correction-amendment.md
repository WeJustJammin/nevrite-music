# Feature: Credit Correction & Amendment

> **Number**: 02.01.06
> **Parent**: [Credit Graph & Discography](./02.01-credit-graph-discography-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The non-adversarial path for fixing a credit that is simply wrong — a misspelled name, a role recorded as "Producer" that should be "Additional Production", a take attributed to the wrong of two guitarists — where **nobody is fighting about it**. Distinct from disputes (`02.05`), which are for contested facts. Most wrong credits are wrong by accident, and routing an accident through an adversarial case is hostile to the people the platform depends on.

**Deep Think addition** — the sweep listed disputes but no agreed-correction path, implying every error is a fight.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (propose on own) | Proposes a correction to any credit naming them — "this was rhythm guitar, not lead", "my name is spelled wrong". Approves corrections others propose to their credits. Cannot unilaterally rewrite an attested credit; the party who attested it has to agree, or it becomes a dispute. |
| Producer | Full (propose on session) | Corrects credits on works they captured — they are usually the one who notices, and usually the one who made the mistake at 2am. Their correction to an unattested credit applies with notice; to an attested one it needs the credited party. |
| Operator | Read-only | Can propose a correction to their room's own credit ("we're 'Studio Two', not 'Studio 2'"). No visibility into contributor corrections on sessions they hosted. |
| Fan | None | No proposal path. Explicit absence and a deliberate divergence from Discogs/MusicBrainz, where crowd correction is the model. Crowd-sourced correction of a *verified* graph would destroy the property that makes it verified — the whole point is that facts come from people who were there. |

## Behavior

An amendment is a **proposed successor** to a credit record, not an in-place edit. The original is superseded, never overwritten, and both remain in history. This is what makes the graph trustworthy to a reader six years later during a dispute: they can see what it said, when it changed, and who changed it.

The approval rule follows the evidence, not the hierarchy:

- Correcting an **unattested** credit — the asserter can amend it; the named party is notified.
- Correcting an **attested** credit — everyone who attested it must agree. Their attestation was of the *old* fact; it cannot silently transfer to a new one.
- No agreement → it is not an amendment. It is a dispute (`02.05`), and it escalates automatically rather than dead-ending.

**Materiality is a flat identity test, and it lives in exactly one place: `02.04.01` D-10.** Any change to **party, role or work** invalidates every attestation on the row and requires re-asking; nothing else does. There is no entailment layer above it — `02.04.02` D-13/DT-08's "carries if the new claim is entailed by the attested one" rule is superseded (DQ-05.1), so this feature never asks whether a role change is a strengthening, a narrowing or a widening. Three consequences:

- This file's own worked correction in the Overview — "a role recorded as 'Producer' that should be 'Additional Production'" — **invalidates its attestations and re-asks**. That is the accepted cost of a flat test, bounded by `02.04.01` D-09's cadence caps and D-21's request collapsing.
- **Instrument changes never invalidate.** Instrument is not part of the claim identity: uniqueness is (party, role, work) (`02.01.01` D-03) and instrument is multi-valued on one record (`02.06` D-16).
- **No taxonomy operation invalidates** (DQ-05.2), including `02.06` D-11's one retroactive pending-alias mapping. A credited party who disagrees with the mapping their pending alias received comes *here* to amend the role — which then runs the flat test like any other role change.

None of this touches D-01 (amendment is supersession), D-03 (a party change is never an amendment) or D-05/DT-03 (the successor's tier is re-derived from who agrees, never inherited).

That escalation is the important design property: the two paths are one continuum, and the system should discover which one it is on rather than making the user classify their own problem up front. A user who has to choose between "correction" and "dispute" before they understand the disagreement will choose wrong.

### Happy Path

1. Someone notices a credit is wrong and proposes the fix inline, from the credit itself
2. System determines whose agreement is required, from the credit's attestation set
3. Those parties are asked, with the old and new values shown side by side
4. All agree → successor record commits; the superseded original stays in history; discographies update
5. Everyone on the credit is told what changed and by whom

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Required approver disagrees | Auto-escalates to a dispute (`02.05`); the proposal becomes the dispute's opening evidence, so nothing is retyped | "This is now a dispute" — with context carried over |
| Required approver never responds | Proposal stays open and **never auto-applies** — waiting out a party can never force an amendment. Reminders fire at day 3 and day 10; at **day 14** with no response, non-response is treated as failure-to-agree and **auto-escalates to a dispute (`02.05`)**, carrying the proposal as opening evidence — the same continuum as an explicit disagreement (D-02, D-06). The record therefore neither silently mutates nor freezes forever. Q-01 remains open only to confirm the exact 3/10/14-day window. | "Waiting on <n> people — becomes a dispute if unanswered by <date>" |
| Correction is cosmetic (spelling of a role label) | Should not require the full agreement circuit; taxonomy label changes (`02.06`) are not credit changes | Applied directly |
| Correction refines the role ("Producer" → "Additional Production") | **Invalidates every attestation on the row and re-asks** (`02.04.01` D-10, flat identity test). No entailment exemption exists — `02.04.02` D-13/DT-08 is superseded. Accepted cost, bounded by `02.04.01` D-09's cadence caps and D-21's collapsing | "The people who confirmed this confirmed the old role — they'll be asked again" |
| Correction adds or refines the **instrument** ("Drums" → "Drums — brushes") | **Never invalidates.** Instrument is not part of the claim identity — uniqueness is (party, role, work) (`02.01.01` D-03) and instrument is multi-valued on one record (`02.06` D-16) | Applied; attestations and tier unchanged |
| Credited party disagrees with the canonical their **pending alias** was mapped to (`02.06` D-11) | Routed here, correctly: the vocabulary is not the venue. The mapping itself never invalidated anything (`02.04.01` D-10 narrowed by DQ-05.2), so this is an ordinary role amendment and runs the flat test | Amendment proposal, with the usual approval circuit |
| Correction changes the *party* (wrong person credited) | Not an amendment — it is a deletion plus a new credit, and it moves reputation between two humans. Forced through dispute | "This reassigns the credit — that's a dispute" |
| Credit is embargoed | Amendment proceeds privately among participants | Participants only |
| Two parties propose conflicting corrections to the same credit at once | Second proposal does not silently overwrite the first; the credit carries at most one open proposal. The later proposer is shown the pending one and can co-sign, counter, or open a dispute | "A correction is already pending on this credit" — with the pending values shown |
| Proposer is also the credit's only required approver (self-attested, unattested credit) | Applies with notice to the named party, per the unattested rule — but a self-only correction that also changes provenance-bearing fields is flagged, so nobody silently self-upgrades their own record | "Corrected — <named party> notified" |
| Credit has no attesters and its asserter is gone (dead/departed/deactivated) | No approval authority can be resolved, so **no correction applies** — fail-safe: an unverifiable change is never auto-committed. The credit enters a defined `correction-blocked` state, stays readable and unchanged in place, and is flagged for Operator review. This interim state is codeable today; Q-02 remains open only for **who** may ultimately authorize an orphaned-credit correction. | "Correction unavailable — no reachable approver on this credit (flagged for review)" |
| Amendment after export (`02.08`) or after royalty registration (domain 10) | Downstream consumers hold a stale fact; re-emission required. This is the expensive case and it is common | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / /write-be-spec: re-emission trigger + royalty correction-filing deadline]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Proposed | Someone submitted a correction | Pending marker on the credit; old vs new shown |
| Awaiting approval | Attesters must agree | "Waiting on <n> people" |
| Applied | All agreed | Successor live; "corrected <date>" with history link |
| Escalated | Someone disagreed, or no response by the day-14 window | Contested marker; dispute opened |
| Correction-blocked | No approval authority resolvable (orphaned credit) | "Correction unavailable — flagged for review"; credit unchanged |
| Withdrawn | Proposer pulled it | Credit unchanged; proposal retained in history |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Correction and dispute are the same feature and should be one flow | Adversarial: both change a credit; two flows is duplication | ❌ **REJECTED on UX, ✅ CONFIRMED on plumbing.** They share a state machine and an evidence trail, but the *social* framing is the entire point. Making a Producer open a "dispute" against a session player to fix a typo poisons a working relationship the platform exists to serve. The correct model is one continuum with a low-friction agreed path that escalates automatically when agreement fails — so the user never classifies their own problem, and the escalation carries context rather than restarting. |
| DT-02 | Amendment can overwrite the credit in place, with an audit log alongside | Simplest data model; audit logs are standard | ❌ **REJECTED** — the graph's value is that a reader can reconstruct what was asserted *at a point in time*, because that is what a dispute six years later actually argues about. An audit log adjacent to a mutable row is a second source of truth that will drift from it. Supersession makes history the primary structure rather than a side-channel. This confirms sub-domain Q-01: the record wants to be append-only, and that is a `/create-prd-architecture` call with real consequences for the locked Supabase decision. |
| DT-03 | The successor credit inherits the superseded credit's provenance tier so a correction never *demotes* a well-attested fact (resolves Q-03) | Correcting a typo on a Producer-attested credit should not throw away three attestations | ❌ **REJECTED** — an attestation is of the *old* value, and the whole model (D-02, `02.04`) is that presence-verified confidence attaches to a specific asserted fact. Inheriting the tier would let a bad actor launder a fabricated value into a high-confidence record by amending a well-attested one. The correct model: when a required attester *agrees* to the successor, that agreement **is** a fresh attestation of the new fact, so the successor's tier is **re-derived** from who agreed — not carried over. A purely cosmetic label change (see edge case) is exempt because the underlying fact is unchanged. This is what the `02.04` cross-cut note means by "re-derived, not inherited." |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Amendment is supersession, never in-place edit | DT-02. History is the product's evidentiary value, not a debugging aid. |
| D-02 | Disagreement auto-escalates to dispute, carrying the proposal as evidence | DT-01. Users must not have to classify their own conflict before they understand it. |
| D-03 | Changing the credited *party* is never an amendment | It moves reputation (and potentially income) between two humans. That is definitionally contested even when both parties are agreeable. |
| D-04 | No crowd correction | Diverges deliberately from Discogs/MusicBrainz. Verified-by-presence is the differentiator; crowd editing dissolves it. |
| D-05 | Successor's provenance tier is re-derived from who agrees, never inherited (resolves Q-03) | DT-03. Attestation is fact-specific; inheritance would let a fabricated value launder into a high-confidence record. Cosmetic label changes are exempt. |
| D-06 | A required approver's non-response is treated as failure-to-agree: after a bounded reminder window (default day 3 / day 10 / escalate day 14) the proposal auto-escalates to a dispute (`02.05`) — it never auto-applies and never freezes | Extends D-02 (no agreement → dispute) to silence, so waiting cannot force an amendment and an absent party cannot freeze the record forever. Resolves the harmful horns Q-01 named; Q-01 now covers only the exact window duration. |
| D-07 | An orphaned credit (no attesters, asserter gone) is never auto-corrected; it enters a defined `correction-blocked` state, stays readable/unchanged, and is flagged for Operator review | Fail-safe default so an unverifiable change is never silently committed. Q-02 remains open only for who may ultimately authorize the correction. |
