# Feature: Public Discography

> **Number**: 02.01.02
> **Parent**: [Credit Graph & Discography](./02.01-credit-graph-discography-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The public page that renders everything a party has been credited on, with the provenance tier visible on every line. This is the **destination that makes capture worth doing** — a Producer will not run a capture flow to feed a database, but they will to have a page that wins them the next client. It replaces the manually-maintained credits list in a Notes app and the PDF one-sheet (`personas.md`, Musician workaround).

It is also the platform's only organic distribution channel (DT-01): the page a professional builds to win work is the same page that pulls their uncredited collaborators into the claim inbox (`02.03.02`). Capture → page → claim → more capture is the only self-reinforcing loop the product has.

A **party** here means any entity that can hold a credit — a person, a Band, or an organization (a studio, a venue, a label). The page is party-type-agnostic by construction: whatever party types `02.01.01` permits to hold credits get a page, on the same mechanics, with role vocabulary and Role Lens differing per type.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own page) | Owns their discography page: designates up to 3 **primary role families** (expanded by default), pins up to 6 highlight credits per family, and sets per-credit visibility via `02.01.05` (Private hides a line from the page without deleting the record). Cannot add, edit or reorder a *credit* here — the page is a projection of `02.01.01`; the only authored state on it is curation (pins, primary families, family order). Sees their own suppressed lines (Private, Embargoed, no-public-work-identity) marked with why; visitors see nothing in their place. Sees the owner-only attestation nudge on every low-tier line (`02.04.01`). |
| Producer | Full (own page) | Identical mechanics — their discography is sales collateral, the thing a client checks before booking. Their page shows their **credits** (producer, engineer, mixer, and any performance roles), and nothing else: no capture-rate, session-count, or platform-behavior statistic (D-10). A Producer who both engineered and played on a work appears in two role families for that one work, because that is two credits (`02.01.01` D-01). |
| Operator | Full (the room's discography) | The room's page: "records made here". Renders **works plus the room's own credit role** (recorded at / mixed at / mastered at / rehearsed at) and the work's public primary artist — never the contributor roster (D-05). Directly commercial: a studio's discography is why it gets booked. A human who owns a studio has **two** pages — their person page and the room's page — because they are two parties; the same work legitimately appears on both. Who may act for a room (owner, manager, staff) is domain 01's mandate model, not this feature's. |
| Fan | Read-only | Browses any public discography; follows a party from it; walks from a record they love to the humans on it. Sees the **simplified** confidence signal only (`02.04.02` D-04) and any applicable plain-language outcome qualifier — no tier taxonomy, no evidence trail, no attestation prompt, no claim affordance, no contested state, no archive filters beyond year and role. Sees **Follow**, never a contact control (D-11). Sees public-tier records only: embargoed, Private and no-public-work-identity records do not exist for them — absent, not greyed, not counted (D-09). A **contested** record is not in that set: a contest is never published and never suppresses, so the line renders unchanged at its existing derived tier with no public marker (D-06; `02.01.01` D-11; credits-attribution CX-16) and counts normally — what the Fan does not see is the contested *state*, not the credit. |

## Behavior

A discography is a **query, not a document** — the current projection of the credit graph for one party. Nothing is authored here except curation. The page's job is to make provenance legible without making it noisy: a reader must be able to tell at a glance which credits are session-captured-and-attested versus self-asserted-and-unconfirmed, because that distinction is the entire competitive claim against Jaxsta, Muso.AI and Discogs.

### Structure

**Grouping is by role family, then chronology** (D-07). A flat reverse-chronological list is the wrong default for the multi-hyphenate — it renders a drummer who occasionally mixes as an incoherent smear. Grouping by taxonomy *leaf* is equally wrong: `02.06` is granular enough that "drums", "percussion" and "programmed drums" would fragment one drummer into three groups of one. The page groups on the taxonomy's **family** tier and renders the leaf role on each line ("Drums — brushes", "Percussion — congas").

**The default view's size is independent of the credit count** (D-04). This is what makes the 4,000-credit session player and the 4-credit newcomer the same page:

| Element | Bound | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Summary header | Always rendered | Total credits, total distinct works, count per role family, active year range. This is what a 4,000-credit page leads with: "1,847 credits across 1,203 works · Drums 1,612 · Engineering 201 · Production 34 · 1996–2026" |
| Role families expanded by default | ≤ 3 | `personas.md` — the Musician holds "3+ simultaneous roles". Owner designates them; absent a designation, the 3 families with the most credits |
| Role families collapsed | All others | Rendered as a one-line row with a count; expands in place |
| Lines per expanded family | ≤ 20 (≤ 6 pinned + ≤ 14 most recent) | The group view is a **shortlist**, not a table |
| Default page maximum | ≈ 60 credit lines | Constant regardless of whether the party has 4 credits or 40,000 |

Everything beyond that lives in the **archive view** — one role family at a time, a paginated table, 50 rows per page, cursor-paginated (never offset: the graph mutates under a reader and offset pagination silently skips and duplicates rows during a merge). Archive filters: year range, leaf role, collaborator, work type, and — for professionals only — provenance tier floor.

**Counts are viewer-relative** (D-09). Every number rendered on the page counts only records visible *to that viewer*. The owner's header says 1,847; a Fan's header on the same page says 1,203, because 644 are Private, embargoed, or lack a public work identity. This is not a caching bug — it is the same rule as `02.01.05` D-04: a count that includes suppressed records leaks their existence, and the existence of the work is usually the confidential part.

**Ordering within a family**: owner-pinned highlights first (in the owner's order), then reverse chronological. The platform never computes "prominence" (D-13) — it has no popularity data it owns, and a computed ranking would put a chart hit above the record the owner is actually proud of. Curation is the owner's; chronology is the fact's.

### Happy Path

1. Fan or client lands on a party's discography — from search, a work page, a shared link, an artist's link-in-bio, or an organic search result
2. System resolves the party, evaluates visibility per viewer (`02.01.05`, enforced at query — never a render filter), and derives each surviving credit's tier (`02.04.02`)
3. Page renders: viewer-relative summary header, ≤ 3 expanded role families of ≤ 20 lines each, remaining families collapsed with counts, provenance tier and any applicable plain-language outcome qualifier on every line — full tier vocabulary for a professional viewer, simplified signal for a Fan
4. Reader narrows: expands a collapsed family, opens a family's archive view, or filters it ("as producer", "2024–2026", "with <other party>")
5. Reader walks the graph: clicks a work (→ its ledger, `02.01.01`, itself viewer-scoped) or a co-contributor (→ their discography)
6. Reader acts — Fan: **Follow** (domain 20). Client: **service enquiry**, routed through domain 05's gating, never a direct message (D-11)
7. Owner viewing their own page additionally sees: suppressed lines with a reason marker, curation controls, and an attestation nudge on every low-tier line ("2 people were in that room — ask them", `02.04.01`) — owner-only, never rendered to a visitor
8. Owner taps a nudge → `02.04.01` sends the request → attester confirms → tier re-derives → the public line changes without the owner touching the page again. Each confirmation pulls a collaborator into the product (DT-01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Party has zero credits | Empty state offering the two on-ramps, not an apology | Owner: "Import your existing credits" (`02.03.01`) / "Start a session" (`02.02`). Visitor: an identity page with no discography section — not "0 credits" |
| Party has only unconfirmed self-asserted credits | Page renders in full; every line tier-marked. Honest but visibly weak — this is intentional pressure toward attestation, not a defect | Tier markers throughout; owner-only attestation nudge per line (`02.04.01`) |
| Party has credits, but every one is Private or embargoed | Page renders **identically to zero-credit** for visitors. Deliberate: "1,847 credits, none shown" tells a competitor the party is working and roughly how much | Visitor: the zero-credit state. Owner: full list, each line marked Private / Embargoed-until-release |
| Credit embargoed (`02.01.05`) | Absent from the public page entirely — not greyed, not "1 hidden credit", not in any count | Nothing. Owner sees it on their own view with an embargo marker and its trigger |
| **Credit contested (`02.05`)** | Stays on the public page **at its existing derived tier, with no public contested marker** (D-06). A contest is an objection, not evidence — it neither raises nor lowers the tier (`02.04.02` D-02), and publishing it would let a free unilateral filing impose a public reputational sanction on a rival's best credit the week of their pitch | Visitor: an unchanged line. Participants and the credited party: contested marker on the record view (`02.01.01`) |
| **Party contests a credit naming *them*** ("I'm not on this record") | They do not wait for adjudication — they set the credit **Private** on their own page unilaterally (`02.01.05`), which stops the public harm immediately. The dispute continues about the record's truth on other parties' pages and the work ledger | The line vanishes from their page within the restrictive-propagation window (D-12); dispute proceeds privately |
| Party merged into another (`02.01.04`, CX-02) | Loser's URL 301s to the survivor; both credit sets union onto one page; **pin sets union too and may exceed the 6-per-family cap** — the cap is enforced on write, never on read. The page renders every merged pin and prompts the survivor to re-curate. Silently dropping a pin would delete an owner's curation without telling them | Redirect; combined page; owner sees "You have 9 highlights in Drums — choose 6" |
| Merge chain (A→B, later B→C) | A's URL 301s directly to C — a single hop, not a chain. A redirect chain on an indexed page bleeds link equity and eventually exceeds crawler hop limits, deindexing the very page DT-01 depends on | One redirect, correct destination |
| Merged party's credits had different visibility | Merge re-evaluates visibility **per credit**, never inheriting the survivor's (CX-01 Q5). An embargoed credit unioned into a public page is a leak caused by an unrelated admin action | Public page shows only what was already public; owner sees both sets, correctly marked |
| **Massively prolific party (session player with 4,000 credits)** | The group view is bounded by construction (D-04) — ≤ 3 families × ≤ 20 lines ≈ 60 lines, whatever the total. The 4,000 rows are the archive view's problem: cursor-paginated, 50/page, filterable. There is no unbounded render path on this feature at any credit count | Summary header leads with the real number ("1,847 credits, 1996–2026"); shortlist below; "View all 1,612 as Drums" opens the archive |
| Work has no public identity (unreleased-and-never-released, or a captured session for a work never published anywhere) | The credit cannot render publicly — there is nothing to render it *against*, and a working title is often the confidential part. Suppressed from the public page; present on the owner's view | Owner: "Untitled session, 12 Mar 2025 — not published". Visitor: absent, uncounted |
| Work ledger is partly embargoed (tracking session public, mixing session confidential) | The work appears on the page. Clicking through shows a viewer-scoped ledger — and the ledger's contributor **count** counts only visible lines. "7 contributors, 4 shown" would leak that 3 exist | 4 contributors, stated as 4 |
| **Credit marked not-in-final-master (`02.01.01` D-09)** — a tracked contribution comped out of the delivered master | The credit is **retained and qualified, not deleted** (`02.01.01` D-09). When it otherwise passes ordinary publication, per-credit visibility, embargo, and public-work-identity gates, it remains in its normal role family and chronology and renders once with the plain-language qualifier **"not in final master."** Its tier is untouched; the qualifier never widens visibility or decides ownership, registration, royalty, payment, union, or neighbouring-rights treatment. A publicly visible qualified line counts once in every ordinary viewer-relative line and aggregate; a suppressed qualified line is absent and counts zero, with no teaser or hidden-count disclosure. | Owner: the line renders with the marker and trigger date. Visitor: the otherwise-visible line plus "not in final master" only — never the owner-only trigger date, comp-out reason, or delivery history. Existing suppression rules render it absent and uncounted. |
| Credit's tier drops after the page was public (retraction `02.04.03`, or amendment invalidation `02.04.01` D-10) | The line stays; its label changes downward. The page others cited now says something weaker | Label change; credited party notified (`02.04.02`) |
| **Ring-detection demotion fires on a credit on this page** (`02.04.04`) | **Nothing on this page changes.** Demotion is score-only — it never moves the rung (`02.04.02` D-19), so the public label is unaffected, and every rung-gated consumer reads the same invariant rung (`02.04.02` D-20). The demotion moves traversal rank (`02.01.03` D-05), search ordering and dispute weighting. **Accepted cost, stated plainly**: a fully corroborated fiction keeps its top-rung label here indefinitely | Nothing, to anyone, ever. No label change, no notification, no reordering of this page |
| Credit amended (`02.01.06`) — a cited line now points at a superseded record | The superseded credit's URL **resolves**, rendering the successor with "this credit was amended on <date>" (D-08). It does not 404 | Successor + amendment notice |
| Suppressed credit's URL is requested directly (deep link from a leaked/cached page) | **404** — indistinguishable from never-existed (D-08, `02.01.05` D-04). A 403 confirms the record exists, which is the leak | Not-found, identical to a nonexistent credit |
| Owner sets a credit Private while a visitor has the page open / a CDN holds it cached | Restrictive change (visible → hidden) must propagate publicly within a short bound; permissive change (hidden → visible) may lag (D-12). The asymmetry is CX-01's safe-direction rule: a stale *hidden* record is a leak with a delay; a stale *shown-as-absent* record is merely a slow update | Owner: "hidden — live within a minute". Visitor: the line disappears on next load |
| Owner curates on two devices at once | Last-write-wins, scoped **per role family**, not per page. Whole-page LWW would let a pin edit in Drums silently clobber a family-order change made in Engineering seconds earlier | No conflict UI; concurrent edits to different families both survive |
| Two parties, same name, not yet resolved (`02.01.04`) | Two pages, both live, each with disambiguators. Never auto-merged | "John Smith (drums, Leeds)" / "John Smith (keys, Bristol)" |
| Party is a Band that holds credits directly | The Band gets its own page with the same mechanics. **Member pages do not inherit the Band's credits** (D-14) — synthesizing a member credit from a band credit manufactures an assertion nobody made, and would attribute a 2014 album to a drummer who joined in 2018. That is the reconstruction failure the product exists to prevent | Member page shows a **Membership** section ("Member of The X, 2012–2018") linking to the Band's page; individual credits appear only where individually asserted |
| Party deletes their account | The party's own discography page ceases to exist (404). Whether their name survives on *other* parties' pages and work ledgers is the joint-record erasure question (`02.01.01` Q-01) and is **not** decided here | 404 on the page; other pages per `/create-prd-security`'s erasure model |
| Party dies | Page persists and keeps rendering — the credits are permanently true and feed neighbouring-rights income after death. Who may then curate or set visibility is estate governance (sub-domain Q-02, domain 01.10), not this feature | Page unchanged; curation controls per 01.10 |
| Imported work title contains a slur, spam, or markup (`02.03.01`) | Every string from an external catalog is treated as untrusted and escaped. **No credit field on this page renders user- or import-supplied markup, ever.** Abusive content is a report → domain 24; the page is not a moderation surface | Escaped text; report affordance on the work, not the credit |
| Scraper harvests the whole public graph | Expected and partly acceptable (DT-04) — the assertion is publishable, the **evidence is not**. Attester identities, session metadata, timestamps, capture origin and traversal paths are never on this page at any tier. Rate limiting is mandatory on this endpoint (`security-first`), stricter unauthenticated than authenticated — proposed at 60 req/min/IP with per-ASN aggregation for datacenter ranges, ratified at `/create-prd-security` | Human readers never hit it; a harvester gets throttled |
| Render fails (query timeout, upstream error) | Returns a real **5xx** — never 200 with an empty or error body. A soft-404 on a public indexed page gets it deindexed, which costs the acquisition channel DT-01 makes the whole feature worth building | Retry surface; correct status to crawlers; never a blank page |
| Graph query is slow for a huge party | The group view's bound (D-04) means the default path never scales with credit count. The archive view degrades to a longer skeleton, not a timeout — reduce the page window before failing | Skeleton, then rows |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Page requested | Skeleton: summary header shape + 3 family blocks of skeleton rows |
| Empty | No credits for this party | Owner: empty state with Import (`02.03.01`) / Start a session (`02.02`) CTAs. Visitor: no discography section — never "0 credits" |
| Suppressed-empty | Credits exist; none visible to this viewer (all Private / embargoed / no public work identity) | Visitor: **identical to Empty** — deliberately indistinguishable. Owner: full list, each line marked with its suppression reason |
| Sparse | Few visible credits, all low-tier | Populated layout plus an owner-only attestation nudge per line (`02.04.01`) and an import CTA. Never shown to visitors — a "this page is thin" signal aimed at a client is self-harm |
| Populated | Visible credits exist | Viewer-relative summary header; ≤ 3 expanded role families of ≤ 20 lines; remaining families collapsed with counts; tier on every line |
| Partial | Some lines resolvable, some not — a work whose identity failed to resolve, a party mid-merge, a tier mid-re-derivation | The resolvable lines render. Unresolvable lines are **omitted from the public view and from counts**, and shown to the owner with "resolving". A public page never renders a half-known fact as a known one |
| Archive | "View all N as <role family>" | Paginated table, 50 rows/page, cursor-paginated; filters: year range, leaf role, collaborator, work type, (professionals only) tier floor |
| Redirected | Party merged into a survivor (`02.01.04`) | 301 to the survivor's page, single hop even after a merge chain; union of both credit sets |
| Error | Query failed | Retry surface + correct 5xx status. Never a blank page, never a 200-with-error — this URL is public and indexed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The discography page is the product's primary organic acquisition surface, not just a profile tab | Every incumbent it replaces (Discogs, AllMusic, Jaxsta) is load-bearing for SEO; a credit page is the canonical answer to "who played X on Y" | ✅ **CONFIRMED** — and it inverts the cold-start problem: the page a Producer builds to win clients is simultaneously the page that pulls in the four uncredited players on it via the claim inbox (`02.03.02`). Capture → page → claim → more capture is the platform's only self-reinforcing loop. This makes the page a MUST, not a nice-to-have. |
| DT-02 | Fans do not care about credits — this page is professional-only | The Fan persona has "no professional stake" and "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" | ❌ **REJECTED** — Discogs and AllMusic are fan products, not professional ones. "Who played that solo" is mainstream fan behavior. But the persona constraint still binds: the Fan's view must be a *reading* surface with no tier jargon, no claim affordances, no attestation prompts. Same data, different product — which is exactly what `personas.md` says the Fan surface must be. |
| DT-03 | Showing provenance tiers publicly will embarrass users into not using the product | Adversarial: nobody wants "unconfirmed" next to their name | ❌ **REJECTED** — inverted. The tier is the *incentive engine*: an unconfirmed line is a visible gap the owner can close in one tap (`02.04.01`), and the person who can close it is a collaborator we then acquire. Hiding tiers would remove both the differentiator and the growth loop. Risk is real but it is a copy/design problem, not a model problem. |
| DT-04 | Publishing the graph publicly hands the moat to any competitor with a scraper — Discogs' entire dataset has been mirrored repeatedly | Adversarial, and the obvious objection to DT-01: the page cannot be both the distribution channel and the vault | ✅ **CONFIRMED as a real risk, and it resolves the feature's shape rather than defeating it.** The moat was never the assertions — those are liner notes, and liner notes are public by nature. The moat is (a) *how the assertion got there* and (b) *the position that let us capture it*. Neither is scrapeable. So the public page exports the **claim** (party, role, work, tier label) and never the **evidence** (attester identities, session metadata, capture timestamps, origin mechanism, traversal paths). A competitor who scrapes every page gets Discogs — which already exists and already lost. This is the concrete design rule the page enforces at every tier, including for authenticated professionals. |
| DT-05 | Publishing a "contested" badge is the honest thing to do — the page's whole claim is transparency about how we know | The apparent implication of D-02 (tier is public) and of transparency-as-differentiator | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the most consequential finding in this pass.** A contest is not evidence; it is an objection. `02.04.02` D-02 says labels describe the *evidence*, and domain D-10 says the platform does not adjudicate truth — a public contested badge does both wrong at once: it publishes a conclusion the platform hasn't reached, sourced from a free unilateral action. The harm is asymmetric and gameable: file a dispute on a rival's best credit the week of their pitch and the platform brands it for you, for months, at no cost. Against that, the cost of *not* publishing is that a possibly-wrong credit stays up at the tier it already had — the state of the world before anyone objected. Contest stays with participants (D-06). The one case that genuinely needs a fast public remedy — "I am not on this record" — is already solved without adjudication: the named party sets it Private unilaterally, today. |
| DT-06 | A public credits page is unambiguously good for its owner | The feature's premise — the page exists to win work | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH CONSTRAINT — the page's biggest lie is omission.** A 20-year Producer with 3 captured credits has a page that reads as a 3-credit career: worse than no page, and it damages precisely the earliest, most valuable professional user. The page must therefore never present itself as a *résumé* (implying completeness) but as *evidence* (implying only what it shows). Concretely: no "complete" or "full" framing anywhere in the copy; the sparse state's owner-facing CTA is import (`02.03.01`), not encouragement; and the summary header's year range is derived from credits present, never from a career the platform can't see. This is also the strongest argument that `02.03.01` Import is closer to the wedge than its Should placement implies — without pre-join history, every professional's page launches thin. Flagged for MoSCoW, not decided here. |
| DT-07 | Grouping by role is enough — pick the role off the credit and group | The obvious reading of D-01, and what the SURFACE pass assumed | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED but underspecified — it breaks on the real taxonomy.** `02.06` is granular by design (an inexact role invalidates a RIN emission), so grouping on the *leaf* role shatters one drummer into "drums", "percussion", "programmed drums", "drum machine" — four groups of one, which is the incoherent smear D-01 exists to prevent, arriving by a different door. Grouping must happen at the taxonomy's **family** tier with the leaf rendered per line. This places a hard requirement on `02.06`: it must expose a stable family/leaf hierarchy, and every leaf must have exactly one family. A flat vocabulary makes this page unbuildable. |
| DT-08 | A discography page is a web page — if a credit changes or goes away, the page just changes | Implicit in "a discography is a query, not a document" | ❌ **REJECTED as too simple — credits databases get *cited*.** AllMusic and Discogs are cited in press, sleeve notes, court filings and academic work; a page that silently rewrites under a citation is a worse authority than the PDF one-sheet it replaces. But citability cannot be absolute either, because confidentiality outranks it (`02.01.05` DT-01). The resolution is an explicit asymmetry (D-08): **correction loses to citability** — a superseded or demoted credit's URL still resolves, rendering the successor with an amendment notice, because the citing reader deserves to find out what changed. **Confidentiality beats citability** — a Private or embargoed credit's URL 404s, indistinguishable from never-existed, because a 403 confirms the record exists and that confirmation is the leak. |
| DT-09 | The Producer's page should show works where they were the *capturer*, as a distinct reputational signal from contributing | The SURFACE pass asserted this; the intuition is that capture discipline is commercially meaningful to a client | ❌ **REJECTED — and it corrects this file's own earlier claim.** "Capturer" is not a role in `02.06`; it is *origin* metadata, and origin is already an input to the tier (`02.04.02`) — so it is already visible, per line, where it belongs. Republishing it as a page-level statistic ("94% of sessions captured") would (a) duplicate the tier's job, (b) publish a platform-compliance scoreboard on a professional's sales page, ranking a 20-year engineer with 3 captured sessions below a newcomer with 5 — DT-06's harm, weaponised, (c) be trivially gamed by capturing throwaway sessions, and (d) violate DT-04 by exporting evidence-shaped data. A Producer who ran a session and contributed has a producer/engineer credit; a Producer who ran one and didn't has no credit, and no page-level consolation prize. |
| DT-10 | A Band's credits should roll up to its members' pages — "drummer, The X, 3 albums" is exactly what a real CV says | Domain-expert expectation; liner notes credit both the act and the personnel | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a real user need, REJECTED as a credit mechanism** (D-14). Inferring a member credit from a band credit manufactures an assertion nobody made — the reconstruction failure the product exists to prevent, committed by the platform itself. It also gets the facts wrong in the ordinary case: membership has *eras*, and roll-up attributes a 2014 album to a drummer who joined in 2018. The need is met without inventing facts: the member's page carries a **Membership** section (sourced from domain 01.03/01.04) linking to the Band's page, and individual credits appear only where individually asserted. Conditional on `02.01.01` Q-03 — if Bands cannot hold credits at all, D-14 is moot and the Membership section stands alone. |
| DT-11 | The page needs a contact affordance, or "time-to-book drops" (Musician success criteria) never happens | The feature's commercial purpose — a client who cannot enquire is a client lost | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED with a hard role split** (D-11). The client's enquiry is the point. But `personas.md` names the Fan anti-persona as "harasses artists through open contact surfaces", and fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude (D-11/D-13) — an open contact control on the highest-traffic public page in the product is that vector, armed and indexed. Split by persona, as `02.01.03` D-03 already does for traversal: Fans get **Follow** (domain 20) and nothing else; clients get a **service enquiry** routed through domain 05, inheriting 05's gating, rate limits and abuse controls. The page owns the CTA's placement, never its channel. |
| DT-12 | Counts are counts — show the party's totals | Trivially obvious, and wrong | ❌ **REJECTED** (D-09). Every count is viewer-relative, because a count that includes suppressed records leaks their existence, and the existence of the work is usually the confidential part (`02.01.05` D-04). "1,847 credits, 1,203 shown" is a breach with extra steps. The owner's header and a Fan's header on the same page legitimately disagree — an implementer must know this is the specification, not a caching defect. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Role-grouped, not flat-chronological, by default | The multi-hyphenate is the structural centre persona (`personas.md`). A flat list of a drummer/mixer/writer's credits reads as noise and misrepresents the career it is meant to prove. |
| D-02 | Provenance tier is shown publicly on every line | It is the competitive claim. A credits page that doesn't show how it knows is Jaxsta with better typography. |
| D-03 | The Fan view is the same data with a different product surface | Per `personas.md` Fan unique constraint — no tier jargon, no claim/attest affordances, reading only. |
| D-04 | **The default view's size is independent of the credit count** — ≤ 3 expanded role families × ≤ 20 lines (≤ 6 pinned + ≤ 14 recent) ≈ 60 lines, whether the party has 4 credits or 40,000. Everything else is the archive view: one family at a time, 50 rows/page, cursor-paginated | Resolves Q-02. The 4,000-credit session player is not an outlier to handle later — it is the *most valuable* professional on the platform, and a 4,000-row page fails the page's only purpose (winning the next client) exactly as badly as a 4-row one. Making the shortlist O(1) means there is no unbounded render path at any scale, and the archive is then a solved problem (a filtered table). "3 primary families" comes from `personas.md`: the Musician holds "3+ simultaneous roles". Cursor, not offset — the graph mutates under a reader, and offset paging silently skips and duplicates rows mid-merge. |
| D-05 | **The room's discography lists works + the room's own credit role + the work's public primary artist — never the contributor roster** | Resolves Q-03. A room's contributor roster is a **client list**, and `02.01.05` states plainly that "a room that leaks its client list loses its clients". Three reasons converge: (a) the room's credit is "recorded at X" — one credit, which entitles it to say the record was made here, not to publish who was in the building; (b) each contributor's visibility is their own per-credit setting and the room has no standing to override it; (c) the Operator's own commercial interest is confidentiality, so the feature that looks more valuable is the one that loses them bookings. Contributors remain reachable by clicking through to the work, scoped by their own visibility. |
| D-06 | **A contest is not published.** A contested credit stays on the public page at its existing derived tier with no public marker; the contested marker is a record-view affordance for participants and the credited party. A party contesting a credit *naming them* sets it Private unilaterally (`02.01.05`) — an immediate remedy requiring no adjudication | DT-05. Resolves the suppression rule `02.01.01` deferred here. A contest is an objection, not evidence; publishing it means the platform brands someone on a free unilateral filing, which is both an adjudication it refuses to make (domain D-10) and a weapon (dispute a rival's best credit the week of their pitch). The cost of not publishing is that a possibly-wrong credit stays at the tier it already had — the status quo ante. |
| D-07 | **Grouping is by role *family* (taxonomy parent tier), with the leaf role rendered per line** | DT-07. `02.06` is granular by design, so leaf-grouping fragments one drummer into four groups of one — D-01's failure by another route. Places a hard requirement on `02.06`: a stable family/leaf hierarchy, every leaf in exactly one family. |
| D-08 | **Confidentiality beats citability; correction loses to it.** A superseded/amended credit's URL resolves to its successor with an amendment notice. A Private or embargoed credit's URL 404s — never 403 | DT-08. Credits databases get cited (press, sleeve notes, filings, academia), so silently rewriting under a citation makes the page a worse authority than the PDF it replaces. But a 403 confirms the record exists, and that confirmation is the leak `02.01.05` D-04 forbids. The asymmetry is deliberate and must be specified, because an implementer will otherwise make both cases consistent — and either consistency is a bug. |
| D-09 | **Every count on the page is viewer-relative** — counts only records visible to that viewer | DT-12. "1,847 credits, 1,203 shown" leaks the existence of 644 confidential works. The owner's header and a Fan's header on the same page legitimately disagree; this is the spec, not a caching defect. |
| D-10 | **No platform-behavior statistic on the page** — no capture rate, session count, response time, or completeness score | DT-09. Origin is already an input to the per-line tier, where it belongs. A page-level score duplicates it, publishes a compliance scoreboard on a professional's sales collateral, ranks a 20-year engineer below a newcomer, is trivially gamed, and exports evidence-shaped data against DT-04. |
| D-11 | **Contact is split by persona**: Fans get Follow (domain 20) only; clients get a service enquiry routed through domain 05's gating. The page never hosts a direct-message control | DT-11. `personas.md` names the Fan anti-persona as a harassment vector, and fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude on the product's highest-traffic public page. Mirrors `02.01.03` D-03's split for traversal — the same adversary, the same page. |
| D-12 | **Visibility propagation is asymmetric**: a restrictive change (visible → hidden) must be publicly effective within ≤ 60 s; a permissive change (hidden → visible) may lag | CX-01's safe-direction rule applied to the page. A stale hidden record is a leak with a delay; a stale absent record is a slow update. 60 s because a Private-set is usually a reaction to an active harm — a leak, a dispute, a harassment episode — and a control that takes longer than a minute reads as a control that doesn't work. Mechanism (cache/CDN invalidation) is `/create-prd-architecture`'s; the requirement is this feature's. |
| D-13 | **The platform never computes prominence.** Ordering within a family is owner-pinned highlights first (owner's order), then reverse chronological | The platform has no popularity data it owns, and a computed ranking puts last week's jingle above a 2015 platinum record — or the chart hit above the record the owner is proud of. Curation is the owner's job; chronology is the fact's. Pins are capped at 6 per family (a shortlist stops being one past that), enforced on write, never on read. |
| D-14 | **A Band's credits do not roll up to member pages.** A member's page carries a Membership section linking to the Band; individual credits appear only where individually asserted | DT-10. Roll-up manufactures assertions nobody made — the reconstruction failure the product exists to prevent, committed by the platform — and misattributes eras (a 2014 album to a drummer who joined in 2018). Conditional on `02.01.01` Q-03. |
| D-15 | **An otherwise publicly eligible not-in-final-master contribution renders once with the plain-language qualifier "not in final master."** | CQ-02 Option B. The record remains retained, tier-unchanged, and in its ordinary role-family/chronological position; the qualifier never widens visibility. It counts once when visible and zero when suppressed. Visitors never receive the owner-only trigger date, reason, or delivery history. This display decision does not determine any Domain 10 registration, ownership, royalty, payment, union, or neighbouring-rights outcome. |
