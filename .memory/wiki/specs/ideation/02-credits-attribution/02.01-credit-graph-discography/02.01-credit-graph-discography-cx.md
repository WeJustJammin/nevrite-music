## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Visibility & Embargo ↔ Credit Search

**Relationship**: Embargo is enforced at the record and evaluated at query time. Traversal reads the graph without passing through the discography page, so a render-layer filter would leak every embargoed credit through search while the page correctly hid it. The suppression must happen inside the traversal itself — an embargoed edge does not exist for a non-participant, so paths through it do not exist either.

The subtle failure: a *path* can leak a credit even when the endpoint is public. If A and B are 2 hops apart via an embargoed session, showing the path ("A → <session> → B") discloses the confidential session. Path rendering (`02.01.03` D-02) must be embargo-aware or the transparency feature becomes the leak.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sets the embargo; sees full paths through their own sessions.
- **Musician**: sees paths through sessions they participated in; embargoed sessions they were not in are invisible.
- **Operator**: sees that their room hosted the session, never the path through it.
- **Fan**: embargoed edges and any path traversing them do not exist.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The record owns visibility; search owns nothing and caches nothing authoritative. Any search index is a derived projection and must be invalidated on embargo change — a stale index is a leak with a delay.
2. **Trigger chain**: Release trigger fires → records become public → index must update. If indexing fails, the credit is public in the record but missing from search — the safe direction. The reverse (index updates, record still embargoed) must be impossible by construction.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the core of it. Participant status in `02.02` determines traversal visibility here. Permission is per-session, not per-user-role.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Release trigger notifies every participant that their credit went live (`02.01.05` happy path 5). Search sends nothing.
5. **State transition conflict**: Embargo lift and a merge (CX-02) can race — merging a party mid-embargo could union an embargoed credit into a public discography. **Resolved (`02.01.05` D-17)**: visibility travels with the **credit**, never with the party. A merge re-points a credit's party without touching its confidentiality, so merge must union credits and re-derive the page per credit — never union two rendered pages. The record refuses to inherit the survivor's visibility (`02.01.01` merge edge case). No leak: the survivor's page shows exactly the credits the survivor is entitled to publish.

### CX-02: Resolution & Merge ↔ Public Discography

**Relationship**: A confirmed merge collapses two parties into one. Every credit that named either now names the survivor, both credit sets union onto one page, and the loser's public URL redirects. Because the discography is public and indexed (`02.01.02` Q-01), the merge is not an internal data operation — it is a change to a live, linked, possibly cited web page.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: confirms merges about themselves; afterwards their page is the union. If wrongly merged, someone else's career is now on their page — visibly, publicly, and to their audience.
- **Producer**: confirms work merges; their session's ledger re-points.
- **Operator**: confirms room merges; the room's discography unions.
- **Fan**: experiences only a redirect and a fuller page. No notice, no participation.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Merge is the writer; discography is a projection with no independent state. No conflict — provided nothing else caches party identity.
2. **Trigger chain**: Confirm → collapse → re-point credits → redirect URLs → invalidate projections. Partial failure is dangerous: credits re-pointed but redirect not installed leaves a 404 on an indexed page. Should be atomic; `/create-prd-architecture` owns whether it can be.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — merge confirmation rights are per-entity-type (`02.01.04` D-02), and the confirmer may not be the page owner (a Producer confirming a work merge changes pages they do not own).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every party whose discography changed should be told. A page changing under its owner without notice is exactly how a wrong merge goes unnoticed for years (`02.01.04` DT-01).
5. **State transition conflict**: Merge racing an embargo lift (see CX-01 Q5) is **resolved** (`02.01.05` D-17: visibility travels with the credit; merge re-derives the page per credit and never inherits the survivor's visibility). Merge racing an in-flight amendment (CX-07) remains real; its ordering is undecided (owner: `/create-prd-architecture`).

### CX-03: Correction & Amendment ↔ Credit Record

**Relationship**: An amendment does not edit a credit — it creates a successor and marks the original superseded. The record's `Superseded` state exists solely to serve this. The pair is the reason sub-domain Q-01 (append-only vs mutable) is open: two features independently demand that history be primary rather than a side-channel.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: proposes on credits naming them; approves proposals on their attested credits.
- **Producer**: proposes on their sessions' credits; usually the one who notices.
- **Operator**: proposes only on the room's own credit.
- **Fan**: no access — deliberate divergence from crowd-corrected competitors (`02.01.06` D-04).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The record owns the fact; amendment owns the proposal. Two concurrent proposals on one credit must not both apply — last-write-wins would silently drop a correction. Serialize per credit.
2. **Trigger chain**: Propose → determine approvers from the attestation set → collect → commit successor. Disagreement escalates to `02.05` carrying context (`02.01.06` D-02) rather than failing.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is derived rather than declared — who must approve comes from `02.04`'s attestation set on that specific credit, not from a role.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Everyone on the credit is told what changed and by whom. Downstream (`02.08` consumers, domain 10 registrations) may need re-emission — the expensive case.
5. **State transition conflict**: Amendment racing a dispute filing on the same credit — both want to change its state. The dispute should win and absorb the amendment as evidence.
