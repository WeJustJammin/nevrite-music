# Feature: Per-Track Contribution Log

> **Number**: 02.02.02
> **Parent**: [Session Capture](./02.02-session-capture-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The attribution of actual work to actual people at the granularity the work happened: this person, on this track, doing this. Where the roll call (`02.02.01`) knows who was in the room, this knows what they did — the difference between "five people were present" and "she played the solo on track 4, he engineered, the bass is a replacement from Tuesday's dep".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own contributions) | Sees and corrects what has been attributed to them; adds a contribution the Producer missed ("I also did the backing vocals on 3"). Cannot attribute work to anyone else — that is the Producer's assertion to make, and letting players attribute each other reopens the self-assertion hole the whole domain closes. |
| Producer | Full | Attributes contributions across tracks. The trust broker (`personas.md`): their attribution carries a higher provenance tier than a self-claim, which is exactly why it must be *their* act and why it must be almost free to perform. |
| Operator | None | The room hosts the session; it does not know who played what. A studio that could see per-track attribution on client work is holding commercially sensitive information it has no reason to hold. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Never sees the log — it is working data. What eventually reaches a Fan is the published credit (`02.01.02`), which is the log's committed output, not the log. |

## Behavior

The log's design problem is stated plainly in `personas.md`: **the Producer's work happens inside a DAW, not a browser**, and any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and fill in a web form will not be used. So the log is either populated from inside the session environment, or it is not populated.

Granularity is the second problem, and it is a genuine trade-off rather than a detail. Take-level attribution is the most truthful (a comp'd vocal is literally seven takes from two singers) and the most useless to a human filling it in. Track-level is coarse (one bass track, one bassist) but matches how people actually talk about sessions. Contribution-level — a named thing a named person did — is what a credit needs.

The honest position at breadth: **track-level is the human unit; take-level is only viable if it is derived, never typed.** If a plugin can read the DAW's own structure, take-level detail is nearly free and should be captured. If capture is manual, asking for take-level detail guarantees no capture at all.

### Happy Path

1. Session is running; roll call is populated
2. Contributions are attributed as work happens — ideally derived from the session environment (track names, routing, who is on which input), not typed
3. Producer adjusts what was derived; the system's guess is a starting point, never the record
4. Each contribution binds a party (from the roll) to a role (from the taxonomy, `02.06`) on a track
5. At close (`02.02.03`) the log becomes draft credit records for confirmation

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nothing is logged during the session | The common case. The close prompt must be able to work from the roll alone — degraded but not empty | Close prompt asks "who did what?" against the roll |
| Track is a comp of multiple performers | Multiple contributions on one track. Correct and common; a single-performer-per-track model is simply wrong | Multiple attributions per track |
| Part is replaced later (dep re-tracks the bass) | Both contributions are true at different times. The replaced one is not deleted — the person did the work, and often got paid for it | Both shown with intervals; superseded-in-the-mix is a mix fact, not a credit fact |
| Contribution attributed to someone not on the roll | Roll is authoritative; the person is added to the roll first (possibly as remote/async) | Prompt to add them to the roll |
| Track names are meaningless (`Audio 1`, `Gtr_FINAL_v2`) | Derivation degrades to nothing. The realistic default — DAW hygiene is not a thing | Manual attribution; derived guesses withheld rather than shown wrong |
| Producer attributes work to themselves that others did | Anti-persona (`personas.md`): the Producer inflating their own contribution. Counter-attestation (`02.04`) is the check — the players see what was attributed and can refuse | Contributors see all attributions at close |
| Session captured offline | Log stays local, syncs later (sub-domain D-03) | "Will sync" marker; capture never blocked |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Session open, nothing attributed | Tracks (if known) with no attributions; the roll offered as the source |
| Derived | Session environment supplied a guess | Attributions marked as suggestions, awaiting the Producer |
| Attributed | Producer confirmed/entered | Clean attribution list |
| Pending sync | Offline capture | Local, marked |
| Committed | Session closed, credits emitted | Read-only; changes now go through `02.01.06` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Capture should be take-level, since that is the truth and the platform's claim is truth | The provenance maximalist position; DDEX RIN supports fine granularity | ❌ **REJECTED as a manual requirement, ✅ CONFIRMED as a derived one.** A comp'd vocal is seven takes from two singers — true, and nobody will ever type it. Demanding take-level guarantees zero capture, and zero capture is the one outcome that kills the thesis. Take-level is only in scope where a plugin reads the DAW's own structure. This is the clearest case in the domain where **the achievable capture beats the complete capture**. |
| DT-02 | The Producer will fill in a well-designed web form at the end of the session | The cheapest possible product; no plugin, no integration, ships in a week | ❌ **REJECTED** — `personas.md` states the constraint as a fact about the persona, not a preference: work happens in a DAW, and a flow requiring them to leave it will not be used. The persona's worst accidental behavior is closing a session without capturing splits, and the design instruction is that the lazy path must be the correct path. A web form makes the correct path the effortful one. **This is what makes `02.02` expensive, and it is not negotiable away** — if the answer is a form, the wedge is Sound Credit, which already exists and already failed. |
| DT-03 | Derived attribution guesses should be shown even when confidence is low — the Producer can just fix them | Standard ML-assist UX; something beats nothing | ❌ **REJECTED** — a wrong guess that the Producer accepts by reflex enters the graph at the **producer-asserted tier**, which is a high-trust tier. Confidently wrong high-tier credits are more damaging than absent ones, because the graph's entire value proposition is that its high tiers are trustworthy. Below the confidence floor, show nothing and ask. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Track-level is the human unit; take-level only where derived | DT-01. Manual take-level attribution is a guarantee of no capture. |
| D-02 | Players attribute only themselves; the Producer attributes anyone | Cross-attribution between players reintroduces unverifiable self-assertion by proxy. The Producer's assertion is meaningful precisely because they ran the room. |
| D-03 | Derived guesses below a confidence floor are withheld, not shown | DT-03. A reflexively-accepted wrong guess enters at a high trust tier. |
| D-04 | A replaced part does not delete the original contribution | The person did the work and was usually paid for it. Mix decisions are not credit decisions. |
