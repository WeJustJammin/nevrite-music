# Feature: Per-Track Contribution Log

> **Number**: 02.02.02
> **Parent**: [Session Capture](./02.02-session-capture-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The attribution of actual work to actual people at the granularity the work happened: this person, on this part, doing this. Where the roll call (`02.02.01`) knows who was in the room, this knows what they did — the difference between "five people were present" and "she played the solo on track 4, he engineered, the bass is a replacement from Tuesday's dep".

It exists because `problem-statement.md` names the root cause as **absence at the point of truth**. The roll proves absence is solved for *presence*; this feature is where absence is solved for *contribution*, which is the fact a credit is actually made of. It is the only place in the product where the sentence "who played what" is answered by someone who knows, at a time when they still know.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own contributions) | Sees every contribution attributed to **them** on this session, on their own phone, without opening the Producer's view. Adds a contribution the Producer missed ("I also did the backing vocals on 3") — which enters as **their own assertion**, not the Producer's. Corrects a contribution attributed to them by **proposing a correction** (D-08), never by overwriting. Sets the AI disclosure axis (`02.10`) on their own contributions only — nobody else can (DT-08). Cannot attribute work to anyone else: letting players attribute each other reopens the self-assertion hole the whole domain closes, and cross-attribution is unverifiable by construction. **Exception**: a Musician holding a delegated log-write grant (D-11) — the assistant engineer — attributes on the Producer's session, and the log records *them* as the asserter. |
| Producer | Full | Attributes contributions across every part, to any party on the roll, in ≤2 taps per contribution (pick party → pick role). Accepts, edits or rejects derived suggestions; bulk-attributes across parts (one act for "all four string players on tracks 8–14"). Grants log-write to a named roll party (D-11). Adjudicates proposed corrections live at the close prompt. The trust broker (`personas.md`): their attribution **of someone else** carries a higher provenance tier than a self-claim, which is exactly why it must be *their* act and why it must be almost free to perform. Their attribution **of themselves** carries no such premium (DT-04) — they are not brokering trust when the subject is them. |
| Operator | None | The room hosts the session; it does not know who played what. A studio that could see per-track attribution on client work is holding commercially sensitive information it has no reason to hold, and the room that surveils its clients' sessions loses the clients. Explicit absence. **Multi-hyphenate clarification**: the house engineer who owns the studio *and* runs the desk has Full access on that session — but as **Producer**, because they are on the roll as engineer, not because they own the walls. Room ownership grants nothing here; roll membership grants everything. This distinction is the whole reason `personas.md` calls the personas lenses rather than account types. |
| Fan | None | Never sees the log — it is working data, and it is working data about sessions that are frequently under NDA (`02.01.05`) or permanently non-public (D-12). What eventually reaches a Fan is the published credit (`02.01.02`), which is the log's committed output at its committed visibility, not the log. |

## Behavior

### The design constraint that decides everything else

`personas.md` states it as a fact about the persona rather than a preference: **the Producer's work happens inside a DAW, not a browser**, and any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and fill in a web form will not be used. So the log is either populated from inside the session environment, or it is not populated. Every rule below is downstream of that.

### What a contribution is

A contribution names **one party, in one role, on one instrument, on one part, over one window** — and records **who asserted it and when** (D-05).

- **Party** — a person, an unclaimed shell (`02.02.01` D-03), or an **entity** (a band, an ensemble, a contracted orchestra). Entities are first-class in `personas.md` and DT-05 is why they must be first-class here too.
- **Role** — from the taxonomy (`02.06`). Never free text at the log's level; a missing role becomes a pending alias (`02.06` D-03) and never blocks the contribution.
- **Instrument** — a separate axis from role (`02.06` D-02), optional. Defaults from the party's roll capacity when that capacity names exactly one instrument; otherwise it is unset rather than guessed.
- **Part** — the thing the work was done to. A DAW track is the best-known instance of a part, and the reason for the feature's name — but the part exists whether or not a track is legible (DT-06).
- **Window** — the interval the work happened in, resolved against the party's roll interval (`02.02.01` D-02).
- **Asserter + assertion time** — who said this and when, relative to the session. Not a tier. The log never stamps a tier; it supplies evidence and `02.04.02` derives (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-09).

**One party may hold several contributions on one part, and this is the normal case, not an edge case.** The multi-hyphenate who played the drums and engineered the take holds two contributions on that take — not one contribution with two roles. Role is the unit that binds to the taxonomy, exports to RIN (`02.08`), carries its own gear (`02.09`), and carries its own AI disclosure (`02.10`). Collapsing two roles into one contribution makes all four of those wrong simultaneously.

### Granularity: the trade-off, and the resolution

Granularity is a genuine trade-off rather than a detail. Take-level attribution is the most truthful (a comp'd vocal is literally seven takes from two singers) and the most useless to a human filling it in. Track-level is coarse (one bass track, one bassist) but matches how people actually talk about sessions. Contribution-level — a named thing a named person did — is what a credit needs.

**Resolved (D-01): part-level is the human unit; take-level is only viable if it is derived, never typed.** If the session environment can read the DAW's own structure, take-level detail is nearly free and is captured. If capture is manual, asking for take-level detail guarantees no capture at all — and zero capture is the one outcome that kills the thesis.

### The three capture channels

| Channel | What it is | Assertion it produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Derived** | The session environment supplies the structure — parts, routing, input assignment, take lanes. Reaches take level. Ceiling is set by Q-01. | A *suggestion*. Never a contribution until a human accepts it. |
| **Manual** | The Producer (or a delegate) attributes directly. Part-level. Always available; the floor the product must work at. | The asserter's assertion, at assertion time. |
| **Delivery** | A remote contributor delivers an artifact — a stem via `07`, a service deliverable via `05`. The delivery *is* the capture event (D-14). | The deliverer's own assertion, self-asserted, awaiting Producer binding. |

**Delivery-as-capture resolves the remote problem** (formerly Q-02). The post-2020 normal case is a contributor who is never in the Producer's DAW at all, and the instinct is to build them a capture form — which is the same mistake as building the Producer one. But the remote contributor already performs an unambiguous act: they send the file. That act carries who, what part, and when. `02.02.04` CX-06 reaches the same conclusion from the evidence side — the artifact is the only corroboration a remote contributor can offer. So the delivery surface is the capture surface, and there is no second form to ignore. Where the delivery happened off-platform (WeTransfer, Dropbox, a link in a DM) the Producer attributes manually and the contribution is honestly weaker: producer-asserted, with no artifact in the platform to corroborate it. That is the correct outcome, not a gap to paper over.

### Derived suggestions: the confidence rule

**A suggestion is pre-filled only when the derivation is deterministic** (D-10): the environment states a fact that maps 1:1 onto exactly one roll party — an input named for a party who is the only roll match, a take lane already labelled with a name on the roll. **Probabilistic matches never pre-fill.** A fuzzy name match, a guess from track ordering, an inference from who is usually on bass — these are allowed to *rank the picker* and nothing more.

The reason is not UX politeness. A wrong suggestion that the Producer accepts by reflex enters the graph as a **producer assertion**, which is a high-trust tier. Confidently wrong high-tier credits are more damaging than absent ones, because the graph's entire value proposition is that its high tiers are trustworthy (DT-03). Below the deterministic bar, show nothing and ask.

### The roll is authoritative

Attribution to a party not on the roll is not blocked and not permitted either — it triggers an **inline roll addition** (CX-01), synchronously, without a modal, because a modal at that moment is friction at exactly the wrong point. The party is added (possibly as a shell, possibly as remote/async) and the attribution completes in the same gesture.

Attribution resolves against **time**: a take recorded at 2pm attributed to the drummer whose roll interval ended at 1pm is detectable, and is flagged — never blocked (D-15). Roll marks are made by a busy human who is late to make them, so the flag fires only when the take falls outside the interval by more than **15 minutes**, and clears with one tap that extends the interval or marks the party remote/async.

### Working data, then facts

The log is mutable while the session is open. At close (`02.02.03`) it commits into draft credit records (`02.01.01`). **After commit the log is read-only** (D-13); an edit opens an amendment (`02.01.06`). The commit is the boundary between working data and facts, and it exists so that the record of what was asserted in the room cannot be quietly rewritten afterwards — which is the failure the whole domain is built against.

### Visibility is captured, not assumed

A contribution carries a **visibility intent** set at capture, defaulting to the session's, with `never publish` as a valid value (D-12). This is not a privacy nicety. Ghost production is a large fraction of commercial pop and is contractually invisible by design; an NDA session is invisible for a fixed window; a writer on a major artist's record may be non-public forever. A log that forces publication is a log the ghost producer cannot use — so they don't log, and the platform loses the fact entirely rather than losing its publication. Capturing a private truth is strictly better than capturing nothing, and `02.01.05` is where the intent is honoured.

### Happy Path

1. Session is running; the roll is populated (`02.02.01`); the Producer is working in the DAW, not in a browser.
2. The session environment reports its structure. Parts appear in the log with no action from anyone.
3. Where a part maps deterministically to exactly one roll party, a **suggestion** is rendered against it, visibly marked as a suggestion and visibly not a record. Where it does not, the part renders bare (D-10).
4. Producer attributes what is not suggested: tap the part → pick the party (roll members first, ranked by any non-deterministic signal) → pick the role from the taxonomy. Two taps. Instrument auto-fills if the party's roll capacity names exactly one; gear (`02.09`) is present as an optional field that is never in the tap path (`02.09` D-02).
5. Producer accepts the suggestions that are right and edits the ones that are not. The system's guess is a starting point; the acceptance is the assertion, and the log records the Producer as its asserter at that moment.
6. A remote contributor delivers a stem through `07`/`05`. The delivery lands in the log as their own self-asserted contribution against the part it was delivered for, awaiting the Producer's binding (D-14).
7. A Musician on the roll opens their phone, sees the four rows naming them, and adds the backing vocal on track 3 that the Producer missed — a self-asserted contribution that will surface at the close prompt as a gap for the Producer to confirm.
8. The roll changes through the day. The dep arrives at 2pm; contributions attributed after that resolve against the dep's interval, and the original bassist's contributions are untouched (D-04).
9. At close (`02.02.03`) the log — every contribution, each with its asserter and assertion time — becomes draft credit records for confirmation. The log freezes; further changes route through `02.01.06`.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nothing is logged during the session | The common case, and the one the design must survive. The close prompt works from the roll alone — degraded but not empty (CX-02) | Close prompt asks "who did what?" against the roll; parts, if known, listed unattributed |
| No session environment at all — no parts are known | Manual capture only. The log's axis inverts: it asks "who did what" against the roll rather than "who did this part" against a track list. Contributions attach to the work (`07`) with a free-text part label (DT-06) | Roll-first list; free-text part label per contribution, capped at 120 plain-text characters |
| Track names are meaningless (`Audio 1`, `Gtr_FINAL_v2`) | Derivation degrades to nothing — no deterministic mapping exists. The realistic default; DAW hygiene is not a thing | Manual attribution; derived guesses withheld rather than shown wrong (D-10) |
| Part is a comp of multiple performers | Multiple contributions on one part. Correct and common; a single-performer-per-part model is simply wrong | Multiple attributions per part, each with its own role, gear and disclosure |
| One person, two roles on the same part (played it *and* engineered it) | Two contributions, same party, same part. Never one contribution with two roles — role is the unit that binds taxonomy, RIN, gear and AI disclosure | Two rows naming the same person; not presented as a duplicate or a warning |
| Part is replaced later (dep re-tracks the bass) | Both contributions are true at different times. The replaced one is not deleted — the person did the work, and usually got paid for it (D-04) | Both shown with intervals; superseded-in-the-mix is a mix fact, not a credit fact |
| Track is deleted in the DAW after contributions were attributed to it | The contribution survives; the person still played it. The part is marked removed-from-session and keeps its label | Contribution intact under a part marked "removed from session" |
| Contribution attributed to someone not on the roll | Roll is authoritative; the party is added inline, in the same gesture, without a modal (CX-01) | Inline roll add; attribution completes in one flow |
| Roll party with contributions is removed from the roll | **Never a silent orphan** (D-09, resolving CX-01 SQ1). Removal is not blocked — people get added by mistake — but it demands a disposition: reassign the contributions to another roll party, or remove them with the party | "X has 3 contributions. Reassign to whom, or remove them too?" — one choice, inline |
| Roll party with **confirmed** contributions is removed | Post-confirmation, the Producer cannot unilaterally erase what a contributor already attested to. Removal becomes a retraction/amendment path (`02.04.03`, `02.01.06`) | "3 of these are confirmed — removing them raises an amendment" |
| Take's recorded time falls outside the attributed party's roll interval | Flagged, never blocked (D-15). Grace of 15 minutes, because roll marks are made late by a working human | Inline flag; one tap extends the interval or marks the party remote/async |
| Producer attributes work to themselves that others did | The named anti-persona (`personas.md`): the Producer inflating their own contribution. Counter-attestation (`02.04`) is the check — the players see what was attributed and can refuse. Additionally, self-attribution is recorded as **self-asserted**, not producer-asserted (D-05, DT-04), so it never carries the trust-broker premium | Contributors see all attributions naming them at close and can dispute inline |
| Producer is genuinely a performer on their own session | The same mechanic, honestly applied. Their playing is real and gets a real contribution — it simply gets the tier a self-claim gets, exactly like everyone else's self-claim | No difference in the capture flow; the difference is in what `02.04.02` derives |
| Musician corrects a contribution the Producer attributed to them | A **proposed correction**, not an overwrite (D-08). Both the Producer's assertion and the Musician's correction survive to the close prompt, where the Producer adjudicates in the room with everyone present — the cheapest moment it will ever be resolvable (`02.02.03` edge cases) | Producer sees "Sam says this was Rhodes, not piano"; one tap to accept |
| Musician adds a self-asserted contribution the Producer never sees | Self-asserted; surfaces at close as a gap for the Producer to confirm or reject. If the Producer never responds, it persists at self-asserted tier — a true credit at an honest tier, which is the domain principle (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-08) | Row marked "you added this — awaiting confirmation" |
| Producer tries to set the AI disclosure axis on someone else's contribution | Refused. `02.10` D-04: you disclose your own contribution only. Disclosing someone else's work as AI is an assertion about their craft and is defamatory if wrong (DT-08) | Disclosure control is absent on rows the Producer does not own; the row shows "disclosure: contributor's to set" |
| Contracted ensemble — 40 players, the Producer knows none of their names | The party is the **entity** (D-07, DT-05). Forcing per-person attribution here guarantees zero capture, because the information does not exist in the room. Individual members resolve later from the ensemble's roster, or never | "London Session Orchestra — strings, tracks 8–14" as one contribution; per-person expansion offered, never required |
| Large session — 40 parties × 12 parts | Bulk attribution: select parties, select parts, one role, one act. Per-contribution tapping at that scale is the same failure as a web form, only slower | Multi-select on both axes; one confirm |
| Assistant engineer does the logging, as they do in every big room | Log-write is delegated to them as a named roll party, and the log records **them** as the asserter, not the Producer (D-11). The alternative — the assistant using the Producer's account — records an assertion the Producer never made, which is a lie at the highest-trust tier | Delegate grant is a toggle on a roll entry; delegated rows read "logged by Jo" |
| Producer logs the session from memory a week later | Permitted, and honestly recorded. `asserted_at` sits outside the session window, so the contribution is producer-asserted but **not** session-captured (DT-10). This is the exact contemporaneity distinction the product exists to make; a log that flattened it would be Sound Credit with extra steps | No friction, no scolding; the tier simply differs at `02.04.02` |
| Two devices edit the log offline and disagree | Contributions are additive: two devices adding the same (party, role, part) converge to one. Two devices *editing the same contribution* do not silently merge — the later edit surfaces as a proposed correction to the asserter (D-08), which is the same mechanic as the Musician's correction and needs no second concept | Conflict resolves at the close prompt with both versions visible |
| Session captured offline (studio basement, no signal) | Log stays local, syncs later (sub-domain D-03). Capture is **never** blocked on network, and no write ever shows a blocking spinner | "Will sync" marker; every control stays live |
| Sync rejected on reconnect — party was removed from the roll on another device | Contribution is not discarded. It is held against the removed party and surfaces as the same disposition choice as a roll removal (D-09) | "Sam was removed from the roll but has 2 contributions — reassign or remove?" |
| Close prompt opens while the Producer is mid-edit | The log stays editable and the prompt reads it live rather than from a snapshot (CX-02 SQ5). A snapshot means the Producer's fix silently does not apply | Edits made during the prompt appear in it immediately |
| Same human is on the roll twice (once as a shell, once as a claimed account) | Duplicate party; attributions may land on both. Resolution is `02.01.04`'s merge, and contributions follow the merged party | Disambiguation offered at attribution time when a likely match exists (`02.02.01`) |
| Role does not exist in the taxonomy ("turntablist doing a live re-edit") | Captured as a pending alias; **never blocks the contribution** (`02.06` D-03). Blocking the fact to protect the vocabulary inverts their importance. When that alias is later mapped to a canonical, the mapping is resolution completion, not a role change: it **never invalidates any attestation** the credit has since accrued (`02.06` D-23, `02.04.01` D-10) | Role accepted; marked pending review. Later: one informational notice per party, never a re-confirmation request |
| Pending alias contains free text that is abusive or defamatory | A user-supplied string on a record about a named third party is a moderation surface, not a vocabulary problem. The log accepts it; whether it publishes is `02.06`/`02.01.02`'s call, and this file asserts only that the log must not be the thing that publishes it unreviewed | Alias visible to session parties; public rendering is `02.06`'s decision (Q-04) |
| Malicious or malformed input from the session environment | Track names, part labels and take-lane names are **untrusted input** — they arrive from a file on a stranger's machine. Capped at 120 characters, stored and rendered as plain text, never as markup | Over-length labels truncate with the full value retained on the record |
| Track contains a sample of another recording | The sampled performers are **not** contributors to this session (DT-11). Clearance and the sampled work's rights are `09`/`11` facts. The log records who was in this session | No contribution created; the sample is flagged to `09` as a clearance obligation |
| A part is entirely AI-generated with no human performer | The contribution names the human who directed it, in whatever role they actually held, with the disclosure axis set by that human (`02.10` D-01, D-04). There is no "AI" party and no "AI" role | Normal row with a disclosure marker |
| Nobody ever confirms anything | The modal long-tail outcome. Contributions persist at their honest tier. Evidence sets the tier; it never gates the credit (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-08) — hiding unconfirmed credits recreates the empty-database failure that killed every competitor | Credits exist, labelled honestly |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Log opening; session state being fetched | Skeleton of the part list. **Locally-held contributions render immediately from the local store** — the log is never blocked on a network round-trip, because the studio has no signal and the Producer has no patience |
| Empty | Session open, nothing attributed, no parts known | The roll, offered as the axis: "who did what?" with an add-contribution affordance per roll party |
| Derived | Session environment supplied deterministic mappings | Parts with suggestions, marked as suggestions and visually distinct from records, awaiting the Producer |
| Partial | One axis known, the other not — parts but no roll, or a roll but no parts | The known axis populated; the missing one offered inline (add people / add a part label). Never an error; this is the normal mid-session shape |
| Populated (Attributed) | Producer confirmed or entered contributions | Clean attribution list, grouped by part, each row naming party + role + asserter where the asserter is not the Producer |
| Error | A write is rejected — party removed on another device, taxonomy unavailable, malformed environment payload | Inline on the affected row, with the disposition choice (D-09) or a retry. **Never a modal, never a full-view error** — one bad row must not take the log down mid-session |
| Pending sync | Offline capture (sub-domain D-03) | Local, marked "will sync"; all controls live |
| Conflicted | Two devices edited the same contribution offline | Both versions held; surfaced as a proposed correction (D-08) and resolved at the close prompt |
| Committed | Session closed, credits emitted | Read-only. Changes route through `02.01.06` (D-13) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Capture should be take-level, since that is the truth and the platform's claim is truth | The provenance maximalist position; DDEX RIN supports fine granularity | ❌ **REJECTED as a manual requirement, ✅ CONFIRMED as a derived one.** A comp'd vocal is seven takes from two singers — true, and nobody will ever type it. Demanding take-level guarantees zero capture, and zero capture is the one outcome that kills the thesis. Take-level is only in scope where the environment reads the DAW's own structure. The clearest case in the domain where **the achievable capture beats the complete capture**. |
| DT-02 | The Producer will fill in a well-designed web form at the end of the session | The cheapest possible product; no plugin, no integration, ships in a week | ❌ **REJECTED** — `personas.md` states the constraint as a fact about the persona, not a preference: work happens in a DAW, and a flow requiring them to leave it will not be used. The persona's worst accidental behavior is closing a session without capturing splits, and the design instruction is that the lazy path must be the correct path. A web form makes the correct path the effortful one. **This is what makes `02.02` expensive, and it is not negotiable away** — if the answer is a form, the wedge is Sound Credit, which already exists and already failed. |
| DT-03 | Derived attribution guesses should be shown even when confidence is low — the Producer can just fix them | Standard ML-assist UX; something beats nothing | ❌ **REJECTED** — a wrong guess that the Producer accepts by reflex enters the graph at the **producer-asserted tier**, which is a high-trust tier. Confidently wrong high-tier credits are more damaging than absent ones, because the graph's entire value proposition is that its high tiers are trustworthy. Below the deterministic bar, show nothing and ask (D-10). |
| DT-04 | The Producer's attribution carries the trust-broker premium regardless of who it names | The obvious reading of `personas.md`'s "their attestation is worth more than a self-claim" | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the sharpest thing in the file.** The premium exists because the assertion is **third-party**: a disinterested-ish person who ran the room says someone else did something. When the Producer attributes *themselves*, the third-party property evaporates and what remains is a self-claim wearing a broker's badge. `02.04.02` already hints at it ("self-interested in their own sessions") without drawing the conclusion. The concrete consequence lands here: **the log must record an asserter per contribution** (D-05), or the tier model literally cannot tell a broker's assertion from a self-claim — and the producer-as-performer is not an edge case, it is most of modern record-making. |
| DT-05 | Every contribution binds to a natural person | The provenance instinct — a person played it, so a person is credited | ❌ **REJECTED.** A contracted ensemble is booked as an entity and the Producer does not know the players' names; a band tracks live and the liner note reads "Performed by The X". `personas.md` states that bands, labels, studios and agencies are **first-class entities** that book and get paid independently of their members, and the Band is modelled as an entity in domain 01. Forcing per-person attribution where the information does not exist in the room produces the one outcome the wedge cannot survive: nothing gets logged. The party is a person, a shell, **or an entity** (D-07). Per-person expansion is offered and never required. |
| DT-06 | The log's unit is the DAW track — hence "per-track" | The feature's own name | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH TENSION — the name over-promises.** A track is knowable only through the session environment, whose existence is Q-01 and whose ceiling is unknown. With no plugin there are no tracks, and a log that requires one has no fallback at exactly the moment it is most needed. The real unit is the **part** — the thing the work was done to — of which a DAW track is the best-known instance and a free-text label is the honest floor. This matters downstream: a spec writer reading "per-track" literally will build a feature that cannot run without a plugin, which inverts the dependency. The name is retained (renaming is not this file's call) and the behavior is specified on the part. |
| DT-07 | Every captured contribution is destined to be published — that is the point of a credit | The public discography (`02.01.02`) is the log's visible destination | ❌ **REJECTED as universal, ✅ CONFIRMED as a default.** Ghost production is a large fraction of commercial pop and is contractually invisible **permanently**, not for an embargo window. Uncredited writers, doctors and additional producers are routine. A capture tool that forces publication is one the ghost producer cannot use — so they don't log, and the platform loses the fact entirely rather than losing its publication. **A private true record beats no record**, and it is still available for a dispute, an amendment, or a contract that expires. Visibility intent is captured at the contribution (D-12) with `never publish` as a first-class value. Nobody has designed for this and it silently excludes a whole professional tier. |
| DT-08 | The Producer, owning the log, can set any field on any contribution including the AI disclosure axis | Log ownership implies field ownership | ❌ **REJECTED** — `02.10` D-04 is explicit: you disclose your own contribution only, because disclosing someone else's work as AI is an assertion about their craft and is defamatory if wrong. This produces a **split-ownership row** that nobody has written down: the Producer owns the attribution, the named contributor owns its disclosure axis, and they are different people with different rights on the same record. The permission model here is not "Producer full, Musician own-rows" — it is per-field, and `/write-be-spec` will get this wrong unless it is stated. |
| DT-09 | Log-write belongs exclusively to the Producer's own account | The Role Lens's obvious reading | ❌ **REJECTED.** Big rooms have assistant engineers, and doing exactly this admin is their job. With no delegation the assistant does it on the Producer's account — recording an assertion the Producer never personally made, at the highest-trust tier, which is precisely the corruption the tier model exists to prevent. Delegation is not a convenience feature; it is an **integrity** feature (D-11). The asserter is the delegate. |
| DT-10 | A contribution logged a week later is the same fact as one logged in the room | It is the same person saying the same true thing | ❌ **REJECTED, and it is the product's whole thesis in miniature.** `problem-statement.md`: "the reconstruction is the failure" — the defect is not that reconstructed facts are false, it is that they are *remembered*, and memory is contested, lossy and self-interested. The log must therefore record **when the assertion was made** relative to the session (D-05), or it flattens contemporaneous capture and week-late reconstruction into one indistinguishable record — at which point WeJammin is a reconstruction engine with a nicer input form, i.e. every competitor. Late logging is permitted and unscolded; it simply is not session-captured. |
| DT-11 | A sampled recording's performers are contributors to the track that samples them | They are audibly on it; provenance maximalism says credit them | ❌ **REJECTED.** They contributed to a *different* recording. The log's scope is what happened in **this** session, asserted by people who were there — and nobody in the room can honestly assert who played on the sampled record. Recording it here would manufacture a session-captured credit for a session those people were never in, which is a fabrication at the highest tier. The sample is real and consequential, but as a **clearance obligation** for `09`/`11`, and the Musician's named worst-accidental (uploading a stem with an uncleared sample, exposing every collaborator downstream) belongs to that path, not this one. |
| DT-12 | A Musician's correction of a contribution about them should overwrite the Producer's version — it is their own work, they know best | Own-data instinct; also the friction-minimising choice | ❌ **REJECTED.** Overwriting destroys the assertion **pair**, and the pair is the valuable artifact: "the Producer said piano, the player said Rhodes" is information, and a silent overwrite discards it while also letting a Musician quietly rewrite the trust broker's record — the self-assertion hole again, entered through the back door. `02.02.03` already establishes that a contributor disputing their row at the prompt is the *best possible outcome*, because the room is still full. A correction routes there (D-08). The platform does not adjudicate truth (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-10); the Producer, in the room, does. |
| DT-13 | Remote/async contributors need a capture surface built for them | They are never in the Producer's DAW, and they are the post-2020 normal case | ❌ **REJECTED as a new surface, ✅ CONFIRMED via delivery.** Building the remote contributor a form is the same error as building the Producer one, and it will be ignored for the same reason. But they already perform an unambiguous act at the right moment: **they send the file.** The delivery carries who, what part, and when — it is a complete contribution assertion that costs zero additional effort, and `02.02.04` CX-06 independently concludes the artifact is the only corroboration available to them. **The delivery is the capture event** (D-14). Where the delivery was off-platform there is no artifact, the Producer attributes manually, and the contribution is honestly weaker rather than fictionally equal. This resolves the file's Q-02. |
| DT-14 | Roll capacity should gate what role a party can be attributed | The roll already knows a person's capacity; using it prevents nonsense | ❌ **REJECTED.** Roles change inside a session constantly — the A&R on the sofa picks up a shaker; the engineer sings a harmony; the drummer's capacity says "drums" the moment before they play the Wurlitzer. Gating attribution on a capacity list makes the Producer edit the roll to record a fact about a part, which is friction at the worst possible moment and will end in the wrong role being picked (`02.06` D-03's lesson: users route around blocks). Capacity **seeds** the picker's default and gates nothing. A contribution outside a party's stated capacity silently extends it. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **02.02.01 Session Roll Call** ([./02.02.01-session-roll-call.md](./02.02.01-session-roll-call.md)) — **shared entity + permission.** The roll is the attributable population; the log holds references into it. Roll-write for others (Producer only) gates log-write for others. Removing a roll party with contributions demands an explicit disposition (D-09) — this file's answer to CX-01 SQ1.
- Touches **02.02.03 Session Close Capture Prompt** ([./02.02.03-session-close-capture-prompt.md](./02.02.03-session-close-capture-prompt.md)) — **trigger dependency.** The log is half of the prompt's input (CX-02) and the log's proposed corrections (D-08) and self-asserted rows (D-14) are what the prompt's reconciliation is *for*. The prompt must read the log live, not from a snapshot.
- Touches **02.02.04 Session Attendance Proof** ([./02.02.04-session-attendance-proof.md](./02.02.04-session-attendance-proof.md)) — **shared entity.** A delivered artifact is simultaneously a contribution (here) and corroboration of remote presence (CX-06). D-14 makes them literally the same event.
- Touches **02.04.02 Provenance Tiers** ([../02.04-attestation-credit-confidence/02.04.02-provenance-tiers-credit-confidence.md](../02.04-attestation-credit-confidence/02.04.02-provenance-tiers-credit-confidence.md)) — **shared entity.** The log supplies two tier inputs that exist nowhere else: **who asserted** (DT-04 — self vs broker) and **when they asserted, relative to the session** (DT-10 — contemporaneous vs reconstructed). Without both, the tier model cannot distinguish the cases its own strength table names.
- Touches **02.06 Role & Instrument Taxonomy** ([../02.06-credit-role-instrument-taxonomy.md](../02.06-credit-role-instrument-taxonomy.md)) — **shared entity.** Every contribution binds role and instrument as separate axes (`02.06` D-02). - Touches **02.09 Gear ↔ Credit Linkage** ([../02.09-gear-credit-linkage.md](../02.09-gear-credit-linkage.md)) — **shared entity.** Gear attaches at contribution granularity ("SM7B on this vocal"), which is why it can only be captured here, and it must never enter the tap path (`02.09` D-02).
- Touches **02.10 AI Contribution Disclosure** ([../02.10-ai-contribution-disclosure.md](../02.10-ai-contribution-disclosure.md)) — **permission.** The disclosure axis lives on the contribution (`02.10` D-01) but is writable only by the named contributor (`02.10` D-04). **Split field ownership on one row** (DT-08) — the highest-risk thing on this file for a BE spec writer to miss.
- Touches **02.01.05 Credit Visibility & Embargo** ([../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.05-credit-visibility-embargo.md](../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.05-credit-visibility-embargo.md)) — **shared entity.** Visibility intent is captured at the contribution, including permanent non-publication for ghost work (D-12, DT-07). Embargo is time-bound; ghosting is not, and `02.01.05` must carry both.

Removing a party whose contributions are already confirmed crosses this line mid-gesture.
- Touches **02.01.04 Credit Resolution & Merge** ([../02.01-credit-graph-discography/](../02.01-credit-graph-discography/)) — **shared entity.** Duplicate parties (a shell and a claimed account for one human) carry duplicate contributions; contributions must follow the merge.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Part-level is the human unit; take-level only where derived | DT-01. Manual take-level attribution is a guarantee of no capture. |
| D-02 | Players attribute only themselves; the Producer attributes anyone | Cross-attribution between players reintroduces unverifiable self-assertion by proxy. The Producer's assertion is meaningful precisely because they ran the room. |
| D-03 | Derived guesses below the confidence bar are withheld, not shown | DT-03. A reflexively-accepted wrong guess enters at a high trust tier. |
| D-04 | A replaced part does not delete the original contribution | The person did the work and was usually paid for it. Mix decisions are not credit decisions. |
| D-05 | **Every contribution records its asserter and its assertion time** | DT-04 + DT-10. Without the asserter, the tier model cannot tell a broker's assertion from the Producer's self-claim. Without assertion time, it cannot tell capture from reconstruction — which is the entire thesis. Both are evidence inputs; the log never stamps a tier (domain D-09). |
| D-06 | A contribution carries exactly one role; one party may hold several contributions on one part | The multi-hyphenate playing *and* engineering a take is normal. Role is the unit that binds taxonomy, RIN export, gear and AI disclosure — collapsing two roles into one row breaks all four at once. |
| D-07 | The party may be a person, an unclaimed shell, **or an entity** | DT-05. Contracted ensembles and live-tracked bands are attributed as entities because the per-person information does not exist in the room. Per-person expansion is offered, never required. |
| D-08 | A Musician's correction is a proposed correction, never an overwrite | DT-12. The assertion pair is the artifact; the close prompt is where it is cheapest to resolve, with everyone still present. |
| D-09 | Removing a roll party with contributions requires an explicit disposition — reassign or remove | Resolves CX-01 SQ1. Blocking is wrong (people are added by mistake); silent orphaning is worse. If the contributions are already confirmed, removal becomes an amendment. |
| D-10 | Only deterministic derivations pre-fill; probabilistic matches rank the picker and nothing more | Sharpens D-03 from a confidence number into a testable rule. A 1:1 environment fact pre-fills; a fuzzy name match never does. |
| D-11 | Log-write is delegable to a named roll party; the asserter recorded is the delegate | DT-09. Assistant engineers do this work. Without delegation they use the Producer's account and manufacture assertions the Producer never made, at the highest tier. Integrity, not convenience. |
| D-12 | A contribution carries visibility intent at capture, including `never publish` | DT-07. Ghost production is permanent, not embargoed. Forcing publication means the fact is never captured at all — and a private true record beats no record. |
| D-13 | The log is read-only after commit; edits route to `02.01.06` | The commit is the boundary between working data and facts. What was asserted in the room must not be quietly rewritten afterwards. |
| D-14 | **Remote/async delivery is the capture event; there is no separate remote capture surface** | DT-13, resolving Q-02. The remote contributor already sends the file; that act carries who, what and when. Off-platform delivery falls back to manual attribution and is honestly weaker rather than fictionally equal. |
| D-15 | A take timestamped outside the attributed party's roll interval flags, never blocks; 15-minute grace | Roll marks are made late by a working human. The check is real (CX-01: the 2pm take attributed to the 1pm drummer) but it is a prompt, not a gate. |
| D-16 | Roll capacity seeds the role picker and gates nothing | DT-14. Roles change inside a session; gating forces a roll edit to record a part fact, and users route around blocks by picking the wrong role. |
