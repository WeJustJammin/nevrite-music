# Feature: Session Roll Call

> **Number**: 02.02.01
> **Parent**: [Session Capture](./02.02-session-capture-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The living list of who is in the session — physical room or remote — and in what capacity. It is the cheapest possible act of provenance: before anything else is captured, the system knows who was there. Everything downstream (the contribution log `02.02.02`, the close prompt `02.02.03`, attendance proof `02.02.04`, the credits themselves `02.01.01`) is scoped by the roll.

It solves the Producer's stated pain (`personas.md`): they are the person who *knows* who played what, and they have no tool that records it at the moment it is true. The roll is the smallest possible version of that tool — one list, maintained in seconds, that turns "who was on that session?" three years later into a lookup instead of a phone call.

Its second function is structural: the roll is where non-users enter the platform. Every session names 3–8 professionals, most of whom are not users, each of whom becomes an unclaimed shell (`02.03`) with a real page waiting for them. Friction in this feature costs users, not merely data.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Config (own entry only) | Joins the roll by invite link, by being added by the session owner, or by walking in and being marked present. Sets their **own capacity list** — `["drums"]`, `["drums", "engineering"]` — never a single choice (D-04): a UX that makes the multi-hyphenate pick one role fails them at the first screen (`personas.md`). Sees **only the roll entries whose presence intervals overlap their own** (D-09) — the people they were in the room with — plus the session owner. Can object to their own entry ("I was not there" / "do not credit me"), which flags but never deletes the owner's assertion (D-10). Cannot add, remove, or re-role anyone else — cross-attribution by players reopens the self-assertion hole the domain exists to close (`02.02.02` D-02). |
| **Producer** | Full (owns the roll) | Adds, removes and re-roles anyone; adds people who are not on the platform by name alone (D-03, sub-domain D-04); marks arrivals and departures; confirms or discards pre-seeded provisional entries (D-05); imports a contractor's personnel list for a large date (D-14). Sees the whole roll including provisional entries and every entry's author. This is the **only administrative act the Producer must perform reliably**, so it must cost seconds, not minutes — one tap per person from suggestions, the entry appearing in **< 100 ms**, and adding a person never waits on connectivity. When the Producer is also a contributor, their own entry is authored by themselves and is therefore self-asserted, not broker-asserted (D-07, DT-06). |
| **Operator** | Read-only, **opt-in per session** (who is in their room) | Sees roll entries for sessions booked in their space when the session owner has enabled room visibility — genuinely useful (who is on site, for access, catering, insurance, after-hours) and genuinely sensitive (a room that surveils its clients' rosters loses clients; a room that can see an NDA'd writer's name has a leak it never asked for). Visibility is a property of the **room's** access grant, not of any individual being present — a studio owner who is personally on the roll as engineer does not thereby grant their staff sight of it (DT-06). Sees names, capacities and presence windows; never the contribution log (`02.02.02` Role Lens). Default is **off** — see Q-03. |
| **Fan** | None | Never sees a roll, at any time, in any state. The roll is pre-publication working data; even after release, what publishes is the **credit** (`02.01.02`), not the attendance sheet. Explicit absence: a public roll would expose who was in a room with whom before anything is released — commercially sensitive to every party and of no value to a Fan, who wants "who played that solo", not a door log. |

## Behavior

### 1. The roll is temporal, not a set

Its whole value is that it changes and the changes are recorded. A drummer tracks 10:00–13:00; a dep replaces them at 14:00; a string player dials in remotely for 40 minutes at 18:00. A flat "who was on this session" list loses all three facts and with them the ability to attribute a take to the right person (D-02).

**One party = one roll entry, holding one or more presence intervals** (D-06). Someone who leaves and returns has two intervals on one entry, never two entries — two entries for one human double-count presence and can produce a duplicated credit.

**Every interval endpoint is marked `observed` or `inferred`** (D-08). Arrivals are usually observed (someone acted). Departures are almost never observed — nobody taps "I am leaving" (DT-08). Unmarked departures are inferred at wrap and stamped `inferred`. An `inferred` endpoint is never allowed to support corroboration in `02.02.04`, because corroborating a presence window with a timestamp the system invented is a fabrication dressed as evidence.

**Capacity is a list, never a single choice** (D-04). Under AFM/MU session rules a player who doubles — plays a second instrument on the same date — attracts an additional fee, so the capacity list is a **payment-relevant fact**, not a taxonomy nicety (DT-05). Capacities bind to the taxonomy in `02.06`.

Capacities have **no intervals of their own** (DT-07). Presence intervals plus the contribution log's timestamps already resolve "engineered in the morning, played bass after lunch"; a second temporal axis on the roll would duplicate `02.02.02` and make the cheapest act in the product expensive.

### 2. The roll is pre-seeded, never empty by default

The dominant failure is not a wrong roll — it is **no roll**, because the Producer was working and never opened the app. So the roll does not wait to be created; it arrives already populated with **provisional** entries drawn from the strongest available source, in priority order (D-05, answering Q-01):

| # | Source | Confidence | Why |
|---|--------|-----------|-----|
| 1 | **Service bookings (05)** for this session | Highest | A booked session player is a roll entry by construction — the booking is a contract naming a person, a role and a window |
| 2 | **Room booking (16/17)** | High for the window and the client party; low for personnel | The room already knows a session is happening, when, and who booked it — before anyone opens the capture app |
| 3 | **Project collaborators (07)** | Medium | The project's active members are the default personnel of its sessions |
| 4 | **Personnel of the most recent prior session on the same project** | Medium-high for repeat work, low for a first session | Sessions on one project recur with the same people; this is the cheapest accurate guess available |
| 5 | **Contractor / fixer personnel list**, imported (D-14) | Highest where it exists | For a string or brass date the personnel list already exists as a document, because the union filing requires it (`02.07`) |

**A provisional entry is a guess, and a guess is never presence.** It is visible to the session owner only, never to other participants, and never counts as present until it is **touched** — the owner confirms it, the person self-joins, or a contribution is attributed to them. At close, the prompt asks about untouched provisional entries **once**; unanswered ones are **dropped, not persisted** (D-05). Persisting an untouched guess as presence would put a confidently-wrong fact into the graph at the producer-asserted tier, which is precisely the failure `02.02.02` DT-03 rejects.

**Honest limit**: pre-seeding does not solve non-adoption. If the Producer never opens the app, the close prompt never fires either. What pre-seeding buys is that when the prompt *is* re-offered (`02.02.03` "skipped → resurfaces"), it has something to ask about instead of nothing — it converts "who was here?" into "were these five people here?", which is a recognition task rather than a recall task, days or weeks after the fact.

### 3. Joining must be as close to free as possible

Three entry paths, all one action:

- **Owner adds** — from suggestions (prior collaborators, project members, room regulars, service bookings), from search, or by typing a name. Author = the owner. This is the trust-broker assertion.
- **Self-join by link** — the session's join link admits the joiner immediately, marked `self-added`. Author = the joiner. A self-added entry is a **self-assertion of presence** and carries no broker weight until the owner touches it (D-07). Frictionless by design: gating self-joins on owner approval would put a queue between a person walking into a room and the record of them being in it.
- **Walk-in** — the owner marks a physically-present person, identical to "owner adds".

The join link is scoped to one session, is rotatable, and **expires 24 hours after session close**. Joins arriving after wrap are accepted within that window and marked `late self-add` with an `inferred` interval; joins after expiry are rejected. If a link leaks, the cost is bounded: strangers become self-added entries the owner can remove, they never gain broker weight, and they never generate a credit (D-01).

### 4. Non-users are addable by name alone

Requiring accounts before capture would make the wedge depend on the network the wedge exists to build (sub-domain D-04). A freehand name creates an **unclaimed shell** (`01`), which surfaces in the claim inbox (`02.03.02`) — the growth loop's second half, aimed at exactly the people the platform most wants.

Freehand entry is constrained (D-15): 1–100 characters, whitespace-only rejected inline, rendered as **plain text and never as markup** (`security-first.md`), and it **never silently binds to an existing party** — a typed name that resembles an existing party creates a *distinct* shell and offers a merge (`02.01.04`); it does not resolve into the existing party. Silent binding is how a fabricator attaches a notable name to their session (DT-09).

Volume is bounded: **freehand additions are rate-limited to 30 per session per hour**, and a roll exceeding **30 entries** must be populated by bulk import or booking pre-seed rather than one-by-one freehand. Nobody legitimately hand-types 40 names — they import the contractor's list — so the limit costs real users nothing and blocks shell-farming (DT-09).

### 5. Presence is never a credit

Rooms are full of people who contributed nothing: the label A&R on the sofa, the artist's partner, the assistant, the runner, the person who came for lunch. A roll entry is **evidence for** a credit and never the credit itself (D-01). The close prompt asks; it does not assume.

### 6. Removal is an assertion about a fact, not a delete

The owner removing an entry they added by mistake is a correction. **A Musician cannot delete their own entry** — self-removal is recorded as an **objection** against the owner's assertion (D-10). Letting a participant silently delete the trust broker's record of them would be the erase-inconvenient-facts hole, and the roll would stop being evidence of anything.

An objection asks the objector which of two things they mean, because they route to different places:

| The objector means | Routes to |
|---|---|
| "I was not there" — a factual dispute | Owner is notified; if unresolved on the roll, it becomes a dispute (`02.05`) |
| "I was there; do not name me" — anonymity (ghost production, NDA, contractual non-attribution) | Presence stands; **publication** is handled by visibility/embargo (`02.01.05`) — the fact is not erased, it is not published |

Conflating these is a real error: a ghost producer's "don't credit me" is not a claim that the session didn't happen.

### 7. Interaction budgets

- Add from suggestion: **1 tap**, entry visible in **< 100 ms**, and adding never blocks on connectivity (capture must survive a studio basement, sub-domain D-03).
- Freehand name: committed on **Enter or blur**; a duplicate check returns within **300 ms** or is skipped rather than blocking the commit.
- Capacity edit: inline, multi-select, no modal.
- Suggestions: capped at **8**, ranked by service bookings for this session → project collaborators → prior-session personnel → the Producer's collaborators in the last 12 months → the room's regulars.

### 8. Notifications

Adding a platform user to the roll notifies them — this is both the consent surface (Q-02) and the objection path (D-10). Fan-out is throttled: **one notification per person per session, deferred 5 minutes and cancelled if the entry is removed inside that window**, so add/remove churn during setup is silent. Adding an unclaimed shell notifies nobody — a shell has no channel, which is the load-bearing unknown in the growth model (domain Q-05).

### Happy Path

1. **Session exists** — created by the Producer, or already existing from a project (07) or a room booking (16/17). The roll is created with it.
2. **System pre-seeds the roll** with provisional entries from the highest-priority available source (D-05) and shows them to the Producer only, marked provisional. If no source exists, the roll opens empty with 8 ranked suggestions.
3. **Producer confirms the provisional entries who actually turned up** — one tap each. Confirmed entries become present, authored by the Producer, with an `observed` arrival.
4. **Producer adds anyone the pre-seed missed** — from suggestions, from search, or by typing a name (creating an unclaimed shell). Platform users are notified after the 5-minute defer window.
5. **Each entry carries a capacity list** — `["drums"]`, `["drums", "engineering"]` — bound to the taxonomy (`02.06`). Multiple capacities are the normal case, not an exception (D-04).
6. **Contributors who arrive later self-join by link or are marked present** — each gets a new `observed` arrival on their own entry.
7. **The roll updates through the day** — a player leaves and the Producer marks it (`observed` departure); a dep arrives at 14:00 as a new entry; a string player dials in remotely at 18:00 and is marked remote-live.
8. **At wrap** the system closes every open interval with an `inferred` departure at the wrap timestamp, and asks about untouched provisional entries once.
9. **The close prompt (`02.02.03`) reads the roll** — nobody is asked to remember who was there. Untouched provisional entries are dropped; touched entries carry into the reconciliation.
10. **The roll goes read-only.** It is now the record of who was there, and it scopes what `02.02.02` could attribute, what `02.02.04` can corroborate, and what `02.01.01` can credit.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Nobody sets up the roll at all** | The dominant failure. Pre-seed (D-05) means the roll is not empty even so: it holds provisional entries from the service booking, room booking, project members or the prior session's personnel. Never touched, they never become presence — but the re-offered close prompt asks "were these five people here?" instead of "who was here?", turning recall into recognition. If no pre-seed source exists, the session is captured with the Producer alone | Producer sees a provisional roll on first open; at close, a single recognition question rather than a blank field |
| **Producer never opens the app at all** | Pre-seeding does not fix this and must not be claimed to. No wrap signal fires, so no prompt fires. The session auto-closes after 6h of inactivity (D-12) and the roll persists as provisional-only — evidence of nothing, dropped at auto-close | Nothing. The session is a stub with no roll |
| **Someone is added who was not there** | Removable by the owner. If a credit already flowed from the entry, removal does not retract the credit — it becomes an amendment (`02.01.06`) or a dispute (`02.05`) | Removal, with an explicit warning that credits derived from this entry are affected and naming them |
| **Person on the roll contributed nothing** | Present ≠ credited (D-01). The A&R on the sofa is on the roll and gets no credit | Roll entry with zero contributions; the close prompt does not offer them a credit row |
| **Musician says "I was not there"** | Self-removal is an objection, not a delete (D-10). The owner's assertion stands, flagged; the entry stops feeding new credit rows pending resolution; unresolved, it becomes a dispute (`02.05`) | Entry marked contested; owner notified; objector asked which of the two objections they mean |
| **Musician says "I was there — don't name me"** (ghost producer, NDA, contractual non-attribution) | Presence stands. This is a **publication** question, not a presence question — routed to visibility/embargo (`02.01.05`). Erasing the presence would be the wrong fix and would corrupt the record for everyone else on it | Entry unchanged; a "not for publication" marker; embargo flow offered |
| **Remote contributor sends a take without ever joining** | They contributed but were never present. The roll accepts an **asynchronous** entry — the normal post-2020 case (`problem-statement.md` Why Now), so this is a first-class path, not a fallback. Their evidence is the artifact, not their presence (`02.02.04` CX-06) | Roll entry marked `remote — asynchronous`, with a contribution window rather than a presence interval |
| **Remote contributor is live in the session** (Source-Connect, Listento, a video call) | Distinct from asynchronous: they *were* in the session, in real time, and could hear the room. Presence intervals apply normally; only the physical-corroboration signals are unavailable | Entry marked `remote — live`, with normal intervals |
| **Two people, same name, one typed freehand** | Creates a shell that may duplicate an existing party. Never silently binds (D-15). Merge offered at type-time if similarity is high, and again at close; unresolved, it routes to `02.01.04` | Inline "did you mean [existing party]?" with an explicit "no — different person" option |
| **Freehand name matches a notable professional** ("Paul McCartney played bass") | The impersonation vector (DT-09). Creates a distinct unclaimed shell that never merges without the real party's claim; enters at the lowest tier (self/owner-asserted, unclaimed, uncorroborated); publication of unclaimed credits naming an unmatched notable party is a visibility question (`02.01.05`) and a topology signal (`02.04.04`) | The typist sees nothing unusual — deliberately. Surfacing "we think you're lying" at type-time trains evasion and insults the 99% who are not |
| **Mass-adding hundreds of names to farm shells** | Rate limit: 30 freehand adds per session per hour; rolls > 30 entries must come from bulk import or a booking pre-seed (D-15). Legitimate large dates import; farmers cannot | "Adding a large ensemble? Import the personnel list" — the legitimate path is offered, not the limit explained |
| **Session runs across days** (previously an open gap, now closed by D-12) | A **session is one continuous working block**, bounded by a wrap signal or by 6h of no roll/log activity, whichever comes first (D-12). Calendar days are irrelevant — a 22:00–04:00 session is one session. Multi-day work is **multiple sessions under one project** (07), each with its own roll. The union's 3-hour "session" is a billing unit (`02.07`), not this unit | Producer sees one roll per working block; the project view (07) shows the sessions in sequence |
| **Session auto-closes on inactivity while people are still in the room** | The 6h threshold is deliberately long — longer than any plausible meal break — but it is a heuristic and it will occasionally be wrong. Auto-close is **reversible**: reopening within 24h restores the roll and re-opens the intervals, marking the auto-inserted departures as withdrawn | "This session auto-closed at 03:12 — reopen?" |
| **Two devices edit the roll offline, then sync** | Per-entry merge, deliberately asymmetric (D-11): **add wins over remove** — a spurious entry costs nothing (presence never credits, D-01) while a lost entry costs a credit nobody ever asks about. Capacity lists **union**. Conflicting intervals **widen to the union** and are marked `conflicted`, so `02.02.04` does not rest corroboration on a window two devices disagree about. The withdrawn removal is preserved and re-offered at close | Badge on the affected entries; a "resolve 2 conflicts" section inside the close prompt. **Never a mid-session modal** — a merge dialog while someone is tracking is the friction that loses the roll |
| **Two co-producers both own the session and both edit the roll live** | Both are owners; both may write. Every edit records its author (D-07), so the roll survives as an accountable record for `02.05`. Concurrent online edits resolve per-entry by the same asymmetric rules | Live list updating under both hands; author visible on long-press |
| **The Producer is also playing** (multi-hyphenate, two roles at once) | Capacity list handles it: `["producer", "bass"]` (D-04). But their entry is **authored by themselves**, so it is self-asserted, not broker-asserted (D-07, DT-06) — the trust broker vouching for themselves is worth exactly what a self-claim is worth | Normal entry. The distinction is invisible here and consumed by `02.04.02` |
| **Self-produced session — one human is Producer and sole Musician** | Every entry is self-authored. Session capture is not itself a trust tier: an in-app solo session carries no more weight than a self-claim (DT-06). The product must not present "captured in session" as if it were corroboration | Normal capture. No claim of verification anywhere in the UI |
| **The studio owner is also the engineer** (Operator + Producer, one human) | Their personal presence does not grant the *room* visibility. Room visibility is an access grant on the room (Q-03), not a consequence of any individual being on the roll — otherwise a studio's other staff inherit sight of a client's roster because the boss engineered the date | Owner sees the roll as session owner. Room staff see nothing unless the room grant is on |
| **A band is added rather than its members** | The roll must resolve to **humans**, because attribution resolves to humans (DT-14). Adding a band expands into one entry per member, each independently removable and re-rolable — the drummer who missed the date is removed without unpicking the band | "Adding The Wreckers — 4 members" → four entries appear, each removable. Resolves the roll-call half of domain Q-12 |
| **Contractor/fixer date — 30-piece string section** | The personnel list already exists as a document because the union filing requires it (`02.07`). Bulk import (D-14) creates one entry per player with capacity, section and desk position where the list carries them. Hand-rolling 30 players would guarantee no roll | Import from the contractor's list; a section-grouped roll rather than a flat list of 30 |
| **A minor is on the roll** (child performer, children's choir) | Creates a record about a person who cannot consent, with statutory weight — in the UK a child performance licence from the local authority is a legal precondition for the work itself. The roll records the fact; whether a shell may exist for a minor, and what is visible pre-claim, is a legal gate, not a preference | Entry created. **Escalated — see Q-04**; this is not resolvable at ideation |
| **Attributions exist for someone the owner then removes** (resolves CX-01 synthesis Q1) | Neither blocking nor silent orphaning. Blocking is wrong — people are added by mistake and the roll must stay cheap to correct. Silent orphaning is worse — attributions pointing at nobody surface as credits with no subject. **Removal is refused only while attributions exist, and the refusal is a prompt**: reassign the attributions, or confirm the work is also being withdrawn, in the same action | "Sarah has 3 attributions on tracks 2, 4, 7. Remove her and: [reassign to…] [withdraw those too] [cancel]" |
| **Network fails mid-edit** | Adding or editing a roll entry never depends on connectivity (§7). Edits are retained and reconciled when the connection returns. Capture is never blocked by connectivity — a studio basement is the design case, not the exception (sub-domain D-03) | No error. A "will sync" marker on the roll header |
| **Edits stay unsynced repeatedly** (> 24h) | The roll on the owner's device remains authoritative and usable. The failure is surfaced to the owner only, because it is theirs to act on, and it must never present as data loss — it isn't | Persistent "not synced since 14:20" banner with a retry; never a destructive prompt |
| **Join link is leaked publicly** | Bounded cost: strangers become `self-added` entries with no broker weight, generating no credits (D-01). The owner removes them. Link is rotatable, and expires 24h after close | Owner sees unexpected self-added entries flagged as "joined by link, not confirmed" |
| **Someone joins by link after the session closed** | Accepted within 24h of close, marked `late self-add` with an `inferred` interval; rejected after expiry. The late joiner is real more often than not — the person who forgot | "Joined after close" marker; owner asked to confirm |
| **The roll is empty and the session is closed** | The close prompt degrades to "who was here?" (`02.02.03` edge cases) — still better than silence, and the pre-seed usually means this does not happen | Minimal roll-first prompt |
| **Async contributor tries to see who else played** | They have no overlapping interval, so they see only themselves and the session owner (D-09). They cannot learn the unreleased personnel of a record they sent one part to | Their own row; the session owner's name; nothing else |
| **Defamatory or abusive freehand name** | Plain text, never markup (D-15). Reportable through the platform's trust & safety path (24). The roll is not a publication surface, so exposure is bounded to the session's participants until a credit publishes — at which point `02.01.02`/`02.01.05` govern | Report affordance on the entry |
| **Two entries turn out to be the same human** (freehand + self-join) | Detected by similarity at close and offered for merge; unmerged, it double-counts presence and can produce two credits for one person. Merge routes to `02.01.04`; intervals union onto the surviving entry (D-06) | "These may be the same person — merge?" at close, not mid-session |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Roll opened | Skeleton of 3 rows for **< 300 ms**. Beyond 300 ms, fall through to Error, never spin. Opening the roll must be effectively instant even with no connection; a spinner here means something is wrong, not that something is slow |
| **Empty** | Session created, no pre-seed source available, nobody added | "Who's on this session?" plus up to 8 ranked suggestions (project collaborators → recent collaborators → room regulars), each one tap. A freehand field is present and focused — the fastest path is always visible |
| **Provisional** (a Partial) | Pre-seed produced entries; none touched | **Owner only.** "We think these people are here — tap to confirm." Entries dimmed, each with confirm/remove. Other participants see an empty roll, because a guess is not presence (D-05) |
| **Populated / Active** | One or more touched entries; session open | Live list: name, capacity list, present/departed marker, remote-live and remote-async badges, provisional entries still dimmed at the bottom. Author revealed on long-press |
| **Partial** | Some entries touched, some provisional; or intervals open with no observed departure; or a self-added entry the owner has not confirmed | The same list with explicit incompleteness — dimmed provisionals, "still here?" affordance on long-open intervals, "joined by link, not confirmed" markers. Never a blocking banner; the roll is allowed to be incomplete all day, because it usually is |
| **Conflicted** | Two devices (or two co-owners) edited the same entry divergently | Per-entry badge. Merge already happened by the asymmetric rules (D-11) — the badge reports it, it does not ask. Resolution is deferred to a "resolve N conflicts" section inside the close prompt. **No mid-session modal** |
| **Pending sync** | Edits made while offline (sub-domain D-03) | "Will sync" marker on the header. Fully editable. Never blocks capture, never warns about data loss |
| **Sync failed** | Edits unsynced for > 24h | Owner-only banner: "not synced since {time}" + retry. Non-destructive; the roll on the owner's device stays authoritative |
| **Error** | The roll cannot be opened, or a write is rejected (rate limit hit, name > 100 chars, whitespace-only) | Inline at the point of failure, never a page-level error: "Name is too long (100 max)" / "Adding a large ensemble? Import the personnel list". The rest of the roll stays usable — one bad write never takes the roll down |
| **Closed** | Wrap signal, or 6h inactivity auto-close (D-12) | Read-only roll: the record of who was there, with `observed`/`inferred` endpoints distinguished. Changes now route through `02.01.06` |
| **Reopened** | Auto-closed session reopened within 24h | Editable again; auto-inserted departures marked withdrawn; intervals re-opened |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Presence implies a credit — being on the roll should generate a credit | Naive reading of capture-at-source; it would make the wedge nearly free | ❌ **REJECTED** — rooms are full of people who contributed nothing: the label A&R on the sofa, the artist's partner, the assistant, the person who came for lunch. Auto-crediting attendance would flood the graph with false credits *at the highest provenance tier*, which is worse than no capture: it would poison the exact signal that differentiates the product. Presence is **evidence for** a credit, never the credit. |
| DT-02 | The roll can be a simple list of people | Simplest model | ❌ **REJECTED** — the roll must be temporal (intervals, arrivals, departures, remote/async participation). Without intervals, a dep who replaced the original drummer at 14:00 cannot be attributed correctly, and the take-level attribution in `02.02.02` has nothing to resolve against. A set loses the very facts the session was captured to preserve. |
| DT-03 | The roll is a credits feature | The obvious framing — it lives in domain 02 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH TENSION** — it is also, structurally, the platform's best acquisition funnel: every session names 3–8 professionals, most of whom are not users, each becoming an unclaimed shell with a real page waiting for them. That makes the roll a growth mechanism as much as a provenance one, and it means friction here costs users, not just data. It also raises a real concern: adding someone by name creates a public-ish record of a person who never consented. Escalated to Q-02. |
| DT-04 | The roll is hand-maintained, one person at a time | Implicit in every mock of "add a person" | ❌ **REJECTED for a whole class of real sessions.** A 30-piece string date, a brass section, a choir: nobody hand-types 30 names, and a product that requires it simply has no roll for orchestral work — which is exactly the work where credits are most valuable and most systematically lost. The music-industry fact nobody here had mentioned: **the personnel list already exists as a document**, produced by the **contractor (fixer)** who hired the players, because the union filing (`02.07`) requires it. The roll should ingest that list, not compete with it. Bulk import (D-14) is not a nice-to-have; it is the difference between covering large dates and not. |
| DT-05 | Capacity-as-a-list is a UX accommodation for the multi-hyphenate | `personas.md` framing — "artist OR producer?" fails them | ✅ **CONFIRMED, and it is stronger than a UX argument.** Under AFM/MU session rules **doubling** — playing a second instrument on the same date — attracts an extra fee. So a roll entry reading `["sax", "flute"]` rather than `["sax"]` is **money**, not metadata, and it is money the player frequently loses today because nobody wrote it down. D-04 was justified on persona grounds and turns out to be justified independently on payment grounds — which also makes the capacity list directly load-bearing for `02.07` and for service payouts (05). Two independent derivations of the same decision is the strongest evidence available at this stage. |
| DT-06 | Session capture is itself a trust signal — a credit captured in-session is better-evidenced than one claimed later | The whole thesis, applied one step too far | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED for multi-party sessions, ❌ REJECTED as a property of capture itself.** What carries weight is *the trust broker asserting a fact about someone else* (`personas.md`: the Producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim). In a self-produced solo session, the Producer and the Musician are one human, every roll entry is self-authored, and "captured in session" means nothing more than "typed into our app rather than someone else's". The same applies narrowly to the multi-hyphenate Producer's own entry inside a multi-party session: broker weight covers their assertions about *others*, never about themselves. Consequence: **every roll entry must record its author** (D-07), and `author == subject` is self-assertion. Without this, the graph's highest tier is trivially farmable by one person with a DAW and an account — the product's core claim, defeated by a solo session. |
| DT-07 | Capacities need their own intervals — the engineer who steps out to play bass at 14:00 | Fidelity instinct; the case is real | ❌ **REJECTED** — the case is real, the mechanism is wrong. Presence intervals plus the contribution log's own timestamps (`02.02.02`) already resolve it: the roll says she was here 10:00–18:00 as `["engineering", "bass"]`, the log says the bass take is 14:20. A second temporal axis on the roll re-implements the log inside the roll, doubles the maintenance burden on the one act that must cost seconds, and creates a new class of disagreement (roll says bass from 14:00, log says 13:50) that resolves nothing anyone needed resolved. **The cheapest act in the product must stay cheap.** |
| DT-08 | Departures are recorded like arrivals | Symmetry | ❌ **REJECTED — the symmetry is false and quietly corrupting.** Arrival has a natural trigger: someone walks in, someone taps, someone joins a link. Departure has none — nobody opens a work app to announce they are leaving, and the person best placed to record it (the Producer) is working. So the roll's departures will be **inferred at wrap**, essentially always. That is fine — until `02.02.04` treats an inferred timestamp as corroboration of a presence window, at which point the product is presenting its own guess back to itself as evidence. Hence D-08: endpoints are marked **`observed` or `inferred`**, and inferred endpoints never support corroboration. The subtle failure this prevents: a fabricated session's inferred intervals would look exactly as clean as a real one's. |
| DT-09 | Freehand name entry is a harmless convenience for capture speed | It is the feature's core friction win (D-03) | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as necessary, and it is also an impersonation vector.** A fabricator types a notable player onto their session; the credit publishes (`02.01.02`); their reputation inflates by association with a person who has never heard of them. The mitigation cannot be a gate — gating freehand entry kills the wedge (sub-domain D-04) — so it is layered instead: never silently bind a typed name to an existing party (D-15); enter at the lowest tier, unclaimed and uncorroborated; volume-limit freehand adds; leave detection to topology (`02.04.04`) and publication to visibility (`02.01.05`). Deliberately **not** surfaced at type-time: an accusatory interstitial trains evasion and insults the overwhelming majority who are simply typing their bassist's name. Escalated to Q-02 alongside consent, because both are the same underlying fact — the roll creates records about people who are not present in the transaction. |
| DT-10 | Everyone on the roll can see the whole roll | The obvious default; it is a shared session | ❌ **REJECTED, replaced by a sharper rule that costs nothing.** Visibility is scoped to **overlapping presence intervals** (D-09): you see the people you were in the room with. This gives up nothing, because those people saw each other with their own eyes — the platform is not disclosing anything they don't already know. What it prevents is real: an **asynchronous** contributor who emailed one part learning the full unreleased personnel of a record, weeks before announcement, from a tool nobody thought of as a leak. The industry-obvious version of this: NDA'd sessions and ghost production are normal, and the parties to them do not expect a capture app to be a roster broadcast. The rule falls out naturally rather than needing a permission model. |
| DT-11 | Removing someone from the roll should delete their entry | It is a list; lists have deletes | ❌ **REJECTED in both directions.** (a) **The owner** removing an entry with existing attributions cannot silently orphan them — attributions pointing at nobody re-surface as credits with no subject — nor can removal be blocked, because people are added by mistake and the roll must stay cheap to correct. The answer is a refusal that is also a prompt: reassign or withdraw, in the same action (resolves CX-01's open synthesis question). (b) **A Musician** deleting their own entry would let any participant erase the trust broker's record of them — the erase-inconvenient-facts hole, which would make the roll evidence of nothing. Self-removal is an **objection** (D-10), and it must ask *which* objection: "I wasn't there" is a dispute (`02.05`); "don't name me" is an embargo (`02.01.05`). Conflating them mistakes a ghost producer for a liar. |
| DT-12 | The roll is credits working data and nothing else | It lives in 02 and feeds 02 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH TENSION — the roll is also the platform's professional graph, created before any credit exists.** "Who has worked with whom" is the substrate of warm intros (03), dep matching and hiring (04, 05), and it is generated by co-presence at zero marginal cost. Nobody has named this, and it is a significant unbooked asset. It is also a leak with a fuse on it: a co-presence edge published before the credit publishes discloses the unreleased session — "X and Y have worked together" is exactly the fact an embargo exists to withhold, and it discloses it *without ever showing a credit*, routing around `02.01.05` entirely. Constraint: **co-presence must not create a visible graph edge before the credit it belongs to publishes.** Logged as a cross-cut; the derived-graph rules belong to 03/04. |
| DT-13 | Offline roll conflicts should resolve last-write-wins, like any document | The standard answer, and the cheap one | ❌ **REJECTED — the two errors are not symmetric, so a symmetric rule is wrong.** A spurious roll entry costs approximately nothing: presence never auto-credits (D-01), so it surfaces at the close prompt and is removed in a tap. A **lost** roll entry costs a credit that nobody will ever be asked about — the exact reconstruction failure the platform exists to prevent, reintroduced by a merge rule. So the merge is deliberately asymmetric: **add wins over remove**, capacity lists **union**, intervals **widen and mark `conflicted`** (D-11). The widening is safe only because of the conflict marker — an unmarked widened interval would silently inflate the corroboration window `02.02.04` scopes against (CX-05). The general principle worth carrying: in a capture product, **prefer recording too much presence over too little**, because the downstream cost of the two is not remotely comparable. |
| DT-14 | A band should be a single roll entry | Bands are first-class entities (`personas.md`, domain 01) and liner notes credit bands | ❌ **REJECTED at the roll, and this answers half of domain Q-12.** Bands genuinely are entities and genuinely do hold credits — but the roll's job is to scope **attribution**, and attribution resolves to humans: "the band was here" cannot tell you who played the solo, and it silently deletes the fact that the drummer missed the date. So adding a band **expands** into one entry per member, each independently removable and re-rolable (D-13). The band-as-credit-holder question is real and untouched by this — a band can hold a credit at `02.01.01` while the roll that evidenced it names five humans. The roll is the wrong place to model the entity; it is the right place to model the room. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Presence never auto-generates a credit | DT-01. Attendance is evidence; the credit is an assertion. Conflating them poisons the graph at its highest-trust tier. |
| D-02 | The roll is temporal — intervals, not membership | DT-02. Deps, partial days and remote drop-ins are the normal case, not edge cases. |
| D-03 | Non-users are addable by name; accounts are never a precondition for capture | Sub-domain D-04. The wedge cannot depend on the network it exists to create. |
| D-04 | A person's capacity is a list, never a single choice | The multi-hyphenate is the structural centre persona; "artist OR producer?" fails them immediately (`personas.md`). Independently confirmed by DT-05: doubling carries a fee, so the list is money. |
| D-05 | **The roll is pre-seeded, never created empty.** Provisional entries are drawn from service bookings (05) → room booking (16/17) → project collaborators (07) → prior-session personnel, are visible to the session owner only, and never count as presence until touched. Untouched provisional entries are **dropped at close, not persisted** | Answers Q-01. The dominant failure is *no roll*, and a roll that arrives pre-populated converts the close prompt's question from recall to recognition. Dropping the untouched is what stops the pre-seed becoming the confidently-wrong high-tier record `02.02.02` DT-03 rejects. Honest limit: this does not solve non-adoption — if the app is never opened, no prompt fires. |
| D-06 | One party = one roll entry, holding many intervals | Two entries for one human double-count presence and can produce two credits. Leave-and-return is a second interval, not a second entry. |
| D-07 | **Every roll entry records its author.** `author == subject` is a self-assertion and carries no broker weight | DT-06. Without this the graph's highest tier is farmable by one person with a DAW and an account. Broker weight comes from asserting facts about *others*, not from the app the assertion was typed into. |
| D-08 | **Interval endpoints are marked `observed` or `inferred`.** Inferred endpoints never support corroboration | DT-08. Departures are essentially always inferred. Feeding the system's own guess back as evidence (`02.02.04`) would make a fabricated session's intervals look exactly as clean as a real one's. |
| D-09 | A Musician sees only the roll entries whose intervals **overlap their own**, plus the session owner | DT-10. Costs nothing — they saw those people with their own eyes — and prevents an async contributor learning an unreleased record's full personnel. |
| D-10 | **Self-removal is an objection, not a delete**, and it asks which objection is meant: "I wasn't there" → dispute (`02.05`); "don't name me" → embargo (`02.01.05`) | DT-11. Silent self-deletion would let any participant erase the trust broker's record. Conflating the two objections mistakes a ghost producer for a liar. |
| D-11 | **Offline/concurrent merge is asymmetric**: add wins over remove; capacity lists union; intervals widen to the union and are marked `conflicted`; withdrawn removals are re-offered at close. Conflicts never raise a mid-session modal | DT-13. A spurious entry costs a tap; a lost entry costs a credit nobody ever asks about. The `conflicted` marker is what keeps the widening safe (`02.02.04` may not lean on it). |
| D-12 | **A session is one continuous working block**, bounded by a wrap signal or **6h of no roll/log activity**, whichever comes first. Calendar days are irrelevant. Multi-day work is multiple sessions under one project (07). Auto-close is reversible within 24h | Resolves the previously-open "runs across days" question. 6h is longer than any plausible meal break and shorter than any overnight gap; it is a heuristic and it is tunable against real data, which is why auto-close is reversible rather than final. The union's 3-hour "session" is a billing unit (`02.07`), not this unit. |
| D-13 | Adding a **band** expands into one roll entry per member | DT-14. The roll scopes attribution and attribution resolves to humans. Answers the roll-call half of domain Q-12; the band-as-credit-holder question is untouched and remains open. |
| D-14 | **Bulk personnel import** for contractor/fixer dates; rolls > 30 entries use it rather than freehand | DT-04. The personnel list already exists because the union filing requires it. Hand-rolling 30 players guarantees no roll for exactly the work where credits are most systematically lost. |
| D-15 | **Freehand names**: 1–100 characters, whitespace-only rejected inline, rendered as plain text never markup, **never silently bound to an existing party** (merge is offered, never applied), rate-limited to 30 per session per hour | DT-09 + `security-first.md`. Silent binding is the impersonation vector; the volume limit blocks shell-farming without costing any legitimate user, because legitimate large dates import. |
