# Feature: Session Roll Call

> **Number**: 02.02.01
> **Parent**: [Session Capture](./02.02-session-capture-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The living list of who is in the session — physical room or remote — and in what capacity. It is the cheapest possible act of provenance: before anything else is captured, the system knows who was there. Everything downstream (the contribution log, the close prompt, attendance proof, the credits themselves) is scoped by the roll.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own presence/role) | Joins the roll — by invite link, by being added, or by walking in and being marked present. Sets their own capacity ("drums", "drums + engineering"), which is the multi-hyphenate's whole problem: a UX that makes them pick one role fails them (`personas.md`). Sees who else is on the roll. |
| Producer | Full (owns the roll) | Adds, removes and re-roles anyone; adds people who are not on the platform (unclaimed shells, D-04); marks arrivals and departures. This is the only administrative act the Producer must perform reliably, so it has to cost seconds, not minutes. |
| Operator | Read-only (who is in their room) | Sees the roll for sessions in their space — genuinely useful (who is on site, for access, catering, insurance and after-hours) and genuinely sensitive. A room that surveils its clients' rosters loses clients. Likely opt-in per session. |
| Fan | None | Never sees a roll. The roll is pre-publication working data; even after release, what publishes is the credit, not the attendance sheet. |

## Behavior

The roll is **temporal**, not a set — its whole value is that it changes and the changes are recorded. A drummer tracks 10am–1pm; a dep replaces them at 2pm; a string player dials in remotely for 40 minutes at 6pm. A flat "who was on this session" list loses all three facts and with them the ability to attribute a take to the right person.

Joining must be as close to free as possible. The most likely failure is not a wrong roll — it is **no roll**, because the Producer was working and never opened the app. Every reduction in friction here is worth more than any feature elsewhere in the sub-domain.

Contributors who are not on the platform must be addable by name alone. Requiring accounts before capture would make the wedge depend on the network the wedge exists to build (sub-domain D-04); those names become unclaimed shells and the claim inbox (`02.03.02`) is how they become users. The roll is therefore also the platform's primary acquisition mechanism, aimed at exactly the people it most wants.

### Happy Path

1. Producer opens the session (or it exists already from domain 07)
2. Producer adds people — from prior collaborators, from the room's regulars, by search, or by typing a name
3. Each present party is marked with their capacity; platform users are notified they have been added
4. Roll updates through the day as people arrive and leave; the system records the intervals
5. At close (`02.02.03`) the roll is what the prompt asks about — nobody is asked to remember who was there

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody sets up the roll at all | The dominant failure. Session is captured with only the Producer; the close prompt has almost nothing to ask about | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — mitigation likely a passive signal (calendar/booking from 16/17, DAW track names) that pre-seeds the roll rather than waiting for the Producer to act |
| Someone is added who was not there | Removable; if a credit already flowed, it becomes an amendment (`02.01.06`) or a dispute (`02.05`) | Removal with a note that credits are affected |
| Person on the roll contributed nothing | Present ≠ credited. Attendance is not a credit — a label rep watching from the couch is on the roll and gets no credit | Roll entry with no contributions; close prompt does not credit them |
| Remote contributor sends a take without ever joining | They contributed but were never present. Roll must accept an asynchronous contributor — this is the normal case post-2020 (`problem-statement.md` Why Now) | Roll entry marked remote/asynchronous |
| Two people, same name, one typed freehand | Creates a shell that may duplicate an existing party; resolved later (`02.01.04`) | Disambiguation offered at type-time if a match is likely |
| Session runs across days | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — is that one session or several? Affects attribution and attendance intervals | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Session created, nobody added | Prompt to add people; prior collaborators and room regulars offered |
| Active | People on the roll, session open | Live list with present/departed markers |
| Pending sync | Captured offline (sub-domain D-03) | Local roll with a "will sync" marker; never blocks capture |
| Closed | Session closed | Read-only roll; the record of who was there |
| Conflicted | Two devices edited the roll offline | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — merge strategy is an architecture question with a product-visible surface |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Presence implies a credit — being on the roll should generate a credit | Naive reading of capture-at-source; it would make the wedge nearly free | ❌ **REJECTED** — rooms are full of people who contributed nothing: the label A&R on the sofa, the artist's partner, the assistant, the person who came for lunch. Auto-crediting attendance would flood the graph with false credits *at the highest provenance tier*, which is worse than no capture: it would poison the exact signal that differentiates the product. Presence is **evidence for** a credit, never the credit. |
| DT-02 | The roll can be a simple list of people | Simplest model | ❌ **REJECTED** — the roll must be temporal (intervals, arrivals, departures, remote/async participation). Without intervals, a dep who replaced the original drummer at 2pm cannot be attributed correctly, and the take-level attribution in `02.02.02` has nothing to resolve against. A set loses the very facts the session was captured to preserve. |
| DT-03 | The roll is a credits feature | The obvious framing — it lives in domain 02 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH TENSION** — it is also, structurally, the platform's best acquisition funnel: every session names 3–8 professionals, most of whom are not users, each becoming an unclaimed shell with a real page waiting for them. That makes the roll a growth mechanism as much as a provenance one, and it means friction here costs users, not just data. It also raises a real concern: adding someone by name creates a public-ish record of a person who never consented. Escalated to Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Presence never auto-generates a credit | DT-01. Attendance is evidence; the credit is an assertion. Conflating them poisons the graph at its highest-trust tier. |
| D-02 | The roll is temporal — intervals, not membership | DT-02. Deps, partial days and remote drop-ins are the normal case, not edge cases. |
| D-03 | Non-users are addable by name; accounts are never a precondition for capture | Sub-domain D-04. The wedge cannot depend on the network it exists to create. |
| D-04 | A person's capacity is a list, never a single choice | The multi-hyphenate is the structural centre persona; "artist OR producer?" fails them immediately (`personas.md`). |
