## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Roll Call ↔ Contribution Log

**Relationship**: The roll defines the attributable population. The log cannot attribute work to someone not on the roll — attempting it prompts a roll edit first (`02.02.02` edge cases). This is deliberate: it forces the two records to stay reconcilable, so that at close (CX-02) the prompt can compute a meaningful gap rather than diffing two unrelated lists.

The temporal property (`02.02.01` D-02) makes this sharper than it looks. The roll is intervals, so the log's attributions must resolve against a *time*: attributing the 2pm bass take to the drummer who left at 1pm is detectable, and should be. Without intervals this check is impossible and the dep case silently misattributes.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: writes both. The only party who can attribute to others.
- **Musician**: config on both, own rows only — their presence, their contributions.
- **Operator**: reads the roll (opt-in, Q-03), never the log. The room does not learn who played what.
- **Fan**: neither exists.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The roll owns party membership; the log owns attribution. The log holds references into the roll. Removing someone from the roll who has attributions is the conflict — orphan the attributions, or block the removal? Blocking is wrong (people are added by mistake); orphaning needs a resolution path. `[PENDING — Step 5]`
2. **Trigger chain**: Attribution to a non-roll party triggers a roll addition. Synchronous and inline — a modal would be friction at exactly the wrong moment.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. Roll write (Producer only for others) gates log write for others. A Musician cannot attribute to anyone else precisely because they cannot add anyone else.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Roll addition notifies the added party (if a platform user). Attribution alone does not — the notification that matters is the close prompt, and pre-notifying every attribution would be noise during work.
5. **State transition conflict**: Both mutate during a live session, possibly from two devices offline. Roll-and-log conflict merge is the concrete product-visible face of sub-domain D-03's architecture routing.

### CX-02: Roll Call + Contribution Log ↔ Close Prompt

**Relationship**: The prompt is a pure function of the other three children's state at wrap. It reads the roll (who), the log (what), and computes the gap (who was here that we have no work for; what work has nobody attached). The gap *is* the prompt's content — it is what a Producer would have to remember otherwise, and it is the reason the prompt can be one action rather than a form.

This is why the close prompt cannot be specified independently. It has no data of its own.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees the whole reconciliation, fixes it, fires it.
- **Musician**: sees exactly one row — theirs — and one tap.
- **Operator**: absent by design (`02.02.03` Role Lens).
- **Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The prompt writes nothing directly — it commits the roll+log into draft credits (`02.01.01`). Post-commit, changes route through `02.01.06`. The commit is the boundary between working data and facts.
2. **Trigger chain**: Wrap signal → reconcile → prompt → per-row confirmations → credit records. Producer skip does **not** roll back: credits still commit at producer-asserted tier (`02.02.03` D-02). Partial confirmation is normal, not a failure — it is the modal outcome.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — a contributor can only confirm rows naming them. The Producer's reconciliation rights come from owning the roll.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The prompt is the fan-out. Present contributors get an in-room buzz; absent ones get an async attestation request (`02.04.01`) at a materially worse conversion rate. This delta is the entire justification for `02.02.03` D-01.
5. **State transition conflict**: Roll or log edited *while* the prompt is open (someone remembers something) — the prompt must be live against its inputs rather than a snapshot, or the Producer's fix silently doesn't apply.

### CX-04: Close Prompt ↔ Attendance Proof

**Relationship**: The most economical connection in the sub-domain. `02.02.04` DT-01 concludes that the strongest honest corroboration is other humans co-signing; `02.02.03` is already collecting a tap from every present human. **The co-sign is a byproduct of the confirmation** — no separate flow, no extra ask, no surveillance.

The subtlety: a contributor confirming *their own* row is a self-assertion, not corroboration. The corroborating value comes from the *set* — four people independently confirming their own rows in the same session mutually corroborate that the session had four people in it. The evidence is the pattern, not the individual tap.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their tap corroborates the session for everyone else, which they are not told and do not need to be.
- **Producer**: their reconciliation is the assertion the taps corroborate.
- **Operator**: contributes room-side corroboration through a different path entirely (booking check-in).
- **Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None. The prompt produces confirmations; corroboration is derived from them. One writer.
2. **Trigger chain**: Confirmation → co-sign signal → tier re-derivation (`02.04.02`). Async and idempotent; late taps raise the tier later (`02.02.04` edge cases).
3. **Permission intersection**: No new intersection — confirmation rights already scope it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None. Corroboration is silent; nobody should be told "your tap corroborated four people".
5. **State transition conflict**: A confirmation and a later retraction (`02.04.03`) race — a retracted attestation must withdraw its co-sign corroboration too, or the tier keeps resting on evidence the person took back.
