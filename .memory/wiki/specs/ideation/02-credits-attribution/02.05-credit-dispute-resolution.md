# Feature: Credit Dispute Resolution

> **Number**: 02.05
> **Parent**: [Credits & Attribution](./credits-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The path for a credit that is **contested** — someone says the record is wrong and someone else disagrees. `problem-statement.md` names this as "the single most litigated failure in music", and the whole platform exists to make it rarer. This feature handles the cases where prevention failed: the pre-WeJammin back catalog, the sessions captured badly, and the genuine disagreements that no amount of capture discipline prevents.

**Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain**: the general dispute machinery — case, parties, evidence, adjudicator, SLA, appeal — is a platform-wide mechanism serving services (05), gear (13), digital goods (14), booking (17), ticketing (19) and rights (09). That machinery is a **cross-cut** (recorded in `credits-attribution-cx.md`, routed to the global CX). What is domain-owned is one flow with credit-specific semantics: what counts as evidence for "I played that solo", and what a contested credit does while the argument runs.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Files a dispute about a credit naming them, or about a credit that should name them and names someone else. Submits the evidence they have — which is usually a memory, a session file date, a text message, or a person who was there. Responds when disputed. The persona whose switching trigger is literally this failure: "a credit that went to someone else" (`personas.md`). |
| Producer | Full | Files and responds — and is the **most valuable participant** in a dispute they did not raise, because they ran the room. Their switching trigger is "getting dragged into a dispute over a split they facilitated but can't evidence"; this feature is what makes the second half untrue for sessions WeJammin captured. |
| Operator | Read-only | Can confirm a session occurred in their room and when. Independent, narrow, occasionally decisive. Not party to the dispute and cannot file about someone else's contribution. |
| Fan | None | Not a party, not a witness, not a voter. Explicit absence — a crowd-adjudicated credit dispute is a popularity contest about a factual question, and the credited parties would be brigaded (`personas.md`, Fan anti-persona). Fans see the contested marker's effect (`02.03.03` D-03: suppression) and nothing else. |

## Behavior

The design principle inherited from `02.03.03` D-01: **ask the people who were there, not an adjudicator who wasn't.** A dispute on a WeJammin-captured session has contemporaneous evidence and identified witnesses; resolution is closer to reading the record than to weighing testimony. A dispute on an imported or reconstructed credit has neither, and the platform is in exactly the position `problem-statement.md` describes as the industry's failure: asking people to remember, years later, at the moment the answer became valuable.

The product should be honest about that split rather than pretending one process serves both. WeJammin's real claim is not "we adjudicate disputes better" — it is **"disputes about work we captured are cheap to resolve, because the record already exists."** For everything else it is a structured argument with a case number.

The escalation ladder, cheapest first:

1. **Direct** — the parties disagree; the system shows both what the record says and who else was there. Most disputes about captured sessions should die here, because someone is simply mistaken and the record settles it.
2. **Witness** — ask the co-present parties (`02.02.01` roll). Not a vote — a request for knowledge, with "don't know" as a first-class answer (`02.04.01` D-01).
3. **Adjudicated** — platform review. Available, expensive, and to be understood as a failure of the two cheaper paths rather than the destination.
4. **Out of scope** — legal. The platform holds a record and an evidence trail; it does not determine legal rights, and pretending otherwise is a liability it should not accept.

While contested, the credit is **held, not hidden**: suppressed from public discography (`02.03.03` D-03), marked for participants, and its downstream effects (royalty registration, tier contribution, traversal weight) paused. Deleting it would let a dispute erase a fact; publishing it would let the platform assert something it knows is questioned.

### Happy Path

1. A party disputes a credit — from the credit itself, or escalated from a failed amendment (`02.01.06` D-02) carrying its context
2. System shows both parties what the record actually says, including capture evidence and who was present
3. Most disputes about captured work end here — the record answers the question and someone was misremembering
4. If not: co-present parties are asked what they know; "don't know" is expected and useful
5. If still unresolved: platform adjudication on the evidence; outcome applied; the credit's history retains the whole argument

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Dispute on an imported credit | No capture evidence, no witnesses on-platform. The majority case for years — same structural dead-end as `02.03.03` CX-03 | Honest framing: "this credit was imported, not captured — we have no record of the session" |
| Both parties are partly right | The realistic outcome for creative contribution. "Who wrote the riff" often has no clean answer, and forcing a binary manufactures a loser | Split/joint credit outcomes must be possible |
| Dispute is really about money | Very common — the credit is contested because a royalty depends on it. The credit question and the ownership question are different (domain 09) and must not be silently merged | Routed; both may be live at once |
| Disputant is serially litigious | Pattern signal to 24, invisible from inside one case | Rate-limited; escalated |
| Witnesses collude | Witness testimony inherits the ring problem (`02.04.04`). Same adversary, and a dispute is a high-value target for it | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Party is gone/dead | Dispute proceeds on record alone; the absent party cannot respond, which is exactly when the record has to carry the weight | Record-only adjudication |
| Dispute during embargo (`02.01.05`) | Runs privately among participants; no public contested marker, because there is no public credit | Participants only |
| Adjudication is wrong | The platform assigned a career fact incorrectly. Appeals belong to 24's machinery, not here | 24's appeal path |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncontested | Normal | No dispute surface |
| Direct | Dispute filed | Both parties see the record and the roll |
| Witness | Escalated | Co-present parties asked |
| Adjudicated | Escalated again | Platform review; SLA from the dispute cross-cut |
| Resolved | Outcome applied | Credit corrected/upheld/split; history retains the argument |
| Held | Contested at any stage | Suppressed publicly; downstream effects paused |
| Abandoned | Filer walked away | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — does the credit revert or stay marked? |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Credit disputes need their own dispute engine, since credits are the core domain | Domain-importance reasoning; the sweep listed it as a peer capability | ❌ **REJECTED** — case management, evidence handling, adjudicator assignment, SLAs and appeals are identical across services, gear, booking, ticketing and rights disputes. Building a second engine here would fork the machinery and guarantee two divergent appeal experiences. **What is genuinely domain-specific is narrow**: what counts as evidence about a musical contribution, and what a contested credit does while held. That is a feature riding a cross-cut, which is why this is `02.05` and not a folder. |
| DT-02 | The platform can determine who is right | The feature's implicit promise, and what users will expect from a "verified credits" product | ❌ **REJECTED, and the distinction is the honest version of the product's claim.** For captured sessions, the platform is not adjudicating — it is *reading a contemporaneous record*, which is cheap and nearly always decisive. For everything else, it is weighing memories years later, which is precisely the failure `problem-statement.md` blames the industry for. The product's real claim is **"disputes about work we captured are cheap"**, not "we settle disputes well". Marketing the latter would promise something the platform cannot do and invite liability (`02.04` Q-03). |
| DT-03 | A dispute has a winner | Standard dispute framing; the case must resolve | ❌ **REJECTED** — creative contribution is frequently genuinely joint, and "who wrote the riff" often has no true binary answer. A resolution model that must produce a loser will manufacture false precision on questions that do not have precise answers, and will do it in the cases that matter most (the contested ones). Split, joint, and "both credited differently" must be first-class outcomes. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Rides the platform dispute cross-cut; does not build its own engine | DT-01. Only credit-specific semantics are domain-owned. |
| D-02 | Contested credits are held and suppressed — never deleted, never published | Deletion lets a dispute erase a fact; publication asserts what the platform knows is questioned. |
| D-03 | Escalation ladder: direct → witness → adjudicated → legal (out of scope) | Each rung is more expensive and less reliable. Adjudication is a failure of the cheap paths, not the destination. |
| D-04 | Joint/split outcomes are first-class | DT-03. Creative contribution is often genuinely joint; a forced binary manufactures false precision. |
| D-05 | Witness requests are not votes | Consistent with `02.04.01` D-01 — "don't know" is expected and useful. Counting votes would let the bigger friend group win a factual question. |
