# Feature: Gear ↔ Credit Linkage

> **Number**: 02.09
> **Parent**: [Credits & Attribution](./credits-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Attaching the gear to the contribution: the SM7B on that vocal, the '62 Jazzmaster on that solo, the room's Neve on the drum bus. It turns two otherwise-separate records into something neither has alone — a **gear discography** ("every record this amp is on") — and it is the reason the domain index cites gear discography as one of domain 02's outputs.

Small in surface area, genuinely novel in effect. No competitor has it because none of them are in the room when the mic is chosen.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own contributions) | Tags the instrument they actually played on a take. Musicians care about this to a degree outsiders find hard to believe — the gear is part of the identity, and "my '68 Deluxe is on 40 records" is a story they will tell. For a gear seller (domain 13), it is also provenance that carries a price premium. |
| Producer | Full | Tags the chain — mic, pre, compressor, room. This is exactly what a producer's session notes already record informally, and exactly what nobody can reconstruct later. Also the person who wants it back: "what did we use on the last record's vocal?" is a question producers ask constantly and answer badly. |
| Operator | Config (room's gear) | The room's gear list (domain 16) is the source for house equipment, and the room's own claim: "records made on our desk". Directly commercial — a studio's gear discography is marketing that sells sessions. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees gear on a public credit where it is published. A real fan behavior (the "what amp is that" question is endless) and pure discovery surface. No tagging, no gear registry access. |

## Behavior

The linkage attaches at **contribution granularity, not credit granularity** — which is why it can only be captured in `02.02.02`. "This session used a Neve" is nearly worthless; "this vocal went through this mic into this pre" is the fact. That granularity is only available at the moment of tracking, from the person who plugged it in. Reconstructed later, it is a guess with a brand name on it.

Two sources, different weight:

- **Registered gear** (domain 15) — a specific physical item with a serial number and an owner. Links to *that* item, which is what makes a gear discography possible and what gives a used-gear listing (domain 13) real provenance.
- **Generic gear** — "an SM7B", not *this* SM7B. Most tagging. Still useful for discovery and for the producer's own recall; carries no provenance.

The design risk is scope creep in the capture flow. `02.02` is already fighting for every second of the Producer's attention (`02.02.03` DT-01), and gear tagging is *optional detail on top of the thing that barely gets done*. If it costs the capture flow anything, it costs credits — and credits are the thesis. It must be a byproduct (derived from the room's gear list, or from a saved chain preset) or it must be genuinely free to skip.

### Happy Path

1. A contribution is being logged (`02.02.02`)
2. Gear is offered from the room's registered list (16), the player's own registry (15), or recent use — not typed
3. Tagged in a tap or skipped in zero
4. Link binds gear to contribution; registered items link to the specific instrument
5. Gear discography accumulates on the item's page (15) and the credit's display (`02.01.02`)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Gear is not registered anywhere | Generic tag by make/model. Useful for discovery, no provenance | Generic tag |
| Registered gear is sold (domain 13) | The discography stays with the **item**, not the seller — that is the entire value ("this amp is on 40 records" is what the buyer is paying for). But the item's history now reveals the previous owner's sessions | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` — real privacy question (Q-01) |
| Gear tagged on an embargoed credit (`02.01.05`) | Follows the credit's visibility. Gear discography must be embargo-aware, or "our Neve is on an unreleased Beyoncé record" leaks the session | Suppressed with the credit |
| Producer tags gear they don't own (studio's) | Normal and correct — the room's gear on the room's session | Room-attributed |
| Gear claim is false ("recorded on a U47") | Nobody attests gear. It sits at self-asserted tier permanently — and a false vintage-mic claim inflates a gear listing's price (13) | Low tier always |
| Tagging costs capture-flow attention | The real risk. Gear detail is optional; credits are not | Zero-cost skip, always |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Untagged | No gear on the contribution | Nothing; never a prompt-shaped nag |
| Generic | Make/model tagged | Gear name on the credit |
| Registered | Linked to a specific item (15) | Item link; contributes to gear discography |
| Suppressed | Parent credit embargoed | Hidden with the credit |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attaches at contribution granularity, never session granularity | "This session used a Neve" is noise; "this vocal through this mic" is the fact. Only available at tracking time. |
| D-02 | Byproduct or nothing — never a prompt in the capture flow | DT-02. Gear detail must not cost credit capture a single second. |
| D-03 | Gear discography follows the item, not the owner | DT-03. It is what a buyer pays for — and it is why the privacy consequence exists. |
| D-04 | Gear claims are never attested; permanently self-asserted tier | Nobody counter-signs a mic choice. A false U47 claim inflating a listing's price is a 13/24 problem, not something the tier model can fix. |
