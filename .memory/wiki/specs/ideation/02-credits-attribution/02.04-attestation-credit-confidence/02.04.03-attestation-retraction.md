# Feature: Attestation Retraction

> **Number**: 02.04.03
> **Parent**: [Attestation & Credit Confidence](./02.04-attestation-credit-confidence-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Taking back a confirmation. Someone attested a credit and now says they shouldn't have — they were wrong, they misread what they were confirming, they were pressured, or they have learned something since. Retraction exists because an attestation the attester cannot withdraw is not a statement of belief, it is a signature under duress, and a graph built on unwithdrawable signatures is not measuring what it claims to measure.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (retracts own attestations) | Withdraws a confirmation they gave. Must state why — retraction is a strong signal and an unexplained one is unusable by everyone downstream. Cannot retract *someone else's* attestation, and cannot use retraction to remove a credit naming them (that is a dispute, `02.05`). |
| Producer | Full | Retracts their own attestation, including on sessions they ran — the highest-impact case, since their attestation carries the most weight and its withdrawal takes the most away. Their retraction on a credit they originally asserted is close to a self-contradiction and should be treated as a dispute precursor. |
| Operator | Config | Retracts room-level attestations ("this session was not in our room after all"). Narrow, and occasionally important — a wrongly-attributed session is a false claim about the room's business. |
| Fan | None | Fans never attest (`02.04.01` Role Lens), so there is nothing to retract. Explicit absence. |

## Behavior

Retraction is **not deletion**. The original attestation and its withdrawal both persist in history, because "she confirmed this in 2026 and withdrew it in 2027" is a materially different fact from "she never confirmed it" — and in a dispute six years later, the difference is the whole argument. This is the same principle as `02.01.06` D-01 (supersession over overwrite), arriving from a different direction and reinforcing sub-domain Q-01: the record wants to be append-only.

Effects cascade immediately: tier re-derives downward (`02.04.02` D-01), the discography line changes on a page that may already have been cited, traversal edge weight drops (`02.01.03`), and any co-sign corroboration the attestation supplied is withdrawn with it (`02.02` CX-04 Q5).

The asymmetry that makes this dangerous: **retraction is a weapon**. Attestations are worth something, so the ability to withdraw one is leverage. `personas.md` records the Producer's worst intentional behavior as "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute" — the same instinct applied here is "retract the credit attestation until I get paid". A retraction system with no friction is a coercion tool aimed at the least powerful party in the room.

But friction cannot be the answer either, because the honest retraction — "I confirmed the wrong thing, I want to fix it" — is exactly what integrity requires and must not be punished. The reasons-required design (D-02) is the provisional compromise: cheap enough to be honest with, structured enough that patterns of weaponisation are visible to `02.04.04` and 24.

### Happy Path

1. Attester finds an attestation they gave and no longer stand behind
2. They retract it, stating why — mistake, pressured, learned otherwise, or "I confirmed the wrong row"
3. Original and retraction both persist; the attestation stops counting as evidence
4. Tier re-derives (`02.04.02`); the credited party is told, with the reason
5. If the retraction implies the credit is false rather than merely unconfirmed, a dispute is offered — never opened automatically

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Retraction used as payment leverage | The anti-persona case (`personas.md`). Pattern-visible to 24; a single instance is indistinguishable from an honest retraction | Reason recorded; pattern escalates |
| Credit drops below a tier used downstream (royalty registration, a booking that cited it) | Downstream consumers hold a stale assessment. The expensive case; same shape as `02.01.06`'s post-export problem | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Attester retracts everything they ever attested | Mass retraction — a leaving user, a compromised account, or a grudge. Rate-limited, and it is a strong 24 signal | Bounded; flagged |
| Retraction on a credit the attester also asserted (Producer) | Near-self-contradiction: they said it happened, now they won't stand behind it. Should read as a dispute precursor, not a tier tweak | Dispute strongly offered |
| Credited party contests the retraction | They cannot force someone to vouch for them. Their recourse is the credit's other evidence, or a dispute about the fact | "This person has withdrawn their confirmation" |
| Retraction after the attester's account is deleted | Does their attestation survive their departure? Unresolved and it decides whether the graph rots as users churn | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` |
| Attester retracts, then re-attests | Allowed but the flip-flop is itself signal — for detection, not for punishment | History shows both |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Active | Attestation stands | Counts as evidence; contributes to tier |
| Retracted | Attester withdrew | No longer evidence; both acts in history |
| Retracted-contested | Credited party disputes the credit after retraction | Dispute (`02.05`) |
| Rate-limited | Mass retraction detected | Blocked; flagged to 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Retraction should be hard, because attestations must mean something | Integrity instinct: cheap withdrawal makes confirmation worthless | ❌ **REJECTED, and the reasoning inverts.** An attestation that cannot be withdrawn is not a belief — it is a signature under duress, and the graph would be measuring what people once clicked rather than what they currently stand behind. Worse, high friction punishes the honest retractor (who is *helping*) while barely inconveniencing the coercive one (who is motivated). The real defence against weaponised retraction is **pattern visibility** (`02.04.04`, domain 24), not friction. Friction filters for motivation, and the bad actor has more of it. |
| DT-02 | Retraction and dispute are the same act — withdrawing support means saying it's false | They look adjacent: both weaken a credit | ❌ **REJECTED** — "I no longer vouch for this" and "this is false" are different claims with different evidentiary weight, and conflating them would make every honest correction an accusation against a colleague. Most retractions are "I confirmed the wrong row at 2am", not "he's lying". Auto-escalating them would teach people never to retract, which costs the graph its only self-correction mechanism. Offer the dispute; never open it (D-03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Retraction preserves history — never deletes the original | "Confirmed then withdrew" ≠ "never confirmed". The difference is the argument in every future dispute. |
| D-02 | Reasons are required | Cheap enough for honesty, structured enough that weaponisation is visible as a pattern. |
| D-03 | Retraction never auto-opens a dispute | DT-02. Auto-escalation makes every honest correction an accusation and kills self-correction. |
| D-04 | Low friction, high visibility | DT-01. Friction filters for motivation, and the bad actor is the motivated one. |
