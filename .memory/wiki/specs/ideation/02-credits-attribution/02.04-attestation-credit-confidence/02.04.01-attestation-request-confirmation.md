# Feature: Attestation Request & Confirmation

> **Number**: 02.04.01
> **Parent**: [Attestation & Credit Confidence](./02.04-attestation-credit-confidence-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

One person confirming another person's credit — the act that makes the graph verified rather than asserted. It is the same act whether it happens as a tap in the room at session close (`02.02.03`, where it converts) or as a request chased by notification weeks later (where it mostly doesn't). This feature owns both, and the gap between their conversion rates is the reason `02.02.03` D-01 exists.

What this feature owns: **the attestation record and the request/answer cycle** — who may ask, who may answer, what the answer means, what it costs to give, and how often anyone may be asked. What it does not own: *when* the in-room ask fires (`02.02.03`), what the answer does to the tier (`02.04.02`), taking an answer back (`02.04.03`), and whether the answers were honest (`02.04.04`).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two directions. **Confirming**: someone credited them, they say yes — the one-tap act. **Requesting**: their credit is unconfirmed and they ask the people who were there to vouch, which is the visible gap on their own discography closing. Cannot attest a credit they have no knowledge of; attesting is a claim to have been there. Cannot attest their own credit (D-11), including a credit held by a band entity they are a member of. |
| Producer | Full (attests as session owner) | Their attestation is the high-value one — `personas.md` makes them the trust broker explicitly. They confirm players' credits on sessions they ran, and their confirmation carries more than a peer's because they were running the room, not just in it. They are also the only party who may request attestation on a credit that is not theirs (D-12) — including on behalf of an unclaimed shell who cannot ask for themselves. Where the credit they are attesting competes with one of their own on the same work, the record carries that conflict (D-20). |
| Operator | Config (room-level facts only) | Attests that a session happened in their room and when — independent, verifiable, and genuinely useful corroboration. **Cannot** attest who played what; a room that vouches for musical contributions is vouching for something it does not know. The restriction is about **presence, not persona** (D-17): the studio owner who personally engineered the session attests as a participant, with full access, because they were in the room. What is limited to room facts is the **room's** attestation — the entity's, not the human's. |
| Fan | None | No attestation at any tier, and no visibility into who attested — which Q-01 now settles for *every* reader rather than only for fans: attester identity never publishes, at any tier, to any viewer (`01.06.02` D-02, `02.01.02` DT-04). A fan vouching for a credit would be vouching for something they cannot have witnessed, and it is exactly the crowd-verification model (`02.01.06` D-04) the product rejects. Explicit absence. |

## Behavior

An attestation is **evidence with a name on it**. Its value comes entirely from the attester having been in a position to know, and from their being accountable for saying so. Both properties are load-bearing: an anonymous attestation is worthless, and an attestation from someone who wasn't there is a lie with a signature.

The design constraints, in order of importance:

1. **Confirmation must cost one tap.** Every additional step is paid in confirmation rate, which is the product's core metric. A form here is a form that doesn't get filled in.
2. **The ask must be reciprocal-feeling, not transactional.** "Did she play on this?" from a colleague converts. A platform-generated compliance request does not.
3. **Refusal must be as easy as confirmation.** If saying no is harder than saying yes, the yeses mean nothing — and the whole tier model then rests on friction rather than truth.

The chase problem: attestation requests to people who have moved on is precisely the reconstruction workflow `problem-statement.md` describes as the industry's failure ("chasing people who have moved on, for a project they've mentally closed"). WeJammin does it better only by making it rarer — the in-room tap is the product; the chase is the fallback that admits the product didn't fire.

### What is being attested

An attestation is scoped to **exactly one credit row: one party × one role × one work** (DT-04). It is never scoped to a session, a person, or a release. "Confirm this session" is not an act anyone can honestly perform — a drummer knows what they played and has no idea who mixed it.

The three facets are **material**. Any change to party, role or work invalidates every attestation on that row and requires re-asking (D-10, DT-07); changes to anything else (track title spelling, session date correction, the work's artwork) do not. Without this rule, "get the drummer to confirm *played drums on Track 1*, then amend the row to *produced Track 1*" is a one-step laundering attack that converts a cheap true attestation into an expensive false one, at the graph's highest tier, with the attester's name on it and no notice to them.

**D-10 is the single materiality boundary, and the test is flat identity — not entailment.** Owner ratification of DQ-05.1 subsumed `02.04.02` D-13/DT-08's entailment layer into this rule: there is no semantic ordering over roles, no "strictly weaker or equal" carry, and nothing above this test. Two exclusions follow and must not be re-litigated downstream:

- **Instrument is not one of the three facets.** Uniqueness is (party, role, work) (`02.01.01` D-03) and instrument is multi-valued on one record (`02.06` D-16), so instrument accretion or refinement ("Drums" → "Drums — brushes") **never** invalidates — it is not part of the claim's identity.
- **No taxonomy operation is a change to the role** (DQ-05.2). Deprecation does not rewrite credits (`02.06` D-04), existing credits never re-resolve (`02.06` D-11), and the one legitimate retroactive re-point — the first mapping of a pending alias — is resolution completion, not a role change: the attester confirmed the *literal*, which stays on the credit line unchanged (`02.06` D-03/D-13).

The accepted cost, stated plainly: a routine, honest role correction ("Producer" → "Additional Production") **does** re-ask. It is bounded by D-09's cadence caps and D-21's request collapsing, and the "nothing happens" promise the entailment rule made on the role axis is withdrawn.

### The three answers

`confirm` · `refuse` · `don't know` — each is exactly one tap (D-01, D-16). No answer is a form, and none is cheaper than the others.

- **Confirm** — "I was there and this is right." Records; may be attributable (Q-01).
- **Refuse** — "I won't stand behind this." Records; **never attributable** to anyone (D-06). A reason is offered *after* the refusal is already recorded, is optional, and never blocks it — because a reason picker in front of the refusal makes no more expensive than yes, and biases the graph toward yes, which is the one bias that destroys it.
- **Don't know** — "I have no basis." Records as an absence of evidence, contributes nothing (`02.04` CX-01), and closes the pair — they have told you they cannot help; asking again is nagging someone for information they do not have.

All three close the request permanently (D-08). The credit's tier re-derives (`02.04.02`), which this feature does not own.

### Silence is the design, not an oversight

A refusal is invisible to both the requester and the credited party (D-06, D-07). This is deliberate and it is the sharpest call in the feature. The alternatives are worse:

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Requester sees **who** refused | Retaliation. The refuser wants to be hired again; in a small industry, an honest refusal ends relationships. Honest refusal is exactly what the graph needs most, so it must be the safest act available. |
| Requester sees **that** someone refused | An anonymous accusation. In a 5-person roll the requester can guess who, so it delivers the retaliation risk *and* the Kafkaesque one. |
| Requester sees **nothing** (chosen) | The requester sees "unconfirmed" — which is true. The pair closes, so they stop asking. The refuser who wants their objection to count is offered the **named** path: a contest (`02.05`), with due process. |

The rule that makes the silence honest rather than merely convenient: **a refusal must produce no user-visible negative on the credit — a credit with one refusal and no confirmations must render identically to a credit nobody answered** (D-07). The credited party is therefore not being denied information that would change their behaviour: in both cases the answer is "go get evidence." The refusal is still real evidence internally, to `02.04.02`'s scoring and to `02.04.04`'s topology.

Negative signal is therefore **either silent-and-private, or named-and-due-process. Never anonymous-and-public.** That is the invariant.

### The nag is spent from the requester's account, not the platform's

Resolves Q-03. When Sarah asks the drummer to vouch for her, the notification says *Sarah* asked — because an unattributed platform request converts badly (constraint 2), and because attribution is what makes the cost land where it belongs. **The reputational cost of the ask is borne by the requester, in relationship capital, with someone they will work with again.**

It follows that the platform may not spend it for them (DT-10). There is **no automatic reminder schedule.** Every nudge is an explicit act by the requester, who is the only party entitled to decide that this relationship can absorb another ask.

**Cadence, per (credit × attester) pair:**

| Ask | Earliest | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Initial request | Immediate | 1 |
| Nudge 1 | ≥ 7 days after the request | 1 |
| Nudge 2 | ≥ 21 days after nudge 1 | 1 |
| Further asks | — | **0 — the pair is closed permanently**, to every requester, forever |

7 days is a working week: a professional who has not answered in a week did not miss it, they deprioritised it. 21 days is past the point where a reminder reads as "still waiting" rather than "still nagging". Two nudges because the third is where a colleague becomes an irritant. These are judgement calls made from the professional-courtesy norms the persona operates in, not derived from data — they are defaults to be measured against confirmation rate and complaint rate post-launch, not constants.

**Per-recipient caps** (DT-11 — the limit above is per-pair, but the harassment is per-recipient):

- ≤ **5** outstanding requests from all requesters combined at any moment.
- ≤ **10** requests per rolling 30 days from all requesters combined.
- Excess **queues silently** — it is never dropped and never delivered early. Bulk requesting (a Producer importing 300 credits and asking everyone) is permitted as an intent and rate-shaped on delivery: allow the ask, shape the arrival.
- After **5 consecutive unanswered** requests, the recipient's request channel goes dormant: further requests queue and surface only in-product, never as push or email. Someone who has ignored five asks has opted out by behaviour, and the platform should read that rather than escalate.
- **Mute** is always available, per-requester and global. It lets an unwilling attester avoid being asked — which changes no outcome, because nobody can be compelled to vouch (`02.04.03` CX-02 Q3), and removes a harassment surface.

### The request must carry enough context to be answerable

A request that arrives without the session's identifying context is asking someone to guess (DT-06), and DT-01 established that guesses enter the graph as evidence. A session player has played 40 sessions since; "did Sarah play bass on *Midnight*?" is unanswerable in isolation and answerable in one second given the date, the room, and one other name.

**Context floor** (D-14): a request is offered only if it can carry the **work**, the **date**, and **at least one other named party who was present**. Below the floor the request affordance is absent — not disabled with an explanation, absent — and the credit stays at asserted tier honestly, per domain D-08 (evidence sets the tier; it never gates the credit).

The structural consequence, stated plainly because it is uncomfortable: **the chase's quality is bounded by the session record's quality, so it works best exactly where it is least needed and fails where it is most needed.** A well-captured session barely needs the chase; a session nobody recorded cannot support one. This is not a flaw to engineer around — it is `problem-statement.md`'s thesis arriving inside this feature, and the correct response is `02.02.03`, not a better chase.

### Who may ask

Only (D-12): the **credited party** on their own credit; the **session owner** (`02.02.01`) on any credit in their session, including on behalf of an unclaimed shell who cannot ask for themselves; and the **close prompt** (`02.02.03`) firing the in-room case. Arbitrary users may not generate requests about arbitrary credits — an open professional-to-professional ask channel is a spam engine wearing a credits badge.

Requests are **suppressed** between parties with an open dispute (`02.05`) or an active block (24) (D-19). Asking your legal adversary to vouch for you is pointless at best and harassment through the platform's own channel at worst.

### The claim is ours; the note is theirs

The confirmation surface renders the claim **canonically from the record** — never from requester-supplied text (D-13, DT-12). An attestation request whose body the requester authors is a phishing surface aimed squarely at professionals, and a confirm tap on a misleading label is a signature obtained by deception.

The requester may attach one optional note: **≤ 200 characters, plain text, no links**, rendered visually subordinate to the canonical claim, and moderated as any other user-to-user message (24). No links is not a detail — a link in an attestation request is the attack.

### Answering is an authenticated act

An attestation is recorded only through an authenticated act (D-18). A link, tapped, may navigate; it may never *record*. The cost is real and lands exactly where it hurts: a logged-out recipient opening an email pays two taps instead of one at the single moment the product is measuring. That trade is accepted — a forgeable attestation is not evidence, and the whole feature is an evidence feature. The delivery-channel consequence is Q-05.

### Happy Path

The async cycle — the path this feature owns end-to-end. The in-room case (`02.02.03`) is the same act with steps 1–4 collapsed into the close prompt and step 6 answered before the room empties.

1. **A credit sits asserted and unattested** — usually an unconfirmed row from the close prompt, sometimes an import (`02.03.01`), sometimes a manual assertion. Its owner sees the gap on their own discography (`02.04.02` D-05: the gap is the engine).
2. **The requester taps "ask the people who were there."** The addressable set is derived from the session roll (`02.02.01`) — never guessed, never inferred from the credit — minus the credited party themselves (D-11), minus anyone muted or blocked (D-19), minus anyone at their per-recipient cap.
3. **System checks the context floor** (D-14). Work + date + ≥1 other named party present → the request is offered. Otherwise the affordance is not shown and the flow ends here.
4. **System sends one request per addressable attester**, attributed to the requester by name, carrying the canonical claim and its context, plus the optional ≤200-char note. Concurrent requests from different requesters about the same credit collapse into the one pending ask (D-21).
5. **The attester opens it** and sees the claim rendered before anything is tappable — party, role, work, date, who else was there. The three controls are inert until the claim and its context have rendered (DT-04): a tap on an unrendered claim is a blind confirm.
6. **The attester taps one of three.** Confirm / refuse / don't-know — one tap each, no form on any of them.
7. **The answer is accepted the instant it is tapped, and the pair closes.** The control resolves immediately and the attester never waits; if the answer cannot be delivered at that moment it is not lost — it is held and confirmed later, and the attester keeps seeing it as accepted (with a quiet pending-until-delivered cue) rather than a failure. The tap is the conversion event, and a failure surfaced at the tap is a permanent loss (DT-14).
8. **If refused**, the reason picker is offered *after* the refusal is already recorded — optional, dismissible, never a gate (D-16). If the reason implies the credit is false rather than merely unvouched, the **contest** path (`02.05`) is offered to the refuser, and never opened for them (D-03).
9. **The credit's tier re-derives** (`02.04.02`); the discography line changes visibly. The credited party is notified on confirmation — it is good news and it is the moment their page got stronger (`02.04` CX-01 Q4). They are **not** notified on refusal or don't-know (D-06, D-07).
10. **If unanswered**, the requester may nudge — twice, on their own initiative, per the cadence. Then the pair closes forever and the credit stays honestly labelled unconfirmed.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody responds | The modal async outcome. Credit stays at asserted tier — it does not decay, disappear or fail | Unconfirmed marker; requester may nudge twice, then never again |
| Attester says "I don't know" | Distinct from refusal and genuinely common — a drummer often doesn't know who mixed it. Must be a first-class answer, or people refuse-by-default to be safe. Contributes nothing; closes the pair | Recorded; neither confirms nor contests; no further asks |
| Attester refuses | Not automatically a dispute. "I don't think that's right" is weaker than "that's false" and forcing it into a dispute punishes honesty. Recorded; contest offered to the refuser only | Refuser sees the contest offer. **Requester and credited party see nothing — still "unconfirmed"** (D-06, D-07) |
| Requester spams attestation requests | Rate-limited per pair (1 + 2 nudges) **and** per recipient (≤5 outstanding, ≤10/30d). Excess queues, never drops | Bounded; requester sees "you've asked" with the nudge control dated |
| Many requesters, one recipient | The per-pair limit does not bind — 6 people each asking once is 6 asks (DT-11). The per-recipient cap absorbs it; the 6th queues | Recipient sees ≤5 at a time |
| Recipient has never answered anything | 5 consecutive unanswered → channel dormant. Further requests queue and surface in-product only, never push/email | Requester's request is accepted and delivered quietly; no lie is told about it |
| Attester was not actually there | Attesting is itself a claim to knowledge. False attestation is a Trust & Safety matter (24), not a credits one | — |
| Two people attest contradictorily | Confirm + refuse on one row. Both recorded; the contradiction is signal to `02.04.02` and `02.04.04`. **Not surfaced** — surfacing it would publish the refusal (D-06) | Nothing. Credit shows its confirmations only |
| Attester later retracts | `02.04.03` — tier re-derives downward | Tier change; requester notified |
| Attester wants to change their answer | Changing a recorded answer *is* retraction (`02.04.03`) followed by a new answer — not an edit. Before an answer is recorded, taps are idempotent | Retraction flow, with its reason requirement |
| Attester double-taps / taps the push twice | However many times one attester taps one credit, it counts once. One attestation, not two | One confirmation |
| **Credit is amended after attestation** | If party, role or work changed → **every attestation on the row is invalidated** and must be re-asked (D-10). Otherwise it stands. Without this, amend-after-attest launders a cheap true confirmation onto an expensive false claim (DT-07) | Attesters notified their confirmation no longer applies; credit drops to asserted; requester may re-ask |
| Attester is removed from the roll while their request is outstanding | **The request stands** (D-15). The roll is the Producer's assertion, not ground truth; voiding on removal makes roll-editing a witness-silencing tool aimed at whoever would refuse (DT-08) | Attester answers normally; removal is recorded alongside the answer as signal |
| Attester is asked about a credit they hold a competing claim to | Recorded with the conflict noted (D-20). A second "produced by" claim on one track is rivalrous — producer points are finite — so the attester is an interested party, not a neutral witness | Normal three-answer surface; conflict is internal (see Q-04) |
| Multi-hyphenate: the Producer ran the session *and* played on it | Normal, not an edge case — everyone in the room holds credits. They attest others' rows as a participant. Their own rows are excluded (D-11) | Their own row shows "waiting on others", never a self-confirm control |
| Multi-hyphenate: the Operator personally engineered the session | They attest as a **participant**, full access — presence is the constraint, not persona (D-17). The room-facts-only limit binds the **room entity's** attestation, not the human's | Full three-answer surface on contribution rows |
| Attester is a member of the band entity holding the credit | Self-attestation via an entity. Excluded from the addressable set (D-11). Whether the band holds credits at all is domain Q-12 | Row shows no attest control for members |
| Credited party is an unclaimed shell | They cannot request. The session owner requests on their behalf (D-12); confirmations accrue to the shell and are waiting at the claim inbox (`02.03.02`) when they join — a materially better first impression than an empty page | Producer sees "asked on their behalf"; claimant later sees a pre-vouched credit |
| Attester is not a platform user | No channel exists to reach them. Q-02 — a live product/legal question, not a design gap | Requester sees them as unaddressable, with no promise made |
| **Embargoed work** (`02.01.05`) | The attester is on the roll and under the same NDA, so asking is fine — but the **notification payload must not name the work**. "Confirm a credit" on a lock screen, details only inside the authenticated surface (DT-13) | Generic notification; full claim after auth |
| Credit is deleted/withdrawn while a request is outstanding | The request dies with it. A tapped notification must land on an explanation, never an orphan or an error | "This credit no longer exists" |
| Attester deletes their account while a request is outstanding | Request dies. Whether their *existing* attestations survive is `02.04.03` Q-01 | Requester's outstanding ask silently closes |
| Open dispute between requester and attester | Requests suppressed both directions (D-19) | No request affordance for that pair |
| Attester has blocked the requester (24) | No request. The block is honoured before any credits logic runs | Requester sees them as unaddressable, with no reason given |
| Requester attaches a link in the note | Rejected at input. No links, ever — an attestation request with a link is a phishing vector aimed at professionals (D-13) | Inline error on the note field; the request still sends without it |
| Tap fails — no connectivity (studio basement) | Answer accepted at the tap and held until it can be delivered; confirmed in the background and, failing that, when the app is next opened. Never a raw error at the tap (DT-14) | Answer appears accepted, with a quiet pending-until-delivered cue; consistent with `02.02` D-03 |
| Two attesters confirm simultaneously at close | Both record independently — no lock, nothing to contend for. The tier must settle once rather than flicker per tap (`02.04` CX-01 Q5); that convergence is `02.04.02`'s | Each sees their own confirmation land |
| Historical credit — witnesses dead, retired, or unreachable | No addressable set. The request affordance is absent; the credit sits at asserted tier permanently and honestly (domain D-08) | "Unconfirmed" with no request CTA and no explanation of absence |
| Compromised account mass-attests | Not detectable here — each attestation is individually plausible. Volume and topology are `02.04.04`'s; account recovery and mass reversal are 24's + `02.04.03`'s | — |
| Off-platform coercion: "confirm my credit and I'll rebook you" | Invisible to the platform by construction. Cannot be solved here; adjacent to `02.02.03` Q-02 (pairing confirmation with payment), which is the same coercion made official | — |

### States

**Attestation record lifecycle** — per (credit × attester) pair:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unaddressable | No roll, no context floor, or attester unreachable/blocked/muted | No request affordance at all — absent, not disabled |
| Unattested | Credit asserted, nobody asked | "Unconfirmed" + request CTA on own credits |
| Requested | Ask sent | "Waiting on <n>"; nudge control, dated to the earliest permitted |
| Queued | Recipient at per-recipient cap or dormant | Requester sees the ask accepted; recipient sees it in-product only |
| Attested | Confirmed | Verified marker; tier reflects who attested |
| Refused | Attester declined | **Refuser** sees their refusal + contest offer. **Nobody else sees anything** — the credit reads "unconfirmed", identical to no answer (D-07) |
| Unknown | Attester said they don't know | Recorded; neutral; pair closed |
| Contradicted | Confirm + refuse on one row | **Not surfaced** — internal signal only (`02.04.02`, `02.04.04`). Surfacing it publishes the refusal |
| Invalidated | Material amendment to party / role / work (D-10) | Attesters told their confirmation no longer applies; credit drops to asserted; re-ask offered |
| Closed | Any answer given, or 2 nudges exhausted | No further asks to that person about that credit, ever, from anyone |
| Retracted | Attester withdrew (`02.04.03`) | Tier change; requester notified |

**Surface states** — the confirmation view:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Request opened; claim + context being fetched | Claim skeleton. **The three controls are inert until the claim and its context have rendered** — a tap on an unrendered claim is a blind confirm (DT-04) |
| Empty | Credit has no addressable attesters (historical, no roll, all muted) | Own credit: "Unconfirmed" with no CTA. The absence is the honest state — offering an ask that goes nowhere is worse than offering none |
| Populated | Claim + context rendered | Party · role · work · date · who else was there · optional note; three equal-weight controls |
| Error | Claim cannot be loaded, or the credit vanished mid-flight | "This credit no longer exists" or a retry — **never** a partial claim with live controls |
| Partial | Context below floor but the request already exists (roll edited after sending) | Claim renders with the context it has and an explicit "we can't show you who else was there" — never a silent gap, because a missing cue reads as an absent fact |
| Queued / offline | Answer accepted but not yet delivered | Answer shown as accepted with a quiet pending-until-delivered cue; never a raw error at the tap (DT-14) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | "I don't know" must be a first-class answer alongside confirm and refuse | Domain-expert expectation nobody stated: session players routinely have no idea who mixed the record, who played on the other tracks, or what the engineer's exact role was | ✅ **CONFIRMED — and its absence would be quietly corrosive.** A binary confirm/refuse forces people with no knowledge to guess. Guessing up inflates the graph with worthless attestations at a high tier; guessing down manufactures contradictions between people who don't actually disagree. Both poison the tier model with noise that looks like signal. The third option is not a nicety — it is what keeps attestation meaning "I know this" rather than "I clicked this". |
| DT-02 | Attestation should be reciprocal — you attest mine, I attest yours | Natural social framing; it would drive volume hard | ❌ **REJECTED** — reciprocal attestation *is* the collusion ring (`02.04.04`), formalised and shipped by us. Building the exchange into the product would make the attack the intended flow and leave the detector fighting the UX. The social nudge to attest is fine; the quid-pro-quo is the thing the graph must not learn. |
| DT-03 | The in-room tap and the async request are different features | They feel entirely different — one is a moment, one is a chase | ❌ **REJECTED** — same act, same record, same evidentiary weight, radically different conversion. Splitting them would duplicate the state machine and invite the two paths to drift apart in what they mean. What differs is timing and delivery, which is `02.02.03`'s concern. Keeping them one feature is also what keeps the conversion gap *visible* rather than hiding it behind two success metrics that each look fine. |
| DT-04 | A credit is one thing, so one confirm/refuse over it is unambiguous | The obvious model, and the one "one tap" invites | ❌ **REJECTED** — a credit is a **compound claim: party × role × work**, and in music the role facet is where the money and the errors both live. "Engineer" vs "assistant engineer" vs "mix engineer" are different jobs with different neighbouring-rights consequences and different union scale; mis-stating one is the single most common credit error there is. A bare refusal on a role error reads as "you're lying" when the truth is "you got my job title wrong", and forcing that conflation makes the honest answer feel like an accusation against a colleague. **The row is the unit** (`02.02.03` D-04 already scopes the in-room tap this way), the three facets are material (D-10), and refusal must be able to say *which facet* is wrong — but only after the tap has landed (D-16), never in front of it. |
| DT-05 | A refusal should be visible to the credited party so they can respond to it | Due-process instinct, and it feels unfair to be silently un-vouched-for | ❌ **REJECTED — and the reasoning generalises into the feature's central invariant.** Three options exist and two are indefensible. Naming the refuser invites retaliation in an industry where the refuser needs to be hired again — which makes honest refusal expensive, and D-03 exists precisely because honesty must be cheap. Reporting an *unnamed* refusal is worse: on a 5-person roll the requester can guess who, so it carries the retaliation risk *and* is an anonymous accusation. The remaining option — the requester sees nothing — is honest only because of D-07: a refusal produces no visible negative, so a refused credit renders exactly like an unanswered one, and the credited party is denied no information that would change what they do next (in both cases: go get other evidence). The refuser who wants their objection to *count* has the named path: contest, with due process (`02.05`). **Negative signal is either silent-and-private or named-and-due-process. Never anonymous-and-public.** Note this is the mirror of `02.04` CX-03's complaint about ring detection — and the difference that makes it acceptable here is that a human made a choice and was offered a named alternative, where detection offers nothing. |
| DT-06 | An async request is the same ask as the in-room one, just later | DT-03 establishes it is the same act, which invites the assumption that it is the same question | ❌ **REJECTED — later is not neutral, it is a different epistemic act.** In the room, "did she play bass?" is a question about the present. Six weeks later it is a question about **memory** — and `problem-statement.md` is unambiguous that memory is "contested, lossy, and self-interested", which is the root cause the entire product exists to route around. A request arriving without the session's identifying context is therefore not a weak version of the in-room tap; it is *the reconstruction workflow*, run by us, and DT-01 tells us the guesses it produces enter the graph as evidence. Hence the context floor (D-14). The consequence is structural and worth naming out loud: **the chase's quality is bounded by the session record's quality, so it works best where it is least needed and fails where it is most needed.** That is not a bug to engineer around — it is the thesis, and the answer to it is `02.02.03`, not a cleverer chase. |
| DT-07 | An attestation, once given, stays attached to its credit | The natural model — it is a record about a credit | ❌ **REJECTED — this is an open laundering hole and the most valuable catch in this pass.** Amendment (`02.01.06`) is a legitimate feature; combined with a persistent attestation it is an attack: get the drummer to confirm the cheap true claim ("played drums on Track 1"), amend the row to the expensive false one ("produced Track 1"), and keep the confirmation. The result is a fabricated credit sitting at the graph's highest tier, carrying a real professional's name as its evidence, with that professional never notified. The attestation is a statement about **a specific claim**, not about a row ID. Any change to party, role or work invalidates it (D-10); everything else does not. Getting the materiality boundary wrong in either direction is costly — too broad and every typo fix triggers a re-ask storm that burns the rate limits and the goodwill; too narrow and the hole stays open. |
| DT-08 | Removing someone from the roll should void their outstanding attestation request — they weren't there, so their answer is worthless | Consistency instinct: the roll defines who is positioned to know (D-14, step 2) | ❌ **REJECTED — it would ship a witness-silencing tool.** The roll is the **Producer's assertion**, not ground truth (`02.02.01`: the Producer "adds, removes and re-roles anyone"). Writing a contributor out of a session is a documented form of credit theft, and if roll-removal voided requests, the Producer who wants a credit unchallenged simply removes whoever would refuse — the platform enforcing the erasure on their behalf. Requests and attestations therefore survive roll removal (D-15), and the removal is recorded alongside the answer, where it is signal rather than a control. The person who genuinely was not there loses one tap answering "I don't know" or refusing. That is the correct party to inconvenience. |
| DT-09 | The Operator's room-facts-only limit (D-05) is a property of the persona | How the Role Lens reads, and how the sub-domain Role Matrix renders it | ❌ **REJECTED / REFRAMED — the constraint is presence, and the attester is sometimes an entity rather than a human.** `personas.md` says the Operator "often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves", and `personas.md` opens by insisting these are lenses on behaviour, not account types. So the studio owner who personally engineered the session is a witness to the contributions and must attest them fully — a persona-keyed restriction would silence the best-placed witness in the room on the grounds of what their business card says. What is actually limited to room facts is the **room's** attestation: the entity vouches that a session occurred in it and when, because that is the whole of what an entity can witness. One human, two attesters, two scopes (D-17). This also exposes the general rule the feature was missing: **nobody may attest what they were not positioned to witness** — of which D-05 is one instance, D-11 (no self-attestation, including through a band entity you are a member of) is another, and both fall out of the same principle rather than being separate rules. |
| DT-10 | The platform should nudge unanswered requests automatically to maximise confirmation rate | Confirmation rate is the product's core metric, and automated reminders are the cheapest lever on it | ❌ **REJECTED — it spends someone else's capital.** The request is attributed to the requester ("Sarah asked you to confirm") because attribution is what makes it convert (constraint 2). That same attribution means the *nag* is also from Sarah — so a platform-scheduled reminder spends **Sarah's** relationship capital, with a colleague she will work with again, without asking her. She would never send the third reminder herself; the platform sending it in her name is the platform making her look desperate to a peer, to move its own metric. Nudges are therefore requester-initiated only (D-08), on a cadence that bounds even them (D-09). This answers Q-03 in both halves: **the requester bears the cost, therefore the requester holds the trigger.** |
| DT-11 | Rate-limiting per (credit × attester) pair is sufficient to prevent harassment | The obvious limit, and the one D-04 originally implied | ❌ **REJECTED — the limit is per-pair, but the harassment is per-recipient.** Six people each asking one well-connected engineer once, about six different credits, is six perfectly compliant requests and one irritated professional. Scale it: a session drummer who played on 200 records is addressable by every one of those rooms. The per-pair cap never binds and the platform becomes a nag engine assembled from individually-polite acts — with each requester able to point at the rules and be right. Hence the per-recipient ceiling (≤5 outstanding, ≤10 per 30 days, queue the excess) and the dormancy rule. Corollary worth carrying: **any per-actor rate limit on a many-to-one channel is measuring the wrong side of the relationship**, which likely generalises well beyond this feature. |
| DT-12 | The requester should be able to write the ask in their own words — it is a social act, so it should sound like one | Constraint 2: the reciprocal-feeling ask converts and the compliance request does not | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a moment, REJECTED as a mechanism.** A colleague's voice does convert, and stripping it entirely makes the ask feel like a compliance email — the exact failure constraint 2 names. But a requester-authored claim is a **signature obtained by deception**: write anything above the buttons, and the confirm tap attaches a real professional's name to whatever the record actually says. The split resolves it: the **claim is rendered canonically from the record and is never requester-authored**; the requester gets one note, ≤200 chars, plain text, subordinate to the claim, moderated like any DM (D-13). **No links** is the load-bearing half — an attestation request is a high-trust message to a professional about their own career, which makes it close to the ideal phishing wrapper, and we would be delivering it. |
| DT-13 | Embargo (`02.01.05`) is about publication, so it has nothing to say about attestation requests | Clean separation of concerns: the attester is on the roll and under the same NDA, so asking them leaks nothing | ❌ **REJECTED — the notification payload is a publication surface.** "Confirm your bass credit on *[unreleased major-label track]*" is legible on a lock screen, on a smartwatch, in a mail client preview, on a screen-shared laptop, over the shoulder of someone on a train. The recipient is authorised; the lock screen is not. This is the leak that loses the professional tier permanently — the sessions worth capturing are exactly the ones under NDA, and one leak of that shape is the whole segment's evidence that we cannot be trusted in the room. Request notifications for embargoed works carry a generic body; the claim renders only inside the authenticated surface. Notification payloads are a data-egress surface across every domain, not a copy decision. |
| DT-14 | A failed write on the confirm tap can surface an error and ask the user to retry | Standard, and correct almost everywhere else in the product | ❌ **REJECTED here specifically, because of where this tap sits.** The tap is the conversion event the entire domain is built to produce, and it happens in the worst network conditions the product has — a studio basement (`02.02` D-03), a load-in, a phone on 1 bar. An error at that instant does not cost a retry; it costs the attestation permanently, because the person has already put the phone away and mentally closed the session. The answer records optimistically and is held until it can be sent — completed in the background, and on the next foreground if it still hasn't landed; the user sees acceptance with a sync marker, never a raw failure. This is the same reasoning as `02.02.03` D-02 (never block the close) applied to the other end of the same act — and the reason idempotency per (credit × attester) is non-optional, since an optimistic write plus a held-and-resent write plus a double-tap is three chances to record one answer twice. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **02.02.03 Session Close Capture Prompt** ([../02.02-session-capture/02.02.03-session-close-capture-prompt.md](../02.02-session-capture/02.02.03-session-close-capture-prompt.md)) — **trigger dependency**: the close prompt fires this feature's confirm act at its best moment. The prompt owns the timing; this feature owns the record, the answer semantics, and everything after the tap. DT-06: the async path is this same act stripped of the context that made it answerable.
- Touches **02.02.01 Session Roll Call** ([../02.02-session-capture/02.02.01-session-roll-call.md](../02.02-session-capture/02.02.01-session-roll-call.md)) — **permission + state race**: the roll defines the addressable set (who may be asked, who may answer). Two live constraints: `02.02.01` Q-01 (the roll may not exist at all) means the addressable set is often empty, which is this feature's Empty state and not an error; and D-15/DT-08 — **roll edits must never void outstanding requests or existing attestations**, or roll-removal becomes a witness-silencing tool.
- Touches **02.01.06 Credit Correction & Amendment** ([../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.06-credit-correction-amendment.md](../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.06-credit-correction-amendment.md)) — **shared entity, and the highest-severity cross-cut in this feature.** DT-07: a material amendment (party / role / work) must invalidate every attestation on the row. If amendment ships without this, amend-after-attest is a one-step laundering attack into the graph's top tier. Neither feature can enforce it alone — 02.01.06 owns the amendment, this feature owns the invalidation, and the materiality boundary must be defined once, in one place, or the two will drift into a hole.
- Touches **02.04.02 Provenance Tiers** ([02.04.02-provenance-tiers-credit-confidence.md](./02.04.02-provenance-tiers-credit-confidence.md)) — **shared entity + trigger dependency**: attestations are the tier's primary input. Two requirements pass across this edge: "don't know" must contribute nothing (`02.04` CX-01), and **a refusal must produce no user-visible negative** (D-07) — a credit with one refusal and no confirmations must render identically to an unanswered one, or the silence in D-06 is a lie and the refuser is exposed by inference.
- Touches **02.04.03 Attestation Retraction** ([02.04.03-attestation-retraction.md](./02.04.03-attestation-retraction.md)) — **state race**: changing a recorded answer *is* retraction plus a new answer, not an edit — the two features share one record and must not offer two ways to alter it. Before an answer is recorded, taps are idempotent; after, retraction's reason requirement (`02.04.03` D-02) applies.
- Touches **02.04.04 Ring Detection** ([02.04.04-attestation-ring-collusion-detection.md](./02.04.04-attestation-ring-collusion-detection.md)) — **other (adversarial)**: DT-02 — the requester↔attester pairs this feature creates *are* the topology detection analyses, and reciprocity is its primary signal. D-02 (no quid-pro-quo mechanic) exists so the product does not manufacture the exact pattern the detector hunts. Refusals and the roll-removal marker (D-15) are inputs detection cannot get anywhere else.
- Touches **02.05 Credit Dispute Resolution** ([../02.05-credit-dispute-resolution.md](../02.05-credit-dispute-resolution.md)) — **trigger dependency + permission**: refusal and contradiction are dispute precursors, never automatic escalations (D-03). The contest is offered to the **refuser only** and is the named path that makes silent refusal defensible (DT-05). Permission: requests are suppressed both directions while a dispute is open between the parties (D-19).
- Touches **02.01.05 Credit Visibility & Embargo** ([../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.05-credit-visibility-embargo.md](../02.01-credit-graph-discography/02.01.05-credit-visibility-embargo.md)) — **notification**: DT-13 — request notification payloads for embargoed works must not name the work. Authorised recipient, unauthorised lock screen. The sessions worth capturing are the ones under NDA.
- Touches **02.06 Credit Role & Instrument Taxonomy** ([../02.06-credit-role-instrument-taxonomy.md](../02.06-credit-role-instrument-taxonomy.md)) — **shared entity**: the role is one of the three material facets (DT-04). Two consequences for 02.06. **First, narrowed by owner ratification (DQ-05.2): no taxonomy operation ever invalidates an attestation.** This note previously asserted that a taxonomy re-map of an existing role is a material amendment that invalidates — it is not. `02.06` D-11 already holds that existing credits never re-resolve and D-04 that deprecation never rewrites credits; the one legitimate retroactive re-point (the first mapping of a pending alias) is **resolution completion, not a role change**, because the attester confirmed the literal and `02.06` D-03/D-13 keep that literal on the credit line unchanged — only the machine-readable canonical is filled in, which the attester cannot see. The counter-model would push mass re-asks (`02.06`'s worked example is 4,000 credits on one alias) through a channel D-09 caps at ≤5 outstanding per recipient, breaching DT-11's harassment ceiling. A credited party who disagrees with the mapping amends via `02.01.06`, which then runs D-10's flat identity test. **Second**, Q-04's "rivalrous" property, if it exists, must live on the taxonomy, since only 02.06 knows that a second "produced by" competes where a second "backing vocals" does not.
- Touches **02.03.02 Claim Inbox** ([../02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding/02.03.02-claim-inbox-suggested-claims.md](../02.03-claiming-cold-start-seeding/02.03.02-claim-inbox-suggested-claims.md)) — **shared entity**: attestations requested on behalf of an unclaimed shell (D-12) accrue to it and wait. A claimant arriving to a credit three colleagues already vouched for is a materially better first impression than an empty page — the growth loop and the evidence loop reinforcing each other.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission**: the attester is sometimes an **entity**, not a human (DT-09) — the room attests room facts; the human who ran the session attests contributions. Requires the identity model to distinguish "acting as the studio" from "acting as myself". Related: a band member attesting the band entity's credit is self-attestation (D-11), which depends on domain Q-12.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **permission + notification**: block and mute lists suppress requests before any credits logic runs (D-19); the ≤200-char note is user-to-user content and moderated as such (D-13); false attestation and request spam are enforced there, never here. This feature supplies pattern data and takes no enforcement action.
- Touches the **Notifications cross-cut** — **notification fan-out**: the per-recipient caps (DT-11) are stated here in credits terms but are not a credits-local concern. An attestation request competes for the same attention budget as every gig alert, message and marketplace notification the platform sends, and a per-domain cap cannot see that budget. The ceiling likely belongs to a platform-level notification governor; this feature should declare its intent, not own the meter.
- Touches **03 Community & Networking** ([../../03-community-networking/](../../03-community-networking/)) — **other**: the ask is a social act between professionals and tone decides conversion. The nag economics (DT-10) are a relationship model, not a notification setting.
- Touches **05 Services Marketplace** ([../../05-services-marketplace/](../../05-services-marketplace/)) — **other**: "confirm my credit and I'll rebook you" is off-platform coercion and invisible here. It is the same trade `02.02.03` Q-02 proposes making official by pairing confirmation with payment release — which would convert extremely well and would make attestation a purchased signal rather than an honest one.
