# Feature: Attestation Request & Confirmation

> **Number**: 02.04.01
> **Parent**: [Attestation & Credit Confidence](./02.04-attestation-credit-confidence-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

One person confirming another person's credit — the act that makes the graph verified rather than asserted. It is the same act whether it happens as a tap in the room at session close (`02.02.03`, where it converts) or as a request chased by notification weeks later (where it mostly doesn't). This feature owns both, and the gap between their conversion rates is the reason `02.02.03` D-01 exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two directions. **Confirming**: someone credited them, they say yes — the one-tap act. **Requesting**: their credit is unconfirmed and they ask the people who were there to vouch, which is the visible gap on their own discography closing. Cannot attest a credit they have no knowledge of; attesting is a claim to have been there. |
| Producer | Full (attests as session owner) | Their attestation is the high-value one — `personas.md` makes them the trust broker explicitly. They confirm players' credits on sessions they ran, and their confirmation carries more than a peer's because they were running the room, not just in it. |
| Operator | Config (room-level facts only) | Attests that a session happened in their room and when — independent, verifiable, and genuinely useful corroboration. **Cannot** attest who played what; a room that vouches for musical contributions is vouching for something it does not know. |
| Fan | None | No attestation at any tier. A fan vouching for a credit would be vouching for something they cannot have witnessed, and it is exactly the crowd-verification model (`02.01.06` D-04) the product rejects. Explicit absence. |

## Behavior

An attestation is **evidence with a name on it**. Its value comes entirely from the attester having been in a position to know, and from their being accountable for saying so. Both properties are load-bearing: an anonymous attestation is worthless, and an attestation from someone who wasn't there is a lie with a signature.

The design constraints, in order of importance:

1. **Confirmation must cost one tap.** Every additional step is paid in confirmation rate, which is the product's core metric. A form here is a form that doesn't get filled in.
2. **The ask must be reciprocal-feeling, not transactional.** "Did she play on this?" from a colleague converts. A platform-generated compliance request does not.
3. **Refusal must be as easy as confirmation.** If saying no is harder than saying yes, the yeses mean nothing — and the whole tier model then rests on friction rather than truth.

The chase problem: attestation requests to people who have moved on is precisely the reconstruction workflow `problem-statement.md` describes as the industry's failure ("chasing people who have moved on, for a project they've mentally closed"). WeJammin does it better only by making it rarer — the in-room tap is the product; the chase is the fallback that admits the product didn't fire.

### Happy Path

1. A credit exists asserted but unattested — usually from the close prompt's unconfirmed rows
2. The people positioned to confirm it are identified from the roll (`02.02.01`), not guessed
3. Each is asked one question about one credit — ideally in the room, at worst by notification
4. They confirm, refuse, or say they don't know — all three are one tap, and all three are useful
5. The credit's tier re-derives (`02.04.02`); the discography line changes visibly

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody responds | The modal async outcome. Credit stays at asserted tier — it does not decay, disappear or fail | Unconfirmed marker; requester can nudge, bounded |
| Attester says "I don't know" | Distinct from refusal and genuinely common — a drummer often doesn't know who mixed it. Must be a first-class answer, or people refuse-by-default to be safe | Recorded; neither confirms nor contests |
| Attester refuses | Not automatically a dispute. "I don't think that's right" is weaker than "that's false" and forcing it into a dispute punishes honesty | Refusal recorded; escalation offered, never automatic |
| Requester spams attestation requests | Rate-limited. An unbounded ask channel between professionals is a harassment surface and a nag engine | Bounded requests per credit |
| Attester was not actually there | Attesting is itself a claim to knowledge. False attestation is a Trust & Safety matter (24), not a credits one | — |
| Two people attest contradictorily | Both recorded; the contradiction is signal, not error. It is also the earliest honest sign of a dispute | Contradiction surfaces; dispute offered |
| Attester later retracts | `02.04.03` — tier re-derives downward | Tier change; requester notified |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unattested | Credit asserted, nobody asked | "Unconfirmed" + request CTA on own credits |
| Requested | Ask sent | "Waiting on <n>" |
| Attested | One or more confirmations | Verified marker; tier reflects who attested |
| Refused | Attester declined | Recorded; no automatic escalation |
| Unknown | Attester said they don't know | Recorded; neutral |
| Contradicted | Conflicting attestations | Contradiction marker; dispute path offered |
| Retracted | Attester withdrew | Tier re-derived down |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | "I don't know" must be a first-class answer alongside confirm and refuse | Domain-expert expectation nobody stated: session players routinely have no idea who mixed the record, who played on the other tracks, or what the engineer's exact role was | ✅ **CONFIRMED — and its absence would be quietly corrosive.** A binary confirm/refuse forces people with no knowledge to guess. Guessing up inflates the graph with worthless attestations at a high tier; guessing down manufactures contradictions between people who don't actually disagree. Both poison the tier model with noise that looks like signal. The third option is not a nicety — it is what keeps attestation meaning "I know this" rather than "I clicked this". |
| DT-02 | Attestation should be reciprocal — you attest mine, I attest yours | Natural social framing; it would drive volume hard | ❌ **REJECTED** — reciprocal attestation *is* the collusion ring (`02.04.04`), formalised and shipped by us. Building the exchange into the product would make the attack the intended flow and leave the detector fighting the UX. The social nudge to attest is fine; the quid-pro-quo is the thing the graph must not learn. |
| DT-03 | The in-room tap and the async request are different features | They feel entirely different — one is a moment, one is a chase | ❌ **REJECTED** — same act, same record, same evidentiary weight, radically different conversion. Splitting them would duplicate the state machine and invite the two paths to drift apart in what they mean. What differs is timing and delivery, which is `02.02.03`'s concern. Keeping them one feature is also what keeps the conversion gap *visible* rather than hiding it behind two success metrics that each look fine. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Three answers: confirm, refuse, don't-know | DT-01. A binary forces guessing, and guesses enter the graph as evidence. |
| D-02 | No reciprocal/quid-pro-quo attestation mechanic | DT-02. It is the collusion ring with better UX. |
| D-03 | Refusal never auto-escalates to a dispute | Punishing honesty with a legal-feeling process teaches people to stay silent, which costs the graph its only negative signal. |
| D-04 | Requests are rate-limited per credit | An unbounded professional-to-professional ask channel is a nag engine and a harassment vector. |
| D-05 | Operator attests room facts only, never contributions | A room cannot know who played what. An attestation about something you cannot witness is not evidence. |
