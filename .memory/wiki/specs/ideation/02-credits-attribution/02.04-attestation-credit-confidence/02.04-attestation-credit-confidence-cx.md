## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Attestation ↔ Provenance Tiers

**Relationship**: Attestation produces evidence; the tier consumes it. The coupling is tighter than a simple producer/consumer relationship because of `02.04.02` DT-03: the tier's visible gap is what *motivates* the attestation in the first place. An unconfirmed line on a discography with a one-tap "ask the people who were there" affordance is the tier model driving its own input. The two features form a loop, and the loop is the domain's growth engine as much as its integrity mechanism.

The subtlety that makes this a real cross-cut rather than a data dependency: `02.04.01` D-01 admits three answers (confirm / refuse / don't-know), and the tier must handle all three. "Don't know" is not a weak confirm — it is an absence of evidence and must contribute nothing. A tier model that treats it as a partial yes would reward asking people who cannot know, which is the opposite of the intended behavior.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: attests, and reads the tier on their own credits. Sees the gap; closes it.
- **Producer**: their attestation is the heaviest single input (trust broker, `personas.md`).
- **Operator**: attests room facts only (`02.04.01` D-05), so contributes to a narrow slice of the tier.
- **Fan**: reads a simplified label (`02.04.02` D-04); attests nothing.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Attestations are owned by their attester; the tier owns nothing and derives everything. No write conflict — but the derivation must be reproducible, or two reads of the same credit disagree.
2. **Trigger chain**: Attest → tier re-derives → discography line changes → traversal weight changes. Async is acceptable; the visible lag between confirming and seeing your page change is a UX cost at exactly the motivational moment.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. Who may attest (`02.04.01` — must have been positioned to know) determines what evidence can exist, which bounds the reachable tier. An Operator can never lift a contribution credit to attested, no matter how many times they vouch.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Confirmation notifies the credited party — and it should, because it is good news and it is the moment their page got stronger.
5. **State transition conflict**: Concurrent attestations from several people at close (`02.02.03`) all trigger re-derivation. Must converge, not thrash; the tier should settle once, not flicker per tap.

### CX-03: Ring Detection ↔ Provenance Tiers

**Relationship**: The domain's integrity backstop, and the only edge in this sub-domain that no persona can see. `02.04.02` DT-02 establishes that a high tier means "well-evidenced", not "true"; `02.04.04` DT-01 establishes that a colluding group produces perfect evidence. The tier model therefore **cannot detect its own worst failure from its own inputs** — every signal it reads comes from the people committing the fraud. Detection supplies the only input that does not come from the participants: the shape of the graph they collectively made.

The uncomfortable property (`02.04.04` DT-02) propagates directly into the tier: because a real tight scene and a ring are nearly indistinguishable, demotion is sometimes wrong, and a wrongly-demoted credit's tier is silently, unappealably incorrect. The tier claims to describe evidence quality and in those cases describes a suspicion instead. Nobody is told. This is the honest cost of D-01 and it is Q-02.

**Role scoping**:
- **All four**: `None`. The edge is invisible by design (sub-domain D-04) — publishing suspicion defames, publishing mechanism arms.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Detection writes a score; the tier reads it as one input among several. Detection must never write the tier directly, or two systems own one number and the tier stops being derivable from evidence.
2. **Trigger chain**: Topology analysis → cluster score → demotion input → tier re-derives → traversal weight drops. Silent throughout. Only a non-topological corroboration escalates to 24 (`02.04.04` D-03).
3. **Permission intersection**: None visible — which is the point, and also the problem: no permission surface means no appeal surface (Q-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: **None, deliberately.** A credited party is *not* told their tier dropped from demotion, even though `02.04.03` D-01 tells them when it drops from retraction. That inconsistency is intentional (retraction has a nameable cause; suspicion does not) and it is the sharpest edge of the DT-03 trade-off.
5. **State transition conflict**: Demotion racing a legitimate late attestation — a credit gaining real evidence while being demoted for looking suspicious. Which wins is undecided and matters most in exactly the false-positive case. `[PENDING — Step 5]`

### CX-02: Retraction ↔ Provenance Tiers

**Relationship**: Retraction is the reason `02.04.02` D-01 (derive, never stamp) is non-negotiable. Evidence moves backwards: a credit publicly labelled verified in 2026 can lose the only attestation supporting it in 2027, and the label must follow. The cascade is wide — discography line (`02.01.02`), traversal weight (`02.01.03`), co-sign corroboration (`02.02.04`), and any downstream consumer that cached the tier.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: retracts their own; is notified when a credit of theirs loses an attestation, with the reason.
- **Producer**: highest-impact retractor — their attestation carries the most, so its withdrawal takes the most away.
- **Operator**: narrow, room-level only.
- **Fan**: sees the simplified label change on a public page, with no explanation and no notice.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The attestation record is the attester's; the tier derives. Retraction preserves history (`02.04.03` D-01), so derivation must read *current* attestations, not all-time — an easy and severe bug: a naive count over history would make retraction a no-op.
2. **Trigger chain**: Retract → attestation inactive → tier re-derives down → notify credited party → offer dispute (never auto-open, `02.04.03` D-03).
3. **Permission intersection**: Only the attester may retract their own. The credited party has no lever — they cannot compel a vouch, and their recourse is other evidence or a dispute about the fact.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The credited party is told, with the reason. Contrast CX-03, where an equivalent tier drop is silent — the difference between a nameable cause and a suspicion.
5. **State transition conflict**: Retraction during an open dispute (`02.05`) — the dispute holds the tier (`02.04.02` states), so the retraction's effect must queue rather than apply underneath the dispute.
