# Feature: AI Contribution Disclosure

> **Number**: 02.10
> **Parent**: [Credits & Attribution](./credits-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Recording whether, where and how AI was involved in a contribution — and disclosing it where disclosure is required or expected. A credits platform launching in 2026 with no position on this is not neutral, it is negligent: DSPs, labels, PROs and statutory regimes are all actively converging on disclosure requirements, and a credit record that cannot express "this vocal is a model output" is already incomplete.

**Deep Think addition** — absent from the sweep's candidate list entirely, which is itself notable: the sweep produced 1,545 raw concepts and none of them was this.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own contributions) | Discloses AI involvement in what they delivered — a generated part, an AI-assisted comp, a modelled instrument. Also the person most exposed if someone *else* fails to disclose: their credit sits next to an undisclosed AI part on a record that later gets pulled or reclassified. |
| Producer | Full | Discloses at the session level and per contribution. The de-facto compliance point, because they assemble the record and they are who a label asks. Their tools increasingly *are* AI (stem separation, pitch/time, generative fills), so the line they must draw is not obvious to them either. |
| Operator | None | The room does not know how a part was made. Explicit absence — a studio cannot disclose what it cannot observe, and asking it to would be asking it to guess about its clients' work. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees disclosure where it is published. A live consumer-facing controversy: some fans care intensely, some do not care at all, and the platform's job is to state the fact plainly rather than to editorialise it. |

## Behavior

The disclosure axis is **orthogonal to role**, not a value within it. A vocal is a vocal whether a human sang it or a model produced it — the role is "Lead Vocals" either way, and what changes is *how it was made*. Putting "AI Vocals" in the taxonomy (`02.06`) would fork the role vocabulary in half and make every downstream role query wrong (`02.01.03` traversal for "vocalists" would silently exclude or include model outputs depending on which fork the tagger picked).

The genuinely hard part is not the data model — it is that **the line is not agreed anywhere.** Pitch correction is not disclosed and never has been. Stem separation is a tool. A generated melody is probably a disclosure. A model trained on the artist's own voice, used by that artist, is contested territory. Every one of those is AI by some definition, and the industry has no settled boundary. The platform cannot invent one — it can only record what the contributor says, with enough structure that when a standard does land, the data maps onto it.

Which suggests: **capture granularity and let the consumers apply their own thresholds.** Record what was AI-involved and how, rather than a boolean "contains AI" that will be wrong under every definition simultaneously and cannot be re-derived later.

#### v1 Structured Disclosure Vocabulary

A disclosure attached to a contribution is **zero or more involvement entries** (a contribution with no entries is "not disclosed" — silence, not a human-origin claim, per D-05). Each entry has this v1 shape:

| Field | Type | Values / Constraint | Notes |
|-------|------|---------------------|-------|
| `involvement_type` | enum (open/versioned) | `generation` \| `assistance` \| `modelling` \| `separation` \| `correction` | The kind of AI involvement. Open enum: new members may be added as the industry line moves, so a landed standard maps onto it rather than forcing a schema break. Versioned (`vocab_version`) so consumers know which member set they are reading. |
| `scope` | enum | `whole` \| `partial` | Whether the AI involvement covers the entire contribution or a portion of it. |
| `scope_detail` | string (optional) | free text, ≤ 280 chars | For `partial`: which portion (e.g. "final chorus vocal only"). Descriptive, not adjudicated. |
| `tool` | string (optional) | free text, ≤ 120 chars | Named tool/model if the contributor chooses to state it (e.g. "iZotope RX", "in-house voice model"). Never required. |
| `subject_is_own_model` | boolean | default `false` | Marks the contested "model of the artist's own voice, used by that artist" case (D-02 territory). Recorded as a fact, judged by no one. |
| `vocab_version` | string | e.g. `"v1"` | The vocabulary revision this entry was recorded against, so a later standard can re-map deterministically. |

**Member definitions (v1):**

| `involvement_type` | Means | Example |
|--------------------|-------|---------|
| `generation` | A part produced by a model | A generated melody or a model-produced vocal |
| `assistance` | Human-led work with AI aid | An AI-assisted comp or edit |
| `modelling` | A modelled instrument or voice | A synthesised/modelled instrument standing in for a played one |
| `separation` | Stem/source separation | Isolating a stem from a mix as a tool step |
| `correction` | Pitch/time correction | Tuning or timing correction applied to a human take |

The enum is **open and additive on purpose**: the platform draws no threshold (D-02), so consumers (label, DSP, PRO) read the structured members and apply their own line. When a standard lands, its categories map onto these members (or add members at a new `vocab_version`) — the historical data re-derives, which a boolean never could. Refinement of the member set to a specific landed standard is tracked in Q-01.

Disclosure is also a **claim about someone's work with reputational stakes in both directions**. Falsely disclosing another person's part as AI is defamatory. Failing to disclose your own may breach a DSP's terms or a label's warranty. That asymmetry means disclosure follows the contribution's author — you disclose your own work, not other people's.

### Happy Path

1. A contribution is logged (`02.02.02`)
2. If AI was involved, the contributor records what and how — structured, not free text
3. Disclosure attaches to the contribution, alongside its role, not inside it
4. It surfaces on the credit where published (`02.01.02`) and in export (`02.08`) where the standard supports it
5. Downstream consumers (label, DSP, PRO) apply their own thresholds to the structured facts

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The line is unclear (pitch correction? stem separation? generative fill?) | The normal case. The platform records the tool and role, not a verdict | Structured options, no boolean |
| Producer discloses AI on someone else's contribution | Not permitted — it is an assertion about how another person made their work, and if wrong it is defamatory | Own contributions only |
| Nobody discloses and AI was used | Undetectable here. The platform records assertions; it does not analyse audio | No detection claim, ever |
| Disclosure standard changes (likely, repeatedly) | Structured capture re-maps; a boolean would not | Re-derivable |
| DSP requires a disclosure the record cannot express | Export degrades, never drops silently — same rule as `02.08` D-04. The recorded disclosure entries are carried where the target format supports them; where the format (RIN / delivery metadata) has no disclosure field, the export is marked degraded and states exactly which disclosure facts were recorded but not carried, before the user commits. The structured entries stay intact on the credit so a later format that carries them re-emits losslessly. | Degraded-export notice listing the un-carried disclosure entries, shown before generation |
| Artist uses a model of their own voice | Contested territory nobody has settled. Recorded as a fact; the platform takes no position | Recorded, not judged |
| Disclosure harms the artist commercially | Real. Disclosure is honest and it may cost them. Not the platform's decision to soften | Recorded as asserted |

### States

**Disclosure-content states** (the fact recorded on the contribution):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not disclosed | No AI asserted | Nothing — absence is not a claim of human origin |
| Disclosed | Contributor recorded involvement | Structured disclosure on the credit |
| Partial | Some contributions disclosed, others silent | Per-contribution, never per-record |
| Suppressed | Parent credit embargoed | Follows the credit — disclosure hidden while embargo holds |

**Display-lifecycle states** (the disclosure surface on a credit view):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Credit view fetching contribution + disclosure records | Skeleton on the disclosure row; credit renders, disclosure fills in |
| Empty | Contribution logged, no AI asserted (the majority case) | No disclosure element at all — not a "no AI" badge |
| Populated | One or more structured disclosures attached | Disclosure summary inline on the credit, expandable to the structured detail |
| Error | Disclosure record fails to load (contribution loaded) | Credit still renders; disclosure row shows "disclosure unavailable — retry", never silently implying no AI |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | AI involvement is a role in the taxonomy — add "AI Vocals", "AI Production" | The obvious placement; roles are what credits have | ❌ **REJECTED** — it forks the role vocabulary in half and breaks every downstream role query. `02.01.03` traversal searching for "vocalists" would include or exclude model outputs depending on which fork the tagger happened to pick, and casting (04) would silently return wrong results. The role *is* Lead Vocals; how it was made is a different axis on the same contribution. This mirrors `02.06` DT-03 (role and instrument are orthogonal) — same structural error, third axis. |
| DT-02 | The platform should define where the AI line is, since it must record something | Someone has to decide, and a credits platform is arguably the natural place | ❌ **REJECTED** — pitch correction has gone undisclosed for twenty-five years; stem separation is a tool; a generated melody is probably disclosable; a model of the artist's own voice used by that artist is genuinely contested. Nobody in the industry agrees, standards are in motion, and any line WeJammin draws will be wrong for some consumer and obsolete within a year. **Record structured facts; let consumers apply thresholds.** A boolean "contains AI" would be wrong under every definition simultaneously and — crucially — cannot be re-derived when the standard lands, whereas structured capture can. |
| DT-03 | The platform should detect undisclosed AI from the audio | It would make disclosure meaningful rather than honour-system, and the tooling nominally exists | ❌ **REJECTED** — detection is unreliable, adversarial, and improving on the wrong side. More importantly it is a **category error about what this domain does**: domain 02 records what people who were there assert, with accountability attached. It does not analyse artifacts and it does not adjudicate truth (`02.04.02` DT-02 makes the same point about tiers). A false AI-detection accusation against a musician's own performance is defamatory and unappealable, and the platform would be making it algorithmically about a person's craft. Disclosure is an assertion; false assertion is a Trust & Safety matter (24), not a signal-processing one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Disclosure is an axis on the contribution, never a role in the taxonomy | DT-01. A role fork breaks every downstream role query. |
| D-02 | Structured capture; the platform defines no threshold | DT-02. The industry has not agreed one, and a boolean cannot be re-derived when it does. |
| D-03 | No AI detection, ever | DT-03. Domain 02 records accountable assertions; it does not analyse artifacts or accuse people algorithmically. |
| D-04 | You disclose your own contribution only | Disclosing someone else's work as AI is an assertion about their craft and is defamatory if wrong. |
| D-05 | Absence of disclosure is not a claim of human origin | The record says what was asserted. Reading silence as a warranty would make every pre-2026 credit a false claim. |
