# Feature: Credit Role & Instrument Taxonomy

> **Number**: 02.06
> **Parent**: [Credits & Attribution](./credits-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The controlled vocabulary of what a person can *be* on a record — roles (Producer, Mixing Engineer, Additional Production, Programmer) and instruments (pedal steel, Rhodes, 808) — plus the aliasing, versioning and extension rules that keep it usable. Every credit binds to it (`02.01.01` D-01), and `02.08` cannot emit valid DDEX RIN without it. Free-text roles would make the graph unqueryable and the export invalid on day one.

**Dual status — deliberate.** This is a domain-owned feature *and* a mechanism many domains consume: casting (04) needs "drummer", services (05) needs service categories, jamming (08) needs instrument matching, community (03) needs role facets, gear (13) needs instrument categories. Domain 02 owns it because **credits are where role precision has consequences** — a DDEX role code must be exact, whereas a service listing can be fuzzy. Recorded in `crossCuts` so the global CX absorbs the shared-vocabulary relationship without duplicating the vocabulary.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Picks roles when crediting or being credited; searches by alias and gets what they mean ("keys" → Keyboards). Requests additions when their instrument is missing — which for a working musician is a real and frequent event, not an edge case. Cannot edit the vocabulary. |
| Producer | Config | The heaviest user: attributing a full session touches a dozen roles, and the distinctions that matter to them are fine-grained ("Produced By" vs "Additional Production" vs "Vocal Production" are three different deals and three different fees). A taxonomy too coarse for that is a taxonomy they will fight. |
| Operator | Config | Room-level roles ("Recorded At", "Mixed At", "Mastered At") and the technical roles their staff hold. A narrow slice, used constantly. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees role labels on discography (`02.01.02`) in plain language. Never sees DDEX codes, alias mappings, or deprecation states. `personas.md`: fans "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" — the label is the whole interface. |

## Behavior

The taxonomy must satisfy three consumers pulling in different directions:

- **DDEX/industry** wants exact codes from a fixed list, or export is invalid (`02.08`).
- **Musicians** want the words they actually use, which are regional, generational, genre-specific and endless.
- **Traversal** (`02.01.03`) wants a structure it can reason over — "drummer" must match "Drums", "Percussion" must be findable as adjacent, "keys" must resolve.

The reconciliation is **aliasing over a canonical core**: a small, stable, industry-anchored set of canonical roles, with an unbounded and growing alias layer mapped onto it. Users see and type their words; the graph stores the canonical; export emits the code. Nobody is asked to learn DDEX.

Extension is the live problem. Music invents roles continuously — "beat maker", "topliner", "sound designer", and whatever 2027 produces — and a taxonomy that cannot grow is one users route around by picking a wrong-but-close role, which silently corrupts the data it was meant to protect. But uncontrolled extension produces the free-text mess it exists to prevent. The provisional shape (D-03): user requests are *captured as aliases immediately* and *promoted to canonical deliberately*, so the user is never blocked and the core never sprawls.

Versioning is not optional. Roles get renamed, split and deprecated; credits asserted under an old role must keep meaning what they meant. A credit from 2026 saying "Programmer" must not silently become something else in 2029 because the vocabulary moved — that would rewrite history through a lookup table.

### Happy Path

1. User attributes a contribution and needs a role
2. They type what they say ("keys", "beats", "tracked it")
3. System resolves through the alias layer to a canonical role, showing what it matched — visibly, so a wrong match is caught
4. Credit binds to the canonical role; the user's label is retained for display where it differs
5. Export (`02.08`) emits the mapped industry code without anyone thinking about it

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Role genuinely does not exist in the taxonomy | Captured as a pending alias, credit proceeds. Blocking the credit to protect the vocabulary is exactly backwards | "We'll add that" — never a dead end |
| User's word means different things by genre/region | Real and unavoidable: "producer" in hip-hop and "producer" in classical are different jobs. One canonical role cannot serve both honestly | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — likely genre-context on the mapping |
| Two aliases map to one canonical, users insist they differ | The taxonomy is too coarse for them and they are probably right; promotion candidate | Request path |
| Canonical role is deprecated after credits use it | Old credits keep their asserted role; new credits cannot use it. History is not rewritten | Old credits unchanged |
| Imported source uses an unmappable role (`02.03.01`) | Held as an unmapped alias rather than force-fitted. Force-fitting is silent data corruption at import scale | Import review flags it |
| DDEX has no code for a role users need | Export must degrade (`02.08`), and the platform's vocabulary is legitimately ahead of the standard's | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Instrument vs role confusion ("Drums" the instrument vs "Drummer" the role) | Two axes, not one list. A credit is a role *and* often an instrument, and collapsing them breaks both | Two fields |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Canonical | Role in the core vocabulary | Normal pick |
| Alias | Maps to a canonical | Resolves transparently, match shown |
| Pending | User-requested, unmapped | Credit proceeds; alias awaiting review |
| Deprecated | Superseded | Not offered for new credits; existing ones untouched |
| Unmapped | Import produced an unknown role | Flagged in import review |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Adopt DDEX's role list wholesale — it is the industry standard and export needs it anyway | Standards-alignment reasoning; it is free and it is what RIN wants | ❌ **REJECTED as the user-facing vocabulary, ✅ CONFIRMED as the export mapping.** DDEX is built for label metadata delivery, not for how musicians describe themselves, and it lags the industry's actual vocabulary by years — a hip-hop producer's "beat maker" and a modern "topliner" have no clean home in it. Forcing users into it means they pick wrong-but-close roles, which corrupts the data more thoroughly than a messy alias layer ever would. The canonical core is DDEX-*anchored* and DDEX-*mappable*; it is not DDEX. |
| DT-02 | This is a cross-cut and should not be a node in domain 02 at all | The Node Classification Gate's own test: it is a mechanism serving 03, 04, 05, 08, 13 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a shared mechanism, REJECTED as a reason to delete the node.** It is genuinely both, and the resolution is ownership rather than location. Domain 02 owns it because credits are the only consumer where precision has *consequences* — an inexact role invalidates a RIN emission and misroutes a neighbouring-rights registration, while an inexact service category just ranks badly. A vocabulary owned by its most demanding consumer stays rigorous; one owned by everybody drifts to the loosest requirement. Recorded in `crossCuts` so the global CX carries the shared relationship. The domain index's founding rationale (D-16) also names it as a justifying capability — deleting it here would contradict a ratified decision on a technicality. |
| DT-03 | Role and instrument are one vocabulary | Simpler; they appear together on a credit line | ❌ **REJECTED** — they are orthogonal axes and collapsing them breaks both. A drummer plays drums (role + instrument); a producer plays nothing (role, no instrument); a session player might play four instruments in one role on one track. One list forces "Drums" and "Drummer" to be either the same entry (losing the distinction that a producer can also play drums) or two unrelated entries (losing the connection entirely). Two fields, one of which is frequently empty, is the honest model. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | DDEX-anchored canonical core with an unbounded alias layer — not DDEX itself | DT-01. Users type their words; the graph stores canonical; export emits codes. |
| D-02 | Role and instrument are separate axes | DT-03. Orthogonal; collapsing loses information in both directions. |
| D-03 | Missing roles are captured as pending aliases and never block a credit | Blocking the fact to protect the vocabulary inverts their importance — and users route around blocks by picking wrong roles, which is worse than a messy alias list. |
| D-04 | Deprecation never rewrites existing credits | A credit means what it meant when asserted. Retroactive vocabulary changes rewrite history through a lookup table. |
| D-05 | Domain 02 owns the vocabulary; other domains consume it | DT-02. Owned by its most demanding consumer, or it drifts to the loosest requirement. |
