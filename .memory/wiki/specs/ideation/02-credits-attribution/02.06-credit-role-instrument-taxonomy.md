# Feature: Credit Role & Instrument Taxonomy

> **Number**: 02.06
> **Parent**: [Credits & Attribution](./credits-attribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The controlled vocabulary of what a person can *be* on a record — roles (Producer, Mixing Engineer, Additional Production, Programmer) and instruments (pedal steel, Rhodes, 808) — plus the aliasing, resolution, versioning and extension rules that keep it usable. Every credit binds to it (`02.01.01` D-01), and `02.08` cannot emit valid DDEX RIN without it. Free-text roles would make the graph unqueryable and the export invalid on day one.

It is not a lookup table. It is the **resolution layer between the words musicians say and the codes the industry reads**, and it is load-bearing in both directions: a wrong resolution puts a false fact on someone's public page (`02.01.02`), misweights a traversal edge (`02.01.03`), and misroutes a neighbouring-rights registration (domain 10). The vocabulary's failure mode is silent — nobody sees a bad mapping until money or a credit is already wrong.

**Dual status — deliberate.** This is a domain-owned feature *and* a mechanism many domains consume: casting (04) needs "drummer", services (05) needs service categories, jamming (08) needs instrument matching, community (03) needs role facets, education (06) needs lesson subjects, gear (13) needs instrument categories. Domain 02 owns it because **credits are where role precision has consequences** — a DDEX role code must be exact, whereas a service listing can be fuzzy. Recorded in `crossCuts` so the global CX absorbs the shared-vocabulary relationship without duplicating the vocabulary.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Picks roles when crediting or being credited; types what they say ("keys", "batteur", "tracked it") and gets what they mean, with the match shown before it binds (D-18). Sees their own typed literal retained on their credit line where it differs from the canonical label. Requests additions when their instrument is missing — for a working musician a real and frequent event, not an edge case — and is never blocked while waiting (D-03). Cannot edit the vocabulary, cannot see another user's pending alias (D-12), and never sees a DDEX code. |
| Producer | Config | The heaviest user by volume: attributing a full session touches a dozen roles through `02.02.02`'s log, where the picker must cost ~1 tap per contribution or it costs credits (`02.09` DT-02's principle applied to the field the thesis actually needs). The distinctions that matter to them are fine-grained — "Produced By" vs "Additional Production" vs "Vocal Production" are three different deals and three different fees, so a taxonomy too coarse for that is one they will fight, and route around by picking wrong-but-close (D-03). They are also the only persona who routinely enters roles *about other people*, which makes their misresolution somebody else's public record. |
| Operator | Config | A narrow slice used constantly: facility roles where the **room is the party**, not a location field (`02.01.01` D-03) — "Recorded At", "Mixed At", "Mastered At", "Rehearsed At" — plus the technical roles their staff hold as individuals. Cannot hold a performer role as a room (D-21), and cannot see who played what on a session they merely hosted (`02.01.01` Role Lens). |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees role and instrument labels on discography (`02.01.02`) in plain language: "John Smith — drums, percussion". Never sees DDEX codes, alias mappings, fidelity grades, pending state, deprecation state, or the canonical/alias distinction. `personas.md`: fans "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" — the label is the whole interface, and the entire resolution machine must be invisible to them. |

> **No persona has Full access.** Musician, Producer, and Operator are Config for selecting a permitted value and proposing a missing one; Fan is Read-only. A proposal is a constrained request to the curation queue, never vocabulary editing. Promotion, deprecation, mapping, and every other vocabulary-editing authority sit with an **admin/governance role that is not a persona** (`personas.md` Q-02: Admin is deliberately outside the persona set). This owner-ratified boundary also governs the identical service-taxonomy action in `05.01.02`.

## Behavior

The taxonomy must satisfy three consumers pulling in different directions:

- **DDEX/industry** wants exact codes from a fixed list, or export is invalid (`02.08`).
- **Musicians** want the words they actually use, which are regional, generational, genre-specific and endless.
- **Traversal** (`02.01.03`) wants a structure it can reason over — "drummer" must match "Drums", "Percussion" must be findable as adjacent, "keys" must resolve, and "Assistant Engineer" must roll up to engineering.

The reconciliation is **aliasing over a canonical core**: a small, stable, industry-anchored set of canonical roles, with an unbounded and growing alias layer mapped onto it. Users see and type their words; the graph stores the canonical; export emits the code. Nobody is asked to learn DDEX.

### The two axes, and their asymmetry

**Role** and **instrument** are orthogonal (D-02). A drummer plays drums (role + instrument); a producer plays nothing (role, no instrument); a session player might play four instruments in one role on one track. The axes are not merely separate — they behave differently:

| Property | Role axis | Instrument axis |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardinality on one credit** | Exactly one. Two roles = two credit records (`02.01.01` D-01) | Zero or many. Four instruments in one Performer role = **one** record with four instruments (D-16) |
| **Internal structure** | `base` + optional `modifier` (Additional, Assistant, Co-, Vocal, Executive, Associate) — D-06 | A functional hierarchy: Rhodes → Electric Piano → Keys (D-07) |
| **Deprecation pressure** | High — job titles are invented, split and retired continuously | Near-zero — a Rhodes is a Rhodes forever. Instruments are added, essentially never deprecated |
| **Admits a party type** | Declared per role; facility roles are organization-only (D-21) | N/A |
| **DDEX mapping** | Required, graded (D-17) | Where the standard carries one; absence is not an export blocker |

The cardinality asymmetry follows directly from `02.01.01`: uniqueness is **(party, role, work)** — instrument is not in the key, so instruments accrete to one record while roles multiply records. This is also what liner notes do: *"John — drums, percussion"* is one line; *"John — drums"* and *"John — mixing"* are two.

### Resolution — three tiers, never a guess

Resolution is where this feature is dangerous, because a confident wrong match is silent corruption with a friendly UI. It is therefore tiered (D-18):

1. **Exact or alias hit** (normalised: case-, diacritic-, punctuation- and whitespace-insensitive) → resolves, **with the match shown**, so a wrong hit is caught by the person who typed it.
2. **Fuzzy hit** → offers up to **5** ranked candidates. **Never auto-selects.** Ranking is seeded by the project's genre and the user's own history (D-10), but ranking is a suggestion order, never a decision.
3. **No hit** → captured as a pending alias; the credit proceeds (D-03).

**Import never fuzzy-resolves** (`02.03.01`). There is no user present to catch a wrong match, and at import scale one bad rule corrupts thousands of records in a batch nobody reads. Imports take tier 1 or they take "unmapped", and unmapped is held for review — force-fitting is the silent data corruption the alias layer exists to prevent.

### Extension — captured instantly, promoted deliberately

Music invents roles continuously — "beat maker", "topliner", "sound designer", and whatever 2027 produces — and a taxonomy that cannot grow is one users route around by picking a wrong-but-close role, which silently corrupts the data it was meant to protect. But uncontrolled extension produces the free-text mess it exists to prevent. The shape (D-03): user requests are **captured as aliases immediately** and **promoted to canonical deliberately**, so the user is never blocked and the core never sprawls.

Two controls keep that honest:

- **A pending alias is requester-scoped** (D-12). It resolves for the person who asked and enters no one else's resolver until reviewed. Otherwise the first user to type "keys" meaning *keytar* poisons resolution for everyone who means *keyboards* — a one-user, platform-wide corruption with no review step.
- **The admission test** (D-14): a canonical entry must be expressible as a **liner-note credit**, and must not already be expressible as an existing base+modifier. "Mixing" (a service category, domain 05) fails the first test. "Assistant Engineer" fails the second — it is Engineer + Assistant. Consumers map onto the vocabulary; they never extend it.

Promotion candidacy is **derived**; the promotion act is **human**. Default candidacy signal: a pending alias requested by **≥10 distinct users across ≥5 distinct works within 90 days** surfaces for review. The threshold is tunable; who holds the pen is Q-02.

### Versioning — the vocabulary must never rewrite history

Roles get renamed, split and deprecated; credits asserted under an old role must keep meaning what they meant. A credit from 2026 saying "Programmer" must not silently become something else in 2029 because the vocabulary moved — that would rewrite history through a lookup table.

The mechanism is D-11: **a credit stores the canonical it resolved to at assertion time, plus the literal the user typed. It never holds a live alias reference and it never re-resolves.** Re-pointing an alias in 2029 changes what *new* input resolves to and touches zero existing credits. Deprecating a role removes it from the picker and touches zero existing credits.

There is exactly **one** legitimate retroactive re-point: the first mapping of a **pending** alias. Those credits were committed with a deliberately deferred resolution — leaving them bound to a string forever would make them permanently unexportable (`02.08`) and unqueryable (`02.01.03`), which is a worse outcome than the re-point. It happens once per alias, it is announced to the asserter and to each credited party (deduplicated to **one notice per party**, not one per credit), and it is informational — anyone who disagrees with the mapping corrects their credit through amendment (`02.01.06`), not by arguing with the vocabulary.

### The vocabulary is never a blocker

Generalising D-03: if the vocabulary is unavailable at capture time, **the credit still commits** with its literal captured, and resolution is deferred rather than abandoned. Blocking the fact to protect the vocabulary inverts their importance — and `02.02` is fighting for seconds of the Producer's attention, so a picker that fails closed loses the thesis to protect a lookup.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is logging a contribution in `02.02.02` and needs a role for a contributor
2. They type what they say — "keys", "beats", "tracked it" — or pick from suggestions ranked by the project's genre and their own recent use
3. System resolves through the alias layer and **shows what it matched** ("keys → Keyboards"), so a wrong match is caught by the person best placed to catch it; an ambiguous input offers ≤5 candidates and waits rather than guessing
4. Where the resolved role admits an instrument, the instrument axis is offered — multi-select, ranked from the room's gear list and the player's registry; where the role admits none (Producer), no instrument field appears at all
5. Credit binds to the **canonical role ID resolved at this moment** plus the user's literal; the resolution is now frozen for this record (D-11)
6. Same person, second role? A second record is created in the same act without re-navigating (`02.01.01` D-01) — the multi-hyphenate is the normal case, not a branch
7. Discography (`02.01.02`) renders the plain-language label, the literal where it differs, and the instruments on one line: "John Smith — drums, percussion"
8. Traversal (`02.01.03`) reasons over the structure — a "keyboard player" query matches the Rhodes credit through the instrument hierarchy; an "engineering" query matches Assistant Engineer through the modifier rollup
9. Export (`02.08`) reads the role's DDEX mapping and its fidelity grade and emits the code, stating any loss before generation — nobody in the flow thought about DDEX once

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Role genuinely does not exist in the taxonomy** | Captured as a pending alias, requester-scoped (D-12); credit proceeds at full tier. Blocking the credit to protect the vocabulary is exactly backwards | "We'll add that" — never a dead end. The credit shows their literal |
| **User's word means different things by genre/region** ("producer" in hip-hop vs classical) | **Genre is not an axis** (D-10). Where the jobs genuinely differ there is a distinct canonical role and the alias layer routes to it ("beat maker" → Beat Maker, not Producer). Where one word covers both, one canonical serves both — because the difference between a hip-hop and a classical producer is not a difference in the *role*, it is a difference in what else is true (the hip-hop producer usually also holds a Composer credit on the composition, `02.01.01` D-06). Encoding that in the vocabulary would make the taxonomy assert ownership, which `02.01.01` D-02 forbids | Genre only re-ranks the picker. The stored role carries no genre and the user is never asked to declare one |
| **Two aliases map to one canonical, users insist they differ** | The taxonomy is too coarse for them and they are probably right. Both aliases keep resolving; the divergence surfaces as a promotion candidate on the candidacy signal | Both users' literals are retained on their own credit lines, so the distinction survives on the page even before the core moves |
| **Canonical role is deprecated after credits use it** | Old credits keep their asserted role and change in **zero** respects (D-11). The role leaves the picker for new credits. Aliases pointing at it re-point to the successor for **new resolutions only** | Old credits unchanged. New credits are offered the successor |
| **Deprecated role is split 1 → 2** (e.g. Programmer → Programmer / Sound Designer) | Its aliases now have two candidates, so they stop resolving silently and start prompting (D-18 tier 2). Existing credits still say Programmer, forever, because that is what was asserted | "Programmer" now asks "which one?"; nobody's 2026 credit moved |
| **Alias is re-pointed to a different canonical** | Zero existing credits change (D-11). Only future input resolves differently | Nothing — the safety is invisible, which is the point |
| **Pending alias is later mapped to a canonical** | The one legitimate retroactive re-point. Credits bound to the alias re-point once; asserter and each credited party get **one notice per party**, deduplicated, informational | "'topliner' is now a role: Topline Writer. Your credit on *X* uses it" |
| **Credited party disagrees with the mapping their pending alias received** | The vocabulary is not the venue. They amend the credit's role through `02.01.06`, which is the existing path for "this credit says the wrong thing" | Amendment proposal, not a vocabulary appeal |
| **A pending alias accumulates 4,000 credits before it is mapped** | Re-point is per-credit, notification is per-party. A 4,000-credit re-point sends hundreds of notices, not thousands (Notification cross-cut) | One notice, listing the affected credits |
| **Two users request the same missing string concurrently** | Deduplicated to one pending entry with two requesters; both scoped to their own resolution; both notified on mapping. The candidacy counter increments by 2 distinct users | Identical experience for both — neither sees the other |
| **Two users request the same string meaning different things** | Requester-scoping (D-12) keeps them from colliding. Review sees one string with two claimed meanings — which is the signal the word is genuinely ambiguous, so it resolves to a **prompt**, not to whichever meaning was reviewed first | Both keep working; neither silently gets the other's meaning |
| **Compound role string** — "Producer/Engineer", "Drummer/Percussionist" | Compound aliases are real, common, and arrive by import at scale. They **expand to a set** of canonical roles (D-09) — two roles, two credit records per `02.01.01` D-01. Resolving a compound to one canonical loses a fact; failing to resolve it loses the credit | "That's two credits: Producer, and Engineer" — shown before it binds |
| **Compound crossing the recording/composition boundary** — "Singer-Songwriter" | Expansion is **work-type-aware** (D-09). Vocals binds to the recording; Songwriter binds to the composition (`02.01.01` D-06) — and if the credit's work has no composition bound, the songwriter half cannot be created. A work-type-blind expansion would put a songwriter credit on a recording, which is unregistrable in domain 10 | "Songwriter needs the composition — this credit is on the recording only" |
| **User picks an instrument in the role field** ("Drums") | The role picker recognises instrument-axis input and offers the corresponding performer role, filling both axes in one act. **Never silently** — collapsing them is D-02's whole rejection | "Drums — did you mean the role *Performer*, instrument *Drums*?" |
| **User tags an instrument with no role** | The instrument axis is only offered *on* a role, so this cannot happen in the picker. Where an import supplies an instrument and no role, the performer role for that instrument's family is proposed and shown, never assumed | "Rhodes → Performer (Rhodes)?" |
| **Session player plays four instruments in one role on one track** | **One** credit record, four instruments (D-16). Uniqueness is (party, role, work) and instrument is not in the key (`02.01.01` D-03), so instruments accrete exactly as evidence does | One line: "Sarah Chen — bass, Rhodes, Wurlitzer, tambourine" |
| **Multi-hyphenate holds two roles on one work** | Two records, two independently-derived tiers (`02.01.01` D-01). The picker adds the second role in the same act — a producer who also played drums is the normal case, not an exception path | Two lines on the ledger; one flow to create them |
| **Room is given a performer role** ("Lead Vocals — The Neve Room") | Refused. Each canonical role declares the party types it admits; facility roles are organization-only and performer roles do not admit a facility (D-21) | The role is not offered for that party; not an error, an absent option |
| **Imported source uses an unmappable role** (`02.03.01`) | Held as an unmapped alias rather than force-fitted, and never fuzzy-resolved (D-18). Force-fitting is silent data corruption at import scale | Import review flags it; the credit still lands, permanently tier-capped per `02.03` D-01 |
| **DDEX has no code for a role users need** | The platform's vocabulary is legitimately ahead of the standard's (DT-01). Every canonical role carries a mapping with an explicit **fidelity grade** — `exact` / `broader` / `none` — plus the nearest-code candidate (D-17). `02.08` reads the grade and degrades to the nearest code while retaining the true role internally (`02.08` edge cases, D-04). A `broader` mapping is never allowed to present itself as `exact` | Mapping and its fidelity shown before emission (`02.08` D-02): "Topline Writer → Composer (broader — the standard has no closer code)" |
| **Credit's role is still a pending alias at export time** | No canonical, therefore no mapping, therefore a pre-flight gap — not a silent drop and not a guess | `02.08`'s gap list: "1 credit has an unmapped role" |
| **Alias literal is abusive, spam, or PII** ("Producer 📞 555-0143", a slur, an ad) | The alias layer accepts free strings that render on public pages read by fans — it is a **UGC surface** (DT-10). The literal is screened before it renders publicly (domain 24's screening cross-cut). Screening failure removes **the string, never the credit**: the record renders with its canonical label, or "Role unspecified" where none resolved | Participants see the literal immediately. The public sees it only post-screening. A rejected string leaves the fact intact and the words gone |
| **Alias literal is 400 characters, or contains markup** | Bounded at entry: **≤64 characters, single line, no URLs, no markup, no control characters**. There is no rejected-at-entry state because the input will not accept it | The field stops at 64 |
| **Alias collides with an existing canonical's name** ("Producer" requested as an alias for something else) | Refused. A canonical's own name is never available as an alias for a different canonical — that is not extension, it is redefinition of a word every other user already relies on | "Producer already means something here" |
| **User tries to enter "AI" as a role** | Refused and **routed**. AI involvement is a disclosure axis on the contribution, never a role (`02.10` DT-01, D-01; CX-12) — the conflation is tempting enough to be worth an explicit intercept rather than a pending alias nobody reviews for six weeks | "That's not a role — mark AI involvement here instead", with the disclosure control inline |
| **Consumer domain wants an entry that is not a liner-note credit** (05's "Mixing" as a service category) | The admission test refuses it for the canonical core (D-14); it lives in the consumer's own vocabulary, mapped onto ours. The **user never perceives the boundary** — they typed a word into a services form and got "we'll add that" | Identical to any other request. The refusal is governance, not UX |
| **Non-English user types "batteur" / "ドラム"** | Aliases are locale-tagged (D-22). A translation is the same mechanism as an alias but different governance — it is verifiable and completable, where an alias is emergent and unbounded. Without locale tagging every non-English user hits "we'll add that" on day one for words that are simply *the word* | Resolves like any alias — if the locale is seeded (Q-04) |
| **Vocabulary is unavailable during capture** | The credit commits with its literal; resolution is deferred, not abandoned (D-19). The unresolved credit appears in the Producer's session review | "Role saved as typed — we'll match it" |
| **Alias is promoted to canonical while a user has the picker open** | Their in-flight resolution is unaffected — resolution is frozen at assertion (D-11), and the picker's next query uses the new canonical | No flicker, no retroactive change to what they just committed |
| **Two reviewers promote overlapping aliases concurrently** | Serialized per string; the second reviewer sees the first's result rather than a stale queue — the same rule `02.01.01` applies to concurrent amendments | "This was just mapped by someone else" |
| **Role is picked, then the credit's party changes** (merge, `02.01.04`) | The role is unaffected — it is a fact about the contribution, not about the identity. If merge collapses two records to one (party, role, work), their instrument sets union (D-16), exactly as evidence unions | One line where there were two; both instrument sets retained |

### Vocabulary Entry States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Canonical | Entry in the core vocabulary; passed the admission test (D-14) | Normal pick, plain label, no state indicator |
| Alias | A string mapped to a canonical, globally | Resolves transparently, match shown ("keys → Keyboards") |
| Pending | User-requested, unmapped, **requester-scoped** (D-12) | Resolves for its requester only; credit proceeds; invisible to everyone else |
| Unmapped | Import produced an unknown role; no user present to resolve it | Flagged in import review; credit lands tier-capped |
| Deprecated | Superseded, split, or retired | Not offered for new credits; existing credits untouched; aliases re-point for new resolutions only |
| Screened-out | Literal failed UGC screening (DT-10) | The string is gone from public surfaces; the credit renders with its canonical label |

### Role Picker View States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Picker opened; vocabulary being fetched | The field is **typeable immediately** — the literal is captured regardless (D-19), so a slow vocabulary never blocks a keystroke. Suggestions fill in behind it |
| Empty | Query matches nothing at any tier | Never a dead end: "We'll add that" plus the pending-alias path (D-03). The picker itself is never empty — the core is seeded (Q-05) |
| Populated | Matches found | Exact hit shown as a resolved match; fuzzy hits as ≤5 ranked candidates, none pre-selected |
| Partial | Vocabulary loaded but the DDEX mapping table is unavailable | Picking works and credits commit; only `02.08` notices, and it notices at pre-flight (D-02 there), not at capture. The capture flow must not know export exists |
| Error | Vocabulary unreachable | **The credit still commits** with its literal (D-19). "Role saved as typed — we'll match it." A picker that fails closed loses the thesis to protect a lookup table |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Adopt DDEX's role list wholesale — it is the industry standard and export needs it anyway | Standards-alignment reasoning; it is free and it is what RIN wants | ❌ **REJECTED as the user-facing vocabulary, ✅ CONFIRMED as the export mapping.** DDEX is built for label metadata delivery, not for how musicians describe themselves, and it lags the industry's actual vocabulary by years — a hip-hop producer's "beat maker" and a modern "topliner" have no clean home in it. Forcing users into it means they pick wrong-but-close roles, which corrupts the data more thoroughly than a messy alias layer ever would. The canonical core is DDEX-*anchored* and DDEX-*mappable*; it is not DDEX. |
| DT-02 | This is a cross-cut and should not be a node in domain 02 at all | The Node Classification Gate's own test: it is a mechanism serving 03, 04, 05, 08, 13 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a shared mechanism, REJECTED as a reason to delete the node.** It is genuinely both, and the resolution is ownership rather than location. Domain 02 owns it because credits are the only consumer where precision has *consequences* — an inexact role invalidates a RIN emission and misroutes a neighbouring-rights registration, while an inexact service category just ranks badly. A vocabulary owned by its most demanding consumer stays rigorous; one owned by everybody drifts to the loosest requirement. Recorded in `crossCuts` so the global CX carries the shared relationship. The domain index's founding rationale (D-16) also names it as a justifying capability — deleting it here would contradict a ratified decision on a technicality. |
| DT-03 | Role and instrument are one vocabulary | Simpler; they appear together on a credit line | ❌ **REJECTED** — they are orthogonal axes and collapsing them breaks both. A drummer plays drums (role + instrument); a producer plays nothing (role, no instrument); a session player might play four instruments in one role on one track. One list forces "Drums" and "Drummer" to be either the same entry (losing the distinction that a producer can also play drums) or two unrelated entries (losing the connection entirely). Two fields, one of which is frequently empty, is the honest model. |
| DT-04 | Every role the industry uses is a flat entry in the core — "Engineer", "Assistant Engineer", "Additional Production", "Co-Producer" are four unrelated roles | The flat-list reflex, reinforced by DDEX itself being a flat code list | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP; resolved by D-06.** Liner notes are built from **modifiers**: Additional, Assistant, Co-, Vocal, Executive, Associate. A flat core is the cartesian product of bases × modifiers — it grows multiplicatively, every new base needs six new alias sets, and traversal loses the one fact it needs most: *these are all engineering credits*. So the canonical role carries `base` + optional `modifier` as structure. Two consequences that make this non-obvious: (1) it stays **one user-facing field** — "Additional Production" is one thing you pick, not two dropdowns, because the Producer lens says this field competes with the session for attention; (2) it does **not** change the DDEX mapping, which is still per-canonical and still flat, because the standard has its own opinion and structure is ours, not theirs. Structure buys the rollup and the small core; it costs the user nothing. |
| DT-05 | The instrument axis should use a real classification — Hornbostel-Sachs, the actual academic taxonomy of instruments | Domain-expert reflex: there is a standard, use the standard; it is complete, unambiguous, and a century old | ❌ **REJECTED, and the reason generalises.** Hornbostel-Sachs classifies by **sound-production physics**, which is orthogonal to every question this platform asks. It files a Rhodes and a Hammond in different top-level families while a casting query needs them one hop apart; it has no home for an 808 that resembles where a musician would look; and nobody has ever searched for a lamellophone player. ✅ **A hierarchy is CONFIRMED** — traversal (`02.01.03`) needs "keyboard player" to match a Rhodes credit, and 04's casting queries are role+instrument queries — but it must be the **idiomatic/functional** hierarchy musicians and gear retailers actually use (keys / guitars / drums & percussion / strings / brass / woodwind / voice / electronic production). That also aligns it with 13 and 15's categories for free, where Hornbostel-Sachs would align with nothing. The general lesson: an external standard is worth adopting only where its questions are our questions, which is exactly why DT-01 keeps DDEX at the export boundary and out of the picker. |
| DT-06 | The instrument axis and gear are the same thing — "808" is an instrument and also a machine, so let the taxonomy carry make and model | `02.09`'s own cross-cut note ("the instrument axis overlaps gear categories"); the overlap is real and the collapse is tempting | ❌ **REJECTED — the boundary is exact and load-bearing in both directions.** The instrument axis names **what was played** (a functional category); gear names **the object** (`02.09`, 13, 15). "Bass, on a '62 Jazzmaster" is instrument `Bass Guitar` + gear `Fender Jazzmaster (1962)`, and they must not merge. Absorb make/model into the instrument axis and it becomes a product catalogue — every guitar model ever built is now a taxonomy request, the core sprawls past any admission test, and it duplicates two domains that own it properly and keep it current. Substitute gear for instrument and the credit becomes uncastable: a producer who tags a mic but no instrument has told 04's casting query nothing about who to hire. The failure is only visible at the 02/13/15 intersection, which is why `02.09` saw the overlap and neither file saw the line. |
| DT-07 | Every input string resolves to at most one role | The implicit assumption of a lookup, and of every step of the happy path before this pass | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP; resolved by D-09.** "Producer/Engineer", "Drummer/Percussionist", "Singer-Songwriter" and "Writer/Producer" are how liner notes and Discogs have written credits for fifty years, and `02.03.01` will import them by the thousand. A one-to-one resolver has two options and both are wrong: resolve to the first half and silently destroy the second fact, or fail to resolve and hand every compound to the pending queue, where the largest single class of imported roles goes to die. So an alias may expand to a **set**, producing two records per `02.01.01` D-01 — the multi-hyphenate arriving as a string. The sharp edge is that expansion must be **work-type-aware**: "Singer-Songwriter" is Vocals (recording) + Songwriter (composition), which are different works under `02.01.01` D-06 and different registrations in domain 10. A work-type-blind expansion puts a songwriter credit on a recording — unregistrable, and generated by our own resolver from a word the user typed innocently. |
| DT-08 | Genre must scope the canonical role — "producer" in hip-hop and "producer" in classical are different jobs, so they need different entries | The breadth pass's own Q-01, and it is a real observation: the two jobs genuinely differ | ❌ **REJECTED; resolved by D-10 — and the rejection is the interesting half.** The difference between a hip-hop producer and a classical producer is not a difference in the **role**; it is a difference in **what else is true**. The hip-hop producer usually also composed, so they hold a Composer credit on the composition (`02.01.01` D-06) and publishing follows from *that* fact, in domain 09. Encode it as `Producer[hip-hop]` and the vocabulary is now asserting an ownership consequence — the exact conflation the domain was founded to prevent (`02.01.01` D-02: the record has no field a split could hide in; here, no *role* a split could hide in). It also forces every user to declare a genre before they can credit anyone, on a platform where genre is contested, plural, and frequently absent. Where the jobs genuinely differ, the honest instrument is a **more precise canonical role** ("beat maker" → Beat Maker), which the alias layer already delivers — and where one word truly covers two jobs with no closer role, the taxonomy is too coarse and that is a promotion candidate, not a genre axis. Genre survives as a **suggestion-ranking signal only**: it re-orders the picker and is never stored on the credit. |
| DT-09 | The alias layer is safe because credits store the canonical, and D-04 already forbids retroactive vocabulary changes | The breadth pass's own reasoning, which addressed deprecation and stopped there | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED, with a hole D-04 does not cover; resolved by D-11.** D-04 stops *deprecation* from rewriting history. It says nothing about **re-pointing an alias**, which is the same history rewrite arriving through the door nobody watches: re-point "keys" from Keyboards to Piano in 2029, and any credit that resolves through the alias at read time silently changes what it says about a session in 2026. So resolution must happen **once, at assertion** — the record holds the canonical ID it resolved to plus the literal, and never a live alias reference. That makes alias editing safe *by construction* rather than by policy, which matters because policy does not survive a well-meaning admin. The one exception is the **first mapping of a pending alias**, and it is a real exception, not a loophole: those credits were committed with resolution deliberately deferred, and refusing to complete it would leave them permanently unexportable and unqueryable — a worse outcome than a single announced re-point. That is the only retroactive re-resolution the system performs, ever. |
| DT-10 | The taxonomy is a data-modelling problem | Everything about how the breadth pass framed it, and how every implementer will frame it | ❌ **REJECTED — it is also an unmoderated UGC surface, and nobody designs for that.** The moment D-03 accepts an arbitrary string from a user and D-01 retains that literal for display, the vocabulary is publishing user-generated text onto public discography pages (`02.01.02`) read by fans — a population `personas.md` and D-13 flag as bringing a moderation profile professionals do not have. A role that reads "Producer 📞 555-0143", or an ad, or a slur, renders next to a real artist's name on a page WeJammin published. The tell is that this appears in no data model, no schema and no edge-case list about vocabularies anywhere — it only exists at the seam between "never block the credit" and "the literal is displayed". Resolved by D-13 and D-12 together: bound the input (≤64 chars, one line, no URLs, no markup), scope pending aliases to their requester so one user's string is not yet anyone else's resolver, and screen the literal before it renders publicly — where screening **removes the string, never the credit**, because the fact was never the problem. |
| DT-11 | The vocabulary should serve its consumers — if 05 needs "Mixing" and 13 needs "Guitar Amplifier", add them | The natural reading of "shared mechanism" (DT-02), and the path of least resistance for every consumer domain | ❌ **REJECTED; resolved by D-14.** This is how DT-02's ownership argument actually gets lost — not by moving the node, but by letting five consumers each add three entries a quarter until the core is a bag of nouns with a DDEX mapping column full of nulls. "Mixing" is a service (05); "Guitar Amplifier" is gear (13/15); neither is anything a liner note has ever said. The admission test is therefore concrete and testable rather than a matter of taste: **can this be written on a liner note as a credit?** and **is it already an existing base+modifier (DT-04)?** Consumers get a **view** over the vocabulary or their own vocabulary **mapped onto** it — never an entry. The user never perceives the boundary, which is what makes the rule survivable: they type a word into a services form and get "we'll add that", identically. |
| DT-12 | The role is display metadata — it says what someone did, it does not decide anything | The reflex that a vocabulary is cosmetic, reinforced by the Fan lens where the label genuinely is the whole interface | ❌ **REJECTED — the role routes money, and that changes what this feature owes; resolved by D-15.** Neighbouring rights split on **featured vs non-featured performer**, and that distinction is derived from the role: it decides who gets paid what by PPL, SoundExchange and their equivalents. A wrong-but-close role — precisely what D-03 says users pick when blocked — therefore misroutes a real payment, which reframes D-03 from a UX kindness into a revenue control. But the second half is the one that matters: the taxonomy must **not encode the outcome**. The featured/non-featured line is defined by each CMO's rules, differs by territory, and changes without asking us; baking it into a canonical role would make our vocabulary quietly assert a rights position (DT-08's error again, arriving from the money side instead of the genre side) and would go stale in a jurisdiction we never look at. So domain 10 holds its own mapping — canonical role → scheme classification, per territory — and the taxonomy stays a vocabulary. The coherence check: DT-08 and DT-12 both reduce to `02.01.01` D-02, reached independently from opposite directions. |
| DT-13 | The vocabulary is English, because DDEX is English | Never stated anywhere, which is exactly the problem — it is an assumption, not a decision | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP; resolved by D-22, scope deferred to Q-04.** A French drummer types "batteur"; a Japanese one types "ドラム". Under the breadth pass's model both are unknown strings, so every non-English user hits "we'll add that" on their first credit — for a word that is simply *the word*. That is a first-run failure for an entire market, produced by a feature nobody would think to internationalise. Translations and aliases are the **same mechanism** (a string resolving to a canonical) with **different governance**: a translation is verifiable and completable by a translator, where an alias is emergent, unbounded and needs a promotion signal. So aliases are locale-tagged, and locale seeds resolution. The line that must not be crossed: the canonical's **display label** localises for the reader's UI, but the credit's **retained literal never translates** — a liner-note credit is a fact stated in a language, and translating it would be the vocabulary editing someone's words. |
| DT-14 | Instrument multiplies records the way role does — four instruments means four credits | Symmetry: the axes look parallel, so they should behave in parallel | ❌ **REJECTED; resolved by D-16.** `02.01.01` D-03 makes a record unique on **(party, role, work)** — instrument is not in the key. So instruments **accrete** to one record exactly as evidence does, while roles multiply records (D-01 there). This is not an arbitrary reconciliation; it is what liner notes already do: *"John — drums, percussion"* is one line, *"John — drums"* and *"John — mixing"* are two. Getting it backwards produces four discography lines for one bassist who also grabbed a tambourine, four independently-derived tiers on one fact, and four dispute surfaces where there is one thing to dispute. It also settles a merge case for free: when `02.01.04` collapses two parties who held the same role on the same work, their instrument sets union, precisely as their evidence sets do. |
