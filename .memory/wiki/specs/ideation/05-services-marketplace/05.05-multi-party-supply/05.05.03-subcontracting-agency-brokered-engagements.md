This feature owns only the **commerce**: an agency selling its roster's work for a commission, and a seller delegating an engagement onward. See sub-domain D-01.

## Overview

Two shapes of one fact: the engagement's seller of record is not the person doing the work. An **agency** sells its roster's services, brokers the deal and takes a commission. A **seller delegates** an engagement they took to someone else — an overcommitted mixer passing a job to a junior, a studio assigning an in-house engineer.

The entity model behind both — who represents whom, with what mandate — is domain 01's. What is here is the money and the credit chain that runs over it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a rostered player, work arrives through their agent rather than direct — normal at every level above the bottom of the market. Their exposure is that they never see the buyer's terms, only what their agent tells them, which is the information asymmetry agencies have always run on. As a seller, delegates work they cannot do; as a delegate, receives it. |
| Producer | Full | Delegates the parts of a job that are not their craft (editing, tuning, comping are routinely passed to specialists) and takes delegated production work themselves. | Operator | Config | The one shape in this sub-domain an Operator genuinely occupies: a studio sells "an engineer" and assigns whichever of its staff is free. The buyer bought the studio's service, not a named human. Configures which of their people can be assigned. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Two layers, like the fixer (05.05.02), but with a different shape: **one job, delegated**, rather than **one job, assembled from many**.

| Shape | Seller of record | Who works | Buyer's expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency-brokered | The agency | A named roster member | Knows who they are getting; the agency is the channel |
| Delegation | The original seller | Someone else | May or may not know — and that is the whole question |
| Studio assignment | The studio | Whichever engineer is free | Bought a facility's service, not a person |

The consent question separates them. Agency-brokered and studio-assignment engagements are **transparent by construction** — the buyer knows they are buying through a channel or from a facility. Delegation is where a buyer who chose a specific person may silently receive a different one, which is the 05.05.01 DT-02 problem arriving through a side door.

### Happy Path (agency-brokered)

1. Agency lists services on behalf of a rostered player (mandate from domain 01)
2. Buyer requests a quote; the agency quotes, including its commission
3. Buyer accepts; escrow captures against the agency engagement
4. Player performs and delivers
5. Acceptance releases escrow; the agency's commission and the player's fee settle from the same release (as 05.05.02 DT-03)
6. **The credit emits to the player**, not the agency

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller delegates without telling the buyer | The 05.05.01 DT-02 problem via a side door | Consent required where the buyer chose a person; not required where they chose a facility. See DT-01. |
| Agency's mandate is revoked mid-engagement (domain 01) | The seller of record loses the right to sell this | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the engagement is live and the authority behind it evaporated |
| Studio assigns an engineer the buyer dislikes | They bought a facility, not a person — but they still care | `[PENDING]` — a right to request a different assignee? |
| Delegate does the work; original keeps the credit | The exact failure 05.05.01 DT-01 forbids | Credit follows the hands |
| Agency takes commission and pays the player late | Same failure as the fixer's (05.05.02 DT-03), same fix | Settle both from one release |
| Delegation chain (A→B→C) | Real in agency work, absurd in practice | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depth limit? |
| Buyer's reputation review | Whose reputation? The agency's channel service, or the player's work? | Two facts. The Reviews cross-cut's per-role model should already carry this. |
| Agency lists a player who did not agree | A real abuse — agencies list people they have loose relationships with | Blocked by domain 01's mandate model, which is the reason that boundary matters |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Consent to substitution depends on what the buyer actually bought — a person, or a capability** | Reasoning about why 05.05.01's absolute consent rule feels wrong here. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it refines the earlier rule rather than contradicting it. 05.05.01 DT-02 says a seller cannot substitute without consent, because the buyer bought a named person's taste. That is right for a listing that *is* a person — "Alex mixes your record". It is wrong for a listing that is a **facility or a channel**: a buyer who books "Rockfield's in-house engineer" or hires through an agency did not choose a human and cannot reasonably demand one. So the rule is not "always consent" but **consent where the purchase was identity-based**, and the discriminator is knowable at listing time (05.01 Q-02: can a listing's seller be an entity?). This is the second time that entity question has surfaced from a different direction — the first was 05.01, and here it determines a consent rule — which is a strong signal it is architectural rather than cosmetic (sub-domain Q-03). |
| DT-02 | **An agency's commission is structurally identical to the platform's take rate, and the two stack invisibly** | Adversarial: what does a domain expert expect that nobody mentioned? Follow the money. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real and uncomfortable interaction. The Payments cross-cut states the services take rate at ~15-20%. An agency commission is typically 10-20% on top. A rostered player therefore nets somewhere near 60-75% of what the buyer paid, and — the important part — **neither deduction is visible to the other party**. The buyer sees a price; the player sees a fee; nobody sees the stack. This is not a pricing bug, it is a transparency question with a real commercial consequence: at some combined rate, agencies and their players take the deal off-platform, which is `meta/personas.md`'s stated Musician "worst intentional" behaviour, provoked by arithmetic rather than malice. Flagged to the Payments cross-cut (its fee engine must model a *stack* of deductions, not a single rate) and surfaced as sub-domain Q-02 rather than decided here — margin disclosure is a product and values call, and forcing it may drive the intermediaries away, taking their rosters with them. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Consent to substitution is required where the purchase was **identity-based**, not where it was facility- or channel-based | See DT-01. Refines 05.05.01 D-02. |
| D-02 | The credit emits to the person who did the work, never to the agency or the delegating seller | Consistent with 05.05.01 D-01 and 05.05.02 D-01. This is the sub-domain's single rule. |
| D-03 | Commission and platform fee settle from one escrow release | Same fix as the fixer's (05.05.02 D-02) — the intermediary must not be the players' cashflow bottleneck. |
| D-04 | The roster/mandate entity model belongs to domain 01; this feature consumes it | Dedup. An agency listing someone who never agreed is blocked by 01's mandate, not by a check here. |
