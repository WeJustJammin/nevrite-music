# Feature: Multi-Seller Bundles & Curated Packages

> **Number**: 05.05.04
> **Parent**: [Multi-Party Supply](./05.05-multi-party-supply-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Buying a finished record rather than a series of services: one purchase that assembles tracking, mixing, mastering and artwork from four independent sellers, delivered as a package. "Release-ready single, £2,400, four weeks" — for the artist who knows they want a record and does not know what a mastering engineer does.

The buyer appeal is obvious. The mechanism is an engagement DAG with cross-seller dependencies, a shared escrow, and a failure mode where one seller stalls and three strangers' money is stuck.

## Behavior

A bundle is a **milestone chain whose stages have different sellers** (05.03.02, with the sellers varying). That is the whole feature and also its whole difficulty: milestones already handle sequencing, staged escrow and per-stage acceptance for one seller. Changing the seller per stage breaks the assumption that one party is accountable for the chain.

Someone must own the bundle: an assembler (usually a producer) who is the buyer's counterparty and carries the risk, or the platform, which would be underwriting strangers' reliability with someone else's record.

Each stage's output is the next stage's requirements input (05.03.01), which is what makes the chain a chain rather than a basket.

### Happy Path

1. Assembler composes a bundle from their own listing plus other sellers' listings, and prices it
2. Participating sellers opt in to the bundle and its terms
3. Buyer purchases; escrow captures the whole bundle
4. Stage 1 (tracking) opens; requirements gate; work; delivery; acceptance; tranche releases to seller 1
5. Stage 1's output feeds stage 2's requirements automatically — the mix engineer receives the tracked stems without the artist ferrying files
6. Chain completes; final acceptance transfers rights and emits credits for every participant
7. Buyer holds a finished record and a complete credit record for it

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller 2 of 4 stalls | The whole chain halts and three sellers' money sits in escrow behind one person's inbox | The failure mode that drives the `wont` proposal — see DT-02 |
| Seller 2 needs replacing | Substitute mid-chain (05.05.01), inheriting seller 1's output | Only viable where 05.05.01 DT-03 says deps work — i.e. not for mixing |
| Buyer rejects stage 2 repeatedly | Revisions are per-milestone (05.03.02 D-04), and the chain waits | Downstream sellers idle, having reserved capacity (05.01.07) |
| Rights across four sellers | Four rights postures, four warranties, one record | Must resolve to a coherent chain of title — see DT-01 |
| Who is the buyer's counterparty? | The assembler, or the platform | If the platform, it is underwriting strangers | 
| One seller's price changes | Bundle price was fixed at purchase | Bundle snapshots all four listings (05.01.01 D-01 applies four times) |
| Seller opts out after the bundle is listed | Bundle is unsellable until refilled | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Cross-seller SLA | Stage 3 is late because stage 2 was late | Whose breach? `[PENDING]` |
| Cancellation mid-chain | Per-stage settlement across four parties, plus the partial-work problem (05.03.05 DT-03) ×4 | `[PENDING]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **A four-seller bundle produces four rights postures and four warranties that must resolve into one coherent chain of title — and this is the feature's genuinely novel value, not its cost** | Reasoning about the intersection with 05.06 and D-18. | ✅ CONFIRMED, in both directions. The risk: a bundle where the session players are work-for-hire, the producer took points, the mixer retained nothing and the artwork is licensed rather than assigned produces a record whose ownership is a mess the buyer cannot see and will discover at their first sync request. Four separate purchases produce the same mess today, invisibly — which is exactly the reconstruction problem the platform exists to prevent, in miniature. The opportunity: because the bundle is one purchase across four engagements, the platform can **compose the chain of title at composition time** and show the buyer, before they pay, exactly what they will own when it is finished. Nothing in the industry can do that. It is a strong argument that this feature is genuinely thesis-aligned rather than a marketplace convenience — and it is why it is recorded as a node despite the `wont` proposal (sub-domain D-03), because the reason to cut it is capacity, not merit. |
| DT-02 | **A bundle's escrow is a shared hostage: one seller's silence freezes three strangers' money** | Adversarial: what is the failure mode, and how bad is it? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the concrete basis for the `wont` proposal rather than a vague "too complex". In a single-seller milestone chain, a stall is between two parties who chose each other and can resolve it. In a bundle, seller 3 has done nothing wrong, has reserved capacity (05.01.07) for a stage that will not open, and is financially blocked by a person they have never met — with no lever except a dispute against someone they have no contract with. Every mechanism this domain relies on assumes a bilateral relationship: cancellation settles between two parties (05.03.05), fault is assigned between two parties (05.03.01 D-04), consent to substitution is given by one buyer (05.05.01 D-02). None of them generalise cleanly to four-way. Building this before those bilateral mechanisms are proven would be building a DAG on foundations that have not carried a straight line yet — and the buyer outcome is already reachable today by purchasing four sequential engagements, which is what the platform is for. Hence `wont`: not wrong, not now. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A bundle is a milestone chain with a varying seller — not a new lifecycle | Reuses 05.03.02 rather than inventing a parallel model. What is new is only the seller-per-stage. |
| D-02 | Someone must be the buyer's counterparty for the whole bundle | An assembler carries the risk; the platform carrying it means underwriting strangers' reliability. |
| D-03 | Each stage's output auto-feeds the next stage's requirements | The bundle's real convenience, and it is not available to four separate purchases. |
| D-04 | The chain of title is composed and shown to the buyer **before** purchase | See DT-01. This is the feature's thesis-aligned value and nothing in the industry does it. |
