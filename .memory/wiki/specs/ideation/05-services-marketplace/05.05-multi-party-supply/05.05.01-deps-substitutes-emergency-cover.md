# Feature: Deps, Substitutes & Emergency Cover

> **Number**: 05.05.01
> **Parent**: [Multi-Party Supply](./05.05-multi-party-supply-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Substituting the human inside a live engagement: the booked player is ill, the mixer's studio floods, the engagement is Thursday and the seller cannot be there. Every working musician's career runs on the dep circuit, and today it runs on WhatsApp at 6am — with no terms, no escrow, and no record of who actually played.

This feature turns that ad-hoc scramble into a **consent-gated counterparty change on a live engagement**: the seller proposes a cover, the buyer approves, and the work's terms, escrow and credit re-point to the person who actually does it. It exists in two shapes — a **planned dep** (known in advance, calm) and **emergency cover** (hours before or mid-engagement, where speed is the entire value). The platform's edge over WhatsApp is that the terms already exist: the dep inherits a defined scope, rate and delivery date instead of "£? — I'll sort you out".

**Scope boundary**: domain 04 (Opportunities & Casting) owns `Dep/Sub Calls with Material-Aware Matching` — finding a dep for a gig that is *posted as an opportunity*. This feature is narrower and different: replacing the seller inside an engagement that already exists, has a buyer, has terms, and has money in escrow.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides, constantly. As the booked seller who cannot make it, proposes a dep rather than simply failing — which today means texting three people and hoping. As the dep, takes work that arrives with the terms, the material and the money already defined, instead of "£? — I'll sort you out". The persona's whole economic model is a portfolio of small income streams; deps are a large fraction of that portfolio, and they are the most informal part of it. |
| Producer | Full | Approves or refuses a dep as the buyer — and this is the persona whose refusal matters most, because they bought a specific person's taste. Also proposes deps as a seller when overcommitted (05.01.07). |
| Operator | Read-only | Narrow and real: for an on-location engagement, a substitution changes **who walks into their room**. They need the identity change for access, load-in, and in some venues for statutory reasons. They see who is coming instead — never the fee, never the reason. |
| Fan | None | Not visible and never surfaced. A substitution is a private B2B counterparty change inside a paid engagement; the fan has no stake in who mixed the record or covered the session, and exposing dep churn would leak commercially sensitive supply-side information. Even on an on-location engagement the fan sees the show, never the personnel change behind it. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A substitution is a **counterparty change on a live engagement**, which makes it structurally unlike anything else in the domain: the escrow's payee changes, the credit's subject changes, the rights transfer's assignor changes, and the reputation's earner changes — all mid-flight, on terms agreed with someone else.

It requires the buyer's consent (DT-02). What is negotiable is who covers, at what rate, and who absorbs any difference.

Two shapes:

| Shape | Trigger | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Planned dep** | Seller knows in advance | Proposed, approved, calm |
| **Emergency cover** | Hours before, or mid-engagement | Time-critical; the platform's value is speed and the pre-existing terms |

### Happy Path

1. Seller cannot fulfil; proposes a dep from their own network or from platform matching
2. System shows the buyer the proposed dep: their verified credits (02), their rate, and what changes
3. Buyer approves
4. Engagement's counterparty changes; escrow payee changes; terms carry over unless renegotiated
5. Dep performs and delivers; the credit emits to **the dep** (05.04.01), because they are who played
6. Rate difference (if any) settles per the agreed rule

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer refuses the dep | Their right — they bought a person, not a slot | Falls to cancellation (05.03.05), with fault sitting on the seller who cannot fulfil |
| Buyer unreachable during emergency window | Consent is mandatory (DT-02) but time-critical — the buyer is asleep at 6am and the session is at 10 | System pushes an urgent, expiring consent request (Notifications cross-cut); if it is not answered before the engagement start it lapses to **Uncovered → cancellation**. The platform will not auto-approve a substitution to save the date — silent substitution is the exact failure it prevents. Buyer sees a missed-consent notice; seller sees the request timed out |
| Dep costs more | System computes the rate delta at proposal time and settles it per the agreed absorption rule; **who bears the delta is a product decision (Q-01)** and both parties see the proposed split before approving | Buyer sees the dep's rate, the delta, and who is charged for it; approval is on the fully-priced proposal, not a blank cheque |
| Dep costs less | The seller pockets a margin on work they did not do | The delta is surfaced to the buyer at approval so the margin is never invisible; whether the seller keeps it or it returns to the buyer is a fairness call — see 05.05.03 |
| Emergency cover at 3 hours' notice | The realistic case, and the hardest | Speed is the product; the terms already exist, which is the platform's actual advantage over WhatsApp. Matching is time-boxed and prioritises the seller's own network (03) over cold platform matching |
| Seller substitutes silently | The failure this feature's consent rule prevents | Blocked at the counterparty-change boundary. A substitution cannot be committed without a recorded buyer approval. See DT-02 |
| Dep is worse; buyer is unhappy | Whose reputation takes it? | The dep's, for their own performance; the original seller's, for the failure to fulfil. Two facts, two records |
| Substitution mid-engagement (3 of 5 milestones done) | The dep inherits half-finished creative work they did not start | Realistic for tracking (transferable material), near-impossible for mixing (see DT-03). Where it is realistic, the engagement is split at the last accepted milestone: work already accepted stays credited to the original seller, work from the substitution point forward credits the dep |
| Original seller has done partial work | One engagement's payment splits across two humans | Payment splits at the milestone boundary — the original is paid for accepted deliverables, the dep for what they deliver from handoff. **The exact split arithmetic for a mid-milestone handoff is a genuine open decision (Q-03)** and defers to `/write-be-spec` |
| Dep is not on the platform | The most common real case — the best dep is a friend with no account | An off-platform dep can be *named* on the engagement for the buyer's consent, but payout, signature and credit-emission all require an account. `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` for the guest-onboarding / lightweight-claim path — see Q-02. **The feature's biggest practical gap.** |
| Dep is not actually free on the date | The proposed cover has a conflicting booking | Availability cross-cut is checked before the dep is shown to the buyer; a conflicted candidate is filtered out of matching and cannot be proposed, so the buyer never approves a dep who then no-shows |
| Dep needs their own cover (a dep-a-dep) | The cover cannot make it either — routine on tour | Each substitution is a fresh consent-gated counterparty change on the *current* live counterparty; there is no special-casing, the chain just re-runs the same flow. The engagement always reflects the most recently approved counterparty. Chain-length or abuse limits defer to Q-04 |
| Substitution proposed while a delivery is in flight (original delivers as the dep is approved) | The original seller ships work at the same moment the buyer approves the substitution — who owns that delivery? | Precedence follows "the party that did the thing wins" (DT-01; 05.03.05 D-16): a delivery **received before the substitution commits** belongs to the original counterparty — it is credited to and paid to them, and the substitution takes effect only for work from that point forward. The commit instant is the buyer's recorded approval (D-02); a delivery timestamped before that instant lands as the original's, a delivery after it lands as the dep's. If the in-flight delivery satisfies the whole engagement, the substitution is moot and is voided (nothing left to cover). Buyer sees the original's delivery credited to the original; both parties see the substitution scoped to remaining (or zero) work. Owner: this feature (see D-05) |
| On-location dep | Operator must know who is coming | Read-only identity notice to domain 16 — the Operator sees the new name for access/load-in, never the fee or the reason |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Cover Needed | Seller flags they cannot fulfil | Urgency level shown (planned vs emergency); matching offered, own-network first |
| Dep Proposed | Availability-checked candidate identified | Buyer sees credits (02), rate, computed delta, and Approve/Refuse |
| Awaiting Buyer Consent | Proposal sent, buyer not yet answered | Time-critical: an expiring consent request; both parties see the countdown. On emergency cover this is the state that decides the outcome |
| Dep Approved | Buyer approves | Counterparty changed; escrow payee re-points; all parties (incl. Operator if on-location) notified |
| Dep Refused | Buyer refuses | → cancellation (05.03.05), fault on the seller |
| Consent Lapsed | Buyer unreachable before engagement start | Request expires unactioned → Uncovered (no silent auto-approval) |
| Covered | Dep performing | Engagement continues under the new counterparty; credit will emit to the dep at acceptance (05.04.01) |
| Uncovered | No dep found, or consent lapsed, in time | → cancellation; the buyer's remedy is a refund and a stranded date |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The credit must follow the hands, not the contract — the dep gets the credit** | Reasoning about D-18 at this intersection: who did the work? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is non-negotiable given the thesis. If the original seller keeps the credit because they hold the contract, the platform has recorded a false fact — a musician credited on a session they were not at — and it did so *deliberately*, in the system whose entire value proposition is that its credits are true because they were captured at source. That is worse than the credits problem the platform exists to solve, because Jaxsta merely has gaps whereas this would have lies. It also enables `meta/personas.md`'s stated Musician "worst intentional" behaviour — "inflates credits to claim work they didn't do" — by making it a *supported feature*. The credit follows the hands. The consequence is that a substitution is not a payee change with a cosmetic label; it is a change to the engagement's subject, and every system downstream (02, 09, 10, reputation) must follow it. |
| DT-02 | **A seller cannot substitute without the buyer's consent** | Adversarial: what if we optimised for speed and let sellers self-serve? | ✅ CONFIRMED — silent substitution is ghost production without the NDA. The buyer commissioned a **named person's taste**, which is the entire basis of purchase in creative services and the reason this domain exists separately from a goods marketplace (D-14: "scoped human output"). A cymbal is a cymbal; a drummer is not a drummer. Allowing silent substitution would mean a buyer who researched a seller's discography, listened to their reel and paid their rate receives work by someone else entirely and is never told — and the credit graph, per DT-01, would then correctly record a stranger's name on the buyer's record. Consent is mandatory. The cost is real: it slows emergency cover, which is the case where speed is the product. That trade-off is accepted, because the alternative destroys the thing being sold. |
| DT-03 | A dep can cover any engagement | Symmetry assumption | ❌ REJECTED — and the asymmetry is instructive. **Performance** deps genuinely work: a bassist deps another bassist because the material is transferable (charts, keys, a reference recording), which is exactly why domain 04's dep matching is "material-aware". **Creative-authorship** deps mostly do not: nobody deps a mix engineer mid-mix. The half-finished mix is printed through the original's chain, comped to their taste, and a substitute would start again — the same non-portability of partial creative work that makes cancellation unanswerable (05.03.05 DT-03). So this feature's realistic scope is performance and time-bound engagements, and offering "find cover" on an in-flight album mix would be selling a rescue that cannot happen. The honest behaviour there is to route to cancellation, not to cover. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The credit follows the hands — the dep is credited | See DT-01. The alternative records a lie in the platform's core asset and enables the persona's stated worst behaviour. |
| D-02 | Substitution requires the buyer's affirmative consent | See DT-02. The buyer bought a named person's taste. Accepted cost: slower emergency cover. |
| D-03 | A refused or failed dep routes to cancellation with fault on the original seller | They could not fulfil. The buyer refusing a substitute is exercising a right, not causing a failure. |
| D-04 | Cover is offered only where deps are realistic — performance and time-bound work | Offering "find cover" on an in-flight album mix sells a rescue that cannot happen. See DT-03. |
| D-05 | When a delivery is in flight as a substitution commits, precedence goes to whoever delivered first: a delivery received before the buyer's recorded approval (D-02) belongs to the original counterparty (credited and paid to them); the substitution scopes only to work after that instant, and is voided if the in-flight delivery satisfies the whole engagement | Applies "the party that did the thing wins" (DT-01; 05.03.05 D-16) to the in-flight race. The commit instant is the buyer's approval timestamp, giving a single deterministic boundary. Resolves the prior `[PENDING]` in 05.05 CX-01 Q5. |
