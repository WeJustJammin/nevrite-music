# Feature: Turnaround, Rush & Service Level Guarantees

> **Number**: 05.01.06
> **Parent**: [Service Listings & Music Pricing](./05.01-service-listings-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The time promise attached to a service — "5 working days for a mix", "48-hour rush at +50%", "master back the same day" — and what happens when it is broken. Turnaround is the second-most-compared attribute after price, and it is the promise most often quietly broken, because sellers quote a turnaround from a fantasy in which the client sends usable stems immediately and asks for no revisions.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares turnaround per listing tier and a priced rush option. Sees, as a buyer, a due date they can plan a release around — the reason turnaround matters is that it sits upstream of a mastering date, an artwork deadline and a distribution submission window (domain 12). |
| Producer | Full | The persona whose turnaround promise is most load-bearing, because they sit in the middle of the chain: their mix is somebody else's mastering input. Sets rush pricing, which is a real revenue line — a producer's rush rate is often 1.5–2× and is bought regularly. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. On-location engagements have a date, not a turnaround — a date is a calendar commitment owned by the Availability cross-cut, not a duration clock. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Turnaround is a **duration**, not a date, and durations need a start event. The start event is **not** purchase. It is the moment the seller has everything they need to begin — requirements satisfied (05.03.01). The clock also **pauses** whenever the ball is in the buyer's court (awaiting revision feedback, awaiting a missing stem, awaiting an approval).

A rush is a priced add-on that shortens the promised duration and typically raises the price; it consumes capacity disproportionately (05.01.07).

An SLA breach needs a defined remedy. Without one, "5 working days" is decoration.

### Happy Path

1. Seller declares turnaround per tier ("5 working days") and, optionally, a rush tier ("48 hours, +50%")
2. Buyer orders; system shows an *estimated* due date and states plainly that the clock starts when requirements are complete
3. Buyer supplies requirements; system verifies the gate (05.03.01) and **starts the clock**, converting the estimate to a committed due date
4. Clock pauses on each buyer-side wait (revision feedback pending, approval pending)
5. Seller delivers within the duration; SLA met

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer never completes requirements | Clock never starts; engagement drifts toward abandonment | "Waiting on your stems since {date}. This job hasn't started." → abandonment path (05.03.05) |
| Revision round requested | Clock pauses at the revision request, resumes when the seller receives feedback | "Clock paused — due date moves to {new date}." |
| Working days vs calendar days | Must be explicit; "5 days" over a bank holiday weekend is a dispute | Due date is rendered as an absolute date+time in the buyer's timezone, never as "5 days" |
| Seller misses the due date | SLA remedy triggers | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — remedy menu (rush-fee refund / partial refund / cancel-with-full-refund / reputation signal only) undecided; see Q-01 |
| Buyer paid for rush, seller delivers at standard speed | The rush premium bought nothing | Rush premium auto-refunds — the one remedy that is clearly correct because the buyer paid for a specific thing they did not receive |
| Seller at capacity accepts a rush anyway | Capacity ceiling (05.01.07) should have blocked it | "You're at capacity. Accepting a rush here puts 3 jobs at risk." |
| Change order accepted mid-job | Duration is renegotiated as part of the change order, not silently extended | New due date shown in the change order before acceptance |
| Seller pauses the clock claiming a buyer wait that isn't real | Pause must be an attributed, contestable event — not a unilateral seller lever. Buyer can dispute a pause; a disputed pause routes to the Disputes cross-cut (domain 24) | "Seller paused this job saying they're waiting on you. You're not? Dispute the pause." |
| Buyer manufactures lateness to trigger a remedy (withholds a trivial approval until the due date passes, then claims breach) | The pause rule already defends this — any buyer-side wait pauses the clock, so a withheld approval cannot make the seller late. The breach clock only runs while the ball is in the seller's court | Buyer sees the clock is paused, not counting down — the manufactured-lateness path is structurally closed |
| DST / bank-holiday shift between quote and delivery moves the absolute due date | Due date is recomputed against the buyer's timezone and the governing holiday calendar (Q-02) at clock-start, then frozen; later DST changes shift the displayed local time, not the instant | Due date always shown as absolute local date+time; a DST note appears if the wall-clock time shifts |
| Engagement is on-location (05.01.05) | Turnaround does not apply — it is a date | Field hidden |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Estimated | Ordered, requirements incomplete | "Estimated due {date} — starts when we have your stems" |
| Running | Requirements gate passed | Committed due date + countdown |
| Paused | Awaiting buyer (feedback/approval/missing material) | "Paused — waiting on you. Due date moves as you respond." |
| Met | Delivered within duration | Green SLA badge on the engagement, feeds reputation |
| Breached | Due date passed without delivery | Breach banner + remedy options |
| N/A | On-location engagement | Field absent |

> **UI render states** (over the lifecycle above): **Loading** — countdown/due-date computing or syncing after a pause/resume event, shown as a skeleton on the date+countdown, never a blank. **Empty** does not occur — a listing without a declared turnaround is invalid (turnaround is a required listing attribute per 05.01.01), and an on-location engagement uses N/A, not empty. **Error** — due-date computation fails (missing timezone/holiday-calendar data, Q-02) → the estimate is shown with a "due date unconfirmed — starts when we have your stems" caveat rather than a wrong committed date; a committed date is never rendered from incomplete calendar data.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The turnaround clock cannot start at purchase — it starts when the requirements gate passes** | Domain expertise: a mix engineer who promises 5 days and receives the stems on day 9 has not broken a promise. Every marketplace that starts the clock at checkout (Fiverr's model) makes its own SLA a lie, and sellers respond by padding turnaround to absurdity, which destroys the attribute's comparability. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the single most consequential decision in the feature, and it is what structurally couples turnaround (declared here on the listing) to requirements gating (enforced in 05.03.01). It is also why 05.01.01 DT-01 (requirements belong on the listing) matters: the clock's start condition must be knowable before purchase. |
| DT-02 | The clock should run continuously once started | Simplicity | ❌ REJECTED — it must pause whenever the ball is in the buyer's court. A buyer who takes 8 days to give revision notes has not made the seller late. Without pausing, the seller's SLA is hostage to the buyer's responsiveness, and the rational seller response is again to pad. The pause rule is what makes the number honest. Consequence: the due date is **mutable**, and every mutation must be visible and attributed, or the buyer experiences it as the seller slipping. |
| DT-03 | An SLA with no remedy is sufficient — reputation will police it | Marketplace precedent (star ratings as the only consequence) | ❌ REJECTED for the **rush** case specifically. A buyer who paid a +50% rush premium and received standard-speed delivery paid for a named thing they did not get; a star rating is not a remedy, it is a complaint. The rush premium must auto-refund. For the *standard* case the remedy is genuinely a product decision (see Q-01) — a punitive refund regime on creative work invites buyers to manufacture lateness, and the seller's leverage (unreleased work) makes it messy. Do not decide this silently. |
| DT-04 | The pause rule is a seller-controlled lever and therefore a fresh abuse surface — a seller could pause on a fabricated buyer-wait to stop their own SLA clock | Adversarial pass on DT-02's consequence (due dates are mutable and seller-triggerable) | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real risk, mitigated not by removing the pause but by making every pause an **attributed, contestable event**: the pause carries a stated reason, is visible to the buyer in real time, and a buyer can dispute it (routing to the Disputes cross-cut / domain 24). A silent pause is the failure; an attributed one is honest. This is the mirror of the buyer-side manufactured-lateness case, and both are closed by the same principle — the party who owes the response is the party the clock runs against, and every transition is logged. |
| DT-05 | "Working days" can be left implicit and everyone will assume the same calendar | Simplicity / avoiding a jurisdiction decision | ❌ REJECTED — a seller in one jurisdiction and a buyer in another do not share a bank-holiday calendar, so "5 working days" resolves to two different dates. Left implicit, it becomes a dispute the moment a holiday falls inside the window. The governing calendar (seller's vs buyer's) is a genuine product decision (Q-02) — but that a single explicit calendar must be *named and frozen at clock-start* is not optional. This is why D-03 renders the outcome as an absolute instant, not a duration. |

- Consumes **Localization, Currency & Timezone** cross-cut — working days, bank holidays and the buyer's timezone are all jurisdiction-dependent; "5 days" is not a portable promise.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The clock starts at the requirements gate, not at purchase | See DT-01. The alternative makes the platform's own SLA dishonest and drives sellers to pad. |
| D-02 | The clock pauses whenever the buyer owes a response | See DT-02. Consequence: due dates are mutable and every mutation is attributed. |
| D-03 | A due date is always rendered as an absolute date+time in the buyer's timezone, never as a duration | "5 working days" across a bank holiday, two timezones and a weekend is four different dates. |
| D-04 | An unmet rush premium auto-refunds | The buyer paid for a named deliverable (speed) and did not receive it. This one is not a judgment call. |
| D-05 | Every pause/resume is an attributed, timestamped, buyer-visible, contestable event | See DT-04. The pause is a seller-triggerable lever, so it must be transparent and disputable, or it becomes an abuse surface. The transition log is also the evidence for any breach dispute. |
