# Feature: Service Category Taxonomy & Attributes

> **Number**: 05.01.02
> **Parent**: [Service Listings & Music Pricing](./05.01-service-listings-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The controlled vocabulary that makes service listings findable and comparable: what crafts exist (mixing, mastering, session performance, topline, arrangement, orchestration, editing/tuning, sound design, repair, artwork, PR), and the facets that qualify them (instrument, genre, service mode, deliverable, toolchain, rights posture). Without it a services marketplace degrades to full-text search over marketing copy, which is exactly why buyers on generic freelance platforms cannot find a metal mixer who works in Pro Tools.

It is not a lookup table, and it is not only a discovery aid. The **craft axis is the join between the marketplace and the credit graph** (DT-04): a craft that maps to canonical roles in `02.06` is what lets a mixing listing be corroborated by 34 mix credits, and what lets `05.01.01`'s cold-start seeding say "You have 34 mix credits. Sell mixing?" at all. That mapping is where consolidation touches provenance (D-18) inside this sub-domain — it is the reason this feature is thesis-adjacent rather than cosmetic.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Selects a craft and its facets when publishing a listing, and may **propose** a missing craft; the proposal enters a curation queue and never blocks publication (D-03). Cannot mint, promote, deprecate, map, or otherwise edit categories — a self-serve taxonomy fragments instantly ("mixing", "Mixing", "mix engineer", "MIXING & MASTERING"). The multi-hyphenate consequence is concrete: the same account carries listings under unrelated crafts (session drums, mixing, gear repair), each with a different facet set, and the taxonomy must not assume one account has one craft. As a **doubler**, declares multiple instruments on one listing (D-08) — sax/flute/clarinet is one seller, not three listings. |
| Producer | Config | Selects the deepest permitted facets and may propose a missing craft; neither capability grants vocabulary curation. The persona most likely to hit a taxonomy gap, because production crafts subdivide further than any other (comping, tuning, editing, programming are separately purchasable jobs). Also the persona whose listings most often span crafts ("Mix + Master"), which is why the primary/derived-secondary split (D-07) exists rather than a single craft field. |
| Operator | Config | Selects or proposes only within the venue/studio-attached branch — backline tech, in-house engineering, piano tuning, PA with operator — but cannot curate vocabulary. The bulk of the taxonomy remains invisible under this lens, and the invisibility is **load-bearing rather than cosmetic**: because craft constrains the legal pricing models (CX-01), a lens that hides the creative crafts also hides `points`, `per-song` and `per-minute` from `05.01.03`. A permission in the taxonomy therefore changes what is priceable (05.01 CX-01, synthesis Q3). |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees craft names as public browse/SEO surfaces ("Mixing engineers in Manchester") — the domain's primary organic acquisition path. Sees plain craft labels and at most genre/location; **never** DAW, summing bus, rights posture, deliverable spec, or any facet whose meaning requires professional knowledge. `personas.md`: fans "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity". The Fan is also the population the free-text qualifier is screened for (DT-06) — they are who reads the page a seller's unmoderated string renders on. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None
>
> **No persona has Full access.** Musician, Producer, and Operator have **Config** access solely to select a permitted taxonomy value and propose a missing one. The taxonomy remains **platform-curated**: curation authority — creating, promoting, deprecating, mapping, or editing vocabulary — sits with an admin/governance role that is **not one of the four personas** (`meta/personas.md` Q-02). Fan is Read-only. This preserves `02.06` D-20's governance boundary while applying the owner-ratified cross-domain RBAC contract.
>
> **Resolved cross-domain RBAC contract (formerly Q-08):** On 2026-07-19, the owner ratified `Config` for Musician, Producer, and Operator and `Read-only` for Fan for the identical select/propose action across service and credit taxonomies. A proposal is a constrained Config write to the curation queue, never an edit to the vocabulary; parent Role Matrix and this Role Lens use that same rule.

## Behavior

The taxonomy is **faceted, not a tree**. A music service is described by several orthogonal axes at once, and any single hierarchy forces a false parent choice: is "remote metal mix in Pro Tools" under Mixing → Metal → Remote, or Remote → Mixing → Metal? A tree makes one of those unreachable (DT-01).

### The axes, their owners, and their cardinality

| Axis | Example values | Owner | Cardinality on a listing | Required? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Craft (primary)** | mixing · mastering · session performance · topline/songwriting · production · arrangement/orchestration · editing & tuning · sound design · repair/tech · artwork · PR/plugging | **This feature** | Exactly 1 | Always |
| **Craft (secondary)** | — | **This feature**, *derived* | 0..n, never typed by the seller | Derived from tiers/add-ons (D-07) |
| Instrument | drums · bass · guitar · cello · voice · … | **`02.06`** (D-05 there) | 0..8 (D-08) | Only where the craft admits it |
| Genre | metal · afrobeats · film/TV · … | Canonical Data cross-cut | 0..5 (D-08) | Never (D-09) |
| Service mode | remote · on-location · hybrid | `05.01.05` | 1..2 | Always |
| Deliverable | stereo mix · stems · multitrack · Atmos · MIDI · charts · notation | `05.04.02` | 1..n | Always |
| Toolchain | DAW · plugin/format constraints | **This feature** | 0..n | Only where the craft admits it |
| Rights posture | work-for-hire · licence · co-ownership · points | `05.06.01` | Exactly 1 | Always (`05.01.01` D-02 — no default) |

**This feature owns the craft and toolchain axes. It owns no other axis** — it declares which axes apply to which craft, and consumes the values from their owners (D-02). The instrument axis is `02.06`'s, not a generic reference store's (D-10).

### Craft-scoped facet applicability

Each craft declares, as curated data: which axes apply, which are required, each axis's cardinality cap, and each axis's **relaxation rank** (D-11). A luthier is never asked for a DAW; a mastering engineer is never asked for an instrument. This is what keeps the seller's form short, and it is why the facet set cannot be global.

### The craft → canonical-role mapping (thesis-critical)

Every craft carries a mapping to zero or more canonical roles in `02.06`, with the same **fidelity grade** shape `02.06` D-17 uses for DDEX (`exact` / `broader` / `none`). Mixing → `Mixing Engineer` is `exact`. PR/plugging → `none`; no liner note has ever said it. The mapping is one-directional: **05 maps onto 02.06 and never extends it** (`02.06` D-14 — "Mixing" fails the liner-note admission test by name). Without this mapping, `05.01.01`'s credit-seeded onboarding and its "a listing's credibility is its verified discography" claim have no join to compute (DT-04).

### Resolution and extension — 02.06's shape, deliberately

A seller who types a craft the vocabulary lacks is never blocked. Resolution mirrors `02.06` D-18: exact/alias hit resolves **with the match shown**; a fuzzy input offers **≤5 ranked candidates and never auto-selects**; no hit routes to the nearest craft plus a bounded free-text qualifier and a curation proposal. The qualifier is **searchable text, never a browsable facet value** — the 05 analog of `02.06` D-12's requester-scoping: one seller's proposed word must not silently become a category everyone browses.

### Buyer intent vs seller precision

The taxonomy is optimised for seller precision, and the buyer does not have it (DT-05). A buyer arriving with "make my song louder" does not know whether they need mixing or mastering — this is the single most common miscategorised order in music services. **Buyer-facing intent aliases** map lay phrasing onto crafts and state the match plainly ("Sounds like you need **Mastering** — that's the loudness/finishing step. If your song is still separate tracks, you need **Mixing** first"). Same alias mechanism as `02.06`, pointed at the opposite population.

### Happy Path

1. Seller opens the listing form and picks a craft — typed or picked; a fuzzy input offers ≤5 candidates and waits rather than guessing
2. System reveals **only the facets that craft declares applicable** (a luthier is never asked for a DAW), each marked required or optional
3. System reveals **only the pricing models legal for that craft** (CX-01, `05.01.03` D-02) — `points` is not offered for a guitar setup
4. Seller sets facet values; required facets block publish with a named missing item; optional ones improve ranking and are labelled as doing so
5. Seller adds tiers/add-ons; any add-on whose own craft differs from the primary registers a **derived secondary craft** (D-07) — shown to the seller as "This also appears under Mastering, labelled as part of a Mix package"
6. System resolves the craft to its canonical roles in `02.06` and, where the seller holds credits in them, offers the corroboration ("You have 34 mix credits — show them on this listing?")
7. Seller publishes; the listing enters Search & Discovery indexing as a point in facet space
8. Buyer browses by any facet combination — or arrives through an intent alias — and results are comparable because the facets are shared, not free text
9. Buyer's empty result relaxes by declared rank, naming what was dropped (D-11)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Craft does not exist in the taxonomy** | Never blocks. Publishes under the nearest craft with a bounded free-text qualifier; a proposal enters the curation queue. The qualifier is searchable text, not a facet value | "We'll review 'stem separation' as a category. Listed under Editing & Tuning for now — buyers searching those words will still find you." |
| **Proposed craft is genuinely new and recurs** | Candidacy is derived, promotion is human (`02.06` D-03's shape). Signal: **≥5 distinct sellers propose the same craft within 90 days**. Threshold is lower than `02.06`'s ≥10-across-5-works because the proposing population is sellers (thousands), not crediting parties (all users), and a missing craft costs a seller discovery **immediately** | Proposer notified on promotion; their listing is offered the re-point, never moved without consent |
| **Single proposal, no recurrence** | Still reviewed — a seller whose craft does not exist has no product. Target: **10 working days**. Tunable; staffing is Q-02 | "Under review" on the seller's listing; no buyer-visible state |
| **Two sellers propose the same string meaning different things** | Deduplicated to one queue entry with two proposers and two claimed meanings — which is itself the signal the word is ambiguous. Resolves to **two crafts or a prompt, never to whichever meaning was reviewed first** (`02.06`'s rule for the same case) | Both keep selling; neither silently gets the other's meaning |
| **Two curators promote overlapping proposals concurrently** | Serialized per string; the second curator sees the first's result rather than a stale queue | "This was just decided by someone else" |
| **Free-text qualifier is abusive, spam, or PII** ("Mixing 📞 555-0143", a slur, an ad) | It is UGC on a public SEO page (DT-06). Bounded at entry: **≤64 chars, one line, no URLs, no markup, no control characters** — identical to `02.06` D-13, deliberately. Screened before public render via domain 24's screening cross-cut. Screening failure removes **the string, never the listing** | Seller sees their string immediately; the public sees it post-screening. A rejected string leaves the listing live under its canonical craft label |
| **Facet stuffing** — seller selects every genre to appear in every browse | Not blocked — D-07 (domain) forbids the platform verifying claims. Countered two ways: hard cardinality cap per axis (D-08), and rank penalty where a claimed genre is corroborated by **zero** credits in the mapped roles. Claim stands; ranking does not reward it | No error. The seller simply does not rank for genres they have never worked in |
| **Seller claims a craft their credit graph does not support** | Permitted and ranked lower. The platform makes claims **attributable**, not verified (domain D-07). Corroboration is a positive signal, never a gate — gating it would hard-block every cold-start seller and every career change | "Add credits in this craft to rank higher" — never "you may not sell this" |
| **Buyer orders the wrong craft** (wants mastering, orders mixing) | Intent aliases intercept at browse (DT-05). Post-order it is a scope problem, not a taxonomy one — routes to `05.02.01` quote/scope or `05.03.03` change order | "This looks like a mastering job — your tracks are already mixed. Switch?" before ordering |
| **Buyer filters to an empty result** | Progressive relaxation by **declared relaxation rank** (D-11), least-selective facet dropped first, **max 3 steps**, each step naming what it dropped. After 3, the cold-start CTA | "No cellists in Leeds. Showing remote cellists — 34 results." |
| **Facet combination is empty and relaxation cannot help** (genuinely no supply) | Stops at 3 steps; shows the seller-side CTA rather than an infinitely widening result set | "Nobody offers this yet. Be the first — or tell us what you were looking for." |
| **Two facet values mean the same thing** (genre drift between domains) | Resolved by the value's **owner**, not locally — genre by the Canonical Data cross-cut, instrument by `02.06`. This feature holds no second vocabulary to drift | No user-visible difference — that is the point |
| **Craft is retired or merged, target permits a superset** of the source's facets and pricing models | Live listings **auto-re-point** and stay findable. A live listing is an offer, not a historical fact — freezing it would silently make it unfindable and cost the seller income (DT-07). Seller notified, informational | "Editing & Tuning is now Audio Editing. Your listing moved — nothing else changed." |
| **Craft is retired or merged, target does NOT permit the listing's pricing model or a required facet** | **No silent strip.** Curation sees the impact count before committing (D-12). Affected listings stay live under the old label, enter `Action needed`, seller notified at day 0 / 7 / 28; at **day 30** the listing **pauses** — an existing `05.01.01` state that destroys nothing and preserves every engagement | "'Production' no longer allows points-only. Pick a pricing model by {date} or this listing pauses." |
| **A craft merge would orphan a live quote or in-flight engagement** | Neither moves. A quote is immutable to expiry (`05.01.01` edge cases) and an engagement references its accepted quote's **listing snapshot**, which freezes exactly as a credit does (DT-07) | No buyer-visible change |
| **Listing's craft changes while a quote derived from it is live** | Listing version increments; the issued quote honours its own snapshot until expiry (`05.01.01` D-01) | "2 open quotes were issued from the previous category. They remain valid until {date}." |
| **Seller lists a service another domain owns** — rehearsal room hire, a lesson, beats for sale | The craft picker is the enforcement point (`05.01.01` Q-01's open mechanism, answered here for the taxonomy's half): these crafts **do not exist** in this vocabulary, so the nearest-craft path routes rather than accepts. Room hire → 16 (perishable time inventory); lessons → 06; **pre-made beats → 14** — a beat is a digital good with a licence, not scoped human output (D-14 platform-wide) | "That's a room booking — list it here instead", with the link. Not an error; an absent option plus a route |
| **Seller lists a service that is repair/custodial** | Craft's declared lifecycle is custodial, so the listing form swaps to intake/estimate and routes to `05.07.01` — not delivery/acceptance | Listing form changes shape at craft selection |
| **"AI mastering" / "AI stem separation" proposed as a craft** | **Refused as a craft and routed** (DT-13). AI involvement is a **disclosure axis** on the listing (`05.06.05`), never a craft — identical to `02.06`'s active intercept of "AI" typed into the role field (`02.10` D-01). Whether an AI-disclosed listing is *permitted at all* remains a values call — Q-01, now materially narrowed | "That's not a category — it's Mastering with AI involvement. Declare it here", with the disclosure control inline |
| **Craft admits an instrument; seller is a doubler** (sax + flute + clarinet on one date) | One listing, multiple instruments (D-08) — mirrors `02.06` D-16's cardinality asymmetry. Doubling **carries a fee** in the real world (`02.02.01` DT-05 records the same fact from the credits side); the fee is `05.01.03`'s, the multi-valued axis is this feature's | One listing: "Woodwinds — sax, flute, clarinet, bass clarinet" |
| **Craft admits no instrument** (mastering, PR) | The instrument axis is not offered at all — not shown empty, not shown disabled | The field does not exist on the form |
| **Non-English seller types a craft in their language** ("mixage", "マスタリング") | Craft aliases are **locale-tagged**, mirroring `02.06` D-22. Without it, every non-English seller hits the proposal path on their first listing for a word that is simply *the word* | Resolves like any alias — if the locale is seeded (Q-04) |
| **Facet value's owner is unavailable at listing time** (`02.06` down, Canonical Data down) | The craft still resolves (this feature owns it) and the listing **saves as a draft**; the owned-elsewhere facets are deferred, not abandoned. Publish is held only because a required facet is genuinely absent — but nothing typed is lost | "Saved. We couldn't load the instrument list — try publishing again shortly." |
| **Craft vocabulary itself is unavailable** | Listing cannot be created — the craft is the form's root and there is no meaningful degraded mode. Unlike `02.06` D-19, **there is no thesis-critical fact racing a closing window here**: a seller publishing a shopfront is at a desk and can return in 5 minutes; a Producer capturing a credit cannot. The asymmetry is deliberate | "Can't load categories right now. Nothing you've typed is lost." |
| **Facet counts are stale** (a listing paused since the count was last refreshed) | A count may lag reality by up to ~5 minutes. A count that overstates by a few is a browse annoyance; a **zero-result click-through is not** — so a facet value whose count is stale-nonzero but truly zero relaxes on arrival rather than showing an empty page | Relaxation message, not an empty result |
| **Seller edits craft on a listing with accumulated reputation** | Reputation is **per-role** (Reviews cross-cut; `05.01.01` DT-03) — changing craft does not carry mix reviews onto a mastering listing. The edit is permitted; the reputation does not follow | "Reviews stay with the work you did. This listing starts fresh in Mastering." |
| **Multi-hyphenate is buyer and seller in the same craft at once** | No conflict — facets are properties of listings, not of accounts. The same account's mixing listing and its "hiring a mixer" browse share one vocabulary and never share state | Nothing. The absence of an interaction is the correct behaviour |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Facet panel fetching; craft picker opening | The craft field is **typeable immediately** — the literal is captured regardless. Facet panel renders as skeleton rows; counts fill in behind. No spinner blocks a keystroke |
| **Populated** | Normal browse | Faceted browse with per-value counts; a count may lag reality by up to ~5 minutes |
| **Empty (cold start)** | Facet combination has no listings | Relaxed results, naming each dropped facet, max 3 steps → "be the first" seller CTA |
| **Partial** | New craft live, facets not yet populated by any listing | Craft browsable; **unpopulated facet values hidden rather than shown at zero** — "Metal (0)" makes a marketplace look dead and is the cold-start failure that reads as abandonment |
| **Partial (seller form)** | Craft resolved, a facet axis owned elsewhere unavailable | Owned-here facets editable; unavailable axis shows "couldn't load — retry"; draft saves with everything typed |
| **Error** | Craft vocabulary unreachable | Listing creation held with nothing lost. Browse degrades to full-text search over listings with an explicit banner — the honest statement that the thing making results comparable is currently absent |
| **Action needed** (listing) | Craft retired/merged; target does not permit this listing's model or a required facet | Listing live under the old label; named missing item; countdown to the day-30 pause |

| DT-01 | A hierarchical category tree is sufficient (the Fiverr/Reverb model) | Marketplace precedent | ❌ **REJECTED** — music services are irreducibly multi-axis (craft × instrument × genre × mode × deliverable × toolchain). Any tree makes one axis primary and the rest unreachable, and the axis buyers lead with **varies by craft**: a mix buyer leads with genre, a session buyer leads with instrument, a repair buyer leads with location. Faceted is the only shape serving all three. |
| DT-02 | The genre and instrument facets should be owned by this domain | Locality assumption | ❌ **REJECTED** — a services-local genre list forks from the one used by Release (12), Licensing (11) and the credit graph (02). Two genre vocabularies in one platform is the classic entity-resolution failure. This domain owns the **craft** axis; it *consumes* instrument and genre. The seam a later reader should watch. |
| DT-03 | The taxonomy is a discovery concern and belongs in the Search & Discovery cross-cut | Node Classification Gate — "is this a mechanism?" | ❌ **REJECTED** — Search owns indexing, ranking and query; it does not own *what crafts exist in music*. That vocabulary is product content with editorial consequences (deciding "stem separation" is a craft **creates a market**; deciding "AI mastering" is a craft is a values call). Precedent: domain 02 retains its own role/instrument taxonomy for the same reason (`02.06` DT-02). |
| DT-04 | The craft axis is an independent vocabulary — it needs no relationship to `02.06`'s canonical roles | The natural reading of D-02 ("we own craft, we consume the rest"), and how the breadth pass left it | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP; resolved by D-05.** D-02 established which axes we *don't* own and stopped. It never asked what the axis we **do** own connects to — and the answer is the thesis. `05.01.01` promises that "a listing's credibility is its verified discography, not its copy" and that cold-start seeding says "You have 34 mix credits. Sell mixing?". **Both are uncomputable without a craft → canonical-role mapping.** There is no join between "sells mixing" and "has mix credits" unless this feature carries one. So every craft maps to 0..n canonical roles with an explicit fidelity grade (`exact`/`broader`/`none`, borrowed wholesale from `02.06` D-17 because the problem is identical: an ungraded `broader` mapping presents itself as exact and the corroboration silently overstates). Mixing → `Mixing Engineer` is exact; **PR/plugging → `none`**, and that is correct, not a gap — no liner note has ever credited a plugger, so a PR listing simply has no credit-graph corroboration available and must not pretend otherwise. This is the seam where consolidation touches provenance (D-18) inside this sub-domain, and nothing in the breadth pass named it. |
| DT-05 | The taxonomy's job is seller precision — a buyer will find their way to the right craft | The implicit assumption of every facet in this file, and of every marketplace taxonomy | ❌ **REJECTED — the buyer's vocabulary is not the seller's, and this is the domain's most common real-world failure.** A buyer arrives saying "make my song louder", "make it sound finished", "make it radio-ready". They do not know whether that is mixing or mastering — and ordering the wrong one is the single most common miscategorised order in music services, routinely producing an engagement where the seller cannot deliver what was imagined and neither party was lying. A taxonomy tuned for the Producer's precision (who correctly distinguishes comping from tuning from editing) makes the buyer's entry point strictly **worse**, because precision raises the knowledge required to enter. Resolved by **buyer-facing intent aliases** (D-06) — the same alias mechanism `02.06` points at musicians, pointed at the opposite population, and **stating the match plainly** rather than silently routing, because a wrong silent route is a wrong order. The sharp part: this is not a search-relevance problem (Search would happily return great mixing results for "make it louder") — it is a **vocabulary** problem, and only this feature can fix it. |
| DT-06 | The free-text qualifier is a harmless interim while a craft proposal is reviewed | The breadth pass's own framing — it read as a courtesy, not a surface | ❌ **REJECTED — it is unmoderated UGC on the domain's primary organic acquisition surface, which is the worst place for it.** The moment a seller's arbitrary string is retained and rendered on a listing, this feature publishes user-generated text onto **public category pages read by Fans** — a population `personas.md` and D-13 flag as bringing a moderation profile professionals do not have, arriving via SEO on a page WeJammin published and Google indexed. "Mixing 📞 555-0143 CHEAPEST RATES" is a plausible first-week listing and it is also **off-platform-leakage-as-a-service**, which makes it commercially motivated rather than merely rude. `02.06` DT-10 found precisely this at the seam between "never block" and "the literal is displayed", and the identical seam exists here — which is itself the finding: **any vocabulary with a never-block escape hatch and a display surface is a UGC surface**, and neither file's breadth pass saw it. Resolved by D-04 with `02.06` D-13's bounds copied deliberately (≤64 chars, one line, no URLs, no markup) so the platform has one rule rather than two, screened via domain 24, and **screening removes the string, never the listing** — the seller's craft was never the problem. |
| DT-07 | A listing freezes its taxonomy resolution at publish, exactly as a credit freezes at assertion (`02.06` D-11) | Consistency reflex — `02.06` is `[DEEP]`, its versioning rule is well-argued, so inherit it | ❌ **REJECTED, and the inversion resolves Q-03.** `02.06` D-11 freezes because **a credit is a historical fact**: "John played drums on this in 2026" must mean in 2029 what it meant when asserted, or the vocabulary rewrites history through a lookup table. **A live listing is not a fact — it is a standing offer.** Freeze it and a craft merge silently orphans it: it holds a category nobody can browse to, it disappears from the surface it was published to reach, and **the seller loses income with no error and no notification** — a failure whose only symptom is absence. So the rule inverts: live listings **re-point**; frozen credits do not. But the inversion is not total, and the exception is what makes it coherent — an **engagement's listing snapshot** (`05.01.01` D-01: "historical engagements retain their listing snapshot for evidence") **is** a historical fact and freezes exactly like a credit, because "what was this seller offering when I accepted?" is an evidentiary question in a dispute. The principle underneath: **freeze what is evidence; re-point what is an offer** — and this one feature serves both, which is why a single inherited rule was always going to be wrong for one of them. |
| DT-08 | Facet cardinality is unbounded — a seller may claim any number of instruments or genres | The breadth pass never asked; unbounded is the default nobody decides | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP; resolved by D-08, in both directions at once.** Unbounded invites facet stuffing (select every genre, appear in every browse) — the oldest marketplace abuse there is, and one the Fan-facing SEO surface amplifies. But a naive cap set at 1-2 breaks a real and *respectable* professional: the **woodwind doubler** legitimately plays sax, flute, clarinet and bass clarinet, and doubling is a priced fact in this industry, not a boast — `02.02.01` DT-05 records the same reality from the credits side, where it says the taxonomy "must be able to express doubling because it carries a fee". So the caps must be set from **domain reality rather than from abuse-fear**: instrument 0..8 (a real doubler reaches 5-6; a genuine multi-instrumentalist 8), genre 0..5 (a working engineer credibly covers metal/rock/punk/alt/pop; a seller claiming 12 is claiming none). Cardinality alone is a blunt control, so the second half matters more: an uncorroborated genre claim (zero credits in the mapped roles) is **ranked down, never blocked** — because domain D-07 forbids the platform verifying claims, and DT-04's mapping is exactly what makes ranking-by-corroboration computable. The two DTs compound: the mapping built for cold-start seeding turns out to be the anti-stuffing control too. |
| DT-09 | Genre on a service means the same thing as genre on a work, so one vocabulary implies one behaviour | The natural consequence of D-02 — shared vocabulary reads as shared semantics | ❌ **REJECTED — same values, different semantics, and the difference decides optionality.** Genre on a *release* (12) is a **classification**: this record is metal. Genre on a *service listing* is a **competence claim**: I am good at metal. The vocabulary must be shared (D-02 stands — forking it is the entity-resolution failure), but the **cardinality and optionality must not be**. A record has a genre; a mastering engineer who masters everything genuinely **has no genre**, and forcing one on them makes them lie to the field. `02.06` D-10 reached the neighbouring conclusion from the role side — genre is "contested, plural, and frequently absent", which is why it refused to make genre an axis on the role at all. Here genre survives as a **facet** (buyers legitimately filter for a metal mixer, and it is often the axis they lead with — DT-01), but it is **never required, always multi-valued, and capped** (D-09, D-08). The general shape: sharing a vocabulary across domains is correct; **inheriting its semantics across domains is not**, and the second does not follow from the first. |
