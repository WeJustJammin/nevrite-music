# Feature: Service Listings, Tiered Packages & Rate Cards

> **Number**: 05.01.01
> **Parent**: [Service Listings & Music Pricing](./05.01-service-listings-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The artifact a music professional publishes to sell their work: what craft they offer, at what tiers, for what price, with what included, what excluded, what buyable as an add-on, and on what rights terms. It is the shopfront the buyer browses, compares and orders from, and the template every quote is rendered from. Without it the marketplace has nothing to transact against.

The **rate card** is the same data seen from the seller's side: one aggregate surface across every listing they run, where a multi-hyphenate can see and change every rate they charge in one place — and the only surface that can tell them what a rate change will actually break.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Publishes listings as a seller ("Session bass, remote, £150/song, 2 revisions, 5-day turnaround"; "Topline writing, points-only") and browses/compares listings as a buyer when hiring a mixer or a player. Runs several listings simultaneously across unrelated crafts — the multi-hyphenate case: the same account sells session drums, mixing and gear repair, and each is a separate listing with its own rate-card row, its own add-on set and its own per-craft reputation. Uses the rate card as the one place every rate they charge is visible and editable, and as the only surface that shows the blast radius of a rate change before it lands (DT-07). Blocked from ordering their own listing (DT-09). |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest seller. Publishes tiered production/mixing/mastering packages (Mix / Mix + Master / Mix + Master + Stems) with per-tier revision counts, deliverable specs and a **per-tier** rights posture. The persona for whom add-ons matter most — Atmos, alternate/TV mix, instrumental, stem delivery and session handover are each separately priced and orthogonal to the tier (DT-02). Also the heaviest buyer: hires players, vocalists and mastering engineers into their sessions from listings. Their demo reel is their strongest sales asset and their largest rights exposure (DT-04), because a Producer's best work is usually work they assigned away. |
| Operator | Config | Does not sell creative output under this lens. Configures listings for venue/studio-attached **human craft** only — in-house engineer, backline tech, piano tuning, PA hire with an operator — priced hourly or day rate (05.01.03 Operator lens). Room hire itself is **not** a listing here: it is perishable time inventory in domain 16, and the taxonomy has no craft for it, which is what makes the boundary enforceable rather than advisory (D-04). Their genuine hard case is selling the room and the engineer as one purchase, which decomposes across two domains (Q-04). Sees a rate card covering only the attached crafts they sell. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees public service listings as SEO/browse surfaces (a producer's page says "takes work"). Sees the craft, the tiers, the rendered "from" price, demo clips and credits. Never sees the pricing-model machinery (05.01.03), the benchmark band (D-05), the seller's queue (05.01.07), or any rate card. Cannot order under the Fan lens — a hobbyist who buys a mix is acting as a Musician and is onboarded into that lens. The explicit absence matters: the listing surface must not assume a professional viewer, because a large share of its inbound traffic is not one. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A listing is a **template**, not an offer. It declares a craft (from 05.01.02's taxonomy), a service mode (05.01.05), one or more tiers, and — per tier — a pricing model (05.01.03), a structured inclusion set, a structured exclusion set, a deliverable spec (05.04.02), a revision allowance, a turnaround (05.01.06), and a rights posture (05.06.01). It additionally declares an add-on set, a requirement set the buyer must supply, and demo media.

A buyer either **orders a tier directly** (fixed-scope path — available only when the tier's pricing model can render from inputs known at checkout) or **requests a quote** (05.02.01), which renders the listing's pricing model against their specific job and snapshots the listing version.

### Price composition — the evaluation order

A quote is an **ordered evaluation, not a sum** (D-03). Add-ons occur in the real market in three shapes and they do not commute:

1. **Base** — the tier's pricing model rendered against the job's inputs (5 songs × £300 = £1,500).
2. **Unit add-ons** — each rendered from its own model against the same job inputs (stems @ £50/song × 5 = £250).
3. **Subtotal** = base + unit add-ons.
4. **Percentage add-ons** — applied to the subtotal in declared order (rush +50% per 05.01.06; Atmos ×1.5).
5. **Fee total** = subtotal + percentage add-ons. **This is the take-rate base.**
6. **Expenses** — travel, per-diem, cartage appended at cost, receipt-backed, **zero take rate** (05.01.05 D-02).
7. **Grand total** = fee total + expenses.

An add-on's pricing model is restricted to `{flat, same-unit-as-tier, percentage-of-subtotal}`. Any other model would require a conversion the platform cannot honestly perform (see edge cases).

### The rate card

The seller-facing aggregate across all their listings: craft · tier · model · rate · add-ons · currency · last changed · pinned-by-contract flag. It supports bulk edit, and bulk edit **must** compute and display its blast radius before applying (DT-07). Rate history is **private** — buyers never see a rate timeline (D-06).

### Happy Path

1. Seller opens listing creation from an empty rate card, or from the credit-seeded CTA ("You have 34 mix credits — sell mixing?"). The CTA pre-selects the **craft only**; it never pre-fills price, posture or capacity (DT-08).
2. Seller selects a craft from the platform-curated taxonomy (05.01.02). System reveals only the facets relevant to that craft (a luthier is never asked for a DAW) and only the pricing models legal for it (CX-01 — points are not offered for a guitar setup).
3. Seller declares service mode (05.01.05). Remote reveals nothing further; on-location reveals travel radius, per-diem, cartage and minimum block, and restricts the model set to day/half-day (CX-07).
4. Seller defines 1–3 tiers. Each tier requires: name, pricing model + parameters, structured inclusion set, structured exclusion set (DT-06), revision count, deliverable spec (05.04.02 vocabulary), turnaround (05.01.06).
5. As each rate is entered, the system renders the benchmark band **in the seller's own pricing model** beside it (05.01.04, CX-02). This happens in the pricing step and nowhere else (D-05).
6. Seller adds add-ons from the craft's add-on vocabulary. Each declares its own pricing model from the restricted set. An excluded item that is given an add-on price flips from "not included" to "available as an add-on" — one list, one flag (DT-06).
7. Seller elects the **rights posture per tier** (05.06.01) and the source-file handover terms (05.04.04). The system requires an explicit election and explains each posture in plain language to the seller — no silent default (D-02).
8. Seller declares the **requirements** the buyer must supply (stem naming convention, tempo map, reference tracks, session sample rate) from the craft's requirement vocabulary. This becomes the gate in 05.03.01 and the start event for the turnaround clock (DT-01, 05.01.06 D-01).
9. Seller sets the concurrency ceiling for the craft (05.01.07) if none exists. A second listing in a craft they already sell **inherits** the existing ceiling — the ceiling is per human per craft (05.01.07 D-02).
10. Seller attaches demo media. Each clip is capped at 60 seconds and is either linked to a verified credit in domain 02 or carries an explicit `unverified` flag (D-09, DT-04).
11. System previews the listing exactly as a buyer sees it: craft, tiers, rendered "from" price, add-ons, turnaround, posture in plain language, requirements, demo reel. **No benchmark band** — that is the seller's pricing view, not the buyer's (D-05).
12. Seller publishes. The publish gate runs: posture elected on every tier · required facets set · demo media transcoded or placeheld · payout account onboarded (warns, does not block). Publish is atomic (D-11).
13. Listing enters Search & Discovery indexing and becomes orderable subject to capacity (CX-03). Buyer browses, compares within the model, and orders a tier directly or requests a quote — which snapshots this listing version and becomes immutable (05.02.01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller edits price while a quote derived from this listing is live | Listing version increments; the issued quote is immutable and honours its own snapshot until expiry (05.02.01) | "2 open quotes were issued from the previous rate. They remain valid until {date}." |
| Seller edits price while an engagement derived from this listing is in flight | Engagement is unaffected — it references the accepted quote version, not the live listing | No buyer-visible change |
| Seller edits price while a **retainer** (05.03.04) references this listing | The contracted retainer rate is pinned to the retainer's term and does not move. A rate card that silently repriced a contracted client would breach the contract from the UI. | "Your retainer with {buyer} is fixed at {rate} until {date}. This change won't affect it." |
| **Bulk rate change** across N listings from the rate card | Blast radius computed and shown **before** apply: live quotes on the old rate, waitlisted buyers who were quoted a figure, pinned retainers, and each listing's resulting benchmark-band position | "This changes 6 listings. 3 open quotes keep the old rate. 1 retainer is locked. 2 listings move from mid-band to upper-band." |
| Seller publishes with no rights posture elected | Publish blocked | "Choose what the buyer owns when they accept. There is no safe default here." |
| Seller elects posture on tier 1 but not tier 2 | Publish blocked — election is per **tier**, not per listing. A "Mix" tier licensing the mix and a "Mix + Stems" tier assigning it are different deals. | "Tier 2 ('Mix + Master') has no rights posture." |
| Seller's payout account is not onboarded (Payments cross-cut) | Publish allowed, order blocked. Publishing is discovery; ordering is money. | "Live, but not orderable — finish payout setup to accept orders." |
| Seller is at capacity for this craft (05.01.07) | Listing stays visible but is not orderable (CX-03) | "Booking again from {date}" + join-waitlist CTA |
| Seller publishes a **second** listing in a craft they are already at capacity in | New listing is born At Capacity. The ceiling is per human per craft (05.01.07 D-02) — publishing more listings does not manufacture throughput, and a seller who believes it does will breach an SLA discovering otherwise. | "You're at capacity for Mixing, so this goes live unorderable. More listings don't add capacity." |
| Craft is custodial (repair/tech) | Listing routes to the custodial job flow (05.07.01): possess-then-quote, not quote-then-work. Delivery/acceptance/rights sections are absent from the form. | Listing form swaps to intake/estimate/custody fields |
| Seller wants a craft that does not exist ("stem separation") | Cannot be minted — the taxonomy is platform-curated (05.01.02 D-01). Seller publishes under the nearest craft with a free-text qualifier and submits a curation proposal. | "We'll review 'stem separation' as a category. Listed under Editing for now." |
| **Listing copy describes a service owned by another domain** (rehearsal room hire, a lesson) | Structurally unsellable rather than policed: there is no craft for it, and a craft is required (D-04). If a seller mis-categorises under a legitimate craft, it is reported to 24 as mis-categorisation. Self-limiting: a room hire routed through this domain's lifecycle has no deliverable, no acceptance and no rights transfer, so it fails at the first milestone rather than succeeding silently. | Reporter: "This isn't the service described." Seller: "Rooms are listed in Venues & Studios. Lessons are listed in Education." |
| Operator wants "room + in-house engineer, £300/day" as one listing | Decomposes. This listing prices the human craft only; the room is domain 16 inventory and links to it. Packaging the two as one purchase is unresolved (Q-04). | "Price your engineer here. The room is priced in your Venue listing." |
| Seller changes the **craft** of a live listing (Mixing → Mastering) | Blocked. Craft determines the legal model set (CX-01), the facet set, the benchmark cohort (CX-08) and the per-craft reputation cohort. Changing it migrates reviews into a craft they were never earned in — reputation laundering by form field. | "You can't change the craft of a live listing. Archive it and publish a new one — your Mixing reviews stay with Mixing." |
| **Seller orders from their own listing** | Blocked at order (not at publish). A self-order manufactures an engagement, a review, a verified credit and an SLA-met badge for the price of the take rate — the cheapest reputation on the platform, and precisely `personas.md`'s Musician "worst intentional" behaviour. | "You can't buy your own service." |
| Seller orders their own listing via an entity they control (studio account → personal account) | The same attack, one indirection away. Blocking it requires the entity-control graph from domain 01. | Depends on Q-01. Until the control graph exists: emitted to 24 as a self-dealing signal, not blocked at order. |
| Two sellers reciprocally order from each other's listings | **Not blocked.** This is a legitimate transaction shape — the multi-hyphenate genuinely hires the people who hire them, constantly. Emitted to 24 as a reciprocity signal for review-ring detection, never auto-actioned. | Nothing. A false positive here accuses a working musician of fraud for doing their job. |
| Listing copy contains contact details ("email me at…") | Off-platform solicitation, detected on publish and on every edit, routed to 24. Not silently stripped — a seller who believes their number published and it did not will simply re-add it. | "Contact details can't go in a listing. Buyers reach you through the engagement thread." |
| Seller prices a tier at £1 to farm cheap reviews | Not blocked. A £1 price is legal and sometimes genuine (a favour, a test, a cold-start loss-leader). Emitted to 24 alongside review velocity. A price floor would punish the cold-start seller the platform needs most. | Nothing visible |
| Demo clip is a track the seller has **no verified credit** on | Publishes flagged `unverified`, visually distinguished, and excluded from the credit-derived credibility signal. Not blocked — the credit graph is incomplete at cold start, and absence of a credit is not evidence of a lie. | "Unverified — link a credit to show this is your work." |
| Demo clip is a commercial master the seller worked on **under work-for-hire** | The seller performed on it and assigned it (05.06.01). The platform hosting and streaming the audio is the platform's reproduction, not the seller's. Clip capped at 60s, credit-linked, takedown-addressable via 24/DMCA. See DT-04 — the credit graph is what makes the platform's knowledge non-deniable. | "Linked to your credit on {track}. 60-second clip." + rights-holder takedown path |
| Demo media still transcoding at publish time | Listing publishes; media slots show a transcoding placeholder. Blocking publish on the Media cross-cut's pipeline makes listing creation hostage to a queue the seller cannot see. | Live. "Audio still processing — buyers will see it in a few minutes." |
| Two devices edit the same listing concurrently | Optimistic-concurrency guard on the listing version; the stale write is **rejected, never merged**. A field-level merge of two rate edits can publish a price neither seller chose. | "This listing changed in another tab. Reload to see the current version." |
| Buyer is viewing the listing while the seller edits it | Buyers always read the published version. Draft edits are invisible until publish. | No change until publish; the buyer's next render shows the new version |
| Buyer opens Order at the instant the seller's edit publishes | The order carries the listing version the buyer was **shown**. If that version is no longer publishable (posture removed, craft retired), the order is rejected rather than silently upgraded to terms the buyer never saw. | "This listing changed while you were ordering. Here's what's different: {diff}." |
| Two buyers order the seller's last capacity slot simultaneously | Reservation half of the Availability cross-cut owns atomic decrement (05.01.07). The listing flips to At Capacity for the loser. | "That slot went while you were checking out. Join the waitlist?" |
| Higher tier priced below a lower tier, or including strictly less | **Warn, do not block.** Non-monotonic tiers are usually a typo and occasionally deliberate (an intro tier priced high to deter tyre-kickers). | "Tier 2 costs more than Tier 3 but includes less. Intentional?" |
| Add-on's pricing model is incompatible with the tier's (per-minute add-on on a per-song tier) | Blocked at listing time. There is no conversion between programme minutes and song count the platform can honestly perform, and guessing it would generate a wrong number the buyer would rely on. | "A per-minute add-on can't attach to a per-song tier — we'd have to guess the run time." |
| Two identical listings from one seller (same craft, same facets, same price) | Legal — DT-03 makes many-per-craft the norm — but collapsed to a single result in Search & Discovery. Not blocked; the seller may be mid-differentiation. | Seller: "These two look identical to buyers — they'll show as one result." |
| **Seller has live listings and stops responding** | Does **not** become At Capacity: capacity counts accepted engagements, and an absent seller accepts none, so they read as maximally available (DT-05). Liveness is fact-derived from response rate (D-08) and demotes the listing to Stale, then Dormant. | Buyer: no "responds in ~2 days" badge; ranked down. Seller: "3 buyers messaged and got no reply. Your listings are hidden until you respond or pause." |
| Seller's account is suspended (24) | All listings hidden from browse and non-orderable. In-flight engagements belong to the engagement lifecycle, not to the listing — a suspension must never orphan work a buyer already paid for. | Direct link: "This listing isn't available." |
| Rights posture elected is not enforceable in the seller's or buyer's jurisdiction (05.06.01 DT-03) | Listing publishes; the posture is **not** silently re-mapped. The platform must not tell a seller they transferred something they may not have. | Deferred — 05.06.01 Q-01. Needs counsel. |
| Network failure mid-publish | Publish is atomic (D-11). The listing either enters the published version wholly or stays Draft. A half-published listing — indexed, orderable, no posture — is the dangerous outcome. | "Couldn't publish — nothing changed. Try again." |
| Seller changes their payout currency after publishing | The rate card re-renders labels; declared rates are **not** converted. £300 must not silently become €300 (a cut) or €352 (a rise) without the seller choosing. | "Your rates are in GBP. Set new rates in EUR, or keep charging in GBP." |
| Buyer's facet combination returns no listings | 05.01.02 owns progressive relaxation; this surface shows the relaxed set and names the dropped facet | "No cellists in Leeds. Showing remote cellists — 34 results." |
| Fan arrives on a listing from an SEO category page | Public read-only view: craft, tiers, "from" price, demo reel, credits. No Order CTA under this lens. | Professional listing with a "Hiring for a project?" entry point, not an Order button |
| Seller archives a listing that has historical engagements | Delisted; every engagement retains its listing snapshot as evidence. The snapshot is what a dispute (24) reads years later. | "Archived. Your 12 completed jobs keep their record." |

### States

**Listing lifecycle**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Seller starts a listing | Editable form, "not visible to buyers" banner |
| Blocked | Publish gate failed (missing per-tier posture / required facet / craft) | Inline blockers list naming the specific missing item and tier |
| Published — Orderable | Gate passed + payout onboarded + capacity available | Live listing with Order and Request Quote CTAs |
| Published — Not Orderable | Payout account incomplete | Live and discoverable; "Request Quote" only, Order disabled |
| Published — At Capacity | Ceiling hit for this craft (05.01.07) | Live listing, Order disabled, waitlist CTA, next-available date |
| Published — Stale | ≥1 buyer message unanswered for 14 days (D-08) | Response badge removed, ranked down. Seller: nudge naming the unanswered buyers. |
| Dormant | No seller session for 60 days **and** ≥3 unanswered messages (D-08) | Hidden from browse; direct link shows "not currently taking work". Seller: reactivation prompt on next login. |
| Paused | Seller pauses (voluntary — distinct from At Capacity) | Hidden from browse; existing engagements unaffected; direct link shows "not currently taking work" |
| Suspended | Account action from 24 | Hidden and non-orderable; in-flight engagements continue under 05.03 |
| Archived | Seller archives | Delisted; historical engagements retain their listing snapshot for evidence |

**Surface states** (buyer listing view · seller rate card)

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing or rate card requested | Skeleton of the tier cards / rate rows — never a spinner over a blank page, because the listing is the SEO surface and its shape must render immediately |
| Empty (seller) | No listings yet | Onboarding CTA seeded from the seller's existing credits — "You have 34 mix credits. Sell mixing?" (craft only — DT-08) |
| Empty (buyer) | Facet combination has no listings | Relaxed results naming the dropped facet (05.01.02), plus a "be the first" seller CTA |
| Populated | Listing published and rendered | Tiers, add-ons, "from" price, turnaround, posture in plain language, requirements, demo reel, per-craft reputation |
| Error | Render/publish/bulk-edit request failed | Named failure + retry. A failed bulk rate edit reports **which listings changed and which did not** — a partial rate change reported as a generic error is a seller charging two different prices without knowing it. |
| Partial | Demo media transcoding, or benchmark band unavailable (cohort below k-anonymity threshold — 05.01.04 Q-01) | Listing renders; the missing element is **hidden**, not shown at zero. A band rendered from 2 sellers is worse than no band. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A listing must carry the **requirements the buyer owes the seller** (stem naming, tempo map, reference), not just what the seller delivers | Domain expertise: the single largest cause of a stalled mix engagement is not the mix — it is the client sending 40 unlabelled stems with no tempo map three days late. Every mix engineer's website has a "how to send me your session" page; no marketplace models it. | ✅ CONFIRMED — requirements are declared on the listing and enforced as a gate in 05.03.01. This is why the turnaround clock cannot start at purchase (05.01.06 DT-01). |
| DT-02 | Tiers are enough; a listing does not need arbitrary add-ons | Fiverr/SoundBetter precedent — tiered packages plus extras | ❌ REJECTED — music services have orthogonal, priced add-ons that do not tier cleanly: stem delivery, Atmos mix, alternate/TV mix, instrumental, extra doubles, session file handover, buyout of a sample. Forcing these into tiers produces a 9-tier listing nobody reads. Add-ons must be first-class and separately priced (D-03). |
| DT-03 | One account = one listing per craft | Simplicity assumption | ❌ REJECTED — the multi-hyphenate (`personas.md`, the structural fact) routinely sells the same craft at different rates to different markets: a mixer charges a label rate and an indie rate; a session player charges a scale rate for union dates and a flat rate for demos. Listings must be many-per-craft-per-account, and reputation attaches **per craft** (Reviews cross-cut already requires per-role reputation), not per listing. |
| DT-04 | **The listing's demo reel is an unlicensed reproduction that the platform hosts, and the credit graph is what makes its knowledge of that non-deniable** | Domain expertise + adversarial: what does a music-industry lawyer see on this page that nobody in the file has mentioned? Every mix engineer's portfolio streams commercial masters they were paid to assign away. Nobody clears it; it is the loudest open secret in the services market (SoundBetter, AirGigs, personal sites alike). | ✅ CONFIRMED, and the second half is the sharp part. A generic host can plead ignorance of what it stores. **This platform cannot**: domain 02 tells it the seller played on the track, 05.06.01 tells it the seller elected work-for-hire, and 09 tells it who owns the result. The platform therefore *knows* it is streaming a master its user does not own — and knowledge is exactly what defeats a passive-host safe-harbour posture. The wedge cuts back. Mitigation is behavioural, not legal-only: clips capped at 60s, credit-linked where the graph allows, `unverified` where it does not, and a takedown path (24/DMCA) that a rights-holder can actually use. Whether 60s of a master the seller assigned is defensible at all is Q-05 — counsel, not design. |
| DT-05 | **The most available-looking seller on the platform is the one who has abandoned it** | Adversarial: trace 05.01.07's capacity model to its inverse. Capacity counts *accepted engagements*. What does the model say about a seller who accepts nothing? | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a clean inversion nobody designs for. A seller who quits reads as permanently Available with an empty queue, an instant next-available date, and (until reviews decay) their old rating. Capacity cannot catch this: the signal it reads is zero, and zero is its healthiest value. Directories rot from exactly this — a services marketplace's real failure is not bad listings, it is dead ones that look alive, and every buyer who orders into silence learns the marketplace does not work. The fix cannot be seller-declared (an absent seller declares nothing) so it must be **fact-derived from response rate** (D-08), which is the same "be present when the fact becomes true" principle as 05.01.03 DT-03's measured duration. |
| DT-06 | **The exclusion list is more load-bearing than the inclusion list, and it must be structured, not prose** | Domain expertise: every scope dispute in a mix engagement is about something the buyer assumed was included — "does the mix include tuning?", "does mastering include a stem master?", "is the instrumental extra?". Sellers write inclusions as marketing copy and exclusions not at all. | ✅ CONFIRMED — free-text exclusions are unenforceable downstream. 05.03.03's change-order mechanism needs to know, mechanically, whether a buyer's request is out of scope; it cannot parse "full mix, professional results". Exclusions are therefore drawn from the same vocabulary as add-ons and deliverables (05.01.02's Deliverable axis, 05.04.02) — which yields a free consequence: **"not included" and "available as an add-on" are one list with one flag** (D-07). A buyer asking for an excluded item that has an add-on price gets a priced change order automatically instead of an argument. |
| DT-07 | **The rate card is a bulk-mutation surface whose blast radius is invisible at the moment of mutation** | Adversarial: the file named "Rate Cards" and specified one sentence about them. What does the surface actually *do*, and what does it do wrong? | ✅ CONFIRMED — a seller raising all rates 20% is one action touching live quotes (which must not move — D-01), waitlisted buyers who were told a figure, **contracted retainers (05.03.04) which legally cannot move**, and each listing's benchmark position (05.01.04). Every one of those consequences is invisible in a rate table. The retainer case is the serious one: a rate card that silently repriced a contracted client would breach a contract from a UI with no confirmation step. Bulk edit must therefore compute and show the blast radius **before** apply, not report it after. |
| DT-08 | A listing can be auto-generated from the seller's verified credits | Cold-start reasoning — the platform already knows they have 34 mix credits, so why make them type it? | ❌ REJECTED — a credit is evidence of having done the work. It is not evidence of wanting to sell it, at what price, on what rights terms, or with what capacity. Auto-generation would publish a shopfront the seller never chose, at a price the platform invented, with a rights posture that **D-02 says can never be defaulted** — the platform would have committed the exact harm 05.06.01 DT-01 exists to prevent, while congratulating itself on reducing friction. The credit graph seeds the **suggestion** (the Empty state's CTA) and pre-selects the **craft**; everything with a consequence stays an election. |
| DT-09 | Self-dealing on a listing is a payments concern — wash trading, owned by the Payments cross-cut | Node Classification Gate applied to the self-order case: is this someone else's mechanism? | ❌ REJECTED — the asset being laundered is not money, it is **the credit graph**, which D-18 names as the platform's entire defensibility. A self-order costs the take rate (~15-20% of a number the seller chooses, so ~£2 on a £10 tier) and yields a completed engagement, a 5-star review, an SLA-met badge, and a *verified credit* — the platform's strongest trust signal, manufactured for pocket change. Payments would see a cleared transaction and no fraud. This is `personas.md`'s Musician "worst intentional" behaviour ("games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation") priced at the cost of a coffee, and the block belongs at the order boundary of the listing that makes it possible. Its harder relative — the same human on both sides via an entity — is not solvable here (Q-01), and its hardest relative — reciprocal ordering between two real sellers — must **not** be blocked at all, because it is indistinguishable from the multi-hyphenate's normal working life. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **05.01.02 Service Category Taxonomy** ([path](./05.01.02-service-category-taxonomy-attributes.md)) — *shared entity + permission*. The craft axis is the listing's required first field, and because it is platform-curated (05.01.02 D-01) it is also **this domain's boundary-enforcement mechanism** (D-04). The Operator's restricted craft set transitively restricts what they can price (CX-01 role scoping).
- Touches **05.01.03 Music Pricing Model Library** ([path](./05.01.03-music-pricing-model-library.md)) — *trigger dependency*. The listing declares which model each tier and each add-on prices under; the model defines the inputs a quote must supply. The add-on model restriction `{flat, same-unit-as-tier, percentage-of-subtotal}` (D-03) is a constraint this feature imposes on that library's polymorphism.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A listing is a template; a quote is an offer; an engagement references the accepted quote | Prevents the "seller raised their rate mid-job" class of dispute entirely, and makes the listing freely editable without side effects. |
| D-02 | Rights posture is an explicit election **per tier** at publish time — no default | A work-for-hire default silently strips session players of rights; a co-ownership default leaves buyers unable to clear their own record. Neither is safe, so there is none. Per tier rather than per listing because tiers routinely differ in what they hand over (05.06.01 DT-01). |
| D-03 | **Add-ons are first-class, priced in their own model from `{flat, same-unit-as-tier, percentage-of-subtotal}`; a quote is an ordered evaluation, not a sum** | Resolves Q-02. All three shapes exist in the real market (stems per song; session handover flat; rush +50%), so neither "independent" nor "multiplier" alone is true. The order matters because percentage add-ons must compose on the subtotal, and expenses must sit **outside** the take-rate base (05.01.05 D-02). The model restriction exists because no other model can be evaluated without a conversion the platform would have to invent. |
| D-04 | **The taxonomy is the domain-boundary enforcement mechanism**: a listing prices human craft only. Time and space are domain 16; teaching is domain 06 | Resolves the breadth pass's open boundary question. No policy engine is needed — a craft is required, the craft set is platform-curated (05.01.02 D-01), and no craft exists for room hire or lessons, so the violation cannot be expressed. Residual mis-categorisation under a legitimate craft is a content report to 24, and is self-limiting: this domain's lifecycle has no deliverable, acceptance or rights transfer for a room hire, so it fails at the first milestone rather than succeeding silently. |
| D-05 | The benchmark band renders in the seller's pricing step only — never in the buyer preview or on the public listing | Corrects the breadth draft, which had the preview showing the band. Upholds 05.01.04 D-01 and CX R-04: a band on a public listing is individual rate exposure wearing a cohort's clothes. |
| D-06 | **Rate history is private.** Listing versions are internal; buyers never see a rate timeline | Resolves sub-domain Q-03 on the same ground as D-05 and CX R-04. A public rate timeline is individual rate exposure over time, and it would make every legitimate rate rise look like gouging a returning client — deterring exactly the rate discipline the benchmark feature exists to encourage. Versions remain fully visible in an engagement's snapshot, where a dispute (24) needs them. |
| D-07 | Exclusions are structured and drawn from the add-on/deliverable vocabulary; "not included" and "available as an add-on" are one list with one flag | See DT-06. Free-text exclusions cannot drive 05.03.03's change orders. |
| D-08 | **Listing liveness is fact-derived from response rate, never seller-declared** — Stale at ≥1 message unanswered for 14 days; Dormant at 60 days without a seller session plus ≥3 unanswered messages | See DT-05. An absent seller declares nothing, so a declared signal is structurally unavailable exactly when it is needed. Thresholds are stated concretely so they are testable; whether Dormant may *hide* a shopfront or only rank it down is a product call (Q-06). |
| D-09 | Demo clips are capped at 60 seconds and are either linked to a verified credit (02) or flagged `unverified` | See DT-04. Not blocked when unverified — the credit graph is incomplete at cold start, and absence of a credit is not evidence of a lie. |
| D-10 | Craft is immutable on a live listing — archive and republish | Craft determines the legal model set, facet set, benchmark cohort and reputation cohort. A mutable craft is reputation laundering via a form field. |
| D-11 | Publish is atomic | A half-published listing is indexed and orderable with no rights posture — i.e. the exact state D-02 exists to make impossible, reached by a network failure instead of a choice. |
