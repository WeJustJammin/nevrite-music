# Feature: Music Pricing Model Library

> **Number**: 05.01.03
> **Parent**: [Service Listings & Music Pricing](./05.01-service-listings-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The set of pricing shapes music work is actually sold in — per-song, per-minute of finished audio, day rate, half-day, per-stem, per-track, hourly, retainer, points (a royalty percentage instead of cash), spec, and hybrids of these. This is the single feature that makes a generic freelance marketplace unusable to music professionals: Fiverr and Upwork model "a price", and a mastering engineer's price is not a number, it is a function of programme duration.

The feature owns three things: **which models exist**, **what parameters each model requires before it can be published**, and **how a model renders into a figure when a buyer supplies their job's inputs**. It does not own the listing (05.01.01), the quote (05.02.01), or the rights term a points election creates (05.06.02) — it owns the shape of the number and the arithmetic that produces it.

**Why this is thesis-critical**: the `points` model has no cash value at purchase time. It cannot be escrowed. It only exists as a *rights* term. That one fact links this pricing feature directly to the provenance wedge (D-18) — a points deal is a split sheet wearing a price tag. And because the craft taxonomy decides which crafts may elect points (CX-01), the taxonomy transitively decides which engagements the platform's flagship protection cannot reach.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Chooses the model that matches how they actually charge, and runs several at once across unrelated crafts — a session drummer sells a day rate for tracking dates and a per-song rate for remote overdubs from the same account, because `meta/personas.md` establishes their roles as simultaneous, not sequential. Sets the parameters that make the model survive contact with a real job: the minimum (DT-04), the volume break where an album stops being 11 singles (DT-05), the day's span in hours and what overtime costs (DT-09). As a buyer, does **not** get an abstract cross-model comparison — sees each candidate seller's model rendered against *their own job* ("for your 5-song album: A £2,000, B quotes 4 days = £2,400"), because the abstract conversion is unknowable (DT-11). |
| Producer | Full | The most complex user of this feature. Prices production as fee + points, mixing per-song with a per-stem add-on, mastering per-minute for film/TV and per-track for records. Elects points, which is where their cash price and their long-term royalty participation diverge (05.06.02) — and sees the two rendered as **two ledgers that are never summed** (DT-07), because a hybrid quote showing one total silently prices a master share at zero. The persona most exposed to DT-12: a Producer holding points on a record from one engagement may rationally underprice their mix on another, and the benchmark band (05.01.04) cannot see why. |
| Operator | Config | Restricted to the models their attached services use — hourly and day rate for a backline tech or in-house engineer. Points, per-song, per-minute, spec and retainer-with-creative-drawdown are not offered under this lens; the restriction arrives transitively, because the Operator lens restricts the craft set (05.01.02) and craft constrains models (CX-01). Note: room hire pricing (peak/off-peak, minimum block, overtime) is **not** here — it is domain 16's perishable-inventory pricing, which is a different problem. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees a rendered price on a public listing ("from £250 / song") and nothing else. The model machinery, the parameters, the benchmark band and the rights leg are never exposed — a Fan will not reason about per-minute versus per-track, and `meta/personas.md` states they will not tolerate professional-tool complexity. A Fan who proceeds to buy is acting as a Musician and is onboarded into that lens. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Pricing is **shape-specific**: a listing declares one model, and each model defines the inputs required to render a number. A quote (05.02.01) or a direct order supplies those inputs and produces a cash figure — or, for points and spec, produces zero cash and a rights term.

### The model library

| Model | Renders from | Required parameters | Typical craft | Cash at purchase? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-song / per-track | count of songs | rate/unit · minimum count · volume breaks | mixing, session performance | Yes |
| Per-minute of programme | duration of finished audio | rate/min · **rounding rule** · minimum charge | mastering, film/TV, podcast | Yes |
| Day rate | days booked | rate · **day span in hours** · overtime rate/hr · minimum days | session performance, on-location engineering, tracking | Yes |
| Half-day | half-days booked | rate · **half-day span in hours** · overrun rule (overtime or promote to full day) | as above | Yes |
| Hourly | hours | rate · minimum hours · billing increment | tech/repair labour, editing | Yes |
| Per-stem / per-part | count of stems or parts | rate/unit · minimum count · **definition of a "part"** | session overdubs, editing/tuning | Yes |
| Retainer | period + allowance | period length · allowance count · **allowance unit definition** · expiry rule · overflow rate | ongoing mixing for a label, an artist's regular engineer | Yes (recurring — 05.03.04) |
| **Points** | % of master and/or publishing | percentage · **asset (master / publishing / both)** | production, topline | **No — zero cash; a rights term (05.06.02)** |
| Spec | nothing | **trigger condition** · the term that vests on trigger | speculative writing/production | No — conditional (DT-10) |
| Hybrid | fee leg + rights leg | both legs' full parameter sets | production | Partial — two ledgers (DT-07) |
| Buyout | usage scope | cash figure · **rights scope granted** (territory, media, term) | advertising, sync sessions | Yes — but it is cash for *rights*, not labour (DT-13) |

### Universal parameters

Every model — regardless of shape — declares these before it can be published. A model missing any of them is **not publishable**; the listing cannot go live (D-03, D-04, D-05):

| Parameter | Why it is mandatory |
|---|---|
| **Minimum** | The rate is the headline; the minimum is what makes the business work (DT-04). A per-minute rate with no minimum bills £20 for a 40-second interlude that took the same setup as a 5-minute song. |
| **Currency** | Defaults to the seller's payout currency. This is the **contract currency** — the figure the seller quoted is the figure the seller is owed (D-09). |
| **Tax treatment** | Rendered figures always state inclusive or exclusive. Ambiguity here is a dispute at invoice time and a compliance exposure (Q-05). |
| **Rounding rule** | Declared per model, never defaulted (D-05). Both practices exist in the industry; the platform picking one silently is wrong for half its sellers. |

### The two-ledger rule

Cash and rights are **different ledgers and are never summed into a total** (D-07). A fee + points quote renders `£2,000` and `3 points on the master` as two separate figures. It never renders `£2,000` alone, because that implies the points are free — and it never renders a blended "estimated value", because the value of 3 points is somewhere between £0 and £200,000 and the platform does not know which (05.06.02: most points earn nothing).

### Render vs snapshot

A **render** is a pure computation: model + parameters + inputs → figures. It holds no state, so two buyers rendering the same listing concurrently cannot race each other (the race in this sub-domain belongs to capacity — CX-03, 05.01.07). A **snapshot** freezes the model, its parameters, the inputs and the rendered figures into a quote version at issue time, and is immutable thereafter (05.02.01 D-02). Later listing edits never reach a snapshot (05.01.01 D-01).

### Happy Path

1. Seller selects a craft on their listing (05.01.02); system offers **only the models legal for that craft** (CX-01) — a luthier is never offered points.
2. Seller selects one model; system reveals that model's required parameter set from the table above plus the universal parameters, and **refuses publish until every required parameter has a value** — a per-minute model with no rounding rule and no minimum is not a price, it is a fragment.
3. Seller sets parameters: rate, unit, minimum, rounding, any volume breaks, currency (defaults to their payout currency), tax treatment.
4. System renders a **worked example** against a typical job for that craft ("a 10-track album: 10 × £300 = £3,000 — minimum of 3 tracks met") and shows it beside the benchmark band **rendered in the same model** (05.01.04, CX-02) — a per-song band shown to a per-minute seller is noise.
5. Seller publishes. The listing declares the model (CX-06). Capacity governs whether the listing is *orderable*, never whether the price *renders* (CX-03) — an at-capacity listing still shows its price.
6. Buyer supplies their job's inputs (5 songs, 38 minutes, 2 days) via a direct order (inputs known) or a quote request (inputs uncertain).
7. System renders the figure and **shows the arithmetic in full**, surfacing the minimum and the rounding whenever either changed the result ("38:12 → 39 min × £30 = £1,170 — rounded up to the whole minute").
8. If the model has a rights leg (points, spec, hybrid, buyout), the render produces **two figures on two ledgers and never sums them**: the cash, and the rights term with its asset and percentage stated separately (05.06.02 D-01 — master and publishing are never one field).
9. If union terms are elected (05.02.03), the rendered fee is floored at scale for that session type; a below-scale render is raised to the floor with the reason shown, or issue is blocked.
10. On quote issue, the model, its parameters, the buyer's inputs and the rendered figures are **snapshotted** into the quote version and become immutable, with an expiry (05.02.01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Job inputs unknown at purchase (song count TBC, duration TBC) | Model cannot render → direct order blocked, quote path forced | "This job needs a quote — we can't price it until we know the run time." |
| Buyer's stated duration ≠ delivered programme duration (per-minute) | Measured duration is the fact; reconcile at acceptance (DT-03). Difference above the declared rounding increment raises a change order (05.03.03), not a silent re-price. | "You quoted 38 min; the master runs 41:30. That's +3 min — raise a change order?" |
| Duration measurement unavailable (Media Handling cross-cut down or unsupported format) | Render proceeds from the **stated** duration, explicitly flagged unverified, and reconciles at acceptance. Pricing never hard-fails on a measurement dependency. | "Priced from your stated 38 min — we'll confirm against the delivered master." |
| Track runs 3:20 on a per-minute model | The **declared** rounding rule applies — up to the whole minute (£120) or to the second (£100). Never a platform default. | Arithmetic shows the rule by name: "3:20 → 4 min (rounds up) × £30 = £120" |
| Seller tries to publish a model with no rounding rule / no minimum / no tax treatment | **Not publishable.** The listing stays in draft. | "A per-minute price needs a minimum and a rounding rule before it can go live." |
| Job falls below the minimum (2-track EP against a 3-track minimum) | Charge the minimum; never the pro-rata figure | "3-track minimum — priced at £900 for your 2 tracks." |
| Volume-break boundary inverts (10 singles cost more than the album flat rate) | Render the **cheaper** side. A render never charges more than the next-larger break (D-06). | "Priced at the album rate (£2,400) — cheaper than 10 × £300." |
| Session overruns the declared day span | Overtime rate applies from the declared span; on a half-day, the declared overrun rule applies (overtime or promotion to a full day) | "9h against an 8h day — 1h overtime at £75 = £675." |
| Points-only engagement | Zero cash means **nothing to escrow**. The engagement's protection is the rights instrument, not the money (DT-01). | Prominent: "You are being paid in points, not cash. Your protection here is the signed split — not escrow." |
| Fee + points hybrid | Two ledgers, never summed (DT-07) | "£2,000 cash **and** 3 points on the master." Never "£2,000." |
| Points elected on a craft where points are not legal | Not offered — the model set is filtered at craft selection (CX-01) | Model absent from the picker; no error needed |
| Points granted where the buyer may not own the asset | Election validated against 09's chain of title (05.06.02 D-04, D-05). This feature renders the term; it does not adjudicate authority. | Handled in 05.06.02 — this feature surfaces the outcome, not the check |
| Spec engagement | Zero cash **and** a term that vests only on a future trigger the platform cannot see from this domain (DT-10) | "Nothing is due unless {trigger}. This is speculative work." |
| Buyer wants a model the seller doesn't offer (buyer wants a day rate, seller sells per-song) | Quote path; the seller may render a bespoke model on the quote | Quote flow with a model-mismatch note |
| Seller changes the model on their listing mid-render | The render snapshots model + params at render time; the in-flight render completes against its snapshot | No user-visible change |
| Seller changes the model while quotes derived from it are live | Issued quotes are immutable and honour their snapshot until expiry (05.01.01 D-01) | Seller: "2 open quotes were issued under your previous model." |
| Two buyers order the same listing concurrently | No race in this feature — a render is stateless. The contested resource is the seller's capacity (CX-03), not the price. | Nothing here; capacity handles it |
| Duration/song count grows after acceptance (the album became 12 tracks, not 10) | Change order, not a re-render (05.03.03) | "That's outside the agreed scope. Raise a change order?" |
| Currency mismatch between buyer and seller | Seller's currency is the **contract currency** (D-09). Buyer sees an indicative conversion with an as-of timestamp; the charge firms at capture. The seller's figure never moves. | "£1,500 (≈ $1,905 — indicative, as of 14:20. You'll be charged in GBP.)" |
| FX moves between render and acceptance | Buyer bears it, bounded by the quote's expiry (05.02.01 D-04 makes expiry mandatory). Buyer's indicative figure refreshes on view; the seller's contracted figure is fixed. | Refreshed indicative figure with a new timestamp on each view |
| Union scale floor applies (05.02.03) | Rendered fee is floored at scale; a below-scale render is blocked | "Below AFM scale for this session type. Minimum is {x}." |
| Union scale exceeds the seller's own rate | The floor wins. This is the **only** place in the domain where a seller's own rate is overridden (05.02.03). | "Your rate is £400; AFM scale for a master session is £480. Priced at £480." |
| Seller sets a rate of £0 or a negative rate | Rejected at publish. Negative is nonsense; £0 is indistinguishable from spec and carries the free-plus-rights-transfer exploitation pattern (Q-04). | "A rate must be above zero. Working for free? Use the spec model — it records what you get if it works out." |
| Malicious/absurd buyer input (999,999 minutes; negative or zero count; non-numeric) | Validated at the boundary. Non-positive and non-numeric rejected; values beyond the model's declared plausible ceiling require the quote path rather than rendering an order. | "That's a 694-day programme — this needs a quote." |
| Render produces a sub-minor-unit figure | Renders are never sub-penny. Remainder allocation on multi-party splits is the Payments cross-cut's concern, not this feature's. | Rounded figure with the arithmetic shown |
| Cross-border sale, seller VAT-registered vs not | The rendered figure declares its tax treatment (D-04). The deemed-supplier / marketplace-facilitator determination is **not** this feature's — routed to the Payments cross-cut and Q-05. | "£1,500 excl. VAT" or "£1,500 incl. VAT" — never bare |
| Expenses on an on-location day rate | **Zero take rate**, separate line, receipt-backed, never inside the fee (05.01.05 D-02, CX-07) | "£900 fee + £120 travel (at cost, no platform fee)" |
| Rush premium applied (05.01.06) | A modifier on the **fee leg only** — never on expenses, never on the rights leg. Points cannot be rushed. | "£1,500 + 50% rush = £2,250. Travel unchanged." |
| Retainer allowance unit is ambiguous (a 90-second interlude vs an 8-minute prog epic) | The allowance unit's definition is a **required** retainer parameter — the platform never defines "a mix" on the seller's behalf (05.03.04 Q-03) | "1 mix = up to 6 minutes / 40 stems. Beyond that draws 2." |
| Retainer + points on the same engagement | Two ledgers again: the retainer's recurring cash and the points term. Neither escrows (DT-01, 05.03.04 DT-02). | Both legs shown; both no-escrow warnings shown |
| Multi-hyphenate holds points on the record via another engagement and prices this mix low | The benchmark band flags them as underpriced. It is comparing cash to cash and cannot see the rights leg on the other deal (DT-12). | Band shown as advisory only — never blocking, never "you're wrong" |
| Seller orders their own listing (same human, two lenses) | Blocked. Self-dealing moves money and manufactures credits; the multi-hyphenate makes it trivially reachable rather than exotic. | "You can't order your own service." |
| Benchmark band unavailable (suppressed cold-start, or the Analytics dependency fails) | The render still works. The band is **advisory** and pricing never blocks on it (D-12). | "We don't have enough data to benchmark this yet." — never an invented number (05.01.04 D-04) |
| Craft retired/remapped in the taxonomy, leaving a live listing on a now-illegal model | The listing's snapshot keeps rendering; new orders are gated pending remap. This feature holds the failure open rather than silently repricing. | "This listing's category changed. Confirm your pricing to keep taking orders." (migration policy: 05.01.02 Q-03) |
| Seller has a valid model but no payout account | Not this feature's gate — capture would succeed and payout would fail. Payout-account gating is the Payments cross-cut's (05.01 CX file). | Handled upstream of order, not at render |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Render in flight (a duration measurement or a benchmark call is outstanding) | Skeleton on the figure; the arithmetic frame renders immediately so the shape of the price is visible before the number is |
| Empty | Craft selected, no model chosen yet | The legal model set for that craft with one-line descriptions — never the full library |
| Incomplete (not publishable) | Model chosen, required parameters missing | Named the missing parameter: "Add a minimum and a rounding rule to publish." Listing held in draft. |
| Unrendered | Published, buyer inputs not supplied | "From {floor}" or "Quote required" |
| Rendered | Inputs supplied, model complete | Concrete figure with the arithmetic shown (5 songs × £300 = £1,500), minimum and rounding surfaced if either bit |
| Rendered — two ledgers | Hybrid / buyout elected | Cash figure **and** rights term, side by side, never summed (DT-07) |
| Rendered — zero cash | Points-only or spec elected | £0 + the rights term, with the no-escrow warning |
| Rendered — unverified input | Per-minute priced from a stated (not measured) duration | Figure + "we'll confirm against the delivered master" |
| Floored | Union scale exceeds the rendered fee | Price raised to scale with the reason and the session type named |
| Blocked | Below a statutory or union floor, or an out-of-range input | Floor explanation + the minimum, or the quote path offered |
| Partial | Fee leg renders, rights leg pending (points asset not yet specified) | Cash shown; rights leg shown as an unresolved blocker, never as absent |
| Error | Render dependency failed (measurement, FX rate, benchmark) | Degrade per the edge-case table — stated input, indicative FX, absent band. Pricing never hard-fails; only the confidence label changes. |
| Snapshotted | Quote issued | Immutable, with expiry (05.02.01) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Points cannot be escrowed, because there is no money at purchase time** | Reasoning about the intersection with the Payments/Escrow cross-cut: escrow requires a captured sum and a release trigger. A points deal captures nothing. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and this is structurally important. It means a points engagement's entire protection mechanism is the **rights instrument** (05.06.02 + Contracts & E-Signature cross-cut), not the money. The escrow-release↔rights-transfer leverage the domain rationale calls "the only leverage that exists at the only moment anyone is motivated" **does not exist for points deals** — the platform's strongest mechanism is absent from exactly the deals where exploitation is most common (unknown producer, points-only, no cash). Flagged to `/create-prd-security` and 24. |
| DT-02 | Every model is the same shape — a rate and a unit — so one pricing form fits all | Simplicity assumption: one form for every price | ❌ REJECTED — mirrors the D-14 reasoning that killed the marketplace merge. Per-minute needs a duration the platform must obtain from the audio (Media Handling cross-cut can measure it); day rate needs a calendar (Availability cross-cut); points needs a rights instrument and a royalty registration downstream (09/10); retainer needs a recurring-billing cycle (Subscriptions cross-cut). These are not one form with a unit toggle — they are different pricing shapes, each requiring inputs and triggering downstream flows the others never touch. Each model must stand on its own required-input set. |
| DT-03 | Per-minute pricing can be rendered from the buyer's stated duration | Convenience assumption | ❌ REJECTED — mastering is priced on the *finished programme* duration, which the buyer routinely misstates (and which changes between the quote and the delivery when a track gets extended). The platform can measure the actual duration from the delivered/supplied audio via the Media Handling cross-cut and reconcile at acceptance. Self-reported duration is a dispute generator; measured duration is a fact. This is a small instance of the whole product thesis: be present when the fact becomes true. |
| DT-04 | **The minimum is the product, not the rate** | Domain expertise: what would a music professional expect that nobody has mentioned? Every real rate card in music has a minimum, and none of the marketplace precedents model one. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it inverts the feature's centre of gravity. "£30/minute" is marketing; "£30/minute, £150 minimum" is the business. Setup, recall, listening, admin and the email thread cost the same for a 40-second interlude as for a 5-minute song, so a rate without a floor prices the *audio* and ignores the *job*. Two distinct minimums exist and both are real: a **unit minimum** ("minimum 3 tracks") and an **engagement minimum** ("£150 whatever you send"). A platform that models only the rate produces listings that are profitable on paper and lose money on every small job — and the seller discovers this on their fourth order, blames the platform, and goes back to email. Minimum is therefore a **required** parameter on every model (D-03), not an optional refinement. |
| DT-05 | An album is priced as N × the single-song rate, and any difference is a discount | The obvious arithmetic | ❌ REJECTED — an album is a **different unit**, not a bulk purchase of singles. Every working mixer has an album rate, and it is not `11 × single` minus a percentage: it reflects that the record has one sonic identity, one reference conversation, one revision cycle across the set, and one delivery. Modelling it as a discount forces the seller to either publish an album price that looks like gouging or fake a percentage off a number they never intended to charge. So **volume breaks are first-class within a model** (1–3 at £X, 4–9 at £Y, album flat at £Z). This is *not* a duplicate of 05.01.01's tiered packages: a package tier varies **scope** (Basic/Standard/Pro — what you get), a volume break varies **quantity** at fixed scope. Conflating them yields the same tangle D-14 rejected at domain level — one shape forced to stand in for two unrelated ones — one fractal level down. Consequence recorded as D-06: a render never charges more than the next-larger break, or the platform has built a pricing cliff that punishes the buyer for ordering ten tracks instead of eleven. |
| DT-06 | "Per-minute" is unambiguous | Naive reading of the model name | ❌ REJECTED — it hides a rounding decision that is a live dispute in mastering. A 3:20 track at £30/min is £100 (billed to the second) or £120 (rounded up to the whole minute), and **both practices are current industry standard** depending on the engineer and the market. There is no defensible platform default: pick "to the second" and every long-form engineer is underpriced by ~8% on every job; pick "round up" and every records engineer looks like they are padding. The rule must be a **declared parameter** (D-05) and must be named in the rendered arithmetic, not buried — a buyer who sees "£120" without seeing "3:20 → 4 min (rounds up)" experiences it as an error. |
| DT-07 | A fee + points quote can render a single total | UI-simplicity assumption — one number is easier to compare | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most consequential rejection in the feature. Cash and rights are **different ledgers with different units, different time horizons and different payors** (05.06.02: master points pay from the label via domain 10; a fee pays from escrow today). Summing them requires valuing the points, and the honest valuation of 3 points is "somewhere between £0 and £200,000, and most likely £0". A quote rendering "£2,000" for a fee+points deal has silently priced a permanent master share at zero and told the buyer they are buying a service — when they are buying a service **and** giving away a piece of their record forever, which `meta/personas.md` names as a different category of act from paying an invoice. So: **two figures, two ledgers, never summed** (D-07). The cost is a busier UI. The alternative is the platform performing, in its own happy path, the exact undisclosed-rights-grant it exists to prevent. |
| DT-08 | The platform should take its ~15-20% services fee in points on points-only deals, since there is no cash to take it from | Following the revenue model to its edge — what happens to the take rate when the deal has no cash? | ❌ REJECTED as a mechanism, ✅ CONFIRMED as a finding. Taking points as a fee would make WeJammin a **rights holder in every record it touched** — a fundamentally different company, with a place in the chain of title (09), a royalty registration (10), a conflict of interest in every dispute it adjudicates (24), and a regulatory profile nothing else in the platform carries. That is not a pricing decision; it is a change of business. So the platform takes **no fee on a points-only deal** (D-08). Two consequences follow and neither is comfortable. First: points-only deals generate **no escrow and no revenue** — the least-protected deal shape is also the least-monetised, so the platform has no commercial incentive to invest in protecting exactly the population (new producers, topline writers, the kid who made the beat) that DT-01 identifies as most exploited. Second: it is a **take-rate-avoidance vector** — a colluding pair can record a nominal points-only deal on-platform and settle cash off it. The vector is partly self-limiting, because a recorded points grant is a *real* grant that executes into 09/10 (05.06.02 D-02) and costs the buyer real equity, so the dodge is not free. But "partly self-limiting" is not a control. Surfaced to `/create-prd-security` and to the owner as Q-06 rather than solved here — the answer is a revenue-model decision, not a design one. |
| DT-09 | "A day" needs no definition — everyone knows what a day rate is | The assumption that makes the model look simple | ❌ REJECTED — "what is a day?" is the most common on-location dispute in session work, and it is entirely preventable. A day is 8 hours or 10; an AFM session is 3; a film scoring day is its own thing; overtime starts somewhere and nobody wrote down where. `meta/personas.md` names the Musician's switching trigger as "losing money to a fact they couldn't prove" — an unpaid third hour of overtime after a 10-hour tracking day is that trigger, verbatim, and it happens because the day rate was a number with no span. So a day/half-day model **must** declare a span in hours and an overtime rate (D-10), and a half-day must declare its overrun rule (overtime, or promotion to a full day — both are real practice). This also feeds 05.02.03 DT-02: overtime derives from the session's actual length, which the platform can know, rather than from a player's claim after a long day — which is precisely the situation in which players do not claim. |
| DT-10 | Spec is just points with a 0% floor — the same shape, cheaper | Model-consolidation instinct: fewer models is better | ❌ REJECTED — spec is **conditional**, and the condition is the whole model. A points deal vests at acceptance: the participation executes into 09/10 and exists whether or not the record ever earns (05.06.02 D-02). A spec deal vests on a **trigger event that may never occur** — the artist cuts the song, the label picks it up, the sync lands. That difference is not a parameter, it is a different lifecycle: spec needs a trigger condition, a term that vests on trigger, and a way to detect the trigger. And the detection is the problem: **"the song was cut" is an event in domain 12 (Release), which this domain cannot see.** A spec deal is therefore the only pricing model whose payoff depends on an event in another domain, arriving possibly years after this engagement terminated. That is structurally the same gap 05.02.03 DT-03 found for union new-use obligations, reached from a completely different direction — which is a strong signal that "an obligation that outlives the engagement" is a domain-level shape, not two coincidences. Recorded as Q-07 and flagged to 12; not invented here. |
| DT-11 | The platform can normalise across models so buyers compare like-for-like — convert everything to an hourly equivalent | The existing Role Lens claim ("comparing a £400/song mixer to a £600/day mixer is the arithmetic the platform must do for them"), tested rather than assumed | ❌ REJECTED **as normalisation**, ✅ CONFIRMED **as job-rendering** — and the distinction is the difference between a useful feature and a fabricated number. To convert £400/song into a day-equivalent you need to know how many days *this seller* takes on *this kind of song* — which is their efficiency, is unknown to the platform, varies by job by a factor of three, and is exactly the thing a seller would game if it became a ranking input. A normalised figure would be invented, would be trusted because the platform said it (the same failure mode 05.01.04 D-04 guards against for benchmark bands), and would be wrong in the seller's favour or the buyer's depending on an assumption neither party agreed to. What the platform **can** do is render each candidate's model against **the buyer's actual job**: "for your 5-song album — A: 5 × £400 = £2,000; B: quotes 4 days × £600 = £2,400". That is not a conversion, it is two renders against one job spec, and every number in it is the seller's own. Where a model cannot render against the job (B sells day rates and does not know how many days until they hear it), the honest output is "quote required" — not an estimate. The Role Lens is corrected accordingly. |
| DT-12 | The benchmark band is valid for every seller in a cohort | Implicit assumption in 05.01.04's cohort model | ❌ REJECTED for the multi-hyphenate holding a rights position — a real and growing case, not an edge. A Producer who holds 3 points on a record (from engagement A) may rationally price their mix on that same record (engagement B) at half their normal rate, because their compensation is split across two deals and two ledgers. The band, which sources from accepted engagements and compares **cash to cash** (05.01.04 D-02), sees only engagement B and reports them at the 20th percentile with "most sellers with your credits charge 2–3× this." The advice is wrong, it is wrong specifically for the sophisticated seller, and — worse — it pollutes the cohort: their £180 becomes a data point that drags the band down for every seller who *isn't* holding points. This is the `meta/personas.md` structural fact biting the pricing data: one human, several simultaneous roles, one record, two engagements. The mitigation available here is cheap and partial: the band is **advisory and never blocking** (D-12), and the render never tells a seller they are wrong. The real fix — excluding or flagging rights-bearing engagements from the cash cohort — is 05.01.04's, and is recorded as a cross-cut note rather than decided in this file. |
| DT-13 | All cash is a fee, so all cash lines can carry the take rate and be modelled the same way | Follows from the model library being a *pricing* library | ❌ REJECTED — **not all cash is paid for labour.** A buyout (standard in advertising and sync sessions) is a one-off payment covering all future usage in lieu of residuals: it is cash, but it purchases a **rights grant** — territory, media, term — not hours. Modelling it as a fee line loses the rights consequence entirely, and the platform records a session fee at the exact moment a perpetual worldwide grant occurred. That is the reconstruction failure the whole product exists to prevent (D-18), manufactured by the pricing feature. So a buyout is a distinct model whose parameters include the **rights scope granted**, and it must be paired with the rights posture in 05.06.01 rather than living as an unexplained number on a fee. This also generalises the two-ledger rule (DT-07): the distinction is not cash-vs-no-cash, it is **labour-vs-rights**. Points and spec are rights with no cash; a buyout is rights with cash; a day rate is labour with cash. The rights leg is what needs the instrument, regardless of whether money moved. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **05.01.01 Listings** ([path](./05.01.01-service-listings-packages-rate-cards.md)) — *shared entity*: a listing declares its model; the listing is the template a quote instantiates and snapshots (05.01.01 D-01). Volume breaks (DT-05) live inside a model here; scope tiers live on the listing there — the boundary must not blur.
- Touches **05.01.02 Taxonomy** ([path](./05.01.02-service-category-taxonomy-attributes.md)) — *permission / trigger dependency*: craft constrains which models are legal (CX-01), which transitively decides which engagements can elect points, which decides which engagements the escrow leverage cannot reach (DT-01). A taxonomy edit is therefore a change to the platform's protection surface. Retired-craft migration is 05.01.02 Q-03.
- Touches **05.01.04 Benchmarking** ([path](./05.01.04-rate-benchmarking-price-transparency.md)) — *trigger dependency, and a data-quality hazard*: the band must render in the same model the seller is pricing in (CX-02), and rendering must never block on it (D-12). DT-12 identifies a pollution vector the band's cohort model does not currently handle — rights-bearing engagements entering a cash cohort.
- Touches **05.01.05 Service Mode** ([path](./05.01.05-remote-vs-on-location-service-mode.md)) — *shared entity*: on-location forces day/half-day models and introduces **zero-rated expense lines** (CX-07, 05.01.05 D-02). Expenses are outside the fee, outside the take rate, and outside any rush modifier.
- Touches **05.01.06 Turnaround & Rush** ([path](./05.01.06-turnaround-rush-service-levels.md)) — *trigger dependency*: a rush premium is a modifier on the **fee leg only**. It never applies to expenses (05.01.05 D-02) or to a rights leg — points cannot be rushed. An unmet rush premium auto-refunds (05.01.06 D-04), which means the fee leg must remain decomposable after render, not collapsed into a single figure.
- Touches **05.01.07 Capacity** ([path](./05.01.07-seller-capacity-queue-intake-limits.md)) — *state race, deliberately absent here*: capacity governs orderability, not price rendering (CX-03). A render is stateless, so concurrent renders do not race; the race is capacity's alone.
- Touches **05.02.01 Quotes** ([path](../05.02-quotes-scope-contracting/05.02.01-custom-quotes-proposals-scope.md)) — *shared entity*: a quote is a rendered, snapshotted model. Immutability and mandatory expiry (05.02.01 D-02, D-04) are what bound this feature's FX exposure (D-09).
- Touches **05.02.03 Union & Session Contracting** ([path](../05.02-quotes-scope-contracting/05.02.03-union-session-contracting.md)) — *trigger dependency*: scale is a floor on the rendered fee, and the **only** place a seller's own rate is overridden. Overtime derives from the day span this feature requires (DT-09) — 05.02.03 DT-02 cannot compute overtime against a day rate that never declared its length.
- Touches **05.03.03 Revisions & Change Orders** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.03-revisions-limits-change-orders.md)) — *trigger dependency*: input drift after acceptance (10 tracks → 12; 38 min → 41:30) is a change order, never a silent re-render.
- Touches **05.03.04 Retainers** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.04-retainers-recurring-engagements.md)) — *shared entity*: the retainer model's engine is the Subscriptions cross-cut, but the **allowance unit definition** is a required model parameter here (05.03.04 Q-03 — "is a mix a mix?"). Retainers are the second no-escrow shape (05.03.04 DT-02), sharing DT-01's hole via a different cause.
- Touches **05.06.01 Rights Posture** ([path](../05.06-rights-warranties-transfer/05.06.01-engagement-rights-posture.md)) — *shared entity*: a buyout's rights scope (DT-13) and a points election are the same deal seen as a posture. - Touches **05.06.02 Producer Points** ([path](../05.06-rights-warranties-transfer/05.06.02-producer-points-backend-participation.md)) — *shared entity*: points is a pricing model whose payload is a rights term. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Each pricing model is its own shape with its own required inputs — not one rate-and-unit form for all | Each model needs different inputs and triggers different downstream flows (a calendar, a rights registration, a recurring-billing cycle). See DT-02. |
| D-02 | Craft constrains which models are legal | Prevents nonsense listings (points for a guitar setup) and keeps the seller's form short. Transitively decides the escrow-reachable set — see CX-01, DT-01. |
| D-03 | Every model declares a **minimum**; a model without one is not publishable | The rate is marketing; the minimum is the business. See DT-04. Both a unit minimum and an engagement minimum are supported because both are real. |
| D-04 | Every cash model declares **currency and tax treatment**; every rendered figure states its tax treatment explicitly | A bare number is a dispute at invoice time and a compliance exposure. The deemed-supplier determination is not ours — see Q-05. |
| D-05 | **Rounding rules are declared per model, never defaulted by the platform**, and are named in the rendered arithmetic | Both practices are current industry standard; a silent default is wrong for half the sellers and looks like padding to their buyers. See DT-06. |
| D-06 | **Volume breaks are first-class within a model**, distinct from 05.01.01's scope tiers; a render never charges more than the next-larger break | An album is a different unit, not a discount. See DT-05. The no-cliff rule prevents punishing a buyer for ordering ten tracks instead of eleven. |
| D-07 | **Cash and rights are two ledgers and are never summed into a total** | Summing requires valuing points, which the platform cannot honestly do. A single total prices a permanent master share at zero. See DT-07. |
| D-08 | **The platform never takes its fee in points** | Taking points makes WeJammin a rights holder in every record it touches — a different company with a conflict of interest in every dispute it adjudicates. See DT-08. Revenue consequence escalated as Q-06. |
| D-09 | The **seller's currency is the contract currency**; buyer-side figures are indicative with an as-of timestamp until capture | The figure the seller quoted is the figure the seller is owed. FX exposure between render and capture sits with the buyer and is bounded by the mandatory quote expiry (05.02.01 D-04). |
| D-10 | Day and half-day models declare a **span in hours and an overtime rate**; half-days declare an overrun rule | "What is a day?" is the most common on-location dispute and it is entirely preventable. See DT-09. Also the input 05.02.03's overtime calculation needs. |
| D-11 | The platform **does not normalise across models**; it renders competing models against the buyer's actual job, or says "quote required" | The conversion factor is the seller's efficiency — unknowable, variable, and gameable. See DT-11. |
| D-12 | Rendering **never blocks on the benchmark band**; the band is advisory and never tells a seller they are wrong | The band cannot see a seller's rights position on another engagement (DT-12), and an absent band must not stop a sale. Consistent with 05.01.04 D-04 (never fabricate). |
