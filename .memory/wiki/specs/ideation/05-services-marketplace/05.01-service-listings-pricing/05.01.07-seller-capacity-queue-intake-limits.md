# Feature: Seller Capacity, Queue & Intake Limits

> **Number**: 05.01.07
> **Parent**: [Service Listings & Music Pricing](./05.01-service-listings-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — **not in the sweep candidate list**

## Overview

How much work a seller can have in flight at once, and what the marketplace does when they are full. Asynchronous creative work has no calendar — a mix engineer does not sell Tuesday 2pm, they sell "three mixes in flight, roughly a two-week backlog". Nothing in the candidate list or the 32 cross-cuts expresses that scarcity, and without it the platform's own success is the failure mode: a well-reviewed engineer gets ranked up, order-bombed, and breaches five SLAs in a week.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets a concurrency ceiling per craft ("max 3 mixes in flight") and sees their queue: what is in flight, what is waiting on the buyer, what their realistic next-available date is. As a buyer, sees an honest backlog before ordering instead of discovering it afterwards. Critically for the multi-hyphenate: the ceiling is **per human, not per listing** — a drummer selling session work, mixing and repair from one account has one finite week. |
| Producer | Full | The persona this protects most. A Producer's throughput ceiling is real and low (a mix is days of work), and their `Primary Pain Point` is already administrative overload. Sets ceilings, and uses the queue as the closest thing this domain has to a work dashboard. |
| Operator | None | Not visible under this lens. An Operator's scarcity is perishable calendar time on a room, which is the Availability cross-cut's model in domain 16 — a fundamentally different problem (see DT-01). |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Capacity is expressed as **concurrency**, not slots: N engagements in an active state at once, per craft, per seller (the human, not the listing). When the ceiling is reached, listings remain visible — delisting a busy seller destroys their discovery and their SEO — but become non-orderable, showing a next-available estimate and a waitlist.

The queue is the seller's view of the same state, and it is where "waiting on the buyer" becomes visible. This matters because a paused engagement (05.01.06) still occupies a concurrency slot even though no work is happening — which is a real and slightly perverse consequence worth surfacing rather than hiding.

### Happy Path

1. Seller sets a concurrency ceiling per craft during listing setup
2. Buyers order; each accepted engagement consumes a slot
3. On reaching the ceiling, listings switch to At Capacity — visible, not orderable, next-available shown
4. An engagement reaching a terminal state (accepted / cancelled) frees a slot
5. Waitlisted buyers are notified in order; the first to convert takes the slot

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller sells remote *and* on-location (05.01.05) | Two scarcity models against one human: a queue and a calendar. They must reconcile or the seller is double-committed. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — reconciliation model undecided; see Q-01. This is the feature's hardest unknown and it is not invented here. |
| Engagement paused awaiting buyer feedback | Still consumes a slot — the seller cannot start a fourth mix because a third might come back any moment | "1 of your 3 slots is held by a job waiting on the buyer since {date}." + nudge-buyer action |
| Rush order (05.01.06) | Consumes disproportionate capacity | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether a rush costs >1 slot, or blocks the ceiling, undecided |
| Seller never sets a ceiling | Needs a defensible default — an uncapped default recreates the exact failure the feature prevents | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02 |
| Waitlisted buyer converts, slot already taken | Race on a scarce resource — the Availability cross-cut's reservation half owns atomic decrement | "That slot went. You're next — we'll tell you." |
| Seller is a Band/Studio/Agency entity, not a person | Capacity is a property of humans; an agency with 6 engineers has 6× a person's ceiling | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on 05.01 Q-02 and 05.05.03 |
| Ranking interacts with capacity | Ranking a full seller highly wastes the impression; ranking them down for being busy punishes success | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — routed to Search & Discovery cross-cut as a ranking input |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available | Below ceiling | Listings orderable |
| At Capacity | Ceiling reached | Listings visible, order disabled, next-available date, waitlist CTA |
| Overcommitted | Ceiling exceeded (via change orders, subcontract-backs, or manual accept) | Seller-facing warning; SLA risk flagged against each in-flight job |
| Paused (seller) | Seller pauses intake | "Not taking work until {date}" — distinct from At Capacity, which is involuntary |
| Empty | No engagements | Queue empty state |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The Availability, Scheduling & Reservation cross-cut already covers this — a seller's capacity is just their calendar** | Node Classification Gate, applied honestly: this node's whole justification depends on the cross-cut *not* covering it, so the objection had to be tested rather than assumed away. | ❌ REJECTED — and the reason is precise. That cross-cut models **perishable time**: a slot exists at a moment, and an unsold Tuesday is gone forever (this is `meta/personas.md`'s stated Operator constraint, and the cross-cut was clearly shaped around it). Asynchronous creative work is not perishable and not slotted: a mix does not happen at 2pm, it happens *over* a fortnight, its duration is uncertain, it can pause for a week waiting on the buyer, and the constraint is **how many can be in flight**, not when. A calendar cannot express "3 mixes at once, each of unknown length, one of which is stalled". Two genuinely different scarcity models. The seam: on-location engagements (05.01.05) *do* consume the cross-cut's calendar — which is why a seller doing both has two scarcity models against one body, and why that is this feature's hardest open question (Q-01) rather than something invented here. |
| DT-02 | **The platform's own success is this feature's absence made visible** | Adversarial: what breaks that nobody mentioned? Trace the Search & Discovery + Reviews cross-cuts through to their consequence. | ✅ CONFIRMED — reputation ranking creates a positive feedback loop into a fixed human ceiling. A well-reviewed engineer ranks up, receives more orders, breaches turnaround (05.01.06), gets worse reviews, and the platform has manufactured the failure by rewarding them. Every services marketplace hits this; the ones without a capacity model (early SoundBetter) leak their best sellers off-platform, because the seller's rational move once oversubscribed is to take work privately where they control intake. That is `meta/personas.md`'s Musician "worst intentional" behaviour — and here the platform would have caused it. Capacity is therefore not a nicety; it is retention. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Capacity is concurrency (N in flight), not calendar slots | Async creative work is not perishable time. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | The ceiling is per human per craft, not per listing | The multi-hyphenate has one finite week regardless of how many listings they publish. `meta/personas.md`: "their roles are simultaneous, not sequential." |
| D-03 | At-capacity listings stay visible and non-orderable | Delisting destroys the seller's discovery and SEO to solve a temporary state. |
