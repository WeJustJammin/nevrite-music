## Overview

The seller's shopfront: what a music professional offers, how it is described so a buyer can find and compare it, and — the hard part — how it is priced. Music does not price like freelance labour: a mastering engineer charges per minute of audio, a mix engineer per song, a session player a day rate or per-stem, a producer may take points instead of cash. This sub-domain owns the listing artifact, the faceted service taxonomy, the polymorphic pricing model library, market rate benchmarks, service mode (remote vs on-location), turnaround/rush terms, and the seller's throughput ceiling.

**Why a sub-domain, not a feature**: the capabilities interact hard. The taxonomy determines which pricing models are even legal for a category (points make no sense for guitar tech work). The pricing model determines what a quote can render. Turnaround is a priced attribute whose clock is started elsewhere. Service mode changes the price shape (travel, per-diem, buyout) and pulls in a room. Capacity gates whether the listing is orderable at all.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Listings and pricing are one sub-domain, not two | The pricing model is not an attribute of a listing, it is the listing's shape — a per-minute mastering listing and a points-based production listing are different artifacts with different quote-render paths. Splitting them would force every pricing change to cross a boundary. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | Service Category Taxonomy is a feature here, not routed to the Canonical Data cross-cut | The cross-cut owns entity resolution and shared reference data (genre, instrument). The *service* taxonomy — what crafts exist and what facets describe them — is product content specific to this domain, and precedent exists (domain 02 retains its own Credit Role & Instrument Taxonomy). The genre and instrument facets are *consumed from* the cross-cut, not forked. | Agent, Node Classification Gate |
| D-03 | Seller Capacity, Queue & Intake Limits added — not in the sweep candidate list | Deep Think addition. The Availability cross-cut models perishable calendar time (a room, a slot). Asynchronous creative work has no calendar — it has queue depth ("3 mixes in flight, 2-week backlog"). Nothing in the candidate list or the cross-cut set expresses this, and without it a well-reviewed engineer is order-bombed into SLA failure on day one. | Deep Think, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
