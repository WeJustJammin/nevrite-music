## The Sub-Domain's Shape

Everything here converges on one event. 05.06.01 declares what will happen, 05.06.02 declares what the seller keeps, 05.06.04 and 05.06.05 declare what is true about the thing being handed over, and 05.06.03 makes all of it happen at once, welded to the money. That convergence is why this is a sub-domain: five features, one moment.

Three of the five independently reached the same principle — **the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them** (05.04.02 DT-01, 05.06.04 DT-02, 05.06.05 DT-03). Three arrivals from three directions makes it a domain-level principle rather than three local rules, and it should be lifted into the domain CX.

## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01 / CX-03: Posture and Points ↔ Transfer

**Relationship**: The posture and the points election are *declarations*; the transfer is *execution*. Between them sits the acceptance event, and the whole domain's differentiator lives in that gap being zero — the declaration does not get filed, remembered, or acted on later; it executes as a byproduct of the money moving.

The failure this prevents is the industry's default state. Today a producer and an artist agree points in a conversation, the producer delivers, the artist pays, and the points exist only as a shared memory that diverges over the following three years. The domain rationale calls the escrow release "the only leverage that exists at the only moment anyone is motivated" — this is that leverage spending itself.

But CX-03 carries a caveat the features state and this file must not soften: **points-only deals have no escrow** (05.06.02 DT-01, 05.01.03 DT-01). The leverage is absent from exactly the deals where the seller is least experienced and least represented. What executes there is the instrument alone, which is weaker — better than a verbal promise, worse than the mechanism the domain advertises. Sub-domain Q-01.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees what they gave up and what they kept, at the moment they are paid — which is the first time in their career it will have been written down while everyone was still friendly.
- **Producer**: the party who elects, and therefore the party who could abuse it. `meta/personas.md` names their worst intentional behaviour as pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention — the counter is that the *contributor* sees the consequence in plain language, not that the Producer is trusted.
- **Operator / Fan**: none. Room hire creates no copyright.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The posture is frozen in the accepted quote (05.02.01 D-01). Neither the listing's current posture nor a later conversation may alter what executes. Single source, immutable, upstream.
2. **Trigger chain**: Acceptance → compose from frozen data → atomic three-leg commit. If any leg fails, all roll back (05.06.03 D-01). The points leg additionally depends on domain 09's aggregate allocation being available at execution time — a dependency this domain cannot satisfy internally (05.06.02 DT-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is CX-07's problem — the *buyer* must have the authority to grant points, and the platform can check it against 09 in a way nobody else can. Whether it should **block** an apparently unauthorised grant is Q-02, because the buyer may hold rights the platform cannot see.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Execution notifies both parties with all three outcomes named in plain language. It must also reach 09 and 10, which are the systems that will still be acting on this in a decade.
5. **State transition conflict**: A points election racing an aggregate-allocation change on the same recording from a *different* engagement — two producers accepting simultaneously, each individually within budget, jointly over 100%. Only 09 can see this. Recorded as 05.06.02 Q-03.

### CX-04 / CX-05: Warranties ↔ Transfer

**Relationship**: The warranties are what make the transferred thing worth having. A rights transfer moves ownership; it does not move *cleanliness*. A buyer can hold a flawless assignment of a master containing an uncleared sample and a bass part nobody played — perfect title to a liability with a false credit attached.

So the warranties are conditions of the transaction, not annotations on it, and the two behave differently at the moment of execution:

- **05.06.04 (clearance)** does not change what executes. It changes what the buyer *relies on* afterwards, and it survives acceptance precisely so it can be relied on. Its value is entirely post-hoc and entirely evidential.
- **05.06.05 (AI)** *does* change what executes: a generated part emits no performance credit. That makes it a live input to the transaction rather than a record attached to it — which is why it sits here and not in a metadata feature.

That asymmetry is worth holding onto: one warranty governs liability, the other governs the record.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: as a seller, both declarations are moments where honesty costs them a sale and dishonesty costs them their record. As a buyer, both are the first reliable answers they have ever had to questions they could previously only ask.
- **Producer**: the aggregator, and therefore the accumulator of everyone else's declarations. Their master is only as clean as the worst stem in it (05.05 composition).
- **Operator / Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both warranties attach to the delivery, and the delivery may be replaced by a recall (05.03.06). Does a recall re-open the warranty? It should — the redelivered artifact is what the buyer holds — and no feature currently says so. Gap worth carrying to Step 5.
2. **Trigger chain**: Delivery → declarations required → publish → acceptance → transfer subject to warranties → archived permanently. The AI declaration additionally forks the credit leg.
3. **Permission intersection**: None. Both are the seller's statements about their own work.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The buyer sees both declarations **before** accepting — after is worthless, because acceptance is what they would have withheld.
5. **State transition conflict**: A warranty found false after the transfer executed. The transfer stands (05.06.03 Q-03 asks whether it *ever* reverses); the remedies diverge sharply — the clearance breach is a liability claim (24 + 09), while the AI breach requires a **credit retraction** (02), which is a different act against a different system and one nothing else in this domain does.

### CX-06: Clearance Warranty ↔ AI Disclosure

**Relationship**: Two warranties, one surface, one posture. Both are made at delivery, both are specific-timestamped-attributable, both refuse detection in favour of declaration, and both survive acceptance. They differ only in subject — *whose material is in this?* versus *whose hands made this?* — and in consequence (liability vs the record).

Their shared refusal of detection is the more interesting fact. 05.06.04 DT-02 rejects sample detection because it would produce confident false negatives and an implicit clearance. 05.06.05 DT-03 rejects AI detection because it would produce career-level false accusations and a badge the platform cannot stand behind. 05.04.02 DT-01 rejects quality assessment for the same family of reason. Three independent arrivals at **"the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them"** — which is now a domain principle and is lifted into the domain CX file.

The platform's actual verification mechanism is social, not technical: domain 02's counter-attestation. The people who were in the room are much harder to fool than a classifier, and that is a genuine structural advantage rather than a consolation prize.

**Role scoping**: Musician and Producer, Full on both. Operator and Fan, none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One declaration surface, two independent claims. Neither gates the other.
2. **Trigger chain**: Both required before delivery publishes. Neither may be deferred to acceptance — the buyer must see them while they still hold the decision.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both render with the delivery.
5. **State transition conflict**: None between them.

It belongs to domain 09's ownership model, not to a pricing election in a marketplace. Recorded as 05.06.05 Q-01. |

A points-holder's participation is unaffected by whether the recording contains a sample — the contamination affects the recording's exploitability, which reaches them through domain 10 as reduced earnings, not through this domain as a term. |
