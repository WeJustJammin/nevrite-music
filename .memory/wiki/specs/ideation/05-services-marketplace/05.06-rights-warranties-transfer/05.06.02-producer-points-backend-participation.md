# Feature: Producer Points & Backend Participation

> **Number**: 05.06.02
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Being paid in a share of what the record earns rather than (or as well as) cash: producer points on the master, a publishing share on the composition, a backend participation that pays out for decades if the record works and never if it does not. Points are how production deals are actually structured above the bottom of the market, and how a producer with no cash and a good ear gets a shot.

They are also the least documented number in music. A new producer asked to take "a few points" has no way to know whether that is generous or predatory, and the person offering knows exactly.

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |

As a topline writer or session player being offered points instead of a fee, sees the offer in context: what points are typical at this level (05.01.04), what this specific deal means, and — bluntly — that they will probably earn nothing. |

Their `Anti-Persona Behavior` — "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" — is *this feature* abused, and it is the abuse the platform is best positioned to prevent, because it holds every party and every signature. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. Time and space earn no points. A studio that took points on records made in it would be making a rights claim, which is a domain 16 decision and a very different business. |

## Behavior

Points are a **rights term with a price tag**, and their defining property is that at the moment of purchase there is **no money** (05.01.03 DT-01). Nothing to capture, nothing to escrow, nothing to release. The engagement's entire protection is the instrument.

Two distinct things routinely called "points":

| Thing | On what | Paid by | Reaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Producer points** | The **master** recording | The label / master owner | Domain 10 (royalties) |
| **Publishing share** | The **composition** | Publishing income (PROs, mechanical) | Domains 09/10 |

They are different assets with different registries and different payors, and a producer may hold both from one engagement. Conflating them is a classic error and a very expensive one.

Points elected here must materialise as a real participation in domains 09/10 at acceptance — otherwise the platform has recorded a promise, which is exactly the thing the industry already has too many of.

### Happy Path

1. Seller elects points (points-only, or fee + points) as the pricing model on their listing (05.01.03)
2. System requires the posture to match (05.06.01) — points and posture are one deal
3. Seller specifies **what** the points are on: master, publishing, or both, with percentages
4. Buyer sees the benchmark band for points at this credit level (05.01.04) — the number nobody has ever been able to look up
5. Both accept; the instrument is signed (Contracts cross-cut)
6. At acceptance, the participation **executes into domains 09/10** — registered, not promised (05.06.03)
7. Royalties flow from 10 for as long as the recording earns, with no further action from this domain

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Points-only engagement | No cash → no escrow → the domain's core leverage is absent | Prominent: "You're being paid in points, not cash. Your protection here is the signed split — not escrow." |
| Points elected but the record is never released | The overwhelmingly common outcome. The seller worked for nothing. | Should be said before signing, plainly. Most points are worth zero. |
| Points on the master vs publishing confused | Different assets, different payors, different registries | Must be distinguished at election, never a single "points" field |
| Points granted by someone who does not own the record | An artist signed to a label may not have the master to give | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-03 |
| Points sum above 100% across engagements | Four producers with 5 points each plus a label's share | Must be checked against the *record*, not the engagement — see DT-02 |
| Buyer wants to buy the points out later | Normal and desirable | Not this domain — a catalog transaction (23/09) |
| Sealed/ghost engagement (05.02.02) with points | A royalty payee is visible in the money trail even when the credit is hidden | 05.02.02 Q-01 — anonymity leaks through 10 |
| Engagement cancelled after points elected | No escrow to split; the argument is over an instrument | 05.03.05 edge case, unresolved |
| Points on a recording later re-recorded | The 2020s' favourite manoeuvre | `[PENDING]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Points-only deals are where exploitation is most common, and they are exactly where the platform's escrow leverage does not exist** | Adversarial: trace 05.01.03 DT-01 through to who it affects. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the uncomfortable core of this feature. The domain rationale's differentiator is "binding rights transfer and split execution to escrow release — the only leverage that exists at the only moment anyone is motivated". A points-only deal has **no escrow**, so that leverage is simply absent. And the deals with no cash are systematically the deals with the least experienced, least represented sellers: the new producer taking points because they cannot command a fee, the topline writer paid in backend, the kid who made the beat. The platform's flagship protection is missing from precisely the population that needs it most — and it is missing structurally, not by oversight. What *can* substitute is the instrument: a signed, timestamped, counter-attested rights term executed at acceptance into 09/10 is still worth vastly more than the current alternative (a verbal "I'll look after you" that `meta/personas.md` names as the Musician's switching trigger). So the honest framing is: for points deals the **contract is the product**, not the escrow. That is a real answer, but it is a weaker one, and it should be surfaced to the owner (sub-domain Q-01) rather than glossed. |
| DT-02 | **Points must be validated against the recording, not the engagement — and this domain cannot see the recording** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with 09? | ✅ CONFIRMED. Points are a percentage of a finite thing. Four producers at 5 points each, a mixer at 1, and a label's share can quietly exceed what exists — and each engagement, viewed alone, looks entirely reasonable. The over-allocation is invisible at the point of election because **this domain sees one deal at a time**; only the recording's aggregate view (domain 09's chain of title) can see the sum. That means this feature has a hard dependency it cannot satisfy internally: election must validate against 09's current allocation for that recording, and if the recording does not exist yet — which it usually does not, since the engagement is what creates it — the validation has to happen at execution time instead. This is a genuine architectural constraint, not a nice-to-have: without it the platform will cheerfully record 115% of a master across five engagements it individually approved, which is the reconstruction nightmare it exists to prevent, generated by its own happy path. |
| DT-03 | The seller elects points, so the seller controls the points | Naive framing | ❌ REJECTED — points are granted **by the party who owns the thing**, and that is the buyer, not the seller. An artist commissioning production can only grant master points if they hold the master; if they are signed, their label does. So the election is the *seller asking* and the *buyer granting*, and the buyer may not have the authority to grant it — a fact neither party may know, and which surfaces years later when royalties do not arrive. The platform is unusually well placed here: it holds the chain of title (09) and can ask "do you actually own this?" at the moment of granting rather than at the moment of collecting. Whether it *should* block a grant it believes unauthorised is a harder question (the buyer may hold rights the platform cannot see), and it is Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Master points and publishing share are distinguished at election — never one "points" field | Different assets, different payors, different registries. Conflating them is expensive and common. |
| D-02 | Points elected here must **execute** into 09/10 at acceptance | A recorded promise is what the industry already has. Execution is the product. |
| D-03 | Points-only deals carry an explicit no-escrow warning, and the instrument is named as the protection | See DT-01. Do not let the seller believe an escrow is holding something. |
| D-04 | Election is validated against the **recording's** aggregate allocation (09), not the engagement | Otherwise the platform records 115% of a master across five individually-reasonable deals. See DT-02. |
| D-05 | Points are granted by the owner (usually the buyer), not elected unilaterally by the seller | See DT-03. The platform can check authority at grant time, which nobody else can. |
