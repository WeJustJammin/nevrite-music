# Feature: Sample, Clearance & Originality Warranty

> **Number**: 05.06.04
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — **not in the sweep candidate list**

## Overview

The seller's affirmative promise about what is *in* what they delivered: that it is their own work, that any third-party material in it is cleared or disclosed, that no uncleared sample, library loop with a restrictive licence, or borrowed stem is riding along inside the file the buyer just bought.

`meta/personas.md` names this exact scenario as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour — *"uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream"* — and the candidate sweep produced no node for it. The personas file found the risk; the sweep did not build the thing that catches it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller, makes an affirmative declaration at delivery — which is the point: not a checkbox buried in terms at signup, but a specific statement about *this* delivery, at the moment they are handing it over. As a buyer, receives a warranty they can actually rely on, from a party the platform can identify, with a timestamped record. Their persona's "worst accidental" outcome is what this exists to make deliberate rather than careless. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-risk persona, in both directions. Producers deliver beats and productions containing samples, library loops, one-shots and material from other sessions — and they are also the party who *aggregates* everyone else's contributions into one master, so a single contaminated stem from a session player becomes their liability and their artist's. Their session may contain material from three previous projects, which is normal practice and a real exposure (05.04.04 edge cases). |
| Operator | None | Not visible. Attendance produces no content; a room does not warrant anything about what was recorded in it. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A warranty is a **condition of the transfer** (05.06.03), made at delivery and surviving acceptance. It is not a settings toggle and not a signup term — it is a specific, timestamped declaration about a specific delivery, and its whole value is that it is attributable.

What the seller declares:

| Declaration | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Original | Everything in it is the seller's own work |
| Contains third-party material — cleared | Samples/loops present; the seller holds the clearance and can evidence it |
| Contains third-party material — licensed | Library/royalty-free material under a licence, with the licence terms stated |
| Contains third-party material — **uncleared** | Present and not cleared. Honest, and the buyer's problem to solve before release. |
| Contains material from another session | Stems, parts, or a bed originating elsewhere |
| **Unknown provenance** | The seller genuinely cannot attest to a sample's origin (e.g. a one-shot from a pack acquired years ago). Distinct from "uncleared" — uncleared means *known and not cleared*; unknown means *cannot be established*. See DT-04. |

The uncleared option must exist. A warranty regime with only "it's clean" as an answer produces false declarations, because the alternative is not delivering — and a false declaration is worse for everyone than a disclosed problem.

The platform cannot verify any of this (DT-02). What it can do is make the claim **specific, timestamped, attributable and permanent**, which is exactly what the current alternative — a verbal "yeah it's all original" — is not.

### Happy Path

1. Seller delivers (05.04.01)
2. System requires the warranty declaration before the delivery publishes — it is part of delivering, not a step after
3. Seller declares; if third-party material is present, they state what and under what basis
4. Buyer sees the declaration alongside the delivery, before accepting
5. Acceptance executes the transfer **subject to** the warranty (05.06.03)
6. Declaration is archived in the provenance ledger, attributable forever

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller declares "original", it contains an uncleared sample | The whole point. The warranty is now evidence of a specific false statement by a named person on a known date. | Liability follows the declaration; 24 + 09 |
| Uncleared material disclosed | Honest. The buyer decides. | "This contains an uncleared sample from {x}. You'll need to clear it before release." |
| Buyer accepts anyway, releases, gets a claim | They knew. The disclosure did its job. | The warranty's value is that it is *provable* who knew what |
| Library loop with a restrictive licence (no resale, no sync) | The most common real case and almost never surfaced | Licence terms must be capturable, not just a yes/no |
| Session handover (05.04.04) contains third-party material | Handing over the session leaks material the seller had no right to pass on | 05.04.04 Q-02 — the warranty and the artifact set interact |
| Multi-party engagement (05.05) | Every contributor warrants their own contribution; the aggregate is only as clean as the worst declaration | The chain composes (05.05.04 DT-01), and so do the warranties |
| Seller genuinely does not know (a sample from a pack from ten years ago) | Extremely common and not dishonest | An "unknown provenance" answer is more useful than a coerced "original" — see DT-04 |
| Seller tries to publish the delivery without declaring | Blocked. The declaration is a gate on publish (05.04.01), not an optional field. There is no "skip" — the delivery does not exist to the buyer until the warranty is made. | "Add your warranty declaration to complete delivery." |
| Seller revises the delivery after declaring, changing what is in the file | The prior declaration no longer describes the delivered artifact. The revision re-opens the gate: a fresh declaration is required, and the superseded one stays in the ledger (it does not disappear — an attributable history of what was claimed at each version). | Prior declaration shown as superseded; new declaration required before the revised file republishes |
| Buyer disputes the declaration's accuracy before accepting | Not a platform ruling — the platform cannot verify (DT-02). The buyer can decline acceptance and raise it with the seller, or open a dispute (24). The declaration is evidence, not adjudication. | "The seller declared X. If you believe this is wrong, decline and resolve with the seller." |
| Warranty false, buyer already released, damages are large | The platform holds the evidence but is not the insurer | The limit of what this feature is — see DT-03 |
| AI-generated content | A different warranty entirely | 05.06.05 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Required | Delivery initiated | Cannot publish without declaring |
| Declared — Original | Seller declares | Shown to the buyer with the delivery |
| Declared — Disclosed | Third-party material declared | What, and on what basis |
| Declared — Uncleared | Honest disclosure of a problem | Buyer warned before accepting |
| Declared — Unknown | Provenance cannot be established | Buyer sees the honest gap, not a coerced "original" |
| Superseded | Delivery revised after declaring | Prior declaration retained in ledger; a fresh one is required to republish |
| Archived | Acceptance | Permanent, attributable, timestamped |
| Breached | Later found false | Evidence for 24 / 09 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **A contaminated delivery is a liability that propagates through every downstream party, and it is the only defect in this domain that gets worse with the platform's success** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with 09, 12 and every collaborator? Cross-referenced with `meta/personas.md`'s stated worst-accidental behaviour. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is why the node exists despite the sweep missing it. Every other failure in this domain is bilateral and bounded: a late mix affects one buyer, a bad delivery costs one refund, an abandoned engagement strands one slot. An uncleared sample in a delivered stem is *transitive* — it propagates into the buyer's master, into the release (12), into every sync licence (11), into every collaborator's split (09), and it surfaces years later attached to whoever has money. The personas file states the outcome exactly: "exposes every collaborator downstream". Worse, the platform's own thesis amplifies it: WeJammin's value is that work *accumulates* and *connects*, and a contaminated contribution therefore contaminates a graph rather than a file. The one thing that turns this from an unbounded mess into a bounded one is a **specific, attributable, timestamped declaration** — not because it prevents the sample, but because it makes the liability land on the person who made the false statement rather than dispersing across everyone who touched it afterwards. |
| DT-02 | The platform should detect uncleared samples automatically | The obvious ask, and worth rejecting explicitly before someone proposes it downstream | ❌ REJECTED. Content-ID-style matching finds *released commercial recordings* in delivered audio. It does not find: a two-second chop pitched down and filtered, a library loop whose licence forbids sync, a bed from an unreleased session, a stem borrowed from a friend's project, or a re-play of someone else's melody. It would produce confident false negatives on the common cases and false positives on legitimate ones (the seller sampling their *own* released record), and — most dangerously — a green "no samples detected" badge would be read as a clearance, creating exactly the implicit assurance 05.04.02 DT-01 warns about, on a question with real legal consequences. The honest posture is **declaration, not detection**: the platform makes the claim attributable rather than pretending to verify it. Detection may have a *supporting* role later (flagging obvious commercial matches as a prompt to the seller, never as a warrant to the buyer), and that is a different feature with a different promise. |
| DT-03 | The warranty makes the platform the buyer's insurer | Adversarial: what does this feature *not* do, and will someone assume otherwise? | ❌ REJECTED, and stating it matters. The platform holds the evidence; it does not underwrite the risk. A false warranty gives the buyer a provable claim against a named seller on a known date — which is enormously better than the current world, where the claim is against someone's memory of a conversation — but it is still a claim they must pursue, and the seller may have nothing. If the product's language implies the platform stands behind the warranty ("Verified clean", "Cleared"), buyers will rely on it in exactly the way that turns an evidence feature into an insurance product the business never priced. The copy is load-bearing: "the seller declared", never "the platform confirmed". |
| DT-04 | **An "unknown provenance" declaration should exist — but it must cost the seller something, or it hollows the whole warranty out** (resolves Q-01) | The genuinely common case (a one-shot from a pack acquired a decade ago) is not dishonest, and forcing it into "original" manufactures a false declaration exactly as D-03 warns. But the obvious risk is that "unknown" becomes the lazy default every seller clicks to dodge accountability — turning an evidence feature into a shrug. | ✅ CONFIRMED. The option must exist (honesty over coercion, per D-03's logic), and it is distinct from "uncleared": uncleared is *known and not cleared*, unknown is *cannot be established*. The hollowing-out risk is real and is answered not by removing the option but by making it **visible and consequential** — an "unknown provenance" delivery is surfaced to the buyer as prominently as an uncleared one, and it does *not* let the seller off the hook: the seller still warrants that the disclosure itself is honest (they genuinely don't know), and a buyer is free to decline a delivery whose provenance the seller can't stand behind. The market, not the platform, prices the "unknown" discount. Whether a seller who over-uses "unknown" across many deliveries earns a trust-signal penalty (24) is a downstream question, not this feature's to decide. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Declaration, not detection** | Automated detection produces confident false negatives on the common cases and a green badge buyers would read as a clearance. See DT-02. |
| D-02 | The declaration is made at delivery, per delivery — never a signup term or a profile setting | Its value is that it is specific, timed and attributable to *this* work. A blanket term is unfalsifiable and therefore worthless. |
| D-03 | An **uncleared** option must exist | A regime whose only answer is "it's clean" manufactures false declarations. A disclosed problem is better for everyone than a hidden one. |
| D-04 | The platform holds evidence; it does not underwrite the risk — and the copy must say so | "The seller declared", never "the platform confirmed". See DT-03. |
| D-05 | The warranty survives acceptance and the transfer | That is the whole mechanism: it is what the buyer relies on after they own the thing. |
| D-06 | An **unknown provenance** declaration exists, distinct from uncleared, and is surfaced to the buyer as prominently as an uncleared disclosure | Resolves Q-01. Honest over coercion (D-03), but made consequential so it cannot become the lazy default. See DT-04. |
| D-07 | A revision to the delivery re-opens the declaration gate; superseded declarations are retained, not overwritten | The declaration must describe the artifact actually delivered; the ledger keeps the full history of what was claimed at each version. |
