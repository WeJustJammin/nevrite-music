# Feature: Engagement Rights Posture & Work-for-Hire Election

> **Number**: 05.06.01
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The declaration of what the buyer owns when the engagement completes: everything (work-for-hire / full assignment), a licence to use it, a share of it (co-ownership), or nothing yet (a points deal where the seller keeps an interest). It is elected on the listing, priced into the tier, confirmed on the quote, and executed at acceptance.

Every session-litigation story in music is a story about this question not having been asked at the time.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Elects a posture as a seller and — the part that matters — is *told what it means* in plain words rather than a term of art. `meta/personas.md` names the persona's worst accidental behaviour as signing "a split sheet they don't understand and [discovering] years later they gave away their publishing". This feature is where that happens or does not. As a buyer, sees exactly what they will own before they pay, which no competitor offers. |
| Producer | Full | Elects posture for their own production work (usually WFH + points, or co-ownership) and *sets* it for the session players they hire — which makes them the party with the power here. Their `Anti-Persona Behavior` — pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention — is precisely this feature abused, and it is why the platform must show the *player* what they are agreeing to, not only the person who chose it. |
| Operator | None | Not visible, structurally. Room hire creates no copyright and transfers none. If a studio ever asserts a master-ownership term for its facility, that is domain 16's decision, not this domain's. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The posture is a **required election** — there is no default, and a listing cannot publish without one (05.06 D-04).

| Posture | Buyer gets | Seller keeps | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work-for-hire / full assignment | Everything in the delivered contribution | Nothing but the credit | Session performance, ghost work, most commissioned production |
| Licence | A defined right to use it | Ownership | Sample packs, library work, some remote session work |
| Co-ownership | A share | A share | Co-writes, topline |
| Points / backend | Ownership, subject to the seller's royalty interest | A participation (05.06.02) | Production deals |

Two things must be true of every posture and are not obvious:

**A work-for-hire seller keeps their credit.** WFH transfers economic rights, not the fact of having played. Domain 02's graph records who did it regardless of who owns it — conflating these is, in the domain map's own words, "the exact error the rights lens flagged".

**WFH may not transfer everything, everywhere.** Performers' rights in several jurisdictions are not fully assignable, and moral rights are inalienable in some. A posture is a claim about the law, and the law varies.

### Happy Path

1. Seller elects a posture per listing tier; the system explains it in plain language and shows the consequence for each side
2. Posture is priced — a WFH master costs more than a licence, and the tier structure should reflect that
3. Buyer sees the posture on the quote in the same plain language, not as a term of art
4. Both accept; the posture freezes into the scope of record (05.02.01)
5. At acceptance, the transfer executes exactly what the posture declared (05.06.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller does not elect | Publish blocked. There is no safe default. | "Choose what the buyer owns when they accept. There's no safe default here." |
| Buyer wants a different posture than the listing offers | Quote path; the seller may offer it at a different price | Posture is negotiable on the quote |
| Seller elects WFH without understanding it | The failure the persona file names explicitly | Plain-language explanation + the specific consequence ("you will not share in the earnings of this recording, ever") |
| Session player on WFH retains neighbouring rights anyway (jurisdiction) | The posture over-claims | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see sub-domain Q-04. Needs counsel. |
| Points elected as the pricing model (05.01.03) | Posture and price are the same decision seen twice | Must reconcile — a points price with a WFH posture is contradictory (05.06.02) |
| Co-ownership without a split percentage | An incomplete posture | Split must be stated, or there is nothing to execute |
| Buyer needs to clear the master for a sync later | Only WFH/assignment or a broad licence supports it | Should be surfaced at election: "If you want to license this to film, you need X." |
| Multi-seller engagement (05.05) | Several postures, one recording | Must compose into a coherent chain of title (05.05.04 DT-01) |
| Sealed/ghost engagement (05.02.02) | A co-owner appears on the split by definition — sealing may be undeliverable | 05.02.02 Q-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unelected | New listing | Publish blocked with an explanation, not an error |
| Elected | Seller chooses | Posture shown on the listing, in plain words |
| Negotiated | Changed on a quote | The quote's posture governs, not the listing's |
| Frozen | Quote accepted | Immutable; part of the scope of record |
| Executed | Acceptance | 05.06.03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **There is no safe default rights posture, so the platform must have none — and this is the domain's single most consequential design decision** | Adversarial: what does the default do, if we pick one? Test both. | ✅ CONFIRMED. Test work-for-hire as default: every session player who lists without reading the fine print silently assigns their rights in perpetuity, and the platform has automated `meta/personas.md`'s stated worst-accidental outcome ("signs a split sheet they don't understand and discovers years later they gave away their publishing") — at scale, by default, in the product that exists to prevent it. Test co-ownership as default: every buyer commissioning a mix ends up co-owning their own record with their engineer, cannot clear a sync without chasing them years later, and the platform has manufactured exactly the fragmented-title mess D-18 says it exists to fix. **Both defaults produce the platform's own stated failure mode**, in opposite directions and with equal reliability. A default is unavoidable in most product decisions; here the honest answer is a forced election, which costs one blocked publish and prevents an entire class of harm. Consequence: listing creation gains required friction, and that friction is the feature. |
| DT-02 | **Work-for-hire transfers economic rights but not the credit — and conflating them would break the platform's thesis at its most load-bearing point** | Reasoning about the 02↔09 seam that the domain map itself flags. | ✅ CONFIRMED. The domain map states it plainly: "a session drummer has a credit and zero ownership — conflating them is the exact error the rights lens flagged". A WFH engagement must therefore emit a full credit to domain 02 *and* a full assignment to domain 09, simultaneously and without either implying the other. The tempting implementer's simplification — "WFH means the buyer gets everything, so credit the buyer" — would delete the performer from the credit graph on precisely the deals where performers are most numerous and least visible (session work is overwhelmingly WFH). That is the same failure as the fixer's vanishing string section (05.05.02 DT-01), reached by a different route, which is a signal that "the credit follows the hands, whatever the contract says" is a **domain-level invariant** rather than a local rule. |
| DT-03 | Work-for-hire is a portable concept the platform can model once | Convenience assumption | ❌ REJECTED — "work for hire" is a **US statutory** term with specific conditions (it applies to employees, or to nine enumerated categories of commissioned work with a signed writing — and a session recording's fit is not automatic). Outside the US it mostly does not exist as such: the equivalent is an assignment, several jurisdictions make performers' rights or moral rights partly inalienable, and some require formalities a click cannot satisfy. So the label is not portable and the *effect* is not uniform: two sellers electing "work-for-hire" in London and Nashville may end up with materially different outcomes. Modelling one posture called WFH and applying it globally would be the platform confidently telling people they own something they may not — which is worse than saying nothing, because they will rely on it. Escalated as sub-domain Q-03; needs counsel, not a design decision. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No default posture — an explicit election is required to publish | Both available defaults produce the platform's own stated failure mode. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | The posture is explained in plain language to **both** parties, at election and at quote | The persona's worst accidental outcome is signing something they did not understand. A term of art shown to someone who does not know it is not disclosure. |
| D-03 | A credit is emitted regardless of posture — WFH transfers economics, not authorship | Domain-level invariant. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | Posture and artifact set are orthogonal | Owning the master does not entitle the buyer to the session. See 05.04.04 DT-03. |
