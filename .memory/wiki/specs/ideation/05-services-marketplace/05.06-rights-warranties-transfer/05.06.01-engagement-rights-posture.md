# Feature: Engagement Rights Posture & Work-for-Hire Election

> **Number**: 05.06.01
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The declaration of what the buyer owns when the engagement completes: everything (work-for-hire / full assignment), a licence to use it, a share of it (co-ownership), or nothing yet (a points deal where the seller keeps an interest). It is elected on the listing, priced into the tier, confirmed on the quote, frozen at acceptance, and executed by 05.06.03.

Every session-litigation story in music is a story about this question not having been asked at the time.

**And it is asked twice, not once.** A music engagement can create two copyrights — the sound recording (master) and the underlying musical work (composition) — and they routinely go to different places in the same deal. A producer taking work-for-hire on the master and 20% of the publishing is not an exotic structure; it is the standard one. A posture elected per *engagement* rather than per *copyright* cannot express it, and the gap it leaves is the single most litigated question in music: *did the producer write anything?* See DT-04.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As seller**: elects a master posture and a composition posture per listing tier, and — the part that matters — is *told what each means in plain words* rather than a term of art. `meta/personas.md` names this persona's worst accidental behaviour as signing "a split sheet they don't understand and [discovering] years later they gave away their publishing". This feature is where that happens or does not. **As buyer**: sees both postures on the quote before paying, with the specific things they will and will not be able to do afterwards (sync, re-release, re-record) named explicitly — which no competitor offers, because no competitor holds the chain of title (D-10). **As the multi-hyphenate**: routinely both, and routinely both in one week, on opposite sides of the same term. |
| Producer | Full | Elects posture for their own production work — usually Points on the master plus a co-owned writer share on the composition, which is the exact deal DT-04 exists to make expressible — and *sets* it for the session players they hire (05.05), which makes them the party with power here. `meta/personas.md` names their worst intentional behaviour as pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention: that is **precisely this feature abused**, and it is why the counter is that the *player* is shown the consequence in plain language on their own quote (D-02), not that the Producer is trusted. |
| Operator | None | Structurally none. Room hire creates no copyright and transfers none — an Operator's inventory is "time and space, not goods or labour" (`meta/personas.md`). **The apparent exception is not one**: in a "room + in-house engineer" bundle (05.01.01 Q-04), the *engineer* elects the posture on their craft, acting as Producer; the Operator sells the hours. If a studio ever asserts a master-ownership term for its facility, that is a **domain 16** decision and a different business — not a posture on a services listing. |
| Fan | None | No election, no negotiation, no instrument, no visibility into a quote. **But the posture text is public**: it renders on the public listing (05.01, where Fan is Read-only), because a buyer cannot be told what they will own *before they pay* unless it is on the shopfront. So a Fan browsing a producer's listing reads "you'll own this outright" as listing copy. That is 05.01's surface rendering this feature's field — it is not access to this feature. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### The election is required, per copyright, and has no default

The posture is a **required election** — no default, and a listing tier cannot publish without one (D-01, sub-domain D-04, 05.01.01 D-02). Both defaults available to us produce the platform's own stated failure mode, in opposite directions, with equal reliability. See DT-01.

There are **two elections per tier**, because there are two copyrights:

| Election | Subject | Vocabulary | Required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Master posture** | The sound recording | Assignment (work-for-hire) · Licence · Co-ownership · Points | Always |
| **Composition posture** | The underlying musical work | Creates none · Assignment · Co-ownership (writer share) · Licence | Always — including the explicit `creates none` |

`Creates none` is a **declaration, not an absence** (D-06). For a mastering engineer it is true and takes one click. For a session guitarist who invents the hook it is false, and a false declaration is attributable to a person and a timestamp — which is the whole point (domain D-07: *the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them*).

> **Naming trap, stated once**: 05.06.02's "publishing share" **is** composition co-ownership. Same object, two names in the industry. The two features use one vocabulary or the platform has two records of one thing — which is the reconstruction problem, rebuilt internally.

### What each posture means

| Posture | Buyer gets | Seller keeps | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assignment / work-for-hire** | Everything assignable in the delivered contribution, for the full term, worldwide | The credit — and nothing else | Session performance, ghost work, most commissioned production |
| **Licence** | A **defined** right to use it (see required parameters) | Ownership | Sample packs, library work, some remote session work |
| **Co-ownership** | A stated share | A stated share | Co-writes, topline, producer publishing |
| **Points / backend** | Ownership, subject to the seller's royalty interest | A participation (05.06.02) | Production deals |

### Every posture declares its parameters, or it is not elected

A posture without its parameters is **unelected**, not partially elected (D-05). Nothing downstream can execute "a licence" (05.06.03 D-03 composes only from frozen data — there is nothing to compose from). The same test applies to a **deferred** posture: "an assignment, later" is unelected until the trigger condition and the vesting term are both set.

| Posture | Required parameters | Why it is required |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment | *(none — it is total)* + the determining facts (D-08) | The label is a conclusion; the facts are what survive. See DT-06. |
| Licence | **Exclusivity** (exclusive / non-exclusive) · **Term** (years, or perpetual) · **Territory** (worldwide, or named) · **Permitted uses** (from the deliverable/use vocabulary) · **Sublicensable** (y/n) | Five fields. Miss one and the buyer holds a right nobody can describe, which is not a right. |
| Co-ownership | **Retained share** per copyright, to **2 decimal places** · **Administration** (who may licence it, and on whose consent) | A share without an administration term is a deadlock (see edge cases — this varies by jurisdiction, Q-01). |
| Points | Asset (master / publishing / both) + percentage — owned by 05.06.02 D-01 | Different assets, different payors, different registries. |
| **Any posture, where effect is deferred** (D-14) | **Trigger condition** — the named event on which effect begins or ends · **Vesting term** — which grant takes effect at that moment, carrying its own full parameter set from the rows above | Two extra fields, and both are load-bearing. A deferred instrument with no named trigger is a grant that can never begin, and one with no vesting term is DT-08's rider by another route — signed paper that says something will happen without saying what. The elected posture's own parameters are still required in full: deferring *when* a licence takes effect does not excuse leaving its territory blank. **Who observes the trigger is not a parameter and is not settled here** — it is `05.01.03` Q-07, now a prerequisite. |

### The posture and the price are one election, not two that agree

CX-02 and the sub-domain index frame points as "one deal seen twice — a pricing model (05.01.03) and a posture". The resolution is not to validate two fields against each other; it is that **electing points as a price *is* electing the points posture** (D-03). One object, rendered in two places. This eliminates the contradiction class rather than detecting it.

The legal combinations, per copyright:

| Pricing model (05.01.03) | Legal **master** postures | Legal **composition** postures |
|---|---|---|
| Cash-only (flat · per-song · per-minute · hourly · half-day · day rate · per-stem · retainer) | Assignment · Licence · Co-ownership | Creates none · Assignment · Co-ownership · Licence |
| Points-only | Points | Creates none · Co-ownership |
| Fee + points (hybrid) | Points | Creates none · Co-ownership |
| **Buyout** | Assignment · Licence | Creates none · Assignment · Co-ownership · Licence |
| **Spec** — a normal posture whose **effect is deferred** to the named trigger (D-14) | Assignment · Licence · Co-ownership | Creates none · Assignment · Co-ownership · Licence |

> The standard producer deal — **fee + points on the master, co-owned writer share on the composition** — is a legal row here and is *inexpressible* under a one-posture-per-engagement model. That is DT-04's whole justification.

Three notes on the rows added 2026-07-30, when the table was reopened to answer the spec gap (run-3 owner queue, `05-services-marketplace#018`):

- **The cash-only row was incomplete.** It enumerated five of 05.01.03's eight cash models; hourly, half-day and retainer had no row at all, so three publishable pricing models had no legal posture set. They behave identically to the other five — cash for labour, rights elected independently — and are now named rather than assumed.
- **A buyout is not a cash-only model** and never was: `05.01.03` DT-13 is explicit that it is *cash for rights, not labour*, and requires it to be **paired with a posture here**. Its master postures are Assignment or Licence and nothing else — the buyout price is paid precisely to extinguish a continuing interest on that copyright, so Points (a continuing participation) and Co-ownership (a retained share) contradict the model. The composition row is unrestricted because a buyout of one copyright says nothing about the other; where the buyout's declared rights scope (territory · media · term) covers the composition too, the same two-posture restriction applies to that election. The Licence parameter set (D-05) *is* where DT-13's declared scope lands — there is no second place to record it.
- **Spec elects a normal posture and defers its effect** (D-14). It is not a fifth posture and does not touch D-07's closure. Whether **Points** may carry deferred effect is genuinely open and is left open — `05.06.02` D-02 has a participation existing from execution whether or not the record ever earns, which is the opposite of vesting on a trigger. See Q-08.

Cash and rights remain two ledgers and are never summed (05.01.03 D-07). This feature elects; it does not price.

### Two things that are true of every posture and are not obvious

**A work-for-hire seller keeps their credit.** WFH transfers economic rights, not the fact of having played. Domain 02's graph records who did it regardless of who owns it — conflating these is, in the domain map's own words, "the exact error the rights lens flagged", and it is domain principle D-08 (*the credit follows the hands*). See DT-02.

**A posture never claims what the buyer owns — only what the seller grants.** Performers' rights are not fully assignable in several jurisdictions; moral rights are inalienable in some; US termination rights cannot be contracted away. The posture is a claim about the law, and the law varies (DT-03, DT-06). So the render is always *"you are granting X"* / *"they are granting you X"* — never *"you will own X everywhere, forever"* (D-12).

### Happy Path

1. Seller opens a listing tier's rights step (05.01.01). Both posture fields are empty; the tier's publish control is disabled with a named reason, not a validation error.
2. Seller elects a **master posture**. The system renders, adjacent and without a term of art: one sentence of what the buyer gets, one sentence of what the seller keeps, and one named irreversible consequence. For Assignment that sentence is fixed: *"You will not share in the earnings of this recording, ever."*
3. Posture-specific **required parameters** appear (Licence → five fields; Co-ownership → retained share + administration). Publish stays blocked until every one is filled. Fields validate on blur with an inline message; none is optional.
4. Seller elects a **composition posture**. If the craft cannot be authorial (mastering, mix, repair, artwork), `Creates none` is offered as a one-click declaration with its consequence stated — *"You're declaring you wrote nothing on this. If you fly in a part you wrote, this is false."* It is a click, not a skip (D-06).
5. System records the **determining facts** alongside the label — governing law, the seller's asserted jurisdiction, commission-vs-employment, craft, and whether a signed writing exists (D-08). The seller sees these as a short "what we're recording" summary; they are not a form to fill (they are already known).
6. Posture is **priced into the tier** by the seller. An assignment of a master costs more than a licence of one; the tier structure carries that, and the platform never suggests the delta (D-10).
7. Tier publishes. The posture renders on the **public listing** in the same plain language — the first time in this market a buyer can read what they will own before contacting anyone.
8. Buyer requests a quote. The quote carries the tier's posture verbatim. Buyer may **negotiate it** (05.02.01) — the seller may offer a different posture at a different price; each re-issue is a new immutable quote version with a diff (05.02.01 D-02, D-03).
9. Buyer accepts. The posture **freezes** into the scope of record (05.02.01 D-01). Neither the listing's current posture nor any later conversation may alter what executes.
10. Throughout the engagement, the pending consequence is visible, not saved for the end: *"On acceptance: you'll assign the master, keep 20% of the publishing, and be paid £2,400"* (05.06.03 D-05).
11. At acceptance, 05.06.03 executes exactly what the posture declared — into 09 (chain of title), into 10 (any participation), and into 02 (the credit, regardless of posture).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Seller does not elect** | Publish blocked. There is no safe default (DT-01). Unelected is a **safe terminal state** — nothing publishes, nothing executes, nobody is harmed. | "Choose what the buyer owns when they accept. There's no safe default here." Publish control disabled with this reason inline — not a submit-time error. |
| **Seller elects Licence and leaves `territory` blank** | The posture is **unelected**, not partial (D-05). A licence with an undefined scope is not a right. | Inline on blur: "A licence needs a territory. 'Worldwide' is a choice — pick it deliberately." Publish stays blocked. |
| **Seller elects Co-ownership with no retained share** | Unelected. There is nothing for 05.06.03 to execute. | "Say what share you're keeping. A co-ownership without a number is a disagreement waiting to happen." |
| **Three co-owners want equal thirds** | 33.33 + 33.33 + 33.33 = 99.99. The most common split in music **does not divide**, and the platform must not fix it silently. No auto-balance, no absorbing the remainder into the largest share, no rounding up the last party. The parties allocate 0.01 explicitly. | "These add up to 99.99%. Someone has to take the extra 0.01% — decide who." Consistent with 05.01.03 D-05: rounding is never defaulted by the platform. |
| **Co-ownership shares across sellers on a multi-seller engagement (05.05) exceed what the buyer holds** | Invisible here — this domain sees one deal at a time. Validated at **execution** against 09's aggregate for the recording, exactly as points are (05.06.02 D-04). | Execution-time block, surfaced by 05.06.03, not a false green at election. |
| **Two quotes on the same recording accepted concurrently, each individually within budget, jointly over 100%** | Only 09's aggregate can see this. Same mechanism as 05.06.02 Q-03; the posture inherits the race, it does not create it. | 05.06.03 / 05.06.02 Q-03. This feature's obligation is to elect against 09, never against the engagement. |
| **Buyer wants a posture the listing does not offer** | Quote path. The seller may offer it at a different price, or decline. Not an error — it is the negotiation the listing tier exists to start. | Posture is negotiable on the quote; every re-issue shows the buyer a diff (05.02.01 D-03). |
| **Seller elects WFH without understanding it** | The persona's named worst-accidental outcome (`meta/personas.md`). This feature is where it is prevented or automated at scale. | Plain-language explanation plus the fixed consequence sentence: *"You will not share in the earnings of this recording, ever."* The term "work for hire" never renders without it. |
| **Session player on Assignment retains neighbouring / performers' rights anyway (jurisdiction)** | **The posture over-claims — so the platform stops claiming.** The instrument grants "everything assignable under the governing law", and both parties are shown a **residual-rights notice** naming that some rights may not be assignable where the seller signed and that those remain the seller's. The platform does **not** assert which rights those are, in which jurisdiction — that is Q-01, and a confident answer would be worse than silence because both parties would rely on it (D-12). | Seller: "Some rights can't be signed away where you are. Those stay yours whatever this says." Buyer: "This grant covers everything the law lets them grant. It may not be everything." Whether those residual rights are then **registered** with domain 10 is Q-06. |
| **Co-ownership means different things in different places** | A US co-owner may grant a non-exclusive licence unilaterally and account to the others; UK joint owners need each other's consent. Same word, materially different asset. This is DT-03 recurring in a second posture, which is why **administration is a required parameter** (D-05) rather than an assumed default. | The administration term is elected explicitly. What the *law* imposes where it conflicts is Q-01. |
| **Points elected as the pricing model (05.01.03)** | Not a contradiction to detect — **one election rendered twice** (D-03). Electing points as a price elects the points posture. | The seller never answers the same question in two places and never sees them disagree. |
| **Assignment posture combined with a points price** | Structurally impossible under D-03 — the combination cannot be constructed, because the price *is* the posture. Previously a validation problem; now a vocabulary one. | The combination is not offerable. |
| **Buyer needs to clear the master for a sync later** | Determined here, years earlier, by a field they may not have read. Only Assignment or a licence whose `permitted uses` include sync supports it. The platform **states the consequence of the posture on the table** ("this licence does not let you put the record in a film") — it does not **recommend** the posture that would (D-10, Q-02). | On the quote: "What this lets you do" / "What this does not let you do", rendered symmetrically for whatever posture is on offer. |
| **Multi-seller engagement (05.05)** | Several postures, one recording. They **compose into a chain of title; they do not unify** (DT-07). The drummer may be Assignment while the fixer's string section is union-contracted with non-assignable neighbouring rights. | Each seller sees and signs their own grant. The buyer sees the composed picture: what they hold and from whom (05.05.04 DT-01). |
| **Buyer demands one uniform posture across all sellers** | Legitimate ask, and normal in the trade — but the platform does not *enforce* it. See DT-07. The buyer may make it a condition of each quote; each seller still elects and signs their own. | Not a platform switch. A per-quote term, N times. |
| **Sealed / ghost engagement (05.02.02) elects Co-ownership or Points** | A co-owner appears on the split by definition, and a royalty payee appears in the money trail — sealing may be undeliverable for these postures. | 05.02.02 Q-01 — open, and this feature is the reason it is open. |
| **Listing's posture is edited while a quote is outstanding** | Nothing happens to the quote. Quotes are immutable per version (05.02.01 D-02); the posture was captured at issue. The invariant: **no mutation upstream of an issued quote may alter that quote's posture.** | The buyer's quote is unchanged. The seller's next quote from that tier carries the new posture. |
| **Bulk rate-card edit changes posture across 40 listings** | The blast radius 05.01.01 DT-07 names, applied to the term with the longest tail. Outstanding quotes are untouched (above). A bulk posture change must render the count of live listings it will alter **before** it applies. | "This changes what buyers own on 40 live listings." Confirmation names the count. |
| **Seller changes their jurisdiction between election and signature** | The recorded jurisdiction is the one **asserted at signature**, timestamped — not at election, and not the account's current value. Postures are jurisdiction-dependent (DT-03), so the fact must be pinned to the act, not to a mutable profile field. | The instrument names the jurisdiction asserted at signing. |
| **Seller is a minor** | Real, common (teenage beatmakers are a material seller population), and **unchecked**. A minor's assignment is voidable in many jurisdictions — the buyer would discover this at their first sync clearance, if ever. The platform does not adjudicate capacity; it records the signer's asserted capacity as an attributable declaration (domain D-07) and never represents an assignment as unimpeachable. | Whether the platform **blocks** a minor from electing Assignment is Q-04 — a counsel and values decision, not a design preference. See DT-09. |
| **Seller is an entity (band / studio / agency), not a person** | Who may elect a posture that binds every member? Delegated authority (`01.03.02` D-05), not a listing field — and an `administer` activity over `publishing` does **not** reach it: `09.01.04` D-06 stands over the mandate, so no agency may name a publisher over a writer share its rostered party did not write. | Q-05 — coordinate with 01. Mirrors domain Q-04. |
| **Seller and buyer are the same human (multi-hyphenate self-deal)** | The assignment is a no-op that would still write a chain-of-title link from a party to themselves. The platform must not create a circular link in 09. Self-dealing as a *payments* concern is 05.01.01 DT-09's; the degenerate rights case is this one's. | Election allowed; execution writes no self-referential grant. |
| **Seller is a member of the buying entity (sells a mix to their own band)** | **Not** a no-op — a real transfer from a human to an entity they partly control, and the correct behaviour is to execute it. If the band later splits, that is 01/09's problem, not a reason to block a normal transaction. | Executes normally. |
| **Seller writes a bespoke term into a free-text field** | There is no such field, and there will not be one. The posture vocabulary is **closed** (D-07). An archived rider that contradicts the executed posture would mean the platform executed one thing and stored another — the reconstruction problem, manufactured internally, in the feature built to end it. See DT-08. | "We can't record a term we can't execute." The honest cost stands: some real deals still cannot be done here. |
| **"Reverts to me after 5 years"** | **Now expressible, and not as a rider.** This is a normal posture whose effect **ends** on a named trigger — the mirror of a spec deal, whose effect **begins** on one. Elect the posture, defer its effect, name the trigger (D-14). The vocabulary did not move: reversion is a property of the instrument, and D-07 is untouched. Resolves Q-07. | The trigger and vesting term are elected fields like any other, and the plain-language render says what happens and when: *"They own this until {trigger}. Then it comes back to you."* |
| **Seller elects a posture whose trigger nobody observes** ("if it gets cut", where nothing is watching 12 for the cut) | The instrument is signed and real, and nothing ever fires it — which collapses D-14 into the two-stage instrument it was chosen over, with better paperwork. **Not answerable in this feature**: which triggers are observable, and by what, is `05.01.03` Q-07, promoted to a **prerequisite** for exactly this reason. Whether the set of electable triggers is therefore closed to observable ones follows from that answer and is not pre-empted here. | `[PENDING — 05.01.03 Q-07]`. Whatever the answer, the seller must not be shown a trigger the platform has no way to see. |
| **Network failure mid-election** | Nothing is partially elected. The tier reverts to Unelected — a safe state that blocks publish. | "That didn't save. Nothing's published." |
| **Malicious input in a Licence `permitted uses` field** | There is no free text. Permitted uses draw from the deliverable/use vocabulary (05.01.01 D-07's precedent: structured, drawn from a controlled list, never prose). | Selection, not typing. |
| **Posture change requested after acceptance, by mutual agreement** | The frozen posture is **never mutated** (D-11). A later agreement is a **new instrument** — a subsequent link in 09's chain, referencing the original, which stands as historical fact. Append-only. It is also **not this domain's transaction**: it is a catalog transaction (09/23), exactly as a points buy-out is (05.06.02). | "That deal is done and recorded. What you want now is a new agreement about the same recording." Routed out of the engagement. |

### States

**Lifecycle states** — the posture as an object:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Unelected** | New listing tier | Publish blocked with a named reason inline, not an error on submit. Safe terminal state — nothing published, nothing executed. |
| **Incomplete** | Posture chosen, required parameters missing | Publish still blocked. Treated as Unelected (D-05), rendered as progress: "2 of 5 licence terms set." |
| **Elected** | Posture + all parameters set | Posture renders on the public listing in plain words. |
| **Offered** | Quote issued | Captured in an immutable quote version (05.02.01 D-02). Listing edits cannot reach it. |
| **Negotiated** | Changed on a re-issued quote | New version + diff (05.02.01 D-03). The quote's posture governs, never the listing's. |
| **Frozen** | Quote accepted | Immutable; part of the scope of record. Pending consequence rendered throughout the engagement (05.06.03 D-05). |
| **Executed** | Acceptance | 05.06.03 — the atomic three-leg commit. |
| **Executed — effect pending** | Acceptance, where the instrument defers effect to a named trigger (D-14) | The instrument is signed and the seller holds executed paper, not a promise to sign. Both parties see the same standing sentence: what takes effect, and on what event. Never rendered as "pending signature" — the signature already happened; it is the *effect* that waits. 05.06.03 D-19. |
| **Vested** | The named trigger is observed and notified (`05.01.03` Q-07) | The deferred grant takes effect and executes on 05.06.03's second path. Both parties told what is now true, in the same words the pending sentence used. |
| **Lapsed** | A deferred instrument whose trigger condition can no longer occur | The grant never takes effect. Nothing executes, and the record keeps the fact that it was signed — a spec deal that did not come in is history, not an absence. |
| **Superseded** | A later, separate instrument on the same recording | The original stands as historical fact; the chain of title gains a link. Never a mutation (D-11). |

**Render states** — the election surface:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Rights step opened | Skeleton on the posture cards. The plain-language consequence text is **never** lazy-loaded separately from the posture it explains — a posture card that renders before its consequence is the disclosure failing (D-02). |
| **Empty** | Nothing elected | Two empty elections with the blocked-publish reason stated, plus the four postures side by side, equally weighted, unranked (D-10). |
| **Populated** | Elected + parameters complete | Both postures, their parameters, the consequence sentences, the residual-rights notice, and the recorded determining facts summary. |
| **Partial** | Parameters incomplete | Which fields remain, inline. Publish blocked. Never a "you can finish this later" affordance — later is after a buyer has read it. |
| **Error** | Save failed | "That didn't save. Nothing's published." Retry. The tier is Unelected, not silently half-set. |
| **Read-only (buyer)** | Quote view | Both postures, both consequence sets ("what this lets you do" / "what it does not"), the residual notice, symmetric across postures. |
| **Read-only (public)** | Public listing | Posture text only. No parameters that are commercially sensitive, no benchmark data (05.01.01 D-05). |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **There is no safe default rights posture, so the platform must have none — and this is the domain's single most consequential design decision** | Adversarial: what does the default do, if we pick one? Test both. | ✅ CONFIRMED. Test work-for-hire as default: every session player who lists without reading the fine print silently assigns their rights in perpetuity, and the platform has automated `meta/personas.md`'s stated worst-accidental outcome ("signs a split sheet they don't understand and discovers years later they gave away their publishing") — at scale, by default, in the product that exists to prevent it. Test co-ownership as default: every buyer commissioning a mix ends up co-owning their own record with their engineer, cannot clear a sync without chasing them years later, and the platform has manufactured exactly the fragmented-title mess D-18 says it exists to fix. **Both defaults produce the platform's own stated failure mode**, in opposite directions and with equal reliability. A default is unavoidable in most product decisions; here the honest answer is a forced election, which costs one blocked publish and prevents an entire class of harm. Consequence: listing creation gains required friction, and that friction is the feature. |
| DT-02 | **Work-for-hire transfers economic rights but not the credit — and conflating them would break the platform's thesis at its most load-bearing point** | Reasoning about the 02↔09 seam that the domain map itself flags. | ✅ CONFIRMED. The domain map states it plainly: "a session drummer has a credit and zero ownership — conflating them is the exact error the rights lens flagged". A WFH engagement must therefore emit a full credit to domain 02 *and* a full assignment to domain 09, simultaneously and without either implying the other. The tempting implementer's simplification — "WFH means the buyer gets everything, so credit the buyer" — would delete the performer from the credit graph on precisely the deals where performers are most numerous and least visible (session work is overwhelmingly WFH). That is the same failure as the fixer's vanishing string section (05.05.02 DT-01), reached by a different route, which is a signal that "the credit follows the hands, whatever the contract says" is a **domain-level invariant** (domain D-08) rather than a local rule. |
| DT-03 | Work-for-hire is a portable concept the platform can model once | Convenience assumption | ❌ REJECTED — "work for hire" is a **US statutory** term with specific conditions (it applies to employees, or to nine enumerated categories of commissioned work with a signed writing — and a session recording's fit is not automatic). Outside the US it mostly does not exist as such: the equivalent is an assignment, several jurisdictions make performers' rights or moral rights partly inalienable, and some require formalities a click cannot satisfy. So the label is not portable and the *effect* is not uniform: two sellers electing "work-for-hire" in London and Nashville may end up with materially different outcomes. Modelling one posture called WFH and applying it globally would be the platform confidently telling people they own something they may not — which is worse than saying nothing, because they will rely on it. **Recurs in a second posture**: "co-ownership" means unilateral non-exclusive licensing with accounting in the US, and mutual consent in the UK — one word, two assets. That a rejected hypothesis fails twice in two postures makes it structural, not a WFH quirk. Escalated as Q-01; needs counsel, not a design decision. |
| DT-04 | **The posture must be elected per copyright, not per engagement — because a music engagement creates up to two, and the standard producer deal sends them to different places** | Domain expertise: what would a music-industry expert expect that nobody has mentioned? Applied to the sub-domain's own inconsistency — 05.06.02 D-01 distinguishes master points from publishing share, and this file did not distinguish master from composition at all. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the most consequential addition in this pass. A services engagement can produce a **sound recording** and a **musical work**, which are separate copyrights with separate owners, separate registries (09) and separate payors (10). The breadth draft elected one posture over "the delivered contribution" — a phrase that quietly assumes there is one thing. There is not. The producer who takes a fee, points on the master, and 20% of the publishing is not an edge case; **it is the modal above-the-floor production deal**, and it is *inexpressible* under one posture per engagement. Worse, the single-posture model does not fail loudly — it fails by leaving the composition question unasked, which is precisely the silence that produces the most litigated question in music: *did the producer write anything?* Note the internal evidence: 05.06.02 already reached half of this rule ("never a single 'points' field — different assets, different payors, different registries") and stopped at points. The general rule was always underneath the special case. Consequences: two elections per tier (D-05); `creates none` becomes a declaration rather than an absence (D-06); the pricing×posture matrix becomes coherent per-copyright rather than contradictory; and "publishing share" (05.06.02) and "composition co-ownership" (here) must be **one vocabulary**, or the platform holds two records of one thing. |
| DT-05 | **The posture is elected before the work exists, so it governs contributions neither party anticipated — and in music the unanticipated contribution is the valuable one** | Adversarial: trace the election's timing against the quote-freeze rule (05.02.01 D-01). When does the posture get decided relative to when the facts become true? | ✅ CONFIRMED. Every other fact this platform captures is captured **at the moment it becomes true** — that is D-18's entire thesis. The posture is the exception: it is elected on a listing in the abstract, frozen at acceptance, and only *then* does the work happen. A session guitarist lists "Assignment", is hired to play a written part, and in the room invents the hook the song is built on. The posture they elected before they knew anything now governs a compositional contribution nobody priced. This is the single most recognisable story in the trade, and the platform's own architecture guarantees it will recur — the quote is immutable (05.02.01 D-02), so the deal **cannot** be re-opened when the surprise happens. The temptation is to allow a post-hoc adjustment, and that must be refused: a mutable posture is a posture the better-resourced party renegotiates after seeing what they got, which is the Producer's named worst-intentional behaviour with a UI attached. So the answer is not mutability — it is (a) forcing the composition election **up front**, for every craft including the ones where it is "obviously" none (D-06), because "obviously none" is exactly the case that surprises; and (b) **restating the frozen composition posture at delivery**, where the seller now knows what they made: *"This engagement assigns anything you wrote. You're delivering now — is that still what happened?"* That is not re-opening the deal. It is showing a person the consequence of a term at the only moment they can actually evaluate it, which is 05.06.03 D-05's principle applied one step earlier. If the answer is "no, I wrote the hook", the deal stands and the disagreement surfaces **now**, while both parties are present and friendly — which is the best outcome available and precisely what the platform exists to manufacture. |
| DT-06 | **The platform records the conclusion and loses the facts that determine whether the conclusion is true — and the record it writes today is the one contested in 2061** | Adversarial, from the platform's own promise: D-18 says provenance is "the value they cannot take with them". Follow that record forward forty years and ask what it needs to have stored. | ✅ CONFIRMED. Storing `posture: work_for_hire` stores an **opinion about the law**, not a fact. Whether it is *true* depends on facts the platform has at election time and would throw away: was the seller an employee or a commissioned party? Does the work fall in one of the nine US enumerated categories? Was there a signed writing before creation? Under which law was it signed, and where was the seller? Those inputs determine a conclusion that is **not stable over time** — US termination rights under §203 are inalienable and cannot be contracted away, so an assignment that is not genuinely work-for-hire is terminable roughly 35 years out no matter what either party agreed or what field the platform stored. The platform will confidently show "perpetual" for four decades and then be wrong in the single moment it was built to be right. This is the deepest version of DT-03's problem: DT-03 says the label does not port across space; DT-06 says it does not port across **time**, and the platform's whole pitch is longevity. The fix is not to answer the legal question — the platform must not, and Q-01 owns it — it is to **record the inputs alongside the conclusion** (D-08), so that whoever adjudicates this in 2061 has the facts rather than a label whose meaning has drifted. Storing the inputs costs a few fields the platform already knows. Not storing them is unrecoverable, because the facts exist only now: nobody will remember in 2061 whether there was a signed writing before creation. **That is the reconstruction problem, and the platform would have caused it by being insufficiently curious on the day.** |
| DT-07 | The platform should let a buyer enforce one uniform posture across every seller on a multi-seller engagement | Convenience assumption — labels do exactly this, so the platform should offer it as a switch | ❌ REJECTED — **postures compose, they do not unify.** The ask is legitimate and normal in the trade (a label wants clean title across the whole session), but making it a platform-enforced switch is wrong in both directions. It would **block** the deals it cannot express: the fixer's string section may be union-contracted with neighbouring rights that are not assignable (05.05.02, 05.02.03) while the drummer's Assignment is clean — a uniform switch either refuses the booking or, far worse, records the string players as having assigned something they did not and could not. It would also **misrepresent** the resulting title: a chain of title is a set of per-contributor grants, and flattening N grants into one term deletes exactly the granularity that makes the chain worth holding — which is the asset (09) the platform exists to be the source of truth for. The buyer's real need is met without the switch: they make the posture a condition of each quote, N times, and each seller elects and signs their own grant. Slower, honest, and it is the only version that survives one contributor being unable to grant what the others granted. |
| DT-08 | A "custom / other — see rider" posture should exist, because real deals have bespoke terms | Realism assumption — the four postures obviously do not cover every deal in music | ❌ REJECTED, though the objection is correct and the cost is real. A bespoke term cannot be **executed**: 05.06.03 D-03 composes the transfer strictly from frozen data, and free text is not data — it is something a human will read later, which is the industry's current state and the thing this platform exists to replace. The failure mode is not that a rider is ignored; it is far worse. A rider saying *"rights revert to the seller after 5 years"* attached to an executed perpetual assignment means the platform **executed one thing into 09 and archived a contradicting thing next to it**. Two records of one deal, disagreeing, discovered years later, at the moment it became valuable — that is the reconstruction problem verbatim, manufactured inside the feature built to end it, and it would be *the platform's own fault* rather than the industry's. And the platform cannot detect the contradiction: reading a rider and comparing it to a posture is exactly the verification domain D-07 says it does not do. So the vocabulary is closed (D-07 here), and the honest consequence is stated rather than hidden: **some real deals cannot be done on WeJammin.** Reversion was named here as the sharpest instance — a common, legitimate term, where pretending otherwise via an archive-only rider would be a lie with a paper trail — and whether to expand the vocabulary for it or accept the gap was left as Q-07. **Updated 2026-07-30**: Q-07 resolved onto neither branch. Reversion is expressible, and *without* expanding the vocabulary, because this annotation had located the problem one level too low. The rider fails because it states an **effect the executed posture contradicts**; reversion states an effect the posture does not contradict at all — the same grant, ending on a named event. That is an instrument property, not a posture, and it is typed rather than prose, which is the exact line this rejection draws. See D-14, and `05.01.03`'s spec model, which is the mirror case reached from the pricing side. The rejection of free text and of the rider stands unchanged and unweakened; what shrank is the set of deals the closure costs us. |
| DT-09 | **The seller's legal capacity to assign is never checked, and the platform's most-likely-to-be-a-minor population is exactly its beatmaker and session-seller population** | Adversarial: what is the failure mode nobody designs for? Follow `meta/constraints.md`'s unresolved age-gating compliance row into this feature. | ✅ CONFIRMED as an exposure; ❌ not resolvable here. A minor's contract is voidable in many jurisdictions — the assignor can disaffirm, sometimes years later. Teenage producers selling beats and production are not a hypothetical population in this market; they are a large and growing one, and they are disproportionately on the exact deal shapes (points-only, cheap assignment) where 05.06.02 DT-01 already established the platform's protections are weakest. So the platform would cheerfully write a chain-of-title link that one party can unwind at will, sell the buyer certainty it does not have, and the buyer would discover it at their first sync clearance — if ever. Note the compounding: `meta/constraints.md` carries age gating as an unresolved compliance item **and** D-13 flags age assurance as a statutory duty that scales with the Fan population. Both treat it as a consumer-protection question. This is the *other* half of it — capacity to contract, on the professional side — and nothing in the corpus currently owns it. What this feature can do is bounded and should be done: record the signer's asserted capacity as an attributable declaration (domain D-07) and **never render an assignment as unimpeachable** (D-12 already forbids "you will own X everywhere, forever", which covers this without a new rule). Whether the platform blocks a minor from electing Assignment at all is Q-04 — a counsel and values decision. Blocking excludes a real and legitimate seller population from the market; not blocking sells buyers a defect. Both are bad, which is why it is the owner's call and not a design preference. |

A *two-stage instrument* (execute nothing now, offer a second instrument when the trigger fires) leaves the seller holding a promise to sign rather than signed paper, which the buyer can simply decline — that is the exploitation pattern `05.01.03` DT-01 names, rebuilt as a product feature, against the population this domain most wants to protect.
