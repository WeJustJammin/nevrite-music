# Feature: Rights Transfer & Split Execution on Acceptance

> **Number**: 05.06.03
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The moment the paperwork becomes true. Acceptance releases the money and, in the same indivisible act, executes the rights posture (05.06.01), registers any points (05.06.02), and emits the credit (domain 02). The buyer pays and vests; the seller is paid and assigns; the record of who did what is written while everyone is present and agreeable.

**One posture shape does not vest at acceptance, and it is deliberate.** Where the instrument defers effect to a named trigger (05.06.01 D-14 — a spec deal, a reversion), acceptance still executes: the instrument is signed, countersigned and archived, the money and credit legs fire normally, and a **contingent interest vests** rather than a grant. The grant itself takes effect later, on the trigger, through a **second execution path** (D-19). The indivisible act above is unchanged for every other deal in the domain; what the deferred case changes is *what* the rights leg commits, never whether the commit is atomic.

This is the domain's stated differentiator and the point where D-18's causal claim — *consolidation is the precondition for provenance* — is either a working mechanism or a slogan. Every other feature in this domain exists to deliver two consenting parties to this moment.

The feature's whole difficulty is that it is one event spanning three systems with three different notions of "done": a ledger entry that is instantaneous, a title record that is permanent, and a bank settlement that takes days and can fail afterwards. Getting "atomic" to mean the right thing across those three is what this file specifies.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona whose entire `Switching Trigger` is this event failing in the old world — "losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove: an unpaid session, a credit that went to someone else, a split they agreed verbally and can't evidence". **As seller**: sees, at the moment of acceptance, exactly what they gave up, what they kept, and what they were paid — recorded, signed, timestamped, and theirs to cite forever. **As buyer**: presses Accept and sees three named consequences before the press, not after. **As a credited non-party** (a session player hired by a fixer, a band member whose entity signed): receives a credit they did not sign for and can attest to or contest — see DT-06. |
| Producer | Full | The persona `meta/personas.md` calls "the capture point", and this is the capture. Their worst accidental behaviour — closing a project without capturing splits, "we'll do it later" — is structurally impossible here, because the split executes as a byproduct of getting paid. **The design must make the lazy path the correct path**, and this feature is where that principle either holds or fails: the seller does not file a split sheet, they accept money, and the split happens. Their worst *intentional* behaviour — pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention — is countered not by trusting them but by DT-06: every human named by this execution is told what was recorded about them, whether or not they were a party to it. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. Room hire creates no copyright and transfers none — a studio does not acquire rights in what was recorded in it. An Operator whose booking sits *inside* a session engagement (16/17) sees the booking's own settlement and nothing of this transfer. If a facility ever asserts a master-ownership term, that is a domain 16 decision, not this one. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A Fan may later *read* the resulting public credit in domain 02, but they have no view of, or part in, the transfer that produced it. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Acceptance (05.04.01) fires an **atomic three-way commit**:

| Leg | System | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Payments, Escrow & Payouts cross-cut | Escrow **releases** to the seller — a ledger event, not a bank transfer (DT-04). In multi-party supply, it allocates to every worker downstream (05.05.02 DT-03). |
| Rights | Domain 09 | The posture executes: assignment, licence, co-ownership share, or points participation into 09/10 |
| Record | Domain 02 | The credit **claim** emits at the elected visibility (05.02.02), naming the person whose hands did the work (05.05 invariant). Attestation is downstream and asynchronous (DT-06). |

All three, or none. This is the **Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer** cross-cut, and this feature is its primary trigger site in the domain.

The instrument behind the transfer comes from the Contracts & E-Signature cross-cut. What is domain-owned is *when* it fires and *what it contains*.

### What "atomic" means here — and what it does not

The three legs do not have equal physics, and pretending they do is the failure mode this feature is most likely to ship with. The commit is atomic over the **decision and its ledger effects**. It is not, and cannot be, atomic over anything that leaves the platform.

| Inside the commit — all or nothing | Outside the commit — asynchronous, failable, never a rollback trigger |
|---|---|
| Escrow **release** (ledger: buyer's held funds → seller's platform balance, plus each downstream worker's allocation) | **Payout** to a bank account (T+1 to T+5 depending on rail; can fail on a closed account, a KYC hold, or a sanctions screen) |
| Rights execution into 09 (assignment / licence / co-ownership / points participation) | Royalty flows from 10, which begin years later and run for decades |
| Credit **claim** emission to 02 at the elected visibility | Counter-**attestation** by the credited humans, which requires a person and a later moment |
| Instrument signature + countersignature + tamper-evident archive | Identifier binding to an ISRC/ISWC at release (12) — see DT-08 |

A failed payout is a **money problem the platform owes the seller**, not a rights problem. It does not unwind an assignment. This is the single most important structural line in the feature after DT-01, because the naive reading of "atomic" makes a master assignment contingent on a session player's bank account, and one KYC hold then unwinds a chain of title.

### The commit has three shapes, not one

| Trigger | Money | Rights | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Final acceptance** (05.04.01) | Full release | **Executes** | Emits |
| **Milestone acceptance** (05.03.02) | Tranche release | **Does not execute** — a contingent interest vests (05.03.02 DT-01) | **Emits** — a credit is a fact about work that happened (05.03.05 D-06) |
| **Final acceptance, effect deferred** (05.06.01 D-14) | Full release — or nothing to release, since spec is zero cash | **Instrument executes; the grant does not.** A contingent interest vests, on the 05.03.02 DT-01 model, and takes effect on the named trigger via D-19's second path | **Emits** — the work happened, whatever the grant is waiting for |

Milestone acceptance is therefore a **two-leg commit** (money + credit) with the rights leg deliberately absent, and DT-01's requirement — the credit leg is committed, never fire-and-forget — applies to it identically. This distinction was under-specified in the breadth pass, which described milestone acceptance as "releases the tranche only": that is true of rights and false of credit.

The **deferred-effect** shape is the third, added 2026-07-30 with 05.06.01 D-14 (run-3 owner queue, `05-services-marketplace#018`). It borrows the milestone shape's mechanism — 05.03.02 DT-01's *contingent interest vests* — rather than inventing one, because the two are the same object reached from different directions: an interest that is real, recorded and not yet a grant. Where they differ is what completes them. A milestone completes at final acceptance, inside this domain, on a schedule both parties agreed. A deferred grant completes on an event this domain cannot see — "the song was cut" is domain 12's (05.01.03 DT-10) — which is why **who observes the trigger is a prerequisite and not a deferral** (05.01.03 Q-07). A contingent interest nobody can complete is the two-stage instrument D-14 exists to avoid, wearing this feature's paperwork.

Three properties hold on the second path, and each is inherited rather than new:

- **It is an execution, not a re-opening.** The parties agreed this at acceptance and signed it then. Nothing about the deal is renegotiated when the trigger fires; the frozen instrument is the sole source (D-03), and the trigger's occurrence is the only new fact.
- **It composes from frozen data, plus the trigger fact.** The posture, its parameters, the trigger condition and the vesting term were all frozen at quote acceptance. 09's live aggregate remains the only live read (D-03) — and it is read *again* at vesting, because years may have passed and the recording's allocation will have moved.
- **It is append-only.** Vesting adds a link to the chain of title; it never mutates the acceptance-time link that recorded the contingent interest (D-10). A spec deal that never triggers is history, not an absence — the record keeps the fact that it was signed.

### Composition inputs — all frozen

The transfer composes **only** from the accepted quote version (05.02.01 D-01) and the delivered artifact set (05.04.04). Nothing composes from live state:

| Input | Frozen at | Why not live |
|---|---|---|
| Rights posture | Quote acceptance | The listing's posture may have changed since; the deal's posture governs (05.06.01) |
| Points: asset (master / publishing) + percentage | Quote acceptance | Same |
| Anonymity level | Quote acceptance | Sealed was a **priced** term (05.02.02) |
| Warranty declarations (05.06.04, 05.06.05) | Delivery publish | The buyer accepted *these* declarations |
| Artifact set + content digests | Delivery publish | A swapped file is a different artifact and cannot be the thing accepted |
| The people whose hands did the work | Delivery publish | The credit follows the hands (05.05 invariant) |

The **only** input read live at execution is domain 09's aggregate allocation for the target work, because points must validate against the recording rather than the engagement and only 09 can see the sum (05.06.02 DT-02 / D-04).

### Idempotency and precedence

Acceptance can arrive twice (a replayed request, a retried commit, a buyer pressing Accept as the auto-accept timer fires). **A given engagement and its accepted quote version execute at most once**; a second arrival changes nothing and returns the outcome of the first, never a second transfer. Where an affirmative acceptance and an expiry race, the **affirmative acceptance wins and is recorded as affirmative** — the distinction between "accepted" and "accepted by expiry" must persist in the record (05.04.01), and it is the fact Q-01 turns on.

### Happy Path

1. Quote is accepted. From this instant the engagement header carries a plain-language **pending-transfer summary** — "On acceptance: you assign the master, you keep your credit and 3 master points, you're paid £1,200" — on every engagement screen, for both parties, until execution. It is never first shown at the moment of acceptance (D-05).
2. Seller publishes a delivery; it passes QC (05.04.02); warranty declarations (05.06.04, 05.06.05) render **with** the delivery, before the buyer holds any decision.
3. Buyer reviews. The Accept control restates the same three consequences in the same words as step 1 — no new information appears at the point of no return.
4. Buyer accepts (or auto-accept fires — see DT-02, which is a real problem).
5. System composes the transfer from frozen data: the accepted quote's posture, points, anonymity level, warranties, and the artifact set with its content digests.
6. System reads domain 09's live aggregate allocation for the target work and validates the points election against it (05.06.02 D-04). A recording that does not yet exist in 09 is created as part of this commit — the engagement is usually what brings it into being.
7. **Atomic commit**: escrow release (ledger) → rights execute into 09 → points register into 09/10 → credit claims emit to 02 at the elected visibility → instrument signed, countersigned and archived tamper-evidently (Contracts cross-cut) → provenance ledger entry written recording *what* executed, *when* (UTC instant), *on whose consent*, and whether that consent was **affirmative or by expiry**.
8. Both parties receive a confirmation naming **all three** outcomes explicitly and in the same plain language as step 1 — not a receipt, not a "thanks", a statement of what is now true.
9. Every human named by the credit leg who was not a party to the transaction (session players in a multi-party engagement, members of a signing band entity) is notified that a credit naming them exists and is invited to attest or contest it (DT-06). Attestation is asynchronous and is never part of the commit.
10. Payout leaves the platform on the rail's own schedule. Its success or failure does not touch legs 2 and 3.
11. The seller's credit graph, the buyer's chain of title, and both parties' finances are now consistent — on day one, not day nine hundred.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Escrow releases, credit emission fails | **The silent failure.** Both parties got what they came for; nobody will ever report it; the platform's core asset has a hole. | Must not be possible — the credit leg is inside the commit. Failure rolls back all three and pages a human. See DT-01. |
| Rights execute, escrow release fails | The seller has assigned their copyright and not been paid — the worst outcome the platform could produce, and precisely what it exists to prevent | Must not be possible. Rolls back all three; engagement returns to `Delivered`; both parties told plainly. |
| Domain 09 unavailable at execution | The commit cannot complete; **fails closed** | "We couldn't complete the acceptance. Nothing has changed — your money is still held and the work is still yours to accept." Engagement stays `Delivered`; the auto-accept clock does **not** advance while the platform is the blocker. |
| E-signature provider unavailable | Same: the instrument is a leg, so the commit fails closed and rolls back | As above. An executed transfer with no instrument is a transfer nobody can prove — which is the platform's failure mode, not an acceptable degradation. |
| **Payout fails after release** (account closed, KYC hold, sanctions screen) | Rights and credit **stand**. The seller's platform balance is intact; the platform owes them money and must resolve the payout. | "Your payment is held — we need your account details / verification. The rights and credit are already recorded." Never "the transfer has been reversed". See DT-04. |
| **Chargeback / payment reversal 30–120 days after execution** | The money leg is undone from outside the system, by a party the platform cannot overrule. Rights and credit stand. This is DT-02's exposure reached through a channel atomicity does not control. | Q-04 — **needs an owner decision.** Every available answer is bad; the platform must pick which party it makes whole. See DT-05. |
| Auto-accept fires (buyer silent) | A copyright assignment executed by inaction | 05.04.01 DT-01 / sub-domain Q-01 — **needs counsel.** The record must mark the consent as `by-expiry` permanently, and as a distinction every downstream system can act on, because everything downstream may need to know the assignment's weakest link. |
| Buyer presses Accept in the same second auto-accept fires | One execution. **Affirmative wins** and is recorded as affirmative. | Nothing unusual — the buyer sees the normal confirmation. The distinction matters to Q-01, not to them. |
| Buyer accepts while the seller is retracting the delivery (05.04.01 allows pre-acceptance retraction) | **Acceptance wins.** The buyer accepted the artifact they were shown; a retraction landing after acceptance is a recall request (05.03.06), not an undo. | Seller: "Too late — {buyer} accepted this at {time}. You can offer a recall." |
| Buyer disputes in the same second as acceptance | **Acceptance wins.** Once executed, the matter is a post-acceptance dispute (24) against a completed transfer, not a freeze of a pending one. | Buyer: "This engagement is already accepted. Raising a dispute now won't hold the money — here's what a dispute can and can't do." |
| Acceptance replayed (retry, double-submit, malicious repost) | Idempotent on engagement + accepted quote version. Returns the first execution's record. | The same confirmation, once. |
| Seller swaps the artifact between delivery and acceptance | Impossible to launder through this feature: the transfer composes from the frozen artifact set with content digests. A different file is a different artifact and was not what was accepted. | Seller: the swap is a retraction + redelivery, which restarts the buyer's window. |
| **Points exceed the recording's remaining allocation at 09** | The commit fails closed. 05.06.02 DT-02 — the sum is invisible from inside one engagement. | Both parties: "This deal grants 5 master points, but only 2 remain unallocated on this recording. This needs resolving before acceptance." Naming the shortfall is the whole value — the alternative is recording 115% of a master. |
| **Two engagements accept simultaneously on the same recording, each within budget, jointly over 100%** | The classic race. Execution serializes per-target-work at 09; the second commit sees the first's allocation and fails closed. | The loser sees the shortfall message above. 05.06.02 Q-03. |
| Buyer lacked authority to grant points | 05.06.02 DT-03. The platform can check against 09 at grant time — uniquely — but the buyer may hold rights 09 cannot see. | Whether this **blocks** is 05.06.02 Q-02. Minimum behaviour: the grant executes with an `authority-unverified` marker on the participation, so 10 knows the claim's provenance decades later, rather than the platform silently recording an unpayable participation. |
| A warranty was false (uncleared sample) | The transfer completed; the buyer owns a liability | 05.06.04 — the warranty survives the transfer, which is the point of it. Remedy is a liability claim (24 + 09), not an unwind. |
| An AI declaration was false (a "performance" was generated) | The transfer stands; the **credit** was wrong | 05.06.05 — remedy is a credit **retraction** in 02, which is a different act against a different system from the clearance remedy. The transfer is untouched. |
| **Multi-party engagement (05.05)** | N credits, N ledger allocations, **one** chain of title. The composition must be coherent (05.05.04 DT-01). | The players are paid from the buyer's release, not the fixer's cashflow (05.05.02 DT-03) — but their *payouts* are outside the commit, so one player's frozen account never rolls back twelve people's credits or the buyer's title. |
| **The seller of record is a band / label / studio entity** | The assignor is an entity; the credited are humans. Two different kinds of thing from one acceptance. | DT-09. A commit that credits the entity deletes the drummer — the fixer failure (05.05.02 DT-01) reached by a second route. |
| **The multi-hyphenate is both the seller of record and one of the hands** (a fixer who also plays; a producer who mixes their own tracking) | One human, two facts: a settlement as seller **and** a performance credit as player. A commit that dedupes "same user → one row" deletes the performance credit. | Both facts render, separately, in the confirmation. This is a third independent route to the same invariant, which is why it is an invariant. |
| **The literal same human is both buyer and seller of record** (one account, or two accounts resolved to one identity, on both sides of the same engagement) | Not a real counterparty relationship — there is no second party to consent, and self-acceptance would let one person move money and rights to themselves unobserved. | **Blocked**, and blocked upstream of this feature: the order is refused at listing/order (05.01.01 DT-09) and self-acceptance is refused at 05.04.01 D-11, so the engagement never reaches execution. This file never sees a same-human commit. |
| **Distinct-but-identity-linked counterparties** (a Musician commissioning their own Producer context that is a separate account/entity; a person contracting with their own band/label entity, per domain 01 identity-linking) | Legal, normal, and a live abuse vector — `meta/personas.md` names "games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation" as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour. Self-dealing produces real credits and real benchmark data at zero cost. | **Not blocked** — a person contracting with their own entity is ordinary and involves two distinct parties who can each consent. The execution marks the transfer `related-party` (identity-linked counterparties per domain 01), the credit carries the same marker, and the deal is **excluded from rate benchmarking** (05.01.04) so it cannot move the numbers other people rely on. The boundary with the row above is exact: **same human on both sides = blocked (05.01.01 DT-09 / 05.04.01 D-11); distinct identity-linked parties = permitted, marked, benchmark-excluded (this file).** |
| Sealed engagement (05.02.02) | The credit emits **suppressed**, not absent | 05.02.02 DT-01 — the platform holds a record it does not display. The commit is identical; only the visibility field differs. |
| Sealed engagement **with points** | The credit is hidden; the royalty payee is not. Anonymity leaks through 10. | 05.02.02 — a known unresolved leak, surfaced here because this is where the participation is created. Warn the seller **at quote time**, not here: at execution it is too late to change the deal. |
| **A credited human is not a WeJammin user** | The credit emits to an unclaimed identity (01/02). It must still emit. | Refusing to credit non-members would lose most of the industry from the graph and would make the record a description of WeJammin's membership rather than of the work. |
| **A credited human contests the credit** (negative attestation) | The credit stands as a **contested claim**. The transfer is untouched. | DT-06 — the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them. A contested credit is more honest than a deleted one. |
| **A credited human requests erasure (GDPR)** after execution | The instrument and the participation are records with a legal basis other than consent. The **display** of a name is a separable question from the **holding** of the record. | DT-10 — suppression to the existing sealed posture, not deletion. Needs counsel; sub-domain Q-06. |
| Buyer's account is deleted / closed after execution | The assignee is gone; the title is not. The chain of title records the party that held it at the time. | Not this feature's remedy — 09 must model a title-holder who has left. Flagged in Cross-Cut Notes. |
| Seller suspended by 24 between quote acceptance and delivery acceptance | The work happened. Rights and credit **execute**; the **payout** is held pending the 24 outcome. | The ledger/payout separation (DT-04) is what makes this answerable at all: the platform can withhold money without corrupting a title record. |
| Engagement frozen by a **pre-acceptance** dispute (24) | Nothing transfers while frozen. The auto-accept clock is stopped. | "This engagement is on hold. Nothing has transferred and nothing will while the dispute is open." |
| Cancelled engagement | Nothing transfers. Credits already earned on accepted milestones stand. | 05.03.05 D-06 — a credit is a fact about work that happened; cancellation is a commercial event, not a retraction of history. |
| **Milestone acceptance** | Releases the tranche and **emits the credit**; **no rights transfer** — a contingent interest vests | 05.03.02 DT-01, deliberately. The buyer owns nothing yet; the drummer who tracked is credited already. |
| **Deferred-effect instrument at acceptance** (spec, reversion — 05.06.01 D-14) | Money and credit legs fire normally; the instrument executes and a **contingent interest** is recorded. The grant itself does not take effect. | D-19. The seller holds signed paper from day one, which is the entire reason this shape was chosen over a promise to sign later. |
| **The named trigger fires, possibly years later** | The second execution path: the deferred grant takes effect, 09's aggregate is re-read at that moment, and a new link is appended to the chain of title. | D-19. No money leg — it settled at acceptance — and no new credit: the credit was a fact about work that already happened. |
| **The trigger fires after the buyer's account is gone, or the recording has moved on in 09** | Vesting still executes against 09's record of the title, exactly as any other link. A departed party is 09's model problem, not a reason to drop a grant that was signed for. | Same treatment as the existing "buyer's account is deleted after execution" row — the chain records the party that held it at the time. |
| **The trigger can no longer occur** (the song was shelved, the option lapsed) | Nothing executes. The instrument is marked `Lapsed` (05.06.01) and the record keeps the fact that it was signed. | Not an erasure and not a failure. A spec deal that did not come in is history — deleting it would delete the evidence the seller did the work. |
| Recall after acceptance (05.03.06) | Work reopens; the transfer does **not** unwind. No new escrow, no new rights transfer, no new credit. | 05.03.06 DT-01 — post-terminal state. The redelivered artifact replaces the delivered one under the *same* title. |
| Recall replaces the artifact the warranties were made about | The warranties should re-attach to the redelivered artifact — the buyer holds *that* file now | Gap named by 05.06 CX §CX-04/05 synthesis Q1 and unowned by any feature. Recorded here as Q-05 because this is the feature that composes warranties into the transfer. |
| Transfer procured by fraud (account takeover — the "buyer" who accepted was not the buyer) | Not a reversal. The transfer was **void from the start**: there was no consent. | DT-07 — `Voided` is a distinct state from `Superseded`, and the record must show the void without erasing the event. Q-03. |
| Buyer and seller later agree to change the posture (a WFH seller and buyer agreeing to co-ownership) | **Not an unwind.** A new transfer in the opposite direction, recorded as a new link. | DT-07. Answers 05.06.01 Q-03: the chain of title is append-only; you cannot un-happen a fact, only add the next one. |
| The transferred work has no ISRC/ISWC — it does not exist as a release yet, and may never | Normal. Title starts before the asset has an identifier. | DT-08 — the binding to identifiers at release (12) is an unspecified step, and without it the record is economically inert. |
| Currency of the engagement ≠ currency of the payout | An FX question on the money leg's ledger amount. Rights and credit are unaffected. | Localization, Currency & Timezone cross-cut. Not a rights concern. |
| Clock skew / DST boundary on an expiry-triggered execution | The record states the UTC instant and the basis (`affirmative` / `by-expiry`) | Timezone-local rendering is presentation; the record is UTC. Never store the local time as the fact. |

### States

The transfer's own lifecycle:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Disclosed** | Quote accepted | The pending-transfer summary in the engagement header, on every screen, for both parties: "On acceptance: you'll assign X, keep Y, be paid Z." Visible throughout, not a surprise at the end (D-05). |
| **Armed** | Delivery published + QC passed | Same summary, plus the warranty declarations and the Accept control restating the three consequences verbatim. |
| **Executing** | Acceptance fired (affirmative or expiry) | Brief; must be atomic. The Accept control is disabled and non-resubmittable. |
| **Executed** | All legs committed | Confirmation naming all three outcomes; the instrument is retrievable; the provenance ledger entry is citable. |
| **Failed** | Any leg failed | Rolled back entirely. Engagement returns to `Delivered`; auto-accept clock does not advance; both parties told plainly; **never** partially committed. |
| **Frozen** | Pre-acceptance dispute (24) | Nothing transfers while frozen. Clock stopped. |
| **Executed — Settlement Pending** | Committed; payout in flight or held (failed rail, KYC, 24 suspension) | Seller: "Rights and credit are recorded. Your payment is {in transit / held because X}." The rights are never described as pending. |
| **Executed — Effect Pending Trigger** | Committed with a deferred grant (05.06.01 D-14) | Both parties, standing: "Signed. {Grant} takes effect if {trigger}." The **instrument** is never described as pending — it is signed and archived; only its effect waits. This is the distinction the whole decision turns on and the UI must not blur it. |
| **Vested** | The named trigger observed and notified (05.01.03 Q-07) | The second execution path ran. Both parties told what is now true, in the words the pending sentence used. The chain of title has a new link. |
| **Lapsed** | The trigger condition can no longer occur | The grant never takes effect and nothing is erased. The instrument, the credit and the money all stand as the facts they were. |
| **Executed — Money Reversed** | Chargeback upheld post-execution | Q-04. The state must exist even before the policy does, because the situation will occur regardless of whether the platform has decided what to do about it. |
| **Voided** | Transfer procured by fraud — no consent existed | The event is shown as void, not erased. DT-07. |
| **Superseded** | A later transfer between the same parties changed the posture | Both links visible in order. The chain of title is append-only. |

Surface states for the transfer summary and the confirmation view:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Composing the transfer from frozen data / reading 09's allocation | The Accept control shows in-progress and is non-resubmittable. No optimistic "Accepted!" — this is the one place in the product where an optimistic UI would tell someone they own something they may not. |
| **Empty** | Quote not yet accepted | No transfer exists. The listing's posture is shown as an *offer*, explicitly not as a deal. |
| **Populated** | `Disclosed` / `Armed` / `Executed` | The three-outcome summary, in plain language, in the same words at every stage. |
| **Error** | Commit failed closed; or points exceed 09's remaining allocation | The specific blocker named with its number ("only 2 of 5 points remain on this recording"), the state it left things in ("nothing has changed"), and the next action. Never a generic failure. |
| **Partial** | `Executed — Settlement Pending` / a credit emitted but not yet attested / points registered against a work with no identifier yet | Each leg's status shown **separately** — the buyer's title, the seller's money, and the record are three facts and must never be collapsed into one green tick. A single "Complete" badge over a held payout is how a user learns to distrust the confirmation. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The credit-emission leg is the one that will be treated as fire-and-forget, and its silent failure is the only one nobody would ever report** | Adversarial: of the three legs, which fails quietly? Follow the incentives of every party after a partial commit. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the single most important line in this feature. Consider each partial failure by who complains. Escrow fails: the seller complains within minutes — it is their rent. Rights fail: the buyer complains at their first sync request — maybe years later, but they complain. Credit fails: **nobody complains, ever.** The buyer has their master, the seller has their money, the engagement shows green, and the only thing missing is the record — which is the asset D-18 says is "the value they cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture". The failure is invisible to every human in the transaction and fatal to the platform's thesis, which means it will not be caught by support tickets, by testing that follows the money, or by any user-facing signal. It is also the leg an implementer is most likely to make asynchronous, because it feels like a notification rather than a transaction. Naming this explicitly at ideation time is the whole point of writing it down: the credit emission must be a **committed leg of the transaction**, not a downstream event, and its failure must page someone. Extended by DT-06 (what emits is a claim) and by the two-shapes table (it applies to milestone acceptance identically). |
| DT-02 | **Rights transfer by auto-accept may be unenforceable, which would hollow out the differentiator exactly where the buyer disengaged** | Adversarial, inherited from 05.04.01 DT-01 and followed through to its consequence here. | ✅ CONFIRMED as an exposure, ❌ NOT resolvable at ideation. Several jurisdictions require a **signed writing** for a copyright assignment; an unrebutted timer is unlikely to satisfy that formality. So the atomic commit may have a leg that is legally softer than the other two: the money genuinely moved, and the assignment may be void. The seller would be paid for an assignment that did not happen, and the buyer would hold a master they do not own and do not know they do not own — a *new* reconstruction problem, manufactured by the mechanism designed to end reconstruction. The obvious mitigation (auto-accept releases escrow; rights require an affirmative signature) breaks atomicity and re-opens the gap between payment and rights that D-18 says is "where most session litigation is born". Both options are bad; the choice needs counsel and an owner decision, not a design preference. **What is decidable now, and is (D-07)**: the consent basis is recorded permanently, as a distinction every downstream system can act on — `affirmative` or `by-expiry`. Whatever counsel decides, every downstream system — 09's title, 10's payouts, 24's disputes, a court in nine years — needs to know which acceptances rest on silence. It is a single durable distinction, and it is the difference between a known-weak link and an unknowable one. Sub-domain Q-01. |
| DT-03 | **The lazy path must be the correct path — the seller must never "file a split", they must accept money and have the split happen** | `meta/personas.md` states this as a design imperative in the Producer's anti-persona note: "The design must make the lazy path the correct path." | ✅ CONFIRMED as this feature's governing constraint, and it is what distinguishes the product from every failed predecessor. Jaxsta, Muso.AI and Sound Credit all ask someone to do an extra thing — to remember, to submit, to file. Nobody does the extra thing, which is why they reconstruct rather than capture. This feature's design test is therefore not "is the split sheet good?" but **"can a user complete this engagement without ever intending to file anything, and still end up with a signed split?"** If a user can get paid without the record being written, the feature has failed regardless of how well it works when used. Practical consequence: no confirmation step whose only purpose is the rights leg, no "would you like to record credits?" prompt, no skippable dialog. The rights execution is welded to the payment because the payment is the thing the user actually wants. **Corollary discovered while writing the states table**: this also forbids an optimistic UI on Accept. The lazy path being correct depends on the lazy user's belief matching reality — telling them "Accepted!" before the commit lands would make the laziest path *wrong* in exactly the cases that matter. |
| DT-04 | **"Atomic" cannot mean what it appears to mean, because one leg leaves the platform — and the naive reading makes a master assignment contingent on a session player's bank account** | Adversarial: what does a payments engineer know that this file did not say? Trace the word "atomic" through three systems with three different notions of "done". | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the most likely implementation defect in the feature. Escrow **release** is a ledger event: instantaneous, internal, transactional. **Payout** is a bank settlement: T+1 to T+5, external, and it fails routinely for boring reasons — a closed account, a KYC hold, a sanctions screen, a wrong IBAN. If the commit is specified as "money moves, rights move, credit emits, all or nothing", an implementer reading it honestly will put the payout inside the transaction, and then the first frozen payout account will attempt to roll back an executed copyright assignment — or, worse, will succeed. In multi-party supply (05.05.02 DT-03) this goes from bad to absurd: twelve players are paid from the buyer's release, so twelve payout rails each get a veto over the buyer's title. One player's bank holiday unwinds a master. The correct line is **the ledger is in the commit; the rail is not**. A failed payout means the platform owes the seller money — a real problem with a real remedy, and one entirely orthogonal to who owns the recording. This also buys the answer to the suspended-seller case for free: the platform can withhold money pending a 24 outcome without corrupting a title record, because withholding is a payout act and the title is already true. The same principle covers counter-attestation (DT-06) and identifier binding (DT-08): each is a real obligation that cannot be inside the commit, and the feature is only coherent once it says so explicitly. |
| DT-05 | **A chargeback reverses the money leg months after the commit, from outside the system, and produces exactly the unpaid assignment the feature exists to prevent** | Adversarial: what can undo a leg after the transaction is over, through a channel the atomicity mechanism does not control? | ✅ CONFIRMED as a live exposure; ❌ NOT resolvable without an owner decision. The atomic commit protects against *internal* partial failure. It has nothing to say about a card network reversing a payment unilaterally 30–120 days later (Visa/Mastercard windows run to roughly 120 days from the transaction or expected-delivery date, longer under some reason codes; the exact window is a payments-provider fact for `/create-prd`). The buyer accepts, the assignment executes, the seller is paid, and four months on the buyer's issuer claws the money back over a "services not rendered" claim that no amount of platform evidence can pre-empt. The platform now has: rights transferred, credit emitted, money gone. Three answers exist and all three are bad. **(a) Rights revert** — the chain of title becomes reversible by a third party the seller never dealt with, and the "permanent record" is permanent only until someone calls their bank. **(b) Rights stand, platform absorbs the loss** — honest, protects both users, and creates an obvious fraud economy: buy a master, accept, chargeback, keep the master, and the platform pays for it every time. **(c) Rights stand, seller is clawed back** — the seller is unpaid for an assignment that stands, which is the precise outcome D-18 names as "where most session litigation is born", now produced by the platform's own settlement policy. This is not a design preference; it is a decision about which party the platform is willing to make whole with its own money, and it has a direct cost. Q-04. What is decidable now: `Executed — Money Reversed` must exist as a state regardless, because the situation occurs whether or not the policy does. |
| DT-06 | **The credit leg emits a claim, not a fact — and counter-attestation can never be part of the commit, which means the platform's most valuable signal is structurally asynchronous** | Reasoning about who this execution names, versus who signed it. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it sharpens DT-01 rather than softening it. The instrument is signed by the parties: the buyer and the seller of record. But the credit follows the hands (05.05 invariant), and the hands are frequently not parties — a session player hired by a fixer, a member of a band entity that signed, a topline writer brought in by the producer. So one acceptance emits credits naming humans who never consented to this transaction and may not know it happened. Domain 02's counter-attestation is what makes a credit worth more than a self-claim — the sub-domain CX file already establishes "the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them" as a domain principle reached from three directions. Follow that here and the conclusion is unavoidable: **attestation requires a human at a later moment, so it cannot be inside an atomic commit, and it must not be**. A commit that waited for twelve players to confirm would never close, and the money would hang on a drummer checking their email. Therefore: the commit guarantees the **emission of an attributable claim**; the attestation is a separate, asynchronous, possibly-never-completed act. This is not a weakness — an emitted-but-unattested credit is already strictly better than the industry's status quo, because it is timestamped, attributable to a named claimant, and contestable by the person named. What it does require is that the product never render an unattested credit as if a person confirmed it, and that DT-01's paging requirement attach to the *emission*, not to the attestation. It also gives the honest answer to a credited party who contests: the claim stands as contested. Deleting it would let the loudest party edit history, which is the failure mode with the worst possible fit for this platform. |
| DT-07 | **"Reversal" is the wrong concept — a rights record cannot be un-happened, only appended to; and the one genuine exception is not a reversal at all** | Adversarial, working back from Q-03 and 05.06.01 Q-03: what would "reverse an executed transfer" actually mean in a system whose entire value is that the record survives? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it resolves 05.06.01 Q-03 and most of this feature's Q-03. Three situations get called "reversal" and they are three different things. **(1) Mutual agreement** — a WFH seller and buyer later agree on co-ownership. This is not an undo; it is a **new transfer in the opposite direction**, freely contracted by the same two parties, and recording it as one preserves the truth that the first transfer happened. Erasing the first link would delete the fact that the seller once assigned everything — which is exactly the kind of fact this platform exists to keep. So: append, mark the earlier link `Superseded`, and the chain of title stays honest. **(2) A successful dispute (24)** — a dispute is about the *deal*: was the work delivered, was it as described, is a refund owed. It is not a title action, and 24 should have **no power to move ownership by default**, because the alternative is a support decision silently rewriting a copyright record that 09, 10, 11 and 12 will all rely on for decades. A refund is a money remedy; if the parties also want the rights back, that is situation (1) and they can contract it. **(3) Fraud — account takeover, forged identity.** This is the genuine exception, and it is not a reversal: there was never consent, so the transfer was **void from the start**. That needs a `Voided` state distinct from `Superseded`, and the record must show the void **without erasing the event** — because a fraudulent transfer that leaves no trace is indistinguishable from a fraudulent transfer that was covered up, and the audit trail is the only defence either party has. The unifying principle: **the ledger is append-only; there is no eraser.** ❌ REJECTED, therefore, is the framing of Q-03 itself ("can an executed transfer be reversed?") — the answerable question is which state a later event puts the link into, and that is now specified. What remains for the owner is only (2)'s boundary: whether 24 may ever compel a counter-transfer. Q-03, narrowed. |
| DT-08 | **The thing being transferred has no identifier yet, and the step that gives it one is specified nowhere — without it the platform's provenance record cannot reach the money it exists to protect** | Domain-expert lens: what would a rights administrator expect that nobody has mentioned? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the gap most likely to be discovered late and expensively. Title here attaches to a **delivered artifact set** — a mix, a stem bundle, a performance — at a moment when it has no ISRC, no ISWC, no release, and quite possibly never will. That is correct and unavoidable: the engagement is usually the thing that *creates* the recording, so demanding an identifier at execution would mean nothing could ever transfer. But the entire economic point of a points participation is that domain 10 collects on it — and 10 collects against **identifiers**, because that is what PROs, the MLC, DSPs and every royalty source in the world key on. So there is a mandatory binding step, years downstream, at release (12): *this ISRC is that artifact set, whose chain of title is this*. Nothing in this domain owns that step; 12's release flow is where it would have to live, and 12 has no reason to know it is load-bearing for a split agreed three years earlier in a marketplace engagement. If the binding is missed, the failure is perfectly silent and perfectly total: the platform holds a beautiful, signed, timestamped, counter-attested record of a 3% master participation, and the royalties flow to whoever the label told the DSP about, forever. **The provenance is real and economically inert** — which is a more sophisticated version of exactly the failure D-18 says the industry already has. This also reframes DT-01's ranking: the credit leg's silent failure is the worst *inside* the commit, and identifier binding is its equal *outside* it. Recorded as Q-07, routed to `/create-prd-architecture` with 09 and 12, because the seam is theirs and neither of them is looking for it. |
| DT-09 | **The assignor may be an entity and the credited are always humans — and a commit that treats them as one party deletes the band** | Reasoning from `meta/personas.md`: "bands, labels, studios, venues and agencies are first-class entities that book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members." | ✅ CONFIRMED, and its value is that it is the **third independent route** to the same invariant. 05.05.02 DT-01 reached "the credit follows the hands" from the fixer case: pay the fixer, credit the fixer, and a twelve-piece string section vanishes. 05.06.01 DT-02 reached it from work-for-hire: the buyer owns everything, so credit the buyer, and every session player disappears from the deals where session players are most numerous. This is the third: a band signs as an entity, the entity is the assignor, and the tempting simplification is to credit the party that signed — which credits *the band* and deletes the drummer. Same deletion, third mechanism. A fourth appears in the edge cases: the multi-hyphenate who is both the seller of record and one of the hands, where a `same user → one row` dedupe silently drops the performance credit. Four arrivals at one rule, from four unrelated directions, is what makes it a **domain invariant** rather than a local guard: *the party that signs and the humans that are credited are different sets, and neither implies the other.* The implementer's instinct in every one of the four cases is the same shortcut, which is precisely why it is worth writing down four times. Practically: the transfer's assignor is whoever the frozen quote says signed, resolved through domain 01's delegated authority; the credit leg reads the hands from the delivery, not the signature block; and the two are composed independently in the same commit. |
| DT-10 | **The platform's core asset is a permanent record of what people did, and one of its own four personas can lawfully demand it be deleted — the sealed posture is already the answer** | Adversarial: what right does a user have that collides head-on with the thesis? Trace `meta/constraints.md`'s GDPR/DSAR line into this feature. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real collision with a genuinely available answer, ❌ NOT closable without counsel. D-18 says the record is "the value they cannot take with them"; GDPR says a data subject may request erasure. A credit names a person and is personal data. Superficially the two are irreconcilable, and the naive resolutions are both wrong: refusing erasure outright pretends the obligation isn't there, while honouring it as deletion means any credited party can silently punch a hole in a chain of title that a buyer, a PRO and a court all rely on — a self-service version of DT-01's silent failure. The way through is a distinction the platform has **already built for a different reason**: 05.02.02 models `Sealed` as "held, not displayed" — the platform keeps a record it does not show. Erasure of a credit maps onto exactly that. Additionally, a signed rights instrument and a royalty participation are records held for contract performance and legal obligation, not on consent, so the erasure right is materially narrower over them than over, say, a profile bio — the *display* of a name is a separable question from the *holding* of the instrument. So the design direction is: **erasure suppresses to sealed; it does not delete**, and the seller's own private record (which 05.02.02 already preserves for sealed work) is what keeps their claim provable if they ever need it. That is a real answer built from parts that already exist, which is a good sign it is the right one. It is still not a decision an ideation file may close — the balance between Art. 17 and the retention bases is exactly the kind of question that turns on facts and counsel. Sub-domain Q-06. |
| DT-11 | Adverse warranty declarations should block the transfer — the platform should not execute an assignment of a master with an uncleared sample or a generated part passed off as a performance | Protective instinct; tested because it is the obvious "safe" call | ❌ REJECTED, and the CX file's R-02 rejects the neighbouring version for the same reason. The buyer **saw** the declaration before they accepted — that is why 05.06.04/05.06.05 render with the delivery rather than at acceptance. Blocking would override an informed buyer's decision about their own record, on their behalf, in a transaction they understand better than the platform does. Worse, it makes honest disclosure the thing that costs the seller the sale, which is a direct incentive to declare falsely — and a false declaration is worth *less* than an honest one, because the whole mechanism runs on attributable claims. The platform would trade an accurate record of a flawed master for an inaccurate record of a clean one, which is a bad trade at any price. The correct posture is unchanged: disclose before the decision, warrant it, execute what the parties agreed, and let the warranty survive so the buyer has a remedy (05.06.04 D-05). The one asymmetry that stands is 05.06.05's: an AI declaration **changes what credit is emitted**, because a generated part produced no performance to credit — that is not blocking the transfer, it is composing it correctly. |

The **only** live read is 09's aggregate allocation. | Nothing about the deal may change between acceptance and execution; but points must validate against the recording, which only 09 can see (05.06.02 D-04). |

Consent at the end is not consent if it is the first time they have seen it. |
| D-06 | **The ledger is in the commit; the payment rail is not.** A failed payout never unwinds a rights transfer — it is a debt the platform owes the seller. | Otherwise a session player's KYC hold rolls back a master assignment. See DT-04. This is what makes withholding a suspended seller's money possible without corrupting a title. |
| D-07 | The consent basis is recorded **permanently, as a distinction every downstream system can act on** — `affirmative` or `by-expiry` | DT-02 is unresolved and may stay unresolved for years. Every downstream system needs to know which acceptances rest on silence. A single durable distinction; the difference between a known-weak link and an unknowable one. |
| D-08 | The commit emits a credit **claim**; counter-attestation is asynchronous and is **never** inside the commit | Attestation needs a human at a later moment. A commit that waited for twelve players to confirm would never close. See DT-06. |
| D-09 | Every human named by the credit leg is notified, **including non-parties** — session players, band members, anyone whose hands are in the delivery | The Producer's worst intentional behaviour is pushing terms through while contributors are not looking. The counter is that the contributor is told, not that the Producer is trusted. |
| D-10 | **The chain of title is append-only.** A posture change by mutual agreement is a new transfer (`Superseded`); a fraudulent transfer is `Voided` without erasure; there is no delete. | You cannot un-happen a fact, only add the next one. See DT-07. Answers 05.06.01 Q-03. |
| D-11 | A dispute (24) has **no power to move ownership by default** | A dispute is about the deal, not the title. A support decision silently rewriting a copyright record that 09/10/11/12 rely on for decades is not a remedy, it is a new problem. Refund is the money remedy; if the parties want rights back, they contract it (D-10). |
| D-12 | The transfer **fails closed**. Any leg unavailable → nothing changes, engagement returns to `Delivered`, and the auto-accept clock does not advance while the platform is the blocker. | A user must never lose ownership to an outage, and must never be timed out by our downtime. |
| D-13 | A given **engagement and its accepted quote version execute at most once**; an affirmative acceptance racing an expiry is recorded as **affirmative** | Replays, retries and races must not produce two transfers. The affirmative/expiry distinction is D-07's recorded consent basis. |
| D-14 | Adverse warranty declarations do **not** block the transfer | The buyer saw them before deciding. Blocking makes honesty cost the sale and manufactures false declarations. See DT-11. The AI declaration still forks the credit leg — that is composition, not blocking. |
| D-15 | Related-party transfers (identity-linked counterparties) execute normally, are **marked** on both the transfer and the credit, and are **excluded from rate benchmarking** | Self-dealing to manufacture reputation is a named persona anti-behaviour. Blocking would break the legitimate case (a person contracting with their own band entity); marking preserves it while denying the abuse its payoff. |
| D-16 | The party that **signs** and the humans that are **credited** are different sets, and neither implies the other | Four independent routes reach this rule (fixer, WFH, band entity, multi-hyphenate-as-hands). It is a domain invariant, not a local guard. See DT-09. |
| D-17 | No optimistic UI on Accept | This is the one place in the product where telling someone the action succeeded before it did would tell them they own something they may not. See DT-03's corollary. |
| D-18 | Each leg's status renders **separately** in any partial state — never one "Complete" badge over a held payout | The buyer's title, the seller's money and the record are three facts. Collapsing them is how a user learns to distrust the confirmation, which is the only artefact this feature produces. |
| D-19 | **A deferred-effect instrument (05.06.01 D-14) executes on two paths.** At acceptance: money and credit legs fire normally, the instrument is signed and archived, and a **contingent interest** is recorded on the 05.03.02 DT-01 model — the grant does not take effect. At the named trigger: a **second execution** takes the grant into effect, re-reads 09's aggregate at that moment, and **appends** a link. No money leg and no new credit on the second path; both settled at acceptance | The indivisible acceptance act (Overview, D-01) was written for deals that vest at acceptance and is unchanged for them.
