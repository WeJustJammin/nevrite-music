# Feature: Rights Transfer & Split Execution on Acceptance

> **Number**: 05.06.03
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The moment the paperwork becomes true. Acceptance releases the money and, in the same indivisible act, executes the rights posture (05.06.01), registers any points (05.06.02), and emits the credit (domain 02). The buyer pays and vests; the seller is paid and assigns; the record of who did what is written while everyone is present and agreeable.

This is the domain's stated differentiator and the point where D-18's causal claim — *consolidation is the precondition for provenance* — is either a working mechanism or a slogan. Every other feature in this domain exists to deliver two consenting parties to this moment.

## Behavior

Acceptance (05.04.01) fires an **atomic three-way commit**:

| Leg | System | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Payments, Escrow & Payouts cross-cut | Escrow releases to the seller (and, in multi-party supply, to every worker downstream — 05.05.02 DT-03) |
| Rights | Domain 09 | The posture executes: assignment, licence, co-ownership share, or points participation into 09/10 |
| Record | Domain 02 | The credit emits at the elected visibility (05.02.02), naming the person whose hands did the work (05.05 invariant) |

All three, or none. This is the **Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer** cross-cut, and this feature is its primary trigger site in the domain.

The instrument behind the transfer comes from the Contracts & E-Signature cross-cut. What is domain-owned is *when* it fires and *what it contains*.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer accepts a delivery (or auto-accept fires — see DT-02, which is a real problem)
2. System composes the transfer from frozen data: the accepted quote's posture, points, warranties, and artifact set
3. **Atomic commit**: escrow releases → rights execute into 09 → points register into 09/10 → credit emits to 02
4. Instrument is signed/countersigned and archived tamper-evidently (Contracts cross-cut)
5. Both parties receive a plain-language confirmation naming all three outcomes explicitly
6. The seller's credit graph, the buyer's chain of title, and both parties' finances are now consistent — on day one, not day nine hundred

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Escrow releases, credit emission fails | **The silent failure.** Both parties got what they came for; nobody will ever report it; the platform's core asset has a hole. | Must not be possible. See DT-01. |
| Rights execute, escrow release fails | The seller has assigned their copyright and not been paid — the worst outcome the platform could produce, and precisely what it exists to prevent | Must not be possible |
| Auto-accept fires (buyer silent) | A copyright assignment executed by inaction | 05.04.01 DT-01 / sub-domain Q-01 — **needs counsel** |
| Points exceed the recording's remaining allocation | 05.06.02 DT-02 — invisible until aggregated at 09 | Execution must validate against 09, not the engagement |
| Buyer lacked authority to grant points | 05.06.02 DT-03 | Surfaces years later as unpaid royalties |
| A warranty was false (uncleared sample) | The transfer completed; the buyer owns a liability | 05.06.04 — the warranty survives the transfer, which is the point of it |
| Multi-party engagement (05.05) | N credits, N settlements, one chain of title | The composition must be coherent (05.05.04 DT-01) |
| Sealed engagement (05.02.02) | The credit emits **suppressed**, not absent | 05.02.02 DT-01 — the platform holds a record it does not display |
| Cancelled engagement | Nothing transfers. Credits already earned on accepted milestones stand. | 05.03.05 D-06 |
| Milestone acceptance | Releases the tranche only — **no rights transfer** | 05.03.02 DT-01, deliberately |
| Recall after acceptance (05.03.06) | Work reopens; the transfer does **not** unwind | 05.03.06 DT-01 — post-terminal state |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Engagement in flight | "On acceptance: you'll assign X, keep Y, be paid Z" — visible throughout, not a surprise at the end |
| Executing | Acceptance fired | Brief; must be atomic |
| Executed | All three legs committed | Confirmation naming all three outcomes |
| Failed | Any leg failed | Rolled back entirely; both parties told plainly; **never** partially committed |
| Frozen | Dispute (24) | Nothing transfers while frozen |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The credit-emission leg is the one that will be treated as fire-and-forget, and its silent failure is the only one nobody would ever report** | Adversarial: of the three legs, which fails quietly? Follow the incentives of every party after a partial commit. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the single most important line in this feature. Consider each partial failure by who complains. Escrow fails: the seller complains within minutes — it is their rent. Rights fail: the buyer complains at their first sync request — maybe years later, but they complain. Credit fails: **nobody complains, ever.** The buyer has their master, the seller has their money, the engagement shows green, and the only thing missing is the record — which is the asset D-18 says is "the value they cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture". The failure is invisible to every human in the transaction and fatal to the platform's thesis, which means it will not be caught by support tickets, by testing that follows the money, or by any user-facing signal. It is also the leg an implementer is most likely to make asynchronous, because it feels like a notification rather than a transaction. Naming this explicitly at ideation time is the whole point of writing it down: the credit emission must be a **committed leg of the transaction**, not a downstream event, and its failure must page someone. |
| DT-02 | **Rights transfer by auto-accept may be unenforceable, which would hollow out the differentiator exactly where the buyer disengaged** | Adversarial, inherited from 05.04.01 DT-01 and followed through to its consequence here. | ✅ CONFIRMED as an exposure, ❌ NOT resolvable at ideation. Several jurisdictions require a **signed writing** for a copyright assignment; an unrebutted timer is unlikely to satisfy that formality. So the atomic commit may have a leg that is legally softer than the other two: the money genuinely moved, and the assignment may be void. The seller would be paid for an assignment that did not happen, and the buyer would hold a master they do not own and do not know they do not own — a *new* reconstruction problem, manufactured by the mechanism designed to end reconstruction. The obvious mitigation (auto-accept releases escrow; rights require an affirmative signature) breaks atomicity and re-opens the gap between payment and rights that D-18 says is "where most session litigation is born". Both options are bad; the choice needs counsel and an owner decision, not a design preference. Sub-domain Q-01. |
| DT-03 | **The lazy path must be the correct path — the seller must never "file a split", they must accept money and have the split happen** | `meta/personas.md` states this as a design imperative in the Producer's anti-persona note: "The design must make the lazy path the correct path." | ✅ CONFIRMED as this feature's governing constraint, and it is what distinguishes the product from every failed predecessor. Jaxsta, Muso.AI and Sound Credit all ask someone to do an extra thing — to remember, to submit, to file. Nobody does the extra thing, which is why they reconstruct rather than capture. This feature's design test is therefore not "is the split sheet good?" but **"can a user complete this engagement without ever intending to file anything, and still end up with a signed split?"** If a user can get paid without the record being written, the feature has failed regardless of how well it works when used. Practical consequence: no confirmation step whose only purpose is the rights leg, no "would you like to record credits?" prompt, no skippable dialog. The rights execution is welded to the payment because the payment is the thing the user actually wants. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Escrow release, rights execution and credit emission commit **atomically** — all three or none | Any partial commit is either unpaid assignment or a silent hole in the platform's core asset. |
| D-02 | The credit-emission leg is a **committed leg of the transaction**, never an async side-effect, and its failure must page a human | It is the only failure no user will ever report. See DT-01. |
| D-03 | The transfer composes only from **frozen** data — the accepted quote version | Nothing about the deal may change between acceptance and execution. |
| D-04 | No step in this flow exists solely to capture rights | The lazy path is the correct path. A skippable "record your credits?" prompt would be the whole product failing. See DT-03. |
| D-05 | The pending transfer's consequences are visible throughout the engagement, not revealed at acceptance | "On acceptance: you'll assign X, keep Y, be paid Z." Consent at the end is not consent if it is the first time they have seen it. |
