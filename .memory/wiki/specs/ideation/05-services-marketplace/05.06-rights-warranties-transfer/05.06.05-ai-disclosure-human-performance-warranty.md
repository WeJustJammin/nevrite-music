# Feature: AI Disclosure & Human-Performance Warranty

> **Number**: 05.06.05
> **Parent**: [Rights, Warranties & Transfer](./05.06-rights-warranties-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — **not in the sweep candidate list**

## Overview

The seller's declaration of what a machine did. A buyer paying £150 for "session bass, remote" is buying a bassist; if what arrives is generated, that is a different product, a different price, and — critically — a different **fact**. A credit that says a human played bass on a record where no human played bass is a false entry in the platform's core asset, and it would be created by the platform's own delivery flow.

The sweep produced no node for this. In 2026 that is a gap, not a judgement call.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona with the most at stake in both directions. As a seller, declares what they used — and the honest ones are competing directly against people who do not. As a buyer, knows what they are getting. The deeper exposure is to their own asset: their career is a verified credit graph, and that graph is worth exactly as much as its worst entry. A credit that says "bass, played by" and is a lie devalues every true credit next to it. |
| Producer | Full | Uses AI tools routinely and legitimately — stem separation, tuning, cleanup, drum replacement, denoise — and none of that makes a delivery "AI-generated". Also the party who *hires* performance and therefore relies on the declaration. This persona is why the feature cannot be a binary switch (DT-02): their normal working chain would trip it every time. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. Attendance is inherently human — an on-location engagement's evidence is that someone physically arrived (domain 02's Session Attendance Proof). |
| Fan | None | Not visible from this domain. Note: consumer-facing AI disclosure on *released* music is a live and separate question for domains 12 and 20, and it will be shaped by DSP policy and law, not by this feature. |

## Behavior

A declaration at delivery, sitting on the same surface as the clearance warranty (05.06.04) and behaving the same way: specific, timestamped, attributable, and a condition of the transfer.

It is **not** a binary. A useful declaration distinguishes what was made from what was processed:

| Declaration | Example | Credit consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Human-performed | A bassist played it | Full performance credit |
| Human-performed, AI-processed | Played, then tuned/denoised/separated | Full performance credit — the human played it |
| AI-assisted composition | Human-authored with generative tools in the loop | `[PENDING]` — a real authorship question |
| AI-generated | No human performance | **No performance credit.** Nobody played it. |

The line the platform actually needs is narrow and answerable: **did a human perform this?** Everything else — was a plugin clever, was a tool generative — is a question about tools, and musicians have been arguing about tools since the Fairlight.

### Happy Path

1. Seller delivers a performance service (05.04.01)
2. System requires the human-performance declaration alongside the clearance warranty (05.06.04)
3. Seller declares
4. Buyer sees it before accepting
5. Acceptance executes the transfer; **the credit emitted to domain 02 reflects the declaration** — a generated part produces no performance credit
6. Declaration archived, attributable

### States

**Declaration lifecycle** — the semantic state of the declaration itself:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Required | Delivery of a performance service | Cannot publish without declaring |
| Declared — Human | Seller declares | Full credit path |
| Declared — Human + AI processing | Seller declares | Full credit path; processing noted |
| Declared — AI-generated | Seller declares | **No performance credit emitted** |
| Archived | Acceptance | Permanent, attributable |
| Breached | Later found false | 24 — integrity, not commerce |

**Surface states** — how the declaration renders on the delivery surface (05.04.01), for the FE spec writer:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty (seller) | Delivery started, nothing declared yet | Declaration control alongside the clearance warranty (05.06.04); publish blocked until answered — the "did a human perform this?" question with the human / human+AI-processing / AI-generated options |
| Populated (seller) | Seller answers | The chosen declaration echoed back, with a plain-language statement of the credit consequence ("A performance credit will be issued" / "No performance credit — nobody played this") before they commit |
| Loading | Acceptance submitted, transfer executing | Progress indicator; declaration is being written into the acceptance transaction (05.06.03) atomically with the transfer |
| Populated (buyer) | Buyer opens delivery to review | The seller's declaration shown **before** the accept action and before the buyer's clock starts — a first-class term, not fine print |
| Error | Declaration write fails, or delivery has no valid performance-service context | Transfer does not proceed; declaration is never silently dropped — retry with the same declaration, or the delivery stays undelivered |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **A false human-performance declaration is not a commercial dispute — it is an attack on the platform's core asset, and it must be classified as one** | Adversarial: trace a generated "session performance" through 02 and D-18. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and the classification is the feature's whole point. If a generated bass part is delivered as a human performance, the acceptance transaction (05.06.03) faithfully emits a credit saying a named human played bass on that record — captured "at source", counter-attested, timestamped, and completely false. D-18 says the credit graph is "the value they cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture"; its worth rests entirely on being **true by construction**. One false credit is a bad credit; a *systematic path* by which false credits enter through the normal happy path is a corrupted asset, and no amount of later cleanup restores confidence in a graph that is known to accept lies. The consequence for design: this cannot be handled as a refund or a rating. A seller who falsely declares human performance has not delivered a bad product, they have poisoned the record — which puts it in domain 24 as an **integrity offence**, alongside credit inflation (`meta/personas.md`'s stated Musician worst-intentional behaviour), not in the commercial dispute path. The remedy is also different: refunding the buyer does nothing about the false credit, which must be retracted. |
| DT-02 | AI disclosure is a binary — AI or not | The obvious framing, and wrong enough to be harmful | ❌ REJECTED — a binary would fire on virtually every professional delivery and therefore mean nothing within a month. Melodyne is AI-adjacent; stem separation is a neural net; drum replacement, denoise, dialogue isolation and modern tuning all involve models. A Producer's ordinary chain would trip a naive "used AI?" flag on every job, sellers would learn the flag is noise, and buyers would learn to ignore it — destroying the disclosure exactly when it starts to matter. The distinction that carries actual meaning is narrow: **did a human perform this?** A part that was played and then tuned was *played*. A part that was generated was not. That line is answerable by the seller without philosophy, maps cleanly onto the credit consequence (a performance credit requires a performance), and does not require the platform to adjudicate whether a tool is "AI" — a question that has no stable answer and never will. |
| DT-03 | The platform can detect AI-generated audio | The tempting technical fix | ❌ REJECTED — for the same reasons as sample detection (05.06.04 DT-02) and more decisively. Detection of generated audio is unreliable today, adversarially fragile, and degrades continuously as generators improve, so any accuracy claim expires quietly. Worse, the failure modes are asymmetric and both bad: a false positive accuses a working musician of faking a performance they gave — a career-level accusation from an automated system — and a false negative issues a clean bill that a buyer relies on. And a "human verified" badge derived from a model would be the strongest implicit assurance the platform ever made, on the one claim its entire value rests on. **Declaration, not detection**, for the third time in this domain (05.04.02 DT-01, 05.06.04 DT-02) — which is a strong enough repetition to be a domain-level principle: *the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them.* What the platform has instead, and what nobody else has, is the counter-attestation of the people who were in the room (domain 02). A generated bass part is hard to falsely attest when the producer, the engineer and the drummer all have to confirm it — which is a social verification mechanism, not a technical one, and it is the platform's actual advantage here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The line is **"did a human perform this?"** — not "was AI used?" | A binary would fire on every professional chain and mean nothing within a month. See DT-02. |
| D-02 | **Declaration, not detection** | Third occurrence of this principle in the domain. Detection here would produce career-level false accusations and a badge the platform cannot stand behind. See DT-03. |
| D-03 | An AI-generated part emits **no performance credit** | Nobody played it. This is the declaration's whole consequence and the reason it is a rights feature rather than metadata. |
| D-04 | A false declaration is an integrity offence (24), not a commercial dispute | Refunding the buyer does not retract the false credit. The harm is to the graph, not the transaction. See DT-01. |
| D-05 | AI processing of a human performance is **not** disclosable as AI-generated | The human played it. Tuning is not generation. |
