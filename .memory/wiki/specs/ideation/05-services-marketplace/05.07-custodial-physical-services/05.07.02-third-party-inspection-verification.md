# Feature: Third-Party Inspection & Verification Reports

> **Number**: 05.07.02
> **Parent**: [Custodial & Physical Services](./05.07-custodial-physical-services-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Paying an independent expert to look at something before money changes hands: a tech checking a £6,000 vintage amp before a stranger buys it, a luthier verifying that a "1964 Strat" is a 1964 Strat, an engineer confirming a console works before it is shipped across a continent. The service is scoped human expertise — this domain's unit — but its **value lands in another domain**: the report is what lets a gear buyer release escrow to a stranger with confidence.

Used gear is the market where condition *is* the price (D-14), and where the buyer cannot see the thing. That is exactly the gap an inspection fills.

## Behavior

An inspection is a **micro-engagement whose output is a report**, and whose purpose is to gate someone else's transaction. Three parties, not two: the requester (usually the gear buyer), the inspector, and the gear seller who must permit access to their item.

The report is structured, not prose — a checklist against a per-category template (electronics, guitars/basses, drums, pianos, PA/console) with photographs and a verdict. Structure matters because the report's job is to be *acted on* by a gear escrow release, and a paragraph of opinion cannot gate anything.

Independence is the entire product. An inspector with a relationship to either party is worth nothing, and this is the one feature in the domain that needs a conflict-of-interest check.

### Happy Path

1. Gear buyer (domain 13) is considering a listing and requests an inspection
2. System matches inspectors by category and proximity (Geo cross-cut) — the item does not move, the inspector does
3. Gear seller consents to access — they can refuse, and refusal is itself information
4. Inspector attends, works the category checklist, photographs, files the report
5. Report delivers to the requester; the inspection engagement's escrow releases (the inspector is paid for looking, whatever they found)
6. **The gear transaction acts on it** (domain 13): buyer proceeds, renegotiates, or walks

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The inspection's value is entirely in domain 13 — it exists to gate a gear escrow release, not to produce a report** | Adversarial: why does anyone buy this? Follow the purpose, not the deliverable. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it reframes the feature. Nobody wants an inspection report. They want to send £6,000 to a stranger for an amp they have only seen photographed, and the report is the instrument that makes that survivable. So the feature's design constraint comes from **domain 13**: the report must be structured enough to be *acted on* by an escrow decision, delivered inside the window during which the gear listing is still available, and trustworthy enough that a buyer will move money on it. A beautifully-written prose assessment delivered four days after the amp sold to someone else is a perfect failure. This also settles the classification (sub-domain D-03): the *labour* is this domain's (scoped human expertise, D-14's unit), and the *trigger and consequence* are 13's. It is a cross-domain touchpoint, not a misplaced node — and 13's drilling should expect an "inspection pending" hold state on a gear listing that this domain cannot create for it. |
| DT-02 | **Independence is the whole product, and this is the only feature in the domain that needs a conflict-of-interest check — which the platform is uniquely able to run** | Adversarial: what would a domain expert expect that nobody mentioned? | ✅ CONFIRMED, twice over. First: an inspector with a relationship to the gear seller is worth precisely nothing, and the used-gear trade is small and incestuous — the local amp tech probably knows the local amp dealer. Every other feature in this domain assumes two parties who *chose* each other; this one assumes a stranger whose value is that they are a stranger. Second, and more interesting: **the platform can actually detect this**, because domain 03 holds a collaboration graph derived from verified work and domain 02 holds a credit graph. "This inspector has done nine jobs for this seller" is a fact the platform has and no classifieds site does. That turns an unenforceable ethical norm into a check. The unresolved tension: an inspection that finds work naturally becomes a repair estimate for the same inspector (05.07.01), which is a *structural* incentive to find work — the same conflict a car MOT centre that also does repairs has, and one the trade has never solved. Naming it is the minimum; whether the platform separates the roles is Q-02. |
| DT-03 | The inspector should be paid only if the deal proceeds | Aligns incentives with the buyer — and is exactly backwards | ❌ REJECTED, and the inversion is the point. If the inspector is paid only on a completed sale, they are financially motivated to pass everything — which destroys the only thing the buyer is purchasing. The fee buys **the looking**, not a verdict, and the inspector must be paid identically for "it's immaculate" and "the transformer has been replaced and it's not a '64". This is a small decision that determines whether the feature has any value at all, and it is the sort of thing an incentive-alignment instinct would get wrong on the way past. It also means the inspection engagement's escrow releases on **report delivery** — independent of the gear transaction it exists to gate — which is why it is a self-contained micro-engagement rather than a stage inside 13's purchase flow. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The report is **structured against a per-category template**, not prose | Its job is to be acted on by an escrow decision. A paragraph of opinion cannot gate anything. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | The inspector is paid on **report delivery**, regardless of the report's contents or the gear deal's outcome | Paying on a completed sale motivates passing everything, destroying the only thing being bought. See DT-03. |
| D-03 | Conflict of interest is checked against the platform's collaboration and credit graphs, not just self-declared | The platform can see "nine jobs for this seller". Nobody else can. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | The inspection is a self-contained micro-engagement in domain 05; the trigger and consequence are domain 13's | Sub-domain D-03. Cross-domain touchpoint, not a relocation. |
| D-05 | The inspector is not a warranty or an insurer | Same posture as 05.06.04 DT-03. A missed fault is a professional judgement, not a guarantee breach. |
