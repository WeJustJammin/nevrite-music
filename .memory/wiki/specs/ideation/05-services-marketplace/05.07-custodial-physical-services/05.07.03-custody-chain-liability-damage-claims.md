## Overview

Who is holding the thing, in what condition, since when — and what happens when it is damaged, lost, or trapped in a workshop that has closed. The sweep candidate named "custodial intake" as a step in the repair flow and modelled none of its consequences. But the custodial fact is not a step; it is the entire risk model of this sub-domain, and it is the only place in domain 05 where the platform brokers the handover of an irreplaceable physical object to a stranger.

A mix engagement's worst outcome is a bad mix and a refund. A repair engagement's worst outcome is a 1959 Les Paul that no longer exists.

## Behavior

A custody record is a chain of **hand-offs**, each with a timestamp, a holder, and a condition state agreed by both parties at the moment of transfer:

```
owner → carrier → seller(workshop) → carrier → owner
```

Each edge is where liability moves and where a dispute will later be anchored. The condition record at each hand-off is the entire evidentiary basis — it is what turns "it had that scratch when you got it" from an argument into a fact. Photographs at intake are the trade's own existing habit; the platform's contribution is making them timestamped, mutual and permanent.

**Declared value** is the field everything else hangs on: it sets the shipping insurance, sizes the seller's exposure, and determines whether a job is worth taking. It is also unverifiable — the platform cannot know a guitar is worth £40,000 — and both parties have opposite incentives to misstate it.

The platform is **not** the insurer (DT-03). It is the record.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer books a custodial job; declares the item and its value
2. Item ships (Shipping cross-cut) insured to the declared value, or is dropped off
3. **Intake condition record**: both parties photograph and confirm the state. Custody transfers to the seller.
4. Work happens (05.07.01). Custody remains with the seller throughout.
5. Return ships; **return condition record**; custody transfers back
6. Buyer confirms receipt and condition; escrow releases; the chain closes complete

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Damage found at return | The claim this feature exists for | Both condition records compared; the delta is the claim |
| Damage was pre-existing | The most common dispute, and the intake record settles it | Which is why intake is mandatory (05.07.01 D-04) |
| Damage in transit | Carrier's liability, and the parties will argue about which leg | The two condition records bracket the carrier's window |
| Item lost in transit | Carrier's insurance, capped at declared value | And the declared value is what nobody thought about at booking |
| Item damaged in the workshop | Seller's liability, and whether it is collectable depends on insurance | Sub-domain Q-01 — **the feature's central unresolved question** |
| Seller has no insurance | Common among independent luthiers, who are the best ones | The claim is against a person, not a policy |
| Workshop closes / seller vanishes with the item | The nightmare, and it happens | Outside what a marketplace can fix. Not outside what it should have recorded. See DT-02. |
| Declared value understated (to save shipping cost) | Buyer's own choice, and it caps their own claim | Must be said at declaration time, not discovered at claim time |
| Declared value overstated (to inflate a claim) | Fraud | Nothing verifies it — see DT-01 |
| Item is irreplaceable at any value | A 1959 Les Paul. Money is not the remedy. | The honest limit of the whole model (DT-02) |
| Buyer abandons the item | Custody with no end | 05.07.01 Q-02 — a lien is a legal instrument the platform cannot grant |
| Repair alters the instrument permanently (a refinish, a re-fret on a vintage neck) | Not damage — it is the job. And it may destroy the instrument's value. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Declared value is the field everything hangs on, it is unverifiable, and both parties have opposite incentives to misstate it** | Adversarial: what single field carries the most risk here, and who lies about it? | ✅ CONFIRMED. Declared value sets the shipping insurance, sizes the seller's exposure, and determines whether the job is even worth taking — a luthier's decision to accept a £200 setup is entirely different if the guitar is worth £600 or £60,000. Yet nothing verifies it. The **buyer** is pulled two ways: understate it and shipping is cheaper (and their own claim is capped, which they will not think about until the day it matters); overstate it and a claim is inflated. The **seller** wants it accurate before accepting and irrelevant afterwards. And the platform, which is neither, is the only party with a stake in it being true and no way to establish it. Two mitigations exist and neither is complete: an inspection (05.07.02) can establish value *before* handover, which is a real synergy between two features that looked unrelated; and domain 15's Gear Registry — one of the map's two whitespace domains — is precisely a place where an instrument's identity, provenance and value could already be established before anyone needs it in a hurry. That is the strongest argument found in this drilling for 15's existence, and it arrived from an unexpected direction. |
| DT-02 | **The worst custodial outcomes are not compensable, and the platform must not imply otherwise** | Adversarial: what does this feature actually fix, and what does it only witness? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it defines the feature's honest scope. Some outcomes have a number: a lost flight case, a damaged modern amp, a snapped mic stand — insurance and a claim resolve them. Others do not. A 1959 Les Paul destroyed in a workshop fire is not a £300,000 problem, it is an *irreplaceable object that no longer exists*, and no payout is a remedy. Similarly, a workshop that closes with twelve instruments inside is a legal mess the platform can neither prevent nor unwind. The temptation — for a marketplace that wants to convert this category — is to paper over that with reassuring language: "Protected", "Guaranteed", "Covered". That would be the same implicit-assurance trap the domain has now rejected three separate times (05.04.02 DT-01 on QC badges, 05.06.04 DT-03 on warranties, 05.06.05 DT-03 on AI detection), and here the stakes are the highest of the three. What the platform honestly provides is a **chain of custody**: who had it, since when, in what condition, evidenced mutually and timestamped. That is genuinely better than the current world of a paper tag and a phone number, and it is not insurance. The copy must say the smaller true thing. |
| DT-03 | Damage and legitimate alteration are the same kind of event | Naive framing — a delta in condition is a delta | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is peculiar to this trade. A refret **permanently alters** the instrument. A refinish destroys a vintage guitar's value outright, and a re-fret on an original vintage neck can halve it. Every one of those is a condition delta between intake and return — and every one is *the job the buyer paid for*. So the condition-record comparison cannot mechanically flag deltas as damage; it must distinguish **authorised alteration** (in the approved estimate) from **unauthorised change** (everything else). This is why the estimate approval (05.07.01) and the custody record are coupled: the approved estimate is the *specification of permitted change*, and the custody chain measures against it rather than against the intake state. A model that treated any change as damage would flag every completed job; a model that ignored change would flag none. The interesting corollary is that a technically-excellent repair can be a catastrophic loss of value, and the platform is not the right party to warn about that — but an inspection (05.07.02) beforehand would be. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Custody is a chain of hand-offs, each with a mutual, timestamped condition record | Each edge is where liability moves and where a dispute will be anchored. |
| D-02 | Declared value is required at booking, is unverifiable, and its consequences are stated at declaration time | Both parties have opposite incentives to misstate it, and the buyer capping their own claim must be told so before it matters. See DT-01. |
| D-03 | The platform provides a **record**, not insurance — and the copy must say the smaller true thing | The worst outcomes are not compensable. Reassuring language would be the implicit-assurance trap at its highest stakes. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | The approved estimate is the **specification of permitted change**; the custody record measures against it, not against the intake state | Otherwise every completed refret is flagged as damage. See DT-03. |
| D-05 | Escrow covers the fee, never the item | An honest statement of the gap: between intake and approval, a stranger holds valuable property with nothing in escrow (05.07.01 DT-02). |
