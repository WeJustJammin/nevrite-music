# Feature: Repair, Tech & Luthier Job Flow

> **Number**: 05.07.01
> **Parent**: [Custodial & Physical Services](./05.07-custodial-physical-services-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The workshop job: a guitar needs a refret, a console has a dead channel, a piano needs regulating. The instrument arrives, the tech assesses it, quotes what it actually needs (which is never what the owner thought), the owner approves, the work happens, the thing goes home. It is one of the few services in this domain where the seller cannot quote accurately without physically holding the item first — which inverts the whole quote-then-work order the rest of the domain is built on.

## Behavior

The flow inverts the domain's normal order because the estimate cannot precede possession:

```
intake (+ condition record) → assess → estimate → approve → work → return
```

There is no requirements gate — the requirement is a physical object that either arrived or did not. There is no revision allowance — a refret is not iterated. There is no auto-accept window — the buyer accepts by receiving their instrument back, which is a physical event the platform cannot fire on a timer. And there is no rights transfer — repairing a guitar creates no copyright.

**The estimate is the fork.** Approve and work proceeds. Decline and the item must come home, which means someone pays for return shipping on a job that never happened.

Scope discovery mid-job is normal, not exceptional: the tech opens it up and finds the truss rod is seized. That is a revised estimate requiring fresh approval, with the instrument already in pieces on someone else's bench — which is the least favourable negotiating position a buyer can be in, and the platform should not pretend otherwise.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer selects a repair listing (05.01.01, custodial craft — the listing form swaps to intake fields)
2. Item ships to the seller (Shipping cross-cut) or is dropped off; **condition recorded at intake by both parties** (05.07.03)
3. Seller assesses and issues an estimate — the real quote, now that they have seen it
4. Buyer approves; escrow captures **at approval**, not at intake (DT-02)
5. Work proceeds; job ticket shows status
6. Item returns; buyer confirms receipt and condition; escrow releases
7. Service record is available to write to Gear Registry (15) — sub-domain Q-02

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **A repair inverts the domain's quote-then-work order, because the seller cannot quote until they are holding the thing** | Reasoning about why this feels wrong against 05.02/05.03. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is why 05.07 is a separate sub-domain rather than a craft in 05.01 (sub-domain D-02). Everywhere else in this domain the quote precedes possession: a mixer hears the stems, prices the job, and the buyer decides before anything of theirs leaves their hands. Here the sequence is intake → assess → estimate → approve, which means **the buyer commits their property before they know the price**. Every consequence follows from that inversion. The estimate is the real quote, so 05.02's whole apparatus (immutable versions, expiry, diffs) applies to the *estimate*, not to the booking. The decline path is expensive rather than free, because the item is 200 miles away in someone's workshop. And the buyer's leverage is structurally weakest at the exact moment the price is set — which is a fact about the trade, not a flaw the platform introduced, but it does mean the platform should not import a flow that assumes an informed, uncommitted buyer. |
| DT-02 | **Escrow must capture at estimate approval, not at booking** | Adversarial: when does the money move, and what breaks either way? | ✅ CONFIRMED. Capturing at booking would mean holding money against a price nobody knows yet — the booking fee is not the job, and the job might be four times it or might be declined entirely. Capturing at approval aligns the money with the moment a real number exists and a real commitment is made, and it means a declined estimate involves no refund at all, only the return-shipping question. The consequence is a genuine gap the platform must be honest about: **between intake and approval, the seller is holding valuable property with no money in escrow and no committed job.** That window is where every custodial horror story lives, and the only thing covering it is the custody chain (05.07.03) — evidence, not money. Worth stating plainly rather than papering over: escrow does not protect the item, it protects the fee, and the item is the valuable part. |
| DT-03 | A repair engagement should reuse the standard lifecycle with the odd states left empty | Consolidation instinct — one state machine is cheaper | ❌ REJECTED. Count what would be empty: requirements gate (no — the requirement is an object), turnaround clock start condition (no — it starts at approval, not at a gate), revision allowance (no — a refret is not iterated), auto-accept (no — acceptance is a physical event; a timer cannot fire "the guitar came back"), delivery + QC (no — nothing is uploaded), rights transfer (no — no copyright is created), source-file handover (no), draft protection (no). That is not a lifecycle with a few unused fields; it is a **different machine with a couple of coincidental overlaps** (an estimate resembles a quote, escrow releases at the end). Forcing them together would produce states that are nonsense on one side, and — worse — would tempt an implementer to fire auto-accept on a repair, which would pay a workshop for a guitar that never came home. The two flows share the engagement *entity* and almost nothing else. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The flow is intake → assess → estimate → approve → work → return | The seller cannot quote until they hold the item. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Escrow captures at **estimate approval**, not at booking | Aligns money with a real number and a real commitment. Accepts an honest gap: between intake and approval the item is held with nothing in escrow. See DT-02. |
| D-03 | This flow does **not** reuse the 05.03 lifecycle | It is a different machine, not a lifecycle with empty fields. Reusing it would risk auto-accepting a guitar that never came home. See DT-03. |
| D-04 | A condition record at intake, confirmed by both parties, is mandatory | It is the only thing that settles a later damage argument, and it costs two minutes and some photos. |
| D-05 | Estimates separate labour from parts | Parts have a cost and a lead time the seller does not control; bundling them hides both. |
