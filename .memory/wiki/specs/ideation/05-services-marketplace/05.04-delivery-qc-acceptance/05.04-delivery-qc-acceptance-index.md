## Overview

The handover: what the seller must produce, how the platform checks it is technically sound before the buyer's clock starts, how a draft is protected while the buyer evaluates it, which artifacts change hands, and the acceptance event that ends the engagement. Acceptance is the most consequential moment in the domain — it is the single trigger that releases escrow, executes the rights transfer, and emits the credit, and it is where the platform's thesis either happens or does not.

**Why a sub-domain, not a feature**: the capabilities are causally chained and mutually constraining. The deliverable spec defines what QC can check; QC must gate delivery rather than acceptance, or the buyer's auto-accept clock runs on a broken file; watermarking protects the draft during evaluation but cannot degrade the audio the buyer is evaluating; and the artifact set (mix? stems? session?) is a separate negotiation from the rights that attach to it.

> **Operator and Fan are None across this entire sub-domain.** This is a deliberate and complete absence, not an oversight. An Operator's engagements deliver **attendance**, not files — an on-location tracking date has no deliverable to QC, no draft to watermark and no session to hand over; their evidence of performance is domain 02's Session Attendance Proof. The Fan must never reach an unreleased deliverable from any surface: this sub-domain holds the platform's highest-value, most leak-sensitive audio, and the Fan lens is where a leak does commercial damage.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | **Source File & Session Handover added — not in the sweep candidate list** | Deep Think addition. The candidate list had a deliverable *spec* (format, LUFS) and rights *transfer* (who owns the copyright), but nothing about which **artifacts** change hands. In music these are three separate negotiations, and the artifact one is the most argued: mix engineers do not hand over session files by default, and every engagement that ends badly ends over this. See 05.04.04 DT-01. | Deep Think, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | QC gates **delivery**, not acceptance | A technically broken file must never reach the buyer and start their auto-accept clock. Gating at acceptance means the buyer's window runs on a file that was never valid. See 05.04.02 DT-02. | Agent, domain expertise |
| D-03 | Acceptance is atomic across three systems: escrow release, rights transfer, credit emission | A partial failure leaves a paid engagement with no credit, or a transferred right with no payment. This is the Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer cross-cut's exact purpose, and this sub-domain is its primary trigger site. See 05.04.01 DT-02. | Agent, D-18 thesis |
| D-04 | The QC **rules** are product-owned; the DSP **engine** is not | What a deliverable must satisfy (48k/24-bit, -14 LUFS integrated, no true-peak over -1 dBTP, no clipping, no DC offset, correct duration) is product. Executing loudness/spectral analysis over full-length multitracks is infrastructure, it belongs to the Media Handling cross-cut, and it collides with the locked Cloudflare Workers constraint. Routed to `/create-prd`. | Agent, Node Classification Gate |
