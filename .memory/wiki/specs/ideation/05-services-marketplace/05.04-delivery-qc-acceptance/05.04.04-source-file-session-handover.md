# Feature: Source File & Session Handover

> **Number**: 05.04.04
> **Parent**: [Delivery, QC & Acceptance](./05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance-index.md)

> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Which **artifacts** change hands, as opposed to which rights. A mix engagement can deliver: a stereo mix; a stereo mix plus alternates (instrumental, TV mix, a-capella); mix stems; the raw multitrack; or the DAW session itself. These are five different products with wildly different values, and the industry's strong default — which almost no client knows — is that **you get the mix, not the session**.

The candidate list had a deliverable *spec* (format, LUFS) and rights *transfer* (who owns the copyright). It had nothing about the artifact set, which is the thing engagements actually argue about.

## Behavior

The artifact set is declared on the listing (05.01.01), priced (often as an add-on — 05.01.01 DT-02), agreed on the quote (05.02.01), and delivered at 05.04.01.

| Artifact | Typical default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stereo mix / master | Always included | The deliverable |
| Alternates (instrumental, TV, a-capella) | Often an add-on | Frequently needed later for sync (11) — the classic recall request (05.03.06) |
| Mix stems | Add-on | Standard for film/TV; a premium for records |
| Raw multitrack | Usually the **buyer's** already | They supplied it as requirements (05.03.01) |
| Consolidated WAVs from zero | The portable compromise | Opens in any DAW — see DT-02 |
| **DAW session** | **Usually NOT included** | The contested artifact. Often refused; when offered, priced. |

Crucially, the artifact question is **orthogonal to rights** (05.06.01). A buyer can own the master outright (work-for-hire) and still not receive the session. Owning a copyright does not entitle you to the artifacts used to create it, and clients almost universally assume otherwise.

### Happy Path

1. Seller declares the artifact set per listing tier, and prices session handover separately if they offer it
2. Buyer sees, on the quote, an explicit list of what they receive — including a plain statement that the session is or is not included
3. If session handover is elected, the format is stated (native session + version, or consolidated WAVs from zero)
4. Delivery includes exactly the agreed set (05.04.01); QC checks what it can (05.04.02)
5. Acceptance transfers the agreed rights and hands over the agreed artifacts — two separate things happening at once

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer assumes the session was included | The archetypal dispute, and it is an *assumption*, not a taste judgement — the class 05.02.01 DT-01's structured exclusions exist to eliminate | Quote states it explicitly; the exclusion list makes it unmissable |
| Session handed over, buyer cannot open it (Pro Tools session, Logic user) | The artifact is real and worthless to them | Format must be stated. See DT-02. |
| Session depends on plugins the buyer does not own | Extremely common; the session opens with half the chain missing and sounds wrong | "This session uses 14 plugins. You'd need them to open it as mixed." |
| Buyer owns the master (WFH) but not the session | Correct and counter-intuitive | Rights and artifacts are orthogonal (DT-03) |
| Recall requested, seller archived the session (05.03.06 DT-02) | The recall is impossible through no fault | This is the second, non-obvious argument for handover: a buyer holding the session can have the recall done elsewhere |
| Session contains third-party material (samples, another artist's stems from a prior session) | Handing it over leaks material the seller had no right to pass on; the platform brokering the transfer is a party to the leak | Handover flow surfaces a clearance attestation before transfer — "confirm this session contains no material you can't pass on"; deep enforcement detail `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` and it touches 05.06.04 |
| Session reveals the seller's templates/chain/method | Not a bug — it is the actual reason for refusal, and it is legitimate | Refusal must be a supported, non-pejorative option |
| Buyer demands the session after acceptance | Not a recall; a new negotiation | "That wasn't in scope — want a quote?" |
| Session is 80 GB | Storage/transfer reality | Media Handling cross-cut; retention cost is real |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Mix engineers do not hand over session files by default, and the platform must make this explicit or every engagement inherits the industry's most common unspoken argument** | Domain expertise: what does an expert know here that no candidate mentioned? | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the reason this node exists. The professional norm is well established and almost universally unknown to clients: **you commissioned a mix, you get a mix.** The session is the engineer's working method — their chain, their templates, their routing, the accumulated craft of a career — and handing it over is qualitatively different from delivering the work. Many engineers refuse outright at any price; those who do offer it charge a premium, because they are selling their method, not their output. The client's assumption is the exact opposite ("I paid for it, it's mine"), and both parties believe their position is so obvious it needs no stating — which is the precise anatomy of a dispute that only surfaces once the money has moved and the relationship is over. A platform that does not force this into the open at quote time is not neutral; it is silently importing the argument into every engagement. Making it an explicit, priced, per-listing declaration costs one field and removes an entire dispute class. |
| DT-02 | **A session file is DAW-locked, so "the session" is not one artifact and handover must specify a format** | Reasoning about what actually arrives | ✅ CONFIRMED — "you'll get the session" is not a meaningful promise. A Pro Tools session is inert to a Logic user; even the same DAW at a different version, or without the same plugins, opens a session that is missing its chain and sounds wrong. So handover has at least three shapes: **native session** (only useful to the same-DAW, same-plugins recipient), **consolidated WAVs from zero** (opens anywhere, loses every plugin and automation but is genuinely portable — the pragmatic compromise the industry already uses), and **stems** (a different thing again). A plugin manifest should accompany a native session, because the buyer's real question is not "can I open it" but "can I open it and have it sound like what I bought". None of this is exotic — it is the daily reality of the craft — and it is invisible to a marketplace model that treats delivery as "a file". |
| DT-03 | **Rights and artifacts are orthogonal, and conflating them is the domain's most seductive error** | Adversarial: trace this feature against 05.06.01 | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is worth stating explicitly in the product, not just the spec. A buyer can hold a full work-for-hire assignment of the master — own the copyright outright, absolutely, forever — and still have no entitlement to the DAW session that produced it. Owning a recording is not owning the tools or the working files used to make it. Clients find this counter-intuitive to the point of disbelief ("I own it, so give me everything"), and an implementer with a tidy mental model will be tempted to bundle artifacts into the rights posture — a WFH tier that "includes everything" — which would quietly force every engineer selling WFH to surrender their method as a side effect of a checkbox. That is a real harm produced by a reasonable-looking simplification, and it is why this is a separate feature rather than a field on 05.06.01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The artifact set is a **separate, explicit, priced declaration** — not implied by the rights posture or the deliverable spec | See DT-03. Bundling artifacts into a rights tier would force engineers to surrender their method via a checkbox. |
| D-02 | Session handover, if offered, must state its **format** (native + version, or consolidated WAVs from zero) | "The session" is not one artifact. See DT-02. |
| D-03 | A native session handover carries a plugin manifest | The buyer's real question is whether it will sound like what they bought. |
| D-04 | Refusing session handover is a supported, non-pejorative option | It is the professional norm and a legitimate protection of craft. The UI must not frame it as withholding. |
