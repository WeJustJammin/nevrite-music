# Feature: Watermarked Previews & Draft Protection

> **Number**: 05.04.03
> **Parent**: [Delivery, QC & Acceptance](./05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Protecting the seller's work while the buyer evaluates it. Between delivery and acceptance there is a window in which the buyer has heard the finished mix and has not yet paid for it — and the honest truth of this market is that a small number of buyers take the file and vanish, and the seller's only recourse today is to have never sent a usable version.

The problem is that music cannot use the standard solution. Stock-audio platforms answer this with an audible voice-tag every few seconds. A client cannot evaluate a mix through a voice-over. The protection must not damage the thing being evaluated.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller, delivers a draft they can afford to have heard. Their persona's `Switching Trigger` is "losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove" — a stolen unpaid mix is that, and today's workaround (send a deliberately degraded version, or a phone recording of the speakers) insults the client and hurts the work. As a buyer, hears the real mix, at real quality, and is aware it is traceable. |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest seller of high-value drafts, and the persona whose `Anti-Persona Behavior` ("withholds stems as leverage") this feature makes unnecessary — withholding is the current protection, and a better protection removes the incentive. As a buyer, receives work from strangers and expects to hear it properly before paying thousands. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. No draft, no file, nothing to protect. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and structurally must never be. An unreleased draft reachable from a Fan surface is a commercial disaster. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two complementary mechanisms, and the choice between them is per-craft:

**Streaming-only preview** — the buyer hears the real, undegraded work through the platform's player (Media Handling cross-cut), via signed expiring URLs, with no download. Zero audio damage. Defeated by anyone willing to record their own output, which does not stop a determined thief but does stop the casual one and makes the theft deliberate.

**Forensic watermarking** — an inaudible, per-recipient identifier embedded in the audio. Does not degrade the listening experience, survives a re-record, and is *evidential*: it does not prevent the leak, it proves who leaked. This is the technique the industry actually uses for pre-release material, and it is the right shape here.

The audible voice-tag approach is rejected outright (DT-01).

Protection ends at acceptance. Once the buyer has paid and the rights have transferred, they get the clean artifact.

### Happy Path

1. Seller's listing declares draft protection (streaming-only, watermarked, or none)
2. Seller delivers; QC passes (05.04.02)
3. Protected version generated per the policy; per-recipient watermark applied if elected
4. Buyer evaluates at real quality through the player, or downloads a watermarked copy
5. Buyer accepts → escrow releases, rights transfer, credit emits (05.04.01)
6. Clean artifact released

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer needs to play it in a car / to a band / to a label | The real reason streaming-only fails. Evaluating a mix on laptop speakers through a browser is not evaluating a mix. | Watermarked download is the answer; streaming-only is unusable for serious mix evaluation and pretending otherwise breaks the feature |
| Buyer takes the draft and disappears | Watermark identifies the recipient; the leak is provable | Evidence for 24 — the protection is evidential, not preventive |
| Buyer legitimately shares with their band/label for a decision | Normal and necessary. A watermark naming the *buyer* survives it and stays correct. | Should not be obstructed |
| Watermark audible / damages the mix | Feature has failed at its one job | Must be inaudible or not used. See DT-01. |
| Stems delivered as drafts | Watermarking 24 stems individually is 24× the DSP and 24× the risk of audible artefacts on exposed material (a solo vocal shows a watermark far more than a full mix) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-03 |
| Seller declines to protect | Their choice — many sellers do not bother | Default is a product decision (Q-02) |
| Auto-accept fires on a protected draft | The buyer now owns it; clean artifact must release automatically | Must not require a seller action — the seller may have moved on |
| Watermarking engine unavailable | Stack collision (05.04.02 DT-03) | Must fail **closed** here — deliver streaming-only rather than an unwatermarked download. Opposite of QC's fail-open, deliberately. |
| Buyer re-records the output to strip the watermark | Forensic watermarking survives re-recording; that is its point | — |

### States

**Protection lifecycle** (the meaningful state machine for this feature):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unprotected | Policy: none | Clean file at delivery |
| Streaming Only | Policy: stream | Player, no download |
| Watermarked | Policy: watermark | Download, per-recipient identifier embedded |
| Processing | Watermark being applied (per-recipient embed queued) | "Preparing your protected copy…" — delivery not yet playable/downloadable |
| Clean Released | Acceptance / auto-accept fires | Unprotected artifact available |
| Protection Failed | Engine unavailable | Fall back to streaming-only; never silently unwatermarked (D-05) |

**Standard UI states** (buyer's evaluation surface):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Protected version resolving / signed URL minting | Skeleton player or progress bar; download button disabled |
| Empty | No draft delivered yet for this engagement | "No deliverable to review yet" — nothing to protect |
| Populated | Protected draft ready | Player (streaming-only) or watermarked download button, with a "traceable copy" indicator so the buyer knows it is marked |
| Error | Signed URL expired, playback fails, or download link 4xx | "This preview link expired — request a fresh one" with a re-mint action; never exposes the clean file as a fallback |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Audible watermarking — the stock-audio voice-tag — is unusable in this domain, and the answer is inaudible/forensic watermarking, not degradation** | Domain expertise: what does the standard solution do here that nobody flagged? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it inverts the feature's purpose. Every generic marketplace protects drafts by *damaging* them: a voice-tag over stock music, a low-res comp, a watermarked JPEG. That logic is coherent when the buyer is evaluating composition or layout — the damage does not obscure what they are judging. It is incoherent for a **mix**, where the product *is* the audio quality: a client cannot assess a low end through a voice-over, cannot hear the reverb tail under a beep, cannot judge a vocal ride on a 96 kbps MP3. A degraded draft does not protect the work, it prevents the evaluation, and the seller is then rejected for a mix the buyer never heard. So protection must be **inaudible and evidential** rather than audible and preventive: the watermark does not stop the theft, it proves it — which is what the industry already does for pre-release material, and is the honest posture for a platform that also holds the identity of everyone involved. It also fits the platform's real advantage: WeJammin knows exactly who the recipient is, so a per-recipient forensic mark is cheap for it and impossible for a Dropbox link. |
| DT-02 | **Draft protection is the mirror of the NDA, and together they make the evaluation window symmetric** | Reasoning about the intersection with 05.02.02 | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a nice structural fact. The buyer's exposure is at *evaluation* going in: they must hand unreleased material to a stranger who may not take the job (05.02.02 DT-02), and the NDA covers it. The seller's exposure is at evaluation coming out: they must hand a finished mix to someone who has not yet paid, and watermarking covers it. Same window, opposite directions, and both are solved by the platform being the party that knows who everyone is. Neither protection is preventive; both are evidential; both rely on the audit log (Provenance Ledger cross-cut) to be worth anything. This suggests they should share an evidence model rather than each inventing one. |
| DT-03 | Watermarking stems is the same problem as watermarking a mix | Naive extension | ❌ REJECTED — it is worse in three ways at once. It is 24× the DSP load on a stack that already cannot run this (05.04.02 DT-03). It is 24× the artefact risk, and the risk is not uniform: an inaudible mark hides easily under a dense full mix and is far more exposed on a solo vocal or a bare piano stem — exactly the stems most worth stealing. And it is arguably pointless, because stems are a *working* deliverable: the buyer is going to import them into a DAW and process them, which is not a leak. The honest position is that stem drafts may need a different protection posture entirely (streaming-only preview of a rough, with stems released clean at acceptance), and that is `[PENDING]` rather than assumed. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Audible watermarking is rejected outright | A degraded draft prevents the evaluation rather than protecting the work. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Protection is **evidential**, not preventive | It proves who leaked; it does not stop them. That is the honest and achievable posture, and it is what the industry already does. |
| D-03 | Watermarks are **per-recipient** | The platform's advantage is that it knows exactly who everyone is. A per-recipient mark is trivial for it and impossible for a file-sharing link. |
| D-04 | Protection lifts automatically at acceptance, including auto-accept | The seller may have moved on; the buyer's clean artifact must not depend on a human action. |
| D-05 | When the watermarking engine is unavailable, **fail closed** (streaming-only), never unwatermarked | Deliberately the opposite of QC's fail-open (05.04.02 D-05): an unchecked file is a known unknown, an unprotected file is a silent loss of the seller's only protection. |
