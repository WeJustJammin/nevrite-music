# Feature: Delivery, Acceptance & Auto-Accept Window

> **Number**: 05.04.01
> **Parent**: [Delivery, QC & Acceptance](./05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The seller hands over the work; the buyer accepts it, or a timer accepts it for them. Acceptance is the single most consequential event in this domain: it releases the escrow, executes the rights transfer, and emits the credit to the graph — all at once. Everything upstream (listings, quotes, requirements gates, revisions) exists to make this moment arrive with both parties agreeing what it means.

The auto-accept window exists because the alternative is worse. A buyer who simply stops responding would otherwise hold a seller's money hostage forever, and no amount of revision limiting fixes a buyer who does nothing at all. The revision limit and the auto-accept window are **two halves of one mechanism** (05.03.03 DT-01): the limit bounds the seller's exposure to a buyer who asks for too much, the window bounds it against a buyer who asks for nothing.

This feature is also where D-18's thesis is either a working mechanism or a slogan. The credit emitted here is captured **at source**, from two parties who are present and agreeable, at the only moment either of them is motivated. Every competitor reconstructs this fact years later from memory. This feature is the reason they have to.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As a seller**: delivers, then watches a countdown that will eventually pay them whether the buyer engages or not — the first time most working musicians will have experienced a payment that does not depend on someone remembering them. Their persona's `Success Criteria` is "income visible in one place"; this is the event that produces it. Sees, throughout the window, exactly what acceptance will do to them: what they assign, what they keep, what they are paid (05.06.03 D-05). Can retract a delivery pre-acceptance (D-05) and redeliver. **As a buyer**: accepts explicitly, requests a revision against their remaining allowance, extends the window once (D-08), or lets it run. This is the persona most likely to be a *touring* buyer — the one auto-accept is most dangerous to, and the reason the extension exists. |
| Producer | Full | Delivers mixes and masters; accepts session work into their projects. The persona whose `Anti-Persona Behavior` — "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute" — this feature structurally removes: the stems are delivered into escrow-backed custody, so withholding is no longer available as leverage in **either** direction. Also the persona whose other anti-pattern ("closes a project without capturing splits — we'll do it later") this feature makes impossible: they do not file a split, they accept money, and the split happens (05.06.03 DT-03). Highest exposure on the buyer side, because a Producer buying session work is usually accepting on **behalf of someone else** — a label, an artist, a band — which is where DT-06's authority problem lives. |
| Operator | None | Not visible, and the absence is load-bearing in two directions. (a) An on-location engagement delivers **attendance**, not a file — but the Operator is not a party to it. They supply the room via domain 16 and see a room booking, never the engagement's delivery, artifacts, or commercial terms (05.01.05 Role Lens). (b) Custodial work (05.07), where the Operator *is* Full, does not use this feature at all: a repaired instrument's acceptance turns on physical condition at handover and is 05.07-owned. See DT-08 — auto-accept is structurally invalid for physical work. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, structurally and permanently. Unreleased masters live here, and per `meta/personas.md` the Fan lens is where a leak does commercial damage. There is no Fan-reachable path to a delivery artifact from any surface, at any state, including post-acceptance. |

## Behavior

**Delivery** is the seller publishing the artifact set (05.04.04) into the engagement. It must pass QC (05.04.02) to *be* a delivery — a file that fails spec is not delivered, it is rejected back to the seller (05.04 D-02), invisibly to the buyer. It must also be **complete** against the frozen artifact set (D-06): there is no such thing as a partial delivery.

**Acceptance** is the buyer's affirmative act, and it fires three things atomically:

| Leg | System | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Payments, Escrow & Payouts cross-cut | Escrow releases to the seller (and downstream workers in multi-party supply) |
| Rights | 05.06.03 → domain 09 | The agreed posture executes: assignment, licence, co-ownership, or points |
| Record | domain 02 | The credit emits at the elected visibility (05.02.02), naming the hands that did the work |

All three, or none (D-02). See DT-02 — this is the single most important constraint in the feature.

**Auto-accept** fires the same three legs on a timer if the buyer neither accepts nor requests a revision within the window. The window **pauses** while the ball is in the seller's court (a revision in progress) and **runs** while it is in the buyer's — the same principle as the turnaround clock (05.01.06 D-02) and the same domain principle as the requirements gate and QC (domain D-09: *no clock runs against a party until the counterparty has done their bit*).

### The window: derivation, bounds and controls

The window is a **duration**, resolved at delivery into an absolute deadline instant. Following 05.01.06 D-03, it is always rendered to users as an absolute date+time in their own timezone, never as "7 days" — a duration spanning a DST transition or a bank holiday is four different dates. The deadline is a fixed instant; display converts.

| Control | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Default** | Per-craft, declared on the listing (05.01.01) and frozen on the accepted quote — see the table below |
| **Platform floor** | **3 business days.** Below this the window is hostile to how music is actually evaluated (DT-05) and a seller cannot shorten past it |
| **Platform ceiling** | **30 calendar days.** Beyond this the seller is financing the buyer's inattention and escrow is effectively stranded |
| **Seller may extend** | Yes, at listing or quote time, up to the ceiling. Generosity is permitted, as with free revision rounds (05.03.03) |
| **Seller may shorten** | Only to the floor. Never below |
| **Buyer may extend** | **Once per delivery**, by the original window length, capped at the ceiling. One tap, no reason required. See D-08 and DT-04 |
| **Redelivery** | Restarts the window at full length — the buyer is evaluating a new artifact |
| **Business days** | The seller's jurisdiction's, per 05.01.06 Q-02, which owns this question. This feature inherits whatever that resolves to and must not answer it independently |

**Proposed per-craft defaults** — the mechanism above is decided; these numbers need owner ratification (Q-02). The reasoning is that the window must be ≥ the buyer's realistic evaluation time and ≤ the seller's financing tolerance, and evaluation time in music is not "listen once" — a mix is judged across several sessions on different systems over days.

| Craft | Proposed window | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Mastering | 5 business days | 1–2 files, fast evaluation, short turnaround upstream |
| Mix — single song | 7 business days | Multi-system evaluation over several sittings is the actual practice |
| Album mix / full production (final acceptance) | 14 business days | Proportionate to the money and to the number of artifacts |
| Remote session performance (a take) | 5 business days | Evaluation is "does it work in the track" — fast |
| Topline / songwriting / arrangement | 7 business days | Subjective, but a single artifact |
| Artwork / design / PR deliverable | 7 business days | Non-audio; no listening practice to accommodate |
| Milestone (non-final, 05.03.02) | 5 business days | Releases a tranche only; lower stakes, keeps the chain moving |
| On-location engagement | 3 business days from session end | Nothing to evaluate — the buyer was there. See DT-03 |

### The acceptance basis has two shapes

Delivery does not always produce an artifact (DT-03). The acceptance basis forks:

| Engagement mode | What is delivered | Acceptance basis | Auto-accept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Remote** (default) | The agreed artifact set, QC-passed | Buyer accepts the artifacts | 3–30 days per craft table |
| **On-location** (05.01.05) | **Attendance** — the person showed up and played | Buyer confirms attendance. A counter-attested Session Attendance Proof (domain 02) satisfies the basis and is treated as affirmative acceptance (D-09) | 3 business days from session end |

The on-location fork matters more than it looks. There is no file, so there is nothing to prompt the buyer to review — the only thing between the seller and payment is the buyer remembering to click. Auto-accept is therefore **more** important for on-location work, not less. And the day rate buys attendance and effort, not a guaranteed usable take: a producer who dislikes the performance has a dispute (domain 24), not a withheld acceptance. This is consistent with domain D-07 — *the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them*.

### Not every acceptance has three legs

Two engagement shapes have no escrow, which reduces the atomic commit to two legs and materially worsens DT-01:

| Shape | Legs that fire | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Retainer** (05.03.04, 05.03 CX-05) | Rights + credit only — paid in advance, no escrow to release | Auto-accept becomes a pure rights assignment by silence, with no money leg to make the event feel like a transaction to anyone |
| **Points-only** (05.01.03 DT-01, domain Q-01) | Rights + credit only — no cash held | Same |

This is recorded because it is where the domain's flagship leverage is thinnest (domain Q-01) and where DT-01's legal exposure is at its worst.

### Happy Path

1. Seller publishes the complete artifact set into the engagement
2. QC (05.04.02) runs on the clean file; it passes (or the seller overrides with a recorded reason, which the buyer will see)
3. Watermarking (05.04.03) applies per the per-artifact-type protection posture; if the engine is unavailable it falls back to streaming-only, never unprotected
4. **Delivery publishes.** The window's deadline is computed and rendered to both parties as an absolute date+time in their own timezone
5. Buyer is notified on every channel they have enabled: *"Delivered. You have until {date, time}. If you do nothing, this accepts automatically and you will own it."*
6. Buyer listens — via the protected preview if drafts are protected (05.04.03)
7. Buyer accepts
8. Engagement enters `Accepting` and locks: revision requests, retractions and disputes are rejected for the duration of the commit
9. **Atomic commit**: escrow releases → rights execute into 09 → credit emits to 02. All three, or none
10. Protection lifts automatically; the clean artifact set is released to the buyer (05.04.03 D-04)
11. Both parties receive a confirmation naming all three consequences explicitly and in plain language — not "Order complete"
12. Engagement closes; the recall window opens (05.03.06); the record shows `Accepted` (not `Auto-Accepted`) permanently

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Buyer goes silent** | Auto-accept fires at the deadline | Escalating reminders — this is the one place the platform must nag loudly, because the consequence is ownership. Notice at delivery, at 50% elapsed, at T-24h and at T-1h (D-07) |
| **Buyer requests a revision** | Window stops; engagement re-enters revision (05.03.03); allowance decrements | "Auto-accept paused — {seller} has your notes" |
| **Revision request lands as auto-accept fires** | **Resolved (D-04).** A **120-second grace** follows the deadline. Any revision request received at or before deadline+120s wins and cancels auto-accept; anything after loses and is routed to recall (05.03.06, per 05.03.03 D-05). The grace is invisible to both parties — publishing it would simply move the race to the new number | Winner: "Auto-accept paused." Loser: "This engagement accepted at {time}. Your request has been raised as a recall instead." |
| **Auto-accept fires late** (platform outage, timer missed) | **Never backdated.** The deadline is a fact; the firing is an effect. A revision request received before the *actual* fire wins, even if it arrived after the nominal deadline. The platform's lateness is the platform's fault, not the buyer's | The buyer never learns they were nearly too late |
| **One of the three atomic legs fails** | The failure this feature exists to prevent. Rolled back entirely. **3 retries, exponential backoff (2s / 8s / 32s)**, then `Acceptance Failed`, both parties told plainly, and it **pages a human** (05.06.03 D-02) | "We could not complete this acceptance. Nothing has changed — your money is still held and nothing has transferred. We are on it." Never a partial commit. See DT-02 |
| **Auto-accept commit fails and rolls back** | The window resumes with **at least 24 hours** restored, so a failed commit cannot instantly re-fire into a retry loop | Buyer gets time back; seller is told why |
| **Escrow release fails — seller's payout account frozen or KYC incomplete** | Rolled back with everything else. But this is preventable and must be prevented: payout readiness is checked **at delivery**, not at acceptance, with escalating notices through the window (D-10). At acceptance it is already too late | At delivery: "Your payout isn't set up. If this accepts, we can't pay you." Escalating through the window. See DT-05 |
| **Seller's account terminated during the window** | Buyer may still accept — they want their artifacts, and blocking them punishes the wrong party. Rights and credit execute; the money leg holds pending resolution rather than failing the commit | Buyer: normal acceptance. Escalated internally |
| **Buyer's account suspended during the window** | **Window pauses.** A suspended buyer cannot act, and auto-accepting against an account the platform has frozen is transferring rights into an account the platform does not trust | Seller: "Paused — {buyer}'s account is under review." |
| **All notification channels hard-bounce** | **Window pauses and flags.** No clock runs against a party the platform failed to reach — the same domain principle (D-09) applied to the platform's own obligation. See D-07 | Seller: "Paused — we can't reach {buyer}. We're working on it." |
| **Buyer never opened the delivery** (zero playback, zero download) | Auto-accept **still fires** — see DT-07, blocking it would reward deliberate non-engagement. But the engagement's evidence record permanently notes zero buyer engagement, because this is the weakest instrument the platform will ever produce | Nothing different to the buyer. The audit record differs permanently |
| **Buyer accepts twice** (double-click, retry, two tabs) | Idempotent. The second accept is a no-op returning the first result. Non-negotiable — the commit moves money and assigns copyright | "Accepted at {time}" — the same confirmation, not a second one |
| **Two authorized buyer-side accepters click simultaneously** | First received wins; the second is a no-op. The record names **who** accepted, not the organization | "Accepted by {name} at {time}" |
| **Seller delivers, then wants to retract** (spotted a mistake) | Allowed pre-acceptance (D-05). Window stops; delivery unpublishes; reason logged. The alternative is a seller watching a known-bad file get auto-accepted | "Retract and redeliver?" → buyer sees "{seller} pulled this delivery: {reason}" |
| **Seller retracts while the buyer is mid-accept** | Acceptance wins if it was received first; the retraction is rejected. Retraction gets **no grace window**, unlike a revision request — the seller created the situation, and the asymmetry is deliberate | Seller: "Too late — {buyer} accepted at {time}." |
| **QC run still in flight when the seller retracts** | Retraction wins; the QC result is discarded (05.04 CX-01) | Seller: retraction confirmed |
| **Seller redelivers voluntarily** (no revision requested) | Allowed. Window restarts at full length. It only ever delays the seller's own money, so there is no abuse vector | Buyer: "{seller} sent an updated version. Your window restarts." |
| **Seller and buyer are the same human, or the seller is a member of the buying entity** | **Self-acceptance is blocked.** A producer who is a member of the band that engaged them cannot accept their own delivery on the band's behalf and release escrow to themselves. If the selling party is the entity's *only* authorized accepter, an independent accepter must be nominated at quote time or the engagement cannot be created. Auto-accept still fires normally — a timer is not self-dealing | "You can't accept your own delivery. {Other accepter} has been asked." See DT-09 — this is the multi-hyphenate, literally |
| **The accepter lacked authority** (an assistant accepted for a label; a manager for an artist) | The transfer may be defective and nobody finds out for years. The platform cannot resolve this alone — it needs the buyer-side entity/authority model. See DT-06 | Escalated, not resolved here |
| **Buyer accepts a *milestone*** (05.03.02) | Releases the tranche only. Does **not** transfer rights and does **not** emit a final credit (05.03.02 DT-01). Deliberately a different event | "Stage 3 accepted — £X released. You don't own the masters yet; that happens at final acceptance." |
| **Partial delivery** (7 of 11 mixes) | **Resolved (D-06).** Not a delivery. An incomplete set does not publish and starts no clock — the same principle as QC gating. If a buyer needs staged acceptance of a multi-item job, that is milestones, declared at quote time (05.03.02 D-02). A seller wanting feedback on work-in-progress uses a **progress share**, which is not a delivery, starts no clock, and cannot be accepted | Seller: "4 of 11 mixes missing. This won't deliver." |
| **Buyer disputes rather than accepts** | Escrow frozen; routed to domain 24. Nothing transfers while frozen. The window stops | "This engagement is in dispute. Nothing will transfer until it's resolved." |
| **Buyer accepts while a dispute is open** | Blocked. The freeze wins | "You can't accept while this is in dispute. Withdraw the dispute first?" |
| **Buyer disputes after auto-accept fired** | Post-terminal. Rights are transferred and money released. Routed to recall (05.03.06) or to 24 as a reversal claim — and whether an executed transfer can *ever* be reversed is 05.06.03 Q-03's, not this feature's | "This engagement accepted on {date}. Here's what we can still do." |
| **Buyer with an "unlimited revisions" listing never accepts** | **Auto-accept is defeated and the engagement has no terminal state.** The two halves of the mechanism only work together. See DT-10 — this is a material argument against 05.03.03 Q-01 | Currently: nothing bounds it. Recorded as a finding against allowing unlimited revisions at all |
| **Buyer drip-requests empty revisions purely to reset the clock** | Bounded by the revision allowance (05.03.03 D-01). Once exhausted, no further resets are possible and the window runs to its end | "0 of 3 rounds left. Further changes need a change order." |
| **Buyer streams the watermarked preview, re-records it, and never accepts** | Auto-accept fires and they pay anyway. The mark is per-recipient and evidential (05.04.03 D-02/D-03), so the leak is attributable regardless | Auto-accept is anti-theft as well as anti-inaction |
| **On-location: seller no-showed** | There is no attendance to accept. Not an acceptance question — routed to dispute (24) and emergency cover (05.05.01) | "{Seller} didn't attend. This won't auto-accept." |
| **On-location: seller attended, buyer never confirms** | Auto-accept fires at session end + 3 business days | Same escalation ladder |
| **Retainer or points-only engagement accepts** | Two-leg commit: rights + credit. No escrow to release | Confirmation must not say "released" — there was nothing to release |
| **DST transition or bank holiday inside the window** | The deadline is a fixed instant, computed once at delivery. Display converts to each party's timezone. Never recomputed in local time | Both parties see the same instant, rendered differently |
| **Empty: no delivery yet** | Nothing to accept | Buyer: "Nothing delivered yet. Due {date}." |
| **Buyer tries to accept during the `Accepting` lock** | Rejected as a no-op (idempotency) | The confirmation, once |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Awaiting Delivery** (Empty) | Engagement in progress, nothing delivered | Seller: committed due date. Buyer: "Nothing delivered yet — due {date}." Plus, throughout: "On acceptance you'll assign X, keep Y, be paid Z" (05.06.03 D-05) |
| **Checking** (Loading) | Upload complete; QC and watermarking running | Seller only. Honest progress — this is slow on large sets, and it is the most impatient moment in the engagement. Buyer sees nothing |
| **QC Failed** | Delivery fails spec | Seller only, with the specific failures. The buyer never sees a broken delivery and their clock never starts (05.04 D-02) |
| **Incomplete** (Partial) | Artifact set is short of the frozen set | Seller only: "4 of 11 mixes missing. This won't deliver." No publication, no clock |
| **Delivered / Auto-Accept Pending** (Populated) | QC passed, protection applied, set complete | Buyer: artifacts + prominent countdown + **Accept** / **Request revision** / **Need more time**. Seller: the same countdown, plus what it will pay them |
| **Window Paused — revision** | Buyer requested a revision | Both: "Auto-accept paused — {seller} has your notes" |
| **Window Paused — unreachable** | All notification channels hard-bounced, or buyer suspended | Seller: "Paused — we can't reach {buyer}." Reason always attributed |
| **Extended** | Buyer tapped "Need more time" | Both: new deadline, and that it was the buyer's choice. The seller must see that the buyer *acted* — that is the point (DT-04) |
| **Retracted** | Seller pulls a delivery pre-acceptance | Buyer: "{seller} pulled this delivery: {reason}." Reason logged permanently |
| **Accepting** (Loading, locked) | Accept fired, manual or auto | Brief. Both: "Completing…" Revisions, retractions and disputes rejected during the lock |
| **Accepted** | Buyer accepted affirmatively | Confirmation naming all three consequences explicitly. Recorded as affirmative consent, permanently |
| **Auto-Accepted** | Timer fired | The same three consequences, marked **accepted by expiry**. The distinction persists forever (D-03) — it is the difference between consent and silence, and DT-01 means it may become legally load-bearing |
| **Acceptance Failed** (Error) | A leg failed after 3 retries | Both, plainly: "Nothing has changed. Your money is still held; nothing has transferred." Pages a human. Window resumes with ≥24h restored |
| **Disputed** | Buyer disputes | Escrow frozen; window stopped; routed to 24. Nothing transfers while frozen |
| **Closed** | Acceptance committed | Recall window open (05.03.06). Engagement read-only |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Auto-accept transfers rights by silence — and that is a legal question the product cannot answer for itself** | Adversarial: what is the failure mode nobody mentioned? Trace auto-accept through 05.06.03. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real exposure, ❌ NOT resolvable here. On a marketplace for goods, auto-accept releases money — a low-stakes default. Here it also executes a **copyright assignment**. A buyer who was on tour and never opened the app now owns a master because a timer expired; or a session player's work-for-hire assignment vested because they did not act. Whether an assignment executed by inaction is enforceable is jurisdiction-dependent and turns on formalities (several jurisdictions require *signed writing* for a copyright assignment — an unrebutted timer is unlikely to satisfy that). This is not a design decision, it needs counsel, and it sits directly on the platform's thesis: if the rights half of auto-accept is unenforceable, the domain's differentiator is decorative in exactly the cases where the buyer disengaged. Recorded as sub-domain Q-01 and escalated to `/create-prd-security`. A plausible mitigation worth testing with counsel: auto-accept releases escrow but the rights transfer requires an affirmative signature (Contracts cross-cut), which splits the atomic event and weakens the mechanism — so it is a real trade-off, not a free fix. **Worst on retainer and points-only engagements**, where there is no money leg at all and the entire event is a rights assignment by silence. |
| DT-02 | **Acceptance is the atomic intersection of three systems, and a partial commit is silent data loss in the platform's core asset** | Reasoning about D-18: acceptance is where "provenance is the wedge" is either true or false. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is why the **Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer** cross-cut exists and why this feature is its primary trigger site. Consider each partial failure. Escrow releases, credit emission fails: everyone is paid, nobody notices, and the credit graph — the thing the user cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture — has a hole. Nobody will ever report this, because both parties got what they came for. Rights transfer succeeds, escrow release fails: the seller has assigned their copyright and not been paid, which is the single worst outcome the platform could produce and is *precisely* the failure the whole product exists to prevent. The three must commit together or not at all, and the credit emission is the leg most likely to be treated as fire-and-forget by an implementer because it is the one nobody is chasing. Flagging it here is the point. |
| DT-03 | Delivery always produces an artifact | The candidate list's framing | ❌ REJECTED — on-location engagements (05.01.05) deliver **attendance**. A session bassist who played the date has delivered; there is no file, nothing to QC, nothing to watermark. Acceptance for these rests on a different basis — the buyer confirming the person showed up and played — and domain 02's Session Attendance Proof is the natural evidence. This means the acceptance event has (at least) two shapes, and a file-shaped model would either block on-location acceptance or force a fake artifact. It also means auto-accept is *more* important for on-location work, not less: there is no file for a buyer to review, so the only thing between the seller and payment is the buyer remembering to click. Resolved into the two-shape fork in Behavior and D-09. |
| DT-04 | **A one-tap "I need more time" is the cheapest available mitigation for DT-01's exposure, and its real value is evidential rather than functional** | Reasoning about DT-01's *equitable* half rather than its formal half: what would a court actually hear? | ✅ CONFIRMED. DT-01 has two distinct problems bundled together and the design has been treating them as one. The **formal** problem is the signed-writing requirement — a button does not fix that, and only counsel can (Q-01). The **equitable** problem is different and is what a buyer would actually argue: *"I never knew. I was on tour. I never agreed to anything."* That argument is devastating against a platform that emailed once and started a clock, and nearly worthless against a platform that notified on four escalating occasions across every channel the buyer enabled and offered a one-tap extension requiring no reason, which the buyer did not use. The extension converts silence into a **record of a choice not to act** — and the buyer who *does* tap it has affirmatively engaged, which is strictly better for everyone. It costs one button and one field. It is the highest-leverage thing in this feature and it was not in the candidate list. Bounded at **one extension per delivery** (D-08); unbounded extension would hand the buyer back the hostage-taking power the window exists to remove. |
| DT-05 | **The escrow leg's most likely real-world failure is the seller's own payout readiness, and checking it at acceptance is checking it too late** | Adversarial: of the three legs, which fails for boring reasons rather than exotic ones? Follow the Musician persona's actual finances. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it inverts where the check belongs. The exotic failures (rights conflicts, points over-allocation) are rare. The boring one is constant: **the seller cannot be paid.** `meta/personas.md` describes a working musician making a living from "a portfolio of small income streams" — this population is disproportionately informal, freelance, sometimes unbanked, frequently paid in cash, and a platform payout is often their *first* formal one. KYC/AML obligations bite exactly here (`meta/constraints.md` lists KYC/AML as an as-yet-unresolved compliance obligation, triggered by vendor payouts and escrow, and deferred to `/create-prd-security`). So the modal escrow failure is not a technical fault, it is a seller who never finished their payout setup. If that is discovered at acceptance, DT-02 forces a full rollback — and there is no good outcome: the buyer accepted in good faith and nothing happened, the seller is unpaid, and the engagement bounces. Every option at that moment is bad. Therefore the check must move **upstream to delivery**, where there is a whole window in which to fix it, and the seller must be told plainly and repeatedly that acceptance will not pay them. The general principle: for an atomic commit, every leg's *preconditions* must be verified before the commit can be triggered, not inside it. Recorded as D-10. |
| DT-06 | **The buyer is frequently not the payer and not the rights recipient — and acceptance is where "who is the buyer?" stops being a UI question and becomes an authority question** | What would a music-industry domain expert expect that nobody has mentioned? | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap, ❌ NOT resolvable in this feature. At any professional level in music, the party who *accepts* is routinely not the party who *pays* and not the party who *owns*: a label pays, an A&R accepts, the label owns; a manager accepts on an artist's behalf; a band member accepts for the band. The domain has asked the seller-side version of this question — 05 Q-04, *"can a listing's seller be an entity?"* — and nobody has asked the buyer-side version, which is worse. If the human who clicked accept lacked authority to take an assignment on the label's behalf, the platform has recorded a transfer that may be defective, and the defect surfaces years later at a sync request. This is the exact mirror of 05.06.02 DT-03 ("buyer lacked authority to grant points"), which means the pattern has now been found twice from two directions — that promotes it from an edge case to a structural gap. **Auto-accept makes it strictly worse**: a timer accepting on behalf of an organisation whose authorised signatory never saw the delivery is DT-01's problem compounded by an authority defect. This feature can do two things and no more: block self-acceptance (D-11) and record *which human* accepted rather than which organisation. The entity/authority model belongs to domain 01 and `/create-prd-architecture`. Recorded as Q-05. |
| DT-07 | Auto-accept should be blocked when the platform has no evidence the buyer ever engaged with the delivery (zero playback, zero download) | Intuition: it feels indefensible to transfer a copyright against someone who demonstrably never opened the file — and unlike most platforms, this one *knows*, because Media Handling sees every play | ❌ REJECTED — and the reason is a perverse incentive that inverts the whole mechanism. If non-engagement blocks auto-accept, then **not opening the file becomes the buyer's winning strategy**: never press play, never pay, keep the money in escrow indefinitely, and the seller has no recourse at all. The rule would reward exactly the behaviour the feature exists to bound, and it would be trivially discoverable — one forum post and it is the norm. The engagement signal is nonetheless genuinely valuable, just not as a gate: a buyer who streamed the mix twelve times and went quiet is a materially different case from one who never opened it, and that difference is worth **recording permanently** in the evidence trail (D-12), where it costs nothing and strengthens the platform's position in any later challenge. Rejecting this cleanly matters because it is an attractive idea that would have made the product worse in a way nobody would notice until sellers stopped getting paid. |
| DT-08 | Auto-accept generalises to all engagement types, including custodial/physical work (05.07) | The obvious extension — 05.07 has jobs, escrow and completion, so why not a timer? | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection also resolves an apparent inconsistency in the sub-domain's Role Matrix (which marks Operator `None` across all of 05.04, while marking them `Full` on 05.07 where they are the heaviest buyer of technical work). Auto-accept's founding premise is that **the buyer has the deliverable and is choosing not to respond.** For physical custodial work that premise is simply false: the buyer may not have the instrument at all — it is still on the luthier's bench, or in transit. Auto-accepting would release escrow for work the buyer has not received and cannot inspect, which is not a timer, it is a forfeiture. The acceptance basis for custodial work is **physical condition at handover**, which is 05.07.03's custody chain — there is no delivery event here, no artifact set, no QC, no watermark, and no window. Furthermore the real-world mechanism for the analogous failure (an owner who never collects) already exists and is not a timer: it is a **repairer's lien**, which is law, and which no platform should attempt to reimplement as a countdown. Conclusion: custodial acceptance is 05.07-owned, this feature does not apply to it, and the Role Matrix is right for a reason nobody had written down. |
| DT-09 | **The multi-hyphenate can be on both sides of the timer, and the platform's own persona model is what creates the hole** | The task of tracing `meta/personas.md`'s central structural fact — "one human routinely occupies three of these lenses in one day" — through an escrow release | ✅ CONFIRMED. Every other marketplace can assume buyer ≠ seller. This one explicitly cannot: the personas file states that roles are "simultaneous, not sequential", and D-19 makes the Band an **entity** that books and pays independently of its members. Compose those two facts and a concrete, common scenario falls out: a producer who is a member of a band, engaged *by that band* to mix its record. The band entity is the buyer. The producer is the seller — and, as a member, plausibly an authorised accepter for the buyer. They deliver their own work, accept it on the band's behalf, and release the band's escrow to themselves. Nobody has done anything obviously wrong at any step, and every check passes. This is not exotic; it is how a large share of independent records are actually made. The rule is therefore that **an accepter who is the seller, or a member of the selling entity, is blocked from accepting** (D-11), and if that leaves the buying entity with no eligible accepter, one must be nominated at quote time or the engagement cannot be created — better to block at creation than to discover it at the only irreversible moment. Auto-accept still fires normally, because a timer has no self-interest. Note the shape of this finding: it exists *because* of the persona model, so a generic marketplace design would never surface it, and it intersects DT-06 — both are the buyer-side authority question, arriving from different directions. |
| DT-10 | **"Unlimited revisions" and auto-accept are mutually annihilating, which turns 05.03.03 Q-01 from a listing-policy question into a structural one** | Adversarial: 05.03.03 DT-01 says the revision limit and auto-accept are two halves of one mechanism. What happens if you remove one half? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a finding this feature owes back to 05.03.03. Auto-accept only fires when the buyer does *nothing*; a revision request pauses the window and a redelivery restarts it. Therefore a buyer with an unlimited allowance can hold an engagement open forever **while remaining perfectly responsive** — request a round, get a redelivery, request another, indefinitely. The window never expires because it never gets the chance to run out. The seller's protection is not weakened, it is **eliminated**, and the engagement has no terminal state whatsoever — which is precisely what 05.03.03 DT-01 said escrow structurally requires. 05.03.03 Q-01 currently frames this as a paternalism trade-off ("blocking it is paternalistic; it is how sellers destroy themselves"), which understates it: the question is not whether the platform should let sellers harm themselves, it is whether the platform can offer a listing option that **removes the escrow model's only exit condition**. If unlimited revisions are permitted, they need their own backstop — a hard engagement-duration ceiling, or a cap on rounds-in-name-only — or the domain hosts engagements that cannot end. Escalated as Q-06 and cross-referenced to 05.03.03 Q-01. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **05.04.02 QC** ([path](./05.04.02-deliverable-spec-automated-audio-qc.md)) — **trigger dependency, strict ordering.** QC gates delivery; a failed file is never a delivery and never starts the window. QC's `Overridden` state (05.04.02 D-04) is visible to the buyer *during* the window, so a seller's override becomes information the buyer weighs before accepting. QC's fail-open posture (D-05) means a window can run on an unchecked-but-flagged file — which is deliberate but worth Step 6's attention, because auto-accept on a flagged file transfers rights over a deliverable nobody verified.
- Touches **05.04.03 Watermarked Previews** ([path](./05.04.03-watermarked-previews-draft-protection.md)) — **shared entity + trigger dependency.** Protection covers exactly the delivery→acceptance window and lifts automatically at acceptance *including auto-accept* (05.04.03 D-04) — the clean artifact cannot depend on a human action, because the whole premise of auto-accept is that no human acted. Watermarking's fail-closed posture can delay or downgrade a delivery to streaming-only, which changes what the buyer is evaluating during the window.
- Touches **05.04.04 Session Handover** ([path](./05.04.04-source-file-session-handover.md)) — **shared entity.** Defines *which* artifacts are delivered; the frozen set is what D-06 tests completeness against. Its `Session Withheld` state must be stated at delivery, not discovered at acceptance. Note the counter-intuitive permission fact (05.04 CX-02/05): a buyer may hold a full work-for-hire assignment and still have no entitlement to the session — rights permissions do not imply artifact permissions, and acceptance does both things at once.
- Touches **05.03.03 Revisions** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.03-revisions-limits-change-orders.md)) — **two halves of one mechanism.** The revision limit bounds the seller's exposure; auto-accept bounds the buyer's inaction. **DT-10 is owed back to this feature**: an unlimited allowance annihilates auto-accept and leaves the engagement with no terminal state. Also owns the race resolved by D-04.
- Touches **05.03.02 Milestones** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.02-milestones-staged-deliverables.md)) — **trigger dependency, deliberately narrowed.** Milestone acceptance fires the money leg only (05.03.02 DT-01). Milestones are also D-06's answer for multi-item jobs, and 05.03.02 Q-03 (minimum value below which milestones are noise) determines whether that answer is usable for small ones.
- Touches **05.03.06 Recall** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.06-post-delivery-support-recall.md)) — **state boundary.** Acceptance opens the recall window and is the sharp, non-negotiable revision/recall boundary (05.03 CX-04). D-04's grace window is what makes that boundary deterministic in the one case where it was ambiguous.
- Touches **05.03.04 Retainers** ([path](../05.03-engagement-lifecycle/05.03.04-retainers-recurring-engagements.md)) — **structural exception.** No escrow (05.03 CX-05), so acceptance is a two-leg commit and auto-accept becomes a pure rights assignment by silence — DT-01 at its worst.
- Touches **05.01.05 Remote vs On-Location** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.05-remote-vs-on-location-service-mode.md)) — **forks the acceptance basis.** Mode is declared on the listing and determines which of the two shapes applies (D-09).
- Touches **05.01.06 Turnaround** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.06-turnaround-rush-service-levels.md)) — **shared convention.** The window inherits its absolute-date rendering (D-03) and its business-days definition (Q-02). This feature must not answer the working-days question independently.
- Touches **05.06.03 Rights Transfer & Split Execution** ([path](../05.06-rights-warranties-transfer/05.06.03-rights-transfer-split-execution.md)) — **one event, two sides.** The rights leg. Its D-05 (pending consequences visible throughout) is what makes acceptance consent rather than surprise.
- Touches **05.07 Custodial & Physical Services** ([path](../05.07-custodial-physical-services/)) — **explicit non-application (DT-08).** Custodial acceptance is 05.07-owned; auto-accept is structurally invalid for physical work. This is why Operator is `None` across 05.04 despite being `Full` on 05.07 — Step 6 should record the boundary rather than treat it as a matrix inconsistency.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **the third leg, and the platform's thesis executing.** Acceptance is the moment the credit becomes true and is captured at source rather than reconstructed. Also: **D-09 couples 02's Session Attendance Proof to on-location acceptance** — a counter-attested proof satisfies the acceptance basis. That coupling needs 02's agreement (Q-04).
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — the transfer writes the chain of title.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission/authority.** DT-06 and DT-09 both need a buyer-side entity and authority model: who may accept on behalf of a label, a band, an artist. Neither is resolvable without it.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — dispute freezes stop the window and block acceptance; post-acceptance claims are reversal claims, whose feasibility is 05.06.03 Q-03's.
- Consumes **Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer** cross-cut — **this feature is its primary trigger site in the domain.** DT-02 and DT-05 are requirements placed on it: all-or-nothing commit, and precondition verification moved upstream of the commit.
- Consumes **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** cross-cut — the money leg, plus the payout-readiness precheck at delivery (D-10) that the cross-cut does not currently describe.
- Consumes **Media Handling & Audio Playback** cross-cut — artifact storage, signed expiring URLs, playback, and the buyer-engagement signal that D-12 records.
- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** cross-cut — the escalation ladder (D-07). **This is the one place the platform must nag hard, because the consequence is ownership** — and it is a place where a cross-cut's ordinary behaviour is wrong: the T-24h and T-1h notices must bypass digest, batching and quiet-hours suppression, and a hard bounce on every channel must **pause the window** rather than fail silently. That last requirement is a real constraint on the cross-cut, not a preference.
- Consumes **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — accepted-vs-auto-accepted persists forever (D-03); so do the notice record, the engagement signal (D-12), and the identity of the accepting human (D-11).
- Consumes **Contracts & E-Signature** cross-cut — possibly the affirmative signature the rights leg requires, if auto-accept-by-silence proves unenforceable (Q-01).
- Consumes **Localization, Currency & Timezone** cross-cut — the deadline is a fixed instant rendered per-party; business days and bank holidays are jurisdiction-dependent.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A file that fails QC is not a delivery | It never reaches the buyer and never starts their clock. Domain principle D-09. |
| D-02 | Acceptance fires escrow release + rights transfer + credit emission **atomically**; 3 retries with exponential backoff (2s/8s/32s), then rollback, plain-language notice to both parties, and a page to a human | Any partial commit is either unpaid assignment or a silent hole in the credit graph. See DT-02. |
| D-03 | `Accepted` and `Auto-Accepted` are distinct and the distinction persists permanently | It is the difference between consent and expiry, and DT-01 means it may become legally load-bearing. |
| D-04 | **Revision beats auto-accept in a tie**, via an invisible **120-second grace** after the deadline. Auto-accept is **never backdated** — if the platform fires late, requests received before the actual fire still win | Resolves the race. Auto-accept's entire justification is that the buyer did nothing; a revision request is proof they did something, so ties must resolve toward it. The outcomes are also asymmetric: a wrong auto-accept transfers copyright irreversibly, a wrong revision costs one round of delay — fail toward the reversible outcome. The grace absorbs mobile latency and client clock skew, costs the seller 120 seconds on a window measured in days, and is invisible because publishing it would move the race to the new number. |
| D-05 | A seller may retract a delivery before acceptance, with a logged reason — but a retraction gets **no grace window** and loses to an acceptance already received | The alternative is watching a known-bad file get auto-accepted. The asymmetry against D-04 is deliberate: the seller created the situation. |
| D-06 | **There is no partial delivery.** A delivery must be complete against the frozen artifact set (05.04.04) or it does not publish and starts no clock. Staged acceptance is milestones (05.03.02), declared at quote time. Work-in-progress sharing is a **progress share** — not a delivery, no clock, not acceptable | Resolves the 7-of-11-mixes case. Same principle as QC gating: no clock runs against the buyer until the seller has actually done their bit. Partial acceptance was already rejected as fracturing the transaction (05.04 CX R-03). |
| D-07 | **Escalation ladder**: notice at delivery, at 50% elapsed, at T-24h and at T-1h. The T-24h and T-1h notices go on every enabled channel and **bypass digest, batching and quiet-hours suppression**. If every channel hard-bounces, the **window pauses** | The consequence of silence is ownership, so this is the one place the platform must nag hard. And no clock runs against a party the platform failed to reach — the domain principle applied to the platform's own obligation. |
| D-08 | The buyer may extend the window **once per delivery**, by the original length, capped at the 30-day ceiling. One tap, no reason required | The highest-leverage line in the feature (DT-04): it converts silence into a recorded choice not to act, which is the answer to the "I never knew" argument. Bounded at one, because unbounded extension hands back the hostage-taking power the window removes. |
| D-09 | The acceptance basis has **two shapes**: artifacts (remote) and **attendance** (on-location). A counter-attested Session Attendance Proof (domain 02) satisfies the on-location basis and is treated as affirmative acceptance. On-location auto-accept runs 3 business days from session end | Resolves DT-03. A day rate buys attendance and effort; dissatisfaction with a performance is a dispute (24), not a withheld acceptance — consistent with domain D-07. Needs 02's agreement to the coupling (Q-04). |
| D-10 | **The seller's payout readiness is verified at delivery, not at acceptance**, with escalating notices through the window | At acceptance it is too late — DT-02 forces a rollback and every available outcome is bad. General principle: an atomic commit's preconditions are verified before it can be triggered, never inside it. See DT-05. |
| D-11 | **Self-acceptance is blocked.** An accepter who is the seller, or a member of the selling entity, may not accept. If that leaves the buying entity with no eligible accepter, one is nominated at quote time or the engagement cannot be created. The record names the accepting **human**, not the organisation | The multi-hyphenate on both sides of the timer (DT-09) — a hole the persona model itself creates. Block at creation, not at the only irreversible moment. |
| D-12 | Buyer engagement with the delivery (playback, download) is **recorded permanently** in the evidence trail but **never gates auto-accept** | Gating it would make non-engagement the buyer's winning strategy (DT-07). Recording it costs nothing and strengthens the weakest instrument the platform produces. |
| D-13 | The window is a **duration** resolved to a fixed instant at delivery, rendered to each party as an absolute date+time in their own timezone; floor **3 business days**, ceiling **30 calendar days**; sellers may extend but never shorten below the floor; redelivery restarts it at full length | Inherits 05.01.06 D-03. A duration spanning DST and a bank holiday is four different dates. The floor protects the buyer's actual evaluation practice; the ceiling stops escrow being stranded. |
