# Feature: Delivery, Acceptance & Auto-Accept Window

> **Number**: 05.04.01
> **Parent**: [Delivery, QC & Acceptance](./05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The seller hands over the work; the buyer accepts it, or a timer accepts it for them. Acceptance is the single most consequential event in this domain: it releases the escrow, executes the rights transfer, and emits the credit to the graph — all at once. Everything upstream (listings, quotes, requirements gates, revisions) exists to make this moment arrive with both parties agreeing what it means.

The auto-accept window exists because the alternative is worse. A buyer who simply stops responding would otherwise hold a seller's money hostage forever, and no amount of revision limiting fixes a buyer who does nothing at all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller, delivers and then watches a countdown that will eventually pay them whether the buyer engages or not — which is the first time most working musicians will have experienced a payment that does not depend on someone remembering them. Their persona's `Success Criteria` is "income visible in one place"; this is the event that produces it. As a buyer, accepts explicitly, or lets the window run. |
| Producer | Full | Delivers mixes and masters; accepts session work into their projects. The persona whose `Anti-Persona Behavior` — "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute" — this feature structurally removes: the stems are delivered into escrow-backed custody, so withholding is no longer available as leverage, in either direction. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An on-location engagement delivers **attendance**, not a file. There is nothing here for this lens — their performance evidence is domain 02's Session Attendance Proof, and their settlement is domain 16/17's. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, structurally and permanently. Unreleased masters live here. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

**Delivery** is the seller publishing the artifact set (05.04.04) into the engagement. It must pass QC (05.04.02) to *be* a delivery — a file that fails spec is not delivered, it is rejected back to the seller (05.04 D-02).

**Acceptance** is the buyer's affirmative act, and it fires three things atomically:

1. **Escrow release** (Payments cross-cut)
2. **Rights transfer + split execution** (05.06.03)
3. **Credit emission** to domain 02, at the elected visibility (05.02.02)

**Auto-accept** fires the same three things on a timer if the buyer neither accepts nor requests a revision within the window. The window pauses while the ball is in the seller's court (a revision in progress) and runs while it is in the buyer's.

### Happy Path

1. Seller delivers; QC passes; artifacts published to the engagement
2. Buyer notified; auto-accept countdown starts and is visible to both
3. Buyer listens — via a protected preview (05.04.03) if drafts are protected
4. Buyer accepts
5. **Atomically**: escrow releases → rights transfer executes → credit emits
6. Engagement closes; recall window opens (05.03.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer goes silent | Auto-accept fires at the window's end | Escalating reminders — this is the one place the platform must nag loudly, because the consequence is ownership |
| Buyer requests a revision | Window stops; engagement re-enters revision (05.03.03) | "Auto-accept paused" |
| Revision request lands as auto-accept fires | Race with materially different outcomes | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — precedence rule needed; see 05.03 CX-04 |
| One of the three atomic steps fails | The failure this feature exists to prevent: escrow releases but the credit never emits → the buyer paid, the seller was paid, and the platform's core asset silently lost a record | Must roll back or compensate. Never partially commit. See DT-02. |
| Buyer accepts, escrow release fails (payout account frozen) | Rights transferred, money not moved | Same atomicity requirement, opposite direction |
| Seller delivers, then wants to retract (they spotted a mistake) | Should be allowed pre-acceptance | "Retract and redeliver?" — the alternative is a seller watching a bad file get auto-accepted |
| Buyer accepts a *milestone* (05.03.02) | Releases the tranche only. Does **not** transfer rights. | Different event, deliberately (05.03.02 DT-01) |
| Partial delivery (7 of 11 mixes) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — milestones are the intended answer, but small multi-item jobs may not use them |
| On-location engagement | No file. Delivery = attendance. | Different acceptance basis entirely — see DT-03 |
| Buyer disputes rather than accepts | Escrow frozen; 24 | Nothing transfers while frozen |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Awaiting Delivery | In progress | Seller: due date. Buyer: countdown. |
| QC Failed | Delivery fails spec | Seller only — the buyer never sees a broken delivery (05.04 D-02) |
| Delivered | QC passed | Buyer: artifacts + auto-accept countdown + Accept/Revise |
| Auto-Accept Pending | Countdown running | Prominent countdown for both |
| Accepted | Buyer accepts | Confirmation naming all three consequences explicitly |
| Auto-Accepted | Timer fired | Same, marked as accepted by expiry — the distinction must persist in the record |
| Disputed | Buyer disputes | Frozen; 24 |
| Retracted | Seller pulls a delivery pre-acceptance | Reason logged |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Auto-accept transfers rights by silence — and that is a legal question the product cannot answer for itself** | Adversarial: what is the failure mode nobody mentioned? Trace auto-accept through 05.06.03. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real exposure, ❌ NOT resolvable here. On a marketplace for goods, auto-accept releases money — a low-stakes default. Here it also executes a **copyright assignment**. A buyer who was on tour and never opened the app now owns a master because a timer expired; or a session player's work-for-hire assignment vested because they did not act. Whether an assignment executed by inaction is enforceable is jurisdiction-dependent and turns on formalities (several jurisdictions require *signed writing* for a copyright assignment — an unrebutted timer is unlikely to satisfy that). This is not a design decision, it needs counsel, and it sits directly on the platform's thesis: if the rights half of auto-accept is unenforceable, the domain's differentiator is decorative in exactly the cases where the buyer disengaged. Recorded as sub-domain Q-01 and escalated to `/create-prd-security`. A plausible mitigation worth testing with counsel: auto-accept releases escrow but the rights transfer requires an affirmative signature (Contracts cross-cut), which splits the atomic event and weakens the mechanism — so it is a real trade-off, not a free fix. |
| DT-02 | **Acceptance is the atomic intersection of three systems, and a partial commit is silent data loss in the platform's core asset** | Reasoning about D-18: acceptance is where "provenance is the wedge" is either true or false. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is why the **Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer** cross-cut exists and why this feature is its primary trigger site. Consider each partial failure. Escrow releases, credit emission fails: everyone is paid, nobody notices, and the credit graph — the thing the user cannot take with them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture — has a hole. Nobody will ever report this, because both parties got what they came for. Rights transfer succeeds, escrow release fails: the seller has assigned their copyright and not been paid, which is the single worst outcome the platform could produce and is *precisely* the failure the whole product exists to prevent. The three must commit together or not at all, and the credit emission is the leg most likely to be treated as fire-and-forget by an implementer because it is the one nobody is chasing. Flagging it here is the point. |
| DT-03 | Delivery always produces an artifact | The candidate list's framing | ❌ REJECTED — on-location engagements (05.01.05) deliver **attendance**. A session bassist who played the date has delivered; there is no file, nothing to QC, nothing to watermark. Acceptance for these rests on a different basis — the buyer confirming the person showed up and played — and domain 02's Session Attendance Proof is the natural evidence. This means the acceptance event has (at least) two shapes, and a file-shaped model would either block on-location acceptance or force a fake artifact. It also means auto-accept is *more* important for on-location work, not less: there is no file for a buyer to review, so the only thing between the seller and payment is the buyer remembering to click. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A file that fails QC is not a delivery | It never reaches the buyer and never starts their clock. |
| D-02 | Acceptance fires escrow release + rights transfer + credit emission **atomically** | Any partial commit is either unpaid assignment or a silent hole in the credit graph. See DT-02. |
| D-03 | Auto-accept is required, because a revision limit does not bind a buyer who does nothing | Without it, the seller's payment depends on the buyer's attention forever. |
| D-04 | `Accepted` and `Auto-Accepted` are distinct and the distinction persists permanently | It is the difference between consent and expiry, and DT-01 means it may become legally load-bearing. |
| D-05 | A seller may retract a delivery before acceptance | The alternative is watching a known-bad file get auto-accepted. |
