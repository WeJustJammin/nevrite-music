# Feature: Retainers & Recurring Engagements

> **Number**: 05.03.04
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)

## Overview

The ongoing relationship rather than the one-off job: a label that keeps a mixer on £3,000/month for up to six mixes, an artist whose regular engineer handles whatever comes up, a producer on a monthly retainer to a publisher. The recurring billing is not the interesting part — the drawdown is. What does "up to six mixes" mean when the fifth arrives on the 29th, and what happens to the two nobody used?

## Behavior

A retainer has two halves and only one of them is this domain's.

**The billing half** — recurring charge, proration, dunning, lapse, plan changes — is the **Subscriptions & Entitlements** cross-cut. That cross-cut already exists and already serves this domain by name. Rebuilding it here would be the classic mechanism-as-node error.

**The drawdown half** is domain-owned and modelled nowhere else: an allowance denominated in domain units (mixes, songs, days, hours), engagements created against it that consume from it, a period boundary, and a rule for what happens to unused allowance. The Subscriptions cross-cut's entitlement model can gate *whether* a feature is available; it does not track "4 of 6 mixes used, 11 days left".

The allowance's expiry rule is the whole product decision: **use-it-or-lose-it** (the seller's protection — they reserved capacity for you), **rollover** (the buyer's protection — they paid for it), or **capped rollover**. Each is defensible; the choice determines who bears the cost of a quiet month.

### Happy Path

1. Seller and buyer agree retainer terms on a quote (05.02.01) — period, price, allowance, expiry rule, scope
2. Subscriptions cross-cut sets up recurring billing; first period charges
3. Buyer raises an engagement against the retainer; it draws one unit from the allowance
4. Engagement runs its normal lifecycle (05.03.01) but with **no per-engagement escrow** — the retainer was already paid
5. Period ends; unused allowance is handled per the expiry rule; next period charges
6. Either party ends the arrangement at a period boundary

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Allowance exhausted mid-period | Further work is priced normally or blocked | "You've used all 6 mixes. Additional work quotes at your retainer rate + {x}." |
| Allowance unused at period end | Expiry rule applies | Per D-02 — must be stated on the quote, never a platform default |
| Buyer dumps 6 mixes in the last week | Allowance says yes; capacity (05.01.07) says no. The retainer sold *access*, not a guarantee of throughput. | "Your retainer covers this, but {seller} has 3 slots. These will queue." |
| Payment fails mid-period | Dunning is the Subscriptions cross-cut's; but an engagement is **in flight** and the seller is working | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — does an in-flight engagement survive a failed retainer charge? See Q-02 |
| Retainer engagements and rights | Six mixes under one retainer, each with its own rights consequence | Rights posture is set once on the retainer and inherited by each engagement (05.06.01) |
| Seller becomes unavailable mid-period | The buyer paid for access they cannot use | Cover (05.05.01) or refund; the retainer's value is the person |
| Retainer scope vs a job outside it | "It's a retainer, just do it" is the abuse mode | The retainer's quote declares its scope; out-of-scope work is a change order (05.03.03) |
| Retainer + points (05.06.02) | A retained producer taking points on everything they touch | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **A retainer is a subscription, so it belongs entirely to the Subscriptions & Entitlements cross-cut** | Node Classification Gate — tested honestly, because if true this node should not exist. | ❌ REJECTED, but only half of it. The **billing** genuinely is the cross-cut's — recurring charge, proration, dunning, lapse — and that cross-cut names this domain as one it serves. What the cross-cut does not model, and could not sensibly model, is **drawdown**: an allowance in domain units, engagements consuming from it, a period boundary, and an expiry rule. An entitlement answers "may this org do this?" — a boolean. A retainer answers "how many of these are left, and for how long?" — a meter with a music-specific unit (a *mix*, a *song*, a *day*). Building the meter into the cross-cut would push domain semantics into a mechanism serving eight domains, which is exactly what the cross-cut extraction is supposed to prevent. So: engine there, drawdown here. |
| DT-02 | **A retainer engagement has no escrow — which quietly removes the domain's core leverage from its most valuable relationships** | Adversarial: trace the escrow↔rights-transfer mechanism (the domain's stated differentiator) through a retainer. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is uncomfortable. The domain rationale calls escrow release ↔ rights transfer "the only leverage that exists at the only moment anyone is motivated". A retainer is paid in advance, so at the moment a retained mix is delivered there is **no money in escrow to withhold** — the leverage is gone. This is the same structural hole as points-only deals (05.01.03 DT-01), arriving from a different direction, and together they mean the platform's flagship mechanism is absent from two of its most professionally significant deal shapes. The mitigation is that rights transfer must attach to the **retainer instrument** signed up front (Contracts cross-cut) rather than to a per-engagement release — i.e. for retainers, the contract does the work escrow does elsewhere. Worth flagging to `/create-prd-security` and to the Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer cross-cut, whose whole premise assumes a payment event at the moment of vesting. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Billing engine = Subscriptions cross-cut; drawdown = this domain | An entitlement is a boolean; an allowance is a meter in music units. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | The expiry rule (use-it-or-lose-it / rollover / capped) is declared on the quote and never defaulted by the platform | It decides who bears the cost of a quiet month — a commercial term, not a setting. |
| D-03 | A retainer sells access, not guaranteed throughput; capacity still governs | Otherwise a retainer is a promise the seller's body cannot keep. |
| D-04 | Rights transfer attaches to the retainer instrument, not to a per-engagement escrow release | Because there is no escrow release. See DT-02. |
