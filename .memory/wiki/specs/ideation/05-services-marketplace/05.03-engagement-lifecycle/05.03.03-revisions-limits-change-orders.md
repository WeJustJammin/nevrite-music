# Feature: Revisions, Revision Limits & Change Orders

> **Number**: 05.03.03
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The economics of "can you just try one more thing": a bounded allowance of revision rounds inside the agreed scope, and a change order for everything outside it. This is where creative services differ most sharply from every other kind of marketplace transaction — the deliverable is subjective, the buyer's taste is legitimate, and there is no objective point at which a mix is finished. Without a bound, an engagement has no terminal state and escrow has nothing to release against.

The feature exists to do three things that today's music market cannot do at all: make "that's out of scope" a rule the platform states rather than a confrontation the seller must start; make the number of rounds a *comparable listing attribute* rather than a lie sellers tell to win work; and make the exit from a scope disagreement a priced, one-click object rather than a relationship-ending argument.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller: sees rounds remaining per engagement (or per milestone), the frozen note set for the round they are working, and a one-click **"this is a change order"** action carrying a reason and a price. This matters more than it sounds — the reason musicians do unpaid revisions is that refusing costs them the relationship, and a rule that exists *before* the argument removes the cost of refusing. They also see the round's own due date (DT-07), which is the first time their revision labour has ever had an SLA attached to it. As a buyer: sees exactly what they have left, what a further round would cost, and a diff of the composed scope of record after every accepted change order. |
| Producer | Full | The persona most damaged by unbounded revisions, because their engagements are the longest and their clients the most opinionated. Structurally, they are also the party who **absorbs the fan-out**: their client's mix revision on day 20 lands on a session bassist whose own engagement closed on day 2, which is a *recall* for the bassist (DT-06) and a cost the producer eats. They therefore need the round counter on their *sold* engagement and the recall windows on their *bought* ones on the same surface. Their `Anti-Persona Behavior` — pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention — has a specific version here: grinding a session player through six free revision rounds on a flat fee. The per-engagement allowance on the player's own quote is what blocks it, and it blocks it against a Producer, not a stranger. |
| Operator | None | Not visible, and specifically so. A revision is remote work on material already in hand — it does not touch a room. A change order that adds a studio day does not reach an Operator as a change order; it reaches them as a **booking** in domain 16, on its own terms. An Operator must never be able to infer an engagement's scope, fee or trouble from their calendar. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, structurally and permanently. Revision notes are candid professional criticism of unreleased work ("the vocal is flat through the second verse") — the most reputationally sensitive text the platform will ever hold. There is no fan-facing view of this feature and no aggregate derived from it (a "3 revisions" badge on a public profile would leak exactly the wrong fact about a seller). See Q-06 on whether even dispute disclosure needs redaction. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two distinct objects, and the whole feature is the line between them:

**Revision** — a request for changes *inside* the accepted scope. Consumes one of N allowed rounds. Free. Pauses the turnaround clock while the seller waits for notes, resumes when the notes freeze (05.01.06 D-02).

**Change order** — a request *outside* the accepted scope. Consumes no allowance. Costs money, and may move the due date. It is a mini-quote: requested, issued, expiring, accepted — and on acceptance it amends the scope of record and captures additional escrow atomically.

A **round** is not a note. A round is a batch of notes and a redelivery. Ten notes in one message is one round; ten messages over ten days is a problem the batching rule exists to prevent.

### The round's mechanics

1. **A round opens when the buyer submits at least one note.** An empty request ("I'll send feedback next week") is not a request and does not open a round — it is the exploit that defeats auto-accept for free (DT-05).
2. **The notes window**: from the moment the round opens, the buyer may add notes to the *same* round at no allowance cost. Seed default **48 hours** (24h on any rush tier — see the per-craft table below).
3. **Freeze**: the window closes on the buyer's explicit "Send to {seller}" (the normal path) or on window expiry. On freeze the note set is immutable, the round dispatches, and the turnaround clock resumes (05.01.06 D-02).
4. **Notes arriving after freeze do not join the round.** They queue as the opening notes of the *next* round, or the seller may voluntarily pull them in — recorded as a seller-granted addition so generosity does not become an expectation silently.
5. **The seller cannot redeliver against an unfrozen round.** The round dispatches on freeze; the seller works the frozen set.
6. **The allowance decrements on redelivery, not on request** (DT-08). A round the seller converts wholly into a change order never happened, and costs the buyer nothing.
7. Every note carries a **resolution status** — `addressed` / `not-addressed, reason` / `disputed` — set by the seller at redelivery and visible to the buyer. This is what makes "did you do all my notes?" answerable instead of arguable.
8. Notes are **anchored to the artifact version they were made against** and stay readable against it forever. A timestamp comment at 0:42 of v1 does not migrate to 0:42 of v2, because 0:42 of v2 is a different bar (Media Handling cross-cut).

### The change order's mechanics

A change order inherits the quote's discipline wholesale (05.02.01 D-02, D-03), because it is a quote:

- **Flow**: buyer requests *or* seller issues unsolicited → seller issues **CO-n** → buyer accepts. Mirrors 05.02.01 exactly.
- **Carries**: scope delta (what is added/changed/removed), price delta, due-date delta, allowance delta (**defaults to 0** — see D-11), and a mandatory expiry (seed default **7 days**).
- **Immutable per version**; a renegotiated CO is CO-n v2 with a diff; supersession voids the earlier version.
- **Acceptance and escrow top-up are one atomic transaction** (05.02.01 D-05). A failed top-up means the CO is not accepted and the scope is unchanged.
- **On acceptance the scope of record recomposes**: the engagement references `quote vN + CO-1..CO-n` rendered as **one composed document with a diff**, never as a pile of amendments. An eight-amendment scope nobody can read is consent in the same fictional sense 05.02.01 DT-02 rejected.
- **A pending change order is the buyer's ball, so auto-accept runs** (DT-09). This is the mechanism that dissolves the change-order deadlock without needing anyone to exit.

### The allowance is bounded by count OR by time — never by neither

An unbounded round count is not generosity, it is the removal of the engagement's terminal state (DT-04), because every request pauses auto-accept. A seller may therefore offer a high count, or an uncounted allowance inside a **revision window** ("unlimited revisions within 30 days of first delivery"), but never an offer bounded by nothing. **Allowance `0` is a legitimate and common value** (union sessions, attended sessions — DT-13); the model must not assume ≥1.

### Per-craft seed defaults (resolves prior Q-02)

Pre-filled onto the quote from the craft taxonomy (05.01.02). **Seller-overridable in both directions.** These are a *starting value*, never a cap or a floor.

| Craft | Rounds | Notes window | Revision turnaround |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mastering — single | 1 | 24h | 2 working days |
| Mastering — album | 2 | 48h | 3 working days |
| Mixing — single | 3 | 48h | 3 working days |
| Mixing — album (per milestone) | 2 | 48h | 5 working days |
| Production (full track/album, per milestone) | 2 | 48h | 5 working days |
| Beat / instrumental — custom | 2 | 48h | 3 working days |
| Session performance — remote overdub | 1 | 24h | 2 working days |
| Session performance — **attended / on-location** | **0** | n/a | n/a — the notes were the session (DT-13) |
| Vocal tuning / editing | 1 | 24h | 2 working days |
| Audio restoration / stem separation | 1 | 24h | 2 working days |
| Composition / arrangement / scoring | 2 | 48h | 5 working days |
| Audio post / podcast edit | 2 | 48h | 3 working days |
| **Union session (05.02.03)** | **0 by rule** | n/a | n/a — every re-cut is a change order at scale (DT-13, Q-04) |

> **Rush inheritance**: if a rush tier is bought (05.01.06), the **revision turnaround inherits the rush tier** and the notes window halves. A 48-hour rush mix with a 5-day revision round is a fiction, and per 05.01.06 D-04 the buyer paid for a named thing (speed) — they did not pay for it once.

### Happy Path

1. Seller delivers (05.04.01); QC passes; buyer notified; auto-accept countdown starts
2. Buyer opens a revision round by submitting a note — timestamped against the audio via the Media Handling player. **Auto-accept pauses.** Round shows `2 of 3 remaining` (not yet decremented — DT-08)
3. Buyer adds four more notes over the next 6 hours; all join round 1 at no extra cost; 48h notes window counting down and visible to both
4. Buyer clicks **"Send to {seller}"** → notes freeze; round dispatches; **turnaround clock resumes**; the round's own due date is committed and shown to both (DT-07)
5. Seller works the frozen set; redelivers within the revision turnaround; marks each note `addressed`. **Allowance decrements to `1 of 3 remaining`**
6. Auto-accept countdown restarts on the new delivery
7. Buyer accepts → escrow releases + rights transfer + credit emission, atomically (05.04.01 D-02); recall window opens (05.03.06)

**The exhaustion fork** (steps 5–7 replaced):

5. Buyer spends round 3; seller redelivers; `0 of 3 remaining`
6. Buyer submits a further note. The platform does not refuse it — it routes it: **"You're out of rounds. {Seller} can quote this as a change order."**
7. Seller issues **CO-1**: `+£120, +3 working days, +1 round`, expiring in 7 days. **Auto-accept resumes** — the ball is the buyer's (DT-09)
8. Buyer accepts → escrow tops up atomically → scope of record recomposes to `quote v2 + CO-1` with a diff → the granted round opens

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Is this a revision or a change order?** | **The taste dispute.** The accepted quote version (05.02.01 D-01) is the arbiter, and its structured exclusions (05.02.01 DT-01) do most of the work. Where it is genuinely silent, no rule resolves it. | Seller proposes "this is a change order" with a reason; buyer disputes → 24; escrow frozen |
| "Make it more like the reference" after 3 rounds | Almost certainly in scope — the reference was a term of the quote | Revision |
| "Actually, let's try it in a different key" | Not a revision. That is a new recording. | Change order |
| Empty revision request ("I'll send notes next week") | **Rejected — it is not a request** (DT-05). Auto-accept keeps running. | "Add at least one note to start a revision round. Auto-accept still fires on {date}." |
| Buyer drips notes one at a time | Notes arriving before freeze join the open round; after freeze they open the next one | "These will be added to round 2, which will use your last remaining round. Send now, or add more first?" |
| Notes window expires with the buyer still typing | Freeze fires; round dispatches with what exists; unsent draft text is preserved and becomes the next round's opening notes | "Round 1 was sent at {time} — your window closed. Anything new starts round 2." |
| Seller tries to redeliver against an unfrozen round | Blocked. The note set is not final; the seller would be working a moving target. | "Waiting for {buyer} to send their notes — window closes {time}." |
| Buyer withdraws a round before the seller redelivers | Free. Nothing was consumed (DT-08). Auto-accept resumes. | "Round withdrawn — you still have 3 of 3." |
| Seller converts the whole round to a change order | No round consumed (DT-08). Auto-accept resumes (DT-09). | Buyer: allowance unchanged + the CO to accept/decline/dispute |
| Seller actions 8 of 10 notes, COs the other 2 | Round **is** consumed (a redelivery happened). The 2 COs are a separate object. | Per-note resolution status: 8 `addressed`, 2 `not-addressed — change order CO-1` |
| Seller redelivers an identical file, no changes made | Not a redelivery. QC checksum is unchanged. Charging a round for a refusal is theft. | Blocked → seller must either change something or return the round with per-note reasons (allowance refunds; auto-accept resumes) |
| Buyer's round 2 contradicts round 1 ("actually the vocal's too loud") | Legitimate. Consumes a round. Taste is allowed to change; the allowance is what bounds it. | A/B against both delivered versions; version history is the record |
| Buyer asks to **revert** to a prior delivered version | **Does not consume a round.** Re-publishing an existing artifact is not new work (DT-11's logic in miniature). | One click: "Restore v1 as the current delivery" — auto-accept restarts |
| Allowance exhausted; buyer refuses the CO and refuses to accept | **Not a deadlock** (DT-09). A pending CO is the buyer's ball, so auto-accept is running. Silence pays the seller. | Countdown, loudly. "Accept the delivery, accept the change order, or raise a dispute by {date}." |
| Allowance exhausted; buyer **disputes** instead of doing nothing | Escrow frozen; 24 takes it. This — not silence — is the seller's real exposure. | Dispute path; see Q-03 on the seller's exit right |
| Seller offers a free extra round | Allowed; the platform does not forbid generosity | Logged as a seller-granted round, so it does not become a silent expectation |
| Seller offers "unlimited revisions" | **Permitted only when time-bounded** (DT-04). An uncounted, undated allowance is not a term, it is the absence of one. | Listing/quote renders it as "Unlimited revisions within 30 days of first delivery" — the window is mandatory. Whether the platform exposes this listing tactic at all is Q-01. |
| Revision requested on a **milestone** (05.03.02) | Allowances are per-milestone (05.03.02 D-04) | Per-stage counter: "Mix stage — 2 of 2 rounds left" |
| Unused rounds on milestone 1 | **Do not roll over.** Banking 3 unused tracking rounds to spend 6 on the mix is exactly the exposure the per-milestone rule exists to bound. | "Rounds are per stage and don't carry over." Stated on the quote, before acceptance. |
| Change order accepted → scope amended | Engagement references `quote v2 + CO-1`, recomposed into one document | Composed scope with a diff, in one place, never scattered across amendments |
| Change order expires unaccepted | Lapses. Scope unchanged. Auto-accept was running throughout and may have already fired. | "CO-1 expired on {date}. Ask {seller} to re-issue?" |
| Change order accepted but the escrow top-up fails | The CO must not exist. Acceptance and capture are one transaction (05.02.01 D-05). | "Payment didn't go through — CO-1 is still open until {expiry}." Scope unchanged. |
| **Revision request lands as auto-accept fires** | **The request wins if its submission timestamp precedes the window's expiry.** Auto-accept exists to punish inaction; a buyer who acted was not inactive — the same reasoning CX-01 already applied to requirements supply. | Deterministic, single authoritative clock. Mechanism is `/create-prd-architecture`'s; the *rule* is D-10. |
| Buyer requests a revision **after** auto-accept fired | Engagement is closed; rights transferred; money released. This is a recall, not a revision. | Routed to 05.03.06 — "This engagement closed on {date}. Recalls available until {date}." |
| **Revision requires re-work by a sub-contracted player** (05.05.02/05.05.03) | **A revision upstream is a recall downstream** (DT-06). The player's engagement may already be accepted and closed, so the producer must raise a *recall* against them — inside the player's window — or buy a new engagement if it closed. | Producer sees: "This note needs new bass. {Player}'s recall window closes {date}." Then: recall, or quote a new session. |
| Sub-contracted player's recall window has already closed | The producer eats it: they pay for a new session out of a fee that assumed one bass take. Their own allowance to their client says nothing about the player's. | "No window left — this is a new engagement with {player}." The cost lands on the Producer. Q-05. |
| Buyer sent the **wrong stems** at the gate; the whole mix is wrong | Not a revision. A requirements defect that surfaced late (05.03.01 D-03). The seller returns the engagement to `Awaiting Requirements`, the round refunds, the clock stops. | Seller-initiated, reason mandatory, visible in the engagement's history — because a seller who does this repeatedly is a 24 problem, not a gate problem |
| **Buyer is a Band entity; two members send contradicting notes** | An engagement contracted by an entity (domain 01) nominates **one approver** at acceptance. Only the approver spends allowance and accepts. Other members may comment — visible to the seller, marked **non-binding**. | Seller sees: "Binding notes from {approver}. 2 non-binding comments from band members." Design-by-committee is not the seller's problem to solve. |
| Band's nominated approver leaves the band mid-engagement | The entity must re-nominate before it can spend a round or accept. Auto-accept keeps running — the buyer's internal governance is not the seller's risk. | "This engagement needs an approver from {band} by {date}." |
| Notes submission fails (network) | Draft persists locally and server-side per keystroke batch; nothing is lost; the round does not open until a note lands | "Not sent — retry" with the draft intact. Never a silent loss. |
| Buyer submits 200 notes in one round | **Not a failure mode.** It is the batching rule working: the round is the unit, the count is the bound. An 11-track album round legitimately runs to 50+ notes. | If the volume exceeds the scope of record, the seller's remedy is the change order — not a note cap |
| Malicious note content (script, injection, abuse) | Escaped on render; never executed. Abusive content is a 24 matter and does not pause the seller's obligations. | Standard content escaping; report action available to the seller |
| **Points-only engagement** (05.01.03 DT-01 / 05.06.02) | No escrow, so a CO has nothing to top up — its consideration is cash, additional points, or nothing. **And the incentive inverts**: a seller holding a permanent royalty interest *wants* the record to be good, so they will revise willingly past any allowance. | Allowance shown but low-stakes; CO form asks for consideration explicitly. Mirrors 05.03.06's points inversion. |
| **Union session** (05.02.03) | Allowance is `0` by rule; every re-cut is a new session call priced at scale. A "free revision" on a union date is asking a player to work off the agreement. | Field locked at 0 with the reason stated; CO renders at scale minimum. Exact rule is 05.02.03's — Q-04. |
| **Attended / on-location engagement** (05.01.05) | Allowance defaults to `0`. The notes *were* the session; the client signed off in the room. A later change is a recall (05.03.06) or a new date. | Field renders `0` with an explanation, not hidden — a silent 0 reads as an omission |
| Retainer drawdown engagement (05.03.04) | Each drawn-down engagement carries its own allowance from the retainer instrument. The retainer's meter counts **engagements**, not rounds. | "Mix 3 of 4 this month — 3 rounds included" |
| Seller sits on a frozen round indefinitely | **The round has its own due date** (DT-07). Breach uses the same remedy path as the original SLA (05.01.06 Q-01). | Revision due date + breach banner. Fast first delivery + glacial revisions is a real seller tactic and this is what closes it. |
| Rush engagement, standard-speed revision | The rush premium bought the *engagement's* speed, not one delivery (05.01.06 D-04) | Revision turnaround inherits the rush tier automatically; if breached, the rush premium is in scope for the auto-refund |
| Engagement in progress, nothing delivered yet | There is nothing to revise. The feature has no surface. | Revision UI absent entirely — not an empty state, an absent one |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not Applicable | No delivery yet, or allowance is 0 (union/attended) | Feature absent, or `0` with its reason stated |
| Rounds Remaining | Delivery accepted into `Delivered` | "2 of 3 revision rounds left" + auto-accept countdown |
| Round Open — notes window | Buyer submits ≥1 note | Both: "Round 2 open — window closes {time}." Clock paused; auto-accept paused; **allowance not yet decremented** |
| Round Frozen / In Revision | Buyer sends, or window expires | Seller: frozen note set + committed revision due date. Buyer: "With {seller} — due {date}." Turnaround clock running |
| Round Returned | Seller declines to action, with per-note reasons | Allowance refunded; auto-accept resumes; buyer may accept, CO, or dispute |
| Exhausted | Allowance spent (decremented at redelivery) | "Further changes need a change order" + the seller's pre-priced extra-round option if they set one |
| Change Order Requested | Buyer asks, or the seller routes an out-of-scope note | Seller: a delta to price. Buyer: "waiting on {seller}" |
| Change Order Issued (CO-n) | Seller issues | Price + new due date + scope diff + expiry countdown + Accept. **Auto-accept running** (DT-09) |
| Change Order Superseded | A later version issued | Read-only, marked superseded, diff to current |
| Change Order Accepted | Buyer accepts + escrow tops up **atomically** | Scope of record recomposed to `quote vN + CO-1..n`, shown as one diffed document |
| Change Order Expired / Declined | Expiry passed, or buyer declines | Scope unchanged; re-issue CTA; auto-accept was never paused |
| Contested | Parties disagree on revision vs change order | Dispute path (24); escrow frozen; **all clocks stop** |
| Error — notes not sent | Network/submit failure | "Not sent — retry." Draft intact. Round has not opened; auto-accept unaffected. |
| Partial — round part-actioned | Seller redelivers addressing some notes | Per-note status: `addressed` / `not-addressed, reason` / `disputed`. Round consumed. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **"Unlimited revisions" is the single largest cause of engagement failure in creative services, and a bounded allowance is what makes escrow releasable at all** | Domain expertise + reasoning about escrow's requirements: escrow needs a release trigger. Acceptance is the trigger. If the buyer can always ask for one more thing, acceptance never arrives, and the money sits in escrow indefinitely while the seller works for free. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the bound is not a seller-protection nicety, it is a structural precondition for the domain's escrow model. But the interesting part is the second-order effect: a revision limit only works if there is a **backstop for the buyer who simply refuses to engage** — otherwise the seller's protection is "the buyer must accept", which the buyer can decline forever. That backstop is auto-accept (05.04.01), which means the revision limit and the auto-accept window are two halves of one mechanism and cannot be specified independently. |
| DT-02 | **The revision-vs-change-order boundary is the taste dispute, and no rule resolves it — but exclusions dissolve most of it before it starts** | Adversarial: where is the unresolvable case? | ✅ CONFIRMED, in both halves. There is genuinely no rule that decides whether "it needs more energy" is inside the scope of a mix — that is a judgement, which is why 24 must exist and why the domain's dispute path is not optional. But the volume is tractable: most disputes are not about taste at all, they are about *assumptions* — the buyer assumed tuning was included, assumed stems were included, assumed an instrumental was included. Those are not judgements, they are unstated terms, and 05.02.01 DT-01's structured exclusions turn each of them into a fact the quote already settled. The design conclusion: invest in exclusions, not in adjudication. Exclusions are cheap and prevent; adjudication is expensive and only resolves. |
| DT-03 | A revision is a note | Naive framing | ❌ REJECTED — a revision is a **round**: a batch of notes plus a redelivery. If a note were a round, a buyer sending "also the snare" three minutes after their first message would have spent two of three rounds, which is absurd and would train buyers to hoard notes anxiously. If rounds were unbounded in time, a buyer could keep one round open for weeks, dripping notes. The batching rule — a round opens on a note, freezes on send-or-expiry, closes on redelivery — is what makes the allowance both fair and countable. Timestamped comments against the audio (Media Handling cross-cut) are the natural note format and make a round legible as a unit of work. |
| DT-04 | **"Unlimited revisions" is not a generous setting — it is the deletion of the engagement's terminal state, because every request pauses auto-accept** | Adversarial, tracing DT-01's own mechanism: if a revision request pauses auto-accept, and the round count is unbounded, then the buyer holds a pause button with infinite presses. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it converts a product question into a structural constraint. DT-01 left "may a seller offer unlimited revisions?" as a pure product decision, on the assumption that the harm was to the seller who chose it. That is wrong. An uncounted allowance defeats the *platform's* backstop, not just the seller's judgement: escrow acquires no release trigger, the capacity slot (05.01.07) never frees, and the engagement joins the population of eternal objects the domain exists to prevent. The resolution is that **an allowance must be bounded by count or by time, and may be bounded by both — but never by neither.** "Unlimited revisions within 30 days of first delivery" is a coherent, marketable, honest term with a terminal state. "Unlimited revisions" full stop is not a term at all. The residual product question is narrow and survives as Q-01: whether the platform exposes the time-bounded variant as a listing tactic. |
| DT-05 | **The exploit is not the buyer with too many notes — it is the buyer with none. An empty revision request is a free, infinite hold on the seller's money** | Adversarial: what is the cheapest way to attack this feature? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it was latent in the surface spec's own words ("clock pauses at the request and resumes when the seller has the notes") — which concedes that the request and the notes are separable events. If they are, a buyer types "I want a revision, I'll send feedback next week", and in one action pauses auto-accept, pauses the turnaround clock, holds the escrow, and occupies a capacity slot — **at the cost of zero notes and, under a decrement-at-request model, one round they could simply repeat**. The fix is that substance defines the request: a round opens only on ≥1 note. That in turn forces the **notes window** to exist, because the anti-drip rule (DT-03) and the "notes must exist first" rule are otherwise in tension — the buyer needs a bounded period to finish thinking, and the seller needs a frozen target. 48h/24h-on-rush, freeze on send-or-expiry, clock resumes at freeze. This is the mechanism DT-03 implied but did not specify. |
| DT-06 | **A revision upstream is a recall downstream — and the multi-hyphenate Producer is the party who silently absorbs it** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with 05.05 (multi-party supply)? Trace one revision note through a subcontracted session. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real, unmodelled break, and it is the sharpest cross-domain failure in this feature. A producer sells an album mix with 3 rounds. They buy a session bassist's overdub, which the producer **accepts on day 2** — releasing escrow, transferring rights, emitting the credit (05.04.01 D-02). On day 20 the client spends round 2: "the bass needs to be more behind the beat." That is squarely inside the mix's scope, so the producer owes it for free — but the bassist's engagement is *closed*. For the bassist this is not a revision at all; it is a **recall** (05.03.06), possible only if their window is still open and they still have the session (05.03.06 DT-02). If the window closed, it is a new engagement, at a new fee, funded entirely by the producer out of a fee that assumed one bass take. **The two features' clocks are unaligned and nothing currently aligns them**: the producer's revision allowance runs for weeks; their suppliers' recall windows expire independently. The design consequence is concrete — a producer buying supply into an engagement they are selling needs their suppliers' recall windows surfaced *against* their own allowance, on one screen, before they accept a supplier's delivery. Whether the platform should go further and structurally couple them (e.g. a supplier window that cannot expire before the buyer's engagement closes) is Q-05, because it imposes a real cost on the session player, who did their job correctly. |
| DT-07 | **The revision round has no declared turnaround, which makes the SLA a promise about first delivery only — and sellers already exploit exactly this gap** | Domain expertise: what does a buyer complain about that the spec is currently silent on? | ✅ CONFIRMED — a genuine hole. The quote declares turnaround (05.01.06) and the clock starts at the requirements gate and stops at delivery. A seller who delivers a mix in 5 days and then sits on round 2 for three weeks has **met their SLA**. This is not hypothetical; "fast first delivery, glacial revisions" is a well-worn tactic on every services marketplace, and it is rational under the rule as written, because only the first delivery is measured. It is also the exact inverse of 05.01.06 DT-02's fairness argument: that rule exists so a slow *buyer* cannot make the seller late, and its unexamined complement is that a slow *seller* is not late either once they have delivered once. So the revision round needs its own committed due date, declared on the quote, seeded per craft, inheriting any rush tier, and breaching into the same remedy path as the original (05.01.06 Q-01). The rush inheritance is the part that is not obvious and is the most clearly correct: 05.01.06 D-04 says an unmet rush premium auto-refunds because the buyer paid for a named thing — and a buyer who paid +50% for 48-hour turnaround did not pay for it to apply once. |
| DT-08 | **The allowance must decrement at redelivery, not at request — otherwise the counter charges the buyer for the seller's decisions** | Reasoning about who controls each event in the round. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it corrects the surface spec's own happy path (which decremented at request). Trace the alternatives. Decrement-at-request means: a buyer who opens a round and immediately withdraws it ("ignore that, I was wrong") has paid for nothing; a seller who converts the entire round into a change order has *charged the buyer a round for refusing to do the work*, then charged them again for the change order; and a seller who returns the round with "I don't hear it" has taken a round for a redelivery that never happened. All three are indefensible, and all three vanish under decrement-at-redelivery, because a round is defined as notes **plus a redelivery** (DT-03) and is therefore incomplete — and uncounted — until the redelivery exists. The rule also has no abuse surface in the other direction: a buyer cannot farm free rounds, because opening a round without notes is impossible (DT-05) and every round that produces work is counted. The one case it deliberately leaves counted is the seller who addresses 8 of 10 notes and change-orders 2 — a redelivery happened, so the round is spent, which is correct. |
| DT-09 | **The change-order "deadlock" is not a deadlock, because a pending change order is the buyer's ball — and auto-accept runs on the buyer's ball** | Adversarial re-examination of the surface spec's stated worst case ("buyer refuses to raise a change order and refuses to accept"). | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it substantially resolves a question that was headed for MoSCoW. The pause rule (05.01.06 D-02, 05.04.01) is not "pause during any disagreement" — it is "**pause whenever the ball is in the seller's court**". A change order sitting unaccepted is not in the seller's court: the seller has delivered, the seller has priced the extra work, and there are exactly three things only the buyer can now do — accept the delivery, accept the CO, or dispute. Silence is therefore inaction, which is precisely what auto-accept exists to terminate. So the passive deadlock dissolves structurally: the buyer who stonewalls a change order gets auto-accepted and the seller gets paid for the work they actually did. What survives is the **active** case: a buyer who disputes rather than stonewalls freezes the escrow and drags the seller into 24 over a scope judgement (DT-02) that no rule can settle. That is a much narrower and more honest problem than "the buyer can hold me hostage forever", and it is what Q-03 now asks. Corollary worth stating: this makes the CO's **mandatory expiry** load-bearing rather than tidy, because an expired CO must not leave a paused engagement behind. |
| DT-10 | The platform should classify a note as in-scope or out-of-scope automatically (read the note text, compare against the quote's inclusions/exclusions, rule on it) | Tempting: it is the domain's highest-volume friction and it looks like a text-classification problem | ❌ REJECTED as adjudication — permitted only as a private prompt to the seller. Three reasons, in ascending order of seriousness. (1) DT-02 already established the correct investment: exclusions prevent, adjudication resolves — a classifier is spend on the expensive half. (2) A classifier is wrong sometimes, and when it is wrong the platform is the *author* of the wrong answer rather than the neutral holder of the scope of record, which is the one asset (05.02.01 D-01) that makes disputes tractable at all. (3) Decisive: **who is shown the output determines what it is.** Shown privately to the seller ("this note may fall outside your stated exclusions — check before you start"), it is a helpful prompt that leaves the judgement where it belongs and costs nothing when wrong. Shown to the *buyer* ("this looks in-scope to us"), it is the platform taking the buyer's side against the seller's professional judgement, pre-emptively, in the exact dispute the seller was structurally too polite to start — which inverts the feature's entire purpose (the platform, not the person, says "that's a change order" — *on the seller's behalf*, never against them). A buyer-facing scope classifier would be the single most damaging thing this feature could ship. Note also that revision notes plus unreleased audio are exactly the payload `security-first` says must not be shipped to a model casually. |
| DT-11 | Unused revision rounds should refund or discount — the buyer paid for 3 rounds and used 1 | The naive-fairness intuition, and a thing buyers will ask for out loud | ❌ REJECTED — it inverts the feature. The allowance is a **ceiling on the seller's exposure**, not a purchased quantity of labour. A mix quoted with 3 rounds is not three times a mix quoted with 1; the price is for the mix, and the rounds describe how much taste-iteration the seller is willing to absorb before the meter starts. Refunding unused rounds would make spending all three the buyer's rational move — use it or lose it — which manufactures precisely the behaviour the bound exists to discourage, and would train buyers to invent notes on a mix they were happy with. Worse, it hands a disputing buyer a new argument: that a round they spent badly (a note the seller addressed correctly, or a preference they reversed) was value they did not receive and is owed back. That is a per-round quality argument, i.e. the taste dispute (DT-02) multiplied by N. The legitimate version of this instinct is the *opposite* direction and already exists: a seller may pre-price an **extra round** on the listing, which is simply a change order with a template — one click at a known price instead of a negotiation. That gives the buyer optionality without giving them a refund claim. |
| DT-12 | Escrow should hold back a revision reserve — release most of the money at delivery, the remainder once rounds are exhausted or waived | Reasoning about seller cash flow on long engagements | ❌ REJECTED — it breaks the domain's most load-bearing invariant. Acceptance fires escrow release + rights transfer + credit emission **atomically** (05.04.01 D-02), and a reserve creates a second, later release trigger with no defined firing rule: rounds are not "exhausted" if the buyer simply never spends them, so the reserve would need its own timeout, which is a second auto-accept — a whole parallel mechanism to solve a problem the first one already solves. It also reintroduces exactly the leverage the escrow exists to delete. 05.04.01's Role Lens names the Producer's anti-persona behaviour as "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute" and credits this domain with structurally removing it; a buyer sitting on a 20% reserve is the same leverage wearing the buyer's hat. The seller's cash-flow problem is real and already has an answer that does not touch the atomicity: **milestones** (05.03.02). |
| DT-13 | Every engagement has at least one revision round — an allowance of 0 is a degenerate case not worth modelling | The implicit assumption in "revision limits" as a framing | ❌ REJECTED — `0` is legitimate, common, and in two cases **mandatory**, and a model that assumes ≥1 breaks on both. (1) **Attended sessions** (05.01.05): a client sitting in the room, or on a Zoom listen-back, gives their notes *live* and signs off in the room. The revisions already happened; they were the session. A later change is a recall (05.03.06) or a new date — not a round. Charging an attended day a revision allowance would be selling something the format already delivered. (2) **Union sessions** (05.02.03): a re-cut is a new session call with its own scale minimum. A "free revision round" on a union date is a request that a player work off the agreement, which the platform must not render as a normal, ticked, default-1 field — 05.02.03 already floors rendered price at scale before issue, and this is the same rule reaching a different field. The design consequences are concrete: allowance is a first-class integer including 0; `0` renders **explicitly with its reason** rather than being hidden (a hidden field reads as an omission and invites the buyer to ask anyway, which is the confrontation this feature exists to remove); and on a `0` engagement the change order is not an escalation path but the *only* path, so it must be one click from a delivered artifact. The exact union rule belongs to 05.02.03 and is flagged rather than asserted — Q-04. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **05.02.01 Quotes** ([path](../05.02-quotes-scope-contracting/05.02.01-custom-quotes-proposals-scope.md)) — **shared entity**: the accepted version is the arbiter of in-scope vs out, and a change order amends it. The CO inherits the quote's version/immutability/diff/expiry discipline wholesale (D-02, D-03, D-04) because it *is* a quote. The structured exclusions (DT-01 there) are this feature's main prevention mechanism (DT-02 here). The recomposed `quote vN + CO-1..n` document is a shared rendering obligation.
- Touches **05.01.06 Turnaround** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.06-turnaround-rush-service-levels.md)) — **trigger dependency, bidirectional**: a round pauses the clock at open and resumes it at **freeze** (not at request — DT-05 sharpens D-02 there); a change order renegotiates the due date. New: the revision round needs its **own** declared turnaround and rush inheritance (DT-07), which is a term this feature requires 05.01.06 to carry on the quote.
- Touches **05.04.01 Delivery & Auto-Accept** ([path](../05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance/05.04.01-delivery-acceptance-auto-accept.md)) — **two halves of one mechanism** (DT-01). The limit bounds the seller's exposure; auto-accept bounds the buyer's inaction. Three specific couplings: an empty request must not pause it (DT-05); a pending change order must **not** pause it (DT-09); the simultaneous-fire precedence rule is D-10 here and the mechanism is theirs (their Q-03).
- Touches **05.03.02 Milestones** ([path](./05.03.02-milestones-staged-deliverables.md)) — **shared entity**: allowances are per-milestone (their D-04), and unused rounds do **not** roll over between stages. A CO on a milestoned engagement inherits their unresolved capture model (their Q-01) — see Q-07.
- Touches **05.03.06 Post-Delivery Recall** ([path](./05.03.06-post-delivery-support-recall.md)) — **state boundary**: acceptance. Before it a change is a revision; after it a recall. Also the DT-06 coupling: this feature's rounds *cause* recalls in other engagements.
- Touches **05.03.05 Cancellation & Kill Fee** ([path](./05.03.05-cancellation-abandonment-kill-fee.md)) — **trigger dependency, now much weaker than the surface pass assumed**: DT-09 dissolves the passive change-order deadlock, so cancellation is no longer the exit for a stonewalling buyer — auto-accept is. Cancellation is still the exit for the *disputing* buyer, and their fee schedule must not punish the party in the right (Q-03, their Q-03).
- Touches **05.03.04 Retainers** ([path](./05.03.04-retainers-recurring-engagements.md)) — each drawn-down engagement carries its own allowance from the retainer instrument. The retainer's meter counts **engagements**, not rounds; no interaction beyond that.
- Touches **05.03.01 Lifecycle & Requirements Gating** ([path](./05.03.01-order-lifecycle-requirements-gating.md)) — a round spent on a mix built from the **wrong stems** is not a revision, it is a late-surfacing requirements defect: engagement returns to `Awaiting Requirements` (their D-03), round refunds, clock stops. A change order may add requirements and re-gate.
- Touches **05.02.03 Union Session Contracting** ([path](../05.02-quotes-scope-contracting/05.02.03-union-session-contracting.md)) — **permission/rule dependency**: the allowance is floored at `0` by union rule and every re-cut is a CO at scale (DT-13). Same shape as their price-floor-before-issue rule, reaching a different field. Exact rule is theirs — Q-04.
- Touches **05.01.05 Remote vs On-Location** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.05-remote-vs-on-location-service-mode.md)) — attended engagements default to allowance `0`; the notes were the session (DT-13).
- Touches **05.01.02 Category Taxonomy** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.02-service-category-taxonomy-attributes.md)) — **the seed defaults table is taxonomy-driven**: rounds, notes window and revision turnaround are per-craft attributes it must carry.
- Touches **05.01.07 Seller Capacity** ([path](../05.01-service-listings-pricing/05.01.07-seller-capacity-queue-intake-limits.md)) — an engagement in revision consumes a slot the whole time. This is why DT-04 and DT-05 are capacity problems, not just escrow ones: an unbounded or falsely-paused engagement leaks a slot to zero.
- Touches **05.05.02 Fixers & Ensemble Contracting** ([path](../05.05-multi-party-supply/05.05.02-fixers-ensemble-contracting.md)) and **05.05.03 Subcontracting** ([path](../05.05-multi-party-supply/05.05.03-subcontracting-agency-brokered-engagements.md)) — **DT-06, the sharpest cross-domain break in this feature**: a revision on the sold engagement is a *recall* on the bought ones, whose windows expire independently. Suppliers' recall windows must be surfaced against the producer's own allowance. Q-05.
- Touches **05.06.02 Producer Points** ([path](../05.06-rights-warranties-transfer/05.06.02-producer-points-backend-participation.md)) — **incentive inversion**: a points-holding seller wants the record good and will revise past any allowance. A CO on a points-only deal has no escrow to top up; consideration is cash, points, or nothing.
- Touches **05.06.03 Rights Transfer** ([path](../05.06-rights-warranties-transfer/05.06.03-rights-transfer-split-execution.md)) — nothing in this feature transfers rights. That is the point: revisions happen entirely inside the unvested window, which is why DT-12's reserve (a second release trigger) is rejected.
- Touches **05.04.02 QC** ([path](../05.04-delivery-qc-acceptance/05.04.02-deliverable-spec-automated-audio-qc.md)) — a redelivery is a delivery: it passes QC or it never reaches the buyer (their D-01/05.04 D-02). QC's checksum is also what detects the identical-file "redelivery".
- Touches **01 Identity & Entities** ([path](../../01-identity-entities/)) — **permission**: an engagement contracted by a **Band entity** nominates one approver; only they spend allowance and accept. Non-approver members' notes are non-binding.
- Touches **07 Music Projects & Collaboration** ([path](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)) — timestamped notes, version history and per-note resolution status are exactly what 07 holds. Inherits the file-locality question (05.03 Q-01 / 05.03.01 DT-03) rather than answering it.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — the taste dispute is genuinely unresolvable by rule and must have an adjudication path. **This domain is likely 24's highest-volume source**, and DT-09 narrows what actually arrives there: not stonewalling buyers (auto-accept handles those) but active scope disputes. See Q-06 on note redaction.
- Consumes **Media Handling & Audio Playback** cross-cut — the timestamped-comment player is the revision-note surface; notes anchor to the artifact **version** and do not migrate across redeliveries.
- Consumes **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** cross-cut — a change order captures additional escrow, atomically with its acceptance.
- Consumes **Contracts & E-Signature** cross-cut — a change order is an amendment to a signed instrument; the composed scope of record is the signed artifact.
- Consumes **Messaging & Contextual Inbox** cross-cut — notes arrive as messages and must be batched into rounds (DT-03) and **frozen** (DT-05). A constraint on how the inbox behaves here: a message is not always a round.
- Consumes **Notifications & Alerts** cross-cut — notes-window closing, round due date, CO expiry. The CO expiry warning is load-bearing (DT-09).
- Consumes **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — round history, per-note resolution status, seller-granted rounds and returned rounds are the dispute evidence.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Revision allowance is a bounded, first-class number | Escrow has no release trigger without it. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | A round = a batch of notes + a redelivery, not a single note | See DT-03. Makes the allowance fair and countable. |
| D-03 | The accepted quote version is the arbiter of revision vs change order | And its exclusions do most of the work before a dispute exists. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | A change order is a mini-quote — priced, expiring, accepted, escrow-topping | It is a renegotiation and should behave like one, not like a status flag. Inherits 05.02.01 D-02/D-03/D-04/D-05 wholesale. |
| D-05 | Post-acceptance change requests are recalls (05.03.06), not revisions | The boundary is acceptance, because acceptance transferred rights and released money. |
| D-06 | **An allowance is bounded by count or by time — never by neither.** "Unlimited" is permitted only inside a revision window | An uncounted, undated allowance deletes the terminal state and defeats auto-accept. See DT-04. |
| D-07 | **A round opens only on ≥1 note.** An empty request is not a request | It is the cheapest attack on the feature: a free, repeatable, infinite hold on escrow and a capacity slot. See DT-05. |
| D-08 | **The notes window** (48h; 24h on rush) bounds note-dripping; freeze on send-or-expiry; the turnaround clock resumes at **freeze**, and the seller cannot redeliver against an unfrozen round | Reconciles "notes must exist first" (D-07) with "no dripping" (D-02). The seller works a frozen target. |
| D-09 | **The allowance decrements at redelivery, not at request** | A withdrawn round, a returned round and a wholly change-ordered round all cost the buyer nothing, because no redelivery happened. See DT-08. Corrects the surface pass. |
| D-10 | **A revision request submitted before the auto-accept expiry wins the race** against auto-accept | Auto-accept punishes inaction; a buyer who acted was not inactive. Same reasoning CX-01 applied to requirements supply. Mechanism (single authoritative clock, submission not receipt) is `/create-prd-architecture`'s. |
| D-11 | **A pending change order does not pause auto-accept**, and a CO's expiry is mandatory | The CO is the buyer's ball. This dissolves the passive deadlock (DT-09) and is why the expiry is load-bearing rather than tidy. |
| D-12 | **A change order does not alter the revision allowance unless it says so** — the CO's allowance-delta field defaults to `0` | Adding 4 tracks to an 11-track mix adds work, not taste-iteration. Rounds are per engagement/milestone, not per track. |
| D-13 | **The revision round has its own declared, per-craft turnaround, which inherits any rush tier** | Otherwise the SLA measures first delivery only, and "fast first delivery, glacial revisions" is rational. See DT-07. Breach uses 05.01.06's remedy path. |
| D-14 | **Allowance `0` is legitimate and is mandatory on union and attended engagements**; `0` renders explicitly with its reason, never hidden | A model assuming ≥1 breaks on both. A hidden field reads as an omission and invites the confrontation this feature exists to remove. See DT-13. |
| D-15 | **Per-craft seed defaults are pre-filled from the taxonomy (05.01.02) and are seller-overridable in both directions** — a starting value, never a cap or a floor | A default is a norm-setting act (prior Q-02). Seeding from craft convention is honest; imposing it is not. Re-seeding from observed data is Q-02. |
| D-16 | **Unused rounds do not roll over between milestones** | Banking 3 unused tracking rounds to spend 6 on the mix is the exact exposure 05.03.02 D-04 exists to bound. |
| D-17 | **A revert to a previously delivered version does not consume a round** | Re-publishing an existing artifact is not new work. |
| D-18 | **Every note carries a per-note resolution status** set by the seller at redelivery (`addressed` / `not-addressed, reason` / `disputed`) | Makes "did you do all my notes?" answerable instead of arguable. It is the cheapest dispute-prevention mechanism after exclusions. |
| D-19 | **Notes anchor to the artifact version they were written against** and never migrate across redeliveries | 0:42 of v2 is a different bar. |
| D-20 | **Scope classification may prompt the seller privately; it must never be shown to the buyer, and never rules** | Buyer-facing classification is the platform taking the buyer's side against the seller's professional judgement, in the exact dispute the seller was too polite to start. See DT-10. |
| D-21 | **An entity-contracted engagement (Band, domain 01) nominates one approver**; only they spend allowance and accept. Other members' notes are visible and marked non-binding | Design-by-committee is the buyer's problem, not the seller's. Auto-accept keeps running while an entity re-nominates. |
| D-22 | **An identical-file redelivery is not a redelivery**; a seller declining a round returns it with per-note reasons and the allowance refunds | Charging a round for a refusal is theft. The seller's legitimate "I don't hear it" is a returned round, not a consumed one. |
| D-23 | **Notes are not capped by count** | A 200-note album round is the batching rule working, not an abuse. The round is the unit; the count is the bound; the seller's remedy for out-of-scope volume is the change order. |
| D-24 | **A seller may pre-price an extra round on the listing** — a change order with a template | Gives the buyer one-click optionality at a known price instead of a negotiation, without creating a refund claim (DT-11). |
