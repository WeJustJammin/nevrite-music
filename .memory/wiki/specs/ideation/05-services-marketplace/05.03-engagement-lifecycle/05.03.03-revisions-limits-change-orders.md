# Feature: Revisions, Revision Limits & Change Orders

> **Number**: 05.03.03
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The economics of "can you just try one more thing": a bounded allowance of revision rounds inside the agreed scope, and a change order for everything outside it. This is where creative services differ most sharply from every other kind of marketplace transaction — the deliverable is subjective, the buyer's taste is legitimate, and there is no objective point at which a mix is finished. Without a bound, an engagement has no terminal state and escrow has nothing to release against.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller, sees how many rounds remain and can decline a request that is out of scope without it being a confrontation — the platform, not the person, says "that's a change order". This matters more than it sounds: the reason musicians do unpaid revisions is that refusing costs them the relationship, and a rule that exists before the argument removes that cost. As a buyer, sees exactly what they have left. |
| Producer | Full | The persona most damaged by unbounded revisions, because their engagements are the longest and their clients the most opinionated. Also, as a buyer of session work, the party most likely to request "one more take" — and their `Anti-Persona Behavior` (pushing terms through while contributors are not paying attention) has a version here: grinding a session player through six free revision rounds on a flat fee. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two distinct objects, and the whole feature is the line between them:

**Revision** — a request for changes *inside* the accepted scope. Consumes one of N allowed rounds. Free. Pauses the turnaround clock while the seller waits for notes, resumes when they have them (05.01.06 D-02).

**Change order** — a request *outside* the accepted scope. Consumes no allowance. Costs money, and may move the due date. It is a mini-quote: issued, expiring, accepted, and on acceptance it amends the scope of record and captures additional escrow.

A **round** is not a note. A round is a batch of notes and a redelivery. Ten notes in one message is one round; ten messages over ten days is a problem the batching rule exists to prevent.

### Happy Path

1. Seller delivers (05.04.01); buyer reviews
2. Buyer submits a revision round — notes batched, ideally timestamped against the audio (Media Handling cross-cut)
3. Allowance decrements; clock pauses at the request and resumes when the seller has the notes
4. Seller redelivers; auto-accept countdown restarts
5. Buyer accepts, or spends another round
6. On allowance exhaustion, the next request must be a change order — priced, issued, accepted, escrow topped up

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Is this a revision or a change order? | **The taste dispute.** The accepted quote version (05.02.01 D-01) is the arbiter, and its exclusions (05.02.01 DT-01) do most of the work. Where it is genuinely silent, no rule resolves it. | Seller proposes "this is a change order" with a reason; buyer disputes → 24 |
| "Make it more like the reference" after 3 rounds | Almost certainly in scope — the reference was in the quote | Revision |
| "Actually, let's try it in a different key" | Not a revision. That is a new recording. | Change order |
| Allowance exhausted, buyer refuses to raise a change order and refuses to accept | The deadlock this feature exists to bound | Auto-accept (05.04.01) is the backstop. If the buyer will not act, the clock decides. |
| Seller offers a free extra round | Allowed; the platform does not forbid generosity | Recorded, so it does not become an expectation silently |
| Buyer drips notes one at a time | Batching rule: a round is opened by a request and closed on redelivery; notes arriving mid-round join it | "These will be added to round 2." |
| "Unlimited revisions" listed by a seller | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — should the platform allow this at all? See DT-01 and Q-01 |
| Change order accepted, scope of record amended | The engagement now references quote v2+CO1 | Amended scope shown in one place, not scattered |
| Revision requested on a milestone (05.03.02) | Allowances are per-milestone | Per-stage counter |
| Buyer requests a revision after auto-accept fired | Engagement is closed and rights transferred. This is the recall case, not a revision. | Routed to 05.03.06 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **"Unlimited revisions" is the single largest cause of engagement failure in creative services, and a bounded allowance is what makes escrow releasable at all** | Domain expertise + reasoning about escrow's requirements: escrow needs a release trigger. Acceptance is the trigger. If the buyer can always ask for one more thing, acceptance never arrives, and the money sits in escrow indefinitely while the seller works for free. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the bound is not a seller-protection nicety, it is a structural precondition for the domain's escrow model. But the interesting part is the second-order effect: a revision limit only works if there is a **backstop for the buyer who simply refuses to engage** — otherwise the seller's protection is "the buyer must accept", which the buyer can decline forever. That backstop is auto-accept (05.04.01), which means the revision limit and the auto-accept window are two halves of one mechanism and cannot be specified independently. Whether the platform permits sellers to *offer* unlimited revisions anyway (it is a real competitive listing tactic) is a product decision — Q-01. |
| DT-02 | **The revision-vs-change-order boundary is the taste dispute, and no rule resolves it — but exclusions dissolve most of it before it starts** | Adversarial: where is the unresolvable case? | ✅ CONFIRMED, in both halves. There is genuinely no rule that decides whether "it needs more energy" is inside the scope of a mix — that is a judgement, which is why 24 must exist and why the domain's dispute path is not optional. But the volume is tractable: most disputes are not about taste at all, they are about *assumptions* — the buyer assumed tuning was included, assumed stems were included, assumed an instrumental was included. Those are not judgements, they are unstated terms, and 05.02.01 DT-01's structured exclusions turn each of them into a fact the quote already settled. The design conclusion: invest in exclusions, not in adjudication. Exclusions are cheap and prevent; adjudication is expensive and only resolves. |
| DT-03 | A revision is a note | Naive framing | ❌ REJECTED — a revision is a **round**: a batch of notes plus a redelivery. If a note were a round, a buyer sending "also the snare" three minutes after their first message would have spent two of three rounds, which is absurd and would train buyers to hoard notes anxiously. If rounds were unbounded in time, a buyer could keep one round open for weeks, dripping notes. The batching rule — a round opens on request, closes on redelivery, and notes arriving in between join it — is what makes the allowance both fair and countable. Timestamped comments against the audio (Media Handling cross-cut) are the natural note format and make a round legible as a unit of work. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Revision allowance is a bounded, first-class number | Escrow has no release trigger without it. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | A round = a batch of notes + a redelivery, not a single note | See DT-03. Makes the allowance fair and countable. |
| D-03 | The accepted quote version is the arbiter of revision vs change order | And its exclusions do most of the work before a dispute exists. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | A change order is a mini-quote — priced, expiring, accepted, escrow-topping | It is a renegotiation and should behave like one, not like a status flag. |
| D-05 | Post-acceptance change requests are recalls (05.03.06), not revisions | The boundary is acceptance, because acceptance transferred rights and released money. |
