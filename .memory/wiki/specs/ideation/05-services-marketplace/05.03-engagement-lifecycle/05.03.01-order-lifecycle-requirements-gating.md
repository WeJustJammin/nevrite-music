# Feature: Order/Engagement Lifecycle & Requirements Gating

> **Number**: 05.03.01
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The engagement itself — the entity created when a quote is accepted, the states it moves through, and the gate that stops the clock and the work until the buyer has actually sent what the seller needs. It is the spine of the domain: escrow hangs off its states, turnaround hangs off its gate, revisions and change orders mutate it, and acceptance terminates it into a rights transfer.

The requirements gate deserves top billing because it is where engagements die. Not in the mix — in the four weeks before the mix, waiting for stems.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | On both sides. As a seller, sees an engagement that cannot start until they have usable material, and does not have to nag for it — the platform holds the gate, which is a small thing that changes the relationship, because chasing your client for their own stems is the most relationship-damaging part of the job. As a buyer, sees an explicit checklist of what they owe rather than a vague "send me your files". |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest user. Runs several engagements concurrently and is usually the party who *knows* what good material looks like, so their listings' requirement templates (05.01.01) are the most demanding. Their `Anti-Persona Behavior` — closing a project without capturing splits, "we'll do it later" — is what the lifecycle's terminal state exists to prevent: acceptance is not a status change, it is the moment the record gets written (05.06.03). |
| Operator | Read-only | Narrow and specific: an *on-location* engagement's schedule appears against their room booking in domain 16, because a room is being held for it. They see the date and whether it is still happening. They never see scope, fee, deliverables or requirements. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An engagement is created by quote acceptance (05.02.01) and references the **accepted quote version** as its scope of record. It is never created by a chat agreement and never by a listing alone.

The **requirements gate** sits immediately after creation. The listing declared what the buyer owes (05.01.01 DT-01) — stems with a naming convention, a tempo map, reference tracks, session notes, a rough mix, a deadline. Until those are supplied and pass a completeness check, the engagement is `Awaiting Requirements`: the turnaround clock has not started (05.01.06 D-01), and the seller is not late.

The gate is a **completeness** check, not a quality one. The platform can verify that 24 files arrived, that they are WAVs at the stated rate, that a tempo map exists. It cannot verify that the stems are printed from the right session, and it must not pretend to.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer accepts a quote; escrow captures; engagement created in `Awaiting Requirements`
2. System shows the buyer their checklist, drawn from the listing's requirement template
3. Buyer uploads stems and supplies the metadata
4. System runs the completeness check; missing/failed items are listed specifically
5. Gate passes → engagement enters `In Progress`; **turnaround clock starts** (05.01.06); both parties see the committed due date
6. Seller works; delivers (05.04.01)
7. Buyer accepts (or auto-accept fires) → escrow releases + rights transfer + credit emission, atomically (05.06.03)
8. Engagement enters `Closed`, with a support window still open (05.03.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Requirements never supplied | The engagement's most common death. Escrow holds money for work that cannot start. | Escalating nudges → abandonment path (05.03.05). Never silent. |
| Requirements supplied but wrong (stems from the wrong session, all clipping) | Completeness check passes; the seller catches it | Seller can **reject requirements back to the gate** — engagement returns to `Awaiting Requirements`, clock stops, with a reason. This must be a first-class action, not a message. |
| Buyer supplies requirements in dribs | Gate is all-or-nothing; partial supply does not start the clock | Checklist shows 18/24 |
| Seller starts work before the gate passes | Their choice; the platform does not police it, but the clock is still stopped | No obstruction — the gate protects the seller's SLA, it does not forbid generosity |
| Scope changes mid-engagement | Change order (05.03.03), which may add requirements and re-gate | New checklist items appear |
| On-location engagement (05.01.05) | Requirements are a room, an address, a load-in time — not stems. Delivery is attendance. | Different checklist, same gate |
| Engagement has milestones (05.03.02) | Each milestone may have its own requirements gate | Per-milestone checklists |
| Buyer disappears after delivery | Auto-accept (05.04.01) is the answer to this specific case | Countdown to auto-accept |
| Both parties agree to change the deal entirely | Change order, or cancel-and-requote | "This is a different job now." |
| Files exceed practical upload size (multitrack sessions run to tens of GB) | Resumable multi-GB transfer is the Media Handling cross-cut's stated capability | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — storage and egress cost is a real constraint; routed to `/create-prd` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Awaiting Requirements | Created | Buyer: checklist. Seller: "waiting on {buyer} — you're not late." |
| Requirements Rejected | Seller sends material back | Reason + what to fix; clock stopped |
| In Progress | Gate passed | Committed due date, countdown |
| Awaiting Buyer | Revision feedback / approval pending | Clock paused (05.01.06 D-02); slot still consumed (05.01.07) |
| Delivered | Seller delivers (05.04.01) | Auto-accept countdown |
| In Revision | Revision requested | Allowance remaining shown |
| Accepted | Buyer accepts / auto-accept fires | Escrow released, rights transferred, credit emitted — atomically |
| Closed | Support window expired | Read-only; evidence retained |
| Cancelled | Either party exits (05.03.05) | Settlement arithmetic shown |
| Disputed | Escalated | Handed to domain 24; escrow frozen |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The requirements gate is where engagements die, and it needs an abandonment path, not just a nudge** | Domain expertise plus the intersection with escrow: an engagement stuck at `Awaiting Requirements` holds the buyer's money, occupies one of the seller's capacity slots (05.01.07), and has no terminal state. Every services marketplace accumulates these. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is what forced 05.03.05 into existence as a Deep Think addition. The important detail is *whose fault* it is: an engagement that dies at this gate died because the **buyer** did not deliver their side, which is the opposite of the usual assumption that a stalled order is the seller's failure. The settlement arithmetic must reflect that, or the platform systematically punishes sellers for buyers' inaction. |
| DT-02 | **The gate must check completeness, not quality — and a seller must be able to reject requirements back to the gate** | Reasoning about what the platform can honestly verify. It can count files, read a header, detect a tempo map. It cannot know the stems were printed from the right session, or that the vocal is the comped take. | ✅ CONFIRMED — with the consequence that a completeness pass is **not** a warrant that the material is usable, so `Requirements Rejected` must be a first-class state that stops the clock and returns the ball. Without it, a buyer's junk material passes the gate, the clock starts, and the seller's SLA is now running on a job they cannot do — which converts the platform's gate from a protection into a trap. The rejection must carry a reason and be visible in the engagement's history, because a seller who rejects everything is a different problem (24). |
| DT-03 | An engagement's working files should live in this domain | Locality assumption | ❌ REJECTED — probably. A mix engagement's stems, revisions, timestamped comments and version history are precisely what domain 07 (Music Projects & Collaboration) exists to hold, and the Media Handling cross-cut already owns the player, waveform and timestamped-comment machinery. Building a second file surface here would put the same audio in two places, and the buyer's stems are usually *already* in a project — they came from the tracking session. But the inverse is also real: not every engagement has a project (a mastering job from an external client arrives as two WAVs and nothing else). The honest position is that this is a genuine architecture question with a real cost either way, and it is escalated as sub-domain Q-01 rather than resolved by assumption here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The engagement is created only by quote acceptance, and references the accepted version | No chat-created engagements; no scope ambiguity at birth. |
| D-02 | The requirements gate is all-or-nothing, and is a completeness check only | Partial supply does not start work or the clock. Completeness is what the platform can honestly verify; quality is not. |
| D-03 | `Requirements Rejected` is a first-class state, not a message | Otherwise a completeness pass on junk material starts the seller's SLA on an impossible job. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | An engagement stalled at the gate is the **buyer's** failure, and the settlement arithmetic must say so | See DT-01. The default assumption in marketplace design is the opposite, and it is wrong here. |
