# Feature: Order/Engagement Lifecycle & Requirements Gating

> **Number**: 05.03.01
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The engagement itself — the entity created when a quote is accepted, the states it moves through, and the gate that stops the clock and the work until the buyer has actually sent what the seller needs. It is the spine of the domain: escrow hangs off its states, turnaround hangs off its gate, revisions and change orders mutate it, and acceptance terminates it into a rights transfer.

The requirements gate deserves top billing because it is where engagements die. Not in the mix — in the four weeks before the mix, waiting for stems. Every services marketplace accumulates a graveyard of orders that were paid for and never started, and the industry-standard explanation ("flaky sellers") is, in music, precisely backwards: the modal dead engagement is a buyer who paid, got busy, and never sent the files, while the seller holds a capacity slot they cannot sell to anyone else.

The gate is also the platform's first and best chance to be *in the room* — the buyer's stems arriving here is the moment the platform gains custody of the material the whole provenance thesis depends on. Whatever else the gate does, it must not push that material off-platform.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | On both sides, often in the same week. **As seller**: sees an engagement that cannot start until they have usable material, and does not have to nag for it — the platform holds the gate and sends the nudges. Chasing your own client for their own stems is the most relationship-damaging part of the job, and the gate is what removes it. They act on `Requirements Rejected` (bounded, 3 rounds — D-11) and on the observation report (D-08), which tells them the vocal stem is digital silence *before* they open the session. **As buyer**: sees an explicit typed checklist of what they owe (`18 of 24 stems · tempo map missing · clearance not acknowledged`) rather than a vague "send me your files", and sees it on the quote *before* accepting (D-05), so the checklist is something they consented to, not something sprung on them. |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest user and the party most exposed in both directions. Runs 3–8 engagements concurrently (05.01.07 D-01), so a single stalled gate is a slot they cannot refill. They are usually the party who *knows* what good material looks like, so their listings' requirement templates (05.01.01) are the most demanding — which is exactly what makes them the exploit surface in DT-08, and why D-05 freezes the checklist at acceptance. Their `Anti-Persona Behavior` — closing a project without capturing splits, "we'll do it later" — is what the lifecycle's terminal state exists to prevent: acceptance is not a status change, it is the moment the record gets written (05.06.03), and no step in this feature lets them skip it. They are also the party who supplies material *as a buyer* on the tracking→mix→master chain (DT-07), so they hit the gate from the other side constantly. |
| Operator | Read-only | Narrow, specific, and mediated entirely through domain 16 — they never open an engagement. An *on-location* engagement (05.01.05) holds their room, so that booking appears on their calendar with the states domain 16 gives it (`Confirmed` / `Cancelled`), and a gate failure reaches them as a **hold release with a date**, never as a reason, a fault, a fee, a scope, a deliverable or a counterparty dispute. Their only structural stake is DT-11: an on-location gate that drifts past their free-cancellation boundary strands a room they could have sold, so the gate's on-location deadline is derived from *their* policy window, not from an arbitrary platform timeout. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — not the surface, not the existence of it. An engagement is a private commercial contract between two named parties; there is no public view, no listing of "engagements in progress", and no fan-facing artifact until a credit emits at acceptance (02) and a release ships (12). A hobbyist who commissions a mix is acting as a **Musician** and is onboarded into that lens — `meta/personas.md`: the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity", and a requirements checklist is nothing but professional-tool complexity. |

## Behavior

An engagement is created by quote acceptance (05.02.01) and references the **accepted quote version** as its scope of record (D-01). It is never created by a chat agreement and never by a listing alone. Creation and escrow capture are one atomic transaction (05.02.01 D-05) — an engagement never exists without its money held.

The **requirements gate** sits immediately after creation. The listing declared what the buyer owes (05.01.01 DT-01); the quote froze that declaration into a checklist the buyer saw before they paid (D-05). Until every item is supplied and passes its completeness check, the engagement is `Awaiting Requirements`: the turnaround clock has not started (05.01.06 D-01), and **the seller is not late**.

The gate is a **completeness** check, not a quality one (D-02). The platform can count files, read a header, confirm a tempo map parses, measure a duration. It cannot know the stems were printed from the right session, or that the vocal is the comped take, and it must not pretend to. This is the domain principle (05 D-07): the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them.

### The requirement item vocabulary

The checklist is not free text. Every item has a **type**, and each type has a defined, mechanical completeness check. This is what makes the gate testable and what makes 05.03.03's change orders able to reason about it (05.01.01 D-07 applies the same logic to exclusions).

| Item type | Example | Completeness check (all of these, all mechanical) |
|---|---|---|
| `file-set` | "24 stems, WAV 48 kHz / 24-bit" | Declared count matched exactly; every file's container + codec + sample rate + bit depth + channel count match the declaration; every file has non-zero duration; every file is mapped to a required role (D-14) |
| `single-file` | rough mix, tempo map, MIDI guide | Present; container/codec matches the declaration; parses without error; non-zero duration |
| `structured-value` | tempo `128 BPM`, key `F#m`, target `-14 LUFS` | Present; correct type; within the declared range |
| `text` | session notes, creative brief | Present; non-empty after whitespace trim |
| `link` | reference tracks (a Spotify/YouTube URL) | Present; well-formed URL. **Never a valid supply for a `file-set` item** (D-16) |
| `date` | buyer's hard deadline | Present; resolves to an absolute instant; in the future at supply time |
| `logistics` | on-location address, load-in time, room contact | Present; address geocodes to a point; load-in time is strictly before the session start time |
| `acknowledgement` | buyer's clearance/originality warranty (D-09) | Ticked; attributed to a named human; timestamped; frozen into the engagement record |

An item's check either passes or fails. There is no partial credit and no override. The gate passes only when **every** item passes (D-02).

### Supply methods

Two, and the order matters:

1. **Attach from a Music Project (07)** — the first-class path (D-07). In music the buyer's stems very often already exist on the platform: they came from the tracking session WeJammin was in the room for. Re-uploading 40 GB the platform already holds is absurd, doubles storage, and severs the link between the stems and the session that produced them — which is the thesis, discarded for no reason.
2. **Upload** — for material that arrives from outside (a mastering job from an external client is two WAVs and nothing else).

Off-platform file links are not a supply method for `file-set` or `single-file` items (D-16). A WeTransfer link cannot be gated (the platform cannot count what it cannot see), it expires before the dispute does, and it moves the provenance-critical material off the platform at the exact moment the platform earned the right to hold it.

### What the gate reports but never enforces

Passing the completeness check is **not** a warrant that the material is usable. The gate additionally emits a **structural observation report** to the seller (D-08) — facts, measured, never a verdict, never a block:

| Observation | Threshold | Why it is a fact and not taste |
|---|---|---|
| Non-uniform sample rate / bit depth across a `file-set` | any file differs from the set's mode | Counting |
| Duration mismatch across a `file-set` | any file's duration differs from the set's modal duration by > 100 ms | Measuring. Bar-1-aligned DAW exports are all the same length; a mismatch means someone exported from the cursor or exported regions — the single most common stem-export error in the industry |
| Digital-silence stem | peak amplitude < −90 dBFS end-to-end | Measuring. A stem exported while muted. Extremely common; invisible until the seller opens the session |
| Consecutive full-scale samples | ≥ 3 samples at 0 dBFS | Measuring. Whether it *matters* is taste — a deliberately clipped drum bus is a choice — so it is reported, never blocked |
| Declared tempo vs. detected tempo | differ by > 1 BPM | Measuring. Reported only; tempo detection is unreliable on sparse material and a hard fail would be the platform being confidently wrong |

The report is seller-facing only. It never blocks the gate, never scores the buyer, and never appears as a quality claim. Its entire job is to let the seller reject in five minutes instead of five days.

### Rejection, and the bound on it

A completeness pass on junk material starts the seller's SLA on a job they cannot do — which converts the gate from a protection into a trap. So the seller can **reject requirements back to the gate** (D-03): a first-class action, not a message. The engagement returns to `Awaiting Requirements`, the clock stops, and the rejection carries a required reason attached to the specific failing items.

But rejection cannot be unbounded, because D-04 makes a stall at the gate the buyer's fault, and every fault-assignment rule creates an incentive to manufacture the other party's fault (DT-08). Rejection rounds are **bounded at 3** (D-11). The third rejection opens `Requirements Deadlock`: a no-fault state offering both parties mutual release (05.03.05) with full escrow return and no kill fee, escalating to domain 24 only if either party contests. A deadlock is not automatically anyone's misconduct — it is two professionals who disagree about what "usable" means, which is a conversation, not a crime.

### Fault, and where it does not accrue

An engagement stalled at the gate is the buyer's failure (D-04) and the settlement arithmetic must say so, or the platform systematically punishes sellers for buyers' inaction. Two carve-outs, both computed from data the platform already holds:

- **Upstream dependency** (D-10). A buyer may declare a requirement as *satisfied-by* an in-flight WeJammin engagement — the mastering gate waits on the mix engagement's delivery, which is the normal shape of the tracking→mix→master chain. While that upstream is in flight and inside its own committed due date, **no buyer fault accrues downstream** and the nudges are suppressed. Nudging a buyer for a file another seller on the same platform owes them is the platform being stupid with information it has.
- **Deadlock** (D-11). Three rejections is no-fault, per above.

The abandonment clock at the gate runs from **gate open**, not from last buyer activity (D-12). An activity-reset clock never fires against a buyer who uploads one file every six days forever, and 18-of-24-forever is not meaningfully better than 0-of-24 — it is worse, because it looks like progress.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer accepts quote v3 → escrow captures → engagement created in `Awaiting Requirements`, atomically (05.02.01 D-05). The buyer has already seen this exact checklist on the quote (D-05), so nothing here is a surprise.
2. System renders the typed checklist: `0 of 8 items · 24 stems · tempo map · reference tracks · target loudness · session notes · clearance acknowledgement · …`, plus the declared total set size (`≈ 4.2 GB expected`) so the buyer knows what they are in for.
3. Buyer chooses **Attach from Project** for the stems — the tracking session is already on WeJammin (07). System resolves 24 files, maps 22 by exact filename match against the required roles, and asks the buyer to map the remaining 2 by hand (D-14 — the platform suggests, never auto-renames).
4. Buyer uploads the tempo map, pastes two reference-track links, enters `-14 LUFS`, types session notes, and ticks the clearance acknowledgement (D-09), which records their name and the timestamp into the engagement record.
5. System runs each item's completeness check. All 8 pass → **gate passes**.
6. Engagement enters `In Progress`; **the turnaround clock starts** (05.01.06 D-01). Both parties see the committed due date as an absolute date+time in their own timezone, never as a duration (05.01.06 D-03): `Due Fri 24 Jul, 18:00 BST`.
7. System delivers the structural observation report to the seller: `24/24 supplied · all 48 kHz/24-bit · all durations within 12 ms · no silent stems · 1 file shows 6 consecutive full-scale samples (BGV_Stack_L)`. The seller reads it, decides the clipped BGV is deliberate, and starts work.
8. Seller works; delivers (05.04.01); QC passes, so it is a delivery (05.04.01 D-01).
9. Buyer accepts (or auto-accept fires) → escrow release + rights transfer + credit emission, **atomically** (05.06.03 D-01). The confirmation names all three consequences explicitly, and none of them were a surprise — they were visible throughout (05.06.03 D-05).
10. Engagement enters `Closed`, with the support/recall window still open (05.03.06).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Requirements never supplied** | The engagement's most common death. Escrow holds money for work that cannot start; the seller's slot (05.01.07) is locked. Abandonment clock runs from gate open (D-12). | Buyer nudged at T+24h, T+72h, T+7d, T+14d; then 05.03.05's escalating abandonment notice with a stated automatic outcome and a date. Seller sees `Waiting on {buyer} — you're not late` plus the same countdown, because they may want to nudge or to release the buyer. Never silent. |
| **Requirements supplied but wrong** (stems from the wrong session; the "vocal" is a scratch take) | Completeness check passes; the observation report may or may not catch it; the seller catches it. Seller rejects back to the gate (D-03). Clock stops. Round counter increments. | Seller: reject action with a required reason bound to specific items. Buyer: `{seller} sent this back — Vox_Lead.wav: "this is the scratch, not the comp" · round 1 of 3`. |
| **Third rejection of the same set** | `Requirements Deadlock` (D-11). No fault, no kill fee, full escrow return on mutual release. | Both: "You've been round this 3 times. Release each other with no fee, or escalate." Only escalates to 24 if contested. |
| **Seller writes an unpassable requirement template** to farm kill fees (DT-08) | Structurally defeated, not policed. The checklist is frozen at acceptance and was on the quote (D-05), so the buyer consented to it; a requirement added later is a change order (05.03.03 D-04), not a gate item; and rejection is bounded at 3 (D-11), so the deadlock exit is no-fault. | Buyer sees the full checklist *before* paying. A seller whose engagements repeatedly deadlock is a pattern for 24, not a gate outcome. |
| **Buyer supplies in dribs** | Gate is all-or-nothing (D-02); partial supply does not start the clock and does **not** reset the abandonment clock (D-12). | `18 of 24 stems · tempo map missing · clearance not acknowledged`. Countdown continues and says so. |
| **Seller starts work before the gate passes** | Their choice; the platform does not police it. The clock is still stopped. | No obstruction — the gate protects the seller's SLA, it does not forbid generosity. |
| **Buyer supplies a replacement set after the gate passed** ("sorry, wrong bounce") | Not an automatic re-gate. The **seller elects**: accept the replacement (engagement returns to `Awaiting Requirements`, gate re-runs, clock restarts from the new pass) or refuse and continue on the original material. | Buyer: "Sent. {seller} decides whether to switch — this may reset your due date." Auto-reset would let a buyer restart the clock at will; auto-refuse would trap the seller with a bad bounce. |
| **Buyer supplies requirements at the instant the abandonment timeout fires** | **Requirements supply wins.** Deterministic precedence, no tie-break by timestamp jitter. The buyer did the thing they were being chased for. (CX-01 synthesis Q5.) | Gate passes; the abandonment notice is withdrawn and the withdrawal is logged. |
| **Buyer uploads while the seller is mid-rejection of the previous set** | The rejection applies to the **set version it named**. New material lands as a new version and re-runs the gate; the stale rejection is recorded in history but does not bounce the new set. | Buyer: "{seller} rejected the previous set — your new upload is being checked." |
| **Two people on a band-entity buyer supply the same item concurrently** | Both retained as versions; neither silently overwrites. The item is not satisfied until exactly one version is nominated as the supply. | "2 versions of `Drums.wav` — pick one." Who is *authorised* to nominate depends on 01's mandate model — Q-06. |
| **Upload completes but the file is truncated / zero bytes / corrupt** | Fails `file-set`/`single-file` completeness (non-zero duration, parses without error). It never counts toward the gate. | Per-file: `Drums_OH.wav — 0 bytes, upload didn't finish. Retry.` |
| **Right count, wrong format** (MP3s where WAV 48/24 was declared) | Fails the container/codec/rate/depth check. This is the check catching the single most common buyer error, and catching it in seconds rather than after a seller opens the session. | `24 of 24 files, but 24 are MP3. This listing needs WAV 48 kHz / 24-bit.` |
| **Sample rates mixed across the set; stems of different lengths; a silent stem** | All *pass* the gate if each file individually matches the declaration and has non-zero duration — but every one of them lands in the seller's observation report (D-08). Never a block. | Seller: `⚠ 3 files at 44.1 kHz, 21 at 48 kHz · Gtr_DI.wav is 2.4 s shorter than the set · Vox_Dbl.wav is digital silence`. |
| **Buyer pastes a WeTransfer/Dropbox link instead of uploading** | Rejected as a supply for `file-set`/`single-file` (D-16). The `link` type exists for reference tracks only. | "Reference links are fine for reference tracks. Stems have to come through WeJammin — that's what lets us hold the clock and settle a dispute." |
| **Buyer attaches from a Project (07), then deletes the project or revokes the seller's access** | The gate passed on material that has now vanished mid-engagement. Treated as a **withdrawal of supply**: the engagement returns to `Awaiting Requirements`, the clock stops, and fault is the buyer's — they removed their own material. | Seller: "{buyer} withdrew the source material. Clock stopped; you're not late." Buyer: "You removed material this engagement depends on. Restore access or re-supply." |
| **The buyer's material contains an uncleared sample** (DT-06) | The gate carries a buyer-side clearance `acknowledgement` (D-09), attributed and timestamped. The platform does not detect the sample — 05.06.04 DT-02 established that detection produces false clearances. What it does is make the claim **attributable** (05 D-07), which is the only honest thing available. | Buyer at supply: "I have the rights to everything in this material, or it's cleared." Named, dated, frozen into the record. |
| **Requirement satisfiable by an upstream WeJammin engagement that is itself late** (DT-07) | Buyer declared `satisfied-by: engagement #123` (D-10). While #123 is in flight and inside its committed due date, **no buyer fault accrues** and nudges are suppressed. Once #123 breaches its own due date, the downstream engagement's gate is blocked by a third party — fault attaches to neither of *its* parties. | Downstream buyer: "Waiting on your mix from {other seller} — engagement #123, due Fri. You're not being chased." Downstream seller: "Blocked upstream, not on your buyer." |
| **The upstream engagement is cancelled** | The downstream gate can never pass through nobody's fault, and the downstream seller has held a slot for weeks. Deterministic behaviour: the downstream engagement moves to `Requirements Deadlock` (no fault) and offers mutual release. Who eats the dead slot is not resolvable here — Q-04. | Both: "The work this depended on was cancelled. Release with no fee, or re-supply from elsewhere." |
| **Files exceed practical upload size** (multitrack sessions run to tens of GB) | Resolved as behaviour; the cost question stays open. Transfer is **resumable and chunked** (Media Handling cross-cut): it survives a closed browser, a dead network and a laptop sleep, resumes from the last confirmed chunk, and **never re-uploads completed bytes**. An interrupted transfer's partial bytes are retained **7 days** for resumption, then discarded with 24h notice. The buyer sees per-file and set-level progress, bytes remaining, and an ETA. The listing's requirement template declares the expected total set size, so the buyer sees `≈ 4.2 GB expected` on the quote, before accepting. | `18 of 24 · 2.1 GB of 4.2 GB · ~14 min left · resumes if you close this`. On resume: "Picking up where you left off — 2.1 GB already here." |
| Any hard per-file or per-set **ceiling** | Deliberately not invented here. It is a function of the storage/egress decision that does not exist yet, and `meta/constraints.md` already flags media storage at scale as open. Routed to `/create-prd-stack` — Q-02. | — |
| **Malicious input**: a non-audio payload renamed `.wav`, an archive bomb, 400 GB of digital silence to burn storage | The `file-set` check is content-based, not extension-based — a file whose actual container/codec does not match the declaration fails the check and never counts. Volumetric abuse is a rate-limiting and quota concern for the platform's abuse surface (24 / `/create-prd-security`), not a gate rule; the gate's contribution is that supply is bounded by a **declared** expected set size, so a 400 GB supply against a 4 GB declaration is anomalous on its face. | `Kick.wav isn't a WAV.` |
| **Scope changes mid-engagement** | Change order (05.03.03 D-04), which may add requirements and re-gate. New items are added by the *accepted change order*, never by the seller unilaterally (D-05). | New checklist items appear, attributed to the change order that added them. |
| **On-location engagement** (05.01.05) | Requirements are `logistics` items — a room, an address, a load-in time, a contact — not stems. Delivery is attendance. And the gate has a **hard deadline**, not an inactivity timeout (D-13, DT-11): it is derived from the room's free-cancellation boundary in domain 16, because that is the last instant the room can be released without someone paying for it. | Different checklist, same gate, plus: `Address and load-in needed by Thu 17 Jul 12:00 — after that the room can't be released for free.` |
| **On-location gate fails at the deadline** | Converts to cancellation at a fixed point (D-13). Domain 16 releases the hold. The Operator learns of a hold release with a date — never a reason, a fee or a fault. | Operator: booking moves to `Cancelled`, room back on sale. Buyer/seller: 05.03.05's settlement arithmetic, shown, not asserted. |
| **Engagement has milestones** (05.03.02) | Each milestone runs its own compressed lifecycle including its own requirements gate (CX-08). Stage 3's stems do not exist until stage 2 is accepted — the chain is causal (05.03.02 D-03), so a single up-front gate is not expressible. | Per-milestone checklists; later stages show `opens after {previous}`. |
| **Retainer engagement** (05.03.04) | Runs the normal lifecycle **minus escrow** (CX-05) — it was paid in advance. The gate is unchanged: a drawn-down engagement still cannot start until its material arrives. What differs is that a gate stall consumes an allowance period the buyer has already paid for, so the fault question has no escrow to settle against. | Same checklist. "This month's allowance is running whether or not you send the files." |
| **NDA'd / ghost-production engagement** (05.02.02) | The gate operates normally, but its notifications must not leak what the NDA exists to hide. Nudges, deadlock notices and abandonment notices name the engagement, never the counterparty, when anonymity is elected. | "Engagement #4471 is waiting on requirements" — no name. |
| **Seller becomes unavailable while the engagement sits at the gate** | Not this feature's exit. Dep/substitute path (05.05.01). The gate state survives the substitution — the incoming seller inherits the checklist as frozen at acceptance (D-05), not their own template. | Buyer: "{dep} is covering. Same checklist — nothing to redo." |
| **The same human is on both sides** (multi-hyphenate; buyer's band entity and the seller share a member) | Permitted — `meta/personas.md`: roles are simultaneous, not sequential, and refusing this breaks the persona model. Two constraints: nudges deduplicate so the platform does not chase one human on behalf of themselves; and both roles' actions are attributed to the *role*, not the human, in the audit log — a self-dealing pattern is a 24 concern, not a gate block. | One notification, both hats named: "You owe yourself stems on #4471." |
| **Buyer accepts, then immediately wants out before supplying anything** | Not a gate outcome — a cancellation with zero consumption (05.03.05 D-02). The seller held a slot for hours, not weeks; the arithmetic should reflect that. | 05.03.05's settlement, with its arithmetic shown. |
| **Both parties agree to change the deal entirely** | Change order (05.03.03), or cancel-and-requote. The gate does not attempt to absorb a new deal. | "This is a different job now." |
| **Engagement stalls at the gate long enough that the seller's rate or capacity changed** | Irrelevant to this engagement. The accepted quote version is the scope of record (D-01, 05.01.01 D-01) and rates are frozen in it. The seller's slot stays consumed at the old terms — which is precisely why the abandonment clock must terminate (D-12). | Nothing changes for either party. That is the point. |
| **Buyer's declared hard deadline (`date` item) is already past when they supply it** | Fails the `date` check (must be in the future at supply time). The gate does not pass on a deadline that was impossible on arrival. | `Your deadline is yesterday. Pick a new one or talk to {seller} about a rush (05.01.06).` |
| **Buyer's declared deadline is sooner than the committed turnaround** | Passes the gate — it is a well-formed date — but the mismatch is surfaced to both parties immediately rather than discovered at delivery. The quote's turnaround is what binds; the buyer's wish does not silently re-date the engagement (05.01.06 D-02: only a buyer-owed pause moves the due date). | Both: `Your date: Wed 22nd. Committed due date: Fri 24th. This needs a rush add-on or a new date.` |

### States

The engagement's lifecycle state machine. Every transition is written to the Audit Log & Provenance Ledger cross-cut — the state history is the only record of who stalled and when, and it is the evidence in any dispute.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Awaiting Requirements | Engagement created (quote accepted + escrow captured) | Buyer: typed checklist, `0 of 8`, expected set size, nudge schedule implied. Seller: "waiting on {buyer} — **you're not late**", plus the same countdown. |
| Requirements Rejected | Seller sends material back (D-03) | Reason bound to the failing items; `round N of 3`; clock stopped. |
| Requirements Deadlock | 3rd rejection (D-11), or an upstream dependency was cancelled | No-fault mutual-release offer, full escrow return, no kill fee. Escalates to 24 only if contested. |
| Blocked Upstream | A `satisfied-by` engagement is in flight (D-10) | Buyer: "waiting on #123, due Fri — you're not being chased." Nudges suppressed; no buyer fault accrues. |
| In Progress | Gate passed — **turnaround clock starts** (05.01.06 D-01) | Committed due date as an absolute date+time in the viewer's timezone (05.01.06 D-03) + countdown. Seller additionally gets the observation report (D-08). |
| Awaiting Buyer | Revision feedback or approval pending | Clock paused (05.01.06 D-02); slot still consumed (05.01.07); every pause attributed. |
| Delivered | Seller delivers and QC passes (05.04.01 D-01) | Artifacts + auto-accept countdown + Accept/Revise. |
| In Revision | Revision round opened (05.03.03 D-02) | `2 of 3 rounds left`; clock paused. |
| Accepted | Buyer accepts, or auto-accept fires | Escrow released + rights transferred + credit emitted, **atomically** (05.06.03 D-01). `Accepted` and `Auto-Accepted` remain permanently distinct (05.04.01 D-04). |
| Closed | Support/recall window expired (05.03.06) | Read-only; evidence retained. |
| Cancelled | Either party exits, or an on-location gate deadline passes (D-13) | 05.03.05's settlement arithmetic **shown**, not asserted as a number. |
| Disputed | Escalated | Handed to domain 24; escrow frozen; nothing transfers while frozen (05.06.03). |

The checklist surface's own UI states:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Checklist opening; a completeness check running; a Project (07) resolving | Skeleton rows per known item; per-item spinner while its check runs — never a single page-blocking spinner, because a 24-file set checks incrementally and the buyer should watch it land. |
| Empty | Gate just opened; nothing supplied | The full checklist with every item unsatisfied and a primary CTA per supply method: **Attach from a project** (first) / **Upload**. Plus the expected total set size. Not a blank slate — the buyer's whole job is on screen. |
| Populated | Every item supplied and passing | `8 of 8 · gate passed · due Fri 24 Jul, 18:00 BST`. The checklist collapses to a receipt; the due date is now the primary object. |
| Partial | Some items pass, some do not | Per-item state — pass / fail-with-reason / not-yet-supplied — with the fails at the top and the specific reason on each (`24 files, 24 are MP3`). Set-level `18 of 24`. Explicit: "the clock has not started." |
| Error | Transfer failed; a check could not run; Project access revoked mid-resolve | Per-item error with a retry that **resumes** rather than restarts. A check that cannot run is never rendered as a fail — it renders as `couldn't check — retrying`, because a failed check and failed material are different facts and conflating them blames the buyer for the platform's outage. |
