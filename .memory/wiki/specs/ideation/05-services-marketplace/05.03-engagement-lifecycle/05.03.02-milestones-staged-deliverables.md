# Feature: Milestones & Staged Deliverables

> **Number**: 05.03.02
> **Parent**: [Engagement Lifecycle](./05.03-engagement-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Breaking a long engagement into staged deliverables, each with its own escrow tranche, so that neither party carries the whole risk of the other's failure across a three-month album production. A producer should not fund an album out of pocket until final delivery, and a buyer should not hand over £12,000 to someone they have never worked with on the strength of a profile.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Proposes or accepts a staged structure on longer engagements. As a seller this is cash flow — a working musician making a living from "a portfolio of small income streams" (`meta/personas.md`) cannot float an album's worth of work for four months. As a buyer, sees money released only against things they have actually received. |
| Producer | Full | The persona milestones exist for. Album production is the archetypal long engagement: pre-production → tracking → comping/editing → mix → master, each a real deliverable a client can hear and sign off. Uses milestones as the project's spine, which is also why the boundary with domain 07 matters (see Cross-Cut Notes). |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An Operator's engagements are single-event (a tech job, a date) and do not stage. Studio *sessions* across an album's tracking period are separate bookings in domain 16, not milestones here. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A milestone is a named stage with its own deliverable, its own escrow tranche, and its own acceptance. Milestones are declared on the quote (05.02.01) — they are a commercial structure, agreed before work starts, not something either party imposes later.

Each milestone runs a compressed version of the engagement lifecycle: it may have its own requirements gate (you cannot mix until tracking is accepted), its own turnaround, its own revision allowance, and its own acceptance that releases its tranche.

Milestones are **sequential by default** in music, because the chain is causal: there is nothing to mix until there is something tracked.

### Happy Path

1. Seller proposes a staged structure on the quote (5 stages, £X each, deliverable per stage)
2. Buyer accepts; escrow captures — see DT-02 for the capture question
3. Milestone 1 opens; its requirements gate applies; work proceeds
4. Seller delivers milestone 1; buyer accepts; tranche 1 releases
5. Milestone 2 opens automatically, taking milestone 1's deliverable as its input
6. Repeat to the final milestone; final acceptance triggers the full rights transfer (05.06.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer accepts milestone 3 of 5, then cancels | Three tranches released, two not. The work delivered is paid; the work not done is not. This is the cleanest cancellation case in the domain — see 05.03.05. | Settlement arithmetic per tranche |
| Buyer rejects milestone 2 repeatedly | The chain stalls; later milestones cannot open | Revision allowance applies per milestone (05.03.03); exhaustion → change order or dispute |
| Rights transfer at which milestone? | The buyer paid for tracking but the master is not finished. Do they own the tracked stems? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-01. **The feature's hardest question.** |
| Seller abandons after milestone 2 | Buyer holds two accepted deliverables and an unfinished job | Cancellation path + possible substitute seller (05.05.01) — but a half-produced album is not portable to another producer in the way a half-built website is |
| Milestone deliverables are drafts, not masters | A tracking milestone's deliverable is raw multitrack — worthless to anyone but this producer | Reinforces DT-01: milestone acceptance ≠ usable rights |
| Parallel milestones (artwork alongside mixing) | Possible but rare; the default is sequential | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Escrow capture: all upfront or per tranche? | Capturing all upfront ties up the buyer's money for months; per-tranche means later tranches may fail to capture | `[PENDING]` — see DT-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Earlier milestone not accepted | Locked, greyed, "opens after {previous}" |
| Open | Previous accepted | Its own lifecycle states (05.03.01) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Milestone acceptance releases money, but it must not transfer rights — the rights transfer belongs to final acceptance only** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with 05.06? Trace a mid-engagement cancellation through the rights model. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and this is the feature's central insight. Consider an album cancelled after tracking is accepted and paid. If milestone acceptance transferred rights per stage, the buyer now owns raw multitracks — which are worth nothing to them (they cannot mix), and the producer has lost control of an unfinished work carrying their sound and their name with no ability to finish it. If it transfers nothing, the buyer paid for tracking and owns nothing at all, which is equally indefensible. The resolution is that milestone acceptance releases the **tranche** and vests a *contingent* interest, while the actual transfer (05.06.03) executes at final acceptance — and the cancellation settlement (05.03.05) is what has to say who ends up with the partial work. That last part is genuinely unresolved and is the domain's most consequential open question about half-finished creative work. It is not something a music platform can copy from Upwork, where a half-built API still runs. |
| DT-02 | Escrow should capture the full engagement value upfront, then release per tranche | The obvious escrow model | ❌ REJECTED as an assumption — it is a real trade-off, not a default. Capturing £12,000 upfront for a four-month album ties up a buyer's money for months and will lose deals to sellers who invoice per stage off-platform (the leakage risk `meta/personas.md` names). Capturing per tranche means a later tranche can fail to capture, leaving the seller working on stage 4 with no money held — which is the escrow's entire purpose gone at exactly the point the seller's exposure is highest. Neither is obviously right, and it is a payments-architecture decision with a product consequence. Flagged to the Payments, Escrow & Payouts cross-cut and recorded as Q-01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Milestone acceptance releases the tranche; it does not transfer rights | See DT-01. Rights execute once, at final acceptance. |
| D-02 | Milestones are declared on the quote, before work starts | A staging structure imposed mid-engagement is a renegotiation, which is a change order. |
| D-03 | Sequential by default | The music chain is causal — nothing to mix until something is tracked. Parallel is possible but is not the shape to design for. |
| D-04 | Revision allowances are per-milestone | A single engagement-wide allowance would be exhausted in stage 1. |
