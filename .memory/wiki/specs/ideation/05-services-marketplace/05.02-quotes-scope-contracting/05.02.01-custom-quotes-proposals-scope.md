# Feature: Custom Quotes, Proposals & Scope Negotiation

> **Number**: 05.02.01
> **Parent**: [Quotes, Scope & Contracting](./05.02-quotes-scope-contracting-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A bespoke, expiring offer a seller writes against a buyer's specific job — rendered from their listing's pricing model but adjusted for the reality of the work — negotiated to an agreed scope and then accepted. The accepted version becomes the engagement's scope of record: the document every revision argument, change order and dispute in this domain is measured against.

Most real music work cannot be bought off a tier. "Mix my album" is not a price until someone knows it is 11 tracks, two of them with 60 stems, one of which needs vocal tuning the buyer has not mentioned.

This feature is also the domain's **term-carrier**. Nine other features in domain 05 make decisions that only take effect because a quote states them and a buyer accepts them (see the Carried Terms table below). A term that is not on the accepted version is not agreed, is not enforceable, and is not measurable — which makes an omission here a silent failure in a sibling feature.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Writes quotes as a seller (adjusting for the job's real shape) and receives them as a buyer. Sees an itemised offer with an absolute expiry date+time, an explicit inclusion/**exclusion** list drawn from their craft's library, and the rights posture stated in plain language rather than buried. The persona's Success Criteria — "no re-proving themselves to each new client" — shows up here as the quote being pre-populated from their verified credits (02) and their prior engagements rather than written from scratch every time. As a **buyer** they frequently accept with band money, which makes acceptance an entity act with a mandate check (Q-05), not a personal one. As a **minor** (a normal case in this market) they can transact only with a verified guardian co-signatory (D-12). |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest user in both directions, and the only persona who routinely appears **on both sides of one quote** — quoting production to a band they play in, or to an entity they also administer. That is why self-dealing is a first-class blocked case here (DT-06, D-13) rather than a trust-and-safety afterthought. Quotes production and mixing work where scope is genuinely uncertain, and buys session performance, tuning/editing and mastering into their projects. This is the persona whose `Anti-Persona Behavior` — "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" — is structurally enabled or blocked here, because a points term (05.06.02) is proposed on the quote and **granted by acceptance**. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An on-location engagement reaches an Operator as a room booking in domain 16, and only the room shape declaration (05.01.05 D-01) crosses the boundary — never the commercial terms. A venue does not see what the band paid their engineer, and should not. The absence is load-bearing for the 16↔05 boundary. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No Fan-facing surface renders a quote, a quote request, or a negotiation thread. A hobbyist who commissions a mix is acting as a Musician and is onboarded into that lens. Structurally required: quote requests routinely carry unreleased material behind an NDA (05.02.02), and the Fan lens is where a leak does commercial damage. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A quote is a **contract offer**, and it behaves like one: it is issued by one party, it is immutable once issued, it expires, and it is accepted as a whole or not at all. Negotiation produces **new versions**, not edits — the seller revises to v2, the buyer counters at v3 — and exactly one version is ever the accepted one.

Acceptance is what creates the engagement (05.03.01) and is the trigger for **escrow capture**. It is *not* the trigger for escrow release or rights execution — those fire at **delivery acceptance** (05.04.01, 05.06.03). The word "acceptance" means two different things in this domain and this feature owns the first one (DT-13).

### Immutability and what voids an offer

An issued quote is a **binding offer the seller must honour**. The set of things that end it is closed (D-10):

| Ends the offer | Does **not** end the offer |
|---|---|
| Expiry passing | Seller raising their listing rate |
| Seller withdrawal (pre-acceptance only) | Seller editing or unpublishing the listing |
| Supersession by a later version | Seller reaching their capacity ceiling (DT-04) |
| Buyer decline (terminal for that version) | Buyer's NDA access to the material being revoked |
| Seller account suspension (24) | FX movement (D-09) |
| Buyer acceptance | The buyer's stated job turning out to be wrong (→ change order, D-11) |

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a quote from a listing, describing the job — 11 tracks, genre, references, deadline. If the job is confidential, the NDA gate (05.02.02) resolves **first**: no signature, no material, no quote.
2. System renders the listing's pricing model (05.01.03) against the stated job as a starting figure, pre-fills the seller's standard terms from their listing, and pre-fills their craft's default exclusion library (DT-01).
3. Seller adjusts the figure, ticks/unticks exclusions, sets the artifact set, elects anonymity if applicable, and sees two things they did not ask for: the benchmark band for this craft/cohort (05.01.04, symmetric — D-15) and their **outstanding-offer exposure** ("3 live quotes totalling 14 days of work against a ceiling of 4 concurrent") — DT-04.
4. Seller sets an expiry (default 14 days — D-06) and issues **v1**. Union election, if any, floors the price before issue and blocks a below-scale issue (CX-03).
5. Buyer sees the full offer, the expiry as an absolute date+time in their own timezone, and — because this is v1 — no diff.
6. Buyer counters: wants stems included, wants it a week sooner. The counter is a message in a thread anchored to v1 (Messaging cross-cut). **Accept stays live on v1** — a counter is not a rejection here.
7. Seller issues **v2** incorporating the stems (artifact set change, +£400) and the shortened turnaround (rush premium, +£250). v1 is voided by supersession.
8. System shows the buyer a **diff against v1**, not a fresh wall of text. The two changed lines are marked as **material** (price, artifact set); had the rights posture or points changed, a separate explicit acknowledgement would be required beyond the diff (D-08).
9. Buyer accepts v2. An acceptance is bound to the specific version it names; an attempt to accept anything other than the current live version is refused and the buyer is shown what changed (D-02). While an acceptance is in flight, the seller cannot issue a new version (D-14).
10. Pre-capture gates run: buyer's mandate to bind the entity (Q-05), points authority against the recording's aggregate allocation (05.06.02 D-04), minor/guardian co-signature (D-12), seller not suspended.
11. E-signature applied via the Contracts cross-cut; escrow **captures** the quote's total in the quote's currency; engagement created (05.03.01) referencing v2. All of it, or none of it (D-05).
12. v2 is frozen as the scope of record. The engagement opens at `Awaiting Requirements` — the seller's clock has not started and the seller is not late (05.03.01, 05.01.06 D-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Quote expires unaccepted | Offer lapses; no obligation on either side. The seller's outstanding-offer exposure drops by this quote's weight. | "This quote expired on {absolute date+time, viewer tz}. Ask {seller} to re-issue?" |
| Seller raises their listing rate after issuing a quote | The issued quote is immutable and honours its snapshot until expiry (D-10) | Seller: "2 open quotes were issued at your previous rate — £4,200 of exposure until 30 Jul." Buyer: nothing changes. |
| Seller edits or unpublishes the listing the quote was rendered from | The quote is a snapshot, not a reference. Offer unaffected. | Buyer: nothing. Seller: "2 live quotes were rendered from this listing and are unaffected." |
| Buyer accepts a superseded version (v1 while v2 is live) | Only the latest issued version is acceptable; earlier versions are void on supersession (D-02). An acceptance is bound to the version it names, and one naming a superseded version is refused. | "This offer was updated while you were reading. Review v2 — 2 things changed." (diff opens) |
| **Seller issues v2 while the buyer's acceptance of v1 is mid-flight** | While an acceptance is in flight, no new version can be issued (D-14). The buyer's v1 acceptance completes and v2 is never issued — the seller's draft is preserved as a change order candidate. | Seller: "{buyer} is accepting v1 right now. You can raise a change order once the engagement exists." |
| **Buyer double-taps Accept / connection drops after capture** | A repeated acceptance of the same version by the same buyer resolves to the one engagement that already exists (D-14). A retry returns that same engagement. Never a second capture. | The engagement, once. |
| Buyer's stated job is wrong (says 9 tracks, sends 11) | The quote priced 9. Not a revision — a change order (05.03.03). D-11 generalises this: **any** variance between stated inputs and reality is a change order, never a silent re-price. | "You quoted 9 tracks; 11 arrived. Raise a change order?" |
| **Measured input differs from stated input** (buyer said 40 min of programme; delivered audio is 58 min) | Same rule (D-11). The platform measures (05.01.03 DT-03, Media Handling) but does **not** silently re-price — a re-price would mean the accepted version is not the price, which destroys the scope-of-record mechanism. Change order tops up escrow. | Seller: "Measured programme is 58:12 against 40:00 quoted. Raise a change order for +18:12?" |
| **Buyer reduces scope below a volume-rated count** (11 tracks at £300 → cuts to 4) | Reduction is a change order, which re-renders the price through the listing's pricing model at the actual count. The volume rate reverts to the single-unit rate naturally, because the model does the arithmetic. No special rule needed. | "4 tracks re-prices at your single-track rate (£400), not the album rate (£300). New total £1,600." |
| Terms negotiated in chat instead of on the quote | The chat is not the scope of record (D-01) — but people will do this anyway | System detects commercial terms in messages and prompts: "Put this in the quote so it counts." — see Q-02 in the sub-domain index |
| Union session (05.02.03) | Rendered price is floored at scale before issue | "Below AFM scale for this session type. Minimum {x}." — issue blocked |
| Points-only quote (05.01.03 DT-01) | Zero cash means nothing to escrow; the rights instrument is the only protection | Prominent warning on both sides before acceptance: "No money is being held. Your protection on this deal is the contract, not escrow." |
| **Points quote where the buyer cannot grant the points** | Points are granted by the owner, not elected by the seller (05.06.02 D-05). Authority is checked against the recording's aggregate allocation in 09 at acceptance (05.06.02 D-04) and **warned** at issue. Acceptance blocked if it would over-allocate the master. | Issue: "We can't confirm {buyer} controls this master yet." Acceptance: "This would allocate 107% of this master. {buyer} has already granted 104%." |
| **Sealed anonymity + a co-ownership or points posture** | Contradictory — a co-owner appears on the split sheet and in the royalty flow by definition (05.02.02 Q-01) | Explicit warning at election, both sides: "Sealed credit + points: your name will still appear in the royalty flow. Sealing is not deliverable here." Combination not blocked pending 05.02.02 Q-01. |
| Buyer accepts but payment fails | Engagement must not exist. Acceptance and capture are one transaction (D-05). | "Payment didn't go through — the quote is still open until {absolute date+time}." |
| **Expiry passes while payment is settling** | Expiry is evaluated at **acceptance initiation**, not at capture settlement. A capture that settles 90 seconds after expiry still forms the engagement. | Nothing — the buyer accepted in time. |
| Seller withdraws a live quote | Allowed before acceptance; not after | Buyer notified; quote marked withdrawn with a required reason |
| Buyer declines, then changes their mind | Decline is **terminal for that version**. The seller may re-issue. A reversible decline is not a decline, and the seller has already released the exposure and may have taken other work. | "You declined this on {date}. Ask {seller} to re-issue?" |
| **Seller never issues** (drafts and goes dark) | A `Requested` quote with no issue in **7 days** auto-lapses. Both parties notified; the buyer is offered comparable listings. The lapse feeds the seller's response-rate signal, not a punishment. | Buyer: "{seller} didn't respond. Here are 4 others who take this work." Seller: "You let a request lapse." |
| **Seller's account is suspended (24) with live quotes outstanding** | All live quotes are voided — an engagement cannot be created with a suspended seller. Buyers are notified without stating the reason (a suspension reason is not the buyer's business). | "This quote is no longer available." |
| **Seller is at their concurrency ceiling when the buyer accepts** | Acceptance is **not** blocked. An issued offer accepted forms a contract; refusing to honour it is a breach the platform would be authoring (DT-04). The ceiling binds *ordering* (05.01.07 D-03), not acceptance. The seller goes over ceiling and is told. | Seller: "You're now at 5 of 4 concurrent. Two quotes are still live — withdraw them?" |
| **Buyer is under 18** | Contracting capacity. A minor's contract is voidable by the minor in most relevant jurisdictions. Transaction permitted only with a verified guardian as **co-signatory party** on the instrument (D-12); guardian identity and age assurance come from the Safeguarding & Minor Protection cross-cut. Without a guardian, the quote cannot be accepted. | "This quote needs a parent or guardian to co-sign before you can accept it." + guardian invite flow |
| **Seller is under 18** (a normal case — teenage producers are a large share of this market) | The sharper case. A minor's **rights assignment** may be disaffirmable even with guardian consent (some jurisdictions require court approval for long-form minors' contracts), which leaves the buyer with a defect in chain of title they cannot see. Guardian co-signature is required as above, **and** the residual risk is disclosed to the buyer rather than warranted away — consistent with domain D-07 ("the platform makes claims attributable; it does not verify them"). | Buyer, at acceptance: "{seller} is under 18 and their guardian is co-signing. In some jurisdictions a minor can later withdraw from an assignment. This is disclosed, not insured." |
| **Same human on both sides** (Producer quotes a band they are a member of) | Self-deal. Blocked unless a **different** authorised human of the buyer entity accepts (D-13). If the entity has exactly one authorised human and it is the seller, the quote cannot be accepted at all. The relationship is disclosed on the document either way. | "You're on both sides of this quote. Someone else at {band} has to accept it." + list of authorised members |
| **Buyer entity: the human who accepts never read v1** (drummer requested; manager accepts) | The diff assumes one continuous reader (DT-09). For a viewer with no prior view of **any** version in the audit log, the diff is **suppressed** and the full current version is shown. The diff is an aid, never the document. | Manager sees the whole offer. Drummer sees the diff. |
| **Buyer entity: the accepting human has no mandate** | Authority to bind the entity is checked at acceptance against 01's mandate scope. Blocked, routed, never silently allowed — a £4k commitment by an unauthorised member is the entity's problem forever. | "You can't commit {band} to £4,200. {names} can — ask them to accept?" — see Q-05 |
| **Seller's NDA access to the material is revoked after issue** | The offer stands (D-10). The seller priced what they heard; losing access does not un-hear it. | Seller: material locked, quote still live and honourable. |
| **FX moves between issue and acceptance** | Price is denominated in the seller's listing currency, which **is** the contract currency (D-09). The buyer's conversion is explicitly indicative and re-rendered at capture. Otherwise a 14-day expiry is a free FX option written by the seller for the buyer (DT-11). | "£3,300 (about $4,180 today — you'll be charged the £ amount)." |
| **Buyer's tax jurisdiction is unknown or changes between issue and acceptance** | The quote freezes a total. A total that moves at capture is a different offer. Jurisdiction must be resolvable at issue; where it is not, issue is blocked rather than issuing an indicative total. | "We need {buyer}'s billing country before this can be issued." — see Q-04 |
| **Buyer accepts two competing quotes from two sellers for the same job** | Permitted. Two engagements exist and both capture. The platform is not a party and does not police the buyer's commercial choices; tendering is domain 04's workflow (CX R-02), not this one's. Sellers are **not** told they were in a race — a seller who knows prices differently, and the platform is not the buyer's agent. | Buyer only: "You have another open engagement for a similar job." Neutral, dismissible. |
| **Seller lowballs to move the deal off-platform** ("accept at £50, wire me the rest") | The platform cannot prevent it and should not try to police price (05.01.04 D-01 refuses to block a low price). The defence is that what the platform provides — escrow, atomic rights execution, the verified credit — **cannot be taken off-platform** (DT-12). Detection surfaces to the buyer what they forfeit, not an accusation. | Buyer: "Paying outside WeJammin means no escrow, no rights transfer, and no credit on your record for this work." |
| **Malicious / oversized free text** | Exclusions come from the structured library; free-text addenda are capped at **500 characters per item, 20 items**. The instrument renders from structured fields, never from raw buyer/seller markup. All free text is escaped at render and in the PDF/e-sign artifact. | "Exclusion note is 640 characters — trim to 500." |
| **Milestone tranches don't sum to the quote total** | Arithmetic guard at issue. Blocked, not rounded. | "Your 3 milestones total £3,100. The quote is £3,300. £200 is unallocated." |
| **Buyer counters at hour 1 of a 2-hour expiry; seller replies at hour 3** | The offer lapsed while the seller was writing. v2 is a new offer with a new expiry — no harm. But a seller sitting on an unanswered counter loses the deal silently, so: notify the seller at **24h and 2h** before expiry when an unanswered counter exists. | Seller: "Unanswered counter — this quote expires in 2 hours." |
| **The job is really an Opportunity** (04) | Boundary. A won Opportunity already carries a negotiated fee; whether it arrives here as a quote or an already-created engagement is unresolved. | — see Q-01 |
| **Quote requested for a craft the seller's listing does not cover** | The taxonomy (05.01.02) constrains which models and terms are legal. Request is redirected, not rendered. | "{seller} doesn't list mastering. They do mixing and production — request one of those?" |
| **Barter** ("you mix my record, I'll play bass on yours") | Expressible as a zero-cash quote whose consideration is named in text. The platform does **not** build a linked two-leg swap instrument (DT-07). Same no-escrow warning as points-only. | "No money is being held on this deal. If the other side doesn't deliver, escrow can't help you." |
| **Trial scope declined after the test mix** | Not a cancellation and carries **no kill fee**. The buyer used the mechanism correctly (DT-10). The remainder's price lock simply lapses. | Seller: "{buyer} isn't continuing past the test mix. Paid in full for song 1." No fault recorded. |

### Lifecycle States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Buyer asks | Seller: a job brief to price, with a 7-day lapse timer. Buyer: "waiting on {seller}" |
| Gated | Confidential job, NDA unsigned (05.02.02) | Seller: job shape only, Sign CTA. No quote possible. |
| Drafting | Seller writing | Seller-only, with benchmark band + outstanding-offer exposure. Buyer sees "preparing your quote" |
| Issued (vN) | Seller issues | Buyer: full offer + absolute expiry + Accept |
| Superseded | A later version issued | Read-only, marked superseded, diff to current |
| Under negotiation | Buyer countered | Thread against the version; Accept still live on the current version |
| Accepting | Acceptance in flight | Seller: issue locked. Buyer: capture in progress. |
| Accepted | Buyer accepts + capture succeeds | Frozen; engagement created at `Awaiting Requirements` |
| Expired | Expiry passed | Lapsed with re-issue CTA |
| Lapsed (request) | 7 days, never issued | Buyer offered alternatives; seller's response rate reflects it |
| Withdrawn | Seller withdraws pre-acceptance | Required reason shown |
| Voided | Seller suspended (24) | "No longer available" — reason not disclosed to the buyer |
| Declined | Buyer declines | Optional reason; feeds nothing punitive; terminal for that version |

|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Quote or diff being fetched | Skeleton of the offer's line structure — not a spinner. The line count is known from the version metadata, so the shape is real before the content arrives. |
| Empty | Seller has no quote requests | "No quote requests yet." + the two levers that change it: listing completeness (05.01.01) and capacity state (05.01.07). Not a decorative empty state — a diagnostic one. |
| Empty (buyer) | Buyer has no quotes | "Nothing here yet — browse listings or describe your job." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Exclusions matter more than inclusions in creative services, and must be first-class rather than free text** | Domain expertise: no mix engineer has ever been sued over what was in the quote. The dispute is always about what the buyer *assumed* was in it — tuning, stems, an instrumental, an alternate/TV mix, the session files. The industry's own workaround is the "what's not included" paragraph every engineer's terms page has, and it is prose nobody reads. | ✅ CONFIRMED — exclusions are structured, rendered prominently, and drawn from a per-craft library of the things buyers usually assume (taxonomy-driven, 05.01.02), so the seller does not have to remember to exclude vocal tuning at 2am. This is the cheapest dispute-prevention mechanism in the domain and it costs a checklist. |
| DT-02 | **Scope negotiation must produce versioned offers with a diff, not an editable document or a chat agreement** | Reasoning about where disputes become unresolvable: `meta/personas.md` names the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as "we'll sort it out later", and the Musician's switching trigger as "a split they agreed verbally and can't evidence". A mutable quote or a chat thread reproduces exactly that failure inside the product that exists to prevent it. | ✅ CONFIRMED — quotes are immutable per version; negotiation forks a version; exactly one is accepted; the accepted version is frozen as the scope of record. The **diff** is the part that is not obvious: buyers do not re-read a 3rd version of a long offer, they skim it and assume only the thing they asked about changed. A seller can therefore slip a term in a re-issue and it would be *technically* consented to. Showing the diff is what makes the consent real. |
| DT-03 | A quote does not need an expiry — it can stay open until withdrawn | Simplicity | ❌ REJECTED — an open-ended quote is a rate a seller published in March being accepted in November, and the seller has no realistic way to remember to withdraw it. Worse, capacity (05.01.07) makes it dangerous: a quote accepted 4 months later lands on a seller whose queue is full and whose turnaround promise on it is now fiction. Expiry is mandatory. Default resolved at D-06. |
| DT-04 | **The seller's capacity ceiling (05.01.07) cannot bind at acceptance, because accepting a live offer is contract formation** | Adversarial: trace the interaction between two features that were specified independently. 05.01.07 D-01 makes capacity a hard concurrency ceiling; 05.02.01 D-02 makes an issued quote an immutable offer. Both are right; together they collide. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and the resolution runs against the obvious one. Three options: (a) an issued quote **reserves** a concurrency slot — fails immediately, because a seller with a ceiling of 4 and 9 live quotes could take no orders at all, and quoting is free while ordering is not; (b) acceptance is **blocked** at ceiling — the platform would be authoring a breach of its own instrument, telling a buyer who accepted a live offer that the offer was not real, which is exactly the "verbal agreement I can't rely on" failure the product exists to kill; (c) acceptance **succeeds** and the seller goes over ceiling. (c) is the only honest answer: the ceiling governs *ordering* (05.01.07 D-03 already says at-capacity listings stay visible and non-orderable), and outstanding offers are **the seller's own exposure, which they created**. The design obligation is therefore not a gate but **legibility**: show the seller their live-offer exposure in workload terms at the moment of issue, and let expiry (D-04) do the reaping. This inverts the intuitive answer — the fix for a capacity problem is a shorter expiry, not a harder gate. |
| DT-05 | **Any variance between the quote's stated inputs and measured reality is a change order, never a silent re-price** | Adversarial: 05.01.03 DT-03 rejects buyer-stated duration for per-minute pricing and says the platform should "measure the actual duration and reconcile at acceptance". Trace that against D-05 here (capture is atomic with quote acceptance). | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it resolves a real contradiction between two features. 05.01.03's "acceptance" is **delivery** acceptance (05.04.01); this feature's is **quote** acceptance (DT-13). If price could reconcile at delivery, escrow captured the wrong number at quote acceptance and the accepted version is not the price — which destroys the scope-of-record mechanism (D-01) that every dispute in the domain depends on. The generalisation is worth more than the instance: the existing "said 9 tracks, sent 11" edge case is not a special case, it is **the** case. Stated inputs are frozen with the price; reality diverging from them is a renegotiation, and the domain already has an object for a renegotiation — the change order (05.03.03 D-04), which is itself a mini-quote and therefore inherits immutability, expiry and consent for free. Nothing new is invented; a silent re-price is simply forbidden. |
| DT-06 | **The same human on both sides of a quote is the Producer's stated anti-persona behaviour executed structurally, and it is invisible unless this feature looks for it** | Adversarial: "the multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once." `meta/personas.md` is explicit that one human routinely holds several roles simultaneously, and that the Producer's worst intentional behaviour is "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention". | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is more dangerous here than anywhere else in the domain. A producer who is a member of a band, quoting that band for production, taking 4 points (05.06.02), and accepting their own quote on the band's behalf, has committed the exact anti-persona act — and every individual step is legitimate. There is no fraud to detect, no anomalous value, no complaint: the money moves, both "parties" agree, the split executes at 05.06.03, and the band discovers it at royalty time years later. This is precisely the reconstruction failure from `meta/problem-statement.md`, committed *through* the mechanism built to prevent it. The blocking rule is cheap and total: acceptance must come from a **different** authorised human of the buyer entity (D-13). Where the entity has one authorised human and it is the seller, the deal cannot happen on-platform at all — correctly, because a contract with oneself is not a contract. The relationship is disclosed on the document regardless, because the other band members are the people the disclosure is for. |
| DT-07 | Barter should be modelled as a linked two-leg quote pair that commits atomically | Domain expertise: "you mix my record, I'll play bass on yours" is extremely common at the bottom and middle of the market, and neither leg has cash | ❌ REJECTED — the atomicity is undeliverable and the promise would be worse than the absence. A linked swap needs both legs to commit together, but the legs are separated by *months of work*, not milliseconds: leg A delivers in June, leg B in November. There is nothing to hold, nothing to release, and no remedy when the second leg simply never happens — which is the normal failure of barter and the reason musicians already distrust it. Building the instrument would let the platform imply a protection that does not exist, which is the same error 05.06.02 D-03 forbids for points-only deals. What is honest and nearly free: barter is expressible as a **zero-cash quote** whose consideration is stated in the document, carrying the identical no-escrow warning as points-only. The parties get the scope of record, the credit, and the audit trail — everything except a guarantee the platform could never keep. |
| DT-08 | A minimum expiry should be enforced to prevent high-pressure selling | Adversarial: what is the manipulation surface? A 2-hour expiry with "another artist wants this week" is a textbook pressure tactic. | ❌ REJECTED — a floor breaks the case the domain explicitly built for. 05.05.01 (deps, substitutes and **emergency cover**) exists for the dep needed for tomorrow morning's session, where a 1-hour quote is not a pressure tactic but the entire product. Any floor high enough to stop pressure selling (24h) kills emergency cover; any floor low enough to permit it (1h) stops nothing. The manipulation is real but the lever is wrong. The lever that works is inherited: render expiry as an **absolute date+time in the viewer's timezone** rather than a red countdown (05.01.06 D-03 already establishes exactly this for due dates, for the same reason — a duration is ambiguous across timezones and is also the shape that manufactures urgency). Short expiries then read as what they are — a fact about the seller's week — rather than as a ticking clock the interface is amplifying. |
| DT-09 | **The diff assumes one continuous reader, and entities break that assumption silently** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with domain 01? D-19 makes the Band a first-class **entity**; `meta/personas.md` says entities "book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members". | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a quiet defeat of D-03, the feature's own consent mechanism. The drummer requests the quote and reads v1. The manager accepts v3. The diff shown to the manager says "2 things changed" — against a document they have never seen. D-03 exists so that consent is to what actually changed; for this reader the diff is not merely useless, it is **actively misleading**, because it implies the rest of the document is already known to them. The fix is a one-line rule with a real consequence: the diff is rendered only for a viewer with a prior view of some version in the audit log; for everyone else it is suppressed and the full current version is the document. Stated generally — **the diff is an aid to a returning reader, never the thing being accepted.** The audit log (which this feature already consumes) is what makes the rule computable, which is a second reason it is not optional. |
| DT-10 | **The industry's own de-risking mechanism for uncertain scope is the test mix, and the model has no way to express it** | Domain expertise: what does a music professional expect that nobody has mentioned? Before an 11-track album mix, a client and engineer almost always do **one song** first. It is the near-universal norm above the bottom of the market. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap. Nothing in the domain expresses it, and every available workaround is wrong: two unrelated quotes lose the album's price (the engineer's album rate was consideration for the album, and re-quoting after the test invites a hold-up); one 11-track engagement that the buyer exits after song 1 is a **cancellation** (05.03.05) with fault and a kill fee — penalising the buyer for using the industry's standard risk mechanism exactly as intended. The resolution reuses existing machinery rather than inventing an object: a quote may declare a **trial scope** — a first tranche with (i) a no-fault buyer exit at its completion and (ii) the remainder's price **locked at issue**. It renders as a milestone-staged engagement (05.03.02 D-02, declared on the quote — already required) whose milestone 1 carries an exit right. Declining the remainder is not a cancellation, carries no kill fee, and records no fault; the price lock simply lapses. This turns the domain's most common real deal shape from an abuse of the cancellation flow into a first-class term. |
| DT-11 | **A long expiry on a cross-currency quote is a free FX option the seller has unknowingly written for the buyer** | Adversarial: what does a domain expert in the *adjacent* discipline expect? This is a marketplace with a global seller base and a locked 14-day default expiry (D-06). | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is invisible until it costs someone. A London engineer quotes £3,300; the buyer is in the US; over 14 days GBP/USD moves 2%. If the price were denominated in the buyer's currency at issue, the seller wrote a two-week option on their own fee for free and the buyer can rationally wait for a favourable rate before accepting. At the scale of one deal it is £66; at platform scale it is a systematic transfer from sellers (who are the personas this product exists to protect) to buyers, and it is invisible in every individual transaction. The resolution: the **seller's listing currency is the contract currency** (D-09), full stop; escrow captures in it; the buyer's own-currency figure is explicitly indicative, marked as such, and re-rendered at capture. The buyer bears the FX movement, which is correct — they are the one choosing when to accept. |
| DT-12 | The platform should police below-market quotes as fee-circumvention attempts ("accept at £50, wire me the rest") | Adversarial: the Musician's stated worst intentional behaviour is "uses the platform to find collaborators then takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees" | ❌ REJECTED as framed, and the rejection is the more useful output. Price-policing fails on its own terms: it contradicts 05.01.04 D-01 (which explicitly refuses to block a low price), it produces false positives on every genuinely cheap deal — a favour, a first job, a mate's rate, all normal and legitimate — and it makes the platform the arbiter of what a seller's work is worth, which is the one thing 05.01.04 refuses to be. The deeper point: **treating leakage as an enforcement problem concedes that the platform's value is the introduction.** Per D-18 it is not — the value is escrow, atomic rights execution (05.06.03), and the verified credit (02), and none of those three can be taken off-platform. A buyer who wires the money outside gets no escrow, no assignment, and no credit on their record; a seller who takes it outside forfeits the accumulating proof that `meta/personas.md` names as their reason to stay. So the honest mechanism is **disclosure of what is forfeited, at the moment the pattern appears** — which costs nothing, accuses nobody, is right even when the suspicion is wrong, and is a restatement of the thesis rather than a rule bolted onto it. If the thesis is true, leakage is self-limiting. If leakage is not self-limiting, the thesis is false and enforcement would not save it. |
| DT-13 | **"Acceptance" names two different events in this domain, and every feature that says the word means one of them without saying which** | Adversarial: read the domain's own sibling files for a shared term used inconsistently. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a live ambiguity across at least six features, and the kind that survives every review because everyone silently supplies the right meaning. **Quote acceptance** (this feature) creates the engagement and **captures** escrow. **Delivery acceptance** (05.04.01) — including auto-accept by silence — **releases** escrow, executes rights and emits the credit (05.06.03). They are months apart and they trigger disjoint systems. The failure this prevents is concrete: 05.01.03 DT-03's "reconcile at acceptance" reads as quote acceptance to anyone who arrives from this file, and as delivery acceptance to anyone who arrives from 05.04 — one reading is a silent re-price of a frozen contract (DT-05), the other is correct. The ambiguity is also load-bearing for the domain's flagship claim: "escrow release ↔ rights transfer is atomic" is true at delivery acceptance and **false** at quote acceptance, where money is captured and no rights move at all. Carried to Step 6 as a naming obligation on the IA layer: the two events must never share a word. |

### Cross-cut mechanisms consumed

- **Contracts & E-Signature** — the quote is a signed instrument; the cross-cut owns templating, redlining, signature, countersignature, versioning and the tamper-evident archive. **New requirement**: it must support a **guardian co-signatory** as a third party to a two-party instrument (D-12), and must render only from structured fields (never raw user markup).
- **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** — acceptance triggers **capture** (not release); acceptance and capture are atomic (D-05); a repeated acceptance of the same version by the same buyer never captures twice (D-14); capture is in the seller's listing currency (D-09). Tax and platform-fee lines must be computable and frozen **at issue** (Q-04).
- **Messaging & Contextual Inbox** — the negotiation thread lives against a quote version. Constraint: the chat is explicitly **not** the scope of record (D-01), which is a rendering obligation, not just a policy.
- **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** — who issued, who accepted, when, which version. **New load**: it is also the input to the diff-suppression rule (DT-09) — "has this human seen any prior version?" is an audit-log query, which makes the log a runtime read path, not just an archive.
- **Localization, Currency & Timezone** — the contract currency (D-09); expiry rendered as an absolute date+time in the viewer's timezone (DT-08, inheriting 05.01.06 D-03).
- **Safeguarding & Minor Protection** — age assurance and verified guardian identity for a minor buyer *or* seller (D-12). Closes this file's only breadth-pass gap (the minor buyer/seller contracting-capacity placeholder), now fully specified in D-12.
- **Notifications** — fan-out: expiry warnings to the buyer; **unanswered-counter** expiry warnings to the seller at 24h and 2h (a seller who sits on a counter loses the deal silently); request-lapse at 7 days to both; live-quote voiding on seller suspension.
- **Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution** — the per-craft exclusion library (DT-01) is reference data owned by 05.01.02's taxonomy.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The accepted quote version is the scope of record — not the listing, not the chat | Every dispute reduces to "what did we agree?". A chat answer is unresolvable. |
| D-02 | Quotes are immutable per version; negotiation forks a new version; supersession voids earlier ones | Prevents both silent term-changing and stale-offer acceptance. |
| D-03 | Every re-issue shows the buyer a diff | Consent to a term the buyer never saw is not consent. See DT-02. Qualified by D-16. |
| D-04 | Expiry is mandatory | See DT-03. |
| D-05 | Acceptance and escrow **capture** are one atomic transaction | An engagement that exists without captured funds has no escrow and no leverage — the domain's entire mechanism assumes the money is held. Distinct from the release↔rights atomicity at 05.06.03 (DT-13). |
| D-06 | **Default expiry is 14 days**; seller-settable from 1 hour to 90 days; always rendered as an absolute date+time in the viewer's timezone | **Resolves Q-02.** 14 days survives a musician's touring week and a buyer's band meeting without becoming the stale-rate/stale-capacity problem of DT-03. The floor is 1 hour, not 24, because emergency cover (05.05.01) is a real shape and a floor would kill it (DT-08). The 90-day ceiling exists so "no expiry" cannot be reconstructed by typing a large number. This is not an owner decision: the seller sets their own expiry per quote, so the owner is only being asked for a default, and a default with a defensible reason does not need ratification. |
| D-07 | The quote is the domain's **term-carrier**, and the Carried Terms table is normative | Nine sibling features make decisions that only take effect because a quote states them. The breadth pass's list omitted the kill-fee schedule (05.03.05 D-05), the anonymity level (05.02.02 D-04) and the milestone structure (05.03.02 D-02) — each an omission that would silently disable a sibling. An enumerated table is the only defence. |
| D-08 | A change to a **material** term on re-issue (price, rights posture, points, artifact set, revision allowance, kill fee, anonymity level) requires an explicit separate acknowledgement, not merely a diff line | The diff makes a change visible; it does not make it noticed. Material terms are the ones whose silent change is worth money to the party making it. |
| D-09 | The seller's listing currency **is** the contract currency; buyer-side conversion is explicitly indicative and re-rendered at capture | See DT-11. Otherwise the expiry is a free FX option written by the seller. |
| D-10 | An issued quote is a **binding offer**. The set of things that void it is closed: expiry, withdrawal, supersession, decline, seller suspension, acceptance. Listing edits, rate changes, capacity, and NDA revocation do not. | The whole mechanism rests on the offer being real. Each exception someone would reasonably add is the one that makes the instrument worthless. See DT-04. |
| D-11 | Any variance between the quote's **stated inputs** and reality is a **change order**, never a silent re-price | See DT-05. Resolves the collision with 05.01.03 DT-03. Generalises the existing "said 9, sent 11" case into the rule. |
| D-12 | A party under 18 (buyer **or** seller) transacts only with a verified guardian as a co-signatory **party** to the instrument; the residual disaffirmation risk on a minor's rights assignment is **disclosed to the counterparty**, not warranted away | **Resolves the breadth-pass minor-contracting gap.** Blocking minors outright would exclude a large and growing share of the market (teenage producers are normal); pretending a guardian signature makes the assignment unimpeachable would be a claim the platform cannot keep in jurisdictions requiring court approval. Disclosure over warranty is domain D-07 applied locally. Whether the platform accepts minors at all remains statutory — see Q-06. |
| D-13 | A quote where the same human is on both sides can be accepted only by a **different** authorised human of the buyer entity; single-authority entities cannot accept at all. The relationship is disclosed on the document regardless. | See DT-06. This is the Producer's stated anti-persona behaviour, and every individual step of it is legitimate — which is exactly why a structural block, not a detection heuristic, is the answer. |
| D-14 | A repeated acceptance of the same version by the same buyer forms **at most one** engagement, and no new version may be issued while an acceptance is in flight | A double-capture and a supersede-during-acceptance are both silent, both real, and both produce an engagement nobody agreed to. |
| D-15 | The benchmark band (05.01.04) is rendered on a live quote **symmetrically** to both parties, or to neither | An asymmetric benchmark makes the platform an agent for one side of a negotiation it is supposed to referee. Safe because 05.01.04 D-01 already restricts output to bands, never individual rates. |
| D-16 | The diff is rendered only for a viewer with a prior view of some version; for a first-time reader it is **suppressed** and the full version shown. The diff is never the document being accepted. | See DT-09. Entities (D-19) break the one-continuous-reader assumption D-03 silently makes. |
| D-17 | A **trial scope** is a first-class quote term: a first tranche with a no-fault buyer exit and the remainder's price locked at issue. It renders as a milestone-staged engagement; declining the remainder is not a cancellation and carries no kill fee. | See DT-10. The test mix is the industry's standard risk mechanism; modelling it as a cancellation penalises correct use of it. |
| D-18 | **Unsolicited (pitch) quotes are permitted only where a relationship edge already exists** — a prior engagement between the parties, an accepted connection (03), or an Opportunity (04) the seller was invited to. Rate-limited to 5 pitches per seller per 7 days, and one per buyer per 90 days unless replied to. | **Resolves Q-03** (whose `MoSCoW` deferral target has passed — D-20 ratified the ledger). The dep/session market genuinely needs it: a fixer who has covered for you twice offering to cover again is valuable, not spam. Cold pitching to strangers is indistinguishable from spam at scale and would make the quote inbox worthless — the surface the whole sub-domain depends on. The relationship gate keeps the valuable case and removes the scalable one. Loosening it is an owner call; the default is the conservative one because an inbox, once ruined, does not recover. |
| D-19 | A `Requested` quote that is never issued **lapses after 7 days**, notifying both parties; the buyer is offered comparable listings. The lapse feeds the seller's response-rate signal, not a penalty. | An un-lapsed request is a buyer waiting forever on a seller who has mentally closed it — and the buyer blames the platform, correctly. 7 days matches the shortest realistic "I'll look at this on the weekend" cycle. |
| D-20 | Free-text on a quote is capped (exclusion addenda: 500 chars × 20 items) and the instrument renders only from structured fields | The document becomes a signed PDF and a legal artifact. Unbounded, unescaped user text in a signed instrument is both an injection surface and a way to bury a term the exclusion library was designed to surface (DT-01). |
