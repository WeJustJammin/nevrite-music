# Feature: Custom Quotes, Proposals & Scope Negotiation

> **Number**: 05.02.01
> **Parent**: [Quotes, Scope & Contracting](./05.02-quotes-scope-contracting-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A bespoke, expiring offer a seller writes against a buyer's specific job — rendered from their listing's pricing model but adjusted for the reality of the work — negotiated to an agreed scope and then accepted. The accepted version becomes the engagement's scope of record: the document every revision argument, change order and dispute in this domain is measured against.

Most real music work cannot be bought off a tier. "Mix my album" is not a price until someone knows it is 11 tracks, two of them with 60 stems, one of which needs vocal tuning the buyer has not mentioned.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Writes quotes as a seller (adjusting for the job's real shape) and receives them as a buyer. Sees an itemised offer with an expiry, an explicit inclusion/exclusion list, and the rights posture stated in plain language rather than buried. The persona's Success Criteria — "no re-proving themselves to each new client" — shows up here as the quote being pre-populated from their verified credits and prior engagements rather than written from scratch every time. |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest user in both directions. Quotes production and mixing work where scope is genuinely uncertain, and buys session performance, tuning/editing and mastering into their projects. This is the persona whose `Anti-Persona Behavior` — "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" — is structurally enabled or blocked here, because a points term (05.06.02) is elected on the quote. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An on-location engagement reaches an Operator as a room booking in domain 16. A venue does not see what the band paid their engineer, and should not. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A quote is a **contract offer**, and it behaves like one: it is issued by one party, it is immutable once issued, it expires, and it is accepted as a whole or not at all. Negotiation produces **new versions**, not edits — the seller revises to v2, the buyer counters at v3 — and exactly one version is ever the accepted one.

The quote carries: the rendered price (05.01.03) and its arithmetic, inclusions, **exclusions** (which matter more than inclusions in creative work), the revision allowance (05.03.03), turnaround (05.01.06), the deliverable spec (05.04.02), the rights posture and any points (05.06.01, 05.06.02), source-file handover terms (05.04.04), the requirements the buyer owes, and expenses if on-location (05.01.05).

Acceptance is what creates the engagement (05.03.01) and is the trigger for escrow capture.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a quote from a listing, describing the job (11 tracks, genre, references, deadline)
2. System renders the listing's pricing model against the stated job as a starting figure and pre-fills the seller's standard terms
3. Seller adjusts, itemises exclusions, sets an expiry, issues **v1**
4. Buyer counters — wants stems included, wants it a week sooner — creating a negotiation thread against v1
5. Seller issues **v2** incorporating the changes and the price delta; system shows the buyer a **diff against v1**, not a fresh wall of text
6. Buyer accepts v2; e-signature applied via the Contracts cross-cut
7. Escrow captures; engagement created (05.03.01); v2 is frozen as the scope of record

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Quote expires unaccepted | Offer lapses; no obligation on either side | "This quote expired on {date}. Ask {seller} to re-issue?" |
| Seller raises their listing rate after issuing a quote | The issued quote is immutable and honours its snapshot until expiry | Seller: "2 open quotes were issued at your previous rate." Buyer: nothing changes. |
| Buyer accepts a superseded version (v1 while v2 is live) | Race. Only the latest issued version is acceptable; earlier versions are void on supersession. | "This offer was updated. Review v2." |
| Buyer's stated job is wrong (says 9 tracks, sends 11) | The quote priced 9. This is not a revision, it is a change order (05.03.03). | "You quoted 9 tracks; 11 arrived. Raise a change order?" |
| Terms negotiated in chat instead of on the quote | The chat is not the scope of record (D-01) — but people will do this anyway | System detects commercial terms in messages and prompts: "Put this in the quote so it counts." — see Q-02 in the sub-domain index |
| Union session (05.02.03) | Rendered price is floored at scale before issue | "Below AFM scale for this session type. Minimum {x}." — issue blocked |
| Points-only quote (05.01.03 DT-01) | Zero cash means nothing to escrow; the rights instrument is the only protection | Prominent warning on both sides before acceptance |
| Buyer accepts but payment fails | Engagement must not exist. Acceptance and capture are one transaction. | "Payment didn't go through — the quote is still open until {expiry}." |
| Seller withdraws a live quote | Allowed before acceptance; not after | Buyer notified; quote marked withdrawn with reason |
| Buyer is under 18 | Contracting capacity issue | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — routed to the Safeguarding & Minor Protection cross-cut |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Buyer asks | Seller: a job brief to price. Buyer: "waiting on {seller}" |
| Drafting | Seller writing | Seller-only; buyer sees "preparing your quote" |
| Issued (vN) | Seller issues | Buyer: full offer + expiry countdown + Accept |
| Superseded | A later version issued | Read-only, marked superseded, diff to current |
| Under negotiation | Buyer countered | Thread against the version; Accept still available on the live version |
| Accepted | Buyer accepts + payment captured | Frozen; engagement created |
| Expired | Expiry passed | Lapsed with re-issue CTA |
| Withdrawn | Seller withdraws pre-acceptance | Reason shown |
| Declined | Buyer declines | Optional reason; feeds nothing punitive |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Exclusions matter more than inclusions in creative services, and must be first-class rather than free text** | Domain expertise: no mix engineer has ever been sued over what was in the quote. The dispute is always about what the buyer *assumed* was in it — tuning, stems, an instrumental, an alternate/TV mix, the session files. The industry's own workaround is the "what's not included" paragraph every engineer's terms page has, and it is prose nobody reads. | ✅ CONFIRMED — exclusions are structured, rendered prominently, and drawn from a per-craft library of the things buyers usually assume (taxonomy-driven, 05.01.02), so the seller does not have to remember to exclude vocal tuning at 2am. This is the cheapest dispute-prevention mechanism in the domain and it costs a checklist. |
| DT-02 | **Scope negotiation must produce versioned offers with a diff, not an editable document or a chat agreement** | Reasoning about where disputes become unresolvable: `meta/personas.md` names the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as "we'll sort it out later", and the Musician's switching trigger as "a split they agreed verbally and can't evidence". A mutable quote or a chat thread reproduces exactly that failure inside the product that exists to prevent it. | ✅ CONFIRMED — quotes are immutable per version; negotiation forks a version; exactly one is accepted; the accepted version is frozen as the scope of record. The **diff** is the part that is not obvious: buyers do not re-read a 3rd version of a long offer, they skim it and assume only the thing they asked about changed. A seller can therefore slip a term in a re-issue and it would be *technically* consented to. Showing the diff is what makes the consent real. |
| DT-03 | A quote does not need an expiry — it can stay open until withdrawn | Simplicity | ❌ REJECTED — an open-ended quote is a rate a seller published in March being accepted in November, and the seller has no realistic way to remember to withdraw it. Worse, capacity (05.01.07) makes it dangerous: a quote accepted 4 months later lands on a seller whose queue is full and whose turnaround promise on it is now fiction. Expiry is mandatory. Default expiry length is a product decision (Q-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The accepted quote version is the scope of record — not the listing, not the chat | Every dispute reduces to "what did we agree?". A chat answer is unresolvable. |
| D-02 | Quotes are immutable per version; negotiation forks a new version; supersession voids earlier ones | Prevents both silent term-changing and stale-offer acceptance. |
| D-03 | Every re-issue shows the buyer a diff | Consent to a term the buyer never saw is not consent. See DT-02. |
| D-04 | Expiry is mandatory | See DT-03. |
| D-05 | Acceptance and escrow capture are one atomic transaction | An engagement that exists without captured funds has no escrow and no leverage — the domain's entire mechanism assumes the money is held. |
