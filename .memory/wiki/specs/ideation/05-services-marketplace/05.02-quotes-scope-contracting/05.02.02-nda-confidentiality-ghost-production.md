# Feature: NDA, Confidentiality & Ghost-Production Anonymity

> **Number**: 05.02.02
> **Parent**: [Quotes, Scope & Contracting](./05.02-quotes-scope-contracting-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Confidentiality as a first-class engagement term: an NDA that gates access to the buyer's unreleased material before a seller can hear it, and the anonymity regime that makes ghost production — a large, real, entirely normal part of the music industry — transactable on a platform whose thesis is that credits should be public.

This feature is where WeJammin's own thesis meets its hardest internal contradiction, and it is better to name that than to design around it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Signs an NDA to hear an unreleased track before quoting on it. As a buyer, requires one before sending an artist's unreleased album to a stranger. As a ghost writer/player, elects an anonymity level — and must be able to see, clearly, what they are trading away: the credit is the asset this platform exists to accumulate for them (`meta/personas.md`: "one identity that carries verified proof of work"), and anonymity spends it. |
| Producer | Full | The persona at the centre of this. Ghost production is a Producer market — writing and producing under someone else's name is how a large share of commercial music is made and how many producers earn. They are also the ones holding other artists' unreleased material, so they are the heaviest NDA *requirer* as well as signer. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An Operator never receives creative material through this domain — an on-location engagement reaches them as a room booking (domain 16). Note: a studio's own confidentiality obligations around who was in the building are real, but they belong to 16, not here. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and structurally must remain so. The Fan lens is where a leak would do commercial damage; unreleased material must never be reachable from any Fan-facing surface. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two related but separable things:

**Confidentiality (NDA)** — a gate. A buyer marks a job confidential; the seller must sign before material is revealed. This sits *upstream of the quote*, because a seller cannot price a mix they are not allowed to hear. The signed instrument comes from the Contracts & E-Signature cross-cut; what is domain-owned is the gating of material access on signature, and the fact that the gate applies to previews, stems and messages alike.

**Ghost-production anonymity** — a spectrum, not a switch. The candidate list treated this as one thing; it is at least four:

| Level | Public credit | Private record | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | Named publicly | Full | Normal engagement |
| Delayed | Named after an embargo (release date + N) | Full | Unreleased material, standard |
| Aliased | Named under a pseudonym | Full, alias↔identity known to platform | Producers with a second identity |
| Sealed | Not displayed at all | **Held, not deleted** | True ghost production |

### Happy Path

1. Buyer marks the job confidential when requesting a quote (05.02.01)
2. Seller sees the job's shape (craft, scale, budget band, deadline) but not the material or the artist
3. Seller signs the NDA via the Contracts cross-cut
4. Material unlocks; seller can hear it and quote against it
5. Anonymity level is elected **on the quote** as a priced term — sealed work commonly costs more, because the seller is selling their name's absence
6. Engagement proceeds; at acceptance the credit is emitted to domain 02 at the elected visibility (see DT-01) — it is recorded, not discarded
7. On embargo expiry (Delayed), the credit surfaces automatically

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller declines to sign | No material, no quote | "This job needs an NDA before you can hear it." |
| Seller signs, hears it, declines the job | NDA survives the engagement not happening — this is the normal and important case | Obligations remain; material access revoked |
| Seller leaks the material | Contractual breach the platform can evidence (audit log shows who had access when) | Dispute path (24); the evidence is the feature's real value |
| Sealed credit + counter-attestation (02) | A sealed credit still needs the people in the room to confirm it, and they are the leak surface | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — how attestation works without revealing the attestor is unsolved; see Q-02 |
| Ghost seller later disputes the deal | Their evidence is a credit they agreed to hide | The sealed record is what makes their claim provable — which is the argument for holding it (DT-01) |
| Delayed credit's embargo never ends (track shelved) | Credit stays hidden indefinitely | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — is there a backstop date? see Q-03 |
| Sealed work generates royalties (05.06.02 points) | A royalty payment to an uncredited party is visible in the money trail even if the credit is hidden | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — anonymity leaks through 10 |
| NDA and rights posture conflict | A sealed WFH engagement and a sealed co-ownership engagement have very different disclosure consequences downstream (a co-owner appears on the split) | Blocked combination or explicit warning — see Q-01 |
| Buyer requests confidentiality *after* material sent | The gate has already failed | "That material is already out. An NDA now covers future use, not what's been seen." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Ghost production requires the credit graph to record a *suppressed* credit, not *no* credit — otherwise the platform's confidentiality feature defeats its own provenance wedge** | Adversarial: what breaks at the intersection with another domain? Trace this feature into domain 02 and D-18. | ✅ CONFIRMED, and this is the most important reasoning in the feature. If a sealed engagement emits nothing, then the platform's core asset — the verified credit graph — has a hole exactly where the most commercially significant work sits, and the hole grows with the platform's success in the professional market. Worse, the ghost seller loses the one thing D-18 promises them ("the reason they can't leave"), so the feature that wins the ghost market destroys the retention mechanism for its best users. The resolution: **record the credit, suppress the display**. The fact is captured at the moment it is true (which is the entire thesis); the visibility is a separate, mutable, contractual property. A sealed credit can later be unsealed by agreement, cited privately in a dispute, counted (anonymously) in the seller's own record, and used to band benchmarks (05.01.04) without ever being displayed. This turns a contradiction into a data-model decision. It also creates a real obligation the owner must accept: **the platform is now holding secrets** — see Q-01 in the sub-domain index. |
| DT-02 | **The NDA gate belongs before the quote, not before delivery** | Domain expertise: a mix engineer cannot price an album they have not heard. Every marketplace that puts confidentiality at the delivery stage has misread where the exposure is. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the exposure is at *evaluation*, when the buyer must hand unreleased material to a stranger who may not even take the job (and, per the edge cases, usually the NDA's most important moment is exactly that: the seller who heard it and walked away). This is why the feature lives in this sub-domain rather than with delivery (05.04), and it is D-02 of the sub-domain index. |
| DT-03 | Anonymity is a boolean — ghost or not ghost | The candidate list's framing ("Ghost-Production Anonymity" as one thing) | ❌ REJECTED — at least four distinct levels exist and they have materially different downstream behaviour. **Delayed** (embargo until release) is by far the most common real case and is not ghost production at all — it is ordinary confidentiality with a timer, and treating it as ghosting would seal credits that everyone intends to publish in six weeks. **Aliased** is a producer's second identity, which the platform can hold coherently (alias↔identity known internally) and which interacts with domain 01's multi-role identity model. Only **Sealed** is true ghost production. Collapsing these to a boolean would either over-seal (destroying credits nobody wanted hidden) or under-serve (no home for the genuine ghost market). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Sealed engagements **record** the credit and suppress its display; they do not discard it | See DT-01. Preserves the thesis; makes anonymity a visibility property rather than a hole in the graph. Requires owner ratification (sub-domain Q-01) because it means the platform holds secrets. |
| D-02 | Anonymity is a four-level spectrum (Open / Delayed / Aliased / Sealed), not a boolean | See DT-03. Delayed is the common case and must not be conflated with ghosting. |
| D-03 | The NDA gate sits upstream of the quote | See DT-02. |
| D-04 | Anonymity level is a **priced term on the quote** | A seller selling their name's absence is selling something. Making it a priced term also forces it to be explicit and negotiated rather than assumed. |
