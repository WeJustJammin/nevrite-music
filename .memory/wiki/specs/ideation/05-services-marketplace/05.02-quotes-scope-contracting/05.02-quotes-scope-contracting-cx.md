## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01 / CX-02: NDA ↔ Quotes

**Relationship**: Confidentiality touches the quote twice, at opposite ends. Going *in*, the NDA is a **gate**: material is locked until signature, and until material is audible there is no quote, because a seller cannot price what they have not heard. Coming *out*, anonymity is a **term**: the level (Open / Delayed / Aliased / Sealed) is elected on the quote and priced there.

The second is the less obvious one and the more important. If anonymity were a setting on a profile or a checkbox at delivery, it would be a default nobody negotiated — and given 05.02.02 DT-01 (a sealed credit is a suppressed credit, not an absent one), that default would silently determine whether the platform's core asset gets populated. Making it a priced quote term forces both parties to see it and agree it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: signs to hear; elects a level that trades their credit — the asset D-18 says is the reason they cannot leave — for money. The UI obligation here is to make that trade legible, not frictionless.
- **Producer**: on both sides. Requires NDAs over the artists' material they hold, and elects sealing on the ghost work they sell.
- **Operator**: no exposure. Never receives creative material through this domain.
- **Fan**: no exposure, and must structurally never have any — unreleased material must be unreachable from every Fan-facing surface.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The quote owns the anonymity term; the NDA instrument owns the confidentiality obligation. They are separate objects with separate lifetimes — critically, the NDA **survives** the quote being declined (05.02.02 edge case), which is the case that matters most and the one a naive "NDA belongs to the engagement" model would lose.
2. **Trigger chain**: Signature → material unlock → quote possible. If signature fails or is refused, no quote exists; nothing to roll back. Acceptance → credit emitted to 02 at the elected visibility. If that emission fails, the engagement is paid and the credit is missing — the exact hole DT-01 exists to prevent. It must be part of the atomic acceptance transaction (see 05.04/05.06 CX).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, strongly. NDA signature is what grants material access; the anonymity level then governs who may see the resulting credit. One consent gates the input, another gates the output, and they are set at the same moment on the same document.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An unsigned NDA blocking a quote must notify the seller (the job looks like it is stalled on them when it is stalled on a signature). Embargo expiry (Delayed) must notify the seller that their credit is now public — some will have forgotten they agreed to it.
5. **State transition conflict**: A buyer marking a job confidential *after* material has been sent is a race the system loses (05.02.02 edge case) — the gate can only govern future access. The honest response is to say so rather than imply retroactive protection.

### CX-03: Union ↔ Quotes

**Relationship**: Scale is the only floor in the domain that overrides the seller's declared rate. Everywhere else the seller's price is the seller's decision (05.01.04 D-01 explicitly refuses to block a low price); here the platform blocks issue below the floor. The asymmetry is deliberate: an underpriced non-union mix harms only the seller who chose it, whereas a below-scale union session is a breach against a third party the seller cannot consent away.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: protected. The floor and the derived fees (doubling, cartage, overtime) are money they routinely fail to collect.
- **Producer**: elects union status — the consequential act, because they are usually the party who knows.
- **Operator / Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Scale tables are reference data owned by an external body. The platform holds a copy, and a stale copy quotes a wrong floor that will be trusted — 05.02.03 Q-01, the feature's central risk.
2. **Trigger chain**: Union election → floor applied at render → below-scale issue blocked. Downstream, acceptance → session data emitted to domain 02 for B-form reporting. If that emission fails, a union session goes unreported, which is a compliance failure, not merely a missing record.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Producer's Config access to the union election changes what the Musician's quote may price at. A permission in one lens sets a floor in another.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A blocked issue must tell the seller *why* (which scale, which session type), or it reads as an arbitrary platform refusal.
5. **State transition conflict**: A session's type can change after the fact — a demo becomes a master — which retroactively changes the applicable scale and triggers new-use obligations years later (05.02.03 DT-03). This domain's engagement lifecycle cannot hold that; it is escalated to 09/10/11.
