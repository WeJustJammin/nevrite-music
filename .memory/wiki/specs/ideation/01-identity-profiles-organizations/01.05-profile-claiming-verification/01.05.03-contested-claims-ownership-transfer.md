# Feature: Contested Claims & Ownership Transfer

> **Number**: 01.05.03
> **Parent**: [Profile Claiming & Ownership Verification](./01.05-profile-claiming-verification-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What happens when two people want the same party, and how a party legitimately changes hands. Contested claims are inevitable — a venue with two managers, a band name two ex-members both want, a shadow party claimed by the wrong person — and ownership transfer is the lawful version of the same operation: a venue is sold, a page is handed to whoever now runs it. Both come down to one question the platform must answer without being in the room: **which of these people is telling the truth?** The platform's honest posture is that it records and evaluates evidence — it does not adjudicate business relationships, and where it cannot decide it freezes rather than guesses.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | The most common contestant. Venue sold, manager left, two people with a legitimate story about the same page. Also initiates lawful transfers — handing the page to the new owner is their exit. Their evidence is business-shaped: incorporation, a lease, a licence. |
| Musician | Full | Contests a claim on a party holding their credits — someone claiming to be them. Also a contestant in the band-name case, which is 01.04.02's dispute arriving here without an agreement to settle it. |
| Producer | Full | Contests as a party, but more often participates as a **witness** — their attestation is the evidence that resolves a person-claim contest, since they were in the room (`personas.md`: trust broker). |
| Fan | None | Not a participant. A fan has nothing to claim and no standing to contest. If a Fan contests something, they are asserting a professional identity and are Musician-shaped by that act. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A contest arises when a second claim lands on a party that is claimed or provisionally claimed. Both claims stay live; **the incumbent does not win by default and the challenger does not displace by default**. Evidence is gathered from each side — the same type-specific methods as 01.05.02, plus counter-attestation — and a decision is made.

Who decides is unresolved (parent Q-03). Where the platform cannot decide, the honest outcome is a **frozen party**: nobody gets control, the record stays readable, and the dispute goes to 24 or to lawyers. A frozen party is a bad outcome and a truthful one; guessing would be worse.

**Transfer** is the consensual path: the current owner hands the party over. Consent makes it cheap, and it should always be easier than contesting — otherwise the lawful route is the hard one.

Every ownership change has a **reversibility window** during which it can be undone. This is what lets 01.05.02 D-02 offer weak-but-fast proof: mistakes are recoverable rather than final.

**Contesting is not free of friction, but it is not paywalled.** The legitimately wronged must never be priced out of reclaiming their own identity, so the first contest against a given target carries no monetary cost. Its cost is instead reputational and rate-limited: an open contest is visible on the contestant's own record, and one contestant may hold at most **one open contest per target at a time** and open at most **3 contests against the same target per rolling 90 days** before further attempts are gated to human review (24). This stops harassment-by-repetition without taxing the first honest claim (proposed defaults — Q-02).

**A non-responding incumbent cannot deadlock the party forever.** Silence is not consent, but indefinite deadlock rewards a squatter. If the incumbent does not respond within **14 days** of both notifications (a proposed default — Q-05), the contest proceeds to decision on the challenger's evidence alone — and "strong" versus "weak" is defined by the **01.05.02 proof tiers**, not by adjudicator judgement (which is itself unresolved — Q-01):

- **Strong** = challenger evidence that would grant **full control** on its own under 01.05.02: a **Tier A** independent control proof, or **Tier B/C at full threshold** (≥2 independent attesters / confirmations). Strong evidence **resolves in the challenger's favour with a reversibility window** (sized by entity type — Q-03).
- **Weak** = challenger evidence that reaches only **provisional threshold** under 01.05.02 (Tier B/C on a single designating attester or single confirmation), plus registry-only corroboration (01.05.02 D-12) or no evidence at all. Weak or absent evidence **still freezes the party** rather than handing it to whoever showed up second.

This maps the timeout resolution directly onto the same tier thresholds a claim would face, so no adjudicator is required to draw the boundary; only a genuinely undecidable *contested* case (both sides present, both credible) waits on the Q-01 adjudicator.

### Happy Path (transfer)

1. Operator sells the venue. New owner needs the page and its five years of show history.
2. Current owner initiates a transfer to the new owner's account.
3. New owner accepts. Both sides consented, so no proof is needed — consent *is* the proof.
4. Ownership moves. History, reviews, bookings and followers stay with the party (01.02.03 D-03); credits earned by the previous owner do **not** (D-05).
5. Public page carries a "recently changed hands" marker for **30 days** (proposed default — Q-06), so an in-flight booker sees the handover happened; after that the change lives only in the immutable audit trail.

### Happy Path (contest)

1. Second claim lands on a claimed venue.
2. Both parties are notified. Neither loses access during the contest — an incumbent stripped on an accusation is a denial-of-service surface.
3. Each supplies evidence within the response window; a non-responding incumbent is handled by the 14-day timeout, not by default forfeiture.
4. Decision made per Q-03. Reversibility window opens, sized by entity type (Q-03).
5. Loser is told why, and what would change the outcome. A silent loss is how people leave forever.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Both claimants have legitimate evidence (two managers, both with domain email) | Platform cannot decide. Party freezes; both are told plainly that we cannot arbitrate their business relationship | "We can't determine who controls this page. Neither of you has access until you resolve it." Route to 24 |
| Incumbent stops responding | 14-day response timeout (proposed default — Q-05). Contest then decided on challenger evidence alone by the **01.05.02 tier test**: evidence at 01.05.02 full-control threshold (Tier A, or Tier B/C with ≥2 independent attesters) wins with a reversibility window; provisional-threshold or registry-only or absent evidence still freezes rather than forfeits | Incumbent: "You have 14 days to respond or this contest proceeds without you." Challenger: countdown + what evidence is still wanted |
| Contest over a party with live obligations (bookings, unsettled money) | Obligations do **not** freeze with ownership. The incumbent retains **operate-only** rights (run committed bookings, message counterparties) but loses ownership rights (transfer, payout redirection, deletion) for the freeze duration; pending payouts held in escrow; no *new* obligations may be created against a disputed party | Incumbent: "Disputed — you can run existing bookings; payouts are held until this resolves." Deeper settlement mechanics: `[PENDING — /write-be-spec, with domain 17]` |
| Simultaneous transfer and contest (owner tries to hand off a party while a contest is open) | Transfer is blocked while any contest is open — you cannot give away what is disputed. Transfer initiated *before* a contest lands still enters its own reversibility window and can be unwound by the contest outcome | "This page has an open dispute and can't be transferred until it's resolved." |
| Malicious contest as harassment (repeatedly contesting a rival's page) | Contest is rate-limited (1 open per target, 3 per 90 days) and reputationally visible; over-threshold attempts gate to 24 | "You've reached the contest limit for this page." See Q-02 |
| Transfer to a bad actor by a compromised account | Reversibility window is the defence; recipient cannot immediately re-transfer or drain payouts during the window | Window active; original owner can unwind; route to 24 |
| Band name contested at dissolution with no recorded agreement | 01.04.02's failure arriving here. This feature cannot fix an agreement that was never made | Freeze; route to 24 and to lawyers |
| Person-claim contest: two people say they are the same session player | Counter-attestation decides — the attesters knew which one was in the room | Attesters asked; strongest available evidence |
| Transfer of a party holding credits earned by the previous owner | **Must not happen.** Credits belong to the party that earned them; transferring a page must never transfer a career | Credits are not transferable with the page; this is a hard invariant |
| Loser attempts to immediately re-contest after losing | New contest on the same target inside the same 90-day window counts against the rate limit; a resolved contest with no new evidence is declined at intake | "This was already decided. New evidence is required to reopen." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The incumbent should win by default | The safe-looking default — it protects against hostile takeovers | ❌ REJECTED — it protects the wrong person exactly when it matters. The most common real contest is a *squatter* incumbent: someone created the venue page, or claimed it with weak proof, and the actual operator arrives later. Incumbent-wins makes the platform's own weak-proof policy (01.05.02 D-02) into a land-grab machine. Both claims must be evaluated on evidence. |
| DT-02 | The platform must resolve every contest | The instinct that a platform owes its users an answer | ❌ REJECTED — some contests are business disputes between two people with equally good stories, and the platform was not in the room. Guessing produces a confident wrong answer with a real victim and real liability. A frozen party is a bad outcome that is *honestly* bad, and it is the same posture as 01.04.04 D-02 (execute nothing where nothing was recorded). Consistency matters: the platform records, it does not adjudicate. |
| DT-03 | Reversibility is what makes fast, weak proof safe — the two decisions are one decision | Reasoning about the tension between 01.05.02 D-02 (rank by speed) and the resulting error rate | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it reframes the sub-domain. 01.05.02 optimises the funnel with weak proof; this feature makes weak proof affordable by making its errors recoverable. Neither is defensible alone: weak proof without reversibility is a land-grab; strong proof without reversibility is a funnel that kills the Operator conversion the domain overview names as core. They must be specified together, which is the clearest evidence that these three features belong in one sub-domain. |
| DT-04 | Freezing a party must also freeze everything it owns (bookings, payouts) | The clean, simple invariant: a disputed thing is fully inert | ❌ REJECTED — the strongest argument against freezing-as-default is exactly this. A venue with tomorrow's sold-out show cannot go dark because two people argue over the page; the fans and the booked band are innocent third parties. So *ownership* freezes while *operation* continues under the incumbent as a neutral custodian (operate-only, no payout redirection, no new obligations, escrowed money). This preserves the "platform doesn't adjudicate" posture without punishing bystanders — the freeze is on control, not on the show. |
| DT-05 | Making contests cost money is the clean fix for harassment | Priced contests self-limit; spammers stop when it costs them | ❌ REJECTED for the first contest — pricing the reclaim of your own stolen identity taxes the victim and rewards the party with deeper pockets (a label vs an individual). The harassment vector is *repetition*, not the first attempt, so the control is rate-limiting + reputational visibility + escalation to 24, not a paywall. Pricing may still gate abusive *repeat* contesting (Q-02), but never the first honest claim. |
