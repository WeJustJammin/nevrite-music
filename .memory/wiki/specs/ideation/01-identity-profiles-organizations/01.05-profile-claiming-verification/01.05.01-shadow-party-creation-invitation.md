# Feature: Shadow Party Creation & Invitation

> **Number**: 01.05.01
> **Parent**: [Profile Claiming & Ownership Verification](./01.05-profile-claiming-verification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The ability to name a party that has no account yet — the horn player who tracked on Tuesday and has never heard of WeJammin, the venue that exists in the world but not in our database, the bandmate who is not signing up today. A shadow party is a real party record with no owner: it can be credited, invited, booked and referenced, and later claimed by the human it describes (01.05.02).

> **Deep Think addition** — no candidate in the sweep's list covers this. It surfaced by asking what actually happens at the Producer's capture moment when half the room has no account.

**Why this is the most load-bearing feature in the domain**: `problem-statement.md` locates the root cause as "absence at the point of truth", and D-18 makes provenance the wedge. The capture moment is a session with five people in it, two of whom are users. If capture requires everyone to sign up first, capture does not happen — and `personas.md` names the exact failure it enables: the Producer who "closes a project without capturing splits ('we'll do it later')". Shadow parties are what make the lazy path the correct path.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Producer | Full | **The primary user.** At the session, credits the horn section by typing three names — no signup wall, no "invite them first", no leaving the flow. Each becomes a shadow party holding a real credit. Invitations go out; the Producer's job is done in the room, which is the only place it was ever going to get done. |
| Musician | Full | Credits collaborators the same way. Also creates shadow parties by naming a band's other members (01.03.01) or a venue that is not listed. Sees which of their collaborators are shadow vs claimed — a shadow collaborator's credit is real but unconfirmed. |
| Operator | Full | Names their in-house engineer on a show, or a support act that has no page. Their most common case is the inverse: **they are the shadow party**, seeded or named by a band, and their entry point to the platform is the invitation to claim it. |
| Fan | None | Cannot create shadow parties. Zero legitimate use, and an unauthenticated party-creation surface handed to the platform's largest population is a spam and impersonation engine. Explicit and permanent absence. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A shadow party is created by *reference* — as a side effect of naming someone in a flow that needed them (a credit, an invitation, a booking, a lineup), never as a standalone "create a person" action. It carries: a name, whatever context the creating flow supplied (instrument, role, the session it came from), the identity of its creator, and no owner.

A shadow party is a real party: it can hold credits, appear in a lineup, be booked. What it cannot do is *act* — no login, no authority, no mandate. It is a subject, not an agent.

Every shadow party carries an invitation to claim it (01.05.02). The invitation is the growth loop: the horn player gets told they have three credits waiting, which is a far better arrival than an empty signup form.

The record of **who created it** is the accountability mechanism that makes an unauthenticated creation surface survivable.

### Happy Path

1. Producer finishes a session. Five people played; two have accounts.
2. In the credit flow (domain 02) they type the other three names, with instruments.
3. System creates three shadow parties, each holding a real credit from a named attester, and asks for a contact route (email/phone) — optional, because the Producer may not have it.
4. Invitations go where they can. The credits exist regardless — **capture is not conditional on delivery**.
5. The horn player gets an email: "Sam credited you on 3 tracks. Claim your profile." They arrive with a career already in it, not an empty form.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No contact route (Producer only knows the name) | Shadow party created anyway, uninvited. It waits. The person may find it later through search, or another collaborator may supply the route | Credit exists, unconfirmed, discoverable |
| Shadow party is created for someone who already has an account | Duplicate detection at reference time — the same problem as 01.02.02, at a moment when the user is mid-flow and will not tolerate friction | Suggests existing users first, as-you-type; creating a shadow is the fallback, not the default |
| Impersonation: someone creates a shadow party for a famous musician and credits them | Real risk. Mitigated by creator attribution (every shadow party knows who made it) and by the credit graph's counter-attestation (domain 02) — an uncorroborated claim by an unknown party is visibly worth nothing | Credit shows as unconfirmed, attributed to its creator; routes to 24 |
| Spam: bulk shadow party creation | Rate limiting and creator accountability. Fans cannot do it at all (Role Lens), which removes the largest population | See Q-01 |
| Two Producers each create a shadow for the same person | Two shadows, one human. Detection catches some; the rest is the Canonical Data merge cross-cut's problem — and the credits must **redirect**, not be rewritten (domain CX-02) | Merge on claim |
| Person is credited but does not want to be (a session they would rather forget; a legal-name exposure) | They can claim and then dispute, or dispute without claiming. Both routes must exist — requiring them to join in order to object is coercive | Dispute route via 24 |
| Shadow party is never claimed — the majority outcome | It persists indefinitely, holding real credits. This is fine and expected: the record's value does not depend on the person showing up | Credit remains, unconfirmed |
| Someone claims a shadow party that is not them | 01.05.02's proof problem. Note the asymmetry: a *person* shadow is far harder to prove than a *venue* shadow — there is no "email at your own domain" for a drummer | See 01.05.02 Q-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Party fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Shadow created with a name only | A minimal page: name + the credit(s) that caused it. Visibly unclaimed |
| Populated | Shadow accumulates credits from several attesters | A profile the person did not build, waiting for them — the strongest possible claim incentive (see domain CX-04) |
| Error | Creation failed | Error with retry. **Never silently drops the credit** — losing a capture at the capture moment is the one unrecoverable failure |
| Partial | Invited, not claimed | Public page marked unclaimed; credits shown as unconfirmed by the subject |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Contributors must have accounts to be credited | The obvious model, and what every credits platform does — it is why Jaxsta/Muso.AI/Sound Credit are databases of the people who signed up rather than of the people who played | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the sweep's most consequential omission. `personas.md` gives the Producer's worst-accidental behaviour as closing a project without capturing splits, and adds: "**The design must make the lazy path the correct path.**" A signup wall at the capture moment makes the lazy path the *wrong* path, and the lazy path always wins. Shadow parties are not a convenience; the thesis does not function without them. |
| DT-02 | Unauthenticated party creation is too dangerous to allow | The security instinct — it is an impersonation and spam surface handed to anyone | ⏳ SPLIT — the danger is real and the feature is mandatory, so the answer is not to block it but to make it *accountable and worthless to abuse*. Three mechanisms: creator attribution on every shadow party; credits from an uncorroborated attester carry no weight in domain 02; Fans (the biggest population) cannot do it at all. Abuse becomes possible but pointless — which is the only stable place to land. |
| DT-03 | The unclaimed profile full of other people's attestations is the claim incentive, and it inverts the cold-start problem | Reasoning about why anyone would claim: `personas.md` says the Musician's switching trigger is "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the domain's growth engine. Every other platform asks a new user to build a profile from nothing. This one has a profile already built — by other people, with facts they did not have to assert. The Producer's capture (the wedge) *is* the acquisition mechanism for their collaborators. Wedge and growth loop are the same event, which is what makes D-18's causal pairing operative rather than rhetorical. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Contributors do not need accounts to be credited | DT-01 — a signup wall at the capture moment defeats the thesis |
| D-02 | Shadow parties are created by reference, never as a standalone action | Creation-as-a-side-effect keeps the Producer in the flow, and gives every shadow a reason to exist |
| D-03 | Every shadow party records its creator | DT-02 — accountability is what makes an unauthenticated surface survivable |
| D-04 | Capture is not conditional on invitation delivery | The credit is the artifact; the invitation is a bonus. Coupling them would lose captures over missing email addresses |
| D-05 | A shadow party is a subject, not an agent — it can be credited, never act | Without this, an unauthenticated creation surface would be an authority surface |
