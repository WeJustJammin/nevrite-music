# Feature: Shadow Party Creation & Invitation

> **Number**: 01.05.01
> **Parent**: [Profile Claiming & Ownership Verification](./01.05-profile-claiming-verification-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The ability to name a party that has no account yet — the horn player who tracked on Tuesday and has never heard of WeJammin, the venue that exists in the world but not in our database, the bandmate who is not signing up today. A shadow party is a real party record with no owner: it can be credited, invited, booked and referenced, and later claimed by the human it describes (01.05.02).

> **Deep Think addition** — no candidate in the sweep's list covers this. It surfaced by asking what actually happens at the Producer's capture moment when half the room has no account.

**Why this is the most load-bearing feature in the domain**: `problem-statement.md` locates the root cause as "absence at the point of truth", and the ratified thesis (ideation-index GD-18) makes provenance the wedge. The capture moment is a session with five people in it, two of whom are users. If capture requires everyone to sign up first, capture does not happen — and `personas.md` names the exact failure it enables: the Producer who "closes a project without capturing splits ('we'll do it later')". Shadow parties are what make the lazy path the correct path.

**The shape of the design, in one line**: creation is never limited, because a lost capture is unrecoverable; the *effects* of creation — invitation dispatch, public visibility, evidentiary weight — are all limited, because those are the only things abuse wants (D-06). This resolves parent Q-01's paradox, which held that unlimited creation was simultaneously thesis-optimal and abuse-optimal: it is thesis-optimal for the *record* and abuse-optimal for the *effects*, and those are separable.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Producer | Full | **The archetypal creator.** At the session, credits the horn section by typing three names — no signup wall, no "invite them first", no leaving the flow. Each becomes a shadow party holding a real credit. Invitations go out; the Producer's job is done in the room, which is the only place it was ever going to get done. Because `personas.md` names them a trust broker, they reach Tier 1 dispatch (D-07) fastest — the first unrelated counter-confirmation of any credit they filed promotes them. Sees, per shadow they created: whether it was matched to an existing party, whether it was claimed, and whether their invitation was dispatched or queued. |
| Musician | Full | Creates shadows in three distinct capacities, not one. (1) **As a collaborator** — credits the people they played with, same flow as the Producer. (2) **As a fixer/contractor** — on a union or orchestral date the person who books the players is a Musician, not a Producer, and they are the only one in the building who knows all seventy names. This is why Musician is Full and not Read-only: the highest-volume legitimate creation event in recorded music is run by this persona. (3) **As a band member** — naming the band's other members (01.03.01) or a venue that is not listed. Sees which of their own collaborators are shadow vs claimed; a shadow collaborator's credit is real but uncorroborated. |
| Operator | Full | Names their in-house engineer on a show, or a support act that has no page, or the sound tech on a lineup (17). Their most common relationship to this feature is the **inverse**: they are the shadow party — seeded or named by a band — and the invitation to claim it is their entry point to the platform. An Operator therefore experiences this feature first as a recipient and only later as a creator. |
| Fan | None | Cannot create shadow parties, cannot be named as one by anyone else's flow, and never sees the creation surface. Zero legitimate use: every flow that creates a shadow (credit, membership, lineup, booking) is a flow a Fan has no access to. The exclusion is also the single largest piece of abuse mitigation in the feature — an unauthenticated party-creation surface handed to the platform's largest population by orders of magnitude (`personas.md`) would be a spam and impersonation engine that no rate limit could contain. Explicit and permanent absence. If a Fan needs to name a collaborator, they are asserting a professional identity and are Musician-shaped by that act (01.01.01). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A shadow party is created by *reference* — as a side effect of naming someone in a flow that needed them (a credit, an invitation, a booking, a lineup), never as a standalone "create a person" action (D-02). It carries: a name, whatever context the creating flow supplied, the identity of its creator, and no owner.

A shadow party is a real party: it can hold credits, appear in a lineup, be booked. What it cannot do is *act* — no login, no authority, no mandate. It is a subject, not an agent (D-05).

The record of **who created it** is the accountability mechanism that makes an unauthenticated creation surface survivable (D-03), and it is the evidence a later contest reads (CX-03).

### What a shadow party is made of

Every shadow party carries exactly these, and nothing else (D-09 — the no-free-text rule):

| Element | Required? | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Yes | 1–120 characters, counted and compared after the name is normalized so visually-identical spellings and compatibility forms collapse to one canonical form. No URLs, no markup, no line breaks. Leading/trailing whitespace stripped, internal runs collapsed to one space. Mixed-script strings (e.g. Cyrillic `А` inside a Latin word) are created but flagged (D-10). |
| **Creator** | Yes | The authenticated party that named it. Immutable. Never hidden from a viewer of the shadow page. |
| **Creating flow + context** | Yes | Which flow created it (credit / membership / lineup / booking) and that flow's structured context: instrument or role, the work or session it attaches to, the date, and the other parties named in the same submission. This is not decoration — it is the only thing that disambiguates a name (DT-06) and the only input duplicate detection has. |
| **Contact route** | No | Email or phone, supplied by the creator, **unverified by definition** — a third party typed it. Never treated as proof of anything (D-04, DT-04). |
| **Identifier** | No | A PRO/union/registry identifier (IPI, ISNI, IPN, AFM local + card number, MU number) if the creating flow had one. A shadow created with an identifier is materially stronger than one created with a name (DT-07). Resolution is 01.09's; carrying it at creation is this feature's. |

There is no bio, no free-text description, no photo, no links, and no creator-supplied prose of any kind on a shadow party. This is deliberate and structural: it is what makes a shadow party page worthless as a spam or SEO artifact, and it is a stronger defence than any rate limit (D-09, DT-08). The subject fills all of that in *after* they claim — which is also the correct answer, since it is their page.

### What "the name" actually is

A shadow party's name is **not a legal identity and not a person** — it is a string one attester associates with one collaborator. The Producer knows "Skoop", not "Kwame Mensah". Real credit sheets are full of nicknames, first-names-only, and instrument-shorthand. Per D-05 (aliases are first-class objects), the name on a shadow party is **alias-shaped**: on claim it becomes an alias of a person (01.01.02), and the person it resolves to may already exist under a different name (DT-10). This is why a shadow party is never "a new user record" and why claiming is sometimes a merge rather than a conversion.

### Creation is unlimited; the effects are tiered

**Creation always succeeds** (D-06). No rate limit, no quota, no reputation gate, and no per-submission cap ever causes a capture to fail. A single submission may name seventy parties, because an orchestral date really does have seventy players and a per-submission cap would break the single most valuable capture on the platform (DT-05).

What *is* tiered is the creator's **dispatch budget** and the shadow's **visibility**, both keyed to the creator's standing in the credit graph (domain 02's counter-attestation is the input; this feature only reads it):

| Creator tier | Definition | Invitation dispatch | Shadow page visibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0** | No counter-confirmed credit from an unrelated attester. A new account is indistinguishable from a spammer at creation time. | 10 invitations per rolling 24h dispatched on submission. Beyond 10: shadows are created, invitations are **queued**, not dropped. | Excluded from platform search and from suggestion surfaces. Reachable by direct link only. |
| **Tier 1** | ≥1 counter-confirmed credit from an unrelated attester. | 100 invitations per rolling 24h. | Indexable and searchable on creation. |

Queued invitations release automatically on the creator's first unrelated counter-confirmation, or on review. A Tier 0 shadow is also promoted to visible independently of its creator once it holds credits from **≥2 unrelated attesters** — corroboration promotes the record even when the creator has no standing.

The 10 / 100 / 2-attester thresholds are agent-set defaults (implementation decisions per `decision-classification.md`), stated here so an implementer needs no clarification. The owner may override them; the *structure* — unlimited creation, tiered effects — is the decision (D-06) and is not a threshold question.

**What the creator is never told**: that they are "rate limited". At the capture moment that reads as an accusation and invites abandonment, which is the failure the whole feature exists to prevent. They are told the truth, which is better: "Credits saved. Invitations are on their way."

### The invitation

Every shadow party with a contact route carries an invitation to claim it (01.05.02). The invitation is the growth loop: the horn player gets told they have three credits waiting, which is a far better arrival than an empty signup form (DT-03).

**The invitation link is not a claim credential** (D-11, DT-04). It authenticates the *link*, never the *person*. It grants exactly two things: read of the shadow page's public view, and entry to the claim flow — where 01.05.02's proof matrix still applies in full. This is non-negotiable: the contact route was typed by a third party who may have typo'd it, and an auto-claiming link would mean a Producer's slip hands a stranger someone else's career, bypassing every proof method 01.05.02 specifies.

**What the invitation discloses**: the attester's display name, the credit count, and the instrument/role. **Not the work titles.** The address is unverified, and unreleased-work confidentiality is a real and routine music-industry condition — embargoed releases, ghost production, NDA'd sessions. Telling an unverified inbox that Sam produced three unreleased tracks is a leak caused by a typo. The linked shadow page shows whatever domain 02 says is publicly visible, which for released work is everything and for embargoed work is nothing — so the incentive survives wherever it legitimately can.

**Dispatch schedule**: 3 attempts — day 0, day 3, day 14 — then stop. A credit from the *same* creator never re-opens dispatch. A credit from a **new, unrelated attester** re-opens exactly one further attempt, lifetime cap 6. The rationale is that "three different people now say you played on things" is a materially different and legitimate message, whereas one person repeatedly nudging is spam.

**"Not you?"** appears in every invitation: one click, no login, unbinds the contact route from the shadow party and stops all further dispatch. It does **not** delete the credit (D-08 — the record of work is never destroyed by any lifecycle event), and it does not assert the shadow is wrong; it asserts the *route* is wrong, which is a different and more likely fact. It is also a legal necessity for unsolicited contact, and it is a high-quality signal: a bounced route is noise, a human saying "not me" is evidence.

### Correction and retraction

A creator may **retract** a shadow party they created, within **24 hours**, if and only if all three hold: no other attester has credited it, no claim is in flight, and no other flow references it. This is not a violation of D-08 — a shadow created 30 seconds ago by a typo holds no record of work, and refusing to let a Producer fix `Jhon` in the room is how you teach them not to capture at all.

After 24 hours, or once any of the three conditions fails, the shadow is no longer the creator's to unmake. It is a record with other people's facts attached, and correcting it is a dispute (24), not a delete.

### Suppression without joining

A person can suppress a shadow party of themselves **without creating an account**, via the dispute route (24), by proving control of the contact route the invitation reached or by counter-attestation from the shadow's own attesters. Suppression removes the public page; the credit remains in the record, visible to its attester and to any dispute process (D-08). Requiring someone to join the platform in order to object to being on it is coercive, and it is exactly the behaviour a person credited under a name they no longer use is least able to tolerate.

### Happy Path

1. Producer finishes a session. Five people played; two have accounts.
2. In the credit flow (domain 02) they start typing the third name. As-you-type suggestions surface existing parties — users first, then shadows — matched on name plus the flow's context (instrument, session, co-attendees).
3. **Suggestions never block the submit control.** If the lookup has not returned within 400 ms, the Producer submits anyway; matching runs after the fact and, if it hits, routes to merge (Canonical Data cross-cut). A capture must never wait on a query.
4. None of the three are known. The Producer names them, with instruments, and submits.
5. System creates three shadow parties — each with a name, the Producer's identity as creator, the session context, and the instrument — each holding a real credit from a named attester. **Creation succeeds unconditionally.**
6. System asks for a contact route per shadow, marked optional and skippable in one action, because the Producer may not have it and asking twice loses the room.
7. The Producer has emails for two of the three. Invitations dispatch to those two (Producer is Tier 1 — their credits have been counter-confirmed before). The third shadow is created uninvited and waits.
8. **Capture is complete at step 5** — steps 6–7 are additive. The credits exist regardless of whether any invitation is ever delivered (D-04).
9. The horn player receives an email: "Sam Okoye credited you on 3 recordings — tenor sax." They open the shadow page and see three credits they did not have to assert, filed by someone who was in the room.
10. They hit "Is this you?" and enter 01.05.02's claim flow. The link got them here; it did not let them in.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No contact route (creator only knows the name) | Shadow created anyway, uninvited. It waits. The person may find it later through search, or another collaborator may supply the route | Credit exists, uncorroborated, discoverable (if visibility tier permits) |
| Creation request fails (network drop at the capture moment) | The one unrecoverable failure. The submission is retried up to 3 times on an exponential backoff — at 2 s, 10 s, and 30 s after the first failure (thresholds are agent-set defaults per `decision-classification.md`, overridable); on the third failure the capture is held on the device and re-offered on next open. **The credit is never silently dropped** | "Not saved yet — retrying." On hard failure: "Saved on this device. We'll file it when you're back." Never a bare error |
| Duplicate-suggestion lookup slow or unavailable | Submission proceeds. Matching is attempted asynchronously; a post-hoc match routes to merge, not to a block | Nothing. The Producer never learns the lookup failed — it is not their problem |
| Shadow created for someone who already has an account, name matches | As-you-type suggests existing users first. Creating a shadow is the fallback, never the default | Suggestions ranked users-first; "None of these" creates the shadow |
| Shadow created for an existing user under a name the platform does not know (an alias) | **The common case, not an edge case** (DT-10). The Producer credits "Skoop"; the account is "Kwame Mensah". No match is possible on a string the platform has never seen. A shadow is created for a party that already exists | Nothing at creation. Resolves at claim as an **alias merge** into the existing person (01.01.02), not a new account |
| Two Producers each create a shadow for the same person | Two shadows, one human. Detection catches some; the rest is the Canonical Data merge cross-cut's problem — and the credits must **redirect**, not be rewritten (domain CX-02) | Merge on claim, or on detection |
| Same creator names the same person twice in one session via two flows (credit, then lineup) | Deduped within the session context: naming "Skoop" in the credit flow and again in the same session's lineup resolves to **one** shadow. Across different sessions, no dedup — the platform cannot know they are the same "Skoop" | One party, two references |
| Double-submit / network retry (Producer taps "add" twice) | The same name, from the same creator, in the same submission resolves to one shadow, not two — a repeated tap or an automatic retry adds nothing | One entry |
| Orchestral date — 70 names in one submission | Accepted in full. No per-submission cap exists (DT-05). Dispatch budget still applies to the invitations, so a Tier 0 contractor's 70 shadows are all created and 10 invitations go immediately | "Credits saved. Invitations are on their way." |
| Tier 0 creator exceeds dispatch budget | Shadows created; invitations queued, never dropped. Released on first unrelated counter-confirmation or on review | Same message as any other capture. No accusation, no block |
| Bulk back-catalogue import by a new account (15 years of sessions) | Simultaneously the highest-value capture and the clearest spam signature. **Not this flow** — routes to a distinct import path with review. Ties to domain Q-05 (pre-platform career) | Routed; not silently rate-limited into uselessness |
| Impersonation: someone creates a shadow for a famous musician and credits them | Real risk. Mitigated by creator attribution (D-03) and by domain 02's counter-attestation — an uncorroborated claim by an unknown party is visibly worth nothing. Tier 0 keeps it out of search entirely | Credit shows as uncorroborated, attributed to its creator; routes to 24 |
| Homoglyph impersonation ("Аdele" with a Cyrillic А) | Mixed-script names are created (blocking them would break every legitimate non-Latin name in a global music market) but flagged for review and held at Tier 0 visibility regardless of creator tier | Created; not indexed; flagged |
| Name field used as a spam/SEO surface ("FREE BTC — click") | Structurally defeated rather than filtered: a shadow page has **no free-text surface** (D-09). URLs and markup are stripped from names; 120-char cap; the page renders a name plus structured credits and nothing else. There is nowhere to put spam | Name rejected at input if it contains a URL; everything else is not worth spamming |
| Producer typos the contact email — invitation reaches a stranger | The stranger gets a link that reads a public page and offers a claim flow. It grants **nothing** (D-11). They cannot claim without passing 01.05.02's proof, which they will fail | Stranger sees a public page and a "Not you?" link. One click unbinds the route |
| Invitation lands in a shared inbox (`info@venue.com`, three readers) | Same answer, and it is why the answer matters: first-clicker does not win, because clicking wins nothing. Proof decides | Whoever claims still proves |
| Invitation hard-bounces | Route unbound after 1 hard bounce (2 soft bounces across the 14-day schedule count as one hard). No further attempts. **Shadow and credit stand** | Creator sees "couldn't reach" against that shadow. Not an error — an invitation to supply a better route |
| Recipient clicks "Not you?" | Route unbound, dispatch stops, credit stands. Logged as evidence — it may mean the route was wrong, or that the shadow itself is misattributed. The platform does not guess which | "Thanks — we won't contact you again." No login, no account, no dark pattern |
| Person is credited under a deadname or a legal name they do not use | The name arrives from a third party who cannot know. The harm is live from creation, before the subject ever hears of us. On claim, renaming propagates to every credit display and the prior string is **not retained on any public surface**. Suppression without joining is available immediately (24). This is consistent with D-08: the *record of work* is preserved; the *string it was displayed under* is presentational, and a name is not a fact about the work | Rename on claim propagates everywhere; no "formerly known as" |
| Person is credited but does not want to be (a session they would rather forget) | They can claim and then dispute, or dispute without claiming. Both routes exist — requiring them to join in order to object is coercive | Dispute route via 24; suppression without an account |
| Person named is deceased | The platform has no liveness signal at creation — the credit is often *why* the record exists (posthumous and catalogue credits are routine). The shadow is created and stands. Once matched to a deceased person (01.10), invitation dispatch stops permanently and the estate route applies. Until then, an invitation may go to a dead person's inbox — a real and unpreventable outcome the "Not you?" route lets a family end in one click | Shadow stands; dispatch stops on match; estate route (01.10) |
| Person named is a minor (a child vocalist, a choir) | No age signal exists at creation, and the attester does not know. The shadow is created and stands — a child's credit is a real fact about the work. Age assurance applies at the **claim**, not at creation (Safeguarding cross-cut, D-13). The invitation itself carries no UGC and no social surface, which bounds the exposure but does not eliminate it | See Q-02 |
| All of a shadow's credits are confidential or embargoed (NDA'd session, ghost production, pre-release) | The shadow exists and holds the credits. It has **no public page at all** — a shadow page renders only what domain 02 says is public, and zero public credits means zero public page. The invitation still dispatches (attester + count + instrument leak nothing about the work) | Nothing public. Invitation says "Sam Okoye credited you on 2 recordings" and links to a page only the claimant will see content on |
| Creator retracts a shadow made in error (typo'd name), within 24h, no other attesters | Retracted. The capture is corrected in the room, which is the only place it will ever be corrected | Undo affordance on the submission |
| Creator tries to retract after a second attester corroborated, or after 24h, or with a claim in flight | Refused. It is no longer theirs to unmake — other people's facts are attached to it | "Someone else has credited this person too. Ask us to correct it." Routes to 24 |
| Shadow is merged (Canonical Data) while a claim on it is in flight | The claim follows the merge target; it is never dropped. Credits redirect, never rewrite (domain CX-02). See CX-03 — the merge-during-contest variant is the cross-cut's | Claim continues against the surviving party |
| Shadow is credited by a second attester while a claim is in flight | Safe and orthogonal — credits accrete to the party; the claim attaches an owner (CX-01). The second credit strengthens the claim rather than disturbing it | Nothing. The claim gets stronger |
| Creator's own party is suspended or contested while their shadows are live | The shadows stand — they hold other people's facts, and a creator's dispute is not the subject's problem. The creator's *tier* drops to 0 for the duration, so pending dispatch queues and new shadows go unindexed | Existing shadows unaffected; new ones quiet until resolved |
| Multi-hyphenate self-credit: a Producer credits themselves under their engineer alias while their account is their artist alias | A shadow of an existing user, created by that user. Permitted — the platform cannot tell it apart from the legitimate case, and D-05 says one human has many names. Sole-attester = zero corroboration weight (domain 02), so it buys nothing. On claim it is an alias merge into their own person | Nothing special. It is not an attack because it does not pay |
| Named party is a venue that already exists and is claimed | Suggestion surfaces it. If the creator insists it is a different room with the same name — which is true far more often than software expects — the shadow is created. The platform suggests; it never blocks (DT-06) | "Did you mean The Lexington, London N1?" · "No, a different one" |
| Person finds their own shadow by searching, no invitation ever sent | The intended fallback for the no-contact-route case, and it only works if the shadow is visible. A Tier 0 creator's shadow is unfindable until corroborated — the cost of the spam gate, paid by the honest new Producer's collaborator | "Is this you?" affordance on the public page (01.05.02 Empty state) |
| Shadow party is never claimed — the majority outcome | It persists indefinitely, holding real credits. This is fine and expected: the record's value does not depend on the person showing up | Credit remains, uncorroborated |
| Someone claims a shadow party that is not them | 01.05.02's proof problem. Note the asymmetry: a *person* shadow is far harder to prove than a *venue* shadow — there is no "email at your own domain" for a drummer | See 01.05.02 Q-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Party fetch in flight | Skeleton. Never blocks a capture in progress — a creation flow does not wait on a read |
| **Empty** | Shadow created with a name only, no credits yet public | A minimal page: name + the credit(s) that caused it + the creator's attribution. Visibly unclaimed |
| **Populated** | Shadow accumulates credits from several unrelated attesters | A profile the person did not build, waiting for them — the strongest possible claim incentive (domain CX-04). No free text, because they have not arrived to write any |
| **Error** | Creation failed | Error with retry, then local hold. **Never silently drops the credit** — losing a capture at the capture moment is the one unrecoverable failure |
| **Partial — invited, not claimed** | Invitation dispatched, no claim | Public page marked unclaimed; credits shown as unconfirmed by the subject |
| **Partial — created, dispatch queued** | Tier 0 creator over budget | Creator: "Credits saved. Invitations are on their way." Subject: nothing yet. The record is complete; only the outreach is pending |
| **Partial — created, not visible** | Tier 0 creator, <2 unrelated attesters, or a mixed-script flag | Direct link only; absent from search and suggestion surfaces. The record exists and is inert |
| **Partial — no public surface** | Every credit on the shadow is confidential or embargoed (domain 02) | Nothing public exists. The invitation still lands; the page has content only for a proven claimant |
| **Suppressed** | Subject suppressed it without joining (24) | Public page gone. Credit intact in the record, visible to its attester and to any dispute process (D-08) |

(1) **The model must be able to carry an identifier at creation**, not only a name, because a shadow created with an AFM local + card number or an IPI is categorically stronger evidence than one created from a nickname, and it makes 01.05.02's weakest case (the person claim) tractable. (2) It relocates the creator: the person filing that report is the **fixer/contractor — a Musician**, not a Producer. The Role Lens must give Musician full creation rights on that ground alone, and the seventy-name submission is not a hypothetical, which is what kills any per-submission cap (DT-05). | DT-08 | Names are ordinary user input; the risk in a public shadow page is what the creator writes on it | The content-moderation default: assume free text, filter it | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection produced the better defence. There *is* no free text (D-09). A shadow party is a name plus structured facts supplied by flows, and the subject writes the prose only after they claim — which is correct anyway, since it is their page. So the spam surface collapses to a single 120-char string, which URL-stripping and a length cap handle. What remains is not spam but **impersonation via the name itself**: homoglyphs (Cyrillic `А` in "Аdele"), zero-width joiners, and confusable scripts. | DT-09 | The invitation should show them what they were credited on — the credits are the whole incentive (DT-03) | Following DT-03's logic straight into a wall | ⏳ SPLIT — the incentive is real and the disclosure is unsafe, so they separate. The address is unverified (DT-04) and confidential sessions are routine in this industry: ghost production, NDAs, embargoed releases. "Sam credited you on *Untitled Idea 4*" sent to a typo'd address leaks an unreleased project to a stranger, caused by nothing worse than a mistyped letter. Split: the **invitation** carries attester + count + instrument, which leak nothing about the work; the **linked page** renders whatever domain 02 already says is public, which for released work is everything. The incentive survives intact wherever it legitimately can, and where it cannot the work was confidential anyway. |
| DT-10 | A person with an account cannot be shadow-credited — we would match them | The assumption duplicate detection is built on | ❌ REJECTED — **and it is the common case, not an edge case.** D-05 makes aliases first-class precisely because one human holds several professional names. The Producer credits "Skoop"; the account is registered as "Kwame Mensah"; the platform has never seen the string "Skoop" and cannot match it. So a shadow is routinely created for a party that already exists, and the platform is structurally unable to prevent it — the alias model *guarantees* this. Two consequences: shadow-of-an-existing-user is a first-class merge input, not an error; and claiming is often an **alias merge into an existing person** (01.01.02) rather than a conversion into a new account. A claim flow that only knows how to mint accounts would fail the platform's most typical user. |
| DT-11 | The platform can tell whether the person it just created a shadow for is alive, or an adult | Never stated, but assumed by every invitation-sending design | ❌ REJECTED — there is no signal, and there cannot be one. The attester is a third party who supplied a name; they were not asked for an age, do not know it, and in the deceased case may be crediting a catalogue session precisely *because* the person is gone. Both cases are routine in music: posthumous and archival credits are ordinary, and child vocalists and choirs are on real records. The honest design admits it: the shadow is created either way (the credit is a true fact about the work), invitations stop permanently once a deceased match is made (01.10), age assurance binds at the **claim** rather than at creation (Safeguarding), and "Not you?" gives a family a one-click end to an email nobody should have sent. The residue is a genuine gap, not a solved problem — Q-02. |
| DT-12 | The creator should be able to delete a shadow they created — it is their record | The ownership instinct, and it collides head-on with D-08 (the record of work is never destroyed) | ⏳ SPLIT — and the split is on **time and corroboration**, not on principle. D-08 protects *the record of work*. A shadow created 30 seconds ago from a typo'd name holds no record of work; it holds a mistake. Refusing to let a Producer fix `Jhon` in the room teaches them that capture is unforgiving, which is exactly how you lose the next capture. But the moment a second attester credits it, or a claim opens, it stops being the creator's mistake and becomes other people's facts. Hence D-12: retraction is a 24-hour, zero-corroboration, no-claim-in-flight window, and everything past it is a correction (24), not a delete. |
