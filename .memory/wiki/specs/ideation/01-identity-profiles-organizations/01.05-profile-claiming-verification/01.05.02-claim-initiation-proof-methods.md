# Feature: Claim Initiation & Proof Methods

> **Number**: 01.05.02
> **Parent**: [Profile Claiming & Ownership Verification](./01.05-profile-claiming-verification-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a real human takes control of an unowned party — the flow, and the proof. Claiming is the platform's conversion event: an Operator whose venue is already listed, a musician whose credits are already there. The proof methods are entity-type specific in their *strongest* tier, because the evidence that you control a venue (email at its domain, a callback to its listed number) has nothing in common with the evidence that you are a session drummer — but they converge on a single universal tier, because the party's contact route was supplied by the person who was in the room (DT-04, DT-05).

Claiming proves a **relationship to a party**. It does not prove a legal identity — that is KYC, routed to Payments at ratification (D-05), and it sits at the payout, not at the front of this funnel (DT-12).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | The archetype. Their venue is listed and three bands have been booking against a page they never made. Claiming is their entry to the platform — and per the domain overview it is "a funnel with its own conversion rate", so every proof step costs conversions. Their strongest evidence is Tier A (domain email, provider-verified listing OAuth, postal challenge to the listed address) — but **most independent venues have no domain email** (DT-05), so the majority of Operators claim on Tier B, the same route as a drummer. Provisional Operators may run the room (accept bookings, edit specs) but may not receive payouts or transfer the page (D-08). |
| Musician | Full | Claims the shadow party holding their credits. Their proof is the hardest in the domain — there is no domain email for a drummer, and their party is frequently named "Big Mike" rather than their legal name, which breaks every name-matching method (DT-10). What works is what the attester knows: the contact route the attester supplied at capture (Tier B) and counter-attestation from the people who credited them (Tier C). Also acts as an **attester** for others' claims. |
| Producer | Full | Claims both — a personal party and a studio. Also the domain's most common *counter-signatory*: their attestation is what makes another person's claim believable, since `personas.md` names them a trust broker whose attestation "is worth more than a self-claim". They are also the **upstream cause** of Tier B proof: the email they typed at capture (01.05.01 Happy Path step 3) is what the horn player later proves possession of. And they are notified when a party they attested to is claimed — they are the person best placed to notice a stranger claiming it (D-11). |
| Fan | None | Nothing to claim — a fan has no seeded presence, and 01.05.01's Role Lens bars them from creating shadow parties, so no fan-created party exists to claim. Explicit absence, and the reason nothing about claiming appears in the fan-shaped product. If a Fan needs to claim something, they are by definition a Musician (01.01.01). Fans are also barred from counter-attestation (sub-domain CX-04): fan attestation would make reputation purchasable at scale. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A claim asserts "this unowned party is me / is mine" and must be **proved before it grants control**. The flow has three parts: a fork on intent, a proof, and a grant.

### 1. Intent fork (first screen, before any proof)

Every claim opens with one question: **"Is this you, or do you represent them?"**

- **"This is me / my business"** → a self-claim. Continues into the proof matrix below.
- **"I represent them"** → a **representation claim**, routed to 01.03.02. It grants *custodial* control, visibly labelled "managed by {claimant}" on the public page, and it is **automatically superseded by a later self-claim that passes its own proof — with no contest** (D-09, DT-08). A manager's authority derives from the artist; it cannot outrank them.

The fork exists because a manager claiming for their artist is legitimate, common, and will otherwise be filed as a personal claim by someone who does not know 01.03.02 exists (the manager's mental model is "I look after this page", not "I need a mandate edge").

### 2. Proof tiers

Methods are offered **ranked by speed, not by strength** (D-02). Tier names describe what the proof is *worth*, not the order they appear in.

| Tier | Method | Entity types | Grants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — independent control proof** | Challenge-response at an address on the entity's **own domain** (`x@thevenue.com` where the party's published site is `thevenue.com`) | Venue, studio, shop, label | **Full control** |
| **A** | OAuth to a business listing whose **provider itself performed offline verification** (postcard, phone callback) **and** whose grant scopes to exactly one party | Venue, studio, shop | **Full control** |
| **A** | OAuth to a DSP artist account that grants **exactly one** artist, matching the party | Artist, alias | **Full control** |
| **A** | Postal challenge to the entity's **publicly listed** address (code valid 30 days) | Venue, studio, shop | **Full control** |
| **A** | Voice callback to the entity's **publicly listed** number (code read back) | Venue, studio, shop | **Full control** |
| **B — attester-designated route** | Challenge-response at the contact route **an attester supplied at capture** (01.05.01 Happy Path step 3), where the attester is not the claimant | **All types** — the universal tier | **Provisional** on one designating attester; **full** on ≥2 independent attesters who supplied the same route |
| **C — counter-attestation** | Confirmation by parties who credited this party (domain 02) | Person, artist, band | **Provisional** on 1 confirmation; **full** on ≥2 **independent** confirmations |
| **C** | Confirmation by existing **confirmed** members of the band | Band | **Full** on ≥1 confirmed member with a signing mandate (01.03); **provisional** otherwise |
| **—** | Match against a **public registry** (IPI, ISNI, PRO membership number) | Any | **Nothing.** Corroborating signal only — never a grant (DT-06) |

**Independence** (for Tier B and Tier C thresholds) means two attesters who are *not*: co-members of the same organisation, in a mandate relationship with each other (01.03), or attesting from the same session/project. Two credits from one producer on one session are one attester, not two.

**The claimant may never be their own attester.** If the designated contact route resolves to an existing account, or if the confirming attester authenticates as the claiming account (including via an org they hold a mandate over), the proof is refused and the claim falls to the next tier. This closes the multi-hyphenate self-attestation loop: a drummer who credited themselves through their own studio's org account has attested to nothing.

**Registries are not proof.** IPI and ISNI are public. Knowing a public identifier proves access to a search box, not identity. A registry match raises displayed confidence and may be *bound* to the party at claim time (01.09), but it never grants control on its own. Proving control of the PRO *account* would be Tier A — it requires an integration that does not exist (Q-02).

### 3. Grant

- **Tier A proof, or Tier B/C at full threshold** → full control. Ownership attaches to the existing party; nothing is created and nothing is migrated, so the accumulated history survives intact (sub-domain CX-01).
- **Tier B/C at provisional threshold** → **provisional control**, visibly provisional to the claimant and to every viewer of the public page.
- **No tier reachable** → the claim **stalls**, it is not denied (see Edge Cases). A denial with no route loses the user permanently.

Provisional control converts to full when the reversibility window (01.05.03 D-03) closes without a contest, or immediately when a stronger proof completes.

### 4. What provisional control may and may not do

> Closes the live gap recorded at sub-domain CX-02 note 3.

**The rule (DT-07): provisional control may do anything the platform can undo, and nothing it cannot.** Provisional control is only safe because it is reversible; any act that outlives the reversibility window escapes the mechanism that makes weak proof affordable (01.05.03 DT-03).

| May | May not |
|---|---|
| Edit the profile, portfolio, media, specs, opening hours | **Transfer** the party to another account (would launder control past the window) |
| Accept, decline and advance bookings — an Operator must be able to run the room, which is the entire point of the fast funnel | **Close, dissolve or archive** the party |
| Reply to enquiries and messages | **Delete** history, credits or reviews (already barred by domain D-08 — the record of work is never destroyed) |
| Confirm or dispute credits attached to the party | **Receive payouts.** Naturally fenced: payout requires KYC (D-05, DT-11) |
| Add members/mandates **without** signing authority | Grant a **signing mandate** or add a member with one (01.03) — a laundering route |
| Bind identifiers as *proposed* (01.09) | **Sign a split, accept a rights assignment, or execute a licence** on behalf of the party (09/10/11) — a rights fact reversibility cannot undo |
| Contest, and be contested | Act as the **confirmed owner** in a contest (01.05.03) |

### 5. Challenge mechanics (all challenge-response methods)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Code | 6 digits, single-use |
| Validity | 15 minutes (email/SMS/voice); **30 days** (postal) |
| Attempts | 5 per code, then the code is burned and a new one must be requested |
| Code requests | Max 5 per contact route per rolling hour; max 15 per route per 24h |
| Claim initiations | Max 5 per account per 24h; max 20 per rolling 30 days. Beyond that, further claims queue for review rather than being refused |
| Idempotency | Re-submitting a code that already succeeded returns the existing grant, never an error or a second grant |

**The invitation link is a pointer, not a bearer token** (D-10, DT-09). Claim links **never expire** — the loop depends on a two-year-old email converting, and an expiring link discards exactly the conversion the loop exists to produce. The link therefore identifies the *party* and opens the flow; it authenticates nobody. Proof is always a **fresh** challenge issued at claim time to the designated route. A forwarded invitation grants its recipient nothing but a form.

### 6. Notification fan-out

> Answers sub-domain CX-01 note 4.

On a granted claim, the party's **attesters are notified**: "Big Mike claimed the profile you credited on 3 tracks." Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is the fraud alarm.** The attester is the only person on the platform who knows who Big Mike is. If a stranger claims the party, the attester is the one who will say so — and their contest is the correction.
2. It closes their loop and confirms their capture worked.
3. It is a re-engagement event.

On a granted claim of a **venue/studio**, parties with **confirmed show or session history** on the page are notified. Same logic: they know who runs the room, and they are the population a mistaken grant harms. This is the correction mechanism for the stale-asset case (D-06).

### Happy Path — Operator claims a venue (highest-value conversion)

1. Operator reaches a private claim entry for their already-recorded, unclaimed venue. No show history is public before the claim/consent and D-13 release gates; the bounded entry offers "Is this you?".
2. They hit **Claim this venue**.
3. First screen, one question: "Is this you, or do you represent them?" → **"This is my venue."**
4. System offers proof methods **ranked by speed, not strength** (D-02). Because the party's published site is `thelexington.co.uk`, an email challenge to an address at that domain is offered first, with the address masked (`b•••••@thelexington.co.uk`) — the system never reveals a contact route it holds to a claimant who has not proved possession of it.
5. Operator picks it. A 6-digit code goes to that address, valid 15 minutes.
6. Operator enters the code. Tier A satisfied → **full control granted**, no reversibility marker needed.
7. The page is theirs — with three bands' show history already on it. **The history is the reward, and it is the reason claiming beats creating a fresh page.**
8. The three bands with confirmed history are notified that the page has been claimed, by whom, and how to contest it.

### Happy Path — Musician claims a person party (highest-volume conversion)

1. Two years ago a Producer credited "Big Mike" on 3 tracks and typed an email address for him (01.05.01 Happy Path step 3). Mike never opened the invitation.
2. Today a bandmate sends Mike the link. He opens it: a page with his name, 3 credits, and the sessions they came from — a profile he did not build.
3. He hits **Claim this profile** → "This is me."
4. System has an attester-designated route on file. It offers a fresh challenge to it, masked (`b•••••@gmail.com`). The two-year-old link proved nothing; the fresh code is the proof (D-10).
5. Mike enters the code. **Tier B, one designating attester → provisional control granted**, visibly provisional.
6. The Producer who credited him is notified: "Big Mike claimed the profile you credited." Nothing is asked of them — their silence is not consent, but their objection is a contest (01.05.03).
7. The reversibility window (01.05.03) elapses with no contest → **provisional converts to full**. Mike now owns a career record he never had to assert, built by someone who was in the room.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **No tier reachable** — session player, no PRO, no DSP page, no contact route on file | Falls to Tier C. Attesters are asked at T+0, reminded at T+72h and T+7d. At T+14d the claim is marked **stalled** — the ask stays open indefinitely and is never closed | "We've asked Sam, who credited you, to confirm. We'll tell you the moment they do." At T+14d: "Sam hasn't replied yet. Your claim is still open — nothing has been refused." Honest waiting state, never a rejection |
| **Nobody ever responds** | The claim remains open forever. A stalled claim is a durable state, not a timeout | Claim stays visible in the claimant's account with the ask's status and a route to add a different proof |
| **Stale asset** — claimant proves control of the domain email of a venue they sold last year | **Proof of control is accepted as proof of ownership** (D-06). The correction is not a stronger gate — it is the notification fan-out plus contest plus reversibility. The three bands with show history are told who claimed it; the real operator is contested into the page by the people who book it | Claim granted. Public page shows "Claimed 16 Jul 2026" and a **"Not who you expected? Contest this"** affordance. Bands with confirmed history receive the notification that makes the error visible |
| **Venue has no domain email** — the majority case; the room runs on a gmail address | Tier A is simply unavailable. Tier B (the route an attester supplied when they created or booked the page) is offered, granting provisional control. The Operator is not blocked; they are slowed by one reversibility window (DT-05) | Domain email is not offered at all — the system never presents a method the party cannot satisfy. Offered: "We'll send a code to the address on file for this venue" |
| **Party's name is not the claimant's name** — "Big Mike", "SMTH", a misspelling the Producer typed | Every name-matching method (registry, DSP) fails for exactly the population that most needs to claim (DT-10). No name check is applied to Tier B or Tier C — the attester knows who they meant, and the attester's designation is the proof | No name mismatch is ever surfaced as a failure. Rename is offered *after* the grant, as an owner action |
| **DSP OAuth succeeds but the artist name does not match the party name** | Not a failure — a signal. Aliases are first-class (D-05 domain-level, 01.01.02): a DSP account under an alias proves the alias. The proof **downgrades to provisional** and the mismatch is raised as an alias-linkage suggestion routed to 01.01.02 | "This account is for **SMTH** — is that another name you record under?" → provisional grant + alias link offer |
| **DSP OAuth returns an account that manages many artists** — a label's or distributor's Spotify for Artists covering 40 acts | Proves the claimant controls the distribution pipeline, not that they are the artist. **Refused as a self-claim; offered as a representation claim** (intent fork) | "This account manages 40 artists, so it proves you represent them, not that you are them. Claim as their representative instead?" |
| **Manager claims on behalf of an artist** | The intent fork catches it at screen one. Representation claim → custodial control, publicly labelled "managed by X", routed to 01.03.02 for the mandate edge | "Is this you, or do you represent them?" — a one-tap fork before any proof is asked for |
| **A later self-claim lands on a party under a representation claim** | The self-claim runs its own proof. On passing, it **supersedes the representation claim with no contest** (D-09) — a mandate derives from the artist and cannot outrank them. The representation edge survives as a proposed mandate for the new owner to accept or reject | Manager: "{Artist} has claimed this profile. Your management of it is now theirs to confirm." Artist: the mandate request, to accept or decline |
| **Fake representation claim** — a stranger claims custodial control of a rising artist's party | Bounded, deliberately. Custodial control is publicly labelled (visible to the artist and their collaborators), fenced by the provisional powers table, and any real self-claim ends it without a contest. Abuse is possible and pointless — the same posture as 01.05.01 DT-02 | "Managed by X" on the public page. That label is the alarm |
| **Two people claim the same party** | Routes to 01.05.03. Both claims stay live; neither gets silent priority | See 01.05.03 |
| **Race: two claims complete proof within the same window** | First-to-complete is granted (provisional or full per its tier). The second is **not dropped** — it converts to a contest against the grant that just landed. No claim is ever silently lost | Second claimant: "Someone else claimed this page while you were proving it. Your claim is now a contest." |
| **Race: claim completes as a contest opens** | The contest wins the race (sub-domain CX-02 note 5). The claim is recorded, the grant is withheld, and the claim enters the contest as a party to it | "This page is being disputed. Your claim is on hold and is part of that dispute." |
| **Race: target party is merged mid-claim** (duplicate shadows, Canonical Data cross-cut) | The claim **follows the redirect** to the surviving party and does not fail. Credits redirect rather than being rewritten (domain CX-02); a claim in flight must redirect for the same reason. Answers sub-domain CX-03 note 5 | Transparent — the claimant is told the party was merged and their claim moved with it |
| **Race: the party is credited by a new attester mid-claim** | Safe and orthogonal — credits accrete to the party, the claim attaches an owner (sub-domain CX-01 note 5). A new attester arriving *raises* the Tier C count mid-flight and may convert provisional to full | Claim strength can go up during the flow, never down |
| **Claimant already has an account and a party** — the shadow is a duplicate of themselves | This is a **merge, not a claim**. Detected when the designated route matches the claimant's own verified contact. Routed to Canonical Data; credits redirect, never rewrite (domain CX-02) | "This looks like you — you already have a profile. Merge these?" not "Claim this" |
| **Self-attestation loop** — a drummer confirms their own claim through an org they hold a mandate over | Refused. Claimant may never be their own attester; a mandate-linked account is the claimant. Falls to the next tier | The org is not offered as a confirming attester, and no reason is exposed that would teach the attack |
| **Attester designated their own address** — the Producer typed their own email for the horn player, by accident or to farm an identity | Detected: the designated route resolves to an existing account. Tier B is **refused** and the claim falls to Tier C, where that same Producer's confirmation is one attester, not a grant | Claimant: "We couldn't verify that route. Let's ask the people who credited you instead." |
| **Collusion ring** — 2+ accounts credit each other and confirm each other's claims | Independence test blocks the trivial 2-account case (co-members, mandate-linked, same session are all one attester). A 5-account ring across fabricated sessions passes. **Known gap — see Q-01** | Grants normally. The mitigation is domain 02's attestation weighting, not this file's gate |
| **Someone claims a party holding credits they did not earn** | The credit graph is the defence — the attesters are asked (Tier C). `personas.md` names this exact anti-persona behaviour ("games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation") | Counter-attestation gate; attesters notified on grant regardless of tier (D-11) |
| **Claim on a party with an active owner** (not a shadow) | This is a transfer, not a claim. Different flow, different bar — consent is the proof (01.05.03 D-06) | "This page has an owner. Ask them to transfer it, or contest their ownership." Both routes offered |
| **Proof method itself is compromised** — claimant hacked the venue's email | Beyond the platform's reach. Mitigated by the reversibility window (01.05.03 D-03) and by the notification fan-out that tells the people with history on the page | Route to 24 |
| **Claim spam** — attempts on 500 venue pages | Rate limits (5/24h, 20/30d per account) plus per-route limits. Excess claims queue for review rather than being refused — a legitimate multi-venue Operator exists and must not be locked out | "You've started a lot of claims. These are queued while we check." |
| **Operator abandons at the proof step** | The funnel's main loss point. Partial claims **persist indefinitely and are resumable** — no expiry, because expiry converts an abandoned claim into a lost user. Every proof step must be justified by what it protects | "Pick up where you left off" on return; the partial claim is listed in their account forever |
| **Network failure after the code was accepted but before the grant recorded** | Idempotent. The code remains valid for its window; re-submitting returns the existing grant, never a second grant or an error | Retry succeeds silently |
| **Claim succeeds against a frozen party** (01.05.03 D-02) | The proof is recorded; the grant is **withheld**. A frozen party has no owner by design, and a claim cannot thaw it | "This page is frozen while a dispute is resolved. Your proof is recorded and will apply when it lifts." |
| **Band claim: ex-member claims the band's party** | Tier C band rule — confirmation by existing *confirmed* members. If the band has none (all members are shadows), it falls back to an artist claim. If the band is dissolving with no recorded agreement, it is 01.04.02's failure arriving here | Confirmed members asked; failing that, artist-claim methods offered; failing that → 01.05.03 freeze |
| **Multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once** — the claimant is also an attester on a *different* party in the same session | Fine and common. Independence is tested per-party, not per-human. The only bar is attesting to your own claim | No friction |
| **Deceased subject** — heirs claim a dead musician's party | Out of scope here. Routed to 01.10 Estates: estate access is a representation edge, not an identity handover (domain CX-09) | "This person has passed away" → estate route, not a self-claim |
| **Claimant is a minor** | Age assurance is routed to Safeguarding at domain boundary-narrowing; this feature does not gate on it. Recorded so the seam is explicit, not silent | Per Safeguarding cross-cut |
| **Person credited who does not want the party** (a session they'd rather forget) | Not a claim problem. Dispute without claiming is a route 01.05.01 already guarantees — requiring them to join in order to object is coercive | Dispute route via 24, no claim required |
| **Malicious input in the claim form** — forged registry number, injected content in a claim note | Registry numbers grant nothing (DT-06). Free-text claim notes are treated as untrusted, escaped on render, and visible to the contest reviewer as claimant-supplied text | No proof value attaches to anything the claimant types about themselves |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Claim state fetch on the public page | Skeleton in the ownership strip; the rest of the page (name, credits, history) renders first — the bait must never wait on the funnel |
| **Empty** | Party unclaimed, no claim started | "Is this you?" affordance on the public page — the funnel's entry. Visible to signed-out visitors, because the horn player arriving from a two-year-old email has no account |
| **Populated** | Claim granted, full control | Ownership; full control; history intact. No provisional marker anywhere |
| **Error** | Proof check failed (wrong code, burned code, refused method) | Clear reason **and an alternative method**. **Never a dead end** — a failed proof with no next step loses the user permanently. The error never reveals an unproven contact route, and never explains *why* a refusal fired when the explanation would teach the attack |
| **Partial — in flight** | Claim initiated, proof not yet submitted | "Claim pending" publicly. Resumable indefinitely; listed in the claimant's account with the next step |
| **Partial — awaiting attesters** | Tier C ask dispatched | "We've asked Sam and Dee to confirm. 0 of 2 so far." Named, so the claimant can chase them off-platform — which is a feature, not a leak: they already know each other |
| **Partial — stalled** | Tier C, no response at T+14d | "Still open. Nothing has been refused." Alternative proof routes offered. Never a closed state |
| **Partial — provisional** | Tier B/C at provisional threshold | Provisional control, **visibly provisional to the claimant and to viewers**. Claimant sees exactly what is fenced and what would lift it. Viewers see "Recently claimed — contest this" |
| **Partial — custodial** | Representation claim granted | "Managed by X" publicly. Custodial powers = provisional powers, minus anything a mandate does not cover (01.03.02) |
| **Partial — withheld** | Proof passed, party frozen or contested | "Proof recorded. Grant on hold." Nothing is lost; nothing is granted |
| **Queued** | Rate limit exceeded, claim held for review | "Queued while we check" — held, never refused |

## Deep Think Annotations

| DT-01 | One claim flow with one proof method works for all entity types | The simplification instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the evidence available for a venue (domain email, listed phone, postal address) and for a session drummer (nothing except other people's word) have no overlap whatsoever. A single method would either be so weak it is theatre, or so strong that no drummer could ever claim their credits — which would strand the population the thesis depends on. This file asserted it as proof at SURFACE; that was wrong. Registry match is downgraded to a corroborating signal that raises displayed confidence and never grants control.
