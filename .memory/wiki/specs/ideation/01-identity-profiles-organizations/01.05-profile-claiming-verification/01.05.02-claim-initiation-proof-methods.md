# Feature: Claim Initiation & Proof Methods

> **Number**: 01.05.02
> **Parent**: [Profile Claiming & Ownership Verification](./01.05-profile-claiming-verification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a real human takes control of an unowned party — the flow, and the proof. Claiming is the platform's conversion event: an Operator whose venue is already listed, a musician whose credits are already there. The proof methods are entity-type specific, because the evidence that you control a venue (email at its domain, a callback to its listed number) has nothing in common with the evidence that you are a session drummer.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | The archetype. Their venue is listed and someone else has been booking into a page they never made. Claiming is their entry to the platform — and per the domain overview it is "a funnel with its own conversion rate", so every step of proof costs conversions. Their proof is the strongest available: domain email, listed phone, postal address. |
| Musician | Full | Claims the shadow party holding their credits. Their proof is the hardest in the domain — there is no domain email for a drummer. What they have is the credit graph itself: the people who credited them can confirm they are who they say. |
| Producer | Full | Claims both — a personal party and a studio. Also the most common *counter-signatory*: their attestation is what makes another person's claim believable, since `personas.md` names them a trust broker whose attestation "is worth more than a self-claim". |
| Fan | None | Nothing to claim — a fan has no seeded presence. Explicit absence, and the reason nothing about claiming appears in the fan-shaped product. If a Fan needs to claim something, they are by definition a Musician (01.01.01). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A claim asserts "this unowned party is me / is mine" and must be **proved before it grants control**. Proof methods vary by entity type, and each yields different confidence:

| Entity type | Available proof (breadth-level) |
|---|---|
| Venue / studio / shop | Email at the entity's domain; callback to the publicly-listed number; postal verification to the listed address; an existing business listing OAuth `[PENDING — Step 5: which providers]` |
| Artist / alias | DSP artist-account OAuth (the industry's de-facto proof of artist identity); control of the alias's established socials |
| Person (session player) | **Weakest.** Counter-attestation by the people who credited them; PRO/union membership matching the name (01.07, 01.09) |
| Band | Confirmation by existing confirmed members; failing that, treated as an artist claim |

Where proof is weak, the claim does not fail — it goes to contest (01.05.03) or grants a **provisional** control that is visible as provisional.

Identity verification (KYC — proving a human is who they say to a legal standard) is **not** this feature; it was routed to Payments at ratification. Claiming proves a *relationship to a party*, not a legal identity.

### Happy Path

1. Operator finds their venue already listed, unclaimed, with three bands' show histories on it.
2. They hit "Claim this venue".
3. System offers proof methods ranked by how fast they are, not by how strong: email at the venue's domain first.
4. They receive and confirm the email.
5. Claim granted. They own the page — with three bands' history already on it. The history is the reward, and it is the reason claiming beats creating a fresh page.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No proof method available (session player with no PRO, no DSP page) | Falls to counter-attestation: the people who credited them confirm. If none respond, the claim stalls — not denied, stalled | "We've asked Sam, who credited you, to confirm." Honest waiting state, not a rejection |
| Claimant proves control of a *stale* asset (the domain email of a venue they sold last year) | Proof of control is not proof of current ownership. A real gap: our strongest venue proof is control of an asset that changes hands | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02 |
| Two people claim the same party | Routes to 01.05.03. Both claims stay live; neither gets silent priority | See 01.05.03 |
| Someone claims a party that holds credits they did not earn | The credit graph is the defence: the attesters are asked. `personas.md` names this exact anti-persona behaviour ("games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation") | Counter-attestation gate |
| Claim on a party with an active owner (not a shadow) | This is a transfer, not a claim. Different flow, different bar | See 01.05.03 |
| Proof method itself is compromised (claimant hacked the venue's email) | Beyond the platform's reach. Mitigated by the transfer's reversibility window (01.05.03) | Route to 24 |
| Operator abandons the flow at the proof step | The funnel's main loss point. Every proof step must be justified by what it protects | Partial claims persist and can be resumed |
| Claimant is a manager claiming on behalf of an artist | Legitimate and common. Should route to representation (01.03.02) rather than personal claim — but they will not know that | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Claim state fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Party unclaimed, no claim started | "Is this you?" affordance on the public page — the funnel's entry |
| Populated | Claim granted | Ownership; full control; history intact |
| Error | Proof check failed | Clear reason and an alternative method. **Never a dead end** — a failed proof with no next step loses the user permanently |
| Partial | Claim submitted, proof pending / provisional control granted | "Claim pending" publicly; provisional control is visibly provisional to the claimant and to viewers |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | One claim flow with one proof method works for all entity types | The simplification instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the evidence available for a venue (domain email, listed phone, postal address) and for a session drummer (nothing except other people's word) have no overlap whatsoever. A single method would either be so weak it is theatre, or so strong that no drummer could ever claim their credits — which would strand the population the thesis depends on. The proof matrix is type-specific by necessity. |
| DT-02 | Stronger proof is better | The security default | ❌ REJECTED — the domain overview calls claiming "a funnel with its own conversion rate", and every proof step costs conversions. An Operator who abandons at step 3 is worth less than a weak claim that got them in and can be contested later (01.05.03). Proof should be *proportionate to what control grants* — and the reversibility of a wrong outcome is a cheaper safeguard than a stronger gate. Ranked by speed, not strength (Happy Path step 3). |
| DT-03 | The credit graph is a proof method, and it is the one that only this platform has | Reasoning about the drummer's case: what evidence exists that someone is a session player? `personas.md` calls the Producer a "trust broker" whose attestation "is worth more than a self-claim, which is precisely what makes counter-attestation valuable" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it closes the loop that makes the domain coherent. Shadow parties are created by attesters (01.05.01); those same attesters are the proof that the claimant is real. The provenance graph, built for credits, turns out to be an identity-verification substrate no competitor can copy — they would need to have been in the room. Nobody in the sweep named this. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Proof methods are entity-type specific | DT-01 — a venue's evidence and a drummer's evidence share nothing |
| D-02 | Methods are offered ranked by speed, not by strength | DT-02 — claiming is a funnel; abandoned claims are the real loss |
| D-03 | Counter-attestation by existing credit attesters is a first-class proof method | DT-03 — the only method available for the population the thesis depends on, and unavailable to competitors |
| D-04 | Weak proof yields provisional, visibly-provisional control rather than rejection | A rejection with no route loses the user; provisional control is reversible (01.05.03) |
| D-05 | KYC is out of scope (routed to Payments at ratification) | Claiming proves a relationship to a party, not a legal identity |
