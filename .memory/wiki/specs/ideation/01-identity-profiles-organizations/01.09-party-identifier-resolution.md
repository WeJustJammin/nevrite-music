# Feature: Party Identifier Resolution

> **Number**: 01.09
> **Parent**: [Identity, Profiles & Organizations](./identity-profiles-organizations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The industry identifiers that let a party be recognised outside WeJammin: ISNI (a public name), IPI/CAE (a writer or publisher at a PRO), IPN (a performer), PRO member numbers, DDEX party IDs, and DSP artist IDs. Without these, the platform's credit graph is a beautiful internal record that no royalty system, registration or delivery will accept. D-10 makes the rights stack the thesis — and every one of those four domains speaks in identifiers, not in names.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Records their IPI (if they are a PRO member), ISNI, and DSP artist IDs. Most working musicians hold one or two and could not tell you what either means — so the surface must explain what each unlocks in terms of money, not standards ("this is how the PRS knows a royalty is yours"). |
| Producer | Full | Same, plus producer-specific identifiers, and per-alias ones — an alias can hold its own ISNI, which is precisely why aliases are objects (01.01.02 D-01). |
| Operator | Config | Mostly none. A venue has no IPI and does not need one; a studio may hold a DDEX party ID if it delivers. Honest absence for the typical Operator — this is the domain's least relevant feature to them. |
| Fan | None | No identifiers, no visibility, no relevance. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A party (person, alias or org) can hold identifiers across several namespaces. Three capabilities, at breadth:

1. **Capture** — record an identifier, with its namespace, and validate its format where the standard allows.
2. **Verify** — confirm the identifier really belongs to this party. This is the hard part: an IPI typed into a form is a claim like any other.
3. **Resolve** — map across namespaces, and handle the fact that one human legitimately holds several IPIs (a writer capacity and a publisher capacity are different IPIs), and that one alias may have its own ISNI distinct from the human's.

Identifiers attach to the party, and — because credits attach to aliases (01.01.02 D-02) — an alias must be able to hold its own. This is not an edge case; it is how DSPs and PROs actually see the world.

Registry **integration mechanics** (which registries have APIs, how sync works, error handling) are architecture and route to `/create-prd`. This file owns what identifiers a party has and what having them unlocks.

### Happy Path

1. Musician joins a PRO and gets an IPI.
2. They record it, and the surface tells them plainly what it unlocks — registering works, receiving royalties, being matched by the MLC.
3. Format validated; verification attempted by whatever method exists.
4. Identifier attaches to the party.
5. Later, a work is registered in domain 09/10, and the registration carries a real identifier instead of a name — which is the difference between being paid and being a black-box entry.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One human, several IPIs (writer capacity, publisher capacity) | Correct and normal, not a duplicate. A model assuming one IPI per person breaks on every self-published writer — which is most of them | Multiple identifiers, each with its capacity |
| Alias has its own ISNI distinct from the human's | Correct and normal. Identifiers attach to the party, and an alias is a party | Per-alias identifiers |
| Identifier is claimed but not verifiable (no registry API for that namespace) | Recorded as self-asserted. Same honesty rule as 01.07 D-01: the method is visible | Unverified marker |
| Wrong identifier recorded (typo, or someone else's IPI) | Format validation catches some. A well-formed IPI belonging to someone else does not fail validation — and downstream it misroutes **royalties** | See Q-01; this is the highest-consequence error in the feature |
| Musician has no identifiers — very common | Fine. The feature must not imply they are incomplete. Most working musicians are not PRO members | Nothing; no nag |
| Party merges with a duplicate holding a different identifier | Both persist against the merged party; conflicts surface rather than resolve silently. A silently-dropped IPI is a silently-dropped royalty stream | See domain CX-02 |
| A registry's record disagrees with ours (name mismatch on a real IPI) | We do not overwrite theirs and cannot correct it — the registry is authoritative for its own record. Both values are held; the mismatch is surfaced, not auto-reconciled, and the identifier stays usable but flagged. WeJammin cannot fix the registry; it can only point the user at the registry's own correction process | A "record mismatch" flag on the identifier, showing our value vs theirs and a link/instructions to correct it at source. Deeper reconciliation policy (who wins, whether we retry, how the flag clears) is owned by **`/write-be-spec`** |
| Two different parties record the same identifier (same IPI on two accounts) | Concurrency/uniqueness conflict — an identifier verified to one party cannot be silently owned by two. Both records are held and the collision is surfaced to both, because a same-namespace duplicate is a signal that one is wrong (typo, stale, or impersonation) and it routes to the same money as the other. **Interim shipped behavior (buildable now):** both records persist, both are flagged unverified for the duration of the collision, and **neither identifier routes royalties or is presented as verified while the collision is open** — the fail-safe is to withhold routing from both rather than guess the owner. The collision opens on the second party recording an already-recorded identifier and clears only when one side is verified against the registry (or withdrawn). | A conflict warning on the identifier for both parties; neither claim is treated as verified, and neither routes royalties, while the collision is open. Deeper arbitration policy (registry-verified side wins, tie-break where neither can verify, notification cadence) is owned by **`/write-be-spec`** |
| Deliberate abuse — user records a known artist's real IPI to intercept their royalties | The malicious sibling of the typo row (DT-03), and the same silent-money failure. Where a namespace has registry verification, the false claim fails verification and stays unverified/flagged; where none exists, it is self-asserted and must never confer verified status or downstream royalty routing on its own | Recorded but explicitly unverified; the identifier cannot be presented as proven or used as strong claim evidence (01.05.02). Detection of intentional vs accidental collision `[PENDING — /create-prd-stack, Q-01]` |
| Identifier used as claim evidence (01.05.02) | Weak on its own — a PRO membership matching a common name proves little | See domain CX-05 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | One identifier per namespace per person | The obvious model | ❌ REJECTED — a self-published songwriter holds a **writer IPI and a publisher IPI**, and they are different numbers for the same human doing two capacities. That is not exotic; it is most independent writers. A one-to-one model silently merges two royalty streams, which is a money bug of exactly the kind D-10 makes the platform's core promise. Identifiers are many-per-party-per-namespace, with capacity. |
| DT-02 | Identifiers are metadata plumbing — an architecture concern, not a product feature | Genuinely tempting; ISNI/IPI *sounds* like schema, and the domain map already routed 24 concerns out as not-product | ⏳ SPLIT — the **integration mechanics** (registry APIs, sync jobs, error handling) are architecture and are routed to `/create-prd`. But *which identifiers a party holds*, and whether they are verified, is a fact about the identity that determines whether a musician gets paid. D-10 makes the rights stack the thesis, and identifiers are the vocabulary that stack speaks. Calling this plumbing would put the precondition for royalty collection outside the product. |
| DT-03 | The verification gap is worse here than anywhere else in the domain, because the failure is silent and financial | Reasoning about consequences: what does a wrong value actually cost, per feature? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it should shape the whole feature. Elsewhere a wrong identity value is embarrassing or contestable — a wrong alias, a bad badge, a disputed credit. A **wrong but well-formed IPI routes someone else's royalties to you, or yours to them**, silently, for years, with no signal to either party. It is the one identity error that self-conceals and compounds. Format validation cannot catch it; only registry verification can, and most namespaces have no API. Nobody in the sweep named this, and it is the strongest argument for prioritising real registry integration over more capture surfaces. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Many identifiers per party per namespace, each with a capacity | DT-01 — a writer IPI and a publisher IPI are the same human in two capacities, and merging them merges royalty streams |
| D-02 | Identifiers attach to any party — person, alias or org | ISNI is per public identity; credits attach to aliases (01.01.02 D-02), so aliases need their own |
| D-03 | Verification state is visible; unverified identifiers are usable but labelled | Same honesty rule as 01.07 D-01 |
| D-04 | The surface explains identifiers in terms of money, never standards | Nobody records an IPI because it is an IPI; they record it because it is how they get paid |
| D-05 | Registry integration mechanics route to `/create-prd`; the identifier record stays product | DT-02 — the seam between plumbing and the fact that determines whether someone is paid |
