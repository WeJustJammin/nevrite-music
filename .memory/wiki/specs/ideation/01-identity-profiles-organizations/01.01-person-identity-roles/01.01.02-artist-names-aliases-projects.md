# Feature: Artist Names, Aliases & Projects

> **Number**: 01.01.02
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The names a person releases, performs and is credited under — modelled as first-class objects, not as a string on the person record. One human routinely holds several: a legal name used on contracts, a session-credit name, and one or more artist aliases with their own catalogues, followings and DSP presence. Credits, releases and follows attach to the **alias**, not always to the human behind it, and the platform must hold both facts at once without collapsing them.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates and retires aliases. Chooses per-credit and per-release which alias is displayed (a session drummer credited under their real name on someone else's record, and under their alias on their own). Sees which alias a given credit, follower or royalty line belongs to. |
| Producer | Full | Holds a producer alias distinct from any artist alias — the industry norm (production credits under one name, artist releases under another). Selects the crediting alias when capturing credits in 07/02 — for themselves and, at the session, for others. |
| Operator | None | A venue's trading name is an organisation attribute (01.02.01), not a person alias. Explicitly out of scope here: modelling "The Lexington" as an alias of its owner would make the venue disappear when the owner changes. |
| Fan | None | Fans have a display name, not a stage name. If a Fan creates an alias they have, by that act, asserted a professional facet (01.01.01) and are Musician-shaped from that point — there is no fan-with-an-alias state. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An alias is a named professional identity owned by one or more person records. It carries: a display name, a public profile surface (01.06), its own follower graph (domain 20), its own credits (domain 02), and optionally its own party identifiers (01.09 — ISNI is per-name, which is precisely why aliases must be objects).

The critical rule: **a credit references an alias, and an alias references a person**. This lets a fan follow the alias without learning the human, lets the human aggregate every alias's work into one portfolio, and lets an alias be retired without erasing the work done under it.

Aliases can be **shared** (a duo releasing under one name) — see Q-01, this is the case that strains the model.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates an alias — display name, avatar, one-line descriptor.
2. System creates an alias profile surface with its own URL and follower graph, linked privately to the person record.
3. They release under it (domain 12). The release credits the alias as display artist.
4. A Producer credits them on a different record, choosing their **real name** for that credit — the two coexist without contradiction.
5. Their personal portfolio (01.06.02) aggregates both; the alias's public page shows only the alias's work.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two different humans use the same alias name (name collision, not a shared alias) | Not blocked — name collisions are legal reality in music. Disambiguation is by identifier (01.09) and credit graph, not by first-come-first-served name locking | Both aliases exist; search disambiguates by credits/location; a "not to be confused with" affordance `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Alias is retired while it has live credits, followers and royalty lines | Alias enters a retired state — profile persists read-only, credits stay attached, royalties keep flowing. Deletion is not offered | "Retiring hides this alias from discovery. Your credits, followers and royalties are unaffected." |
| An alias is transferred to another person (a name sold, or passed on) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — genuinely unresolved. Transfer must not rewrite historic credits (they were true when made) but must redirect future ones | `[PENDING]` |
| A shared alias's co-owners fall out | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — this is band dissolution (01.04.04) wearing an alias costume, which is the evidence for Q-01 | `[PENDING]` |
| Alias name is a registered trademark held by someone else | Platform records the conflict, does not adjudicate it. Routes to 24 and to 01.04.02 for the band case | Dispute path via domain 24 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Alias fetch | Skeleton alias header |
| Empty | Person has no alias | Their credits display under their person record name; a "release under a different name?" affordance |
| Populated | 1+ aliases | Alias list on settings; each with its own public page, follower count and credit count |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |
| Partial | Alias created, nothing released or credited under it yet | Alias page renders with an empty portfolio — visibly a claim, not a career |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A stage name can be a field on the person record | The obvious, cheap model — and what the sweep's candidate list implied by not mentioning aliases at all | ❌ REJECTED — it fails on first contact with reality. Richard D. James is Aphex Twin *and* AFX *and* Polygon Window, each with a separate catalogue and following. A field holds one value, so the second alias forces a second account, which fragments the credit graph — the exact failure this platform exists to fix. Aliases must be objects. |
| DT-02 | Credits attach to humans | Naive provenance model | ❌ REJECTED — DSPs, DDEX and the whole release chain treat the *display artist* as the entity. A credit that only knows the human cannot render a release page; a credit that only knows the alias cannot pay the human. It must reference the alias and resolve to the person. This is an ontology decision with catastrophic retrofit cost — hence the Must proposal. |
| DT-03 | An alias is just an organisation with one member | Tempting unification — would collapse 01.01.02 into 01.02 | ❌ REJECTED for the common case, ⏳ OPEN for the shared case (Q-01). A solo alias has no members, no treasury, no governance and no dissolution — modelling it as an org means every alias carries four empty concepts. But a duo's shared alias has all four. The model may need both, which is the honest answer at breadth. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Aliases are first-class objects owned by person records, not strings | DT-01 — the field model fails on the second alias and the failure is unrecoverable |
| D-02 | Credits reference the alias; the alias resolves to the person | DT-02 — both facts are needed, by different consumers (release chain vs payout chain) |
| D-03 | Retirement, not deletion | Work done under a name outlives the name. Deletion would evaporate provenance |
