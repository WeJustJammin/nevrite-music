# Feature: Artist Names, Aliases & Projects

> **Number**: 01.01.02
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The names a person releases, performs and is credited under — modelled as first-class objects, not as a string on the person record. One human routinely holds several: a legal name used on contracts, a session-credit name, and one or more artist aliases with their own catalogues, followings and DSP presence. Credits, releases and follows attach to the **alias**, not always to the human behind it, and the platform must hold both facts at once without collapsing them.

The feature exists because the alternative — a stage-name field — fails on the second alias and the failure is unrecoverable (DT-01). It owns: what an alias is, who owns it and for which period, what name it shows and at what address, which of a person's names is displayed on any given credit, and what happens when a name is retired, renamed, transferred, shared or contested.

**Scope boundary — three seams this file does not cross**:
- **Shared names are not aliases.** The moment a name is co-owned by 2+ people it is a band org (D-04) and governance is 01.04's. This file owns the routing, not the governance.
- **"Project" is not a third entity.** See DT-11. Note the word is overloaded: domain **07 Projects** means a *body of work* (a session, a record). This file's "project" means a *name*. This file does not claim 07's noun.
- **The alias never signs anything.** A split sheet needs a legal identity (01.01.04); an alias is a public identity and cannot contract.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates, renames, retires, un-retires and transfers their own aliases — and nobody else can, including their manager and their bandmates (D-08). Chooses per-credit and per-release which of their names is displayed: a session drummer credited under their real name on someone else's record and under their alias on their own. Decides, per alias, whether it is publicly linked to their other names or kept separate (D-07). Sees which alias each credit, follower and royalty line belongs to. |
| Producer | Full | Holds a producer alias distinct from any artist alias — the industry norm (production credits under one name, artist releases under another), and routinely holds both on the *same* release (E-17). Selects the crediting alias when capturing credits in 07/02 — for themselves, and at the session for others. Critically: they attest the **fact** (who played what); they do **not** own the **display name** it renders under — that stays with the credited person (D-06). |
| Operator | None | A venue's trading name is an organisation attribute (01.02.01), not a person alias. Explicitly out of scope: modelling "The Lexington" as an alias of its owner would make the venue disappear when the owner changes. An Operator who also performs holds an alias **as a Musician** — the persona is a lens on behaviour, not an account type (`personas.md`), so this is not an exception to the `None`. |
| Fan | None | Fans have a display name, not a stage name — no handle, no public alias page, no follower graph of their own. If a Fan creates an alias they have, by that act, asserted a professional facet (01.01.01) and are Musician-shaped from that point — there is no fan-with-an-alias state. The facet is added **inside the same Create action, disclosed before the write** (D-10, D-11): never silently, never behind a separate onboarding flow, and — since DQ-01.A — never as a separate confirm either. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An alias is a **named professional identity owned by exactly one person record** (D-04 — co-ownership is a band, not an alias). It carries:

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Display name** | 1–100 characters, full Unicode (music is global: `坂本龍一`, `Sigur Rós`, `†††`). **No uniqueness constraint, ever** (D-05). |
| **Handle** | The public address (`wejamm.in/{handle}`). 3–30 chars, `[a-z0-9-]`, case-insensitive. **Unique across every public identity on the platform** — aliases and orgs share one namespace, because they share one URL space. Confusable-normalised before the uniqueness check (D-05). |
| **Ownership history** | An ordered list of dated periods, **not an owner field** (D-03). A credit resolves to whoever owned the alias *on the date of the credit*. |
| **Public surface** | Its own profile page (01.06), its own follower graph (domain 20), its own credits (domain 02), optionally its own party identifiers (01.09 — ISNI is per-name, which is precisely why aliases must be objects). |
| **Linkage** | Whether this alias is publicly connected to its owner's other names. **Private by default** (D-07). |

Two rules do the structural work:

1. **A credit references an alias; the alias resolves to a person, through the ownership period containing the credit's date** (D-02, D-03). This lets a fan follow the alias without learning the human, lets the human aggregate every alias into one portfolio, lets an alias be retired without erasing work done under it, and lets an alias change hands without falsifying history.
2. **The attested fact and the displayed name are separately owned** (D-06). The Producer attests that this person played bass. Which of that person's names renders is the person's call, and changing it is not a re-attestation and not a provenance rewrite.

**Retirement, never deletion** (D-01), with one narrow exception: an alias holding **zero** credits, releases, followers, posts and identifiers is deletable, because there is no provenance to protect (DT-08). Emptiness is the condition — not recency.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens "Release under a different name" from their settings, or from any release/credit flow that asks which name to use.
2. They enter a display name (`Polygon Window`). System asks the one question that decides the model: **"Is anyone else in this?"** — because a shared name is a band, not an alias (D-04).
3. They answer no. System proposes a handle derived from the display name (`polygon-window`), confirms it is free after confusable-normalisation, and shows the exact URL that will result: `wejamm.in/polygon-window`.
4. System detects they hold no `performer` facet and adds it **as part of this same Create action**, disclosed on the creation screen before anything is written: *"Creating this name adds Performing to your account. Nothing you already have changes."* One line, no confirm step, no decline branch, no separate onboarding (D-10, D-11; 01.01.01 D-12/D-14). The facet added is `performer` — always, and only.
5. System asks the linkage question once, in plain terms: **"Show on your profile that this is you?"** Default is **no** (D-07). Answer is per-alias and reversible.
6. Alias and its handle are created all-or-nothing: profile surface, follower graph and acting context (01.01.03) all exist from that moment, or none do; no separate step enables any of them.
7. They release under it (domain 12). The release credits the alias as display artist. DSP delivery carries the alias, not the human.
8. A Producer credits them on a different record, choosing their **real name** for that credit. The two coexist without contradiction — different names, one person, both true.
9. Their personal portfolio (01.06.02) aggregates both **for them**. The public view shows only what step 5 permitted; the alias's public page shows only the alias's work.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| E-01 | Two different humans use the same alias **display name** (collision, not a shared alias) | Not blocked. Name collisions are legal reality in music and the platform is not a registry. Handles differ (they must — one namespace), so the two are separately addressable. Disambiguation is by identifier (01.09), credit graph and location — never by first-come name locking (D-05) | Both aliases exist. Search lists both, disambiguated by credit count, collaborators and city — not by signup order. Each page carries a "not to be confused with" link to the other **only when** both hold ≥1 credit, so an empty impersonation cannot force itself onto a real artist's page |
| E-02 | Someone creates an alias named after a famous artist to farm follows or credits | Creation is not blocked — the same rule as E-01, and a block would need the platform to be a registry of fame. Separation comes from verification (01.05) and the credit graph: the impersonator's page is visibly a claim with nothing behind it (Partial state) | Real artist's page carries the verified marker; impostor's does not. Report path via domain 24 |
| E-03 | Homoglyph handle attack — `аphex` with a Cyrillic `а` registered against `aphex` | **Blocked.** Handles are confusable-normalised before the uniqueness check — visually indistinguishable characters are treated as the same address — so the two collapse to one and the second registration fails. Display names are **not** normalised — `Sigur Rós` must be spellable | "That address is already taken" with alternatives. The attacker gets no distinct address |
| E-04 | Alias is retired while it has live credits, followers and royalty lines | Alias enters `retired`. Profile persists read-only, credits stay attached, royalties keep flowing to the person who owned it on each credit's date, handle keeps resolving. Deletion is not offered (D-01) | "Retiring hides this alias from discovery. Your credits, followers and royalties are unaffected, and you can bring it back." |
| E-05 | Retirement is attempted while a release under this alias is delivered-but-not-live | **Blocked.** DDEX delivery references the display artist; changing it mid-flight causes a delivery mismatch at the DSP that the platform cannot reconcile | "This name has a release in delivery. You can retire it once {release} is live (est. {date})." with a link to the release |
| E-06 | Retirement is attempted while drafts, unsent messages or pending credit invitations exist in that acting context | Not blocked. Drafts and pending invitations **reassign to the person record**; nothing is discarded. This answers the orphaning risk flagged in sub-domain CX-02 | "3 drafts and 1 pending credit request will move to your own account." |
| E-07 | Person wants a retired alias back | Un-retire. Same handle, same followers, same credits, same ownership history. Retirement is a visibility state, not a lifecycle terminus | Alias returns to `active`; followers notified on the same rule as E-08 |
| E-08 | Followers of a retired alias | Follow persists — a retired name's back catalogue is exactly what a fan followed it for. Followers are notified **only if** the alias holds ≥1 release or ≥1 credit; an abandoned empty claim retires silently, because notifying anyone about it would be noise about a career that never happened | Fans of a real alias: "{alias} has been retired. Their releases stay in your library." Fans of an empty alias: nothing |
| E-09 | An alias is transferred to another person (a name sold, or passed on) | Permitted, **two-sided and disclosed** (D-03). Ownership history gains a new dated period; historic credits keep resolving to the previous owner, because they were true when made. Future credits resolve to the new owner. Nothing is rewritten | Both parties confirm. Receiving party has **7 days** to accept or the transfer lapses. See E-10 for the follower consequence |
| E-10 | Followers of a transferred alias — they followed the *person*, not the *string* | **Disclosed, never silent.** A silent transfer hands a stranger an audience that consented to someone else — the legacy-act problem (a name touring with no original members) rendered as a database write. Every follower is notified; the alias page carries a transfer banner for **30 days**; the ownership history is **permanently public** on the alias page | Followers: "{alias} is now run by someone else. Keep following?" with a one-tap unfollow. Not a dark pattern — the default is that the follow persists, and the choice is offered once |
| E-11 | A manager, label or bandmate with an acting mandate (01.03.02) tries to transfer or retire the artist's alias | **Blocked.** A mandate lets someone act *as* an alias; it never lets them dispose of it. Managers taking control of artist names is a recurring, documented industry harm, and the platform must not be the instrument (D-08). Requires the owning person | "Only {person} can transfer or retire this name. You can post and reply as it." See Q-06 for the contractual label-owned-name case |
| E-12 | Transfer attempted on a retired alias, or retirement attempted mid-transfer | Both blocked — the states are mutually exclusive. Un-retire first; or let the transfer lapse/complete first | "This name is retired. Bring it back before transferring it." / "A transfer to {person} is pending. Cancel it before retiring." |
| E-13 | Transfer attempted while a release is in delivery | **Blocked**, same reason as E-05 — and worse: the payee would change mid-delivery | "This name has a release in delivery. Transfer once {release} is live." |
| E-14 | Two people race for the same handle at creation | First to complete wins; the loser's creation fails cleanly, **all-or-nothing** — no half-created alias left holding a handle it never got (E-15) | "That address was taken a moment ago." Three alternatives offered, display name preserved in the form |
| E-15 | Network or system failure mid-creation | Creation is all-or-nothing: the alias and its handle both exist, or neither does. A failure never leaves a handle reserved against an alias that does not exist — that would burn the namespace invisibly | Retry with the entered display name and handle preserved |
| E-16 | Two devices edit the same alias simultaneously | Independent display fields (name, avatar, descriptor): the most recent edit wins — the cost of a lost avatar edit is nil. **Handle change is exclusive**: the second writer is rejected, because a silently lost rename means the user believes their URL changed when it did not | "Your address was changed on another device. Reload to see the current one." |
| E-17 | **The multi-hyphenate on one release**: a Producer produces a record, plays bass on it, and wants the production credit under their producer alias and the performance credit under their artist alias — while a third name is the release's display artist | Fully supported, and normal. A credit's display name is chosen **per credit**, not per person per release (D-06). Three distinct public identities on one release resolving to two humans is a correct output, not a conflict to reconcile | Credit list shows both names. The person's own view shows both are them; the public view shows the link only if D-07 linkage permits it |
| E-18 | The inference channel: a release credits `Aphex Twin` as artist and `Richard D. James` as writer, publishing a linkage the user deliberately kept private (D-07) | The platform **cannot prevent** the juxtaposition — the credit list is a public document and both facts are true and required. It **must warn at the moment it would happen**, before publication, because after delivery the inference is permanent and un-retractable. This is the failure mode nobody designs for: privacy lost not by a leak but by two correct disclosures side by side | Blocking disclosure before delivery: "This release shows {alias} and {other name} together. Anyone reading it can tell they are the same person. Continue / use one name / change the writer credit." |
| E-19 | Person deletes their account (GDPR erasure) while the alias holds credits | Alias is **not** deleted. Ownership link severs; alias retires; credits persist. A credit is not solely the erasing person's data — it is also the attester's record and every co-contributor's, and deleting it would evaporate *their* provenance to satisfy one party. The public identity outlives the account link | Alias page renders as retired, unowned, credits intact. **The legal position is not settled — Q-04.** |
| E-20 | A claimed alias (01.05) has credits older than the platform | Ownership history **begins at the claim date and says so**. The platform never asserts it knows who owned a name in 1995. Pre-platform credits attach to the alias with no ownership period behind them | Ownership history renders "Known from {claim date}" — never a fabricated start. Pre-platform credits show the alias, and no resolved person |
| E-21 | Bulk alias creation — spam, squatting, enumeration | Rate-limited: **5 aliases per person per rolling 30 days**. A person with 8 aliases is real (Richard D. James); 8 in a month is not. Limit is liftable on request | "You've created 5 names this month. Get in touch if you need more." |
| E-22 | Handle churn burning the namespace | Handle changes limited to **2 per alias per rolling 12 months**. Old handles **resolve permanently and are never re-issued** — releasing one would let a stranger capture every press link, every QR code and every bio that ever pointed at it, which is a hijack with the platform's own redirect as the weapon (D-09) | "You can change this address twice a year. {old-handle} will keep working forever." |
| E-23 | Credited person wants to change which of *their* names shows on an existing credit | Permitted at any time — it is their name, and the attested fact does not move (D-06). **Before delivery**: silent, immediate. **After delivery**: applies to WeJammin surfaces immediately and queues a DSP metadata update via 12, which the platform cannot guarantee the DSP honours | Before: changes silently. After: "Updated here. {DSP} may take weeks to show the new name, and may not." |
| E-24 | The attester disagrees with the display-name change | No standing. They attested a fact, not a name (D-06). If they dispute the *fact*, that is 02's correction flow and 24's dispute path — not this file's | Attester sees the credit's display name changed; the fact and their attestation are untouched |
| E-25 | Two people both claim the same alias — the ghost-production case | Not adjudicated here. The alias has one owner (D-04); a competing claim routes to 01.05 (claiming) and 24 (dispute). The platform records the conflict, does not resolve it | Dispute path via 24 |
| E-26 | Alias name is a registered trademark held by someone else | Platform records the conflict, does not adjudicate it, and does not search registries — consistent with 01.04.02's Behavior. Routes to 24, and to 01.04.02 for the band case | Dispute path via domain 24 |
| E-27 | An alias display name is itself abusive (defamation, slur, targeted harassment) | Not an identity problem — a content problem. Display names are user content and route to 24's moderation like any other public string. This file imposes no taste rule, only length and encoding | Report path via domain 24 |
| E-28 | Solo alias becomes shared — a second person joins (a solo project becomes a duo) | **Promotes to a band org**, non-destructively: handle, followers, credits, ownership history and release history all carry over. Same pattern as the Fan→Musician upgrade (01.01.01 DT-03) — the upgrade must never cost the user what they already built. Governance capture (01.04) fires at the promotion, which is the earliest honest moment for it (DT-04). **Authority arrives with the promotion (DQ-02.4; 01.03.03 D-07..D-12)**: everyone named at capture who becomes a confirmed permanent member is seeded with all seven activities. Book, sign, spend and settle are capped at **USD 1,000** when money is committed; list, release, and administer remain in the seeded scope. Skipping governance capture therefore no longer means "nothing was decided" — the skip must be **legible, not silent** (domain CX-12) | "Adding someone makes this a band. Nothing is lost — same page, same followers, same credits. Everyone here can book, sign, spend and settle up to $1,000 when money is involved. They can also list, release and change each other's permissions. You can change that later. Now: who owns the name if it ends?" |
| E-29 | Shared name reverts to one person — a band dissolves and one member keeps the name | **No demotion path.** The org persists with one member. A name that has been co-owned carries a governance history and a dissolution record that an alias has nowhere to put; demoting it would evaporate exactly the record 01.04.04 exists to produce | Handled entirely by 01.04.04. This file's only rule is that the door does not swing back |
| E-30 | A shared alias's co-owners fall out | Not this file's. By D-04 it was never an alias — it is a band, and this is band dissolution (01.04.04) with its governance already captured at E-28/step 2 | Routes to 01.04.04 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Alias fetch in flight | Skeleton alias header. Handle and display name render from cache if present — the user's own name is never a spinner |
| Empty | Person holds no alias | Credits display under their person record name. One low-pressure "Release under a different name?" affordance — dismissible, and never a nag (matching 01.01.01's Empty state posture) |
| Populated | 1+ aliases | Alias list on settings, each with handle, follower count, credit count, linkage state and ownership status. Each has its own public page and its own acting context (01.01.03) |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry. The person is **never** presented as alias-less on failure — that would silently hide their own public identities and invite them to re-create one that already exists |
| Partial — empty claim | Alias created, nothing released or credited under it yet | Alias page renders with an empty portfolio — **visibly a claim, not a career**. This is the state that makes E-02 impersonation self-defeating and E-01 disambiguation honest |
| Partial — retired | Retired (E-04) | Read-only page, retired marker, handle still resolving, credits and back catalogue intact and still discoverable by direct link, absent from search |
| Partial — in transfer | Transfer offered, not yet accepted (E-09) | Owner sees "pending transfer to {person}, expires {date}, cancel". Recipient sees the offer. Public sees **nothing** — an unaccepted transfer is not a fact yet |
| Partial — transferred | Transfer completed (E-10) | Transfer banner for 30 days; ownership history permanently public |
| Partial — unowned | Owner erased (E-19) | Retired, unowned, credits intact, no resolvable person |
| Partial — unknown provenance | Claimed alias with pre-platform credits (E-20) | "Known from {claim date}". Pre-platform credits show no resolved owner rather than a guessed one |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A stage name can be a field on the person record | The obvious, cheap model — and what the sweep's candidate list implied by not mentioning aliases at all | ❌ REJECTED — it fails on first contact with reality. Richard D. James is Aphex Twin *and* AFX *and* Polygon Window, each with a separate catalogue and following. A field holds one value, so the second alias forces a second account, which fragments the credit graph — the exact failure this platform exists to fix. Aliases must be objects. |
| DT-02 | Credits attach to humans | Naive provenance model | ❌ REJECTED — DSPs, DDEX and the whole release chain treat the *display artist* as the entity. A credit that only knows the human cannot render a release page; a credit that only knows the alias cannot pay the human. It must reference the alias and resolve to the person. This is an ontology decision with catastrophic retrofit cost — hence the Must proposal. |
| DT-03 | An alias is just an organisation with one member | Tempting unification — would collapse 01.01.02 into 01.02 | ❌ REJECTED for the solo case, ✅ CONFIRMED for the shared case — which is DT-04's resolution of Q-01. A solo alias has no members, no treasury, no governance and no dissolution; modelling it as an org makes every alias carry four empty concepts. A duo's shared name has all four, and is therefore already a band. The model needs both, split at co-ownership. |
| DT-04 | **A shared alias is a band org, not an alias with co-owners** — and the alias-creation flow is therefore the earliest possible governance-capture moment | Resolving Q-01. Test: co-ownership brings members (01.03.01), decision rules (01.04.01), name ownership (01.04.02) and dissolution (01.04.04) — which are exactly the four things 01.04's index says define a band | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it pays a dividend nobody named. "Alias with co-owners" would re-implement all four inside the alias model *without* the governance capture 01.04 exists to force — a shared alias with no dissolution rule is precisely the harm 01.04.02 was written to prevent. Routing it to the org model instead means step 2 of the Happy Path ("is anyone else in this?") fires the governance capture **at the moment the name is invented — when nobody has a stake in lying about it**. This is D-18's thesis (capture the fact while everyone is still friendly) applied to the band rather than to the session, and it lands earlier than 01.04 could ever reach on its own. Nobody creates a band name while angry. |
| DT-05 | **Transferring an alias silently transfers its audience** | Reasoning from the follower graph being per-alias (D-02) plus the industry's legacy-act problem — names touring with zero original members is among music's most persistent consumer-deception disputes | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is an undisclosed-principal problem wearing a database write. A fan followed Aphex Twin because of Richard James; a transfer with no disclosure hands that consent to a stranger. The platform would be manufacturing precisely the deception that the legacy-act disputes litigate — with better plumbing. Hence E-10: notify every follower, banner for 30 days, ownership history permanently public. The disclosure is not politeness; it is the only thing that keeps the follow meaningful. |
| DT-06 | **The attested fact and the displayed name are separately owned** | Reasoning about the capture moment: the Producer picks the crediting alias at the session (per the file's own Role Lens), and will sometimes pick wrong | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it dissolves a would-be provenance hole. If the display name were part of the attestation, correcting it would be a rewrite, and the platform would either forbid a person from fixing their own name or permit attestation edits. Both are wrong. Splitting them: the attester owns *what happened*, the credited person owns *which of their names shows*. A display-name change is then not a rewrite at all — it touches nothing the attester asserted. This also gives 01.01.04's deadname case a clean mechanism at the credit level, not just the profile level. |
| DT-07 | **Alias↔alias linkage is itself a disclosure, and must be private by default** | Reasoning from real practice: producers routinely keep aliases deliberately unlinked (ghost production, genre separation, a dance alias kept away from a session career). The file's Happy Path said "linked privately to the person record" — but never made the *public* link a choice | ✅ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap. Portfolio aggregation (01.06.02) and payout resolution both need the link; the *public* does not. If aggregation is public by default, the first thing the platform does for a producer with two aliases is out them — publishing a connection they spent a career keeping separate. Default no, per alias, reversible. This is 01.01.04's fail-closed principle (DT-01 there) applied one level up: the leak must require an act, not an omission. |
| DT-08 | Deletion is never permitted (D-01, retirement-not-deletion) | Testing the file's own decision against the trivial case | ⏳ NARROWED, not upheld. D-01 protects *work*; an alias with zero credits, releases, followers, posts and identifiers has none. Forcing permanent retirement on a typo litters the settings surface forever and burns a handle for nothing. The exception is conditioned on **emptiness, not recency** — which is the honest formulation, since an alias empty for three years is as safe to delete as one empty for three seconds. D-01 stands everywhere it has a subject. |
| DT-09 | **Releasing an old handle turns the platform's own redirect into a hijack weapon** | Reasoning about E-22's rename case against the press-link reality: a bio, a QR code on a poster, and a press page all outlive a rebrand | ✅ CONFIRMED — if `wejamm.in/old-name` is re-issuable, whoever takes it inherits every link the artist ever printed, and the platform faithfully delivers the artist's audience to them. Permanent resolution, never re-issued, is the only version where a rename is safe. Handles are cheap; the namespace can afford it. Paired with the 2-per-12-months limit so the affordance is not a namespace tax. |
| DT-10 | **No display-name uniqueness makes name squatting economically pointless** | Reasoning about the second-order effect of D-05, rather than defending it only on "music says collisions are legal" | ✅ CONFIRMED — an unexpected dividend. On a platform that locks names, squatting has a victim and therefore a price. Here, squatting `Nova` denies nobody the name `Nova` — the squatter holds only an empty page (Partial — empty claim) that the credit graph ranks below every real artist. The attack surface collapses to the *handle* namespace, which is exactly where rate limits and empty-deletion (DT-08) already bite. The decision that looked like a concession to music's messiness is also the security posture. |
| DT-11 | **"Project" is a third entity alongside person and alias** | The node's own title says "Artist Names, Aliases & **Projects**", and no prior version of this file addressed the third noun | ❌ REJECTED — it collapses. Silk Sonic is two people under one name = a band org (DT-04). Polygon Window is one person under one name = an alias. A "project" is only ever one of those two with an expected end date, and an end date is an attribute, not an entity. Adding it would create a third thing whose every behaviour is delegated to one of the other two. **Boundary caught in passing**: domain **07 Projects** already owns "project" meaning *a body of work* — this file must not claim that noun, or two domains end up with a `project` entity meaning different things. Recorded so the collision is not rediscovered downstream. |
| DT-12 | An alias should require verification (01.05/01.07) before it can be credited | Would kill E-02 impersonation at the root | ❌ REJECTED — it walls off the long tail to stop a problem the credit graph already solves. Most working musicians will never verify anything, and gating credits on verification means the platform's core record only works for people who are already legible to institutions — the exact inversion of who this is for. Mirrors 01.01.01 D-02 (facets are self-asserted by design). The impostor's cost is already an empty page next to a credited one. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([../../02-credits-attribution/](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **shared entity, the single most load-bearing reference in the domain**. The credited party is an alias, resolved through the ownership period containing the credit's date (D-03). Also a **permission** seam: 02 must not let an attester edit a credit's display name (D-06, E-24), and must let the credited person change it without triggering a re-attestation (E-23).
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([../../12-release-distribution/](../../12-release-distribution/)) — **trigger dependency, bidirectional**. Display artist on a release is an alias; DDEX/DSP delivery requires it. 12 must **block** alias retirement (E-05) and transfer (E-13) while a release is delivered-but-not-live, and must accept queued display-name updates post-delivery (E-23) without promising the DSP honours them.
- Touches **20 Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan** ([../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)) — **shared entity + notification fan-out**. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Aliases are first-class objects owned by person records, not strings. Retirement, not deletion — **except** an alias with zero credits, releases, followers, posts and identifiers, which is deletable | DT-01 (the field model fails on the second alias, unrecoverably) + DT-08 (retirement protects work; where there is no work there is nothing to protect) |
| D-02 | Credits reference the alias; the alias resolves to the person | DT-02 — both facts are needed, by different consumers (release chain vs payout chain) |
| D-03 | **An alias carries an ownership *history* — dated periods — not an owner field. A credit resolves through the period containing its date. Transfer is permitted, two-sided (7-day acceptance window), and disclosed** | Resolves **Q-02**. Same shape as 01.01.04 D-03 ("signed documents keep the name that was true when signed"): a credit was true when made and must never be rewritten. An owner *field* forces a choice between falsifying history and forbidding transfer; a history needs neither. Also the only model that keeps royalties correct on a sold name (10 cross-cut) |
| D-04 | **A shared name is a band org (01.02.01 type `band`), not a co-owned alias. Aliases are single-owner by construction. Alias creation asks "is anyone else in this?" and routes accordingly** | Resolves **Q-01**, per DT-04. Co-ownership brings members, decision rules, name ownership and dissolution — 01.04's exact definition of a band. Consistent with sub-domain D-02 ("an alias is a projection of a professional self, not an entity with members and a treasury") rather than a contradiction of it: the shared case was always the org case. Dividend: governance is captured when the name is invented, not when the band is dying |
| D-05 | **Display names have no uniqueness constraint, ever. Handles are unique across all public identities, confusable-normalised, first-come. Disambiguation is by credit graph and identifier, never by name locking** | Resolves **Q-03** — the tension dissolves because it was never one field. "Music says no" is true of the *name*; "search UX says yes" is true of the *address*. Both get what they need. DT-10: this also makes name squatting pointless, and DT-03 (homoglyphs) closes the address-side attack |
| D-06 | **The attested fact and the displayed name are separately owned. The attester owns what happened; the credited person owns which of their names shows. Changing the display name is not a re-attestation** | DT-06 — otherwise correcting one's own name is either forbidden or an attestation edit, and both are wrong |
| D-07 | **Alias↔alias public linkage is per-alias, private by default, reversible** | DT-07 — publishing the link is the platform's decision to out a producer who spent a career keeping names apart. Fail closed (01.01.04 DT-01, one level up). Private linkage still serves portfolio and payout, which need the link but are not the public |
| D-08 | **Transfer and retirement require the owning person. A mandate never confers them** | E-11. A mandate lets someone act *as* a name; disposal of the name is not an act you perform on someone's behalf. Managers taking control of artist names is a recurring industry harm and the platform must not be its instrument. Contractual exception left open — Q-06 |
| D-09 | **Old handles resolve permanently and are never re-issued. Handle changes: 2 per alias per rolling 12 months. Alias creation: 5 per person per rolling 30 days** | DT-09 — a re-issuable old handle makes the platform's redirect the hijack. The limits stop the permanence from becoming a namespace tax; both numbers are calibrated to real behaviour (rebrands are rare; 8 aliases is real, 8 in a month is not) and are tunable without touching the model |
| D-10 | **AMENDED (DQ-01.A)**: alias creation adds the `performer` facet **inside the single Create action, disclosed before the write** — never silently, never as a separate flow, and (amended) **never as a separate confirm** | The "one confirm" framing is withdrawn, and with it the precedent it cited. 01.01.01's Happy Path step 3 is a **user-initiated** add ("They hit 'Sell something'"); an alias-implied facet is an **implied** add, which 01.01.01 D-12 governs and which forbids prompting. The objection this decision was written to carry — that a silent add changes the user's navigation without consent — is answered by the pre-write disclosure line rather than by a decision point. Silence and prompting are different axes, so D-12 and this decision are both satisfied at once; and a decline branch has nowhere to sit against E-14/E-15's all-or-nothing creation contract or sub-domain CX-04's no-alias-without-a-facet rule |
| D-11 | **The implied facet is always `performer`, and only `performer`** — never `writer`, never derived from what the alias is for | DQ-01.B. Ratifies this file's Happy Path step 4 and 01.01.01's standing rule rather than introducing a purpose signal alias creation does not carry; obtaining one means asking, which is the role prompt 01.01.01 D-01/D-12 ban, and a wrong inference reveals the wrong markets. Consequence recorded honestly: the production-only alias — this file's own Role Lens names it as the industry norm — gets a `performer` claim it did not ask for, and the discoverability that follows. See Q-08 |
