# Feature: Legal Identity vs Public Identity

> **Number**: 01.01.04
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The separation between the identity a contract or a payout requires (legal name, address, tax details) and the identity the platform shows the world (display name, alias, avatar). These are different facts with different audiences, and conflating them is how a musician's home address ends up on a public gear listing, or a trans artist's deadname surfaces on a split sheet years after they changed it. This feature models legal identity as a **separately-authorised surface** of the person record rather than fields on it, so that exposing it requires an explicit act.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Maintains their legal identity once; controls which counterparties see it and when. Sees an explicit disclosure moment ("Signing this split sheet will show {legal name} to the other 3 signatories") rather than discovering the exposure afterwards. Can change their legal name without rewriting historic public credits. |
| Producer | Full | Same control over their own record. Additionally *receives* legal identity from contributors at the capture moment — a split sheet needs real names — and must be shown that they are receiving something sensitive, not a profile field to paste into a Dropbox folder. |
| Operator | Full | Their personal legal identity is separate from the org's registered details (01.02.01). A sole-trader Operator is the case where they collapse — and the one where the user is least likely to notice the leak. |
| Fan | Config | Holds legal identity only where a transaction demands it (a ticket in a real name, a merch delivery address). Never asked for it to browse or follow. Their exposure surface is a purchase, not a profile — and the delivery address must never reach the seller's public view of them. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two distinct identity surfaces on one person record:

- **Public identity** — display name, alias(es), avatar, bio, location-to-city-granularity. Default-visible. This is what 01.06 renders.
- **Legal identity** — legal name, address, DOB where required, tax identifiers. Default-invisible to *everyone*, including other members of your own band. Disclosed only into a specific counterparty relationship, for a specific reason, at a moment the user sees.

The rule is that legal identity is never a read-through on the profile: there is no path from "view this person" to their legal name. Access is granted by a *transaction or contract that requires it*, is scoped to the counterparties of that transaction, and is logged.

KYC/KYB verification of legal identity is **not** this feature — it was routed to Payments during domain-map ratification. This feature owns the model, the disclosure moments, and the visibility rules; Payments owns proving the identity is real.

### Happy Path

1. Musician signs up and uses a stage name. No legal identity is requested.
2. They agree a split in domain 09. The flow states plainly: signing discloses their legal name to the other signatories, and why (a split sheet with a stage name is not enforceable).
3. They confirm. Legal identity is captured once and disclosed *to those signatories only*.
4. Their public profile is unchanged — the alias still fronts it.
5. A later, unrelated counterparty in domain 13 sees only the public identity. The disclosure did not become global.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User changes their legal name (marriage, transition, deed poll) | New legal name applies going forward. Historic contracts keep the name that was true when signed — they are evidence, not a profile. The old name is **not** shown to anyone new | "Your new name applies to future agreements. Existing signed documents are unchanged." Plus a route to 24 for the case where the old name's presence is itself the harm |
| Deadname / former name appears on a historic public credit | This is a real harm, not an edge case. Public credits must be alias-referenced (01.01.02), so the fix is a public-identity change and needs no contract rewrite | Public credits update with the alias; contracts do not |
| A counterparty screenshots and republishes a disclosed legal name | Platform cannot prevent it; routes to 24. Materially: this is why disclosure is scoped and logged — so the leak has a provenance | Report path via domain 24 |
| Sole trader: legal identity and trading identity are the same person | Trader-status disclosure (01.08) legally requires a real name and address to be **public** on marketplace listings. The collapse is lawful and mandatory — but the user must understand it before listing | "As a trader, your name and address will be publicly shown on your listings. This is required by law." — a blocking disclosure, not a footnote |
| Stalking/safety case: user must not have their city or legal name inferable | **Interim posture — fail closed to most-restrictive.** When a user enables safety mode: public location granularity is dropped from city to none (no city, no region); no location-derived scalars are exposed (no "near you" distance, no local-gig proximity ranking, no venue-adjacency hints); legal identity disclosure requires an extra confirmation even inside a transaction that would otherwise capture it; and search/listing surfaces suppress the user from location-filtered results (locate-suppression on). This most-restrictive default is the shipped behavior until Q-01 unifies a single cross-domain posture. Any surface that cannot honour suppression fails closed (hides the user) rather than exposing them | "Safety mode is on. Your location is hidden everywhere, you won't appear in location-based searches, and disclosing your legal name always asks first." A single toggle with a plain summary of what it turns off, not a per-surface checklist |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Record fetch | Public identity renders from cache; legal surface never cached client-side |
| Empty | No legal identity captured (the normal state for most users most of the time) | Nothing — no nag, no "complete your profile" pressure toward disclosure |
| Populated | Legal identity captured | A disclosure ledger: who has been shown it, for what, when |
| Error | Fetch failed | Public identity still renders; legal surface fails closed |
| Partial | Legal identity captured but unverified by Payments | Public surfaces unaffected; payout surfaces show the Payments-owned verification gate |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Legal name can be a field on the person record with a `private` visibility flag | The cheap model everyone ships | ❌ REJECTED — a flag is a filter that must be applied correctly at every read path, in 24 domains, forever. One missed filter is one permanent doxx. Modelling legal identity as a separate, separately-authorised surface makes the leak require an explicit act instead of an omission. This is the "fail closed" version of the same requirement, and the reason for D-03 on the parent index. |
| DT-02 | This is a privacy cross-cut, not an identity feature | Reasonable — visibility rules serve every domain | ⏳ SPLIT — the general mechanism (audience scoping on any field, anywhere) is a cross-cut and is escalated. But *which identity a person has, and which one a given flow legally requires* is identity ontology, and no other domain can answer it. The split sheet needs the legal name; the release needs the alias; only this domain knows both exist. |
| DT-03 | Nobody in the sweep named this, and its absence would have been discovered at the first split sheet | Candidate list has 12 items, none about name privacy; `personas.md` never mentions it either | ✅ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap. The tell: the platform's core flow (capture a split at the session, in front of everyone present) is *precisely* a forced disclosure of legal identity to a room of people. The wedge feature and the privacy failure are the same moment. That intersection is unmanaged in the candidate list. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Legal identity is a separate authorised surface, not flagged fields | DT-01 — fail closed, not fail open-by-omission |
| D-02 | Disclosure is scoped to a transaction's counterparties and logged | Makes exposure auditable and gives leaks a provenance |
| D-03 | Legal name changes apply forward; signed documents keep the name that was true when signed | Contracts are evidence. Rewriting them would destroy the thing they exist to prove |
| D-04 | KYC verification is out of scope here (routed to Payments at domain-map ratification) | Do not re-absorb what the boundary narrowing already extracted |
