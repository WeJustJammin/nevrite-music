# Feature: Person Record & Multi-Role Facets

> **Number**: 01.01.01
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The single human record every other party relationship on the platform hangs off, carrying a set of **simultaneous** role facets rather than one account type. A working musician who is also a mixer, a band member, a studio engineer and an occasional gear seller holds all five facets at once; the record never asks them to choose. Role facets are additive and reversible, and adding one unlocks surfaces without hiding the ones already in use.

The record exists to solve one problem: `personas.md` states the Musician's Unique Constraint as *"their roles are simultaneous, not sequential. Any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer OR a seller?' fails them immediately."* Every competitor's identity model is a fork at signup. This record is the refusal to fork — and because `problem-statement.md` locates the root cause as *absence at the point of truth*, the record must also be creatable **before its human ever signs up** (01.05.01). A record that only exists at signup cannot be credited at the session, and a platform that cannot credit at the session has no wedge.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Owns their record. Adds/removes role facets (`performer`, `writer`, `producer`, `engineer`, `teacher`, `seller`, `tech`) at any time from a single settings surface — never a wizard that forks the account. Sees every facet's surfaces simultaneously in one navigation, not behind a mode toggle. Sees, per facet, how many live obligations sit behind it (the number that blocks removal). Their free-text descriptors ("mixing engineer", "session drummer, jazz/soul") are edited on the profile (01.06), not here — the facet is the switch, the descriptor is the sentence. |
| Producer | Full | Same record type as Musician — the persona difference is behavioural, not structural. Typically holds `producer` + `engineer` + `performer`. The `producer` facet specifically is what makes them offerable as a **session owner** in 07 (create a project, invite contributors, run the capture flow) rather than merely a service provider in 05 — that distinction is the whole reason `producer` and `engineer` are separate facets. |
| Operator | Full | Holds a person record distinct from the venue/studio org they operate. Their personal facets (often `engineer`, sometimes `performer`) are separate from the org's capabilities — the venue's PA spec is not a fact about them. There is deliberately **no `operator` facet**: the ability to list a room, take a booking or settle a show comes from their membership mandate in that org (01.03), never from a claim on their person record (D-08). |
| Fan | Config | Has a person record with **zero professional facets by default** and never sees facet management until they ask for it. Zero facets *is* the fan shape — there is no `fan` facet, no fan flag, no fan account type. Adding any professional facet is the frictionless, non-destructive Fan→Musician upgrade path: nothing they already had (follows, tickets, purchases) is lost or migrated. The reverse is equally available and equally non-destructive (D-07). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### What the record is

A person record represents **exactly one human**. It carries: a display name, a set of role facets, and links to everything else that describes that human — aliases (01.01.02), memberships (01.03.01), claims (01.05), credits (domain 02), the separately-authorised legal-identity surface (01.01.04), and the payable-party link Payments owns.

The record has **two independent axes**, and conflating them is the error this file exists to prevent:

| Axis | Values | Owned by |
|---|---|---|
| **Existence** | created at signup · created by reference (someone named them in a credit, lineup, invitation or booking) | this file (signup) + 01.05.01 (reference) |
| **Ownership** | unowned (shadow) · owned (claimed/registered) · contested | 01.05 |

A record created by reference is a real person record with no owner and no login. It can be credited, listed in a lineup and booked; it cannot act. "A person record is created at signup" is **false** as a general statement, and any downstream model that assumes it will break at the Producer's capture moment (DT-05).

### What a facet is — and what it is not

A role facet is a **self-asserted claim about what this person professionally does**. It is not a permission, not a credential, and not a job title.

Three axes are independent, and every one of them has been conflated by at least one competitor:

| Axis | Question it answers | Owned by |
|---|---|---|
| **Facet** (claim) | Does this person do X? | this file |
| **Visibility** (revelation) | Does the platform show them X's tools right now? | this file (D-07) |
| **Permission** (authorisation) | May this person do X, here, to this resource? | Roles & Permissions cross-cut |

A facet **reveals** surfaces and makes the person discoverable in that capacity. It never authorises anything. Verification of a facet, where it exists at all, is 01.07's separate assertion. Suspension of a facet's activity is 24's permission revocation, and does **not** remove the facet — it remains true that the person is a seller while they are barred from selling (DT-02 applied).

### The facet vocabulary — closed, platform-owned

The vocabulary is **closed**. This is forced, not chosen: a facet's defining function is to reveal a surface set, and an open string reveals nothing. If a user types `beatmaker`, there is no surface for the platform to unlock. Self-description — which genuinely *must* be open, because "topliner", "cellist for hire" and "amp tech" are how musicians actually describe themselves — is a **profile descriptor** (01.06), not a facet. Two different things wore one name in the breadth pass; D-04 separates them.

**Generation rule (D-05)**: *a facet exists if and only if it reveals a surface set that no other facet reveals.* Anything that would only differ in display is a descriptor.

Applying the rule yields **7 facets** (exact membership pending owner ratification — Q-02):

| Facet | Asserts | Reveals | Common descriptors under it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `performer` | Plays or sings, on record or stage | 04 Opportunities (as available player) · 07 Projects (join as contributor) · 08 Jamming · 12 Release (as display artist) · 17 Live Booking (as act) | drummer · session vocalist · touring guitarist |
| `writer` | Writes, composes, arranges | 09 Rights · 10 Royalties · 11 Licensing (as a work owner) | topliner · composer · lyricist · arranger |
| `producer` | **Owns the room** — runs sessions, shapes records | 07 Projects (**create/own** a project, invite contributors, run the capture flow) · 05 Services (offer production) | executive producer · beatmaker · vocal producer |
| `engineer` | Captures and finishes audio | 05 Services (offer) · 07 Projects (join) | tracking engineer · mixing engineer · mastering engineer |
| `teacher` | Teaches | 06 Education (tutor side) | guitar tutor · vocal coach · theory |
| `seller` | Sells goods | 13 Gear Marketplace · 14 Digital Goods | private seller · dealer (trader status is 01.08's, not a facet) |
| `tech` | Instrument, backline and live technical work | 05 Services (offer) · 18 Show Production (crew) | guitar tech · drum tech · FOH · monitor engineer |

Two facets the rule explicitly **does not** produce, and why:
- **No `operator`** — the ability to list a room or settle a show is org-derived; it comes from a membership mandate (01.03), never a facet (DT-11). Two sources of truth for "may this person list a rehearsal room" would drift until one had a hole.
- **No `fan`** — zero facets is the fan shape. A fan flag would be a second way to express the same state, and the two would disagree.

Instrument is **not** a facet (D-10). A drummer is `performer`; "drums" is a descriptor. Facet-level instruments would produce ~40 facets each revealing the identical surface set — the rule forbids it, and the highest-volume search on the platform ("need a bassist for Tuesday") is a descriptor query, not a facet query.

### Facet lifecycle

- **Adding** is one confirm, inline, from wherever the user hit the surface. Never a separate onboarding flow, never a new account.
- **Each add and each remove is an independent operation on a single facet**, never a write of the whole facet set (D-11). A set-level write loses a concurrent addition silently.
- **Removing** is blocked only by **live obligations** — never by history (D-06). The distinction is load-bearing: a retired session drummer removing `performer` must not lose or hide 400 credits. Credits are historic facts about a party; a facet is a present claim about what they do. Blocking removal on history would make removal impossible forever for exactly the users with the longest careers.
- **Removing does not delete.** Nothing done under a facet is touched (D-03, and domain D-08's single invariant).
- **Implied facets** are auto-added with a visible, undoable notice — never prompted (D-12). Creating an alias (01.01.02) implies `performer`, **always and only `performer`** (D-14) — never `writer`, and never inferred from what the alias is for. The add is **disclosed before the write, inside the single Create action**: one line of copy on the creation screen — *"Creating this name adds Performing to your account. Nothing you already have changes."* — and then the alias and the facet are written together. There is **no prompt, no gate and no decline branch**: a prompt at that moment ("are you a performer?") would be the account fork D-01 bans wearing a softer word, and a refusal has nowhere to sit against 01.01.02's all-or-nothing creation contract (E-14/E-15) or sub-domain CX-04's rule that there is no state where someone holds an alias but no professional facet.

**Live obligations** (the closed list that blocks removal), per facet:

| Facet | Blocks removal while |
|---|---|
| `seller` | Any listing is live, any order is unfulfilled, any return window is open, any checkout is in flight |
| `teacher` | Any lesson booking is future-dated or any course cohort is running |
| `producer` | Any project they own is open, or any contributor invitation is outstanding |
| `engineer` / `tech` | Any service engagement is accepted-and-undelivered |
| `performer` | Any confirmed booking is future-dated |
| `writer` | Any split sheet is issued-and-unsigned (a signed one is history and does not block) |

The obligation check is **re-evaluated atomically at confirm time, not at page load** — a stale "you have 0 active listings" that passes and then removes while a sale lands is a race that costs a buyer their order.

## Happy Path

1. Fan signs up with an email and a display name. **No role question is asked** — no "what do you do?", no account-type picker. They get a working account immediately, with zero facets.
2. Months later they want to list a cymbal. They hit "Sell something" from 13, from the marketplace page they were already browsing.
3. System detects no `seller` facet and offers to add it **inline, without leaving the listing flow**: one confirm, stating plainly what it does ("This adds Selling to your account. Nothing you already have changes.").
4. They confirm. The facet is written; the listing flow continues from the same step, no reload, no re-entry, no separate onboarding.
5. Selling surfaces appear in their navigation alongside — not instead of — what was already there. Their follows, tickets and purchase history are untouched and still visible. Nothing was migrated, because nothing needed to be.
6. Their profile (01.06) now offers a descriptor field for this facet ("private seller — drums and cymbals"). It is optional; the facet works without it.
7. Later they add `performer`, the same way, from an Opportunities listing in 04. Both facets are live at once; nothing is hidden to make room.
8. Their settings surface shows two facet chips, each with its live-obligation count. Neither is primary. There is no third state where the account "becomes" a musician account — it always was one, and it always was a fan account, because those were never different objects.

## Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User removes a facet with live obligations (open listings, a future lesson, an unsigned split sheet) | Removal blocked, not silently cascaded — deleting a facet must never delete the work done under it (D-03) | "You have 3 active listings as a seller. Close or transfer them before removing this role." with a link to each of the 3 |
| User removes a facet with **only historic work** behind it (400 credits, 0 live obligations) | **Removal succeeds.** History never blocks (D-06). Credits, royalties and the public portfolio are untouched; only the authenticated surfaces disappear | "Selling removed. Your 400 credits and your profile are unchanged." |
| Obligation check passes at page load, then an order lands before the user confirms removal | Check is re-run atomically at confirm; removal fails with the *current* obligation list, not the stale one | "A sale came in while you were here — you now have 1 unfulfilled order. Removal cancelled." |
| User adds every facet at once to "see everything" | Allowed — facets are self-asserted, not gated. Surfaces get dense, which is a visibility problem, not a validity problem, and visibility is a separate axis (D-07) | Full navigation; a nudge that unused surfaces can be **hidden without removing the facet** |
| Long-tenured multi-hyphenate: 7 facets, navigation at maximum density, and removal is blocked by live work in all 7 | This is the **predictable end state**, not an edge case — facets are additive in practice because working musicians rarely stop doing anything. Density is managed on the visibility axis: surfaces are collapsible per facet, reversibly, and collapsing never un-asserts the claim | Per-facet "hide these tools" toggle; the facet chip stays lit on settings and the profile |
| Fan adds a professional facet, then regrets it | Facet removed; account returns to fan-shaped UI (zero facets = fan shape, no flag flipped); no data loss either direction | Confirmation that nothing is deleted, only hidden |
| **Musician→Fan**: a 20-year veteran leaves the industry and removes all facets | Authenticated UI becomes fan-shaped. Their **public profile does not** — it is a 20-year portfolio and remains one. "Fan-shaped" describes the logged-in surface set, never the public page (DT-10) | Their own navigation goes quiet; visitors still see the career |
| A facet is asserted falsely (claims `engineer`, has never engineered) | Nothing blocks it — the facet is a claim, not a credential. Cost of the lie is borne by 01.06.02 (an empty credit-backed portfolio) and 01.07 (no badge). This is the platform's answer to `personas.md`'s "inflates credits to claim work they didn't do" — the graph, not a gate | Their profile shows the facet with zero verified credits behind it, visually distinct from a credited one |
| **Two humans share one account** (a duo, a couple, a band's shared login) | **Tolerated, never blessed, and self-limiting** (D-09). Blocking is unenforceable (detection requires fingerprinting whose false-positive locks a musician out of their career record). Migration is impossible (splitting requires knowing which act belonged to which human — that information does not exist, and inventing it fabricates provenance, which is strictly worse than ambiguity). Instead: the platform removes the *reason* — every legitimate need for a shared login (one public name, everyone can post, one inbox) is served by a shared alias (01.01.02) or a band org + acting-context switcher (01.01.03), so the temptation has a destination. And the case is **structurally self-limiting**: the moments that matter for provenance all require legal identity (01.01.04), which is one human's. A split sheet signed from a shared account discloses **one** legal name and binds **one** human — the other occupants signed nothing, and the record correctly says so | No block, no warning at signup. At the first legal-identity disclosure: "Signing this shows {legal name} to the other 3 signatories and binds **you**, personally." Where the shared-login shape is detected passively (e.g. an alias with two humans behind it), a non-blocking route to the supported shape: "Sharing this name with someone? Bands and shared aliases let you both act under it — and both get credited." Residual policy at payout/KYC: Q-01 |
| Shadow party's facets — a Producer credits "Sam — horn" for a person who has no account | The shadow record carries **other-asserted** facets, inferred from the referencing flow (credited on horn → `performer`), marked as other-asserted with the attester named. They reveal nothing (a shadow cannot act) and authorise nothing. On claim (01.05.02), the claimant **inherits** them and may remove any without a reason | Claimant sees: "Sam Reeves credited you as a performer on 3 tracks. We've added Performing to your account — remove it if that's not you." |
| The human signs up independently while a shadow record for them already exists | Duplicate. Two person records for one human must **redirect, not rewrite** (domain CX-02). Facets union across the merge; other-asserted facets stay marked as such | Duplicate detection at signup; claim flow (01.05.02) rather than a silent merge |
| Concurrent facet edit from two devices/tabs (adds `seller` on phone, `teacher` on laptop) | Both survive. Each add is an independent single-facet operation (D-11), so there is no set-level last-write-wins to lose one | Both chips present on both devices after refresh; no conflict prompt |
| Facet write fails mid-flow (network drop at Happy Path step 4) | **Fails closed.** The facet is not added, and the flow does **not** proceed into a surface the user has no facet for. The original CTA is restored with the user's in-progress input preserved | "Couldn't add Selling — check your connection. Your draft listing is saved." with retry |
| Person record loads but the facet list fetch fails | Renders from last-known facets with a banner. **Never** silently renders a facet-less account — that would hide the user's own surfaces and read as data loss. Never silently renders *extra* facets either | "Showing your last-known roles — we couldn't refresh them." Retry affordance |
| Trust & Safety (24) suspends a person's ability to sell | This is **permission revocation, not facet removal**. The facet stays — it remains true that they are a seller. The surfaces gate on permission, not on the claim (DT-02) | Selling surfaces present but blocked with 24's reason and appeal route — not vanished, which would read as data loss and hide the appeal |
| A minor holds a `seller` or `teacher` facet | The facet is **not** blocked — claims are free, and gating the claim would put an age question in the one flow D-01 bans questions from. The gate is at the **act**: the first listing, the first lesson booking. Age assurance is a cross-cut (D-13); this file owns only the seam | Facet added normally. At first listing: the Safeguarding cross-cut's gate. Statutory thresholds: Q-03 |
| User asserts a facet whose domain has not shipped yet (`teacher` before 06 is live) | The facet is **stored and displayed** — it is a true claim, and D-20 retained all 195 Musts with sequencing deferred, so early users will routinely outrun the surfaces. Facet assertability is never gated on shipped domains, or the earliest and most valuable users lose their claims | Facet chip present with "Teaching tools coming soon" — not hidden, not silently dropped |
| The platform splits or retires a facet (e.g. `engineer` → `tracking` + `mixing`) | Existing holders are **mapped to the successor(s)**; a claim is never silently dropped. A retirement with no successor converts the facet to a descriptor on the profile rather than deleting the assertion. The vocabulary is closed and platform-owned (D-04), so its evolution is the platform's liability, not the user's | "We've split Engineering. You now hold Tracking and Mixing — adjust if that's not right." |
| Display name at signup impersonates a well-known party ("Rick Rubin") | **Not blocked.** Names are not unique and never will be — collisions are legal reality in music (01.01.02). The credit graph disambiguates: an impersonator has zero credits from named attesters, which is precisely the proof no competitor can manufacture. Adjudication routes to 24 | Both records exist; search ranks by credit graph; report path via 24 |
| Multi-hyphenate acts in two roles on the same session (produces it **and** plays drums on it) | **No conflict, by construction** — this is the normal case the facet model exists for, not an edge case. They hold `producer` and `performer` simultaneously and are credited twice in domain 02, once per role. Facets are not contexts: the acting-context ambiguity (one action, two contexts) is 01.01.03's Q-01 and does not reach this record | Two credits on one release, both theirs, no prompt to choose |
| Deceased user's record (01.10) | Facets **freeze**. The estate cannot add one — a facet is a self-assertion about a profession, and only the person can make it; an estate asserting "he was a producer" is a third party writing a claim in the first person. The estate cannot remove one either: removal would edit the historic self-description of a career. Live obligations transfer to the estate representation edge (01.10, domain CX-09) | Facets render read-only with the legacy-account treatment |
| Fan record with zero facets, viewed by a stranger | Whether a zero-facet record has a public page at all is unresolved at the sub-domain level (01.01 Q-03 / domain Q-10). This file's position: the **record** always exists; its **visibility** is 01.06's archetype question, not this record's | Deferred — see 01.01 Q-03 |

## States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Record fetch in flight | Skeleton profile header. Facet chips render from cache if present rather than flashing empty — an empty facet row on a 7-facet user reads as data loss |
| Empty | New account, zero facets, no aliases | Fan-shaped account: no professional surfaces, no facet management, no switcher (01.01.03 DT-03). A single low-pressure "Are you a musician?" affordance, **permanently dismissible** — not a nag, not a completion meter, because a completion meter is the account fork wearing a progress bar |
| Populated | 1+ facets asserted | Facet chips on the settings surface, each with its live-obligation count; each facet's surfaces live in navigation simultaneously. No primary, no default, no ordering that implies one |
| Error | Record or facet fetch failed | Error with retry. The account is **never** presented as facet-less on failure — that would silently hide the user's own surfaces. Falls back to last-known facets with an explicit banner; if there is no cache, professional surfaces are blocked rather than guessed at |
| Partial | Facets asserted, none verified, no credits behind them | Profile renders facets as **claims** — visually distinct from verified/credited ones (01.06.02, 01.07). This is not a degraded state; it is the honest state of a new professional, and it must not read as an error |
| Unowned (shadow) | Record created by reference (01.05.01), no owner | **A visitor's view, not an account view** — nobody is logged in as this record. Other-asserted facets render with their attester named ("credited as a performer by Sam Reeves"). A claim affordance fronts the page. Mechanics are 01.05's; the record's existence in this state is this file's |

| DT-12 | Two humans on one account can be blocked, or detected and migrated | The thesis-protective instinct — a shared account makes the attested party ambiguous, and attribution is the whole product | ❌ REJECTED on both branches, ✅ CONFIRMED as a design obligation. **Blocking** needs detection; detection needs device/behavioural fingerprinting; a false positive locks a musician out of their own career record — the single worst outcome this platform can produce. **Migrating** needs to know which act belonged to which human, and that information does not exist anywhere; reconstructing it is exactly the "asking people to remember" failure `problem-statement.md` names as the root cause, except performed by us. The obligation that survives is different and better: **remove the need, and let the structure limit the damage.** Every reason to share a login is already served by a shared alias or a band org + switcher, and the moments that matter (splits, contracts, payouts) require legal identity, which is one human's — so a shared account degrades gracefully at exactly the boundary where ambiguity would cost something. See D-09. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No primary role, no account type, no onboarding fork | The multi-hyphenate constraint (`personas.md`) makes this a correctness requirement, not a preference |
| D-02 | Facets are self-asserted and unverified by design | Verification is 01.07's job. Gating facets on verification would make the platform unusable for the long tail who hold no union card |
| D-03 | Facet removal never cascades to delete work | Deleting a role must not delete the record of what was done under it — that is the exact evaporation the platform exists to prevent. Domain D-08's single invariant, first instance |
| D-04 | **Facet vocabulary is closed and platform-owned; self-description is open and lives on the profile (01.06)** | DT-04. A facet's function is revealing a surface set — an open string reveals nothing. The open-vocabulary need is real but was mis-served: it is a descriptor. Resolves 01.01.01 Q-02 and sub-domain-level pressure for an open taxonomy |
| D-05 | **Generation rule: a facet exists iff it reveals a surface set no other facet reveals** | Makes the vocabulary derivable rather than negotiated, and gives every future "should X be a facet?" a test instead of an argument. Yields 7 facets; excludes instrument, union membership, trader status, operator and fan |
| D-06 | **Live obligations block facet removal; history never does** | DT-06. Blocking on history means the users with the longest careers can never remove a facet. A credit is a historic fact about a party; a facet is a present claim. The obligation list is closed and per-facet, and is re-evaluated atomically at confirm time |
| D-07 | **Facet, visibility and permission are three independent axes** | DT-09. The SURFACE version separated claim from permission but fused claim with visibility. The 7-facet multi-hyphenate with live work in all 7 has no remedy under the fused model except to stop being who they are |
| D-08 | **No `operator` facet — org-derived capability comes from the membership graph (01.03), never a facet** | DT-11. Two sources of truth for one authority drift until one has a hole — the same argument domain D-02 used to merge candidates 04 and 05. Generalises: facets cover only what a human asserts about themselves |
| D-09 | **Shared accounts are neither blocked nor migrated: the need is removed and the structure limits the damage** | DT-12. Blocking needs fingerprinting whose false positive locks a musician out of their career record. Migration needs information that does not exist and would fabricate provenance. Instead, every legitimate need is served by a shared alias or a band org + switcher, and the provenance moments require legal identity, which is one human's. Resolves Q-01's product half |
| D-10 | **Instrument is not a facet; it is the canonical descriptor** | DT-07. ~40 instruments each revealing the identical `performer` surface set fails D-05. But matching in 04/05/08 runs on it, so it must be structured — escalated as a cross-cut, not buried in free text |
| D-11 | **Each facet add/remove is an independent single-facet operation, never a set write** | A set-level write silently loses a concurrent addition from another device. The multi-hyphenate is precisely the user with two tabs open |
| D-12 | **Implied facets are auto-added with a visible, undoable notice — never prompted.** **AMENDED (DQ-01.A)**: the notice is **pre-write** — one disclosure line inside the single Create action, shown before the facet is written | Resolves the sub-domain CX-04 open call from this side. A prompt at alias creation ("are you a performer?") is D-01's banned fork in softer words; `personas.md` requires the lazy path to be the correct path. **The amendment adds disclosure without adding a decision**: auto-add stands, the ban on a prompt stands, and 01.01.02 D-10's objection to a *silent* add is satisfied — silence and prompting are different axes, which is why the two files only appeared to contradict. Copy reuses the ratified seller sentence pattern from Happy Path step 3. Accepted limit: a user who reads nothing on the creation screen ends up exactly where a silent auto-add would have left them — the disclosure improves the informed case only |
| D-13 | **Facet assertability is never gated on whether the facet's domain has shipped** | D-20 retained all 195 Musts with sequencing deferred, so the earliest users will routinely outrun the surfaces. Dropping their claims silently would punish exactly the users who arrived first |
| D-14 | **Alias creation implies exactly one facet: `performer`. Never `writer`, and never inferred from what the alias is for** | DQ-01.B. Ratifies the standing rule of this file and 01.01.02 rather than introducing a purpose signal alias creation does not carry — asking what an alias is *for* is the role prompt D-01 and D-12 both ban, and a wrong inference reveals the **wrong** markets under D-05, which is worse than an over-assertion the user can trim. Cost recorded honestly: a production-only alias receives a `performer` claim it did not ask for. It is bounded by D-02 (facets are self-asserted, not credentials) and D-06 (a fresh `performer` has no live obligations, so it drops in one act). Residual open question at 01.01.02 Q-08 |
