# Feature: Acting Context Switcher

> **Number**: 01.01.03
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The affordance that answers "who is doing this?" — the surface where a person chooses whether they are acting as themselves, as one of their aliases, or on behalf of an organisation or artist they hold a mandate for. Every write on the platform is attributed to an acting party, and a multi-hyphenate switches contexts several times a day: replying to a booking enquiry as their band, invoicing as their studio, listing a cymbal as themselves.

The acting party is not cosmetic. It determines **who is contractually bound**, **who is credited**, **whose legal identity a disclosure moment discloses** (CX-05), and **where money lands**. A wrong acting party on a split attestation produces exactly the unfalsifiable record `problem-statement.md` exists to prevent — a fact captured at source that is *wrong* at source, which is worse than no fact at all, because it looks attested.

**Scope boundary**: this file owns the *product* half — the context list, how it is derived, how the switch is made visible, what it changes, and what the platform does when the wrong one is used. The *enforcement* half (propagating the acting party through every call site and authorising it) is the Roles & Permissions cross-cut's; the transport mechanics (how a party identity travels Workers → Supabase and is enforced at row level) are architecture. See D-02 and domain CX §"Acting-context propagation & RLS enforcement".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Switches between self, each alias (01.01.02), each band/org they hold a mandate for (01.03.03), and each party they represent (01.03.02). The current context is shown **persistently in the header as avatar + party name in text** — never avatar-only, never behind a menu — because the cost of posting as the wrong party is contractual, not cosmetic. Contexts are derived, never listed by hand. The heavy case is a Musician who also manages or agents for others: 10–20 contexts, and the list is a working tool, not a settings page. |
| Producer | Full | Switches between self, their producer alias, their artist alias, and their studio org. This matters most **at the capture moment**: a credit or split captured in 07/02 records *which party attested it*, and an attestation by "Metropolis Studios" carries different weight from one by a person nobody has heard of. Also the persona most exposed to the sticky-context hazard — the studio front-desk machine is shared and the last context persists (DT-12). |
| Operator | Full | Acts as the venue/studio org for enquiries, holds, bookings, listings and settlement (16, 17, 19). Frequently *both* buyer and seller, so switches between org and self within one session. Works on a phone at a loading dock: the indicator must survive a 360 px viewport and a hurried thumb, and "you are the venue right now" must be unmissable at arm's length. Reads the counterparty's acting context too — their standing question is "can this person actually bind the artist?" (01.03.02). |
| Fan | None | Has exactly one context — themselves. The switcher is **not rendered, not collapsed, not greyed out: absent**. A fan who never acts for anyone must never learn this concept exists. This absence is not a special case or a feature flag — it falls out of the derivation (D-01): no aliases, no mandates, one context, no control. The switcher first appears at the person's **second** context, which is the first alias created or the first mandate accepted — never at facet addition (01.01.01), which changes what you can do, not who you are. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### Derivation

The context list is **derived, never configured**. It is the union of:

1. The person record itself (01.01.01) — always present, always context #1, never removable.
2. Each alias they own (01.01.02) — including shared aliases, subject to 01.01.02 Q-01.
3. Each organisation where their membership (01.03.01) carries a mandate permitting at least one activity (01.03.03).
4. Each party they represent under an active representation edge (01.03.02) — within the edge's scope, territory and term.

Losing a mandate, retiring an alias, or letting a representation term lapse removes a context with **no separate cleanup step and no revocation sweep**. This is why derivation was chosen over configuration: a configured list is a list that goes stale, and a stale acting context is a person acting for a party that no longer authorises them.

Membership **without** a mandate does not produce a context. Being in a band you cannot act for is a fact about the band's people surface, not an entry in your switcher — offering a context in which every action is denied is the "shown-and-denied" pattern the Partial state rejects.

**What the ratified band default does to source #3** (01.03.03 D-07..D-10, DQ-02.4/.5/.6/.7). For a **band**, every `confirmed` `permanent` member is seeded with all seven activities up to USD 1,000, so the context appears **at the moment they confirm** and nobody configures anything — confirmation *is* the grant, which is why the acceptance screen must say what is being received (01.03.03; sub-domain CX-01 §4 treats silently gaining authority as a security event). For a **`touring`, `staff` or `honorary`** band member, and for **every member of a studio, venue, label, shop or agency** who has not been granted something explicitly, there is **no context at all** — a visible absence that must be explained on the surface rather than merely enforced. Both halves fall straight out of D-01: nothing here is configured, and nothing changes in this feature's mechanics.

### What a switch changes

| Changes | Does not change |
|---|---|
| The attributed actor on every write | Which notifications you receive (union across all contexts — DT-06) |
| The inbox/thread **view** scope | Your session, your login, or your person record |
| Which surfaces are shown (an org context with no `list` mandate shows no listing surface) | Any other tab, window or device (DT-05) |
| Whose legal identity a disclosure moment discloses (CX-05) | Anything already written — attribution is immutable, and reattribution is never offered (D-04) |
| The money destination on any transaction | The subject/credited party, which is chosen inside the action (D-05) |

### Ordering and scale

The list is **stably ordered and never reorders itself**:

1. Pinned contexts — user-set, maximum 5, in user-defined order.
2. Self.
3. Aliases, in creation order.
4. Organisations and represented parties, alphabetical by display name.

Order is computed at list open and **frozen while the list is open**. A context that appears or disappears mid-open (mandate granted or revoked) does not reflow the list under the user's thumb; it applies on next open, and an in-flight tap on a now-absent row fails closed with the revocation message rather than hitting the adjacent row.

At **≥8 contexts** a type-ahead filter appears above the list, matching on display name, substring, case-insensitive, filtering on each keystroke. Below 8 the list renders in full with no filter.

### Attribution depth

What the *counterparty* sees is not always what the actor selected (DT-04):

| Context type | Counterparty sees | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Self | The person | No third party involved |
| Alias | The alias only | The alias *is* the public identity (CX-03); revealing the human behind it is a doxx (01.01.04) |
| Organisation (band/studio/venue) | The org, **plus the acting human on any attested or binding action** | You cannot evaluate a signature without knowing who signed and under what mandate. On non-binding actions (a post, a follow) the org alone is shown |
| Represented party (manager for artist) | **Always dual: "{Agent} on behalf of {Artist}"** | The Operator's standing question is "can this person bind the artist?" (01.03.02 Role Lens). Concealing the agent would answer it wrongly, and music runs on knowing who you are actually dealing with |

### Confirmation at write time

For any action in the **attested**, **money**, or **third-party-visible** classes (see Reversibility), the acting party is named **inside the primary action control**, not beside it: the button reads `Reply as The Hold Steady`, `Sign as Metropolis Studios`, `Publish as AFX` — never `Reply`, `Sign`, `Publish` with the party in adjacent helper text. Helper text is not read; button labels are.

### Reversibility (resolves Q-02)

Reversibility is a function of **whether a third party has relied on the fact**, not of the action's type (DT-14):

| Class | Examples | Window | After the window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attested facts** | Credit attestation (02), split signature (09), contract signature, mandate grant (01.03.03), claim assertion (01.05.02) | **0 s — no undo, ever** | Correction is a **new, superseding record**; both remain visible with both attesting parties and both timestamps. The wrong record is never deleted or rewritten — a rewritten attestation is a provenance hole, which is the one thing this platform may not produce (domain D-08) |
| **Money movements** | Invoice issued, deposit taken, payout initiated, settlement posted | **0 s — no undo** | Reversal is a new counter-transaction, owned by the Payments cross-cut. Identity never reverses money |
| **Third-party-visible communications** | Reply to an enquiry, DM, comment, thread post | **60 s before delivery** | No unsend. Edit only, with visible edit history and original retained. 60 s is chosen because the wrong-party error is noticed *immediately* (the confirmation named the party) and 60 s is short enough that a counterparty is unlikely to have read it |
| **Public publications** | Listing published (13/14), post, release announcement (12) | **60 s hard undo — fully unpublished, no trace — only while view count = 0** | Once viewed ≥1 time: retract-with-tombstone. The record that it existed survives; the content does not |
| **Private / internal state** | Drafts, saved filters, pinned contexts, unsent composer content | **Unlimited** | Fully reversible. No third party has relied on anything |

The switcher itself never offers **reattribution**. An action taken as the wrong party is retracted (if the window allows) and re-taken in the right context, or it stands. "Change who this was from" is not an affordance at any tier — a credit or a contract that can be reattributed after the fact is not evidence of anything.

### Per-view binding

The acting context is **view state, not account state** (DT-05). Switching on your laptop does not switch your phone. Opening a second tab does not rebind the first. A composer or multi-step flow **binds the context it was opened under at open time**; if the context changed elsewhere before submit, submit is blocked and the user re-confirms explicitly against the context now shown.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is browsing as themselves. The header reads `● Jamie Okonkwo` — avatar plus name in text.
2. A booking enquiry arrives for their band, The Hold Steady. The notification is delivered (notifications are union-scoped, DT-06) and badged `The Hold Steady`.
3. They open it. The thread shows it is addressed to the band, and a banner states they are currently acting as themselves.
4. The banner offers the switch inline: `Switch to The Hold Steady to reply` — from the context they are already looking at, not from a settings page.
5. They tap it. The header changes to `● The Hold Steady`, colour-differentiated from the self context. No page reload, no re-login.
6. They type a reply. The send control reads `Reply as The Hold Steady`.
7. They send. A 60 s undo affordance appears (third-party-visible class). They do not use it.
8. The reply is delivered attributed to The Hold Steady. The venue sees the band as counterparty — not a person they have never heard of. Because this is a non-binding communication in an org context, Jamie's name is not surfaced to the venue; the band's own audit trail records that Jamie sent it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User acts in the wrong context (posts as self, meant the band) | Action is attributed to the party that was active. Reversibility is by class, per the table above. Reattribution is never offered | Undo affordance for 60 s on communications/publications; nothing on attested facts — the correction is a superseding record |
| Mandate revoked while the user is mid-action in that context | Context disappears from the list on next open; the in-flight action fails authorisation at the call site (cross-cut's job), never silently succeeds. Draft is preserved, never discarded | "You no longer have permission to act for {org}. Your draft is saved." Draft is readable, not sendable, and offers "re-open as yourself" |
| Mandate revoked between list-open and row-tap (race) | Tap fails closed against the revocation, not against whichever row now occupies that pixel. Order is frozen while open, so the tap cannot silently hit a neighbour | "You no longer have permission to act for {org}." List refreshes |
| User holds mandates for 20 parties (a manager, an agency) | Stable ordering (pinned → self → aliases → alphabetical). Type-ahead filter renders at ≥8 contexts. **The list never reorders by recency** — see D-06 | Pinned five at top; filter field; alphabetical remainder |
| Deep link into an org-scoped resource while in the wrong context | System offers to switch rather than showing a bare 403 — the user has the right, just not the active context | "This is your band's booking. Switch to {band} to reply?" |
| Deep link carries a context parameter intended to auto-switch | **Never auto-switches.** The link may pre-select the row in the offer; the switch always requires an explicit tap by the user | Offer banner, one tap. No silent rebind (DT-15) |
| A single action legitimately spans contexts (studio invoices; the engineer is credited) | Resolved: these are **two actions, not one** (DT-09). The invoice's acting party is the studio (who is bound, who is paid). The credit's acting party is whoever attests it; the credit's **subject** is the person, chosen inside the credit action — exactly as 01.01.02 D-02 already established for alias selection. One acting party per action, always; the subject is a separate choice | Invoice: `Issue as Metropolis Studios`. Credit: `Credit: Jamie Okonkwo (engineer)` chosen within the credit flow, attested by whichever party is acting |
| Two members of one band both reply as the band to the same promoter simultaneously | **Unmanaged collision.** Org contexts are shared inboxes and nothing in the derivation prevents two humans occupying one context at once. Mitigation: live presence on org-context threads | "Sam is also viewing this as The Hold Steady" · "Sam is replying…" — presence indicator, not a lock. Locking a band's inbox would be worse than a double-reply (DT-10) |
| Two co-owners of a shared alias act as it simultaneously | Same collision, one level down. Recorded as evidence for 01.01.02 Q-01 (shared alias may be an org) — this file does not resolve the ontology, only its switcher consequence: shared aliases get the same presence indicator | Presence indicator on shared-alias contexts |
| Sticky context on a shared machine (studio front desk, band practice room laptop) | Context persists per-device. The next person sits down inside the previous engineer's studio context. **Mitigation: context reverts to self after 12 h of inactivity**, and any org/represented context re-confirms on the first attested/money action after a session gap | On return: `You're acting as yourself` (reverted). On first binding action after a gap: "Confirm you're acting as {org}" |
| Two humans genuinely share one login | Out of scope here and unresolved at the root — 01.01.01 Q-01. The switcher cannot repair an account whose *person* is ambiguous; it faithfully attributes to a party whose actor is already wrong | — (routed to 01.01.01 Q-01) |
| Network drops between switch and write (Operator on a phone at a loading dock) | **Fails closed to no-write.** Never writes under the last-known-good context, never queues a write against an unconfirmed context | "Couldn't switch — you're still acting as {previous}." Write is not attempted |
| Context list fetch fails entirely | Falls back to the **self** context and says so. Never silently acts as the last-cached org — a cached org context is a mandate assertion the platform cannot currently verify | "We couldn't load your other contexts. You're acting as yourself." Retry offered |
| Mandate is narrow (can read the band's inbox, cannot spend) | Context is available; unavailable actions are **absent**, not shown-and-denied. Over-limit offers escalate to a capable holder (01.03.03 D-06) rather than dead-ending | Booking surface present, payout surface absent. On an over-limit offer: "This is above your limit. Sent to {name} to approve." |
| Mandate is per-capability on a multi-type org (mandated for the shop, not the venue — D-06 global, 01.02.03) | The switcher lists **parties, not capabilities**. One org = one context; capability narrowing is within-context surface filtering, identical to the narrow-mandate row above | One `Metropolis` context; shop surfaces present, venue surfaces absent |
| New mandate granted or representation accepted while the user is logged in | New context appears at next list open, and **announces itself once** — a manager who accepts representation and does not know they can now act is a manager who will not act | Toast: "You can now act as {artist}." Dismissible, shown once |
| Alias retired while a draft authored in that context exists | Draft is preserved and readable, not sendable in the retired context (01.01.02 D-03 keeps the work; the context is gone). Offers re-open as another context | "This draft was written as {alias}, which is retired. Re-open as yourself?" — resolves the CX-02 §2 pending |
| User attempts to act as a **shadow party** (01.05.01) | **Impossible by construction.** A shadow party is a subject of other people's attestations, never an actor. No alias ownership, no membership, no mandate ⇒ no derivation path ⇒ no context. This is what stops shadow parties being sockpuppets (domain Q-06) | Shadow party never appears in any switcher (DT-08) |
| Platform admin/support needs to act as a user to debug | **Not this switcher, ever.** Admin impersonation has no derivation path (no alias, no mandate) and admitting it would require a non-derived context, breaking D-01 for every user. It is a separate mechanism, out of this domain, always dual-attributed and always logged | Not rendered here. Routed to 24 / personas Q-02 (DT-07) |
| Estate executor acts for a deceased user's party (01.10) | Falls out of the derivation with no new concept — the executor's authority is a representation edge (01.03.02) carrying a mandate, so the estate simply appears as a context. Dual-attributed like any representation | `{Executor} on behalf of the estate of {artist}` |
| Represented artist is a band, not a person | Representation is party↔party (01.03.02), so the band appears as a represented context. *Who at the band could grant it* was a mandate question already settled upstream; the switcher does not re-ask it | Band appears as a represented context, dual-attributed |
| Acting as an org that has no payout method configured | Action proceeds; **settlement blocks** at the Payments boundary, not at the switcher. The switcher does not gate on downstream readiness — that would make identity own a payments precondition | Action succeeds. At settlement: "{org} has no payout method." Routed to Payments |
| User switches context mid-way through a multi-step flow (a booking wizard) in another tab | The flow **binds the context it opened under**. Submit revalidates: displayed context ≠ submitting context ⇒ blocked with explicit re-confirm. The flow is never silently rebound | "You started this as {A} but you're now acting as {B}. Continue as {A}, or restart as {B}?" |
| User pins a context, then loses the mandate for it | Pin is dropped silently with the context. A pin is a view preference over a derived list, never a second source of truth that could resurrect a revoked context | Context and its pin both absent |
| User has 2 contexts and never switches (the overwhelmingly common Musician case) | Switcher renders, costs one line of header, and is never opened. This is correct — the indicator's job is *stating* the context, not *changing* it. Its value is passive | Persistent `● {name}` indicator |
| Context switch itself is mistaken for a notifiable event | It is not. Nobody wants "Sam switched to the band" alerts. The switch is written to the audit trail and is **pullable** (visible on the org's activity surface), never **pushed** | Nothing. Visible on request in the band's audit trail |
| Malicious: user crafts a request asserting a context they do not hold | Rejected at the call site by the Roles & Permissions cross-cut, which resolves the mandate itself and never trusts a client-asserted party. The switcher is a UI over a derivation, not the authority | 403 from the cross-cut. Attempt is logged |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Context list resolving on app open | Current context renders **immediately from the last-known device value** (it is view state, DT-05) so the header never flickers or renders party-less; the list itself shows skeleton rows. A stale current-context is safe to *display* because every write revalidates the mandate at the call site |
| **Empty** | Exactly one context exists — Fan, or a new Musician with no aliases and no mandates | **The switcher is not rendered at all.** No collapsed control, no disabled chip, no "you have 1 context" copy. See Role Lens: the absence is the feature |
| **Populated** | 2+ contexts | Persistent header indicator: avatar + party name in text, colour-differentiated for org and represented contexts. Full list on tap, stably ordered, type-ahead filter at ≥8 |
| **Error** | Context list fetch failed | Falls back to the **self** context and says so explicitly: "We couldn't load your other contexts. You're acting as yourself." Never silently acts as the last-cached org. Retry offered |
| **Partial** | Mandate exists but is scoped narrowly (can read the band's inbox, cannot spend), or a multi-type org grants capability on one type only | Context available; unavailable actions **absent** rather than shown-and-denied. The header indicator is identical to a full-mandate context — the narrowing is expressed by what is on the page, not by decorating the switcher |
| **Reverted** | Org/represented context held for 12 h with no activity (shared-machine mitigation, DT-12) | Header shows `● {self}` with a one-time note: "You were acting as {org}. Switched back to you." One tap to return |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Acting context is purely a UI convenience and could be inferred from the resource being acted on | Plausible simplification — "you're in the band's inbox, so you're obviously the band" | ❌ REJECTED — inference silently guesses at the one thing that must never be guessed. The acting party determines who is contractually bound, who is credited, and where money lands. A wrong inference on a split attestation is exactly the unfalsifiable record the platform exists to prevent. It must be explicit and visible. |
| DT-02 | The switcher is a cross-cut, not a feature of this domain | The gate's own test — it touches every domain | ⏳ SPLIT, not rejected — the *mechanism* (acting party propagation and authorisation at every call site) genuinely is a cross-cut and is recorded as one; the *derivation rule* and the *switcher surface* are identity's product. The seam matches the one the domain overview already draws (entity model here, enforcement in the cross-cut). Recorded in the domain CX file and escalated. |
| DT-03 | The Fan must never see this control, and that requires the empty state to be absence, not a disabled control | `personas.md` Fan Unique Constraint: "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity... their surface must be a different product wearing the same brand" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it generalises: a derived context list gives this for free. Because Fans have no aliases and no mandates, the list has one entry and the control does not exist. No feature flag, no fan-mode branch. The derivation *is* the fan-safety mechanism. |
| DT-04 | Attribution is uniform: the counterparty sees the acting party and nothing else | The obvious model, and the one every "post as page" implementation ships | ❌ REJECTED for representation contexts. | DT-05 | The acting context is account-level state, synced across the user's devices and tabs | How it "should" work — one identity, one current context, consistent everywhere | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the failure mode nobody designs for. Account-level context means tab 2's switch silently rebinds tab 1, and they reply to a venue as the wrong artist with no signal that anything changed. Google's account switcher has precisely this bug and it is merely annoying there; here it misattributes a binding communication. Context is **view state**: per-tab, per-device, bound at composer open, revalidated at submit. |
| DT-06 | Notification scope should follow the acting context — you are the band, so you get the band's notifications | Symmetry with the inbox scope, which does follow context | ❌ REJECTED as stated, ✅ CONFIRMED in a narrower form. Delivery is **union across every context**, each notification badged with its party. Only the inbox **view** scopes. The distinction between delivery scope and view scope is load-bearing and was unnamed. |
| DT-07 | Platform admin/support "act as user" can reuse this switcher | Cheap reuse — it is the same shape | ❌ REJECTED — and the reason is structural, not policy. What makes this switcher safe is D-01: every context is *derived* from a fact the user's own graph contains. Admin impersonation has no alias, no membership, no mandate — so admitting it requires a context that is not derived, which breaks the derivation invariant for every user simultaneously and reintroduces the whole stale-context class. Admin impersonation is a separate mechanism, outside this domain, always dual-attributed, always logged. Routed to 24 / personas Q-02. |
| DT-08 | Shadow parties (01.05.01) should be actable — someone has to speak for the unclaimed party | Tempting, because the Producer created the shadow party and holds the context of the session | ❌ REJECTED — a shadow party is a **subject** of other people's attestations, never an actor. It has no owner, so no alias ownership, no membership, no mandate, and therefore no derivation path into any switcher. This is not a rule that had to be added: the derivation already forbids it. And it matters — an actable shadow party is a sockpuppet with which a Producer could attest their own splits from both sides, converting the growth loop (domain CX-04) into an abuse loop and directly worsening domain Q-06. The derivation defends the thesis for free, twice (see also DT-03). |
| DT-09 | One action can legitimately have two acting parties (the studio invoices, the engineer is credited) | The file's own Q-01 — and it is a real music case, not a hypothetical: this is how every studio bills | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real case, ❌ REJECTED as a two-actor model. The case dissolves under a distinction the sweep never drew: **acting party** (who performs and is bound) vs **subject party** (who the fact is *about*). These are two actions in a bundle, not one action with two actors. The invoice's acting party is the studio; the credit's subject is the engineer, chosen inside the credit action. 01.01.02 D-02 already established exactly this shape for aliases — a Producer credits a drummer under the drummer's real name while acting as themselves. The one-acting-party-per-action model holds; it needed a companion concept, not an exception. Resolves Q-01. |
| DT-10 | An org context is a single-occupancy context | Unstated assumption in the original file — the switcher lists parties as if one person occupies one at a time | ❌ REJECTED — an org context is a **shared inbox** and nothing in the derivation prevents two mandate holders occupying it simultaneously. Two band members both reply as the band to the same promoter; the promoter gets two contradictory answers from one party. Considered and rejected: **locking** the context (a lock on a band's inbox is worse than a double-reply — it makes the band unresponsive, and unresponsive is how bookings are lost). Mitigation is presence, not exclusion: "Sam is also replying as The Hold Steady." Same collision recurs one level down on shared aliases, which is further evidence for 01.01.02 Q-01. |
| DT-11 | The mandate vocabulary already covers what the switcher is used for | Implicit in D-01 — contexts derive from mandates, so mandates must describe the actions | ✅ CONFIRMED as a genuine gap, and it is upstream of this file. 01.03.03 D-01 fixes the coarse activity set as book/sign/spend/list/release/settle/administer — **there is no communication verb**. Yet the switcher's dominant daily use is communication: replying to an enquiry, answering a promoter, chatting to a fan. 01.03.02's own Happy Path has an agent "replying to booking enquiries" under live-booking scope, so communication is currently *implied by* `book` rather than granted. That means granting `book` silently grants the right to speak as the party, and there is no way to grant speech without commerce or commerce without speech. Raised as Q-02 — resolving it means changing a sibling's locked vocabulary, which this file must not do. |
| DT-12 | Context persistence across sessions is a pure convenience with no downside | The obvious UX default — remember where I was | ❌ REJECTED — the music-specific counter-case is the **shared professional machine**: a studio front desk, a rehearsal-room laptop, a venue box-office terminal. `personas.md` puts the Operator "on a phone at a loading dock" and the Producer "inside a DAW, not a browser"; neither is at a personal desk. Sticky context on a shared machine means the next engineer acts as the previous engineer's studio, and does so *invisibly*, because the indicator states the context correctly — it is the human's assumption that is stale, not the display. Mitigation: revert to self after 12 h of inactivity, and re-confirm the context on the first attested/money action after a session gap. |
| DT-13 | Ordering the context list by recency is obviously right for the 20-context manager | The breadth pass's own open marker said "needs ordering by recency/relevance" — and every switcher in software does this | ❌ REJECTED — recency ordering is *itself* a wrong-context hazard. A list that reorders between openings is a list you cannot learn, so the hurried tap (the Operator at the loading dock, the exact user recency is meant to serve) lands on whatever migrated into position 2 since yesterday. Muscle memory is the safety mechanism here and recency destroys it. Order is stable: pinned → self → aliases → alphabetical, frozen while open. Pinning is the user's own recency mechanism, applied deliberately rather than inferred. Resolves the ordering gap. |
| DT-14 | Reversibility is a property of the action's type (a post is undoable, a signature is not) | The file's own Q-02, framed as a taxonomy of actions | ⏳ REFRAMED, then CONFIRMED. Type is a proxy for the real variable: **whether a third party has relied on the fact**. This is why "published listing with 0 views" and "published listing with 1 view" have different answers despite being the same action type, and why an attested credit has no window at all — the counter-party's reliance begins at attestation, instantly, by definition. Reliance-based reversibility yields the table in Behavior and, importantly, yields *no undo* precisely where D-08 (the record of work is never destroyed) demands it. Resolves Q-02. |
| DT-15 | A deep link may carry a context and auto-switch the user into it | Convenience — the link knows which party the resource belongs to | ❌ REJECTED — it is an attack. A link that silently rebinds the acting party lets an attacker deliver "reply to this as your band" to a member, or worse, get an attested action taken under a party the user never chose. It also directly contradicts DT-01: the acting party must be explicit, and a URL is not the user being explicit. The link may **pre-select the row in the offer**; the switch always costs one deliberate tap. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **Roles & Permissions** (cross-cut) — **the hard seam.** This file derives and presents contexts; the cross-cut resolves and authorises the acting party at every call site and never trusts a client-asserted party (see the malicious-assertion edge case). Stated in the domain overview and D-02.
- Touches **01.03.01 Membership Records** and **01.03.03 Mandate Scope** ([../01.03-membership-representation-mandate/](../01.03-membership-representation-mandate/)) — **derivation dependency**: mandated memberships are context source #3. Membership *without* mandate yields no context. Domain CX-01. **New from 01.03.03 D-07..D-10 (DQ-02.4/.5/.6/.7)**: for a band, a `confirmed` `permanent` edge is mandated by default, so the context appears on confirmation with no configuration step; `touring`/`staff`/`honorary` members and members of every non-band org type get **no band context** until an explicit grant, and that absence must be explained rather than merely enforced. Also the source of the communication-verb gap (DT-11, Q-02).
- Touches **01.03.02 Representation & Roster** ([../01.03-membership-representation-mandate/01.03.02-representation-roster-relationships.md](../01.03-membership-representation-mandate/01.03.02-representation-roster-relationships.md)) — **derivation dependency + attribution rule**: represented parties are context source #4, and are the only context type that is *always* dual-attributed (DT-04). Term expiry removes the context with no sweep (their D-04).
- Touches **01.01.02 Aliases** ([./01.01.02-artist-names-aliases-projects.md](./01.01.02-artist-names-aliases-projects.md)) — **derivation dependency**: aliases are context source #2 (CX-02). Retirement removes the context but preserves drafts authored in it (resolves the CX-02 §2 pending). Shared aliases inherit the shared-inbox collision (DT-10), which is fresh evidence for their Q-01.
- Touches **01.01.01 Person Record** ([./01.01.01-person-record-role-facets.md](./01.01.01-person-record-role-facets.md)) — **derivation dependency**: context #1, always present (CX-01). Note the seam: **facet addition never creates a context.** A facet changes what you can do; a context changes who you are. The switcher appears at the second *context*, not the second facet.
- Touches **01.01.04 Legal vs Public Identity** ([./01.01.04-legal-identity-vs-public-identity.md](./01.01.04-legal-identity-vs-public-identity.md)) — **permission intersection**: the acting context selects *whose* legal identity a disclosure moment discloses (CX-05). Signing as the band discloses the band's registered details and the mandate that permitted it; signing as yourself discloses your legal name. Getting this wrong either over-discloses a person or produces an unenforceable signature.
- Touches **01.05.01 Shadow Party Creation** ([../01.05-profile-claiming-verification/01.05.01-shadow-party-creation-invitation.md](../01.05-profile-claiming-verification/01.05.01-shadow-party-creation-invitation.md)) — **negative dependency, load-bearing**: a shadow party can never be an acting context (DT-08). This is the structural reason shadow parties cannot be used as sockpuppets to self-attest splits, and it is direct input to domain Q-06 (shadow party creation limits).
- Touches **01.10 Estates & Legacy Accounts** ([../01.10-estates-legacy-accounts.md](../01.10-estates-legacy-accounts.md)) — **derivation, no new concept**: an executor's authority is a representation edge, so the estate appears as a normal dual-attributed context.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([../../02-credits-attribution/](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **shared entity + the thesis moment**: the attesting party on a credit is the acting context at attestation time, and the credited **subject** is a separate choice made inside the credit action (DT-09). Both facts are recorded; conflating them is the Q-01 confusion.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([../../09-rights-ownership/](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **binding authority**: the acting party on a split signature is who is bound. A signature under a context whose mandate lacked `sign` is void (01.03.03), which the cross-cut must catch before, not after.
- Touches **Payments** (cross-cut, routed to `/create-prd`) — **money destination**: the acting context determines where money lands. The switcher does **not** gate on payout readiness; a context with no payout method acts freely and blocks at settlement (see edge case). Identity never reverses money — reversal is a counter-transaction there.
- Touches **Audit Trail** (cross-cut) — **every context switch and every attributed write is logged immutably.** The switch is **pullable, never pushed**: visible on the org's activity surface on request, never a notification. This log is the only real protection against a mandate holder acting against the party's interest within scope (01.03.02, 01.03.03).
- Touches **Notifications** (cross-cut) — **delivery is union across all contexts, badged by party; only the inbox view scopes** (DT-06). This is a hard requirement on the notification cross-cut, not a preference: context-scoped delivery rebuilds the fragmentation the platform sells the cure for.
- Touches **Real-Time Presence** (cross-cut, D-15) — the shared-inbox presence indicator (DT-10) rides the presence mechanism already extracted from domain 08. No new mechanism needed; a new consumer.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — admin impersonation is explicitly *not* this switcher (DT-07) and belongs there or to architecture.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Context list is derived from person + aliases + mandated memberships + active representations — never configured | Removes a whole class of stale-permission bugs, needs no revocation sweep, and pays out three times for free: Fan-safety (DT-03), shadow-party non-actability (DT-08), and the structural argument against admin impersonation (DT-07) |
| D-02 | This file owns derivation + surface; the Roles & Permissions cross-cut owns propagation + enforcement | Matches the seam the domain overview already drew; avoids duplicating enforcement in 24 nodes |
| D-03 | Current context is persistently visible as **avatar + party name in text**, never avatar-only, never in a menu | The cost of acting as the wrong party is contractual and financial. A 24 px avatar at arm's length on a loading dock is not a statement of identity |
| D-04 | Reattribution is never offered at any tier. Wrong-context actions are retracted-and-retaken, or they stand | An attestation that can be reassigned after the fact is not evidence of anything, which would void the wedge (D-18) |
| D-05 | One acting party per action, always. The **subject** party (who is credited / who the fact is about) is a separate choice made inside the action | DT-09 — resolves Q-01 without an exception to the model. 01.01.02 D-02 already established this shape for aliases |
| D-06 | The context list is **stably ordered and never reorders itself**: pinned (≤5) → self → aliases (creation order) → orgs and represented parties (alphabetical). Frozen while open. Type-ahead filter at ≥8 contexts | DT-13 — recency ordering destroys the muscle memory that is the actual safety mechanism, for exactly the hurried user it claims to serve |
| D-07 | Reversibility is determined by **third-party reliance**, not action type: attested facts and money 0 s; communications and publications 60 s; private state unlimited. Published-with-0-views unpublishes cleanly; published-with-views retracts to a tombstone | DT-14 — resolves Q-02, and yields "no undo" exactly where domain D-08 requires the record of work to be indestructible |
| D-08 | Acting context is **view state**, not account state: per-tab, per-device, bound at composer open, revalidated at submit | DT-05 — account-level context silently rebinds other tabs, which misattributes binding communications for the platform's highest-volume switcher user |
| D-09 | Attribution depth varies by context type × action class: alias → alias only; org → org, plus the human on binding actions; representation → always dual (`{Agent} on behalf of {Artist}`) | DT-04 — a uniform model answers the Operator's standing question ("can this person bind the artist?") wrongly |
| D-10 | Notification **delivery** is union across all contexts, badged by party; only the inbox **view** scopes | DT-06 — context-scoped delivery rebuilds the six-tab fragmentation problem inside the product that sells the cure |
| D-11 | Org contexts are shared-occupancy. Collisions are mitigated by **presence, never by locking** | DT-10 — a lock on a band's inbox makes the band unresponsive, which costs more than a double-reply |
| D-12 | Org/represented contexts revert to self after 12 h of inactivity, and re-confirm on the first attested/money action after a session gap | DT-12 — the shared studio/venue machine is the normal case in this industry, not an edge case |
| D-13 | Deep links never auto-switch. They may pre-select the row in an offer; the switch always costs one deliberate tap | DT-15 — silent rebind by URL is an attack, and contradicts DT-01 |
| D-14 | The switcher lists **parties, not capabilities**. A multi-type org (D-06 global) is one context; capability narrowing is within-context surface filtering | Keeps the switcher a list of *who*, which is the only question it exists to answer. Capability is a property of the page, not of the identity |
| D-15 | Context switches are logged and **pullable, never pushed** | The band's audit trail is the protection; "Sam switched to the band" as a notification is noise nobody wants and would train people to ignore the badge that matters |
