# Feature: Acting Context Switcher

> **Number**: 01.01.03
> **Parent**: [Person Identity & Roles](./01.01-person-identity-roles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The affordance that answers "who is doing this?" — the surface where a person chooses whether they are acting as themselves, as one of their aliases, or on behalf of an organisation they hold a mandate for. Every write on the platform is attributed to an acting party, and a multi-hyphenate switches contexts several times a day: replying to a booking enquiry as their band, invoicing as their studio, listing a cymbal as themselves.

**Scope boundary**: this file owns the *product* half — the context list, how it is derived, how the switch is made visible, and what it changes. The *enforcement* half (propagating the acting party through every call site and authorising it) is the Roles & Permissions cross-cut's, and the transport mechanics are architecture. See Decisions D-02.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Switches between self, each alias, and each band/org they hold a mandate for. Sees the current context persistently — not in a hidden menu — because the cost of posting as the wrong party is public and embarrassing. Contexts they can act in are derived, never manually listed. |
| Producer | Full | Switches between self and their studio org. This matters most at the capture moment: a credit or split captured in 07 records *which* party attested it, and a Producer attesting as their studio carries different weight from attesting personally. |
| Operator | Full | Acts as the venue/studio org for enquiries, bookings, listings and settlement (16, 17, 19). Often on a phone at a loading dock — the switcher must survive a small screen and a hurried tap, and must make "you are the venue right now" unmissable. |
| Fan | None | Has exactly one context — themselves. The switcher is not rendered, not collapsed, not greyed out: absent. A fan who never acts for anyone must never learn this concept exists. This absence is the feature. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The context list is **derived**, never configured. It is the union of: the person record itself (01.01.01), each alias they own (01.01.02), each organisation where their membership carries an acting mandate (01.03.01 + 01.03.03), and each party they represent under a representation agreement (01.03.02). Losing a mandate removes a context with no separate cleanup step.

Switching context changes: the attributed actor on every write, the inbox/notification scope, the surfaces shown, and the money destination on any transaction. That last one is why the switch must be conspicuous rather than convenient.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is browsing as themselves; the current context is visible in the persistent header.
2. A booking enquiry arrives for their band. They open it.
3. System shows the enquiry is addressed to the band, and that they are currently acting as themselves.
4. They switch context to the band — one tap, from the context they're already looking at, not from a settings page.
5. They reply. The reply is attributed to the band, and the venue sees the band as the counterparty — not a person they've never heard of.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User acts in the wrong context (posts as self, meant the band) | The action is attributed to the party that was active. Reattribution after the fact is **not** silently offered — a credit or a contract attributed to the wrong party and then rewritten is a provenance hole | An undo window on low-stakes actions `[PENDING — Step 5: which actions are reversible]`; nothing on attested facts |
| Mandate is revoked while the user is mid-action in that context | Context disappears from the list; the in-flight action fails authorisation at the call site (cross-cut's job), not silently succeeds | "You no longer have permission to act for {org}." Draft preserved, not discarded |
| User holds mandates for 20 orgs (a busy manager, an agency) | List is long. Needs ordering by recency/relevance `[PENDING — Step 5]` | Searchable context list |
| Deep link into an org-scoped resource while in the wrong context | System offers to switch rather than showing a bare 403 — the user has the right, just not the active context | "This is your band's booking. Switch to {band} to reply?" |
| A single action legitimately spans contexts (invoicing as the studio for work credited to the person) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — real in music (studio bills, engineer is credited) and the naive one-context-per-action model does not obviously hold it | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Context list resolving | Current context shown from cache; list skeletons |
| Empty | Only one context exists (Fan, or a new Musician with no aliases/bands) | Switcher not rendered at all — see Role Lens |
| Populated | 2+ contexts | Persistent current-context indicator; list on tap |
| Error | Context list fetch failed | Falls back to self context and says so — never silently acts as the last-cached org |
| Partial | Mandate exists but is scoped narrowly (e.g. can read the band's inbox, cannot spend) | Context available; unavailable actions absent rather than shown-and-denied |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Acting context is purely a UI convenience and could be inferred from the resource being acted on | Plausible simplification — "you're in the band's inbox, so you're obviously the band" | ❌ REJECTED — inference silently guesses at the one thing that must never be guessed. The acting party determines who is contractually bound, who is credited, and where money lands. A wrong inference on a split attestation is exactly the unfalsifiable record the platform exists to prevent. It must be explicit and visible. |
| DT-02 | The switcher is a cross-cut, not a feature of this domain | The gate's own test — it touches every domain | ⏳ SPLIT, not rejected — the *mechanism* (acting party propagation and authorisation at every call site) genuinely is a cross-cut and is recorded as one; the *derivation rule* and the *switcher surface* are identity's product. The seam matches the one the domain overview already draws (entity model here, enforcement in the cross-cut). Recorded in the domain CX file and escalated. |
| DT-03 | The Fan must never see this control, and that requires the empty state to be absence, not a disabled control | `personas.md` Fan Unique Constraint: "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity... their surface must be a different product wearing the same brand" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it generalises: a derived context list gives this for free. Because Fans have no aliases and no mandates, the list has one entry and the control does not exist. No feature flag, no fan-mode branch. The derivation *is* the fan-safety mechanism. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Context list is derived from aliases + memberships + representations, never configured | Removes a whole class of stale-permission bugs and gives Fan-safety for free (DT-03) |
| D-02 | This file owns derivation + surface; the cross-cut owns propagation + enforcement | Matches the seam the domain overview already drew; avoids duplicating enforcement in 24 nodes |
| D-03 | Current context is persistently visible, not hidden in a menu | The cost of acting as the wrong party is contractual and financial, not cosmetic |
