# Feature: Mandate Scope & Delegated Authority

> **Number**: 01.03.03
> **Parent**: [Membership, Representation & Mandate](./01.03-membership-representation-mandate-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-29

## Overview

What a membership or representation edge actually **permits** — the difference between being in a band and being able to sign a contract for it. A mandate is the authority attached to an edge: which activities the holder may perform on the party's behalf, up to what value, with what constraints. This feature owns the mandate *data* and the surfaces where people grant and read it; the Roles & Permissions cross-cut owns enforcing it at every call site.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Grants and reads mandates on their band: who can accept a gig, who can spend, who can sign. Reads their own mandate elsewhere ("in this band I can book, not sign"). The surface must state authority in plain language — "Sam can accept bookings but can't sign contracts" — because a permission matrix is not a thing a drummer will read. |
| Producer | Config | Configures mandates for studio staff (an assistant can book the room, not issue refunds). Crucially, they *rely* on mandates at the capture moment: a split attested by someone with no mandate to bind the band is worthless, and they need to know that at the session, not later. |
| Operator | Config | Configures venue staff mandates (a booker can hold and confirm; only the owner settles). Also *reads* the counterparty's mandate before advancing a show — "is the person confirming this actually able to?" |
| Fan | None | No mandates, ever. A fan acts only for themselves. This is the structural reason the acting-context switcher never renders for them (01.01.03 DT-03). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A mandate qualifies an edge (membership or representation) with a scope. Scope dimensions: **activity** — a closed set of **seven**: book / sign / spend / list / release / settle / administer (D-01, ratified at DQ-02.3) — **value ceiling** (can accept a £300 gig, not a £30k tour; those figures are narrative illustration, **never** the platform default, which is D-08's), **party** (which org or which alias), and inherited **term** from the edge itself. On a **representation** edge the activity axis is conjoined with a second axis the edge supplies — **domains**, a subset of `live booking / recording / publishing / sync / merch` (01.03.02 D-05) — and authority requires both (D-11). A **membership** edge supplies no domain list and resolves to **all** domains, so a member's mandate is bounded by activity, ceiling, party and inherited term alone (01.03.01 D-19).

Two rules make it coherent:

1. **Mandates cannot exceed the granting party's own authority.** A manager with live-booking scope cannot sub-delegate recording scope they never had.
2. **Every org must always have at least one full mandate holder.** Otherwise it is unadministrable, which is why departure (01.03.01) and creation (01.02.02) both check it.

Enforcement is not here. This file produces the answer; the cross-cut asks the question at every call site.

### The default mandate — what an unconfigured party runs on

Almost every band will live on the default forever (sub-domain CX-01: most bands never open this surface), so the default **is** the product. It is ratified, and it is not one rule but four.

| # | Rule | Applies to |
|---|------|------------|
| **Peer seed** (D-07) | Every **`confirmed`** membership edge whose current capacity period is `permanent` carries the **full seven-activity set**. The creator's owning mandate (01.02.02 D-07) is the **first instance of that same set — not a superior grade** | Band orgs |
| **Value ceiling** (D-08) | Seeded authority is capped at **USD 1,000 per act**, configurable per band. Acts above the ceiling escalate to the owning-mandate holder, who is uncapped | Band orgs |
| **Capacity keying** (D-09) | The seed attaches to `permanent` only. `touring`, `staff` and `honorary` carry **presence with zero authority** until something is granted explicitly. This is a **rebuttable default**, never a derivation | Band orgs |
| **Band only** (D-10) | `studio`, `venue`, `label`, `shop` and `agency` get **no seed**: the creator's owning mandate plus explicit grants, and nothing else | Every other org type |

Three consequences follow directly and must be legible on the surface, not discovered:

- **Confirmation is the moment authority arrives.** An unconfirmed edge still carries zero mandate (01.03.01 Behavior rule 2), so seeding never hands authority to someone who has not accepted. But acceptance now *is* a grant of authority, and sub-domain CX-01 §4 says silently gaining authority is a security event — so the invitation and the acceptance screen must both state what the accepter is about to receive.
- **The seed includes `administer`.** Any confirmed permanent member can therefore alter another member's mandate, invite, and remove. That is **wider than the equal-shares general partnership** 01.04.01 D-04 discloses as the legal default, so **the governance disclosure copy and this mandate surface must say the same thing** — a band told "equal partners" and then shown a screen where a bandmate can remove them has been told two different stories about itself. **`administer` does not reach authorship** (DQ-R2-01): seeded or granted, with or without the `publishing` domain, it confers no authority to name a publisher over a writer share the represented party did not write. A co-writer's publisher rows stay that co-writer's alone. The boundary is the **authorship** of the share, not delegation itself: `administer` plus `publishing` on a representation edge is exactly how a publisher administers the represented writer's **own** share (`01.03.02`:79), and D-06 bars only the reach past it (`09.01.04` D-06, which stands over the mandate).
- **A touring or staff member has no acting context for the band** until granted something, because contexts derive from mandated edges (01.01.03 D-01, domain CX-01). They are in the lineup and they cannot post as the band. A band may grant them the full set explicitly, and the surface must say so rather than leaving the absence to be inferred.

### Happy Path

1. Band's owning member opens the band's people surface.
2. For each member, mandates are shown as plain-language statements, not a checkbox grid — and for a band that has configured nothing, those statements **are the defaults**, shown as defaults rather than as choices someone made. Each carries a second sentence saying what it depends on: *"Sam can do anything for this band up to $1,000. That's the default for permanent members — you can change it, and touring or staff members get nothing until you give it to them."*
3. Nobody has to grant the drummer anything: as a confirmed permanent member they already hold all seven activities up to the default USD 1,000 ceiling (D-07, D-08, D-09), because they are in the band.
4. The drummer's acting context for the band (01.01.03) offers every activity within that ceiling, and nothing above it.
5. A $2,000 offer arrives. The drummer sees it but cannot accept it; the system routes it to the owning-mandate holder — who is uncapped (01.02.02 D-07) and guaranteed to exist (D-04) — rather than dead-ending (D-06). **This path is live in the default state**, not only after someone configures something.
6. The band tunes from there: the touring keys player hired for the summer holds presence and no authority until someone grants it (D-09), and the band may raise or lower its own ceiling (D-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Action exceeds the mandate's value ceiling | Blocked at the call site (cross-cut) and **escalated**, not just refused — the offer routes to a holder who can accept. **Reachable in the default state** since D-08: every seeded mandate carries the USD 1,000 ceiling, so there is always a threshold to exceed and always an uncapped owning-mandate holder to escalate to (D-04, 01.02.02 D-07) | "This is above your limit. Sent to {name} to approve." |
| Last full-mandate holder tries to leave or be removed | Blocked (01.03.01 Edge Cases, 01.02.02 Edge Cases). See domain CX-06 | "Give someone else admin first." |
| Mandate granted by someone who did not have it to give | Prevented by rule 1. Silent sub-delegation escalation is a classic authority bug and the reason the rule is explicit | "You can't grant permissions you don't have." |
| Member acts within mandate but against the band's wishes | Not a platform failure — the mandate was granted. Governance (01.04.01) is the remedy, and the audit trail is the evidence | Action attributed, logged, visible to the band |
| A band with no mandates configured at all (the overwhelmingly common case) | **RESOLVED — D-07..D-10.** Every confirmed permanent member holds all seven activities up to USD 1,000; the creator's owning mandate is the same set, uncapped; touring/staff/honorary hold nothing; no other org type is seeded at all. The surface states this as a default, in the same words the governance disclosure uses (01.04.01 D-05) | "Everyone who's a permanent member can act for the band up to $1,000. Above that, it goes to {name}." |
| **The split-push** — a member uses seeded authority to push a deal or a split through while the others are not paying attention (`personas.md`'s worst-intentional behaviour) | **Accepted cost of D-07, stated plainly.** Peer seeding maximally enables it. There is no pre-act gate below the ceiling; the defences are all after the fact and all already ratified: universal member visibility of every movement (01.04.03 D-01), the attributed audit trail, and the ceiling itself for anything above USD 1,000 | Every member sees the act, who did it and when — nobody has to ask |
| A **touring** or **staff** member tries to act for the band | Blocked — they hold presence, not authority (D-09), so no acting context for the band exists for them at all. The absence must be **explained on the surface**, not merely enforced, or it reads as a bug to the person it hits | "Touring members don't get to act for {band} unless the band gives them permission. Ask {name}." |
| A band that came into existence from a **shared alias capture** (01.01.02 E-28, domain CX-12) | Everyone named at capture who becomes a confirmed permanent member inherits the full seeded set — the band was created sideways, but the default is the same default. Domain CX-12 already requires the governance-capture skip to be **legible, not silent**; that requirement now carries authority as well as governance, because skipping capture no longer means "nothing was decided" | At capture: "Everyone here will be able to act for the band up to $1,000 each. You can change that later." |
| A **studio, venue, label, shop or agency** with nothing configured | No seed (D-10). The creator holds the owning mandate; every other member holds presence only until granted something explicitly, which is what the Producer's and Operator's Role Lens already describe them doing | Staff surface shows explicit grants and nothing implied |
| Mandate revoked mid-flight (member is negotiating a booking when it is pulled) | In-flight action fails authorisation at the call site; draft preserved | "You no longer have permission to do this." |
| Off-platform authority (the band told Sam he could sign, verbally) | Invisible. The platform only knows recorded mandates — and this gap is the whole reason the feature exists | — |

### States

|-------|---------|---------------|

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Mandate is a permission system and therefore belongs entirely to the Roles & Permissions cross-cut | The gate's cross-cut test — and a verifier already made this argument against the whole domain | ⏳ SPLIT, deliberately, and the seam is the one the domain overview already drew. The cross-cut answers "may this actor do this to this resource" — a mechanism. This file answers "what has this band agreed Sam may do for it" — an agreement between people, with a UI, a consent moment and a governance link. The cross-cut *reads* the mandate. Collapsing them would put a product agreement inside an enforcement mechanism, where no musician would ever find it. |
| DT-02 | Mandates should be granular — a full permission matrix per member | The enterprise instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a permission matrix is a thing no band will ever configure, so every band would run on defaults, and the feature would be decorative. Music's real mandates are coarse and few: can book, can spend, can sign. Plain-language statements over a handful of coarse activities will get used; a matrix will not. (**Prose corrected at DQ-02.3** — this annotation originally said "four", which was incidental to its argument and contradicted D-01's ratified seven. The enum is seven; the argument against a matrix is unchanged. Accepted: seven plain-language statements per member is closer to the matrix this annotation dislikes than four would have been.) |
| DT-03 | The default mandate is the entire product decision here, and it is a trap in both directions | Reasoning about what happens when nobody configures anything — which is what will happen | ✅ CONFIRMED. "Everyone can do everything" matches how bands see themselves and enables `personas.md`'s worst-intentional Producer behaviour ("pushes a bigger split through while contributors aren't paying attention"). "Only the creator" is safe but makes whoever typed the name first the owner of the band — a structural injustice the platform would be inventing. Neither is right, and the answer is a product decision (Q-01). Nobody in the sweep named this. **ANSWERED (DQ-02.4/DQ-02.5): both horns are taken, not one.** The custodial horn is refused outright on this annotation's own reasoning (D-07 peer seed). The "everyone can do everything" horn is taken with a bound rather than whole — a value ceiling (D-08) and a capacity key (D-09) — so the split-push this annotation names is still enabled below USD 1,000 and is contained above it. The residual cost is accepted and recorded in the Edge Cases, not designed away. |

- Touches **Audit Trail** (cross-cut) — mandate grants and revocations must be logged; a mandate is an authority claim and its history is evidence.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Mandates are coarse (book / sign / spend / list / release / settle / administer), not a granular matrix | DT-02 — granularity nobody configures is decorative. **Enum ratified at seven (DQ-02.3)**: this decision and the Behavior section already agreed on the same seven; DT-02's "four" was an incidental count inside an argument about matrices and has been corrected to "a handful". `release` and `settle` stay individually addressable — the seed (D-07) and the money authorities in 01.04.03 both need them separable |
| D-02 | Authority is stated in plain language, not as a permission grid | The audience is a drummer, not an IT admin |
| D-03 | A mandate can never exceed the granting party's own authority | Prevents silent sub-delegation escalation |
| D-04 | Every org must retain at least one full mandate holder | Enforced at departure (01.03.01) and creation (01.02.02); an ownerless org is unadministrable |
| D-05 | Resolution fails closed | An unresolvable mandate must never yield elevated authority |
| D-06 | Over-limit actions escalate to a capable holder rather than dead-ending | A refusal with no route is how people take the deal off-platform. **Made operative by D-08**: with no default ceiling this decision was inert by construction — nothing was ever over limit — so the escalation path and Happy Path steps 5 existed only for bands that had configured something |
| D-07 | **Peer seed: every `confirmed` band membership edge carries the full seven-activity set. The creator's owning mandate is the first instance of that same set, not a superior grade** | DQ-02.4, resolving Q-01. DT-03 is this file's own verdict on the alternative — custodial seeding makes whoever typed the name first the owner of the band, "a structural injustice the platform would be inventing" — and `personas.md` requires the lazy path to be the correct path while sub-domain CX-01 confirms the lazy path dominates. 01.03.01 costed the alternative operationally: under "creator only" every band is one death away from unadministrable. Directionally consistent with the equal-shares partnership default 01.04.01 D-04 already discloses. **Accepted cost**: this maximally enables `personas.md`'s split-push; detection is after the fact, via 01.04.03 D-01 transparency and the audit trail |
| D-08 | **Default value ceiling of USD 1,000 per act on seeded authority, configurable per band. Over-ceiling acts escalate to the owning-mandate holder, who is uncapped** | DQ-02.5. Repairs peer seeding's one structural defect (D-06 inert) using only ratified parts — value ceiling is already a scope dimension here, and the escalation target is guaranteed to exist by 01.02.02 D-07 plus D-04. Denominated in **USD** per `meta/constraints.md`'s US-first revision; the GBP figures in this file and in 01.04.01 are narrative illustration and **must not** be read as defaults. **Accepted**: asymmetry returns above the ceiling — the creator is the only uncapped holder — which is why 01.03.01 D-14's succession offer and its Q-04 selection rule stay live for the over-ceiling half instead of becoming dead code |
| D-09 | **The seed attaches to `permanent` capacity only, as a REBUTTABLE DEFAULT. `touring`, `staff` and `honorary` carry presence with zero authority until explicitly granted** | DQ-02.6. D-07's justification is symmetry among the people who *constitute* the band; it does not reach a hired touring player or an honorary name, and 01.03.01 already treats touring governance standing as different. **This required amending sub-domain R-02 in the same pass** — that rejected pair bars *deriving* mandate from capacity ("drummers can't sign"), and it still does; what is now permitted is a rebuttable **default** keyed to capacity, which anyone with `administer` can overturn in one act. A band may grant a touring member the full set explicitly and the surface must say so. Consequence: a touring hire does not inherit `settle` authority over band money on day one (01.04.03) |
| D-10 | **Band only. `studio`, `venue`, `label`, `shop` and `agency` default to the owning mandate plus explicit grants — no seed** | DQ-02.7. The whole case for a peer seed is the invisible general-partnership default 01.04.01 D-04 discloses, and 01.04 index D-01 scopes that to bands in terms while denying it for other orgs ("a limited company has directors and articles; a studio has an owner"). Uniform seeding would push `settle` and `sign` onto every confirmed staff edge of a venue or shop, contradicting the Operator Role Lens in spirit and forcing trader-status and payments consequences nobody accepted. **HONESTY NOTE**: no source states what a non-band org defaults to when nothing is configured — this is a ratification, not an inheritance. Two default models must therefore be explained to users, not one |
| D-11 | **Authority on a representation edge is the conjunction of two flat axes — the action's verb must be in the edge's `activities` AND the action's domain in the edge's `domains`. A membership edge carries the activity axis only and resolves to all domains** | DQ-R2-01, ratified by the owner 2026-07-29; the axis pair is specified at 01.03.02 D-05 and the membership rule at 01.03.01 D-19. **Compatible with DT-02 by construction**: two flat lists are at most 7 + 5 = **12** plain-language statements per edge — "may book and settle, for live and recording" — not the 35 addressable cells a 7×5 grid would be, so the matrix this file rejected stays rejected and D-02 still holds. The conjunction happens at the call site, where the Roles & Permissions cross-cut already asks the question; this file supplies both lists and nothing else. **Accepted cost**: territory, term and commission stay edge-level and do not vary per domain |
