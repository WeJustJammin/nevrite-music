# Feature: Membership Records & Lifecycle

> **Number**: 01.03.01
> **Parent**: [Membership, Representation & Mandate](./01.03-membership-representation-mandate-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Who is in what, in what capacity, and since when — for bands, studios, labels, venues, agencies and shops alike. Membership is **time-bounded and typed**: a permanent member, a touring member, a staff engineer, a former member. The time bound is the load-bearing part: "who was in the band when that record was made" is a different question from "who is in the band now", and the second one cannot answer the first.

The feature exists because `problem-statement.md` locates the root cause as *absence at the point of truth* — facts "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." Band tenure is exactly such a fact, and it is contested at exactly that moment: when a royalty statement arrives for a record made six years and two lineups ago.

**What this feature is not**: it is not the record of who played on what. That is domain 02, and the distinction is the single most important boundary in this file (D-05, D-15, DT-04).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Joins and leaves bands; sees every band they are or were in, as tenure ranges ("2015–2019, 2023–present") rather than a boolean. Their tenure history is career evidence — "toured with X, 2019–2022" is a fact a booker checks, and it survives leaving acrimoniously. Invites others by handle, email or from the credit graph; accepts, declines, or **rejects an assertion someone made about them** ("this band says I was their bassist in 2011 — I wasn't"). Confirms or contests a back-dated leaving date, which is the one number in this feature worth money. |
| Producer | Full | Manages studio staff (engineers, assistants, techs) as members with capacities and mandates — churn is a business fact, not an emotional one. Also *joins* things: a producer is frequently a band member too, which is the multi-hyphenate showing up in the membership graph rather than the persona model. Their operational dependency is the reverse direction: at the capture moment they need to know whether the person in front of them can bind the band (01.03.03), and that answer is only real if the membership edge is `confirmed`. |
| Operator | Full | Manages venue staff: bookers, sound engineers, door, promoters. Higher churn, lower drama — but a departed booker holding calendar and deposit access is a live operational risk, so revocation is immediate and complete while the tenure record is untouched. Reads the counterparty's lineup only insofar as advancing a show needs it; the lineup is not their source for who is playing (that is 18). |
| Fan | None | Not in the graph, in either direction. A fan is never a member of anything, and — the half that matters — **a fan can never be named as one**. Following is domain 20 and is deliberately a different relationship with no authority attached (DT-03). The absence is the point: a follow must never leak into the mandate graph, and the lineup surface must never be reachable as a fan-writable object. A fan sees a band's public lineup as page content, the same way they see its name. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A **membership edge** connects a person (or, for org↔org staffing, a party) to an org and carries:

| Attribute | What it holds | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Parties** | person → org | One edge per person per org per tenure |
| **Capacity periods** | permanent / touring / staff / honorary, each with its own date range | Versioned, not overwritten (D-10) |
| **Tenure** | start date, optional end date | Date-only for display; an authority-bearing transition also records an instant (D-16) |
| **Confirmation state** | `confirmed` \| `asserted` \| `disputed` | Authority requires `confirmed`, always (D-02) |
| **Provenance tier** | `attested-at-source` \| `asserted` | Was this recorded while it was true, or typed in later? (D-07) |
| **Mandate** | reference to 01.03.03 | Presence without authority is the normal state for most members |
| **Attribution** | who created the edge, who confirmed it, who ended it | The accountability substrate |

Five rules make it coherent:

1. **Append-only.** Leaving a band sets an end date; it does not delete the edge. This is what makes 2019's lineup answerable in 2026, and it is the domain invariant (domain D-08), applied here for the fifth independent time.
2. **Consent is required, but it may be pending.** Membership is never established by unilateral assertion of *authority*. It may be established by unilateral assertion of *history* — an org naming its own lineup, including people with no accounts — because otherwise the cold-start is fatal (01.05.01: shadow parties "appear in a lineup"). The reconciliation: an unconfirmed edge is a **claim about the past**, never a **grant of power**. It carries zero mandate, yields no acting context, and is invisible on the named party's own profile until they confirm it (D-08, DT-05).
3. **Capacity is on the edge, not on the person.** The same human is a permanent member of one band and a staff engineer at a studio, simultaneously.
4. **Revocation and preservation are separate operations.** Access stops now; the record does not move.
5. **Membership is a period relationship with an org. A credit is an event relationship with a work.** A session engagement produces a credit (domain 02), never a membership (D-05). There is no inference path from credit → membership, and none from lineup → record personnel (D-06, D-15).

Membership is established by invitation and acceptance, or by assertion and later confirmation. Someone adding themselves to a band would be a reputation land-grab; someone silently *gaining authority* over a band without consent would be worse.

The graph is a primary input to the acting-context list (01.01.03) and a **secondary, non-authoritative** context for credit attribution (domain 02) — see D-15.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates a band (01.02.02) and opens its people surface. They are the sole member, and — per 01.03.03's default — the sole mandate holder.
2. They invite three people: one by handle (an existing user), one by email, one picked from the credit graph (someone they have already been credited alongside).
3. System resolves each target. The handle resolves to a user; the credit-graph pick resolves to a party; the email matches nobody, so a shadow party is created (01.05.01) with the invitation attached.
4. Each invitee sees a pending invitation stating, in plain language, the band, the inviter, the proposed capacity, the proposed start date, and the mandate they would receive — "You'd be able to accept bookings for this band."
5. Two accept, each choosing their capacity (drums; drums + backing vocals). System creates two `confirmed` edges, provenance tier `attested-at-source`, start date = the accepted date, authority effective at the instant of acceptance.
6. The band becomes an actable context for each of them (01.01.03). The band's page shows a three-person lineup; the third person shows as **Invited**, not as a member.
7. Because the band formed in 2011 and this is 2026, the creator back-fills the history: they assert the founding lineup, including a former bassist with no account. Those edges are created `asserted`, provenance tier `asserted`, and appear on the band's page labelled *unconfirmed* — and on nobody else's page at all.
8. The shadow-party bassist is emailed. Eighteen months later they claim their profile (01.05.02) and are shown the pending assertion: "The Meridians say you were their bassist, 2011–2014. Is that right?" They confirm. The edge becomes `confirmed`; the provenance tier stays `asserted`, because it was still typed in from memory and the platform does not launder a reconstruction into evidence.
9. Two years on, the drummer's role changes: they take over front-of-house on the band's own shows. Their capacity period `permanent (2026–2028)` closes and `permanent + staff (2028–)` opens. Tenure is unbroken; the capacity history is a list.
10. The drummer eventually leaves. They set the end date to today. Access is revoked at that instant: the acting context disappears, their mandate terminates, in-flight drafts are preserved but unauthorised.
11. The band's page now shows a current lineup and a **former members** section — present, not hidden. The records the drummer played on still credit them, unchanged, because those credits never depended on this graph (D-15).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Sole full-mandate holder tries to leave or be removed** | Departure blocked until a successor mandate exists — an ownerless org is unadministrable (01.03.03 D-04). See domain CX-06 | "You're the only person who can administer {band}. Give someone else that role first." |
| **Sole full-mandate holder dies, is suspended by 24, or deletes their account** | The block above cannot apply — nobody can be prevented from dying. The org enters `unadministrable` at the instant authority ends, detected on the terminating event, not by a sweep. A **succession offer** goes to the longest-tenured remaining `confirmed` member, announced to *every* member simultaneously, with a **14-day contest window** (long enough that a grieving or blindsided band notices; short enough that the org is not frozen through a release cycle). Uncontested → the offer vests. Contested → governance (01.04.01) if terms exist, else 24. No remaining members → the party stays unadministrable and exits to 01.10 (estates). Resolves the gap flagged at domain CX-09 Q5. See Q-04 | Every member: "{band} has no administrator. {name} has been offered the role — 14 days to object." Sole-member case: routed to 01.10, catalogue keeps earning into an unadministrable party |
| **Member is removed acrimoniously and disputes it** | Removal takes effect immediately (operationally necessary — access must stop), the edge gets an end date, and the dispute routes to 24. The **historic** edge is not touched, because it is evidence | Removed member keeps their credits and their tenure history; loses access |
| **The band back-dates the leaving date to before a record was made** | The high-value attack, and the reason the end date is not a free-text field. An end date **in the past** is a retroactive claim, not a fact: it is recorded as `claimed`, the departing member is notified with the specific date and what it changes, and it does not display as settled until they confirm. Contested → 24. An end date of *today* needs no confirmation (DT-08) | Departing member: "The Meridians recorded your leaving date as 3 March 2021 — 8 months earlier than you may remember. Confirm or contest." Public: "Left 2021 *(date contested)*" |
| **Departure is real but must stay quiet — a tour is on sale, a record is in press** | Effect and announcement are separate (DT-06). The end date is effective immediately (access stops); publication can be embargoed by **mutual agreement only**, max 180 days (one release or touring cycle), auto-publishing on expiry. **Either party may publish unilaterally at any time** — an embargo is a mutual agreement to stay quiet, and consent to silence is revocable. The platform will not gag a person about their own career on their employer's behalf (D-13) | Both parties: "Departure recorded, not yet public. Publishes {date}, or whenever either of you chooses." Public: current lineup unchanged until publication |
| **A band asserts a famous musician was a member, to inflate credibility** | Contained by construction, not by policing. The assertion appears **only on the asserting org's page**, labelled unconfirmed, and **never on the named party's profile, EPK, or search result** (D-08). The impersonation payoff is entirely in appearing on the victim's surface; removing that removes the incentive. Creator attribution (01.05.01) makes it accountable. Volume limits are domain Q-06 | Asserting band: "Unconfirmed — awaiting {name}." Named party's page: nothing. Public: "Unconfirmed claim by {band}" |
| **The named party rejects an assertion — "I was never in that band"** | Edge → `disputed`, **hidden from public display everywhere**, retained for the dispute record (never destroyed — domain D-08), routed to 24. Deliberately asymmetric with a confirmed membership someone later regrets, which stays: **consented facts are permanent; unconsented claims are not facts.** An assertion its own subject denies has zero evidentiary weight, and displaying it is a defamation surface | Named party: "Removed from public view. Sent to review." Asserting band: "{name} disputes this. It's no longer public while it's reviewed." |
| **Someone tries to add themselves to a band** | Cannot happen in the authority sense — a self-created edge is `asserted`, carries no mandate, no context, and does not render on the band's page. Only the org can assert *its own* lineup; only the person can assert *their own* history — and each is invisible on the other's surface until the other confirms. The land-grab has no surface to land on | "Sent to {band} to confirm. It won't appear on their page until they do." |
| **Session player: member or not?** | **RESOLVED — not a member.** A session engagement produces a credit (domain 02), never a membership edge (D-05, DT-04). The lineup and the record's personnel are two different lists and have been for sixty years of liner notes; conflating them floods the lineup with everyone who ever played one date and destroys the question the graph exists to answer. Three routes already exist for the three real cases: **credited** (one session — the overwhelming majority), **represented** (a studio booking its engineers out — 01.03.02), **member** (a standing arrangement, invited and accepted) | Session player: a credit on the record, no lineup entry, no context for the band. Band page: session players appear under the release's personnel, not under Lineup |
| **A player has been credited on 8 of the band's tracks over 3 years — surely they're a member?** | The platform may **prompt**, never **infer** (D-06, DT-11). An inference would manufacture a consented relationship from unconsented data, and retroactively hand governance standing (01.04) to someone the band never voted in | Band admin: "You've credited Sam 8 times. Invite them to the lineup?" — a suggestion, requiring the full invite→accept flow |
| **Touring member who is not a "real" member** | A member: standing arrangement, invited, accepted. Capacity `touring` distinguishes them. Their credits are real; their governance standing (01.04.01) is not the same. This is exactly why capacity is on the edge | Lineup shows Touring separately from Permanent — as every band's own website does |
| **A member's capacity changes over time (roadie → sound engineer → member)** | **RESOLVED — one continuous tenure, versioned capacity periods** (D-10). Behaviorally: tenure is unbroken (one row, "2015–present"), capacity is a list ("crew 2015–2017, staff 2017–2020, permanent 2020–"), and any date-scoped query resolves against the capacity period, not the tenure. Both consumers get what they need: the UI wants continuity, the graph wants the history. Whether this is one edge with child periods or successive linked edges is a representation choice, not a behavior one → Q-02 | "Sam Okafor — member since 2015" with an expandable capacity history. Never two rows for one unbroken tenure |
| **A former member rejoins** | A **new tenure period**, never a reopened one (D-11). Tenures never merge — the gap is a fact, and "1999–2003, 2011–present" is how every lineup in music is written. The new tenure gets its own capacity, its own mandate, its own start | One row, two ranges: "Sam Okafor — 1999–2003, 2011–present" |
| **Two admins invite the same person simultaneously** | One edge. The second invitation collapses into the first and is attributed to both inviters. Idempotent by target party, not by invitation | Second admin: "{name} already has a pending invitation from {first admin}." |
| **The invitee accepts twice (double-click, retry, two devices)** | No-op. Returns the existing confirmed edge; start date and authority instant are those of the *first* acceptance | Confirmation screen, once. No duplicate |
| **Accept and remove race — the invitee accepts at the same instant the admin revokes the invitation** | First write wins; the loser sees the resolved state rather than an error. If acceptance won, the admin removes an actual member (a separate, attributed act with an end date) rather than silently cancelling | Whichever lost: "This was already resolved — {name} is now a member" / "This invitation was withdrawn." |
| **Member leaves and an admin removes them at the same instant** | Same terminal state, so no conflict to resolve. One end date (the earlier instant), and attribution records **both** acts — who left and who removed matters socially even when the date does not | Lineup: former member, one date. Audit: both events |
| **Invitation accepted after the inviter has left the band** | Valid. The invitation was an act of the org, not of the person — it was authorised when it was made. Rejecting it would make every departure silently void a queue of pending invites | Accepted normally. Band notified: "{name} accepted an invitation from {former member}." |
| **Invitation accepted after the org dissolved (01.04.02)** | Invalid — there is nothing to join. The invitation lapses at dissolution rather than dangling | "{band} no longer exists. This invitation has expired." |
| **The band splits into two orgs, both claiming the lineage (01.04.02)** | The pre-fork tenure history is **copied to both successors, not moved** (DT-07). It is true of both: both lineages genuinely contain those years. Moving it to one makes the other's history a lie; leaving it on the dead parent orphans it from both. Which successor owns the *name* is 01.04.02's and 01.02's question, not this file's | Both orgs show the shared history, each marked with its fork point. Neither claims exclusivity from this graph |
| **A person is both a member of the band and its manager (01.03.02), and leaves the band** | The membership edge ends; the representation edge does not. Their authority is the union of both edges, so the org context survives the departure — which falls out correctly because contexts are derived (01.01.03 D-01). See sub-domain CX-03 | Lineup: former member. Context switcher: band still present, now via representation |
| **One human holds authority on both sides of a transaction** — a member of the band *and* staff at the studio the band is booking | **Self-dealing.** A two-party agreement gets satisfied by one human confirming twice, and only this graph knows it — the transacting domain sees two consenting parties. Membership must therefore expose "these two parties share a human with authority on both sides" as a **queryable conflict-of-interest signal**; detection is this graph's obligation, enforcement is the transacting domain's and the Roles & Permissions cross-cut's (DT-09) | Both orgs' other mandate holders: "Sam holds authority for both {band} and {studio} on this booking." Not blocked here — surfaced, attributed, and left to the domain that owns the transaction |
| **"Who was in the band when this record was made?" for a record tracked over six months** | The lineup **cannot** answer it and must never be asked (D-15). A release is a date *range* and a lineup query takes a date; the honest answer lives in domain 02's per-session facts. This is the payoff of D-05: the question that looked like this graph's reason to exist is actually the credit graph's, and the lineup answers a narrower question it can answer exactly — "who was in this band on date D" | Release page: personnel from 02. Band page: lineup. Two lists, never cross-derived |
| **Same-day boundary — a member accepts a booking at 14:00 on the day their departure takes effect** | Two precisions (D-16). Authority-bearing transitions record an **instant**, so the authority question has an exact answer. Displayed and asserted tenure dates are **date-only**, because nobody knows the hour they joined a band, and the consumer that would care about the hour (record personnel) has been routed to 02 anyway | Public: "Left 12 March 2028." Audit: the exact instant, and whether the 14:00 booking was authorised |
| **Member requests erasure of their membership under GDPR** | Collides head-on with domain D-08. "Sam was in this band 2019–2022" is simultaneously Sam's personal data, the band's history, four other people's professional record, and the provenance context of a body of work — **it is jointly-authored data, and a unilateral erasure right over a jointly-authored fact is incoherent** (DT-10). Equally, claiming a subject has no rights over a record about them is untenable. Interim posture: the edge is retained, the request routes to the erasure flow, and 24/legal decides. Not fabricating a legal answer → Q-03 | "This record involves other people. Your request has been sent for review." |
| **A member's account is suspended by 24** | Authority ends at the instant of suspension; the tenure edge does not end (they have not left the band). If they held the sole full mandate, the succession path above fires | Lineup: still a member. Context: gone. Band: "{name}'s access is suspended." |
| **The org itself is suspended or banned by 24** | Every edge persists; every mandate is inert for the duration. Suspension is an access state of the org, not a lifecycle event of its memberships | Members: "{band} is suspended. You're still listed as a member; nobody can act for it." |
| **Member dies** | Edge ends with a date, like any departure; mandate terminates immediately; estate handling routes to 01.10 via a **representation** edge, so the estate acts as itself on behalf of the party and can never attest in the dead person's name. Their historic credits and their share are unaffected. If they were the sole mandate holder, see row 2. See domain CX-09 | Memorialised presence in the lineup as a former member, with the tenure intact |
| **Network failure mid-invite — the invitation may or may not have sent** | The edge is the source of truth, not the message. A `pending` edge exists either way; invitation *delivery* retries 3 times with exponential backoff, then surfaces as undelivered with a copyable link. **Capture is never conditional on delivery** (01.05.01's rule, inherited) | "Invitation created. We couldn't reach {email} — send them this link." |
| **Graph fetch fails on a band page** | Never renders an empty lineup on failure — an empty lineup is a factual claim ("this band has no members") and rendering it on an error is the platform inventing a fact | Error with retry, in place of the lineup section. The rest of the page renders |
| **Invitation spam — a person is invited to 200 bands they have never heard of** | Same containment as the assertion case: a pending invitation appears on **nobody's** public surface. The cost of the attack is therefore inbox noise, not reputation damage. Rate limits are domain Q-06 | Invitations inbox, dismissable in bulk. Nothing public |
| **A shadow party is invited, never claims, and the band needs its lineup to be complete anyway** | Fully supported and expected — this is the normal state, not a degraded one. The edge is `asserted`, the lineup is complete and honest about its confidence, and nothing is blocked | Lineup complete; unclaimed members shown as unconfirmed, with no stigma attached to the band |
| **The invitation email was typo'd and the wrong human claims the shadow party** | The band is notified on every confirmation, by name — so a wrong-person confirmation lands in front of the people best placed to spot it. The claim itself is 01.05's to adjudicate | Band: "{name} confirmed they were your bassist 2011–2014." One click to contest |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Graph fetch in flight | Skeleton lineup with the correct row count where known, so the layout does not jump |
| **Empty** | Org with one member (the creator) | Lineup shows one, with a prompt to invite. **The band that is really a solo project is a legitimate resting state** — no "incomplete profile" stigma, ever. An Operator's venue with one member is likewise normal |
| **Populated** | 2+ confirmed members | Current lineup, grouped by capacity (Permanent / Touring / Staff). **Former members in a separate, non-hidden section** — collapsed is acceptable; absent is not, because the whole feature exists to keep them visible |
| **Error** | Fetch failed | Error with retry, scoped to the lineup section. Never an empty lineup — that would misrepresent the band as a fact |
| **Partial** | Invitations pending, and/or asserted-but-unconfirmed edges present | Three visually distinct tiers: confirmed members, **Invited** (pending), **Unconfirmed** (asserted). Each labelled, none hidden, none rendered as equivalent to a confirmed member. An org whose lineup is entirely unconfirmed is a valid and common day-one state |
| **Unadministrable** | Zero confirmed full-mandate holders (death, suspension, deletion) | A banner to every member: no one can act for this org; a succession offer is live with its 14-day window and its contest link. Read paths continue working — the org is frozen, not broken |
| **Contested** | An asserted edge was rejected, or a back-dated end date was contested | The disputed element is removed from public view and marked in-review to the parties. The rest of the lineup is unaffected — one dispute never blanks a lineup |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Membership is current-state — a list of who is in the band | The obvious model, and the one every band page on the internet uses | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the domain's version of the thesis. `problem-statement.md`: facts get "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." Lineup at time-of-recording is exactly such a fact. A current-state list answers "who is in the band" and destroys "who was in the band in 2019" — which is the question that matters when the royalties arrive. Membership must be time-bounded and append-only. |
| DT-02 | Removing a member should remove their traces | Intuitive after an acrimonious split, and users will ask for it | ❌ REJECTED — it is the evaporation the platform exists to prevent, requested by the user and executed by the platform. Operational access must be revoked instantly; the historic record must be untouchable. The two are separate concerns and only the first is what the angry user actually needs. |
| DT-03 | Following (domain 20) must never create a mandate edge | Reasoning about the Fan persona's total absence from this graph | ✅ CONFIRMED as a boundary worth stating explicitly. Both are "person → party" edges and the temptation to unify them is real. But a follow is unilateral, unconsented by the target, and infinitely scalable; a membership is bilateral, consented, and authority-bearing. One shared edge type and a fan could inherit authority by following. Kept structurally separate. |
| DT-04 | A session player is a member with a narrow mandate | Parent Q-01 / domain Q-09 — framed as the domain's highest-volume unresolved relationship | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the file's central resolution (D-05). The distinguishing structural fact: **membership is a period relationship with an org; a credit is an event relationship with a work.** A session player has a *date*, not a *period* — they were hired for Tuesday. Making them members floods the lineup with everyone who ever played one date, at which point "who was in the band in 2019" is unanswerable noise and the graph has destroyed the only question it exists to answer. The music domain settled this sixty years ago and nobody has ever confused the two lists: every liner note in history separates **Members / Former / Touring** from the album's **Personnel**. Every band's own website does it. Every Wikipedia band page does it. The answer to "does the membership graph or the credit graph carry the majority of music's working relationships?" is **the credit graph** — which is also the thesis-coherent answer, because credits are the provenance artifact and provenance is the wedge (D-18). |
| DT-05 | Bilateral consent (D-02) and shadow-party lineups (01.05.01) are in direct contradiction | Reading 01.05.01's claim that a shadow party "can appear in a lineup" against this file's own D-02, which requires acceptance a shadow party cannot give | ✅ CONFIRMED — a real contradiction between two `[SURFACE]` files, and the fix is a distinction neither had: **consent to authority and assertion of history are different acts.** An unconfirmed edge is a claim about the past, not a grant of power. It exists, it displays on the asserting org's page labelled unconfirmed, and it carries zero mandate and no acting context. This satisfies 01.05.01's "a subject, not an agent" exactly, preserves D-02's actual purpose (nobody gains authority without consenting), and unblocks the cold start — a 2011 band can enter its real lineup in 2026 without hunting down a bassist who left in 2014. Without this the choice is a graph that is either empty or unsafe. |
| DT-06 | The end date of a membership is a single fact with a single moment | The obvious model — one date, one truth | ❌ REJECTED. In music the **operational** departure and the **public** departure are routinely months apart: a member quits in March, it is announced in September, and the tour that was on sale in between is the reason. A platform that publishes the end date the instant it is set **outs the departure**, with real commercial consequences for a tour on sale or a record in press — the platform doing damage no one asked it to do. Effect and announcement separate (D-13). The embargo is mutual and either party can break it, because the alternative is the platform enforcing a gag order on a person about their own career on their employer's behalf. Coherent with the platform's existing machinery: 02.01.05 is Credit Visibility & Embargo — the concept already exists in the credit graph and was simply never applied to tenure. |
| DT-07 | When a band forks (01.04.02), the pre-fork lineup history goes to one successor | The instinct that history has one owner, and one of the two is the "real" band | ❌ REJECTED — the pre-fork history is **true of both**, so it must be **copied, not moved**. Giving it to one makes the other's history a lie the platform authored; leaving it on the dead parent orphans it from both. This is the most litigated fact in legacy music (two touring lineups, one name) and a graph that assumes one band = one linear lineage cannot represent it. Note the scope line: which successor owns the *name* is 01.04.02's and 01.02's fight, and this file must not pre-empt it. What membership owes is that a fork never destroys or reassigns a tenure that both successors genuinely contain. |
| DT-08 | Departure is unilateral in both directions, so the end *date* can be set unilaterally too | The current model's implicit assumption — D-02 guards the start date bilaterally and says nothing about the end | ❌ REJECTED — a hole big enough to drive a royalty claim through. The band back-dates the leaving date to before the record was made, and the ex-member is written out of the lineup-at-time. This is precisely the "he left before the album" / "no, I was still in the band" fight, and the current model hands one side the pen. The fix inherits DT-02's shape (revocation and record are separate): the *effective revocation* stays unilateral because access must stop, but a **retroactive end date is a claim, not a fact** — it requires the departing member's confirmation and routes to 24 when contested. An end date of today needs nothing; it is not contestable in any interesting way. Nobody in the sweep named the end date as an attack surface; the start date got all the attention because consent is a start-date-shaped idea. |
| DT-09 | The multi-hyphenate holding authority on both sides of a deal is a permissions problem | The natural read — it looks like an authorisation question, so it looks like the Roles & Permissions cross-cut's | ✅ CONFIRMED as real, but **split**, and the split matters. Sam is in the band *and* on the studio's staff; the band books the studio; one human satisfies a two-party agreement by confirming twice. The transacting domain sees two consenting parties and is structurally blind — **only this graph knows the two parties share a human**. So detection is unavoidably membership's obligation (nobody else has the data) while enforcement belongs to the transacting domain and the cross-cut. This is what breaks at the intersection with 05/13/17, and it is invisible from inside any of them. `personas.md` names the multi-hyphenate as music's defining structural fact and the platform's whole ontology bends around it — the same fact that makes the product necessary makes self-dealing the default rather than the exception. |
| DT-10 | A membership record is the member's personal data, so erasure rights apply cleanly | The GDPR default posture, and the one a DSAR flow would implement without thinking | ❌ REJECTED as clean — the record is **jointly-authored**. A unilateral erasure right over a jointly-authored fact is incoherent: exercising it edits four other people's careers. But the mirror claim — that a subject has no rights over a record about them — is equally untenable. This is a genuine collision between domain D-08 (the record of work is never destroyed) and a statutory right, and `constraints.md` leaves GDPR as an unresolved upstream constraint with no ratified answer to inherit. **Not fabricating a legal answer** → Q-03. | DT-11 | Membership can be inferred from repeated credits — 8 credits over 3 years means they're in the band | The recommender instinct, and it would populate the graph for free — genuinely tempting given the cold-start problem DT-05 is solving | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection generalises into a rule (D-06): **the platform may prompt, never infer, a consensual relationship.** Three failures compound. Adding a second layer buys nothing and creates a bootstrap paradox: each member's membership needs approval from members whose own membership needed approval, and the creator (01.02.02) can never be attested by anyone, because at that instant there is nobody. The platform cannot fix that (the past is the past), but it must not launder it: the edge carries a **provenance tier** — `attested-at-source` (recorded while it was true) vs `asserted` (typed in later) — reusing 02.04.02's own machinery rather than inventing one.
