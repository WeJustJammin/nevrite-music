# Feature: Membership Records & Lifecycle

> **Number**: 01.03.01
> **Parent**: [Membership, Representation & Mandate](./01.03-membership-representation-mandate-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who is in what, in what capacity, and since when — for bands, studios, labels, venues, agencies and shops alike. Membership is **time-bounded and typed**: a permanent member, a touring member, a session player, a staff engineer, a former member. The time bound is the load-bearing part: "who was in the band when that record was made" is a different question from "who is in the band now", and the second one cannot answer the first.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Joins and leaves bands; sees every band they are or were in. Their membership history is career evidence — "toured with X, 2019–2022" is a fact a booker cares about, and it survives leaving. Invites others to the band; accepts invites. |
| Producer | Full | Manages studio staff (engineers, assistants, techs) as members with capacities. Also *joins* things — a producer is frequently a band member too, which is the multi-hyphenate showing up in the membership graph rather than the persona model. |
| Operator | Full | Manages venue staff: bookers, sound engineers, door, promoters. Their membership churn is higher than a band's and less emotional — but a departed booker's access to the calendar is a real operational risk, so departure must be immediate and complete. |
| Fan | None | Not in the graph. A fan is never a member of anything — following is domain 20 and is deliberately a different relationship with no authority attached. The absence is the point: follows must never leak into the mandate graph. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A membership edge connects a person to an org and carries: capacity (what they do there), status (current / former), a start date, an optional end date, and a mandate (01.03.03). Memberships are **appended, never overwritten** — leaving a band sets an end date; it does not delete the edge. This is what makes 2019's lineup answerable in 2026.

Membership is established by invitation and acceptance, not by unilateral assertion. Someone adding themselves to a band would be a reputation land-grab; someone being added to a band without consent would be worse.

The graph is a primary input to the acting-context list (01.01.03) and to credit attribution (domain 02).

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates a band (01.02.02) and invites three people by handle, email, or by picking them from the credit graph.
2. Invitees who have accounts get an in-app invitation; those who do not become shadow parties (01.05.01) with an invitation attached.
3. Each accepts, choosing their capacity (drums; drums + backing vocals).
4. Membership edges exist with start dates. The band becomes an actable context for each of them (01.01.03).
5. Two years later, one leaves. The edge gets an end date. The old records still credit them correctly, and the band's page shows current and former lineups distinctly.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Member leaves while holding the only owning mandate | Departure blocked until a successor mandate exists — an ownerless org is unadministrable | "You're the only person who can administer {band}. Give someone else that role first." See domain CX-06 |
| Member is removed acrimoniously and disputes it | Removal takes effect immediately (operationally necessary — access must stop), the edge gets an end date, and the dispute routes to 24. The **historic** edge is not touched, because it is evidence | Removed member keeps their credits and their membership history; loses access |
| Someone is added to a band without their consent | Cannot happen — membership requires acceptance. A band can *credit* a non-member (domain 02) without making them a member | Invitation pending until accepted |
| Session player: member or not? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see parent Q-01. This is the single highest-volume relationship in working music and its classification is unresolved | `[PENDING]` |
| Touring member who is not a "real" member | Capacity distinguishes them. Their credits are real; their governance standing (01.04.01) is not the same. The distinction is exactly why capacity is on the edge | Lineup shows touring vs permanent |
| Member dies | Edge ends; mandate terminates immediately; estate handling routes to 01.10. Their historic credits and their share are unaffected | See domain CX-09 |
| A member's capacity changes over time (roadie → sound engineer → member) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — is that one edge with a mutable capacity, or successive edges? The credit graph needs the second; the UI wants the first | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Graph fetch | Skeleton lineup |
| Empty | Org with one member (the creator) | Lineup shows one; a prompt to invite — the band that is really a solo project is a legitimate resting state |
| Populated | 2+ members | Current lineup; former members in a separate, non-hidden section |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry. Never renders an empty lineup on failure — that would misrepresent the band |
| Partial | Invitations pending | Pending members shown as pending, distinctly from accepted ones |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Membership is current-state — a list of who is in the band | The obvious model, and the one every band page on the internet uses | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the domain's version of the thesis. `problem-statement.md`: facts get "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." Lineup at time-of-recording is exactly such a fact. A current-state list answers "who is in the band" and destroys "who was in the band in 2019" — which is the question that matters when the royalties arrive. Membership must be time-bounded and append-only. |
| DT-02 | Removing a member should remove their traces | Intuitive after an acrimonious split, and users will ask for it | ❌ REJECTED — it is the evaporation the platform exists to prevent, requested by the user and executed by the platform. Operational access must be revoked instantly; the historic record must be untouchable. The two are separate concerns and only the first is what the angry user actually needs. |
| DT-03 | Following (domain 20) must never create a mandate edge | Reasoning about the Fan persona's total absence from this graph | ✅ CONFIRMED as a boundary worth stating explicitly. Both are "person → party" edges and the temptation to unify them is real. But a follow is unilateral, unconsented by the target, and infinitely scalable; a membership is bilateral, consented, and authority-bearing. One shared edge type and a fan could inherit authority by following. Kept structurally separate. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Membership is time-bounded and append-only | DT-01 — the whole point is answering "who was in the band when" |
| D-02 | Membership requires invitation + acceptance; never unilateral in either direction | Self-adding is a reputation land-grab; being added without consent is worse |
| D-03 | Capacity is on the edge (permanent / touring / session / staff), not on the person | The same human is a permanent member of one band and a session player in another, simultaneously |
| D-04 | Access revocation and historic-record preservation are separate operations | DT-02 — the angry user needs the first and must not get the second |
