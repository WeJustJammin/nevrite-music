# Feature: Representation & Roster Relationships

> **Number**: 01.03.02
> **Parent**: [Membership, Representation & Mandate](./01.03-membership-representation-mandate-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The edges where one party acts for another without being part of it: a manager for an artist, a booking agent for a roster, a publisher administering a catalogue, a label representing a band's recordings, a sublabel under a group. Representation differs from membership in that it is **scoped, term-bounded and commercial** — it covers a defined territory, a defined activity, and a defined period, and it usually costs a percentage.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Grants representation to a manager or agent and sees, in one place, exactly what they have handed over: which activities, which territories, until when. Can end it. This is the surface where "my manager can book but not sign" stops being a verbal understanding. |
| Producer | Full | Represented for production work (a producer manager is standard), and may represent others — a studio that books its engineers out is representing them. Their represented status affects who counterparties in 05 actually negotiate with. |
| Operator | Config | Rarely represented; frequently a **counterparty** to representation — they need to know whether the person answering a booking enquiry can actually bind the artist. Configures who at their venue can deal with agents. The value to them is knowing the mandate is real. |
| Fan | None | Not in the graph and must not see it. Representation is commercially sensitive: who manages whom, on what terms, is not public information, and a fan-visible roster would leak the industry's private structure. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A representation edge connects a representing party to a represented party and carries: scope (which activities — live booking, recording, publishing, sync, merch), territory, term (start, end, notice), a mandate (01.03.03), and optionally commercial terms (commission).

The **roster** is the reverse view of the same edges: an agency's roster is every artist that has granted it live-booking representation. It is a view, not a separate object — which means a roster can never contain an artist who has not consented, because the edge is the consent.

Representation is **bilateral and revocable**. A manager cannot add an artist to their roster; the artist grants it.

### Happy Path

1. Manager sends a representation request to an artist: scope (live + recording), territory (worldwide), term (2 years).
2. Artist reviews. Every scope item is shown as a plain-language statement of what the manager will be able to do on their behalf.
3. Artist accepts. The edge exists, and the artist appears on the manager's roster.
4. The manager can now act as the artist in the scoped contexts (01.01.03) — replying to booking enquiries, but not, say, signing away masters, unless that scope was granted.
5. On expiry the edge lapses and the contexts disappear. No cleanup step, no lingering access.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Representation ends acrimoniously mid-term | Access terminates immediately on revocation; the **commercial tail** (commission on deals the agent sourced) does not, and the platform does not adjudicate it | Access revoked. Sunset/commission dispute routes to 24 and to Q-01 |
| Two agents both claim to represent the same artist for the same scope | The graph prevents it if both are on-platform (conflicting edges detected). Off-platform claims are invisible to us | "{Artist} already has live booking representation." Overlap flagged, not silently allowed |
| An agent books a show after the term expired | Cannot — the acting context is gone (01.01.03 D-01 derivation). This is the clean payoff of deriving contexts rather than configuring them | Context absent; deep link offers nothing |
| Artist is a band, not a person | Representation is party↔party, so a band is represented like anyone else. But *who* accepts on the band's behalf requires a mandate (01.03.03) and possibly a governance decision (01.04.01). **Sharpened by 01.03.03 D-07..D-10 (DQ-02.4/.5/.6/.7)**: for a band, *any* `confirmed` `permanent` member can accept, because `sign` and `administer` are seeded — subject to the USD 1,000 per-act ceiling, above which it escalates to the uncapped owning-mandate holder. A `touring`, `staff` or `honorary` member cannot, and no member of a studio, venue, label, shop or agency can without an explicit grant | See domain CX-05 |
| Manager acts against the artist's interest within their granted scope | Not a platform failure — the scope was granted. But the audit trail makes it visible, which is the only real protection | Activity attributed to the manager acting as the artist, always visibly |
| Representation is real but the agreement is off-platform (the normal case in year one) | Edge can be recorded without on-platform signed terms — otherwise the graph excludes the whole industry as it exists today | Edge with `terms: off-platform`; see Q-01 |
| Org↔org affiliation (sublabel under a group) | Same edge type, mandate differs (parent index D-03) | Affiliation shown on both org pages |
| Representation request sent to a party not yet on WeJammin | The edge cannot form — the edge *is* the represented party's consent (D-01), and a party who does not exist cannot consent. The request becomes an invitation; the edge is created only when they join and accept | "Invited {name} — representation activates when they join and accept." No roster entry, no acting context, until then |
| Represented party acts directly in a scope they also delegated | Both can act — delegation grants authority, it does not remove the principal's own. The manager replying to a booking and the artist replying to the same booking are both valid; the platform does not lock the principal out of their own affairs | Both actions attributed to their actual actor (artist as self, or manager acting-as-artist); no "someone else is handling this" block. Collision on the *same* enquiry is a 04/17 concern, not resolved here — `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` for the concrete last-write/notify rule |
| Representation revoked while a deal sourced under it is mid-flight | Acting context ends immediately (D-04); an enquiry the agent opened does not auto-cancel — it detaches to the artist to continue or close directly | Agent loses the context; artist sees the in-flight enquiry surface on their own acting context. Commission tail on it routes to Q-01 / 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A roster is an object the agency owns and curates | How every agency website works — a page listing their artists | ❌ REJECTED — an owned roster is a list an agency can put anyone on, which is a claim, not a fact, and precisely the kind of unverified assertion this platform exists to replace. Modelling roster as the *reverse view* of consented edges makes "we represent X" checkable by construction. Same move as credits: the reverse-index is the honest object. |
| DT-02 | Representation and membership are different enough to model separately | Their surface language is different (a manager is not "in" the band) | ❌ REJECTED — hence the merge (parent D-01). Both are party→party edges carrying a mandate. A band member who can sign for the band and a manager who can sign for the band have *identical* authority and must not be authorised by two different mechanisms. Different UI, one graph. |
| DT-03 | Requiring signed on-platform terms would exclude the entire existing industry | Reasoning about adoption: `personas.md` switching triggers are about proving facts, not about migrating paperwork | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the adoption trap. Every representation relationship that exists today was agreed off-platform, often on a handshake. A model requiring signed terms before recording the edge means the graph is empty on day one and the acting-context list is useless for exactly the people (managers, agents) who drive volume. Record the edge; make terms optional; let signed terms be the upgrade. |
| DT-04 | Representation needs a sub-delegation mechanism — an agent represents the artist, then hands a specific tour to a junior colleague | Real agency practice: an artist signs *with the agency*, but a named agent works the account day-to-day, and coverage is reassigned. A naive model would add "agent delegates to sub-agent" edges | ❌ REJECTED — this is the org case in disguise, and adding a delegation-of-delegation edge would build a second authority mechanism (exactly DT-02's error). The artist's representation edge points to the **agency** (an org, first-class per personas.md). *Which human at the agency* acts is a membership (01.03.01) + mandate (01.03.03) question inside the org, not a new representation edge. One graph holds: the represented→agency edge grants authority; org-internal membership routes it to a person. Reassigning the account changes nothing on the artist's edge, which is correct — the artist represented the agency, not the individual. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Roster is a reverse view of consented edges, never an owned list | DT-01 — makes "we represent X" verifiable rather than assertable |
| D-02 | Representation edges are scoped, territory-bounded and term-bounded | These are the actual dimensions of music representation; a boolean "manages" edge would be useless to every consumer |
| D-03 | On-platform signed terms are optional; the edge can be recorded without them | DT-03 — requiring them empties the graph on day one |
| D-04 | Expiry removes the acting context automatically | Falls out of 01.01.03 D-01's derivation; no revocation sweep needed |
