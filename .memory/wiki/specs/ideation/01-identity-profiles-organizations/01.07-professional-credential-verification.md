# Feature: Professional, Union & Credential Verification

> **Number**: 01.07
> **Parent**: [Identity, Profiles & Organizations](./identity-profiles-organizations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Assertions about a party's professional standing, backed by an issuer who is not the platform and not the subject: union membership (AFM, MU, Equity), PRO membership (ASCAP, BMI, PRS, GEMA), teaching qualifications, public liability insurance, an entertainment licence, a PAT certificate. These are the facts a counterparty needs before a transaction — a venue without public liability cover cannot host a show, and a teacher working with minors needs a background check — and they differ from role facets (01.01.01) in that somebody else issued them.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Submits union and PRO membership, and (for domain 06) teaching qualifications and any background check required to work with minors. Sees which of their credentials are verified, by what method, and when they expire. Their union card is a real signal in session work — it says something about rates and conditions that no self-assertion can. |
| Producer | Full | Submits PRO membership and any studio-level credentials (insurance, electrical safety). Also *reads* others' — a producer hiring a session player under a union agreement needs to know the player's status before the session, not after. |
| Operator | Full | The heaviest user. Their credentials are conditions of trading: public liability insurance, entertainment/alcohol licence, capacity certification, PAT testing, accessibility compliance. A venue whose insurance lapsed is a venue that legally cannot host tomorrow's show — which makes expiry here operationally urgent rather than cosmetic. |
| Fan | None | Holds no credentials. But the Fan is why some of them exist: a venue's licence and insurance protect the audience, and a teacher's background check protects a minor. Their interest is real and entirely indirect — they see a badge, not a credential system. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A credential is a claim about the subject, issued by a third party, with an expiry. It carries: type, issuer, identifier, validity period, and a **verification method** — which determines what it is worth:

| Method | Confidence |
|---|---|
| Issuer API / registry check | High — the issuer said so directly |
| Document upload + manual review | Medium — depends on the reviewer |
| Self-asserted, unverified | Low — no better than a role facet |

The verification method must be visible wherever the credential is. An unverified credential displayed like a verified one is worse than no credential, because it converts an honest gap into a false assurance.

**Expiry is the hard part.** A credential is a statement about a moment, and most are annual. An expired insurance certificate rendering as a badge is the platform actively misleading a band into a room that is not covered.

This is not KYC (routed to Payments) and not age assurance (routed to Safeguarding). Both were extracted from this domain at ratification. Credential verification proves a *professional assertion*, not a legal identity or an age.

### Happy Path

1. Operator lists their venue and reaches a section asking for public liability cover.
2. They upload the certificate. It carries an insurer, a policy number and an expiry.
3. System verifies as far as its method allows and records the expiry.
4. Venue page shows the cover as verified, with its method visible.
5. Sixty days before expiry, they are reminded — because the cost of finding out on the night is a cancelled show.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Credential expires | Badge stops rendering as current. It does **not** silently vanish — a band that booked partly on that badge deserves to know it lapsed | Owner: escalating reminders. Viewers: "expired {date}", not absence. See domain CX-10 |
| Expired credential is load-bearing for a live booking | The show is booked into a room with no cover. The platform can see this and staying silent is not neutral. At Level 1: the lapse is surfaced — the credential flips to `expired` on the venue page and the affected booking is flagged to the parties who relied on it (per D-02). Whether the platform *also blocks new bookings* against a lapsed condition is a liability posture, not a display fact | Owner: escalating expiry reminders. Affected band/booker: a "cover lapsed" flag on the booking. Blocking-vs-notify policy `[PENDING — Q-01 → /create-prd-security]`, coordinated with domain 17 |
| Forged document passes manual review | Real risk of the document-upload method, and the reason method visibility matters — medium confidence is honestly medium | Route to 24 |
| Issuer has no API (most unions and many insurers) | Falls back to document review. Most credentials will be medium confidence, and pretending otherwise would be the real failure | Method shown honestly |
| Credential is genuine but not the subject's (someone else's certificate) | Name matching is weak. A PRO membership matching a common name proves little — the same weakness as person-claim proof (01.05.02) | See domain CX-05 |
| Credential is required by another domain (06 requires a background check to teach minors) | The requirement belongs to the consuming domain; this file supplies the record. Gating is theirs | Domain 06 gates its own surfaces |
| Subject deletes a credential to hide an expiry | Deletion of a *lapsed* credential that a counterparty relied on is an evidence problem, not a housekeeping action. At Level 1: a credential that was *relied upon by a live or historical booking* is not hard-deleted — it becomes a tombstone (`removed by owner {date}`, prior expiry preserved) so a counterparty's reliance survives the owner's attempt to erase it. An unrelied, never-current credential can be deleted outright | Owner: can remove, but a relied-upon record shows as withdrawn, not gone. Viewer of a past booking: still sees what was relied upon. Exact retention window and tombstone visibility rules `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`, aligned with 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A badge is binary — verified or not | The universal pattern, and the one every platform's blue check has already discredited | ❌ REJECTED — the confidence is entirely in the *method*, and most issuers here have no API, so most credentials will be document-review-grade at best. Rendering a document-review badge identically to a registry-verified one manufactures a false assurance about someone's insurance cover. Method must be visible. Same principle as 01.06.02 DT-02 (a credit's worth is a function of who attested it) applied to credentials. |
| DT-02 | Credential verification is the same problem as profile claiming (01.05.02) — both are "verification" | Their names invite the merge, and both were adjacent candidates in the sweep | ❌ REJECTED — different subject, different evidence, different failure. Claiming proves *control of a party*; a credential proves *an assertion about a party*. A union card is not proof you own a profile (many AFM members share a name), and control of a venue's email says nothing about its insurance. Recorded as a rejected pair in the domain CX file rather than silently dropped, because the merge will be proposed again. |
| DT-03 | Expiry is the whole feature, and the Operator's case makes it safety-critical rather than administrative | Reasoning about what actually goes wrong: `personas.md` gives the Operator's worst-accidental behaviour as publishing a stale gear list so "a band arrives to a room that can't run their show" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is worse than the sweep's framing suggests. The gear-list case is an inconvenience; the *insurance* case is a legal and physical safety failure, and the platform is the party displaying the badge the band relied on. Every credential here is a statement about a moment, and a platform that renders lapsed credentials as current is not passively wrong — it is actively asserting something false. Expiry handling is the feature; issuance is the easy half. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Verification method is visible wherever the credential is | DT-01 — most issuers have no API, so most badges are medium confidence and must say so |
| D-02 | Expired credentials render as expired, never as absent | DT-03 — someone relied on that badge and deserves to know it lapsed |
| D-03 | The consuming domain owns whether a credential is *required*; this file owns the record | Otherwise identity would encode domain 06's and 16's policy |
| D-04 | Fails closed — an unresolvable credential never renders as verified | A false assurance about insurance is worse than no information |
| D-05 | KYC and age assurance stay out (routed to Payments and Safeguarding at ratification) | Do not re-absorb what the boundary narrowing extracted |
