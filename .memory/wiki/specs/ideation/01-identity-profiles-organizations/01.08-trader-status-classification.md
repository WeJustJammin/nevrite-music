# Feature: Trader vs Private Seller Classification

> **Number**: 01.08
> **Parent**: [Identity, Profiles & Organizations](./identity-profiles-organizations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Whether a seller is acting in the course of a business or as a private individual — a classification with legal force, not a preference. Under EU/UK consumer law the distinction decides whether a buyer gets a 14-day right of withdrawal, a statutory warranty and a redress route, or none of them; and marketplace operators have duties to collect trader details, display them, and tell buyers plainly when consumer protections do **not** apply. The classification is an attribute of the identity; enforcement of what it implies belongs to the commerce domains.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The hard case, and the most common one. A working musician selling a cymbal is a private seller; the same musician flipping ten pedals a year is probably a trader, and will not know it. They declare status and supply trader details if they are one — including a real name and address that become **public** on their listings, which they will not expect. |
| Operator | Full | Almost always a trader — a studio selling time or gear is trading by definition. For them the classification is uncontroversial and the disclosure is routine. |
| Producer | Full | Same as Musician: a producer selling services through 05 is trading; selling an old preamp probably is not. The line runs through one person's account, which is why this attaches to identity rather than to a shop entity. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The protected party**, and the reason the feature exists. They see the disclosure — "this seller is a private individual; consumer protections do not apply" — before they buy. It is the one identity attribute whose whole purpose is to be read by someone else. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A party carries a trader classification: `private`, `trader`, or `undetermined`. Traders supply and publish: legal name, geographic address, contact details, and (where applicable) a company or VAT number. Private sellers publish none of that — and buyers are told, prominently, what that costs them.

The classification is **self-declared but not self-determining**. Whether someone is a trader is a matter of fact in law (volume, regularity, profit motive, whether goods were bought to resell), not of choice, and platforms are expected to act on obvious mismatches rather than take the declaration at face value.

This file owns: the classification record, the declaration flow, the trader details, and the disclosure obligation it creates. It does **not** own: consumer rights mechanics (returns, withdrawal windows, warranties), which belong to 13/14/05, or tax/VAT treatment, which routes to Payments and compliance.

### Happy Path

1. Musician lists a cymbal in 13. They have no classification.
2. Before the listing can publish, the flow asks — in plain language about their situation, not by asking them to self-assess a legal test.
3. They are a private seller. Classification recorded.
4. Their listing carries the required disclosure: private seller, no consumer protections.
5. A Fan sees it before buying and knows what they are and are not getting.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Private seller's activity crosses into trading (volume, regularity, buying to resell) | Detection signals are observable from platform data — sustained active-listing count over a rolling window, repeat-category velocity, buy-then-relist patterns, revenue concentration. What the platform *does* on a trip (silently prompt re-declaration, soft-reclassify to `undetermined` and re-gate, or block) is the live decision — Q-01. The one settled point: taking the declaration at face value while watching the signals is not a defensible posture. The concrete numeric threshold is jurisdiction-specific and deferred to `/create-prd-security` (Q-02) | Depends on disposition: a re-declaration prompt inline on next listing, or a blocked publish with the situational flow re-shown. Detail of the disposition UX is `[PENDING — Q-01 owner decision]` |
| Trader declares private to avoid consumer obligations | The abuse case the law anticipates. Detection + 24 | Route to 24 |
| Musician discovers trader status publishes their home address | A real and severe collision: the law requires a geographic address; a working musician selling from home has no business address to give. This is 01.01.04's privacy model meeting a statutory publication duty, and the law wins | Blocking disclosure before listing: "As a trader, your name and address will be publicly shown." See domain CX-08 |
| Cross-border sale — different jurisdictions, different thresholds | The classification's meaning varies by where the buyer is. Modelling one boolean for a global marketplace is optimistic | See Q-02 |
| A band (org) sells merch | The org is the seller and is almost certainly trading. Classification attaches to the acting party, which for a band is the band | See domain CX-08 |
| Seller is `undetermined` and tries to list | Cannot. The disclosure is mandatory, so classification is a precondition of listing rather than a profile nicety | Listing blocked with the declaration flow inline |
| Musician sells one cymbal, is asked to self-assess a legal test | They will get it wrong, and a wrong answer is a legal exposure for them and for us | Situational questions, never "are you a trader?" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Classification fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | `undetermined` — every user before their first listing | Nothing, until they try to sell. Never asked of someone who is not selling |
| Populated | Declared | Status on their listings; trader details public if trader |
| Error | Fetch failed | **Fails closed** — no classification means no listing. Publishing without the disclosure is a legal breach, not a degraded experience |
| Partial | Trader declared, details incomplete | Listing blocked until complete — a trader listing without published details is the exact breach |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a marketplace concern and belongs in 13/14, not identity | The obvious placement — it only matters when selling | ❌ REJECTED — the classification is a property of the **party**, not of the listing. D-14 established three separate marketplace domains (05, 13, 14); a seller's trader status is the same fact in all three, and the same person selling a cymbal in 13 and a plugin in 14 is one trader or one private seller, not two. Putting it in a marketplace domain would triple it and guarantee three divergent answers about one legal fact. Identity owns it; the marketplaces enforce it. |
| DT-02 | This is a cross-cut — it serves 05, 13, 14, Payments and tax | The gate's cross-cut test, applied honestly | ⏳ SPLIT — the *enforcement* (blocking a listing, rendering a disclosure, applying withdrawal rights) genuinely is spread across the commerce domains, and their MoSCoW governs when it bites. But the classification *record* and the declaration *flow* are a fact about the identity with a real user-facing surface. Same seam as 01.01.03 and 01.03.03: identity holds the fact, the consumer enforces it. |
| DT-03 | The Fan's read-only lens is the entire point of the feature, and this is the only identity attribute built to be read by someone else | Reasoning about who each identity attribute is *for* — every other one serves its subject | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it reframes the feature. `personas.md` gives the Fan's worst-accidental behaviour as buying wrongly, and D-11/D-13 flag statutory duties that scale with consumer reach. Trader status is not a profile field the seller maintains for themselves — it is a **disclosure to a stranger who is about to spend money**, and the law's whole intent is that the stranger reads it. That makes the disclosure surface (13/14's) more important than the record surface (this file's), and it means a design that hides the disclosure to reduce purchase friction is not a UX choice but a breach. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Classification is a party attribute owned by identity, not a listing attribute | DT-01 — one legal fact about one person, across three marketplace domains |
| D-02 | Self-declared but not self-determining; the platform acts on obvious mismatches | Trader status is a matter of fact in law; taking a declaration at face value while watching someone trade is not defensible |
| D-03 | The declaration flow asks situational questions, never "are you a trader?" | Self-assessing a legal test produces wrong answers and exposes both parties |
| D-04 | Classification is a precondition of listing; fails closed | Publishing without the disclosure is a breach, not a degraded experience |
| D-05 | The trader's address publication is disclosed *before* listing, prominently | 01.01.04's privacy expectation is about to be lawfully overridden; discovering that afterwards is a betrayal |
