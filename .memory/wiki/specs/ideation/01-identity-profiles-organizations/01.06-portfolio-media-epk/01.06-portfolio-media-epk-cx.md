## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Credit-Backed Portfolio ↔ Profile Page Composition

**Relationship**: The portfolio is the largest and most valuable attested section on the page, and the reason 01.06.01's asserted/attested distinction is a hard rule rather than a style choice. Without the portfolio, the page is a bio and some links — a Linktree. With it, the page contains facts its subject could not have written, and the layout has to make that legible to a stranger in seconds.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their page's credibility comes from the section they cannot edit.
- **Producer**: the same, plus they are the supplier of other people's.
- **Operator**: reads the page for the portfolio; the bio is decoration to them.
- **Fan**: reads it as discovery, without industry literacy — which constrains how corroboration is displayed.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The credit graph (02) owns the data; both features read it. The subject can write emphasis/order (composition state), never content. No merge case, by construction — the two writers touch disjoint fields.
2. **Trigger chain**: Credit attested in 02 → portfolio view changes → page section changes. No action by the subject, and none possible. A credit disputed later changes the page under them.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the sub-domain's defining rule — page-edit rights explicitly exclude attested sections. Anything that lets composition override attested content is a regression to a Linktree.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A new credit should notify its subject — someone said something true about them and their page changed without their involvement.
5. **State transition conflict**: A credit disputed while the page renders it — the page must show disputed state, never silently drop it (01.06.02 D-05).

### CX-02: Credit-Backed Portfolio ↔ EPK Generation

**Relationship**: The EPK's entire advantage over a hand-built PDF is the portfolio. A conventional EPK's credit list is the artist's word; this one's is the graph's — and it stays current because it is a view. Both properties come from the portfolio, not from the EPK. It follows that the EPK is only as strong as the graph, which is why 01.06.02's pre-platform-career gap (DT-03/Q-01) lands here at the worst possible moment: the veteran's pitch.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: pitches with cited credits; the veteran pitches with a thin page.
- **Producer**: pitches a service with a discography.
- **Operator**: the recipient — the only party who benefits from the credits being checkable.
- **Fan**: absent from the EPK entirely.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both are views over 02. The EPK adds emphasis and a purpose; it stores no credits of its own.
2. **Trigger chain**: Credit changes → live EPK changes, **including EPKs already sent**. That is the feature and the hazard (01.06.03 Q-01): a disputed credit can alter a pitch that landed last week.
3. **Permission intersection**: A band's EPK requires a mandate to send (01.03.03); the credits in it belong to individuals, not to the band. A band EPK foregrounding a member's personal credits is a real and unmodelled question.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Open events notify the sender. Do the *credited parties* know their credits are being used in someone else's pitch? Probably not, and probably fine — but unstated.
5. **State transition conflict**: A credit disputed between send and open. Unresolved — 01.06.03 Q-01.

### CX-03: Profile Page Composition ↔ EPK Generation

**Relationship**: The same facts under the same asserted/attested rule, aimed at two different situations. The page is a destination — a stranger arrives, unprompted, and needs orientation. The EPK is an argument — a named recipient was sent it for a reason and needs persuading about one thing. One composition engine, two purposes; the divergence is emphasis, not content.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer**: author both.
- **Operator**: receives the EPK, visits the page. Notably, they may do both for the same band — and the two must not contradict.
- **Fan**: page only.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Page emphasis and EPK emphasis are separate state. Changing one must not change the other — the page is not a pitch.
2. **Trigger chain**: Both regenerate from the same facts. Neither triggers the other.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical rule — asserted editable, attested not.
4. **Notification fan-out**: EPK opens notify; page views do not (or the Musician gets a firehose).
5. **State transition conflict**: None — separate emphasis records over shared read-only facts.

### CX-04: Credit-Backed Portfolio ↔ Profile Page Composition (aliases)

**Relationship**: Aliases fork the portfolio into two legitimate views. The human's page aggregates every alias's credits (their full career); each alias's page shows only that alias's (the project's identity). Both are true, and a stranger's arrival route determines which they see — a fan arriving from a release lands on the alias; a booker arriving from a credit may land on the human.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician/Producer**: hold aliases; both views are theirs.
- **Operator**: cares about the human's full career.
- **Fan**: cares about the alias, and often should not learn the human (01.01.04).

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — 1, 3, 5 deferred to Step 5. (2) A credit attaches to an alias and appears on both that alias's page and the human's aggregate. (4) No additional fan-out. **Open**: whether the human's page publicly reveals which aliases are theirs. It is exactly the link 01.01.04 exists to control, and this is the surface most likely to leak it by accident — a personal page aggregating three aliases' credits has, by that act, disclosed that those three aliases are one person. Flagged as a real risk for Step 5.
