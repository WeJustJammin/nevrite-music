# Feature: Credit-Backed Portfolio

> **Number**: 01.06.02
> **Parent**: [Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK](./01.06-portfolio-media-epk-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The body of work behind a party, assembled from what other people attested rather than from what its subject typed. Every credit in it was supplied by someone who was in the room — a producer who ran the session, a bandmate, a venue that booked the show — and the subject cannot edit any of it. This is `personas.md`'s success criterion made concrete: **credits are cited rather than claimed**.

> **Deep Think addition (split)** — the sweep's candidate bundled portfolio into "Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK" as a presentational concern. Splitting it out is deliberate: a portfolio nobody can edit is a different product from a portfolio its owner writes, and it is the only asset here a competitor cannot manufacture retroactively.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their portfolio assembles itself from credits others gave them. They control emphasis and grouping (lead with the production work), never content. Their pain — "every new client requires re-proving who they are and what they've played on" — ends here: the proof is standing, cited, and someone else said it. |
| Producer | Full | Same on their own portfolio; they are also its largest *supplier* — every session they capture credits at builds someone else's. This is the double role that makes the graph grow: the trust broker's attestation is worth more than a self-claim, so their capture creates value for people who are not them. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads portfolios to decide bookings — "has this band actually played rooms this size?" — and their venue's own attested history (shows played there) is a portfolio in the same sense. They consume it as a hiring signal, which is exactly what makes it worth having. |
| Fan | Read-only | Browses it as discovery: what has this artist played on, who did they play with. The fan is why the portfolio must be legible without industry literacy — the same artifact serving a booker and a curious listener. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The portfolio is a **view over the credit graph** (domain 02), not a stored document. It reads: credits where this party is the credited party, the attester of each, corroboration level, and the work each attaches to. It renders grouped and ordered, with confidence legible.

Three properties define it:

1. **Not editable by its subject.** Emphasis, grouping and ordering are theirs; content is not.
2. **Attribution is visible.** Every credit shows who said it. An unknown attester's word is visibly worth less than a known one's — the credit is not binary.
3. **It exists before its subject does.** An unclaimed shadow party has a portfolio (01.05.01 DT-03), and that is the claim incentive.

Credit *semantics* — what a credit means, how it is captured, how counter-attestation weights it — belong to domain 02. This file owns the identity-side view: what the portfolio says about **this party**.

### Happy Path

1. Producer captures credits at a session (domain 02). The drummer is one of five credited.
2. Drummer's portfolio gains a credit — attributed to the producer, corroborated by three others in the room. They did nothing.
3. A month later a booker looks them up before hiring.
4. Booker sees the work, and sees who says so. No re-proving, no PDF, no "here's my Notes-app credit list".
5. The drummer gets hired on facts that other people supplied. That is the whole thesis, in one page view.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Empty portfolio — new user, nobody has credited them | Honest emptiness, and the platform's real cold-start problem: value arrives only when *someone else* acts | Empty state pointing at the only fix — get credited, or claim a shadow that already has credits |
| Credit is disputed | Shows as disputed, not hidden. Hiding contested facts would make the portfolio a marketing surface again | Disputed marker; route to 24 |
| Self-credit with no corroboration | Renders with visibly minimal weight. `personas.md` names the anti-persona who "inflates credits to claim work they didn't do" — the answer is not to block it but to make it obviously worthless | Uncorroborated marker |
| Attester is a shadow party (someone credited by a non-user) | Weakest corroboration — an unclaimed party's word. Real risk: two shadows attesting each other manufactures a career from nothing | See Q-02; weighting is 02's |
| Party has aliases — whose portfolio is it? | The human aggregates across aliases; each alias shows only its own. Both views are true and needed | See domain CX-04 |
| Credit is accurate but the subject wants it gone (a record they disown) | Cannot delete — it is not theirs. Dispute or de-emphasise | 01.06.01 Q-02 |
| A whole career predates the platform | The portfolio's structural weakness: it can only hold what was captured. A 20-year veteran's page can look thinner than a newcomer's | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-01. This is the adoption problem in one sentence |
| Party merges with a duplicate | Credits must **redirect**, not be rewritten — a rewrite would break the immutability the portfolio's value rests on | See domain CX-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Credit graph query | Skeleton entries |
| Empty | No credits | Honest empty state; the one route out |
| Populated | Credits exist | Grouped work with attribution and corroboration visible |
| Error | Query failed | Error with retry. **Never renders an empty portfolio on failure** — showing a veteran as having done nothing is the worst possible misrepresentation |
| Partial | Some credits pending confirmation from the parties named | Pending shown as pending; the record is honest about its own maturity |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A portfolio is a thing you build | Every portfolio product ever made — SoundBetter, LinkedIn, a PDF one-sheet, a Linktree | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection is the feature. A self-built portfolio is a claim, and `problem-statement.md` says the industry's whole failure is that "they are asking people to remember" — a self-built portfolio asks people to *assert*, which is worse. Making it unbuildable-by-its-subject is the only thing here a competitor cannot copy: Jaxsta and Muso.AI can ingest liner notes, but they cannot make the producer who ran the session attest on the day, because they were not in the room. |
| DT-02 | A credit is binary — you have it or you don't | The obvious model, and how every credits database works | ❌ REJECTED — a credit's value is entirely a function of **who attested it**. `personas.md` calls the Producer a trust broker whose attestation "is worth more than a self-claim, which is precisely what makes counter-attestation valuable". A binary credit throws that away and hands the anti-persona a free reputation machine. Corroboration must be visible on the face of the portfolio, not buried in a detail view. |
| DT-03 | The portfolio's greatest weakness is that it cannot hold the past, and this is the adoption problem in one artifact | Reasoning about the veteran's case: a 20-year session player's page is emptier than a newcomer's who did one session last week | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it needs an answer before launch, not after. The platform's value proposition to its most credible users inverts on day one: the people with the most to prove have the least on their page. Options (self-asserted historic credits marked as such; import from Discogs/MusicBrainz/AllMusic; retroactive counter-attestation by old collaborators) all dilute the "attested at source" purity that is the entire differentiator. Nobody in the sweep named this. Escalated as Q-01. |
