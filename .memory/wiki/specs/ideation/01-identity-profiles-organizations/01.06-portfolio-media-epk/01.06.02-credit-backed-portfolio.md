# Feature: Credit-Backed Portfolio

> **Number**: 01.06.02
> **Parent**: [Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK](./01.06-portfolio-media-epk-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The body of work behind a party, assembled from what other people attested rather than from what its subject typed. Every credit in it was supplied by someone who was in the room — a producer who ran the session, a bandmate, a venue that hosted it — and the subject cannot edit any of it. This is `personas.md`'s success criterion made concrete: **credits are cited rather than claimed**.

> **Deep Think addition (split)** — the sweep's candidate bundled portfolio into "Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK" as a presentational concern. Splitting it out is deliberate: a portfolio nobody can edit is a different product from a portfolio its owner writes, and it is the only asset here a competitor cannot manufacture retroactively.

The seam is the word **party**. `02.01.02` renders one party's page — and an alias *is* a party (01.01.02 D-01/D-02), a Band *is* a party, a room *is* a party. A **human** is not: their career is spread across aliases that `02.01.02` renders as strangers to each other. Nothing in domain 02 aggregates a human, because domain 02 does not know which parties are one person — that fact lives in identity (01.01.02 D-07), and it is privacy-governed.

So: **02 owns the party's discography. 01.06.02 owns the identity's portfolio.** Where they touch the same pixels, 02's `[DEEP]` decisions win and this file cites them.

## Behavior

The portfolio is a **view over the credit graph** (domain 02), not a stored document. It reads: credits where this party is the credited party, the derived rung of each, and the work each attaches to. It renders grouped and ordered, with confidence legible.

Four properties define it:

1. **Not editable by its subject.** Emphasis, grouping and ordering are theirs; content is not.
2. **Evidence is characterised, never exposed.** Every credit carries how well it is known. It never carries **who** said it (D-02, DT-05).
3. **It never claims to be complete.** It is evidence, not a résumé (D-06, DT-07).
4. **It exists before its subject does.** An unclaimed shadow party has a portfolio (01.05.01 DT-03) — the claim incentive, and a publication about a human who never consented (DT-09).

### What publishes and what does not

This is the feature's load-bearing rule, and the breadth pass got it wrong.

| Element | Publishes? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The claim — party, role, work | ✅ Yes | Liner notes are public by nature (`02.01.02` DT-04) |
| The evidence **class and count** — "Confirmed by 3 people who were there" | ✅ Yes | `02.04.02` D-02, D-04 |
| The rung label (professional viewer) | ✅ Yes | `02.01.02` D-02 |
| **Attester identity** | ❌ **Never, at any tier, to any viewer including authenticated professionals** | `02.01.02` DT-04 |
| Session metadata, capture timestamps, capture origin, traversal paths | ❌ Never | `02.01.02` DT-04 |
| A contested marker | ❌ Never publicly | `02.01.02` D-06 |
| A party-level aggregate score ("94% verified") | ❌ **Never. There is no party-level tier** | `02.04.02` D-14 |
| A platform-behaviour statistic (capture rate, session count, response time) | ❌ Never | `02.01.02` D-10 |
| Per-year credit density / activity timeline | ❌ **Never** (D-06, DT-07) | This file; forced by `02.01.05` D-05 |

**Co-contributors are not attesters, and the distinction is what saves the feature.** The portfolio publishes who the subject *worked with* — that is a credit on a shared work, public by nature, and walking it is the point (`02.01.03`). It never publishes who *vouched for* the subject. Everyone on the roll is a collaborator; only some of them attested. Conflating the two would put a private, socially-costly act on a public page and hand a scraper the evidence trail that is the entire moat.

**"Cited rather than claimed" survives this.** The booker does not need the citer's name; they need to know that independent co-present confirmation exists and how much. That is what the evidence sentence carries. The breadth pass conflated *cited* with *the citation is signed in public* — they are different, and only the first is defensible.

### The identity projection — what this file uniquely owns

`02.01.02` renders a party. This file renders a **human**, and a human's portfolio is the union of their aliases' credits (01.01.02 D-02: credits reference the alias; the alias resolves to the person).

| View | Contents | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| **Alias portfolio** | That alias's credits only. Rendered by `02.01.02` as an ordinary party page | Everyone (subject to `02.01.05`) |
| **Human portfolio — owner's view** | Every alias, always, including privately-linked ones. Plus Confidential, embargoed, Unlisted and imported credits, each marked | The human, and only the human |
| **Human portfolio — public view** | Only the aliases whose linkage is **public** (01.01.02 D-07: per-alias, private by default, reversible) | Everyone |

**The aggregate must be computed over the visible set, never filtered from the complete one** — the same rule as `02.01.02` D-09's viewer-relative counts, arriving from the alias direction instead of the embargo one. A human's own header may say 1,847 credits where their public header says 412, and the difference must not be observable. This is the specification, not a caching defect.

**And the leak is not only in the credits (DT-10).** Three derived facts disclose a private alias link even when no private credit renders:

1. **Aggregate scalars.** An active-year range of "1996–2026" on a public page whose visible credits only span 2011–2026 can only be true if a hidden alias was counted. Every scalar — range, totals, per-family counts — is computed over the visible set only.
2. **The collaborator graph.** If the public human page shows "worked with X" and the only edge to X runs through a privately-linked alias, the link is disclosed. Both endpoints are public; the *path* is the leak. This is `02.01.05` DT-08's traversal-path finding arriving from the alias direction, and it binds identically.
3. **Merge (DT-13).** Merging two parties unions their credits (`02.01.02`). If one was privately linked to an alias, the merge publishes the link — a disclosure caused by an unrelated admin action. Merge must re-evaluate linkage visibility per alias, exactly as `02.01.05` D-17 requires it to re-evaluate confidentiality per credit.

**The generalisation, and it is testable**: *nothing derived from a privately-linked alias may vary observably on the human's public portfolio.* One invariant, rather than every future surface independently remembering the rule. It is `02.01.05` D-05's shape, and it is the answer domain CX-04 flagged for this step.

### The pre-platform career

Domain 02 settled the mechanism while this file was still asking the question. The portfolio **does** hold a career that predates the platform:

- Imported credits (`02.03.01`) land at **rung 1**, permanently source-marked, reviewed before they touch the page (`02.03.01` D-02), and **can never be promoted** — not by claiming (`02.04.02` D-03), not by cross-source agreement (`02.03.01` D-03), not by any later act.
- Contemporaneity is unbackfillable by construction (`02.04.02` DT-10), so rung 6 is closed to the past for everyone, honestly and permanently.
- Import is therefore **gravity, never evidence** (`02.03.01` DT-01). The purity holds because the rung holds.

So the veteran's twenty years appear, marked as what they are: a stranger typed them into a database. That is genuinely weaker than four people confirming on the day, and the graph says so forever. What remains is not a mechanism question but a product judgment — see Q-01.

### Never a résumé (DT-07)

The portfolio implies **only what it shows**. No copy anywhere may frame it as complete, full, or a career. `02.01.02` DT-06 reaches this from the thin-page direction; this file reaches it from a worse one — **the portfolio's silences are legible, and the best work makes the biggest holes.** The concrete rule that follows: **no chronological density on any public surface** — no activity graph, no per-year counts, no timeline spine. A *range* ("1996–2026") is a fact; a *timeline* is an invitation to read a gap. This is also already required by `02.01.05` D-05, since a per-year count is a derivation over embargoed credits that varies observably.

Credit *semantics* — what a credit means, how it is captured, how attestation weights it — belong to domain 02. This file owns the identity-side view: what the portfolio says about **this party**, and about the **human** behind it.

### Happy Path

1. Producer captures credits at a session (`02.02`). The drummer is one of five credited. The session inherits confidentiality from the project (`02.01.05` D-01); nobody fills in a form.
2. Drummer's portfolio gains a credit — asserted by the session owner at rung 3, lifted to rung 6 when two co-present players confirm at session close (`02.04.02`). The drummer did nothing.
3. The credit is invisible to the world while the record is unreleased, and **accrues its rung privately** during the embargo (`02.01.05` DT-18) — so it arrives on release day already confirmed, not as a tier-zero row chasing people who have moved on.
4. Domain 12 reports the release; the credit publishes within 60 seconds (`02.01.05` D-03).
5. A month later a booker looks the drummer up before hiring — arriving at the alias page from the record, or at the human page from a credit.
6. Booker sees the work, and sees **how well it is known**: "Confirmed by 3 people who were there." Not who. No re-proving, no PDF, no "here's my Notes-app credit list".
7. The drummer gets hired on facts that other people supplied, characterised by evidence they cannot inspect and the drummer cannot fake. That is the whole thesis, in one page view.
