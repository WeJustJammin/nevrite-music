# Feature: EPK Generation & Sharing

> **Number**: 01.06.03
> **Parent**: [Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK](./01.06-portfolio-media-epk-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The artifact you send to someone who has to decide about you — a promoter, a booker, a label, a sync supervisor. An electronic press kit is a curated, purpose-built package: bio, photos, music, live video, credits, tech requirements, contact. Musicians currently assemble it by hand as a PDF, a Dropbox folder or a Squarespace page, rebuild it for every pitch, and let it go stale the moment they do. Here it is generated from facts the platform already holds, and it stays current because it is a view rather than a document.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Generates an EPK for a specific pitch — a festival, a support slot, a sync brief — with the emphasis that pitch needs. Sends it. Sees whether it was opened, which is the difference between silence and information. The credits section is the part they never have to write or update. |
| Producer | Full | Generates a client-facing kit: discography, room, rates, turnaround, testimonials. Their pitch is a service sale, not a booking, so the same generator serves a different argument. |
| Operator | Read-only | The primary **recipient**. They receive EPKs when booking and are the reason the artifact exists — so the design constraint is theirs: `personas.md` puts them on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk. An EPK that needs a big screen and a PDF reader fails its actual audience. They also send the inverse artifact (a tech pack), which is domain 18's. |
| Fan | None | An EPK is a B2B artifact. A fan gets the public profile — which is a better, livelier surface for them anyway. Explicit absence: showing fans an EPK would be showing them a sales document about a person they already like. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An EPK is a **generated view with a purpose**, assembled from data the platform already holds: identity and aliases (01.01), the credit-backed portfolio (01.06.02), verified badges (01.07), media, and — for live pitches — tech requirements from domain 18.

Two properties make it worth more than a PDF:

1. **It cannot go stale.** It is a view, so it reflects today's facts. A PDF one-sheet is wrong the day after it is exported.
2. **Its credits are attested.** A conventional EPK's credit list is the artist's word. This one's is the graph's — which is precisely the claim a promoter has no way to check today.

Sharing is a link, per recipient, with visibility into whether it was opened. Whether an *exportable* artifact is also required is parent Q-02, and it is a real adoption question — bookers live in email.

**Live view, with a sent-state watch (resolves Q-01).** The EPK never snapshots its data — a recipient always sees today's truth, because showing a promoter a stale attested credit would forfeit the entire "the credits are the graph's" advantage that makes this artifact worth more than a PDF. The staleness hazard is handled on the *sender's* side instead: each send records which facts were foregrounded, and if one of those facts materially changes while the pitch is live — a credit is disputed, a badge expires, a foregrounded release is taken down — the sender is notified so they can re-pitch or add context. The recipient is never silently shown something the sender didn't intend to pitch without the sender knowing it changed. Snapshotting was rejected: a frozen kit is a PDF with extra steps, and reintroduces exactly the staleness the view exists to kill.

### Happy Path

1. Musician wants a festival support slot.
2. They generate an EPK for it: pick the alias, lead with live video, include the tech spec, emphasise the credits that matter to this promoter.
3. System assembles it from existing facts. They write nothing except a short pitch note.
4. They send a link to the promoter.
5. Promoter opens it on their phone. Credits are cited, not claimed — each carries how well it is known ("Confirmed by 3 people who were there"), never who attested it (01.06.02 D-02: attester identity publishes at no tier, to no viewer, and a named recipient on a no-login link is the least protected viewer there is). The artist sees it was opened.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recipient will not click a link — wants an attachment | The adoption wall. Bookers work in email and forward attachments to colleagues; a link that dies behind a login is worse than a PDF | See parent Q-02. Unresolved and important |
| EPK sent, then facts change (a credit is disputed, a badge expires) | The live view updates — this is the feature. The hazard (what was pitched ≠ what is now seen) is caught on the sender's side: the send watches the foregrounded facts and alerts the sender on a material change (dispute, expiry, takedown). The recipient always sees current truth | Sender gets a "a credit in the EPK you sent to {recipient} was disputed" notice; recipient sees the corrected kit, never a stale attested claim |
| Thin portfolio (new user, or a veteran with a pre-platform career) | The EPK is only as good as the graph behind it, so 01.06.02's DT-03 problem lands here at its most visible and most consequential moment | Generation still works; the artifact is honestly thin |
| Recipient forwards it to colleagues | Expected and supported — booking decisions are made by a room, not one booker. The per-recipient link is a *tracking wrapper over public content*, not an access gate, so a forward opens fine for a stranger with no account. Forwarded opens count against that recipient's link; the sender sees "opened 4 times" and cannot honestly be told *who* the extra openers were | Additional opens on the same recipient's link; no signup wall for the forwardee |
| Link is shared publicly | An EPK may contain rates and contact routes not meant for the world | Needs expiry/revocation. See Q-02 |
| A band's EPK — who may send it? | It is the band pitching; sending requires a mandate (01.03.03). Two members sending conflicting EPKs to one promoter is a real embarrassment | See domain CX-01 |
| A band's EPK — what may it *contain*? | A different question from the row above, and unsettled. The band is the pitching party, but the credits that make the pitch persuasive are held by its members individually, and the tree bars the inverse direction outright — no roll-up from a band credit into a member's portfolio (`02.01.02` D-14; 01.06.02 Edge Cases). Nothing states whether the traffic runs the other way | Not specified. See **Q-04** |
| A *human*-scoped EPK sent by someone with privately-linked aliases | Unsettled, and it is a disclosure rather than a publication. 01.06.02 D-07 computes the human portfolio over publicly-linked aliases only — but it scopes that invariant to the **public** portfolio, and an EPK goes to one named recipient who is usually not a user at all | Not specified. See **Q-05** |
| Legal name or address in an EPK | Contact details are a disclosure. No path from the EPK to the legal identity surface (01.01.04 D-01) | Never rendered |
| Recipient has no account | Must work for a complete stranger with no login. The promoter is not our user and never will be | Public link; no signup wall |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An EPK is a document you export | The universal current practice — a PDF one-sheet, which `personas.md` explicitly lists as the Musician's workaround | ❌ REJECTED as the *model*, though possibly required as an output (Q-02). A document is stale on export and its credits are the artist's word. A view is current and its credits are the graph's. But the recipient — a promoter in an email thread — may simply not accept a link, which is why rejecting the model does not settle the artifact. Model and format are separate decisions. |
| DT-02 | One EPK per artist | How every EPK tool works | ❌ REJECTED — an EPK is an *argument*, and the argument differs by recipient. A festival slot pitch, a sync brief and a label pitch need different emphases and different credits foregrounded. One EPK means one argument, which means a generic one, which is why every hand-built EPK is mediocre. Per-pitch generation is nearly free once the facts are already held — which is the whole advantage of generating rather than authoring. |
| DT-03 | The EPK is where the platform's value becomes legible to people who are not users | Reasoning about who actually reads it: the promoter is not our user and may never be | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it changes the feature's strategic weight. The EPK is a **distribution surface**, not a convenience: it is a WeJammin artifact that lands in the inbox of every promoter, label and supervisor its users pitch — people who have never heard of us. Each one shows attested credits they cannot get anywhere else, and every one of those promoters is a future Operator claim (01.05.02). The EPK is the domain's outbound growth loop the way shadow parties are its inbound one. Nobody in the sweep named this, and it argues the artifact must work *perfectly for a stranger with no account, on a phone*. |
| DT-04 | A sent EPK should snapshot at send time to protect the sender from the pitch changing under them (Q-01) | The obvious fix for the "what was pitched ≠ what is now seen" hazard | ❌ REJECTED. Snapshotting freezes the attested credits into a static claim — which is exactly a PDF, and forfeits the live-view advantage that is the whole reason to generate rather than author. The real risk is not that the recipient sees *new* truth (they should); it is that the sender is *unaware* the pitch changed. So the fix is a **sent-state watch on the sender's side**: keep the view live, notify the sender when a foregrounded fact materially changes. The recipient always sees current truth; the sender is never surprised. This resolves Q-01 without reintroducing staleness. |

| Q-03 | Do share links expire or get revoked, given an EPK may carry rates and contact routes? | Agent | `/create-prd-security` |
| Q-04 | **[OWNER]** **May a band's EPK foreground credits that belong to its members personally — and who consents?** The "who may send it" row settles authority (a mandate, 01.03.03); it never settled *content*. A band holds its own credits, but "our drummer played on {record}" is the strongest line in a support-slot pitch and that credit is the drummer's. The tree bars the inverse — no roll-up from a band credit into a member's portfolio (`02.01.02` D-14; 01.06.02 Edge Cases, "synthesising a member credit from a band credit manufactures an assertion nobody made") — and is silent in this direction, which is not the same as permitting it. Three coherent shapes: **band-held credits only** (safe, and discards the pitch's best material); **member credits with per-member consent at generation** (honest, and adds a consent round-trip to an artifact D-02 makes per-pitch and therefore frequent); **member credits by default**, on the ground that they are already public on the member's own page (fastest, and lets a band pitch on the strength of a member who has since left — the legacy-act shape 01.01.02 DT-05 refuses elsewhere). Which one is a call about what a band may claim about its people. Recorded from sub-domain CX-02 §3, which named it "a real and unmodelled question" and left it untracked. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-05 | **[OWNER]** **Does a *human*-scoped EPK disclose the sender's privately-linked aliases to the named recipient?** 01.06.02 **D-07** aggregates only publicly-linked aliases and forbids any derived fact varying observably — but it scopes that invariant to "the human's **public** portfolio", and an EPK is a per-recipient disclosure, not a publication. 01.06.02's own cross-cut note to this file records the gap in as many words: a human-scoped EPK "discloses the sender's alias set to that recipient... and unmodelled." The multi-hyphenate is the whole case: a producer pitching a mix service *wants* this one booker to see three names' worth of career, and spent that career keeping those names apart in public. Fail-closed (inherit the public visible set; the sender opts an alias in per send) and sender's-choice (the kit is theirs to compose, and they chose the recipient) point opposite ways. Note the asymmetry that makes it urgent rather than academic: a one-tap "include {alias}" on a frequent artifact is an easy accidental disclosure, and D-06 makes the link a forwardable tracking wrapper — so it lands in a room the sender never chose, permanently. | User | `/create-prd-security` |
