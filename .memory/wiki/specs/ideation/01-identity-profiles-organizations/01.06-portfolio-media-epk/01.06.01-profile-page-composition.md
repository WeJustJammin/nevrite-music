# Feature: Profile Page Composition

> **Number**: 01.06.01
> **Parent**: [Portfolio, Media Reel & EPK](./01.06-portfolio-media-epk-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What a party's page is made of, and who decides. One composition model serves a drummer, a band, a venue and a studio — the same artifact with different sections, driven by what the party *is* (01.01.01 facets, 01.02.01 types) and what it has *done* (credits, shows, listings). The page is the platform's most-visited surface and the artifact hiring decisions are made from, so composition is not a layout preference; it is what a stranger learns in eight seconds.

The file exists because three things collide here and nowhere else. **The whole party is visible at once** — no other domain sees a person's five facets, their aliases, their credits and their rooms simultaneously. **The platform's thesis becomes a visual fact or it doesn't** — provenance (D-18) is an abstraction until a stranger can tell, without a legend, which parts of this page its subject wrote. And **the page is the only surface in the domain that renders for a party who has not consented to exist on it** (parent D-03) — an unclaimed page is a document published about a person, assembled entirely from other people's assertions.

This file owns **behavior**: what a page is made of, in what order, who may write which parts, and what happens when any of it is missing, contested, concurrent or hostile. Visual treatment is `/create-prd-design-system`'s — but D-02 below makes one visual property a correctness requirement rather than a preference.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Composes their page. The sections that matter are mostly not theirs to write — credits, collaborators and history are supplied by the graph. What they control is **emphasis and the asserted layer**: photo, bio, descriptors, links, reel selection. Their specific composition problem is the domain's hardest: five simultaneous facets and no primary role to sort them by (01.01.01 D-01). Resolved by D-07 — their **record** is one filtered chronology of work (roles are properties of each credit, never section headers), and their **detail** is sectioned per facet. They set the default filter ("lead with production"); they cannot make the drumming unreachable. |
| Producer | Full | Same model, different economics: their page converts a client where a Musician's establishes credibility. That does **not** buy them a different spine (D-06) — a rate card above a discography is a pitch from someone you have no reason to believe yet. Their conversion need is served by an **action in the header** ("Hire", "Check availability"), not by promoting the asserted layer above the attested one. Their room, rates, turnaround and testimonials live in the detail layer, sectioned under `producer`/`engineer`. They are also the page's largest off-page *supplier*: every session they capture builds someone else's record layer. |
| Operator | Full | Composes the venue/studio/rehearsal page — the most information-dense artifact in the domain, and the org-side multi-hyphenate (one address that is a rehearsal room, a studio and a 120-cap venue — domain D-06). Serves a Fan deciding whether to go and a band deciding whether to play, **without becoming two pages** (D-08): the calendar is the Now layer (volatile, fan-facing, leads because it is non-empty for a venue and empty for most musicians), show history is the record, and the room spec is the detail — sectioned by type, because a PA spec and a rate card share no fields. Their operationally-relied-upon attributes carry a confirmation age and render flagged when stale (01.02.01 D-03/D-07/D-08) — this page is where `personas.md`'s Operator worst-accidental ("publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show") is either prevented or committed. |
| Fan | Config | A page exists for every party — a Fan's included, because it is the same object (D-04, and 01.01.01 D-07's existence/visibility split). What varies is **discovery, not archetype**: with zero professional facets and no attested content, D-03 derives away every professional section on its own, so a Fan's page is thin without a fourth page type being designed. Whatever remains is configured (photo, display name, follow visibility), never composed — no reordering, no emphasis, no reel. Whether a Fan's page is *findable* is a product decision the composition model does not need answered (parent Q-01 / domain Q-10). `personas.md` requires the Fan surface to be "a different product wearing the same brand", and this is the page where that is most tempting to violate: giving fans emphasis controls would be professional-tool complexity sold to the one persona with no professional stake. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### The unit of provenance is the fact, not the section

The breadth pass said sections divide into **asserted** (bio, photos, links, rates — the subject wrote them) and **attested** (credits, collaborators, shows played, badges — other parties supplied them). The division is right; the **granularity was wrong**, and two ratified upstream decisions already prove it:

- **01.02.01 D-06** — an org attribute "may carry **counterparty confirmations** alongside the org's own assertion". A venue's asserted 120-capacity claim, confirmed by twelve bands who played it, is one fact with two provenance layers. It is not an asserted section and not an attested section.
- **The reel** (DT-07) — an asserted *selection* of clips over *attested* works. The subject chose the 30 seconds; the graph says they were on the record.

So: **provenance is a property of every fact; a section is only a container, and a section may mix.** Every fact on the page renders with its own provenance state:

| Provenance state | Meaning | Who can change the fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Asserted** | The subject said it | The subject |
| **Attested** | A named other party said it, on the record, at the time | Nobody on this page — only the attester, via domain 02 |
| **Confirmed-assertion** | The subject said it; named counterparties corroborated it (01.02.01 D-06) | The subject owns the claim; the confirmations are not theirs to add or remove |
| **Creator-asserted** | A third party said it about an unclaimed party who has never spoken (01.05.01) | The creator, until claim; then the claimant |
| **Disputed** | Attested, then contested (01.06.02 D-05) | Nobody — renders marked, never hidden, never dropped |

D-01 is unchanged and unweakened: **the subject cannot edit attested content.** What D-05 adds is that "attested" is a per-fact state, so a subject editing their capacity claim does not touch the twelve confirmations attached to it, and a section is never wholesale one thing or the other.

The visual distinction between these states must survive every layout decision downstream (D-02). A page where a self-claimed credit looks identical to an attested one has thrown away the platform's only structural advantage.

### The spine — one model, four layers

Available sections derive from the party's facets/types **and from data that exists** (D-03). A drummer with no listings has no "for sale" section, not an empty one. There is no scaffolding, no placeholder, no "add your rates" prompt occupying space.

Every page — person, alias, band, label, venue, studio, agency, collective — is the same four layers in the same order (D-06):

| # | Layer | Contains | Volatility | Present when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Header** | Who this is: display name, handle, descriptors, photo, claim state, badges (01.07), classification markers (01.08), and the page's actions (Follow · Hire · Book · Claim) | Low | Always |
| 2 | **Now** | What a visitor can act on today: announced shows, open-for-work status, a venue's calendar, a live listing | Highest | **Only when non-empty** |
| 3 | **Record** | What the party has done, and who says so: the credit-backed portfolio (01.06.02), shows played, collaborators, rooms worked in | Append-only | Only when non-empty |
| 4 | **Detail** | What the subject says about themselves: bio, reel, rates, room spec, gear, links, tech requirements | Low | Only when non-empty |

The ordering is **descending by decision latency**, not by importance: what a visitor can act on *today* precedes what they use to *decide*, which precedes what they read if still unsure.

**The layer order is the platform's and is not user-reorderable.** This is what makes one spine serve two audiences without adapting to either (D-08), and the mechanism is D-03 rather than any per-viewer logic: a venue always has a Now (`personas.md`: "their calendar is the business"), so its page leads fan-facing. A working musician usually has no announced show, so their Now is absent and their page leads with the record — booker-facing. **The data does the adapting.** Nothing is guessed about the viewer, and two people looking at the same URL see the same page.

### The composition law — filter what is homogeneous, section what is heterogeneous

This resolves the domain's hardest composition problem (Q-01) and the venue's two-audience problem (Q-03) with one rule:

- **The Record layer is filtered, never sectioned.** Attested facts are homogeneous — a credit is a credit whether it was earned producing, drumming or engineering. Role is a **property of each credit** (a multi-hyphenate who produced a record *and* drummed on it holds two credits on it — 01.01.01's ratified normal case), not a section header. The default view is every work, reverse-chronological. Filter chips, derived only from facets **with evidence behind them**, narrow it.
- **The Detail layer is sectioned by facet/type.** Asserted facts are heterogeneous — a PA spec, a rate card and a mixing turnaround share no fields and cannot be interleaved. Sectioning is forced here, not chosen.

The five-facet wall never forms, because the wall was only ever a consequence of composing by role. Compose by *work* and the roles collapse into it: a stranger does not need "this person is a drummer AND a producer" as two competing walls; they need "this person made these 30 records, and here is what they did on each." The org side falls out identically — a rehearsal-room/studio/venue's *history* is one filtered list of things that happened at that address; only its *specs* fork.

### Who writes what

| Writer | May write | May never |
|---|---|---|
| The subject (or a full-mandate holder) | Asserted layer; emphasis; reel selection | Attested facts; layer order; the visual treatment (D-10) |
| A mandate holder (01.03.03, `administer`) | The org's asserted layer, attributably | Exceed the granting party's authority (01.03.03 D-03); transfer or retire the identity (01.01.02 D-08) |
| An estate (01.10, representation edge) | A marked estate notice | Rewrite first-person asserted content; add or remove facets (frozen — 01.01.01) |
| The graph (domains 02, 16, 17, 01.07) | The attested layer, continuously, without the subject's involvement | Be edited from this page by anyone |
| A shadow party's creator (01.05.01) | The creator-asserted header, until claim | Act as the party (01.05.01 D-05) |

**Emphasis** is the subject's only lever over the attested layer, and it is precisely the visibility axis (01.01.01 D-07) applied to the public surface instead of the authenticated one. It orders and weights; it never removes, and it never makes a facet's work unreachable — a booker looking for the drumming must find it on a page that leads with production. Emphasis is **not** the primary role that 01.01.01 D-01 bans: D-01 bans a primary role as a *property of the record* — one value, structural, forking the account, driving defaults across 24 domains. Emphasis is a *property of one page* — presentational, reversible, per-surface, and it un-asserts nothing. DT-01 over there is explicit that "defaults must come from observed behaviour, not a declared primary"; a page default the user set on their own page is not a declared primary, and downstream readers must not collapse the two.

### Happy Path

1. A person record exists — created at signup, or created by reference when a Producer credited them at a session they never logged into (01.01.01 DT-05). Either way the page exists from the first moment, carrying a name and nothing else.
2. A stranger visiting it now sees the Header and an honest empty state: no scaffolding, no "0% complete", no invented sections. One route out — get credited, or claim a page that already has credits.
3. A Producer captures a session (domain 02). A credit attaches to this party, attributed and corroborated. **The Record layer appears on its own.** The subject did nothing and was not asked.
4. The subject is notified that their page changed because someone said something true about them (CX-01) — the first thing the platform ever tells them that no competitor could have told them.
5. They add the parts the graph cannot supply: a photo, a bio, and their descriptors ("session drummer, jazz/soul" · "mixing engineer"). The descriptors map to a controlled vocabulary as they type, and free text they invent is kept and displayed rather than coerced (D-15) — because these descriptors, not their facets, are what 04/05/08 match on (DT-05).
6. Months pass. Credits accrue from four different kinds of work. The Record stays **one** reverse-chronological list; filter chips appear only for the facets with evidence behind them. No wall forms.
7. They set the default filter to lead with production. Drumming is one chip away, never gone (D-09).
8. A booker lands from a credit on a record they liked. Header: who, what they do, verified badges. No Now (no announced shows). Record: 30 works, each showing what this person did on it and **who said so**. They stop reading at the fifth credit and send a message.
9. The subject was hired on facts other people supplied, in a form they could not have faked and did not have to write. That is the thesis in one page view.

| **Multi-hyphenate with five facets** | **RESOLVED (D-07)** — the record is one filtered chronology; role is a property of each credit, not a section header. | Concurrent edit — two mandate holders, **same section** | Conflict surfaced, never silently overwritten. Composition's only duty is that the bio cannot **look** like anything but a bio (D-10) | Renders as asserted text. Report route via 24 |
| Unclaimed page is indexed by search engines | A page about a real person, in search results for their name, containing claims they never made, before they consented to exist here. Discogs and MusicBrainz set precedent; precedent is not permission | Composition's answer is D-17 (a correction route that does not require a claim). The indexing decision itself is Q-06 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Page fetch | Header resolves first and independently; lower layers render section-level skeletons. A skeleton is only shown where a section is known to exist — an absent section must never flash in and out |
| Empty | No facts yet, no asserted content | Honest empty page: header, and one route to a real fact. No scaffolding, no completion meter (D-11), no invented sections |
| Populated | Facts accrued | The four-layer spine, with every fact carrying its provenance state (D-05) and the attested treatment legible to a stranger with no legend (D-02) |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry. **Never renders a partial page as complete**, and never renders a failed section as absent (D-12) — on this page absence means "no data exists", so an error rendered as absence is a factual claim the platform did not intend to make |
| Partial | Some layers resolved, the record still resolving | Section-level skeletons in the unresolved layers; resolved layers are usable. Never a page that looks finished while its best content is missing |
| Unclaimed | Party created by reference (01.05.01), no owner | The record renders in full; the asserted layer does not exist because nobody has spoken. The header is creator-asserted and names its creator. A claim affordance fronts the page; a correction route sits beside it and does not require claiming (D-17) |
| Frozen | Party deceased (01.10) | Legacy treatment. Facets and asserted content read-only; a marked estate notice may be added; the record is untouched and permanent |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The profile is a presentation layer over other domains' data, therefore not a real feature | The strongest argument against this sub-domain — a verifier already used a version of it against the whole domain | ❌ REJECTED — the domain overview settles the destination question ("the most-visited page type... the artifact hiring decisions are made from"), but there is a better reason. The page is where the platform *decides what verified means* to a stranger in eight seconds. That decision cannot live in 02 (which knows credits but not the person's other four facets) or in 16 (which knows rooms but not reputations). Composition is the only place the whole party is visible at once, and it is a product decision, not a rendering one. |
| DT-02 | Users should have rich composition control | The creator-platform instinct — musicians are expressive people and will want it | ⏳ SPLIT — control over *asserted* sections, none over *attested* ones. The distinction is the feature. A page where the subject can edit their credits is a Linktree with extra steps, and every competitor already has one. The value is precisely that some of the page is not theirs to write. How much control over the rest is a design question (parent Q-03) — but the line itself is not negotiable. **Sharpened by DT-06**: the line is not drawn between sections, it is drawn between facts. |
| DT-03 | The visual distinction between asserted and attested content is the platform's entire differentiator, expressed in a layout decision | Reasoning from `personas.md`: the Musician's success criterion is "credits are **cited** rather than claimed" — that word is doing a lot of work | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a constraint on `/create-prd-design-system`, not just this file. If a self-written bio and an attested credit render with the same visual weight, the platform has spent its whole thesis on a page that looks like every other music profile. The distinction has to be legible to a stranger who has never heard of us and will not read a legend. Nobody in the sweep named this; it is the point at which provenance either becomes visible or does not. |
| DT-04 | The page should adapt to its viewer — show a booker the credits, show a fan the shows | The obvious fix for the venue's two-audience problem (Q-03), and every personalisation instinct in modern product design | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection produced the better answer. Three fatal objections. (1) **A page that renders differently per viewer is not a credibility artifact** — "I saw X on your page" stops being checkable, two bookers comparing notes see different pages, and screenshotting (how bookers actually forward things) becomes misleading. For a *provenance* platform this is self-harm. (2) **The stranger has no signal** — the highest-value visitor is a promoter with no account (01.06.03 DT-03), so adaptation would work worst exactly where the page matters most. (3) It is **unnecessary**, which is the interesting part: D-03 already derives sections from data that exists, and a venue's calendar is non-empty while a session drummer's is empty — so the same fixed spine leads fan-facing on a venue page and booker-facing on a musician page **with no adaptation at all**. The data adapts; the page does not. What survives is a labelled entry point ("Play here"), which is navigation, is in the URL, and is shareable. |
| DT-05 | The bio/descriptor field is asserted decoration — the soft human part, low stakes | What the breadth pass implied by listing it beside "photos, links" as a thing "the graph cannot supply" | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the sharpest structural finding in the file. 01.01.01 D-04 relocated self-description **onto this page** ("the facet vocabulary is closed; self-description is open and lives on the profile"), and 01.01.01 D-10 established that **matching in 04/05/08 runs on descriptors, not facets** — because instrument fails the facet generation rule. Chaining them: the descriptor field on this page is the **matching key for the platform's highest-volume query** ("need a bassist for Tuesday"). 01.01.01 explicitly escalated structured descriptors as "a cross-cut this file surfaces but does not own" — and the input surface landed here, unlabelled. If this field is a free-text box, the platform's most-run search is a string match. The fix does not need inventing: 01.02.01 D-09 already ratified the exact pattern for the same shape of problem ("accept free-text input mapped to a controlled vocabulary; unmapped values are kept and displayed, never coerced to 'Other'"). Applied as D-15. |
| DT-06 | Sections are either asserted or attested — the division is clean and section-level | This file's own breadth pass ("Sections divide into two kinds, and the distinction is the point") | ❌ REJECTED as stated, ✅ CONFIRMED as the principle at the right granularity. Two ratified upstream decisions already produce facts the binary cannot hold. **01.02.01 D-06**: an org attribute may carry counterparty confirmations alongside its own assertion — a venue's 120-cap claim corroborated by twelve acts is one fact with two provenance layers, and it belongs to no section kind. **The reel** (DT-07): an asserted *selection* over *attested* works. So provenance is a property of the **fact**, and a section is only a container that may mix. D-01 survives intact and unweakened; what changes is the unit. This matters downstream: a design system told "attested sections look like X" builds a component; told "every fact carries its provenance", it builds a vocabulary — and only the vocabulary can render a confirmed assertion. |
| DT-07 | A credit proves you were there; it does not prove you are any good — and nothing in this sub-domain lets a stranger *hear* the party | Reasoning about the actual hiring decision a booker makes, plus a gap audit: "**Media Reel**" is in the sub-domain's own name and in no feature file | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a hole in the sub-domain, not just this file. The whole attested-credit apparatus establishes **presence**, and presence is not competence — a booker hiring a session drummer wants to hear them play, and every music platform that works is audio-first (SoundCloud, Bandcamp, SoundBetter's samples). A page that proves someone was in the room and never lets you hear what they did there fails the decision it exists to serve, which is arguably the Jaxsta failure mode wearing a nicer page. The sweep candidate was named "Portfolio, **Media Reel** & EPK"; the three children are composition, portfolio and EPK; the reel dissolved between them and nobody owns upload, clip selection, formats, ordering, rights or takedown. Composition owns the reel as a *section* (asserted selection over attested works, role travelling with the clip — see DT-08); whether the reel needs its own sibling feature is a structural call for Step 6 — Q-03. |
| DT-08 | The reel is a portfolio problem | The obvious classification, given it sits beside the credits and shows the same works | ❌ REJECTED — **the reel is a rights problem wearing a portfolio's clothes**, and this is the file's hardest cross-domain finding. A session player does not own the master they played on. Posting 30 seconds of a label's record on your profile is standard practice and legally grey everywhere in the industry — and here it stops being grey, because **the platform's own provenance graph turns the page into a self-labelled infringement index**. A reel item captioned "Drums on 'Song' by 'Artist' — attested by their producer" identifies the infringing use, the work, and the rightsholder to serve, with an attestation as evidence. *We built the takedown targeting system and pointed it at our own users.* Nobody designs for this; the industry's grey area survives on obscurity, and attestation removes obscurity. | DT-09 | D-03's elegance ("absent sections, not empty ones") is purely an anti-clutter rule | How the breadth pass framed it — a reaction against scaffolding and completion nags | ❌ REJECTED as the whole story, ✅ CONFIRMED as a rule with a second-order consequence nobody named: **D-03 makes absence semantically load-bearing.** Once absent means "no data exists", absence is a *factual claim the page makes*, and every other path that can produce absence becomes a way to lie. A credits section that vanishes on a failed fetch tells every visitor that a 20-year veteran has done nothing — the worst misrepresentation this page can produce, on the page hiring decisions are made from, caused by a network error. D-03's own elegance is what creates the trap, which is why it needed saying (D-12). |
| DT-10 | The asserted/attested distinction is safe once the design system renders it — the risk is that we fail to *build* the distinction | DT-03's framing, and the natural way to think about a visual guarantee | ❌ REJECTED as complete — **the distinction is attackable from the asserted side, and that is the side nobody watches.** If the attested treatment is a visual state (a marker, a glyph, a weight), then any asserted content that can imitate that treatment defeats it: a bio typed as "★ VERIFIED PRODUCER · 400 CREDITS", a display name carrying the verification glyph, a user-uploaded image of a badge. This is *not* the impersonation case 01.01.02 D-05 handles — that is impersonating a **person**, and the credit graph disambiguates it. This is impersonating a **state**, which the credit graph cannot disambiguate because there is no rival party to compare against. Twitter shipped the identical hole (blue check in the display name) and it took years and a rendering change to close. Two consequences fall out and both are composition's: **the attested treatment is a reserved visual vocabulary no asserted content may enter** (fixed rendering for asserted text, no rich text, verification glyph set excluded from names/handles/descriptors), and **user theming is banned** — because the moment the subject controls the visual system, D-02 stops being a platform guarantee and becomes a suggestion. That second one has a real adoption cost (D-10), and it is worth paying. |
| DT-11 | The page should show profile completeness — a strength score, a "your profile is 40% complete" nudge | LinkedIn, every marketplace, every onboarding playbook. On *this* page it is worse, because **the sections that matter are not the subject's to fill**. A completeness meter over a page whose best content is other people's attestations is scoring a musician on how much other people have said about them — telling a new player they are "40% complete" when the missing 60% is credits only strangers can give, and telling a 20-year veteran with a pre-platform career (01.06.02 DT-03) that their career is incomplete. It converts the platform's honest empty state — *the page is empty because nobody has credited you yet, and here is the one route out* — into a personal failing. The empty state's job is to point at the one action that fills it, and that action belongs to someone else. |
| DT-12 | A "primary role" is banned (01.01.01 D-01), therefore the page cannot have a default view either | The strict reading of D-01, and the trap this file would fall into by being careful | ❌ REJECTED — the two are different objects and collapsing them would leave every multi-hyphenate's page an unordered wall in the name of a rule that was never about ordering. D-01 bans a primary role as a **property of the record**: one value, structural, forking the account at signup, driving defaults across 24 domains, and answering "are you an artist OR a producer?" — the question `personas.md` says fails the Musician immediately. Page emphasis is a **property of one page**: presentational, per-surface, reversible, asserting nothing, and set by the subject on their own page after the fact. 01.01.01 DT-01 is explicit that "defaults must come from observed behaviour, not a declared primary" — and a default the user set on their own page is neither a declared primary nor a fork. It is the visibility axis (01.01.01 D-07) applied to the public surface instead of the authenticated one, which is a move the three-axis model already licenses. Logged because a downstream reader *will* collide these two and either ban a necessary control or permit a banned one. Constrained by D-09: emphasis orders, never removes, and never makes a facet's work unreachable. |
| DT-13 | The trader disclosure must render on the profile, because 01.08 D-01 makes trader status a **party** attribute owned by identity — and the profile is the party's page | The honest reading of two ratified decisions colliding: domain CX-08 says trader law "lawfully demolishes" 01.01.04's privacy model, and this file's breadth pass claimed a legal-name leak is "structurally impossible" — both cannot be true | ❌ REJECTED, and the collision dissolves on inspection. CX-08 states the duty precisely: name and address are "published **on their listings**". The statutory duty attaches where a consumer is about to be bound — the listing and the checkout — **not to the identity surface**. So the two ratified positions were never in conflict; the breadth pass's edge case was right about the page and silent about why. The separation is clean and worth making explicit (D-14): the page may render the **classification** (a fact about the party, no legal-surface read, and per the domain index the Fan's read-only access to 01.08 "is the *point*"); the **address disclosure** binds to the commerce surface. A listing card on the page is a summary that links out; the card is not the point of binding, so the card carries no disclosure. Whether any jurisdiction attaches the duty to a trader's *profile* rather than their listings is a legal question this file cannot answer and must not guess — Q-05. |
| DT-14 | A Fan needs a fourth, radically thinner page archetype — the domain has been asking this since the sweep (parent Q-01, domain Q-10) | The framing of the open question itself, which presumes the answer requires a new page type | ❌ REJECTED as a *composition* question, ✅ CONFIRMED as a *discovery* question — and the reframing is what unblocks it. The question assumes facet count predicts page shape, and 01.01.01 DT-10 already disproves that: a 20-year veteran who removes every facet is zero-facet and unambiguously has a page, while a Fan is zero-facet and might not. Identical inputs, opposite intuitions — so facets are not the predicate. **Data is.** D-03 derives sections from facets *and existing data*, so a Fan with no professional facets and no attested content gets no professional sections **automatically**, with no fourth archetype designed, no branch, and no violation of D-04's one model. The veteran gets a career page from the same rule, because they have the data. What genuinely remains open is not what a Fan's page *looks* like — that is settled and derived — but whether it is **indexed, linked and findable**, which is the existence/visibility split (01.01.01 D-07) again and is a product decision about a consumer population, not a composition one. The composition model needs no answer to ship; discovery does. Parent Q-01 / domain Q-10 should be read as a discovery question. |

| D-17 | **An unclaimed party's correction/removal route must not require a claim** | The page publishes assertions about someone who never consented to exist here. | D-18 | **A reel item inherits the role from its credit, not just the work** | DT-08's cheap half. A true tambourine credit clipped to sound like the drum performance is a false impression made credible by our own attestation. The role travelling with the clip closes the laundering at no cost |
