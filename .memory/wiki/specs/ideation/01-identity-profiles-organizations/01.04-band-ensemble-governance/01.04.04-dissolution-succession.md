# Feature: Dissolution & Succession

> **Number**: 01.04.04
> **Parent**: [Band & Ensemble Governance](./01.04-band-ensemble-governance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens when a band stops. The name, the catalogue, the royalty streams, the followers, the unsettled money and the members' shares all have to go somewhere, and the moment they need deciding is the moment the people involved are least able to decide anything together. This feature executes the disposition the band recorded when it still agreed (01.04.01, 01.04.02) and — where it recorded nothing, which is most bands — makes the state of affairs legible rather than pretending the band still exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a member: initiates or participates in dissolution per the decision rules. Sees what happens to their share, their credits, and the catalogue. Their credits survive absolutely — the band ending does not un-play the record they played on. As a **former** member, they retain standing over their historic share (Q-03 on the parent index). |
| Producer | Read-only | Usually not a member — but they hold credits and often points on the catalogue the dissolution disposes of. They get notification and visibility, not a vote. A dissolution that silently reassigns a catalogue their royalties ride on is a real harm, and read-only visibility is the minimum defence. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees the public consequence only: whether this band can still be booked, and if the name has transferred, which party it now resolves to. A venue holding a booking for a band that just dissolved needs exactly that fact and nothing else. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the public consequence: the band's page states it is no longer active. This is not cosmetic — the followers are an asset the name carries (01.04.02 DT-03), and if the name transfers, the fan's follow follows it. They deserve to know that happened. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Dissolution is a governed event, not a delete. It sets the band's terminal lifecycle state (01.02.02) and resolves the disposition of: the **name** (per 01.04.02's rule — retired, transferred, or contested), the **balance** (per 01.04.03 — it cannot be orphaned), the **catalogue and rights** (domain 09 — this file does not decide ownership, it triggers 09's flow), the **membership graph** (all edges get end dates; nothing is deleted), and the **credits** (untouched, permanently).

The invariant: **dissolution disposes of the party's assets and authority; it never touches the record of what was done.** Every credit, every membership edge, every historic term stays exactly as it was.

Where the band recorded no terms, the platform executes nothing and states the position: the assets have no recorded disposition and the members must agree or litigate. Honest inability beats an invented default.

### Happy Path

1. Band agrees to stop. A member with the standing to do so (01.04.01's rules) initiates dissolution.
2. System shows every open item: an unsettled balance, two live bookings, the name's recorded disposition, the catalogue's rights position.
3. Members work through them — the recorded terms supply the answers they agreed years ago, when they liked each other.
4. All required parties confirm per the decision rules.
5. Band enters `dissolved`. Its page persists read-only stating it is no longer active; every credit is intact; the catalogue continues generating royalties to whoever 09 says owns it; former members keep their history.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No recorded terms — most bands | Platform cannot execute a disposition it was never given. It states the position, freezes nothing, and offers the members a route to agree now | "This band has no recorded agreement on the name, the balance or the catalogue. Here is what each of you would need to agree." |
| Members do not all agree to dissolve | Not dissolved. A band is not dissolved because one member wants it to be — that is a departure (01.03.01), not a dissolution | Departure path offered instead |
| Live obligations exist (bookings, unsettled money, an undelivered release) | Dissolution blocked on them, same rule as closure (01.02.02) | Itemised list with routes to resolve each |
| Name transfers to one faction | Followers transfer with the name (01.04.02 DT-03) — an audience built by four people goes to one. The platform can see this and should not do it silently | Fans see the band's page change hands `[PENDING — Step 5: what exactly are they told?]` |
| A member has died | 01.10 handles the estate; their share passes to it. Their credits and membership history are untouched | See domain CX-09 |
| A non-member stakeholder (producer with points, former member with a share) objects | Standing is unresolved — parent Q-03. Notification is the floor; a vote is not obviously right | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Band dissolves; two ex-members re-form under the name years later | The dissolved party persists; a new party would fork the history. Reactivation vs re-creation is unmodelled | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Dissolution is a fiction — the band just quietly stopped (the actual common case) | Nobody initiates anything. The band goes dormant (01.02.02), and every open question stays open until money appears years later | This is the real-world default, and the honest reason this file's flow is a `could` while the *terms it executes* are earlier |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Dissolution state fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Band active — the normal state | No dissolution surface |
| Populated | Dissolution initiated | Open items, decisions needed, who has confirmed |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry. Never renders a band as dissolved on a read failure |
| Partial | `dissolved`, disposition incomplete (name contested, balance unsettled) | Public page: "no longer active". Members: the unresolved items, still open. Partial dissolution is a real and durable state |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Dissolution is a rare edge case and can be deferred indefinitely | It happens once per band, years in — the case for `could` | ⏳ PARTIALLY REJECTED — the *flow* is late, but its **data preconditions are not deferrable at all**. Executing a disposition years from now requires membership edges with dates (01.03.01 D-01), recorded terms (01.04.01), and a name record (01.04.02) — all captured while the band was friendly. If those are not captured early, the dissolution flow has nothing to execute and the platform is just another place to reconstruct from memory. **The flow is a `could`; the data it consumes is not.** That distinction is the honest MoSCoW argument here. |
| DT-02 | Dissolution should clean up the band's data | The instinct of every CRUD model, and what angry members will ask for | ❌ REJECTED, absolutely. This is the platform's single worst possible action: `problem-statement.md` exists because "the proof of it evaporates", and deleting a dissolved band's record would evaporate the credits of everyone who ever played with them — third parties who are not part of the dispute and never consented. Dissolution disposes of assets and authority; it never touches the record. |
| DT-03 | The band's dissolution is the moment the platform's whole thesis is either proved or exposed | `problem-statement.md`: facts are "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable" | ✅ CONFIRMED — dissolution *is* that moment, precisely described. It is the platform's proof event: either the facts are there (captured on the day, by people with no reason to lie) or the platform is another place to argue from memory. Everything else in this sub-domain exists to make this moment survivable — which is the strongest available argument for capturing 01.04.01/01.04.02 early even though nobody wants to think about them. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Dissolution disposes of assets and authority; it never touches the record of work | DT-02 — deleting would evaporate uninvolved third parties' careers |
| D-02 | With no recorded terms, the platform states the position and executes nothing | Inventing a default disposition for a contested asset would be the platform taking a side |
| D-03 | Dissolution requires the band's own decision rules to be satisfied; one member wanting out is a departure, not a dissolution | Otherwise any member could unilaterally dissolve a partnership they are one quarter of |
| D-04 | Live obligations block dissolution | Consistent with 01.02.02's closure rule; a dissolved band with a confirmed show is a venue's problem, not just the band's |
