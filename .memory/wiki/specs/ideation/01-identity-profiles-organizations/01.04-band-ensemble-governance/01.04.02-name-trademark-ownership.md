# Feature: Name & Trademark Ownership

> **Number**: 01.04.02
> **Parent**: [Band & Ensemble Governance](./01.04-band-ensemble-governance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who owns the band's name — recorded before it is worth fighting over. The name is usually the band's most valuable asset and the one nobody thinks to assign: it accrues the followers, the reputation, the tour history and the catalogue association, and at dissolution it is the single most litigated item in music. This feature records the band's intent about the name (all members jointly, the founders, one person, the entity) and surfaces any registered trademark; **it does not register or adjudicate anything** (parent D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a member: records who the band agrees owns the name, and what happens to it if the band splits or if a member leaves. Reads it. Sees whether a trademark is registered and by whom. Non-members see nothing of the internal agreement. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees only the public consequence: which entity the name currently resolves to, so they book the right band. A venue that books "Wolf Alice" needs to know which party that name currently is — which matters precisely when a split has produced two claimants. |
| Producer | None | No standing. They may hold credits on records made under the name, but the name itself is the band's asset and the producer has no claim to it. Explicit absence — a producer's stake at dissolution is their credits (01.04.04), not the name. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The fan's relationship to the name is following it (domain 20); who *owns* it is private until a dissolution makes it public through 01.04.04. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Records the band's stated intent on name ownership: which parties own it, on what basis, and the disposition rule if a member leaves or the band dissolves (name stays with the entity / with a named member / with the majority / retired entirely).

Separately, records any **registered trademark**: mark, jurisdiction, classes, registrant, status. This is a factual record supplied by the band — the platform does not search registries or verify registration `[PENDING — see Q-02]`.

Explicitly out of scope: registering a trademark, opposing one, assessing common-law rights, resolving a conflict. Those are legal services. The platform's contribution is that the intent was captured while everyone still agreed.

### Happy Path

1. Band records governance terms (01.04.01). Name ownership is one section.
2. System states the default plainly: absent an agreement, name rights in most jurisdictions arise from use and are likely held jointly by whoever was in the band — which usually means nobody can use it alone and everybody thinks they can.
3. Band agrees: the name belongs to the band entity; a leaver keeps no claim; if the band dissolves, the name is retired unless three of four agree otherwise.
4. All members confirm. The record is versioned and dated.
5. Two years later, at a split, the record exists and was made when nobody had a stake in lying about it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No agreement recorded, band splits, both halves keep gigging under the name | The platform holds no answer, because the band never gave it one. This is the failure the feature exists to prevent, and it is not recoverable after the fact | Two claimant parties; 01.04.04 dissolution flow; realistically, a lawyer |
| Another band on the platform already uses the name | Not blocked. Name collisions are legal reality (01.01.02 Edge Cases) and the platform is not a registry | Both exist; disambiguation by credits/location |
| A third party holds a registered trademark for the name | Recorded as a fact if the band tells us. The platform does not detect it and cannot advise | Route to 24 for a dispute |
| A leaving member claims joint name rights against the recorded agreement | The record is evidence, not a verdict. Its power is that it predates the dispute | Evidence surface |
| Trademark registration lapses | Status is only as current as the last time the band updated it. Uncertain-freshness data presented as current is worse than no data | Same confirmation-age treatment as 01.02.01 D-03 |
| Band is a shared alias rather than an org (01.01.02 Q-01) | Then name ownership has no governance home, because aliases have no governance | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — real evidence for 01.01.02 Q-01 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The name is just the band's display string | The model every platform uses — and the reason none of them can help at a split | ❌ REJECTED — the name is the band's most valuable asset. It carries the followers (domain 20), the reputation, the tour history and the catalogue association. Modelling it as a string means the asset has no owner, no disposition rule and no record, which is exactly why it is music's most litigated item. It needs an ownership record even though the platform will never adjudicate it. |
| DT-02 | The platform should check trademark registries at band creation | Attractive — it would catch conflicts early | ❌ REJECTED for this pass. It would make band creation slow and expensive (01.02.02 D-01 says creation is cheap), produce false positives across classes and jurisdictions, and imply an assurance the platform cannot give. A negative result would be read as "you're clear", which is legal advice we are not qualified to give. Recorded as Q-02 in case the owner wants it as an opt-in later. |
| DT-03 | The name outlives the band, and the followers make that concrete | Reasoning about D-11: fans follow the alias/band, not the humans | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it sharpens the whole feature. At dissolution the name has a following that keeps existing. If one ex-member takes the name, they inherit an audience that was accumulated by four people — a real transfer of value the platform can see happening and nobody has ever recorded the intent for. This is also why the fan-facing consequence in 01.04.04 is not cosmetic. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Name ownership is a recorded, versioned agreement, not a display string | DT-01 — it is the band's most valuable and most contested asset |
| D-02 | The platform records and surfaces; it does not register, search, verify or adjudicate | Parent D-02. Anything else is legal advice we cannot give |
| D-03 | Trademark data is band-supplied and carries a confirmation age | DT-02 + consistency with 01.02.01 D-03 |
| D-04 | The default (joint rights from use) is stated plainly to bands with no agreement | Same reasoning as 01.04.01 D-04 — the harm is unawareness, and disclosure is cheap |
