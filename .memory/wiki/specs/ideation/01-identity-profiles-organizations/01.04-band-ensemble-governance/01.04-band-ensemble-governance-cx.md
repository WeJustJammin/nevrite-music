## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Partnership Terms ↔ Treasury Mandate

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's sharpest unresolved problem. Governance says "£2,000+ needs three of four"; the mandate graph says "the bassist can spend up to £500". Both are recorded, both are displayed as true, and nothing reconciles them. Governance ought to be the source and mandate the derived enforcement — but mandate exists from org creation (01.02.02) while governance arrives years later, so the dependency currently runs backwards.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: lives the contradiction — they read the terms and believe them, while the mandate is what actually gates the action.
- **Producer**: affected where a member of the band; otherwise reads only "can this person pay me".
- **Operator**: unaffected — never sees either.
- **Fan**: unaffected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: **Unresolved.** Two records claim authority over the same question, with no precedence rule. This is 01.04.03 Q-01 and it must be answered before either is specified.
2. **Trigger chain**: Recording terms *should* regenerate mandates. Today it does not, so a band that agrees "3 of 4 to spend" and does not also edit the mandate graph has agreed nothing operative.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, recursively — amending terms is a decision governed by the current terms, and changing a mandate requires an administer mandate. Two recursions over the same subject, through different mechanisms.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A term change that alters what someone may do must notify them. Currently only mandate changes notify (01.03 CX-01).
5. **State transition conflict**: Terms and mandate edited concurrently by different members produces a silently contradictory state. Cannot be fixed at the edit site; needs the precedence rule from (1).

### CX-02: Partnership Terms ↔ Dissolution

**Relationship**: Dissolution consumes terms recorded years earlier and, crucially, the *version in force* matters — a band that changed its terms in 2025 dissolves under 2025's terms, but a 2023 decision is still judged by 2023's. Versioning (01.04.01 D-01) exists for this consumer.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the terms decide whether they can dissolve, and what they keep.
- **Producer**: read-only; their stake (credits, points) is untouched by terms they were never party to.
- **Operator**: sees only bookability.
- **Fan**: sees only the page state.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Terms are immutable-once-versioned; dissolution reads them. No write conflict by construction.
2. **Trigger chain**: Dissolution initiated → decision rules evaluated → confirmations gathered → terminal state. If confirmations never complete, the band stays active — a stalled dissolution is not a dissolution, and the band being stuck is the honest outcome.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — the defining case. Standing to dissolve comes from terms; with no terms, nobody has more standing than anyone else, which is why 01.04.04 D-02 executes nothing.
4. **Notification fan-out**: All members; plus, per 01.04.04's Role Lens, credit-holders and followers see consequences.
5. **State transition conflict**: A dissolution racing a new booking — 01.04.04 D-04 blocks on live obligations, so the booking wins and dissolution waits.

### CX-03: Name & Trademark ↔ Dissolution

**Relationship**: The most consequential disposition. The name carries the follower graph (01.04.02 DT-03), so transferring the name to one faction transfers an audience that four people built. The platform is the only party that can see this happening in real time, which is an argument that it must not do it silently.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the disposition rule they recorded (or did not) decides who keeps the name.
- **Producer**: no standing on the name (01.04.02 Role Lens: None).
- **Operator**: needs to know which party the name now resolves to, or they book the wrong band.
- **Fan**: their follow moves with the name. They are the transferred asset, and 01.04.04 Q-02 asks what they are told.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The name record belongs to the band; at dissolution two factions may both claim it. With no recorded rule the platform holds no answer (01.04.04 D-02) — correct, and unsatisfying.
2. **Trigger chain**: Dissolution → name disposition → follower graph moves (domain 20) → Operator-facing resolution changes (domain 17). A three-domain chain from one governance event.
3. **Permission intersection**: Enforcing the recorded rule means acting on a legal question (01.04.02 Q-01). Displaying it is safe; executing it is not obviously ours to do.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Members, followers, and any venue holding a booking under that name. The widest fan-out in the domain.
5. **State transition conflict**: Contested name + live bookings = a venue holding a contract with a party that may not exist tomorrow. Routes to 17 and 24.

### CX-04: Treasury Mandate ↔ Dissolution

**Relationship**: Authority must outlive the party. A dissolved band with an unsettled balance still needs someone able to move that money — so dissolution cannot terminate the treasury mandate, only narrow it to settlement. This inverts the usual rule that ending a party ends its mandates.

**Role scoping**: Musician only (members). Producer read-only where owed money by the band.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The balance belongs to the dissolved party. Who may move it is the terms' answer; who *can* is the mandate's.
2. **Trigger chain**: Dissolution → balance must be disposed → treasury mandate persists in a settlement-only form → once zero, mandate ends. If nobody holds a treasury mandate at dissolution, the money is stranded — which is why 01.04.03 D-04 blocks the last holder from leaving.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — settlement authority is narrower than spend authority and is not the same grant.
4. **Notification fan-out**: All members see the final distribution. Non-negotiable — this is the last chance for transparency to do its job.
5. **State transition conflict**: Dissolution completing while a distribution is in flight. The balance-must-be-zero gate prevents it.

### CX-05: Partnership Terms ↔ Name & Trademark

**Relationship**: Name ownership is one section of the governance terms, sharing the versioning, the all-member confirmation rule, and the plain-language default disclosure. They are separate features because the name has a public consequence (resolution, followers) that no other term has — but they are recorded in one flow, because a band will sit down for this once, if ever.

**Role scoping**: Musician (members) only for the agreement; Operator/Fan see only the name's public resolution.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One versioned terms record with a name section. No conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: Terms agreed → name disposition in force. Both feed 01.04.04.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical — all members confirm.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Members only. The name agreement is private until dissolution makes it visible.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — same record, same confirmation gate.
