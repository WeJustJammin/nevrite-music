# Feature: Partnership Terms & Decision Rules

> **Number**: 01.04.01
> **Parent**: [Band & Ensemble Governance](./01.04-band-ensemble-governance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a band decides things, recorded while it still agrees. Which decisions need everyone, which need a majority, which one person can just make; what a member's share of the band's income is; what happens to that share if they leave. Every band already has an answer to these questions — imposed by partnership law by default, unread by any of them — and the platform's contribution is capturing the band's *actual* intent at a moment when nobody is angry.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a member: proposes terms, agrees them, reads them. Sees in plain language what the band has agreed — "a gig over £1,000 needs three of us to agree" — and what the legal default is if they agree nothing. Non-members see nothing; this is private. |
| Producer | Read-only | Only where they are a member (then Musician-level). Where they are not: read-only visibility of the band's *decision surface* they must interact with — specifically, whether the person confirming a split at their session can bind the band. They do not see the band's internal terms; they see the answer to one question. |
| Operator | None | A venue does not see a band's partnership terms. They need to know whether the person confirming a booking can bind the band — which is 01.03.03's mandate answer, not this file's terms. Explicit absence: leaking band internals to a booker would be a serious confidentiality failure. |
| Fan | None | Never. A band's internal agreements are private commercial information with no fan-facing dimension whatsoever. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Governance terms recorded at breadth cover: **decision thresholds** (which categories of decision need unanimity / majority / any-one-member), **income shares** (how band income splits between members — distinct from songwriting splits, which are domain 09's), **departure terms** (what a leaver keeps: their share of past income, of the catalogue, of the name), and **admission terms** (how a new member is added and on what share).

Terms are versioned and dated: what the band agreed in 2024 governs 2024's decisions, even after the terms change. Amending terms is itself a decision governed by the current terms — the recursion is intentional and finite.

The platform records; it does not enforce a legal outcome and does not advise (parent D-02).

### Happy Path

1. Band has been going a year; a real gig fee arrives. One member starts governance terms.
2. System shows the **default**: in the absence of an agreement, the law likely treats them as an equal-shares general partnership, with every member able to bind the others. This is stated as information, not advice — and for most bands it is the first time they have seen it.
3. They discuss and record what they actually intend: equal shares; gigs any one can accept; anything over £2,000 needs three of four; the name stays with the band.
4. All members confirm. Terms are versioned, dated and visible to every member.
5. Six months later a decision arises. The terms are already there — nobody reconstructs anything from memory.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Band never records terms — the overwhelmingly common case | Legal default applies (equal partnership in many jurisdictions) plus 01.03.03's default mandate. The platform states the default rather than pretending governance is absent | An honest, non-nagging statement of what currently governs them |
| Members do not all confirm | Terms are not in force. A partially-agreed agreement is not an agreement, and showing it as one would be worse than showing nothing | "Agreed by 2 of 4" — explicitly not in force |
| Terms are amended; a past decision is later questioned | The version in force at the time governs. Versioning is the whole reason this is not a mutable settings blob | Timeline of versions with dates |
| A member disputes terms they confirmed | Platform is not an adjudicator (parent D-02). It provides the record: what was agreed, by whom, when | Evidence for 24 / for their lawyer |
| Terms conflict with the law of the band's jurisdiction | Platform records intent; it cannot validate against 50 jurisdictions' partnership law | See Q-01 — and this is the strongest argument against offering templates |
| Terms conflict with 01.03.03 mandates (terms say 3 of 4 to spend; mandate says the drummer can spend) | Genuine conflict. Governance should be the source and mandate the derived enforcement — but nothing today makes that true | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see domain CX-06; this is the sharpest gap in the sub-domain |
| A member joins after terms are agreed | Admission terms should cover it. If they do not, the new member is governed by terms they never agreed to | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Band governance is a niche feature for successful bands | Reasonable — most bands never earn enough for it to matter, which is why it is proposed `could` | ⏳ PARTIALLY REJECTED — the *flow* is late-cohort, but the framing is wrong in an important way. Every band **already has governance**: in most jurisdictions, no written agreement means a general partnership with equal shares and every member able to bind the others. Bands do not lack governance; they lack *awareness* of the governance imposed on them. That reframes the feature's minimum viable form: not an agreement builder, but a disclosure of the default. The disclosure is cheap and its absence is the harm. |
| DT-02 | Income shares here duplicate songwriting splits in domain 09 | They look identical — both are percentages between the same people | ❌ REJECTED, and conflating them is a classic, expensive error. Band income shares (gig fees, merch, the band's money) and songwriting splits (who wrote the song) are routinely *different* for the same band — the drummer gets a quarter of the door and none of the publishing, which is one of music's most common structural resentments. One model for both would force them equal and encode a falsehood into the record. Separate, and 09 owns the rights side. |
| DT-03 | Governance terms and 01.03.03 mandates must not be independently editable | Reasoning about the conflict case in Edge Cases | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real, unresolved design flaw at breadth. Terms say "3 of 4 to spend"; a mandate says the drummer can spend. Both are recorded; both are shown as true; they contradict. Governance ought to be the source of truth and mandate the derived enforcement — but that inverts the current dependency, where mandate exists from creation and governance arrives later. Flagged (Q-01 on 01.04.03, domain CX-06) rather than papered over. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Terms are versioned and dated; the version in force at decision time governs | A mutable settings blob would destroy the evidence value, which is the only value |
| D-02 | Terms require confirmation from all members to be in force | A partially-agreed agreement displayed as agreed is worse than no agreement |
| D-03 | Band income shares are modelled separately from songwriting splits (09) | DT-02 — they are routinely different and conflating them encodes a falsehood |
| D-04 | The minimum viable form is **disclosure of the default**, not an agreement builder | DT-01 — every band already has governance; almost none know what it is |
