# Feature: Treasury Mandate

> **Number**: 01.04.03
> **Parent**: [Band & Ensemble Governance](./01.04-band-ensemble-governance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Who may touch the band's money. A band that gets paid is a party with a balance — gig fees, merch takings, royalties, an advance — and someone has to be able to receive it, spend it, and split it out to members. This feature records that authority: who can receive on the band's behalf, who can spend and up to what, and how band income reaches individual members. It is the one governance capability that becomes urgent the moment the band earns anything, which is why it sits ahead of the rest of the sub-domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a member: sees the band's balance, what came in, what went out and who authorised it. Holds a treasury mandate if the band gave them one. Reads their own share. The transparency is the feature — most band money disputes are not theft, they are one person's shoebox of receipts. |
| Producer | Read-only | Where they are a member, Musician-level. Where they are not: read-only on the one fact they need — whether the band can actually pay them for the session. A studio invoicing a band cares that a payable party with authority exists, not what the balance is. |
| Operator | None | A venue pays the band; it does not see the band's treasury. Their concern (who to pay, and whether that discharges the debt) is answered by the payable-party link, not by this file. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Never. A fan buying a ticket or merch is a customer of the band, with no visibility into where the money goes internally. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Records three things: who holds **receive** authority (the band's payable party link, owned by Payments), who holds **spend** authority and up to what ceiling, and the **distribution rule** by which band income reaches members. The distribution rule is one of exactly three shapes: **equal** (split N ways), **by income share** (the percentages fixed in 01.04.01), or **manual per event** (the mandate holder proposes a split, members see it, and — above the spend ceiling — governance's threshold from 01.04.01 must clear it). A band picks one default rule; a single inbound event may override to `manual` when the split is bespoke (a guest player on one gig).

Treasury mandate is a specialisation of 01.03.03's mandate model with money attached — it is not a second authority system. What this file adds is the band-specific consequence: transparency to all members regardless of who holds the mandate. Every member sees every movement, even the ones they could not have made.

**It therefore inherits the ratified default (01.03.03 D-07..D-10, D-06)**, and three consequences are this file's to state:

- `spend` and `settle` are **seeded** on every `confirmed` `permanent` band membership edge, up to the platform default ceiling of **USD 1,000 per act**, configurable per band. A band is never without spend authority once it has one confirmed permanent member.
- A **touring, staff or honorary** member does **not** inherit settle authority over band money on day one — presence is not authority (D-09 there), and money is where that distinction is least forgiving. The band may grant it explicitly.
- What can still be missing is **`receive`**: the payable-party link is Payments', not a mandate, which is what the "money waiting" states below are actually about.

KYB, the payable party and the payout rails belong to Payments (routed out of this domain at ratification). This file owns who is authorised, not how money moves.

### Happy Path

1. Band plays a gig; the venue settles (domain 17). Money is due to the band.
2. Band has a payable party (Payments) and a treasury mandate. Every permanent member could already spend up to the platform default (01.03.03 D-08); this band has configured its own figure and agreed its own threshold above it — the bassist can receive and spend up to £500, and anything more needs three of four (01.04.01's threshold). Both numbers are the band's, not the platform's.
3. Funds land against the band. **Every member** is notified — not just the mandate holder.
4. Bassist distributes per the recorded rule: equal quarters.
5. Each member receives their share, and every member can see the whole movement: in, out, by whom, when.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Band earns before it can take the money | **Corrected at DQ-02.4**: this is no longer an *authority* gap for a band with a confirmed permanent member, because `spend` and `settle` are seeded (01.03.03 D-07). What is missing is the **payable party** — Payments' concern, not a mandate. Money is held against the band, undistributable, and it must not be a dead end | "$400 is waiting for {band}. Nobody's set up where it should go yet." with the route to fix it |
| A band with **no confirmed permanent members** earns — an unclaimed band org, or one whose lineup is all touring/staff | Genuinely no seeded money authority (01.03.03 D-09/D-10, 01.02.02 D-07's custodial ceiling). Money is held, surfaced with the route, never silently | "Someone needs permission to handle {band}'s money." |
| Two mandate holders spend against the same balance at once, total exceeds the balance | Movements are serialised against the recorded balance; the second spend that would overdraw is rejected, not silently allowed to go negative. A band balance cannot go below zero on the platform's own record | Second spender sees "Only £120 remains — this movement needs £300" — never a negative balance |
| Mandate holder spends within their limit, band disagrees | Not a platform failure — the mandate was granted. Transparency is the protection: every member saw it | Movement visible and attributed |
| Mandate holder leaves the band | Departure is blocked while they are the only treasury holder (same rule as 01.03.01/01.02.02). Money with no authorised holder is a trap | "You're the only person who can handle {band}'s money." See domain CX-06 |
| Governance says 3-of-4 to spend; mandate says the drummer can spend £500 *(figure illustrative; the platform default is USD 1,000 — 01.03.03 D-08)* | Contradiction, both recorded, both displayed as true | **Still unresolved, and now unavoidable**: `[PENDING]` re-pointed at Q-01 here and at **01.03.03 Q-03** (revisit the flat ceiling against band-agreed thresholds once 01.04.01 governance capture ships) — the old `Step 5` deferral has expired, that stage having run. Sharpened by DQ-02.4/.5: the seed puts a mandate on **every** confirmed permanent edge, so the contradiction surface is every member rather than one. See 01.04.01 DT-03/D-05 and domain CX-06 |
| Band dissolves with a balance | Balance distribution is part of dissolution (01.04.04); the treasury mandate persists until it is settled — dissolution cannot orphan money | See domain CX-07 |
| Member's share is subject to a deduction they dispute (a loan, a broken amp) | Platform records the movement and its stated reason; it does not adjudicate | Evidence for 24 |
| Tax on band income | Platform does **not** compute, withhold, or file tax. It records each distribution with its amount, date, recipient, and stated reason, and exposes those records as a downloadable per-member statement (a tax *input*, not a tax *return*). Any withholding or filing obligation is Payments/compliance territory — see Q-03 | Downloadable distribution statement per member: "Your share of {band} income, {period} — export for your records" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Treasury is a payments concern and does not belong in identity | KYC/KYB and payouts were explicitly routed to Payments at ratification, so the instinct is to send this too | ⏳ SPLIT — correctly. Payments owns the payable party, the rails, KYB and the money movement. What is left, and what only this domain can answer, is **who the band has agreed may act on its money** — an agreement between people, derived from governance, enforced through the mandate graph. Payments cannot answer it because it does not know the band's decision rules. The seam matches the domain's existing one. |
| DT-02 | Only the mandate holder needs to see the money | The natural read of "authority" — and how a shoebox of receipts works | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's real value. `personas.md` names the worst-intentional Producer behaviour as pushing something through "while contributors aren't paying attention". Band money has the identical failure mode, and it is usually not theft — it is one person handling everything and nobody else being able to see. Universal member visibility costs nothing and removes the entire class. Authority and visibility must be decoupled. |
| DT-03 | Treasury mandate becomes urgent much earlier than the rest of governance | Reasoning about ordering: which governance capability bites first? | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it should drive the MoSCoW split within this sub-domain. Partnership terms, name ownership and dissolution all bite at year 3+. Treasury bites at the first paid gig, which for many bands is week one — and D-05..D-08 put commerce in the platform from the start. A band that gets paid and cannot distribute it is a hard stop with money sitting in it. This is why treasury is proposed `should` while its three siblings are proposed `could`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Authority and visibility are decoupled — every member sees every movement | DT-02 — removes the entire class of "one person had the shoebox" disputes at no cost |
| D-02 | Treasury mandate specialises 01.03.03; it is not a second authority system | Two authority systems would drift, and the drift would be about money |
| D-03 | Payments owns rails/KYB/payable party; this file owns who the band authorised | DT-01 — do not re-absorb what ratification extracted |
| D-04 | A band cannot lose its last treasury mandate holder while it holds money | Money with no authorised holder is a trap the platform would have built |
| D-05 | Income with no mandate holder is surfaced with a route, never silently held | The dead-end version is how bands go back to cash |
| D-06 | **Treasury authority inherits 01.03.03's ratified default rather than starting empty**: `spend` and `settle` are seeded on every confirmed `permanent` band edge up to the USD 1,000 platform default; `touring`, `staff` and `honorary` members get **no** money authority until granted; non-band org types get none at all | DQ-02.4/.5/.6/.7 applied to money, per D-02 (this is a specialisation, not a second system). Two corrections follow: the "no treasury mandate exists" state is now about the **payable party** (Payments), not authority; and D-04's last-holder block binds mainly on single-member orgs and non-band types, since a multi-member band cannot lose its last seeded spender while it has a permanent member. The band's own configured ceiling and governance threshold override the default in both directions |
