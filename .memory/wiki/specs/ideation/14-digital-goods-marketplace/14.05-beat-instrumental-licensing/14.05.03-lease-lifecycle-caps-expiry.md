# Feature: Lease Lifecycle, Caps & Expiry

> **Number**: 14.05.03
> **Parent**: [14.05 Beat & Instrumental Licensing](./14.05-beat-instrumental-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens to a beat lease *after* the checkout — the part of the transaction that lasts years.
A lease is not a purchase that completes; it is an obligation with a stream cap, a sales cap and a
term, attached to a record the artist releases into the world. This feature tracks that obligation
and — the only thing that makes it humane — tells the artist before they breach it rather than
after.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The obligation is theirs.** The artist who leased the beat, released the track, and is now accruing streams against a cap they agreed to in ten seconds. They need to see their live leases, their consumption against caps, and their expiry dates — and to be *told* at 70%, not sued at 200%. |
| Producer | Reports | The beatmaker sees the state of leases they sold — which are live, which are near caps, which expired. This is commercial intelligence (a track approaching a cap is an upgrade sale, Q-02) but it is **not** a control surface: the producer cannot alter a bound lease (14.02 CX-02). Reports, deliberately, not Full. |
| Operator | None | No column. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. A fan streaming the track is *generating* the cap consumption and will never know this exists. |

## Behavior

A lease is minted at purchase with caps and a term drawn from the tier (CX-03). The lifecycle
tracks it: consumption against caps, approach warnings, term expiry, and what — if anything — the
platform does at each threshold.

The uncomfortable truth this feature has to confront: **enforcement is contractual, not
technical**. On expiry the artist still has the untagged WAV; files do not un-download (CX-05).
The track stays on Spotify. The platform cannot pull a release. So the honest scope is
*notification, record-keeping and evidence* — not enforcement — and pretending otherwise would
sell both parties a guarantee that doesn't exist.

Where consumption data even comes from is unresolved (Q-01) and is the feature's central weakness.

### Happy Path

1. Artist purchases a tier; a lease is minted with caps (e.g. 10,000 streams) and a term (e.g. 2 years).
2. Artist releases the track (domain 12); the lease is recorded as an encumbrance in the clearance chain (09).
3. Streaming accrues; consumption is tracked against the cap (Q-01).
4. At 70% of cap, the artist is notified — with an upgrade path, not a warning letter.
5. Artist upgrades to unlimited (Q-02); the lease's caps are renegotiated and re-bound.
6. Had they not, the cap is reached and the record shows the lease exceeded — evidence for whatever comes next.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cap exceeded on a track that finally worked | The model's defining event; the artist is in breach at the exact moment of success | Proactive notification at 70% + an upgrade path. A breach discovered by a lawyer is a product failure, not a legal one. |
| Term expires; the track stays on DSPs | The platform cannot pull a release. Enforcement is contractual. | Honest position — see DT-01. Notify both parties, record the fact, do not claim to enforce |
| Where do stream counts come from? | The artist's DSP data (domain 10/22) — if they have it, if they're distributing via WeJammin (12), and if they let us read it | `[PENDING]` — Q-01. Without this the caps are unmeasurable and the whole tier model is decorative |
| Artist released via another distributor | We have no consumption data at all | Self-reported, or nothing — see Q-01 |
| Exclusive sold on the beat (CX-02) | Existing leases survive or terminate per policy; either way the lessee must be told | Notification to every live lessee |
| Lease expires; artist re-leases the same beat | Should be trivially possible and is a real revenue path | Renewal flow |
| Beat refunded after a lease-backed release | 14.02.05 Q-02 at its sharpest — is the released record retroactively unclean? | `[PENDING]` — index Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Lease list fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | No leases held | Empty state |
| Populated | Live leases | Per-lease: caps, consumption (if known), term remaining, upgrade path |
| Error | Fetch failed | Retry; cached list |
| Partial | Consumption data unavailable | **The common case** — "cap: 10,000 streams · consumption: not tracked (released outside WeJammin)". Honest, not a fabricated zero |
| Expired | Term ended | Lease shown expired with the date; renewal offered |
| Exceeded | Cap passed | Recorded as a fact; upgrade offered; no technical enforcement claimed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Expiry and caps are enforced — that is what makes a lease a lease | The candidate's framing ("auto-delist" implies automated enforcement throughout), and the plain meaning of "cap" | ❌ REJECTED — **nothing here is enforceable and the domain must say so out loud.** On expiry the artist holds an untagged WAV that does not un-download (CX-05); the track is on Spotify, Apple and YouTube, and WeJammin cannot pull it. On cap exceedance we cannot mute a stream. The entire "enforcement" is a contract between two users that the platform *witnessed*. That sounds like a weakness and is actually the correct reframing: what the platform can uniquely provide is **an unambiguous, timestamped record of what was agreed, by whom, and what was consumed against it** — which is the project's thesis (D-18) applied to beat leases. The industry's failure here is not weak enforcement; it is that when a dispute arrives, nobody can prove which tier was bought or what it said. We can. So the feature's honest scope is notification, record-keeping and evidence — and every word of UI must avoid implying a technical guarantee that does not exist. |
| DT-02 | Cap tracking is a matter of counting streams | The obvious implementation reading, and what the tier tables imply by quoting stream numbers | ❌ REJECTED — **we almost never have the data, and this quietly undermines the entire tier model.** Stream counts live with the DSPs. WeJammin sees them only if the artist distributed through domain 12 *and* connected their royalty data (10/22) *and* the track is matched correctly. An artist who leased a beat here and released through DistroKid — which is the normal case, and precisely the fragmentation the project exists to fix — is invisible to us. So a 10,000-stream cap on that track is a number in a contract that nobody measures, on either side. The honest states are `tracked` / `self-reported` / `unknown`, with `unknown` being the majority at launch, and the UI must show that rather than a fabricated zero. It also reveals the domain's real dependency: **beat leases only become measurable when the artist is also using WeJammin to release and collect** — which is D-18's consolidation-enables-provenance argument arriving in a place nobody expected it, and a genuine argument for why this sub-domain gets more valuable as the platform consolidates rather than as it adds beat features. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The feature's scope is notification, record-keeping and evidence — **not** enforcement, and the UI never implies otherwise | Per DT-01. Nothing here is technically enforceable; claiming a guarantee we cannot deliver misleads both parties. |
| D-02 | Consumption state is three-valued: tracked / self-reported / unknown, with `unknown` shown honestly | Per DT-02 — a fabricated zero on an unmeasurable cap is worse than admitting we cannot see it. |
| D-03 | The producer gets Reports, not Full — they cannot alter a bound lease | Per 14.02 CX-02. Bound terms are frozen; a control surface here would let a vendor rewrite someone's obligation retroactively. |
