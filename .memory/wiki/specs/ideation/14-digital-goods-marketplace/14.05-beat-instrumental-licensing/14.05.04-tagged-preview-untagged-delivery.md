# Feature: Tagged Preview & Untagged Delivery

> **Number**: 14.05.04
> **Parent**: [14.05 Beat & Instrumental Licensing](./14.05-beat-instrumental-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The producer tag — a spoken or sung vocal stamp laid over a beat's public preview ("*Metro
Boomin want some more*") — and the untagged files that unlock at the tier the artist bought. The
tag is why a beatmaker can publish a complete, high-quality instrumental on the open internet
without giving it away: the beat is fully audible, fully judgeable, and unusable.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | The artist shopping. Hears the tag over every beat while browsing; gets untagged files on purchase at their tier — MP3, WAV, or trackout stems. Cannot configure anything. |
| Producer | Config | The beatmaker. Sets their tag, its placement and frequency. This is a real craft decision, not a setting: too sparse and the beat is stealable, too dense and nobody can judge the music. A recognisable tag is also **free advertising on every track it appears on** — which is why producers want it in the *sold* record too (Q-01). |
| Operator | None | No column. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | Read-only | The only Fan touchpoint in this sub-domain, and only if beat pages are public (Q-02). A fan browsing a producer's page hears tagged beats. They cannot buy, and would have no use for a lease. |

## Behavior

On upload, a tagged preview is generated by overlaying the producer's tag at intervals across the
full instrumental. The preview is public and streamable — that is the point. Untagged artifacts
are held and unlocked per tier (CX-04): an MP3 lease unlocks an MP3; a trackout tier unlocks the
stems.

Tag placement is the whole design: intervals must be frequent enough that removing them by editing
destroys the beat, sparse enough that a buyer can hear the music. Get it wrong in either direction
and the feature fails — silently, as either theft or lost sales.

On an exclusive sale (14.05.02), the preview comes down with the listing (CX-01) — the beat leaves
the internet.

### Happy Path

1. Producer uploads an instrumental and selects/uploads their tag.
2. System generates a tagged preview, overlaying the tag at the producer's chosen interval.
3. Beat publishes; the preview streams publicly on the beat page and in search.
4. Artist streams the full tagged beat and decides.
5. Artist buys a tier; the untagged artifacts for that tier unlock in their library (CX-04).
6. Artist records over the untagged WAV and releases.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Tag placed too sparsely | The beat is editable — cut the tagged bars, loop the rest, and it's stolen | Producer's choice; the platform should recommend a minimum density, not just accept any value |
| Tag placed too densely | Nobody can hear the music; the beat doesn't sell | Producer's choice — the failure is silent and they will blame the platform's traffic |
| Artist buys MP3 lease, later needs the WAV | Tier upgrade (14.05.01 Q-02) — the untagged WAV exists, it's just locked | Upgrade path |
| Trackout tier: what are the stems? | "Trackout" is undefined in the market — 8 stems or 60? Grouped or raw? | Vendor must declare; otherwise it's a dispute at delivery |
| Someone screen-records the preview and strips the tag with a de-noiser | Possible, imperfectly. AI stem separation has made this materially easier than it was. | Honest: the tag is a deterrent, not a lock — see DT-02 |
| Exclusive sale removes the preview (CX-01) | The beat vanishes from the internet, including from anyone's shared links | Expected behaviour |
| Producer changes their tag after publishing | New tag re-generates the public preview for all unsold tiers; already-sold untagged files are frozen at the tier the buyer purchased and are never re-stamped (a delivered lease is immutable, CX-03). Regeneration is async — see Partial state. | "Preview updating" on the beat page until the new tagged render lands |
| Non-owner tries to publish/preview someone else's instrumental | Rejected at upload — only the uploading Producer (or their org, domain 01) owns the artifact and can set its tag | "You don't have permission to publish this beat" |
| Musician requests untagged files at a tier they didn't buy (URL-guessing the WAV/stems) | Blocked at the entitlement gate (14.03.01) — the untagged artifact only resolves for a buyer holding that tier's lease; an MP3 lessee cannot pull the trackout stems | 403 / "This file isn't part of your licence" |
| Bulk scraping the public previews (scripted download of the whole catalog) | Previews are public by design, but mass automated pull is abuse — rate-limit per IP/session and serve only the tagged render, never the master | Throttled; repeat offenders see a challenge. Untagged masters are never exposed to scraping — they sit behind the entitlement gate |
| Two buyers race to lease the same non-exclusive tier at once | Non-exclusive tiers are unlimited-inventory — both succeed, both get their untagged files; no lock needed (contrast the exclusive race, CX-01) | Both see their files unlock normally |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The producer tag is branding — a watermark for marketing | The intuitive read; it looks and sounds like an audio watermark, and 14.03.05 exists two sub-domains away | ❌ REJECTED — and the pair is worth separating precisely because a spec writer would build them with one mechanism (`14.05-beat-instrumental-licensing-cx.md#R-02`). A forensic watermark (14.03.05) is **inaudible, covert, defensive, identifies the buyer, and has failed if you can hear it**. A producer tag is **audible, overt, promotional, identifies the seller, and has failed if you *can't* hear it**. Inverse on every axis. And the tag's function is not branding at all — it is the **access-control model of the entire beat business**. It is what allows a beatmaker to put a finished, uncompressed, fully-judgeable instrumental on the public internet and still sell it. No other product type in domain 14 can do this: a sample pack must be truncated or degraded to be previewed safely (14.04.01 Q-01), and a plugin needs a trial (14.02.06). The beat market solved the audition problem two decades ago with a vocal stamp, and it works better than any DRM in this domain. |
| DT-02 | The tag protects the beat | The whole premise of the feature, and of the market that runs on it | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **AI stem separation has quietly broken it, and nobody in the candidate list noticed.** Tag removal used to require destroying the beat around it; current source-separation tools isolate and strip a vocal tag from a mix with results that are frequently good enough for a demo, and improving. So the tag is now a *deterrent and a friction*, not a lock — much like watermarking (14.03.05 Q-01) faces the same erosion from a different direction. Two consequences worth stating rather than discovering. First, the tag's real remaining value is **attribution and reach**: an untagged stolen beat is anonymous, so the tag mostly ensures the producer's name travels with the music, which is a credits problem (domain 02) more than a DRM one. Second, this is a fresh argument for the whole sub-domain's rights model living in 09/11 (index Q-01): if technical protection is eroding across the board, the durable asset is the **provable record of who made it and what was licensed** — which is D-18's thesis, and which the platform can actually deliver. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A beat cannot publish publicly without a tag | Per DT-01 — the tag is the access-control model; an untagged public preview is giving the beat away. |
| D-02 | The tag and the forensic watermark (14.03.05) are separate mechanisms and must not share an implementation | Per DT-01/R-02 — inverse goals on every axis (audible/inaudible, seller/buyer, promotional/defensive). |
| D-03 | The platform recommends a minimum tag density rather than accepting any interval | Too-sparse tagging is theft the producer won't notice until the beat is on someone's record. |
