# Feature: Download Attribution & Accrual

> **Number**: 14.10.02
> **Parent**: [14.10 Contributor Revenue & Per-Download Royalty Pool](./14.10-contributor-revenue-royalty-pool-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Counting whose sound was taken. Under the credit model a buyer spends a credit on one loop out of
a pack of 800, and that event — not a sale — is what earns the contributor money. Attribution
records it, accrues it against the pack, and produces the count the rate model prices (CX-01). It
is the meter the whole pool runs on, and if it is wrong, everyone is paid wrong.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Sees their own downloads and accrual — which loops earned, how often. Genuinely useful feedback: it tells a creator which of their sounds people actually use, which no pack sale ever reveals. Cannot see anyone else's. |
| Producer | Reports | Same, at volume. The persona with enough catalog to read the data as a signal about what to make next. |
| Operator | None | No column. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

A credit-spend on an individual asset is the accruable event. It records: the asset, its pack, the
contributor(s) via the split (CX-02), the period, and the credit spent. Accruals roll up per pack
per period, and at period close the rate model values them (CX-01).

Under pro-rata the in-period accrual is an **estimate** (14.10.01 D-02) — the value of a download
is unknowable until every download on the platform is counted.

The counting must be defensible: a contributor cannot audit our database, so the count is a number
they must take on trust, and that makes correction handling (DT-01) more consequential than the
counting itself.

### Happy Path

1. Subscriber spends a credit on a loop (14.07.02).
2. The asset is delivered (14.03.01) and lands in their library permanently.
3. An accrual event is recorded against the asset's pack for the period.
4. The contributor sees it in near-real time as an estimate.
5. Period closes; the rate is computed (14.10.01); accruals are valued and split (14.10.03).
6. A statement is issued; the payouts cross-cut moves the money.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer downloads the same loop twice (new machine, re-download) | Is that one accrual or two? A re-download is not new use. | Should be deduplicated per (buyer, asset) — see Q-01. Counting it twice pays contributors for nothing and dilutes the pro-rata pool for everyone else |
| Credit farming (14.07.02) — mass-download then cancel | The pool pays out on downloads funded by one month's fee | Dilutes every honest contributor's pro-rata share. Pool-integrity control (14.10.01 D-03) |
| Buyer downloads, never uses the sound | Indistinguishable from real use; downloads are a proxy for value, not a measure of it | Structural to the model — see DT-02 |
| Download reversed after a period closed and paid | Recovering from a paid contributor ≠ adjusting an open accrual | `[PENDING]` — CX-01 q5 |
| Pack has no agreed split (14.10.03) | Accrual is correct; there is nobody to pay it to | Blocks at payout, which is the design pressure that makes splits happen (14.10.03 DT-01) |
| Contributor disputes the count | They cannot audit us; the number is trust | Per DT-01, statements must be *evidentiary*, not summary |
| An asset appears in two packs by different contributors | Which pack accrues? Both, one, or is the asset the accruing unit? | **Resolved by scope — nothing accrues, so the collision does not arise.** Per Q-03 below: this feature is **WONT** ([moscow-ledger.md](../../moscow-ledger.md) §WONT — *"No pool, no attribution (contingent on 14.07.02)"*) and [ideation-index.md](../../ideation-index.md) D-59 records *"pool funding and download accrual are WONT"*. D-02 (the **asset**, not the pack, is the accruing unit) is preserved for whenever the feature returns, at which point this case becomes live again and is still unspecified |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Attribution is a counter — increment on download, sum at period close | The candidate's framing ("Per-Download Royalty Pool"), which treats counting as the trivial part | ❌ REJECTED — **the counting is trivial and the *provability* is the product, and this is the one place in domain 14 where the platform's whole thesis has an obvious home.** A contributor cannot audit our database. They receive a number and must believe it — which is exactly the position every musician is already in with every royalty statement they have ever received, and exactly the failure D-18 names: facts about your work, held by someone else, unverifiable, surfacing years later when they are contested. The industry's entire royalty-reconciliation misery is this pattern. So the statement here cannot be a summary; it must be **evidentiary** — per-asset, per-period, per-download, with the rate and its derivation shown, reconciling to the penny. That is not a nicety, it is the only version of this feature consistent with the platform's reason for existing. And it is cheap for us and impossible for an incumbent to retrofit, because we hold the events at source. If WeJammin issues an opaque royalty statement, it has become the thing it was built to replace. |
| DT-02 | A download measures a contributor's value — that's what the pool pays for | The model's foundational assumption, inherited unexamined from Splice | ❌ REJECTED — **a download measures curiosity, not use, and the gap is where the model quietly fails its contributors.** A producer spends a credit, drags the loop in, and deletes it ten minutes later. Another loop becomes the hook of a record that streams a hundred million times. The pool pays both the same £0.02, once, forever. The contributor whose sound is *in a hit* earns exactly as much as the one whose sound was auditioned and discarded — which is not a rounding error, it is the model paying for the wrong event. And WeJammin is uniquely placed to notice, because the asset carries its terms into the project (14.02.05) and the project becomes a release (12): **we can see which loops actually made it into records.** No sample store can do that, because no sample store is also where the work happens — which is D-18's consolidation-enables-provenance argument, arriving somewhere nobody expected it. That does not automatically mean paying on *use* rather than downloads (use is slower, sparser, and harder to explain), but it means the download-only model is a choice made blind, and this platform would be choosing it with its eyes open and better data available. Flagged as Q-02 rather than resolved — it is a business-model decision of the first order. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Statements are evidentiary — per-asset, per-download, with the rate's derivation shown, reconciling to the penny | Per DT-01. An opaque royalty statement makes WeJammin the thing it was built to replace. |
| D-02 | The **asset** is the accruing unit, not the pack | A buyer spends a credit on one loop out of 800; accruing to the pack loses the only signal a contributor actually wants. |
| D-03 | Re-downloads by the same buyer are deduplicated per (buyer, asset) | A re-download is not new use; counting it pays for nothing and dilutes every other contributor's pro-rata share. |
