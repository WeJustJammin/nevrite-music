# Feature: Pool Funding & Rate Model

> **Number**: 14.10.01
> **Parent**: [14.10 Contributor Revenue & Per-Download Royalty Pool](./14.10-contributor-revenue-royalty-pool-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What a download is worth, and where the money comes from. Subscription revenue (14.07.02) funds a
pool; the pool is divided among contributors according to downloads. The rate model — fixed cents
per download, or a pro-rata share of the pool — is the single decision that determines whether
this is a fair distribution or a black box, and contributors have no say in it either way.

| Producer | Reports | Same. At volume, the persona most likely to compare what the pool paid against what a direct sale would have. |

## Behavior

A share of subscription revenue funds a pool per accounting period. The pool is divided among
contributors by download volume (14.10.02), then split among a pack's makers (14.10.03).

Two rate models, and the choice is the feature:

- **Fixed rate** — a download is worth £X, always. Predictable for contributors; an **unbounded liability** for the platform, because a download spike costs money the subscription revenue may not cover.
- **Pro-rata** — pool ÷ total downloads. Safe for the platform by construction; means a contributor's per-download value **falls when other people's packs get popular**, through no action of their own (CX-01).

Under pro-rata the rate is unknowable until the period closes, so any accrual shown in-period is an
estimate and must say so.

### Happy Path

1. Subscribers pay; a defined share of revenue funds the period's pool.
2. Downloads accrue against packs throughout the period (14.10.02).
3. Period closes; the rate is computed (fixed, or pool ÷ total downloads).
4. Each pack's accrual is valued; splits are applied (14.10.03).
5. Statements are issued; the payouts cross-cut moves the money.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Download volume spikes under a **fixed** rate | Payouts exceed pool revenue; the platform funds the gap | The unbounded-liability case — why fixed rates are rare in real pools |
| Popular new pack captures volume under **pro-rata** | Every other contributor's rate drops though nothing about their pack changed | Must be stated plainly, or it reads as the platform skimming |
| Contributor compares pool earnings to a direct sale | A £15 pack sold once beats a great many £0.02 downloads | The model's honest weak spot — see DT-02 |
| Credit farming (14.07.02) — subscribe, mass-download, cancel | One month's fee generates downloads the pool must pay for | Distorts the arithmetic; rate limits are a pool-integrity issue, not just abuse |
| Contributor asks how the rate was calculated | Under pro-rata it depends on every other download on the platform | Transparency has no clean answer — see Q-01 |
| Period closes, then a download is reversed (refund/farming clawback) | Adjusting a closed, paid period vs an open accrual are different problems | `[PENDING]` — CX-01 q5 |
| Platform's revenue share of the pool | Undisclosed by every incumbent | See Q-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The rate model is a pricing detail — pick fixed or pro-rata and move on | The candidate's framing ("Per-Download Royalty Pool") treats the pool as a mechanism with an obvious internal | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the whole product, and the two options have opposite victims.** Fixed rate is honest and legible to a contributor ("£0.02 a download, always") and hands the platform an unbounded liability: a volume spike costs money the subscription revenue may not have collected. Pro-rata is safe by construction and quietly unjust — a contributor's earnings fall because *someone else's* pack got popular, which is impossible to explain and indistinguishable from the platform skimming. There is no third option that is fair and safe. And the choice has a structural consequence nobody asks about: under pro-rata, **a contributor cannot be told what they earned until every download on the platform is counted**, so every in-period number is an estimate (CX-01). A store that shows a firm figure and then revises it downward has taught its contributors that the pool lies. This is a product/values decision with a named loser either way (index Q-02), not an implementation choice. |
| DT-02 | A pool is good for contributors — recurring income from a back catalog | Splice's pitch, and the reason the model exists at all | ❌ REJECTED as unexamined, because **the pool inverts the platform's own thesis for the population it depends on.** Do the arithmetic from the contributor's side: a £15 pack sold outright once earns £15 (less the cut). The same pack in a pool at ~£0.02/download needs ~750 downloads to match a *single sale*. Splice-scale volume makes that work; a new store's volume does not, and won't for years. So at launch the pool pays creator vendors dramatically less than perpetual sales would — while WeJammin's realistic supply is precisely those creator vendors (14.01.01 DT-02), the people the platform must attract first and can least afford to underpay. Worse, it takes their pricing power away entirely (see the Role Lens): a musician can price a pack, but cannot price a download. Personas.md's Musician "makes a living from a portfolio of small income streams" — the pool converts one of those streams from *priced by them* to *set by us*. That is a real argument that the pool is a **scale-dependent model** the store should not adopt before it has scale, and it points the same way as 14.07 index Q-01: perpetual first. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The rate model is stated plainly to contributors, including how pro-rata makes their rate depend on others' downloads | Per DT-01. An unexplained rate that moves is indistinguishable from skimming, and that suspicion is unrecoverable. |
| D-02 | Under pro-rata, in-period figures are labelled estimates and never shown as firm | A number that revises downward teaches contributors the pool lies. Estimating honestly costs nothing. |
| D-03 | Rate-limiting on credit downloads is a pool-integrity control, not merely an abuse control | Per 14.07.02's farming case — farmed downloads dilute every honest contributor's pro-rata share. |
