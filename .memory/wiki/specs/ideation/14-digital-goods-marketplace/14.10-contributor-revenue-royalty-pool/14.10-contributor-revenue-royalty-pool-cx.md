## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Attribution ↔ Rate Model

**Relationship**: Attribution produces the download counts; the rate model turns them into money.
Under a **fixed** rate the relationship is simple arithmetic. Under a **pro-rata** rate (pool ÷
total downloads) it is circular: no contributor's per-download value can be known until every
download in the period has been counted, which means earnings are unknowable in real time and a
contributor's rate *falls when someone else's pack gets popular*.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees accrual as a number that moves for reasons they did not cause. Deeply confusing unless the model is stated plainly.
- **Producer**: same, at volume; the persona with enough downloads to notice the rate drifting.
- **Operator / Fan**: no column in this sub-domain.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Attribution owns the counts; the rate model owns the pool arithmetic. No shared writes — but under pro-rata the rate is derived from the counts and cannot be frozen mid-period.
2. **Trigger chain**: Download → attributed → accrued. Period close → rate computed → accruals valued → payout via the payouts cross-cut. If the rate is pro-rata, the accrual shown during the period is an *estimate* and must say so.
3. **Permission intersection**: Contributors see their own counts; never anyone else's. But under pro-rata, other people's downloads determine their earnings — a real transparency problem with no clean answer (14.10.01 Q-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Period close produces a statement.
5. **State transition conflict**: A refund (14.09) or a credit-farming clawback (14.07.02) after a period closed and paid out. Recovering from a contributor who has been paid is a different problem from adjusting an open accrual.

### CX-02: Attribution ↔ Splits

**Relationship**: Attribution's output is per-**pack** ("this pack earned £4.20 this period"). The
pool pays **people**. Between those two facts sits the split, and without it the money either goes
entirely to whoever uploaded the pack or nowhere at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the contributor who played on someone else's pack — a drummer whose kit samples are in a pack a friend assembled. The persona this protects, and the one who gets nothing if the split doesn't exist.
- **Producer**: usually the pack owner who proposes the split, and the party with the power in the negotiation.
- **Operator / Fan**: not applicable.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The split is agreed among contributors (14.10.03) and read by the payout. It must be **frozen per period** — changing a split mid-period retroactively rewrites who earned what.
2. **Trigger chain**: Period close → accrual valued → split applied → per-person amounts → payouts cross-cut. A pack with an unagreed split blocks at this step — which is the design pressure that makes the split get agreed (14.10.03 DT-01).
3. **Permission intersection**: Every contributor sees the split they are party to. Whether they see the *earnings* of a pack they hold 10% of is a real question — arguably yes, or their 10% is unverifiable.
4. **Notification fan-out**: All contributors on a split change; all on a statement.
5. **State transition conflict**: A split changed after a period closed but before payout. Must not retroactively alter a closed period's arithmetic.

### CX-03: Splits ↔ Rate Model

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. Per-download rates in this model are cents. A three-way split
of £0.03 is £0.01, £0.01 and £0.01 — and the rounding error is a third of the payment. At pool
scale, rounding policy is not an implementation detail; it is a material term.

**Synthesis questions**: Deferred — Medium confidence, and contingent on index Q-02 (fixed vs
pro-rata rate) and index Q-01 (whether the pool exists at all).

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.10.03 Splits | 14.09 Refunds/Revocation | A refunded *pack purchase* has nothing to do with a pool split — the pool is funded by subscription revenue (14.07.02), not by pack sales, so a refunded sale never entered it. The genuine adjacent problem is **credit-farming clawback** (14.07.02's abuse case), which is an attribution correction (CX-01 q5), not a split concern. Recording this because "refunds must reverse the split" sounds obviously right and is answering a question from the wrong monetisation model. |
| R-02 | 14.10.01 Rate Model | 14.07.04 Bundles & Sales | Discounting a pack's *purchase price* has no effect on a pool that is not funded by purchases. The two live in different monetisation models entirely (perpetual sale vs subscription pool) and never read each other. A spec writer reasonably assuming "a sale reduces contributor earnings" would be importing a storefront's logic into a royalty system. |
