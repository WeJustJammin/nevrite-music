## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Waiver Capture ↔ Adjudication

**Relationship**: Adjudication's first question is not "is this buyer being reasonable" but "what
was this buyer told, and what did they agree to". The captured waiver — its timestamp, its wording,
and whether it preceded the first byte — is the record that answers it. No waiver means the
statutory withdrawal right survives, and a refund refusal within 14 days is unlawful in the EU/UK
whatever the store's policy says.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: consented at download; the waiver is what a refusal cites back to them.
- **Operator**: may be a business buyer outside consumer protection entirely (index Q-03) — in which case the waiver was never needed and contract terms govern.
- **Fan**: the population consumer law most protects, arriving through domain 20's shared delivery gate (14.03.01) — so the gate must fire there too, not just in the store.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The waiver is an immutable record on the order. Adjudication reads it and never writes.
2. **Trigger chain**: Waiver captured → download unlocks (14.03.01). Later: refund requested → waiver read. If no waiver exists, statutory rights apply and the case is over before it starts.
3. **Permission intersection**: A waiver cannot be captured retroactively, and a download that proceeded without one leaves the buyer's statutory rights fully intact.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None at capture — it is a checkout moment, not an event.
5. **State transition conflict**: A buyer waiving and then requesting withdrawal within 14 days anyway. The waiver defeats it *only* if it was validly captured — express consent plus acknowledgement, before delivery. A pre-ticked box or a buried clause fails both limbs and the waiver is worthless.

### CX-02: Adjudication ↔ Revocation

**Relationship**: A granted refund returns money instantly and attempts to remove an entitlement
that the buyer may have downloaded to three machines, activated offline, and used on a client
record. Under the fail-open activation posture (14.02.02 D-01) the clawback may never land at all.
So a refund is, operationally, a decision to give money back and accept that the goods are gone.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees the refund; their library row goes to revoked with a reason.
- **Producer**: as vendor, loses both the sale and the product. Their revenue is clawed back through the payouts cross-cut, possibly after payout.
- **Operator**: a clawback landing on a room mid-session is the same blast radius as 14.02.04's.
- **Fan**: not affected — no entitlement to claw back on a music purchase beyond access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Adjudication owns the decision; revocation owns the entitlement's state. Clean handoff.
2. **Trigger chain**: Refund granted → funds return (sync, via payments cross-cut) → clawback attempted (async, best-effort). The two must not be conditioned on each other — a failed clawback cannot hold the buyer's money hostage.
3. **Permission intersection**: Revocation must reach delivery (14.03 CX-01) — a revoked entitlement's *next* download must fail even if the current one cannot be stopped.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Buyer (refund + revocation), vendor (sale reversed). If the vendor was already paid out, the payouts cross-cut must recover it.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Yes** — revocation racing an in-flight multi-GB download (14.03 CX-01 q5). Kill mid-stream or let it complete? The bytes are already 60% delivered and the buyer has been refunded.

### CX-03: Waiver Capture ↔ Revocation

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. The waiver's timestamp marks the moment the good became
unreturnable — which is also the moment revocation lost its teeth. Before it, nothing was
delivered and a "refund" is a cancellation with no clawback needed. After it, clawback is
best-effort forever.

**Role scoping**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

**Synthesis questions**: Deferred — Medium confidence. The useful open question is whether a
pre-download refund is modelled as a *distinct, trivial path* (cancel, no clawback, no
adjudication) rather than running the full machinery. It almost certainly should be.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.09.03 Revocation | 14.02.04 Serial Blacklisting | They perform the identical state change and are still not the same feature (index D-02, 14.02.04 D-03). A refund is a routine commercial event with no accusation attached; a blacklisting is an allegation of abuse requiring due process, notification and appeal. Merging them either burdens every refund with an accusation's ceremony, or strips due process from an accusation — and the second is how a platform ends up killing a professional's tools with no appeal route. |
| R-02 | 14.09.02 Adjudication | 14.09.01 Waiver Capture — *as a policy relationship* | The narrower evidentiary relationship (CX-01) is the real one. The tempting broader claim — that the waiver *sets* refund policy — is false: the waiver removes a statutory floor, it does not define what the store chooses to offer above that floor. A store can waive the withdrawal right and still grant generous refunds (Steam does exactly this). Conflating them would let a legal instrument masquerade as a product decision, which is precisely how "all sales final" gets adopted without anyone choosing it (index Q-01). |
