# Feature: Digital Refund Eligibility & Adjudication

> **Number**: 14.09.02
> **Parent**: [14.09 Digital Refunds, Withdrawal & Revocation](./14.09-digital-refunds-revocation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Deciding whether to refund something that cannot be returned. Every other refund in commerce has a
reversal — the box comes back, the service was not rendered, the seat was resold. Here the buyer
keeps the goods whatever we decide, so a refund is not an exchange; it is a **judgement about
whether the sale should have happened**, made with the buyer already in possession.

Two things make that judgement tractable rather than arbitrary. First, the platform holds a
**contemporaneous record** of what the buyer was told at the buy button — the compatibility verdict,
the bound terms, the waiver, the delivery event — so most cases are a lookup, not an opinion
(DT-02). Second, WeJammin owns the rights stack (D-10), so for the store's realistic launch
inventory (creator content — DT-A) it can see something no other store can: whether the good was
**placed** in a record (DT-04). That is the closest thing to a usage meter that exists here, and it
is better than the playtime meter the obvious comparison (Steam) relies on.

The feature's boundary: it **decides**. It does not capture the waiver (14.09.01), execute the
clawback (14.09.03), move money (CX-M01), or run the appeal (24.02/24.04). It reads a record,
applies policy, emits a decision with a reason code and a cause attribution, and hands off.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Full** (requests) · **Reports** (as vendor) | Submits a request from the library row (14.03.03) picking a reason code, not writing an essay. Sees which specific record the decision relied on, quoted back verbatim ("the check said *untested* for Ableton 12 on 2026-07-02, before you bought"), the decision, and an appeal route into 24.02. As a vendor of their own packs: sees refund **count, rate and cause attribution** for each product — never the buyer's identity (DT-07). Cannot decide, block, or contest an individual case; can challenge a *pattern* through 24. |
| Producer | **Full** · **Reports** (as vendor) | Same mechanics at higher values, and the persona most likely to hold a *legitimate* "not as described" claim — they are the one who opens the plugin in a real session at 2am and finds the Kontakt library needs full Kontakt (14.01.03), or that the "24-bit/48k" pack is upsampled. Also the archetypal vendor (DT-A), so this persona sits on both sides of the same case more often than any other — which is exactly why the vendor side is reports-only. |
| Operator | **Full** | Requests on behalf of a studio. **May be a business buyer outside consumer protection** (index Q-03) — in which case the statutory floor (withdrawal, conformity) does not apply and contract terms alone govern. The platform cannot currently tell business from consumer (14.02 Q-03), so today it must assume consumer and over-protect. Their distinctive exposure is downstream: a clawback (14.09.03) landing on a room mid-session, triggered by nothing more dramatic than a refunded duplicate purchase. |
| Fan | **Read-only** | Fan purchases (domain 20) are adjudicated under **that domain's policy**, not the store's — different price band, different buyer, different tone. What is identical is the physics and the floor: the delivery layer is shared (14.03.01), so the good is equally unreturnable and the statutory rights are the same (in fact they bite hardest here, since the Fan is the only persona guaranteed to be a consumer). A fan never sees the store's adjudication surface; they see domain 20's. |

## Behavior

### The record is the input, not the request

Adjudication's first question is not "is this buyer being reasonable" but **"what did we tell them,
and when?"** Five artifacts, all captured before or at the moment of sale, all immutable:

| Artifact | Source | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| The **compatibility verdict snapshot** shown at the buy button | 14.01.04 DT-02 | Whether the store claimed it would run — and on which declared rig |
| The **bound terms version** | 14.02.05 D-02 | What the buyer was permitted to do, frozen at purchase |
| The **waiver** — wording, timestamp, and that it preceded the first byte | 14.09.01 / CX-01 | Whether the statutory withdrawal right survives |
| The **delivery event** | 14.03.01 | Whether anything was delivered at all |
| The **placement signal** (`downstream_propagation`) | 14.02.05 Q-02 / DT-04 | Whether the good has been consumed into a record |

Adjudication **reads and never writes** these (CX-01 q1). It reads the *snapshot*, never the live
object — rigs are mutable and a live read is both gameable and a silent rewrite of history (DT-10).

### The statutory floor sits underneath any policy

Three distinct rights, routinely conflated, with different triggers and different remedies:

1. **Withdrawal (change of mind)** — 14 days from *contract conclusion* for digital content
   (2011/83/EU Art. 9(2)(a)), i.e. from **purchase, not download**. Killed early by a valid waiver
   (Art. 16(m)); survives intact if the waiver failed either limb (CX-01 q5). Where it survives,
   reimbursement is due **within 14 days of being informed** (Art. 13(1)) — a hard statutory clock,
   not an SLA the store chooses.
2. **Conformity (not as described / not fit for purpose)** — Directive (EU) 2019/770 and UK CRA
   2015. **No waiver touches this** (14.09.01 DT-02). But the remedy is a *hierarchy*, not an
   automatic refund: bring into conformity first, then price reduction or termination (DT-06).
3. **Contract terms** — the only floor for a business buyer (index Q-03).

Everything above these floors is a product decision the store makes on purpose or by default
(`#R-02`, Q-01).

### Four outcome classes, and cause attribution on every one

Every decision emits **(outcome, reason code, cause)**. The cause is not cosmetic: it drives the
money (who absorbs it), the vendor's metrics (DT-08), and the store's own QA loop.

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cancelled** | Pre-delivery. Not adjudication at all — a cancellation. No case, no clawback (CX-03). |
| **Granted** | Full refund of the original charged amount. Triggers clawback (CX-02). |
| **Granted in part** | A severable defect in a bundle, or a conformity price-reduction remedy. |
| **Refused** | With the specific record quoted, plus an appeal route (D-04). |

| Cause | Who absorbs it |
|---|---|
| `platform-badge-error` — we badged "will run" from correct inputs and were wrong | Platform |
| `vendor-matrix-error` — the vendor's declared matrix was wrong (14.01 Q-02) | Vendor revenue reversed |
| `vendor-nonconformity` — the artifact does not match the listing's declared fields | Vendor revenue reversed |
| `buyer-rig-error` — the buyer's self-declared rig was wrong (14.01.04 Q-02) | **Q-03 — unresolved** |
| `statutory-withdrawal` — no valid waiver, within 14 days | Platform or vendor — Q-04 |
| `buyer-change-of-mind` — discretionary, above the floor | Per policy (Q-01) |
| `fraud-suspected` | Refused/held; routes to 24.03 |

**The buyer's outcome never depends on which cause fires.** A false "will run" badge refunds the
buyer whether the wrong input came from us, the vendor, or the buyer (D-02, refined by DT-09) —
that is what "standing behind the badge" means, and it is what makes 14.01.04 worth trusting. Cost
allocation is a separate, internal question, and it is the only part still open.

### What the store cannot see — and the one thing it can

There is no client, so there is no playtime (DT-03). The only signals are: a download happened
(binary, fires seconds after purchase), elapsed time, and — uniquely — **placement**. If a pack's
asset is bound into a release's rights record (09/12 via 14.02.05), the good has been consumed in
the only sense that matters commercially. That is the store's refund meter (DT-04), and it exists
only because of D-10.

### Refund and clawback are not conditioned on each other

Money returns synchronously via CX-M01; clawback is attempted asynchronously and is best-effort
forever (14.09.03 D-01). A failed clawback must never hold the buyer's money hostage (D-03). The
goods are gone either way — which is why refund policy is a **trust decision, not a recovery
decision** (14.09.03 DT-01).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer opens the library row for a purchase (14.03.03) and selects **Request a refund**.
2. System presents a fixed list of **reason codes** — *won't run on my rig* · *not as described* ·
   *bought the wrong thing* · *changed my mind* · *other* — plus an optional free-text field marked
   as seen by WeJammin staff only, never by the vendor (DT-07).
3. Buyer submits. The request is **idempotent per order**: a second submit within an open case joins
   that case and does not open a second one.
4. System loads the record snapshot: waiver captured `2026-07-02T14:11:07Z` before the first byte;
   verdict at buy button = `will run` against rig *"Studio Mac"*; terms `v3` bound, commercial use
   permitted; delivery completed `2026-07-02T14:12:40Z`; `downstream_propagation` = none.
5. Statutory floor is evaluated **first**. Valid waiver ⇒ no withdrawal right. Reason code is
   *won't run on my rig* ⇒ this is a **conformity** claim, which no waiver touches.
6. The automatic path fires: verdict was `will run`, the rig's declared fields match the vendor's
   declared matrix, so the badge was ours and it was wrong. Outcome **Granted**, cause
   `platform-badge-error`. **Elapsed: under 60 seconds from submission** — it is a lookup (D-05).
7. Buyer sees the decision, the record it relied on quoted verbatim, and the refund amount:
   **the original charged amount in the original charge currency**, never re-converted (D-08).
8. Funds are returned **to the original payment instrument only** (D-09) via CX-M01. Tax reversal
   is handed to CX-M02.
9. Clawback is queued asynchronously (14.09.03): the library row goes to `revoked` with reason,
   trigger, date and an appeal route — and never disappears (14.09.03 D-04).
10. The vendor is notified: sale reversed, product, amount, reason code and cause
    `platform-badge-error` — **not the buyer's identity** (DT-07). Because the cause is ours, this
    refund is attributed to the platform and does **not** count against the vendor's refund rate
    (DT-08). Their revenue is not reversed.
11. The badge that was wrong is fed back to 14.01.04 / 14.08 as a QA signal against that product's
    compatibility matrix — the store auditing its own claim (DT-02).

**Variant — the pre-delivery cancellation** (the common, trivial path; not adjudication):

1. Buyer purchased; the entitlement was minted (14.02.01); no waiver was taken and no byte moved.
2. Buyer selects **Cancel this purchase** from the library row.
3. System confirms `delivery_event = none` and `waiver = none`.
4. Full refund, immediately, to the original instrument. **No case, no reason code required, no
   clawback, no vendor notification beyond the reversed sale** (D-06, resolving CX-03's question).
5. Statutory backstop: this path must remain available for **14 days from purchase** (Art. 9(2)(a))
   regardless of the store's discretionary policy, and it is exactly the path 14.09.01 D-03
   promises to a buyer who declined the waiver.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| "It doesn't run on my machine" — and we badged it compatible | Not a change of mind; a **conformity** claim, which no waiver reaches. The recorded verdict (14.01.04 DT-02) is the evidence and it points at **us**. Automatic path, cause `platform-badge-error`. | Granted in under 60s, with the badge quoted back and an apology that names the specific claim we got wrong |
| **Buyer's self-declared rig was wrong** (14.01.04 Q-02) | **RESOLVED as behavior**: the buyer is **refunded regardless**, cause `buyer-rig-error`. The badge is the store's claim; a claim conditioned on the buyer having described their Mac correctly is not a claim, and D-02's value evaporates the moment a buyer must litigate their own OS version. Detected by comparing the rig snapshot against the failure the buyer reports. | Granted. "The check ran against *Studio Mac — macOS 13*; the plugin needs macOS 14. Your money is back — please update the rig so this doesn't happen again." |
| — *cost allocation for the above* | **Q-03, open**: platform, vendor or buyer absorbs it. Note the trap: making the **buyer** absorb it is the one option that contradicts the row above, since "you eat it" *is* a refusal. Realistically platform-or-vendor. | Nothing — an internal ledger question |
| Vendor's compatibility matrix was wrong (14.01 Q-02) | Same buyer outcome, different cause: `vendor-matrix-error`. Vendor revenue reversed, and the product's matrix is flagged to 14.08 QA. Repeat offences are a listing-quality problem, not a refund problem. | Buyer: granted. Vendor: "your declared matrix said VST3/Win11; the buyer's Win11 rig could not load it" |
| Badge said `untested`; buyer proceeded anyway | The store made **no claim**, so this is not a conformity failure — it is the arguable case, and the only genuinely discretionary one in the compatibility class. Routes to review, decided by policy (Q-01). | "The check said *untested* for Ableton 12 before you bought" — then whatever policy says. Never silence |
| **No verdict recorded at all** (buyer had no saved rig at purchase) | DT-02's lookup returns nothing, so the automatic path cannot fire → human review. **And the checkout is flagged**: a purchase completed with zero compatibility information is a store failure of a different kind (14.01.04 Q-01), and the absence of a verdict must not be read as the buyer's fault. | "Under review" with a 3-business-day target. Internally: a checkout-quality defect logged against 14.01.04 |
| **Buyer edits their rig, then requests a refund** | Adjudication reads the **snapshot recorded against the order**, never the live rig (D-10/DT-10). Editing a rig after purchase changes nothing about a past verdict. Without this the attack is trivial: declare a rig you don't own, buy, edit, claim incompatibility, refund. | "The check ran on 2026-07-02 against the rig as it was then." The current rig is not the evidence |
| No waiver captured, request within 14 days of **purchase** | Statutory withdrawal right survives; refusal is unlawful in the EU/UK. The case is over before policy is consulted (CX-01). Reimbursement due within 14 days of notification (Art. 13(1)). | Granted, cause `statutory-withdrawal`. No reason code required — "any reason" is the right |
| Buyer never downloaded | Not a refund at all — a **cancellation** (D-06). No case, no clawback, no adjudication machinery. | Instant full refund. The distinct trivial path CX-03 asks for |
| **Refund of a pack whose loop is in a released track** | **RESOLVED as behavior**: `downstream_propagation` ≠ none ⇒ **not auto-refundable**; routes to human review with the placement enumerated (which release, which asset). Default disposition: **refuse the change-of-mind refund** — the good was consumed (DT-04) — but **grant a conformity refund** if the defect is real, since a defective good does not become non-defective by being used. On grant: **revoke future use, preserve past clearance** (14.02.05 Q-02, 14.09.03 Q-03). The legal ruling on whether past clearance actually survives is Q-05. | "This pack is used on *[release]*. Refunding removes your right to use it going forward; it does not un-clear the record already out." |
| Pack placed in an **unreleased** project (07) but not in a release | Placement without release is not consumption — the record is not out, nothing is cleared, and a producer trying a loop in a session is exactly the evaluation the store cannot otherwise offer (DT-05). Auto-refundable; the placement is severed. | Granted. The project's clearance state updates to reflect the removed grant |
| Serial refund abuser — buy, use, refund, repeat | The goods are unrecoverable, so abuse is **pure loss** with no offsetting return. Pattern detection is a **24.03 fraud-risk** function, not a per-case judgement — an individual case is never refused on suspicion; a *pattern* suspends the discretionary path above the statutory floor. The statutory floor is never suspended, for anyone. | Case-by-case: granted. On a pattern: "discretionary refunds are paused on this account" with a 24.02 appeal. Never a silent shadow-ban |
| Self-purchase / wash sale (buyer = vendor, or same payment instrument) | Refunding your own sale is a fee-and-rank exploit, not a refund. Routes to 24.03 before any money moves. | Held pending review, stated plainly |
| Vendor already paid out | Clawback of **vendor revenue**, not just the buyer's licence — via CX-M01's reserve/negative-balance machinery. A creator vendor may have spent it. This is the structural argument for a payout hold window matched to the refund window. | Vendor: "£15 recovered from your next payout." If the balance is negative, CX-M01 owns the recovery, not this feature |
| **Vendor has exited** (14.08.05) | No revenue to reverse and nobody to notify. The platform absorbs it — and this must not become a reason to refuse. The buyer's continuity promise (14.08.05) is worth nothing if it collapses at the first refund. | Granted normally. The buyer never learns the vendor is gone from a refusal |
| Exclusive beat refund (14.05.02 Q-04) | Unwinding a **copyright transfer** and resurrecting terminated leases. Probably impossible — the sharpest refund case on the platform. Behavior here: exclusives are **not auto-refundable**; the case is escalated, and the atomicity guarantee (14.05.02 D-01) means an unwind is a compensating transaction in 09, not a state flip here. | "This purchase transferred copyright. It cannot be reversed automatically." Escalated to 24, with 09's rights record as the constraint |
| Bundle: buyer refunds 1 item of a 5-item bundle bought at 70% off (14.07.04) | Bundle refunds are **all-or-nothing** unless the defect is severable. Where severable, the partial refund is priced at the item's **allocated share of the price actually paid**, never its list price. Refunding a £15 list item from a £25 five-pack must not return £15. | "This item is £5 of what you paid. Refund this item for £5, or the whole bundle for £25?" |
| Upgrade/crossgrade: refund the base after buying the upgrade (14.07.05) | The upgrade's price was conditioned on holding the base. Refunding the base invalidates the upgrade's pricing basis and yields a paid-up product acquired at a discount that no longer applies. **Refund both or neither** — presented as one decision, never silently. | "Refunding *[base]* also reverses *[upgrade]*, because the upgrade price required owning it. Refund both?" |
| **Entitlement already transferred to another buyer** (14.06.02) | **Not refundable.** The seller was already made whole by the resale. Revoking an innocent third party's licence to fund the original buyer's refund is the worst outcome available on this feature. | "You sold this licence on 2026-06-14. It is no longer yours to return." |
| Hardware returned, bundled software kept (14.06.03) | The software's revocation trigger is the hardware return, not a refund decision — this feature is not in the loop. Named here so the boundary is explicit. | Handled by 14.06.03 |
| **Bridged product** (iLok/PACE, 14.02.03) | We cannot revoke what PACE controls (14.09.03). The refund is still granted where the floor requires it — the store's inability to recover is not the buyer's problem — but the **exposure is total**, which is why bridged-product refund policy must differ and be **disclosed pre-purchase** (14.02.03 Q-02). | Buyer: granted per policy. Pre-purchase: the differing policy stated on the listing, not discovered at refund time |
| "Not as described": listing declares 24-bit/48 kHz, delivered file headers say 16-bit/44.1 kHz | **Auto-adjudicable.** The listing's declared fields and the artifact's own headers are both held by the platform; a mismatch is a lookup, not an opinion — DT-02 extended to the artifact itself. Outcome Granted, cause `vendor-nonconformity`, and the listing is flagged. | Granted, with the mismatch shown: "you were sold 24/48; the files are 16/44.1" |
| "Not as described": pack upsampled from a lossy source | Spectrally detectable in principle (a hard shelf around 16 kHz), but this is a **claim about a technique**, not a header field — the store should not auto-refuse or auto-grant on a heuristic it cannot defend. Human review with the spectral evidence attached. Whether the store runs this check at all is Q-06. | "Under review" — never an algorithmic verdict presented as fact |
| "Works with Kontakt" — but needs **full** Kontakt, not the free Player (14.01.03) | The single most common complaint in the sample-library market. If the host-dependency model recorded `requires: Kontakt (full)` and the buyer proceeded, it is arguable. If the listing said "Kontakt" ambiguously, **the ambiguity is the store's** — our taxonomy, our listing form, our verdict. Granted. | Granted, cause `platform-badge-error` or `vendor-matrix-error` by which field was wrong. An ambiguous field is never the buyer's error |
| Preset bank references wavetables it does not ship (14.04.02 Q-02) | The product is unusable as delivered. `vendor-nonconformity` where the bundling field was declared and wrong; where the field does not exist yet, the store cannot blame the vendor for not filling it in — platform absorbs, and the missing field is a 14.04.02 listing defect. | Granted. Vendor sees which declaration was missing or false |
| **Vendor ships a fix within the cure window** (DT-06) | Under 2019/770 Art. 14 / CRA 2015 the remedy hierarchy gives conformity-by-cure precedence over termination. **Recommended behavior**: no cure path under £50 (waiting a week for a £15 pack fix is absurd, and the refund costs less than the friction); at £50+, the vendor may cure within **5 business days**, and the buyer may **decline the cure and take termination** at any point. Buyer's choice, never the vendor's. Legal sequencing is Q-07. | "The vendor says a fix ships Friday. Wait for it, or take your money back now?" — with *now* always available |
| Trial → purchase conversion, no waiver taken (14.02.06 Q-03) | The download happened days ago for free; payment arrives afterwards with no delivery left to gate (14.09.01 DT-01). Adjudication finds **no waiver**, so it must treat the withdrawal right as **surviving** — the honest reading, and the one that fails safe. A buyer who used a plugin for a fortnight then withdraws is an unattractive outcome the store created by not gating the moment. | Granted as `statutory-withdrawal`. Internally: a defect logged against the trial→purchase flow, not against the buyer |
| Refused refund at day 20 returns as a **chargeback** at day 100 | Card-scheme dispute windows run far past any store refund window (commonly ~120 days from transaction — confirm at `/create-prd`). A refusal does not close the exposure; it **converts** it into a more expensive one: the disputed amount **plus** a scheme fee, plus a dispute ratio the platform carries. A refusal must be worth more than the chargeback it risks. | Nothing at refusal time. The cost lands months later, on the platform, in 24.04 |
| Chargeback filed instead of a refund request | The buyer skips us entirely; we lose the fee and the goods. Routes to **24.04 Transaction Disputes**, not here. This feature's contribution is **evidence**: the record snapshot is exactly the representment package (24.09), and it is unusually strong — timestamped consent, the exact wording shown, and the delivery event. | Handled in 24.04. The store's evidence quality is the one advantage the record buys back |
| Duplicate / double-submitted request | Idempotent per order. A second submit joins the open case; it never opens a second case, and never double-refunds. | "You already have a request open on this purchase" |
| Two sessions race the same decision | The decision is single-writer per order. The loser reads the winner's outcome; the record is written once. Never two refunds, never two clawbacks. | The outcome, once |
| Refund lands mid-payout batch | The payout hold and the refund contend for the same balance. CX-M01 owns the ordering; this feature must never assume the money is still there — see the `vendor already paid out` row, which is the losing case. | Vendor sees a held or reversed payout line, with the order cited |
| Refund granted while a 60 GB download is at 60% | Adjudication **does not wait** for the transfer and does not decide its fate — whether the stream is killed is 14.09.03 Q-01 / 14.03 CX-01 q5. Naming the boundary because the temptation to solve it here is real: the decision is instant and the physics are downstream. | Buyer: refunded. What happens to the transfer is 14.09.03's answer |
| **Account takeover**: attacker refunds every purchase to a new destination | Refunds return to the **original payment instrument only** (D-09) — never a user-nominated destination, never a store-credit swap on a fraud path. This makes mass-refund ATO worthless to an attacker: the money goes back to the victim's own card. | The victim's card is credited. The attacker gets nothing. Routes to 24.03 |
| Buyer requests GDPR erasure with an open case (CX-M18) | The case's evidentiary record is retained under the legal-claims basis; the identity link is what erasure touches. This feature must not be the reason erasure fails, and must not delete the evidence a live dispute needs. Boundary owned by CX-M18. | Handled by CX-M18; this feature declares the retention need, not the rule |
| Refund reason free-text contains abuse aimed at the vendor | The free-text field is **never shown to the vendor** (DT-07), which removes the harassment surface by construction rather than by moderating it. Staff-visible only; abuse in it is a 24.01 matter. | The field is labelled honestly at submission: "WeJammin staff only" |
| Currency drift between purchase and refund (CX-M20) | Refund the **original charged amount in the original charge currency** (D-08). Re-converting at today's rate means the buyer receives a different number than they paid, which is a defect regardless of which way FX moved. | The exact figure from the receipt |
| VAT / marketplace-facilitator tax was remitted on the sale (CX-M02) | The refund must reverse the tax, in the buyer's jurisdiction, possibly across a filing boundary. Owned by CX-M02; declared here because a refund that returns the net and keeps the VAT is a compliance failure wearing a rounding error. | The gross amount back |
| Operator buying as a business (index Q-03) | May sit **outside** consumer protection entirely: no withdrawal right, no statutory conformity remedy, contract terms only. The platform cannot tell (14.02 Q-03), so today it assumes consumer and over-protects. Over-protecting is the correct failure direction — the reverse is unlawful. | Consumer treatment, until the platform models the distinction |
| Fan buys an £8 album via domain 20 | Domain 20's policy adjudicates, not this feature. But the delivery layer is shared, so the good is equally unreturnable and the floor is identical — and the Fan is the only persona **guaranteed** to be a consumer, so the floor is hardest here. | Domain 20's surface, domain 20's tone. Here they are in a session on Thursday (personas.md: roles are simultaneous). The band survives the transaction |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Request submitted; record snapshot being read | "Checking your purchase record" — seconds, not a spinner with no end |
| **Empty** | No refund requests on the account | Not shown. The control lives on the library row, not in an empty list |
| **Submitted** | Request accepted and de-duplicated | Case reference, the reason code chosen, and **the expected decision path stated up front** — "usually instant" or "reviewed within 3 business days" |
| **Auto-decided** | The record decided it (DT-02) — badge error, no-waiver-within-14-days, artifact mismatch | The outcome within **60 seconds**, with the record quoted verbatim |
| **Populated** | Case open and progressing | Status, the specific artifacts under consideration, the target date, and what happens next |
| **Partial / Under review** | Automatic path inapplicable — `untested` badge, no verdict recorded, placement detected, lossy-source claim, exclusive | "Under review" **with a stated target date** (3 business days), not silence. Silence is what generates chargebacks (D-05) |
| **Target breached** | Review target passed without a decision | The buyer is told **proactively**, before they ask, with a new date. A stuck refund is a trust event and a chargeback generator |
| **Error** | Record read failed, or the money rail failed after a grant | Explicit and specific. **A granted refund that fails to pay out is the worst state on this feature** — it must retry (3 attempts, exponential backoff) then escalate to a human, never silently revert the decision |
| **Granted** | Approved | Funds returning to the original instrument, exact amount and currency, expected arrival; library row goes to `revoked` with a reason |
| **Granted in part** | Severable bundle defect, or a conformity price-reduction remedy | The amount and **why it is not the whole** — with the allocated-price arithmetic shown, not asserted |
| **Cancelled** | Pre-delivery cancellation (D-06) | Instant confirmation. No case language, no reason code, no "review" — it was never a dispute |
| **Withdrawn** | Buyer withdraws their own request (e.g. accepted a cure) | Case closed by the buyer, re-openable within the statutory window. Withdrawing must never forfeit a right |
| **Refused** | Denied | **The reason, citing the record** — "the compatibility check showed `untested` for your DAW before purchase" — plus an appeal route into 24.02. Never "denied" alone (D-04) |
| **Escalated** | Appeal filed, or the case exceeded this feature's competence (exclusive, fraud pattern) | The case now lives in 24 with a reference. This feature shows the pointer and stops claiming ownership |
| **Superseded** | A chargeback was filed on the same order (24.04) | The refund case is subsumed; the scheme decides now, not us. Shown honestly rather than left open forever |

| DT-01 | Refunds are a cost centre — minimise them; the plugin industry's "all sales final" exists for good reason | The market norm this store would be listing into, and the obvious read of an unreturnable good | ❌ REJECTED — **"all sales final" is not a policy the plugin industry chose; it is the equilibrium of a market with no trust, and it costs more than it saves.** Look at what buyers do in response: they never buy on impulse, they wait for the demo, they trawl forums for compatibility reports, they wait for the 70%-off sale (14.07.04 DT-02), and every purchase becomes a research project. Steam faced the identical structural problem — unreturnable digital goods — and answered with a famously generous near-automatic policy, and that policy is a material part of why people hand Steam money without thinking. For a **new** store with no reputation, selling goods buyers cannot inspect (14.04.01 DT-01) from vendors they've never heard of, a generous refund policy is not a cost centre; it is the single cheapest substitute for the trust we don't have yet. The store's realistic inventory makes this sharper, not weaker: a £15 creator pack's refund costs £15, while the buyer's confidence to try an unknown vendor is worth far more than that. | DT-02 | Adjudication is a judgement call — a human reads the request and decides | The natural framing of "adjudication", and how most marketplaces run refunds | ❌ REJECTED — **the platform already holds the record that decides most cases, and it usually indicts the store rather than the buyer.** The compatibility verdict shown at the buy button is recorded against the order (14.01.04 DT-02); the terms bound at purchase are frozen (14.02 CX-02); the waiver's timestamp is immutable (CX-01); whether a download occurred is known. So "it doesn't work on my Mac" is not a matter of opinion — either we badged it "will run" (our error, refund automatically, and flag the vendor's matrix as inaccurate per 14.01 Q-02) or we badged it "untested" and the buyer proceeded anyway (a different, arguable case). That converts most disputes from he-said-she-said into a lookup, which is the *same capability the whole platform is built on*: D-18's thesis is that a contemporaneous record beats a reconstruction from memory. Refunds are that thesis applied to commerce. The corollary nobody asks for: **the record makes the store accountable to itself.** A badge shown at the buy button becomes evidence against us later, which is exactly the discipline that makes 14.01.04's badge worth trusting in the first place. |
| DT-03 | Port Steam's model wholesale — under 2 hours' use and under 14 days, near-automatic refund | DT-01 leans on Steam as precedent, so the mechanism should port with the policy | ❌ REJECTED — **Steam's generosity is affordable because Steam owns the client and can meter playtime. WeJammin has no client and no meter.** The project is single-surface responsive web (D-02/D-12); a desktop companion is an open question, not an asset (domain Q-05), and the DAW bridge is an unbuilt cross-cut (CX-M12). So the store cannot see whether a plugin was ever opened. The only usage signal it holds is **whether a download occurred** — a binary that fires seconds after purchase and is therefore useless as a bound. This matters because it means DT-01's recommendation, taken naively, produces a policy **strictly more generous than Steam's**: with no usage bound, the only lever left is elapsed time, so a buyer could use a plugin fully for 13 days and withdraw. That is precisely the leak Q-02 names, and DT-01 has no answer to it — which is the honest state of the argument until DT-04. | DT-04 | Having no usage meter, the store must adjudicate on time alone and accept the leak | The forced conclusion of DT-03 | ❌ REJECTED — **the store has a better meter than Steam's, for the inventory it will actually sell, and it exists only because of the thesis.** Playtime is a proxy for consumption; for creator content there is a *direct* signal. A sample pack is consumed when its loop is **placed in a record** — and WeJammin, uniquely among stores, can see that: the terms registry carries a `downstream_propagation` signal (14.02.05 Q-02), the rights record in 09 knows which assets a release cleared, and 12 knows when it went out. Splice cannot see this. Plugin Boutique cannot see this. They sell files and lose sight of them at the download; WeJammin sells a **grant of rights that travels downstream** (DT-C) and stays attached. So the refund meter is: *did this good become part of a record?* Not *how long did you have it?* — which is both fairer (a producer who auditioned a pack for two weeks and never used it has consumed nothing) and tighter (a producer who released a track with the lead loop cannot claim change-of-mind at day 13). ✅ **CONFIRMED as the store's structural advantage on refunds.** It converts Q-02's abuse leak from "unanswerable" to "bounded on the inventory that matters", and it makes 14.02.05 Q-02 tractable: the propagation signal is not just a clearance artifact, it is the eligibility input. Honest limit: **it does not exist for plugins.** A compressor is never "placed". So the leak DT-03 identified survives in full for the plugin catalog — which is a further reason (with DT-A) that the plugin apparatus is the weaker half of this domain. |
| DT-05 | The plugin industry's "all sales final" is paired with something, and that pairing is why it holds | Domain knowledge: every serious plugin vendor ships a time-limited demo, and has for twenty years | ✅ CONFIRMED — **and it is the argument that finally kills the inheritance.** "All sales final" is not a naked position; it is the second half of a bargain whose first half is the **demo**. FabFilter, iZotope, u-he, Xfer all ship functioning time-limited trials. The buyer's protection is not a refund; it is that they *already ran the thing on their own rig in their own session before paying*. That bargain is coherent. The problem is that **WeJammin's realistic launch inventory is the one category where it cannot be struck.** DT-A says the store's day-one vendors are producers with folders selling packs, presets, beats and templates — and you cannot demo a sample pack. A pack's "demo" is an audio preview, which is by construction the pack sounding its best in a finished mix, mixed by someone who knows it (14.04.01 DT-01, 14.04.02 DT-01's demo-vs-product gap). Presets are worse: 14.04.02 D-03 concedes the domain has no enforcement on them at all. Beats are sold from a player that is, again, the seller's mix. **So the store proposes to sell exactly the goods that cannot be trialled, and the industry position it might inherit is only defensible for the goods that can be.** Inheriting "all sales final" here means taking the harsh half of someone else's bargain and dropping the half that made it fair. That is not conservatism; it is a category error. This is the mechanism DT-01 was arguing without: the refund is not generosity, it is **the trial the product type cannot have**. |
| DT-06 | A defective sale means an automatic refund — the buyer was sold something broken, they get their money back | 14.09.01 DT-02 and this file's own earlier framing ("no waiver touches it") | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **the statutory remedy for non-conforming digital content is a hierarchy, not a refund, and the vendor has a right to cure first.** Directive (EU) 2019/770 Art. 14 and the UK CRA 2015 both sequence it: bring the content into conformity (repair/replace) within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience; only then price reduction or termination. So a vendor who ships a fix has, in law, answered the claim — and the store that refunded instantly gave away money the floor did not require. Two things follow, in opposite directions. **Commercially**: for a £15 pack the cure path is absurd — the coordination costs more than the refund, and telling a buyer to wait five days for a fix on a £15 purchase destroys exactly the trust DT-01 is trying to buy. **For a £400 plugin**: cure is what the *vendor* wants, often what the *buyer* wants (they wanted the plugin, not the money), and 14.03.02's versioning machinery already exists to deliver it. Hence the recommendation in the edge cases: no cure path under £50; at £50+ a 5-business-day cure window with **the buyer free to decline the cure and terminate at any point** — the choice is the buyer's, never the vendor's, because a vendor-controlled cure window is just a refusal with a timer. The wider finding: **this file was treating "statutory floor" as a synonym for "refund", and it is not.** The floor is *a remedy*, and the store is free to be more generous than it — which is the whole of Q-01 restated at the conformity boundary rather than the withdrawal one. |
| DT-07 | The vendor should be able to see and contest a refund on their own product — it is their money | Every marketplace instinct, and how Gumroad, Bandcamp and Etsy work | ❌ REJECTED, **and the identity disclosure with it.** Three reasons, ordered by strength. (1) *The vendor cannot verify anything.* The claim is about a rig they do not have, a badge they did not issue, and a record they cannot read. Letting them contest it means letting the party holding the money veto its return on the basis of nothing. (2) *DT-02 means most cases indict the store, not the vendor.* A vendor "contesting" a `platform-badge-error` refund is contesting **our** mistake, which is not theirs to litigate. (3) The decisive one: **on WeJammin the buyer and the vendor are frequently the same social graph.** personas.md's structural fact is that roles are simultaneous — the vendor of the pack is a Producer who is in a session with the buyer on Thursday and in their band next month. On Splice they are strangers separated by a corporate storefront; here they are two named users of a platform whose entire thesis is that it knows who worked with whom. So naming the buyer to the vendor injects a social event into a routine commercial one, on a decision the vendor has no power over — pure downside, zero decision value. Therefore: the vendor receives the sale reversal, the product, the amount, the **reason code and the cause attribution** — and not the buyer's identity, and never the free-text. If a **pattern** justifies naming (the same buyer refunding one vendor repeatedly), that is a fraud case in 24.03 with due process, not a line in a vendor dashboard. Note this is the same reasoning shape as 14.09.03 `#R-01`: the state change is routine, so it must not carry an accusation's ceremony — and naming your accuser is ceremony. |
| DT-08 | Refund rate is a clean vendor quality signal — surface it and let it discipline the catalog | Obvious marketplace mechanic, and genuinely diagnostic: a 40% refund rate means a broken pack | ❌ REJECTED as stated; ✅ CONFIRMED only with **cause attribution and internal scope**. The signal is real — a pack refunding constantly is mislabelled, mis-sampled, or missing its wavetables (14.04.02 Q-02), and 14.08's QA has no better detector. But the naive version is defamatory **by this file's own construction**: D-02 says a false "will run" badge refunds automatically, and DT-09 says the badge's inputs are unverified — so the store generates refunds *caused by the store* and would then charge them to the vendor's rate. A vendor whose pack is perfect and whose matrix was right, refunded because our checker was wrong, would carry the number. That is not a metric; it is an error we are blaming on someone else. Hence: every refund carries a **cause**, only vendor-caused refunds count toward a vendor's rate, and platform-caused refunds are counted against **14.01.04's** accuracy instead — the store's own scoreboard, which DT-02 says is the point. And it stays **internal / vendor-visible**, never public: on a creator vendor with 6 sales, 2 refunds is 33%, a number that is statistically meaningless and reputationally fatal, aimed at exactly the day-one vendor population DT-A says the store depends on. A public refund rate on a long-tail creator catalog is a trust mechanism that eats its own supply side. |
| DT-09 | D-02 is safe as written — "a false `will run` badge is the store's error, refund automatically" | This file's own decision, taken from DT-02 and left unexamined | ❌ REJECTED as written — **D-02 makes the store financially liable for two error classes it did not cause, because the badge's inputs are unverified.** 14.01.04 D-01 is explicit: rigs are **self-declared**, not auto-detected, forced by the single-surface web constraint. And 14.01 Q-02 concedes the vendor's compatibility matrix may itself be wrong. So a false badge has three possible upstream causes — our logic, the vendor's declaration, the buyer's declaration — and D-02, read literally, charges all three to us. That is an unbounded liability created by a decision that *sounds* like integrity, and it scales with catalog size and buyer carelessness, neither of which the store controls. The resolution is to notice D-02 is conflating two things: **the buyer's outcome** and **the cost allocation**. The buyer's outcome must stay unconditional — a badge the buyer must litigate is not a badge, and "you described your Mac wrong" is a refusal wearing a technicality; it would destroy 14.01.04's value at the exact moment the badge is being tested. But the **cost** can and must be allocated by cause: `platform-badge-error` → platform; `vendor-matrix-error` → vendor revenue reversed; `buyer-rig-error` → genuinely open (Q-03), because it is the one case where nobody at fault is holding the money. This is the refinement that lets D-02 survive contact with 14.01.04 D-01, and it is why every decision on this feature emits a cause (D-07). |
| DT-10 | Adjudication reads the buyer's rig to check the compatibility claim | The obvious implementation: you have the rig, you have the product, compare them | ❌ REJECTED — **it reads the recorded verdict snapshot; the live rig is not evidence and reading it is a trivially exploitable hole.** Rigs are mutable by design (14.01.04 D-02: a buyer holds multiple named rigs and edits them as machines change). A verdict is recorded against the order at the buy button (14.01.04 DT-02). If adjudication re-derives the verdict from the current rig, three things break: (1) *the attack* — declare a rig you don't own, buy, edit the rig to something incompatible, claim it won't run, refund, repeat; the store's own record manufactures the buyer's evidence; (2) *the honest drift* — a buyer who upgraded their OS legitimately in month two now shows a mismatch the store never claimed anything about, and a refusal cites a verdict that was never shown to anyone; (3) *the principle* — a re-derived verdict is a **reconstruction**, and D-18's whole thesis is that reconstructions lose to contemporaneous records. Adjudication reconstructing a verdict from present state is the platform committing, on its own commerce surface, the exact failure it exists to prevent everywhere else. The snapshot is immutable, it is what the buyer actually saw, and it is the only defensible input. |
| DT-11 | While an entitlement exists, a refund is always at least *possible* — the machinery can always run | The implicit assumption behind "refund → revoke", and behind CX-02's clean handoff | ❌ REJECTED — **an entitlement that has been transferred out (14.06.02) is not refundable, and treating it as refundable produces the worst outcome available on this feature.** The used-licence market means the original buyer may have **sold** the licence: they were made whole by the resale, and the entitlement now sits with an innocent third party who bought in good faith through the store's own escrow. Refunding the original buyer requires either (a) revoking the third party's licence to fund it — punishing the one participant who did nothing wrong, and destroying the trust 14.06.02's escrow exists to create — or (b) paying the original buyer twice for one licence, once by the resale and once by us. Both are worse than refusing. So: **transfer out closes the refund path**, permanently, and it should be stated at the point of transfer, not discovered afterwards. The generalisation is worth naming because it recurs: a refund assumes the buyer still holds what they are returning, and on this platform that assumption has at least three exits — resale (14.06.02), placement in a record (DT-04), and copyright transfer (14.05.02 Q-04). Each one is a case where the goods have **moved on** in a way money cannot follow. "Irreversible" was only ever the first of them. |

Without the separation, D-02 becomes an unbounded platform liability. |
| D-08 | Refunds return the **original charged amount in the original charge currency** — never re-converted at today's rate | Per CX-M20. Re-conversion means the buyer receives a different number than they paid; that is a defect in whichever direction FX moved. |
| D-09 | Refunds return to the **original payment instrument only** — never a user-nominated destination, never a store-credit swap on a fraud path | Cheap, decisive account-takeover control: it makes mass-refund ATO worthless because the money goes back to the victim's own card. |
| D-10 | Adjudication reads the **verdict snapshot recorded against the order**, never the live rig | Per DT-10. Rigs are mutable (14.01.04 D-02); a live read is trivially gameable and is the platform committing, on its own commerce surface, the reconstruction failure D-18 exists to prevent. |
| D-11 | The vendor receives the reversal, reason code and cause — **never the buyer's identity or free-text** | Per DT-07. The vendor cannot verify the claim and does not decide the case, so identity serves no decision — while on this platform buyer and vendor are frequently the same social graph (personas.md). Naming requires a fraud pattern and 24.03's due process. |
| D-12 | A transferred-out entitlement (14.06.02) is **not refundable**, and this is stated at the point of transfer | Per DT-11. The seller was made whole by the resale; the alternatives are punishing an innocent third-party buyer or paying twice for one licence. |
| D-13 | A placed asset (`downstream_propagation` ≠ none) is **not auto-refundable**; it routes to review. Change-of-mind is refused; a genuine conformity defect is still granted | Per DT-04. Placement is consumption — the store's real usage meter. But a defective good does not become non-defective by being used, so the two claim types must not collapse into one rule. |
| D-14 | Fraud patterns may suspend the **discretionary** path above the floor; they never suspend the **statutory floor**, and never decide an individual case on suspicion | Per the abuse edge case. Refusing a statutory right on a risk score is unlawful whatever the score says, and a silent suspension is a shadow-ban — 14.09.03 DT-02's due-process argument applies to the buyer here exactly as it does to the vendor there. |
