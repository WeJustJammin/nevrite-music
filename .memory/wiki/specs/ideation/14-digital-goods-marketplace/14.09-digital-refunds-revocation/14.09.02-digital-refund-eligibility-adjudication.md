# Feature: Digital Refund Eligibility & Adjudication

> **Number**: 14.09.02
> **Parent**: [14.09 Digital Refunds, Withdrawal & Revocation](./14.09-digital-refunds-revocation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Deciding whether to refund something that cannot be returned. Every other refund in commerce has a
reversal — the box comes back, the service was not rendered, the seat was resold. Here the buyer
keeps the goods whatever we decide, so a refund is not an exchange; it is a **judgement about
whether the sale should have happened**, made with the buyer already in possession.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (requests) · Reports (as vendor) | Requests a refund when a plugin won't run, a pack isn't what the demo suggested, or they bought the wrong thing. As a vendor of their own packs, sees refund rates but does not decide cases — their income is the money going back. |
| Producer | Full · Reports | Same, at higher values. Also the persona most likely to have a *legitimate* "not as described" claim, because they are the one who checks whether a plugin actually works in a real session. |
| Operator | Full | Requests on behalf of a studio; may be a business buyer outside consumer protection (index Q-03), in which case contract terms rather than statutory rights govern the case. |
| Fan | Read-only | Fan purchases (domain 20) route through that domain's policy, not the store's — but the underlying irreversibility and the statutory floor are identical, because the delivery layer is shared. |

## Behavior

Adjudication reads the record and applies policy. The record is the point: the captured waiver
(CX-01), the **compatibility verdict shown at the buy button** (14.01.04 DT-02), the terms bound
at purchase (14.02.05), whether a download occurred, and what was actually delivered.

The statutory floor sits underneath any policy and cannot be waived away: goods must be **as
described and fit for purpose**. A plugin that does not run on the rig the store badged as
compatible is not a change of mind — it is a defective sale, and no waiver touches it (14.09.01
DT-02).

A granted refund returns money instantly and triggers a best-effort clawback (CX-02) that may
never land. The two must not be conditioned on each other.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a refund with a reason.
2. System reads the record: waiver captured at <time>, compatibility badge said "will run", terms permitted commercial use, download completed.
3. Policy is applied — including the automatic paths where the record decides it (DT-02).
4. Refund granted; funds return via the payments cross-cut.
5. Clawback attempted asynchronously (14.09.03); the buyer's library row goes to revoked with a reason.
6. The vendor's revenue is reversed through the payouts cross-cut — possibly after they were already paid.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| "It doesn't run on my machine" — and we badged it compatible | Not a change of mind; a defective sale. The recorded verdict (14.01.04 DT-02) is the evidence and it points at **us**, not the buyer. | Should be near-automatic. See DT-02 |
| Buyer's self-declared rig was wrong (14.01.04 Q-02) | The badge was right given wrong input; who eats it? | `[PENDING]` — 14.01.04 Q-02, unresolved and directly consequential here |
| No waiver captured, request within 14 days (EU/UK) | Statutory right survives; refusal is unlawful | Granted. The case is over before policy is consulted (CX-01) |
| Refund of a pack whose loop is in a released track | Money back; the record is already out | `[PENDING]` — 14.02.05 Q-02 / index Q-02. Recommend: revoke future use, preserve past clearance |
| Serial refund abuser — buy, use, refund, repeat | The goods are unrecoverable, so abuse is pure loss | Pattern detection, and the tension with generosity is real (Q-02) |
| Vendor already paid out | Clawback of *vendor* revenue, not just the buyer's licence | The payouts cross-cut must recover; a creator vendor may have spent it |
| Exclusive beat refund (14.05.02 Q-04) | Unwinding a copyright transfer and resurrecting terminated leases | Probably impossible — the sharpest refund case on the platform |
| Chargeback instead of a refund request | The buyer skips us entirely; we lose the fee and the goods | Routes to the disputes cross-cut (24), not here |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Request submitted | "Reviewing your request" |
| Empty | No refund requests | Not shown |
| Populated | Case open | Status, the record considered, expected timeline |
| Error | Processing failed | Explicit; a stuck refund is a trust event and a chargeback generator |
| Partial | Automatic path inapplicable, human review needed | "Under review" with a stated timeframe, not silence |

| Refused | Denied | **Reason citing the record** — "the compatibility check showed 'untested' for your DAW before purchase" — plus an appeal route into 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Refunds are a cost centre — minimise them; the plugin industry's "all sales final" exists for good reason | The market norm this store would be listing into, and the obvious read of an unreturnable good | ❌ REJECTED — **"all sales final" is not a policy the plugin industry chose; it is the equilibrium of a market with no trust, and it costs more than it saves.** Look at what buyers do in response: they never buy on impulse, they wait for the demo, they trawl forums for compatibility reports, they wait for the 70%-off sale (14.07.04 DT-02), and every purchase becomes a research project. Steam faced the identical structural problem — unreturnable digital goods — and answered with a famously generous near-automatic policy, and that policy is a material part of why people hand Steam money without thinking. For a **new** store with no reputation, selling goods buyers cannot inspect (14.04.01 DT-01) from vendors they've never heard of, a generous refund policy is not a cost centre; it is the single cheapest substitute for the trust we don't have yet. The store's realistic inventory makes this sharper, not weaker: a £15 creator pack's refund costs £15, while the buyer's confidence to try an unknown vendor is worth far more than that. This is a product decision (index Q-01) — but the default should be argued for, not inherited from an industry whose refund culture is a symptom of its dysfunction. |
| DT-02 | Adjudication is a judgement call — a human reads the request and decides | The natural framing of "adjudication", and how most marketplaces run refunds | ❌ REJECTED — **the platform already holds the record that decides most cases, and it usually indicts the store rather than the buyer.** The compatibility verdict shown at the buy button is recorded against the order (14.01.04 DT-02); the terms bound at purchase are frozen (14.02 CX-02); the waiver's timestamp is immutable (CX-01); whether a download occurred is known. So "it doesn't work on my Mac" is not a matter of opinion — either we badged it "will run" (our error, refund automatically, and flag the vendor's matrix as inaccurate per 14.01 Q-02) or we badged it "untested" and the buyer proceeded anyway (a different, arguable case). That converts most disputes from he-said-she-said into a lookup, which is the *same capability the whole platform is built on*: D-18's thesis is that a contemporaneous record beats a reconstruction from memory. Refunds are that thesis applied to commerce. The corollary nobody asks for: **the record makes the store accountable to itself.** A badge shown at the buy button becomes evidence against us later, which is exactly the discipline that makes 14.01.04's badge worth trusting in the first place. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Adjudication reads the recorded evidence first and applies policy second | Per DT-02. The record (badge, waiver, terms, delivery) decides most cases without judgement, and it is the same contemporaneous-record thesis the platform is built on. |
| D-02 | A "will run" badge that turns out false is the **store's** error and refunds near-automatically | Per DT-02. The badge is our claim; standing behind it is what makes 14.01.04 worth trusting. |
| D-03 | Refund and clawback are not conditioned on each other | Per CX-02 q2. A failed clawback must never hold the buyer's money hostage — the goods are gone either way. |
| D-04 | A refusal cites the specific record it relied on, and carries an appeal route | "Denied" generates a chargeback; "the check said untested for your DAW before you bought" generates understanding or a legitimate appeal. |
