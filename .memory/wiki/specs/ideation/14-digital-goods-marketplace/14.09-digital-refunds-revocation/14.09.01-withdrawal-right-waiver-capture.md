# Feature: Withdrawal-Right Waiver Capture (EU/UK)

> **Number**: 14.09.01
> **Parent**: [14.09 Digital Refunds, Withdrawal & Revocation](./14.09-digital-refunds-revocation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The consent moment that makes a digital sale final. EU/UK consumer law gives a buyer 14 days to
withdraw from a distance purchase for any reason — including a downloaded plugin — unless they
**expressly consent** to immediate delivery, **acknowledge** that doing so loses the right, and are
**sent confirmation of both on a durable medium** (DT-03). Miss any of those and the store has no
enforceable "no refunds" position in its two most likely launch markets, whatever its policy says.

The feature is small on screen — one sentence, one checkbox, one click — and load-bearing
everywhere else. It sits on the delivery path (14.03.01), it is half the evidence adjudication
runs on (CX-01, DT-09), and it is the moment the goods stop being returnable, which is the moment
clawback stops working (CX-03, 14.09.03 DT-01).

Two things it is **not**. It is not the refund policy — it removes a statutory floor and says
nothing about what the store offers above it (DT-02, `#R-02`, index Q-01). And it is not the moment
the right dies: **the first byte is** (DT-09). The click only makes the death lawful.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Ticks one unticked box and clicks download, once per purchase, before their first byte of it. Sees a single plain sentence — not a checkbox on a PDF, not a clause. The persona least likely to read anything, which is exactly why the wording has to carry the meaning in one line and why nothing else may be bundled into that box (D-05). Receives the confirmation email that completes the third limb (DT-03) and mostly ignores it — the record is for the day they contest it. Re-downloading the same purchase in 2036 never re-asks (E-21). If they decline, they wait: on day 15 the gate stops appearing and the download simply works, because there is no longer a right to waive (DT-05). |
| Producer | Full | Same act, higher stakes and a more complex cart. Buys three plugins at once, downloads one on the deadline day: **the waiver is per line item**, so the two they have not fetched are still cancellable for 14 days (DT-04). Downloads one 5 MB loop out of an £60 800-file pack and has thereby waived the whole pack — one asset fetch is performance of the pack's supply (E-11), which is legally clean and commercially harsh. **As a vendor**, they do two things this feature depends on: they declare whether they sell as a trader (DT-07), which decides whether their buyers meet this gate at all, and they fetch their own builds through the vendor portal (14.08), which has no buyer, no consumer contract and therefore **no gate** (E-16) — one human, two paths, the same day. |
| Operator | Full | Same gate, deliberately not branched. A studio buying as a business may sit outside consumer protection entirely (index Q-03) — and the platform does not classify buyers and should not (DT-06): the Recital 17 "predominant purpose" test lands exactly on this project's defining persona fact, the multi-hyphenate, and the cost is asymmetric (a surplus waiver is harmless; a skipped one is a void position). So the Operator ticks the same box as everyone. Their real exposure is elsewhere: the room's library sits in a departed engineer's account (14.02 Q-03), so *whose* waiver is on the order may not be *whose* studio paid. |
| Fan | Read-only | The population consumer law exists to protect, arriving through the **shared delivery layer** (14.03.01 E-24) on domain 20 music purchases. Read-only *here* because this feature owns the rule and domain 20 owns its presentation: a fan buying an £8 album meets the same legal requirement in a different product, and must not be walked through a legal ceremony designed for a £400 plugin — one sentence, one box, a download link, nothing else. They are also the persona most likely to be a **minor**, whose click waives a right under a contract that may itself be voidable (E-18), and the one whose confirmation email is most likely to be suppressed by a notification preference (DT-08). |

## Behavior

### The three limbs

A waiver is valid only if **all three** are satisfied. Any one missing and the statutory right
survives intact, silently, until a regulator or a chargeback finds it.

| # | Limb | What satisfies it | What voids it |
|---|------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Prior express consent** to performance beginning inside the withdrawal period | An affirmative act by the buyer, before the first byte, whose only meaning is consent | A pre-ticked box; a clause in the T&Cs; consent bundled into a "Download" button's primary meaning (D-05); consent taken from someone who is not the contracting buyer (E-17) |
| 2 | **Acknowledgement** that consenting loses the right | Wording, shown at the moment of the act, naming the 14-day right and its loss, in a language the buyer is served in (DT-11) | Wording that describes immediate delivery but not the consequence; wording in a language the buyer's Member State requires be localised |
| 3 | **Confirmation on a durable medium**, including confirmation of limbs 1 and 2 (DT-03) | A pushed artifact the buyer keeps — email body, or an attached/downloaded copy | A link to an account page (a website is not a durable medium); a confirmation suppressed by a notification preference (DT-08); a hard-bounced email |

### Where and when it fires

The waiver is captured at the point of **first delivery of a line item**, not at payment (D-01),
and it gates the download (D-02, 14.03.01 gate 3). It is **per entitlement, never per order**
(D-04) — a three-item cart produces three independent waiver moments, and downloading the
compressor does not touch the right over the un-fetched reverb.

The gate has a **shelf life** (D-06). The withdrawal right expires 14 days after the contract is
concluded whether or not anything was delivered, so from day 15 there is no right to waive and the
gate must not appear. That rule is conditional: if the pre-contract withdrawal information was not
given at checkout, the window is 12 months + 14 days and the gate persists for all of it (DT-05).

The gate does **not branch on who the buyer is** (DT-06) or **where they are** (D-08). It branches
on exactly two things: whether the *seller* is a trader (DT-07 — if not, there is no statutory
right and no gate) and the *wording*, which is chosen per locale and fails toward the strict
regime when location evidence is absent or contradictory (D-08).

### What is recorded

The record is immutable and is half of what adjudication rests on (CX-01, DT-09). It carries:
purchaser and holder as separate principals (14.02.01 D-05 — they diverge, see E-17); the
entitlement; the **frozen full text** of the wording shown, plus its version and locale (D-07 — a
version pointer into mutable copy destroys the evidence the day someone edits it); the
server-side timestamp; the tax-determined buyer location and the seller's declared trader status,
so a later reader knows *which regime applied and why the gate did or did not fire*; the
confirmation's dispatch and bounce state; and the fact that it preceded the first byte.

That last fact is not proved by comparing timestamps. It is proved **causally**: no waiver row, no
transfer grant, therefore no bytes (D-03, DT-09). The timestamp is human-readable colour; the
enforceable fact is that the grant cannot exist without the row.

### Concrete parameters

| Parameter | Value | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Withdrawal window | **14 days from conclusion of the contract** (not from delivery) | Art 9(2)(a) CRD: for digital content not on a tangible medium the clock starts at conclusion. This is why an undownloaded purchase self-resolves on day 15 (D-06). |
| Window if pre-contract information was not given | **12 months + 14 days** | Art 10 CRD. The gate's shelf life is contingent on checkout doing its job (CX to Cart/Checkout). |
| Consent act | **One unticked checkbox**, nothing else bundled into it; download control inactive until ticked | Satisfies "express" (an actively ticked box is the canonical affirmative act) without making the download button do double duty (D-05). One click — a fan tolerates it; a ceremony they would not. |
| Wording | **One sentence**, server-rendered, frozen verbatim onto the record | D-07. A legal artifact, not product copy (routed to `/create-prd`). |
| Confirmation dispatch | **Enqueued within 60 s** of capture; never blocks the download | The download already has its consent. Blocking a paid buyer on an async email is user-hostile, and email delivery is unverifiable at request time. |
| Confirmation failure window | Hard bounce or suppression detected within **72 h** → waiver marked `unconfirmed` | Long enough for a bounce to land; short enough that the order's true status is known before a typical dispute. |
| Effect of `unconfirmed` | The order is treated as **having no waiver** by adjudication (14.09.02) | DT-03. Limb 3 failed; the right survives. Recording it as valid would be a lie with money attached (14.09.03 D-01's principle, applied here). |
| Idempotency | **One waiver row per entitlement.** A concurrent second commit is a no-op returning the first | E-05. Two rows with two wording versions is two answers to a question that must have one. |
| Gate suppression | Order age **> 14 days** from conclusion **AND** pre-contract information recorded as given | D-06. Asking to waive a right that expired is theatre, and implies the buyer still had one. |
| Retention | **7 years** from the later of conclusion or last delivery | Longest realistic limitation period on a claim arising from the sale (6 years, Limitation Act 1980 s.5, England & Wales) plus margin. See DT-10. |
| Erasure | Survives a GDPR erasure request; minimised to the fields listed above | Art 17(3)(e) — necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims. DT-10. |
| Territorial branch | **None.** The gate fires for every buyer; only the wording varies | D-08. One path, one record; a misclassification costs a sentence, not a waiver. |

### Happy Path

1. Buyer completes checkout. Pre-contract withdrawal information is given there (Cart/Checkout
   cross-cut) and the fact is recorded — this is what makes the day-15 rule available later (D-06).
2. Entitlement is minted (14.02.01), holder and purchaser recorded separately (14.02.01 D-05).
3. Buyer opens their library (14.03.03) and clicks download on the entitlement row.
4. Delivery runs its gates (14.03.01). Gate 3 finds no waiver row for this entitlement, and the
   order is 2 days old, so the gate is live.
5. System renders, inside the existing "Preparing your download…" state and with no page
   navigation: the size and version disclosures 14.03.01 owes (step 3 there), plus **one sentence**
   and **one unticked checkbox** — wording resolved server-side for the buyer's locale.
6. Buyer ticks the box. The download control becomes active. Nothing else is bundled into that tick.
7. Buyer clicks download. The client echoes the wording version and the render nonce; the server
   verifies that wording was actually rendered to this buyer for this entitlement.
8. The waiver row commits — frozen wording text, version, locale, server timestamp, purchaser,
   holder, entitlement, location determination, seller trader status. Immutable from this instant.
9. The durable-medium confirmation is enqueued (≤ 60 s) — email body carrying the same verbatim
   wording, plus a copy the buyer can save immediately. Marked as **transactional**, exempt from
   notification preferences (DT-08).
10. Only now is the transfer grant minted (14.03.01 D-04). The row exists, therefore the grant may
    exist — the causal ordering that *is* the evidence (D-03, DT-09).
11. The first byte moves. **This is the moment the right is extinguished** — not step 6 (DT-09).
    The delivery log (14.03.01 D-08) records that it began, and it is the other half of the
    evidence pair.
12. No bounce within 72 h → the waiver is `valid` and stays on the order record for 7 years.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| E-01 | **Buyer declines the waiver** | Entirely legitimate and fully supported (D-09). They keep their 14-day right and cannot download yet. It costs them at most 14 days, because on day 15 the gate self-disables (D-06) and the download proceeds with no waiver ever captured — a lawful choice with a bounded price, not a dead end. | "You can download once you accept that delivery starts immediately. Or cancel for a full refund within 14 days — or wait: from <date> this download will start without asking." The third option is stated, because concealing it would be the hostile version of an honest rule. |
| E-02 | **Buyer never downloads at all** | No performance, so no waiver was ever needed and the right stands untouched until day 15 (D-06). A refund here is a trivial cancellation with **no clawback** — nothing was delivered (CX-03). | A distinct, one-click "Cancel this order" path on the library row for 14 days, not the refund-request machinery of 14.09.02. The full apparatus for a case with no facts in dispute is ceremony. |
| E-03 | **Buyer ticks the box and then never downloads** | **The right survives.** Art 16(m) requires that performance has *begun*; consent alone extinguishes nothing (DT-09). The waiver row exists and is inert. If they cancel on day 10, they cancel successfully despite having consented. | Nothing at tick time. On cancellation: granted, with no argument. Any refusal citing "you agreed" here would be unlawful and would be citing the wrong record. |
| E-04 | **Download proceeds with no waiver captured** (order < 15 days, information given) | Structurally impossible, not policy-prevented: no row, no grant, no URL, no bytes (D-02/D-03, 14.03.01 gate 3, E-26 there). | The gate. |
| E-05 | **Two tabs, two download clicks, one order** | Idempotent by entitlement: first commit wins, the second reads it back and proceeds. Two rows with two wording versions would be two answers to a question that must have exactly one. | Nothing. Both downloads start. |
| E-06 | **Connection drops between the tick and the commit** | No row committed → no grant → no bytes. The buyer re-consents on retry. The failure mode is a repeated question, never a delivery that outran its gate. | The gate again. Mildly annoying; the alternative is a void refund position. |
| E-07 | **Capture succeeds, grant mint fails** | No bytes moved, so the inert row is harmless (per E-03 it extinguishes nothing on its own). The retry finds the existing row (E-05) and does not re-ask. | 14.03.01's error state for the real failure. Not a second waiver prompt — re-asking implies the first was invalid. |
| E-08 | **Confirmation email hard-bounces** (limb 3 fails — DT-03) | Within 72 h the waiver flips to `unconfirmed`. The bytes are long gone; the buyer's statutory right is **restored** because we never completed the third limb. Adjudication treats the order as unwaived. The store eats it, honestly, rather than recording a defence it does not have. | Nothing at the time. In-product prompt to fix the email address; a corrected address does **not** retroactively rescue the waiver — limb 3 is "provided before/at performance", and performance is over. |
| E-09 | **Confirmation suppressed by a notification preference** (DT-08) | Same outcome as E-08, from a cause nobody would ever look for: a marketing unsubscribe voiding a legal position. Prevented by classifying the confirmation as transactional and exempt (D-12) — but if it ever routes through preference evaluation, this is silent and total. | Nothing, ever. That is precisely the danger. |
| E-10 | **Multi-item cart: 3 plugins, 1 downloaded** | Per-line-item (D-04). Items 2 and 3 keep their 14-day right and are individually cancellable at full price. The order is not an atom; the supplies are. | Library shows three rows in three states: one delivered and final, two "cancellable until <date>". |
| E-11 | **One 5 MB loop fetched from an £60, 800-file pack** | Performance of the *pack's* supply has begun, so the whole pack is waived. The entitlement is the pack (14.02.01); 14.03.01's per-asset granularity is two payloads under one authorisation, not two contracts. Legally clean; commercially brutal, and a live argument for a policy above the floor (index Q-01). | The gate fires on the first asset fetch, and the sentence says **pack**, not file. A buyer who thinks they are trying one loop must not discover later that they bought 800. |
| E-12 | **Subscription/credit download** (14.07.02) — delivery happens hundreds of times | Resolved (D-10). No new ceremony: each **credit spend is itself an affirmative act** concluding a contract for that asset, so the sentence rides the spend control the buyer is already clicking. The *subscription* is a different animal — a service, not a completed supply, so it carries its own 14-day right with pro-rata deduction for use (Art 14(3)), which is 14.07.02's to model, not this gate's. | One extra line on the spend button. Not a gate per download. |
| E-13 | **Trial → purchase conversion** (14.02.06 Q-03) | Resolved (D-11). The files were delivered days ago for **free**, so no paid contract's performance was bypassed — but conversion concludes a new paid contract whose performance (clearing the expiry, per 14.02.06 D-01's in-place upgrade) begins immediately. So the waiver moment is the **conversion click**, with different wording: the buyer already has the bytes; what starts now is permanent authorisation. Without this, the buyer holds a 14-day right over a plugin they have used for a fortnight, against a best-effort clawback (14.09.03 DT-01) — we would refund and lose the goods. | On the convert button: "You already have this installed. Paying unlocks it permanently, starting now — which means you lose the 14-day right to cancel." |
| E-14 | **Freeware / £0 entitlement** (14.02.06) | No gate. Art 16(m)'s exception is framed around a contract placing the consumer under an obligation to pay; with no payment there is no refund to protect. Terms still bind (14.02.06 D-01) — that is 14.02.05's act, and it is not this one. | The terms agreement, not the waiver. Two different clicks meaning two different things; conflating them would make both worthless. |
| E-15 | **Gift purchase** (14.02.01 Q-01, D-05) | The one shape where D-01 cannot hold, and the sharpest case here. The purchaser holds the withdrawal right and is **structurally absent** from the delivery moment; the recipient is present but is not the contracting party, so their click waives nothing (D-13). Resolution: the purchaser consents **at checkout** — the only moment they exist in the flow — which is still *prior* to performance and satisfies all three limbs. This mirrors 14.02.01's gift wrinkle exactly, inverted: terms freeze at purchase but are agreed by the recipient; the waiver is agreed by the purchaser but performed on the recipient. | Purchaser at checkout: the sentence, adapted ("delivery to Sam starts as soon as they accept"). Recipient at download: the terms agreement only — no waiver, because it is not theirs to give. |
| E-16 | **Multi-hyphenate: a Producer downloads their own build** | **No gate.** Delivery serves entitlements; a vendor has none over their own product and there is no consumer contract to withdraw from (14.03.01 E-16). The vendor-portal path must not inherit this gate. If the same human also *bought* someone else's pack that morning, both paths run on one account the same day and both are correct. | "Download build" (vendor portal): straight through. "Download" (library): the gate. Two controls, two places, two meanings. |
| E-17 | **Consent captured from the wrong principal** | Rejected at commit. The waiver binds to the **purchaser** (the contracting consumer), not the holder, and the two diverge routinely on this platform (14.02.01 D-05: studio pays, room holds; producer expenses to a client). A holder's tick on a purchaser's contract is E-15's problem in a non-gift shape and is worth exactly as much: nothing. | The holder is not shown a consent they cannot give. |
| E-18 | **Fan is a minor** (D-13's age-assurance consequence) | The waiver is worth what the underlying contract is worth, and a minor's contract is generally voidable. So a perfectly captured waiver from a 14-year-old defends nothing — the attack is on the contract, not the consent. This gate cannot fix it and must not pretend to. | Nothing here. The exposure belongs to age assurance (24 / D-13) and is recorded, not solved, at this node. |
| E-19 | **Buyer's location is unknown or the evidence is contradictory** | Fails toward the strict regime: EU/UK wording is shown (D-08). A misclassified German buyer given neutral wording has an unacknowledged consequence and a void waiver; a misclassified American given EU wording has a surplus sentence. Only one of those costs anything. | The EU/UK sentence. |
| E-20 | **Buyer outside the EU/UK/EEA** | The gate still fires — no territorial branch (D-08) — but with neutral wording that does not assert a right they never had. Naming a 14-day right to a buyer who has none is a misrepresentation that could manufacture the very right it describes. | "By downloading now you agree delivery starts immediately." No mention of a cancellation right. |
| E-21 | **Re-download in 2036 of a 2026 purchase** (14.03.01 DT-04/E-20) | Passes. Not because the waiver row survived — under D-14's retention it may have been purged years earlier — but because the order is a decade old, so D-06's shelf-life rule disposes of the gate before the record is even consulted. **This is the collision DT-05 quietly prevents**: without the shelf-life rule, a retention policy deleting the waiver would make the delivery gate refuse a legitimately owned product forever, and the perpetual promise (14.08.05, 14.03.02 D-01) would break a decade out, for a reason nobody would ever diagnose. | Nothing. It just downloads, as promised in 2026. |
| E-22 | **Seller is not a trader** — a used licence bought from another musician (14.06.02) | No gate (DT-07). Consumer withdrawal rights run against traders; a C2C sale has none to waive. But the marketplace has its own duty here: it must have **told the buyer before purchase** that the seller is not a trader and that consumer rights do not apply (Art 6a(1)(b)–(c) CRD). That disclosure is ours, not the vendor's. | At the listing, before the buy button: "Sold by an individual, not a business — consumer cancellation rights don't apply." Then no gate at download. |
| E-23 | **Vendor's trader declaration is wrong** | We rely on the declaration (Art 6a is explicit that the disclosure is made "on the basis of the declaration of that third party"), so the disclosure duty is discharged — but the *withdrawal right* is decided by the seller's objective status, not their tick. A trader who declared otherwise leaves their buyers with an unwaived, undisclosed statutory right, and the store's position on those orders is void. | Nothing until it surfaces. The declaration is a checkbox and a liability; the mitigation is that vendors who behave as traders (volume, catalog, VAT registration) are visible to us and their declaration is contestable in 24. |
| E-24 | **Wording is edited after capture** | Prevented, not detected. The record stores the **frozen full text** (D-07). A version pointer into mutable copy means the evidence changes retroactively when marketing tidies a sentence, and it changes *silently*, and nobody discovers it until the one day it matters. | Nothing. This is a correctness constraint, not a state. |
| E-25 | **Client submits its own wording string** | Rejected. The wording is resolved server-side and the client echoes only a version identifier and a one-time render token, which the server verifies was actually issued to this buyer for this entitlement. Anything else lets the record be authored by the party it is evidence against. | The gate reopens with a plain "Start the download again" — never the client's text on record. |
| E-26 | **Waiver replayed against a different entitlement** | Rejected. The row binds to one entitlement (D-04); a consent given for the compressor is not a consent for the reverb, and reusing it is exactly the per-order fallacy DT-04 rejects, executed by an attacker instead of by a designer. | The gate, for the entitlement that actually needs one. |
| E-27 | **Client clock is manipulated to fake a pre-delivery timestamp** | Irrelevant by construction. Timestamps are server-side, and the load-bearing proof is causal, not temporal: the grant cannot exist unless the row committed first (D-03, DT-09). There is no timestamp comparison to defeat. | Nothing. |
| E-28 | **Buyer stops at 40% and claims non-delivery** (14.03.01 E-23) | Two records, two questions. The withdrawal right is **gone** — performance *began*, and "begun" is the statutory test, not "completed". But a 40% partial that never verified is a genuine **failure to supply**, which is a stronger claim than withdrawal ever was (termination for non-performance, Art 13–14 Digital Content Directive 2019/770). The buyer loses the weak right and gains the strong one, and the waiver is irrelevant to the case. | Adjudicated in 14.09.02 on delivery telemetry (14.03.01 D-08), not on this record. |
| E-29 | **Buyer requests erasure of their account** (GDPR Art 17) | The waiver row survives, minimised, under Art 17(3)(e) — necessary for the defence of legal claims (DT-10). It does not survive forever: it is purged at 7 years (D-14). Nobody designs the deletion of a legal-evidence record, and the two failure modes are opposite and both bad: delete early and the store's position evaporates; keep forever and "immutable evidence" is an unlawful retention dressed in a virtue. | A DSAR response that names this retention and its basis, rather than silently keeping it. |
| E-30 | **Pre-contract information was never given at checkout** | The withdrawal window is 12 months + 14 days (Art 10), so the gate persists for a year and the day-15 rule never fires. Worse, it fires *invisibly*: the gate looks identical, so the defect surfaces as "why is this order still asking?" and nobody connects it to a missing checkout disclosure. | The gate, indefinitely. Which is correct, and is why the checkout dependency is a stated cross-cut rather than an assumption. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Download requested; delivery's gates running | Nothing distinct — the gate renders inside 14.03.01's existing "Preparing your download…" state. It adds a decision, never a page. |
| **Empty** | Purchased, never downloaded, order < 15 days | Library row with a download control that opens the gate, and — per E-02 — a plain "Cancel for a full refund until <date>" beside it. |
| **Gate** | Waiver required and not yet captured | One sentence, one unticked box, an inactive download control. Locale-selected wording; EU/UK wording when location evidence is absent (E-19). |
| **Declined** | Buyer did not tick, or navigated away | Withdrawal right retained; download withheld; refund available; **and the date the gate stops asking** (E-01). Never a dead end and never a silent one. |
| **Populated** | Waiver `valid`, delivery begun | Download proceeds. The order record shows what was agreed, verbatim, and when. |
| **Not required** | Order > 14 days with information given (D-06); £0 entitlement (E-14); non-trader seller (E-22); vendor-portal fetch (E-16) | Nothing at all. The gate's absence is a correct state, not a missing one — and for the 2036 re-download it is the state that keeps the perpetual promise (E-21). |
| **Partial** | Waiver committed, confirmation dispatched, no bounce verdict yet (< 72 h) | Nothing user-facing. Internally the order's defence is **provisional**, and 14.09.02 must read it as provisional rather than as valid. |
| **Unconfirmed** | Confirmation hard-bounced or was suppressed (E-08, E-09) | The honest state: limb 3 failed, the buyer's right is back, the goods are gone. Never dressed up as success (14.09.03 D-01's principle). In-product: fix your email — with no claim that fixing it repairs the waiver. |
| **Error** | Capture failed to commit | Download blocked. Never proceed without the record (D-02/D-03) — a delivery that outruns its gate is not recoverable afterwards, at any price. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The waiver is a checkout term — put it in the terms of sale and take it at payment | The obvious implementation, and where every store puts its legal text | ❌ REJECTED — **it must be captured at the moment of first delivery, and it must be its own affirmative act.** The Directive's exception (2011/83/EU Art. 16(m)) requires prior express consent *and* acknowledgement of the consequence, before performance begins. Payment is not performance; **download is**. So a waiver taken at checkout is premature — the buyer has not yet started receiving anything, and the right hasn't yet had anything to attach to. Worse, a checkout term buried among others fails the "express" limb: a pre-ticked box or a clause in the T&Cs is not express consent, and the whole waiver is void. What makes this structurally significant rather than a legal footnote: it puts a **consent gate on the delivery path** (14.03.01), which is why the download URL cannot be minted without it (14.03.01 D-01) and why this sub-domain exists rather than a refund button. It also means the trial→purchase flow (14.02.06 Q-03) quietly bypasses the moment entirely — the download happened days ago, for free, and payment arrives afterwards with no delivery left to gate. **Standing exception found at Step 5**: the gift purchase (E-15, D-13), where the buyer is structurally absent from the delivery moment and checkout is the only place they exist. |
| DT-02 | Capture the waiver and the store's refund exposure is handled — that's what it's for | The point of the instrument; it is literally called a waiver | ❌ REJECTED — **the waiver removes a statutory floor; it does not decide what the store offers above it, and conflating the two is how "all sales final" gets adopted without anyone choosing it** (`#R-02`, index Q-01). A valid waiver means the buyer cannot *demand* a refund under withdrawal rights. It says nothing about whether granting one is good business. Steam waives the withdrawal right and then offers a famously generous 2-hour/14-day policy — and that policy is a large part of why people trust Steam with money. The plugin industry waives it and offers essentially nothing, which is why plugin buyers are trained to wait for demos, trawl forums, and never buy on impulse. Both are *legal*; they are different products. And the waiver is silent on the statutory right that actually bites: goods that are **faulty or not as described** (14.09.02 DT-01) — no waiver touches that, so a plugin that doesn't run on the rig we badged as compatible (14.01.04 DT-02) is refundable regardless of what the buyer clicked. |
| DT-03 | The waiver has two limbs — express consent and acknowledgement — and the file said so | This file's own [SURFACE] text, and the CX-01 evidence line, both of which name exactly two | ❌ REJECTED — **there are three, and the third is the one a software team will never build, because it is not on the screen.** Art 16(m) as amended requires, alongside consent and acknowledgement, that *the trader has provided confirmation of the contract in accordance with Art 8(7)* — which means confirmation on a **durable medium**, including confirmation of the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement. And the CJEU has already closed the obvious shortcut: in *Content Services v Bundesarbeitskammer* (C-49/11) information merely made accessible via a link to a website is not "provided on a durable medium". So the account page showing "you agreed on 16 July" is worth nothing; a pushed artifact the buyer keeps is the requirement. The consequence is a specific and unglamorous one: **a waiver is not complete when the buyer clicks — it completes when the confirmation lands**, which makes an email delivery failure a *legal* failure (E-08). It also means the record has a state a two-limb model has no room for: `unconfirmed` (E-08, E-09, the Partial state). A team that models this as consent-and-acknowledgement ships a store whose waivers are all defective in a way that is invisible in the product, invisible in QA, and visible only to a regulator. |
| DT-04 | The waiver is captured per order — one purchase, one consent, one record | The file's own [SURFACE] Behavior section ("it records: the buyer, the **order**…"), and the way every checkout in existence is shaped | ❌ REJECTED — **the waiver is per line item, because performance is per supply.** Art 16(m) attaches to *the supply of digital content*, not to a basket. A Producer buys three plugins in one cart and downloads one on a deadline day: performance began for that one. The other two are undelivered supplies whose withdrawal right is fully intact, and a store that treats the order as the atom will refuse those cancellations citing a waiver that never covered them — unlawfully, and with a written record proving it. The inverse is worse and more likely: one waiver row keyed to an order silently authorises delivery of every item on it, so the first download becomes the gate for all of them and items two and three ship with no consent at all. The nuance that makes it non-obvious is **E-11**: the atom is the *entitlement*, not the file. A pack is one supply, so fetching a single 5 MB loop from it begins performance of all 800 files — because 14.03.01's per-asset granularity is two payloads under one authorisation, not two contracts. So the rule is neither "per order" nor "per file": it is **per entitlement**, which is the object 14.02.01 already mints, and which is the only unit that tracks what was actually contracted for. |
| DT-05 | The gate should be shown until a waiver is captured — that's what a gate is | The obvious control-flow reading, and the shape of D-02 ("no waiver, no download") | ❌ REJECTED — **the gate has a shelf life, and the right expires on its own.** Art 9(2)(a): for digital content not on a tangible medium, the 14 days run **from the conclusion of the contract**, not from delivery. So a buyer who declines on day 1 and returns on day 15 has no withdrawal right left to waive — nothing was ever delivered, and the clock ran anyway. Continuing to ask them is not merely pointless: it *asserts* a right they no longer have, and a store that keeps demanding a waiver it does not need is manufacturing the appearance of one. This converts the decline path from a stand-off into a bounded wait (E-01), which is what makes D-09's "declining is not a dead end" true rather than aspirational. There is a hard conditional: **Art 10** extends the window to 12 months + 14 days when the trader failed to give the pre-contract withdrawal information — so the day-15 rule is only sound if checkout did its job, which makes an apparently unrelated checkout disclosure load-bearing for this gate's behaviour a year later (E-30). And it quietly rescues something bigger: without this rule, D-14's retention purge would delete a 2026 waiver and leave the 2036 delivery gate refusing a legitimately owned product forever (E-21) — the perpetual promise (14.08.05, 14.03.02 D-01) broken a decade out by a data-retention job, for a reason no one would ever trace back to here. |
| DT-06 | The gate must know whether the buyer is a business, because a business has no withdrawal right (index Q-03) | The index's own open question, and the standard e-commerce answer: a VAT-number field and a "buying as a business" checkbox | ❌ REJECTED as a branch — **the classification is not merely unmodelled, it is genuinely undecidable for this platform's central persona, and the cost is asymmetric so the branch should not exist.** "Consumer" is an objective status: a person acting outside their trade. Recital 17 CRD adds the dual-purpose test — where a contract's trade purpose is "so limited as not to be predominant in the overall context", the person is *still a consumer*. Now read personas.md: the defining structural fact of this market is the **multi-hyphenate** — "a drummer who also mixes, sits in three bands, engineers at a studio". A working musician buying a £50 compressor they will use on two paid sessions and forty personal demos is exactly the case Recital 17 was written for, and there is no answer, only a judgement made years later by someone who was not there. So a self-declaration field would collect an answer the buyer cannot reliably give and we cannot verify, and then *branch on it*. The asymmetry decides it: a waiver captured from a business buyer who never had the right is harmless surplus — at worst an agreed contract term; a waiver **skipped** for a "business" buyer who turns out to be a consumer is a void position on a delivered good, discovered in a dispute. Capture for everyone; never ask; never branch. This resolves the **gate's** half of index Q-03. The other half — modelling org-held entitlements (14.02 Q-03) — is untouched, and is a real problem for other reasons. |
| DT-07 | Trader status is a question about the buyer | Follows naturally from DT-06's framing and from index Q-03, which asks only about the buyer | ✅ CONFIRMED that it matters — **but about the *seller*, and that inversion is the one this domain actually turns on.** Consumer withdrawal rights run against a **trader**. Sell a used licence (14.06.02) from one musician to another and there is no trader in the transaction, no withdrawal right, and no waiver to capture — the gate must not fire. What a multi-vendor marketplace of *musicians* cannot avoid is that this determines an obligation on **us**, not on the vendor: Art 6a(1)(b)–(c) CRD (inserted by Directive (EU) 2019/2161, applicable since 28 May 2022) requires an online marketplace to tell the consumer, **before they are bound**, whether the third-party seller is a trader — on the basis of that party's declaration — and, where they are not, that consumer protection rights do not apply. That is a disclosure duty at the *buy button*, sourced from a declaration at the *vendor portal* (14.08.01), enforced by a gate at *download*: three features and a statutory duty that appears in none of them today. And it lands directly on domain Q-04 ("does a plugin-developer persona exist?"): the domain has no column for a commercial software vendor and 14.08 is designed for "a Musician with a folder" — which is precisely the population whose trader status is ambiguous, one-by-one, forever. The store cannot answer it, and per Art 6a it does not have to; it has to **ask, record, and disclose**. That is cheap, and its absence is a compliance defect with a fixed date attached rather than a design opinion. |
| DT-08 | The confirmation is an email; email is a solved problem | The natural reading of DT-03's third limb once you accept it | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary, ❌ **REJECTED as safe — and this is the failure mode nobody designs for.** The durable-medium confirmation is, in practice, an email. Every platform routes email through a notification system, and every notification system evaluates preferences. The domain's own cross-cut map lists **Notifications** as serving "All" domains, with a payload list that is entirely marketing-adjacent — update available, lease expiring, trial ending. Drop the waiver confirmation into that pipe and a buyer who unsubscribed from "store emails" in 2027 has every subsequent waiver **silently voided** (E-09): limb 3 never completes, the product shows a green tick, adjudication reads a valid waiver, and the store believes it holds a defence it lost to a preference toggle. There is no error, no bounce, no log line that looks wrong. The fix is one classification — the confirmation is **transactional and exempt from preference evaluation** (D-12) — and it is only findable if you have already accepted DT-03's third limb, which is why the two travel together. The general shape is worth naming because it will recur across this platform: **a marketing convenience quietly destroying a legal position, on a path where the failure is indistinguishable from success.** |
| DT-09 | The waiver is the evidence adjudication rests on — capture it and the record is complete (CX-01) | CX-01, stated exactly that way, and this file's own [SURFACE] Behavior section | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary, ❌ **REJECTED as sufficient — the waiver never extinguishes the right; the first byte does.** Art 16(m) opens with a precondition everyone skips: the exception applies *"if the performance has begun"* **and** the consent/acknowledgement/confirmation limbs are met. Consent alone does nothing (E-03) — a buyer who ticks the box and closes the tab keeps all 14 days, and a refusal citing "you agreed" would be unlawful and would be citing the wrong record. So the store's defence is a **pair**: the waiver row (this feature) plus proof that delivery began (14.03.01's per-grant transfer telemetry, D-08 there — which that file introduced precisely because "14.09.02 currently has no stated source for that fact"). Two features each holding half a defence, and neither file saying so. It has a second edge: "begun" is not "completed", so the 40%-and-abandoned case (E-28) *has* lost the withdrawal right — while gaining a stronger claim, failure to supply. The engineering corollary is the one that makes this cheap: since the enforceable fact is that **no waiver row means no grant means no bytes** (D-03), the ordering is **causal, not temporal**. There is no clock to trust, no skew to reason about, and no client timestamp to forge (E-27) — the sequence is guaranteed by the gate's existence rather than asserted by a field. CX-01's framing should be sharpened at Step 6 from "the waiver is the evidence" to "the waiver plus the delivery log are the evidence, and the waiver alone proves nothing". |
| DT-10 | The waiver record is immutable, therefore it is kept forever | The word "immutable" in this file's own [SURFACE] text, and the sibling principle that a library row never vanishes (14.03.03 D-04) | ❌ REJECTED — **immutable is not eternal, and nobody designs the deletion of a legal-evidence record.** Two obligations point in opposite directions and both are real. The record must outlive any claim arising from the sale — six years for a simple contract claim in England and Wales (Limitation Act 1980 s.5), five in Scotland, shorter in several Member States — so a 2-year retention is a store that loses every dispute from year three. Meanwhile GDPR's storage limitation says data is kept no longer than necessary, and an erasure request will arrive: the answer is **Art 17(3)(e)** — erasure does not apply where processing is necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims — which means the row **survives an erasure request** but only in a minimised form and only for a bounded period (D-14: 7 years, minimised to the principals, the entitlement, the frozen wording, the timestamp, the regime determination, the confirmation state, and the delivery-begun fact). Both failure modes are silent and opposite: purge early and the defence evaporates with no error; keep forever and "immutable evidence" is an unlawful retention wearing a virtue. And the collision has teeth beyond compliance — a naive purge would make the 2036 delivery gate refuse a legitimately owned product, which DT-05's shelf-life rule is the only thing preventing (E-21). |
| DT-11 | EU/UK is two regimes, so there are two wordings | The file's own title, and the reasonable assumption that "EU" is one jurisdiction for this purpose | ❌ REJECTED as stated, and the correction cuts both ways. **The substantive rule is not 27 regimes**: the CRD is a *full harmonisation* directive (Art 4) — Member States may not maintain diverging provisions — so the 14 days, the Art 16(m) exception, and the three limbs are the same in Dublin and Dortmund, and there is no 27-way branch on the *rule* to build. But Art 6(7) expressly reserves one thing to Member States: **language requirements** on contractual information, "so as to ensure that such information is easily understood by the consumer". And limb 2 is an *acknowledgement* — a sentence the buyer must be taken to have understood. An English sentence shown to a German consumer, whose habitual residence keeps its mandatory protections under Rome I Art 6 regardless of what our terms choose, is a weak acknowledgement and possibly no acknowledgement. So the artifact that must be localised is exactly the one that carries the legal weight, on a platform whose locked constraints (`meta/constraints.md`) mention no i18n at all and whose surface table declares one English web app. The decision this forces is a product one and it belongs to the owner (Q-04): either the waiver sentence is localised for each launch territory, or launch sales are limited to territories the wording is defensible in. Doing neither means selling into the EU with a waiver whose second limb is arguable in 20-odd languages — which is the most expensive way to discover it. |
