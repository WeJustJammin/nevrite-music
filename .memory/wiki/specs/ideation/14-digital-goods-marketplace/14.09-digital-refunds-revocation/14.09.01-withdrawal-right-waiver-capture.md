# Feature: Withdrawal-Right Waiver Capture (EU/UK)

> **Number**: 14.09.01
> **Parent**: [14.09 Digital Refunds, Withdrawal & Revocation](./14.09-digital-refunds-revocation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The consent moment that makes a digital sale final. EU/UK consumer law gives a buyer 14 days to
withdraw from a distance purchase for any reason — including a downloaded plugin — unless they
**expressly consent** to immediate delivery **and acknowledge** that doing so loses the right,
before delivery begins. Miss that moment and the store has no enforceable "no refunds" position in
its two most likely launch markets, whatever its policy says.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Consents before their first download of each purchase. Sees a single, plain sentence — not a checkbox on a PDF. The persona least likely to read anything, which is exactly why the wording has to carry the meaning in one line. |
| Producer | Full | Same. Higher purchase values mean a higher stake in having understood it, and a higher chance of contesting it later. |
| Operator | Full | **May not have the right at all** — a studio buying as a business sits outside consumer protection, so the waiver may be unnecessary and contract terms govern. The platform does not currently model business-vs-consumer buyers (14.02 Q-03), so it cannot tell. See index Q-03. |
| Fan | Read-only | The population consumer law exists to protect, arriving through the **shared delivery layer** on domain 20 music purchases (14.03.01). The gate must fire there too — and a fan buying a £8 album should not be walked through a legal ceremony designed for a £400 plugin. Different surface, different tone, same legal requirement. |

## Behavior

The waiver is captured at the point of first delivery, not at payment (DT-01), and gates the
download (14.03.01). It records: the buyer, the order, the exact wording shown, the timestamp, and
the fact that it preceded the first byte. That record is immutable and is the evidence adjudication
rests on (CX-01).

Validity has two limbs and both must be satisfied: **express consent** (an affirmative act — never
a pre-ticked box, never a buried clause) and **acknowledgement of the consequence** (the buyer is
told they lose the withdrawal right). A waiver that fails either limb is worthless, and the
statutory right survives intact.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer completes checkout; the entitlement is minted (14.02.01).
2. Buyer requests their first download.
3. The gate presents one plain sentence: immediate access now, and the 14-day cancellation right is lost.
4. Buyer affirmatively consents.
5. The waiver is recorded — wording, timestamp, order — immutably.
6. The download unlocks (14.03.01) and the first byte moves.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer declines the waiver | Entirely legitimate. They keep their 14-day right and simply cannot download yet. | "You can download once you accept immediate delivery, or cancel for a full refund within 14 days" — **not** a dead end |
| Buyer never downloads | No waiver, no delivery; a refund is a trivial cancellation with no clawback (CX-03) | Should be a distinct, simple path, not the full machinery |
| Download proceeds with no waiver captured | The store's refund position collapses for that order | Structurally impossible: no waiver, no URL (14.03.01 D-01) |
| Pre-ticked box or a clause in the T&Cs | Fails both limbs; the waiver is void and nobody notices until a regulator or a chargeback | The failure is silent, which is why the wording is a legal artifact, not copy |
| Buyer trials, then converts mid-trial (14.02.06 Q-03) | The download happened days ago under a *free* trial with no waiver; payment arrives afterwards | `[PENDING]` — the waiver moment is bypassed entirely. Genuinely unresolved |
| Operator buying as a business (index Q-03) | May be outside consumer protection; the waiver may be unnecessary | The platform cannot tell business from consumer today |
| Fan buys an £8 album (domain 20) | Same legal requirement, wildly different context | The gate must not import professional-tool ceremony onto the fan surface |
| Subscription/credit download (14.07.02) | Each credit-spend is a delivery of digital content | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — per-download waivers would be intolerable UX |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Download requested | Gate rendering |
| Empty | Purchased, never downloaded | Library row with a download control that opens the gate |
| Populated | Waiver captured | Download proceeds; the waiver is visible on the order record |

| Declined | Buyer said no | Withdrawal right retained; download withheld; refund available for 14 days |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The waiver is a checkout term — put it in the terms of sale and take it at payment | The obvious implementation, and where every store puts its legal text | ❌ REJECTED — **it must be captured at the moment of first delivery, and it must be its own affirmative act.** The Directive's exception (2011/83/EU Art. 16(m)) requires prior express consent *and* acknowledgement of the consequence, before performance begins. Payment is not performance; **download is**. So a waiver taken at checkout is premature — the buyer has not yet started receiving anything, and the right hasn't yet had anything to attach to. Worse, a checkout term buried among others fails the "express" limb: a pre-ticked box or a clause in the T&Cs is not express consent, and the whole waiver is void. What makes this structurally significant rather than a legal footnote: it puts a **consent gate on the delivery path** (14.03.01), which is why the download URL cannot be minted without it (14.03.01 D-01) and why this sub-domain exists rather than a refund button. It also means the trial→purchase flow (14.02.06 Q-03) quietly bypasses the moment entirely — the download happened days ago, for free, and payment arrives afterwards with no delivery left to gate. |
| DT-02 | Capture the waiver and the store's refund exposure is handled — that's what it's for | The point of the instrument; it is literally called a waiver | ❌ REJECTED — **the waiver removes a statutory floor; it does not decide what the store offers above it, and conflating the two is how "all sales final" gets adopted without anyone choosing it** (`#R-02`, index Q-01). A valid waiver means the buyer cannot *demand* a refund under withdrawal rights. It says nothing about whether granting one is good business. Steam waives the withdrawal right and then offers a famously generous 2-hour/14-day policy — and that policy is a large part of why people trust Steam with money. The plugin industry waives it and offers essentially nothing, which is why plugin buyers are trained to wait for demos, trawl forums, and never buy on impulse. Both are *legal*; they are different products. And the waiver is silent on the statutory right that actually bites: goods that are **faulty or not as described** (14.09.02 DT-01) — no waiver touches that, so a plugin that doesn't run on the rig we badged as compatible (14.01.04 DT-02) is refundable regardless of what the buyer clicked. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The waiver is captured at first **delivery**, not at payment, as its own affirmative act | Per DT-01. Payment is not performance; a checkout term fails the "express" limb and voids the waiver silently. |
| D-02 | No waiver, no download URL | Per 14.03.01 D-01. A delivery that outruns the gate destroys the store's refund position for that order. |
| D-03 | Declining the waiver is a supported path, not a dead end | The buyer keeps their statutory right and can still cancel. Punishing a lawful choice is both hostile and pointless. |
