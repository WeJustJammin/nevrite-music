# Feature: Licence Transfer Escrow & Conditional Settlement

> **Number**: 14.06.02
> **Parent**: [14.06 Used Licence Transfer & Resale](./14.06-used-licence-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Making a licence sale between two strangers safe. The problem is trust asymmetry with no natural
resolution: the seller will not surrender a licence before payment, the buyer will not pay for a
licence that might not transfer, and unlike a physical good there is no shipment to track and no
way to inspect what you bought before it is irrevocably yours. Escrow holds the money while the
platform executes the transfer atomically.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sells a plugin they've moved on from; buys one they couldn't otherwise afford. For a persona who "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products" (personas.md), the used market is genuinely how they acquire tools — this is the feature that matters most to them in the sub-domain. |
| Producer | Full | Both sides, at volume. Years of accumulated licences they no longer use; also the buyer with the judgement to know what a used licence is worth. |
| Operator | Full | Studio clear-outs — retiring a room's tools, usually alongside the hardware (14.06.03). The Operator's one substantial presence in domain 14. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. No licences, nothing to transfer. |

## Behavior

A transfer is a **change of holder on an existing entitlement** (index D-03), not a new sale. The
buyer inherits the seller's bound terms (14.02 CX-02), version range, and history — that
inheritance is the legal basis of the transaction, and minting a fresh entitlement would destroy
it.

Settlement is atomic and multi-part: funds captured, seller's entitlement stripped and their
machines deactivated (via 14.09.03's clawback, per `#R-02` — not a second mechanism), buyer's
holder record written, vendor fee paid if policy demands one (14.06.01), platform cut taken, funds
released to the seller. Any part failing must roll the whole thing back — a half-transferred
licence has two claimants or none.

Policy is re-checked at settlement (CX-01) because it can change while a listing sits.

### Happy Path

1. Owner lists a licence; the registry confirms it is transferable (CX-01).
2. Buyer offers; funds are captured into escrow, not to the seller.
3. Policy is re-checked at settlement.
4. Vendor approval is requested if the policy demands it (with a timeout — 14.06.01 Q-03).
5. Transfer executes atomically: seller's entitlement stripped and machines deactivated, buyer's holder record written, terms carried across.
6. Funds release: vendor fee, platform cut, remainder to the seller.
7. Buyer activates on their own machines (14.02.02); the licence's history is intact.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seller's machines are still activated at transfer | The buyer gets a licence with 0 free seats — technically theirs, practically useless | Deactivation is part of the atomic settlement, not a courtesy step |
| Seller's machine is offline and cannot be deactivated | Under fail-open (14.02.02 D-01), the seller's copy keeps working after they sold it | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the fail-open posture's cost, arriving here |
| Vendor approval never comes (14.06.01) | Escrow hangs with the buyer's money in it | Timeout → funds returned; the sale fails cleanly rather than silently |
| Vendor changes policy mid-escrow (CX-01) | Recommend: in-flight escrows complete under listing-time policy | Otherwise a vendor can cancel a stranger's sale after their money moved |
| Buyer refunds the used licence (14.09) | Does it bounce back to the original seller, who has already been paid and moved on? | `[PENDING]` — see Q-02; a three-party unwind with no clean answer |
| Seller sells the same licence twice concurrently | Two buyers, one entitlement | Hard lock on the entitlement during escrow — same class of race as 14.05.02 CX-01 |
| Licence is DRM-bridged (14.02.03) | We cannot move what PACE controls | Transfer refused at listing, not at settlement |
| Seller's entitlement is revoked mid-escrow (14.02.04) | Selling a licence under investigation | Escrow must abort; funds return |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|

| Settled | Transfer complete | Buyer's library gains the row with its history; seller's loses it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Licence escrow is like gear escrow (13) with no shipping — simpler, since nothing moves | The natural read: strip the physical leg from a used-gear sale and this is what's left | ❌ REJECTED — removing the shipment removes the **only thing that makes escrow work**, and the result is harder, not simpler. A used-gear escrow has a natural conditional: money releases when the box arrives and the buyer inspects it. There is a tracking number, a delivery event, an inspection window, and a return path if the amp is dead. A licence transfer has **no inspection window and no reverse gear** — the moment it lands it is irrevocably the buyer's, and if it doesn't work (vendor won't honour it, iLok won't accept it, seller's seats are stuck) there is nothing to send back and no event to condition on. So the release condition cannot be "buyer is satisfied"; it has to be "the transfer executed", which means the platform must *guarantee the execution* rather than merely witness it. That is a fundamentally different escrow — closer to a registrar than a stakeholder — and it is why D-14's "everything shared is already a cross-cut" reasoning holds here too: the payments rail is shared, the settlement semantics are not. |
| DT-02 | The seller has one thing to give: the licence | The two-party framing implied by "transfer", and by every marketplace's mental model | ❌ REJECTED — **there are three parties, and the one who isn't in the room decides everything.** The vendor sets policy (14.06.01), may charge a fee, may require approval, and may withdraw the right entirely mid-escrow. So a "used licence sale" is really a *tri-party novation*: the seller's contract with the vendor is extinguished and a new one created with the buyer, and only the vendor can authorise that. Which surfaces something no candidate mentions: the vendor is an **absent party with veto power over a transaction whose money is already in escrow**, and their silence is as damaging as their refusal (14.06.01 Q-03). Every failure mode here traces back to that structure — the hanging approval, the mid-escrow policy change, the dead vendor freezing their whole second-hand market (14.08.05). A two-party escrow model would specify none of it, and would discover all of it in production. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Settlement is atomic across funds, seller-stripping, deactivation, buyer-writing and vendor fee — any failure rolls back entirely | A half-transferred licence has two claimants or none. There is no partial state that is safe. |
| D-02 | The release condition is "the transfer executed", not "the buyer is satisfied" | Per DT-01 — there is no inspection window on a licence and no reverse gear. The platform must guarantee execution, not witness it. |
| D-03 | Vendor approval carries a hard timeout; on expiry the escrow fails and funds return | Per DT-02 — an absent third party with veto power must not be able to freeze a buyer's money indefinitely by saying nothing. |
