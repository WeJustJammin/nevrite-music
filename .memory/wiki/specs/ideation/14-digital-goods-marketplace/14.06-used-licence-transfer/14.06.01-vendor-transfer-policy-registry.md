# Feature: Vendor Transfer Policy Registry

> **Number**: 14.06.01
> **Parent**: [14.06 Used Licence Transfer & Resale](./14.06-used-licence-transfer-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The structured record of whether a given vendor's licence may be resold, and under what
conditions. Every vendor has a different answer — some permit transfer freely, some charge a fee,
some require their own approval, some impose a cooldown after purchase, and many forbid it
outright — and today that answer lives in a paragraph of an EULA nobody reads. Without this
registry a used-licence market cannot exist; with it, it can exist exactly as far as vendors allow.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only · Config for own products | Checks "can I sell this?" before bothering to list — and, more usefully, "is this used listing real?" before buying one. As a vendor of their own packs, sets a policy, though creator content is rarely resold (index Q-01). |
| Producer | Config as vendor · Read-only as buyer | As a vendor of commercial software, this is their control surface over the second-hand market that competes with their new sales. As a buyer, the persona most likely to have a library worth reselling — years of accumulated tools they've moved on from. |
| Operator | Read-only | Studio clear-outs. Needs to know which of a room's retired tools have resale value *before* pricing the sale — a non-transferable licence is worth £0 and changes what the hardware is worth (14.06.03). |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. No software licences, nothing to transfer. |

## Behavior

Each product carries a structured transfer policy — expressed through the terms registry
(14.02.05), since a transfer right is a licence term, not a separate vocabulary. The clauses that
actually vary in this market:

- transferable / not transferable
- transfer fee (vendor-charged, and to which party)
- cooldown after original purchase (blocks flipping)
- one-time-only vs unlimited transfers
- vendor approval required per transfer
- territory constraints (see index Q-03 — EU law may override)
- transfers only with hardware (OEM/bundled, CX-03)

The registry gates every transfer at listing and again at settlement (CX-01), because policy can
change while a used licence sits listed.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor sets a transfer policy at submission (14.08.01), from templates.
2. Policy publishes with the product and renders on the listing — *before* purchase, so a new buyer knows the resale value of what they're buying.
3. An owner opens their library (14.03.03); each row shows its transfer state plainly.
4. Owner lists a transferable licence; the registry is checked and the listing is allowed.
5. A buyer opens the used listing and sees the policy's conditions (fee, approval, cooldown).
6. At settlement the policy is re-checked (CX-01); the escrow (14.06.02) proceeds.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor changes policy to non-transferable while a licence is listed | The listing is dead; the seller must hear it from us | Notification; recommend in-flight escrows complete under the listing-time policy (CX-01) |
| Vendor requires per-transfer approval | The transfer has a human in the loop who may never answer | Escrow must have a timeout; funds cannot hang indefinitely on a vendor's silence |
| DRM-bridged product (14.02.03) | Transfer is PACE's mechanism, not ours — we cannot move an iLok licence | "Managed in iLok — transfer through their system" |
| Vendor has exited (14.08.05) | **DQ-MG-03:** the approval requirement stands and transfers freeze — see `14.08.05` D-05. The registry stores a policy, not an evaluator: there is no record shape for "objective eligibility rules", no defined evaluator and no appeal path, so auto-approving on the vendor's recorded terms would commission a new mechanism rather than apply an existing one. | "Transfers frozen — this policy required the vendor's approval" |
| Policy says transferable; EU *UsedSoft* says transferable regardless | Aligned — no conflict | — |
| Policy says **non**-transferable; EU *UsedSoft* may say otherwise | The registry's "vendor decides" model may be legally wrong in the EU | `[PENDING — /create-prd]` — index Q-03 |
| Vendor sets a transfer fee exceeding the licence's used value | Transfer is nominally permitted, economically dead | Honest display of the total cost — a permitted-but-pointless transfer is a "no" dressed as a "yes" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing/library fetch | Policy row skeleton |
| Empty | Vendor hasn't set a policy | Publish gate — an unstated policy is a dispute waiting to happen; default silently to non-transferable and buyers are misled |
| Populated | Policy set | Plain-language statement on the listing and the library row |
| Error | Fetch failed | Fail closed: transfer blocked rather than assumed permitted — an unverifiable transfer must not settle |
| Partial | Bridged product; policy is the third party's | "Transfer handled outside WeJammin" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Building the registry unlocks the used-licence market — the domain index says transfer is "technically impossible without" it | The domain index's own framing, which presents the registry as the missing key | ❌ REJECTED — the registry is **necessary but nowhere near sufficient, and the index's framing hides the real blocker.** It is a faithful record of vendors' answers, and the answer from every vendor worth reselling is *no*. Waves, Native Instruments and iZotope allow transfer through their own systems, with their own fees, precisely so they control it — they have no reason to grant a new store a policy they administer themselves. The vendors who *would* permit free transfer are the ones whose products are too cheap to resell. So the registry perfectly enumerates a market that is empty at both ends. The honest reframing: the registry's value is not unlocking resale but **making the resale question answerable at the point of the original purchase** — "this £400 plugin is non-transferable; this one isn't" is real consumer information that no store surfaces today, and it is worth building for *that* even if the second-hand market never materialises. That is a different feature justification than the index's, and it survives Q-01's doubt. |
| DT-02 | Transfer policy is a vendor's decision to make | The framing of the whole sub-domain, and the plain reading of a EULA | ⏳ DEFERRED — escalated to index Q-03, because it may be **legally false in the EU**. *UsedSoft v Oracle* (CJEU, C-128/11) held that the distribution right in a software copy is exhausted on first sale, and that a perpetual licence sold for a one-off fee is functionally a sale — so the rightsholder cannot oppose resale, and a contractual ban is unenforceable. If that reasoning applies here, the registry's core model ("the vendor decides") is wrong in a major territory, and the correct model is *territory-aware*: vendor policy governs where it lawfully can, and an EU perpetual licence carries a transfer right the vendor cannot remove. That is a materially different feature — and it interacts with 14.09.01's withdrawal-waiver logic, since both hinge on whether a "licence" is legally a sale. This needs a lawyer, not an agent, and it should be asked before the registry is designed rather than after. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Transfer policy is expressed through the terms registry (14.02.05), not a parallel vocabulary | A transfer right is a licence term. A second system means a transferred licence's terms and its transfer right live in different places and can contradict. |
| D-02 | Policy is stated on the *original* listing, before the first purchase | Per DT-01 — the resale question is consumer information at the point of buying new. This is the registry's most defensible value and it does not depend on the second-hand market existing. |
| D-03 | A policy lookup failure fails closed — transfer blocked, not assumed permitted | An unverifiable transfer that settles hands the buyer a licence the vendor may not honour. |
