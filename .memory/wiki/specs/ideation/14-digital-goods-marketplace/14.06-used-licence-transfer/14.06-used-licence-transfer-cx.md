## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Policy Registry ↔ Escrow

**Relationship**: Every transfer is gated on a policy lookup: is this vendor's licence
transferable, under what conditions (fee, cooldown, one-time-only, vendor approval required), and
in what territory? The escrow may not settle a transfer the policy forbids — it would broker a
void transaction and hand the buyer a licence the vendor will not honour.

The subtlety: policy is checked at **listing** and again at **settlement**. A vendor can change
policy while a used licence sits listed, and the buyer must not discover it after paying.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees "transferable" or "not transferable" on their own library rows before they bother listing.
- **Producer**: as a vendor, sets policy on their own products — mostly moot, since creator content is rarely resold (index Q-01).
- **Operator**: studio clear-outs; needs to know what's sellable before valuing the sale.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Policy is vendor-owned. The escrow reads it and never writes. But a policy change mid-listing creates a real conflict — see 5.
2. **Trigger chain**: List → policy check → offer allowed. Settle → policy re-check → transfer executes. If the re-check fails, funds must return, not hang.
3. **Permission intersection**: Direct and total. Vendor policy determines whether the seller — who *owns* the licence — may sell it at all. That is unusual: ownership without the right to alienate.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A policy change to non-transferable must notify anyone with that product listed. Their listing is now dead and they should hear it from us.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Yes** — vendor sets non-transferable while an escrow is in flight. Recommend: in-flight escrows complete under the policy in force at listing; new listings are blocked. Otherwise a vendor can cancel a stranger's sale after their money moved.

### CX-02: Hardware Bundle ↔ Escrow

**Relationship**: The bundle's difficulty is that one transaction has two settlements with
different physics. The licence transfers instantly and irreversibly; the hardware ships, is
inspected, and may be returned. Settling the licence on payment means the buyer can refund the
hardware and keep the licence; settling on delivery means the seller's licence is stranded in
escrow while a box crosses a continent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the common seller — an interface with its bundled software.
- **Operator**: studio clear-outs, at volume; hardware and licences retired together.
- **Producer**: same as Musician.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The physical listing lives in domain 13; the entitlement in 14.02.01. Two owners, one transaction — a genuine coordination problem, not a modelling artifact.
2. **Trigger chain**: Hardware sale → licence transfer. **What if the hardware is returned after the licence moved?** The licence must claw back (14.09.03) — and the buyer may already have activated it. Unresolved.
3. **Permission intersection**: The hardware's buyer must be eligible to hold the licence — which may require a vendor account they don't have (14.02.03's iLok problem, arriving in a used sale).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both parties, at both settlements. A silent half-completion here is a dispute.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Yes, and it is the sub-domain's sharpest.** Hardware return racing licence activation. See 14.06.03 DT-01.

### CX-03: Hardware Bundle ↔ Policy Registry

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. OEM/bundled licences ("lite" editions shipped with
interfaces) frequently carry different — usually stricter — transfer terms than the same vendor's
retail licences. Some are explicitly tied to the hardware unit and transfer *only* with it, which
is the one case where bundling helps rather than hurts.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.06.01 Policy Registry | 14.06.03 Hardware Bundle | As a *listing-time* relationship: rejected in favour of CX-03's narrower claim. The registry does not know or care that a licence is being sold alongside hardware — it answers "is this transferable", full stop. The bundle's special policy handling (OEM terms) is a property of the *licence*, recorded in the registry like any other, not a bundle-specific behaviour. Modelling a distinct edge would imply the registry has bundle-awareness it should not have. |
| R-02 | 14.06.02 Escrow | 14.02.02 Activation & Seats | Tempting — a transfer surely must deactivate the seller's machines. But that is 14.09.03's clawback mechanism, already specified, triggered here rather than reinvented. A direct edge would duplicate the revocation path and create a second way to strip an entitlement, which is exactly the kind of divergence that leaves a seller's copy quietly running after they sold it. |
