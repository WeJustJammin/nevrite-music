# Feature: Vendor Onboarding & Product Submission

> **Number**: 14.08.01

> **Status**: [SURFACE]

## Overview

How someone becomes a seller here and gets a product listed — identity and payout setup, then the
submission flow that produces a listing (14.01.01), a compatibility matrix (14.01.02), a
dependency declaration (14.01.03), licence terms (14.02.05), a transfer policy (14.06.01), a
monetisation model (14.07), and the attestations that make all of it accountable. It is the
narrowest point in the domain: everything the store sells passes through here, and every
assurance the store offers is either captured here or invented later.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The realistic vendor** (14.01.01 DT-02) — a working musician with a folder of samples, presets or beats. Not a software company. The flow must be completable by someone who has never written an EULA, doesn't know what a support matrix is, and is doing this between sessions. Every required field is a chance for them to abandon. |
| Producer | Full | The archetypal vendor: packs, presets, templates, beats. Also the only persona plausibly shipping *executable* code, and therefore the only one who meets 14.08.02's gate. |
| Operator | Config | A studio selling its own templates or room presets — real but rare. Needs org-level vendor identity (14.02 Q-03), which is unresolved. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. Fans do not sell digital goods; a fan selling music is domain 20's artist-side, not this. |

## Behavior

Two phases. **Onboarding** establishes a vendor identity: who they are, KYC where payouts demand
it (a cross-cut), tax status, payout destination, and — per CX-05 — the continuity terms that bind
them at exit (14.08.05). **Submission** produces a product: upload, structured metadata,
type-conditional required sections, attestations, and a QA gate (CX-01/CX-02) before publish.

The attestations are this feature's most consequential output and the domain's thinnest defence
(DT-01): the vendor asserts they hold the rights they are granting (14.02.05 DT-03), and declares
AI-generated content (sub-domain D-04 — a platform cross-cut, captured here).

### Happy Path

1. Musician signs up as a vendor; identity, tax and payout details are captured via the payouts cross-cut.
2. They agree the vendor terms, including continuity obligations (CX-05).
3. They start a submission and pick a product type; the form renders only what applies to that type.
4. They upload the artifact; QA/QC runs async (CX-01/CX-02).
5. They complete the structured sections: metadata, terms from templates, transfer policy, pricing.
6. They make the rights attestation and the AI declaration.
7. QA passes; the listing publishes into the catalog and, for software, into a release channel (14.08.04).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor abandons mid-submission | The realistic outcome for a musician facing 9 required sections between sessions | Draft persists; progressive completion, not a wall |
| Vendor doesn't know what to put in "licence terms" | Almost universal — they are a musician, not a lawyer | Templates with plain-language choices (14.02.05 D-01), never a text box |
| QA fails (CX-01) | Publish blocked | Actionable reason, not a verdict — "your VST3 isn't signed" not "rejected" |
| Vendor attests rights they don't hold | **The domain's central exposure** (14.04.01 DT-02). The platform cannot verify an upstream chain. | Attestation makes it attributable, not prevented — see DT-01 |
| Executable submitted by an unknown new vendor | Anyone can ship code that runs unsandboxed in a professional's DAW | Sub-domain Q-01 — the gate that has no agreed answer |
| Vendor is an org (studio, label) | Vendor identity is personal today | `[PENDING — /create-prd-architecture]` — 14.02 Q-03 |
| Vendor's payout/KYC fails after products are published | Products live, money undeliverable | Publish should gate on payout readiness, not the reverse |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An attestation is a checkbox — the vendor ticks "I own the rights", we're covered | The implicit model behind candidate 24 and every marketplace's terms of service | ❌ REJECTED — **an unattributable checkbox transfers no risk and prevents nothing; an attributable attestation is a different product.** The store's exposure (14.04.01 DT-02) is a vendor granting rights they never held, fanned out to hundreds of buyers with a WeJammin receipt vouching for each. A ticked box in a terms flow is worth nothing at that point — nobody can say who ticked it, against which specific claim, on what date, or what they were shown. What has value is an attestation that is **specific** (this pack, this claim: "no uncleared third-party recordings"), **attributed** (to a verified identity from onboarding, not an anonymous account), **timestamped**, and **durable** (it survives into the buyer's clearance chain, 14.02.05). That is the difference between a disclaimer and evidence — and evidence is the one thing this platform is structurally good at producing (D-18). The uncomfortable corollary: **it does not stop the harm.** It makes it traceable to a person and gives 400 exposed buyers something to point at. That is worth building and worth being honest about. |
| DT-02 | Vendor onboarding should be frictionless — the store needs supply, so make listing easy | The obvious marketplace growth instinct, and correct for most marketplaces | ❌ REJECTED as a single policy, because **this store has two supply types with opposite risk profiles and one gate cannot serve both.** Listing a *sample pack* is low-risk to the buyer's machine and high-risk to their *rights* — the harm is an uncleared break that surfaces years later in a released record. Listing a *plugin* is the reverse: an executable that runs unsandboxed inside a professional's DAW with full access to their machine and their client's session, where the harm is immediate and total. Frictionless onboarding for content is right and necessary — the realistic supply is a musician with a folder (14.01.01 DT-02) and every extra field loses vendors the store cannot afford to lose. Frictionless onboarding for **executables** is how a store becomes a malware vector with its own name on the receipt, and no plugin marketplace does it. So the answer is almost certainly *two* paths — open for audio, gated for code (sub-domain Q-01) — and treating "vendor onboarding" as one policy is how that decision gets made by default instead of on purpose. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attestations are specific, attributed, timestamped and durable — never an anonymous checkbox | Per DT-01. An unattributable tick transfers no risk and gives exposed buyers nothing to point at. |
| D-02 | Required sections are type-conditional; a WAV pack never sees a support matrix | Per 14.01.01 D-01 and CX-02. Every irrelevant required field costs a vendor the store cannot afford to lose. |
| D-03 | Licence terms are authored from templates, never a free-text box | Per 14.02.05 D-01 — vendors are musicians, not lawyers. A text box produces copy-pasted EULAs that bind nobody and cannot propagate. |
| D-04 | Publish gates on payout readiness | Live products with undeliverable money is a vendor-trust failure with no clean recovery. |
