# Feature: Vendor Onboarding & Product Submission

> **Number**: 14.08.01
> **Parent**: [14.08 Vendor Portal, Build Submission & QA](./14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How someone becomes a seller here and gets a product listed — identity and payout setup, then the
submission flow that produces a listing (14.01.01), a compatibility matrix (14.01.02), a
dependency declaration (14.01.03), licence terms (14.02.05), a transfer policy (14.06.01), a
monetisation model (14.07), and the attestations that make all of it accountable. It is the
narrowest point in the domain: everything the store sells passes through here, and every
assurance the store offers is either captured here or invented later.

This feature owns the **flow and the attestations**. It does not own the objects the flow
produces: the listing's field schema is 14.01.01's, the clause vocabulary is 14.02.05's, the
transfer vocabulary is 14.06.01's, the models are 14.07's, and the checks are 14.08.02's and
14.08.03's. What is *only* here — and exists nowhere else on the platform — is the moment a
vendor states where their material came from and what they are granting, in a form that survives
into a stranger's clearance chain years later.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The realistic vendor** (14.01.01 DT-02) — a working musician with a folder of samples, presets or beats. Not a software company. Meets tier 1 and tier 2 of the gate (D-05) and never tier 3's executable path. Never sees a support matrix, a notarisation error, or a host-dependency field on a `sample-pack` (D-02). The persona the **provenance declaration** (D-06) is written for: they can answer "where did this come from" truthfully and cannot answer "do you hold the rights" at all (DT-03). The persona who abandons — every required field is a chance to lose them, which is why identity is asked at publish and not at signup (DT-06). |
| Producer | Full | The archetypal vendor: packs, presets, templates, beats. The only persona plausibly shipping *executable* code, and therefore the only one who meets 14.08.02's gate. Also the multi-hyphenate collision case: submitting a pack while holding an entitlement to someone else's pack whose loops are *in* it (D-07, edge case 12), and while being a credited contributor on a third listing (14.10.03). Their vendor lens and buyer lens are one surface with a `View as buyer` toggle (14.01.01 D-09) — never a role picker. |
| Operator | Config | A studio selling its own templates or room presets — real but rare. The **org vendor** case: the studio, not the engineer who clicked, must be the attesting party, or the studio's catalog dies with an employee's account. Both branches of that are specified (edge case 14); the ruling is 14.02 Q-03. Their engineer-level authority question (who may sign an attestation that binds the studio?) is **Q-05** and is genuinely new. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface, and structurally so. Fans do not sell digital goods; a fan selling music is domain 20's artist-side, not this. Per personas.md the Fan "will never file a split sheet" — an attestation is a heavier professional artifact than a split sheet, and rendering a provenance questionnaire to a fan would be exactly the professional-tool complexity they will not tolerate. |

## Behavior

Two phases, and the ordering between them is the feature's central design decision (DT-06).
**Onboarding** establishes a vendor identity. **Submission** produces a product. The `[SURFACE]`
draft assumed onboarding completes before submission begins; it does not, and building it that
way loses the store its supply (D-05).

### The three-tier gate (D-05)

The cost of each tier is paid at the moment its benefit becomes obvious, never before.

| Tier | What is asked | What it unlocks | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Platform account** | Nothing new. Every user has one (domain 01). | Browsing, drafting is *visible* | Already authenticated ⇒ **every upload is attributable to a person from byte one**. Nothing in this flow is ever anonymous, which is what makes deferring tier 3 safe. |
| **2 — Vendor terms** | One screen: vendor terms **including continuity obligations** (CX-05). Plain language: "if you stop selling, buyers keep what they bought." | Saving a draft, uploading, QA | Cheap, fast, and must precede the first *sale* — not the first draft. CX-05's leverage requirement is satisfied by agreement before any buyer exists. |
| **3 — Payout readiness** | KYC, tax status, payout destination — via the Payouts/Escrow/KYC cross-cut. | **Publish** | D-04. The expensive, abandonment-prone ask lands *after* the vendor has invested and when the reason is self-evident: we need somewhere to send the money. |

Tier 3 gates publish, never drafting (D-04, D-05). A vendor with a finished, QA-passed listing and
incomplete KYC sees one named blocker with one path to clear it — not a wall they hit before
writing a word.

### Submission

Type first, and final once published (14.01.01 D-03). The five types — `plugin`, `sample-pack`,
`preset-pack`, `template`, `beat` — determine which sections are required (D-02); a WAV pack never
sees a support matrix. Upload runs QA/QC async (CX-01/CX-02) while the vendor keeps filling in
sections; the upload never blocks the form. Terms come from templates (D-03), never a text box.

### The attestations

This feature's most consequential output and the domain's thinnest defence (DT-01). Two
declarations, orthogonal, and conflating them destroys both (DT-05):

**1. The provenance declaration** (D-06) — product-level, not per-file (consistent with 14.02.05
D-07 and DT-11: a per-file matrix produces false precision). The vendor declares which source
categories are **present**, and names sources for the risky ones:

| Category | Vendor declares | Follow-up | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `original-recording` | I recorded, performed or programmed this | none | Low |
| `commissioned` | I paid someone to make it and hold the rights | who (optional) | Low |
| `licensed-third-party` | From a library or pack I licensed | **which** — required | Medium; **machine-checkable when the source is WeJammin (D-07)** |
| `contains-third-party-recording` | Contains material from a commercial recording | which; cleared? by whom? | **The domain's central exposure** (14.04.01 DT-02) |
| `public-domain-or-cc` | PD or Creative Commons material | which licence — required | Medium |
| `generative-model-output` | A model produced audio present in this product | routes to the AI declaration | Disclosure, not rights |

**2. The AI declaration** (D-08) — enumerated uses, never one binary question. Only *generative*
output propagates a disclosure obligation into the buyer's release (14.02.05 D-12).

> **Stem separation is a provenance question, not an AI question** (DT-05). Separating a
> commercial record to lift a break is the most common uncleared-sample vector in the market. It
> is performed by an "AI tool", so a single AI question captures it — and captures it in the box
> that propagates a *disclosure* obligation rather than the one that flags a *rights* problem.
> It belongs in `contains-third-party-recording`. Getting this wrong files the store's biggest
> risk under its smallest one.

### The contradiction gate (D-09)

The platform cannot verify an upstream chain (DT-01, 14.02.05 DT-09). It **can** detect a record
that contradicts itself, and that requires no verification at all:

| Contradiction | Blocked because |
|---|---|
| `contains-third-party-recording` + `cleared: no`, with `commercial_use: permitted` / `royalty_basis: royalty-free` | The vendor has declared they cannot grant what they are selling. Publishing it is publishing a known-false record. |
| Custom clause contradicts a structured clause | 14.02.05 D-13 — this is the gate that rejection lives at. |
| Declared source is a WeJammin product whose terms forbid `resale_in_isolation` | D-07 — the one class the platform genuinely verifies. |
| Declared format absent from the binary | 14.08.02 / 14.01.01 Q-01. |

Blocking a self-contradictory record is not the platform adjudicating rights. It is the platform
declining to publish a statement whose own author says is false — and the honest vendor gets
stopped and told exactly why, which is the entire value.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens a listing draft from anywhere in the store. They already have a platform account (01); **no vendor signup wall, nothing asked yet** (D-05 tier 1).
2. First draft save prompts one screen: vendor terms including continuity obligations (CX-05, D-05 tier 2). Plain language, ~5 clauses, not a KYC flow.
3. They pick a product type — `sample-pack`. Type is final once published (14.01.01 D-03). The form renders only that type's required sections (D-02): 9 sections, not the plugin path's 12.
4. They upload the artifact. QC starts async (CX-02) and returns progressive per-file results; the form stays usable throughout.
5. They complete the structured sections: listing metadata and ≥1 `contents-only` demo (14.01.01 D-06), licence terms from a template (14.02.05 D-01), transfer policy (14.06.01), monetisation model and price (14.07).
6. They complete the **provenance declaration** (D-06). They tick `original-recording` and `licensed-third-party`, naming a WeJammin pack they bought. The platform resolves it against their entitlement and that pack's terms automatically (D-07) — `resale_in_isolation: forbidden` would block; the loops are used *inside* new material, so `derivative_use: permitted` resolves clean. Zero vendor effort.
7. They complete the **AI declaration** (D-08). No generative output; they used a denoiser, which is declared and **does not** propagate (14.02.05 D-12).
8. They sign the attestation. It binds: this product, these specific declared claims, their platform identity backed by KYC legal identity (D-11), timestamped, immutable (D-10).
9. **Publish gate**: payout readiness (D-04, tier 3). KYC/tax/payout are requested now — after the investment, with the reason self-evident. They complete it.
10. QC returns pass; payout ready → publish. The listing enters the catalog. The attestation is now durable evidence in every buyer's clearance chain (14.02.05).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | Vendor abandons mid-submission | The realistic outcome for a musician working between sessions (14.01.01 DT-02). Draft persists **per-section as completed**, never as one submit (14.01.01). Progressive completion, not a wall. | On return: "Draft restored — 6 of 9 required sections complete." Per-section checklist. |
| 2 | **Draft outlives the schema** (DT-09) | Vendor returns after 8 months; the required-sections schema gained a field, or their licence template was deprecated. A draft is validated against the **current** schema at resume, never at save, and **never publishes against a schema it was not validated against**. Still-valid sections stay filled; changed sections are marked stale and re-presented. A deprecated template is never silently bound — binding it would freeze the vendor to a legal position the platform retracted (14.02.05 D-02 makes it immutable afterwards). | "Some things changed while you were away — 2 sections need another look." Named, not a reset. Never a silent republish of stale terms. |
| 3 | **Abandoned draft with a 4 GB artifact** | Pure storage cost, zero revenue — 14.07.01 DT-01's liability arriving before a sale exists. Structured sections are kept **indefinitely** (kilobytes, and they are the vendor's work). The **artifact** is deleted after inactivity, notified at T-30 and T-7. Recommendation: **180 days** — a musician's gap between sessions is months, and a season is the honest unit. The number is a cost call; the behaviour is not. | T-30: "Your draft's upload will be removed on [date] — publish or re-upload later. Your text stays." After: draft intact, artifact slot empty, re-upload in one click. |
| 4 | Vendor doesn't know what to put in "licence terms" | Almost universal — they are a musician, not a lawyer. | Templates with plain-language choices (14.02.05 D-01), never a text box (D-03). |
| 5 | QA/QC fails (CX-01/CX-02) | Publish blocked. Audio findings are **dismissible warnings** by default (14.08.03 D-01); executable findings are **unconditional blockers** (14.08.02 D-01). The two gates have opposite postures and the vendor must never see one wearing the other's copy. | Actionable reason, never a verdict — "your VST3 isn't notarised" not "rejected". |
| 6 | **Vendor attests rights they don't hold** | **The domain's central exposure** (14.04.01 DT-02). The platform cannot verify an upstream chain, and fingerprinting is the wrong answer (14.02.05 DT-09). What changes under D-06: they are no longer attesting a *legal conclusion* they cannot evaluate — they are stating **facts about sources**. "This is all my own recording" is a false statement of fact, not an honest mistake of law (DT-03). Attestation makes it attributable and enumerable (14.02.05 edge case 4's blast radius), never prevented. | On a later claim: named, specific, dated. The remedy — indemnity, insurance, bond, or accepted exposure — is **14.02.05 Q-03**, not resolved here. |
| 7 | **Vendor's declared source is a WeJammin product** (D-07) | The one class the platform genuinely verifies, with no false positives and no false negatives: it holds both the entitlement and the terms. Checks `resale_in_isolation` (forbidden by default, 14.02.05) and `derivative_use`. Loops used *inside* new material → clean. Loops **resold as loops** → blocked. This is a records check on the platform's own data, not detection. | Clean: nothing, it resolves silently. Blocked: "Pack Y's licence doesn't allow reselling its loops as loops. You can use them *in* your own material — not repackage them." Names the pack, the clause, and their entitlement. |
| 8 | **Vendor declares uncleared third-party recording, then grants royalty-free commercial use** | Self-contradiction (D-09). Publish blocked — not a rights judgment, a refusal to publish a record whose own author says is false. | "You've said this contains an uncleared recording, and you're selling it as royalty-free for commercial use. Those can't both be true. Fix one." |
| 9 | **Vendor answers the AI question wrong, honestly** (DT-05) | A single "is this AI-generated?" is unanswerable in 2026 — generative audio, stem separation, drum replacement, pitch correction and denoising are all "AI" and have nothing in common. D-08's enumerated uses are each answerable; the category is not. | Specific questions with specific consequences: "Did a model produce audio in this product?" (propagates). "Did you use AI processing — denoise, stem separation, drum replacement?" (does not propagate; stem separation routes to provenance). |
| 10 | **Vendor used stem separation on a commercial record** | Filed under provenance, **not** AI (DT-05). It is a rights event wearing an AI costume. Routes to `contains-third-party-recording` → edge case 8's gate. | "Separating a released record makes this a third-party recording, whatever tool you used. Which record?" |
| 11 | **Executable submitted by an unknown new vendor** | Anyone can ship code that runs unsandboxed in a professional's DAW. The gate that has no agreed answer. | **Q-01** — and 14.08.02 Q-01 offers the cleanest resolution: decline executables at launch entirely. The realistic inventory is content anyway (14.01.01 DT-02). |
| 12 | **Multi-hyphenate: vendor submits a pack containing loops from a pack they bought here** | Extremely common and completely legitimate — this is what sample libraries are *for*. Resolved by D-07 automatically (edge case 7). The vendor does not have to know their own licence's clauses; the platform already does. | Nothing, when clean. This is the "lazy path is the correct path" requirement (personas.md, Producer) applied to submission. |
| 13 | **Vendor submits their own product and later buys it** | Blocked — self-purchase games sales rank and is a laundering surface (14.01.01 D-09). Their own placement mints a zero-price self-entitlement instead (14.02.05 D-16). | No buy control. "You're the vendor — use *View as buyer*." |
| 14 | **Vendor is an org (studio, label)** | Vendor identity is personal today; the ruling is **14.02 Q-03** and is not this feature's to make. Both branches are specifiable and both are specified. **If entitlements/identities can bind an org**: the attesting party is the **org**, an authorised member signs *on its behalf*, the attestation records both (org as principal, member as signatory), payout binds the org's KYC, and the catalog survives the member leaving. **If they cannot**: every studio's catalog is owned by whichever engineer clicked, dies with their account, and the store must say so at tier 2 rather than discover it at exit — an Operator publishing under a personal identity has been mis-sold the feature. | Org-capable: "Publishing as [Studio]. You're signing on its behalf." Not org-capable: tier 2 states plainly "vendor accounts are personal — this catalog will belong to you, not [Studio]." Never silent. |
| 15 | **Vendor's payout/KYC fails after products are published** | Publish gates on payout readiness (D-04), but readiness **decays** — documents expire, addresses change, sanctions re-screens fire years later. The listing auto-pauses: out of the catalog for new buyers, **fully live for existing owners** (14.01.01) — downloads, versions, terms untouched. Money owed is held, not lost. | Vendor: "Your listing is paused — your payout account needs attention." Buyer: not in catalog. Owner: unaffected. |
| 16 | **Stage name ≠ legal name** (DT-07) | The norm in music, not an edge case: a drummer trading as "Kaidо" has a passport saying something else. KYC returns the legal name; the store must display the professional one. The attestation is **attributed to the platform identity, backed by the KYC-verified legal identity, and the legal identity is never displayed** (D-11). Both are needed — legal makes it enforceable, professional makes it meaningful to a buyer. A KYC/display mismatch is **expected**, never a fraud signal. | Vendor: "The name on your payout account doesn't need to match your artist name." Buyer: the artist name and the verified credits (14.01.01 D-07). Never a legal name (`security-first`: PII does not leak into a storefront). |
| 17 | **Vendor is a minor** (DT-10) | A 15-year-old beatmaker is a completely realistic vendor — the beat market is full of them — and cannot contract, cannot KYC, and cannot be paid. Tier 1 and 2 are reachable; tier 3 is structurally impossible. Silently letting them build a listing they can never publish is the cruellest version of this. Tier 2 must state the age requirement **before** the work, not at the publish gate. | At tier 2: "You'll need to be [age] to get paid here." Not at step 9, after three hours of work. The age, the guardian/managed-account path, and whether one exists at all: **Q-04**. |
| 18 | **Sanctioned or embargoed jurisdiction** | KYC screening fails at tier 3. Same shape as 17: the vendor has already invested. Unlike 17, the platform cannot know before screening. | Named at the publish gate with the reason stated to the extent legally permitted, and the draft preserved — a screening outcome can change. |
| 19 | **Vendor resubmits while QA/QC is in flight** | The later artifact wins; the earlier verdict **must not publish a superseded build** (CX-01 q5). | "New upload — re-checking. Your previous results no longer apply." |
| 20 | **Two org members submit the same product concurrently** | Only live if 14.02 Q-03 permits org vendors (edge case 14). Drafts are per-listing, not per-member; last write wins **per section**, with the section showing who changed it. No lock — a lock on a 9-section form held by an absent colleague is worse than a conflict. | "[Member] updated the pricing section 2 minutes ago." |
| 21 | **Vendor edits the attestation after publish** | Rejected. The attestation is **versioned and immutable once bound** (D-10), exactly as terms are (14.02.05 D-02) and for the same reason: it is a buyer's evidence, and an editable attestation is not evidence. A corrected claim is a **new attestation version appended**, timestamped, with the prior one preserved. Buyers bound to the old one are notified where the change is material (a claim withdrawn). | Vendor: "You can't edit a signed attestation. Add a correction — buyers who bought under the old one will be told." |
| 22 | **Vendor withdraws a claim retroactively** ("actually that break wasn't mine") | The good outcome, and the flow must not punish it — a vendor who self-reports is doing the platform's job for it. Appends a correcting version (21), triggers 14.02.05 edge case 4's blast-radius enumeration, and routes to 24. **Must never be harder than staying silent.** | Vendor: a one-click "correct a claim" path in the portal, not a support ticket. Affected buyers: notified individually and specifically per 14.02.05 edge case 4. |
| 23 | **Attestation signing races publish / network fails mid-sign** | The attestation and the publish are **atomic** — a published listing with an unbound attestation is exactly the unattributable checkbox D-01 exists to prevent. Failure fails the publish, never the attestation-less publish. | "We couldn't record your attestation — nothing was published. Retry." Never a partial publish. |
| 24 | **Vendor changes product type mid-draft** | Permitted **before** first publish, rejected after (14.01.01 D-03). Switching orphans the old type's sections. Sections common to both types (identity, media, terms, transfer, monetisation, attestation — ~80% per 14.01.01 D-02) **carry over**; type-specific ones are dropped with a named warning. | "Switching to `plugin` will drop your 2 audio demos and add a compatibility matrix. Your terms, pricing and attestation stay." |
| 25 | **Vendor submits a duplicate of their own live product** | Not blocked — a legitimate re-release, a Vol. 2, or a split product (14.02.05 D-07 *requires* splitting when file terms differ). Flagged to the vendor, not to a reviewer. | "This looks like [existing listing]. New version of it, or a separate product?" Links the upgrade path (14.07.05). |
| 26 | **Vendor submits a duplicate of *another* vendor's live product** | The platform can detect byte-identical or near-identical artifacts it already stores — again a records check, not fingerprinting. This is not automatically theft (a distributor, a reseller, a co-owner), so it **flags for review** rather than blocking. Auto-blocking would accuse the innocent, which is 14.02.05 DT-09's error. | Vendor: publishes, held for review if Q-02 rules review exists; otherwise publishes with the collision recorded and routed to 24 on complaint. |
| 27 | **Malicious input in attestation free-text** (source names, custom clauses) | Structured fields are enumerated values, not text. The free-text fields (source names) are rendered to buyers and to 24's reviewers ⇒ escaped, length-bounded, never raw HTML — same posture as 14.01.01's description. A custom clause contradicting a structured one is rejected here (14.02.05 D-13). | "Formatting isn't supported here." |
| 28 | **Upload fails at 90% of a 4 GB artifact** | Resumable, per 14.03.01. The vendor's other 8 sections are unaffected — the upload is not the submission. | Resume from the byte, not the beginning. Progress persists across sessions. |
| 29 | **Vendor GDPR-erases their identity after publishing** | Terms survive unconditionally (14.02.05 edge case 15 / D-09). The contested part is narrower and lands **here**: DT-01 requires a **named** asserter, and erasure removes the name. This feature does not decide it — sub-domain 14.02 Q-02, routed to `/create-prd-security`. What is committed regardless of the ruling: **erasure never makes an attestation unresolvable**; the claims, the timestamp and the product binding survive. Only the identity line's rendering is in question. | Buyer: the attestation resolves, claims intact, attributed either to the vendor's name or to "vendor identity erased under a data-protection request" — never a broken or missing record. |
| 30 | **Vendor completes everything, QA passes, then never completes KYC** | The cost of D-05's ordering, stated honestly: a finished product that cannot publish. This is the *correct* trade — the alternative (DT-06) loses the vendor at step 1 instead of step 9, and a vendor at step 9 has every incentive to finish. | One named blocker, one path: "Your pack passed all checks. Add a payout account to publish." Not a wall — a door with a handle. |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Portal fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | No products submitted | Vendor empty state with a submission CTA. **No signup wall** (D-05) — the CTA starts a draft, it does not start a KYC flow |
| Draft — Partial | Draft incomplete | Per-section checklist: "6 of 9 required sections complete". Section-level, never a progress bar over one submit (edge case 1) |

| Awaiting Payout Readiness | All sections complete, QA passed, tier 3 incomplete | "Your pack passed all checks. Add a payout account to publish." One blocker, one path (edge case 30) |
| Publishable | All gates green | Publish control live, with what is about to become permanent stated: type, terms version, attestation |
| Populated | Products submitted/live | Per-product state: draft, in QA, live, paused, delisted |
| Paused | Payout readiness lapsed after publish (edge case 15) | Vendor: "Your listing is paused — your payout account needs attention." Owners unaffected; distinct from delisted |
| Blocked | QA failed, or a declared contradiction (D-09) | Named blocker + the exact remedy. Never "rejected" (14.08.02 D-03) |
| Error | Upload or attestation-signing failure | Specific, actionable reason with the exact remedy. Attestation failure never leaves a partial publish (edge case 23) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An attestation is a checkbox — the vendor ticks "I own the rights", we're covered | The implicit model behind candidate 24 and every marketplace's terms of service | ❌ REJECTED — **an unattributable checkbox transfers no risk and prevents nothing; an attributable attestation is a different product.** The store's exposure (14.04.01 DT-02) is a vendor granting rights they never held, fanned out to hundreds of buyers with a WeJammin receipt vouching for each. A ticked box in a terms flow is worth nothing at that point — nobody can say who ticked it, against which specific claim, on what date, or what they were shown. What has value is an attestation that is **specific** (this pack, this claim: "no uncleared third-party recordings"), **attributed** (to a verified identity from onboarding, not an anonymous account), **timestamped**, and **durable** (it survives into the buyer's clearance chain, 14.02.05). That is the difference between a disclaimer and evidence — and evidence is the one thing this platform is structurally good at producing (D-18). The uncomfortable corollary: **it does not stop the harm.** It makes it traceable to a person and gives 400 exposed buyers something to point at. That is worth building and worth being honest about. |
| DT-02 | Vendor onboarding should be frictionless — the store needs supply, so make listing easy | The obvious marketplace growth instinct, and correct for most marketplaces | ❌ REJECTED as a single policy, because **this store has two supply types with opposite risk profiles and one gate cannot serve both.** Listing a *sample pack* is low-risk to the buyer's machine and high-risk to their *rights* — the harm is an uncleared break that surfaces years later in a released record. Listing a *plugin* is the reverse: an executable that runs unsandboxed inside a professional's DAW with full access to their machine and their client's session, where the harm is immediate and total. Frictionless onboarding for content is right and necessary — the realistic supply is a musician with a folder (14.01.01 DT-02) and every extra field loses vendors the store cannot afford to lose. Frictionless onboarding for **executables** is how a store becomes a malware vector with its own name on the receipt, and no plugin marketplace does it. So the answer is almost certainly *two* paths — open for audio, gated for code (sub-domain Q-01) — and treating "vendor onboarding" as one policy is how that decision gets made by default instead of on purpose. |
| DT-03 | The rights attestation should ask the vendor to assert that they hold the rights they are granting | The universal framing — what the `[SURFACE]` draft said, what every marketplace's terms say, and what DT-01's "make it attributable" improvement still assumes | ❌ REJECTED — **"do you hold the rights?" is a legal conclusion, and we are asking it of someone structurally unqualified to reach it.** Personas.md is explicit: the realistic vendor is a working musician, not a lawyer, doing this between sessions. Whether a chopped, pitched, time-stretched 2-second break constitutes infringement is a question that occupies actual courts. Asking a drummer to certify the answer produces one of two things: a lie, or an abandonment. Both are failures, and the lie is the *designed* outcome for an honest person with 95% certainty and 5% doubt — the form gives them nowhere to put the 5%. **What a musician can answer truthfully is where the material came from.** I recorded it. I commissioned it. I licensed it from X. It contains a sample from a record. That is a *fact they possess*, not a conclusion they must derive. And this is precisely the project's own thesis turned inward: the problem statement says the industry's defect is "asking people to remember" and reconstruct — a rights attestation asks for a **self-interested legal conclusion**, which is strictly worse than asking for a memory. A provenance declaration captures facts at source, which is what this platform exists to do. Three consequences follow, and all three are improvements: (a) the honest vendor with doubt can now be honest, and the platform *learns something* instead of receiving a tick; (b) a false declaration is a **false statement of fact**, materially harder to defend than an honest mistake of law — the attestation gets *stronger* by asking for less; (c) structured sources become machine-checkable for the one class where the platform holds the records (DT-04), which a free-form legal conclusion never could. Recorded as D-06. |
| DT-04 | The platform cannot verify vendor rights claims at all — attestation is the ceiling | 14.02.05 DT-09's rejection of fingerprinting, correctly reasoned, and its conclusion generalised one step too far | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **there is exactly one class of claim the platform can verify perfectly, and it is the one class nobody thought to check: material sourced from WeJammin itself.** 14.02.05 DT-09 rejects fingerprinting for sound reasons (it answers "is this the same recording", not "does this vendor hold the rights"; it misses the guilty and accuses the innocent; and it manufactures a false assurance that increases the platform's liability). All true — and all of it is about **detection**. This is not detection. When a vendor declares "these loops came from pack Y, which I bought here", the platform holds **both sides of the transaction**: the entitlement (14.02.01) and the terms it was bought under (14.02.05). Checking whether pack Y's `resale_in_isolation` clause permits what this vendor is doing is a **records lookup on the platform's own data** — zero false positives, zero false negatives, no inference, no model, no assurance manufactured. And the case is not exotic: producers building packs from other packs' material is what sample libraries are *for*, and repackaging loops as loops is the specific, common, and squarely-forbidden abuse (`resale_in_isolation: forbidden` is 14.02.05's default). This is only reachable **because** of DT-03 — a free-text "I own the rights" is unqueryable, while a structured `licensed-third-party → WeJammin pack Y` resolves against a table. So DT-03's reframe is not merely more honest; it manufactures the platform's only real verification. The scope is narrow and must stay narrow: this checks *declared* WeJammin sources, and a vendor who lies about the source is not caught by it. Claiming otherwise would be DT-09's error in a new costume. Recorded as D-07. |
| DT-05 | The AI declaration is one question: "is this AI-generated?" | Sub-domain D-04 and the AI cross-cut, which treat AI provenance as a single attested fact | ❌ REJECTED — **the question is unanswerable in good faith, and the box it lands in is the wrong box for the actual risk.** By 2026 "did you use AI" spans: a generative model producing a finished riff (Suno/Udio-class); stem separation lifting a break from a master; drum replacement; pitch correction; noise reduction. A producer who denoised a field recording and a producer who generated a topline both answer "yes", and the platform learns nothing from either. Worse, the propagated `disclosure_ai_generated` obligation (14.02.05 D-12) then lands on every release built with any of it — an obligation that fires on everything fires on nothing, and DSPs asking for AI disclosure do not mean "was a denoiser involved". **The sharper failure is a misfiling.** Stem separation is the single most common uncleared-sample vector in the current market: separate a commercial record, lift the break, sell it. It is performed by an "AI tool", so a single AI question captures it — and captures it in the **disclosure** box, which propagates a labelling obligation, rather than the **provenance** box, which would flag that this product contains a third-party master (edge case 8's gate). The store's largest risk gets filed under its smallest one, and the record looks *more* complete for it. So: enumerated uses, each with its own consequence (D-08), and stem separation is a provenance question that happens to involve AI. This does not contradict D-04 or 14.02.05 D-12 — D-12 already established that "AI" is two clauses composing in opposite directions. This is that finding arriving at the capture point and discovering the fact itself needs the same decomposition the clauses did. |
| DT-06 | Onboarding precedes submission — establish the vendor, then let them list | The `[SURFACE]` draft's own Happy Path ("1. signs up as a vendor; identity, tax and payout captured… 3. starts a submission"), and how every marketplace onboards | ❌ REJECTED — **and this contradicts the file's own D-04, which is how the error survived.** D-04 says *publish* gates on payout readiness. The Happy Path gated *signup* on it. Only one of those can be true, and the Happy Path's version is the one that kills the feature. The realistic vendor's trigger is not "I want to become a seller" — it is "I made a pack and someone asked to buy it" (14.01.01 DT-02). Meeting that person with KYC document upload and a tax questionnaire, before they have written a word or invested a minute, front-loads the two most abandonment-prone steps in the entire flow at the point of **lowest** commitment. DT-02 warns that every required field is a chance to lose a vendor the store cannot afford to lose; this arrangement spends them all first. **Invert it, and sunk cost works for completion instead of against acquisition**: let them build, upload, and pass QA — *then* ask for payout details, when the vendor has hours invested and the reason is self-evident ("we need somewhere to send the money"). The obvious objection — that deferring identity permits anonymous uploads of malicious or illegal material — dissolves on inspection, and the reason is worth stating precisely: **what is deferred is KYC, not identity.** Every user holds an authenticated platform account (domain 01) from tier 1, so every byte uploaded is attributable to a person from the moment it lands. Bank-grade legal identity is required to *receive money*, which is exactly what tier 3 gates. This yields a clean three-tier structure (D-05) where each cost is paid when its benefit is obvious. The honest cost is edge case 30 — a finished product that cannot publish — and it is the better failure: a vendor at step 9 finishes, a vendor at step 1 leaves. |
| DT-07 | The KYC identity and the platform identity are the same identity | Every generic KYC integration assumes it, because outside music it is usually true | ❌ REJECTED — **in music, legal name ≠ trading name is the norm, not the exception, and treating a mismatch as a red flag would flag the entire user base.** A session drummer trades as "Kaidо"; the passport says something else; the payouts may route to a band account or an LLC. Meanwhile this feature has two requirements pulling in opposite directions. DT-01 requires the attestation be **attributed to a verified identity** — that verification comes from KYC, i.e. the legal identity. And 14.01.01 D-07 makes the vendor identity block a platform-composed section rendering **verified credits (02)** — the store's single strongest trust signal, and it attaches to the *professional* identity, because that is the name on the records. Neither can be dropped: the legal identity makes the attestation enforceable, the professional identity makes it meaningful to a buyer. So they are **two fields with two jobs**, and the resolution is that the attestation is attributed to the platform identity, **backed by** the KYC-verified legal identity, with the legal identity never displayed anywhere in the store (D-11). This is also a hard `security-first` requirement — a vendor's legal name is PII and a storefront is the last place it belongs. Getting this wrong has two failure modes and both are bad: leak the legal name into the listing (a privacy breach, and one that will out people), or reject the professional name as unverified (reject every real vendor). The mismatch is **expected input**, and the flow must say so out loud (edge case 16) or vendors will assume they have done something wrong. |
| DT-08 | Vendor status should be gated on a verified credit graph — leverage the platform's unique asset to filter bad actors | The natural conclusion from 14.01.01 DT-07 (verified credits are the store's strongest trust signal), and a genuinely attractive idea | ❌ REJECTED — **it is circular, and it would strangle the store at launch.** The credit graph is populated by sessions that happen *on WeJammin* (02, 07). A new store has no sessions, so it has no graph, so nobody qualifies, so there is no inventory, so there is no reason to run a session here, so there is never a graph. Gating supply on the wedge before the platform exists to create the wedge is a bootstrap that never starts. It also fails on its own terms: the realistic vendor's credits are on records made *before* WeJammin existed and are therefore absent from the graph — so the gate would exclude **precisely the vendors 14.01.01 DT-02 identifies as the store's only viable day-one inventory**, while admitting a well-connected newcomer with three platform sessions and no track record. The confusion is category-level: 14.01.01 D-07 makes the identity block a **differentiator** — where credits exist, they are shown, and they answer "why buy from someone I've never heard of" better than any competitor can. A vendor with no credits sells with no credits shown, and the buyer prices that in. **Turning a display signal into an access gate destroys the supply and solves nothing** that the attestation (D-06) and KYC (D-05 tier 3) do not address more directly and more honestly. The generalisable lesson, which reaches well past this feature: **provenance is the wedge (D-18), but the platform cannot be gated on the wedge before it is present enough to produce it.** Consolidation comes first for a structural reason, not a strategic one. |
| DT-09 | A draft is a saved form — restore it and the vendor carries on | The obvious model, and what "Draft restored — 6 of 9 sections complete" (14.01.01) implies | ✅ CONFIRMED as insufficient — **a draft here routinely outlives the schema it was written against, and nobody designs for it.** This is not a rare race: DT-02 establishes that the realistic vendor abandons mid-flow *by definition* — a musician doing this between sessions returns in weeks or months. Meanwhile the platform is shipping: the required-sections schema gains a field, a licence template is deprecated (14.01.01 explicitly contemplates deprecated templates), the transfer vocabulary gains a clause. So "6 of 9" is a statement about a schema that no longer exists, and the failure is silent — the vendor publishes a listing that is under-specified against current requirements, or worse, **binds a deprecated licence template**, which 14.02.05 D-02 then makes immutable. That last one is the sharp edge: the platform freezes a vendor to a legal position it retracted, permanently, on an entitlement it will honour forever. The fix is unglamorous and cheap: validate at **resume**, never at save; never publish against a schema the draft was not validated against; keep valid sections, name stale ones. Recorded as D-12. It earns its place by being the failure that is certain to happen, costs almost nothing to prevent, and is invisible until it is permanent. |
| DT-10 | Vendors are adults who can contract and be paid | Unstated everywhere, assumed by every KYC flow, and false often enough to matter | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap — **the beat market runs on teenagers, and this flow has no path for them.** A 15-year-old selling beats is not a hypothetical edge case; it is one of the most common vendor profiles in the single product type (`beat`) the store is most likely to actually have inventory for. They can reach tier 1 and tier 2 and are structurally unable to reach tier 3: they cannot contract, cannot KYC, cannot be paid. Under D-05's ordering this is *worse* than under the rejected DT-06 ordering — the minor invests three hours and hits an impassable wall at step 9, where the old design would have stopped them at step 1. That is the one place DT-06's inversion has a real cost, and it must be paid explicitly: **tier 2 states the age requirement before the work begins** (edge case 17), which is the only cheap mitigation available and costs the adult vendor one line of text. What the platform actually *does* about it — a guardian-consented account, a managed/label account holding the payout, or simply an age floor honestly stated — is a legal and product decision with real compliance weight (`meta/constraints.md` already carries age gating as an unresolved compliance area), and it is not an agent's call. **Q-04.** Recording it because it is the kind of gap that gets discovered by a support ticket from an angry parent rather than by a spec. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attestations are specific, attributed, timestamped and durable — never an anonymous checkbox | Per DT-01. An unattributable tick transfers no risk and gives exposed buyers nothing to point at. |
| D-02 | Required sections are type-conditional; a WAV pack never sees a support matrix | Per 14.01.01 D-01/D-02 and CX-02. Every irrelevant required field costs a vendor the store cannot afford to lose. |
| D-03 | Licence terms are authored from templates, never a free-text box | Per 14.02.05 D-01 — vendors are musicians, not lawyers. A text box produces copy-pasted EULAs that bind nobody and cannot propagate. |
| D-04 | Publish gates on payout readiness | Live products with undeliverable money is a vendor-trust failure with no clean recovery. |
| D-05 | **Three-tier gate: platform account (free) → vendor terms + continuity (drafting) → KYC/tax/payout (publish).** Identity for *attribution* exists from tier 1; identity for *payment* is asked at tier 3. | Per DT-06, and this is what D-04 already implied — the `[SURFACE]` Happy Path contradicted its own D-04 by gating signup on payout details. Each cost is paid where its benefit is obvious. Deferring KYC is safe precisely because it is not deferring *identity*: every upload is attributable to a platform account (01) from byte one. |
| D-06 | **The provenance declaration states SOURCES (facts), never a rights conclusion.** Enumerated categories at product level; `licensed-third-party` and `contains-third-party-recording` require the source to be named. | Per DT-03. "Do you hold the rights?" asks a musician for a legal conclusion they cannot reach — producing a lie or an abandonment. "Where did this come from?" asks for a fact they possess. This is the project's own thesis (capture facts at source, don't ask for reconstructed conclusions) applied to the store's own capture point. It also makes the attestation *stronger*: a false source is a false statement of fact, not an honest mistake of law. |
| D-07 | **Declared WeJammin-sourced material is verified automatically against the vendor's entitlements and those products' terms** (`resale_in_isolation`, `derivative_use`). Scope is strictly declared sources — this is not detection. | Per DT-04. The one class the platform can verify with zero false positives and zero false negatives, because it holds both the entitlement (14.02.01) and the terms (14.02.05). Reachable only because D-06 made sources structured. Must stay narrow: a vendor who lies about the source is not caught by it, and claiming otherwise repeats 14.02.05 DT-09's error. |
| D-08 | **The AI declaration enumerates specific uses, never one boolean. Only generative output propagates a disclosure obligation.** Stem separation is filed as **provenance**, not AI. | Per DT-05. "Did you use AI" is unanswerable in good faith and an obligation that fires on everything fires on nothing. The misfiling is the sharper harm: stem separation is the market's most common uncleared-sample vector and a single AI question files it under disclosure rather than rights. Consistent with 14.02.05 D-12, which already split "AI" into two clauses composing in opposite directions. |
| D-09 | **A declaration that contradicts the terms being granted blocks publish, naming the contradiction.** | The platform is not adjudicating rights — it is declining to publish a record whose own author says is false. Requires no verification whatsoever. Same shape as 14.02.05 D-13's custom-vs-structured rejection, which this feature's surface also owns. The honest vendor gets stopped and told exactly why, which is the value. |
| D-10 | **An attestation is versioned and immutable once bound.** A correction is a new version appended, never an edit; prior versions are preserved and materially-affected buyers are notified. | Same physics as 14.02.05 D-02 and for the same reason: it is a buyer's evidence, and editable evidence is not evidence. Critically, **self-correction must never be harder than silence** (edge case 22) — a vendor who withdraws a claim is doing the platform's job for it. |
| D-11 | **The attestation binds the platform identity, backed by the KYC-verified legal identity. The legal identity is never displayed.** A legal ≠ display name mismatch is expected input, never a fraud signal. | Per DT-07. Legal identity makes the attestation enforceable; professional identity makes it meaningful to a buyer (14.01.01 D-07's credits attach to the professional name). Both jobs, two fields. Also a hard `security-first` requirement — a vendor's legal name is PII and a storefront is the last place it belongs. |
| D-12 | **Drafts are validated against the current schema at resume, never at save, and never publish against a schema they were not validated against.** A deprecated licence template is never silently bound. | Per DT-09. The realistic vendor abandons for months by definition (DT-02) while the platform ships. Silent publication against a stale schema under-specifies a live listing; silently binding a deprecated template freezes a vendor permanently (14.02.05 D-02) to a position the platform retracted. Cheap to prevent, invisible until permanent. |
| D-13 | **Attestation and publish are atomic.** Signing failure fails the publish. | A published listing with an unbound attestation is exactly the unattributable checkbox D-01 exists to prevent — arriving through a network error instead of a design choice. |
