## Overview

The supply side: how a vendor gets a product into the store, what the platform checks before it
reaches a buyer, how updates roll out without breaking sessions, and what happens when a vendor
leaves. This is where the store either becomes a trusted place to buy executable code and audio
with rights attached — or a distribution channel for malware and uncleared samples with WeJammin's
name on the receipt.

**Why this is a sub-domain**: five capabilities over one lifecycle — onboard, submit, verify,
release, exit. Submission produces artifacts QA gates; QA's verdict controls what rollout can
publish; rollout depends on the archive it can roll back to; exit is the terminal state that all
of them must survive. They are one pipeline, not five tools.

**Where the domain's real risk lives (see 14.04.01 DT-02, 14.02.05 DT-03)**: the candidate list
spends four nodes on outbound protection (DRM, watermarking, blacklisting, anti-piracy) and one on
inbound verification. Classification found that inverted. Piracy costs a vendor a sale. A pack
sold with rights its vendor never held costs *every buyer* their record — and it enters through
this sub-domain.
