# Feature: Release Channels, Staged Rollout & Rollback

> **Number**: 14.08.04
> **Parent**: [14.08 Vendor Portal, Build Submission & QA](./14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's 24 candidates.

## Overview

Controlling *who* gets a new build and *when*, and undoing it when it goes wrong. Candidate 19
covers submission and QA but stops at publish, as though shipping were the end of a vendor's
lifecycle. It is the beginning of the risky part: a plugin update that changes a filter curve or
crashes at 96 kHz reaches every buyer at once, and some of those buyers are mid-album with a
client in the room.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sits on the default channel and rarely thinks about it. As a content vendor, never meets this feature — content has no runtime and nothing to stage (`#R-02`). |
| Producer | Full | Both roles, sharply. **As vendor**: ships through channels, watches a cohort, halts or rolls back with a required withdrawal reason. **As buyer**: the victim of a bad update — the persona whose 200-track session breaks. The same human on both sides of the incident. |
| Operator | Read-only | Wants the **slowest channel available**. Rooms are pinned to known-good builds and updated on a maintenance window (14.03.02 DT-02); "stable, 30 days behind" is a feature, not a compromise. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

A QA-passed build (CX-03) enters a **channel** — beta, current, or stable/delayed — rather than
publishing to everyone. Entitled holders on that channel are offered the update (never forced,
14.03.02 D-02); the offer fans out progressively (14.03 CX-02 q4).

**Rollback** withdraws a build from further distribution and carries one immutable
`withdrawal_reason`: `superseded`, `defective`, or `malicious`. The actor initiating the
rollback records the reason; delivery consumes it (14.03.01 D-11), so a rollback never has a
silent untyped state. `superseded` and `defective` withdraw a recommendation; `malicious` stops
delivery grants immediately.

Critically, withdrawal is **not deletion** (14.03.02 D-02): buyers who already have a build keep
it. For a superseded or defective build, the previous version remains fetchable from the legacy
archive (14.03.02). A malicious build is no longer offered and its in-flight grants are stopped;
the platform cannot un-install a copy already on a buyer's machine.

Field signals (crash reports, refund spikes, support volume) feed back to QA (CX-03) and should be
able to halt a rollout in progress (Q-02).

### Happy Path

1. Vendor's build passes QA (CX-03).
2. Vendor publishes to the beta channel; opted-in holders are offered it.
3. No adverse signal after a set period; vendor promotes to current.
4. The offer fans out progressively to holders on current, with release notes describing what changed *musically* (14.03.02 D-03).
5. Operators on the stable channel receive it 30 days later, on their own maintenance window.
6. Had a crash spike appeared, the rollout halts and the vendor rolls back with reason `defective`; the previous build is already archived and fetchable.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| v3 subtly changes a sound; mixes in progress drift | The failure this feature exists for, and QA cannot catch it (CX-03) | Staged exposure limits the blast radius; the archive is the remedy |
| `superseded` or `defective` rollback races an in-flight download | The artifact is valid; the recommendation changed | Download completes (14.03.01 D-11) |
| `malicious` rollback races an in-flight download | Continuing to distribute a payload identified as harmful is unsafe | Transfer is killed within 30 s; grant is void and the partial is flagged unsafe (14.03.01 D-11) |
| Vendor rolls back a build buyers already installed | Cannot un-install; withdrawal ≠ deletion | Buyers are offered the previous version; nothing is taken away |
| **Vendor has exited** (CX-04) | Nobody can roll back, fix, or answer. The last build is permanent. | The legacy archive is the only recourse — 14.03.02 DT-01's argument, again |
| Vendor pushes to current, skipping beta | Their catalog, their call — but the blast radius is other people's sessions | See Q-01: does the platform mandate staging for anything? |
| Crash reports spike but the vendor is asleep | The rollout keeps landing on more buyers | Automatic halt on threshold — Q-02 |
| Content vendor asks for staged rollout | Nothing to stage (`#R-02`) — a pack downloads or it doesn't | Feature not offered for content |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Channel state fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Product has one build, no channels used | No channel UI — absent, not empty |
| Populated | Build in a channel | Channel, cohort size, adoption, adverse signals |
| Error | Publish to channel failed | Named reason; the build stays where it was |
| Partial | Rollout in progress | "Offered to 12% of holders" with live signal |
| Halted | Threshold breached or vendor stopped | Distribution paused; existing installs untouched |
| Withdrawn | Rolled back with required `withdrawal_reason` | `superseded` / `defective`: not offered to anyone new; archive remains fetchable by prior holders. `malicious`: no new or in-flight grants; existing installs cannot be removed. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rollout control is a vendor convenience — nice for big developers, unnecessary for a small store | Why the sweep omitted it: it reads as release-engineering infrastructure rather than product | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the only response this domain has to its most likely real-world incident, and without it three other features are decorative.** 14.03.02 DT-02 established that this user base *actively fears updates*, and rationally: a plugin version is not a bug-fix level, it is the sound. The predictable incident is not exotic — a vendor ships v3, it changes a filter curve or crashes at 96 kHz, and it lands on every buyer simultaneously, including a Producer mid-album and an Operator mid-session with a client billing by the hour. Nothing in candidate 19 can respond to that: QA already passed it (CX-03 — QA validates a binary in isolation and cannot simulate a 200-track session), and the archive (14.03.02) holds the old build but nothing *stops the new one spreading*. Staged rollout is what converts "everyone is broken" into "3% are broken and we halted". It is also what makes the archive **actionable** rather than merely present — the archive is the escape hatch, this is the hand that reaches for it. |
| DT-02 | Channels are for vendors to manage their releases | The obvious framing, borrowed from app stores where the publisher picks the cohort | ❌ REJECTED — **the channel belongs to the buyer, not the vendor, and this inversion is the whole point.** In an app store the publisher decides who gets the beta. Here, the person who must not be surprised is the *Operator running a session tonight* and the *Producer mid-album* — and only they know that. A vendor cannot know which of their buyers is three weeks from a delivery deadline. So the primitive is a **buyer-selected channel**: "put me on stable, 30 days behind" is a standing instruction from someone protecting their livelihood, not a preference the vendor grants. This inverts the usual model and matches how professional audio actually works — studios pin builds and update on maintenance windows, deliberately, and have done for decades. It also composes cleanly with 14.03.02 D-02 (updates are offered, never forced): the channel decides *whether an offer is even made*, and the buyer decides whether to take it. Two locks, both held by the person with the most to lose. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The release channel is **buyer-selected**, not vendor-assigned | Per DT-02. Only the buyer knows they are mid-album or running a session tonight; a vendor cannot know that and should not decide it. |
| D-02 | Rollback withdraws from distribution; it never deletes | Per 14.03.02 D-02. Buyers keep what they have; the previous build stays fetchable. Anything else takes away something someone owns. |
| D-03 | A build must pass QA before entering **any** channel, including beta | Per CX-03 q3. A beta channel is not a bypass around a safety gate (14.08.02 D-01). |
| D-04 | Every rollback carries an immutable `withdrawal_reason`: `superseded`, `defective`, or `malicious` | The rollback initiator records the reason. Delivery branches on it: superseded and defective transfers complete; malicious transfers are stopped (14.03.01 D-11). |
