# Feature: Audio Content QC & Metadata Verification

> **Number**: 14.08.03
> **Parent**: [14.08 Vendor Portal, Build Submission & QA](./14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Checking audio content before it publishes — clipping, true-peak, loudness, dead silence,
truncated files, wrong sample rates — and verifying the declared musical metadata (key, BPM) that
the whole search index depends on (14.04 CX-01). This is the content half of the submission gate,
sharing nothing with executable QA but the moment it fires (`#R-01`).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Their gate.** The realistic vendor (14.01.01 DT-02) is a musician with a folder of samples, and this is the only QA they will ever meet. It must read as a helpful engineer's second pair of ears — "3 of these clip, 12 are labelled in the wrong key" — rather than a gatekeeper rejecting their work. |
| Producer | Full | Same, at higher volume and with higher standards. Also the persona most likely to have *deliberately* clipped or distorted content, which is why QC must warn rather than refuse (DT-01). |
| Operator | None | No column. Studios do not publish sample packs. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

On submission of audio content (CX-02), each file is analysed: peak and true-peak levels,
integrated loudness, DC offset, dead silence at head/tail, truncation, declared vs actual sample
rate and bit depth. Separately, musical metadata is **extracted** (key, BPM) and cross-checked
against the vendor's declaration.

The central design principle is that most findings are **warnings, not blockers** (DT-01) — a
clipped break may be the product. Extraction disagreeing with a declaration is a *signal*, not a
verdict, and whose value wins is unresolved (14.04.01 Q-03, and 14.04.04 D-04's recommendation:
record both, rank the agreement).

For a 5,000-file pack this is long-running and async (CX-02 q2).

### Happy Path

1. Vendor uploads a pack (14.08.01).
2. Each file is analysed; metadata is extracted per file.
3. Extraction is cross-checked against the vendor's declared key/BPM.
4. Results return as a per-file report: 3 clip, 12 have head silence, 8 disagree on key.
5. Vendor fixes what they meant to fix and dismisses what was intentional.
6. Publish proceeds; the extracted metadata feeds the index (14.04 CX-01) with a confidence value (14.04.04 D-04).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Clipped file — deliberately (a crunchy break, a distorted 808) | Blocking this would refuse the product the vendor meant to make | Warning, dismissible with a reason. Never a blocker — see DT-01 |
| Extraction says F#, vendor says F | One of them is wrong and we cannot tell which | Both recorded; agreement ranked higher (14.04.04 D-04). Not silently overridden |
| Key is genuinely undefined (kick, noise sweep, atonal texture) | Forcing a value poisons the index (14.04.04 DT-02) | Nullable, honestly | 
| 5,000-file pack | QC takes a long time | Async with progressive results; the vendor is not blocked at a spinner |
| Dead silence at file head | Ruins drag-and-drop timing in a DAW — a real defect the vendor won't notice | Warning with the offset; the kind of finding that earns the feature its trust |
| Pack contains an uncleared third-party recording | **QC cannot see this.** Loudness analysis says nothing about provenance. | The domain's central exposure and this gate is blind to it — see DT-02 |
| Sample rate declared 48k, files are 44.1k | Straightforward mismatch | Blocker — the spec sheet would lie (14.01.01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | QC running | "Checking 5,000 files — 3,200 done" |
| Empty | No audio submitted | Not shown — software vendors never meet this |
| Populated | Report ready | Per-file findings grouped by type, with severity |
| Error | Analysis failed | Retry; a QC failure on content should not permanently block a publish the way it does for code |
| Partial | Some files analysed | Progressive results |
| Blocked | A genuine blocker (declared/actual format mismatch) | Named blocker + remedy |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Audio QC should reject substandard content — clipping, silence, wrong loudness are defects | The candidate's phrasing ("audio QC" listed alongside malware scanning as a gate), which imports executable QA's binary pass/fail | ❌ REJECTED — **most "defects" in music content are intentional, and a gate that cannot tell the difference will refuse the product the vendor meant to make.** A clipped, crushed 808 is the sound; head silence may be a deliberate pre-roll; a -3 LUFS lo-fi break is a genre, not an error. Applying executable QA's binary posture here (14.08.02 D-01: unconditional, non-waivable, fails closed) would reject the store's most plausible inventory for being *correctly made*. The right shape is inverted: **warnings the vendor can dismiss with a reason**, not blockers — with a hard blocker reserved for the narrow class where the *listing would lie* (declared 48k, actual 44.1k). The valuable findings are the ones the vendor didn't intend and can't hear: a 4 ms head silence that ruins drag-and-drop timing, a truncated tail, a DC offset. That reframes the feature from gatekeeper to **an engineer's second pair of ears** — which is also the only framing a musician-vendor will tolerate between sessions. |
| DT-02 | QC verifies the content is what it claims to be | The natural read of "quality control + metadata verification" — the feature name implies verification of claims | ❌ REJECTED, and the gap is the domain's whole risk profile in one line. QC verifies the claims that **don't matter much** (is it 48k, is it clipping, is it really 142 BPM) and is structurally blind to the one that matters most: **does the vendor hold the rights to this audio?** Loudness analysis cannot see a Motown break. Key extraction cannot see an uncleared vocal. So the store's central exposure (14.04.01 DT-02: a pack sold with rights its vendor never held, exposing every downstream release) passes straight through this gate untouched, and the only thing standing between it and 400 buyers is an attestation (14.08.01 DT-01) that traces harm rather than preventing it. The honest options are narrow and all cost real money: **audio fingerprinting against commercial recordings** at submission (the same capability 14.05.02 Q-03 wants for re-sold exclusives — worth noticing they are one investment), human review on flagged submissions, or accepting the exposure and pricing it. Naming this here rather than discovering it in production is most of what this annotation is for. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Findings are dismissible warnings by default; blockers are reserved for cases where the listing would lie | Per DT-01. Most "defects" in music content are intentional, and refusing them rejects the store's realistic inventory for being correctly made. |
| D-02 | Extraction disagreeing with a vendor's declared metadata is recorded as a signal, not an override | Per 14.04.04 D-04 — record both, rank the agreement. Neither source is reliably right. |
| D-03 | QC does not claim to verify provenance, and the UI must not imply it | Per DT-02 — the gate is blind to the domain's central risk, and implying otherwise is worse than saying nothing. |
