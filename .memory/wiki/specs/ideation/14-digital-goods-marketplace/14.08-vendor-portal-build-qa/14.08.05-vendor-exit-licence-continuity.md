# Feature: Vendor Exit & Perpetual Licence Continuity

> **Number**: 14.08.05
> **Parent**: [14.08 Vendor Portal, Build Submission & QA](./14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What happens when a vendor leaves — voluntarily, by going out of business, or by being removed.
The store sold their products as **perpetual** (14.07.01), which is a promise the platform made
and the vendor's departure does not dissolve. Every buyer's licence must keep working, every
download must keep serving, and every archived version must stay fetchable — indefinitely, with no
further revenue and no vendor left to care.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own exit) · Read-only (as buyer) | As a vendor, may delist their own packs — and must not be able to take buyers' purchases with them. As a buyer, should notice nothing: their library row says "no longer sold", downloads work, licence intact. |
| Producer | Config · Read-only | Same. Also the persona most exposed as a buyer — a plugin they used on a client's record must still open that record in five years, whether or not its maker is still trading. |
| Operator | Read-only | **Rooms depend on exited vendors' tools routinely** — studios run software from companies that folded years ago. This is not a hypothetical for them; it is Tuesday. Their requirement is total: the tool must keep working, forever, without a vendor. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

Exit is the terminal state of the vendor lifecycle. The obligations that make it survivable are
agreed at **onboarding** (CX-05) — a departing vendor has no incentive to cooperate, and the only
leverage is the agreement they already signed.

On exit: listings are delisted (nothing new sells); artifacts and every archived version stay
stored and servable (14.03.01, 14.03.02); entitlements remain active (14.02.01); buyers are
notified that the product is no longer sold and their access is unaffected. The vendor cannot roll
back or ship a fix ever again (CX-04), so their last build is permanent — which is why the archive
is load-bearing (14.03.02 DT-01).

Unresolved and material: rent-to-own schedules mid-flight (14.07.03 DT-02), transfer policies
requiring a vendor approval nobody can give (14.06.01), and activation if the platform ever
depended on the *vendor's* infrastructure.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor agrees continuity terms at onboarding (CX-05).
2. Years later, the vendor exits.
3. Listings delist; nothing new sells.
4. Artifacts, all versions, and entitlements are untouched.
5. Buyers are notified: "no longer sold — your licence and downloads are unaffected".
6. A buyer changes machines in 2033 and re-downloads. It works.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor tries to delete their catalog rather than exit cleanly | Must be **structurally impossible**, not merely forbidden (CX-05 q5) — buyers hold perpetual rights to those artifacts | Delisting removes them from sale; nothing is deleted |
| Buyer is 6 of 12 payments into rent-to-own (14.07.03 DT-02) | Keep paying to whom? Convert free? Refund? Every answer costs someone, and the platform is the only party still present. | `[PENDING]` — genuinely unsolved anywhere; 14.07.03 Q-02 |
| Vendor's transfer policy required their approval (14.06.01) | The entire second-hand market for their catalog freezes permanently | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Vendor exits with a broken last build | No fix will ever come; the archive's previous version is the only recourse | 14.03.02 DT-01 and CX-04, arriving together |
| Vendor is **removed** for cause (fraud, malware, uncleared content) | Do buyers keep a product we removed for being harmful? Perpetual promise vs active harm. | See DT-02 — these are not the same event and must not share a path |
| Storage cost of a departed vendor's 100 GB catalog, forever | Pure cost, zero revenue, indefinitely | 14.03 Q-03 / 14.07.01 DT-01 — the perpetual liability, crystallised |
| Vendor's product depended on *their* activation server | If they hosted it, their exit kills the product regardless of our promise | See Q-02 |
| Buyer's purchase is in flight at the instant of exit (payment authorised, entitlement not yet written) | The exit must not orphan an in-flight order — either the entitlement completes and joins the perpetual set, or the payment is voided; never charged-without-access | Order resolves to "owned" or is refunded within the checkout window; delisting applies only to *new* carts, not committed ones |
| Buyer with an active download stream when delisting fires | Delisting removes the listing, not the artifact (D-02); in-progress and future re-downloads must be unaffected | Download continues; library row flips to "no longer sold" without interrupting the transfer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing/library fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Vendor active | Nothing — an absent state |
| Populated | Vendor exited | Listing: "no longer sold". Library: unchanged, downloads live |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Vendor exit is an edge case — vendors mostly stay, so this is a tidy-up feature | Why candidate 23 reads as an afterthought at position 23 of 24 | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the moment the store's central promise is tested, and in music software it is routine rather than rare.** This industry's history is a graveyard: companies fold, get acquired and sunset their catalogs, or simply stop caring — and studios are still running tools from vendors that vanished a decade ago (see the Operator lens: this is Tuesday, not a hypothetical). What makes it structural rather than incidental is the asymmetry it exposes: the vendor took their revenue in year one and left; **the platform's obligation continues for twenty**. Store 100 GB forever, serve it on every machine change, honour every archived version, with zero further income and nobody to escalate to. So this feature is not tidy-up — it is where 14.07.01 DT-01's "unbounded liability sold at a fixed price" actually lands, and where the word "perpetual" either means something or is revealed as marketing. Every other feature's promise routes through here eventually. |
| DT-02 | Exit is exit — one path, whether they left or we removed them | The candidate's single framing ("Vendor Exit & Perpetual Licence Continuity"), which treats departure as one event | ❌ REJECTED — **there are two events with opposite obligations, and conflating them forces one wrong answer onto both.** A vendor who *retires* has done nothing wrong: buyers keep everything, the promise holds cleanly, and the only cost is ours. A vendor **removed for cause** — malware (14.08.02), an uncleared sample chain (14.04.01 DT-02), fraudulently re-sold exclusives (14.05.02) — is a different situation entirely, and the perpetual promise now points the wrong way: do we keep serving a plugin we removed for shipping malware? Keep honouring a sample pack whose vendor never held the rights, when continuing to serve it arguably deepens every buyer's exposure? Here "your licence is unaffected" is not reassurance, it is us continuing to distribute the harmful thing. The buyer is *also* a victim and stripping their purchase punishes them for our failure to catch it. There is no clean answer — but there are clearly two paths (retirement vs removal), and they must be specified separately with 24 owning the removal case (Q-01). A single path guarantees whichever one we picked is wrong half the time. |
| DT-03 | On a clean retirement, buyers should be notified loudly — "the vendor you bought from has left the platform" — so they're informed | The instinct that a vendor departure is news the buyer needs, symmetrical with any account-status change | ✅ CONFIRMED that buyers are notified, but **the notification is deliberately quiet and reassurance-first, not an alarm.** For a *retirement*, nothing the buyer holds actually changes — licence intact, downloads live, every archived version fetchable — so a loud "your vendor has left" push manufactures anxiety about a promise the platform is silently keeping, and trains buyers to distrust perpetual ownership on a store whose entire thesis is that ownership is real. The correct surface is a **passive library-row state** ("no longer sold — your licence and downloads are unaffected"), with an active notification reserved for the case where something genuinely changes for the buyer: a **removal-for-cause** (DT-02 / Q-01), where access may be revoked and silence would be negligent. Tone tracks obligation: retirement is a status change the buyer can safely ignore; removal is an event they must be told about. This keeps the reassuring default from diluting the one alert that matters. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Continuity obligations are agreed at onboarding, not negotiated at exit | Per CX-05. A departing vendor has no incentive to cooperate; the signed agreement is the only leverage that exists. |
| D-02 | A vendor cannot delete artifacts buyers hold perpetual rights to — structurally, not by policy | Per CX-05 q5. "Forbidden" is a rule someone breaks; "impossible" is a guarantee. |
| D-03 | Retirement and removal-for-cause are **separate paths** with separate obligations | Per DT-02 — one promise cannot serve both, and a single path is wrong half the time. |
| D-04 | On retirement, buyers get a **passive, reassurance-first** library-row state; active notification is reserved for removal-for-cause where access actually changes | Per DT-03 — a loud "your vendor left" alert manufactures anxiety about a promise the platform is silently keeping, and dilutes the one alert (removal) that a buyer must not miss. |
| D-05 | Delisting on exit applies only to **new** carts; orders already committed at the instant of exit resolve to owned-or-refunded, never charged-without-access | In-flight purchases must not be orphaned by the exit transition; the perpetual set a buyer joins is defined at commit, not at the exit timestamp. |
