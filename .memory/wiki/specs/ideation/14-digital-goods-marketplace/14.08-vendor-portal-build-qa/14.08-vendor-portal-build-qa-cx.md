## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Submission ↔ Build QA

**Relationship**: A submitted executable is scanned, signature-verified and validated
(pluginval/auval) before it can publish. The gate is unconditional for code: a plugin runs
unsandboxed inside a professional's DAW with full access to their machine and their client's
session.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the software vendor; sees pass/fail with actionable reasons, not a verdict.
- **Musician**: as a pack vendor, mostly bypasses this — a WAV has no binary (CX-02).
- **Operator**: not affected — no submission workflow.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The artifact is submission-owned; QA writes a verdict against it. Single writer each.
2. **Trigger chain**: Submit → scan → verdict → publish allowed/blocked. Async (scanning a 4 GB installer is not synchronous). Failure to scan must block, not pass — an unscanned binary must never publish.
3. **Permission intersection**: A vendor cannot override a QA verdict. Whether *platform staff* can force-publish is a real question (14.08.02 Q-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Vendor notified with the failure reason. A verdict with no reason is unactionable and generates support load.
5. **State transition conflict**: A vendor resubmitting while a scan is in flight — the later artifact must win, and the earlier verdict must not publish a superseded build.

### CX-02: Submission ↔ Audio QC

**Relationship**: The same submission gate routes audio products to entirely different checks —
loudness, clipping, silence, true-peak, declared key/BPM verification (14.04 CX-01). None of these
apply to a compressor binary, and none of QA's checks apply to a WAV.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the pack vendor; this is *their* gate, and it must be helpful rather than gatekeeping — a producer with a folder is the store's realistic supply (14.01.01 DT-02).
- **Producer**: same.
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None. Extraction may *disagree* with a vendor's declared metadata — whose value wins is 14.04.01 Q-03.
2. **Trigger chain**: Submit → QC → verdict. A 5,000-file pack's QC is long-running and async.
3. **Permission intersection**: Vendor cannot override. Warnings vs blockers should differ — a clipped sample may be intentional (a distorted break), so QC must warn rather than refuse.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Per-file results; "3 files clip, 12 are mislabelled in key".
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-03: Build QA ↔ Staged Rollout

**Relationship**: QA gates what rollout can publish; rollout's field failures feed back as
evidence QA's checks are incomplete. This is the only loop in the sub-domain that makes anything
improve — QA validates a binary in isolation, but the failures that matter (a plugin that crashes
in a 200-track session at 96 kHz, or changes a filter curve) only appear in the field.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: as vendor, ships through channels; as buyer, is the field failure.
- **Operator**: wants the slowest channel available — rooms update deliberately (14.03.02 DT-02).
- **Musician**: mostly on the default channel and unaware.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The verdict is QA-owned; the channel assignment is rollout-owned.
2. **Trigger chain**: QA pass → eligible for rollout → staged publish. A crash-report spike should be able to halt a rollout automatically (14.08.04 Q-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Only QA-passed builds may enter any channel, including beta.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Progressive — that is the whole point (14.03 CX-02 q4). A staged rollout notifies a cohort, not the base.
5. **State transition conflict**: A rollback racing an in-flight download branches on the required `withdrawal_reason` (14.08.04 D-04 / 14.03.01 D-11): `superseded` or `defective` completes because the artifact remains valid; `malicious` kills the transfer within 30 s and flags the partial unsafe.

### CX-04: Staged Rollout ↔ Vendor Exit

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. An exited vendor cannot roll back, cannot ship a fix, and
cannot answer a support question. Their last build is permanent. The legacy archive (14.03.02) is
then the only recourse a buyer has — which is another way of saying the archive is not optional

### CX-05: Submission ↔ Vendor Exit

**Relationship**: The continuity obligations that make 14.08.05 possible — artifacts stay,
licences keep working, downloads keep serving — must be agreed at **onboarding**. A vendor
walking away has no incentive to cooperate, and no leverage exists at that point except the
agreement they already signed.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer / Musician**: as vendors, agree to continuity terms before their first sale.
- **Operator**: not affected as a vendor; heavily affected as a buyer of exited vendors' tools.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — the agreement is a submission-time artifact read at exit.
2. **Trigger chain**: Onboard → agree continuity terms. Exit → enforce them. Years apart.
3. **Permission intersection**: A vendor cannot unilaterally withdraw artifacts that buyers have perpetual rights to. This is the point of agreeing it up front.
4. **Notification fan-out**: At exit, buyers are notified that the product is no longer sold and their access is unaffected.
5. **State transition conflict**: A vendor attempting to delete their catalog rather than exit cleanly — must be structurally impossible, not merely forbidden.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.08.02 Build QA | 14.08.03 Audio QC | They look like one feature (both are "check the upload before it publishes") and share **nothing** — disjoint product types, disjoint checks, disjoint failure meanings. A malware scan on a WAV is theatre; a loudness check on a .vst3 is nonsense. Merging them (as candidate 19 did) forces every submission through a pipeline where most checks cannot apply, and produces a verdict object whose fields are mostly null — the same defect D-14 rejected at domain level. |
| R-02 | 14.08.03 Audio QC | 14.08.04 Staged Rollout | Content does not stage. A sample pack has no crash reports, no field telemetry, and no cohort worth measuring — it either downloads or it doesn't. Staged rollout exists to catch runtime failures in the wild, and content has no runtime. Recording this because "roll out content gradually" sounds prudent and would buy nothing while adding a state machine to the store's most plausible product type. |
