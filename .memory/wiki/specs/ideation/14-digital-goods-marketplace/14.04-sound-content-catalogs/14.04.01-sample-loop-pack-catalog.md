# Feature: Sample & Loop Pack Catalog

> **Number**: 14.04.01
> **Parent**: [14.04 Sound Content Catalogs](./14.04-sound-content-catalogs-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Selling collections of audio — loops, one-shots, breaks, vocal chops, foley — with the musical
metadata that makes them findable and the per-file audition that makes them buyable. This is the
most plausible thing domain 14 ever sells, because the vendors are the platform's own users: a
producer with a folder can list today, with no business-development motion and no reseller
agreement (14.01.01 DT-02).

It exists to solve two problems at once, and only one of them is obvious. The obvious one is
**supply**: WeJammin cannot obtain FabFilter, but it already has the drummer who recorded the kit.
The non-obvious one is **the thesis** (D-18): a purchased loop is a *fact about a record* — an
input with an ownership chain — and the store is standing in the room at the exact moment that
fact becomes true, holding a receipt. Nowhere else on the platform is the capture cheaper. A sample
store that only moves files is a file shop next to a rights platform; this one is designed to feed it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (buyer + vendor) | **Buys**: auditions before buying, searches by tempo and key against a track in progress, needs to know a loop is clean before it reaches a release. **Sells**: this is a real income line in the portfolio personas.md describes — a session drummer listing their kit samples between gigs. Their exposure runs both directions: as a vendor they can accidentally sell an uncleared chop (personas.md's named worst-accidental, fanned out to strangers); as a buyer they can accidentally build a record on someone else's. They will not read an EULA, and the design must not require it. |
| Producer | Full (buyer + vendor) | The core persona on both sides. **As buyer**: searches against the open session — "142, G minor, something dusty" — and needs per-file audition because the pack is 800 files and 6 of them matter. **As vendor**: the archetypal pack author. Declares musical metadata, authors terms (14.02.05), and — critically — makes the upstream-rights attestation (14.02.05 DT-03) and names the contributors (14.10.03 D-01). Personas.md: their worst accidental behaviour is closing a session without capturing splits; here that failure ships as a product with money attached, so the lazy path must be the correct path. Their work is in a DAW, not a browser — anything requiring them to leave the session to fill in a web form will not get done. |
| Operator | None | No column, deliberately. A venue or studio operator sells time and space (personas.md: "their inventory is time and space, not goods or labour"); they buy tools for rooms, not loops for a creative practice. An Operator sample-buying workflow would be invented, not observed. **What they see instead**: nothing — the catalog is absent from their surface. The one adjacent case (a studio clear-out) is 14.06, not this. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface, structurally. A fan has no DAW and no use for a loop, and personas.md is explicit they will not tolerate professional-tool complexity. **What they see instead**: nothing here. Their only digital-goods touchpoint is buying an artist's music through domain 20, which reuses the delivery layer (14.03) and never this catalog. |

## Behavior

A pack is a listing (14.01.01) of product type `sample-pack` whose spec sheet is musical rather
than technical: file count, formats (WAV/AIFF), sample rate and bit depth, total size, and per-file
metadata (BPM, key, instrument, loop vs one-shot). Its contents are indexed per file (CX-01) so
buyers can browse and audition individual sounds before purchase. **The pack is the billing unit;
the file is the searchable unit; the asset is the unit of use** (14.03 CX-05) — one purchase becomes
N addressable assets in the buyer's library, each carrying the terms it was bought under.

### 1. Audition is the inspection, not a player widget

A plugin gets a trial (14.02.06); a WAV cannot expire — per 14.02.06 DT-02, **audition is the trial
for content**, and it inherits the priority that feature would otherwise have. It plays the actual
file, streamed, complete — not a truncation and not a curated demo reel.

- **Complete, not truncated.** A 10-second slice of a 4-bar loop is fine; a truncated one-shot is
  meaningless, and a truncated 8-bar melodic loop hides the resolution the buyer is deciding on.
  Truncation degrades the decision without degrading the theft (a scraper reassembles clips).
- **Lossy, not lossless.** Audition is served at a lossy quality sufficient to judge a sound in
  context on monitors, audibly inferior to the 24-bit source on a full-range system. Basis: this
  is the market standard (Splice built a business on it) and the only protection that does not tax
  the buyer. Exact posture is Q-01 — a **product** decision, with a recommendation below.
- **Fast.** Audition must begin sounding within **P95 < 300 ms** of the click. A producer auditions
  40–60 loops in a sitting; at ~500 ms of latency per click the browse stops being a browse and
  becomes a chore, and the buyer leaves. This is a correctness requirement for the feature's core
  loop, not a performance nicety.
- **Free and unmetered for browsing.** No entitlement is required to audition (that *is* the
  audition); the store scope shows metadata and audio for unowned packs (CX-01 q3).

### 2. Metadata: declared, extracted, and reconciled per field — not per pack

Resolves Q-03. Both a vendor declaration and a platform extraction exist; **neither globally
overrides the other, because their reliability differs by field.** Treating "metadata" as one
trust decision is the error — these are three epistemically different classes:

| Field class | Fields | Extraction reliability | Policy on disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic** (read from the file) | sample rate, bit depth, channel count, duration, file format, byte size | **Exact** — it is a header read, not an estimate | **Extraction wins, unconditionally.** A declaration that disagrees means *the listing would lie* → hard blocker at submission per 14.08.03 D-01. "48 kHz / 24-bit" that is actually 44.1/16 is a misdescription, and no waiver touches misdescription (14.09.01 DT-02). |
| **Derivable** (arithmetic, given one vendor input) | BPM of a **loop** | **Near-exact** — a loop's tempo is arithmetic once bar count is known: a 4-bar loop of 6.760 s is 142.0 BPM. Not estimation. | Computed value wins; disagreement >1% is flagged to the vendor at submission as "your loop is not the bar count you declared, or the tempo is wrong." Vendor may correct the bar count; they may not override the arithmetic. **BPM on a one-shot is null, never zero and never inherited from the pack.** |
| **Estimated** (genuinely hard) | key, instrument, character/genre tags | **Unreliable** — key detection is a real open problem, and frequently meaningless (a kick has no key; a noise sweep has no key; a modal loop has several defensible answers) | **Neither wins. Record both, rank the agreement** (14.04.04 D-04, 14.08.03 D-02). Declared-only, extracted-only, and both-agree are three different states; only *both-agree* ranks highly. Extraction **never silently overwrites** a vendor's declared key — per 14.04.04 DT-02, complete-and-wrong is worse than absent, and a confident wrong extraction that overrides a right declaration is the worst of the four outcomes. |

Fields are **nullable and honest** — never force-filled (14.04.04 D-03). A vendor who declares no key
for a percussion pack has done the right thing and must not be nagged into inventing one.

### 3. The file carries its own metadata — written at ingest, not at delivery

Per DT-03. The index knows the loop is 142 BPM in G minor; **the file must know too.** The
destination is a DAW, not our web page — a loop dropped into a session at the wrong tempo, requiring
manual warping, is a broken product regardless of how good the search was. So the audio carries
embedded tempo/key/loop-point metadata in the file itself (the market's long-standing convention:
ACIDized WAV, Apple Loops, embedded loop markers), written from the reconciled values above.
Confidence travels: an *estimated* key is embedded only where declaration and extraction agree —
we do not stamp a guess into a file that will outlive our database.

**Where the write happens is settled, and it is not here.** Per `14.03.01` D-12 the embed runs
**once at ingest**, in `14.08.03` audio QC, into the stored master — not per delivery. The reason is
DT-03's own: "142 BPM, G minor" is a fact about the *product*, identical for every buyer, so
computing it per buyer bought nothing and cost the delivery layer its integrity contract (a single
published SHA-256 cannot describe bytes that are rewritten on the way out). The buyer-visible
behaviour is unchanged — they drag the file in and it stretches — because the master they are
served already carries the metadata. A **metadata correction** is consequently a re-write of the
master and a new hash, which lands on `14.03.02` D-05, not on the delivery path.

### 4. Rights flow inbound before they flow outbound

Terms (14.02.05) are bound at purchase and travel with every asset into the buyer's library
(14.03 CX-05) and onward into projects (07) and releases (09/12). But the store's real exposure
runs the other way (DT-02, domain DT-B): the defence is **attestation at submission** (14.08.01),
not marking bytes on the way out (14.03.05). Two attestations are required and they are different
questions — "is this yours?" and "if it is licensed, does that licence permit *this*?" (DT-06).

### 5. Removal is not deletion, and a rights takedown is not a revision

Resolves the breadth pass's open removed-loop case, and distinguishes two events the pack lifecycle
conflates:

| Event | Trigger | For new buyers | For existing buyers | Notification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vendor revision** | Vendor ships v2: adds, replaces or drops loops | New version sells | **Nothing is taken.** The v1 asset remains entitled, downloadable from the legacy archive (14.03.02), and present in the library — an asset already in a session cannot vanish because a vendor shipped v2 (14.03 CX-06). Buyer tags survive (14.03 CX-05 q1). | Optional "v2 available" |
| **Rights takedown** | The loop is uncleared / repudiated / DMCA'd | Distribution stops immediately (14.09.03 D-02 — delivery is the part we can guarantee) | Files already on disk cannot be recalled and releases cannot be un-released. The asset's terms record is marked **repudiated**, with date and reason. The entitlement row persists with the reason and never silently disappears (14.09.03 D-04). | **Mandatory, and this is the feature's most important act** — see DT-05 |

Packs are therefore **append-and-correct for existing buyers, versioned for new ones**. This
answers sub-domain Q-01 for this feature's half: content packs do version, but versioning is never
destructive to a held entitlement — which is why immutability is not needed to get immutability's
safety.

### Happy Path

**Vendor side**

1. Vendor uploads a pack (a folder of audio) through the vendor portal (14.08.01).
2. Audio QC runs (14.08.03): deterministic fields are read from headers; loop BPM is computed from
   declared bar counts; key is estimated. Findings are **dismissible warnings** by default — a
   crushed 808 is the sound, not a defect (14.08.03 D-01) — with one hard blocker class: the
   declared spec disagrees with the file (the listing would lie).
3. Intra-catalog content matching runs (DT-04): files are matched against the catalog's existing
   content. Exact matches against another vendor's pack halt the submission for review.
4. Vendor declares musical metadata per file. Anything unknown stays null. Extraction disagreements
   are shown as "we heard G minor; you said B♭ minor" — recorded, not resolved.
5. Vendor selects licence terms from templates (14.02.05 D-01) and — because the packs's contents
   are loops — answers the **master/publishing** question explicitly (DT-07), not under a
   "royalty-free" label that settles only half of it.
6. Vendor makes the two rights attestations (DT-06): *(a)* the audio is theirs or cleared, and
   *(b)* where any of it is licensed from elsewhere, that licence permits resale as part of a pack.
7. Vendor names contributors and proposes splits (14.10.03 D-01). Publish is **not** blocked on
   confirmation; **payout** is (14.10.03 D-02). A solo vendor claims 100% explicitly (14.10.03 D-03).
8. Pack publishes. Per-file indexing runs async — the pack is buyable and browsable-by-pack
   immediately, fully searchable-per-file when indexing completes (target **P95 < 10 min for packs
   ≤ 1,000 files**; progress is shown, not hidden).

**Buyer side**

9. Buyer searches "142 BPM, G minor, dusty break" across the store (14.04.04). Facets are
   relational: 71 and 284 BPM are in scope (half/double time), B♭ major is in scope (relative major).
10. Buyer auditions individual loops in-browser — the actual files, complete, streamed, lossy.
11. Buyer opens the pack: file list with waveforms, per-file metadata, agreement-confidence visible
    where it matters, and the terms rendered as three plain-language answers above the buy button
    (14.02.05 D-01) — never as an EULA.
12. Buyer purchases. Terms *version* is frozen onto the entitlement at issuance (14.02 CX-02).
13. The withdrawal waiver is captured at **first download**, not at checkout (14.09.01 D-01) — no
    waiver, no download URL (14.09.01 D-02); declining is a supported path (14.09.01 D-03).
14. The pack fans out into the buyer's asset library: one purchase, N addressable assets (14.03
    CX-05), each carrying its terms reference and its embedded metadata.
15. Buyer uses a loop in a project (07). The asset's terms travel into the clearance chain (09) and
    are checked at release (12) — where sample and AI disclosure are increasingly demanded.
16. Three years later a rights question arises. The answer is a record with a receipt behind it,
    not a memory. **That is the entire point of the domain having this feature.**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **— Rights: inbound (the domain's central exposure, DT-02) —** | | |
| Pack contains an uncleared third-party sample | **The domain's worst case.** Every downstream buyer's release is exposed, each with a WeJammin receipt vouching for its cleanliness. Attestation at submission makes it *attributable*, not *prevented* (14.02.05 Q-03). QC is structurally blind to it — loudness analysis cannot see a Motown break (14.08.03 DT-02). | Vendor: an attestation naming them personally, durably. Buyers: nothing — until the takedown fires, at which point see below. |
| Vendor resells loops from another store's "royalty-free" pack | **The common case, and it is not the Motown case** (DT-06). The upstream licence exists but forbids resale-in-isolation — the exact clause 14.02.05 lists as near-universal for loops. The vendor is not lying; they misunderstand "royalty-free" to mean "resellable". | A second attestation asking the *specific* question, not the general one: "if any audio here is licensed from elsewhere, does that licence permit reselling it inside a pack?" — with the plain-language note that royalty-free almost never does. |
| The same loop appears in two vendors' packs | Intra-catalog content match (DT-04). Either vendor B copied vendor A, or one sound was legitimately licensed into both — indistinguishable from the audio alone. | Exact match → submission halts for review, both vendors' attestations surfaced to the reviewer. Perceptual (non-exact) match → recorded as a signal, not a block. **Never resolved automatically**: an automated "vendor B is a thief" verdict on ambiguous evidence is the accusation-without-due-process failure 14.09.03 DT-02 exists to prevent. |
| Vendor uploads AI-generated content without disclosing | Flows to the AI Provenance & Disclosure cross-cut; DSPs increasingly require the disclosure at release (12). The sharper problem is volume, not disclosure — see DT-08. | Attestation required at submission. AI-declared packs are labelled in the catalog, because a buyer's DSP will ask them and they must be able to answer. |
| Vendor's attestation is simply false | The platform cannot verify an upstream chain (14.02.05 Q-03). Fingerprinting against commercial recordings is the only automated defence and it costs real money (14.08.03 Q-01). | Traceability, not prevention: the attestation names a person who asserted it, with a timestamp. Exposure is attributable rather than diffused into nobody. |
| Pack taken down for a rights defect; 400 buyers hold it, some have released records | Distribution stops; local copies cannot be recalled; releases cannot be un-released; the clearance those buyers hold was **never valid** — this is not 14.02.05 Q-02's refund case, where the clearance was good at the time. | **Every holder is notified, naming the specific removed assets and the reason.** Only WeJammin knows who they are. See DT-05 — this is the only mitigation that exists. |
| Buyer refunds a pack (14.09) but already released a track with it | Different from the above: the clearance was valid when bound. Recommendation stands — revoke future use, preserve past clearance (14.02.05 Q-02, 14.09.03 Q-03). | Entitlement revoked forward; the release's clearance record retains the terms version and the receipt that was valid at the time. |
| **— Rights: the multi-hyphenate, acting in two roles at once —** | | |
| Producer sells a pack made from a band's session; the drummer played it | **The nastiest case in this feature, and splits do not fix it.** The drummer consented to their performance being on *a record*. Nobody asked whether it could become a commercial sample pack sold to strangers in perpetuity — a materially different grant. 14.10.03 D-01 captures the *percentage*; it does not capture the *permission*. A 30% share of a use they'd have refused is not consent. | Contributor confirmation must ask two questions, not one: "do you agree to this split?" **and** "do you agree this recording is sold as a sample pack?" A contributor may accept the split and refuse the use — and that refusal blocks publish, because unlike a split, it is not something money can settle later. |
| Vendor buys their own pack | Legitimate (testing the buyer flow) and abusable (inflating download counts, farming the royalty pool 14.10). | Purchase permitted; **excluded from download counts, rankings and pool accrual** by same-payee detection. Not blocked — blocking it teaches vendors to use a second account, which is worse. |
| Producer publishes a pack containing loops from a pack they bought here | The chain problem, arriving with money attached (14.10.03 Q-02). Our own terms registry knows the upstream terms machine-readably — the one case where the platform *can* answer the resale question rather than ask it. | The attestation is pre-answered from the bound terms: "the pack you sourced this from forbids resale in isolation — this submission cannot proceed." The only place DT-06's question has a real answer. |
| **— Audition —** | | |
| Audition streams the full file at full quality | The audition *is* the product; nobody buys. | Lossy, complete, unmetered. The buying decision survives; the reassembly is worth less than the pack. See Q-01. |
| Bot scrapes all 800 auditions and reassembles the pack | The reassembled artifact is a lossy, unlabelled version of a £10–£50 product, obtained at real effort. **The person who does this was never a buyer** — the exact asymmetry 14.02.06 DT-01 established for trial abuse: every tightening punishes the honest browser (who legitimately auditions hundreds of files), and the evader loses nothing when refused. | Abnormal-volume detection is a **signal for review**, not an automatic block. A producer auditioning 400 loops in an evening is the intended behaviour and must never be rate-limited into stopping. |
| Audition of a file removed between browse and purchase | The buyer heard it; the pack no longer contains it. | The audition player reports the specific file as withdrawn and why-class (revised / taken down). Purchase proceeds without it; if the withdrawn file was the reason for the purchase, that is a refund case (14.09.02 — "not as described"). |
| Network drops mid-audition | Streamed, so nothing is lost but the sound. | Playback stops with an inline retry on that file. The browse and the result set survive — never a page-level error for one file's stream. |
| Audition unavailable while the catalog is searchable | Search results a buyer cannot hear are close to worthless (14.04.04). | Degrade honestly: results render with audition disabled and a stated reason. Never a silent dead play button. |
| **— Metadata (see Behavior §2) —** | | |
| Vendor's key metadata is wrong | Poisons every search it appears in, and the buyer blames the search, not the vendor (14.04.04). | Recorded alongside extraction, never overridden; the disagreement lowers rank. Systematic offenders flow to QA (sub-domain Q-03). |
| Vendor's declared spec disagrees with the file | The listing would lie. This is the one hard blocker class (14.08.03 D-01). | Submission blocked: "you declared 48 kHz/24-bit; these 340 files are 44.1 kHz/16-bit." |
| Key is genuinely ambiguous or meaningless | A kick has no key; a noise sweep has no key; an atonal texture has no key. | Null, and null is correct. Nothing is force-filled (14.04.04 D-03). |
| Vendor declares a 4-bar loop that is actually 3.5 bars | Arithmetic disagrees with the declaration >1%. Almost always a trimming error — a real defect the vendor cannot hear and would want to know about (14.08.03 DT-01's "second pair of ears"). | Warning at submission with the computed value: "this is 4.57 bars at your declared 142 BPM — the tail may be trimmed short." |
| Pack padded with near-duplicates ("1,000 loops!" — 400 are one loop at four tempos) | Real market practice; file count is the headline number vendors inflate. | Intra-pack near-duplicate detection (DT-04's mechanism, turned inward). The listing shows **distinct sounds** alongside file count. Not blocked — variants are legitimate — but the buyer sees both numbers. |
| Some files have metadata, others don't | The normal state, not an error. | Partial state: searchable on what exists, browsable for the rest. Never an empty column pretending to be a value. |
| **— Scale, concurrency, races —** | | |
| 5,000-file pack | Indexing is async; browsing must not require loading 5,000 rows. | Progressive: "indexing 3,200 of 5,000". Buyable throughout. |
| Buyer purchases while indexing is incomplete | The purchase is valid — indexing is a search concern, not an entitlement one. | Library shows the assets appearing as they index; the entitlement is complete from the moment of purchase. |
| Vendor edits metadata while indexing runs | Two writers on the same per-file record. | Last-declared wins for the declaration; extraction is not re-run (the audio did not change). Index reconciles on completion. No partial mixed state is ever served — a file is either indexed at a version or not indexed. |
| Vendor unpublishes mid-checkout | Buyer is at the buy button on a pack that no longer exists. | Checkout fails **at payment, before any charge**, with the reason. Never charge for a pack that cannot be delivered. |
| Two vendors submit identical packs simultaneously | Neither is published yet; the intra-catalog match has nothing to match against. | Both halt on match-against-each-other. Reviewed as one case; the tie is broken by evidence, not by timestamp — first-to-upload is not first-to-own. |
| Revocation lands mid-download of a large pack | 14.09.03 Q-01, arriving here. | Deferred to that feature — it owns the answer. This feature's obligation is that the *library row persists with a reason* (14.09.03 D-04). |
| **— Malicious input —** | | |
| Pack archive contains path-traversal filenames (`../../`) | A folder of audio is an archive from an untrusted user. | Rejected at submission with the offending paths named. Structural, not content — a hard blocker. |
| "Audio" file is not audio (executable, zip bomb, 900 MB "loop") | Untrusted upload masquerading as content. | Rejected: content is validated as decodable audio, not by extension. |
| Metadata injection into facets (BPM `999999`, key `<script>`, 40 KB "instrument" tag) | The facets are user-authored strings and numbers rendered to other users and fed to an index. | Range-validated (BPM outside a musical range is rejected, not clamped), tags are a controlled vocabulary plus bounded free text, all rendered escaped. |
| 4-hour file declared a "loop" | Not a loop; likely a whole track or an upload error. | Warning, not a block — a 4-hour field recording in a foley pack is legitimate. Loop-classification is dropped rather than asserted. |
| Empty pack, or a pack of silence | The degenerate submissions. | Empty → blocked (nothing to sell). All-silence → warning per 14.08.03 D-01, because "silence" may be intentional (room tone, noise floor samples) and a gate that cannot tell would reject a correctly-made product. |
| **— Boundaries with siblings —** | | |
| Buyer wants 6 loops from an 800-file pack | The pack is the billing unit; per-file purchase is a different economic model with different terms and a different contributor pool. | See Q-02 — the Splice-credits question, and a genuine fork. |
| Pack contains presets as well as audio (a drum library with Kontakt patches) | The product-type boundary is porous (CX-05). | Modelled as one product with mixed content: it is one thing the vendor made, one thing the buyer buys, and one price. Facets are per-content-type (CX-02) so the patches index on preset facets and the WAVs on sample facets — **the mixed pack is exactly why the index needs per-type schemas rather than a shared one.** |
| A template (14.04.03) bundles this pack's loops | Redistribution of another vendor's licensed content, forbidden by the very terms it was sold under (CX-04). | Not this feature's fix — but its terms record is what makes the answer knowable. See 14.04.03 DT-02. |
| Buyer's collaborator uses the pack in a shared project (07) | Terms bind the buyer; the project is shared. The collaborator is probably unlicensed (14.02.05 Q-04) — common and invisible. | Not resolvable here; the asset carries its terms and 07/14.02.05 owns whether they reach a collaborator. Flagged loudly rather than assumed away. |
| Vendor exits the platform (14.08.05) | Perpetual entitlements outlive the vendor's interest in honouring them. | Entitlement and library access persist; that is what perpetual means (14.08.05). The pack delists for new buyers. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Catalog/listing fetch in flight | Grid skeleton with waveform placeholders. Never a spinner over a blank page — the grid shape is known before the data is. |
| **Empty (search)** | No packs match the query | Facet-relaxation suggestions grounded in musical relationships, not "try again": "no 142 BPM in G minor — try ±4 BPM, 71/284 BPM, or B♭ major." |
| **Empty (vendor draft)** | Vendor has uploaded nothing / declared no terms | Publish gate stating what is missing, in order: audio → terms → attestations → contributors. |
| **Empty (library scope)** | Buyer owns nothing matching | The honest answer is "nothing you own." Store results appear as a separate band, **never interleaved** (14.03.04 D-02) — the library scope's answer must be unambiguous. |
| **Populated** | Packs listed | Grid with waveforms, audition controls, key/BPM (with agreement confidence where it matters), price, terms answers. |
| **Error (catalog)** | Fetch failed | Retry; cached results if available. |
| **Error (index)** | Index unavailable | Fall back to browse-by-pack. Never a blank page (14.04.04). |
| **Error (audition)** | Stream failed for one file | Inline retry on that file only. The result set survives. |
| **Error (terms)** | Terms fetch failed | Full text as fallback. **Never render an assumed permissive default** (14.02.05) — a wrong "yes, commercial use is fine" is the worst string this product can display. |
| **Partial (indexing)** | Pack published, per-file index incomplete | Buyable, browsable by pack, not yet fully searchable per file. "Indexing 3,200 of 5,000." |
| **Partial (metadata)** | Some files have key/BPM, others null | Searchable on what exists. Nulls render as absent, not as "unknown" implying a lookup will fill them. |
| **Partial (attestation)** | Vendor submitted, attestations incomplete | Cannot publish. Distinct from the split state — splits block *payout*, attestations block *publish*, because a wrong split is fixable with money and a wrong attestation is not fixable at all. |
| **Repudiated** | Rights takedown fired on a held asset | The asset persists in the library, visibly marked with date and reason, never silently removed (14.09.03 D-04). This state exists because the buyer needs to act on it, and a vanished row would tell them nothing. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Per-file audition is a UI nicety — a preview player on a listing | The candidate's parenthetical ("per-file audition") reads as a display detail | ❌ REJECTED — it is **the entire pre-purchase inspection for this product type**, and it inherits the weight 14.02.06 DT-02 assigns to trials. A plugin can be trialled; a WAV cannot expire, so there is no trial to offer. Meanwhile the good is non-returnable the instant it downloads (14.09.01) — so without audition, buying an 800-file pack is an irreversible bet on a marketing reel and a genre tag. That is a worse deal than the market standard (Splice built a business on auditioning before buying) and buyers will read it as one. The tension nobody names: audition must be *good enough to decide with* and *not good enough to use*, and those two requirements point in opposite directions (Q-01). It is a peer of the compatibility checker, not a player widget. |
| DT-02 | The vendor risk is buyers pirating packs | The candidate list's framing — watermarking, DRM, blacklisting all point this way | ❌ REJECTED — the far larger and wholly unmodelled risk is **the platform selling a pack whose vendor never held the rights**. A bedroom producer chops a Motown break, ticks "royalty-free, commercial use OK", and sells 400 copies; 400 releases now carry an uncleared sample, each with a WeJammin receipt vouching for its cleanliness. Piracy costs a vendor a sale; this costs 400 *buyers* their records and exposes the platform that brokered it. Personas.md names the mechanism exactly ("uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream") — the store multiplies its blast radius from a band to a customer base. | DT-05 | When a pack is taken down for a rights defect, the platform's obligation ends at stopping distribution | The natural read of a takedown — it is a *stop* instruction, and 14.09.03 D-02 says delivery is the part we can guarantee | ❌ REJECTED — **the obligation *starts* there.** Stopping distribution protects future buyers and the platform. It does precisely nothing for the 400 people who already bought it, half of whom have shipped records built on a loop that was never clean — which is the entire harm DT-02 identifies, and it has already happened by the time a takedown fires. And here is the fact that makes this non-optional: **only WeJammin knows who they are.** No DSP knows, no PRO knows, the buyers do not know, and the vendor who caused it has every incentive to say nothing. We hold the receipt list. A platform that holds the only list of injured parties and stays quiet has made a choice, and it is not a defensible one. So the takedown's notification is not customer service — it is the *only mitigation that exists*, and it converts an unrecoverable loss into a survivable one (a buyer who knows can clear the sample retroactively, replace it, or pull the track before the claim arrives; a buyer who doesn't finds out from a copyright strike). Two consequences. First, it is the exact inverse posture from the quiet clawback (14.09.03 D-01 — never overstate to a vendor); here the failure is *understating to a buyer*. Second, it is a fan-out we can only aim if we know *use*, not just purchase — which is DT-06's problem, and it means a takedown notifies everyone who bought the pack rather than everyone affected. | DT-06 | The receipt proves the loop is cleared, so the purchase is the provenance capture | 14.02.05 DT-02 and domain DT-C — "a purchased sample *is a fact about a recording*, and the store is present at the exact moment it becomes true, with a payment receipt as evidence" | ❌ REJECTED **as stated — and this is the seam under domain 14's thesis claim.** The receipt proves **the right**, not **the use**. Those are different facts and the clearance record needs the second one. What the store observes is "this person bought 6 packs"; what a release's clearance record must assert is "this track contains loop 47 from pack X under terms v2". Two honest consequences: (1) 14.02.05's happy-path step 5 ("buyer uses an asset in a project; the asset carries its terms") is *conditional on the use being observable*, which it is not today, and the condition should be stated rather than assumed; (2) it re-prices domain Q-05 — a desktop/DAW surface is not a convenience for large downloads, it is the difference between capturing use and inferring it, which is the difference between provenance and a spreadsheet. ✅ The salvageable, still-strong claim: **the purchase is the cleanest capture of the *right* that exists anywhere in music** — no memory, no reconstruction, a receipt behind it. | DT-07 | "Royalty-free" settles what a buyer owes on a loop | The term of art, used universally in this market, and 14.02.05's own clause list ("royalty-free vs royalty-bearing") | ❌ REJECTED — **"royalty-free" is ambiguous between the master and the composition, and the industry has been quietly litigating the gap for a decade.** Two rights live in a melodic loop: the recording (master) and the underlying musical work (publishing). "Royalty-free" is near-universally understood to mean *free of master royalties* — you may use the recording without per-unit payment. It does not automatically settle whether a 4-bar chord progression, used as the harmonic bed of a released song, produced a **compositional contribution** — and some sample labels do claim a writer share on exactly that basis. A producer buys an £8 pack, builds a hit on loop 47, and discovers the publishing question was never asked, let alone answered. That is D-18's failure mode with a receipt in the room, which is worse than the ordinary one, because everyone thought it was handled. The distinction is also *predictable from the metadata this feature already collects*: **loop vs one-shot** and **melodic vs percussive** are exactly the axes that decide whether a publishing claim is even coherent. A one-shot kick is a short functional sound with a thin-to-nonexistent compositional claim; an 8-bar melodic loop is a composition, and using it creates a derivative work. So this feature's metadata is not just a search facet — it is the **strongest available predictor of which packs will produce a rights claim**, which is a fact about the terms registry and about domain 10, not about search. Concretely: the terms template for a pack containing melodic loops must ask the publishing question explicitly rather than let "royalty-free" imply an answer to it. The precise legal boundary (is a one-shot copyrightable? does a 2-bar loop cross the threshold?) is genuinely unsettled and jurisdictional — an owner/counsel call (Q-04), not an agent's. But the *product* failure is not unsettled at all: the question is currently not being asked. |
| DT-08 | Undisclosed AI content is a disclosure problem, handled by the AI Provenance cross-cut | The cross-cut's existence (domain D-03) and the sweep's candidate 24, which frames it as an attestation | ❌ REJECTED **as sufficient — the disclosure machinery is right and it does not address the actual risk, which is volume.** Generating 500 usable loops is now nearly free and nearly instant, while recording a drum kit costs a day, a room, and a drummer. A store whose entire supply thesis is user-uploaded content (domain DT-A) has therefore built its inventory strategy on the one supply that just became infinite. The failure is not that some packs are secretly AI — it is that an unbounded, zero-marginal-cost supply out-competes the human catalog on volume, buries it in search, and turns the store into a content farm with a rights problem attached. Two aggravations. First, the attestation is **unanswerable in good faith** for AI output: a vendor cannot attest to an upstream chain their model will not disclose, so the honest AI vendor and the dishonest one produce the same tick. Second, it collides with 14.04.04 Q-02: once the catalog is large, **ranking is the product** — and ranking a catalog where supply is free means ranking is the only thing standing between the store and worthlessness, which makes it the highest-value gaming target on the platform (a gaming surface with a zero-cost supply behind it). This is a moderation-load and catalog-economics fact (D-13's shape, arriving in a marketplace) before it is a labelling one, and it is presently owned by nobody. Escalated as Q-05 — it plausibly bears on domain Q-03 (is creator content really the launch inventory?), because the answer changes if the creators can be out-supplied by a script. |
