# Feature: Sample & Loop Pack Catalog

> **Number**: 14.04.01
> **Parent**: [14.04 Sound Content Catalogs](./14.04-sound-content-catalogs-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Selling collections of audio — loops, one-shots, breaks, vocal chops, foley — with the musical
metadata that makes them findable and the per-file audition that makes them buyable. This is the
most plausible thing domain 14 ever sells, because the vendors are the platform's own users: a
producer with a folder can list today, with no business-development motion and no reseller
agreement (14.01.01 DT-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Buys packs for their own work; **sells** packs made from their own sessions. For a working musician this is a real income stream in the portfolio personas.md describes — a drummer selling their kit samples. Auditions before buying; searches by tempo and key. |
| Producer | Full | The core persona on both sides. As buyer: searches against the track in progress and needs per-file audition because a pack is 800 files and 6 of them matter. As vendor: the archetypal pack author, and the one who must declare metadata and — critically — attest the upstream rights (14.02.05 DT-03). |
| Operator | None | No column here. A venue or studio operator buys tools for rooms, not loops for a creative practice. Explicit absence: an Operator sample-buying workflow would be invented, not observed. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface, structurally. A fan has no DAW and no use for a loop; personas.md is explicit that they will not tolerate professional-tool complexity. |

## Behavior

A pack is a listing (14.01.01) of product type `sample-pack` whose spec sheet is musical rather
than technical: file count, formats (WAV/AIFF), sample rate and bit depth, total size, and per-file
metadata (BPM, key, instrument, loop vs one-shot). Its contents are indexed per file (CX-01) so
buyers can browse and audition individual sounds before purchase.

Audition is this feature's answer to the inspection problem: a plugin gets a trial (14.02.06), but
a WAV cannot expire — per 14.02.06 DT-02, **audition is the trial for content**. It must play the
actual file (not a teaser reel), streamed rather than downloaded, at a quality that supports a
buying decision without being the product.

Terms (14.02.05) are bound at purchase and travel with every asset into the buyer's library
(14.03 CX-05) and onward into projects (07).

### Happy Path

1. Vendor uploads a pack; audio QC runs (14.08.03) and metadata is extracted/verified (CX-01).
2. Vendor declares musical metadata, licence terms, and the upstream-rights attestation (14.08.01).
3. Pack publishes; contents are indexed per file, async.
4. Buyer searches "142 BPM, G minor, dusty break" across the store (14.04.04).
5. Buyer auditions individual loops in-browser — the actual files, streamed.
6. Buyer purchases; the pack fans out into their asset library (14.03 CX-05), each asset carrying its terms.
7. Buyer uses a loop in a project (07); the terms travel into the clearance chain (09).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor's key/BPM metadata is wrong | Poisons every search it appears in, and the buyer blames the search | Extraction cross-checks the declaration; systematic offenders flow to QA (index Q-03) |
| Pack contains an uncleared third-party sample | **The domain's worst case.** Every downstream buyer's release is exposed; personas.md names this as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour, here fanned out to strangers at purchase volume. | Attestation at submission makes it attributable, not preventable — 14.02.05 Q-03 |
| Audition streams the full file at full quality | The audition *is* the product; nobody buys | Streamed, rate-limited, and per Q-01 possibly marked — but a degraded audition can't support a buying decision either |
| 5,000-file pack | Indexing is async; browsing must not require loading 5,000 rows | Progressive: "indexing 3,200 of 5,000" |
| Buyer wants 6 loops from an 800-file pack | Pack is the billing unit; per-file purchase is a different economic model | See Q-02 — this is the Splice-credits question |
| Pack revised, a loop removed for a rights issue | Collides with released records built on it | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — 14.03 CX-06, 14.02.05 Q-02 |
| Vendor uploads AI-generated content without disclosing | Flows to the AI Provenance & Disclosure cross-cut; DSPs increasingly require the disclosure at release (12) | Attestation required at submission |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Per-file audition is a UI nicety — a preview player on a listing | The candidate's parenthetical ("per-file audition") reads as a display detail | ❌ REJECTED — it is **the entire pre-purchase inspection for this product type**, and it inherits the weight 14.02.06 DT-02 assigns to trials. A plugin can be trialled; a WAV cannot expire, so there is no trial to offer. Meanwhile the good is non-returnable the instant it downloads (14.09.01) — so without audition, buying an 800-file pack is an irreversible bet on a marketing reel and a genre tag. That is a worse deal than the market standard (Splice built a business on auditioning before buying) and buyers will read it as one. The tension nobody names: audition must be *good enough to decide with* and *not good enough to use*, and those two requirements point in opposite directions (Q-01). It is a peer of the compatibility checker, not a player widget. |
| DT-02 | The vendor risk is buyers pirating packs | The candidate list's framing — watermarking, DRM, blacklisting all point this way | ❌ REJECTED — the far larger and wholly unmodelled risk is **the platform selling a pack whose vendor never held the rights**. A bedroom producer chops a Motown break, ticks "royalty-free, commercial use OK", and sells 400 copies; 400 releases now carry an uncleared sample, each with a WeJammin receipt vouching for its cleanliness. Piracy costs a vendor a sale; this costs 400 *buyers* their records and exposes the platform that brokered it. Personas.md names the mechanism exactly ("uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream") — the store multiplies its blast radius from a band to a customer base. This inverts where the domain's real defensive investment goes: not marking bytes on the way out (14.03.05), but attesting rights on the way in (14.08.01, 14.02.05 DT-03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Per-file audition of the actual audio is in scope from the start, not a later enhancement | Per DT-01 — it is the only pre-purchase inspection this product type has, and the good is non-returnable. |
| D-02 | Pack contents are indexed per file; the pack is the billing unit, the file is the searchable unit | Per CX-01. Nobody searches for a pack. |
| D-03 | Upstream-rights attestation is required at submission, not optional | Per DT-02 — the platform's real exposure is inbound, not outbound. |
