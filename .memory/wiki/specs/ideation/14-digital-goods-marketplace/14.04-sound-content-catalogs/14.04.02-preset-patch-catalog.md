# Feature: Preset & Patch Catalog

> **Number**: 14.04.02
> **Parent**: [14.04 Sound Content Catalogs](./14.04-sound-content-catalogs-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Selling settings rather than sound — Serum banks, Kontakt patches, Diva presets, channel-strip
chains. The defining fact is that the product is **worthless without something else**: a preset is
a small text file that only means anything inside a specific synth the buyer must already own, at
a version that understands it. This makes the dependency graph (14.01.03) load-bearing here in a
way it never is for a WAV.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Buys presets to get a sound they can't dial in; sells banks made from their own patches. Low-effort income for a working musician — a preset bank is a byproduct of work already done, which fits the portfolio-of-small-streams economics personas.md describes. |
| Producer | Full | Both sides, heavily. As buyer, needs to know the *exact* synth and version before buying — a Serum 2 bank in Serum 1 is a folder of nothing. As vendor, the archetypal author. Also the persona who hits the audition problem hardest (DT-01). |
| Operator | None | No column. A studio does not buy preset banks; rooms need tools, not patches. Explicit absence. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. A fan owns no synth to load a preset into. |

## Behavior

A preset bank is a listing (14.01.01) of product type `preset-pack` whose spec sheet is almost
entirely a **dependency declaration**: target product, version range, and edition constraint
(14.01.03 D-01). It indexes on a different vocabulary from samples (CX-02) — synth, category
(bass/pad/lead/keys), character — with no BPM and usually no key.

Audition is harder than for samples: a preset makes no sound by itself. The vendor must supply
rendered audio demos of the presets in use, which are *representations* of the product rather than
the product — and that gap is where buyer disappointment lives (DT-01).

Presets are almost always key-less content: nothing to activate, nothing to blacklist
(14.02.04 DT-01), and a file trivially copyable. The enforcement story here is weaker than
anywhere else in the domain.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor declares the target synth, version range and edition constraint at submission.
2. Vendor uploads the bank and rendered audio demos of the presets in use.
3. System resolves the dependency against the catalog (14.01.03); publishes.
4. Buyer opens the listing; the dependency renders with owned/not-owned/unknown state.
5. Buyer auditions the rendered demos; searches by synth and category (14.04.04).
6. Buyer purchases; the bank lands in the library, terms bound (14.02.05).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer doesn't own the target synth | The entire product is unusable — a folder of unreadable text | Dependency check *before* purchase (14.01.03) is the whole defence |
| Buyer owns the synth but a version below the range (Serum 1 for a Serum 2 bank) | Dependency resolves to `unsatisfied` on the version axis, not the ownership axis — the buyer *has* the synth but it cannot read the file | "You own Serum, but this bank needs 2.0+. Yours is 1.3." — version is called out separately from ownership so the buyer knows an upgrade (not a purchase) fixes it |
| Target synth is Serum — **not sold here** | Dependency state is `unknown`, not `unsatisfied` (14.01.03 D-02); we cannot see a Native Instruments or Xfer library | "Requires Serum 1.3+ (not sold here) — check you have it" |
| Presets use wavetables/samples not shipped in the bank | Loads with missing components; sounds wrong or silent | Vendor must declare bundled vs referenced content — see Q-02 |
| Synth updates and changes preset format after purchase | **P-12 (D-04):** an **external compatibility change**, not a vendor conformity defect — the bank breaks through no fault of vendor or buyer and neither can fix it. The entitlement and the artifact are untouched (the buyer keeps both); what is lost is function, and that is disclosed rather than remedied. No default refund and no vendor obligation attach to a third-party change. The library entry stays with a compatibility flag. | "This bank targets Serum 1.x; your Serum is 2.0, which changed the preset format. The bank may not load." — the entry stays in the library, flagged, with the vendor's own update (if any) surfaced when it ships |
| Dependency delisted between add-to-cart and checkout (vendor exits mid-purchase) | Concurrency: the target synth was purchasable when the buyer added the bank, gone by checkout. Purchase is blocked at the gate rather than completing a sale for a product the buyer can no longer satisfy through us | "The synth this bank needs is no longer available here — you'll need to already own it to use this. Continue anyway?" |
| Buyer copies the bank to a friend | Nothing stops it. No key, no activation, no watermark (a preset has no audio payload to mark). | Honest position: this product type is unenforceable — see DT-02 |
| Preset bank for a synth whose vendor exits our store | Dependency delisted; the bank is still fine for existing owners but uncompletable for new buyers | 14.01.03 edge case |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Presets audition like samples — play the file, hear the product | The natural read, given 14.04.01's audition model and the sub-domain's shared shape | ❌ REJECTED — **a preset makes no sound.** It is a settings file; audio only exists when it is loaded into a synth the buyer may not own, played with notes someone chose, through a chain someone built. So what a buyer auditions is a *rendered demo the vendor produced* — a performance of the product, not the product. The gap between them is where every preset-buying disappointment lives: the demo has a melody, reverb, sidechain and mix bus; the preset, loaded cold, sounds thin. This is not a UI difference from 14.04.01, it is a different epistemic situation — for samples the audition *is* the good, for presets it is an advertisement. Honest options: standardise demos (dry/wet, single note vs phrase), or accept that presets carry irreducible pre-purchase uncertainty and that the refund policy (14.09) must absorb it. Neither is free, and the candidate list treats this as a listing type with no special problem. |
| DT-02 | Preset banks need the same protection as software | The candidate's flat treatment — "Preset & Patch Marketplace" alongside plugin sales, inheriting the domain's DRM apparatus | ❌ REJECTED — **this product type is structurally unenforceable and the domain should stop pretending otherwise.** A preset has no serial (nothing to blacklist, 14.02.04 DT-01), no activation check, and no audio payload to watermark (14.03.05 — you cannot mark a text file of parameter values). It is a few kilobytes, trivially copied, and the copy is bit-identical and fully functional. Every enforcement mechanism in this domain misses it entirely. That is worth stating plainly rather than discovering during spec-writing, and it has a real consequence: preset banks sell on *trust, price and the vendor's reputation*, not protection. Which points somewhere uncomfortable for the domain map — the thing that makes a preset bank sellable is the vendor's identity and credits (domains 01, 02), not this store's licensing machinery. The store is a checkout; the reputation is the product. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Presets index on their own facet schema (synth, category, character), not the sample schema | Per CX-02. A pad has no BPM; a shared schema produces empty columns — the nullable-column defect D-14 rejected, at metadata level. |
| D-02 | The dependency declaration is a required, gating field for this product type | Per the Role Lens: without the target synth, the product is literally unusable. This is the one field that must never be optional. |
| D-03 | The domain does not claim enforcement it does not have on preset banks | Per DT-02. Honest positioning beats a DRM story that misses the product type entirely. |
| D-04 | **P-12:** a host-update break is an **external compatibility change** — flag the library entry, disclose, **never revoke**; no default refund and no vendor conformity obligation | Ratifies into this file the pattern already locked at `14.07.01` D-04 ("'perpetual' is a promise about the entitlement and the artifact, **not a guarantee of function** — stated narrowly at the buy button, with the gap disclosed") and D-06 (a third-party state change means permit + mandatory disclosure + never revoke). That gives OS drift, lapsed dependencies and host breaks **one consistent story** instead of three. Classifying it as a vendor conformity defect would assign liability for a third party's act, against `14.09.02`'s supply-time conformity scope; a platform goodwill remedy would create discretionary cost with no source-defined trigger on a low-value product. What this decision does **not** supply is proactive detection — see Q-04. |
