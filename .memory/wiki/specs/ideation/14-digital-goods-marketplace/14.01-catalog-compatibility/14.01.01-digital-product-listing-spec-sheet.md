# Feature: Digital Product Listing & Spec Sheet

> **Number**: 14.01.01
> **Parent**: [14.01 Catalog & Compatibility](./14.01-catalog-compatibility-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The canonical record of a digital product for sale — what it is, who made it, what it costs, what
you get in the box, what it demands of your machine, and what you may do with the output. Unlike a
physical gear listing (13), there is no condition and no photograph of the actual unit; the listing
*is* a spec sheet, and its accuracy is the entire pre-purchase trust surface because the buyer
cannot inspect the good before committing to a non-returnable download (14.09.01).

Two facts make this listing different from every other store's. First, it must be **honest about a
combinatorial answer** — format × OS × CPU × host × dependency — where a wrong answer is a refund,
not a disappointment. Second, per DT-04, **the listing is a rights artifact, not just a sales
page**: the licence terms it displayed at the moment of purchase are what a track's clearance chain
(domain 09) will cite years later, which means the listing must remain renderable *as it appeared
then*, not merely reachable now.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Buyer**: reads the spec sheet, checks the 14.01.04 badge above the buy button, reads "also requires" (14.01.03) before committing. The lowest-patience reader — will not open a matrix table; the badge and the manifest headline are the whole surface for them. **Vendor**: the realistic day-one vendor per DT-02 — a musician with a folder of samples, presets or beats. Authors title, media, demos (labelled per DT-05), manifest, price, licence terms and the type-conditional required sections inside 14.08.01. Never ships an executable, so never sees a support matrix (D-02). |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest buyer **and** the archetypal vendor. **Buyer**: the only persona who reads the raw manifest — format/count/sample rate/unpacked size decide whether a 4 GB pack is worth a session's disk. Reads the vendor identity block (DT-07) because on this store, unlike Splice, they can see whose records the seller actually played on. **Vendor**: maintains the listing across versions (14.03.02) — a new build is a matrix change, not just a number — and is the persona who hits the multi-hyphenate collision: viewing their own listing while also holding a contributor split on someone else's (14.10.03). |
| Operator | Read-only | Buys studio software from the listing; never authors one. Reads three fields specifically: seat/licence terms (14.02.02), total unpacked size (a room's system drive is finite and shared), and the version range (14.07.01 D-01) — because a room pinned to an old OS needs to know what it is buying into. Cannot edit any field, including on a listing bought for their room. |
| Fan | None | The professional store is not on the fan surface. A fan buying music from an artist page (domain 20) never sees this listing type — they see a release, and only the delivery layer (14.03) is shared. Presenting a fan with a file manifest and a CPU-architecture row would be exactly the professional-tool complexity personas.md says they will not tolerate. |

## Behavior

A listing is a **structured record with type-conditional required sections**, not a rich-text blob
(D-01, D-02). The structure is what makes the store searchable (14.04.04), checkable (14.01.04),
and filterable — and what lets the platform block a publish that would predictably generate a
refund.

**One entity, five product types, per-type required-section schemas** (D-02). The types are
`plugin` · `sample-pack` · `preset-pack` · `template` · `beat`. Type is chosen first and is
**immutable after first publish** (D-03), because it determines which sections are required and
retyping a live listing orphans the data those sections hold.

### Required sections by product type

| Section | plugin | sample-pack | preset-pack | template | beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity — title, type, vendor block (DT-07) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Media — ≥1 image | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Audio demo, `made-with` (DT-05) | optional | ✅ ≥1 | ✅ ≥1 | ✅ ≥1 | ✅ ≥1 |
| Audio demo, `contents-only` (DT-05) | n/a | ✅ ≥1 | ✅ ≥1 | optional | n/a — the beat *is* the contents |
| File manifest — formats, file count, unpacked size, download size | ✅ (installers) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Compatibility matrix (14.01.02) | ✅ full axes | ⚠️ collapses to file format + sample rate + bit depth | ✅ host axes only | ✅ full axes | ⚠️ collapses to file format |
| Host dependencies (14.01.03) | optional | optional (a Kontakt library declares one; a WAV pack does not) | ✅ ≥1 — a preset without its synth is an unopenable file | ✅ ≥1 | n/a |
| Licence terms reference (14.02.05) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ per-tier |
| Monetisation model (14.07) | ✅ scalar price | ✅ scalar price | ✅ scalar price | ✅ scalar price | ✅ **per-tier prices** (D-08) |
| Transfer policy (14.06.01) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | n/a — exclusivity is the transfer (14.05.02) |
| Rights + AI attestation (14.08.01, domain CX) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

`✅` required to publish · `⚠️` required but axis-collapsed · `optional` offered, not gated ·
`n/a` section not rendered at all (absent, not empty — see States)

### Buyer-state rendering

The listing renders differently for a buyer depending on what they already hold. This is not
cosmetic — a store that sells you what you own is the most common store defect (DT-03), and
ownership here is **not boolean**: perpetual licences are version-bounded (14.07.01 D-01), so
ownership is a function of `(buyer, listing, version-range)`.

| Buyer state | Buy control renders as |
|---|---|
| Owns nothing | `Buy — £X` |
| Owns a version in the listing's current range | `You own this` + `Download` (→ 14.03.03) |
| Owns a version **below** the current range | `You own v1 · Upgrade to v3 — £X` (14.07.05) |
| Owns it and it was revoked (14.09.03) | `Buy — £X`, no history shown; revocation is not a purchase bar |
| Is the vendor | `Edit` + `View as buyer` (D-09); the buy control renders under the buyer lens — self-purchase is permitted and flagged, not blocked (14.02.01 D-11) |
| Is a contributor with a split (14.10.03) | Normal buy control + a `You're credited on this` marker |
| Logged out | `Buy — £X`; badge degrades to the 14.01.04 "Add your setup" CTA |

### Edit semantics

An edit to a published listing creates a new **listing revision**. Revisions matter for two
reasons and are otherwise invisible:

1. **Price and terms bind as displayed, not as current** (D-04, following domain CX-04 q5).
2. **The revision displayed at purchase is snapshotted onto the order** (D-05), so a delisted or
   re-termed listing can still be rendered as the buyer saw it when a clearance chain (09) or a
   refund adjudication (14.09.02) asks what they were told.

Price and terms diverge deliberately under D-04. Price is a merchandising number and locks in the
buyer's favour. Terms are the vendor's legal position and cannot be silently held against them —
so a terms change breaks the cart line's consent rather than freezing it.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor opens the submission flow (14.08.01) and picks a product type — the type determines which sections above are required, and the choice is final once published (D-03).
2. Vendor fills title, description, media, and audio demos; each demo is labelled `made-with` or `contents-only` (D-06). For a sample-pack or preset-pack, publish is blocked until at least one `contents-only` demo exists.
3. Vendor uploads the build/content for QA (14.08.02) and declares the file manifest; QA cross-checks the manifest against the uploaded artifact.
4. Vendor completes the compatibility matrix (14.01.02) and declares host dependencies (14.01.03) — publish is blocked until both are complete for the types that require them.
5. Vendor selects a monetisation model (14.07), attaches licence terms from a platform template (14.02.05 D-01), sets a transfer policy (14.06.01), and signs the rights + AI attestations (14.08.01 D-01).
6. System runs QA; on pass, and if payout readiness holds (14.08.01 D-04), the listing publishes and enters the catalog. The vendor identity block (D-07) is composed from the vendor's platform identity (01) and verified credits (02) — the vendor authors none of it.
7. A buyer opens the listing; the checker (14.01.04) renders a per-rig verdict badge above the buy button, and 14.01.03 renders "also requires" with live owned / not-owned / unknown state.
8. Buyer adds to cart. The price displayed at add-to-cart is held for **7 days** (D-04); the terms version displayed is recorded against the cart line.
9. Checkout is handled by the platform cart/payments cross-cut. At commit, the listing revision the buyer saw is snapshotted onto the order (D-05).
10. First delivery captures the withdrawal waiver (14.09.01); the entitlement binds the terms version that was displayed, not the vendor's current one (14.02.05 D-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor publishes with an empty compatibility matrix | Publish blocked at gate (CX-03) | Vendor: inline "Add at least one supported format and OS before publishing" on the blocked section |
| Vendor publishes a preset-pack with no host dependency declared | Publish blocked — a preset without its synth is an unopenable file, and the declaration is the product's most important field (14.01.03) | Vendor: "Which synth are these presets for? Buyers cannot open them without it." |
| Vendor publishes a sample-pack with only a `made-with` demo | Publish blocked (D-06) | Vendor: "Add a walkthrough of the actual files. A demo track isn't the pack." |
| Declared download size materially understates actual build size | QA (14.08.02) flags mismatch between manifest and uploaded artifact; publish blocked | Vendor: "Manifest says 800 MB; upload is 4.2 GB. Correct the manifest or re-upload." |
| Manifest file **count** understates the artifact (200 declared, 20,000 actual) | Same gate as size. Count is not cosmetic — it is what tells a buyer whether "4 GB" is 200 loops or an unusable dump | Vendor: "Manifest says 200 files; upload contains 20,412. Correct the manifest." |
| QA reads the binary and finds a format the vendor did not declare (ships CLAP, declared VST3 only) | Under-declaration is not a safety failure — publish proceeds, vendor is prompted | Vendor: "Your build also contains CLAP. Add it to the matrix to be found by CLAP buyers." |
| QA reads the binary and finds a declared format **absent** (declares AAX, no AAX binary) | Over-declaration is a refund generator. Publish blocked | Vendor: "You declared AAX but the build contains no AAX plugin." See Q-01 for whether QA's read overrides the declaration for non-executable types. |
| Listing depends on a product the store does not sell (e.g. full Kontakt) | Dependency recorded as **external**; state is `unknown`, not unsatisfied (14.01.03 D-02); no add-to-cart offered | Buyer: "Also requires Kontakt 7 full (not sold here) — check you have it" |
| **Vendor raises price while the item sits in buyers' carts** | Cart line keeps the price displayed at add-to-cart for the 7-day hold (D-04). At hold expiry the line re-prices with a visible diff and checkout blocks until the buyer accepts the new price | Buyer: "Price changed from £29 to £39 while this was in your cart. Accept the new price to continue, or remove it." |
| **Vendor lowers price while the item sits in buyers' carts** | Cart line re-prices **down immediately**; no consent required — a lower price is never adverse (D-04) | Buyer: "Price dropped to £19 — your cart updated." |
| **Vendor changes licence terms while the item sits in buyers' carts** | Terms do **not** lock (D-04). The cart line's consent is invalidated and checkout blocks until the buyer reviews the clause diff — binding a buyer to terms the vendor has withdrawn is as wrong as switching them silently | Buyer: "The licence for X changed. Review what's different before you buy." + clause-level diff. |
| Vendor delists while the item sits in a cart | Cart line becomes unpurchasable; the line is not silently removed | Buyer: "X is no longer for sale. Removed from your total — remove the line to continue." |
| **Beat exclusivity sold (14.05.02) while two artists both hold it in cart** | Auto-delist is atomic with the exclusive sale (14.05.02 D-01). Exactly one cart converts; the loser's line is invalidated at checkout, not at add-to-cart — a race the buyer cannot see coming | Losing buyer: "This beat was sold exclusively while you were checking out. You have not been charged." + link to the beatmaker's remaining catalog. |
| Vendor edits the product **type** after publish (sample-pack → plugin) | Rejected (D-03). Type determines the required-section schema; retyping orphans the sections the old type required and leaves the new type's gates unfilled on a live listing | Vendor: "Product type can't change after publishing. Create a new listing and retire this one." |
| Two vendors of an org/band entity (domain 01) edit the same listing draft concurrently | Last-write-wins would silently destroy a matrix. Section-level edit locks; a section held by another editor is read-only with attribution | Vendor B: "Sam is editing the Compatibility section." Sections not held remain editable. |
| Vendor's session drops mid-draft after 40 minutes of work | Draft persists per-section as completed, not as one submit (the completion-of-work rule the vendor flow depends on — a lost draft is a lost vendor the store cannot afford, per DT-02) | Vendor on return: "Draft restored — 6 of 9 required sections complete." |
| **Vendor buys their own listing** | Permitted and flagged, never blocked (14.02.01 D-11). Blocking it contradicts personas.md's structural fact — one human is vendor and buyer in the same day (14.02.01 DT-10) — and teaches vendors to buy through a second account, which is strictly worse (14.04.01). The sales-rank and laundering exposure is answered at issuance instead: the entitlement is flagged as self-purchase (holder principal == the product's vendor principal, or an org they control) and excluded downstream from payout (14.10), sales rank, review eligibility and loyalty accrual (14.07.05). The control lives in the buyer lens the vendor already has — `View as buyer` (D-09), Q-07 | Vendor: `Edit` + `View as buyer`; under the buyer lens, the ordinary buy control. Nothing is said to the buyer (14.02.01) — the vendor dashboard shows the sale excluded from earnings, with the reason. |
| **Vendor puts an off-platform purchase link in the description** ("buy direct on my site, 30% off") | Publish blocked. This is the Musician's named anti-persona — using the platform to find the buyer, then taking the deal off-platform to dodge fees. Informational links (a YouTube walkthrough, the vendor's site) are allowed; links to an external checkout/cart/payment page are not (D-10) | Vendor: "External purchase links aren't allowed. Links to your site and demo videos are fine." |
| Vendor puts HTML/script in the description or title | Rejected at input; description is structured/limited markup, never raw HTML — the listing is rendered to every buyer in the store | Vendor: "Formatting isn't supported here. Use the description's list and heading controls." |
| Vendor puts a third party's trademark in the title ("Serum Presets by X") | Not auto-blocked — nominative use is legitimate and *necessary* here; a Serum preset pack must say Serum. Handled by the Disputes cross-cut on complaint, not by a publish gate that would break the store's most common product name | Vendor: publishes. Rights holder: routes through Disputes (24). |
| **Payout readiness lapses after publish** (vendor's payout account is suspended) | Publish gated on payout readiness (14.08.01 D-04), but readiness is not permanent. Listing auto-pauses: leaves the catalog for new buyers, stays fully live for existing owners (downloads, versions, terms) | Vendor: "Your listing is paused — your payout account needs attention." Buyer: listing not in catalog. Owner: unaffected. |
| Buyer already owns a version inside the current range | No buy control (DT-03) | "You own this" + Download → 14.03.03 |
| Buyer owns v1; listing now sells v3 outside their range | Upgrade path, not a repurchase (14.07.05) | "You own v1 · Upgrade to v3 — £29" — never a bare "Buy" that would sell them what they hold |
| Buyer's purchase was refunded and the licence revoked (14.09.03) | Listing renders normally with a live buy control. Revocation removed an entitlement; it is not a purchase bar | Buyer: standard listing. No "you refunded this" shaming. |
| Listing delisted — vendor **retirement** (14.08.05 D-03) | Leaves the catalog; remains reachable and renderable for existing owners, at the revision they bought (D-05). Downloads and archive intact (14.07.01 D-03) | Owner: read-only "No longer sold — you still own this" + downloads. Non-owner: 410-equivalent "no longer available". |
| Listing delisted — vendor **removed for cause** (14.08.05 D-03) | Separate path, separate copy. Owners must be told the entitlement's status even though the platform must not publish an accusation | Owner: "This product was removed from sale. Your licence and downloads are unaffected." — and see Q-05 for the case where the cause was *stolen rights*, where "unaffected" may be false. |
| Buyer opens a listing whose licence-terms template version the platform has since deprecated | The bound terms version renders regardless — an entitlement's terms are immutable (14.02.05 D-02) and a deprecated template is still the contract | Owner: their terms, as bound. Vendor: prompted to move the *live* listing to the current template; existing entitlements untouched. |
| Buyer views a listing from a different country than the vendor | Price displays in the buyer's currency with the tax treatment stated, because place of supply for digital goods is the **buyer's** location (domain CX / EU VAT MOSS) — not the vendor's, as it would be for gear (13) | Buyer: "£29 incl. VAT" — the displayed number is the number charged. |
| Logged-out visitor opens the listing | Full spec sheet renders; no owned-state, no rig, no verdict | Badge shows the 14.01.04 "Add your setup to check compatibility" CTA — never a green tick by default |
| Demo audio fails to load; rest of the listing loads | Listing renders; the demo player errors in place. Does **not** block the buy control — but for a sample-pack, the demo *is* the trust surface | Buyer: "Demos unavailable — try again" with retry, inline. |
| Manifest is enormous (a 100 GB library, 40,000 files) | Manifest renders as a summary (formats, counts, sizes, sample rate/bit depth) with the full file tree behind a disclosure. The headline numbers are what the buyer decides on | Buyer: summary; "View all 40,412 files" expands. |
| Listing has a matrix but no manifest (a legacy/imported record) | Partial state. The listing renders with the manifest section marked missing rather than silently absent — an absent manifest on a type that requires one is a defect, not a design | Buyer: "The vendor hasn't listed what's in this pack." Vendor: listing flagged incomplete, not auto-delisted. |
| **Producer views their own listing while also holding a contributor split on a different vendor's pack** | Two lenses on one surface. Vendor lens wins on their own listing (D-09); the contributor marker only appears on listings they did *not* author. The multi-hyphenate never sees a role picker | Own listing: Edit + View as buyer. Others': normal buy control + "You're credited on this". |
| Operator's room buys a listing; a second staff member opens the same listing | Ownership is held by the org (domain 01), so both see `You own this` — a room's licence is not a person's | Both: "Your studio owns this" + Download. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing requested | Skeleton with media frame and spec-sheet rows. The compat badge skeletons separately and never renders a provisional green (14.01.04). |
| Empty | Vendor started a draft | Draft form with the type-conditional required-section checklist ("3 of 9 complete") and the publish gate visible from step one — the vendor must see what publishing will cost before investing. |
| Populated | Listing published | Full spec sheet: vendor identity block, media, labelled demos, manifest summary, matrix, "also requires", terms, price, compat badge, buy control resolved to buyer state. |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry. If the buyer has viewed this listing before, the cached spec sheet renders with a staleness marker — a stale manifest is more useful than a blank page, but must not be passed off as current. |
| Partial | Matrix present but rig incomplete or absent | Listing renders fully; the badge shows "Complete your rig profile to check compatibility" instead of a verdict. Also covers: external dependencies in `unknown` state (14.01.03 D-02), and a required section missing on an imported record. |
| Paused | Payout readiness lapsed, or vendor-initiated pause | Out of catalog for new buyers; fully live for existing owners. Vendor sees the reason and the remedy. |
| Delisted — retired | Vendor exit (14.08.05), beat exclusivity sold (14.05.02) | Owners: read-only listing at their purchased revision, no buy control, "no longer sold — you still own this", downloads intact. Non-owners: "no longer available". |
| Delisted — removed for cause | Platform removal (14.08.05 D-03) | Owners: read-only listing + a status notice distinct from retirement. Never an accusation the platform cannot stand behind. See Q-05. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A digital listing is basically a gear listing (13) minus shipping, so it should reuse the same entity | The obvious consolidation instinct; the merge D-14 already rejected at domain level | ❌ REJECTED — confirms D-14 at feature granularity. A gear listing's price is a function of *condition* on a qty=1 non-fungible unit; a digital listing's price is a function of *licence terms* on infinite stock. There is no condition field, no quantity, no shipping, and no photograph of the actual unit — but there is a matrix, a manifest, a dependency graph, and an EULA that a gear listing has no slot for. Sharing the entity yields the ~40-nullable-column `listing` D-14 predicted. |
| DT-02 | The store's viable day-one inventory is third-party commercial plugins (FabFilter, iZotope, Valhalla) | Reading D-06 ("digital plugins for DAWs") literally | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the most consequential finding in the domain. Commercial plugin vendors have no reason to list on a new store; that requires a business-development motion WeJammin cannot run at launch, and Plugin Boutique/Splice already hold those relationships. But **WeJammin's own users are the vendors** for sample packs, presets, beats and templates — a producer with a folder can list on day one with no BD required. The listing entity must therefore be *excellent* for creator-made content (14.04, 14.05) and merely *adequate* for third-party plugins. This inverts the MoSCoW the candidate order implies. Escalated as a product decision — see domain index Q-03. |
| DT-03 | The buy control is a property of the listing | The default model — a listing has a price and a button | ❌ REJECTED — the buy control is a function of `(buyer, listing, version-range owned)`. A store that offers to sell you what you already own is the most common defect in software retail, and it is *worse* here than elsewhere: perpetual licences are version-bounded (14.07.01 D-01), so "do you own this" has no boolean answer. A buyer holding v1 of a product now selling v3 owns it and doesn't — the correct control is `Upgrade`, not `Buy` and not `You own this`. Both boolean collapses are wrong: one sells a duplicate, the other hides a legitimate upgrade sale. This is why the buyer-state table exists and why the listing cannot be rendered without resolving owned state first. |
| DT-04 | A delisted listing needs to stay *reachable* so owners can still download | The `[SURFACE]` pass's own edge case — the obvious, and insufficient, answer | ✅ CONFIRMED **and materially sharpened — this is where the feature touches the thesis (D-18, DT-C)**. Reachable is not enough; it must be **renderable as it appeared at purchase**. A purchased sample pack is a fact about a recording: its licence terms are a constraint on every track built with it (14.02.05), and the store is present at the exact moment that constraint becomes true, with a payment receipt as evidence. Years later, a clearance chain in domain 09 or a sync licence in 11 will ask *what was this buyer granted* — and a listing that renders **today's** terms is evidence of nothing. The vendor may have re-termed, re-priced, re-versioned or exited since. Therefore the listing revision displayed at purchase must be snapshotted onto the order (D-05), and the read-only owner view must render that snapshot rather than the live record. Nowhere else in domain 14 is the gap between "a file shop" and "the rights stack's cleanest capture point" this narrow — and the difference is one snapshot. |
| DT-05 | The audio demo is a demo — a fair sample of the product | The universal assumption. Nobody in the industry believes it | ✅ CONFIRMED as a defect the store can fix cheaply, and a domain expert would flag it immediately. The oldest trick in sample-pack retail is the **demo track that is not the pack**: a professionally mixed, mastered, arranged piece in which the pack contributes a fraction of what you hear, and the drums, mix and arrangement — the reasons it sounds good — are not for sale. Every experienced producer already discounts these demos, which means the demo currently transmits *no information*, which means the store's primary trust surface is dead weight. The fix is a label, not a ban: `made-with` (a track built using the product) vs `contents-only` (a straight walkthrough of the actual files, unprocessed). Sample-packs and preset-packs must ship at least one `contents-only` demo to publish (D-06). This matters more here than at Splice because of DT-02 — the vendors are unknown musicians, not brands, so the demo is doing work a reputation would otherwise do. |
| DT-06 | The listing needs a review score to be trustworthy | The domain index's own Q-12 — "a store with no reviews is not a store" | ❌ REJECTED **as a node here** — Reviews & Ratings is a platform cross-cut serving 05/13/14/16 (domain CX), and building a domain-14 review system would duplicate it. But the rejection produces a requirement rather than dismissing the concern: the listing must **reserve the surface** and, more importantly, must not depend on it. Reviews need volume, and a new store's listings have zero — a launch listing will show no score for months. That is precisely why DT-05's honest demo, DT-07's identity block, and the 14.01.04 badge are load-bearing rather than nice-to-have: **they are the trust surface that has to work when the review count is zero.** Confirm the cross-cut is owned (domain Q-12); do not build it here. |
| DT-07 | Vendor identity on a listing is a name and a logo, like every other store | How Plugin Boutique, Splice and ADSR model it — a brand string | ❌ REJECTED — and this is where consolidation × provenance shows up inside domain 14. On every other store the vendor is a brand you either recognise or don't; an unknown vendor is unsellable, which is why those stores are brand-gated and why DT-02's creator inventory has nowhere to go. On WeJammin the vendor is a **platform identity (01) carrying verified credits (02)** — the producer selling a drum pack has a graph showing the records they actually played on and mixed, attested by other people rather than self-claimed. That is a trust signal no plugin store can produce and no new vendor can fake, and it is available on day one with zero reviews (DT-06). So the identity block is a **required, platform-composed section** — the vendor authors none of it (D-07), because a vendor-authored credit list is a claim, and the whole point is that it isn't one. This is the single strongest answer to "why would I buy a pack from someone I've never heard of", and the candidate list never asked the question. |
| DT-08 | A listing has *a* price | Implicit in every store, and in this feature's own `[SURFACE]` draft | ❌ REJECTED — it breaks at the 14.05 intersection, and the break is structural rather than cosmetic. A beat listing does not sell a file at a price; it sells **rights tiers** — MP3 lease, WAV lease, stems lease, exclusive — at different prices, with different stream caps, different terms, and, for the exclusive, a transaction that destroys the product (14.05.02). A scalar `price` field forces the beat catalog to either lie (show the cheapest tier as "the" price) or live outside the listing entity (splitting the catalog D-02 just unified). Therefore price is **per-tier where the type has tiers** (D-08), with a displayed range in catalog contexts. The same shape does not recur in the other four types, which is why this is a per-type schema variance and not a reason to reject D-02. |
| DT-09 | The listing's biggest risk is a vendor overstating compatibility | What the whole sub-domain is organised around | ❌ REJECTED as *the biggest* — it is real but bounded (DT-B). An overstated matrix costs one buyer one refund. The listing's larger exposure is **a pack sold with rights its vendor never held**: the manifest, the demos and the terms all render fine, QA passes (there is no binary to scan), the buyer builds a record on it, and a WeJammin receipt vouches for the clearance. Per DT-04 that receipt is exactly what domain 09 will cite. So the listing's rights + AI attestation (14.08.01 D-01) is doing heavier work than the matrix — and it is the section with no automated check behind it. Recorded here rather than solved: the remedy (audio fingerprinting) is routed to `/create-prd-architecture` in the domain CX, and the policy half is Q-05. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The listing is a structured record with type-conditional required sections, not free text with optional metadata | Free-text specs cannot be checked, filtered, or notified on — which disables 14.01.04, 14.04.04, and CX-01's staleness notification. Structure is the precondition for every other feature in the sub-domain. |
| D-02 | **One listing entity, five product types, per-type required-section schemas** — not separate types for creator content vs commercial plugins. **Resolves Q-02.** | DT-02 established that the *economics* differ; the question was whether the *shape* does. It doesn't. All five types share identity, media, manifest, terms, monetisation, transfer policy and attestation — roughly 80% of the record. What varies (matrix axes, host deps, per-tier price) is already type-conditional per 14.08.01 D-02. Separate entities would duplicate the shared majority and split the catalog and search index in two. This is exactly the precedent 14.04.04 D-01 set for metadata ("one index, per-type facet schemas") and the inverse of D-14's gear merge — there the shared part was ~nothing, here it is most of it. |
| D-03 | Product type is chosen first and is **immutable after first publish** | Type determines the required-section schema. Retyping a live listing orphans the sections the old type required and leaves the new type's publish gates unfilled on a record buyers can already see. A new listing is cheap; a silently under-specified live listing is a refund. |
| D-04 | **Price binds as displayed for a 7-day cart hold; licence terms do not bind and instead break consent on change** | Follows domain CX-04 q5 ("bind what was displayed, not what is current at commit") but splits it, because price and terms are different objects. Price is a merchandising number — locking it protects the buyer, and re-pricing *down* immediately is never adverse, so it needs no consent. Terms are the vendor's legal position: freezing withdrawn terms onto a new sale binds the vendor to something they have retracted, possibly for a legal reason, so a terms change must instead invalidate the cart line's consent and force a clause diff. 7 days because a cart is a shopping session, not a wishlist — the wishlist (with its own CX-01 notification) is the durable surface, and an unbounded hold is a vendor-hostile price freeze. |
| D-05 | **The listing revision displayed at purchase is snapshotted onto the order**; the owner's read-only view renders that snapshot, not the live record | Per DT-04. This is the feature's thesis touchpoint. A delisted listing that renders today's terms is evidence of nothing, and the terms displayed at purchase are what domain 09's clearance chain and 14.09.02's adjudication both have to cite. It also makes "delisted but reachable" mean something: reachable to *what*, at *which revision*, is the question the `[SURFACE]` draft left open. |
| D-06 | Audio demos are labelled `made-with` or `contents-only`; `sample-pack` and `preset-pack` require ≥1 `contents-only` demo to publish | Per DT-05. An unlabelled demo track is the industry's oldest misdirection and transmits no information to an experienced buyer — which kills the listing's primary trust surface precisely where DT-02 says the inventory will come from. The label is nearly free; the honesty is the product. |
| D-07 | The vendor identity block is a **required, platform-composed section** — the vendor authors none of it | Per DT-07. It renders platform identity (01) and *verified* credits (02). A vendor-authored credit list is a self-claim, and the entire value is that this one isn't — it is attested by other people. This is the only trust signal that works at zero reviews (DT-06) and the only one no competitor can produce. |
| D-08 | Price is **per-tier where the product type has tiers** (`beat`), with a displayed range in catalog contexts; scalar elsewhere | Per DT-08. A beat sells rights tiers, not a file at a price. A scalar field forces the beat catalog to misrepresent its cheapest tier as "the" price or to live outside the entity D-02 just unified. Per-type schema variance is the pattern this feature already uses; this is one more instance of it, not an exception to it. |
| D-09 | A vendor viewing their own listing gets the vendor lens with a `View as buyer` toggle; never a role picker | The multi-hyphenate is the platform's structural fact (personas.md) — a Producer is a vendor here, a buyer two tabs over, and a credited contributor on a third listing simultaneously. Asking "are you buying or selling?" fails the persona immediately. One surface, lens resolved from context, toggle for the case the context can't infer. Self-purchase is **not** blocked on this surface: the structural fact that kills the role picker also kills the block (14.02.01 DT-10), so the buy control stays reachable under `View as buyer`, and the sales-rank and laundering exposure is handled by flagging at issuance and excluding downstream from payout, rank, reviews and loyalty (14.02.01 D-11) — never by removing the control. |
| D-10 | External **purchase** links are rejected at publish; external informational links are allowed | The Musician's named anti-persona is using the platform to find the counterparty and taking the deal off-platform to dodge fees. A "buy direct, 30% off" link in a description is that behaviour with the store paying for the discovery. Blocking *all* external links would break legitimate and useful ones (a walkthrough video, the vendor's own site) — the line is at an external checkout, not at the domain name. |
