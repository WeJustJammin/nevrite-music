# Feature: Machine-Readable Licence Terms Registry

> **Number**: 14.02.05
> **Parent**: [14.02 Licensing, Activation & Entitlement](./14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's 24 candidates.

## Overview

The structured, versioned statement of what a buyer may *do with the output* of a digital good —
not whether the software runs, but whether the record made with it can be released, sold, sync
licensed, or credited. A sample pack's terms are not paperwork attached to a purchase; they are a
**constraint on every track built with it**, and they are the only thing in this domain that
connects a file shop to the project's thesis (D-18).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this protects. Buys a pack, uses loop 47 in a track, releases it — and the terms they bought under determine whether that release is clean. Sees terms as plain-language answers at the point of use ("can I use this commercially? yes"), never as a wall of legal text they will not read. |
| Producer | Full | Authors terms as a vendor of packs/presets; consumes them as the person who puts a client's record at risk. Personas.md names this exactly: their nightmare is delivering work whose clearance they cannot evidence. As vendor, must be able to express real terms (royalty-free, no-resale-in-isolation, credit-required) without writing an EULA. |
| Operator | Read-only | A studio buying tools cares whether client work made in the room is clean, but does not author terms and rarely reads them. Read-only is honest — inflating this to Full would be a generic role description, not a real one. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface, and structurally so: personas.md is explicit that the Fan "will never file a split sheet". Terms are a professional artifact; a fan buying an album has a consumer licence handled entirely by domain 20. |

## Behavior

Each product carries a terms record — versioned, immutable once bound (CX-02), authored from a
small set of platform templates rather than free text. The registry expresses the clauses that
actually recur in music digital goods:

- commercial use permitted / not permitted
- royalty-free vs royalty-bearing (and if bearing, on what basis)
- resale of the asset in isolation (near-universally forbidden for loops)
- credit/attribution required
- exclusivity (relevant for beats — 14.05)
- AI-training use permitted / forbidden
- territory and term, where bounded

The consequential part is not storage but **propagation**: when an asset bought here is used in a
Project (07) and that project becomes a Release (12), the bound terms must travel with it as a
clearance attestation into Rights (09). This is capture-at-source applied to purchased inputs.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor authors terms at submission (14.08.01) by selecting from templates and setting clause values.
2. Terms are versioned and published with the listing; rendered as plain-language answers, not prose.
3. Buyer sees the answers at the buy button and agrees at checkout.
4. Issuance (14.02.01) freezes the terms *version* onto the entitlement (CX-02).
5. Buyer uses an asset from the pack in a project (07); the asset carries its terms reference.
6. The project becomes a release (12); the terms flow into the release's clearance record (09) as an attestation with a purchase receipt behind it.
7. Three years later a rights question arises; the answer is a record, not a memory.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor tightens terms after a sale | Cannot reach bound entitlements (CX-02). New terms apply to new buyers only. | Existing holder: informational notice that does **not** read as a change to their rights |
| Buyer refunds a pack (14.09) but already released a track using it | The classic bomb. Does revocation retroactively un-clear a released record? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02. Almost certainly: revoke future use, preserve past clearance. |
| Pack contains an uncleared third-party sample; vendor's terms grant rights they never held | Every downstream release is exposed. Personas.md names this as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour. | `[PENDING]` — the platform cannot verify a vendor's upstream chain; see Q-03 |
| Asset used in a project by a collaborator who did not buy the pack | Terms bind the *buyer*, but the project is shared (07). Does the collaborator inherit the licence? Usually not. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a real and common failure |
| Terms and the beat-licensing tier (14.05.01) disagree | Beat tiers *are* terms with a price attached; two systems expressing one concept | Resolved by 14.05 reading this registry rather than defining its own — see Cross-Cut Notes |
| Buyer never opens the terms (universal) | Plain-language answers are shown inline at the buy button, so agreement is informed without reading an EULA | Three answers above the buy button, not a checkbox on a PDF |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing/library fetch | Terms section skeleton |
| Empty | Vendor draft, terms unset | Publish gate: "Choose a licence template before publishing" |
| Populated | Terms bound | Plain-language answers + link to full text |
| Error | Fetch failed | Full text as fallback; never render an assumed permissive default |
| Partial | Vendor used a custom clause outside the templates | Structured answers where possible, free text flagged as unstructured — and therefore **not propagable** to 09 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Licence terms are an EULA — a document to store and display | The industry default; what every store does | ❌ REJECTED — a document is unpropagable, and propagation is the entire value. Prose terms cannot flow into a release's clearance record (09), cannot be checked when a project (07) is delivered, and cannot answer "is this track clean" three years later without a human reading a PDF. Structured clauses can. The store that models terms as a document is a store; the store that models them as data is part of a rights platform. This is the single design choice that decides which one domain 14 is. |
| DT-02 | Domain 14 is a consolidation domain with no provenance stake — it sells files, the thesis lives in 02/09/10 | The domain map's own framing ("user-directive / core", a marketplace next to a rights stack) | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the most important finding in domain 14.** D-18 says provenance is the wedge and consolidation is the platform, causally linked: being where the work happens is the precondition for capturing facts at source. A purchased sample *is a fact about a recording* — an input with an ownership chain — and the store is present at the exact moment it becomes true (the purchase). Nowhere else on the platform is a cleaner capture point: no memory, no reconstruction, a payment receipt as evidence. Yet the sweep's 24 candidates model licensing purely as access control and never once as a grant of rights that travels downstream. Without this node, domain 14 is a file shop adjacent to a rights platform; with it, the store *feeds* the rights platform. The candidate list's blind spot here mirrors the industry's: everyone builds the reconstruction engine, nobody instruments the moment. |
| DT-03 | The dangerous gap is buyers misusing assets | The vendor's-eye view of licensing | ❌ REJECTED — the dangerous gap is **vendors granting rights they do not hold**. A bedroom producer uploads a pack containing a chopped Motown break, ticks "royalty-free, commercial use OK", and every buyer who releases a track built on it inherits an uncleared sample. Personas.md names this precisely as the Musician's worst *accidental* behaviour ("uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream") — and here it fans out to strangers, at purchase volume, with WeJammin's own terms record vouching for it. The platform cannot verify a vendor's upstream chain (Q-03). What it *can* do is make the attestation explicit, attributable and durable at submission (14.08.01), so exposure is traceable to a person who asserted it rather than diffused into nobody. That is a materially different product than a checkbox, and it is not in the candidate list either. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Terms are structured clauses from platform templates, not vendor-authored prose | Per DT-01. Prose cannot propagate, and propagation is the value. Also: vendors are musicians, not lawyers — asking them to write an EULA produces copy-pasted garbage that binds nobody. |
| D-02 | Terms are versioned and immutable once bound to an entitlement | Per CX-02. A vendor cannot retroactively restrict a released record. Contract law, not preference. |
| D-03 | Custom clauses outside the templates are permitted but flagged unstructured and do **not** propagate to 09 | Escape hatch without a hole: an unstructured clause is honest about being unusable downstream, rather than silently breaking the clearance chain. |
