# Feature: Machine-Readable Licence Terms Registry

> **Number**: 14.02.05
> **Parent**: [14.02 Licensing, Activation & Entitlement](./14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's 24 candidates.

## Overview

The structured, versioned statement of what a buyer may *do with the output* of a digital good —
not whether the software runs, but whether the record made with it can be released, sold, sync
licensed, or credited. A sample pack's terms are not paperwork attached to a purchase; they are a
**constraint on every track built with it**, and they are the only thing in this domain that
connects a file shop to the project's thesis (D-18).

The registry owns three things and nothing else:

1. **The vocabulary** — the finite set of clauses that can be expressed about a digital good.
2. **The versioning and binding** — a terms version is immutable once bound to an entitlement (CX-02).
3. **The composition semantics** — how two or more bound terms sets combine into the single
   constraint that actually governs a finished record (DT-08). Only the vocabulary's owner can
   define how its clauses compose; the *application* of that composite is domain 09's and 12's.

It does not enforce, take down, adjudicate, or verify. It states, freezes, and propagates.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this protects. Buys a pack, uses loop 47 in a track, releases it — and the terms they bought under determine whether that release is clean. Sees terms as plain-language answers at the point of use ("can I use this commercially? yes"), never as a wall of legal text they will not read. The **composite view** (DT-08) is theirs specifically: at release, one screen saying "this record carries 3 obligations and 1 unresolved conflict", not eleven PDFs. Also the persona who discovers a `credit_required` clause turned into a pending credit on their release (DT-04) and either satisfies it or explains why not. |
| Producer | Full | Authors terms as a vendor of packs/presets/beats; consumes them as the person who puts a client's record at risk. Personas.md names this exactly: their nightmare is delivering work whose clearance they cannot evidence. As vendor, expresses real terms (royalty-free, no-resale-in-isolation, credit-required, 2-year lease with a 50k stream cap) by choosing template clause values — never by writing an EULA. As buyer, the persona most exposed to the **ghost-production orphan** (D-04): they hold the licence and are contractually invisible on the release, so their grant must be evidenced in the rights record (09), not the public credits (02). |
| Operator | Read-only | A studio buying tools cares whether client work made in the room is clean, but does not author terms and rarely reads them. Reads the composite view when a room's output is delivered to a client. Read-only is honest — inflating this to Full would be a generic role description, not a real one. Their one live dependency is the org-holder question (sub-domain Q-03): if entitlements cannot bind an org, every engineer's use of a studio-bought pack is an orphan under D-04. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface, and structurally so: personas.md is explicit that the Fan "will never file a split sheet". Terms are a professional artifact. A fan buying an album (domain 20) mints an entitlement through the shared delivery layer, but it binds a **platform-owned consumer listening licence** (D-14) — never a vendor-authored terms version, never a clause table, never the word "licence" in the UI. They see "my purchases". |

## Behavior

Each product carries a terms record — versioned, immutable once bound (CX-02), authored from a
small set of platform templates rather than free text. A vendor picks a template and sets clause
values; a **terms version** is created on publish (not on edit) and is thereafter append-only.

### The clause vocabulary

Three kinds of clause, and the distinction is load-bearing because they compose differently
(DT-08):

**Permissions** — what the holder *may* do. Compose by **intersection** (most restrictive wins).

| Clause | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `commercial_use` | permitted / non-commercial-only | permitted |
| `royalty_basis` | royalty-free / royalty-bearing (+ basis: per-unit, percentage, per-stream) | royalty-free |
| `derivative_use` | permitted / forbidden — the core grant: the loop *inside* a finished track | permitted |
| `resale_in_isolation` | forbidden / permitted — the loop *as a loop*, resold | forbidden |
| `collaborative_use` | holder-only / holder's-contributed-works | holder's-contributed-works (D-05) |
| `ai_training_permitted` | yes / no | no |
| `sync_permitted` | yes / no / separate-negotiation → routes to domain 11 | yes |
| `broadcast_permitted` | yes / no | yes |
| `exclusivity` | non-exclusive / exclusive (14.05.02 only) | non-exclusive |
| `transfer` | see 14.06.01 — a transfer right is a term, expressed through this vocabulary, not a parallel one | not-transferable |

**Obligations** — what the holder *must* do. Compose by **union** (all obligations accumulate).

| Clause | Values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `credit_required` | none / required (+ credit string + party identity) | Creates a **credit obligation in domain 02**, not a sentence (D-11, DT-04) |
| `disclosure_ai_generated` | fact, vendor-attested at 14.08.01 | Propagates as a **disclosure obligation** on the buyer's release (D-12, DT-07) |
| `stream_cap` | integer + basis (streams / units sold) | Measurement is notification-only and only where WeJammin distributed (D-15, DT-06) |
| `sales_cap` | integer | Same |

**Bounds** — the envelope. Compose by **intersection**.

| Clause | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `term_length` | perpetual / bounded (months from purchase date) | perpetual |
| `territory` | worldwide / enumerated territory list | worldwide |

**Custom clause** — free text. Permitted, flagged unstructured, **does not propagate** (D-03).
A custom clause that contradicts a structured clause is a submission-time rejection (D-13).

### The consequential part

Not storage but **propagation**. When an asset bought here is used in a Project (07) and that
project becomes a Release (12), the bound terms must travel with it as a clearance attestation
into Rights (09). This is capture-at-source applied to purchased inputs.

A **placement** (asset dropped into a project) records: asset, terms version id, entitlement id,
placing holder id, timestamp. That tuple is the atom of the chain. At release, the composite is
computed across every placement, and the result is one of: `clear` / `clear-with-obligations` /
`conflicted` / `unknown`. `unknown` is never `clear` (D-10).

### Happy Path

1. Vendor authors terms at submission (14.08.01) by picking a template and setting clause values — e.g. `commercial_use: permitted`, `royalty_basis: royalty-free`, `resale_in_isolation: forbidden`, `credit_required: none`, `ai_training_permitted: no`.
2. On publish, a terms **version** is minted (append-only, immutable) and attached to the listing.
3. The listing renders the terms as plain-language answers above the buy button — three answers, not a PDF: "Use it in a commercial release? **Yes.** Pay royalties? **No.** Resell the loops themselves? **No.**"
4. Buyer clicks buy; the checkout carries the terms version id **that was rendered to them**, not whatever is current at commit.
5. Payment confirms; issuance (14.02.01) freezes that terms version id onto the entitlement, atomically with the entitlement mint (CX-02). Snapshot failure fails the whole issuance.
6. Buyer opens a project (07) and places loop 47. The placement records terms version, entitlement, placing holder, timestamp.
7. The project becomes a release (12). The clearance check (09) resolves every placement's terms version and computes the composite.
8. Composite returns `clear-with-obligations`: one obligation — the beat lease in the same record requires producer credit. A pending credit appears in domain 02 (D-11).
9. Buyer satisfies the credit; the release's clearance record (09) records: for each placed asset, the holder, the terms version, the purchase receipt, and the composite result at release time.
10. Three years later a rights question arises. The answer is a record, not a memory: *on 2026-07-16, holder X purchased pack Y under terms version 3, receipt Z, and released track T under a composite of terms versions 3, 11 and 2.*

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | Vendor **tightens** terms after a sale | Cannot reach bound entitlements (CX-02, D-02). New version applies to new buyers only. | Existing holder: informational notice that does **not** read as a change to their rights — "this vendor published new terms; yours are unchanged" |
| 2 | Vendor **loosens** terms after a sale | The inverse, and nobody designs for it. The holder is stuck on worse terms than the vendor now offers. Newer versions are **opt-in, holder-initiated, never automatic** (D-08) — the platform cannot rank two clause sets, and "newer" is not "better" on every axis. | Library row: "Newer terms available — compare." Per-clause diff. Adopting rebinds the entitlement forward and is logged as a holder-initiated event. Past clearances stay bound to the old version. |
| 3 | Buyer refunds a pack (14.09) but already released a track using it | **Revoke future use, preserve past clearance** — the clearance attestation is a historical fact, not a live pointer, and revoking an entitlement does not rewrite history; it appends an event. New placements against the revoked entitlement are rejected. The fraud vector (buy → release → refund → free clearance) is handled by the registry exposing a `downstream_propagation` signal (placement count + release clearance count) that 14.09 reads: a propagated entitlement is not auto-refundable. Recommendation only — **Q-02** is the owner's ratification. | Holder: library row revoked with reason; released track's clearance shows "entitlement subsequently revoked (refund, 2026-09-01) — clearance as at release date preserved". Refund attempt on a propagated entitlement: "this licence is cited by 1 released record — a refund needs review." |
| 4 | Pack contains an uncleared third-party sample; vendor's terms grant rights they never held | Every downstream release is exposed. The platform cannot verify a vendor's upstream chain — that is not solvable and pretending otherwise is worse than admitting it (DT-09). What the registry **can** do, and does: every terms version carries the attesting vendor's identity and timestamp immutably, and the registry can enumerate the **blast radius** on demand — every entitlement bound to that terms version, and every release carrying a clearance attestation derived from it. Exposure becomes a list of named people, not a fog. The *remedy* (indemnity / insurance / active check) is **Q-03**. | On an upstream claim (domain 24 / DMCA): affected holders notified individually with the specific asset, the specific release, and the attestation that is now contested. Not a blanket "an issue has arisen". |
| 5 | Asset used in a project by a collaborator who did not buy the pack | **RESOLVED — D-04.** Terms bind the **holder**, not the project. The holder's grant extends to works the holder contributed to, where contribution is evidenced by the release's **rights record (09)**, not its public credits (02). If loop 47 was placed by X and X appears in the release's rights record → coherent. If X does not → **orphaned clearance**. | Release gate: "Loop 47 was placed by X under X's licence. X is not on this record's rights list — X's licence may not cover this release." Remedies offered: add X to the rights record, buy your own licence, or remove the asset. |
| 6 | **Ghost production** — the producer holds the licence and is *contractually invisible* on the release | The exact case that breaks a naïve version of D-04, and it is common, not exotic. Resolved by the D-04 wording: the orphan check reads the **rights record (09)**, which can list a party privately, not the **public credits (02)**. Clearance ≠ credit. A ghost producer is invisible in 02 and present in 09. | Nothing visible on the public record. The rights record carries the holder; the composite resolves `clear`. |
| 7 | A `credit_required` clause collides with a ghost agreement | Vendor's pack requires "Prod. by V" in the public credits; the release is a ghost production whose whole premise is that a name does not appear. The obligation and the arrangement are **irreconcilable** — and the registry says so rather than silently picking one. | Release gate: "Sample pack Y requires a public credit to V. This record's arrangement conflicts with that. Resolve before release." |
| 8 | Two placed assets carry **contradictory clauses** | Composite returns `conflicted` (DT-08). Example: loop A `ai_training_permitted: no`, loop B `yes` → composite `no` (intersection, resolvable). Example: pack Y `credit_required: required`, beat C `credit_required: none` → union → credit to Y required (resolvable). Genuine irreconcilables are rarer and are named individually (row 7). | One screen: "This record carries 3 obligations and 1 conflict", each traced to its asset and its terms version. Never a silent resolution. |
| 9 | **Territory** — pack licensed for North America, release targets worldwide (12) | Composite territory = intersection of all bounds = North America. The release's target exceeds its clearance. | Release gate: "Your clearances cover North America. This release targets worldwide. Pack Y is the constraint." |
| 10 | **Bounded lease expires on a live release** (DT-05) | The most-violated clause in the beat market. Term is computed from purchase date; the obligation propagates into the release record (12). Notify at **T-90 / T-30 / T-7 days**, then flag the release `out-of-term`. **Never auto-takedown** (D-15) — the platform is not the artist's censor and may not even be the distributor. Whether an obligation to act exists at all is **Q-06**. | Artist: "Your lease on beat C ends 2028-04-01. Your release is live. Renew, upgrade, or take action." Producer (vendor): same fact, subject to Q-05. |
| 11 | **Stream cap approached / exceeded** (DT-06) | Measurable **only** where WeJammin distributed the release (12) and holds the counts (22 / 10). Elsewhere: unmeasurable, and the registry says so rather than guessing. Behavior is notification, never enforcement (D-15). | Artist at 86% of cap: "Beat C's lease caps at 50,000 streams. You're at 43,120. Upgrade now?" Outside WeJammin distribution: "Cap: 50,000 streams. We can't measure this — you're responsible for tracking it." |
| 12 | Terms and the beat-licensing tier (14.05.01) disagree | Cannot happen by construction. Beat tiers **are** terms with a price attached; 14.05 expresses tiers through this registry (domain CX-08, 14.05 D-03) rather than defining a parallel vocabulary. | N/A — a single clearance chain covers pack-cleared and lease-cleared tracks identically. |
| 13 | Buyer never opens the terms (universal) | Plain-language answers inline at the buy button, so agreement is informed without reading an EULA. Clickwrap enforceability is a legal-form question, not a product one → `/create-prd`. | Three answers above the buy button, not a checkbox on a PDF. |
| 14 | Vendor **exits** (14.08.05) or **delists** the product | Terms versions are **never deleted** (D-09). A bound version is evidence — deleting it destroys every downstream holder's proof. Tombstoned as `withdrawn`; text stays resolvable forever for every entitlement bound to it. | Holder's library: terms link still resolves, marked "vendor withdrawn — these terms remain in force for your licence". |
| 15 | **GDPR erasure** of a vendor whose terms are bound to live entitlements | Same physics as row 14, opposite direction, and genuinely contested — the terms version is a contract the *buyer* relies on. **Registry position**: the clause **values** survive unconditionally (they are the buyer's rights, not the vendor's data), and the terms version stays resolvable. The contested part is narrower than it first appears — the vendor's **personal identifiers on the attestation** (DT-03 needs a named asserter; erasure removes the name). The registry does not decide that conflict alone: it is sub-domain **Q-02**, routed to `/create-prd-security`. What the registry commits to regardless of the ruling: erasure never makes a bound clause set unresolvable. | Holder: terms link resolves, clauses intact, marked "vendor account closed". The attestation line renders either the vendor's name or "vendor identity erased under data-protection request" depending on the Q-02 ruling — never a broken record. |
| 16 | Terms **version race** with checkout | The buy page carries the version id it rendered; checkout binds **that** id (CX-02 synthesis Q5). If a new version published between render and commit, the displayed version still binds — it is what the buyer agreed to. If the rendered page is **older than 24 hours** (a tab left open over a weekend), re-render and require fresh agreement before commit. | Stale tab: "These terms were updated. Here's what changed." Per-clause diff, then buy. |
| 17 | **Concurrent vendor edits** — two tabs, or two members of a vendor org | A version is created on **publish**, not on edit, and versions are append-only. Two rapid publishes produce two versions; the newest is offered to new buyers, both stay resolvable. No lock is needed because immutability makes the race harmless. | Second publisher: "Terms were published by [other member] while you were editing. Yours is now version 5." |
| 18 | **Race: placement commits while entitlement is being revoked** | Two gates, because the window between them can be years. Checked at **placement** time (revoked → placement rejected) and re-checked at **release** time. A placement that committed before revocation stands; the release gate re-evaluates it under row 3's rule. | At placement: "This licence was revoked — you can't add this asset." At release: the row-3 message. |
| 19 | **Malicious vendor input** — permissive structured clauses, restrictive custom text (or the reverse) | Structured clauses are **authoritative** for propagation (D-13). A custom clause contradicting a structured one is rejected at submission (14.08.01) with the specific conflict named — not a generic "invalid". This closes the obvious hole in the D-03 escape hatch: a vendor cannot use free text to secretly mean the opposite of what propagates. | Vendor at submission: "Your custom clause says resale is permitted; your structured terms forbid it. Fix one." |
| 20 | **Custom clause present** at release gate | Does not propagate (D-03). The composite degrades to **human review**, not a silent pass. | "Pack Y's terms include a custom clause we can't evaluate. Read it before you release." Link to the text. |
| 21 | **Terms missing/unresolvable** on a placed asset (partial issuance failure, migrated record) | Treated as **prohibitive, not permissive** (D-10). Fail closed at every gate. Composite returns `unknown`, which is never `clear`. | Release gate blocks: "We can't evidence the terms for loop 47. We won't tell you it's clear when we don't know." |
| 22 | **Network/fetch failure** at the release gate | Fail closed. Never "assumed clear". Retry with the holder's cached composite shown as stale, clearly labelled. | "We can't verify your clearances right now — this isn't a rejection. Retry." Never an assumed permissive default. |
| 23 | **The multi-hyphenate as vendor and user of their own pack** | A Producer sells pack Y, then uses loop 47 from it on a client's record. They need no entitlement — they hold the rights. But the chain must still be **complete**, or the release shows an unattributed asset and the composite returns `unknown` (row 21) on the vendor's own product. A **zero-price self-entitlement** is minted at first placement (D-16) so the chain closes. Their own `credit_required` clause naming themselves does **not** generate a pending credit — deduplicated against the holder identity. | Nothing. It just works, and the record is complete. This is the "lazy path is the correct path" requirement from personas.md, applied to the vendor case. |
| 24 | **Terms are per-product; a vendor wants per-file terms** | One loop in a 200-loop pack contains a licensed third-party sample with different terms. Terms bind at **product** level (D-07). A vendor needing per-file terms must **split the product**. Honest, explicit limit — the alternative is a per-file clause matrix nobody will fill in correctly, producing false precision. | Vendor at submission: "Terms apply to the whole product. If one file's terms differ, list it separately." |
| 25 | **Org holds the entitlement, individual engineer places the asset** (Operator) | Structurally row 5 at org scale, and both branches are specifiable. **If** entitlements can bind an org (14.02.01 DT-02 / sub-domain Q-03): "holder" in D-04 resolves to the **org**, an engineer acting for the org is covered, and the orphan check tests whether the *org* appears in the release's rights record (09). **If** they cannot: every studio purchase orphans on first use by anyone but the buyer, and the only honest behaviour is to flag every one of them. The registry does not re-decide **Q-03** — it declares its dependency: D-04's "holder" must be a principal that may be a person **or** an org, exactly as 14.02.01 DT-02 recommends. | Org-holder exists: nothing — it resolves `clear`. Org-holder does not exist: "Loop 47 was placed under [engineer]'s licence, bought by [studio]. This record doesn't list [engineer]." — i.e. the Operator persona sees a false alarm on every studio purchase, which is why this is a blocker and not a nicety. |
| 26 | Fan buys an album from an artist page (domain 20) | Mints an entitlement (14.02.01 Role Lens) bound to a **platform-owned consumer listening licence** (D-14) — not vendor-authored, not versioned per-vendor, no clause table, no composite. | "My purchases." The word "licence" never appears. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing / library / release-gate fetch | Terms section skeleton; the buy button is disabled until terms render — a buy button above unrendered terms is an uninformed agreement |
| Empty | Vendor draft, terms unset | Publish gate: "Choose a licence template before publishing" — publish blocked, not warned |
| Populated | Terms bound | Plain-language answers (3–5 lines) + link to full text + version id |
| Error | Fetch failed | Full text as fallback where cached; composite fails closed (`unknown`). **Never render an assumed permissive default.** Retry offered; the failure is labelled a failure, not a verdict |
| Partial | Vendor used a custom clause outside the templates | Structured answers where possible; free text flagged as unstructured and therefore **not propagable** to 09. Composite degrades to human review (row 20) |
| Superseded | Vendor published a newer version; holder still bound to theirs | "Newer terms available — compare." Per-clause diff. Holder's rights unchanged unless they opt in (D-08) |
| Withdrawn | Vendor exited (14.08.05) or delisted the product | Terms still resolve, marked "vendor withdrawn — these terms remain in force for your licence". Never a dead link (D-09) |
| Conflicted | Composite at release finds an irreconcilable obligation pair (rows 7–9) | "This record carries N obligations and M conflicts", each traced to its asset and terms version. Whether this blocks or warns is **Q-07** |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Licence terms are an EULA — a document to store and display | The industry default; what every store does | ❌ REJECTED — a document is unpropagable, and propagation is the entire value. Prose terms cannot flow into a release's clearance record (09), cannot be checked when a project (07) is delivered, and cannot answer "is this track clean" three years later without a human reading a PDF. Structured clauses can. The store that models terms as a document is a store; the store that models them as data is part of a rights platform. This is the single design choice that decides which one domain 14 is. |
| DT-02 | Domain 14 is a consolidation domain with no provenance stake — it sells files, the thesis lives in 02/09/10 | The domain map's own framing ("user-directive / core", a marketplace next to a rights stack) | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the most important finding in domain 14.** D-18 says provenance is the wedge and consolidation is the platform, causally linked: being where the work happens is the precondition for capturing facts at source. A purchased sample *is a fact about a recording* — an input with an ownership chain — and the store is present at the exact moment it becomes true (the purchase). Nowhere else on the platform is a cleaner capture point: no memory, no reconstruction, a payment receipt as evidence. Yet the sweep's 24 candidates model licensing purely as access control and never once as a grant of rights that travels downstream. Without this node, domain 14 is a file shop adjacent to a rights platform; with it, the store *feeds* the rights platform. The candidate list's blind spot here mirrors the industry's: everyone builds the reconstruction engine, nobody instruments the moment. |
| DT-03 | The dangerous gap is buyers misusing assets | The vendor's-eye view of licensing | ❌ REJECTED — the dangerous gap is **vendors granting rights they do not hold**. A bedroom producer uploads a pack containing a chopped Motown break, ticks "royalty-free, commercial use OK", and every buyer who releases a track built on it inherits an uncleared sample. Personas.md names this precisely as the Musician's worst *accidental* behaviour ("uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream") — and here it fans out to strangers, at purchase volume, with WeJammin's own terms record vouching for it. The platform cannot verify a vendor's upstream chain (Q-03). What it *can* do is make the attestation explicit, attributable and durable at submission (14.08.01), so exposure is traceable to a person who asserted it rather than diffused into nobody. That is a materially different product than a checkbox, and it is not in the candidate list either. |
| DT-04 | `credit_required` is a clause the platform can only *state* — honouring it is the buyer's problem | The industry default: credit requirements live in a PDF and are honoured approximately never | ✅ CONFIRMED (as an obligation, not a sentence) — a domain expert's first question about a sample-pack licence is "does it require credit?", because a large share of packs and nearly every beat lease do. WeJammin is the only store on earth that already owns the credits domain (02). A `credit_required` clause that does not **create a pending credit entry** on the release is decorative — it reproduces the PDF in JSON. The clause must fire an obligation into 02 and surface at the release gate (12). This is the second place the store touches the thesis, and it is cheaper and less contested than the clearance chain: nobody disputes that they agreed to credit the pack. Recorded as D-11. |
| DT-05 | Terms are static properties of a purchase — set once, read later | The store's-eye view: a licence is a thing you have | ✅ CONFIRMED as false; terms are **temporal, and they expire against a live release**. Beat leases (14.05) are routinely 1–2 year terms. The industry reality: the lease expires, the artist doesn't notice, the distributor doesn't take it down, and the producer discovers years later — at which point the track has streams and the conversation is a lawyer's. This is arguably the most-violated clause in the beat market, and it is violated *by inattention*, not by intent. A registry that models `term_length` but never fires at expiry is a trap that looks like a feature. Term must propagate into the release record (12) and generate T-90/T-30/T-7 notifications. Recorded as D-15 and edge case 10; the obligation-to-act question is Q-06. |
| DT-06 | Stream and sales caps are unenforceable everywhere, so don't model them | The correct read of every other beat store — the beatmaker cannot see the artist's streaming numbers, so the cap is honour-system | ✅ CONFIRMED **with hard scoping** — this is the one place the cap is measurable, and only sometimes. Where WeJammin distributed the release (12), it holds the counts (22 / 10). Nowhere else. The valuable behaviour is not enforcement — it is telling the artist "your lease caps at 50,000 and you're at 43,120", which today literally nobody knows until it is a problem. Modelled as a clause; measured best-effort where distribution is ours; **notification only, never auto-takedown** (D-15). Where unmeasurable, the registry says so rather than implying a check exists. The temptation to over-claim here is the DT-09 error wearing different clothes. |
| DT-07 | The AI clause is one thing: "you may not train on these samples" | What every vendor is currently bolting onto their EULA | ✅ CONFIRMED as insufficient — **"AI" is two clauses and the sweep conflated them.** One is a **permission** governing the buyer's conduct (`ai_training_permitted`). The other is a **fact about the asset** (`disclosure_ai_generated`) — was this pack AI-generated? — which is not a permission at all and does not govern the buyer; it creates a **disclosure obligation on their release**, because DSPs and PROs are moving to require AI disclosure (12). A single clause cannot carry both: one intersects, the other unions. The domain CX already routes the AI attestation cross-cut to capture at 14.08.01 (domain D-03); this is where the captured fact acquires its downstream consequence. Recorded as D-12. |
| DT-08 | Terms govern a purchase — "my licence for this pack" | Every store models it this way, because every store only sells | ✅ CONFIRMED as the wrong unit — **terms govern a *record*, and a record has many terms.** A finished track uses loop A (credit required, no AI training, NA-only), preset B (royalty-free, worldwide) and beat C (2-year lease, 50k stream cap). No store models the thing the artist actually needs: *can I release this, and what do I owe?* That answer is a **composite** — the intersection of every bound permission, the union of every bound obligation, the intersection of every bound bound. This is the single most valuable capability in the file and nobody has it, because nobody else holds both the purchase and the release. Boundary: the registry owns the **composition semantics** (only the vocabulary's owner can define how its clauses combine); 09/12 own the **application**. This also sharpens Q-01 rather than answering it — vocabulary and composition here, application there, is a coherent split. Recorded as D-06. |
| DT-09 | The platform should verify vendor rights claims with audio fingerprinting at submission (Content ID for packs) | What a large platform would do, and what an owner would reasonably ask for after reading DT-03 | ❌ REJECTED — **and the reason matters more than the rejection.** Fingerprinting answers "is this the same recording"; the question is "does this vendor hold the rights". Different questions. A 2-second Motown break, chopped, pitched and time-stretched, will not match — which is precisely the material DT-03 is about. Meanwhile a legitimate pack of original one-shots will false-positive against every record that ever used a similar library. So it misses the guilty and accuses the innocent. Worse: it would create the **appearance of verification**, and a buyer who believes the store checked is *more* exposed than one who knows it didn't — the platform would have converted an honest unknown into a false assurance, and taken on the liability that comes with having "checked". Rejecting this does **not** close Q-03; it removes the most tempting wrong answer to it. |
| DT-10 | Buyers should be able to negotiate custom terms with vendors | Marketplace instinct: real licensing is negotiated | ❌ REJECTED — at £10–£50 creator content (14.01.01 DT-02) the transaction cost of a negotiation exceeds the price of the good, so it would be used by nobody and built for everybody. Structurally worse: a terms set bound to exactly one entitlement is not a *version*, it is a contract — it destroys the versioning model (D-02) and makes propagation meaningless, because 09 cannot reason about a population of one-off clause sets. Where bespoke terms genuinely matter — exclusive beat sales (14.05.02) and sync (11) — those are separate features with their own negotiation surfaces and their own escrow. The registry's power comes from a *small finite vocabulary*; negotiation is the mechanism that would destroy it. |
| DT-11 | Terms should bind per-file, so a vendor can express different rights for different loops in one pack | Sounds more precise, and the underlying need is real (one licensed file in an otherwise-original pack) | ❌ REJECTED — a per-file clause matrix across a 200-loop pack will not be filled in correctly by a musician doing this between sessions (14.08.01 Role Lens), and a matrix filled in carelessly produces **false precision**, which is worse than a coarse truth: the composite would report `clear` from clause values nobody actually set. Product-level binding forces the honest move — a vendor whose files genuinely differ must **split the product**, which also makes the difference visible to the buyer *before* purchase. Recorded as D-07. |
| DT-12 | When a vendor publishes better terms, existing holders should be upgraded automatically | Generosity instinct; also what a naïve reading of "the buyer should get the best deal" implies | ❌ REJECTED — the platform **cannot rank two clause sets**. A version that loosens `ai_training_permitted` while tightening `territory` is not "better"; it is different, and which side of that trade a given holder wants depends on facts the platform does not have. Auto-upgrading also silently mutates the terms a released record was cleared under, which is the exact harm D-02 exists to prevent — committed by the platform instead of the vendor. The correct shape is **holder-initiated opt-in with a per-clause diff** (D-08, edge case 2). This rejection is the mirror of DT-02's confirmation: rights do not change without the rights-holder's act. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **14.02.01 Issuance** ([path](./14.02.01-licence-issuance-entitlement-record.md)) — **trigger dependency, High**: the terms version is frozen onto the entitlement at purchase, atomically; snapshot failure fails issuance. See `14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-cx.md#CX-02`.
- Touches **14.02.02 Activation & Seats** ([path](./14.02.02-activation-seats-machine-authorisation.md)) — **null relationship** (`#R-01`): terms govern output, seats govern machines. No terms clause is enforceable by an activation check. Recorded so a spec writer does not build one.
- Touches **07 Music Projects & Collaboration** ([path](../../07-music-projects-collaboration/)) — **shared entity, High**: the **placement** (asset + terms version + entitlement + placing holder + timestamp) is created here and is the atom of the whole chain. 07 must persist it on every asset drop, and must reject placements against revoked entitlements (edge case 18). Without this, nothing downstream works.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **shared entity, High — the thesis link**: bound terms propagate as a clearance attestation. Two specific dependencies: (a) 09 **applies** the composite this registry defines (D-06); (b) the D-04 orphan check reads 09's **rights record**, not 02's public credits — this is what makes ghost production work (edge case 6).
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **trigger dependency, High**: per DT-04/D-11, a `credit_required` clause creates a **pending credit obligation** in 02. 02 must accept an obligation originating from a purchase rather than from a session, and must deduplicate a self-credit (edge case 23).
- Touches **12 Release & Distribution** ([path](../../12-release-distribution/)) — **trigger dependency, High**: clearance is checked at release; the composite is computed here and gates (or warns — **Q-07**) there. Also carries the AI disclosure obligation (D-12) and the `out-of-term` flag (D-15).
- Touches **22 Analytics** ([path](../../22-analytics-market-intelligence/)) and **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — **Medium, scoped**: the only source of the stream/sales counts that make `stream_cap` measurable, and **only** for releases WeJammin distributed (DT-06). Outside that, the clause is stated and explicitly unmeasured.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **notification fan-out, High**: on an upstream rights claim (DMCA), 24 triggers the **blast-radius enumeration** (edge case 4) — every entitlement bound to the contested terms version and every release derived from it, notified individually and specifically.
- Touches **14.05 Beat & Instrumental Licensing** ([path](../14.05-beat-instrumental-licensing/)) — **shared vocabulary, High**: beat lease tiers ARE terms with prices. 14.05 reads this registry rather than defining a parallel one (domain `#CX-08`, 14.05 D-03). Beat leases are also the primary source of `term_length` (DT-05) and `stream_cap` (DT-06) — the two clauses that make the registry temporal rather than static.
- Touches **14.06.01 Vendor Transfer Policy Registry** ([path](../14.06-used-licence-transfer/14.06.01-vendor-transfer-policy-registry.md)) — **shared vocabulary, High**: a transfer right is a licence term, expressed through this vocabulary. 14.06.01 is a consumer of the registry, not a second one.
- Touches **14.08.01 Vendor Submission** ([path](../14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa/14.08.01-vendor-onboarding-product-submission.md)) — **trigger dependency, High**: where terms are authored (from templates, never a text box), where the upstream-rights attestation is captured (DT-03), and where the structured-vs-custom contradiction is rejected (D-13).
- Touches **14.08.05 Vendor Exit & Continuity** ([path](../14.08-vendor-portal-build-qa/)) — **High**: vendor exit tombstones terms versions, never deletes them (D-09). A bound version is a holder's evidence and must outlive the vendor.
- Touches **14.09 Refunds & Revocation** ([path](../14.09-digital-refunds-revocation/)) — **trigger dependency, High**: the registry exposes a `downstream_propagation` signal (placement + release-clearance counts) that refund eligibility reads (edge case 3). Revocation appends an event; it never rewrites a past clearance.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission, High**: "holder" in D-04 must be able to be an **org**, or every studio purchase orphans on use (edge case 25). Blocked on sub-domain Q-03 / 14.02.01 DT-02.
- Touches **20 Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan** ([path](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)) — **Medium**: fan purchases bind a platform-owned consumer listening licence (D-14). The registry's job here is to **not** appear.
- Touches **11 Music Licensing** ([path](../../11-music-licensing/)) — **boundary, Medium**: `sync_permitted: separate-negotiation` routes out of this registry into 11. Recorded so the boundary is explicit rather than a silent gap.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Terms are structured clauses from platform templates, not vendor-authored prose | Per DT-01. Prose cannot propagate, and propagation is the value. Also: vendors are musicians, not lawyers — asking them to write an EULA produces copy-pasted garbage that binds nobody. |
| D-02 | Terms are versioned and immutable once bound to an entitlement | Per CX-02. A vendor cannot retroactively restrict a released record. Contract law, not preference. |
| D-03 | Custom clauses outside the templates are permitted but flagged unstructured and do **not** propagate to 09 | Escape hatch without a hole: an unstructured clause is honest about being unusable downstream, rather than silently breaking the clearance chain. |
| D-04 | **Terms bind the holder, not the project.** The holder's grant extends to works the holder contributed to, where contribution is evidenced by the release's **rights record (09)** — not its public credits (02). A placed asset whose placing holder is absent from the release's rights record is an **orphaned clearance** and is flagged at the release gate. | Resolves Q-04. A licence grants rights to a licensee; a project is not a licensee. But "holder-only" read literally would break every collaborative record, which is most records. Evidencing against the **rights record** rather than public credits is what makes ghost production work (edge case 6) — clearance is not credit, and conflating them would flag the most common professional arrangement in the beat market as unclean. |
| D-05 | `collaborative_use` is a first-class clause axis (`holder-only` / `holder's-contributed-works`), defaulting to `holder's-contributed-works` | Vendors genuinely differ, and D-04 states the platform's default rule — but the *vendor* sets the grant, not the platform. A registry that hard-codes D-04 would be asserting terms the vendor never agreed to. The default matches the prevailing market position (use in your productions); a vendor wanting purchaser-only can say so, and the composite will enforce the stricter value. |
| D-06 | The registry owns the **composition semantics** (permissions intersect, obligations union, bounds intersect, most-restrictive-wins per axis); domains 09 and 12 own the **application** | Per DT-08. Only the vocabulary's owner can define how its clauses combine — a composition rule defined in 09 would drift the moment a clause is added here. Conversely, the registry must not gate a release; that is 12's surface. This split is also the most coherent answer available to Q-01. |
| D-07 | Terms bind at **product** level, not per-file. A vendor whose files carry different terms must split the product. | Per DT-11. A per-file matrix produces false precision from fields nobody filled in carefully, and false precision in a clearance chain is worse than a coarse truth. Splitting also surfaces the difference to the buyer *before* purchase. |
| D-08 | Newer terms versions are **opt-in, holder-initiated, never automatic** — presented as a per-clause diff | Per DT-12. The platform cannot rank two clause sets; "newer" is not "better" on every axis. Auto-upgrade would silently mutate the terms a released record was cleared under — the exact harm D-02 prevents, committed by the platform instead of the vendor. |
| D-09 | Terms versions are **never deleted** — vendor exit or delisting tombstones them as `withdrawn`; the text stays resolvable forever for every bound entitlement | Per edge cases 14–15. A bound version is the holder's evidence. Deleting it destroys the proof the entire feature exists to create, at the moment the vendor stops caring — which is exactly when the holder needs it. |
| D-10 | Unknown or unresolvable terms are treated as **prohibitive**, never permissive. The composite returns `unknown`, and `unknown` is never `clear`. | The Error state's existing rule generalised. A permissive default is a lie told at the moment of maximum consequence; the honest failure ("we don't know") is actionable and the dishonest success is not. Fail closed at every gate, including network failure (edge case 22). |
| D-11 | `credit_required` creates a **pending credit obligation in domain 02**, not a sentence in a document | Per DT-04. WeJammin is the only store that owns the credits domain; a clause that does not fire an obligation reproduces the PDF in JSON. This is the cheapest, least-contested thesis link in the domain — nobody disputes they agreed to credit the pack. |
| D-12 | "AI" is **two clauses**: `ai_training_permitted` (a permission governing the buyer, intersects) and `disclosure_ai_generated` (a vendor-attested fact about the asset that propagates as a disclosure obligation on the buyer's release, unions) | Per DT-07. One clause cannot carry both — they compose in opposite directions and govern different parties. The domain's AI cross-cut is captured at 14.08.01 and acquires its downstream consequence here. |
| D-13 | Structured clauses are **authoritative** for propagation. A custom clause that contradicts a structured clause is a submission-time rejection (14.08.01), naming the specific conflict. | Closes the obvious hole in D-03: without this, a vendor sets permissive structured clauses that propagate while free text secretly says the opposite, and the clearance chain carries a fiction. |
| D-14 | Fan purchases (domain 20) bind a **platform-owned consumer listening licence** — not a vendor-authored terms version. No clause table, no composite, no terms UI on the fan surface. | Per personas.md: the Fan has no professional stake, will not tolerate professional-tool complexity, and "will never file a split sheet". They see "my purchases". Stated explicitly so a spec writer does not render a clause table to a fan buying an album. |
| D-15 | Cap and term measurement is **notification-only**. Never auto-takedown, never auto-enforcement. Term expiry notifies at T-90 / T-30 / T-7 days then flags the release `out-of-term`. | Per DT-05/DT-06. The platform is not the artist's censor and frequently is not even their distributor. The value is that someone finally knows the number; acting on it is the parties' business. Whether an obligation to act exists at all is Q-06. |
| D-16 | A **zero-price self-entitlement** is minted when a vendor places their own product into a project | Per edge case 23. The vendor needs no grant — they hold the rights — but the *chain* must be complete or the composite returns `unknown` on their own pack. Makes the lazy path the correct path (personas.md, Producer), applied to the vendor case. |
