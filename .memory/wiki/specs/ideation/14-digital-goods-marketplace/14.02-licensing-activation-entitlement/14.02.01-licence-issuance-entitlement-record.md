# Feature: Licence Issuance & Entitlement Record

> **Number**: 14.02.01
> **Parent**: [14.02 Licensing, Activation & Entitlement](./14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The record of what a buyer acquired — created at purchase, and the object every other feature in
this sub-domain reads or mutates. It is the thing that makes a digital sale different from a gear
sale: buying a cable transfers an object, buying a plugin creates an *ongoing relationship* with
a stated scope, a seat allowance, bound terms, and a revocability that a cable does not have.

It is also this domain's only structural stake in the thesis (D-18, domain DT-C). An entitlement
is not merely a receipt — it is a **dated, evidenced statement that a specific principal acquired
specific rights under a specific terms version**, minted at the exact moment the fact became true,
with a payment behind it. When a track built on that pack is contested three years later (domain
09), the entitlement is the only artifact that can answer without asking anyone to remember. That
is capture-at-source applied to purchased inputs, and it is why the record — not the key, not the
file, not the download — is the primary object here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Holds entitlements for everything they've bought; sees them in the library (14.03.03) with product, model, **version range** (D-04), seats used/total, and terms link. The version range is the field they act on: it tells them whether v2 is free or £79 (14.07.05) *before* they buy, not two years later. As a vendor of their own packs their sales *mint* entitlements they see only in aggregate (count, state distribution, revocation reasons) — never a named buyer's licence, which is not the vendor's business. Buying their own product is permitted and flagged (D-11). |
| Producer | Full | Same as Musician but at volume — hundreds of entitlements across years is the normal case (14.03.03 D-02). The persona most affected by the record's scope fields: before using a pack on a paid client's record they must read the bound terms version and get an answer to "can this ship commercially?" (14.02.05). Also the persona that buys **for someone else** — a pack on a client's budget, a plugin the label pays for — which is why purchaser and holder are separate fields (D-05). Buys bundles; needs each item independently refundable and independently transferable, which is why a bundle mints N entitlements, not one (D-07). |
| Operator | Full | Entitlements bought by a studio should belong to the **studio**, not the engineer who clicked buy — the engineer leaves, the plugins stay. Resolved behaviourally: the holder is a **principal**, selected at checkout, which may be a person or an org (D-05); the purchaser remains the individual who paid. The org-modelling half is sub-domain Q-03 (`/create-prd-architecture`). The Operator is the persona that will exercise the org path on nearly every purchase and the one for whom a person-only record is a business defect, not a nicety. |
| Fan | Read-only | The only touchpoint: music bought from an artist page (domain 20) also mints an entitlement, because the delivery/library machinery is shared (see domain index). The fan never sees the word "entitlement" — they see "my purchases", with a title, a date, and a download. No store access, no seat map (a consumer music purchase has no seat allowance at all), no terms UI (the consumer licence is domain 20's, not the 14.02.05 registry's), no version range, no transfer. Their record is the same object rendered with five fields suppressed. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

At order completion, an entitlement is minted binding: **purchaser** (who paid — for receipt,
refund and tax), **holder** (the principal who bears the rights — person or org, D-05), product,
**version range** (D-04), monetisation model (14.07), seat allowance, terms version (14.02.05,
frozen per CX-02), **allocated price and currency** (D-07), origin (purchase or grant, D-06), and
state.

Issuance is atomic with order completion — for a paid order that means confirmed payment capture.
A payment without an entitlement is a support ticket; an entitlement without a payment is theft.
Both are unrecoverable-by-default failures, so neither is allowed to exist even transiently: the
terms snapshot, the seat allowance and the entitlement commit together or none of them do (CX-02
q2 — an entitlement with no terms is unenforceable and unclearable).

**Not every entitlement comes from a sale** (D-06). Freeware (14.02.06), NFR copies, promotional
grants, competition prizes and vendor comps to endorsees all mint the same record through a
**grant** path that records a grantor and a reason instead of a capture. The record is identical;
only its origin differs.

**One entitlement per product per holder, always** (D-07). A bundle (14.07.04) mints N of them,
each carrying its apportioned share of the bundle price. An upgrade extends one in place
(14.07.05 D-01). A trial converts one in place (CX-06). Two entitlements for one product means
two seat pools, two terms lineages, and a library that lies.

**The issuance fact is indelible** (D-08). Revocation, transfer, expiry and account erasure change
state and holder — they never delete the record of what was acquired, from whom, on what date,
under which terms version. This is the whole of the record's evidentiary value (DT-05): a
revocation that erases the purchase destroys the clearance proof for a record already released.

For products bridged to third-party DRM (14.02.03), issuance instead deposits a code into that
system and the local record becomes descriptive rather than authoritative (CX-05).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer adds a product to the cart. The listing's **displayed** terms version, price, seat allowance, monetisation model and version range are captured onto the cart line at that moment.
2. Buyer completes checkout via the platform cart/payments cross-cut, selecting the **holder principal** — defaulting to their personal principal, or an org they hold a purchasing role in (domain 01).
3. Buyer agrees to the terms as displayed (14.02.05 renders them as three plain-language answers above the buy button, not an EULA wall).
4. Payment capture confirms.
5. Issuance mints the entitlement in the same transaction: purchaser, holder, product, version range, model, seat allowance, allocated price + currency, origin `purchase`, terms version frozen (CX-02), state `active`.
6. Entitlement appears in the buyer's library (14.03.03) within 5 seconds of capture confirmation.
7. Buyer requests their first download; the withdrawal waiver gate (14.09.01) is presented and consented to; downloads unlock (14.03.01).
8. If the product needs activation, a seat is available to the holder against this record (14.02.02, CX-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Payment captured, issuance fails | Issuance retried 3× with exponential backoff (1s / 4s / 16s). Still failing → the order enters reconciliation, alerts within 15 min, and is resolved by a human or by automatic reversal. Unresolved after 24h → automatic full refund, vendor notified, purchaser told what happened. Never silently dropped, never the buyer's problem to chase. | "Purchase confirmed — preparing your licence" → resolution within 5s in the normal case; if reconciliation is entered, an explicit "we're finishing this, we'll email you within 24h" — never a blank library |
| Terms snapshot fails during issuance | The whole issuance rolls back and the order enters reconciliation (CX-02 q2). An entitlement with no bound terms is unenforceable and unclearable — a partial commit here is worse than a failure. | Same as above — one failure path, not two |
| Payment capture reverses months later (**chargeback**) | Not a refund and must never be conflated with one (D-10). Card schemes allow ~120 days; SEPA direct debit allows 8 weeks unconditionally and 13 months for unauthorised claims — long after payout (14.10). Revocation runs through 14.09.03 with trigger `chargeback`, distinct from `refund`. Loss allocation is Q-03, not issuance's call. | Holder: library row shows revoked, reason "payment reversed", appeal route. Vendor: reported as a chargeback, **not** as a refund they granted |
| Async payment method (SEPA / bank debit / BNPL) — capture confirms days later | Entitlement minted in `pending-payment` (D-09). It grants **nothing**: no download URL, no activation, no seats, no waiver gate. Visible so the buyer knows the order exists. | "Awaiting payment — your licence unlocks when it clears" with the expected date |
| Async payment ultimately fails | State → `payment-failed`. The row persists (D-08) rather than vanishing. | "Payment didn't clear" + retry-purchase CTA. A disappeared order is indistinguishable from a bug |
| Buyer already owns the product | Blocked before payment by D-07 (one per product per holder). Converted to the useful adjacent action: seat top-up (14.02.02), upgrade if out of version range (14.07.05), or gift (Q-01). | "You already own this — add seats, or upgrade to v2 for £79" |
| Duplicate capture from a PSP retry, or a double-clicked checkout | Issuance is idempotent on the order line, not on the request. One entitlement; the duplicate capture is auto-refunded within 1h. | Nothing — one purchase, one licence, one charge |
| Buyer buys during a trial (CX-06) | Existing trial entitlement upgraded in place: expiry cleared, terms rebound per CX-02, seats set from the purchased model. Not duplicated. | Nothing changes in their DAW — it just stops expiring |
| Purchase lands *after* trial expiry already deactivated a machine | The upgrade reactivates the bound machines rather than requiring a manual re-auth (CX-06 q5). Requiring a buyer to re-authorise after paying is the defect the single-record design exists to prevent. | Software resumes; no re-activation step |
| **Purchase for an org where the buyer is an individual** | Resolved: holder is a **principal** selected at checkout (D-05). Personal is the default; an org appears in the selector only if the buyer holds a purchasing role in it (domain 01). Purchaser stays the individual; holder is the org. The engineer leaves, the plugin stays. Org *modelling* remains sub-domain Q-03 (`/create-prd-architecture`); the issuance behaviour does not wait on it. | Holder selector at checkout: "Buying for: Me / Ravenscourt Studios". Library shows the org's entitlements under the org context |
| Buyer selects an org they do not hold a purchasing role in | Rejected at checkout, before capture — never at issuance. Holder eligibility is resolved server-side from the buyer's org memberships, never from client input. | "You don't have purchasing permission for Ravenscourt Studios" with a request-access route |
| **Gift purchase (buyer ≠ holder)** | Expressible from day one because purchaser and holder are separate (D-05). If a gift flow ships (Q-01): the entitlement mints in `pending-acceptance`; the recipient must accept before it becomes `active`. The **terms version is frozen at purchase** (the vendor cannot swap it underneath the gift) but the **agreement to those terms is the recipient's, taken at acceptance** — the purchaser cannot consent on behalf of someone else to terms that will bind *their* released records. Unaccepted after 90 days → auto-refund to purchaser, entitlement to `expired`. At launch, absent that flow, checkout sets holder = purchaser and no gift path is offered. | Recipient: "Sam bought you Analog Drums — accept and agree to the licence". Purchaser: pending, then accepted-or-refunded |
| Gift to a recipient with no WeJammin account | `pending-acceptance` with no holder bound — there is no identity to bind rights to yet. Invitation issued; the record cannot become `active` until an account exists to hold it. Same 90-day window. | Recipient: an email invitation. Purchaser: "waiting for Sam to claim it" |
| **Buyer's account is deleted under GDPR erasure** | Resolved as far as behaviour allows: erasure detaches PII (purchaser identity, contact, payment reference) and reduces the holder to an opaque principal id. The entitlement itself **survives in pseudonymised form** — it is a contractual obligation to a vendor and the evidence behind any release cleared under it (DT-05); destroying it destroys the vendor's sale record *and* the erasing buyer's own rights proof. The honest consequence: with no authenticable holder, the licence becomes unusable — nobody can log in to exercise it. **The buyer must be told this before erasure completes**, because it is irreversible and they are unlikely to have modelled it. Whether law permits retaining even the pseudonymised record is genuinely contested — sub-domain Q-02 → `/create-prd-security`. | Pre-erasure: "Deleting your account permanently ends access to 47 licences you bought. The purchase records are kept in anonymised form because vendors and any releases you cleared with them rely on them. This cannot be undone." |
| **Bundle purchase** (14.07.04) | Mints N entitlements — one per product (D-07) — each carrying its **apportioned share** of the bundle price in the transaction currency. Each is independently refundable, transferable (14.06), revocable and terms-bound. | Library shows 5 products, not "1 bundle". The bundle exists on the receipt, not in the rights layer |
| Partial refund of one bundle item | Refunds that entitlement's **allocated** price, not a list price and not 1/5 of the total. The other four survive untouched. Without a per-entitlement allocated price this arithmetic has no correct answer, and both refunds and payouts (14.10) silently drift. Refund policy remains 14.09.02's. | "Refunding Analog Drums — £14 of your £70 bundle" |
| **Exclusive beat sale** (14.05) | Issuance of an exclusive is not an ordinary mint (D-12): it atomically terminates the product's availability *and does **not** revoke prior non-exclusive leases*. Grandfathering existing leases is the industry norm; revoking them would break records already released under a valid licence and is not what an exclusivity buyer is actually sold. The exclusive buyer must be told the prior-lease count **before** capture, since it is the thing they are buying and it is not zero. | Exclusive buyer, pre-purchase: "This beat has 12 active leases. Exclusivity ends new sales; existing leases continue." Then: beat delisted |
| Exclusive sale races a lease purchase | Exclusivity acquisition locks the product. In-flight lease checkouts are rejected **before capture**, with any authorisation hold released within 1h. If a lease captured first, it captured first — it becomes lease #13 and the exclusive buyer's disclosed count updates before their own capture. Both buyers cannot both be right; the loser must never be the one who already paid. | Lease buyer: "This beat was just sold exclusively — you have not been charged" |
| **Vendor's product is removed for a rights violation** (14.08 / 24) | The buyer did nothing wrong and this is domain 14's real exposure (domain DT-B), inbound not outbound. Delivery stops (14.03), entitlement → `suspended` with the reason, and the purchaser is refunded **regardless of the refund policy** — the store sold something the vendor had no right to sell, with a WeJammin receipt vouching for it. Whether their *already-released* work stays cleared is the bomb at 14.02.05 Q-02. | "This product was withdrawn for a rights claim. You've been refunded in full. If you've released work using it, [what this means for your release]" |
| Vendor delists the product, or exits the platform (14.08.05) | Entitlements are **unaffected**. The record does not die with the listing: the library row renders, downloads serve, the archive stays fetchable (14.07.01 D-03). A perpetual licence that dies with its vendor was a rental. | Nothing changes. The listing is gone from the store; the licence is not |
| Listing mutates between add-to-cart and capture (terms version, price, seat allowance, model, or version range) | The cart line binds what was **displayed** (CX-02 q5). If any of those five changed before capture, the checkout **re-presents the changed field and requires fresh consent** rather than silently binding either the old or the new. Silently binding the new overwrites what the buyer agreed to; silently binding the old lets a stale cart buy a withdrawn offer. | "The licence terms for this pack changed while it was in your cart. Here's what's different — agree to continue" |
| Buyer's account is suspended (domain 24) between capture and issuance | Issuance completes — they paid. The entitlement mints `active`; delivery and activation are gated by the suspension, not by the entitlement. Taking the money and minting nothing is theft with a moderation excuse. | "Purchase complete. Downloads are unavailable while your account is under review" + appeal route |
| **Refund/revocation (14.09.03) races an in-flight issuance** | The revocation is **queued against the order**, never dropped. It applies the moment the record commits. Dropping it because "there is nothing to revoke yet" mints a live entitlement for a refunded order — a silent free copy, discoverable only by audit. | Buyer: refund confirmed, library row revoked. No window where both are true |
| Concurrent checkouts for the same product by the same holder (two tabs, two devices) | Idempotency is on the order line; the second is rejected by D-07 before capture. Only one charge, only one record. | Second tab: "You already own this" |
| **Vendor buys their own product** (multi-hyphenate — the structural fact in personas.md) | Permitted (a producer legitimately buys a colleague's pack, and a vendor's own principal may hold an org's copy), but **flagged at issuance** as self-purchase: holder principal == vendor principal, or the holder is an org the vendor controls. Downstream exclusions follow: no payout (14.10), no sales-rank contribution, no review eligibility, no loyalty accrual (14.07.05). Without the flag, wash-trading is a free, undetectable way to buy rank on a new store. | Nothing to the buyer. The vendor's dashboard shows the sale excluded from earnings with the reason |
| Multi-hyphenate in two roles in one cart — vendor of pack A, buyer of pack B | Fully independent. The cart nets nothing against their vendor earnings; issuance for B is unaffected by their vendor status on A. The self-purchase flag is evaluated per line against the *product's* vendor principal, never against the cart. | An ordinary purchase and an ordinary sale, in the same day, as personas.md says is normal |
| Bridged DRM (CX-05): iLok deposit fails after capture | We have taken money for a licence in a system we do not control. Deposit retried 3× over 60s; still failing → entitlement stays `pending-external` and **is never marked `active`**. If the bridge is unavailable > 15 min, the buyer is offered a one-click full refund without argument. Marking it active because our row committed would be a lie about someone else's system. | "Your licence is being deposited into iLok" → success, or "iLok is unreachable — refund now?" |
| Bridged DRM: buyer has no iLok account | Entitlement mints `pending-external`; the deposit blocks on the buyer creating one. The record is honest that it describes something WeJammin does not hold. | "Add your iLok account ID to receive this licence" — surfaced before purchase where the listing knows (14.01.02) |
| **Malicious input**: client tampers with holder, price, terms version, product or seat allowance at checkout | Every one of those is resolved **server-side from the order and the listing**, never accepted from the client. The cart line records what was displayed; the server re-derives it. Client-supplied values are ignored, not validated-then-trusted. | Nothing — the tamper has no effect |
| **Malicious**: issuance request replayed to mint duplicates | Idempotency key is the order line. A replay returns the existing entitlement, never a second one. | Nothing |
| Fan buys music from an artist page (domain 20) | Same record, same machinery. Origin `purchase`, no seat allowance (a consumer music purchase has no machines), terms are domain 20's consumer licence rather than a 14.02.05 registry entry, no version range, no transfer. Five fields suppressed in presentation. | "My purchases" — title, date, download. The word "entitlement" never appears |
| Freeware / NFR / promo / prize (D-06) | Minted through the **grant** path: no capture, no waiver (no payment ⇒ no distance-selling withdrawal right — 14.02.06 Q-03), origin records the grantor and reason. Otherwise identical, including terms binding. NFR grants are marked non-transferable (14.06). | Library row identical to a purchase, labelled by origin ("Free", "NFR") |
| Currency: a partial refund three years after purchase | The allocated price is recorded in the **transaction currency** at issuance. FX has moved; refunding a re-converted figure gives an arbitrary number. The recorded figure is the one that must survive. | Refund in the currency they paid, at the figure they paid |
| Purchase timestamp disputed years later | The record carries the **platform's** capture timestamp, never a client clock. This timestamp plus the frozen terms version is the entire answer to "what was true on the day" (DT-05); a client-supplied time makes the evidence forgeable by the party with the most incentive. | A date on a receipt that holds up in a rights dispute |

### States

Presentation states (library, 14.03.03 — which is a rendering of this record, never a second source of truth):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Library fetch | Skeleton rows |
| Empty | No purchases or grants | Library empty state with store CTA |
| Populated | Entitlements exist | List with product, model, version range, seats used/total, terms link, origin |
| Error | Fetch failed | Retry; **cached list if available** (14.03.03 D-03) — a buyer must never see "you own nothing" on a transient error |
| Partial | Issued but bridged to external DRM, or awaiting async payment | "Managed in iLok" rather than a serial we control; or "awaiting payment" |
| Revoked | Refund (14.09.03), chargeback (D-10), or blacklist (14.02.04) | Row persists (14.09.03 D-04) showing revoked, with reason, trigger and appeal route |

Entitlement lifecycle states — the record's own state machine:

| State | Trigger | Grants | Transitions to |
|-------|---------|--------|---------------|
| `pending-payment` | Async capture (SEPA / BNPL) not yet confirmed | Nothing — no download, no activation, no seats | `active` on capture; `payment-failed` on failure |
| `pending-acceptance` | Gift minted, recipient has not accepted (Q-01) | Nothing | `active` on acceptance; `expired` after 90 days (+ auto-refund to purchaser) |
| `pending-external` | Bridged DRM deposit not yet confirmed (CX-05) | Nothing locally; the external system is authoritative | `active` on deposit; refund offered if the bridge is down > 15 min |
| `active` | Capture confirmed, or grant issued | Downloads (post-waiver, 14.09.01), seats, terms | `expired`, `revoked`, `suspended` |
| `expired` | Trial elapsed (14.02.06); unaccepted gift; bounded-term licence ends | Nothing | `active` on trial→purchase conversion (CX-06) |
| `revoked` | Refund, chargeback, transfer stripping the seller (14.06.02), hardware return (14.06.03), blacklist (14.02.04) | Nothing. Best-effort — "revoked-but-running" is real and accepted (14.09.03 D-01) | `active` only via successful appeal (domain 24) |
| `suspended` | Product withdrawn for a rights claim; holder account under review (24) | Nothing, pending resolution | `active` on resolution; `revoked` on adverse finding |
| `payment-failed` | Async capture failed | Nothing. Row persists (D-08) | Terminal — a new purchase mints a new record |

> Every transition preserves the issuance fact (D-08). No state — including `revoked` and
> post-erasure — deletes what was acquired, from whom, on what date, under which terms version.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An entitlement is a licence *key* — a string the buyer copies into a plugin | The candidate's framing ("Licence Issuance, Delivery"); how a 2005 software store worked | ❌ REJECTED — the key is an *artifact* of one enforcement strategy, not the thing being sold. Most of this store's realistic inventory (DT-02 in 14.01.01: sample packs, presets, beats, templates) has **no key at all** — there is nothing to type a serial into; a WAV is a WAV. Yet those purchases absolutely have an entitlement, and it is the most consequential one on the platform because its *terms* govern whether a released record is clearable (14.02.05). Modelling entitlement as key makes the key-less majority of the catalog unmodellable and quietly drops the domain's only link to the thesis. The record is primary; the key, where one exists, hangs off it. |
| DT-02 | An entitlement belongs to a person | The default; every consumer store does this | ⏳ DEFERRED — escalated to sub-domain Q-03. A domain expert would immediately object: **studios buy plugins, and studios are not people.** The Operator persona's whole economic unit is a business whose tools must outlive its staff; personas.md also establishes bands/labels/studios as first-class *entities* (domain 01), not sugar over a user account. A person-only entitlement means every studio purchase is held hostage by whichever engineer's account it landed in — the exact "proof evaporates when the person moves on" failure the project exists to fix, reproduced in the commerce layer. Not an agent call (it's architecture with product consequences); routed to `/create-prd-architecture` with a strong recommendation that entitlements bind to a principal that may be a person **or** an org. **Step 5 note**: the *behaviour* no longer waits on it — D-05 specifies a holder principal selected at checkout, which is correct under either modelling outcome. |
| DT-03 | The purchaser and the holder are the same party | The default in every consumer store; the shape the breadth pass assumed | ❌ REJECTED — they diverge constantly in this market, and in three different directions at once. A studio's card pays for a plugin the *studio* holds (Operator). A producer expenses a pack to a *client's* project (Producer — personas.md: "owns the room, coordinates contributors"). A label buys tools for an artist. A gift is the trivial case, not the motivating one. Separating payer from rights-holder is free now and structurally impossible later: retrofitting a purchaser field means re-deriving who paid for every historical entitlement from payment records that may have been erased (GDPR) or reversed (chargeback), for records whose whole value is being un-reconstructed evidence (DT-05). One field now; an unfixable archaeology problem in year three. |
| DT-04 | Every entitlement originates from a payment | The breadth pass's D-02 ("issuance is atomic with payment capture") states this as a rule | ❌ REJECTED — and D-02 as written is literally unimplementable for inventory the sub-domain already commits to. **Freeware is in scope** (14.02.06 is a ratified feature, and freeware entitlements have no payment to be atomic with). So are the plugin industry's standard non-sale origins that a domain expert would expect without being asked: **NFR** copies to reviewers and endorsees, **educational** licences with non-commercial terms, promotional grants, and competition prizes. All of them are real entitlements — they bind terms, they occupy seats, they appear in a library, they need revoking when eligibility was fraudulent. None of them are sales. Confirmed consequence: issuance has two trigger paths, purchase and **grant** (D-06), producing one identical record. The alternative — a second "free licence" object — reproduces exactly the two-records defect CX-06 exists to prevent. |
| DT-05 | The entitlement is a rights-evidence object, not just an access record — it must be citable by Rights (09) and Release (12) years after issuance | Domain DT-C; thesis D-18; 14.02.05's propagation chain | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the reason this feature exists at all rather than being a row on an order. 14.02.05 propagates *terms* downstream into a release's clearance record; the entitlement is the **evidence behind that attestation** — the dated proof that this principal actually acquired those terms, with a payment behind it. That makes the store the cleanest capture point on the platform (domain DT-C): unlike a split sheet, nobody has to be persuaded to file it, because they were buying something anyway. The load-bearing consequence is that the issuance fact must be **indelible** (D-08). A revocation that deletes the purchase, an erasure that deletes the buyer, a transfer that overwrites the holder, or a vendor exit that removes the product would each destroy the proof for a record already released — and destroy it precisely when it becomes contested and valuable, which problem-statement.md identifies as the single most litigated failure in music. State changes; history does not. |
| DT-06 | A refund is the only way an entitlement loses its funding | The sub-domain models 14.09 (refunds) as the sole revocation trigger; nothing anywhere mentions chargebacks | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap — nobody has designed for it. A **chargeback is not a refund**: no vendor consent, no policy check, no platform decision, a fee attached, and it arrives *months* later. Card schemes allow roughly 120 days; SEPA direct debit allows 8 weeks unconditionally and **13 months** for an unauthorised claim. By then the vendor has been paid out (14.10) and the buyer has had the files for a year. D-02's atomicity guarantees an entitlement at capture — but capture is *reversible for a third of a year*, so "issuance is atomic with payment" quietly means "atomic with a provisional event". Two obligations follow for issuance: the trigger must be recorded **distinctly** from a refund (D-10) — reporting a chargeback to a vendor as a refund they granted is a lie with money attached, and 14.09.03 D-01 already establishes that misreporting recovery to vendors is unacceptable — and the loss allocation is a real unfunded question (Q-03). Digital goods are the single highest-chargeback category in commerce; a marketplace that has not decided who eats one has decided by default. |
| DT-07 | Payment capture is an instantaneous event | Implicit in D-02's "atomic with payment capture" | ❌ REJECTED — for card payments, near enough. For SEPA direct debit, bank transfer, and BNPL — all standard in the EU/UK markets 14.09.01 identifies as the store's two most likely launch markets — capture confirms **days** later. There is no atomic moment to be atomic with. Only two coherent answers exist: restrict digital goods to instant-capture methods (a real, defensible product choice that costs conversion), or support a `pending-payment` entitlement that grants nothing (D-09). Fabricating a third — minting `active` on *authorisation* — hands out downloads on money that may never arrive, for a good that cannot be recovered (14.09.03: best-effort clawback is the physics). Whether the store accepts async methods at all is Q-04; the record must be able to express the answer either way. |
| DT-08 | A bundle is one entitlement | The natural reading of "buy the bundle"; how a receipt looks | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection has arithmetic consequences nobody has costed. Each product in a bundle is independently refundable (14.09), independently transferable (14.06 — a vendor may permit transfer on one and forbid it on another), independently revocable, and carries its **own** terms version (14.02.05). A single bundle entitlement can express none of that. So: N entitlements (D-07), each carrying its **allocated** price — the bundle discount apportioned. That last field is the one that looks optional and is not: without it, a partial refund of one item from a 5-for-£70 bundle has no correct amount (list price over-refunds; £14 flat over-refunds the cheap item and under-refunds the expensive one), and 14.10's per-download royalty pool cannot attribute revenue to the right contributor. The apportionment rule itself is a pricing decision (14.07.04); the *requirement that one exists on each record* is this feature's. |
| DT-09 | An exclusive beat sale is an ordinary issuance | 14.05 sits behind a cart like everything else | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the clearest case of a music-industry expectation that no generic commerce model contains. Selling exclusivity does two things a normal mint does not: it **terminates the product's availability** (that is what the buyer is paying for — every subsequent sale would destroy the thing sold), and it must **not** revoke prior non-exclusive leases. Grandfathering existing leases is the market norm, and the reason is provenance-shaped: a lease-holder released a record under a valid licence, and retroactively voiding it would un-clear finished work — the exact harm 14.02.05 Q-02 flags and the exact failure D-18 says this platform exists to prevent. Which means the honest thing the exclusive buyer is actually buying is "no *new* leases", and the prior-lease count is material information they must see **before** capture (D-12). Most beat stores bury it; that is a defect, not a convention worth copying. |
| DT-10 | Vendors and buyers are disjoint populations | The assumption behind every marketplace's fraud model | ❌ REJECTED — personas.md's opening structural fact forbids it: one human is routinely a vendor and a buyer in the same day, and "a persona model of 'user has a role' is wrong for this product". A vendor buying their own product is therefore **normal** and cannot be blocked. But it is also **wash-trading**: on a new store with no reputation, buying your own pack to manufacture sales rank, reviews and loyalty credit (14.07.05) is free, undetectable and effective. Both are true simultaneously, which is why the answer is neither "block" nor "ignore" but **flag at issuance** (D-11) and exclude downstream from payout (14.10), rank, reviews and loyalty. The flag must key on the *product's vendor principal* per line, not on the cart or the account — a producer who sells pack A and buys pack B in one order is doing two ordinary things, not one suspicious one. |
| DT-11 | The entitlement should record the buyer's rig at purchase, so compatibility is provable later | 14.01.04's saved rig profile exists and is right there; it would make "it doesn't work on my system" adjudicable | ❌ REJECTED — the rig at purchase is not a term of the licence, and putting it on the record makes a RAM upgrade a **licence event**. Machine identity is already owned by activation (14.02.02) at the correct granularity, with fingerprint tolerance as its own open question (14.02.02 Q-02); duplicating it here creates two machine records that will disagree, and the one on the immutable entitlement is the one that cannot be corrected. The compat check belongs where it is (14.01.04) as *advice at the buy button*. Binding it would also quietly convert a refund conversation ("this doesn't run on my Mac") into a licence-validity conversation, which is much worse for the buyer. |
| DT-12 | An unused entitlement should expire | Some vendors want it; inactive-licence reclamation exists in enterprise software and would cap the perpetual storage liability the domain has not costed (domain DT-D) | ❌ REJECTED — non-use is not abandonment, and in this market it is not even unusual. A musician who does not open a plugin for three years still owns it; a studio holds tools for the one session a year that needs them; personas.md's Musician runs a portfolio of small income streams, not a daily practice with every tool. Reclaiming on inactivity would also directly contradict 14.07.01's perpetual promise ("pay once, keep it forever") and D-08's indelibility — and it would break the single most valuable moment the library has (14.03.03 DT-01: the buyer returning years later to reinstall a career's worth of tools on a new laptop). If the perpetual storage liability is genuinely unaffordable (domain DT-D, 14.07.01 Q-01), the answer is pricing or format scope, not silently repossessing what people bought. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The entitlement record is primary; the licence key is an optional artifact of it | Per DT-01. Most realistic inventory has no key; a key-centric model cannot represent it. |
| D-02 | Issuance is atomic with **order completion** — for a paid order, confirmed payment capture | A payment without an entitlement, or vice versa, are both unacceptable failures with no clean recovery. **Sharpened at Step 5**: "order completion" not "payment", because grants have no payment (D-06) and async capture has no atomic moment (D-09). The atomicity is unchanged; the trigger is stated correctly. |
| D-03 | Terms version is frozen onto the record at issuance, not referenced live | Per CX-02 — the buyer's rights cannot be retroactively edited by the vendor. Also a legal requirement, not just a design choice. |
| D-04 | The entitlement carries a **version range**, required and displayed at the *original* purchase — **resolves Q-02** | Not "all future versions forever". Per 14.07.01 D-01 and 14.07.05 D-01: the range is what decides whether v2 is a free update or a paid upgrade, and it is the buyer's second-most-important question. Vendors need to express both "free lifetime updates" (unbounded range) and paid majors (bounded) — a record that cannot express the range forces one policy on every vendor. Displayed at purchase, not discovered two years later. |
| D-05 | Issuance records **purchaser** and **holder** as separate principals; a holder may be a person or an org; both default to the buyer | Per DT-03 and DT-02. Free now, unfixable later. Resolves the org-purchase and gift edge cases behaviourally without waiting on sub-domain Q-03's modelling, and is correct under either outcome of it. |
| D-06 | Issuance has two trigger paths — **purchase** and **grant** — producing one identical record; a grant records grantor and reason | Per DT-04. Freeware (14.02.06) is ratified scope and has no payment; NFR, educational, promo and prize grants are market-standard. A second "free licence" object reproduces the two-records defect CX-06 exists to prevent. |
| D-07 | **One entitlement per product per holder, always.** A bundle mints N; each carries its **allocated price and currency** | Per DT-08 and 14.07.05 D-01. Two records for one product means two seat pools, two terms lineages, a library that lies. The allocated price is what makes partial refunds (14.09.02) and per-contributor payout (14.10) arithmetically answerable. |
| D-08 | The **issuance fact is indelible**. State and holder change; the record of what was acquired, from whom, on what date, under which terms version, never does | Per DT-05. This is the record's evidentiary value and this domain's thesis stake (domain DT-C). A revocation, erasure, transfer or vendor exit that deletes the purchase destroys the clearance proof for work already released — at exactly the moment it becomes contested. |
| D-09 | A `pending-payment` entitlement grants **nothing** — no download, no activation, no seats — and is visible so the buyer knows the order exists | Per DT-07. Minting `active` on authorisation hands out an unrecoverable good against money that may never arrive; hiding the order entirely reads as a failed purchase and generates a duplicate. |
| D-10 | A **chargeback is recorded as a distinct trigger from a refund**, everywhere: the holder's library, the vendor's reporting, and 14.09.03's revocation | Per DT-06. No vendor consent, no policy check, arrives after payout. Reporting it to a vendor as a refund they granted is a lie with money attached — 14.09.03 D-01 already rules that unacceptable for clawback reporting; the same principle binds here. Loss allocation is Q-03, not this feature's. |
| D-11 | **Self-purchase is permitted and flagged** at issuance (holder principal == the product's vendor principal, or an org the vendor controls); downstream excludes it from payout, sales rank, reviews and loyalty accrual | Per DT-10. personas.md forbids blocking it — one human is vendor and buyer in the same day. Without the flag, wash-trading rank on a store with no reputation is free and undetectable. Evaluated per order line against the product's vendor, never against the cart. |
| D-12 | **Exclusive issuance** (14.05) atomically terminates the product's availability and does **not** revoke prior non-exclusive leases; the prior-lease count is disclosed before capture | Per DT-09. Grandfathering is the market norm and provenance-correct — voiding a lease un-clears a released record. What the buyer actually gets is "no *new* leases", so the existing count is the material fact, not a footnote. |
