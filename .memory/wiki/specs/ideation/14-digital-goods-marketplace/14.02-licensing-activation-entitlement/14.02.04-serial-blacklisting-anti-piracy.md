# Feature: Serial Blacklisting & Anti-Piracy Enforcement

> **Number**: 14.02.04
> **Parent**: [14.02 Licensing, Activation & Entitlement](./14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Killing a licence that is being abused — a serial posted on a warez forum, a key resold outside
the platform, an entitlement bought with a stolen card. This is the platform's most dangerous
power over its own users: a false positive does not inconvenience someone, it **stops a
professional from working**, possibly mid-session with a paying client in the room.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only own state · Config as vendor | Sees their licence's state and, if it is ever revoked, must see *why* and how to appeal. As a vendor of their own packs, may report abuse — but their packs are usually key-less (14.02.01 DT-01), so there is nothing to blacklist. |
| Producer | Config as vendor · Read-only as holder | As a vendor of commercial software, wants abused serials killed. As a holder, is the persona most damaged by a false positive — the one running a session with a client billing by the hour. Both sides live in one human, which is exactly the multi-hyphenate tension personas.md warns about. |
| Operator | Read-only | A blacklist landing on a room mid-session is the worst blast radius on the platform. Needs to see licence state per room before a session, not discover it at downbeat. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. Fan purchases are content with no serial to blacklist. |

## Behavior

A serial or entitlement is set to a revoked/suspended state that activation (14.02.02) reads and
refuses (CX-03). The hard problems are **reach** and **due process**, not the state change:
already-activated machines hold local authorisation and may never call home again — so a blacklist
is asynchronous at best and ineffective at worst, particularly under the fail-open posture
14.02.02 D-01 recommends.

Every blacklisting must carry a reason, notify the holder, and expose an appeal route into
domain 24. A silent kill is indistinguishable from a bug, and the holder will treat it as one —
generating a support ticket instead of a deterrent.

### Happy Path

1. Abuse is detected or reported (vendor report, leaked-serial monitoring, chargeback signal).
2. The case is adjudicated — per Q-01, by the platform rather than unilaterally by the vendor.
3. Entitlement/serial set to revoked with a recorded reason.
4. Activation begins refusing (CX-03); already-authorised machines lose authorisation on their next check-in, whenever that is.
5. Holder is notified with the reason and an appeal route (domain 24).
6. On a successful appeal, the state is restored and any spent seats are reinstated.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| False positive on a legitimate professional | Work stops. This is the failure mode that matters most. | Reason + immediate appeal + (proposed) a grace window before enforcement bites, so a session finishes |
| Blacklisted machine never calls home again (offline, fail-open grace) | Blacklist never lands; enforcement is theoretical | Nothing — which is the honest outcome and must not be reported to the vendor as "revoked" |
| Vendor blacklists a buyer they're in a dispute with | Weaponised enforcement — the vendor uses a kill switch as leverage in a disagreement | Blocked pending adjudication if Q-01 resolves to platform-adjudicated |
| Chargeback triggers automatic blacklist, but the chargeback was fraud against the *buyer* | The premise is itself the defect: a chargeback never enters this feature. It is a **revocation** trigger, not an accusation — D-03 keeps the two apart precisely because one is a routine commercial event and the other is a charge requiring due process, 14.02.01 D-10 records the chargeback as its own trigger distinct from a refund, and CX-19 lands it as `revoked/reversed` on the entitlement. An *automatic* blacklist is doubly excluded: D-01 makes every blacklisting platform-adjudicated, which no automatic rule is | Reason, notification and an appeal route into domain 24 (D-02) — never a silent kill, and never the word "piracy" on a stolen-card victim's account. What evidence reverses the reversal once the issuer confirms third-party card fraud is re-pointed from the completed `/ideate-discover` Step 5 to `/create-prd` (Q-04 below); who bears the loss is already tracked as 14.02.01 Q-03 |
| Product is DRM-bridged (14.02.03) | **We cannot blacklist it** — PACE controls it. See `#R-02`. | Vendor: "This product is managed in iLok; request revocation there." |
| Serial leaked and mass-activated before detection | Seats exhausted by strangers; the legitimate holder is locked out of their own purchase | Seat reclaim (14.02.02 D-02) plus re-issuance, not blacklisting the victim |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | State check | Skeleton on the licence row |
| Empty | No enforcement actions exist | Nothing — an absent state, not an empty one |
| Populated | Licence active | Normal library row |
| Error | State check failed | Under fail-open (14.02.02 D-01), treated as active — an unreachable check must not read as revoked |
| Suspended | Under investigation | "This licence is under review" with reason and appeal link |
| Revoked | Adjudicated abuse or refund (14.09.03) | Reason, date, appeal route — never a bare failure |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Anti-piracy enforcement is a core capability of a digital goods marketplace | The candidate's presence in the sweep; every incumbent has it | ❌ REJECTED at the *scale the candidate implies*, on this catalog. Blacklisting only bites products with a serial and an activation check — and per 14.02.01 DT-01 and 14.01.01 DT-02, the realistic launch inventory is sample packs, presets, beats and templates, which have **no serial and nothing to check**. You cannot blacklist a WAV. For key-less content the actual enforcement mechanism is forensic watermarking (14.03.05) plus takedown — which is why candidate 22's bundling of "watermarking" with "blacklisting" was a category error worth splitting (sub-domain D-01). This feature is real but small, and it serves inventory the store may never obtain. Proposed `could`, not `must`. |
| DT-02 | A vendor should be able to blacklist their own product's serials | The obvious ownership intuition — it's their software | ❌ REJECTED — this hands one user a kill switch over another user's livelihood with no due process, and the multi-hyphenate structure makes it worse than in a normal marketplace. Per personas.md, **the same human is vendor and buyer**; a producer selling a preset pack and a producer running a session are one person wearing two hats. A vendor in a payment dispute could kill a buyer's tools as leverage — which is precisely the Producer anti-persona behaviour personas.md already names ("withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute"), reproduced with a bigger weapon. Blacklisting must be **platform-adjudicated** (domain 24), vendor-*requested*. The cost is latency on genuine abuse; the alternative is a platform where users can disable each other. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Blacklisting is platform-adjudicated; vendors request, they do not execute | Per DT-02. The multi-hyphenate structure means vendor and buyer are the same population — unilateral kill switches let users disable each other. |
| D-02 | Every enforcement action carries a reason, a notification, and an appeal route | A silent kill is read as a bug, generates a ticket, and deters nobody. Due process is also what makes a false positive survivable. |
| D-03 | Blacklisting (abuse) and revocation (refund) are separate features despite sharing a state transition | Different triggers, different due process, different personas. Merging them would apply refund's routine mechanics to an accusation. |
