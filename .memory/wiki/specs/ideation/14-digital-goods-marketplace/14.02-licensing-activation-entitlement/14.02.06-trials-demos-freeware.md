# Feature: Trials, Demos & Freeware Entitlements

> **Number**: 14.02.06
> **Parent**: [14.02 Licensing, Activation & Entitlement](./14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Letting someone use a digital good before (or without) paying — a time-limited trial, a
feature-limited demo, or a permanently free product. For software you cannot inspect before
buying and cannot return after downloading (14.09), the trial is not marketing; it is the
**substitute for the try-before-you-buy that physical gear gets for free** when you plug it in at
a shop.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Trials a plugin on the one machine they own. The persona most reliant on trials because they cannot afford to be wrong — personas.md notes they "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products", so a bad purchase is a real loss, not a rounding error. |
| Producer | Full | Trials against a real session, which is the only test that matters — a plugin that works in isolation and crashes in a 200-track project has failed. Needs the trial long enough to reach a mix, which is days, not minutes. Also the vendor setting trial length on their own products. |
| Operator | Full | Trials in a room before committing to seats for four rooms (14.02.02). The trial's job here is de-risking a multi-seat purchase, so a single-machine trial answers the wrong question. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. There is no fan-facing product here to trial; music from an artist page (domain 20) has its own preview model. |

## Behavior

A trial is an **entitlement with an expiry** (CX-06) — the same record as a purchase, not a
parallel structure (sub-domain D-02). Conversion to a paid entitlement mutates it in place:
expiry cleared, terms rebound (CX-02), seats set. This is what prevents the classic defect where
paying customers get deactivated because their trial record expired alongside a new purchase
record.

Demo feature-limiting (noise bursts, disabled save, silence every 30 seconds) is implemented in
the **vendor's binary**, not by WeJammin — we cannot reach inside someone else's plugin. What is
ours: issuing the trial entitlement, and refusing to issue it repeatedly to the same machine.

Trial abuse prevention keys on machine identity, reusing the fingerprint activation already
computes (CX-04) rather than building a second identity system.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a trial from a listing.
2. System checks trial history for the machine fingerprint (CX-04).
3. No prior trial → entitlement issued with an expiry; download unlocks (14.03.01).
4. Buyer uses the product; reminders fire before expiry (the conversion moment).
5. Buyer purchases; the **same** entitlement is upgraded in place (CX-06) — nothing in their DAW changes, no reactivation.
6. Had they not purchased, the entitlement expires and authorisation lapses.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Trial expires mid-session | Fail-open posture (14.02.02 D-01) should mean the session survives to a save point rather than a plugin vanishing from a live mix | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on 14.02.02 Q-01 |
| Trial farming — new machine ID per trial (VM, spoofed fingerprint) | Detected as best-effort; determined evasion succeeds | Nothing user-facing; refusal messaging must not teach evasion |
| Legitimate second trial: buyer trialled on a dead laptop, now has a new machine | Refused as a repeat, punishing an honest buyer for a hardware failure | Appeal route — an unappealable refusal converts a would-be customer into an ex-customer |
| Buyer purchases *after* trial expiry deactivated the machine | Upgrade must reactivate, not demand a manual re-auth (CX-06) | Works again on purchase, no extra steps |
| Freeware product | Entitlement with no expiry and no payment — still needs terms (14.02.05), because free ≠ unlicensed | Normal library row; terms still bound |
| Trial of a DRM-bridged product (14.02.03) | Trial is the third party's mechanism, not ours | "Trial managed in iLok" |
| Trial of a sample pack | Meaningless — a WAV cannot expire. Trials apply to software, not content. | No trial offered; preview is the audition player instead (14.04.01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Trial requested | "Starting your trial…" |
| Empty | Eligible, not started | "Try free for N days" on the listing |
| Populated | Trial active | Days remaining, prominent; purchase CTA |
| Error | Trial refused | Reason (already trialled / not eligible) + appeal route, never a bare "no" |
| Partial | Trial active but expiring soon | Escalating reminder; the conversion moment |
| Expired | Trial ran out | Entitlement expired, product listed in library as "trial ended" with purchase CTA |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Trial abuse prevention is a meaningful defence worth investing in | The candidate's framing ("Trial Abuse Prevention" named as a co-equal capability) | ❌ REJECTED at the investment level implied. Fingerprint-based trial limits stop the casual repeat, not the determined evader — VMs and spoofed fingerprints defeat them, and always have. Meanwhile every tightening punishes the honest buyer whose laptop died and who now cannot re-trial. The asymmetry is brutal: the evader loses nothing when refused (they spin up another VM), while the legitimate buyer loses the ability to evaluate a product and simply doesn't buy it. Best-effort, appealable, cheap — and explicitly **not** hardened. A harder line optimises against a population that isn't paying anyway, at the cost of the population that would. |
| DT-02 | Trials are a marketing feature — a `could`, cut early if scope tightens | Where "trials" usually lands on a roadmap | ❌ REJECTED — and the reason is structural rather than promotional. This store sells goods the buyer **cannot inspect and cannot return** (14.09.01: the EU withdrawal right is waived at download). Physical gear (13) gets try-before-you-buy free — you plug it in at the shop. Strip out the trial and a plugin purchase becomes an irreversible bet on a spec sheet, which is a materially worse deal than the market standard and will read as one. So the trial is the *only* pre-purchase inspection this domain has, which makes it a peer of the compatibility checker (14.01.04), not a promotion. Its honest caveat: it applies to software, and the store's realistic launch inventory is content (14.01.01 DT-02) — you cannot trial a WAV. For that inventory the audition player (14.04.01) does this job, which is why *that* feature inherits the priority this one would otherwise have. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A trial is the same entitlement record as a purchase, with an expiry — not a separate object | Per CX-06/sub-domain D-02. Two records means the trial expires under a paying customer and their software stops. |
| D-02 | Trial abuse prevention is best-effort, appealable, and deliberately not hardened | Per DT-01. Hardening optimises against non-buyers at the expense of buyers. |
| D-03 | Demo feature-limiting is out of scope — it lives in the vendor's binary | We cannot reach inside a third-party plugin. Modelling it would be specifying someone else's code. |
