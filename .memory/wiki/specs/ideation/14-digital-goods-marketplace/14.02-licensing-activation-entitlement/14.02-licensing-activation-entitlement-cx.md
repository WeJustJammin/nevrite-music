## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Issuance ↔ Activation & Seats

**Relationship**: The entitlement record carries the seat allowance; activation decrements it by
binding a machine fingerprint. Deactivation returns a seat. All seat arithmetic reads and writes
the entitlement, making it the single source of truth and the contention point.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: 1–2 machines; rarely thinks about seats until a machine dies with a seat still bound to it.
- **Producer**: many machines, frequently reinstalled; the persona that hits seat exhaustion legitimately and is most damaged by an unforgiving model.
- **Operator**: seats are rooms and rooms have staff turnover; needs an org-level view of "which room holds which seat" (index Q-03).
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The entitlement owns the seat count. Concurrent activations from two machines against a last remaining seat must not both succeed — this needs a real lock, not a read-then-write.
2. **Trigger chain**: Activation → seat decrement, sync. If the decrement fails the activation must fail closed (software doesn't run) rather than open — but failing closed during a paid session is exactly the harm index Q-01 questions the whole model over.
3. **Permission intersection**: Only the entitlement holder may activate. For an org-held entitlement (Q-03), "holder" is unresolved.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Seat exhaustion notifies the holder. An Operator's exhaustion should notify the org, not the individual who happened to click.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — a refund-driven revocation (14.09.03) racing an activation. Revocation must win; an in-flight activation against a revoked entitlement must be rejected.

### CX-02: Issuance ↔ Terms Registry

**Relationship**: At purchase, issuance snapshots the terms version onto the entitlement. This
freeze is what makes the downstream clearance chain (14.02.05) durable: three years later, when a
track's rights are contested (domain 09), the question is what the terms said **on the day of
purchase**, and only the frozen reference can answer it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the beneficiary — their released track's clearance rests on the terms they bought under, not the ones in force today.
- **Producer**: as vendor, may edit terms going forward but cannot reach backwards into sold entitlements.
- **Operator**: read-only.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Terms are vendor-owned and versioned, immutable once bound. The entitlement holds a reference, not a copy — but the referenced version can never be edited or deleted, only superseded.
2. **Trigger chain**: Purchase → snapshot, sync, part of the same transaction as issuance. If the snapshot fails, issuance must fail — an entitlement with no terms is unenforceable and unclearable.
3. **Permission intersection**: A vendor's edit permission on terms does not extend to already-bound versions. This is the whole point.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A vendor publishing new terms may notify existing holders, but the new terms do not apply to them — so the notification is informational and must not read as a change to their rights.
5. **State transition conflict**: A terms edit racing a checkout — the checkout must bind whichever version was displayed to the buyer, not whichever is current at commit.

### CX-03: Blacklisting ↔ Activation & Seats

**Relationship**: Blacklisting sets the entitlement (or a specific serial) to a revoked state that
activation reads. The hard part is reach: already-activated machines hold local authorisation and
may never call home again.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: a wrongly blacklisted serial stops paid work — a false positive here is a business-destroying event, not an inconvenience.
- **Operator**: a blacklist hitting a room mid-session is the worst-case blast radius.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Entitlement state is single-owner (platform/vendor); no merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Blacklist → activation rejection, async in practice (offline machines re-check on their own schedule, or never).
3. **Permission intersection**: Vendor-initiated blacklisting on a buyer's licence is a serious power. Whether a vendor can do this unilaterally or needs platform adjudication is 14.02.04 Q-01.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The holder must be told, with a reason and an appeal route (domain 24) — a silent kill is indistinguishable from a bug.
5. **State transition conflict**: Blacklist racing a legitimate activation — reject, then let appeal restore.

### CX-04: Trials ↔ Activation & Seats

**Relationship**: Trial abuse prevention needs to recognise "this machine already had its trial",
which is the machine-identity capability activation already owns. Reusing it avoids a second
fingerprinting system — but binds a low-stakes marketing feature to the highest-privacy data in
the sub-domain.

**Role scoping**: See 14.02.06 Role Lens.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Trial history is keyed on the same fingerprint as activation. Shared read, separate write.
2. **Trigger chain**: Trial request → fingerprint lookup → issue or refuse.
3. **Permission intersection**: Trial refusal must not leak *why* precisely enough to teach evasion, yet must be appealable — a genuine tension.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Trial expiry notifies the holder; that's the conversion moment (14.07).
5. **State transition conflict**: Trial expiring mid-session, or trial→purchase conversion racing expiry. See CX-06.

### CX-05: DRM Bridges ↔ Issuance

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. For PACE/iLok products, WeJammin issues nothing enforceable —
it deposits an activation code into iLok's system and becomes a reseller. The entitlement record
then describes something WeJammin does not control and cannot revoke, which breaks the assumptions
14.02.04 and 14.09.03 rest on.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: read-only, and the persona this pairing confuses most — "the code went to my iLok instead of here" (14.02.03 Role Lens). Their library row is descriptive only and marked externally managed, and that disclosure has to land at checkout rather than after payment (14.02.03 D-01).
- **Producer**: the persona it exists for. An AAX plugin for Pro Tools is PACE-managed by industry default, so their entitlement record is a receipt while the enforceable licence lives in someone else's system. As a *vendor* they never meet it — their own inventory is key-less (14.02.01 DT-01) and has nothing to bridge.
- **Operator**: room-held purchases whose enforcement lives in a third party's account. The entitlement can name the org (14.02.01 D-05) while iLok names a person, which is the org-vs-person holder question (sub-domain Q-03) arriving through a door WeJammin does not control.
- **Fan**: not affected — no software, no activation, no iLok (14.02.03 Role Lens).

**Synthesis questions**: Deferred — Medium confidence; contingent on reseller integration being obtainable at all (14.02.03 Q-01), where the deferral is re-pointed from the completed Step 6 synthesis. One shape is already tracked rather than deferred: a deposit into an account belonging to someone other than the buyer (14.02.03 Q-04).

### CX-06: Trials ↔ Issuance

**Relationship**: A trial and a purchase are the same entitlement at different points in its
lifecycle, not two records. Conversion mutates expiry and terms in place.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: buys during a trial and expects nothing to change except that it keeps working.
- **Operator**: trials in one room, buys seats for four — conversion also changes seat allowance.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Same record; no conflict by construction — which is the reason for the design.
2. **Trigger chain**: Purchase → entitlement upgraded (expiry cleared, terms rebound per CX-02, seats set). Must be atomic with payment.
3. **Permission intersection**: None new.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Confirmation to holder; the install should not require reactivation.
5. **State transition conflict**: Purchase landing *after* trial expiry has already deactivated a machine — the upgrade must reactivate rather than require a manual re-auth.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.02.05 Terms Registry | 14.02.02 Activation & Seats | Tempting (both feel like "licence enforcement") but they operate on unrelated objects. Terms govern what you may do with the *output* — can this loop go in a commercial release. Seats govern how many *machines* run the software. No terms clause is enforceable by an activation check, and no seat limit appears in an EULA's grant of rights. Coupling them would imply the platform can technically enforce usage rights, which it cannot and should not claim. |
| R-02 | 14.02.03 DRM Bridges | 14.02.04 Blacklisting | For iLok-managed products WeJammin is not the enforcement authority (CX-05) — it cannot blacklist what PACE controls. The pair looks natural and is actually a null relationship, which is worth recording precisely because a spec writer would otherwise assume the blacklist covers bridged products. |
| R-03 | 14.02.06 Trials | 14.02.05 Terms Registry | A trial's terms are the vendor's trial EULA — a static document, not a downstream-clearance grant. Nobody clears a commercial release against a trial licence; if they did, the terms would forbid it. No chain to model. |
