# Feature: Download Delivery & Resumable Transfer

> **Number**: 14.03.01
> **Parent**: [14.03 Delivery, Versioning & Library](./14.03-delivery-versioning-library-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Moving the bytes the buyer paid for onto their machine, authorised against their entitlement and
survivable across a dropped connection. The scale is what makes this non-trivial: sample libraries
routinely run 40–100 GB, they are re-fetched every time a machine is replaced, and a transfer that
cannot resume is a transfer that never completes on a hotel wifi or a studio's saturated uplink.

The feature exists because every other promise in this domain terminates here. A perpetual licence
(14.07.01), a legacy archive (14.03.02), a vendor-exit continuity guarantee (14.08.05) and a
library row that never vanishes (14.03.03 D-04) are all worth exactly nothing if the bytes will
not come down. This is the last mile of every commitment domain 14 makes.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | One machine, domestic broadband, a 60 GB fetch that runs overnight. Needs to know it is safe to close the laptop lid — and the honest answer is no (E-04). Re-downloads years later on a new laptop and expects it to still be there; **this is the persona who meets the cold-storage restore wait (DT-04)**, because their 2026 purchase is exactly the artifact nobody has touched since. Will never verify a checksum by hand — verification must be automatic and invisible, and the failure must be legible (E-19). |
| Producer | Full | Downloads the same product to 3–5 machines, repeatedly, against a deadline. The persona for whom "start again from 0%" after a 60 GB failure is a workday lost. Repeated fetches across machines must not be read as abuse (D-06). Fetches archived versions, not just latest (CX-02) — a 2021 mix needs the 2021 build. **Also the persona who is a vendor of their own packs**, so one account legitimately fetches through both the buyer gate and the vendor gate on the same day (E-16). |
| Operator | Full | Installs per room. Frequently on a locked-down, proxied or metered studio network — the proxy that strips `Range` headers (E-08) is their failure, not anyone else's. Sometimes air-gapped, in which case a browser download on a connected machine and a manual carry on a drive **is** the workflow (Q-02, now resolved: D-09). The only persona who will run `shasum` by hand, which is why the checksum is published as copyable text and not merely checked for them. Their structural blocker is elsewhere: the studio's library sits in a departed engineer's personal account (14.02 Q-03), so per-room installs can stop working for reasons delivery cannot fix. |
| Fan | Read-only | The shared path, deliberately unrecognisable. A fan buying an album on an artist page (domain 20) traverses this same authorised gate — but at 80 MB–1 GB, single version, no seats, no archive, and no transfer manager. They see a **download link**, not a transfer. They must still meet the withdrawal-waiver gate (14.09.01) because the law does not care about file size — in a plain sentence, not a professional-tool ceremony. Whether their music is watermarked is unresolved and has a different privacy profile entirely (14.03.05 Q-02). |

## Behavior

Delivery is never open. Every fetch is authorised against a live check, and every fetch is scoped
to one buyer. The unit of delivery is a **transfer grant**: a buyer-scoped, artifact-bound
authorisation minted after all gates pass, valid for a bounded window, and re-checked on every
byte-range request made against it.

**The grant model exists to reconcile two requirements that a naive design makes incompatible.**
Authorisation demands that a leaked link cannot serve bytes forever; resumability demands that the
URL a browser retries be the *same* URL it started with — a freshly-minted signed URL is, to a
browser, a different download, and the resume silently becomes a restart. The reconciliation
(D-04) is to move the control off the URL's lifetime and onto the request: the grant's URL is
**stable for the grant's window**, and **every range request re-authorises**. The URL's TTL was
never the security boundary; the per-request check is.

What the gates are, in order (all synchronous — any failure means no grant, never a partial
transfer):

1. **Entitlement** — state `active` for this buyer and this product (CX-01, owned by 14.02.01).
2. **Version entitlement** — the requested version falls inside the entitlement's range (CX-03).
3. **Withdrawal waiver** — captured before the first byte of this order (14.09.01 D-02).
4. **Artifact resolution** — a build exists for the requested (version, platform) (CX-02).
5. **Artifact availability** — the artifact is in hot storage, or a restore is initiated (DT-04).

Version resolution (CX-02) defaults to the latest the buyer is entitled to; the archive is
reachable by explicit selection. Where the vendor has enabled watermarking (14.03.05), the stamp
is applied in this path, which makes the artifact per-buyer and therefore uncacheable (CX-04) —
and, per DT-03, forces the mark to be **deterministic per grant** or resume cannot work at all.

**Delivery has two granularities.** A pack is fetchable as a whole archive *and* per-asset, because
CX-05 makes each of a pack's hundreds of loops an individually addressable object and `#R-03`
requires those fetches through this same gate. "Download the pack" and "download this one kick"
are the same authorisation with different payloads.

### Concrete transfer parameters

| Parameter | Value | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Grant window | **72 hours** from mint, resumable throughout | 100 GB at 4 Mbit/s ≈ 56 h. A 24 h window fails the pathological-but-real case (a rural Musician on a 60 GB library) and a link expiring mid-transfer is a self-inflicted failure. |
| Re-authorisation | **Every range request** | The control. Makes a long window safe and makes revocation land (D-05). |
| Concurrent streams per grant | **2** | Permits a retry overlapping a stalled stream. A grant shared with 50 people hits the cap immediately and degrades the sharer's own transfer first — the cap, not the TTL, is what makes a shared link useless. |
| Concurrent transfers per account | **5**, queued beyond | The Producer's stated legitimate ceiling is a 5-machine install (D-06). Generous for a human, useless for a scripted catalog dump. Excess **queues**, never denies (D-07). |
| Stall detection | **no bytes for 60 s** → interrupted | Distinguishes a dead TCP connection from a slow one. |
| Auto-retry | **3 attempts, backoff 2 s / 8 s / 32 s**, then manual resume | Covers a transient drop without a human; a fourth attempt is a network the client cannot fix. |
| Checksum | **SHA-256, published as copyable text** beside the download control | Auto-verified for the Musician; hand-verifiable for the air-gapped Operator (D-09). |
| Checksum-fail retry | **1 automatic re-verify**, then a support path | A single mismatch is usually a corrupt transfer; a second is a stored-artifact defect and must escalate, not loop. |
| Revocation kill latency | in-flight stream terminated **within 30 s** | Bounds the window in which a refunded buyer can race the clawback (D-05). |
| Cold-storage restore | artifacts unfetched **180 days** → archival; first fetch restores in **up to 12 h**, buyer emailed | The price of the perpetual promise (DT-04). Disclosed **before** the click, never discovered after it. |

### Happy Path

1. Buyer opens their library (14.03.03) and clicks the download control on an entitlement row.
2. System resolves the artifact — latest version the buyer is entitled to, for their platform (CX-02).
3. System shows, **before any bytes move**: download size, **unpacked size** (D-02), version, SHA-256, and — if the artifact is archived — the restore wait (DT-04).
4. System runs the five gates: entitlement `active`, version in range, waiver captured (14.09.01), build exists, artifact hot.
5. All pass → a transfer grant is minted, buyer-scoped and artifact-bound, with a stable URL valid 72 h.
6. If the vendor enabled watermarking (14.03.05), the grant records the mark seed so every byte range regenerates byte-identically (DT-03).
7. Transfer begins. Progress shows **bytes transferred / total and an estimated finish time**, not only a percentage — "38.2 of 61.4 GB · finishes ~04:20" is actionable for a multi-hour transfer; "62%" is not.
8. Connection drops at 60%. The client detects the stall after 60 s and auto-retries (2 s / 8 s / 32 s), issuing a range request from the byte offset against the **same** grant URL.
9. Each range request re-authorises. Entitlement still `active` → bytes resume from 38.2 GB, never from zero.
10. On completion, SHA-256 is verified automatically against the published checksum.
11. Verification passes → the library row records the completed transfer (date, version, bytes served) as the evidence 14.09.02 will need if this order is ever disputed (D-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| E-01 | 60 GB transfer, connection drops repeatedly | Stall detected at 60 s; 3 auto-retries with backoff; range resume from offset against the same grant URL. The 72 h window outlives any legitimate transfer. | "Resuming from 38.2 GB…" then normal progress. Never a restart. |
| E-02 | **Browser tab closed mid-transfer** | The browser's download manager owns the transfer, not the tab — it continues. Closing the **browser** or restarting the OS leaves a partial the browser can resume from its own Downloads UI, and it will succeed because the grant URL is still live and still honours `Range`. **The wall (D-03)**: WeJammin cannot read, resume or verify a partial file it does not own. Our obligations are exactly three — always honour `Range`, keep the grant alive for 72 h, and never mint a new URL for the same grant. | Library row: "Started 22:14 — 38.2 of 61.4 GB. If it stopped, resume it from your browser's downloads; the link stays valid until Sunday 22:14." Clicking the library control again starts a **new** download and says so before it does. |
| E-03 | **Laptop lid closed / machine sleeps** | The transfer suspends with the machine and resumes on wake, inside the 72 h window. Beyond 72 h the grant is dead and the browser's resume fails. | Stated up front at step 3: "Safe to sleep. This link stays valid for 72 hours." An honest promise beats a silent failure at hour 80. |
| E-04 | Buyer's disk fills at 90% | Pre-empted, not discovered. **Unpacked** size is shown before the transfer starts (D-02) — a 40 GB download that becomes 90 GB on disk is a failure after hours of work. | "Download 41 GB · needs 94 GB free once unpacked." No free-space *check* is possible from a browser; the number is the mitigation. |
| E-05 | **Entitlement revoked (14.09.03) mid-transfer** — buyer refunded, 60% through 60 GB | **Killed (D-05).** This falls out of D-01 rather than needing a new rule: a resumed range request *is* a next download request, and 14.09.03 D-02 already binds us to fail the next download. A single continuous stream is terminated at the next chunk boundary within 30 s. The partial fails checksum, so nothing usable was handed over. No grace window — 14.09.03 Q-02's grace argument is about **activation** (a clawback stopping a live session); a stopped transfer stops nothing. | "Download stopped — your licence for X was revoked on 16 Jul (refunded at your request). The partial file is incomplete and will not verify." Appeal route per 14.09.03 D-04. |
| E-06 | Revocation appealed and overturned mid-window | Entitlement returns to `active`; the next range request re-authorises and passes; the browser's partial resumes if it survives and the grant window has not expired. | "Your licence was restored. Resume from your browser's downloads, or start again." |
| E-07 | **Vendor withdraws the version mid-transfer (14.08.04 rollback)** | **The reason decides (DT-05).** Withdrawn as *superseded* or *defective* → the transfer completes; the artifact is valid, only the recommendation changed (CX-02 q5). Withdrawn as **malicious or rights-infringing** → the transfer is killed within 30 s, the grant is void, and the partial is flagged as unsafe. CX-02 q5's blanket "let it complete" is wrong for the malware case. | Superseded: nothing — completes normally, "a newer build is available". Malicious: "This build was withdrawn for a security issue. The download was stopped. Delete the partial file." |
| E-08 | **Corporate/studio proxy strips `Range`** | The server answers `200` with the full body where `206` was requested. Naively this silently restarts a 38 GB transfer from zero and the buyer watches hours evaporate. The client detects `200`-where-`206`-asked and **stops before writing**. | "Your network doesn't support resuming downloads. Continuing means re-fetching all 61.4 GB from the start. Continue / Cancel." Never silent. |
| E-09 | **Resume against a different artifact** — partial is v2.4.0, grant now resolves v2.4.1 | Structurally impossible: the grant is bound to an **immutable artifact identity** (product, version, platform, build). A range request whose artifact identity does not match is refused — never spliced. Splicing two builds produces a file that is corrupt in a way no checksum catches until unpack. | `409` — "This download is for a different build. Start again." |
| E-10 | **Watermark regenerated on resume** (CX-04 × resume) | Would be catastrophic and is designed out. A per-download random mark shifts byte offsets, so resumed bytes do not align with the partial and the file is garbage that fails verification with no explanation. Per DT-03 the mark **must** be deterministic per grant — the grant records the seed and every range regenerates byte-identical output. | Nothing. This is a correctness constraint on 14.03.05, not a user-visible state. |
| E-11 | Same product fetched to 5 machines in a day | Legitimate Producer behaviour and explicitly not abuse (D-06). No volume cap. The limits that exist are **concurrency** (5/account, 2/grant), which a human never notices and a script hits instantly. | Nothing. |
| E-12 | 6th concurrent transfer started | **Queues, never denies** (D-07). A paid buyer is never refused their goods for a capacity convenience. | "Queued — starts when one of your 5 active downloads finishes." Reorderable. |
| E-13 | Download URL shared with a friend | The grant is buyer-scoped and re-authorises per range against the **buyer's** entitlement — so it does serve, within the window. The TTL was never the deterrent. Two things are: the 2-stream cap (a link shared to a group degrades the sharer's own transfer first) and, decisively, the watermark (14.03.05) — the leaked file carries the sharer's identity. | Nothing to the sharer. Traced later as an adjudicated case in domain 24, never a unilateral block. |
| E-14 | **Scripted mass-fetch** — buyer owns 200 packs, scripts all 200 | Concurrency cap (5) turns a parallel dump into a serial one. Volume is not the signal; **fan-out is** — the same grant streaming from many distinct networks at once. Signal, not block: CX-01 q4 is explicit that treating a touring musician as a threat is the wrong default. | Nothing visible. Fan-out surfaces as a security signal in 24, not as a locked account. |
| E-15 | **Egress amplification** — "download, cancel at 1%", looped 10,000 times | Each cycle forces a fresh watermark stamp and 1% of egress. Given DT-02's uncosted economics this is an attack on the cost model, not on availability. Mitigation: a grant is **reused, not re-minted**, for the same (buyer, artifact) inside its 72 h window, so the loop re-fetches one already-stamped artifact rather than commissioning 10,000. The 2-stream cap bounds the rest. | Nothing. |
| E-16 | **Multi-hyphenate: a Producer downloads their own pack** | Two paths, one human, and they must not be confused. Delivery serves **entitlements** — the vendor has none over their own product. A vendor fetching their own build goes through the vendor portal (14.08), which is a different authorisation with different logging. If they also *bought* someone else's pack, both paths run on the same account the same day and that is normal. Corollary: a vendor's own-build fetch is **not** watermarked with a buyer identity — there is no buyer. | Two controls in two places, labelled differently: "Download" (library) vs "Download build" (vendor portal). |
| E-17 | **Operator's library is in a departed engineer's account** | Delivery refuses correctly — no entitlement for this account. The mechanism is right and the outcome is wrong, and this feature cannot fix it: entitlements bind to a person (14.02 Q-03). The studio paid and the studio cannot download. | "You don't own this." Which is true, and is exactly the "proof evaporates when the person moves on" failure the project exists to end. Blocker recorded, not solved here. |
| E-18 | No build exists for the buyer's platform | The entitlement is real; the artifact is not (CX-02, 14.01.02). Not a delivery bug — a catalog gap. | "You own this, but the vendor hasn't published a Windows build. See refund options" (14.09.02) — never a silent empty list. |
| E-19 | **Checksum fails on completion** | One automatic re-verify (a single mismatch is usually a corrupt transfer). A second failure means the **stored artifact** is bad — every buyer of that build will hit it. Escalate: alert vendor and platform, quarantine the build from new grants. Never tell the buyer to "try again" indefinitely. | First: "Verifying…" then a silent retry. Second: "This file didn't verify. That's on us, not your connection — we've flagged it with the vendor. You'll be emailed when it's fixed." |
| E-20 | **Artifact is in cold storage** — 2036 fetch of a 2026 build | The perpetual promise's real texture (DT-04). Restore initiated, up to 12 h, buyer emailed on availability. The grant's 72 h window starts **on restore**, not on request. | Disclosed at step 3, before the click: "This build hasn't been fetched since 2027. Retrieving it takes up to 12 hours — we'll email you." Not a spinner. |
| E-21 | Vendor has exited (14.08.05) | Bytes still serve. Perpetual is a storage obligation, not a listing state, and it survives the vendor caring. | Normal download. "No longer sold — your licence and downloads are unaffected." |
| E-22 | Partial pack — 3 of 5,000 files failed to store | Verification is at the **archive** level, so an incomplete pack fails as a unit rather than delivering 4,997 files and a lie. Same escalation as E-19. | "This pack didn't verify" + vendor/platform alert. Not a partial success. |
| E-23 | Buyer stops at 40%, then claims the goods never arrived | Delivery records bytes served, ranges, completion and verification per grant. That log is the answer to "did you actually receive it?" and is the evidence 14.09.02 adjudicates on (D-08). A 40% partial that never verified is a genuinely incomplete delivery and should be read as one. | Nothing at transfer time. Surfaces as evidence in a 14.09.02 case. |
| E-24 | Fan buys an £8 album (domain 20) | Same gate, same waiver requirement (14.09.01), radically different presentation. No version selector, no archive, no resume UI, no checksum on screen — a 90 MB file does not need a transfer manager and a fan will not tolerate one. | A download link and one plain sentence of waiver. Nothing else. |
| E-25 | Metered studio/cellular network, 60 GB | We cannot detect the meter. What we can do is state the number before the click and make pausing genuinely safe — the 72 h window means "stop now, finish on Monday's wifi" is a real option, not a lost transfer. | Size shown pre-transfer; "Safe to pause — link valid until Sunday 22:14." |
| E-26 | Waiver never captured (14.09.01) | No grant, no URL, no bytes — structurally, not by policy. Declining is a supported path, not a dead end (14.09.01 D-03). | The waiver gate, then either the download or "You can download once you accept immediate delivery, or cancel for a full refund within 14 days." |
| E-27 | Watermark stamping fails mid-delivery | Per 14.03.05 D-03: serve unmarked, log the incident, never block a paying buyer for an anti-piracy convenience. **But** the grant must record that this artifact is unmarked — an unmarked file later found on a warez site must not be traced to whoever the seed *would* have named. | Nothing. Incident logged. |
| E-28 | Grant expires at 72 h with the transfer unfinished | The browser's resume gets `403`. The buyer must start again from the library — which mints a new grant and, for a watermarked artifact, a new seed, so the partial is unusable. This is a real loss of hours and the only defence is disclosure. | "This download link expired. Starting again will re-fetch from the beginning." Stated at step 3 with the expiry time, not discovered at hour 73. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Download requested; gates running | "Preparing your download…" — entitlement, version, waiver, artifact resolution, grant mint, watermark seed. Sub-second for a hot artifact. |
| **Restoring** | Artifact is in cold storage (E-20) | "Retrieving from archive — up to 12 hours. We'll email you." A distinct state, not a long Loading, because the honest unit is hours and a spinner lies. |
| **Empty** | Nothing purchased | Library empty state with a store CTA. No downloads exist to show. |
| **Populated** | Entitlement `active`, artifact hot | Download control with download size, unpacked size, version, SHA-256 (copyable), and the grant expiry once started. |
| **Transferring** | Bytes moving | "38.2 of 61.4 GB · ~04:20 · link valid until Sun 22:14". Bytes and finish time, not a bare percentage — the unit a human decides on. |
| **Partial** | Transfer interrupted (E-01, E-02) | Byte offset retained by the browser; library shows "Started 22:14 — resume from your browser's downloads". Never a restart-from-zero presented as a resume. |
| **Queued** | 6th concurrent transfer (E-12) | "Queued — starts when one of your 5 active downloads finishes." Reorderable, never refused. |
| **Verifying** | Transfer complete | "Verifying…" — brief, automatic, invisible when it passes. |
| **Error** | A gate failed, or transfer/verification failed | **Reason-specific, always.** Revoked / not entitled to this version / no build for your platform / expired link / didn't verify / network. A generic failure on a good someone paid for is never acceptable. |
| **Stopped** | Revocation (E-05) or malicious withdrawal (E-07) | "Download stopped — <specific reason>." With an appeal route where one exists (14.09.03 D-04), and an instruction to delete the partial where the artifact is unsafe. |
| **Expired** | Grant window elapsed (E-28) | "This link expired. Starting again re-fetches from the beginning." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Resumable multi-GB download is a solved problem — byte-range requests and a CDN | The candidate's phrasing bundles it as a routine capability | ❌ REJECTED — two constraints in this project collide here and neither is negotiable. **First**, the surface: candidate 08 openly names a *Desktop Library Manager*, because Splice, Native Access and Kontakt all ship native apps for exactly this. A browser tab cannot reliably fetch 100 GB in the background, survive a close, install to a user's sample folder, or verify and unpack. But D-02/D-12 ratify single-surface web. So the honest position is that the web can do *adequate* delivery (byte-range, resume within a session) and cannot do what the candidate describes — and I will not invent a desktop surface to close the gap (index Q-01). **Second**, watermarking (CX-04) makes each artifact per-buyer and therefore uncacheable, which removes the CDN the "solved problem" assumes. Both facts must reach `/create-prd-architecture` rather than be discovered there. |
| DT-02 | Delivery cost is a rounding error on a digital sale — no marginal cost, that's the point | The standard intuition about digital goods, and the reason people assume digital margins are ~100% | ❌ REJECTED — this domain has the **worst storage-and-egress profile on the platform**, and it is structurally unbounded. A one-time purchase of a 100 GB library buys a *perpetual* obligation: store it forever, and serve it again on every machine change, for decades (14.08.05 makes this explicit — a vendor exits and we keep storing their catalog for buyers who paid once). Egress is per-fetch, unbounded, and — thanks to CX-04 — uncacheable for watermarked content. Meanwhile the revenue was a single transaction with a marketplace cut. Sample libraries are the pathological case: largest files, most re-downloads, lowest prices. Nobody has costed this (index Q-03) and it is the kind of fact that decides whether a domain is viable, not merely how it is built. |
| DT-03 | Watermarking and resumability are independent concerns that happen to meet in this path | CX-04 models the collision as a *caching* problem and stops there; 14.03.05 names edge caching as the architectural cost and nothing else | ✅ CONFIRMED, **and the collision is worse than CX-04 says: naive watermarking makes resume structurally impossible.** A forensic mark is per-buyer and, in the obvious implementation, generated fresh at each download. But a mark changes byte offsets. Resume a transfer at byte 38,200,000,000 against a *re-generated* artifact and the incoming bytes do not align with the partial on disk — the file is silently corrupt, fails verification with no explanation the buyer can act on, and does so only at the end of a multi-hour transfer. The constraint this forces is hard and specific: **the mark must be deterministic per grant** — the grant records the seed, and every range request regenerates byte-identical output for that (buyer, artifact, grant). This is a correctness requirement that 14.03.05 does not state and CX-04 does not model, and it is exactly the kind of thing discovered in implementation, at the cost of the feature. Routed to `/create-prd-architecture` as a constraint, not a preference. |
| DT-04 | "Perpetual availability" means a download button that works in 2036 | 14.03.02 D-01 ("every version a buyer was entitled to remains permanently fetchable") and 14.08.05's continuity promise, both of which read as binary — the file is there or it isn't | ✅ CONFIRMED with a consequence nobody has drawn: **perpetual availability has a latency, and the buyer meets it.** DT-02 establishes that storing 100 GB per product forever, on a one-time margin, is economically hostile. The only affordable way to hold decade-old artifacts is cold/archival storage — and cold storage is cheap precisely because retrieval is slow. So the 2036 fetch of a 2026 build is not a download; it is a **request and a wait**, measured in hours. This is not a defect to hide behind a spinner: it is the honest shape of the promise, and the Musician re-installing a career's worth of tools onto a new laptop is the exact persona who meets it, on the exact day they are least patient. Two obligations follow. It must be **disclosed before the click** (E-20) — "up to 12 hours, we'll email you" is tolerable; a progress bar that never moves is a support ticket and a trust event. And the 72 h grant window must start **on restore**, not on request, or the buyer's window is consumed by our storage economics. The deeper point: DT-02 asked "who pays for perpetual storage?" and treated it as a finance question. It is also a **UX** question, and the answer leaks into the product whichever way the owner decides. |
| DT-05 | A vendor withdrawing a version mid-transfer should let the transfer complete — the artifact is valid, only the recommendation changed | CX-02 q5, stated as a blanket rule | ❌ REJECTED as a blanket rule — **it is right for the common case and dangerous for the one that matters.** Withdrawal (14.08.04) has at least three reasons and they are not interchangeable. *Superseded* — a better build shipped: let it complete, obviously; the buyer's artifact is fine. *Defective* — it crashes on Apple Silicon: let it complete; a crashing plugin is a disappointment, and killing the transfer helps nobody who already has it on two other machines. *Malicious or rights-infringing* — the build ships malware, or the pack contains a sample the vendor never cleared (DT-B, the domain's real exposure is inbound): **kill it within 30 s, void the grant, tell the buyer to delete the partial.** Finishing that transfer means we knowingly completed the delivery of a payload we had already identified as harmful, to a customer, with a WeJammin receipt vouching for it. The distinction costs one field on the withdrawal event — a **reason** — and CX-02 currently has no field to carry it. Without it the delivery layer cannot tell "this got better" from "this is dangerous", and defaults to serving both. |
| DT-06 | Delivery's job ends when the bytes land on the buyer's disk | The feature's own name, and the frame every edge case above inherits | ❌ REJECTED — **for this domain's flagship content type, the bytes landing is the middle of the job, not the end.** A domain expert would say this immediately and nobody in the sweep did: a Kontakt library is not a file, it is a file *plus* an unpack into a specific folder structure *plus* a registration with the host so the DAW can find it. Native Access exists as a product because those three steps are one job for a user and three jobs for a computer. A zip in `~/Downloads` is an ingredient, not a meal — the buyer is now doing manual work with a 90 GB archive and a folder path they have to be told. This is not an argument for building a desktop app (D-03 stands; I will not invent a surface D-02/D-12 deny). It is a sharpening of index Q-01: the question is not "can a browser move 100 GB?" — with the grant model above, adequately, yes. **The question is whether delivery-without-installation is a sellable experience for large-format instruments**, and the honest answer is that every incumbent concluded it was not. That is the fact that should reach the owner's decision, and DT-01 was not carrying it. |
| DT-07 | Egress cost (DT-02) can be mitigated the way every large-catalog distributor mitigates it — peer-assisted distribution, as Blizzard and Steam have done for decades | Reasoning from DT-02: if egress is the structural cost, attack the egress | ❌ REJECTED — **structurally unavailable to a watermarked catalog, and the reason is the same one that kills the CDN.** P2P works because every peer holds *identical* blocks to trade. Per-buyer watermarking (CX-04) means no two buyers hold the same artifact, so there is no swarm and nothing to seed. The rejection is worth logging because it closes a door the architecture stage would otherwise spend time opening — and because of what it reveals when set beside DT-08: watermarking does not merely cost us the CDN. It costs us **every** standard mitigation for exactly the cost DT-02 identifies as possibly domain-fatal. |
| DT-08 | A 60 GB library's v2.1 update should be a ~200 MB delta patch, not a 60 GB re-download | Standard practice — Native Access, Steam, every OS updater. A domain expert would assume it exists, and its absence is the kind of thing that reads as amateurish rather than as a decision | ✅ CONFIRMED as an expectation, ❌ **REJECTED as achievable under watermarking — and this is the finding that should change a decision.** Binary deltas require a known, shared base: patch v2.0 → v2.1 assumes every holder's v2.0 is byte-identical. Watermarking guarantees it is not. So the buyer re-fetches 60 GB to receive a 200 MB fix, and DT-02's uncosted egress is multiplied by every update the vendor ships. Set beside DT-07 and CX-04, a pattern completes: **watermarking costs the CDN, P2P, and delta patching — the three standard defences against the exact cost that DT-02 flags as possibly domain-fatal.** 14.03.05 presents watermarking as an anti-piracy choice with a caching side effect (its DT-02). It is bigger than that: it is a decision to forgo every economy of scale in digital distribution, on the largest files in the catalog, to protect content whose theft costs a vendor a sale. That reframes 14.03.05 Q-03 (vendor-enabled vs platform-default) from a catalog-consistency question into a **cost-structure** question, and it strengthens the case that watermarking should be opt-in per product — cheap on a £15 loop pack, plausibly ruinous on a 100 GB library. Not my decision to make (14.03.05 owns it); mine to hand over with the evidence attached. |
| DT-09 | Progress should be shown as a percentage — that is what a progress bar is | The universal default | ❌ REJECTED — right for 90 MB, useless for 90 GB. "62%" answers no question a musician has. The questions are "can I go to bed?", "will this finish before the session at 10?" and "is it safe to close the laptop?" — which need **bytes, an estimated finish time, and the grant expiry**, all three (step 7, Transferring state). This is small, and it is the difference between a transfer manager and a spinner with ambitions. Logged because the multi-hour transfer is this feature's normal case, not its extreme one, and every default inherited from small-file downloads is wrong here by construction. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every fetch is authorised per request against a live entitlement check, buyer-scoped, never a static link | Per CX-01. A shareable static link makes the entitlement decorative and revocation unenforceable. |
| D-02 | Unpacked size is shown before a transfer starts, not just download size | A 40 GB download that unpacks to 90 GB fails on the buyer's disk after hours. Pre-empting it is trivial; discovering it late is not. A browser cannot check free space — the number is the whole mitigation (E-04). |
| D-03 | The web surface delivers *adequate* transfer; the candidate's desktop-manager scope is not built by assumption | Per DT-01. The gap is raised as index Q-01 rather than closed by inventing a surface D-02/D-12 deny. Per DT-06 the sharpened question is not "can a browser move 100 GB" (with D-04, adequately yes) but "is delivery-without-installation sellable for large-format instruments". |
| D-04 | **The delivery unit is a transfer grant: a stable, buyer-scoped, artifact-bound URL valid 72 h, re-authorised on every range request** | The reconciliation of D-01 with resumability. A freshly-minted signed URL is a *new download* to a browser, so "short-lived URL" and "resumable" are incompatible as stated — the resume silently becomes a restart. Moving the control from the URL's TTL to the per-request check makes a long window safe: the TTL was never the security boundary. 72 h because 100 GB at 4 Mbit/s ≈ 56 h. **Refines CX-01's "short-lived URL" phrasing; Step 6 should carry the refinement.** |
| D-05 | **An in-flight transfer is killed when its entitlement is revoked**, within 30 s; no grace window | Resolves 14.09.03 Q-01. It falls out of D-01 rather than needing a new rule — a resumed range request *is* a next download, and 14.09.03 D-02 already binds us to fail it. Killing costs the buyer nothing they can use (the partial fails checksum) and denies a deliberate race (request refund → start 60 GB → complete). 14.09.03 Q-02's grace argument is about **activation** stopping a live session; a stopped transfer stops nothing. |
| D-06 | No volume cap on downloads. Limits are on **concurrency** (5/account, 2/grant), never on count | Per CX-01: the Producer's repeated multi-machine fetches are the normal case and must not be read as abuse. Concurrency caps are invisible to a human and immediate to a script — they discriminate on the axis that actually separates the two, which a volume cap does not. |
| D-07 | A capacity limit **queues**, never denies | A paid buyer is never refused their goods for our convenience (E-12). Denial on a perpetual entitlement is a broken promise; a queue is a wait. |
| D-08 | Delivery records per-grant transfer telemetry (bytes served, ranges, completion, verification) as adjudication evidence | Per E-23. "Did the buyer receive the goods?" is a question 14.09.02 must answer and has no source for. A 40% partial that never verified is a genuinely incomplete delivery and should be adjudicated as one. |
| D-09 | **The air-gapped carry needs no dedicated feature — it needs two properties delivery already owes everyone** | Resolves Q-02. (1) The SHA-256 is published as **copyable text**, so the Operator verifies by hand on a machine that has never met us. (2) The artifact requires **no network at unpack time** — delivery embeds no phone-home. With both, "download on a connected machine, walk the drive across" is already the workflow, and inventing an air-gap feature would be inventing ceremony. The genuine air-gap problem is **activation**, which is 14.02.03's, and that boundary is now explicit rather than mutually assumed. |
| D-10 | Progress is reported as **bytes + estimated finish + grant expiry**, never a bare percentage | Per DT-09. The multi-hour transfer is this feature's normal case; every default inherited from small-file downloads is wrong here. "62%" answers no question a musician actually has. |
| D-11 | A transfer stopped for a **malicious** withdrawal is killed and the partial flagged unsafe; a *superseded* or *defective* withdrawal completes | Per DT-05. Completing a delivery of a payload we have already identified as harmful — to a paying customer, with our receipt on it — is not a defensible default, and CX-02's blanket rule currently produces exactly that. |
