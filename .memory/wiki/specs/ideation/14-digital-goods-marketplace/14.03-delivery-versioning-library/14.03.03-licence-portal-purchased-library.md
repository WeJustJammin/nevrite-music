# Feature: Licence Portal & Purchased Library

> **Number**: 14.03.03
> **Parent**: [14.03 Delivery, Versioning & Library](./14.03-delivery-versioning-library-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The buyer's permanent answer to "what do I own, how do I get it again, and what does it let me
do?" — every entitlement (14.02.01) rendered as something a musician can act on: download it, see
which machines hold seats, read the terms that govern a record made with it, fetch an old version.

It is three things at once, and the file is only coherent if all three are held together:

1. **The authorisation gate for all delivery** (CX-01) — no entitlement, no bytes.
2. **The consolidation promise (D-18) made concrete** for digital goods — one place for everything
   you bought, across years and vendors, when the receipt email is long gone.
3. **The store's contribution to the provenance chain** (domain DT-C) — the point-in-time record
   of what licensed a sound on the day a record was made, which is the only question the rights
   stack (09/11/12) ever actually asks of this domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their whole purchase history in one place — 10–60 entitlements accumulated over years across vendors they no longer remember. The moment that matters is the **new laptop**: they arrive here after a machine dies, on unfamiliar wifi, needing everything back. They rarely use search, always use the download control, and read terms exactly once — the week they release something. As a vendor, their *sales* are 14.08, never this page. |
| Producer | Full | Volume user: hundreds of entitlements across a decade. Needs "that Rhodes library I bought in 2019" and has forgotten the vendor, so the library must be searchable by product, vendor, order reference **and licence key** (they have keys in old emails with no memory of what they unlock). The only persona who uses the **as-of view** (DT-03) — a clearance desk asks what licensed bar 33 of a 2029 delivery, and the answer is a 2027 fact. Also the persona who buys packs on a client's behalf, with the client's money, and holds a licence for a record they do not own. |
| Operator | Full | **Blocked on 14.02 Q-03** — but no longer blocked *behaviourally*. A studio's library should belong to the studio and be visible to whoever staffs the room tonight. While entitlements bind to a person, the org context renders an honest empty state naming the constraint (D-07), never a silent zero. Sees the holder principal on every row, so "this room's Kontakt belongs to Dave's personal account" is visible before Dave leaves — not discovered after. |
| Fan | Read-only | The shared machinery, deliberately unrecognisable. A fan who bought an album on an artist page (20.04.03) sees "my purchases" — a list of music with download links, reached from the artist's page or their own account, never from the store. **No seats, no versions, no terms, no as-of view, no export beyond the files, no holder column.** Per personas.md they will not tolerate professional-tool complexity; every column above exists to answer a question a fan does not have. |

## Behavior

The library is a **presentation of entitlements, never a second store of truth** (D-01). It reads
14.02.01's records and renders them; it writes nothing about ownership. Two stores of "what you
own" will diverge, and the divergence surfaces as a paying buyer being told they own nothing.

### Row anatomy — what a single library row renders

A row is the intersection of **four independent axes**, and conflating any two of them produces a
wrong row (D-13):

| Axis | Values | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Entitlement state** | `active` · `refund-pending` · `revoked` · `expired` · `superseded` · `transferred-out` | 14.02.01 / 14.09.03 / 14.07.02 / 14.07.05 / 14.06.02 |
| **Vendor state** | `trading` · `retired` · `removed-for-cause` | 14.08.05 |
| **Management authority** | `wejammin-managed` · `externally-managed` (iLok/PACE/Steinberg) | 14.02.03 D-01 |
| **Holder principal** | the person or org the entitlement binds to | 14.02 Q-03 |

Plus, per row: product name (current) · vendor name (current) · monetisation model (14.07) ·
seat usage (14.02.02) · version entitlement range (CX-03) · bound terms version (14.02.05,
frozen at issuance per 14.02.01 D-03) · acquisition date + order reference · the download control.

**Why the axes must stay separate**: a retired vendor's product is still `active` and still
downloadable (14.08.05); an `externally-managed` product may be `active` with a seat count we
cannot see; a `revoked` row may still be the correct licence for a record released in 2028
(DT-03). Collapsing these into one "status" chip forces a single wrong word onto four facts.

### The list

- **Default sort**: acquisition date, most recent first. **Page size 50**, with explicit
  "load more" — a Producer's several hundred rows must not render as one wall (D-02).
- **Search** matches product name, vendor name, order reference, and licence key. Input is
  debounced so results settle once the user stops typing; matches highlight; no results is a
  state, not an error (see edge cases).
- **Filters**: product type · vendor · entitlement state · vendor state · management authority ·
  holder principal. Filters compose; the active filter set is always visible and clearable in
  one action.
- **Never blended with the store.** Owned and buyable are searched with one vocabulary and shown
  as separate bands — "also in the store: 12 results" — never interleaved (D-10, inheriting
  14.03.04 D-02). The library is the one place on the platform where "can I use this right now,
  legally" has an unambiguous answer, and a blended result makes every row a question.

### The as-of view (DT-03)

The library answers two questions, not one:

- **Present tense** — "what do I hold now?" — drives downloads, seats, upgrades, bundles.
- **Past tense** — "what did I hold on `{date}`, and under which terms?" — drives clearance.

Any date from account creation to today resolves to the entitlement set and the **terms versions
frozen at that time** (14.02.01 D-03), with the vendor and product names *as they were then*. A
row that is `revoked` today renders `active` in an as-of view of a date when it was. This is not a
history log for the curious; it is the only mechanism by which a purchase made at the moment of
truth (D-18) remains recoverable as evidence years later.

### Scoping — holder principals are never blended (D-07)

The library is scoped to exactly one holder principal at a time, and **every row renders its
holder explicitly**. A person who holds personal entitlements and is a member of an org that holds
entitlements sees two libraries, or one list with an explicit holder column and a filter — never a
merged one. Blending makes it impossible to answer *"what leaves with me when I leave this
studio?"*, which is the exact question the Operator case turns on, and which is the commerce-layer
instance of the failure the whole project exists to fix ("proof evaporates when the person
moves on").

This holds under either resolution of 14.02 Q-03, which is why it is specifiable now.

### Ownership is not one question (DT-05)

Four features read this feature's ownership state, and they are asking four different things. The
library answers each in its own terms; a single `owns(buyer, product)` answer is wrong for at
least two of them:

| Caller | Question | What `revoked` means to it | What `externally-managed` means to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14.01.03 Host deps | "Will this run?" | Not owned → won't run | **Unknown**, not "no" — a bridged Kontakt licence works and is invisible to us |
| 14.07.04 Bundles | "Would this bundle charge you twice?" | Not owned → charge | Owned if reported; else unknown → do not silently charge |
| 14.07.05 Upgrades | "Which version do you hold?" | Not owned → no upgrade price | Version range per the external system, or unknown |
| 14.02.05 Terms | "May a release use this?" | **Maybe yes** — for a record made while active (14.02.05 Q-02) | Terms are ours; management authority is irrelevant |

### Delivery authorisation (CX-01)

Every download authorises through here, per request, against a live entitlement check, via a
short-lived buyer-scoped URL (14.03.01 D-01). There are no static links. Revocation therefore
takes effect on the **next** request (14.09.03 D-02) — the part the platform can actually
guarantee — and the row must never claim `active` while it is not.

### Purchases fan out (CX-05)

Buying a pack creates **one** library row and *hundreds* of assets in the asset library. The two
are different objects answering different questions — "what did I buy" vs "which kick did I use"
— and expansion is asynchronous. The row is downloadable immediately; the assets arrive behind it.

### Happy Path

1. Producer's studio Mac dies. On a replacement machine, on hotel wifi, they open their library.
2. System renders skeleton rows, then the live list: 214 entitlements, most recent first, page 1
   of 50. Filter and search controls are visible above the list.
3. Producer types "rhodes" — they bought *something* Rhodes-shaped in 2019 and cannot recall the
   vendor. Search settles after they stop typing and returns 3 rows across 3 vendors, matches highlighted.
4. Producer recognises the 2019 purchase by its acquisition date and order reference. The row
   shows: `active` · vendor `trading` · `wejammin-managed` · holder "them, personal" · 1 of 2 seats
   used · entitled to v1.x · terms v2 "royalty-free, commercial use permitted, no resale of the
   raw samples".
5. Producer clicks download. The library authorises against the live entitlement, mints a
   short-lived URL, and hands off to delivery (14.03.01), which states unpacked size **before**
   the transfer starts (14.03.01 D-02) — 8 GB down, 19 GB unpacked.
6. Transfer completes. On first launch the plugin activates; a seat is spent (14.02.02) and the
   row's seat cell moves to 2 of 2 within the open session, without a page reload.
7. The dead Mac still holds the other seat. Producer uses self-service seat reclaim (14.02.02
   D-02) from the row; after the stated cooldown the seat is free and the cell reads 1 of 2.
8. Producer opens **Legacy Versions** on the row and fetches v1.4 — the build the 2021 album was
   mixed against — because v1.9 changed the filter curve (14.03.02 D-03 release notes said so).
   Both builds remain fetchable (14.03.02 D-01).
9. Six weeks later a label's clearance desk asks what licensed the vibraphone loop on a track
   delivered from that album. Producer opens the **as-of view**, sets 2021-03-04, and the library
   renders the entitlement set as it stood that day — including the frozen terms v2, and including
   one pack whose row today reads `revoked` because it was refunded in 2024.
10. Producer exports the as-of result and sends it to the clearance desk. What that export is
    *worth* outside WeJammin is Q-02 — see DT-04.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Live fetch fails (any error) | Stale cached list rendered from the last successful fetch, explicitly marked. a bounded number of automatic retries with increasing backoff, then a manual retry control. **Never the Empty state** (D-03/D-04) | "Showing your library as of {timestamp} — couldn't reach WeJammin. Retrying…" + Retry |
| Live fetch exceeds 10 s | Same stale path; retry continues in background. On success the list swaps in place and the marker clears | Stale list, then a silent swap — no jarring reload |
| Fetch **succeeds** and returns zero rows | Empty state. Empty requires an *authoritative* zero (D-04) | "Nothing here yet" + store CTA |
| Fetch fails **and there is no cache** — new device, first visit, bad wifi | Error state, not Empty. This is the highest-stakes failure in the feature: a paid buyer on a new laptop seeing "you own nothing" is a panic event and a support ticket | "Couldn't load your library. This is a connection problem — your purchases are safe." + Retry |
| Entitlement rows load; seat data (14.02.02) fails | Rows render; seat cell degrades independently. A seat lookup failure must not take the library down | Seat cell: "unavailable — retry" |
| Terms are a custom unstructured clause (14.02.05 D-03) | Row renders; no structured terms summary; no propagation downstream — honest about being unusable, rather than silently breaking the clearance chain | "Custom terms — read in full" (link) |
| Version entitlement unknown (bridged, 14.02.03) | Row renders; version cell shows the external system as the authority | "Managed in iLok — version rights shown in your iLok account" |
| Externally-managed row generally (14.02.03 D-01) | Marked throughout. Refunds, transfers and revocation may not behave like a WeJammin purchase, and this was disclosed at checkout — the library must not be the first place they learn it | "Managed in iLok — refunds and transfers may not apply" |
| 800 entitlements | 50/page, server-side search and filter, acquisition-date-desc default. A chronological wall is unusable at Producer scale (D-02) | Paged, searchable, filterable list |
| Search returns 0 rows | Not an error. Store search is offered as a **separate action**, never as blended results (D-10) | "No purchases match 'rhodes'." + Clear search + "Search the store instead" |
| Search returns 400 rows | Renders paged; filter chips suggested (vendor, type, year) rather than a truncated list | Paged results + "Narrow by: vendor · type · year" |
| Buyer pastes a licence key from a 2019 email with no memory of what it unlocks | Search resolves the key to its row | The row, key match highlighted |
| Same product bought **twice under different terms versions** | Two rows. Never merged (D-08). They were bought under different frozen terms and may govern different records — merging them silently picks one, and the picked one may be the wrong licence for a released track | Two rows, each with its own terms-version badge and acquisition date; "2 licences" |
| Duplicate purchase of the identical product | Blocked at checkout (14.02.01). If one lands anyway (gift, bundle overlap), both rows persist and are distinguished by acquisition date + order reference | "You already own this" — resolved before it reaches the library |
| Bundle bought where 3 of 5 items are already owned (14.07.04) | 5 rows; the 3 pre-owned products now show two entitlements each, per D-08 | Two rows per pre-owned product, not one merged row |
| Gifted entitlement (14.02.01 Q-01, if gifting ships) | Recipient's library: holder = recipient, purchaser = gifter reference. Gifter's library: a `gifted` row that grants **no** download | Gifter sees "Gifted to {recipient} on {date} — no download" |
| Refund requested, adjudication in flight (14.09.02) | Row → `refund-pending`. **Downloads remain available** — a pending claim is not a finding | "Refund under review — your licence is unaffected until a decision" |
| Refund granted → revocation (14.09.03) | Row → `revoked` with reason, trigger and appeal route (14.09.03 D-04). **The row never disappears** — a vanished purchase is indistinguishable from a bug and destroys trust in the whole library | "Revoked — refunded on {date}" + Appeal |
| Chargeback lands weeks after issuance | Row → `revoked`, reason "payment reversed". The appeal route is the payment dispute, not our appeals process — routing them to us would be a dead end | "Revoked — payment reversed on {date}" + link to the payment dispute |
| Revocation lands while the library is open | Row updates in place within the session; a toast names the row. **Never a silent flip** — a row that quietly changes meaning while being read is worse than a reload | Toast: "{product} was revoked — refunded on {date}" |
| Revocation lands while a download from that row is in flight | Row flips to `revoked` immediately — it must not claim `active` while bytes move. Whether the transfer itself is killed is **not this feature's call** (14.09.03 Q-01 / CX-01 q5) | Row reads `revoked`; the transfer's own UI states its outcome |
| Entitlement transferred out (14.06.02) | Row → `transferred-out`; downloads stop; history persists. The counterparty's identity is **not** rendered — a transfer is not a introduction | "Transferred on {date} — no longer downloadable" |
| Entitlement expires (subscription lapse 14.07.02, trial 14.02.06) | Row → `expired`. Distinct from `revoked`: no fault, no accusation, expected, and resubscribing restores it. Rendering a lapsed subscription as "revoked" reads as an accusation against an honest customer | "Expired {date} — resubscribe to restore" |
| Entitlement superseded by an upgrade (14.07.05) | Row → `superseded`; **still downloadable**, because the old version is still entitled and a 2021 project needs that build (14.03.02 D-01) | "Superseded by v2 — v1.x still available" |
| Vendor retires (14.08.05 retirement) | Vendor-state axis changes only. Downloads unaffected; store links removed | "No longer sold — your licence and downloads are unaffected" |
| Vendor removed for cause (14.08.05 Q-01) | Row persists with a stated reason and a link to the disclosure. Whether downloads continue is 14.08.05 Q-01's ruling — **but whichever way it rules, the buyer is notified with a reason before the row's capability changes.** A silent downgrade of a paid row is never acceptable | "{Vendor} was removed on {date} — {reason}" + disclosure link + a stated download position |
| Vendor removed for **uncleared rights**, and the pack is already in a released record (14.02.05 Q-02/Q-03) | The library does not adjudicate — it makes the exposure **visible**. The row carries the removal reason and a clearance warning linking to the affected release(s) via 09/12. This is the domain DT-B blast radius arriving at a specific buyer's specific record | "Rights issue reported for this pack. It appears in 2 of your releases." + link to the clearance record |
| Vendor renames or rebrands | Row shows the **current** vendor name; the name-at-purchase is preserved in the as-of view. Otherwise a 2027 receipt cites a company that appears never to have existed | Current name, with "purchased from {old name}" in the as-of view |
| Product renamed or delisted | Same principle — current name in the list, name-at-purchase in the as-of view | Current name; delisted products show no store link |
| Assets not yet expanded (CX-05, async) | Row is fully downloadable immediately; expansion progress is the **asset library's** problem, surfaced here only as a note | "Download available now — 4,812 sounds still being indexed" |
| GDPR erasure requested (resolves 14.02 Q-02's library half) | Account → erasure-pending for **30 days** (consistent with the one-month response duty). The library renders read-only with a banner and presents a **mandatory export step** (D-11) — a right the buyer already holds, not a courtesy. On completion the account and its library cease to exist. What survives on the *vendor's* side of the entitlement record is **14.02 Q-02's ruling, not this feature's**: the library is a presentation (D-01) and cannot outlive its account | Banner: "Your account will be erased on {date}. Export your library first — after erasure it cannot be recovered." + Export |
| Portability export requested with no erasure | Export produced; nothing deleted. Contents and external verifiability per Q-02 (DT-04) | "Export ready" |
| Buyer dies; an executor needs the tools to finish a posthumous record (DT-08) | **No mechanism exists.** Perpetual licences bound to a person, seats bound to their machines, no successor path. Routed to Q-05 rather than invented | `[Q-05 — /create-prd]` — an honest gap, not a specified behavior |
| Engineer opens the library on a studio machine; the entitlement is personal | Row shows holder "{person} · personal". No blending (D-07). The studio can see, tonight, what it does not own | Holder chip on every row |
| Operator opens the studio's org library while entitlements are person-bound (14.02 Q-03) | An **honest empty state naming the constraint** — never a silent zero, which would read as data loss | "Bell Street Studios holds no licences. Licences bought by staff belong to their personal accounts." + link to Q-03's status |
| Multi-hyphenate is buyer **and** vendor of the same product | The row appears in their library like any purchase. Their *sales* live in 14.08 and never appear here — two questions, two pages. Buying your own product is normal (testing the buyer flow) and is not a signal by itself | A normal row; no sales data |
| Producer buys a pack on a client's behalf, with the client's money, for the client's record | The entitlement lands in the **Producer's** library and the row shows the Producer as holder. The library never implies the client holds anything. Whether the terms bind the buyer or the project is 14.02.05 Q-04 — unresolved, and this is the common case that makes it urgent | Holder = the Producer. No client reference |
| Enumeration attempt — a crafted or guessed entitlement reference belonging to another buyer | Identical not-found response to a non-existent reference. No distinction between "exists but not yours" and "doesn't exist" — the distinction is an oracle for who owns what (DT-09's leak by another route) | "Not found" — the same for both |
| Expired or replayed download URL | Rejected. A fresh authorisation is required per request (14.03.01 D-01) | "This link expired. Download again from your library." |
| As-of query for a date before the account existed | Empty as-of result with an explicit statement, not an error | "You had no account on 2015-06-01." |
| As-of query on a date when terms had since been revised | Renders the terms **frozen at that time** (14.02.01 D-03), not today's — this is the entire point | Terms v2 badge with "as of 2021-03-04" |
| As-of date near a boundary (bought 23:50 local, record stored in UTC) | Resolves against the recorded instant; the UI states which timezone it is rendering | "Times shown in {tz}" |
| New laptop, 40 entitlements, ~600 GB total — buyer looks for "Restore all" | **There is no restore-all** (DT-06/D-12). Downloads are per-product and sequential; total unpacked size is stated up front (14.03.01 D-02). This is index Q-01's consequence landing on the most emotionally-loaded moment in the feature, and it is named as a weakness rather than papered over | "40 products · 604 GB unpacked. Download one at a time." — no bulk control |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Library fetch initiated (first paint, filter change, page change) | Skeleton rows at page size; filter and search controls interactive immediately — a Producer knows what they're searching for before the list arrives |
| Empty | Fetch **succeeded** and returned zero rows (D-04) | "Nothing here yet" + store CTA. Fans see the 20.04.03 equivalent, not a store CTA |
| Populated | Entitlements exist | Paged, searchable, filterable rows; each showing entitlement state, vendor state, management authority, holder, seats, version range, terms version, acquisition date + order ref, download control |
| Error | Fetch failed **and** no cache exists (new device, first visit) | "Couldn't load your library. This is a connection problem — your purchases are safe." + Retry. **Never** the Empty state |
| Stale | Fetch failed or exceeded 10 s **and** a cache exists | Cached list, explicitly marked with its timestamp, retrying in background. Downloads from a stale row still authorise live — a stale *list* never means a stale *permission* |
| Partial | Some rows externally managed (14.02.03); or seat/terms/version data failed independently; or assets still expanding (CX-05) | Rows render with per-cell degradation: "Managed in iLok — refunds and transfers may not apply" · "Seat info unavailable" · "4,812 sounds still being indexed". The list never fails wholesale because one cell did |
| Erasure-pending | GDPR erasure requested; 30-day window open | Read-only list + banner: "Your account will be erased on {date}. Export your library first." Export is a mandatory step, not a suggestion (D-11) |
| As-of | Buyer selects a historical date | The entitlement set as it stood, with names, states and terms versions of that day; a persistent banner "Viewing your library as of 2021-03-04" and an exit control. Downloads are **disabled** in this view — it is evidence, not a shopping mode |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The library is an account page — order history with download buttons | The candidate's framing ("Customer Licence Portal"), and how most stores build it | ❌ REJECTED — order history is *transactional* and this is *durational*. An order is a fact about a past moment, closed forever; an entitlement is a live relationship with state that changes for years afterwards without the buyer doing anything — seats fill and free, versions accrue, a vendor exits, a licence is revoked, terms govern a release made in 2029. The tell is the time horizon: nobody opens an order history from 2019, and *everybody* reinstalls a 2019 plugin on a new laptop. Building this as order history produces a page that is correct on day one and useless on day 1,000, which is precisely when this domain's promise (perpetual availability) is actually tested. |
| DT-02 | This library is domain 14's | The domain index's own framing — 14 owns delivery, 20 depends on it | ⏳ DEFERRED — escalated to sub-domain Q-02, and the evidence has grown rather than shrunk during classification. The domain index already concedes 20 (fan music purchases) reuses this machinery. But the same shape recurs across the map: Education (06) delivers lesson materials to enrolled students; Services (05) delivers finished work to a client who paid; Projects (07) delivers stems to collaborators. All four are "an entitled party fetches a versioned file, permanently, gated on a right they hold". One dependant is a seam; **four dependants is a cross-cut wearing a domain costume**. I am not overturning a ratified framing (D-16) on my own authority — but the Node Classification Gate says a mechanism serving many domains is a cross-cut, and by its own test this qualifies. Flagged loudly for the owner rather than quietly reclassified. |
| DT-03 | The library answers "what do I own?" | The feature's own framing, and DT-01's durational correction | ✅ CONFIRMED — **and radically incomplete.** A music-industry domain expert never asks a producer what they own *today*. A label's clearance desk, a sync agent, a distributor's rights check — they ask what licensed the vibraphone loop in bar 33 of a record delivered in 2029, and the answer is a fact about 2027. Present tense and past tense diverge exactly where the money is: a refunded pack (14.09.03) reads `revoked` today but was `active` when the track was cut; a terms revision means the governing clauses are the ones frozen at *that* purchase (14.02.01 D-03), not the vendor's current ones; a vendor removed for cause (14.08.05) leaves rows whose present state says nothing about their state on the release date. 14.02.01 D-03 already freezes terms onto the record — **that is half a mechanism, and the half that exists is inert without a way to render it at a chosen point in time.** So the library carries an as-of view, and this is precisely the join where the store *feeds* the rights platform (domain DT-C) rather than sitting beside it. Without it, D-18's claim that the purchase is a clean capture point is true at the instant of capture and unrecoverable forever after — which is the industry's exact failure, reproduced by us, in the one place we had the fact in our hands. |
| DT-04 | Whether the library can be exported is a values decision about lock-in (Q-02) | Problem-statement Q-02 — "is the lock-in earned or hostile?" — landing in this domain | ✅ CONFIRMED that it matters — ❌ REJECTED as the right question. Export is not ours to grant or withhold. An entitlement is a contract between the **buyer and the vendor**; the digital-goods marketplace is multi-vendor (global D-05/D-06), which makes WeJammin a *facilitator*, not the counterparty. A facilitator that can extinguish a buyer's evidence of a third party's contract by deleting an account is not exercising lock-in, it is holding someone else's agreement hostage — and separately, GDPR Art. 20 gives an EU buyer portability regardless of our product view. So "can it be exported" resolves to **yes, by construction**. The live question is what an export is *worth*: a JSON list of product names proves nothing outside WeJammin; the artifacts sit in our storage (14.03.01 DT-02); the terms are only machine-readable inside our registry (14.02.05 D-01). An export that a buyer's lawyer, a label's clearance desk or a competitor's importer cannot **verify** is a compliance gesture wearing portability's clothes. Q-02 is therefore re-scoped: not *whether*, but whether the export is verifiable evidence outside the platform — which is a real cost, a real values call, and the concrete answer to problem-statement Q-02 for this domain. |
| DT-05 | The library exposes ownership to whoever needs it — 14.01.03, 14.07.04, 14.07.05 and 14.02.05 all ask the same question | The obvious read: four callers, one predicate | ❌ REJECTED — **four different questions wearing one word, and one answer is wrong for at least two of them.** 14.01.03 asks "will this run?" — and a bridged iLok Kontakt licence (14.02.03) satisfies it while being invisible to us, so the honest answer to a compatibility check is often *unknown*, not *no*. 14.07.04 asks "would this bundle charge you twice?" — and must count a `superseded` row as owned (you hold v1; the bundle's v1 is not new value) but a `transferred-out` row as not. 14.07.05 asks "which version do you hold?" — and a `revoked` row must not price an upgrade. 14.02.05 asks "may a release use this?" — and a `revoked` row may still be **yes** for a record made while it was active (14.02.05 Q-02). So `revoked` means not-owned, not-owned, not-owned and *maybe-owned* to the four callers. Getting this wrong is either charging a buyer twice for something they own, or telling a clearance desk that a released record is unlicensed when it is not. Each consumer's question must be named in the spec, not inferred from a shared word. |
| DT-06 | The library needs a "Restore Purchases" button — every app store has one | The universal affordance; what a buyer arrives at this page expecting | ❌ REJECTED — the affordance presumes the store *is* the installer, on a device with a filesystem. WeJammin is a browser tab (D-02/D-12), so "restore all" means initiating an unbounded set of multi-GB downloads that 14.03.01 DT-01 already establishes the web cannot do reliably for **one** product. A Producer's realistic library after five years is several hundred GB across 200 entitlements; restore-all on a new laptop is a request the surface physically cannot honour, and shipping the button would be a promise that fails at the worst possible moment. So reinstall is necessarily per-product and sequential. **This is not a design preference — it is index Q-01's consequence landing on the single most emotionally-loaded moment in this feature** (the dead laptop), and it is named here so the weakness is visible in the MoSCoW rather than discovered at implementation. |
| DT-07 | The library should show what you've spent — a running total, value-of-your-library, purchases by year | Standard store analytics; Steam's "value of your account" | ❌ REJECTED as owned here — ✅ CONFIRMED as an unrecorded cross-cut. Two separate findings. (1) A spend total rendered in a store is a shaming mechanic pointed at the persona least able to absorb it: personas.md says the Musician "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products", and a store that greets them with "you have spent £4,200" is monetising guilt. (2) But the underlying need is real and belongs elsewhere: **every professional persona's plugin and pack purchases are deductible business expenses**, and this library holds the receipts — vendor, date, amount, order reference — that a producer currently rebuilds from PDF invoices in a shoebox each January. Those are perpetual licences bound to a dead person's account, with seats bound to their machines, and no successor path — the estate inherits the studio, the hard drives and the copyrights, and cannot open the sessions. Every store binds this way and every store's answer is "contact support", which in practice means an executor emails a help desk and hopes. This library is the first place an executor looks. The platform *has* transfer machinery (14.06) but it is vendor-policy-gated and shaped for a live seller negotiating a price. Note the compound: resolving 14.02 Q-03 in favour of org-held entitlements would fix this for studios and leave every individual musician exactly where they are — so Q-03 is not a superset of this. | DT-09 | Vendors should be able to see who owns their products — it's their customer list | A request that will absolutely be made, and that sounds reasonable | ❌ REJECTED — buyer identity does not fall out of this feature, at any granularity, ever. The reasoning is already settled immediately adjacent: CX-04 establishes that the watermark map belongs to the platform rather than the vendor, explicitly because "a vendor could deanonymise their buyers". A vendor querying the library for holders is the *same hole opened from the other side*. And the multi-hyphenate makes it worse than a generic privacy concern: producers are vendors **and** buyers of each other's work (personas.md), so a vendor-readable holder list lets a competitor enumerate exactly which packs a rival producer works from — competitive intelligence dressed as CRM, in a market where "what are they using" is a genuinely valuable secret. Vendors get counts and cohorts (14.08.03), never names, unless the buyer opts in through some other act. Logged because rejecting this later, under commercial pressure, is much harder than rejecting it now. |

- **14.02.01 Issuance** ([path](../14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement/14.02.01-licence-issuance-entitlement-record.md)) — *shared entity*: this is that record's presentation, never a second source of truth (D-01). Its D-03 (terms frozen at issuance) is the precondition for DT-03's as-of view.
- **14.02.02 Seats** ([path](../14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement/14.02.02-activation-seats-machine-authorisation.md)) — *state race*: the seat cell mutates outside this page (activation on another machine, self-service reclaim with cooldown). The row must update in place, and a seat-lookup failure must degrade to one cell rather than the whole list.
- **14.02.05 Licence Terms Registry** ([path](../14.02-licensing-activation-entitlement/14.02.05-licence-terms-registry.md)) — *shared entity + trigger dependency*: the frozen terms version is a row field, and the as-of view is how those terms reach a clearance question years later. - **14.09.03 Revocation & Clawback** ([path](../14.09-digital-refunds-revocation/14.09.03-licence-revocation-entitlement-clawback.md)) — *state race + notification*: revocation reaches delivery immediately (its D-02) and must land on an open library page in place, never silently. - **14.01.03 Host-Dependency Graph** ([path](../14.01-catalog-compatibility/14.01.03-host-dependency-graph.md)) — *permission*: reads owned-product state, and per DT-05 needs "unknown" as a first-class answer for bridged hosts.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The library is a presentation of entitlements, never a second source of truth | Two stores of "what you own" will diverge, and the divergence surfaces as a buyer being told they don't own something they paid for. |
| D-02 | The library is searchable and filterable from the start, not a chronological list | Per the Producer lens — hundreds of entitlements across years is the normal case, not the extreme one. 50/page, search across product, vendor, order reference and licence key. |
| D-03 | A transient failure renders a stale cached list, never an empty one | Per DT-01's time horizon: this page is load-bearing for people reinstalling a career's worth of tools. "You own nothing" is never an acceptable transient state. |
| D-04 | **Empty requires an authoritative zero.** The Empty state renders only on a *successful* fetch returning zero rows; absence of an answer is Error, never Empty | The testable form of D-03, and the rule that covers D-03's blind spot: a *new device* has no cache to fall back to, which is exactly the new-laptop scenario this feature exists for. |
| D-05 | The library carries an **as-of view** — the entitlement set, states, names and frozen terms versions at any chosen past date | Per DT-03. 14.02.01 D-03 freezes terms onto the record; without a way to render the frozen state at a point in time, that freezing is inert. This is the join where the store feeds the rights stack. |
| D-06 | Rows are never deleted. State changes; history persists | Per 14.09.03 D-04, agreed from both sides. A vanished purchase is indistinguishable from a bug, and one unexplained disappearance destroys trust in every other row on the page. |
| D-07 | The library is scoped to one holder principal at a time; principals are never blended; the holder is rendered on every row | Blending makes "what leaves with me when I leave this studio?" unanswerable — the commerce-layer instance of the failure the project exists to fix. **Holds under either resolution of 14.02 Q-03**, which is why it is specifiable now rather than blocked on it. |
| D-08 | Two entitlements for one product are never merged into one row | They carry different frozen terms versions and different acquisition dates, and may govern different records. Merging silently picks one, and the picked one may be the wrong licence for a released track. |
| D-09 | Entitlement state, vendor state, management authority and holder are **four independent axes**, rendered separately | Per 14.08.05 D-03 and 14.02.03 D-01. A retired vendor's product is still active; a bridged product's seat count may be unknown; a revoked row may still be a valid past licence. One status chip forces a single wrong word onto four facts. |
| D-10 | Owned and store results are never interleaved | Inherits 14.03.04 D-02. This is the one page where "can I legally use this right now" has an unambiguous answer; a blended list makes every row a question and corrodes the terms chain. |
| D-11 | An erasure request routes through a **mandatory** export step, inside a 30-day window | Per DT-04: portability is a right the buyer already holds, not a courtesy we grant. Erasing a library without offering the export first destroys the buyer's only evidence of contracts with third parties. |
| D-12 | There is no "restore all" | Per DT-06. Per-product, sequential, with unpacked size stated up front (14.03.01 D-02). Named as a **weakness** traceable to index Q-01, not dressed up as a design choice. |
| D-13 | Buyer identity is never readable by a vendor through this feature | Per DT-09, and consistent with CX-04's ruling on the watermark map. Vendors get counts and cohorts (14.08.03), never names. |
| D-14 | Downloads are disabled in the as-of view | It is evidence, not a shopping mode. A download authorised from a historical view would be authorising against a state that is no longer true. |
