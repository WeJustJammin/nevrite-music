# Feature: Creative Asset Library — Tagging, Search & Sync

> **Number**: 14.03.04
> **Parent**: [14.03 Delivery, Versioning & Library](./14.03-delivery-versioning-library-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The buyer's library of **sounds**, not purchases. Buying a sample pack is one transaction and
800 usable objects; a career of packs is tens of thousands of loops, one-shots and presets that
are worthless if unfindable. This feature is the index over them — buyer-owned tags, favourites,
musical search (14.04.04), and the question every producer actually asks: *which kick did I use
on that track?*

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Tags and favourites the sounds they return to. The lighter user — a few dozen packs — but the same need: find the sound, not the receipt. |
| Producer | Full | **The power user this exists for.** Tens of thousands of assets accumulated over years across hundreds of packs. Searches by key and BPM against the track in progress, not by pack name — nobody remembers which pack a kick came from. Personas.md places their work inside a DAW, which is exactly where this index cannot reach without the surface question (index Q-01) being answered. |
| Operator | None | A studio does not curate a personal loop library — it buys tools for rooms (14.02.02), not sounds for a creative practice. Explicit absence: modelling an Operator asset library would be inventing a workflow the persona does not have. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. A fan buying an album has files, not assets; nothing to tag, nothing to search, no musical metadata to search by. |

## Behavior

An entitlement to a pack fans out into per-asset records (CX-05) carrying the pack's musical
metadata (BPM, key, instrument, one-shot vs loop) and, critically, its **terms** (14.02.05) — so a
loop dragged into a project brings its licence with it. On top of that: buyer-owned tags,
favourites, collections, and search.

Buyer-created organisation is buyer-owned data and must survive pack revisions (CX-06), refunds
in some form, and — per 14.03.03 Q-02 — should be exportable.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer purchases a pack; assets expand asynchronously into the library (CX-05).
2. Each asset carries inherited metadata: BPM, key, instrument, pack, vendor, terms.
3. Buyer searches "140 BPM, F minor, kick" across everything they own.
4. Buyer auditions results in-browser via the audition player cross-cut.
5. Buyer favourites one and tags it "album-3".
6. Buyer uses the asset in a project (07); the asset's terms travel with it into the clearance chain (14.02.05).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 5,000-file pack purchased | Expansion is async; the library is not blocked at checkout | "Adding 5,000 sounds to your library…" with progressive availability |
| Pack revised, an asset the buyer tagged is removed (CX-06) | Buyer-owned tags must not be orphaned silently, and an asset used in a released record cannot simply vanish | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — collides with 14.02.05 Q-02 |
| Pack refunded (14.09) after assets were tagged and used | Assets must go; the buyer's tags and their record of *having used it* arguably should not | `[PENDING]` — see Q-03 |
| Vendor's metadata is wrong (key labelled F, actually F#) | Buyer's search returns musically wrong results; trust in the index collapses | Buyer override on their own copy; systematic errors flow back to QA (14.08.03) |
| Buyer wants a sound they *don't* own | The index only covers entitlements; the store is elsewhere | Search offers "also in the store" as a distinct, clearly-separated result set — see DT-02 |
| Assets used in a DAW, not a browser | **The wall.** The index lives on the web; the work happens in a DAW. Without a local surface the buyer must search here, download, and drag manually. | `[PENDING]` — index Q-01 |

|-------|---------|---------------|

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The asset library is a view over purchases — a folder tree of packs | The intuitive model, and how a naive store would render it; the candidate bundles it with the licence portal (candidate 09) as if they were one page | ❌ REJECTED — the unit of *purchase* and the unit of *use* are different objects with different lifetimes, and conflating them (as candidate 09 does) produces a library of packs when the producer needs a library of sounds. Nobody thinks "I need something from Vintage Drums Vol. 3"; they think "I need a dusty kick at 92". The pack is packaging — a billing artifact — and it is forgotten the moment the transaction clears. This is why 14.03.03 and this feature were split (sub-domain D-01): one answers "what did I buy", the other "what can I use", and they are asked by different people at different times for different reasons. The asset must therefore be first-class and addressable, carrying its own metadata and its own terms, not reachable only by traversing its pack. |
| DT-02 | The asset library should search across the store too — owned and buyable in one result set, so buyers discover what they're missing | The obvious merchandising win; Splice blurs exactly this line and monetises it | ❌ REJECTED as a *blended* result set, though the two searches may share an index. The blend corrupts the one place on the platform where the answer is unambiguous. "What can I use right now, legally, in this session" is a question with a clean answer — you own it, its terms permit it — and mixing in results that require a purchase and a new licence turns a work tool into a storefront. The Producer under deadline needs a set they can drag from without checking anything; a blended list makes every result a question. Worse, it corrodes the terms chain (14.02.05): if a buyer drags an unowned loop from a search result into a project, the clearance record is a lie by default. Keep the store searchable and clearly separated — "also in the store: 12 results" as a distinct band, never interleaved. This is a product decision (Q-02) since it trades revenue against trust, but the recommendation is firm. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Assets are first-class addressable objects, not a folder view of packs | Per DT-01. The unit of purchase and the unit of use differ; conflating them is the defect that makes big libraries unusable. |
| D-02 | Owned and store results are searchable with one vocabulary but presented as separate bands, never interleaved | Per DT-02. Blending corrupts "what can I legally use right now" and undermines the terms chain. |
| D-03 | Buyer-created tags, favourites and collections are buyer-owned data that survives pack revisions | Buyer effort invested in organisation must not be destroyed by a vendor's release. |
