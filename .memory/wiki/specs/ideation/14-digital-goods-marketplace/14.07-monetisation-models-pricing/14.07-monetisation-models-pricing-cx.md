## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Rent-to-Own ↔ Perpetual

**Relationship**: A rent-to-own entitlement is a perpetual entitlement with a payment schedule and
an unpaid balance. The final payment clears the balance and the record becomes perpetual **in
place** — no new entitlement, no re-download, no reactivation. This mirrors the trial→purchase
design (14.02 CX-06) for the same reason: two records means one of them expires under a paying
customer.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona this exists for — capital-constrained, buying a £400 tool at £34/month.
- **Producer**: same, at higher values.
- **Operator**: read-only — studios capitalise purchases rather than financing them.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One entitlement record throughout; the balance is a field on it. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Final payment → balance cleared → model flips to perpetual. Must be atomic with payment. A cleared balance that doesn't flip leaves a buyer who has paid in full still renting.
3. **Permission intersection**: Access during the schedule is equivalent to perpetual — the buyer works with the tool. Missing a payment is where it diverges (14.07.03 DT-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Payments remaining, and the completion moment. The completion is worth marking loudly — it is the buyer's win.
5. **State transition conflict**: Vendor exit (14.08.05) mid-schedule. Buyer is 6 of 12 payments into a tool whose vendor has left. Keep paying? To whom? Convert free? **Genuinely unresolved and unaddressed anywhere in the market** — see 14.07.03 DT-02.

### CX-02: Upgrades ↔ Perpetual

**Relationship**: An upgrade is only a coherent concept if the entitlement is version-bounded
(14.03 CX-03). "You own v1.x; v2 is £79" requires knowing where v1.x ends. Where the vendor
promised lifetime updates, no upgrade exists to sell.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: discovers at v2 whether their purchase included it. Must be visible at *original* purchase, not at upgrade time.
- **Producer**: as vendor, sets the update policy; as buyer, resents late discovery.
- **Operator**: multi-seat upgrades — four rooms is four upgrades, and the price should reflect that.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Version range is an entitlement property; the upgrade reads it and writes a new range on completion.
2. **Trigger chain**: v2 publishes → entitled holders get an update, unentitled get an offer (14.03 CX-03). Async, and the two messages must not cross.
3. **Permission intersection**: The version range *is* the permission.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Two populations, two very different messages. Sending "free update!" to someone who owes £79 is a trust event.
5. **State transition conflict**: Upgrade purchased while a refund of the base product is in flight — the buyer upgrades something they're returning.

### CX-03: Bundles ↔ Upgrades

**Relationship**: Both read "what does this buyer already own" and both must adjust price
accordingly. A bundle with 5 products where the buyer owns 2 should charge for 3 — or credit the
2 — and doing neither is the most reliable way to lose a good customer.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the beneficiary; also the person insulted by a bundle that charges them twice.
- **Producer**: as vendor, must decide whether partial-ownership bundles discount or credit.
- **Operator**: bundles across rooms with mixed ownership — the messy real case.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both read the library (14.03.03); neither writes to it until purchase.
2. **Trigger chain**: Bundle added to cart → ownership check → price adjustment. Sync, at cart time — a price that changes at checkout is worse than a wrong price.
3. **Permission intersection**: None new.
4. **Notification fan-out**: "You own 2 of these 5 — price adjusted" is a trust-building moment worth surfacing explicitly.
5. **State transition conflict**: Buying a bundle item separately while the bundle is in the cart.

### CX-04: Subscription ↔ Perpetual

**Relationship**: A library holds both kinds simultaneously and the buyer must always be able to
tell them apart — before subscribing, while subscribed, and at the moment of lapse. The industry's
chronic failure is that users cannot tell what they keep when they stop paying, so they assume
they lose everything and never subscribe, or assume they keep everything and are furious.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: personas.md's persona who "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products" — the one most damaged by ambiguity here, and the reason index Q-03 exists.
- **Producer**: same at volume.
- **Operator**: a room whose tools vanish on a lapsed card is an outage.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Distinct entitlements with distinct models; the conflict is *presentational*, not structural — and presentational failure here is total.
2. **Trigger chain**: Lapse → subscription entitlements expire → perpetual entitlements untouched. Must never over-reach. A lapse that revokes a perpetual licence is the worst bug this domain could ship.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — a lapsed subscription can un-satisfy a *dependency* (14.01.03) of a perpetual product, so a perpetual purchase can stop working through no fault of its own.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Pre-lapse warning must state exactly what will stop working and what will not.
5. **State transition conflict**: Payment failure racing a session in a studio. Under fail-open (14.02.02 D-01) the session should survive.

### CX-05: Subscription ↔ Rent-to-Own

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. Both charge monthly. One accrues ownership, one rents access.
On a bank statement they are indistinguishable, and buyers conflate them — then discover after 18

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 14.07.04 Bundles | 14.07.01 Perpetual | A bundle is a packaging mechanic that is indifferent to the model of what's inside it — a bundle of subscriptions, rent-to-owns or perpetuals prices the same way. The relationship people expect here is really CX-03 (bundles read *ownership*), which is a different claim: the input is the library, not the model. Modelling bundle→perpetual would imply bundles only work on owned goods. |
| R-02 | 14.07.02 Subscription | 14.07.04 Bundles | "Subscribe and get a bundle free" is a promotion, not a structural interaction — it is 14.07.04's ordinary discount machinery with a subscription as the trigger. No shared state, no trigger chain that isn't already CX-03's. Recording it because it *sounds* like an obvious pair and would add a coupling with no behaviour behind it. |
