# Feature: Upgrade, Crossgrade & Loyalty Credit

> **Number**: 14.07.05
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Pricing that depends on what the buyer already owns. An **upgrade** moves you from v1 to v2 at a
reduced price. A **crossgrade** rewards owning a *related or competing* product — the plugin
industry's oldest customer-acquisition move ("own any competitor's EQ? £49 instead of £199").
**Loyalty credit** is accumulated goodwill spendable across the store. All three read the same
state as bundles (CX-03), which is why they live together.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Upgrades when a tool they rely on moves versions; crossgrades because it is the only way a £199 plugin becomes affordable. Loyalty credit matters disproportionately to a persona living on small income streams. |
| Producer | Full | The persona with the deepest library, so the one for whom crossgrade eligibility is most often true — and most often invisible, because their competing products were bought elsewhere (DT-01). |
| Operator | Full | **Multi-seat upgrades.** Four rooms is four upgrades, and the price must reflect that rather than pretending a studio is one buyer. Blocked in part on 14.02 Q-03 (org-held entitlements). |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

**Upgrade**: reads the entitlement's version range (CX-02, 14.03 CX-03). If v2 falls outside it,
an upgrade is offered at a price; the entitlement's range is extended on purchase rather than a
second entitlement minted (index D-03 — one record, one product).

**Crossgrade**: reads ownership of a qualifying product. Where that product was bought on
WeJammin, we know. Where it wasn't — which is nearly always (DT-01) — we do not.

**Loyalty credit**: accumulated balance spendable across the store. Per 14.07.02 DT-01, credit is
stored value with the same liability implications, whether it was bought or granted.

### Happy Path

1. v2 publishes; entitled holders get a free update, unentitled holders get an upgrade offer (CX-02) — two populations, two very different messages.
2. Buyer sees "£79 to upgrade — you own v1.4" in their library and on the listing.
3. Buyer purchases; the entitlement's version range extends in place.
4. v2 downloads (14.03.01); the same record, same seats, same terms lineage.
5. Elsewhere: a buyer owning a qualifying product sees crossgrade pricing automatically applied at cart time.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Crossgrade qualifier owned **elsewhere** (the normal case) | We cannot see a buyer's Waves or NI library. The offer's target audience is invisible to us. | See DT-01 and Q-01 — proof-of-ownership upload is the industry's ugly answer |
| "Free update" message sent to someone who owes £79 (CX-02 q4) | A trust event, and entirely avoidable | Two populations must be resolved before either message sends |
| Buyer upgrades while a refund of the base product is in flight | Upgrading something they're returning — and the upgrade's price was conditioned on holding the base | `[OWNER]` — see Q-04. 14.09.02's edge table proposes "refund both or neither", but its own cross-cut note to this feature disclaims it: the unwind rule "remains open here" |
| Operator upgrading 4 seats | Four upgrades, one decision | Multi-seat upgrade pricing — blocked on 14.02 Q-03 |
| Vendor promised "lifetime updates" | No upgrade exists to sell; the range is unbounded | Correct behaviour: free update. But it must have been visible at original purchase (14.07.01 D-01) |
| Loyalty credit granted, then the buyer refunds the purchase that earned it | Credit clawback, or a free-money loop | `[PENDING]` — see Q-03 |
| Buyer abandoned a rent-to-own (14.07.03 DT-01) | Their £238 could persist as loyalty credit toward that product — the humane alternative to forfeit | Depends on 14.07.03 Q-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Eligibility check | Skeleton on the price |
| Empty | No upgrades/crossgrades available | Normal price, no offer |
| Populated | Eligible | "£79 to upgrade — you own v1.4" with the reason stated |
| Error | Eligibility check failed | Show the full price *and* say the check failed — never silently deny an entitled discount |
| Partial | Qualifier owned externally, unverified | The honest gap — "own a competing EQ? Prove it" (Q-01) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Crossgrade eligibility is a lookup against what the buyer owns | The candidate's framing, and how upgrades genuinely work | ❌ REJECTED — **the entire point of a crossgrade is to reward a product the buyer bought somewhere else, which is precisely the thing we cannot see.** An upgrade reads our own entitlement record and works perfectly. A crossgrade's qualifier is "you own any competitor's compressor" — bought at Plugin Boutique in 2019, from a store that will never tell us. So the offer's target audience is, by construction, invisible to the platform making it. The industry's answer is an ugly manual one: upload a screenshot of your licence portal, a receipt, a serial — reviewed by a human, gamed constantly, and a terrible first experience for a buyer we are trying to *win from a competitor*. This is the same three-valued-state problem as the dependency graph (14.01.03 D-02: satisfied/unsatisfied/**unknown**), and it has the same resolution: `unknown` is the common case and must be its own state with its own flow. The sharper consequence is strategic — a new store's crossgrade offers are aimed at exactly the users it has no data on, so this feature is *most* needed when it works *least*. |
| DT-02 | Loyalty credit is a retention mechanic — grant it freely, it costs little | The framing implied by lumping it into candidate 18 with bundles and sales, as if it were a discount | ❌ REJECTED — **granted credit is the same stored-value liability as purchased credit (14.07.02 DT-01), and it is worse, because it was never funded.** A purchased credit has cash behind it; a granted credit is a promise of goods with nothing behind it at all, and it still has to be honoured when spent — including on a *third-party vendor's* product, which means the platform pays that vendor real money for a credit it gave away. So loyalty credit is a marketing expense that arrives disguised as a discount and lands on the payouts cross-cut. There is a genuinely good use for it (14.07.03 DT-01: an abandoned rent-to-own's payments persisting as credit toward that product) — but "grant it freely, it costs little" is precisely backwards. It costs full retail, to a third party, at a moment we don't control. That belongs in the compliance and cost conversation (index Q-02), not in a pricing feature's fine print. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An upgrade extends the existing entitlement's version range in place; it does not mint a second entitlement | Per index D-03. Two entitlements for one product means two seat pools, two terms lineages, and a library that lies. |
| D-02 | Crossgrade eligibility is three-valued — owned-here / not-owned / unknown-external | Per DT-01. `unknown` is the common case; collapsing it either denies entitled buyers or gives the product away. |
| D-03 | An eligibility-check failure shows the full price **and** discloses the failure | Silently denying an entitled discount is indistinguishable from not having the offer, and the buyer never finds out they were overcharged. |
