# Feature: Subscription & Credit Economy

> **Number**: 14.07.02
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Recurring access instead of ownership, and the credit currency that usually accompanies it — the
Splice model: pay monthly, receive credits, spend them on individual sounds, keep what you
downloaded. It solves a real problem (a producer needs 6 loops, not an 800-file pack at £60) and
creates two new ones: a stored-value instrument the platform must honour, and a user base that
loses access when a card expires.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this ostensibly serves — capital-constrained, can't drop £60 on a pack for one kick. Also the persona personas.md says "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products", which is the pain WeJammin exists to end. Selling them a seventh subscription is a genuine tension, not a rhetorical one (index Q-03). |
| Producer | Full | High-volume consumer of sounds; the model fits their usage pattern best. Also a vendor whose content earns from the pool (14.10) rather than from sales — a completely different income shape. |
| Operator | Config | Awkward fit. A room's tools cannot vanish on a lapsed card; studios buy perpetual (14.07.01). If a room subscription exists at all it needs org billing that 14.02 Q-03 has not resolved. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. A fan has no use for sample credits. |

## Behavior

A subscription mints a recurring entitlement and, typically, periodic credits. Credits are spent
on individual assets; assets downloaded with credits are **kept perpetually** even after the
subscription lapses — that is the model's core promise and the reason people trust it.

Everything else lapses: subscription-gated catalog access, unspent credits (or do they roll?), and
any subscription-model software. Perpetual entitlements are untouchable (CX-04).

The credit currency is the part that needs the most care: it is stored value the platform has
taken money for and owes goods against. Whether it is a platform currency or per-vendor scrip is
unresolved (index Q-02), and the answer has regulatory weight.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer subscribes; a recurring entitlement is minted and credits are issued.
2. Buyer searches the catalog (14.04.04), auditions, and spends a credit on a single loop.
3. The asset lands in their library (14.03.04) as a **perpetual** asset — kept forever.
4. The contributor accrues from the pool (14.10.02) on that download.
5. Buyer cancels; downloaded assets remain; catalog access ends.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Card fails mid-session in a studio | Under fail-open (14.02.02 D-01) the session survives; a hard lock here would stop paid work over a billing event | Grace period, not a cliff |
| Unspent credits at cancellation | The sharp one. Expire (the buyer paid for nothing) or roll (an unbounded liability) or refund (real money out) | `[PENDING]` — see Q-01; every option has a loser |
| Buyer assumes cancelling loses their downloads | The Adobe pathology — ambiguity here suppresses subscription *and* poisons trust (CX-04) | The keep-forever promise must be stated at cancellation, loudly, and be true |
| Subscription lapse un-satisfies a dependency (14.01.03) of a perpetual product | **RESOLVED — 14.07.01 D-06** (which resolves 14.07.01 Q-03): the lapse **never** writes state on the perpetual entitlement. It stays `active`, downloads serve, the archive stays fetchable. What stops is the *software*, because its host stopped — and the dependency is disclosed at the buy button rather than discovered at lapse | "You still own this. It won't open until your {X} subscription is active." Never "revoked", never silence |
| Credits as platform currency (index Q-02) | Stored value = e-money implications; the marketplace becomes a bank | Routed to `/create-prd` — a regulatory question, not a pricing one |
| Vendor exits with unspent credits earmarked for their catalog | Per-vendor scrip becomes worthless; platform currency is unaffected | An argument for platform currency, with its own cost |
| Credit farming — subscribe, mass-download, cancel | The pool (14.10) pays contributors for downloads funded by one month's fee | Rate limits; and it distorts the pool's arithmetic — see 14.10 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A credit is a discount token — a pricing mechanic | The candidate's framing ("Credit Economy" listed among pricing models, alongside perpetual and rent-to-own) | ❌ REJECTED — **a credit is stored value, and issuing it makes the marketplace a custodian of prepaid funds.** The buyer paid real money for a balance the platform owes goods against; unspent credits are a liability on our books, not a marketing device. That drags in questions no pricing feature has: does the balance expire (and is expiring prepaid value even lawful in the buyer's territory)? Is it refundable? Does it survive our insolvency? If it is a **platform** currency (index Q-02) spendable across vendors, it starts to resemble an e-money instrument with the regulatory attention that implies — for a project whose compliance surface (`meta/constraints.md`) is already carrying PCI, KYC/AML, marketplace-facilitator tax and DSA. Modelling credits as a discount would put a stored-value instrument into production under a pricing feature's specification. That is the kind of thing that gets discovered by a regulator rather than a spec review. |
| DT-02 | Subscription is a standard monetisation model — offer it because the market does | The candidate list's flat treatment; Splice's success makes it look obviously correct | ⏳ DEFERRED — escalated to index Q-03, because it collides with the project's own thesis and I should not resolve that alone. Personas.md states the Musician's constraint plainly: they "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products", and that fragmentation-by-subscription is a named part of the pain WeJammin exists to end. Selling them subscription #7 is at minimum ironic and at worst self-refuting — the platform that promised to consolidate their costs becomes another line item on the card. There is a genuine counter-argument: **one** subscription replacing six is precisely consolidation, and D-18's "consolidation wins the user" reading supports it. But that argument only holds if the subscription spans the platform rather than this domain — a WeJammin membership, not a sample-credits plan. That distinction is a product and business-model decision of the first order, not a monetisation option to be inherited from a candidate list. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Assets downloaded with credits are kept perpetually after a lapse | The model's core promise. Without it nobody subscribes, and with it ambiguous nobody trusts it (CX-04). |
| D-02 | Payment failure enters a grace period, never an immediate cliff | Per 14.02.02 D-01's fail-open posture — a billing event must not stop a paid session in a studio. |
| D-03 | Credits are treated as stored value with a liability, not as a discount mechanic | Per DT-01 — the modelling choice has regulatory consequences that a pricing feature would never surface. |
