# Feature: Perpetual Purchase & Ownership

> **Number**: 14.07.01
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Pay once, keep it forever. The baseline model, and the one every other model in this sub-domain is
defined against — rent-to-own converts *into* it (CX-01), subscription is defined by *not* being
it (CX-04), upgrades exist because it is version-bounded (CX-02). It is also the model that
commits the platform to an obligation with no end date and no further revenue.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Buys and keeps. For a persona who "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products" (personas.md), perpetual is not a preference — it is the only model that fits their economics. As a vendor of their own packs, prices them perpetually because that is what content sells as. |
| Producer | Full | Same, at volume, plus a professional's specific requirement: a tool used on a client record must still open that record in five years. A perpetual licence is the only model that promises it. |
| Operator | Full | **Studios buy perpetual, deliberately.** A room cannot lose a tool mid-project because a card expired. Perpetual is the model that matches a business asset — capitalised, depreciated, and not at the mercy of a payment failure. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. Fan music purchases (domain 20) are perpetual too, but that model lives there — this is the store's. |

## Behavior

At purchase, an entitlement (14.02.01) is minted with model `perpetual`, no expiry, a seat
allowance, bound terms (14.02 CX-02), and a **version range** (14.02.01 Q-02). The version range
is the field that quietly does the most work: it determines whether v2 is a free update or a paid
upgrade (CX-02), and it must be visible at the *original* purchase rather than discovered at
upgrade time.

"Perpetual" is a promise with three parts, all of which must survive vendor exit (14.08.05): the
licence keeps working, the downloads keep coming (14.03.01), and the archived versions stay
fetchable (14.03.02).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer purchases; a perpetual entitlement is minted with a stated version range.
2. Buyer downloads, activates, works.
3. Years pass; the buyer changes machines and re-downloads (14.03.01) — still theirs.
4. v2 publishes; if in range, it is a free update; if not, an upgrade offer (14.07.05).
5. The vendor exits the platform (14.08.05); the licence, downloads and archive are unaffected.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Vendor exits (14.08.05) | The perpetual promise is now WeJammin's alone — the vendor is gone and we store, serve and honour forever with no further revenue | Buyer: nothing changes. That is the point, and the cost (14.03 Q-03) |
| Subscription lapse touches a perpetual entitlement (CX-04) | **The worst bug this domain could ship** — revoking something the buyer owns | Must never happen; perpetual entitlements are untouchable by subscription state |
| Perpetual product depends on a subscription product (14.01.03) | Buyer owns it forever and it stops working when a *different* entitlement lapses | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — CX-04 question 3; perpetual-in-name-only |
| Version range unstated at purchase | Buyer assumes lifetime updates; discovers at v2 that they owe £79 | Range is a required, displayed field at purchase — not an upgrade-time surprise |
| Refund of a perpetual purchase (14.09) | Revocation of something sold as forever; legitimate but jarring | Refund window is short and the withdrawal waiver (14.09.01) is captured at download |
| Platform itself shuts down | The honest limit of "perpetual" — nobody's licence server outlives their company | See Q-02; this is the real answer to problem-statement Q-02 (earned vs hostile lock-in) |
| Buyer's account deleted under GDPR erasure | Perpetual entitlement erased with it | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` — 14.02 Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Library fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing purchased perpetually | Library empty state |
| Populated | Perpetual entitlement held | "Yours forever" with version range, seats, terms |
| Error | Fetch failed | Cached, marked stale — never "you own nothing" (14.03.03 D-03) |
| Partial | Vendor exited | Still yours; "no longer sold" on the listing, downloads intact |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Perpetual is the simple model — the one with no lifecycle, the easy default | The intuitive read: pay once, done. It is the *absence* of a subscription's machinery | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the model with the longest and most expensive lifecycle, and its costs land on the platform rather than the vendor.** A subscription's obligation ends when the payment does; a perpetual sale creates a commitment with no end date and no further revenue: store the artifact forever (14.03 DT-02's uncosted 100 GB), serve it on every machine change for decades, keep every archived version fetchable (14.03.02 D-01), and — per 14.08.05 — honour all of it after the vendor has left and stopped caring. The one-time margin was taken in year one; the cost accrues for twenty. "Perpetual" is not the absence of a lifecycle, it is an **unbounded liability sold at a fixed price**, and it is the single largest unexamined commitment in domain 14. Nobody has costed it (14.03 Q-03) and it plausibly decides whether large-format content is sellable here at all. |
| DT-02 | Perpetual means the buyer's ownership is safe | The word's plain meaning, and what the buyer believes they are buying | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **"perpetual" is bounded by the platform's own existence, and that boundary is exactly the question the project has already asked itself.** If WeJammin runs the activation server (14.02 Q-01) and stores the artifacts, then every perpetual licence here dies when WeJammin does — which is a fact the industry has demonstrated repeatedly and buyers have learned to fear. This is not a corner case; it is problem-statement Q-02 ("is the lock-in **earned** or **hostile**? If a user leaves, what do they take?") landing in the most concrete possible place. A perpetual licence whose continued function depends on our uptime is hostile lock-in wearing the word "ownership". The mitigations are real product decisions with real costs — offline/perpetual authorisation that survives us, exportable licence proofs, an escrow of artifacts — and each is worth more to this domain's credibility than any feature in it. Escalated as Q-02 rather than assumed away. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The version range is a required, displayed field at the *original* purchase | Per CX-02. Whether v2 is free is the buyer's second-most-important question and they currently discover it two years late. |
| D-02 | Subscription state can never revoke a perpetual entitlement | Per CX-04. The single worst bug available to this domain — revoking what someone owns. |
| D-03 | The perpetual promise survives vendor exit: licence works, downloads serve, archive stays | Per DT-01/14.08.05. It is the promise's entire content; a perpetual licence that dies with its vendor was a rental. |
