# Feature: Perpetual Purchase & Ownership

> **Number**: 14.07.01
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Pay once, keep it forever. The baseline model, and the one every other model in this sub-domain is
defined against — rent-to-own converts *into* it (CX-01), subscription is defined by *not* being
it (CX-04), upgrades exist because it is version-bounded (CX-02). It is also the model that
commits the platform to an obligation with no end date and no further revenue.

The word "perpetual" is doing more work than it looks. It is simultaneously a **pricing choice**
(one payment), a **promise** (we never take this away), an **unbounded liability** (DT-01), and —
in the EU — a **legal trigger** that may override a vendor's own transfer policy (DT-06). Most of
this feature is the consequence of the industry using one word for four things and then discovering
at year three which of them it did not mean.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Buys and keeps. For a persona who "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products" (personas.md), perpetual is not a preference — it is the only model that fits their economics. Acts on two fields at the buy button: the **version range** (is v2 free or £79?) and the **dependency qualification** (does this need something I must keep paying for? — D-06). Over a 20-year career they will move this licence across ~7 machines, which is why the seat cap must be concurrent and not cumulative (D-05). As a vendor of their own packs, prices them perpetually because that is what content sells as — and per DT-05 their content is the only inventory on which "perpetual" is actually deliverable. |
| Producer | Full | Same, at volume, plus a professional's specific requirement: a tool used on a client record must still open that record in five years. A perpetual licence is the only model that promises it — and per D-04 it promises the *artifact*, not that the 2026 binary still runs on a 2031 OS (DT-05). The persona most exposed to that gap, because they are the one reopening a five-year-old mix for a client who is paying. Frequently reinstalls across studio Mac / tour laptop / render box (14.02.02), so cumulative deactivation caps would end their licence before their career. |
| Operator | Full | **Studios buy perpetual, deliberately.** A room cannot lose a tool mid-project because a card expired. Perpetual is the model that matches a business asset — capitalised, depreciated, and not at the mercy of a payment failure. Their seats are **rooms**, so D-05 (concurrent, not cumulative) is the difference between an asset and a consumable: a room re-imaged every year must not exhaust the licence. Buys with the **org as holder** (14.02.01 D-05) — the engineer leaves, the plugin stays, forever, which is the whole point of the model for this persona. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface — consistent with the Role Matrix (`❌ None` across all of 14.07). Fan music purchases (domain 20) are perpetual too, and they mint the same entitlement record through the shared delivery layer (14.02.01), but the *model* is domain 20's consumer licence: no version range, no seats, no terms UI, no upgrade path, nothing this feature governs. A fan never chooses a monetisation model — they press buy. The word "perpetual" never appears on their surface. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

At purchase, an entitlement (14.02.01) is minted with model `perpetual`, no expiry, a seat
allowance, bound terms (14.02 CX-02), and a **version range** (14.02.01 D-04). The version range
is the field that quietly does the most work: it determines whether v2 is a free update or a paid
upgrade (CX-02), and it must be visible at the *original* purchase rather than discovered at
upgrade time.

**What "perpetual" promises, exactly** (D-04). WeJammin promises three things it can actually
keep, and they are the three that survive vendor exit (14.08.05), dependency lapse (D-06) and
non-use (14.02.01 DT-12):

1. **The entitlement is never revoked for non-payment.** There is no non-payment. No subscription
   state, no lapse, no inactivity, no seat exhaustion and no vendor departure can write `revoked`
   on a `perpetual` record. The only paths that ever can are the ones where the money came back
   or the sale was unlawful: refund (14.09.02), chargeback (14.02.01 D-10), transfer stripping the
   seller (14.06.02), blacklisting a leaked serial (14.02.04), or a rights withdrawal (14.08/24).
2. **The download always serves.** On every machine change, for decades (14.03.01).
3. **Every entitled version stays fetchable.** Not just the latest (14.03.02 D-01).

**What it does not promise, and must say so at the buy button**: that the software *runs*. Function
depends on the buyer's OS, their DAW, and any dependency's own vendor — none of which WeJammin
controls or can escrow (14.08.05 Q-02). Conflating "we will never take this away" with "this will
always work" is what makes "perpetual" a lie in this industry, and the lie is always discovered by
the buyer, alone, years later, on a new laptop. So the promise is stated in the narrow, keepable
form and the gap is disclosed rather than papered over (DT-03, DT-05).

**Dependencies** (D-06, resolving Q-03). A perpetual product may depend on another product
(14.01.03) whose model is a subscription. This is permitted — forbidding it is unimplementable for
external dependencies (14.01.03 D-02's `unknown` edge state: we cannot know whether a third-party
host moves to subscription next year) and would delist the largest real category of content in the
market. But the composite is not sold as unqualified ownership. Two cases, and they are not the
same:

- **Dependency sold on WeJammin under a recurring model** — knowable. The ownership statement is
  qualified at purchase and permanently in the library: "Yours forever — requires an active
  Kontakt subscription to run."
- **Dependency external** (Kontakt, Serum, Pro Tools) — `unknown` per 14.01.03 D-02. The
  vendor's *published* model is displayed as declared, advisory metadata that can change. We state
  what we know and mark it as what it is, rather than asserting a fact about someone else's
  business.

In both cases, when the dependency lapses, **the perpetual entitlement is untouched** — still
`active`, still downloading, still fetchable from the archive. What stops is the *software*,
because its host stopped. That distinction is exact, it is the whole of D-02/D-04, and the library
must show it rather than either revoking (a lie about ownership) or staying silent (a lie about
function).

**The liability is real and unpriced.** Per DT-01 this is the single largest unexamined commitment
in domain 14, and it is routed to `/create-prd`, not hidden here (Q-01).

### Happy Path

1. Buyer opens a listing. The buy button displays four fields as a unit, not a footnote: **price**,
   **version range** (D-01), **seat allowance**, and — where any dependency edge exists — the
   **dependency qualification** (D-06). If any dependency's model is recurring, the ownership
   statement reads "Yours forever — requires an active {X} subscription to run", never a bare
   "Yours forever".
2. Buyer adds to cart. The cart line binds what was **displayed** (14.02.01 CX-02 q5) — price,
   terms version, seat allowance, model `perpetual`, version range.
3. Buyer selects the **holder principal** at checkout — personal by default, or an org they hold a
   purchasing role in (14.02.01 D-05). The Operator selects the studio; the engineer who clicked
   buy is recorded as purchaser, not owner.
4. Buyer agrees to the terms as displayed (14.02.05 renders them as three plain-language answers,
   not an EULA wall).
5. Payment capture confirms. Issuance mints the entitlement in the same transaction: model
   `perpetual`, **no expiry**, version range, seats, terms frozen, allocated price + currency,
   state `active` (14.02.01 D-02).
6. Entitlement appears in the library (14.03.03) within 5 seconds of capture, labelled **Yours
   forever**, with version range, seats used/total, terms link, and any dependency qualification.
7. Buyer requests their first download. The withdrawal waiver gate (14.09.01 D-01/D-02) is
   presented and consented to — **this is the moment the sale becomes final**, not payment.
   Downloads unlock (14.03.01).
8. Buyer activates, works. Seats spend against this record (14.02.02).
9. **Years pass.** Buyer changes machines: they deactivate the old (or self-service reclaim the
   stranded seat — 14.02.02 D-02) and activate the new. The seat returns and re-spends. There is
   no lifetime cap on how many times this may happen (D-05). They re-download from 14.03.01 —
   still theirs.
10. **v2 publishes.** If it falls inside the entitlement's version range, it is a free update,
    offered and never forced (14.03.02 D-02). If it falls outside, the buyer gets an **upgrade
    offer** (14.07.05) — and the two messages must not cross (CX-02 q4).
11. **The vendor exits** (14.08.05). The licence keeps working, downloads keep serving, the archive
    stays fetchable (D-03). The listing disappears from the store; the entitlement does not. Nothing
    changes for the buyer — which is the point, and the cost (Q-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Vendor exits** (14.08.05) | The perpetual promise is now WeJammin's alone — the vendor is gone and we store, serve and honour forever with no further revenue. Per 14.08.05 D-01/D-02 this is agreed at onboarding and enforced structurally: a departing vendor **cannot** delete artifacts buyers hold perpetual rights to. | Buyer: nothing changes. Listing shows "no longer sold"; library row, downloads and archive are identical to yesterday |
| **Subscription lapse touches a perpetual entitlement** (CX-04) | **The worst bug this domain could ship** — revoking something the buyer owns. Prevented structurally, not by policy: there is no code path from subscription billing state to a `perpetual` entitlement's state (D-02). Billing writes to subscription entitlements only; a `perpetual` record is not addressable by it. | Nothing. The library row does not change, flicker, or warn. A perpetual licence must not even *look* at risk during someone else's lapse |
| **Perpetual product depends on a subscription product** (14.01.03) — **RESOLVED, see D-06** | Permitted, disclosed, never silently revoked. Dependency model is displayed at purchase (known case) or shown as declared vendor metadata (external / `unknown`, 14.01.03 D-02). On lapse: entitlement stays `active`, downloads serve, archive fetches — the *software* stops because its host stopped. We never revoke; the thing may still stop running (D-04) | Purchase: "Yours forever — requires an active Kontakt subscription to run." On lapse: "You still own this. It won't open until your Kontakt subscription is active." Never "revoked", never silence |
| Perpetual product depends on a subscription product **sold by the same vendor** | Permitted by D-06 but flagged — this is the shape where a vendor sells "forever" that only runs while you pay them monthly (the Waves pathology). The disclosure is identical; whether the store *allows* the self-dealing shape is a policy call, not an agent's — Q-05 | Same qualification as above. The policy question is the owner's |
| **Version range unstated at purchase** | Cannot occur — blocked at publish, not at purchase. Version range is a required field on the listing (D-01); a product without one cannot go live (14.08.01). The failure mode this prevents: buyer assumes lifetime updates, discovers at v2 that they owe £79 | Vendor: publish blocked, "Set the version range — buyers must know at purchase whether v2 is included." Buyer: never sees the gap |
| **Vendor promised an unbounded range, then ships v2 as a new product** to defeat it | Range laundering — not structurally preventable (we cannot forbid a vendor from making a new product) and a real market practice. What is available: the unbounded range is on the **indelible** entitlement (14.02.01 D-08), so the record contradicts the claim. The successor is surfaced to affected holders as a **crossgrade/loyalty offer** (14.07.05), not at full price, and the pattern is reportable (24) | Holder: "You own X v1 with lifetime updates. X2 is a new product — here's your owner's price." Not a silent full-price listing |
| **Refund of a perpetual purchase** (14.09) | Revocation of something sold as forever — legitimate but jarring. Mitigated by the sequencing rather than the policy: the refund window is short and the withdrawal waiver (14.09.01 D-01) is captured **at first download**, so the buyer affirmatively ends the statutory right *before* the file arrives. Declining the waiver is a supported path (14.09.01 D-03) | "You're giving up your 14-day cancellation right by downloading now" — an affirmative act, before the bytes move |
| **Chargeback 120 days later** (cards) / **13 months** (SEPA unauthorised) | A perpetual entitlement **can** be revoked here, and this does not contradict D-02: the money came back. Trigger recorded as `chargeback`, distinct from `refund` everywhere (14.02.01 D-10) — reporting it to a vendor as a refund they granted is a lie with money attached. Revocation is best-effort; "revoked-but-running" is real and accepted (14.09.03 D-01). Loss allocation is 14.02.01 Q-03 | Holder: row shows revoked, reason "payment reversed", appeal route. Vendor: reported as a chargeback, not a refund |
| **Vendor removed for cause** (malware, uncleared rights) | Not perpetual's call — 14.08.05 D-03 makes retirement and removal-for-cause separate paths, and 14.08.05 Q-01 owns the question. What this feature contributes: the perpetual promise was made by *us*, so if the promise is broken here it is broken by us and the buyer is refunded regardless of policy (14.02.01's rights-withdrawal row). Continuing to serve may deepen their exposure; stripping punishes them for our failure | "This product was withdrawn for a rights claim. You've been refunded in full." Plus what it means for work already released |
| **Buyer's 5th laptop in 12 years** | Seat returns and re-spends. **No cumulative cap** (D-05): a lifetime deactivation limit converts a forever licence into one that expires on your fourth machine. Concurrent seats are capped; re-allocation over time is not. Stranded seat from a dead machine → self-service reclaim with cooldown (14.02.02 D-02) | "2 of 2 seats in use — release one" → released → activates. Never "you have used your 3 deauthorisations" |
| **The 2026 binary will not run on the 2031 OS, and the vendor is gone** (DT-05) | Every promise honoured, buyer has nothing. Archive serves the artifact (14.03.02 D-01); nobody can compel a departed vendor to recompile for a new architecture. Not fixable — which is precisely why D-04 states the promise as entitlement + artifact and discloses the gap at purchase rather than letting it be discovered here | Library: still yours, still downloadable, marked with its last known compatible OS (14.01.02). The honest version of a bad day |
| **Platform itself shuts down** | The honest limit of "perpetual" — nobody's licence server outlives their company. This is problem-statement Q-02 (earned vs hostile lock-in) at its most concrete. See Q-02 | Unresolved by design, not by omission. The owner decides what we promise here |
| **Buyer's account deleted under GDPR erasure** — **RESOLVED behaviourally** | Per 14.02.01 D-08/Q-05: erasure detaches PII and reduces the holder to an opaque principal id; the entitlement **survives pseudonymised** — it is the vendor's sale record and the buyer's own clearance proof for anything released under it. Honest consequence: with no authenticable holder, the licence becomes unusable — nobody can log in to exercise it. The buyer is told **before** erasure completes, because it is irreversible. Whether law permits retaining even the pseudonymised record is 14.02 Q-02 → `/create-prd-security` — legal input, not an agent's opinion | Pre-erasure: "Deleting your account permanently ends access to 47 licences you bought. The purchase records are kept in anonymised form because vendors and any releases you cleared with them rely on them. This cannot be undone." |
| **Rent-to-own final payment converts to perpetual** (CX-01) | Balance cleared and model flips to `perpetual` **in place** — same record, no new entitlement, no re-download, no reactivation. Must be atomic with the payment: a cleared balance that does not flip leaves a buyer who has paid in full still renting | The completion is marked loudly — it is the buyer's win, not a silent field change |
| **Product goes on sale 3 days after a perpetual purchase** | No retroactive price adjustment (D-07) — the market norm, and re-pricing settled sales makes every purchase a bet on next week. But the promo channel (14.07.04) **must not email the buyer who bought it 3 days ago**. Suppression window: 30 days on the same product, same holder | Buyer: nothing. Not a "50% off — the thing you just paid full price for!" email, which is the cheapest possible way to lose a customer |
| **Entitlement fetch succeeds, product listing is gone** (vendor exited mid-session) | Library renders from the **entitlement**, never from the listing (14.02.01: the record must not be reachable only *through* a live listing). A dead listing must not blank a live licence | Full row: product name, version range, seats, download. Marked "no longer sold" |
| **Library fetch fails** (network / transient) | Cached list served, marked stale (14.03.03 D-03). A buyer must **never** see "you own nothing" on a transient error — for a perpetual holder that is not an error state, it is a heart attack | Cached rows + "Showing your saved library — reconnecting" |
| **Two tabs, two devices, same product** | Blocked before payment by 14.02.01 D-07 (one entitlement per product per holder). Converted to the useful adjacent action: seat top-up, or upgrade if out of range | "You already own this — add seats, or upgrade to v2 for £79" |
| **Multi-hyphenate: vendor of pack A buys perpetual pack B in the same cart** | Fully independent. Self-purchase is flagged per **line** against the *product's* vendor principal, never against the cart (14.02.01 D-11). Selling A and buying B in one day is two ordinary things, not one suspicious one — personas.md says this is the normal case | An ordinary sale and an ordinary purchase |
| **Vendor buys their own perpetual product** | Permitted and flagged (14.02.01 D-11); excluded from payout (14.10), sales rank, reviews and loyalty accrual (14.07.05). Without the flag, wash-trading rank on a store with no reputation is free and undetectable | Nothing to the buyer. Vendor dashboard shows the sale excluded, with the reason |
| **Perpetual entitlement is transferred** (14.06) | The promise travels with the **entitlement**, not the person: the holder changes, the seller is stripped (14.06.02), the version range, terms version and perpetual model are unchanged. The buyer of a used licence inherits exactly what the original buyer had — that is the thing being sold. Whether the vendor may forbid the transfer at all is DT-06 / 14.06 Q-03 | New holder: identical row, identical range. Seller: revoked, reason "transferred" |
| **Malicious**: client tampers with model, version range, price or seats at checkout | Every one resolved **server-side** from the order and the listing; client-supplied values ignored, not validated-then-trusted (14.02.01). A client-set version range would be a free upgrade forever | Nothing — the tamper has no effect |
| **Malicious buyer** claims "you promised lifetime updates" three years on | Settled by the record, not by memory: the version range and terms version are frozen at issuance and indelible (14.02.01 D-03/D-08). This is the thesis (D-18) applied to the store's own disputes — the artifact answers without asking anyone to remember | Both parties shown the same dated record |
| **Async payment** (SEPA/BNPL) not yet cleared | Entitlement mints `pending-payment` and grants **nothing** — no download, no activation, no seats (14.02.01 D-09). It is not perpetual yet; it is not anything yet | "Awaiting payment — your licence unlocks when it clears", with the expected date |
| Buyer never opens the product for 3 years | Nothing happens. Non-use is not abandonment (14.02.01 DT-12) — a musician who has not opened a plugin for three years still owns it, and reclaiming on inactivity would contradict this feature's entire promise. If the storage liability is unaffordable (Q-01), the answer is pricing or format scope, not silent repossession | Nothing. It is still there, which is what they bought |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Library fetch in flight | Skeleton rows — never an empty library rendered as the loaded state |
| **Empty** | No perpetual entitlements held (may still hold subscription content) | Library empty state with store CTA. Must distinguish "you own nothing perpetual" from "you own nothing" — a subscriber with credits-downloaded assets (14.07.02 D-01) owns those perpetually and must see them here |
| **Populated** | Perpetual entitlement `active` | "Yours forever" + version range + seats used/total + terms link + origin. Plus dependency qualification where one exists (D-06) |
| **Error** | Fetch failed | Cached list, marked stale (14.03.03 D-03) — **never** "you own nothing" |
| **Partial — vendor exited** | Listing withdrawn, entitlement intact (14.08.05) | Still yours; "no longer sold" on the listing; downloads and archive unchanged |
| **Partial — dependency unsatisfied** | Dependency subscription lapsed, or edge state is `unknown` (14.01.03 D-02) | "You own this. It won't open until your {X} subscription is active" — or, for `unknown`, "Requires {X} — we can't verify whether you have it". Ownership never in doubt; function stated honestly |
| **Partial — awaiting payment** | Async capture pending (14.02.01 D-09) | "Awaiting payment" — grants nothing, visible so the buyer knows the order exists |
| **Revoked** | Refund, chargeback, transfer, blacklist, rights withdrawal — **never** subscription lapse or non-use (D-02) | Row persists with reason, trigger and appeal route (14.09.03 D-04). A silent disappearance is indistinguishable from a bug |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Perpetual is the simple model — the one with no lifecycle, the easy default | The intuitive read: pay once, done. It is the *absence* of a subscription's machinery | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the model with the longest and most expensive lifecycle, and its costs land on the platform rather than the vendor.** A subscription's obligation ends when the payment does; a perpetual sale creates a commitment with no end date and no further revenue: store the artifact forever (14.03 DT-02's uncosted 100 GB), serve it on every machine change for decades, keep every archived version fetchable (14.03.02 D-01), and — per 14.08.05 — honour all of it after the vendor has left and stopped caring. The one-time margin was taken in year one; the cost accrues for twenty. "Perpetual" is not the absence of a lifecycle, it is an **unbounded liability sold at a fixed price**, and it is the single largest unexamined commitment in domain 14. Nobody has costed it (Q-01) and it plausibly decides whether large-format content is sellable here at all. |
| DT-02 | Perpetual means the buyer's ownership is safe | The word's plain meaning, and what the buyer believes they are buying | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **"perpetual" is bounded by the platform's own existence, and that boundary is exactly the question the project has already asked itself.** If WeJammin runs the activation server (14.02 Q-01) and stores the artifacts, then every perpetual licence here dies when WeJammin does — which is a fact the industry has demonstrated repeatedly and buyers have learned to fear. This is not a corner case; it is problem-statement Q-02 ("is the lock-in **earned** or **hostile**? If a user leaves, what do they take?") landing in the most concrete possible place. A perpetual licence whose continued function depends on our uptime is hostile lock-in wearing the word "ownership". The mitigations are real product decisions with real costs — offline/perpetual authorisation that survives us, exportable licence proofs, an escrow of artifacts — and each is worth more to this domain's credibility than any feature in it. Escalated as Q-02 rather than assumed away. |
| DT-03 | "Perpetual" is a promise that the buyer keeps a **working thing** | What the buyer hears, and what every store's marketing implies. The breadth pass treated the promise as atomic | ❌ REJECTED — and this rejection is the spine of the feature (D-04). The promise decomposes into things WeJammin controls (the entitlement is never revoked for non-payment; the download serves; every entitled version stays fetchable) and things it does not (the buyer's OS, their DAW, a dependency vendor's business model, the ABI the binary was compiled against). The industry sells the union and can only deliver the first half — which is why every "perpetual licence" scandal has the same shape: nobody lied at the point of sale, they just promised something they had no mechanism to keep. **A promise you cannot keep is worth less than a narrower one you can**, because the buyer eventually finds out which one they bought, and finds out alone, on a new laptop, years later. Confirmed consequence: state the promise in the narrow keepable form and *disclose the gap at the buy button* — which is what makes D-06's dependency qualification mandatory rather than a nicety, and what makes DT-05's OS-drift case honest instead of a betrayal. |
| DT-04 | A perpetual licence's seat allowance is a bounded number, and that is the whole of the seat story | 14.02.01 mints a seat allowance; 14.02.02 owns activation. Perpetual appears to add nothing | ❌ REJECTED — **"forever" and "3 machines" interact, and the interaction is where a well-known industry trap lives.** Seat *count* is bounded; machine *turnover* over a perpetual licence's life is not. personas.md's Musician runs a 20-year career; the Producer holds studio Mac + tour laptop + render box and "frequently reinstalls"; the Operator re-images rooms. That is on the order of seven machines per seat over the licence's life — and several vendors in this market cap **cumulative deactivations** ("you may deauthorise 3 times"), which silently converts a forever licence into one that expires on your fourth laptop. The buyer paid once for perpetual and got a consumable. Nobody in the sub-domain stated the rule either way, and 14.02.02 D-02 (self-service reclaim) implies but does not say it. Confirmed consequence: **the cap is concurrent, never cumulative** (D-05) — a constraint this feature places on 14.02.02, because it follows from the *model*, not from activation. |
| DT-05 | The permanent archive (14.03.02 D-01) is what makes the perpetual promise deliverable | The archive exists precisely so old versions stay fetchable; it looks like the answer | ✅ CONFIRMED but **radically insufficient, and the insufficiency points somewhere useful.** Compose the three things this domain has already ratified: a perpetual licence + a permanent archive + an exited vendor (14.08.05). In 2031 the buyer fetches a perfectly preserved 2026 binary that will not run on their OS, and no one has failed: we honoured every promise and they have nothing. Nobody can fix this — no store can compel a departed vendor to recompile for a new architecture, and 14.08.05 Q-02 already concedes we cannot escrow what a product needs to run. **The archive preserves the artifact, not the capability**, and the gap between those is the whole of DT-03. The non-obvious payoff is what this says about inventory: a **WAV is format-stable for decades; a plugin binary is compiled against a moving ABI**. Perpetual is a promise the store can actually keep on sample packs, presets and stems — and cannot keep on the plugin catalog it does not have. That is a third, independent argument for domain DT-A (creator content is the realistic inventory), arriving from durability rather than from business development: **creator content is not merely what we can obtain, it is the only inventory on which "perpetual" is deliverable.** |
| DT-06 | Choosing "perpetual" is a pricing decision with no legal consequences | The whole sub-domain frames monetisation as pricing — five ways to charge for the same object | ❌ REJECTED — **the model field is the trigger for the EU exhaustion doctrine, and 14.06's entire premise may hinge on it.** In *UsedSoft v Oracle* (C-128/11, 2012) the CJEU held that the distribution right in a downloaded software copy is **exhausted** on first sale — so the rightsholder cannot prevent resale of a "used" licence — and the ruling turns explicitly on the licence being *perpetual* (unlimited in time) for a one-off fee. A subscription is not exhausted; a perpetual sale is. So 14.06.01's per-vendor transfer-policy registry may be faithfully encoding vendor preferences that are **void** in the EU/UK, which 14.09.01 identifies as the store's two most likely launch markets. 14.06 Q-03 already asks this — what is new here is (a) **this feature is where the trigger is set**, so the registry cannot answer it alone, and (b) a distinction nobody in the domain has drawn: *UsedSoft* rests on the Software Directive, and in *Tom Kabinet* (C-263/18, 2019) the CJEU went the **other way** for e-books, treating non-software digital content as communication to the public with **no exhaustion**. If that split holds, the resale right applies to the **plugins the store may never obtain** (domain DT-A) and **not** to the sample packs that are its realistic inventory — meaning the used-licence "sharpest wedge" (domain Q-08) is legally strongest exactly where the store has no supply. That complements CX R-02's economic objection (creator content is priced too low to resell) from an entirely independent direction. Flagged, not decided: I am reasoning from known case law, and its application is a lawyer's call, not an agent's — Q-04. |
| DT-07 | Perpetual is the buyer-friendly model; subscription is the exploitative one | The moral framing the sub-domain leans on — index Q-03 asks outright whether selling subscriptions contradicts the thesis, and personas.md's Musician "cannot afford per-tool subscriptions across six products" | ❌ REJECTED as too simple, and the inversion is uncomfortable enough to be worth stating. **Perpetual is the model that puts the buyer's long-term interest most at risk, because it is the only one whose obligation outlives its revenue.** A subscription that stops is *honest*: the money and the service end together, on the same day, and nobody is surprised. A perpetual sale funds a twenty-year obligation from a single year-one payment — which means the promise is only as good as the platform's ability to keep subsidising it out of **other** revenue, i.e. out of exactly the subscriptions and new sales the model's advocates want fewer of. Every abandoned perpetual promise in this industry's history was abandoned by a company that had stopped selling. This does not argue for subscriptions (index Q-03 is the owner's, and the thesis objection stands); it argues that **"we sell perpetual because we're the good guys" is not a strategy** — it is a liability (DT-01) that requires a funding answer, and the honest positions are to price it, bound the format scope, or state the platform-shutdown answer out loud (Q-02). The buyer-friendly model is the one most likely to be quietly abandoned. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The version range is a required, displayed field at the *original* purchase | Per CX-02; = 14.02.01 D-04. Whether v2 is free is the buyer's second-most-important question and they currently discover it two years late. Enforced at **publish**, not at purchase — a listing without a range cannot go live. |
| D-02 | **Subscription state can never revoke a perpetual entitlement** — structurally, not by policy | Per CX-04. The single worst bug available to this domain. There is no state-write path from subscription billing to a `perpetual` record. The only paths that may revoke one are those where the money came back or the sale was unlawful: refund, chargeback (14.02.01 D-10), transfer (14.06.02), blacklist (14.02.04), rights withdrawal (14.08/24). Not lapse, not inactivity, not seat exhaustion, not vendor exit. |
| D-03 | The perpetual promise survives vendor exit: licence works, downloads serve, archive stays | Per DT-01 / 14.08.05. It is the promise's entire content; a perpetual licence that dies with its vendor was a rental. Made real by 14.08.05 D-02 — the vendor *cannot* delete what buyers hold rights to. |
| D-04 | **"Perpetual" is a promise about the entitlement and the artifact, not a guarantee of function** — stated narrowly at the buy button, with the gap disclosed | Per DT-03/DT-05. We promise: never revoked for non-payment, download always serves, every entitled version stays fetchable. We do not promise the software runs — that depends on the buyer's OS, their DAW, and any dependency's vendor, none of which we control or can escrow (14.08.05 Q-02). This is not weaseling; it is the difference between a promise we keep and one the buyer discovers we broke, alone, on a new laptop, in 2031. |
| D-05 | A perpetual entitlement's seat cap is **concurrent, never cumulative** — unlimited re-allocation over the life of the licence | Per DT-04. A lifetime deactivation limit converts a forever licence into one that expires on your fourth laptop. personas.md's careers are measured in decades and machines in dozens; the Operator re-images rooms. A constraint this feature places on 14.02.02, because it follows from the model rather than from activation. |
| D-06 | A perpetual product **may** declare a dependency on a subscription product, but the ownership statement is qualified at purchase and permanently in the library; the dependency's lapse **never** writes state on the perpetual entitlement — **resolves Q-03** | Forbidding it is unimplementable: 14.01.03 D-02's `unknown` edge state exists precisely because a third party's model is unknowable, and a rule that cannot be evaluated is not a rule. It would also delist the largest real category of content (every Kontakt library). So: permit + mandatory disclosure + never revoke. The buyer keeps ownership and loses function, and both facts are stated. The word "perpetual" is conditional in this case — which is exactly why the condition is displayed at the buy button rather than discovered at lapse. |
| D-07 | No retroactive price adjustment when a product goes on sale after purchase; but promo notifications (14.07.04) suppress holders who bought the same product within **30 days** | No price protection is the market norm, and re-pricing settled sales makes every purchase a bet on next week. The suppression window is the agent's implementation call (a trust/UX judgement, per `decision-classification.md`); emailing "50% off!" to someone who paid full price 3 days ago is the cheapest possible way to lose them. |
