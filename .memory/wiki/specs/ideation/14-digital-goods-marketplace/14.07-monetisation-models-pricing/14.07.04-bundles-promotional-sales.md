# Feature: Bundles & Promotional Sales

> **Number**: 14.07.04
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Selling several products together at a lower price, and running time-boxed discounts. Music
software is a famously promotional market — the plugin industry runs on perpetual sales, Black
Friday bundles, and £29 flash deals on £199 products — and any store that lists in it inherits
those expectations. The interesting part is not the discount arithmetic but what happens when the
buyer already owns half the bundle (CX-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full as buyer · Config as vendor | Buys bundles because that is how tools become affordable at their income level. As a vendor, bundles their own packs — "all 5 of my drum packs, £40" is the most natural thing a creator vendor does. |
| Producer | Full · Config | Same, and the persona most likely to already own part of any bundle — years of accumulated purchases means partial overlap is their normal case, not an edge case. |
| Operator | Full | Buys bundles for rooms; the mixed-ownership case across four rooms is genuinely messy (CX-03) and unresolved while 14.02 Q-03 is open. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

A bundle is a priced collection of listings. At cart time (not checkout — CX-03 q2) the buyer's
library (14.03.03) is checked and the price adjusts for what they already own. A time-boxed sale
is a scheduled price on a listing or bundle.

Each admitted promotion version freezes one positive **standalone selling price** for every exact
member listing/model in the bundle's settlement currency (D-04). It is the independently executable
non-bundle price version available in that currency when the promotion version is admitted; a nominal
list price, equal split, vendor-authored weight, buyer-specific credit or this bundle's own price is
not the basis. A missing, zero, negative, differently denominated or non-independent member price
fails the promotion closed. Later standalone-price changes require a new promotion version and do
not rewrite carts, entitlements, refunds or accruals already pinned to the old one.

For full-bundle consideration `B` and frozen member prices `s_i`, the unrounded member share is
`B × s_i / Σs`. Convert once to settlement-currency minor units using the existing largest-remainder
rule with immutable `bundle_member_id` as the stable tie key; allocated shares must sum exactly to
`B`. Compute that full vector before ownership adjustment. At cart and again before capture, charge
only the sum of shares for members the holder does not already own; owned-member shares are shown
and deducted, mint no entitlement and earn no payout in this acquisition. Each newly acquired
entitlement pins its member share, promotion version, basis kind and standalone-price version for
refund and contributor-accrual use. For a multi-vendor bundle, this vector is the proposed explicit
split and every participating vendor must accept that immutable promotion version before sale.

The model is indifferent to what's inside: a bundle of perpetuals, subscriptions or rent-to-owns
prices the same way, which is why bundle→perpetual is a rejected pair (`#R-01`) — the input is
*ownership*, not model.

### Happy Path

1. Vendor (or platform) defines a bundle with member listings and a price; the promotion version
   freezes every member's valid standalone-price version and computes the proportional allocation.
2. Buyer adds it to cart; the library is checked.
3. Buyer owns 2 of 5 → their two frozen allocated shares are deducted and the three remaining
   shares are shown explicitly.
4. Checkout rechecks ownership and the pinned promotion version; payment captures the sum of the
   three remaining shares and mints three entitlements carrying those exact allocations.
5. Sale ends on schedule; the price returns.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer owns 2 of 5 bundle items (CX-03) | Deduct those members' frozen D-04 allocated shares; mint and charge only for the other three | "You own 2 of these — £X deducted" with each member share shown |
| Member lacks a valid standalone selling price in the bundle currency | Promotion version cannot be admitted or offered; never substitute list price, equal weighting, vendor weighting or runtime FX | Vendor sees the exact member and missing/invalid price-version requirement |
| Buyer bought the product yesterday at full price, today it's 70% off | The plugin market's routine cruelty; buyers learn to never buy at full price, which trains the whole market into discount-only behaviour | See Q-02 — a short price-protection window is a real option |
| Bundle price changes while it sits in a cart | A price that changes at checkout is worse than a wrong price | Cart-time lock, per CX-03 q2 |
| Buyer buys a bundle item separately while the bundle is in the cart | The bundle must re-price, not double-charge | Re-check at checkout with the change surfaced |
| Vendor bundles a product with an active rent-to-own schedule | Buyer is 6 payments into a product now in a £40 bundle | `[OWNER]` — see Q-04. Sharper than the sale case and **not** covered by its answer: 14.07.01 D-07 bars retroactive adjustment on a *settled* purchase, and a mid-schedule balance is not settled |
| Bundle spans vendors | D-04's immutable member allocation vector is the explicit proposed revenue split; every participating vendor accepts the exact promotion version before sale | Each vendor sees its member shares and acceptance state; no sale until all accept |
| Bundle member delisted mid-sale (vendor exit, exclusivity) | The bundle is now 4 of 5 | Bundle must re-price or withdraw, not silently short the buyer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Cart price calculating | Skeleton on the line item |
| Empty | No bundles/sales active | Normal listings at normal prices |
| Populated | Bundle in cart | Itemised: what's included, what you own, the adjustment and why |
| Error | Ownership check failed | **Fail toward the buyer**: never charge full price on an unverified check — hold the cart rather than risk double-charging |
| Partial | Buyer owns some members | Adjusted price with explicit per-item reasoning |
| Expired | Sale window closed | Price returned; anything in-cart at the old price honoured or clearly re-priced before payment |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A bundle is a discounted basket — the hard part is the arithmetic | The candidate's framing ("Bundles, Sales, Upgrade/Crossgrade & Loyalty Credit" as one pricing lump) | ❌ REJECTED — **the arithmetic is trivial and the state is the whole feature.** What makes a bundle hard is the question "what does this buyer already own?", which requires the library (14.03.03) as a live input to pricing, per-item, at cart time. Get it wrong and you charge a loyal customer twice for a product they bought from you — the single most effective way to convert a repeat buyer into an ex-buyer, and it happens because the store treated pricing as a property of the *listing* rather than of the (listing, buyer) pair. This is the same state that upgrades and crossgrades read (CX-03), which is precisely why candidate 18's two halves belong in one sub-domain (index D-01): they are one question about ownership wearing two price tags. A store that models bundles as baskets will discover this in production, one insulted customer at a time. |
| DT-02 | Sales and discounts are vendor tools — let vendors run whatever promotions they want | The market norm; plugin retail runs on aggressive, constant discounting | ❌ REJECTED as unbounded, because **the plugin market's discount culture is a pathology the store would be importing wholesale, not a feature.** When a £199 product is £29 every eight weeks, buyers learn — correctly — that full price is for suckers, and the market trains itself into discount-only purchasing. Every vendor then discounts harder to compete, and the ratchet only turns one way. The specific cruelty this produces is a buyer who paid full price on Tuesday watching the same product hit 70% off on Thursday, with no recourse; they don't blame the vendor, they blame the store, and they never buy at full price again. Mature marketplaces answer this with **price protection** — a short window where a price drop refunds the difference automatically (Q-02) — which costs real money and is worth it, because it converts the store's biggest trust-destroying event into its most surprising trust-building one. Also worth naming: for creator-made content (14.01.01 DT-02), where a vendor's £15 pack *is* their income, importing a race-to-£2 discount culture is actively hostile to the population this domain depends on. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Ownership is checked at **cart time** and the price adjusts with an explicit per-item reason | Per DT-01/CX-03 q2. A price that changes at checkout is worse than a wrong price, and silent adjustment wastes the trust the adjustment earns. |
| D-02 | An ownership-check failure fails toward the buyer — hold the cart rather than risk charging twice | Double-charging a loyal customer is the worst outcome available to this feature. |
| D-03 | Multi-vendor bundles require an explicit revenue-split rule before they can be offered | A single discounted price across vendors has no natural apportionment; leaving it implicit means the payout cross-cut invents one. |
| D-04 | Bundle consideration is allocated in proportion to frozen standalone selling prices | Owner decision 2026-08-27. Freeze each exact member's valid standalone-price version in one settlement currency, compute `B × s_i / Σs`, then use largest remainder on stable `bundle_member_id` so shares sum exactly. Deduct already-owned shares; pin acquired shares to entitlements/refunds/accruals. Missing basis fails closed; list-price, equal and vendor-authored weighting are rejected. Multi-vendor participants accept the computed immutable vector before sale. |
