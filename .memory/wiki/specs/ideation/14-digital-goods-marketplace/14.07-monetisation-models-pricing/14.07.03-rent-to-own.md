# Feature: Rent-to-Own

> **Number**: 14.07.03
> **Parent**: [14.07 Monetisation Models & Pricing](./14.07-monetisation-models-pricing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Paying for an expensive tool in instalments, using it throughout, and owning it at the end — pay
£34/month for 12 months, own the £400 plugin, stop early and keep nothing but lose nothing more.
It is the only model in this sub-domain that lets a capital-constrained musician acquire a
professional tool, and it is consumer credit wearing a checkout button.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The persona this exists for.** personas.md: "makes a living from a portfolio of small income streams" and cannot fund a £400 outlay. Rent-to-own is how they acquire a tool that pays for itself while paying for itself. |
| Producer | Full | Same at higher values — a £2,000 orchestral library is not a lump-sum purchase for most working producers. Also a vendor deciding whether to offer it on their own products. |
| Operator | Read-only | Studios capitalise rather than finance; a business buys the tool. Read-only is honest — inflating this to Full would invent a workflow the persona doesn't have. |
| Fan | None | Not on the fan surface. |

## Behavior

A rent-to-own entitlement is a **perpetual entitlement with a payment schedule and an outstanding
balance** (CX-01) — not a subscription, not a separate object. The buyer has full use throughout.
The final payment clears the balance and the model flips to perpetual **in place**: same record,
no re-download, no reactivation.

Stopping early is the model's defining moment and its main design question (DT-01): the buyer
loses access and their payments were rent. Nothing is owed and nothing is owned.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer selects rent-to-own on an eligible product; the schedule and total are shown — including the total vs the outright price.
2. First payment captures; a rent-to-own entitlement is minted with a balance.
3. Buyer downloads, activates, and works with the full product from day one.
4. Payments continue; the library (14.03.03) shows payments remaining and balance.
5. Final payment clears the balance; the entitlement flips to perpetual in place (CX-01), atomically.
6. Buyer is told, loudly — it is their win.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer stops at 6 of 12 payments | Access ends; £204 paid; nothing owned. Whether *any* credit persists is the model's central question (DT-01). | Must be stated before the first payment, not discovered at month 7 |
| **Vendor exits at payment 6 of 12** (CX-01 q5) | Buyer is mid-purchase of a product whose vendor has gone. Keep paying? To whom? Convert free? Refund? | `[PENDING]` — genuinely unsolved anywhere in the market. See DT-02 |
| Card fails mid-schedule | Under fail-open (14.02.02 D-01), grace before access ends | Grace period with a clear deadline |
| Buyer confuses this with subscription (CX-05) | 18 months of payments and they think they own something, or vice versa | Distinct naming and a visible balance — "3 payments to own" is not "next billing date" |
| Total instalments exceed the outright price | Normal for financed purchases and legitimate — but it must be visible | Total cost shown against the outright price at selection |
| Buyer completes payments; the flip fails | Buyer has paid in full and is still renting | Must be atomic (CX-01 q2) |
| Product is refunded mid-schedule (14.09) | Partial refund of an instalment plan | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Schedule fetch | Skeleton |
| Empty | No rent-to-own plans | Not shown |
| Populated | Plan active | "8 of 12 payments — £136 to own" with the balance visible |
| Error | Payment failed | Grace period, explicit deadline |
| Partial | Grace period | "Payment failed — access continues until <date>; your progress is safe" |
| Completed | Final payment | Flipped to perpetual, marked plainly: "Yours forever" |
| Abandoned | Buyer stopped | Access ended; per DT-01, whether progress persists is the open question |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Stopping early means you lose everything — that's the deal, and it's what makes the model work for vendors | The standard rent-to-own construction, and Splice's actual behaviour | ❌ REJECTED as an unexamined default, because **it is the most punitive possible answer applied to the most vulnerable persona, and the alternatives are not obviously worse for the vendor.** The Musician who stops at payment 7 has paid £238 toward a £400 tool and owns nothing; the money was rent for months they may not even have used. Meanwhile personas.md's switching trigger for this persona is *"losing money to a fact they couldn't prove"* — losing £238 to a schedule they didn't finish is close cousin to that, and it is a memorable, tellable grievance. Three real alternatives exist: **credit persistence** (payments become loyalty credit toward that product later — 14.07.05), **pause** (freeze the schedule, keep progress, lose access), or **partial conversion** (£238 buys the lite edition, where one exists). Each keeps the buyer in the relationship rather than converting them into someone who warns their friends. This is a product/values decision (Q-01) with a real vendor cost — but shipping the punitive default *by inheritance*, without anyone choosing it, is the failure mode worth naming. |
| DT-02 | Rent-to-own is a pricing option — offer it on expensive products | The candidate's framing (listed alongside perpetual and subscription as a monetisation model) | ❌ REJECTED — **it is consumer credit, and the platform is the creditor.** The buyer receives the full product now and pays over time; that is financing, whatever the checkout calls it. It carries a regulatory perimeter (consumer credit authorisation, affordability, APR-equivalent disclosure — territory-dependent and not addressed anywhere in `meta/constraints.md`'s compliance table), a default-risk exposure the platform absorbs, and a genuinely unsolved failure mode nobody in the market has answered: **what happens when the vendor exits mid-schedule** (CX-01 q5)? The buyer is 6 of 12 payments into a contract with a party who has left. They cannot complete a purchase from a vendor who is gone; they cannot get their £204 back from someone with no relationship to us any more; and per 14.08.05 we have promised the product keeps working. Every answer costs someone money, and the platform is the only party still in the room. This is not a pricing option — it is a credit product with an unowned tail risk. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | One entitlement record throughout; the final payment flips the model in place, atomically | Per CX-01. Two records means the buyer pays in full and keeps renting, or re-downloads 40 GB for no reason. |
| D-02 | Total cost of the schedule is shown against the outright price at selection | Instalments exceeding the lump sum is normal and legitimate — concealing it is not. |
| D-03 | Rent-to-own and subscription are visually and verbally distinct: a balance ("3 payments to own"), never a billing date | Per CX-05 — they look identical on a bank statement and mean opposite things. |
