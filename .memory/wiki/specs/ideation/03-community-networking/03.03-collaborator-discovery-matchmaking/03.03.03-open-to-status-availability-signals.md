# Feature: Open-To Status & Availability Signals

> **Number**: 03.03.03
> **Parent**: [Collaborator Discovery & Matchmaking](./03.03-collaborator-discovery-matchmaking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The supply side's declaration of **appetite** — what I want to do next, which is routinely different from
what my credits prove I have done. The session drummer who wants to co-write, the mixer who is done taking
mix work, the band open to support slots but not to festivals. Credits (domain 02) are the capability axis;
this is the intent axis, and the matcher (03.03.02) needs both. It is also the one input on this platform
that **decays silently** — nothing in the world falsifies a stale "open to work", which makes decay
handling the feature's central problem rather than a detail.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares appetite per role — and per personas.md this is where a single availability boolean fails hardest: their roles are *simultaneous*, so "open to session work, open to co-writes, not open to unpaid, not taking new bands" are four concurrent truths. Any UX forcing one status onto the multi-hyphenate reproduces the "are you an artist OR a producer?" failure personas.md names explicitly. |
| Producer | Full | Declares session-owner appetite ("taking mix work from October"). Per personas.md they will not leave the DAW to maintain a web form — so if keeping this true requires discipline, it will be false within weeks. DT-01 is therefore not an optimisation but the difference between the signal working and not. |
| Operator | Config | Config-only, and deliberately narrow: an Operator's real availability is a **calendar with hard resource constraints** (domain 16/17) — a room is booked or it is not. Per personas.md their inventory is "time and space… perishable and physically constrained", which is a different kind of fact from "I fancy a co-write". Conflating them would let a matcher treat a booked Tuesday as a mood. What they may declare here is appetite for *kinds* of work (open to hosting rehearsals, not to club nights). |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-05). Per personas.md the Fan "will never file a split sheet" — they have no appetite to declare in a collaborator market. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A signal is (role, appetite, scope, freshness). Scope carries the qualifiers that decide whether a match
is worth anyone's time — remote or in-the-room (which per D-15 is a physics question, not a preference),
paid or split-based, genre, commitment level. The "not open to unpaid" / "split-based only" collision is
the most common real mismatch in music collaboration, and the market should surface it before contact
rather than after a wasted exchange (`03.03-collaborator-discovery-matchmaking-cx.md#CX-02`).

Freshness is the hard part. Per DT-01, an expiring signal is the only honest design.

### Happy Path

1. User declares an appetite for a role, with scope qualifiers
2. System records it with a freshness horizon
3. The signal becomes a scoring input (03.03.02) and is matched against open calls (03.03.04)
4. As the horizon approaches, the user is prompted to confirm or let it lapse — one tap, in a surface they already open

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Signal goes stale — set 8 months ago, year now full | The ghost problem: every match containing them is a false positive, and two dead leads teach the demand side to distrust the whole market | Expiry (DT-01). This is the feature's primary failure mode, not an edge case |
| User sets every appetite to maximise inbound | The "open to everything" spammer degrades the market's precision for everyone | See DT-03 — appetite breadth should probably cost something |
| Signal contradicts booking calendar (open to gigs, calendar full) | Two systems disagreeing about the same person | Calendar (16/17) is authoritative on *time*; this is authoritative on *appetite*. They are different facts and should not be reconciled — but the matcher should read both |
| User forgets they set a signal and gets contacted | Mild annoyance, or a bad look if the approach was serious | Confirmation prompts before expiry keep the signal conscious |
| Signal is set but reachability policy (03.04.04) blocks everyone | Advertising availability while being unreachable | Should be surfaced as a contradiction to the user — this is a real self-inflicted dead end |
| Producer's signal never updates because they live in a DAW | Per personas.md, the predictable outcome for this persona | DT-01 + DT-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Profile/settings opening | Skeleton |
| Empty | No signals declared | Not matchable on appetite — and per DT-02 this should be the *default* rather than an opt-out |
| Populated | Signals active | Signals with freshness shown |
| Error | Write failed | Retry |
| Expiring | Freshness horizon approaching | Prompt to confirm or lapse |
| Lapsed | Horizon passed without confirmation | Signal is inactive; scoring stops reading it. User is told it lapsed rather than discovering it silently |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Open-to status is a profile field the user maintains | The obvious design; how every platform does it | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **decaying claim**, and it is the only input in this domain that nothing in the world falsifies. A credit is fixed by a past event; a calendar is corrected by a booking; an appetite set in January is simply wrong by June and nothing tells anyone. Per personas.md, neither the Producer (in a DAW) nor the Musician (running a portfolio of gigs) will tend a form. Therefore signals must **expire by default** and require a cheap refresh. This is the single most important call in this feature: an unexpiring signal set makes the whole matcher untrustworthy within one year, and an untrusted matcher is not used. |
| DT-02 | An expert would expect this to be opt-**in**, defaulting to silent | Adversarial reasoning about the Producer/Operator inbound flood (03.01.02 DT-01) | ✅ CONFIRMED: defaulting people to "open" maximises marketplace liquidity and destroys the experience of the exact personas whose presence makes the platform valuable. A Producer defaulted to "open to mix work" gets the "check out my beat" flood on day one and leaves. Liquidity gained by default-on is fake liquidity — it is people who did not choose to be there, and their non-response teaches the demand side that the market is dead. |
| DT-03 | Appetite breadth should be unlimited | Naive | ⏳ DEFERRED to Step 5: a user open to everything is either genuinely versatile (the multi-hyphenate, which is the *core persona* — personas.md explicitly warns against penalising simultaneity) or gaming inbound. These are indistinguishable from the signal alone, and penalising breadth would penalise the persona this product is built for. Possible discriminator: breadth is credible when the credit graph evidences it across those roles. Recorded as Q-02 rather than guessed. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Signals expire by default and require cheap refresh | DT-01 — the only input nothing falsifies; without expiry the matcher is untrustworthy within a year. |
| D-02 | Signals are opt-in, defaulting to silent | DT-02 — default-on liquidity is fake liquidity and burns the Producer persona immediately. |
| D-03 | Appetite is per-role and multiple, never a single status | personas.md — the multi-hyphenate's roles are simultaneous; one status reproduces the "artist OR producer?" failure. |
| D-04 | Appetite (here) and calendar availability (16/17) are different facts and are not reconciled | An Operator's booked Tuesday is a hard resource fact; an appetite is not. Merging them would let a matcher treat a booking as a preference. |
