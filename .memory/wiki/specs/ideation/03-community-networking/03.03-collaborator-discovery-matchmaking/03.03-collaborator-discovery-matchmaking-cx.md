## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Open-To Signals ↔ Match Scoring

**Relationship**: The signal is the only declaration of *appetite* the platform has. Credits (domain 02)
prove what someone has done; open-to status says what they want to do next, which is frequently different
— the session drummer who wants to co-write, the mixer who is done taking mix work. The scorer needs both
axes: capability (evidenced, from the graph) and appetite (declared, from the signal). The hazard is that
appetite decays fast and silently. A musician who set "open to co-writes" eight months ago and has since
filled their year is now a false positive poisoning every match containing them, and unlike a credit there
is no external event that falsifies the signal.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona this pair exists for. Per personas.md their roles are simultaneous, so they hold several concurrent open-to signals with different appetites ("open to session work, not to unpaid co-writes") — a single availability boolean fails them immediately, exactly as a single role selector would.
- **Producer**: declares session-owner appetite ("taking mix work in Q4"). Per personas.md they live in a DAW and will not maintain a web form — so decay handling (DT-01 on 03.03.03) is what determines whether their signal is ever true.
- **Operator**: config-level only — their availability is a calendar in domain 16/17, which is a different mechanism with hard resource constraints. Conflating "open to collaborate" with "the room is free Tuesday" would be a category error.
- **Fan**: no access (D-05).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The signal is owned by 03.03.03; the scorer reads it. No contested write. But the *interpretation* is contested — the scorer must know how to discount an aging signal, which means decay policy is a shared concern with no obvious owner.
2. **Trigger chain**: Setting a signal makes someone matchable; expiry makes them unmatchable. If expiry fails silently, the market fills with ghosts — the failure mode that kills two-sided markets, because the demand side stops trusting the results after two dead leads.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — reachability policy (03.04.04) governs whether a match result can actually be contacted. A high-scoring match you cannot reach is worse than no match: it is a visible dead end.
4. **Notification fan-out**: If matching is push (parent Q-04), a new signal could trigger notifications to open calls that now match — turning a passive declaration into an active broadcast the declarer did not ask for.
5. **State transition conflict**: A signal can flip while a call is being matched against it. The consistency cost is low (a stale match is a minor annoyance) and eventual consistency is acceptable here — unlike the split-capture flows elsewhere in the platform.

### CX-02: Open Calls ↔ Open-To Signals

**Relationship**: This is the market clearing. A call declares a need ("vocalist, indie-folk, split-based,
remote OK"); a signal declares an appetite ("open to co-writes, remote, no unpaid work"). They must speak
the **same vocabulary** or matching degenerates into text search — which is why parent Q-03 (who owns the
skill taxonomy) is not a technical footnote but the precondition for this pair working at all. The
"no unpaid work" / "split-based" collision above is the common case, not an edge case: it is the single
most frequent mismatch in real music collaboration and the market should surface it before contact, not
after a wasted exchange.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: occupies both sides routinely — posts a call for their own track in the morning, answers someone else's in the afternoon. Per personas.md this simultaneity is the persona's defining structural fact, so any UX asking "are you looking or offering?" fails.
- **Producer**: mostly the demand side (they run sessions and need contributors), but per D-02 the moment money and a selection process enter, this belongs to domain 04 — a boundary the Producer will not perceive and will cross constantly.
- **Operator / Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: No shared entity — a call and a signal are independent objects joined only at match time.
2. **Trigger chain**: Publishing a call triggers matching against the signal set. Failure is retryable and non-critical. If matching is push, publishing also triggers notification fan-out to matched parties.
3. **Permission intersection**: Reachability (03.04.04) gates responses; a call from a stranger with no graph path may be filtered before it reaches anyone.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Yes — and this is the pair's main abuse surface. A call that notifies everyone matching "vocalist" is a broadcast spam tool wearing a matchmaking costume. Volume limits are load-bearing.
5. **State transition conflict**: A call filled while responses are in flight — late responders must be told, or the market teaches people that responding is pointless.

### CX-03: Match Scoring ↔ Search & Browse

**Relationship**: Scoring is what makes search something other than a directory. The domain overview names
directories as the repeated failure, and the reason is that in music everyone claims every skill, so an
unranked list of self-declared "producers" is noise. Scoring against the verified collaboration graph
(D-03) is what makes a result list mean something. In the other direction, search is where scoring becomes
visible and therefore auditable — and gameable (DT-03 on 03.03.02).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: read-only consumers of the score; they never set it, and per DT-02 on 03.03.02 they should probably never see the number.
- **Operator**: read-only browse of people, but their real discovery need is acts to book — domain 04/17, not here.
- **Fan**: no access (D-05).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — search reads scores, scoring owns them.
2. **Trigger chain**: A search triggers scoring per candidate. At scale this is the load problem routed to `/create-prd-architecture` (2-hop graph traversal per candidate per query on Postgres).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — results must respect reachability (03.04.04) and blocks (domain 24). A blocked party must not appear at all, not appear-and-fail.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None — search is pull-only. This is what makes it the safe half of the pair.
5. **State transition conflict**: Scores drift as the graph changes; result sets are non-repeatable between queries. Acceptable — but "I saw someone yesterday and can't find them today" is a real complaint that needs a saved-results answer.
