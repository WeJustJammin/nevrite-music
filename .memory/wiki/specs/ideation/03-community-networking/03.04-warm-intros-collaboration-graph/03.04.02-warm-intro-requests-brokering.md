# Feature: Warm Intro Requests & Brokering

> **Number**: 03.04.02
> **Parent**: [Warm Intros & the Collaboration Graph](./03.04-warm-intros-collaboration-graph-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The product built on the graph: "you and X have four records together — would you introduce me?" What makes
this work where LinkedIn's equivalent does not is that the platform can **state the relationship's basis**.
The broker cannot plausibly claim not to know the target, and the requester can pick the strongest path
rather than the shortest. Per D-04 on the parent, intros are double opt-in: the broker consents before the
target is ever told anyone is asking.

This is also the sanctioned door through the reachability wall (`03.04-warm-intros-collaboration-graph-cx.md#CX-03`),
which makes it load-bearing for keeping deals on-platform.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Requests intros to people they need — a booker, a producer, a label contact. Per personas.md their central pain is "every new client requires re-proving who they are"; a warm intro substitutes a trusted party's credibility for a cold pitch. They also broker for others, which is how the graph's value circulates rather than being extracted. |
| Producer | Full | The **most-asked broker** on the platform, by construction: per personas.md they run the sessions, so they sit at the centre of the graph. They did not sign up to be an introduction service, and per personas.md they live in a DAW — so every intro request is an interruption of paid work. DT-03 (broker fatigue) is therefore structural, not incidental. |
| Operator | Full | Brokers between acts, promoters and engineers locally — per personas.md they know everyone in the scene, and their edges come from bookings (03.04.01 DT-03). They request intros to acts they want to book. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent Role Matrix). Per personas.md, an open contact surface to artists is explicitly named as an anti-persona vector ("harasses artists through open contact surfaces"). A Fan's route to an artist is the follow and domain 20, not a professional intro. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The flow, per D-04, is **three-party and strictly ordered**:

1. Requester finds a path (03.04.01) and picks a broker from the candidates, seeing each path's evidence
2. Requester asks the **broker** — not the target. The target is not contacted and learns nothing
3. Broker decides. They can decline silently, and the target never knows the request existed
4. Only on broker consent is the target approached, carrying the broker's vouch

The ordering is the design. An intro that reaches the target before the broker agrees is a cold contact
with extra steps, and it spends the broker's relationship without their permission.

verbatim, writes their own, or merely opens a channel is unresolved (Q-02).

### Happy Path

1. Requester views a target they cannot reach directly (03.04.04) and sees candidate paths with evidence
2. Requester selects a broker and states what they want — briefly, and per DT-04 the ask must be specific
3. Broker is notified with the request, the requester's evidenced credits, and their own path to the target
4. Broker consents; the target receives the intro carrying the broker's vouch
5. The target responds or does not — and per D-02 below, a non-response must be a clean, blameless outcome

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Broker declines | Must be silent to the target and low-cost to the broker | The target never knows. The requester is told "not proceeding" without a reason — a reason would make declining socially expensive, and an expensive decline becomes a non-decline (DT-03) |
| Broker ignores it | The default outcome for a busy Producer | Expiry, framed as neutral. An unbounded queue of guilt is how the broker stops opening the surface entirely |
| Broker fatigue — a central Producer gets 40 requests a week | Structural certainty for the most valuable persona (DT-03) | Volume limits on requests *toward* a broker, not only from a requester |
| Path is stale — one session in 2009 | The broker barely remembers the target; the intro is a cold ask wearing a warm costume | Decay (Q-03 on 03.04.01). Stale paths burn brokers faster than anything else |
| Target's wall rises mid-brokering | The intro should still complete — the broker's consent is the authority (`#CX-03`) | Completes |
| Requester spams brokers instead of targets | The wall's own bypass becomes the spam channel (`#CX-03` synthesis Q2) | Intro requests need their own limits — the exemption is the attack surface |
| The intro succeeds and the parties take the deal off-platform | Per personas.md, named anti-persona behaviour ("finds collaborators then takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees") | Unresolved. Note the platform should not fight this with hostility — per problem-statement.md Q-02 the lock-in must be *earned*, not hostile |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Path/broker resolution | Skeleton |
| Empty | No path to the target | "No route" — with the honest alternative being a direct approach if their wall permits |
| Requested | Sent to broker | Pending; requester sees only that it is pending |
| Brokered | Broker consented | Target approached with the vouch |
| Declined | Broker declined | Requester told, no reason; target never involved |
| Expired | Broker did not act | Neutral close — no blame framing to any party |
| Error | Write failed | Retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Warm intros are valuable because they are warm | The obvious framing; the domain overview | ✅ CONFIRMED but sharpened: warmth is not the mechanism — **evidence** is. LinkedIn intros fail because the broker often barely knows the target and both parties know it, so the vouch carries no information. Here the platform states "you played on 4 records together", which is checkable. The feature's value is exactly proportional to the graph's evidentiary quality, which means it inherits every defect of 03.04.01 — including being empty at cold start (DT-01 there). An intro feature shipped before credits have volume is a feature with nothing to broker. |
| DT-02 | The target should be told someone wants an intro, so they can opt in | Seems respectful; maximises the target's agency | ❌ REJECTED → became parent D-04. Telling the target *is* the contact — the wall is breached the moment they read "Rob wants to meet you", and the requester has achieved reach without the broker's consent. Worse, it spends the broker's relationship without asking: the target now associates the broker with an unsolicited approach. Double opt-in with the broker first is the only ordering where a decline is free and invisible, and a free decline is what keeps brokers participating. |
| DT-03 | Broker fatigue is a risk to monitor | Product-management framing | ❌ REJECTED as a mischaracterisation — it is a **structural certainty**, not a risk. The graph's most central nodes are, by construction, the Producers who run the most sessions (03.04.01 Role Lens). Centrality earned by work means the hardest-working people receive the most interruptions, and per personas.md every interruption pulls them out of a DAW where they are being paid. Left alone, the mechanism systematically punishes the platform's most valuable persona in proportion to their value — and their exit removes the provenance capture point (D-18). Fatigue management is therefore a **core requirement of the feature**, not a setting: caps on inbound requests, one-tap declines, and a decline that costs nothing socially. |
| DT-04 | An expert would expect an intro to require a **specific ask**, not "I'd love to connect" | Reasoning about what makes a broker able to say yes | ✅ CONFIRMED: a broker can only vouch for something specific. "Introduce me to X" gives them nothing to say and makes them the author of an awkward message; "I'm looking for a mixer for a folk record in November and X's work on Y is exactly it" writes their message for them. This is the same insight as DT-02 on 03.01.02 (mandatory context) and for the same reason: the person being asked to act needs enough to decide in seconds. The pattern recurring twice in one domain suggests it is a domain-level principle — recorded in the domain CX. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Double opt-in, broker first — the target is never contacted before broker consent | Parent D-04; DT-02. The only ordering where a decline is free and invisible. |
| D-02 | Declines are silent, reasonless, and blameless; ignored requests expire neutrally | DT-03 — a socially expensive decline becomes a non-decline, and the broker stops opening the surface. |
| D-03 | Intro requests carry a mandatory specific ask | DT-04 — a broker can only vouch for something specific; vagueness makes them author the awkwardness. |
| D-04 | Broker inbound volume is capped | DT-03 — fatigue is structural; without a cap the mechanism punishes the most valuable persona in proportion to their value. |
