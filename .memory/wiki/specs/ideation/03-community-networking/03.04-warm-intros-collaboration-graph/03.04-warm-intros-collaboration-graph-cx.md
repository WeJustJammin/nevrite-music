## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Graph & Path Finding ↔ Warm Intro Brokering

**Relationship**: An intro request is not "please introduce me to X" — it is "you and X have three sessions
together, would you introduce me". The path finder produces the candidate brokers and, critically, the
*evidence* for each ("you played on 4 records with them"). That evidence is what distinguishes this from
LinkedIn's intro feature, where the broker frequently barely knows the target and everyone knows it. Here
the platform can state the relationship's basis, so a broker cannot plausibly claim not to know the person,
and the requester can choose the strongest path rather than the shortest.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the primary requester. Per personas.md their pain is "every new client requires re-proving who they are" — a warm path is the cheapest possible proof, because it substitutes someone else's credibility for a cold pitch.
- **Producer**: the highest-value **broker**. Per personas.md they are a trust broker whose "attestation is worth more than a self-claim" — and per the multi-hyphenate structure they sit at the centre of many sessions, which makes them the shortest path to most people. This means they will be asked constantly, which makes DT-03 on 03.04.02 (broker fatigue) a structural certainty, not a risk.
- **Operator**: both — they broker between acts and know everyone locally, and they request intros to acts they want to book.
- **Fan**: no access. Per personas.md an open contact surface to artists is explicitly an anti-persona vector ("harasses artists through open contact surfaces").

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The graph is read-only here — it is derived from domain 02 and nothing in this sub-domain writes it. No conflict, and that is a deliberate property: an intro must never be able to manufacture a graph edge, or the graph becomes forgeable.
2. **Trigger chain**: Requesting an intro reads the path, then notifies the broker. If the graph is stale (a credit was disputed after derivation), the path may cite a relationship that is no longer evidenced — so path evidence must be resolved at request time, not cached from an earlier render.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — the broker's own reachability policy (CX-03) governs whether the *requester* can reach the *broker* to ask. An intro request is itself an inbound contact and is subject to the same wall.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The broker is notified; per D-04 the target learns nothing until the broker consents. This ordering is the whole design.
5. **State transition conflict**: A path can vanish mid-request if the underlying credit is disputed (domain 02/24). The intro should fail closed rather than proceed on a path that no longer exists.

### CX-02: Graph & Path Finding ↔ Reachability Policy

**Relationship**: This is the same computation serving two purposes, and recognising that is what makes the
reachability feature cheap to build. "Is this person within 2 hops of me?" is a path query; "may this person
DM me?" is the same query with a policy threshold attached. The consequence is that reachability inherits
every one of the graph's defects: if the graph is empty at cold start (Q-04 on the parent — the veteran with
0 edges), then a 2-hop reachability wall makes the platform unusable on day one for everyone, because nobody
is within 2 hops of anybody. **Reachability cannot ship before the graph is dense**, which is a sequencing
fact with direct MoSCoW consequences.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer / Operator**: the personas the wall exists for — structurally inbound-flooded because everyone wants access to the person who runs the session or books the room.
- **Musician**: on both sides — they want to reach people and they want to not be flooded. Per personas.md they are "not a star", so their wall should be low; a working musician who walls themselves off loses work.
- **Fan**: config-level. Per personas.md they are the population that produces the harassment risk toward artists, and the wall is what protects the artist without removing the Fan's follow relationship.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — both read the same derived graph.
2. **Trigger chain**: Every inbound contact attempt (DM, connection request, call response) triggers a path query. This makes graph traversal a **hot path on every message send**, not a background job — the single largest architecture consequence of this sub-domain, routed to `/create-prd-architecture`.
3. **Permission intersection**: This *is* the permission intersection. It is the mechanism by which the graph governs the messaging cross-cut, the connection request flow (03.01.02), and call responses (03.03.04).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A blocked contact attempt should be silent to the sender, or the wall becomes a probe for mapping someone's network.
5. **State transition conflict**: The graph changes as credits are confirmed, so reachability changes silently underneath users. Someone reachable yesterday may not be today. This is acceptable but must never produce a *partial* send.

### CX-03: Reachability Policy ↔ Warm Intro Brokering

**Relationship**: The wall and the door. A high reachability wall is only tolerable because a legitimate
route exists through it — and per personas.md the alternative is worse than spam: the anti-persona "uses the
platform to find collaborators then takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees". A wall with no door does
not stop contact; it moves contact to Instagram DMs, where the platform sees nothing, captures no
provenance, and earns no fee. The intro is what keeps the approach on-platform, which means this pair is
directly load-bearing for the business model, not merely for user experience.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer / Operator**: set high walls, receive intro requests as the sanctioned inbound.
- **Musician**: uses the door constantly; per personas.md their switching trigger includes "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin" — the door is also the acquisition channel.
- **Fan**: no door (no intro access) — deliberately. The Fan's route to an artist is the follow and domain 20's D2F surfaces, not a professional intro.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — policy is config, intro is a request object.
2. **Trigger chain**: An intro request must be exempt from the wall it is designed to bypass, or the feature cannot function. But that exemption is itself the attack surface: if "request an intro" is unwalled, it *becomes* the spam channel, and the spammer simply addresses their pitch to the broker instead. Intro requests therefore need their own volume limits (DT-03 on 03.04.02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — the requester must be able to reach the *broker*, even if they cannot reach the target. Reachability is evaluated against the broker, not the target.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The broker is notified; the target only after consent (D-04).
5. **State transition conflict**: A target's wall can rise mid-brokering. The intro should still complete — the broker's consent is the authority at that point, and an intro that fails because a setting changed while it was in flight would be inexplicable to all three parties.

## Rejected Pairs

| R-01 | 03.04.02 Warm Intro Brokering | 03.04.01 Graph (as a writer) | Considered whether a successful intro should create a graph edge — the platform observed a real connection form, so why not record it? Rejected, and the reason is load-bearing: per D-02 the graph's entire value is that it is **derived from evidenced work**, not from social events. An intro proves two people were introduced, not that they collaborated. Admitting intro-edges would make the graph forgeable by the cheapest possible action (introduce yourself to yourself via a colluding broker) and would reintroduce the "accept?"-click worthlessness the domain exists to escape. If the intro leads to a session, domain 02 will record it — through evidence, as it should. |
| R-02 | 03.04.03 Referral Requests | 03.04.04 Reachability Policy | Considered whether a referral should raise the referred party's reachability toward the referrer's network — a "vouched-in" pass. Rejected for this pass: it makes reachability socially transitive and therefore farmable (collect referrals to unlock access to a network), and it converts a recommendation about *competence* into a permission about *access*, which are different claims. The broker vouching for your mixing ability has not agreed to hand you their contact list. |
