# Feature: Local Jam & Open Mic Discovery

> **Number**: 03.09
> **Parent**: [Community & Networking](./community-networking-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Finding the recurring, informal, turn-up-and-play events in a city — the Tuesday jazz jam, the Sunday open
mic, the monthly beat cypher. These are how a local scene physically reproduces itself: they are where
musicians who have never met end up playing together, which is the event that eventually becomes a
collaboration edge (03.04.01). They are also the single most under-served category in music software —
they currently live on a chalkboard, a Facebook group, and word of mouth.

**Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain**: one capability — discover recurring informal events near me.
Per DT-02 the harder half (the recurring-event model) is a gap in domain 17, not a second capability here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds jams to play at — and per personas.md this is the persona for whom this matters most: a working musician new to a city has no network, and the jam is the standard, centuries-old mechanism for acquiring one. It is also where a musician between paid work keeps playing. Per DT-01 this is the feature that best serves the newcomer the rest of the domain structurally buries. Filters by radius/scene, genre, standard (pro vs open), and sign-up requirement; sees which scene contacts usually attend. |
| Producer | Full | Lower relevance — per personas.md their work happens in a DAW and a jam is not a session. They may attend to find players, which is a real but secondary use: it is scouting, and it is how a producer meets the drummer they later hire. Same screen as the Musician, filtered toward instrument/genre they need. |
| Operator | Full | **The supply side.** Per personas.md their inventory is perishable ("an unsold Tuesday is gone forever") — a recurring jam night is precisely how a room monetises a dead Tuesday, and it costs them almost nothing to run. Listing their jam here is directly in their interest, which makes them a willing data source rather than a reluctant one. They create/edit their own jam listing, set the informal attributes (backline, sign-up, standard, genre), and confirm it is still running to keep it out of the Stale state (D-01). |
| Fan | Full | Per D-11 fans are first-class users; an open mic is genuinely a fan-attendable event and per personas.md their success criterion is not missing local live music. This is one of the few features where the Fan and the Musician want the same screen for different reasons — the Fan wants to watch, the Musician wants to play. Fan sees the same discovery view; the "standard" attribute (see D-02) tells them whether it is a spectator-friendly open mic or a musicians-only jam. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Discovery of recurring informal events by geography and time — "what's on near me this week that I could
turn up and play at". The essential attributes are the informal ones nobody models, and per D-02 they ARE
the feature rather than decoration:

- **Backline** — is there a house kit / amps / PA, or do I bring everything?
- **Sign-up** — turn up and play, list on the night, or sign up in advance?
- **Standard** — pro jam, all-comers open mic, or beginner-friendly? (the pro/open boundary of the Fan edge case)
- **Genre** — jazz, blues, hip-hop cypher, singer-songwriter, etc.
- **Cadence** — weekly / monthly / date pattern, and the next occurrence.

A listing without these is a chalkboard photo. Discovery is scoped either to the user's scene (03.06.01) or a
geographic radius, sorted by next occurrence, and every listing carries a freshness signal (see States) so a
stale night is never silently presented as fact (D-01).

Per DT-02 the underlying object — a **recurring event with no booking** — does not obviously exist in
domain 17, whose model is one-off bookings with money attached. **This feature discovers the recurring event;
it does not model it (D-03).** Where that entity lives — here, 16, or 17 — is the feature's structural
question, recorded as Q-01 and deferred to `/ideate-validate`; it does not block the discovery behavior
specified above.

### Happy Path

1. User opens local discovery, scoped to their scene (03.06.01) or a chosen radius
2. Recurring jams and open mics render, sorted by next occurrence, each showing the informal attributes
   (backline, sign-up, standard, genre, cadence) that decide whether to go
3. User filters — e.g. "open standard, this week, within my scene, has backline"
4. User sees who else from their scene is going or usually goes (social proof drawn from 03.06)
5. User opens a listing: full attributes, next occurrence, location, and its freshness signal
6. They turn up and play — and per DT-03 the platform's opportunity is what happens *after* that

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Listing is stale — not confirmed recently** | The chalkboard problem: informal events change without announcement, and stale listings send people to empty pubs. This is the feature's core data-freshness risk. Never shown silently as fact (D-01). | Listing is badged **"Unconfirmed — last confirmed {date}"**, de-emphasised and sorted below confirmed listings; a "Still running? / Moved?" prompt is offered to anyone who knows. |
| **Jam exists but nobody has listed it** | The common case at launch — jam data is far harder to source than venue data (Q-03). | Empty or thin results for the area, with an **"Add a jam you know about"** CTA and, for Operators, "List your night" (they are the willing source per Role Lens). |
| **Recurring event has an exception** (no jam on bank holidays; venue closed) | The mundane complexity of recurrence that every calendar underestimates. Whoever owns the entity (Q-01) inherits the full recurrence-exception model. | Next occurrence reflects the exception where known; deep exception handling is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / /write-be-spec]`. At Level 1 the honest behavior is: show next occurrence, allow a one-off "cancelled this week" note. |
| **Fan turns up to a musicians-only jam** | The pro/open boundary is social and unwritten; getting it wrong is embarrassing for everyone. The "standard" attribute (D-02) must be honest enough for someone to say "this is not for beginners". | The **standard** badge (Pro / Open / Beginner-friendly) is shown prominently on every listing and in filters, so a Fan or newcomer self-selects before travelling. |
| **Musician new to a city** | The exact user this feature exists for (DT-01) — no network, no credits, invisible to every evidence-gated mechanism in the domain. | A working discovery view scoped to a radius even with an empty scene graph; the jam is the on-ramp that requires no platform permission. |
| **Jam is listed by someone who does not run it** | Stale/wrong info attributed to a venue that never agreed — the same claiming problem as 03.06.04. | Unverified listings are marked as such; the Operator can **claim** their night to become the authoritative editor (mirrors 03.06.04's claiming pattern). |
| **Two users report the same jam as moved/dead at once** (concurrency) | Conflicting confirmations arrive close together; the listing must not flip-flop. | Latest confirmation wins for the freshness date; conflicting "moved" reports surface the listing as **Unconfirmed** pending an Operator/owner resolution rather than silently trusting one report. |
| **Spam / abusive listing** (fake jam, promotional dumping) | Open contributor input invites abuse — a moderation concern shared with domain 24. | Reporting affordance on every listing; unverified listings carry less weight in ranking. Full moderation policy is `[PENDING — cross-cut with 24 Trust & Safety]`. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Discovery opening or filter applied | Skeleton list of listing cards |
| Empty | No jams in the chosen scene/radius | Honest empty state (common at launch) with "Add a jam" CTA; Operators see "List your night" (per Role Lens, they are the fix) |
| Populated | One or more jams found | Listings sorted by next occurrence, each with backline / sign-up / standard / genre badges and a freshness signal |
| Unconfirmed | Listing not confirmed within the freshness window (proposed 30 days; exact window `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`) | Badged "Unconfirmed — last confirmed {date}", de-emphasised, sorted below confirmed listings (D-01, consistent with 03.06.04 D-02) |
| Error | Discovery fetch failed | Error message with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a minor discovery feature | Its position in a 16-candidate list | ❌ REJECTED: it is disproportionately important because of **who it serves**. Every other mechanism in this domain is evidence-gated — matching (03.03.02 D-01), the graph (03.04.01), reachability (03.04.04), referrals (03.04.03 D-01). A musician with no credits is invisible to all of them (03.03.01 DT-02, the rich-get-richer defect). A jam is the opposite: you turn up, you play, and everyone hears whether you can. It is the oldest, cheapest, least gameable credentialing mechanism in music, and it requires no platform permission. Along with contests (03.08.02 DT-02), it is one of only two on-ramps this domain has for the uncredited — and unlike contests it costs the platform nothing to enable, because the events already exist. |
| DT-02 | The recurring event belongs to domain 17 (Live Booking) | The natural home for events | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's real structural finding: **domain 17's model does not fit**. Per personas.md the Operator's booking flow is "enquiries → holds → confirms → takes deposit → advances → settles" — a one-off transaction with money. A jam is a *standing arrangement* with no booking, no deposit, no settlement, frequently no fee, and an open-ended cast of whoever turns up. It is closer to a venue attribute ("this room has a jam on Tuesdays", domain 16) than to a booking. The recurring-no-money event is a genuine gap that neither 16 nor 17 obviously owns, and it should be raised with them rather than silently modelled here. Recorded as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **the jam is where a collaboration edge is born, and the platform is not there for it** | Reasoning against D-18's "be in the room" thesis | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the thesis-relevant insight. Per problem-statement.md the root cause is "absence at the point of truth" — and a jam is a point of truth: strangers play together, and some of those pairings become bands, sessions and records. The platform has no way to know it happened. The obvious move — "check in at this jam" — is a consumer-social mechanic with a well-earned reputation for being ignored, and per personas.md this is not a persona that performs for an app. A weaker, more honest version: the jam's *organiser* (usually the Operator, who has an interest — DT-02's Role Lens) records who played, which is the same counter-attestation pattern that makes credits trustworthy (domain 02) with a much lower bar. That would make a jam a credential-generating event and would connect this feature to the graph. Speculative; recorded as Q-02, not designed here. |
| DT-04 | Freshness can be solved by asking the platform to verify listings | The obvious "just keep data fresh" reflex | ❌ REJECTED: there is no authoritative source to verify against — that is the whole problem (jam data lives on chalkboards, Q-03). Freshness is therefore a *social* mechanism, not a data-integrity one: it comes from the people with an interest — the Operator confirming their own night, and attendees flagging "still running / moved". This is why D-01's "mark unconfirmed, never assert as fact" is the correct posture rather than a fallback: the honest signal is the feature, and it reuses the exact claiming/confirmation pattern of 03.06.04 rather than inventing a new one. |

## Decisions
