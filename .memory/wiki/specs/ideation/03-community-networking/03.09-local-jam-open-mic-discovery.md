# Feature: Local Jam & Open Mic Discovery

> **Number**: 03.09
> **Parent**: [Community & Networking](./community-networking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Finding the recurring, informal, turn-up-and-play events in a city — the Tuesday jazz jam, the Sunday open
mic, the monthly beat cypher. These are how a local scene physically reproduces itself: they are where
musicians who have never met end up playing together, which is the event that eventually becomes a
collaboration edge (03.04.01). They are also the single most under-served category in music software —
they currently live on a chalkboard, a Facebook group, and word of mouth.

**Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain**: one capability — discover recurring informal events near me.
Per DT-02 the harder half (the recurring-event model) is a gap in domain 17, not a second capability here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds jams to play at — and per personas.md this is the persona for whom this matters most: a working musician new to a city has no network, and the jam is the standard, centuries-old mechanism for acquiring one. It is also where a musician between paid work keeps playing. Per DT-01 this is the feature that best serves the newcomer the rest of the domain structurally buries. |
| Producer | Full | Lower relevance — per personas.md their work happens in a DAW and a jam is not a session. They may attend to find players, which is a real but secondary use: it is scouting, and it is how a producer meets the drummer they later hire. |
| Operator | Full | **The supply side.** Per personas.md their inventory is perishable ("an unsold Tuesday is gone forever") — a recurring jam night is precisely how a room monetises a dead Tuesday, and it costs them almost nothing to run. Listing their jam here is directly in their interest, which makes them a willing data source rather than a reluctant one. |
| Fan | Full | Per D-11 fans are first-class users; an open mic is genuinely a fan-attendable event and per personas.md their success criterion is not missing local live music. This is one of the few features where the Fan and the Musician want the same screen for different reasons — the Fan wants to watch, the Musician wants to play. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Discovery of recurring informal events by geography and time — "what's on near me this week that I could
turn up and play at". The essential attributes are the informal ones nobody models: is there a backline?
Do I need to sign up in advance or turn up at 8? What's the standard — is this a pro jam or a beginner
open mic? Is it my genre?

Per DT-02 the underlying object — a **recurring event with no booking** — does not obviously exist in
domain 17, whose model is one-off bookings with money attached.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — where the recurring-event entity lives (here, 16, or 17)
is unresolved and is the feature's structural question.

### Happy Path

1. User opens local discovery, scoped to their scene (03.06.01) or a radius
2. Recurring jams and open mics render with the informal attributes that decide whether to go
3. User sees who else from their scene is going or usually goes
4. They turn up and play — and per DT-03 the platform's opportunity is what happens *after* that

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **The jam is dead or has moved** | The chalkboard problem: informal events change without announcement, and stale listings send people to empty pubs | The feature's core data-freshness risk. Per personas.md the Operator's own anti-persona behaviour is "publishes a stale gear list" — the same decay, applied to a night |
| Jam exists but nobody has listed it | Most of them, at launch | Seeding (03.06.04) — but jam data is far harder to source than venue data |
| Recurring event with exceptions (no jam on bank holidays) | The mundane complexity of recurrence, which every calendar underestimates | `[PENDING]` — and per DT-02 whoever owns the entity inherits this |
| Fan turns up to a musicians-only jam | The pro/open boundary is social and unwritten, and getting it wrong is embarrassing for everyone | The "standard" attribute needs to be honest, which means someone has to be willing to say "this is not for beginners" |
| Musician new to a city | The exact user this feature exists for | See DT-01 |
| A jam is listed by someone who does not run it | Stale/wrong info attributed to a venue that never agreed | Same claiming problem as 03.06.04 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Discovery opening | Skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing local | Honest — and common; per personas.md the Operator is the one who can fix it by listing |
| Populated | Jams found | Listings with informal attributes |
| Stale | Listing not confirmed recently | Marked as unconfirmed, not silently shown as fact (consistent with 03.06.04 D-02) |
| Error | Fetch failed | Retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a minor discovery feature | Its position in a 16-candidate list | ❌ REJECTED: it is disproportionately important because of **who it serves**. Every other mechanism in this domain is evidence-gated — matching (03.03.02 D-01), the graph (03.04.01), reachability (03.04.04), referrals (03.04.03 D-01). A musician with no credits is invisible to all of them (03.03.01 DT-02, the rich-get-richer defect). A jam is the opposite: you turn up, you play, and everyone hears whether you can. It is the oldest, cheapest, least gameable credentialing mechanism in music, and it requires no platform permission. Along with contests (03.08.02 DT-02), it is one of only two on-ramps this domain has for the uncredited — and unlike contests it costs the platform nothing to enable, because the events already exist. |
| DT-02 | The recurring event belongs to domain 17 (Live Booking) | The natural home for events | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's real structural finding: **domain 17's model does not fit**. Per personas.md the Operator's booking flow is "enquiries → holds → confirms → takes deposit → advances → settles" — a one-off transaction with money. A jam is a *standing arrangement* with no booking, no deposit, no settlement, frequently no fee, and an open-ended cast of whoever turns up. It is closer to a venue attribute ("this room has a jam on Tuesdays", domain 16) than to a booking. The recurring-no-money event is a genuine gap that neither 16 nor 17 obviously owns, and it should be raised with them rather than silently modelled here. Recorded as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **the jam is where a collaboration edge is born, and the platform is not there for it** | Reasoning against D-18's "be in the room" thesis | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the thesis-relevant insight. Per problem-statement.md the root cause is "absence at the point of truth" — and a jam is a point of truth: strangers play together, and some of those pairings become bands, sessions and records. The platform has no way to know it happened. The obvious move — "check in at this jam" — is a consumer-social mechanic with a well-earned reputation for being ignored, and per personas.md this is not a persona that performs for an app. A weaker, more honest version: the jam's *organiser* (usually the Operator, who has an interest — DT-02's Role Lens) records who played, which is the same counter-attestation pattern that makes credits trustworthy (domain 02) with a much lower bar. That would make a jam a credential-generating event and would connect this feature to the graph. Speculative; recorded as Q-02, not designed here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Stale listings are marked unconfirmed, never shown silently as fact | Consistent with 03.06.04 D-02 and the domain-level rule about never overstating what is known. |
| D-02 | The informal attributes (backline, sign-up, standard, genre) are the feature, not decoration | They are what decides whether someone goes; a listing without them is a chalkboard photo. |
| D-03 | This feature does not model the recurring event itself — it discovers it | DT-02 — the entity's home is unresolved between 16 and 17 and should be settled there, not invented here. |
