# Feature: Scene Graph & Density Map

> **Number**: 03.06.02
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The collaboration graph (03.04.01) filtered to a scene's membership, and the density read that comes off
it. This is what makes "the Bristol scene" a measurable object rather than a phrase: 200 people who have
played together is a scene; 200 unconnected signups in the same postcode is a mailing list. Per the domain
overview, density is the platform's cold-start metric — and per D-18, measuring it in **evidenced
collaborations** rather than headcount is the thesis applied to community.

It is also, per DT-02, the only honest way for a user to answer "is this platform actually used where I am?"

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads their scene's density to judge whether it is worth their attention, and sees where they sit in it — who they are connected to locally, who they are two hops from. Per personas.md their pain is re-proving themselves to each new client; a dense local graph is the map of who already knows them by evidence. |
| Producer | Read-only | Reads density to find the local supply. Per personas.md they are structurally the most central node (03.04.01), so their own position in a scene graph is prominent — which is earned by sessions run, not by networking, and is exactly the property that makes it trustworthy. |
| Operator | Read-only | Density around their room is their **catchment** — commercially the most actionable read of this data on the platform. Per personas.md their inventory is perishable ("an unsold Tuesday is gone forever"), so knowing which acts and engineers are dense nearby is directly monetisable. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent Role Matrix). Consistent with 03.04.01 — a browsable map of who works with whom exposed to a consumer population is the same exposure the graph declines to make (03.04.01 Q-02), and per personas.md the Fan has no professional use for it. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The scene graph is a **derived view**, not a new graph: 03.04.01's edges, restricted to a scene's members.
Density is a read over that view. Per `03.06-scenes-communities-cx.md#CX-02` it inherits everything about
the underlying graph — including its cold-start emptiness (03.04.01 DT-01) and its sybil exposure
(03.04.01 DT-04), where a fake local ring would manufacture a fake dense scene.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the density metric itself is unresolved. Edge count,
connected-component size, average path length and clustering coefficient say different things, and per DT-01
the choice is a product decision because the number will be *shown to users*.

### Happy Path

1. User opens their scene
2. System filters the collaboration graph to the scene's membership
3. Density and the user's own position render — who they are connected to, who is central, how connected the scene is
4. User uses it to find people or to judge whether the scene is real

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Seeded scene with no real members** | The graph is empty even though the scene looks alive (03.06.04) — seeded venues and gigs are not collaboration edges | The honest gap in the seeding strategy: seeding makes a scene look populated, not *connected*. See DT-03 |
| Sybil ring manufactures local density | Per 03.04.01 DT-04, a ring of colluding accounts produces a dense fictional scene — and here it is *visible* as a compelling density number | The graph's integrity problem inherits directly; a corrupted scene graph is a corrupted growth metric too |
| Density is low and shown honestly | Per 03.06.01 DT-04 this is correct but discouraging | The tension is real: honesty about thinness may cost the signup that would have fixed it |
| Scene graph exposes who works with whom, at scale | The same competitive-intelligence exposure 03.04.01 Q-02 declines | Restricted to members; not a public map |
| Traversal cost of a whole scene's graph | Far heavier than a single path query | Routed to `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Two scenes overlap heavily (a city and its dominant genre) | Two views of nearly the same graph, reported as two densities | Cosmetic, but confusing if the numbers differ for unclear reasons |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Scene opening | Skeleton — this is an expensive read |
| Empty | No collaboration edges among members | Honest — and per DT-03 this is the normal state of a seeded scene |
| Populated | Edges exist | Density and position |
| Thin | Few edges | Shown as thin (03.06.01 D-04) |
| Error | Traversal failed | Degrade to membership-only view |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Density is a metric for the platform's operators | The natural reading — it is a growth KPI | ❌ REJECTED as the primary framing: it is a **user-facing** answer to the question every new user actually asks, which is "is anyone I care about here?". Per the domain overview the whole cold-start bet is that a local scene *feels alive*, and feeling is exactly what a density map either delivers or fails to. That makes the metric choice a product decision, not an analytics one — because the number is shown, and a number that says something a musician knows to be false about their own city destroys credibility instantly. It is also a growth KPI, secondarily. |
| DT-02 | Showing density is risky because low numbers discourage signups | The growth-team objection | ❌ REJECTED, and this is where 03.06.01 DT-04 gets its teeth: a musician **already knows** their city's scene. If WeJammin claims Bristol is thriving and they can see it is not, the platform has been caught lying about the one category of fact it exists to be trustworthy about (problem-statement.md: the alternative to reconstruction-from-memory). Honest thinness costs a signup; dishonest density costs the thesis. The asymmetry is not close. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would notice that nobody mentioned: **seeding populates a scene but cannot connect it** | Reasoning across `#CX-01` and this feature | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is an honest limit on the cold-start strategy that the domain overview does not acknowledge. Seeding (03.06.04) derives venues, gigs and locally-credited artists — those make a scene look *populated*. But collaboration edges come from **confirmed credits on this platform** (03.04.01 D-01), which no seeded scene has. So a seeded Bristol scene shows 60 venues, 400 gigs, and a density of zero. The two features tell contradictory stories about the same place on the same screen. Either the density map is hidden until real edges exist, or seeding must also derive *edges* from public credit data — which reintroduces unverified claims into the graph, the exact thing 03.04.01 D-01 forbids. This is a genuine unresolved conflict between two of this domain's own decisions, and it should be escalated rather than papered over. Recorded as Q-01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Density is user-facing first, a growth metric second | DT-01 — it answers the question every new user asks, and the number is shown. |
| D-02 | Density is measured in evidenced collaborations, never headcount | D-18 applied to community — headcount is the vanity metric that lets a dead scene look alive. |
| D-03 | Honest thinness over flattering fiction | DT-02 — a musician knows their own city; being caught inflating costs the thesis. |
