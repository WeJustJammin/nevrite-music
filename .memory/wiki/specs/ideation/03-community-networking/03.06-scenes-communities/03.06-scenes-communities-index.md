## Overview

The places music people actually gather, partitioned the way music actually partitions: by **city** (the
Bristol scene), by **genre** (UK drill), by **craft** (mastering engineers). Per the domain overview, local
scenes are "the only tractable cold-start strategy: 200 users in one city feels alive, 20,000 scattered
globally feels dead" — which makes this sub-domain not one feature among sixteen but the **mechanism by
which the entire platform becomes non-empty**.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: four interacting capabilities. Membership defines a scene's
population; the scene graph is that population's density made visible; stewardship governs it locally; and
seeding creates scenes *before* anyone joins so they are never empty on arrival. Seeding constrains
membership (you join something already alive); density determines whether stewardship is needed at all.

**Merged from three candidates**: `Local Scenes & Geo Communities` (09), `Local Scene Graph` (10) and
`Genre & Craft Scenes` (11) were one mechanism with three partition keys. Splitting them would have produced
three copies of the same membership, moderation and seeding machinery — see D-01.

**Deep Think addition**: `Scene Seeding, Claiming & Cold Start` (03.06.04) was **not** in the candidate
list, and it is what makes the domain overview's own cold-start claim true rather than aspirational.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Candidates 09, 10 and 11 merged — a scene is one mechanism with a **partition key** (geo / genre / craft) | They differ only in what defines membership. Splitting them triplicates membership, stewardship, seeding and moderation machinery for no behavioural gain — the same reasoning D-14 used to keep the three marketplaces *apart* (irreconcilable schemas) applied in reverse here, where the schemas are identical. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | Geo scenes are **primary**; genre and craft scenes are secondary | Per the domain overview, geography is the cold-start strategy. A genre scene has the same global-and-dead problem as the platform itself (UK drill producers scattered worldwide), so it inherits the disease rather than curing it. Geo is the only partition that produces local density, and density is what makes a scene feel alive. | Domain overview; D-15 (physical collaboration radius) |
| D-03 | Fans are **members** of geo scenes but not of craft scenes | Per D-11 fans are first-class users, and a city's music scene genuinely includes its audience — that is what a scene *is*. But per personas.md a Fan has no craft; a fan in "mastering engineers" is either lost or a nuisance. Genre scenes are the ambiguous middle — see Q-02. |
| D-04 | Scenes exist **before** anyone joins them (seeding, 03.06.04) | An empty scene is worse than no scene: it proves the platform is dead at exactly the moment a new user is deciding. Per the domain overview the cold-start claim depends on the scene feeling alive on arrival, which it cannot do if it starts at zero. | Deep Think, DT-01 on 03.06.04 |
| D-05 | Scene stewards are Musicians/Producers/Operators acting in a role — **not a fifth persona** | The sweep's provisional persona list included "scene organiser"; D-19 ratifies four personas. Per personas.md a scene organiser is a working musician or an Operator wearing another hat — consistent with the multi-hyphenate structural fact. | D-19; personas.md |
