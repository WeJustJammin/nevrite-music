# Feature: Scene Definition, Membership & Joining

> **Number**: 03.06.01
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What a scene *is* and who is in it. A scene is one mechanism with a **partition key** (D-01 on the parent):
geo (the Bristol scene), genre (UK drill), or craft (mastering engineers). Per D-02 geo is primary, because
geography is the only partition that produces local density, and per the domain overview density is the
whole cold-start strategy.

Membership is deliberately **overlapping and unranked** — a person is in several scenes at once, exactly as
the multi-hyphenate persona is several roles at once (personas.md). Any design forcing a primary scene
repeats the "artist OR producer?" failure in a new place.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Joins their city, their genres, their crafts — and per personas.md holds several simultaneously without ranking them. Their scene is where the cold-start problem is solved or not: per the domain overview, 200 people in their city feels alive and is what makes them return. A musician who plays three genres in two cities is in five scenes, and that is normal, not an edge case. |
| Producer | Full | Joins their city and craft scenes. Per personas.md they will not do setup work — so their scenes should be **inferred and offered**, not configured (DT-02). Their credits already say where they work and with whom. |
| Operator | Full | Joins their city scene, where they are structurally an anchor: per personas.md their room is where the scene physically happens. Per 03.06.04 they are likely already in the seed data before they arrive. |
| Fan | Full (geo only) | Per D-03 they are members of **geo** scenes — a city's music scene genuinely includes its audience, and per personas.md the Fan's need ("played their city") is exactly geo-shaped. Not craft scenes: a fan in "mastering engineers" is lost. Genre is the ambiguous middle (parent Q-02). Their volume (D-13a) means geo scenes are majority-fan by headcount, which is a moderation fact (D-13b) before it is a product one. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A scene has a partition key, a definition, and a membership. Joining is low-friction and reversible; per
D-04 on the parent the scene already exists and is already populated when you arrive (03.06.04).

Per parent Q-03 it is unresolved whether a scene is a *place* (its own feed and surface) or a *weighting*
(scene membership tilts the main feed). The cold-start claim probably needs a place — a weighting is
invisible, and an invisible scene cannot feel alive.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — geo boundary definition (parent Q-01) is genuinely
unresolved: London is one scene or twenty, and both answers are defensible.

### Happy Path

1. A new user's city scene already exists, seeded with venues, gigs and locally-credited artists (03.06.04)
2. System infers candidate scenes from their credits, location and stated genres, and offers them (DT-02)
3. User joins one or several; membership is immediate, reversible, and unranked
4. Scene content weights their feed and/or gives them a place to go (parent Q-03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Empty scene** | Per D-04 this must not happen — an empty scene proves the platform is dead at the worst possible moment | Seeding (03.06.04). This is the failure the sub-domain exists to prevent |
| Scene boundary disputes (is Croydon South London?) | Cultural arguments with no correct answer, and people care about them intensely | Parent Q-01. Overlapping membership defuses most of it — you can be in both |
| User moves city | Their old scene stays relevant (their network is there); their new one is empty of their connections | Membership is multiple and unranked, which handles this natively — but per DT-03 the *touring* case is different and harder |
| Genre scene is global and therefore dead | Per D-02 genre inherits the platform's own disease rather than curing it | Genre scenes are secondary; expectations set accordingly |
| Scene majority-fan by headcount (D-13a) | A professional scene where fans outnumber pros 100:1 is not a professional scene | Parent Q-02 — this is why the fan/genre question decides whether this is one product or two |
| Nobody joins anything | Per DT-02 the likeliest failure: joining is a chore nobody does | Inferred membership, offered not requested |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Scene opening | Skeleton |
| Seeded | Scene exists, no real members | Venues, gigs, locally-credited artists — alive-looking by construction |
| Populated | Real members | Members, activity, density (03.06.02) |
| Joined | User is a member | Scene content weights their feed / their place |
| Thin | Few members, low density | Honest — per DT-04, pretending is worse than admitting |
| Error | Fetch failed | Retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Geo, genre and craft scenes need separate designs | The sweep listed them as three candidates (09, 10, 11) | ❌ REJECTED → parent D-01. They differ only in the predicate defining membership; the machinery (join, leave, density, stewardship, moderation, seeding) is identical. Three designs means three implementations of one thing and three places for the moderation model to diverge. Note this is the *inverse* of D-14's marketplace reasoning: there, the schemas were irreconcilable so the merge was rejected; here the schemas are identical so the split is rejected. Same test, opposite answer — which is a good sign the test is real and not a rationalisation. |
| DT-02 | Users will join scenes | The premise of every community product | ❌ REJECTED as stated: joining is a chore, and per personas.md neither the Producer (in a DAW) nor the Musician (running a portfolio of gigs) does chores for a platform's benefit. But the platform **already knows**: their credits say what they play and with whom, their gigs say where, their location says which city. Scene membership should be **inferred and offered** ("you're in Bristol and you've played with 4 people here — this is your scene"), not requested. This is the same "make the lazy path the correct path" instruction personas.md gives about split capture, applied to membership. A product that requires joining will have empty scenes and will blame its users. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **touring breaks the geo scene model** | Reasoning about how musicians actually move; personas.md's live/gig context | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap. A touring musician is in Bristol tonight, Manchester tomorrow, Glasgow Thursday — and per personas.md the Operator's world is built around exactly those visits. The static "your city" model serves the resident and fails the visitor, who is the person with the *most* acute need for a local scene: they are in a strange city for 18 hours and want to know who is around, whose studio is free, who plays what. That is a **temporary, high-intent scene membership** — and it is structurally the same object as the conference/event mode (03.11), which the sweep listed as an unrelated candidate. Two candidates, one mechanism: *temporal scene membership*. Recorded as Q-01 here and cross-referenced from 03.11. |
| DT-04 | A thin scene should be padded to look alive | The obvious growth-hack, and the temptation seeding creates | ❌ REJECTED: seeding with *real* facts (venues that exist, gigs that happened) is honest — those are true statements about the city. Padding with fake activity or inflated member counts is a lie, and it fails on contact with reality within one visit: a musician knows their own city's scene and will notice instantly that the platform is claiming things that are not true. Per problem-statement.md the entire product is a claim to be more trustworthy than reconstruction-from-memory; getting caught inflating a scene costs the thesis, not just the metric. Thin scenes are shown as thin. |
