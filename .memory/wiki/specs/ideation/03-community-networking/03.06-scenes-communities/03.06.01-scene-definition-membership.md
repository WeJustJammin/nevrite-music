# Feature: Scene Definition, Membership & Joining

> **Number**: 03.06.01
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What a scene *is* and who is in it. A scene is one mechanism with a **partition key** (D-01 on the parent):
geo (the Bristol scene), genre (UK drill), or craft (mastering engineers). Per D-02 geo is primary, because
geography is the only partition that produces local density, and per the domain overview density is the
whole cold-start strategy.

Membership is deliberately **overlapping and unranked** — a person is in several scenes at once, exactly as
the multi-hyphenate persona is several roles at once (personas.md). Any design forcing a primary scene
repeats the "artist OR producer?" failure in a new place.

The feature's load-bearing property, established in this pass, is that **membership is deliberately
worthless** (D-08). Joining grants no authority, no ranking weight, no density contribution, and no
standing. Everything consequential in this sub-domain — density (03.06.02), stewardship (03.06.03),
matching (03.03.02), ranking (03.02.02) — reads **evidence**, never membership. That is domain D-03
("evidence beats participation") applied here, and it is what licenses membership to be free, instant,
unverified and reversible: a fake member is inert, so there is nothing to defend against at the door.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Joins the cities they **work** in — not the one they live in (DT-06). A session drummer living in Reading and tracking in Hackney is offered the London scene, because their credits and bookings say London; a profile "city" field would say Reading and be wrong. Per personas.md they hold several roles at once, so a musician who plays three genres in two cities is in five scenes, and that is normal, not an edge case. Their scene is where the cold-start problem is solved or not: per the domain overview, 200 people in their city feels alive and is what makes them return. On tour they hold **visiting** membership of tonight's city, derived from the booking (D-07). |
| Producer | Full | Joins their work city and their craft scenes. Per personas.md they will not do setup work — so their scenes are **inferred and offered**, never configured (DT-02): their credits already say what they engineer and with whom, their sessions say where. They are structurally the most central node in any craft scene's graph (03.06.02 Role Lens), which is earned by sessions run rather than by joining — the distinction D-08 turns into a rule. Rarely tours, so visiting membership is mostly inert for them. |
| Operator | Full | Joins their city scene, where they are structurally an anchor: per personas.md their room is where the scene physically happens. Per 03.06.04 they are likely already *in* the seed data before they arrive — but seed presence is not membership, and claiming their venue (03.06.04) gives them authority over **the venue record only, never over the scene** (DT-10, `#CX-01` synthesis Q3). Their geo membership is single and stable — a venue does not tour — which makes them the one persona for whom the static model was always sufficient. |
| Fan | Full (geo only) | Per D-03 they are members of **geo** scenes — a city's music scene genuinely includes its audience, and per personas.md the Fan's need ("played their city") is exactly geo-shaped. Not craft scenes: a fan in "mastering engineers" is lost. Genre is the ambiguous middle (Q-01). Critically, per DT-11 they hold the same *membership* but see a **different surface**: the Fan's Bristol is gigs, venues and artists; the professional's Bristol is people, work and density. Per personas.md their surface "must be a different product wearing the same brand" — so their volume (D-13a) does not drown the professional's view, though it remains a moderation fact (D-13b). Fans never hold visiting membership: a fan travelling to a gig is buying a ticket (domain 19), not joining a scene. |

## Behavior

A scene has a **partition key**, a **definition**, and a **membership**. Joining is low-friction and
reversible; per D-04 on the parent the scene already exists and is already populated when you arrive
(03.06.04). Four properties define the mechanism.

### 1. Membership is inferred and offered — never required, never automatic

Per DT-02, joining is a chore and nobody does chores for a platform's benefit. The platform already holds
the answer: credits say what you play and with whom (domain 02), bookings say where (17), sessions say what
you engineer (07). So the system **derives candidate scenes and offers them**.

The offer is not a formality — it is the **consent gate**, and it is why inference stops short of
auto-joining. An inferred membership that joins you automatically *publishes a fact about you*: "this person
is in the Bristol scene" discloses that they work in Bristol, to Bristol. Inference may propose that; only
the user may assert it. **Inferred, offered, never automatic** (D-03).

Inference evidence, in priority order:

| Rank | Evidence | Scene inferred | Why it beats the one below |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirmed credits + sessions (02, 07) | geo (work city), genre, craft | Counter-attested; the strongest fact the platform holds |
| 2 | Confirmed bookings (17) | geo (work city) | A real engagement at a real address |
| 3 | Claimed venue/studio (16) | geo | An Operator's room is a fixed physical fact |
| 4 | Self-stated genres/crafts (01) | genre, craft | Self-asserted; sufficient for a low-stakes affiliation |
| 5 | Profile location (01) | geo | **Fallback only** — per DT-06 it names where you sleep, not where you work |

A user with no evidence at any rank (a brand-new account) is offered scenes at rank 5 only, and the offer is
labelled as a guess — per domain D-04, never overstate what is known.

### 2. Geo boundaries start coarse and subdivide only on evidence

Parent Q-01 ("London is one scene or twenty") is resolved by **error asymmetry**, not by picking a culture's
side (DT-05). A boundary drawn too large produces a scene that is dense but loose — a Walthamstow musician
is offered a Croydon session, which is annoying. A boundary drawn too small produces twenty scenes at
one-twentieth the density each — and per the domain overview density *is* the cold-start strategy, so that
outcome is not annoying, it is fatal. Therefore:

- A geo scene's default boundary is the **largest defensible local unit** — the metro/travel-to-work area,
  not the borough, not the postcode.
- Finer-grained scenes ("South London") are **added later, as flat overlapping peers**, when density inside
  the coarse scene supports the split. Adding is never nesting: per `#R-01` a scene hierarchy is rejected,
  and per D-02 membership overlaps, so a person is simply in both London and South London.
- The platform **never subdivides automatically**. A split is a cultural act, and a platform performing it
  unprompted owns the argument it starts (DT-09). Splits are proposed by stewards (03.06.03) and are
  subject to that feature's density gate.

The boundary dispute does not need a winner because **overlap absorbs it**: if Croydon's status is
contested, a Croydon musician holds both memberships and the argument costs nothing.

### 3. Membership has two kinds: resident and visiting

DT-03 confirmed that the static "your city" model serves the resident and fails the visitor — who has the
*most* acute need for a local scene. Resolved here (D-07):

| Property | Resident membership | Visiting membership |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Persistent until left | Bounded; expires automatically |
| Origin | Inferred from work evidence (rank 1–5 above), offered | Derived from a **confirmed booking** (domain 17), offered; or self-declared |
| Window | — | The engagement's own dates from 17 (load-in → curfew), widened to **arrival −1 day / departure +1 day**. Where 17 holds no times, defaults to the engagement date ±1 day |
| Self-declared cap | — | Max **3 concurrent** visiting memberships, max **14 days** each |
| Expiry | — | Automatic and unmissable. Per personas.md nobody will remember to close it — the same "will not tend a form" fact behind 03.11 D-01 |
| Counts toward density? | No (D-08) | No (D-08) |
| Relaxes contact walls? | No | **No** — see boundary below |

**The boundary with 03.11**: this feature owns *temporal membership*; it does **not** own wall relaxation.
A touring musician joining Manchester for 18 hours is joining a scene. A conference attendee (03.11) is
joining a scene **and** opening their reachability wall (03.04.04) — a materially stronger claim, which is
exactly the privacy-posture difference 03.11 DT-02 cites for keeping itself separate. Stated as a
separation: **03.11 = temporal membership (this feature) + bounded wall relaxation (03.04.04)**. Whether
that composition means 03.11 folds in remains 03.11's Q-02 and domain Q-10, at `/ideate-validate` — this
pass narrows the question rather than pre-empting it.

**Location is never taken from the device** (D-09). Visiting membership derives from a booked engagement or
a deliberate declaration. Per D-13b the platform carries a harassment-risk population, and per 03.11 Q-03
near-real-time location broadcast is already this domain's strongest privacy claim; deriving it silently
from a device would make it the *default*.

### 4. A scene is a place, and additionally a weighting

Parent Q-03 poses place-vs-weighting as a choice. **The dichotomy is false** (DT-12). A weighting has no
surface: it cannot be arrived at, cannot be judged, cannot be shown as thin (D-04), and cannot make 200
people in your city *feel* alive — and feeling is the whole cold-start claim. Worse, per domain Q-09 the
feed may not survive MoSCoW at all, so defining the scene *as* a feed weighting makes the platform's
cold-start mechanism depend on its most-likely-to-be-cut feature.

So: a scene **is** an addressable place. If a feed exists, membership **also** weights it (03.02.02). Place
is required; weighting is an additive optimisation with a dependency.

### Happy Path

1. A new user signs up. Their city scene already exists, seeded with venues, gigs and locally-credited artists (03.06.04) — they arrive somewhere alive rather than founding something empty (`#CX-01`)
2. System derives candidate scenes from evidence, highest rank available (§1 table) — credits and bookings first, profile location only as a labelled guess
3. System **offers** them, with the reason attached and visible: "You've played 4 gigs in Bristol — this is your scene." Per domain D-04 the offer states what it is based on, so a wrong inference is legible as a wrong inference rather than as the platform being confidently wrong about the user's own life
4. User accepts one or several. Membership is immediate, reversible, unranked, and grants nothing (D-08)
5. The scene opens as a **place** (§4) — the seeded venues and gigs, the members, and the density read (03.06.02), each labelled honestly including when density is zero
6. User acts inside the scene: finds people, sees what is on, judges whether the scene is real
7. A booking is confirmed at a venue in another city (domain 17). System offers **visiting** membership of that city for the engagement's window (§3)
8. User accepts; the visiting scene appears alongside their resident scenes, marked as visiting with its expiry visible
9. The window passes. Visiting membership expires automatically, with no action required and no wall state to restore (§3)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Empty scene** | Per D-04 this must not happen — an empty scene proves the platform is dead at the worst possible moment | Seeding (03.06.04). This is the failure the sub-domain exists to prevent |
| **User leaves, inference re-adds them next week** | The design as originally stated contained this bug: D-03 infers, D-02 says reversible, and a re-run of the inference silently undoes the user's decision. The platform would be experienced as ignoring an explicit choice — worse than never having offered | Leaving writes a **durable negative signal** (D-11): the scene is suppressed from all future offers permanently, until the user re-joins by hand. Leaving is not a removal, it is an instruction |
| **Musician lives Reading, works London** (DT-06) | A residence-keyed model puts them in a scene they have no professional life in and excludes them from the one they do — the commuter case is the *norm* in music, not an exception | Work evidence (rank 1–2) outranks profile location (rank 5). They are offered London. Reading is offered only if they have evidence there too — in which case they are in both, correctly |
| **Roster enumerated / scraped** (DT-07) | A geo scene's roster is the highest-value scrape target on the platform: a pre-segmented list of every working professional in a city, and per D-13a also a consumer PII list | **No complete enumeration** (D-10). Aliveness is delivered by aggregate counts and a bounded, non-exhaustive sample. Rate/abuse limits → `/create-prd-security` |
| **Fake account joins Bristol** | Nothing. Membership is unverified by design and grants nothing (D-08) — density is evidence-gated (03.06.02 D-02), stewardship is density-gated (03.06.03 D-03) | A sybil holds an inert membership. The sybil risk lives in the **graph** (03.04.01 DT-04), which is where it is defended; importing verification here would tax every honest user to inconvenience nobody |
| **Members churned; roster still says 200** | A scene whose members all left three years ago still reports 200 members — the roster is the one aliveness figure that is *not* evidence-gated, so D-04's honesty rule leaks here | A member with no evidenced activity in the scene for **12 months** is shown as **dormant** and excluded from the aliveness figure (D-12). The 12-month threshold is a proposal, not a certainty — flagged in Q-05 |
| **Visiting membership derived from a booking that is then cancelled** | The membership's whole basis disappears. Silently keeping it makes the platform assert a presence that is now false | Visiting membership is revoked when its originating engagement is cancelled (domain 17), on the same event. If the user still intends to travel, they may self-declare (§3, capped) |
| **Visiting membership expires mid-conversation with a local** | The scene disappears while a real conversation is live — the expiry destroys the thing it existed to create | Expiry removes **scene membership**, never anything created inside it. Conversations, connections (03.01.02) and rolodex entries (03.05) persist — which is 03.11 DT-03's finding ("the value is what survives the event") applying identically to the touring case |
| **User declares visiting membership in 40 cities** | To appear ubiquitous, or to enumerate 40 rosters | Capped at 3 concurrent, 14 days each (§3). A human is physically in one place; 3 covers travel-day overlap and a genuine two-city week |
| **Scene boundary disputed** (Croydon / South London) | Cultural arguments with no correct answer, and people care about them intensely | Resolved structurally (§2): coarse default + flat overlap. You are in both. The platform never adjudicates a boundary it did not need to draw |
| **Two names for one place** (Bristol / BS postcode / "the South West") | Naming a scene is a status act (DT-09) | Names are **descriptive and geographic**, never honorific — "Music in Bristol", not "The Bristol Scene". The honorific is a claim about who counts, and the platform declines to make it |
| **User moves city** | Their old scene stays relevant (their network is there); their new one is empty of their connections | Relocation **adds, never removes** (D-13). Work evidence in the new city produces an offer; the old membership persists until the user leaves it. Multiple unranked membership handles this natively — the platform must not infer departure from arrival |
| **Steward redefines/renames a scene while a user is joining it** | Concurrent write against a definition the joiner is looking at | The join targets the scene, not its rendered definition — it succeeds. The user's next view shows the current definition. Definitions are steward-editable (03.06.03) but never invalidate an in-flight join |
| **A scene splits while the user is a member** | §2: a split *adds* a flat peer; it does not partition the existing membership | The user keeps the coarse membership and is **offered** the finer one. No membership is ever moved or removed by a structural change — that would be an automatic join by another name |
| **Operator claims a venue and expects scene authority** (DT-10) | The multi-hyphenate acting in two roles: an Operator claiming their room in 03.06.04 is also a Musician member of that scene | Claiming confers authority over the **venue record only**. Scene authority is stewardship (03.06.03), which is density-gated and separately appointed (parent Q-04). `#CX-01` synthesis Q3: "the first person to join Bristol must not own Bristol" |
| **Genre scene is global and therefore dead** | Per D-02 genre inherits the platform's own disease rather than curing it | Genre scenes are secondary; expectations set accordingly, and no cold-start claim is made for them |
| **Scene majority-fan by headcount** (D-13a) | The premise assumes one shared surface — see DT-11 | Per personas.md the Fan's surface is "a different product wearing the same brand", so fan volume does not drown the professional's view of the same scene. It remains a **moderation** fact (D-13b) and the genre question stays open (Q-01) |
| **Fan in a town with 2 venues** | The geo scene is real but tiny — the honest case the seeding strategy cannot fix, because there is nothing to seed from | Shown as thin (D-04). Per 03.06.04 Q-01 a region with no seed data gets the empty state the strategy exists to prevent; a fan is the persona most likely to be the one who finds it |
| **Nobody joins anything** | Per DT-02 the likeliest failure: joining is a chore nobody does | Inferred membership, offered not requested (D-03), with the reason attached |
| **Vouching demanded** ("let members approve joins") | Would make the scene honorific and "real" (DT-09) | Rejected — DT-13. Four independent failures; see the annotation |
| **Network failure mid-join** | User taps join, connection drops — did it work? | Join is idempotent: re-issuing it is safe and non-duplicating. The scene renders joined-or-not, never ambiguous |
| **Partial evidence** — location evidence but no genre/craft | Common for a new Operator or a musician with one credit | Geo scene offered; genre/craft offers withheld rather than guessed. Per domain D-04, a guess presented as an inference is the defect this platform exists to fix |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Scene opening or offers computing | Skeleton |
| Empty | No scene exists for this region — no seed data (03.06.04 Q-01) | The honest empty state the strategy exists to prevent, labelled as "not yet mapped", never as "no music here" |
| Seeded | Scene exists, no real members | Venues, gigs, locally-credited artists — alive-looking by construction, every record **visibly derived and unclaimed** (03.06.04 D-02) |
| Offered | Inference produced a candidate the user has not answered | The offer with its reason attached, and its evidence rank legible. Dismissible; dismissal is not a "no" (only leaving is — D-11) |
| Populated | Real members exist | Members, activity, density (03.06.02) |
| Joined (resident) | User accepted an offer or joined by hand | Scene content weights their feed (§4) and the scene is a place they can go |
| Joined (visiting) | Booking confirmed (17) or self-declared | Scene marked **visiting**, with its expiry visible and its origin stated ("you play here Thursday") |
| Expiring | Visiting window nearing its end | Notice of expiry and, per 03.11 DT-03, what persists: conversations, connections and rolodex entries survive; membership does not |
| Expired | Visiting window passed, or its booking cancelled | Membership gone with no action required. Nothing created inside the scene is removed |
| Thin | Few members, low density | Honest — per DT-04, pretending is worse than admitting |
| Dormant | Member with no evidenced scene activity for 12 months (D-12) | Still a member; excluded from the aliveness figure. The user is not told they are dormant — it is a counting rule, not a status punishment |
| Partial | Some evidence ranks available, others absent | Geo offered, genre/craft withheld — with the absence visible rather than filled with a guess |
| Error | Fetch or join failed | Retry. A failed join is unambiguous — never optimistically rendered as joined |

| DT-01 | Geo, genre and craft scenes need separate designs | The sweep listed them as three candidates (09, 10, 11) | ❌ REJECTED → parent D-01. They differ only in the predicate defining membership; the machinery (join, leave, density, stewardship, moderation, seeding) is identical. Three designs means three implementations of one thing and three places for the moderation model to diverge. Note this is the *inverse* of D-14's marketplace reasoning: there, the schemas were irreconcilable so the merge was rejected; here the schemas are identical so the split is rejected. Same test, opposite answer — which is a good sign the test is real and not a rationalisation. |
| DT-02 | Users will join scenes | The premise of every community product | ❌ REJECTED as stated: joining is a chore, and per personas.md neither the Producer (in a DAW) nor the Musician (running a portfolio of gigs) does chores for a platform's benefit. But the platform **already knows**: their credits say what they play and with whom, their gigs say where, their location says which city. Scene membership should be **inferred and offered** ("you're in Bristol and you've played with 4 people here — this is your scene"), not requested. This is the same "make the lazy path the correct path" instruction personas.md gives about split capture, applied to membership. A product that requires joining will have empty scenes and will blame its users. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **touring breaks the geo scene model** | Reasoning about how musicians actually move; personas.md's live/gig context | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real gap. A touring musician is in Bristol tonight, Manchester tomorrow, Glasgow Thursday — and per personas.md the Operator's world is built around exactly those visits. The static "your city" model serves the resident and fails the visitor, who is the person with the *most* acute need for a local scene: they are in a strange city for 18 hours and want to know who is around, whose studio is free, who plays what. That is a **temporary, high-intent scene membership** — and it is structurally the same object as the conference/event mode (03.11), which the sweep listed as an unrelated candidate. Two candidates, one mechanism: *temporal scene membership*. **Resolved this pass** → D-07 and Behavior §3, with the 03.11 boundary stated as a composition: 03.11 = temporal membership + wall relaxation. |
| DT-04 | A thin scene should be padded to look alive | The obvious growth-hack, and the temptation seeding creates | ❌ REJECTED: seeding with *real* facts (venues that exist, gigs that happened) is honest — those are true statements about the city. Padding with fake activity or inflated member counts is a lie, and it fails on contact with reality within one visit: a musician knows their own city's scene and will notice instantly that the platform is claiming things that are not true. Per problem-statement.md the entire product is a claim to be more trustworthy than reconstruction-from-memory; getting caught inflating a scene costs the thesis, not just the metric. Thin scenes are shown as thin. |
| DT-05 | The geo boundary question ("London is one scene or twenty") needs the *right* answer | The framing inherited from parent Q-01, which calls both answers defensible | ❌ REJECTED — it needs the *safe* answer, and the two errors are wildly asymmetric. Too-large: a Walthamstow musician is offered a Croydon session. Cost: mild irrelevance in a mechanism (offers) the user can ignore. Too-small: twenty scenes at one-twentieth the density, and per the domain overview density **is** the cold-start strategy — "20,000 scattered globally feels dead" is the same failure at metro scale. Cost: the sub-domain's entire premise. The errors are also asymmetric in **reversibility**: splitting a scene that got too dense is a success event; merging twenty dead scenes is a public admission the platform mis-drew the map of someone's home city. So: default coarse, subdivide only on evidence, never automatically. This does not decide whether Croydon is South London — it makes the platform not need to know, because overlap (D-02) lets the user be in both. Resolves parent Q-01 and the boundary gap the breadth pass left open in Behavior. |
| DT-06 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **a scene is where you work, not where you live** | Reasoning about music's actual economic geography against personas.md's income-portfolio fact | ✅ CONFIRMED and it invalidates the obvious implementation. Musicians live where rent is cheap and work where the studios and venues are — this is not an edge case, it is the profession's default: session players commute into London, Nashville, Berlin, LA from wherever they can afford. Per personas.md the Musician "makes a living from a portfolio of small income streams", which is precisely the pattern that forces the commute. A "your city" model keyed to a profile location field therefore does two harms at once: it puts them in a scene where they have no professional life (Reading), and it excludes them from the only scene that matters to their career (London). And per DT-02 the profile field is *also* a chore, so the model is asking for effort in exchange for a wrong answer. The platform's own evidence already holds the right answer — credits (02), sessions (07) and bookings (17) all carry a location, and every one of them names a *work* location. Hence the evidence-rank table in §1, with profile location demoted to a labelled fallback. |
| DT-07 | What a domain expert would notice that nobody mentioned: **the roster is the platform's highest-value scrape target, and this feature owns it** | Adversarial reasoning about what a scene *is* as an object, rather than as an experience | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is a hole in this feature specifically. 03.04.01 Q-02 declines to build a browsable industry-wide graph, and 03.06.02 restricts the density map to members — but a **roster is not a graph**, and nobody has said whether it is enumerable. A geo scene roster is a pre-segmented list of every working musician, producer and engineer in a named city: for a recruiter, a rival platform's cold-start, or a spammer, it is more valuable than the graph, and it is cheaper to take because it needs no traversal. Per D-13a it is *also* a consumer PII list, since geo scenes are majority-fan by headcount — so the same object is simultaneously a competitive-intelligence asset and a data-protection exposure. The tension is real and worth stating honestly: the cold-start claim **requires** the user to see that the scene is alive, which means the population must be legible while not being harvestable. Product answer (D-10): aliveness is delivered by aggregate counts plus a bounded, non-exhaustive sample; there is no complete paginated roster, because a complete roster is the artefact being protected. Full mitigation is rate-limiting and abuse detection, which is architecture — routed to `/create-prd-security`. |
| DT-08 | Leaving a scene is the trivial inverse of joining | The obvious symmetry | ❌ REJECTED — and the asymmetry is a genuine bug in this file's own prior decisions. D-03 says membership is inferred; D-02 says it is reversible. Compose them: the user leaves Bristol, the inference re-runs on Tuesday, and the platform silently puts them back. From the inside, the product ignored an explicit instruction — which is materially worse than never having offered, because the offer at least admitted it was a guess. Every inference-driven membership model must therefore treat leaving as a **durable negative signal**, not a state change: leaving means "never offer me this again", permanently, until the user re-joins by hand (D-11). Note the corollary that dismissing an *offer* must be weaker than leaving — dismissal is "not now", leaving is "no" — because collapsing them makes the offer surface unusable: a user who dismisses an offer while busy has not decided anything. |
| DT-09 | The platform can name and bound scenes neutrally | The convenient assumption behind §2 | ⚠️ CONFIRMED-WITH-MITIGATION, and it constrains naming rather than boundaries. In music, "the Bristol scene" is not a synonym for "musicians in Bristol" — it is a **status object**, an in-group with contested membership that people argue about sincerely and at length. If WeJammin asserts "you are in the Bristol scene", it is making a status claim about a cultural object it does not own, and it will be read as gatekeeping — by the people it included and, far more loudly, by the people it did not. The mitigation is not curation: deciding who is "really" in the scene is the exclusion power 03.06.03 DT-01 already rejects as a livelihood act, and doing it algorithmically is that same act with no accountability. The mitigation is **framing**: the platform's object is descriptive ("Music in Bristol" — everyone who works here) and never honorific ("The Bristol Scene" — the ones who count). A descriptive object cannot gatekeep, because it makes no claim about status; it also defuses the boundary argument, since nobody fights over who is inside a *description*. This is the same restraint as domain D-04 (never overstate what is known), applied to a claim about people rather than about facts. |
| DT-10 | Joining a scene should grant some standing in it | Every community product's assumption; and the Operator's intuition on claiming their venue | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection is what makes the rest of the design safe (D-08). Membership grants **nothing**: not density (03.06.02 D-02 measures evidenced collaborations), not stewardship (03.06.03 D-03 is density-gated and separately appointed), not ranking weight (domain D-03: evidence beats participation), not authority over seed data (`#CX-01` synthesis Q3). Three things follow, and each is load-bearing: (1) joining can be free, instant and unverified, because there is nothing at the door worth forcing — a sybil member is inert, so verification would tax every honest user to inconvenience nobody; (2) the multi-hyphenate case resolves cleanly — an Operator who claims their venue (03.06.04) gains authority over the **venue record**, and remains an ordinary member of the **scene**, so "the first person to join Bristol owns Bristol" cannot happen by construction rather than by policy; (3) it explains *why* membership can be overlapping and unranked (D-02) without the design collapsing — five memberships confer five times nothing. A membership that granted standing would need to be scarce, verified, and adjudicated, which is a different and much worse feature. |
| DT-11 | A majority-fan geo scene drowns the professionals in it | Follows from D-13a, and is the strongest argument for excluding fans from geo scenes | ❌ REJECTED — **the premise smuggles in an assumption this feature never made**, namely that everyone in a scene looks at one shared surface. Per personas.md the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" and their surface "must be a different product wearing the same brand". The Fan's Bristol is gigs, venues, and artists to follow; the professional's Bristol is people, work, and density (03.06.02, which fans cannot see at all). Same membership object, different lens — which is precisely what the Role Lens is for, and it is already how the parent's Role Matrix reads (Fan: `None` on the density map). | DT-12 | A scene is either a place (own feed) or a weighting (tilts the main feed) | Parent Q-03's framing | ❌ REJECTED as a dichotomy — the "or" is false, and the two are not even the same kind of thing. **Place is required**: an invisible weighting cannot be arrived at, cannot be judged, cannot be shown as thin (D-04), and cannot make 200 people in a city *feel* alive — and per the domain overview "feels alive" is the literal cold-start claim, with feeling requiring a surface to feel it on. **Weighting is additive and conditional**: it presupposes a feed, and per domain Q-09 the feed is itself under question as a Must, with 03.02.02 DT-02 finding the Fan's real value rides on an alert path that bypasses ranking entirely. | DT-13 | Scene membership should require vouching or approval by existing members | The instinct that follows directly from DT-09 — if a scene is a status object, make the status real | ❌ REJECTED, and it fails four independent ways, which is itself informative: (1) it is a chore for *two* people — the joiner and the voucher — and per DT-02 neither does chores for the platform's benefit, so the voucher queue is where scene growth would go to die; (2) it rebuilds the "accept?" click-graph that domain D-02 names as the *repeated* failure of LinkedIn-for-music, in the one sub-domain whose entire job is producing density; (3) it delegates an exclusion power to peers who are frequently direct competitors, which is 03.06.03 DT-01's rejected livelihood act arriving through a side door — softer-looking, because refusing to vouch is passive, and therefore harder to appeal than an explicit removal; (4) it is unbuildable at cold start by construction — per D-04 a seeded scene has **no members at all**, so the first real user in Bristol has nobody to be vouched by. Any one of these is disqualifying. Recorded because the instinct is a good one and will recur: the thing vouching is reaching for — a scene that means something — is delivered instead by D-08 (membership means nothing; **evidence** means everything). |
