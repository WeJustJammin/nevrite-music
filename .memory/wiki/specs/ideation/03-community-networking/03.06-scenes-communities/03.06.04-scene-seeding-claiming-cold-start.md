# Feature: Scene Seeding, Claiming & Cold Start

> **Number**: 03.06.04
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Deep Think addition** — this feature was **not** in the sweep's candidate list. The domain overview
> asserts that local scenes are "the only tractable cold-start strategy", but nothing in the 16 candidates
> made that true: every one of them assumed users already existed. See DT-01.

## Overview

Scenes exist before their members do. Per D-04 on the parent, the Bristol scene is derived from facts that
are already true about Bristol — the venues in it (domain 16), the gigs that happen there (17), the artists
credited on records made there (02) — so that on the day the first Bristol user signs up, they arrive
somewhere already alive rather than founding something empty.

This inverts the normal community bootstrap. Asking the first 20 users to create and populate a scene is
asking the people with the least reason to care to do the most work, and they will not — per personas.md
neither the Producer (in a DAW) nor the Musician (running a portfolio of gigs) does chores for a platform.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Arrives to a city already mapped — venues they have played, gigs they were at, artists they know of. Per personas.md their switching trigger includes "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin"; a seeded scene manufactures a weaker version of that recognition without needing the collaborator to have arrived first. They may correct seed data about themselves. |
| Producer | Config | Sees the studios and sessions of their city on their first screen, with zero setup. Per personas.md this is the only way they encounter a populated product — any flow requiring configuration will not be used. |
| Operator | Config | **The most affected persona.** Their venue is likely *in* the seed data before they sign up (DT-02) — powerful (their room is on the map) and hazardous (their room is on the map without consent, possibly with stale specs, which is precisely the failure personas.md names: "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show"). Claiming their listing is their entry point to the platform. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent Role Matrix). Per D-03 they are members of a seeded geo scene and consume it — the local gig map is exactly what personas.md says they came for — but they do not seed, claim, or correct it. A fan editing a venue's specs is a data-integrity hazard with no upside. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Seeding derives a scene from real, verifiable facts. Per 03.06.01 DT-04 the constraint is absolute:
**only real facts** — venues that exist, gigs that happened, credits that are documented. Padding with
fabricated activity fails on contact with a musician who knows their own city, and per problem-statement.md
this product's entire claim is trustworthiness about exactly this category of fact.

Claiming is the handover: a real party (an Operator claiming their venue, an artist claiming their entry)
takes ownership of a seeded record. Per `03.06-scenes-communities-cx.md#CX-04` claiming is also a candidate
answer to "who becomes the steward" (parent Q-04).

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the seed data's **sources** are the feature's gating
question and are unresolved (Q-01). They determine whether this is buildable at all.

### Happy Path

1. Before launch in a city, the platform derives the scene from public/licensed data — venues, gigs, locally-credited artists
2. First Bristol user signs up and finds 60 venues, 400 documented gigs, and artists they recognise
3. They join, and they are joining something alive (`#CX-01`)
4. An Operator finds their room already listed and claims it — correcting the specs as their first act
5. Claimed records become authoritative and community-owned; seed data recedes

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Seed data is wrong** — a venue that closed, a gig that never happened, stale gear specs | The platform is publishing false facts about real businesses, unprompted. Per personas.md a stale gear list means "a band arrives to a room that can't run their show" — the harm is concrete | Seed records must be visibly **unclaimed/derived**, not presented as verified. See DT-03 |
| Operator objects to being listed without consent | A real business finds itself on a platform it never joined | DT-02 — needs an opt-out that is easier than a complaint |
| Seeded scene shows zero density (03.06.02 DT-03) | Populated-looking scene, zero collaboration edges — two of this domain's features telling contradictory stories on one screen | **Unresolved conflict** — 03.06.02 Q-01 |
| Wrong claim — someone claims a venue they do not own | Control of a real business's presence handed to an impostor | Verification required; the same problem domain 16 must solve for listings generally |
| No seed data available for a region | That city has no scene and gets the empty state the strategy exists to prevent | Q-01 — the strategy is only as good as the data coverage |
| Seed data licensing | Scraped gig data may not be redistributable; per D-09 the predecessor's claims are not evidence of rights | Q-01 — this is a legal gate, not a technical one |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Seeded | Derived, unclaimed | Scene populated with visibly-derived records |
| Claimed | Real party took ownership | Authoritative, community-owned record |
| Disputed | Two parties claim one record | `[PENDING]` — routes to 16/24 |
| Stale | Seed data aged with no claim | Should decay or be marked, not silently persist as fact |
| Unavailable | No seed data for the region | The empty state the strategy exists to prevent |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The domain's stated cold-start strategy had no mechanism in the candidate list** | Deep Think: reading the domain overview's own claim against its 16 candidates | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is why the feature exists. The overview asserts local scenes are "the only tractable cold-start strategy" because "200 users in one city feels alive, 20,000 scattered globally feels dead". But every candidate (join a scene, browse a scene, moderate a scene) presupposes the 200 users already exist. The strategy was a *restatement of the problem* — scenes solve cold start once scenes are populated, and populating them is the cold start. The only escape is that the scene must not start at zero, which means deriving it from facts that exist independently of the platform's adoption. Without this feature the domain's headline claim is circular. |
| DT-02 | Seeding venues is uncontroversial because the data is public | The convenient assumption | ❌ REJECTED: listing a real business on a platform it never joined, with specs it did not supply, is a real act with real consequences. Per personas.md the Operator's *own* worst accidental behaviour is publishing a stale gear list that causes a band to arrive at a room that cannot run their show — and seeding does exactly that, on their behalf, without their knowledge. It is also a pattern that has drawn real hostility toward map and review products (businesses listed without consent, unable to correct their own entry). The mitigations — visibly-derived status, an easy opt-out, claiming as the correction path — are all cheap, and skipping them turns the growth strategy into an antagonism campaign against the exact persona domains 16/17/19 depend on. |
| DT-03 | Seeded and real records can look the same | Simplicity; it makes the scene look better | ❌ REJECTED: a derived record is an *inference*, and a claimed one is an *assertion by the responsible party*. Presenting them identically means the platform is vouching for facts it guessed. Per problem-statement.md the product exists because the industry's records are unreliable reconstructions — shipping unmarked inferences as facts on day one would reproduce, as the platform's own founding data, precisely the defect it was built to fix. Seed records must be visibly derived and visibly unclaimed. This is the same principle as 03.03.01 D-03 (degraded results must be labelled) and 03.04.01 D-03 ("unknown" ≠ "no path") — a **domain-level rule** about never overstating what is known, appearing for the third time. |
| DT-04 | Failure mode at the intersection with the graph: seeding cannot bootstrap collaboration edges | Reasoning against 03.04.01 D-01 | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is an honest limit on this feature, cross-referenced from 03.06.02 DT-03. Public credit data could theoretically seed edges ("these four played on that record") — but 03.04.01 D-01 says edges come only from **counter-attested** credits on this platform, precisely so the graph cannot be forged. Seeding edges from public data would inject exactly the unverified claims the graph exists to exclude, and would do it at scale, in the platform's own voice. So: scenes can be seeded, the graph cannot. The consequence is a seeded scene with visible population and zero density, which is 03.06.02's Q-01 and remains genuinely unresolved. |
