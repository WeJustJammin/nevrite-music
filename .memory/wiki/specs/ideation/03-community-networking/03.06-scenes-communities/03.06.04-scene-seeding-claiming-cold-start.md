# Feature: Scene Seeding, Claiming & Cold Start

> **Number**: 03.06.04
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Deep Think addition** — this feature was **not** in the sweep's candidate list. The domain overview
> asserts that local scenes are "the only tractable cold-start strategy", but nothing in the 16 candidates
> made that true: every one of them assumed users already existed. See DT-01.

## Overview

Scenes exist before their members do. Per D-04 on the parent, the Bristol scene is derived from facts that
are already true about Bristol — the venues in it (domain 16), the gigs that happen there (17), the artists
credited on records made there (02) — so that on the day the first Bristol user signs up, they arrive
somewhere already alive rather than founding something empty.

This inverts the normal community bootstrap. Asking the first 20 users to create and populate a scene is
asking the people with the least reason to care to do the most work, and they will not — per personas.md
neither the Producer (in a DAW) nor the Musician (running a portfolio of gigs) does chores for a platform.

The feature has two halves and they are in tension. **Seeding** is the platform making assertions about real
businesses and real events that no participant supplied. **Claiming** is the only mechanism that converts
those assertions back into statements their responsible party actually stands behind. Everything specified
below exists to make the gap between the two halves survivable: seeding is what the growth strategy needs,
and claiming is what stops the growth strategy from being an act of aggression against the Operator persona
that domains 16, 17 and 19 are built on.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Arrives to a city already mapped — venues they have played, gigs that were announced there. Per personas.md their switching trigger includes "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin"; a seeded scene manufactures a weaker version of that recognition without needing the collaborator to have arrived first. **They cannot claim anything here** — they own no premises (venue claiming is Operator-shaped) and per D-12 no person-record exists for them to claim. Their two concrete powers: (1) **report** a seed record as wrong (a closed room, a cancelled gig) — they are the correction engine, because per 03.06.01 DT-04 a musician knows their own city better than any data source; (2) correct the record of a gig **they performed at** via domain 02's attestation flow, never via this feature (D-10). |
| Producer | Config | Sees the venues and commercially-trading studios of their city on their first screen, with zero setup. Per personas.md this is the only way they encounter a populated product — any flow requiring configuration will not be used. **Their own room is deliberately absent**: per D-11 a home studio is a residential address and is never seeded, so the honest Producer experience is "my city's rooms are here, mine is not, and I add it in one action". Same report power as the Musician. Where they run a commercial studio they are acting as an **Operator** for the claim (personas.md's multi-hyphenate fact), not as a Producer. |
| Operator | Config | **The most affected persona.** Their venue is likely *in* the seed data before they sign up (DT-02) — powerful (their room is on the map) and hazardous (their room is on the map without consent, possibly with stale specs, which is precisely the failure personas.md names: "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show"). Claiming is their entry point to the platform and, per DT-07, it is **also their exit**: the delist action is the first thing available post-claim, because there is no way to prove you want your listing gone without proving the listing is yours. Post-claim they hold the room's forward-looking specs, calendar and listing — and **nothing** about who historically performed there (D-10). |
| Fan | None | No access (parent Role Matrix). Per D-03 they are members of a seeded geo scene and consume it — the local gig map is exactly what personas.md says they came for — but they do not seed, claim, correct, or report here. A fan editing a venue's specs is a data-integrity hazard with no upside, and per D-13a fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude, so a fan-writable correction surface is a vandalism surface with a 100:1 volume advantage over the people who know the answer. Their observations are not discarded: a fan who notices a closed venue reports through domain 24's general reporting, which routes to review without conferring correction authority. Note the asymmetry: the Fan holds no power over this data and is the population most exposed to its errors (DT-09 — they are the ones who travel to an announced gig that never happened). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### What seeding asserts, and what it refuses to assert

Seeding derives a scene from real, verifiable facts. Per 03.06.01 DT-04 the constraint is absolute:
**only real facts** — venues that exist, gigs that were announced, credits that are documented. Padding with
fabricated activity fails on contact with a musician who knows their own city, and per problem-statement.md
this product's entire claim is trustworthiness about exactly this category of fact.

Three hard limits on what may be seeded, each derived below:

| Seeded | Never seeded | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Premises** — a commercially-trading room, keyed by physical location, with a trading-name history | The trading name as the record's identity | DT-05 — a room outlives its name and its operator |
| **Events** — a gig that was *announced* at a premises on a date, with the billed artist names as attributes of that event | The claim that the gig *happened* | DT-09 — an announcement is not a performance |
| — | **Person-profiles** — no account, profile, or claimable record is created for any human who has not signed up | D-12 / DT-11 — a shadow profile is the 03.05.01 hazard at public scale |
| — | **Residential addresses** — house shows, home studios, garage rehearsal rooms, "address on RSVP" gigs | D-11 / DT-11 — publishing them at scale is doxxing, and a large fraction of real music production happens in them |
| — | **Collaboration edges** | D-04 / DT-04 — edges come only from counter-attested credits (03.04.01 D-01) |

### Source discipline

Seed data sourcing and its licensing are the feature's **gating legal question and remain open (Q-01)** —
whether any given source may be redistributed is a decision for the owner and `/create-prd`. The *behaviour*
around sources does not wait on that answer and is fixed here:

1. **Every seed record carries its source set.** A record with an unrecordable provenance is not published.
   The platform does not make an assertion it cannot attribute — that is problem-statement.md's own indictment
   of the industry's reconstruction engines, applied to the platform's founding data.
2. **Corroboration before publication (D-06).** A seed record is published only with **≥2 independent
   sources agreeing**, or **1 first-party fact** (a licensed registry entry, a companies/licensing record, a
   direct feed from the premises itself). A single scraped source is never sufficient — see DT-06, where the
   single-source failure is not merely inaccuracy but the publication of another product's deliberate trap
   entry.
3. **Source revocation cascades.** If a source's licence lapses or is withdrawn, every **unclaimed** record
   whose corroboration set drops below the threshold is unpublished within 24h. **Claimed** records are
   unaffected — by then the assertion belongs to the claimant, not the source (D-08).
4. **Unclaimed seed records are not published to search engines (DT-14).** The "derived / unclaimed" label
   that makes a seed record honest on-platform (D-02) does not survive a search snippet. A claim makes the
   record indexable, at the claimant's option, defaulting on.

### Claiming, and the fact that opting out *is* claiming

Claiming is the handover: a real party takes ownership of a seeded record and their assertion supersedes the
platform's inference permanently. Per `03.06-scenes-communities-cx.md#CX-04` claiming is also a candidate
answer to "who becomes the steward" (parent Q-04).

The naive design has an **easy opt-out** and a **verified claim** as two separate flows. That is incoherent
(DT-07): to remove a venue's listing you must show you speak for the venue, which is exactly the claim
verification burden. An opt-out easier than a claim is an opt-out a competitor can fire at a rival's room.
So there is one flow with two exits:

| Action | Who | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| **Report** a record as wrong/closed/never-happened | Musician, Producer, any signed-in professional account ≥30 days old | None — it is a signal, not an authority |
| **Claim** a record | The party with authority over the premises | Domain 16's venue-claim verification (this feature does not build its own — D-14) |
| **Correct** a claimed record | The claimant | Already verified |
| **Delist** a claimed record | The claimant | Already verified — available as the **first** post-claim action |
| **Refuse permanently** — a tombstone barring re-seeding | The claimant, at delist time | Already verified — see Q-03 |

**Verification is domain 16's, not this feature's (D-14).** This feature states two requirements on it:
per personas.md the Operator's inventory is perishable ("an unsold Tuesday is gone forever"), so a claim
pending for three weeks costs them money the platform caused them to lose — **target: verification decided
within 5 business days**, with the record showing `Claim Pending` and the claimant's supplied corrections
held but not published until the decision. And the claim flow must be reachable **from the unclaimed record
itself**, not from an account settings page, because the Operator arrives at the record via a link someone
sent them, not via a signup funnel.

### The seed pipeline never overwrites a claim

Per D-08: **a claim permanently ends seed writes to that record.** Refresh may notify the claimant ("your
listed capacity differs from the licensing register") but never writes. The alternative — a nightly refresh
that reverts the operator's correction — makes the Operator correct their room, watch it revert, and conclude
the platform is broken and hostile. It is the most predictable way to destroy the exact relationship the
strategy depends on (DT-08).

### Happy Path

1. Before launch in a city, the platform derives candidate premises and events from licensed/public sources
2. Each candidate is corroborated — published only at ≥2 independent agreeing sources or 1 first-party fact (D-06); residential addresses are dropped regardless of source (D-11); no person-records are created (D-12)
3. Published seed records render with a visible **derived / unclaimed** marker and are excluded from search-engine indexing (D-02, DT-14)
4. First Bristol user signs up and sees their city: 60 premises, 400 **announced** gigs, and the artist names billed on them — labelled as announcements, not as performances (D-09)
5. They join, and they are joining something alive (`#CX-01`)
6. Alongside the city facts, the platform states its own position honestly and separately: *"WeJammin in Bristol: no evidenced collaborations yet"* — the two numbers never share an axis (D-13)
7. An Operator arrives at their room's unclaimed record via a link, and claims it
8. Domain 16 verifies authority; target decision ≤5 business days; record shows `Claim Pending` meanwhile
9. On verification the record flips to **Claimed**: seed writes to it stop permanently (D-08), the Operator's specs supersede the derived ones, the derived/unclaimed marker is replaced by their authorship, and search indexing becomes available at their option
10. Their first available post-claim action is **delist** — the opt-out and the claim are the same door (DT-07)
11. Claimed records are authoritative and community-owned; seed data recedes to a provenance footnote

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Seed data is wrong** — a venue that closed, stale gear specs | The platform is publishing false facts about a real business, unprompted. Per personas.md a stale gear list means "a band arrives to a room that can't run their show" | Record carries the derived/unclaimed marker (D-02) — never presented as verified. Report → 1 report shows an "accuracy disputed" banner; 3 distinct qualifying reports in 30 days → **Suppressed** |
| **Operator objects to being listed without consent** | A real business finds itself on a platform it never joined | The claim flow, reachable from the record, with **delist** as the first post-claim action (DT-07). An "easy opt-out" that skips verification is not built — it would let a competitor delist a rival's room |
| **Seeded scene shows population and zero density** (03.06.02 DT-03) | Two of this domain's features telling contradictory stories on one screen | **RESOLVED (D-13)**: they are answers to different questions and must not share an axis. City facts answer "is Bristol a music city?"; density answers "is WeJammin used in Bristol?". Rendered as two separate statements with two named subjects, not one blended "scene health" number. Answers 03.06.02 Q-01 |
| **Same room, two trading names** (DT-05) | Two seed records for one physical room; a claim on one leaves the other unclaimed and contradicting it | Premises identity is the location, not the name (D-05). Two source records at one location merge into one premises with a trading-name history; a claim covers the room |
| **Venue changes hands — outgoing and incoming operator both claim** | Both claims are legitimate. Venues change hands constantly; this is not fraud, it is a handover | Not a single-winner dispute. Resolves as **both, split by date**: authority over the room from the transfer date forward goes to the incoming operator; the historical record stays attributed to the period's operator. Routes to domain 16 with this as the expected shape, not to 24 |
| **Two simultaneous claims on one record** (concurrency) | Two verification flows race on one record | First claim locks the record to `Claim Pending`; the second is queued, not rejected — a rejected second claimant with a real title has no path back. If claim 1 verifies, claim 2 is re-routed as a **dispute** (below), not silently dropped |
| **Wrong claim — an impostor claims a venue they do not own** | Control of a real business's presence handed to an impostor | Domain 16's verification is the gate (D-14). The blast radius is bounded by D-10: a claim confers the room's forward specs and calendar, never the historical performance record |
| **Trap / honeypot record** (DT-06) | A scraped competitor source contains a deliberately fictitious entry; publishing it is both evidence of copying and a direct violation of D-01 ("real facts only"), committed unknowingly | Prevented by corroboration (D-06): a trap entry exists in exactly one source by construction, so it never reaches the ≥2-source threshold |
| **Announced gig that never happened** (DT-09) | Cancellations and postponements are the majority failure of public gig data. "400 documented gigs" would include gigs that were advertised and never played — and a musician knows which ones | Seeded events are labelled **Announced**, never **Performed** (D-09). A gig becomes Performed only on first-party evidence (a settlement in 17, a counter-attested credit in 02) — none of which a seed has |
| **Residential address in a source** (DT-11) | House shows and home studios appear in public gig listings, often with addresses that are deliberately semi-private ("address on RSVP"). Seeding publishes a private home as a music venue, at scale | Dropped at ingestion (D-11) regardless of source or corroboration. Home studios are Operator-created, never seeded — a permanent, accepted coverage gap in exactly the rooms where much production happens |
| **Operator-Musician edits their own performance history via their venue claim** (DT-10) | The multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once. A venue owner who also plays claims their room, then edits the record of a gig *they performed at their own venue* — an interested party editing the platform's founding provenance data | Blocked (D-10). A venue claim grants the room's forward-looking specs, calendar and listing. Historical performance facts are corrected only via domain 02's attestation, which requires counter-attestation from the other parties |
| **Source revokes its licence after seeding** | Published records lose their corroboration basis retroactively | Unclaimed records dropping below threshold are unpublished within 24h. Claimed records are unaffected — the assertion is the claimant's now (D-08). Users see affected unclaimed records disappear with no notice; a claimed record shows no change |
| **Competitor mass-flags a rival's venue** (malicious input) | Reports are the unverified path, so they are the abusable one | Suppression requires **3 distinct reporters** within 30 days; reports from accounts <30 days old do not count toward the threshold; reports are rate-limited to 5/account/day. Suppression never deletes — a suppressed record is removed from scene surfaces, still reachable by direct link, and still claimable. Only a verified claimant deletes |
| **Fan reports a closure at fan volume** (D-13a) | Fans are 100:1 by headcount and are often right about closures | Fan reports route to domain 24's general reporting and do not count toward the 3-reporter suppression threshold. The signal is captured; the authority is not conferred |
| **Claim lands while the record is Suppressed** (state race) | A record suppressed by reports is exactly the record its owner most wants to fix | Suppression never blocks claiming. On verification the record leaves Suppressed directly to Claimed, and the outstanding reports are surfaced **to the claimant** as their first correction queue |
| **Claimed record abandoned — Operator deletes their account** | The room still exists; the claim does not | Record reverts to **Unclaimed** and becomes re-claimable. It does **not** revert to seed-written (D-08 is permanent for that record's derived fields) — the claimant's last corrections persist as the record's state, attributed to a deleted author, because the room's specs are facts about the room, not personal data. Contrast 03.05.01 Q-03, where the equivalent shadow records are private and deletion is the defensible answer |
| **Duplicate: one room seeded twice, one copy claimed** | Claiming record A leaves record B unclaimed, derived, contradicting the claimed one | Premises merge on location (D-05). A post-claim duplicate discovered later merges *into* the claimed record; the unclaimed copy's derived fields never overwrite the claimed ones |
| **City has no seed coverage** | That city gets the empty state the strategy exists to prevent | **Unseeded** state — stated honestly ("we have no reliable data for this city yet"), never an empty scene pretending to be new. The first Operator to create a premises here seeds it first-party. Per DT-12 this is the strategy's structural weak point and it is worst where the platform's value is highest |
| **Two bands with the same name on seeded gig listings** | Name collision is routine in music | Harmless by construction: per D-12 the billed name is an **attribute of the event**, not a link to a person-record. No entity is created, so nothing mis-resolves. This is the 03.05.01 DT-02 hazard designed out rather than mitigated |
| **Artist rebrands after a seeded gig** | The historical listing carries a name nobody uses now | The event record is a fact about that date and is not rewritten. Name-history resolution is domain 01/02's problem; this feature does not chase it |
| **Network failure mid-claim** (partial data) | Claim submitted, verification state unknown to the user | Claim submission is idempotent on the record — a resubmit joins the existing pending claim rather than creating a second one. Record shows `Claim Pending` with the submission timestamp; the Operator is never left unsure whether they claimed |
| **Seeded venue sits outside any defined scene boundary** | The room exists; the scene it belongs to does not (parent Q-01, 03.06.01 Q-03) | Premises are seeded independently of scene boundaries; a premises with no scene renders in domain 16's directory and joins a scene when one is defined around it. Seeding is not blocked on the boundary question |
| **Licensing gate fails entirely — no lawful source exists** | The domain overview's cold-start claim fails, not just this feature | Q-01. MoSCoW and the parent's D-04 must know: without seeding, 03.06.01's "arrive somewhere alive" is unbacked and the sub-domain's headline claim reverts to circular (DT-01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Scene or record opening | Skeleton |
| Unseeded | No source coverage for the region | Honest statement: no reliable data for this city yet, plus the first-party create path. **Not** an empty scene presented as new (DT-12) |
| Seeded / Unclaimed | Corroborated (D-06) and published | Record with a visible **derived / unclaimed** marker, its source basis, and a claim entry point. Not search-indexed (DT-14) |
| Reported | ≥1 qualifying report | Record plus an "accuracy disputed" banner; still visible, still claimable |
| Suppressed | ≥3 distinct qualifying reporters within 30 days | Removed from scene surfaces; reachable by direct link; still claimable. Never auto-deleted |
| Claim Pending | Claim submitted, verification in flight | `Claim Pending` with submission timestamp; supplied corrections held, not published; target decision ≤5 business days |
| Claimed | Verification passed | Authoritative, authored record. Seed writes stopped permanently (D-08). Search indexing available at claimant's option |
| Disputed | 2+ verified claimants on one record | Both claims held; routes to domain 16 with the date-split shape as the expected resolution (venue handover), escalating to 24 only where authority itself is contested. Record shows contested ownership rather than silently favouring one party |
| Stale | Claimed=No **and** no corroborating activity in 12 months | Marked stale, not silently persisted as fact. **12 months** because a grassroots room hosting nothing for a full year has either closed or stopped being a music venue — and a year covers seasonal and festival-site venues that a shorter window would wrongly kill |
| Delisted | Verified claimant removed it | Gone from all surfaces. Re-seedable unless Refused |
| Refused | Claimant delisted with permanent refusal | Never re-seeded. The tombstone itself is data held about a non-participant — see Q-03 |
| Partial | Some seed fields corroborated, others not | Corroborated fields shown; uncorroborated fields absent, not guessed. A premises with a confirmed address and no confirmed capacity shows no capacity — per 03.03.01 D-03 and 03.04.01 D-03, "unknown" is stated, never inferred |
| Error | Ingestion or claim submission failed | Retry. A failed ingestion publishes nothing — the platform's default on error is silence, not a guess |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The domain's stated cold-start strategy had no mechanism in the candidate list** | Deep Think: reading the domain overview's own claim against its 16 candidates | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is why the feature exists. The overview asserts local scenes are "the only tractable cold-start strategy" because "200 users in one city feels alive, 20,000 scattered globally feels dead". But every candidate (join a scene, browse a scene, moderate a scene) presupposes the 200 users already exist. The strategy was a *restatement of the problem* — scenes solve cold start once scenes are populated, and populating them is the cold start. The only escape is that the scene must not start at zero, which means deriving it from facts that exist independently of the platform's adoption. Without this feature the domain's headline claim is circular. |
| DT-02 | Seeding venues is uncontroversial because the data is public | The convenient assumption | ❌ REJECTED: listing a real business on a platform it never joined, with specs it did not supply, is a real act with real consequences. Per personas.md the Operator's *own* worst accidental behaviour is publishing a stale gear list that causes a band to arrive at a room that cannot run their show — and seeding does exactly that, on their behalf, without their knowledge. It is also a pattern that has drawn real hostility toward map and review products (businesses listed without consent, unable to correct their own entry). The mitigations — visibly-derived status, an easy opt-out, claiming as the correction path — are all cheap, and skipping them turns the growth strategy into an antagonism campaign against the exact persona domains 16/17/19 depend on. |
| DT-03 | Seeded and real records can look the same | Simplicity; it makes the scene look better | ❌ REJECTED: a derived record is an *inference*, and a claimed one is an *assertion by the responsible party*. Presenting them identically means the platform is vouching for facts it guessed. Per problem-statement.md the product exists because the industry's records are unreliable reconstructions — shipping unmarked inferences as facts on day one would reproduce, as the platform's own founding data, precisely the defect it was built to fix. Seed records must be visibly derived and visibly unclaimed. This is the same principle as 03.03.01 D-03 (degraded results must be labelled) and 03.04.01 D-03 ("unknown" ≠ "no path") — a **domain-level rule** about never overstating what is known, appearing for the third time. |
| DT-04 | Failure mode at the intersection with the graph: seeding cannot bootstrap collaboration edges | Reasoning against 03.04.01 D-01 | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is an honest limit on this feature, cross-referenced from 03.06.02 DT-03. Public credit data could theoretically seed edges ("these four played on that record") — but 03.04.01 D-01 says edges come only from **counter-attested** credits on this platform, precisely so the graph cannot be forged. Seeding edges from public data would inject exactly the unverified claims the graph exists to exclude, and would do it at scale, in the platform's own voice. So: scenes can be seeded, the graph cannot. The consequence is a seeded scene with visible population and zero density — see DT-13, which resolves it. |
| DT-05 | A venue is identified by its name | The obvious modelling choice, and what every source's data looks like | ❌ REJECTED — **a room outlives its name and its operator.** This is the single most music-specific fact about venue data and nothing upstream mentions it. The room you played in Bristol in 2019 is very often the same four walls, load-in and ceiling height under a different trading name, a different licensee and a different PA. Grassroots venues rebrand, change hands and reopen constantly. If the seed keys on the trading name: the same room appears twice from two sources, a claim on one leaves the other unclaimed and contradicting it, and the room's real continuity — its capacity, its access, its curfew, all of which are properties of the *building* — is fragmented across records that each know a fraction. Worse, the previous operator's history attaches to the new one. So the seeded unit is the **premises**, keyed by physical location, carrying a trading-name history; a claim is a claim on the room, dated from the claimant's tenure. This one decision is what makes DT-06's duplicate merge and the handover case (both-claims-legitimate) tractable rather than pathological. |
| DT-06 | A single reliable source is enough to publish a seed record | Efficiency; sources cost money and licensing, so use the one you licensed | ❌ REJECTED, and the reason is not the obvious one. The obvious objection is accuracy. The non-obvious one: **directory and map products have long seeded deliberate fictions into their own data to detect copying** — trap streets, paper towns, fictitious dictionary entries and phantom listings all exist for exactly this purpose. A WeJammin venue list derived from a single scraped competitor source will, with near-certainty, contain that competitor's trap entries. The platform then publishes a venue that does not exist — which is (a) a direct violation of D-01, committed unknowingly, in the platform's own voice, on the founding data of the city it is trying to win, and (b) self-authenticating evidence of copying, sitting on a public page, which is precisely what the trap was built to produce. The fix is the same as the fix for ordinary accuracy: **≥2 independent sources or 1 first-party fact** (D-06). A trap entry exists in exactly one source by construction — that is what makes it a trap — so corroboration eliminates the entire class for free. Rarely does a legal hazard and a quality bar have the same remedy; take it. |
| DT-07 | An "easy opt-out" (D-03) and a verified claim are two separate flows | The existing decision as originally written | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **the two cannot be separated, and D-03 as written is self-contradictory.** To remove your venue's listing you must show you speak for the venue; that is the claim verification burden. An opt-out *easier* than a claim is an unverified delete button on a real business's presence — pointed at by a competitor, it removes a rival's room from the map with no proof and no recourse. An opt-out *as hard as* a claim is a claim. So there is one flow with two exits: **claim, then delist**, with delist as the first post-claim action. This repairs D-03's intent rather than discarding it: the intent was that a listed-without-consent party must not have to file a complaint and wait. They don't — they take one action (claim) that is on the record itself, and the exit is immediately available. The unverified path still exists but confers a *signal*, not an *authority*: reports suppress (downrank + banner), never delete. This is the general rule the whole feature runs on — **verification gates authority, never visibility of the objection.** |
| DT-08 | Seed data should be refreshed continuously from source | Obviously right — stale data is the enemy (DT-02, and the Stale state exists) | ❌ REJECTED for claimed records, and this is the highest-stakes rule here. Once claimed, the operator's assertion is authoritative — that is the entire point of D-02's derived/claimed distinction. A refresh pipeline that overwrites claimed data with source data silently un-does the operator's correction. The Operator then corrects their room's capacity, watches it revert overnight, corrects it again, watches it revert, and concludes the platform is broken and hostile — and they are right. That is the most predictable, most self-inflicted way to destroy the exact relationship domains 16, 17 and 19 are built on, and it would be introduced by a background job nobody designed as a product decision. So: **a claim permanently ends seed writes to that record (D-08).** Refresh may still *notify* the claimant of a divergence ("the licensing register lists a different capacity") — that is useful, it is how a genuinely stale claimed record gets fixed, and it leaves the correction where it belongs: with the person responsible for the room. |
| DT-09 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody has mentioned: **"400 documented gigs" is 400 announcements, and an announcement is not a performance** | Reasoning about what public gig data actually is | ✅ CONFIRMED and it goes straight at the thesis. Every public gig source is a **listings** source: it records that a show was advertised. Cancellations, postponements and quiet no-shows are endemic — they are the normal failure of live music, not an edge case, and the listing is very often never updated. So the seeded Bristol scene would assert that 400 gigs *happened* on evidence that only supports 400 gigs were *announced*. Read that against problem-statement.md: the product exists because the industry records facts by reconstruction from unreliable traces, and here the platform's own founding dataset would be exactly that — an inference about the past dressed as a record of it. It also fails on contact with the same person 03.06.01 DT-04 warns about: a Bristol musician knows which of those shows were pulled. And the Fan carries the cost (personas.md: "buys a ticket for the wrong city/date" is already their worst accidental behaviour; the platform pre-loading cancelled shows industrialises it). Hence D-09: seeded events are **Announced**. Performed requires first-party evidence — a settlement (17) or a counter-attested credit (02) — which by definition a seed does not have. The pleasing consequence: "Announced → Performed" is a state transition the platform earns by being in the room, which is D-18's whole argument, visible in the seed data's own lifecycle. |
| DT-10 | Failure mode at the intersection of the multi-hyphenate and the venue claim: **an Operator-Musician can edit the record of their own performance** | Reasoning about personas.md's structural fact against the claim's scope | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the intersection failure nobody designs for. One human, two roles, one day (personas.md's opening claim). They own a Bristol room and they play in a band. They claim their room. If a venue claim confers authority over the room's *event records*, they can now edit the historical record of a gig they performed at their own venue — adjusting the billing, the date, the lineup — as an interested party, over the platform's founding provenance data, with a credential the platform issued them for a different purpose. It is not even obviously abuse from inside: it is one screen, it says "your venue, your gigs", and the edit looks like housekeeping. The scope rule is therefore explicit (D-10): **a venue claim grants the room's forward-looking specs, calendar and listing, and nothing about who historically performed there.** Historical performance facts are corrected only through domain 02's attestation, which requires the other parties to counter-attest — the same mechanism that protects the graph (03.04.01 D-01) protecting the seed. This also bounds the impostor-claim blast radius (an impostor gets a room's listing, not the ability to rewrite the city's history) and is why that edge case is survivable rather than fatal. |
| DT-11 | Seeding is a places-and-events question, and people can be added later | The framing inherited from the original Q-02 | ❌ REJECTED as a deferral — the answer is available now and the default must be **no person-records, ever, and no residential addresses, ever**. Two separate arguments, both music-specific. **(a) People**: a seeded person-record is a public shadow profile. 03.05.01 built its entire design around the fact that a shadow record mis-resolving attaches statements to an innocent person invisibly (DT-02 there), and made reconciliation author-confirmed to stop it — at a scale of one author, privately. Seeding people does the same thing publicly, at city scale, with no author to confirm anything, in a domain (music) where name collision is routine. And per 03.04.01 D-01 a seeded person node has no edges, so it is a dead node that exists only to be wrong about someone. Most damning: personas.md names "a credit that went to someone else" as a Musician's *switching trigger* — the platform would be publishing guessed credits about people who never signed up, which is the precise harm it exists to fix, committed as its opening move. Billed artist **names** still appear, as attributes of a seeded event ("The Louisiana, 4 March — Idles"), which is republishing the event's own public billing and creates no entity to mis-resolve. That preserves most of the recognition the happy path needs. **(b) Residential addresses**: house shows, DIY gigs and home studios are a huge and real part of music, and their addresses are deliberately semi-private — "address on RSVP" is the norm precisely because the room is someone's home. Public listings contain these anyway. Seeding them publishes private homes as music venues, at scale, with the platform's name on it. Dropped at ingestion regardless of corroboration (D-11). The accepted cost is a permanent coverage gap in exactly the rooms where much production happens — and per personas.md the Producer's own studio is often one of them, which is why the honest Producer experience is "my city's rooms are here, mine is not, I add it." |
| DT-12 | Seeding coverage is a data-engineering problem that improves with effort | The implicit assumption in "derive the scene from public data" | ❌ REJECTED — **coverage inverts with need, and no amount of effort fixes it.** Public gig and venue data is rich where the market is large and thin where it is small: London and New York are exhaustively documented; a small city's scene lives in a Facebook group and a WhatsApp thread. So the cold-start strategy delivers its best experience exactly where the platform faces the fiercest incumbents and the least differentiated value, and its worst experience exactly where its value proposition is strongest — the musician nobody can find, in a city no listings site covers. That is a strategic inversion, not a data gap, and it is invisible from inside a seeding pipeline that reports coverage as an average. Two consequences the owner should hold: launch-city selection is a **product** decision that this data constrains rather than serves (Q-04), and the `Unseeded` state must be a real, honest, designed state rather than an error — because it is the state of the cities the platform most needs to win. |
| DT-13 | The seeded-population / zero-density contradiction (03.06.02 Q-01) requires choosing between hiding density and forging edges | The framing both features inherited | ❌ REJECTED — the framing is the bug, and the contradiction dissolves once the two numbers are read for their **subjects**. "60 venues, 400 announced gigs" is a fact about **Bristol**. "Zero evidenced collaborations" is a fact about **WeJammin**. They are answers to different questions and were only ever contradictory because they were imagined on one axis, as a single "scene health" reading. Put them on one axis and every option is bad: blend them and the platform lies about one of the two; hide density and the platform conceals its own thinness (violating 03.06.02 D-03); seed edges and it forges the graph (violating 03.04.01 D-01). Split them by subject and all three problems vanish at once, because the honest sentence is available and reads well: *"This is Bristol: 60 rooms, 400 announced gigs. This is WeJammin in Bristol: nobody yet — be first."* Nothing is hidden, nothing is inflated, no edge is forged, and the zero stops being a verdict on Bristol and becomes a statement about the platform's age, which is what it always was. The number never changed; the implied subject did, and that was the entire defect. This is the same discipline as D-02 and 03.04.01 D-03 — never overstate what is known, and be precise about *what* is known — applied to a number instead of a record. Resolves 03.06.02 Q-01 and this feature's former Q-03, subject to owner ratification. |
| DT-14 | Seeded pages should be search-indexed — indexed pages are how Operators discover their listing and claim it | The growth argument, and it is genuinely the strongest one for indexing | ❌ REJECTED: it is also, precisely, the argument that made directory products hated. An unclaimed seed record is an inference. Indexing it publishes the platform's *guess* about a real business to the entire internet, where it competes with — and can outrank — that business's own page, and where the "derived / unclaimed" marker that makes it honest on-platform (D-02) does not survive a search snippet. The Operator's first contact with WeJammin then becomes discovering that a stranger's page about their venue, containing specs they never supplied and possibly wrong (DT-02), is what their customers find when they search the room's name. That is the antagonism DT-02 warns about, delivered at maximum blast radius, with the platform's growth as the stated motive. So: **unclaimed seed records are not indexed; a claim makes the record indexable at the claimant's option, defaulting on.** The growth cost is real and accepted — indexed pages would drive claims. The replacement discovery path is the claim link on the record, shareable and sendable, which is how the Operator actually arrives (someone sends it to them), and it inverts the relationship: the platform's public voice about your business begins when you say it may, not before. |
