# Feature: Scene Stewardship & Local Moderation

> **Number**: 03.06.03
> **Parent**: [Scenes & Communities](./03.06-scenes-communities-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who looks after a scene, and what "looking after" means. Per D-05 on the parent, a steward is **not a fifth
persona** — the sweep's provisional "scene organiser" is a Musician, Producer or Operator wearing another
hat, consistent with personas.md's multi-hyphenate structural fact. Stewardship is local governance: keeping
a scene's definition honest, welcoming arrivals, and handling the moderation that a distant platform team
cannot judge (is this person actually from Bristol? is this beef or abuse?).

Per `03.06-scenes-communities-cx.md#CX-03` it is probably **density-gated** — a 30-person scene governs
itself, and appointing a steward over it manufactures a politics the scene does not have.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | May steward a scene they are part of — per personas.md this is the multi-hyphenate wearing another hat, and it is unpaid work they do because they care about the scene. That is also the risk: unpaid volunteer moderation is a well-known burnout and capture surface, and the platform would be extracting real labour for free (DT-03). |
| Producer | Config | May steward a craft scene, where their standing is evidenced by work rather than claimed. Per personas.md they will not leave the DAW — so a steward role demanding regular attention will be abandoned, and an abandoned steward is worse than none (they hold authority nobody can use). |
| Operator | Config | The most natural geo-scene steward: per personas.md their room is where the scene physically happens, and they already know everyone locally. Also the most conflicted — a venue owner governing the scene that supplies and buys from them has an obvious commercial interest in who is prominent in it (DT-02). |
| Fan | Read-only | Per D-03 fans are members of geo scenes and are subject to stewardship without holding it. Per D-13a they are the majority by headcount in any geo scene, and per D-13b bring a distinct moderation profile — so a fan-majority scene is *governed by* professionals but *populated by* fans, which is a legitimacy question (Q-03). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the steward's actual powers are unresolved and are the
feature's central question. The plausible range, from weakest to strongest:

| Power | Risk |
|---|---|
| Curate a scene's featured content | Low — taste, not authority |
| Welcome/onboard arrivals | Low |
| Correct a scene's definition or boundary | Medium — boundaries are contested (parent Q-01) |
| Remove content from the scene | High — content moderation by an interested party |
| Remove *people* from the scene | Highest — exclusion from a local professional community is a livelihood act |

The last is where this feature stops being community management and becomes something a platform should
think very hard about (DT-01).

Per parent Q-04, who appoints stewards is unresolved; claiming (03.06.04) is one candidate answer, and
`#R-02` forecloses the graph-centrality answer.

### Happy Path

1. A scene crosses a density threshold where governance is needed (`#CX-03`)
2. A steward is established — by claiming, appointment, or another route (parent Q-04)
3. Steward curates, welcomes, and handles local judgement calls
4. Escalations that exceed local judgement route to domain 24

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Scene capture** — the loudest or most commercially-interested person governs the city | The classic local-community failure. Per DT-02 the Operator is simultaneously the most natural steward and the most conflicted | Parent Q-04. Self-appointment invites this directly |
| Steward excludes a professional rival | Exclusion from a local scene is a livelihood act performed by a competitor | DT-01 — this is why removal powers are the wrong default |
| Steward abandons the role | Per personas.md, likely — they have paid work to do. An abandoned steward holds authority nobody can exercise | `[PENDING]` — needs lapse/rotation |
| Fan-majority scene governed by professionals | Legitimate on professional grounds, illegitimate on democratic ones | Q-03 |
| Steward is the abuse | Local authority is exactly what a determined bad actor would seek | Escalation to 24 must never route *through* the steward |
| Nobody wants the job | The likeliest outcome in a thin scene, and stewardship is unnecessary there anyway | `#CX-03` — density-gate it |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Ungoverned | Below density threshold | No stewardship — correct for a small scene |
| Unclaimed | Above threshold, no steward | Scene runs on platform defaults; claimable |
| Stewarded | Steward active | Curated scene |
| Lapsed | Steward inactive | `[PENDING]` — reverts to unclaimed |
| Escalated | Issue exceeds local judgement | Domain 24, bypassing the steward |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Stewards should be able to remove people from a scene | The obvious community-moderation power; every forum has it | ❌ REJECTED as a default: this is not a subreddit. Per personas.md a geo scene is a **local professional community**, and being excluded from it is being excluded from the map of who works in your city — a livelihood act, performed by a peer who is frequently a direct competitor. The platform would be delegating an economic sanction to an interested party with no due process. Curation powers (what is featured) are a different order from exclusion powers (who exists here), and conflating them because both are called "moderation" is how this feature would do real harm. Removal belongs to domain 24, with process. |
| DT-02 | The Operator is the natural steward | Their room is the scene's physical centre; personas.md | ⏳ DEFERRED — genuinely two-sided. **For**: per personas.md they know everyone locally, they are already the anchor, and their reputation "accrues from shows that went smoothly", so they have a real stake in the scene's health. **Against**: they buy from and sell to the people they would govern. A venue owner deciding who is prominent in the Bristol scene has an obvious commercial interest, and their anti-persona behaviour (personas.md: "misrepresents the room's specs to win bookings") shows the incentive to distort exists. Not resolvable by an agent — recorded as Q-02. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **stewardship is unpaid labour the platform is extracting** | Reasoning about volunteer moderation as an economic fact | ✅ CONFIRMED and it deserves an explicit product position rather than silence. Volunteer moderation is real work — Reddit, Discord and Stack Overflow all run on it, and all three have had public crises about exactly this. Per personas.md every candidate steward is a working professional with "a portfolio of small income streams" whose time is literally their income; asking them to do unpaid governance is asking them to fund the platform's operations from their own earnings. The options (pay them, give them platform benefits, keep powers so small the labour is trivial, or don't build it) are all defensible, but drifting into unpaid volunteer moderation *by default* is how this becomes a grievance. Note it also interacts with D-04 on the parent: seeded scenes have no volunteers by definition. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Stewards are Musicians/Producers/Operators in a role, not a fifth persona (parent D-05) | D-19 ratifies four personas; personas.md's multi-hyphenate fact covers this natively. |
| D-02 | Stewards do not remove people from scenes | DT-01 — exclusion from a local professional community is a livelihood act and belongs to 24 with process. |
| D-03 | Stewardship is density-gated | `#CX-03` — a 30-person scene needs no governance; appointing one manufactures politics. |
| D-04 | Escalation to domain 24 never routes through the steward | The steward may be the problem; a local authority must not gate the appeal against itself. |
