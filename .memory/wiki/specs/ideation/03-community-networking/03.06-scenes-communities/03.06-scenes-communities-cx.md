## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Seeding & Claiming ↔ Definition & Membership

**Relationship**: This is the pair the domain's entire cold-start strategy rests on. Per D-04, a scene must
be alive **before** its first real member arrives, because an empty scene does not read as "early" — it
reads as "dead", and it delivers that verdict at the precise moment a new user is deciding whether the
platform is worth their time. Seeding inverts the normal community-product bootstrap: instead of asking the
first 20 users to create a place and populate it (which none of them will do), the platform derives the
scene from facts that already exist in the world — the venues in a city (domain 16), the gigs happening
there (17), the artists credited on records made there (02) — so that the Bristol scene has 60 venues and
400 documented gigs on the day the first Bristol user signs up.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: arrives and finds their city already mapped, including people they know. Per personas.md their switching trigger includes "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin" — a seeded scene manufactures a weaker version of that recognition without needing the collaborator to have arrived first.
- **Producer**: sees the studios and the sessions of their city. Per personas.md they will not do setup work; a seeded scene means their first screen has content without a single configuration action.
- **Operator**: the most affected — per 03.06.04 DT-02, their venue is likely *in* the seed data, meaning they may find themselves already listed before signing up. That is powerful (their room is on the map) and hazardous (their room is on the map without their consent, possibly with stale specs — the exact failure personas.md names: "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show").
- **Fan**: per D-03 they are geo-scene members; the seeded scene is their local gig map, which per personas.md is precisely what they came for.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: A seeded scene is platform-authored; a claimed one is community-owned. The conflict is at handover — per CX-04, when a steward claims it, who owns the seed data they did not create? If they can edit it, the platform's derived facts and the steward's edits diverge with no reconciliation rule.
2. **Trigger chain**: Seeding creates the scene → a user joins → density crosses a threshold → stewardship activates (CX-03). If seeding fails for a region (no venue data), that city has no scene and its users see the empty state the strategy exists to prevent.
3. **Permission intersection**: Membership of a seeded scene must not imply any authority over the seed data. Otherwise the first person to join Bristol owns Bristol.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A seeded scene has no members to notify — which is its point. It generates content without generating obligation.
5. **State transition conflict**: A seeded entity (a venue) whose real owner later joins and claims it (domain 16) must reconcile with the seed — the same reconciliation problem as the rolodex's shadow contacts (03.05.01), appearing a second time in this domain and probably needing one shared answer.

### CX-02: Definition & Membership ↔ Scene Graph & Density

**Relationship**: Density is the only metric that means anything about a scene, and it is why the merged
geo/genre/craft mechanism (D-01) still needs geo to be primary (D-02). Membership counts are vanity; what
matters is whether the members are *connected* — 200 people in Bristol who have played together is a scene,
and 200 unconnected people in Bristol is a mailing list. The density map reads the collaboration graph
(03.04.01) filtered to a scene's membership, which means a scene's health is measured in evidenced
collaborations, not signups. That is the domain thesis (D-18) applied to community: a scene is real by
construction or it is not real at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: read density to judge whether a scene is worth their attention, and appear in it as nodes.
- **Operator**: density around their room is their catchment — commercially, the most actionable read of this data on the platform.
- **Fan**: no access to the graph (parent Role Matrix). A map of who works with whom, exposed to a consumer population, is the same exposure 03.04.01 declines to make.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Membership is owned by 03.06.01; the density map is a derived read. No write conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: Joining changes density; density crossing thresholds may activate stewardship (CX-03) or change ranking weights (03.03.02 DT-03's density-aware proximity).
3. **Permission intersection**: The density map must respect the graph's own visibility rules — a scene view must not become the browsable industry-wide graph that 03.04.01 Q-02 declines to build.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None directly.
5. **State transition conflict**: Density is a moving read of a moving graph; no consistency requirement beyond eventual.
