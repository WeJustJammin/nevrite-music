# Feature: Follows (Asymmetric)

> **Number**: 03.01.01
> **Parent**: [Connections, Follows & Endorsements](./03.01-connections-follows-endorsements-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The cheap, unilateral, no-consent edge: I want to know what you do next. A follow requires nothing from
the target and obliges them to nothing, which is what makes it the correct edge for the overwhelming
majority of the graph's volume — every Fan→artist relationship, and every "I track this producer's work"
relationship between professionals who have never met. It is the read set the activity feed fans out to
(03.02.01), it is the carrier of the alert path that D-11 makes load-bearing for the Fan persona
(03.02.02 D-04), and it is the graceful fallback when a professional connection is ineligible or declined
(`03.01-connections-follows-endorsements-cx.md#CX-02`).

It is also the **only edge on the platform that carries no user-authored content**. A follow has no note,
no message, no free text — the follower asserts nothing about the target and publishes nothing to anyone.
That is not an accident of scope; it is the structural reason a follow is the correct Fan-facing edge and
a connection is not (D-02 on the parent), and it is why this feature carries the platform's largest edge
volume at the platform's smallest moderation cost.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Follows other musicians, producers, bands, studios and venues to track releases, credits and gig announcements. Being followed is a career metric they will read as reach — and per personas.md they are also the *entity* being followed via their band, so their own follower count and their band's are distinct numbers they will compare, and their multi-hyphenate output means their followers receive events from up to 8 domains at once (DT-05). Per D-08 they must choose which acting entity a follow is made as — the "Follow" they press on their phone is made by whichever identity is active, and getting that wrong silently routes their alerts to the wrong feed. |
| Producer | Full | Follows collaborators and studios; their follower list is largely other professionals rather than fans, so follower count is a poor vanity metric for them and a useful supply signal ("who is watching my output for hire signals"). Per personas.md they will not tend a feed, so the only part of this feature they experience is the **notification** of a new follower — which is exactly why D-06's graph-derived notification tiering matters more for them than for anyone: a digest they ignore costs nothing, but the one follow from a named engineer is a hire signal they must not miss. |
| Operator | Full | Follows the acts they book and the local scene; their followers are the musicians and fans who want to know what is on at the room. A venue's follower list is effectively their local demand signal, and is the closest thing this domain has to their marketing channel. **The highest-volume legitimate follower on the platform** — a booker tracking every act in a 200-mile radius may follow thousands, which is why D-09 rejects a follow cap in favour of a rate limit. Per 03.06.04 their venue may have accrued followers *before they ever signed up* (DT-08). |
| Fan | Full | The **only** graph edge available to them (D-02 on the parent). Follows artists, bands and venues; the follow is the promise that they will be told when that artist does something actionable near them — which is the Fan's entire success criterion per personas.md ("never misses a show by an artist they follow"). Per D-05 their follow carries an alert scope so that following a working musician for gigs does not deliver that musician's cymbal listings. Per D-04 their follow grants the artist **no right to contact them** — that is domain 20's consent surface, not this edge. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A follow is a directed edge from a follower entity to a followed entity, created in one action, revocable
in one action, requiring no approval and producing no notification on revocation. It is the primary input
to feed fan-out (03.02.01) and to the alert routing that D-11 makes load-bearing for the Fan persona.

Per D-04 on the parent, both endpoints are **entities**, not necessarily people: a band follows a venue, a
studio follows a label. The follower count displayed on a profile is therefore a count of entities, and a
human who follows an artist both personally and via their band has produced two edges — which is correct,
not a bug, and is the reason D-07 forbids labelling any follower count as a count of *people*.

### The acting-entity problem

Because both endpoints are entities and because personas.md's first structural fact is that one human
routinely occupies three personas in a day, **"who is following" is never implicit**. A drummer who is
also in two bands and co-owns a studio has four entities that can hold this edge. Per D-08 the Follow
control resolves an explicit acting entity, defaults to the personal identity, and renders its state for
*that entity only* — never the union. A control showing "Following" because the user's band follows the
target, when the user personally does not, is a silent alert-routing failure the user cannot detect: they
believe they will be told about the gig, and they will not be.

### What the edge carries

The edge carries exactly one piece of reader intent — an **alert scope** (D-05) — and nothing else. It
carries no note, no relationship label, no privacy flag. Scope is a bounded enum, not a per-event-type
matrix: per personas.md the Producer will not leave the DAW to configure anything and the Fan "will not
tolerate professional-tool complexity", so the default is the real interface (03.02.02 Role Lens) and any
design depending on configuration has already lost both personas.

### What a follow is not

A follow is **not** a marketing consent, **not** a fan record, and **not** a mailing-list subscription
(D-04). Domain 20 holds the per-(fan, artist-entity) record and its legal basis (20.01.02); this feature
holds a graph edge. The artist will assume otherwise (DT-04) and the product must not let them.

### Happy Path

1. User opens an entity profile (Musician, band, studio, venue) — the Follow control renders in an
   indeterminate state and resolves current edge state for the **acting entity** within P95 < 200 ms
2. User selects Follow — the control applies optimistically and switches to a followed state in the same frame
3. System creates the edge with no approval step; the write is idempotent per (follower entity, followed
   entity), so a double-click, a two-device race, or a retry yields one edge and one count increment
4. System confirms the edge and states the promise in concrete terms rather than a generic success toast —
   "You'll be told when {entity} announces a show near {reader's city}" — because per personas.md the Fan's
   success criterion is the alert, and an unstated promise is an unmeasurable one
5. The followed entity's future feed-eligible events begin fanning out to the follower's feed (03.02.01),
   filtered by the edge's alert scope (D-05); alert-class events bypass ranking entirely (03.02.02 D-04)
6. The followed entity is notified **only if** the new follower is within 2 hops of their collaboration
   graph (03.04.01) or shares an active scene (03.06.01); every other new follower accrues to a daily
   digest, at most one per day (D-06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Following an entity that has blocked you** | Edge creation is refused; the block is a cross-cut owned by domain 24 and is authoritative over this feature | The follow control is absent, and the profile is not reachable at all — a refusal message would confirm the block exists |
| **You have blocked the target, then attempt to follow** | Refused. Following someone you blocked is incoherent; the platform must not silently unblock to satisfy the action | The Follow control is absent; the profile renders with the block state and an unblock affordance. Unblocking does not restore any prior follow — the edge was destroyed, not suspended (see below) |
| **Block lands while a follow write is in flight** | The block wins unconditionally. Per `#CX-02` note 5, blocking must collapse the follow and the connection atomically — a block that severs the connection but leaves the follow means the blocked party keeps receiving content, which is a harassment-adjacent failure | The follower's optimistic UI reverts to an unfollowed state and the profile becomes unreachable, with no explanation (an explanation discloses the block) |
| **Followed entity is deleted or merged** (duplicate band records reconciled in domain 01) | Edges re-point to the surviving entity rather than dangle. If the follower already followed **both** records, the two edges collapse to one — the follower count decrements by one and this is correct, not data loss | Follow silently persists against the merged entity; no user-visible event. The count change is not announced |
| **Follow write lands mid-merge** (target entity is being reconciled in domain 01) | The write must land on the surviving entity or be retried after the merge completes. It must never create an edge against a record that is about to disappear | Optimistic state holds; reconciliation is invisible. If it cannot resolve within the retry budget it reverts with the generic retry affordance |
| **Follower entity is deleted** (user deletes their account) | All outbound edges are removed and every followed entity's count decrements. Per D-07 the count is a live audience number or it is a lie | Followed entities see no notification — an unfollow is never notified (D-03), and a deletion is an unfollow the user did not consciously make |
| **Follower entity is suspended** (domain 24 enforcement) | The edge persists but is excluded from counts and from fan-out for the duration of the suspension. A restored account restores its edges | The followed entity's count drops with no explanation; per domain 24's disclosure model the enforcement action is not narrated to third parties |
| **Followed entity is suspended** | Fan-out stops; the edge persists **dormant** so that a restored entity does not lose its audience to a temporary enforcement action. Permanent removal deletes the edges | Followers see the entity stop producing events. No notice — a notice would disclose an enforcement action, which is domain 24's call to make, not this feature's |
| **Following your own entity** (a drummer opening their own band's profile) | Refused. A member already receives their entity's activity by membership (domain 01), and self-follow is a free follower-count increment for anyone with entities to spare | The Follow control is replaced by a membership indicator, not greyed out. An entity may never follow itself |
| **Following a Fan account** | The control is not rendered. Per 03.02.01's Role Lens a Fan "emits almost nothing (reactions, at most)" — following one subscribes to an empty stream. This is evidence bearing on parent Q-02 (Fan↔Fan edges), derived from the feed's event-source model rather than decided here (DT-07) | No Follow control on a Fan profile. Nothing to explain, because nothing is missing |
| **Following a seeded, unclaimed record** (a venue derived by 03.06.04 that has never signed up) | Permitted, and the edges are held against the derived record. On claim, the Operator inherits an audience they did not build. Per domain law D-04 (never overstate what is known) the followers followed an *inference*; when it becomes an *assertion by a responsible party*, they are told | Follower sees a one-time notice that the venue they followed is now operated by its real owner, with an unfollow affordance in the same surface. The Operator sees their inherited follower count marked as pre-claim in origin (DT-08) |
| **Fan follows an artist with no upcoming activity** | Edge is created but produces nothing — the cold-start failure that makes the follow feel broken on day one (DT-03) | The follow's confirmation states the promise, not a result: "You'll be told when {artist} announces a show near {city} — nothing scheduled right now." The artist's profile shows an explicit *nothing yet* state rather than an empty list, and where 03.06.04 seed data covers the region, documented past gigs render as history so the profile is not blank. The failure this avoids is a Fan concluding on day one that the follow did nothing |
| **Followed entity emits across domains the follower did not want** (a Fan follows a working musician for gigs; the musician lists a cymbal in 13, confirms a credit in 02, and frees a studio Tuesday in 16) | The alert scope on the edge (D-05) filters at fan-out. Without it, personas.md's multi-hyphenate structural fact makes every followed professional a multi-domain firehose | Fan receives gigs and releases; professional event classes never render for them (03.02.01 Role Lens: "professional event classes are noise to them") |
| **Bulk/automated following** (a growth-hack bot, or a scraper enumerating the graph) | Rate limited per **acting human account, aggregated across every entity they act for** — 60 follow-creations per rolling hour, 500 per rolling 24 hours. Per-entity limits would be trivially evaded by the multi-hyphenate's four entities. Sustained breach across many pairs routes to domain 24 as an abuse signal, not as a rate-limit refusal | A legible refusal naming the limit and the reset time — "You've followed a lot today. You can follow again in 3h 12m." Per 03.01.02's precedent, a limit that is not legible reads as a bug and generates a support ticket instead of a behaviour change |
| **Onboarding import trips the rate limiter** ("import who I already listen to" — personas.md Fan workflow step 1) | The import is **one reviewed action**, not N follow actions, and is exempt from the per-hour limit. Bounded at 2,000 entities per import and 3 imports per rolling 30 days. Without the exemption the product's own stated onboarding path fails the abuse control that exists to protect it | A review screen listing what will be followed, with the count stated, before anything is written. Follows land as a single batch |
| **Follow/unfollow churn** ("follow-bait" — repeatedly following to reappear in a target's notifications) | ≥3 follow→unfollow cycles on the same pair within 7 days pins the pair to its current state for 24 hours and refuses further toggles. Independently, a re-follow of the same pair within 30 days generates **no** notification regardless of graph distance — the notification is the payload the abuse is after | The abuser sees the pin and its expiry. The target sees nothing, which is the point |
| **Concurrent follow and unfollow in flight** (two devices, or a rapid double-toggle) | Writes serialize per (follower, followed) pair; the resolved state is the one matching the user's **last observed intent**, not the last request to arrive. Arrival-order-wins produces a state the user did not choose and cannot explain | The control settles on the state the user last selected. No error |
| **Network failure mid-write** | Optimistic UI holds; 3 retries at 1s / 2s / 4s. A follow is idempotent, so retry is always safe. After the budget the control reverts to its true prior state with an explicit retry affordance — never a silent revert, which reads as the tap not registering | "Couldn't follow {entity} — retry" with the control returned to its real state |
| **Offline follow** (personas.md: the Operator works "on a phone at a loading dock") | Queued with a 24-hour TTL and replayed on reconnect. Multiple queued toggles on one pair collapse to the final intent — a queue that replays follow→unfollow→follow as three writes generates two spurious notifications | Control shows the intended state with a pending indicator. On expiry the queued action is dropped and the user is told, rather than silently discarded |
| **Malicious input** | There is no input. A follow carries no user-authored content — no note, no message, no label. The only abusable surfaces are **volume** (rate limit), **churn** (the pin above), and **the notification it generates** (D-06) | N/A — and this is the structural reason the follow is the correct Fan-facing edge (D-02 on the parent): the highest-volume edge on the platform has the lowest moderation cost |
| **Follower count inflated by purchased followers** | Sybil resistance is a cross-cut to domain 24, not solvable inside this feature. The consequence is sharper than vanity: per DT-06 a follower count is booking currency in domain 17, so buying followers is **revenue-motivated fraud**, not ego. Mitigated structurally by domain law D-03 — evidence outranks participation, so follows are never a ranking or matching input (03.02.02 D-02) | N/A — surfaces as degraded ranking quality elsewhere, and as a bad guarantee in a booking negotiation |
| **Third party enumerates an entity's follower list** | Refused. A public follower roster is a harvestable audience list — a competitor's mailing list, a spammer's target set, and a scalper's demand map. An entity's own audience is domain 20's fan record (20.01.01), not a public roster on this profile | No public followers roster is rendered for any entity, to any persona. The entity sees its own audience in domain 20, where consent and legal basis are modelled |
| **A third party reads "who X follows"** | Per DT-09 a following list leaks strategic intent in music specifically. Following lists are visible to the acting entity only | Own following list is a management surface. No third-party view |
| **Multi-hyphenate follows as the wrong entity** | The edge is created against the wrong follower entity, so alerts route to an entity's feed rather than the person's. This is a silent failure — the user believes they are subscribed | Per D-08 the control names the acting entity when more than one is available ("Follow as: Rob / Nightshift / Foundry Studio"), and the confirmation names it back. The cost of the extra affordance is one line; the cost of getting it wrong is a missed gig, which is the Fan's and the Musician's single stated failure |
| **A band splits; who keeps the followers?** | The band entity's followers belong to the band entity. A departing member takes nothing and arrives at their personal entity with zero. This is the same problem 20.01.05 (Fan List Ownership Transfer) owns, and it must have one answer, not two | Handled in 20.01.05. This feature does not fork the audience; it holds edges against whichever entity survives domain 01's resolution |
| **Age-restricted event class reaches a minor follower** | The follow is **not** the gate. Per 03.02.01 D-02 the emitting domain owns eligibility, so an age-restricted event is filtered at fan-out by its emitter (D-13c statutory duties). A follow edge that tried to enforce age would be enforcing 23 domains' rules in the wrong place | Nothing — the event does not arrive |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Profile opening; edge state resolving for the acting entity | Follow control renders indeterminate until edge state is known (P95 < 200 ms). It must not flash "Follow" at someone you already follow, nor "Following" at someone you do not — a false "Following" invites a tap that *creates* the edge the user meant to destroy |
| **Empty** | User follows nobody | Feed empty state with a geo-weighted follow CTA — and per DT-03 this is the moment the product either bootstraps them or loses them. Never an apology; a widening CTA (03.02.02 States) |
| **Populated** | Edges exist | Following list (own view only); feed populated per alert scope |
| **Error** | Edge write failed after the retry budget | Optimistic UI reverts to true state with an explicit retry — a follow is idempotent so retry is always safe |
| **Partial — followed, silent** | Followed entity exists but has produced no events | Profile shows followed state; feed contribution is zero. Rendered as an explicit "nothing yet — you'll be told first", never as an empty list, which reads as breakage |
| **Partial — followed, suppressed** | Follower has muted the entity or narrowed the alert scope (03.02.02) without unfollowing | Edge live, fan-out filtered. **The target is never told**, ever (03.02.02 D-05) — the mute exists precisely so you can stop seeing your bandmate's side project without it being a social incident |
| **Partial — followed, dormant** | Followed entity suspended (24) or claimed-pending (03.06.04) | Followed state holds; no events. No explanation of a suspension — that disclosure is domain 24's to make |
| **Pending (offline)** | Follow queued, awaiting reconnect | Intended state with a pending indicator; dropped with notice at 24h TTL |
| **Rate-limited** | Limit breached | Control disabled with the limit and its reset time stated verbatim |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Follows and connections can be one edge type with a `mutual` flag | Standard social-graph modelling; most platforms do this | ❌ REJECTED: the consent models differ (unilateral vs bilateral), the Fan is deliberately excluded from one and not the other (D-02), and the abuse profiles are unrelated. A shared table with a status enum forces every read to know which semantics apply, and makes D-02 a runtime check instead of a structural fact. |
| DT-02 | Follow notifications are trivial | Obvious-path assumption | ❌ REJECTED: D-13a states fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude. A working musician who signs 200 fans in a week gets 200 notifications of an event they cannot act on. Follow notifications for high-follower entities must aggregate or suppress — this is a product decision forced by the persona mix, not a scaling detail. **Resolved at Step 5 by D-06**, which replaces the obvious volume threshold with a graph-derived rule (DT-06 below). |
| DT-03 | The follow graph's real problem is discovery, not mechanics | Domain overview: "200 users in one city feels alive, 20,000 scattered globally feels dead" | ✅ CONFIRMED: the mechanic is trivial and the cold start is the whole difficulty. A Fan who follows three artists with no local activity sees an empty product. This is why scene seeding (03.06.04) is a dependency of this feature feeling alive, not a nice-to-have — and why follow suggestions must be geo-weighted from day one. |
| DT-04 | An expert would expect follow to be splittable by content type ("notify me about gigs, not releases") | Bandsintown/Songkick precedent — the Fan's stated need is gigs specifically, not everything | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real expectation: personas.md defines the Fan's success as show attendance, and a Fan following for gig alerts does not want release-note noise. **Resolved at Step 5**: the control lives on the edge, per D-05 — see DT-05 for why this product cannot put it in global preferences. |
| DT-05 | **The followed entity is a single content channel, as it is on every consumer platform** | The assumption every follow implementation is built on — on Instagram, an artist is one stream | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the sharpest music-specific finding in the feature. personas.md's *first structural fact* is the multi-hyphenate: the followed entity is a session drummer **and** a mixer **and** a member of two bands **and** a gear seller. 03.02.01's Role Lens confirms it from the other side — the Musician is a "high-volume emitter" whose "work in 8+ domains generates the events other people's feeds consume". So a Fan who follows a working musician for gigs is subscribing to a cymbal listing, a confirmed credit, a freed studio Tuesday and a release, all from one edge. **This makes per-edge alert scope (D-05) a structural requirement of this product specifically, not a refinement.** It also kills the global-preference alternative outright: a global "notify me about gigs" cannot express "gigs from these three, everything from my band", and per-entity intent is precisely what a follow *is*. The preference has to live where the intent lives. |
| DT-06 | **A follower count is a vanity metric, so the risk of displaying it is demoralisation** | The original framing of Q-03 | ❌ REJECTED as an under-reading. In music a follower count is **booking currency**: promoters and venues use audience numbers to set guarantees, support slots and whether a call gets returned at all. Domain 17 (Live Booking & Settlement) is a live consumer of exactly this signal. That reframes the whole risk — buying followers is not ego, it is **revenue-motivated fraud with a direct payback**, which is a materially harder adversary than a vanity gamer. Three consequences: (1) follows must never be a ranking or matching input, which domain law D-03 (evidence beats participation) and 03.02.02 D-02 already independently require; (2) Q-03 is not a UX question about feelings, it is a question about whether the platform mints a currency; (3) at small N a public count is a **real-time unfollow detector** — a musician with 12 followers watching it drop to 11 knows who left, and personas.md is explicit that the core Musician persona is "not a star". |
| DT-07 | **A follow grants the artist permission to contact the follower** | Not a hypothesis anyone proposed — an *assumption the artist will make*, which is why it needs stating | ❌ REJECTED, and this is a boundary nobody had drawn. A follow is a platform-internal read subscription. It is not marketing consent, and it does not put the follower on a mailing list — 20.01.02 (Consent & Legal Basis) owns the legal basis and 20.01.01 owns the fan record, which treats a follow as *one observation* among ticket scans and purchases. The failure mode is not the platform breaking the rule; it is the **artist assuming the rule does not exist** and reading "4,000 followers" as "4,000 contactable addresses". The product must never present a follower set as an audience the entity can message. This also resolves the feature's original Q-01: a follow and a fan record are **not the same thing counted twice** — the follow is an edge, the fan record is a per-(fan, artist-entity) record that *observes* the edge (20.01.01 D-03: "first observation creates the record"). They legitimately produce different numbers, because a fan record can exist with no follow (a guest ticket purchase) and a follow can exist with no other observation. Per domain law D-04 they must therefore be **labelled differently and never reconciled** — "followers" and "fans" are different populations, and presenting either as the other is the overstatement the domain law forbids. |
| DT-08 | Follows on seeded, unclaimed records are a nice bonus for the Operator | The convenient reading of 03.06.04 | ✅ CONFIRMED as a hook **and** a hazard, and it is the strongest onboarding mechanic in this sub-domain. 03.06.04 seeds venues from real facts before their operators sign up; those records can accrue followers. An Operator therefore arrives to a claimed room with an audience they did not build — which per personas.md is a far stronger switching trigger than any feature ("enough musicians are already on WeJammin that being absent costs them bookings"). The hazard is the mirror: those followers followed a **derived record**, and 03.06.04 D-02 requires seed records be visibly derived. When the record becomes an assertion by a responsible party, the follower's subscription has materially changed — a real business is now sending them things. Per domain law D-04 (never overstate what is known) they must be told at the claim boundary, with an unfollow affordance in the same surface. Cheap to build; skipping it means the platform silently converted an inference-follow into a business-follow, which is exactly the substitution the domain law exists to prevent. |
| DT-09 | The "who I follow" list is harmless to expose — it is the least sensitive thing in a social graph | Universal convention; every consumer platform makes it public | ❌ REJECTED for this domain specifically. In music, a following list is **strategic intent in the clear**. A musician who follows twelve labels in a week is shopping a record. A band that starts following three booking agencies is between agents. A producer following a competitor's roster is reading their supply. None of this is true on a consumer platform where following is taste — here the edge is professional and the pattern is legible to anyone in the industry who looks. Following lists are visible to the acting entity only. This is a rare case where the music domain's professional/consumer blend makes a universal convention actively wrong. |
| DT-10 | A cap on how many entities you may follow is a standard, cheap abuse control | Precedent — most platforms cap follow counts | ❌ REJECTED: the cap punishes the exact persona it would look like it protects. Per personas.md the Operator books a room and legitimately tracks every act in their radius; per DT-03 and 03.02.02 D-03 geography is a primary axis, and a 200-mile radius contains thousands of acts. A booker at 4,000 follows is doing their job; a bot at 4,000 follows got there in an hour. **The distinguishing signal is rate, not total** — so the rate limit is the control and there is no cap. Rejecting the cap costs nothing and preserves the Operator persona's core use of this feature. |
| DT-11 | Accepting a connection should auto-create a mutual follow, as a convenience | `#CX-02` synthesis question 2, left open at breadth; and near-universal precedent | ❌ REJECTED — and the reason is specific to this platform. 03.01.02 DT-03 confirms connections are **pre-seeded from verified credits**: the graph arrives populated. If accepting a connection auto-followed, credit-seeding would manufacture follows at scale — every person you ever worked with, subscribed to, without an act of interest, inflating follower counts with graph plumbing at exactly the moment DT-06 establishes that counts are currency. It is also unnecessary: 03.02.01's Behavior already routes connections as a **secondary fan-out set with a different weight**, so the content arrives without the edge. Auto-follow buys nothing and corrupts the one number that has to stay honest. This resolves `#CX-02` note 2 from this side — flagged for Step 6 synthesis, since the CX file is not this feature's to edit. |
| DT-12 | **The failure nobody designs for: the follow notification is the product, and the follow is the packaging** | Reasoning from personas.md's Producer ("will not tend a feed") against DT-02 | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it inverts D-06's design. For the Producer — the persona whose exit removes D-18's capture point — the feed is not a surface they visit. The *only* part of this entire feature they will ever experience is a notification saying someone followed them. So the notification is not a side effect of the follow to be suppressed at volume; for the platform's most important persona it is the **whole feature**, and the naive volume threshold from DT-02 would suppress the one signal that matters. Hence D-06 tiers on **graph distance, not volume**: an unknown fan digests, a named engineer at 2 hops notifies. It scales for free (a band with 40k fan followers gets a digest automatically, because fans are not in its collaboration graph) and it never suppresses the hire signal. Honest cost: this puts a 2-hop traversal (03.04.01) on every follow **write**, at D-13a fan volumes — the domain index already routes graph traversal on hot paths to `/create-prd-architecture`, and this is a third hot path it did not enumerate. Recorded rather than hidden; if the traversal proves unaffordable, the fallback is a cached graph-distance snapshot, and that is an architecture decision, not a product one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Follow requires no approval and is revocable unilaterally | Any consent step makes it a connection, which is a different edge with a different purpose (03.01.02). The value of the follow is precisely that it is free. |
| D-02 | Both endpoints are entities, not users | Per personas.md, bands/studios/venues act independently of their members. A user↔user edge cannot express "band follows venue". |
| D-03 | **Unfollow is never notified, to anyone, ever** | Symmetry with 03.02.02 D-05 (mutes are silent). A visible unfollow between professionals is a social incident the product would be causing for no benefit. Note the honest tension this creates with a public follower count at small N (DT-06) — the count leaks what the notification withholds, which is an argument feeding Q-02. |
| D-04 | **A follow is not consent to be contacted** | DT-07. The follow is a platform-internal read subscription; marketing consent and legal basis are 20.01.02's. The follower set is never presented to an entity as a messageable audience, because the artist's default assumption is that it is one. |
| D-05 | **The follow edge carries a bounded alert scope; scope does not live in global preferences** | DT-04 + DT-05. Per-entity intent is what a follow *is*, and a global preference structurally cannot express "gigs from these three, everything from my band". Bounded enum, never a per-event-type matrix — per personas.md the Producer will not leave the DAW and the Fan will not tolerate complexity, so the **default is the real interface**. Exact enum values depend on the event-source catalogue (03.02.01 Q-01) — see Q-01 below. |
| D-06 | **Follow notifications tier on graph distance, not on follower volume** | DT-02 + DT-12. Individual notification iff the new follower is within 2 hops of the collaboration graph (03.04.01) or shares an active scene; otherwise a daily digest, max 1/day. Volume thresholds would suppress the Producer's hire signal — the only part of this feature that persona experiences. Graph tiering scales for free (a band's 40k fans are not in its collaboration graph) and never drops the signal that matters. Honest cost: a traversal on every write (routed to `/create-prd-architecture`). |
| D-07 | **A follower count is a count of entities and is never labelled as people; suspended and deleted followers are excluded** | Parent D-04 makes edges entity-to-entity, so one human following via three entities is three edges. Domain law D-04 (never overstate what is known): a count that includes banned accounts, or that calls entities people, is a number the platform is asserting and cannot support. |
| D-08 | **The acting entity is always explicit; the control renders state for that entity only, never the union** | personas.md structural fact 1 — one human is routinely four entities. A "Following" state derived from the union means a user believes they will be alerted and will not be, and the failure is undetectable until they miss the gig. One line of UI against the Fan's and Musician's single stated failure. |
| D-09 | **No cap on follows; rate limit only** | DT-10. The Operator legitimately tracks thousands of acts in their radius; the distinguishing signal between a booker and a bot is rate, not total. 60/rolling hour and 500/rolling 24h per acting human account, aggregated across entities (per-entity limits are evaded by the multi-hyphenate's own entity set). The onboarding import path is exempt as one reviewed action, bounded at 2,000/import and 3 imports/30 days. |
| D-10 | **No public follower roster; following lists are visible to the acting entity only** | DT-09 + the enumeration hazard. A public roster is a harvestable audience list; a public following list is strategic intent in the clear, which is true in music and not on consumer platforms. An entity's own audience lives in domain 20, where consent is modelled. |
| D-11 | **Follows on unclaimed seeded records are permitted and transfer on claim; followers are told at the claim boundary** | DT-08. The strongest onboarding hook in the sub-domain (the Operator arrives to an audience), but the subscription materially changes when an inference becomes an assertion by a responsible party. Domain law D-04 requires the disclosure; it costs one notice with an unfollow affordance. |
| D-12 | **No auto-follow on connection accept** | DT-11. Credit-seeded connections (03.01.02 DT-03) would manufacture follows at scale and inflate the one number DT-06 shows is currency. Unnecessary anyway — 03.02.01 already routes connections as a secondary fan-out set, so the content arrives without the edge. |
