## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Feed Composition ↔ Ranking & Controls

**Relationship**: Composition decides what *may* appear; ranking decides what *does*. The coupling is
tight because the source types are not interchangeable — a gig announcement 200 miles away decays over
days and dies at the door time; a confirmed credit never decays because it is a permanent fact about a
person you care about; a used-cymbal listing decays the moment it sells. Ranking cannot be a single
recency-times-affinity function over a homogeneous stream, because the stream is not homogeneous. The
reader's controls close the loop: suppressing a source type should stop the fan-out, not merely filter
at render — otherwise the system pays the full cost of delivering content it knows will be hidden.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the hardest case — their multi-hyphenate identity means all source types are legitimately relevant (gear listings because they flip gear, gigs because they play them, credits because they collect them). No source can be pruned by role, only by their explicit control.
- **Producer**: session- and project-shaped events dominate; per personas.md they live in a DAW and will not tend a feed, so their controls must default well because they will never open the settings.
- **Operator**: a narrow band — acts they book, local scene activity, their own room's calendar. Most of the platform's event volume is noise to them.
- **Fan**: gigs and releases only, per personas.md. Every professional event class is noise, and their volume (D-13a) means bad defaults here are the platform's single largest waste of compute.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Composition owns the event stream; controls own the reader's preferences. No contested write — the reader never mutates the event, only their view of it.
2. **Trigger chain**: A control change should invalidate any precomputed feed for that reader. If fan-out is on write, changing a control means either rebuilding that reader's feed or accepting staleness. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`, and it is the decision that routes to the not-product architecture question (fan-out on read vs write).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — per D-04 each emitting domain decides eligibility. A private project's events (07) must never reach composition, so the permission check is upstream of ranking, not a filter applied after.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The feed and the notification system are separate surfaces reading a shared event stream, with different thresholds. The same credit confirmation may notify the contributor and merely rank for their followers.
5. **State transition conflict**: An event can be retracted after fan-out — a credit disputed (02/24), a gig cancelled (17), a listing withdrawn (13). The feed must handle retroactive removal of already-delivered items, which a naive append-only feed cannot.

### CX-02: Native Posts ↔ Ranking & Controls

**Relationship**: This is the asymmetry that decides whether the feed stays trustworthy. Every other
event source is a **byproduct of real work** — you cannot fake a confirmed credit without a Producer
counter-attesting, you cannot fake a settled show. Native posts cost nothing and can be produced at
will. If ranking treats a post and a credit as comparable objects competing on engagement, the loudest
poster outranks the most-hired musician, and the feed becomes an Instagram clone WeJammin has no reason
to win. Ranking must therefore weight *evidenced* events structurally above *asserted* ones — which is
the domain thesis (D-18) expressed as a ranking rule.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona most tempted to post for reach and most damaged if posting beats working. Per personas.md the anti-persona "games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation" — an engagement-ranked feed hands them a cheaper way to do it.
- **Producer**: posts least (they are in a DAW), works most. A post-favouring feed systematically buries the trust-broker persona whose presence is what makes the platform valuable.
- **Operator**: posts operationally ("Tuesday free, backline in") — arguably their posts *are* work events and should be structured rather than freeform.
- **Fan**: consumes; per D-11 they are first-class users but their engagement volume (D-13a) would dominate any engagement-weighted signal, meaning fan behaviour would rank the professional feed.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — posts are owned by 03.02.03, ranking reads them.
2. **Trigger chain**: Posting triggers fan-out to followers. Failure is non-critical (retry); there is no compensating action needed.
3. **Permission intersection**: Post visibility scope (public / connections / scene) constrains fan-out; the Fan read-only rule (D-03 on 03.02.03) means fans never enter the author set.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Reactions notify the author — the highest-volume, lowest-value notification class on the platform, and per D-13a the one most likely to require aggregation.
5. **State transition conflict**: Editing a post after fan-out — does the delivered copy update? Deletion after reactions exist? `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
