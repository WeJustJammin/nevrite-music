# Feature: Conference & Industry Event Networking Mode

> **Number**: 03.11
> **Parent**: [Community & Networking](./community-networking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A time-boxed, event-scoped mode: "I'm at SXSW / NAMM / ADE for four days — who else is here, and who should
I meet before Thursday?" It is the digital replacement for the badge, the lanyard scan and the delegate
directory, and it inherits music conferences' actual dynamic: everyone is there specifically to meet people,
walls come down for 72 hours, and then it is over.

Per DT-02 this is structurally a **temporal scene** — the same mechanism as 03.06.01, with an expiry and a
different privacy posture. That connection also links it to a gap found independently in 03.06.01 DT-03:
the touring musician, who has the same need for the same reason.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Finds who is at the conference worth meeting, and is findable. Per personas.md their pain is "every new client requires re-proving who they are" — a conference is 72 hours of doing exactly that, repeatedly, badly, and their evidenced credits are what would replace the pitch. |
| Producer | Full | Attends to meet clients and collaborators. Per personas.md they are normally walled off and inbound-flooded (03.04.04) — and this is the one context where that inverts: they came here to be reached, for four days only. That inversion is the feature's whole justification (DT-01). |
| Operator | Full | Venue and studio operators attend industry events to meet promoters, agents and suppliers. Per personas.md they are frequently both buyer and seller, so their conference agenda runs in both directions at once. |
| Fan | None | No access. This is an industry-professional mode; a conference delegate list exposed to a consumer population is a contact-harvesting and access-seeking surface, and per personas.md fans "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity" — they do not want this. Note the honest complication: some music conferences (SXSW) are also public festivals, so the *event* has fans even though this *mode* does not. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

For the event's duration, an attendee opts into a scoped mode where: attendance is declared, other attendees
are discoverable, reachability (03.04.04) is temporarily relaxed **for attendees only**, and matching
(03.03.02) is scoped to people who are physically here. At the end it expires — the relaxed reachability
reverts, the attendee list stops being queryable, and per DT-03 what persists is the design question.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — event sourcing (does WeJammin hold a conference registry?
does an organiser create the event? is it self-declared?) is unresolved and gates the feature.

### Happy Path

1. User declares attendance at an event (however the event exists — Q-01)
2. Their reachability relaxes for fellow attendees, for the event's duration only
3. Matching scopes to attendees, surfacing paths (03.04.01) — "3 people you should meet; you're 2 hops from each"
4. They meet people; the mode expires; walls return automatically without any action

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Relaxed reachability does not revert** | A user opened their walls for a conference and is still flooded in March | Expiry must be automatic and unmissable. Per personas.md nobody will remember to close it — the same "will not tend a form" fact that drives 03.03.03 D-01 |
| Attendance declaration is location broadcasting | "I am at this place, now" is a different privacy claim from anything else in this domain, and per personas.md the platform has a harassment-risk population (fans) — though they are excluded here | See DT-03 |
| Someone declares attendance without attending | To harvest the delegate list, or to reach walled people | Verification is hard; the wall relaxation is the prize, which makes false attendance worth faking |
| Event does not exist in the platform | Most events, at launch | Q-01 — the gating question |
| Conference is also a public festival (SXSW) | The event has fans; this mode does not | The honest complication in the Fan lens above |
| The 72 hours pass and the connection was never recorded | The conference's classic failure — a great conversation, a lost card, no follow-up | DT-03: this is the feature's actual opportunity |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Inactive | Not attending anything | Normal platform behaviour, normal walls |
| Declared | Attendance declared, event not started | Pre-event discovery |
| Active | Event running | Scoped discovery, relaxed reachability, attendee matching |
| Expiring | Event ending | Warning that walls are about to return, and what persists |
| Expired | Over | Walls restored automatically; attendee list no longer queryable |
| Error | Fetch failed | Retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a thin novelty feature — a directory with a date on it | The sceptical reading, and a fair first impression | ❌ REJECTED: it is the **one context where the domain's central tension inverts**. Everywhere else, the Producer and Operator personas need walls (03.04.04 DT-01) because they are structurally flooded and their exit would remove the platform's provenance capture point. At a conference they have paid money and travelled specifically to be reached — for 72 hours, the wall is the problem rather than the solution. A feature that flips reachability from closed to open, safely and reversibly, for a bounded window, is doing real work: it is the pressure-release valve on the domain's most restrictive mechanism. That is not a directory. |
| DT-02 | This is unrelated to Scenes (03.06) | The sweep listed them as separate candidates (11 and 16) | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **temporal scene**, and the connection was found independently from the other side: 03.06.01 DT-03 identifies the touring musician (in Bristol tonight, Manchester tomorrow) as a gap in the static geo-scene model, needing exactly this — temporary, high-intent, expiring membership of a place they are in right now. Two candidates, discovered from opposite directions, describing one mechanism: **time-boxed scene membership**. The remaining question is whether that means this feature folds into 03.06 or stays separate. I keep it separate for this pass because its **privacy posture differs materially** — a geo scene is a persistent, low-stakes affiliation; this broadcasts "I am physically at this place this week" and relaxes contact walls, which is a much stronger claim. Same mechanism, different risk profile. Recorded as Q-02 for the owner. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **the value is in what survives the event, not during it** | Reasoning about the actual conference failure mode | ✅ CONFIRMED and it relocates the feature's centre of gravity. During a conference nobody is on their phone in an app — they are in a bar talking, and any product competing for attention *during* the event loses to the event. The failure everyone actually has is afterwards: you met someone brilliant on Wednesday, you have a photo of their card or nothing at all, and by the following week the momentum is gone. So the feature's real job is the **persistence layer**: the connection made at the event survives the expiry, in a form that is still legible in March ("met at ADE 2026, they engineer at X, you were introduced by Y"). That is also where it feeds the domain's other mechanisms — a conference meeting is a real relationship with no verified credit behind it, which is precisely the case 03.01.02 exists for (the manual connection for a relationship with no credit yet), and it is exactly what the private rolodex (03.05) is for. Three features that looked unrelated converge on this moment. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Reachability relaxation is bounded and reverts automatically | DT-01 — the wall's release valve only works if it re-closes without the user remembering, per personas.md's "will not tend a form" fact. |
| D-02 | Scoped to attendees only; Fans have no access | An industry mode; a delegate list exposed to a consumer population is a harvesting surface. |
| D-03 | The feature's centre is the persistence layer, not the live event | DT-03 — nobody is in an app during a conference; the failure is the week after. |
