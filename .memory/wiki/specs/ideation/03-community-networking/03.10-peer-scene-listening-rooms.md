# Feature: Peer & Scene Listening Rooms

> **Number**: 03.10
> **Parent**: [Community & Networking](./community-networking-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Informal live audio rooms where a scene listens together — a producer playing works-in-progress for
feedback, a scene's Friday listening session, a peer critique round. Per the domain overview the boundary
here was deliberately drawn: **the monetised/ticketed half of listening parties moved to Fanbase & D2F
(domain 20)**, resolving a three-way collision between this domain, 20 and Real-Time Jamming (08). What
remains is peer/scene sessions.

Per D-15 this feature **consumes** the ratified `Real-Time Rooms, Presence & Audio Transport` cross-cut —
it does not own transport. That decision is already made and this file does not reopen it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Hosts and joins peer listening sessions — playing a rough mix to their scene and getting reactions. Per personas.md they are "not a star"; this is not a broadcast to an audience, it is showing work to peers, which is a different social act with a different tolerance for polish. |
| Producer | Full | The most natural host: per personas.md they run sessions and are the de-facto project manager, and playing a WIP for trusted ears is a real part of the craft. But per personas.md they live in a DAW — and a listening room requiring them to leave it, upload a bounce, and host a web session is exactly the flow they will not use (DT-02). |
| Operator | Full | Low relevance — per personas.md they sell time and space, not creative output. They might host a scene session as a community act, which is a thin use case honestly stated rather than inflated. |
| Fan | None | **No access** — and this is the boundary the domain overview drew. A fan-facing listening party is a monetisable D2F event and belongs to domain 20. Admitting fans here would re-open the three-way collision that boundary resolved, and would change the social act entirely: a peer critique with an audience is not a peer critique. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A scoped, ephemeral audio room where a host plays material and participants respond. Per D-15 the transport,
presence and room mechanics come from the cross-cut; what this feature owns is the **scene-scoped, peer,
unmonetised** framing — who is invited, what the social contract is, and what happens to the material.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — most mechanics are the cross-cut's, and are not
inventable here before it is specified.

### Happy Path

1. Host opens a room scoped to a scene (03.06.01) or to specific peers
2. Invited participants join via the Real-Time Rooms cross-cut
3. Host plays material; participants listen and respond
4. Room ends; per DT-01 what persists is the unresolved question

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Unreleased material is recorded by a participant** | The host played an unreleased track to a room; someone captured it. The platform cannot prevent this — it is a speaker and a phone | DT-01: this is the feature's defining risk and it is unsolvable technically |
| Latency makes group listening incoherent | Per D-15 the ~25–30 ms desync ceiling caps real-time audio radius | Listening (one-way) is far more tolerant than jamming (interactive) — this is a genuine difference from domain 08's constraint |
| Nobody comes | A scene listening room with two people is worse than none — it is a small public failure in front of peers | The cold-start problem at its most personally exposing |
| Feedback is brutal / a pile-on | Peer critique among professionals, with reputations present | Routes to 24, but the social dynamic is the real issue |
| Host plays someone else's unreleased work | Rights exposure via a social feature | `[PENDING]` |
| A fan somehow attends | The boundary breaks and this becomes a D2F event | Scoping must be enforced, not conventional |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **playing unreleased material to a room is a rights event, not a social one** | Reasoning against D-10 (the rights stack is the thesis) and personas.md's uncleared-stem failure | ✅ CONFIRMED: the entire point of a peer listening session is playing work that is **not released** — that is why the feedback is wanted. So a social feature in this domain routinely handles the most sensitive assets on the platform, and per personas.md the Musician's worst accidental behaviour is exposing collaborators through an uncleared upload. Three sub-questions nobody has asked: (a) can participants capture it — no, not preventably, it is a speaker and a phone; (b) does the room persist as a recording, and if so who owns a recording of someone else's unreleased work plus five people's spoken reactions; (c) does playing an unreleased track to 30 peers constitute publication in any sense that matters to domain 09/12? On a platform whose thesis (D-10) is holding the ownership record end-to-end, a casual social feature quietly touching unreleased masters deserves an explicit position rather than an assumption. Recorded as Q-01. |
| DT-02 | Producers will host listening rooms because playing WIPs to peers is real | The obvious product logic — it is a genuine craft practice | ⏳ DEFERRED, with a real doubt. Per personas.md the Producer's unique constraint is that "their work happens inside a DAW, not a browser" and "any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and go fill in a web form will not be used". Hosting a listening room means: stop, bounce, upload, schedule, get people to attend, host. That is not a lower-friction version of what they do now — what they do now is send a private link to three people, which takes twelve seconds and is asynchronous. This feature competes with a Dropbox link and loses on every axis except liveness, and liveness is what makes it *harder*, not easier. Not a rejection — a synchronous room is genuinely different for a scene ritual — but a real doubt about the persona fit that argues for a low MoSCoW placement. |
| DT-03 | The domain overview's boundary (peer here, ticketed to domain 20) is clean | The ratified boundary | ✅ CONFIRMED as correct, with one honest complication: the **same host** will want both, and will not perceive the boundary. A producer plays a WIP to their scene on Friday (here) and a listening party to their fans on Saturday (domain 20) — same audio, same mechanic, different audience and monetisation. The boundary is right (the products genuinely differ: one is critique among peers, the other is a monetisable fan event), but the two features must not have divergent room UX, or the user experiences the platform's internal organisation as friction. Both consuming the same Real-Time Rooms cross-cut (D-15) is what makes that tractable — which is a point in favour of D-15's extraction. |
