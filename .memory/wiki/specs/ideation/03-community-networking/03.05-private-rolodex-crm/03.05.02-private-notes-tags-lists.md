# Feature: Private Notes, Tags & Lists

> **Number**: 03.05.02
> **Parent**: [Private Industry Rolodex & Contact CRM](./03.05-private-rolodex-crm-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What you actually think, written down. "Great player, always late." "Pay him fast or he chases." "Her
partner books the Roundhouse." Tags and lists organise it — *first-call drummers*, *avoid*, *owes me a
favour*. This is the institutional memory a working professional carries in their head and loses when
they change phones, and per personas.md it currently lives in a Notes app.

It is also **the single most legally hazardous object in domain 03**: unflattering personal data about an
identifiable person, authored by a third party, held by the platform, invisible to its subject — who
nonetheless has rights over it that its author does not control (DT-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Notes on bookers, engineers, bandmates — mostly logistics and money ("pays on the night", "always late"). Per personas.md their switching trigger is "losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove"; these notes are the private, unprovable version of exactly that, which is why they keep them. |
| Producer | Full | Notes on contributors and clients across sessions. Per personas.md they reconstruct facts "months later, chasing people who have moved on" — their notes are the reconstruction aid. The irony is sharp: this feature is a *better memory*, while the platform's thesis (D-18) is that memory is the problem and capture-at-source is the fix. |
| Operator | Full | Notes on acts and promoters — "brought 200 people", "left the room a state", "cancelled twice". Commercially the most consequential notes on the platform and the most defamation-adjacent: an Operator's private note that an act is unreliable can cost that act work if it ever leaks or is shared informally. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-03). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Notes are freeform text attached to a contact record (03.05.01); tags and lists are the retrieval structure
over them. Everything is private to the author with no sharing surface at all (parent D-01) — per DT-01
there is no partial-sharing design worth building.

Per `03.05-private-rolodex-crm-cx.md#R-01`, notes must **never** influence any shared computation — not
match scoring, not ranking, not the graph. A private opinion that changes a public ranking has leaked
through that ranking.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — retention, export, and the DSAR answer are unresolved and
are the feature's gating questions, not its details.

### Happy Path

1. User opens a contact record and writes a note
2. Note is stored privately, encrypted, attached to the record
3. User tags the contact and adds them to a list
4. Later, the user finds the person via the tag/list and reads their own note before making contact

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Note leaks to its subject** | Career and relationship damage, potentially defamation liability | The failure that makes the feature not worth building if it is even slightly likely. There is no graceful degradation here |
| Note attached to the wrong person via mis-reconciliation (03.05.01 DT-02) | Defamatory content about an innocent person, invisible to them, unnoticed by the author | Author-confirmed reconciliation only (D-02 on 03.05.01) |
| Subject files a DSAR | Per GDPR they may be entitled to know what is held about them | Unresolved and serious — DT-02. Honouring it exposes the author; refusing it may not be lawful |
| Author subpoenaed / platform served in a dispute | Private notes are discoverable evidence in exactly the litigation the platform exists around | Per problem-statement.md the most litigated failure in music is splits — and this feature stores contemporaneous private opinions about the counterparties. See DT-03 |
| Note read by platform staff | An internal-access surface over the industry's private opinions | Encryption at rest is the stated rule (security-first); this should arguably be encrypted such that staff *cannot* read it |
| User writes notes about a Fan | Fans are users (D-11) but not professionals — a private file on a fan is a different thing entirely | `[PENDING]` — likely should be prohibited |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Notes could be optionally shareable — with a band, a team, a studio | The obvious extension; every CRM does this, and a band sharing a promoter list is genuinely useful | ❌ REJECTED → parent D-01. The value of a private note is exactly its privacy: "always late" is writable only where he cannot read it. Introduce sharing and every note is written defensively, which makes them useless; and the moment a note is shared with a band, it is one member's screenshot away from its subject. Worse, per personas.md bands are *entities with changing membership* — a note shared with "the band" is shared with whoever joins it in three years. There is no coherent middle ground. If team-shared notes are wanted, that is a different feature with a different contract, not a flag on this one. |
| DT-02 | **This is the domain's largest compliance exposure, and it is structurally strange: the subject has rights over data the author considers theirs** | Reasoning about GDPR against third-party-authored personal data | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the finding this feature turns on. A note about an identifiable person *is* their personal data, and WeJammin — not the note's author — is plausibly the controller. So the subject may have rights of access, rectification and erasure over a private note written about them by someone else. Honouring a DSAR exposes the author and destroys the feature's premise; refusing may be unlawful. Note the same structure appears at 03.01.03 DT-04 (endorsements): third-party statements about a person, where the subject's rights and the author's expectations conflict. **It is the same problem twice and needs one answer**, not two. This is precisely the kind of finding that should shape `/create-prd-security` rather than surface during implementation. |
| DT-03 | Failure mode at the intersection with Rights (09) and Disputes (24): these notes are discoverable evidence | Adversarial reasoning; problem-statement.md ("the single most litigated failure in music") | ✅ CONFIRMED and darkly ironic. The platform exists because splits get litigated years later from memory. This feature stores contemporaneous, timestamped, private opinions about the exact counterparties in those disputes — making WeJammin a repository of discoverable evidence in the litigation its own thesis is about. A user's private "he agreed to 20% verbally" is either their salvation or their liability, and either way the platform is now in the discovery process. The honest read: this is an argument for the *public* provenance record (domain 02/09) and against the private notes feature, because the former is designed to be evidence and the latter accidentally is. Reinforces parent D-04 (cut candidate). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Notes are never shareable, in any form | DT-01 — sharing makes them defensive and therefore useless, and one screenshot from their subject. No coherent middle ground. |
| D-02 | Notes never influence any shared computation | `#R-01` — a private opinion changing a public ranking has leaked through it. |
| D-03 | The endorsement problem (03.01.03 DT-04) and the notes problem are one problem and get one answer | DT-02 — third-party statements about a person where subject rights and author expectations conflict. |
