## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Contact Records ↔ Private Notes

**Relationship**: This pair is what makes the sub-domain a system rather than three utilities. A note is
attached to a contact record, and per D-02 most contact records start as **shadow contacts** — people who
are not on WeJammin. When such a person later joins, reconciliation must re-point the notes from the shadow
record to the real account **without any signal to that account**. The person now has a WeJammin identity
carrying a stranger's private file, and must never learn of it. That is simultaneously the feature's whole
utility (your notes survive the person joining) and its central ethical problem (parent Q-02).

The reverse case is worse: if reconciliation is wrong — two people with the same name, a merge in domain 01
— notes about person A land on person B. "Doesn't pay on time" attached to the wrong engineer is defamatory
content the subject cannot see and cannot correct, authored by someone who did not make the error.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: keeps notes on bookers, engineers and bandmates. Per personas.md their workaround is "a spreadsheet"; this is that spreadsheet with the reconciliation problem attached.
- **Producer**: the heaviest user — per personas.md they are the de-facto project manager coordinating contributors, and they are the persona most likely to hold notes on people who have "moved on".
- **Operator**: keeps notes on acts and promoters ("brings a crowd", "left the room a state") — commercially the most consequential notes on the platform, and the most defamation-adjacent.
- **Fan**: no access (D-03).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The contact record is owned by 03.05.01; notes reference it. On reconciliation the record's identity changes underneath the notes — a re-point, not a merge. If two authors independently created shadow records for the same person, each keeps their own private record: **there is no shared entity, and there must not be**, or one user's notes become discoverable through another's.
2. **Trigger chain**: A shadow contact joining triggers reconciliation → re-point notes (CX-01) and reminders (CX-02). If it fails partway, notes and reminders point at different records for one person. It must be atomic.
3. **Permission intersection**: None internally — everything here is private to one author (D-01). The intersection that matters is external: the *subject's* GDPR rights over notes they cannot see (parent Q-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: **Never.** No event in this sub-domain may notify the person described. A single leak here is a serious harm, not a bug.
5. **State transition conflict**: Reconciliation racing with a note write — a note written during reconciliation must land on the surviving record, not the retiring shadow.

### CX-02: Contact Records ↔ Follow-Ups

**Relationship**: A reminder is a pointer to a contact plus a time. Reconciliation must carry reminders
across, or a follow-up set against a shadow contact silently stops firing at exactly the moment the person
became reachable on-platform — the worst possible timing, because that is when the follow-up finally became
actionable. The inverse failure is duplication: reconciliation that copies rather than moves produces two
reminders for one person, and the user distrusts the whole feature after seeing that twice.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer / Operator**: identical needs here — a reminder that fires against the right person.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Reminder holds a reference to the contact record; the record is authoritative.
2. **Trigger chain**: Reconciliation re-points reminders. Failure means orphaned or duplicated reminders — see above.
3. **Permission intersection**: None internally (D-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Reminders notify **the author only**, never the subject. This is the one notification path in the sub-domain and its direction is inviolable.
5. **State transition conflict**: A reminder firing during reconciliation must resolve to one contact, not two.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 03.05.02 Private Notes | 03.04.01 Collaboration Graph (as an input) | Considered whether private notes could weight the public graph or match scoring — the user's own opinion of a collaborator is genuinely the best signal available about them. Rejected decisively: any read of private notes by a public-facing system makes them **inferable from the outside**. If my private "avoid" note demotes someone in a ranking anyone can observe, the note has leaked through the ranking. Private data must never influence a shared computation, and this is a hard architectural rule, not a preference. |
| R-02 | 03.05.01 Contact Records | 03.01.02 Professional Connections | Considered auto-creating contact records from connections, and vice versa. Rejected: they are opposite objects. A connection is mutual, consensual and public; a contact record is unilateral, unconsented and secret. Auto-creating a private file on everyone you connect with is surveillance-by-default; auto-connecting to everyone you keep notes on would expose the rolodex's contents through the connection graph — the exact leak D-01 forbids. |
