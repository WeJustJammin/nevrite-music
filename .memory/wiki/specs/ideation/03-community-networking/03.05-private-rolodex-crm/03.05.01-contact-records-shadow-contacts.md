# Feature: Contact Records & Off-Platform Shadow Contacts

> **Number**: 03.05.01
> **Parent**: [Private Industry Rolodex & Contact CRM](./03.05-private-rolodex-crm-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The private contact record — and critically, one that can describe **someone who is not on WeJammin**. Per
D-02 on the parent this is not an import convenience; it is the feature's precondition. A working musician's
rolodex is mostly people who have never heard of this platform: the promoter who books the Thursday, the
guy with the van, the A&R who took a meeting once. A rolodex holding only WeJammin users is a worse copy of
the follow list.

The system's defining event is **reconciliation** — a shadow contact joins the platform and their record
must attach to the real account, silently (`03.05-private-rolodex-crm-cx.md#CX-01`).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Keeps records on bookers, engineers, bandmates, the van guy. Per personas.md their career "exists in fragments across six platforms that share no identity" — the rolodex is the fragment they currently keep in a Notes app, and the one they most obviously own. Most of their contacts are off-platform, permanently. |
| Producer | Full | The heaviest user. Per personas.md they are "the de-facto project manager" coordinating contributors, and their pain is that they later reconstruct facts "chasing people who have moved on" — a contact record is how you find someone who moved on. Their records span sessions, studios, and clients who will never sign up. |
| Operator | Full | Keeps records on acts, promoters, engineers, suppliers. Per personas.md they run the business "on the side of engineering or performing themselves", so their rolodex is their business's actual memory — and per personas.md much of it is currently a paper diary and a phone. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-03). A fan maintaining private, annotated records on identifiable musicians is a stalking affordance with no product justification. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A contact record is private to its author (D-01 on the parent) and holds identity fields plus whatever
routing information the author has — a phone number, an email, an Instagram handle. It exists in two states:

- **Shadow** — describes a person with no WeJammin account. All data is author-supplied.
- **Reconciled** — linked to a real account. The platform's data supersedes the author's guesses; the author's private notes (03.05.02) persist, invisibly.

Per parent Q-01 the shadow state is where the compliance exposure concentrates: identifiable personal data
about a non-user, held by the platform, authored by a third party, with the subject unaware.

### Happy Path

1. User creates a record for someone — a name, and whatever contact routing they have
2. Record is private and immediately usable for notes (03.05.02) and reminders (03.05.03)
3. That person later joins WeJammin
4. Reconciliation links the shadow record to the real account; notes and reminders re-point atomically
5. The author now sees their private notes alongside the person's real profile. The person is told nothing, ever

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Wrong reconciliation** — two engineers named Dave Smith | Notes about person A attach to person B. Per `#CX-01` this is defamatory content about the wrong person, invisible to the subject, and the author will not notice either | The most damaging failure in this sub-domain. Reconciliation must be author-confirmed, never automatic on a fuzzy match (D-02) |
| Two users each hold a shadow record for the same person | Two private records, no shared entity — and per `#CX-01` there must **not** be one, or one author's notes become discoverable through another's | Working as intended; costs deduplication elsewhere |
| Contact-list import from a phone | The obvious onboarding path, and it uploads hundreds of identifiable non-users at once — turning parent Q-01 from an edge case into the default | See DT-03 — this is likely the single largest privacy decision in domain 03 |
| Shadow contact joins and finds nothing | No signal to them, by design | Nothing — this is correct |
| The person asks the platform what it holds on them (DSAR) | Per GDPR the subject can ask; the data sits in strangers' private rolodexes | Unresolved and serious — parent Q-01. The platform likely cannot answer honestly without exposing the authors |
| Author leaves the platform | Their private records on non-users persist in the platform's storage with no author | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — deletion is probably the only defensible answer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rolodex opening | Skeleton |
| Empty | No contacts | Empty state with CTAs — and per DT-03 the import CTA is where the compliance decision actually bites |
| Shadow | Record for a non-user | Author's own data only |
| Reconciled | Linked to an account | Platform profile + private notes side by side |
| Ambiguous | Multiple candidate matches | Author must confirm — never auto-resolve (D-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Contacts are WeJammin users, and off-platform contacts are a later import feature | The natural assumption for a social platform | ❌ REJECTED → became parent D-02. Per personas.md the working musician's world is "Reverb + SoundBetter + Bandsintown + Splice + DistroKid + a WhatsApp thread + a spreadsheet" — almost nobody in their rolodex is here, and at launch *nobody* is. A rolodex restricted to platform users is empty on day one and duplicative on day 1000. Shadow contacts are the feature; the on-platform case is the minority. This inverts the build: reconciliation is core, not a nicety. |
| DT-02 | Reconciliation should be automatic on a confident match | Efficiency; it is what a good product would do | ❌ REJECTED: per `#CX-01` a wrong match attaches private, unflattering, defamation-adjacent notes to an innocent person — invisibly to them, and unnoticed by the author, who has no reason to re-check a match the system made. Name collision in music is routine, and the cost of a false positive is severe and undetectable while the cost of a false negative is one confirmation tap. Author-confirmed only. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **phone contact-list import is the obvious onboarding and the largest privacy decision in this domain** | Reasoning about how a rolodex actually gets populated | ✅ CONFIRMED: nobody types 400 contacts. Every CRM's onboarding is an address-book import — which means the realistic v1 of this feature uploads hundreds of identifiable non-users' names, numbers and emails to WeJammin's storage, on day one, with no notice to any of them. That converts parent Q-01 from a theoretical question into the product's default behaviour, and it is a pattern regulators have specifically pursued in other social products. This one insight is most of why D-04 (cut candidate) is on the parent. |
| DT-04 | Failure mode at the intersection with the collaboration graph: a rolodex is a graph the platform did not earn | Adversarial reasoning against D-18 | ✅ CONFIRMED as a tension worth naming: the public graph's value is that it is *derived from evidence* (03.04.01 D-01). The rolodex is the opposite — asserted, private, unverifiable. Holding both is coherent (they answer different questions) but the product must never blend them, and R-01 in the CX file forbids the obvious blend. The deeper point: a rolodex full of shadow contacts is a map of the industry that WeJammin acquires without any of those people participating, which is commercially valuable and ethically the weakest thing in this domain. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Shadow contacts are first-class, not an import feature (parent D-02) | DT-01 — the rolodex is mostly non-users, permanently, and entirely at launch. |
| D-02 | Reconciliation is author-confirmed, never automatic | DT-02 — a false positive attaches unflattering notes to an innocent person, invisibly and undetectably. |
| D-03 | Two users' shadow records for one person never merge | `#CX-01` — a shared entity would make one author's notes discoverable through another's. |
| D-04 | Nothing here ever signals the subject | Parent D-01 — the sub-domain's inviolable rule. |
