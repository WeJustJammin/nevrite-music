# Feature: Judging, Voting & Anti-Gaming

> **Number**: 03.08.03
> **Parent**: [Contests, Challenges & Beat Battles](./03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a winner is chosen, and how that choice is protected from being bought. Per D-03 on the parent, **public
voting is not the default** — in music contests a public vote measures the entrants' follower counts, not
the work, which defeats the one property that makes contests worth building (per 03.08.02 DT-02, that a
newcomer can be seen).

The platform has an advantage no other contest host has: per 03.04.01 it **knows the conflicts of interest
from the graph**. A judge who has four sessions with an entrant is a fact WeJammin holds and a fact
Soundcloud does not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Judges (when appointed) and is judged. Per personas.md they hold several roles, so a drummer judging a drum challenge has evidenced standing that a general "judge" role would throw away — judging appointments should be craft-scoped, and the graph already knows who has standing in which craft. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-standing judge — per personas.md their attestation carries weight because they have been in the room. Also the most likely to be conflicted: their centrality (03.04.01) means they have edges to many entrants, which is exactly what makes them qualified and exactly what compromises them. |
| Operator | Config | Appoints judges for a contest they organise; does not judge craft themselves (consistent with 03.01.03 D-03 — an Operator has no standing on craft). |
| Fan | Read-only | **Watches** — and per D-11 fans are first-class users of things built for them; a beat battle is genuinely spectator content and this is a legitimate fan surface. But they do not vote (D-04): per D-13a their volume makes any public vote a poll of the entrants' fanbases, which is DT-01's whole argument. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Judging models, from the brief (03.08.01):

| Model | What it measures | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Expert panel (craft-scoped, graph-evidenced standing) | The work | Default per D-03 |
| Organiser's choice | The organiser's taste | Honest, if declared |
| Public vote | The entrants' follower counts | Rejected as default (DT-01) |
| Peer vote (entrants judge each other) | Mixed — and reciprocity-prone | See DT-03 |

Conflict detection reads the graph (`03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-cx.md#CX-03` synthesis 3). Per
`#R-01` the graph must **never** weight a judge's authority — only detect their conflicts. The two look
similar and behave oppositely.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — conflict thresholds (how many shared credits disqualify?)
and whether judging is blind.

### Happy Path

1. Submissions close; the judging model was fixed in the brief
2. System flags judge/entrant conflicts from the graph, before judging starts
3. Judges evaluate — blind, if the model says so (DT-02)
4. Verdict is issued; per `#CX-04` a contested verdict must resolve before money moves

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Public vote turns into a follower-count contest** | The format's value inverts — the best-known entrant wins regardless of the work | D-03; DT-01 |
| Judge is conflicted (edges to an entrant) | Per personas.md the best judges are the most central, so the best judges are the most conflicted | Graph-based detection (`#CX-03`) — a real advantage no other contest platform has |
| Vote brigading / bought votes | The standard attack on any public vote | Rejecting public voting as default removes most of the surface |
| Judge never judges | The contest stalls indefinitely — a common, mundane, fatal failure | Deadline plus fallback needed |
| Losing entrants feel it was rigged | 39 of 40 lose; their experience decides whether the format survives in the scene | Legible verdicts. See DT-04 |
| Result overturned after payout | Practically unrecoverable | Disputes resolve before money (`#CX-04`) |
| Sybil ring votes / judges | Inherits 03.04.01 DT-04 | Routes to 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Public voting is the natural judging model — it is democratic and drives engagement | The convention for online contests; it is what every platform does | ❌ REJECTED → parent D-03. A public vote in a music contest measures **whose fans turned up**, not whose work was best. It is a promotion mechanic wearing a judging costume, and it destroys the format's only structural value (03.08.02 DT-02: a newcomer can be seen on merit). Worse here than elsewhere: per D-13a fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude, so an entrant with a modest fanbase beats an unknown genius by arithmetic, every time. It would also make contests a **reason to buy followers**, adding a new incentive to game a graph that 03.04.01 DT-04 already flags as a sybil target. The same participation-vs-evidence rejection appears in 03.02.02 DT-01, 03.03.02 DT-04 and 03.07 DT-01 — this is a domain-level principle, not four coincidences. |
| DT-02 | Judging should be blind | Fairness intuition | ⏳ DEFERRED — genuinely two-sided. **For**: blind judging is the only way 03.08.02 DT-02 works; if judges see names, the credited producer beats the unknown on reputation and the on-ramp closes. **Against**: this platform's entire thesis (D-18) is that provenance and identity make things trustworthy — blind judging deliberately discards the evidence the platform exists to provide, and conflict detection needs identities anyway. Possible resolution: identities known to the *system* (for conflict flagging) and hidden from the *judge* during evaluation. Recorded as Q-01 rather than decided. |
| DT-03 | Peer voting (entrants judge each other) is a cheap judging model | It needs no appointed panel and scales | ❌ REJECTED for this pass: entrants have a direct interest in each other losing, and per the reciprocity pattern seen three times already in this domain (03.01.03 DT-01 endorsements, 03.04.03 Q-03 referrals, 03.03.02 DT-04 scoring), music communities are small and reciprocal. Peer voting in a scene means the bloc with the most friends wins, which is public voting with extra steps and a smaller electorate. |
| DT-04 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **the losers' experience is the product** | Reasoning about the 39:1 ratio | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the insight most likely to be missed. Every contest produces one satisfied user and thirty-nine disappointed ones, and the thirty-nine decide whether the format survives in that scene — a beat battle that felt rigged is not re-entered, and the scene's producers say so to each other. So the feature's real success metric is not the winner's satisfaction but **whether losers feel the process was legitimate**, which means: legible verdicts, visible conflict handling, and reasoning rather than a bare result. This inverts the design priority — the winner announcement is the easy part, and the losing-entry experience is where the engineering should go. It also reinforces D-03: an expert panel can explain itself, and a public vote cannot explain anything except that someone had more followers. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Public voting is not the default judging model | DT-01 — it measures follower counts and destroys the format's only structural value. |
| D-02 | The graph detects conflicts; it never weights authority | `#R-01` — centrality measures how much you work, not your taste or fairness. |
| D-03 | Peer voting is not offered | DT-03 — entrants have an interest in each other losing, and music scenes are small and reciprocal. |
| D-04 | Verdicts are legible and reasoned | DT-04 — the 39 losers decide whether the format survives; legitimacy is the product. |
| D-05 | Judging appointments are craft-scoped | An Operator has no standing on craft (03.01.03 D-03); a general judge role discards evidence the graph holds. |
