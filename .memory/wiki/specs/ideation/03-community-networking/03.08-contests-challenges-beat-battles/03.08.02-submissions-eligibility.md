# Feature: Submissions & Eligibility

> **Number**: 03.08.02
> **Parent**: [Contests, Challenges & Beat Battles](./03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Entering a contest: the submitted work, and the rules deciding whether it counts. The governing rule is
that eligibility is evaluated **at intake** (`03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-cx.md#CX-02`) — a
submission rejected after the work is done is the free-work harvest (03.03.04 DT-03) with a deadline
attached, and worse, because the entrant worked believing the rules admitted them.

Per DT-02 this feature carries the sub-domain's strongest thesis argument: a contest submission may be the
only route by which an **uncredited newcomer acquires their first piece of evidenced work** — which would
make contests a graph-bootstrapping mechanism rather than a diversion.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Submits work. Per personas.md they are "not a star" building from a portfolio of small income streams — and per 03.03.01 DT-02 the platform's evidence-first ranking structurally buries them if they have no credits. A contest is one of the few surfaces where they can be *seen* without being hired first, which makes this feature disproportionately important to the newest users. |
| Producer | Full | Submits to craft challenges. Per personas.md their standing is already evidenced by sessions, so they have less to gain — and their entry raises a fairness question (a 400-credit producer in a scene beat battle against newcomers is not obviously a contest). |
| Operator | None | No access (parent Role Matrix). Per personas.md they "sell time and space rather than creative output" — they have nothing to submit. They organise (03.08.01), they do not enter. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-04). No craft to submit. They watch. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A submission is a work plus an acceptance of the brief's frozen terms (03.08.01). Eligibility is evaluated
at intake and frozen at submission time (`#CX-02` synthesis 5) — an entrant whose eligibility lapses
mid-contest (they leave the scene) is not retroactively disqualified.

Per `#R-02` a post is never a submission: entering must be a deliberate act, because it is an acceptance of
rights terms, and per personas.md accidentally accepting terms you do not understand is the Musician's named
worst-accidental behaviour.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — audio handling, format requirements and storage depend on
the unresolved media strategy (constraints.md).

### Happy Path

1. Entrant reads the brief, including its rights terms, presented plainly
2. Entrant submits their work and explicitly accepts the terms — a deliberate act, never a side effect
3. Eligibility is evaluated at intake; pass or fail is immediate and legible
4. Submission enters the judged set, its eligibility and terms frozen

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Ineligible entry discovered at judging** | The free-work harvest with a deadline (`#CX-02`) | Must fail at intake. Anything uncheckable at intake is not eligibility, it is a scheduled dispute |
| Submission contains an uncleared sample | Per personas.md the Musician's worst accidental behaviour, now exposed to a contest's whole audience and the organiser | Real hazard, unresolved. Routes to 09/24 — and note the platform invited the derivative work (03.08.01) |
| Entrant's eligibility lapses mid-contest | They leave the scene between entering and judging | Frozen at submission; not retroactively disqualified |
| Entrant withdraws after judging starts | Judges have already spent time; other entrants may be affected | `[PENDING]` |
| Losing entries — what happens to them? | 40 people made work; 39 lost. The work exists and its ownership is per the brief | 03.08.04 — and per DT-02 this is where the credit question bites |
| Duplicate/plagiarised submission | Someone submits another's work | The graph cannot help — a newcomer has no evidence either way. Routes to 24 |
| Established Producer enters a newcomer beat battle | Legal, and it defeats the format's only real value (DT-02) | See Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Submission form opening | Skeleton with terms shown plainly |
| Empty | No submissions | Organiser sees zero — the contest's own cold-start risk |
| Submitted | Entry accepted | Confirmation with the accepted terms restated |
| Rejected | Failed intake eligibility | Immediate, legible reason — **before** work is wasted where possible |
| Frozen | Judging began | Entry locked |
| Error | Upload failed | Draft preserved; a lost submission near a deadline is unforgivable |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Eligibility can be checked when convenient | Naive | ❌ REJECTED → `#CX-02`. Checking at judging means the entrant did the work under rules that turn out not to admit them, which is indistinguishable from the free-work harvest scam 03.03.04 DT-03 identifies — and here it is worse, because a deadline forced them to hurry. Anything the brief requires that cannot be verified at intake is not an eligibility rule; it is a dispute the organiser is scheduling. That constraint should feed back into what 03.08.01 permits a brief to require. |
| DT-02 | **Contests are the only surface where an uncredited newcomer can acquire evidenced work** | Deep Think against 03.03.01 DT-02 (the rich-get-richer defect) and parent Q-02 | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the strongest argument for building this sub-domain at all. The platform's evidence-first design (03.03.02 D-01, 03.04.01 D-01) has a structural defect: you need credits to be discoverable, and you need to be discoverable to get hired, and you need to be hired to get credits. Every mechanism in this domain reproduces that loop — matching, ranking, the graph, reachability, referrals. A contest breaks it: an unknown producer submits a beat, is judged on the work rather than their history, and wins visibly. **If a contest result generates a credit (parent Q-03), contests become the graph's on-ramp** — and this sub-domain moves from "fun" to thesis-critical. If it does not, they are entertainment. That single unresolved question determines this sub-domain's entire MoSCoW placement, which is why Q-01 escalates it rather than guessing. |
| DT-03 | Failure mode at the intersection with Rights (09): the platform invited the infringement | Adversarial reasoning against 03.08.01's remix formats | ✅ CONFIRMED as a distinct hazard from the ordinary UGC case. When a user uploads an uncleared sample to their own project, they did that. When they upload it to a remix contest the platform framed, promoted and set the terms for, WeJammin's position is materially different — it solicited the derivative work. Per D-10 the platform claims to hold the ownership record end-to-end; being the invitation to an infringement inside its own contest feature is a self-inflicted wound in its thesis domain. This raises the bar on 03.08.01's rights check from "check the organiser's rights" to "and think about what the brief invites entrants to do". |
