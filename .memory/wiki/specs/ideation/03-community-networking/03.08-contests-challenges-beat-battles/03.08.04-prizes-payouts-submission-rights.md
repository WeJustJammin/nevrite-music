# Feature: Prizes, Payouts & Submission Rights

> **Number**: 03.08.04
> **Parent**: [Contests, Challenges & Beat Battles](./03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What the winner gets, and — far more consequentially — **what happens to everyone's work**. A contest with
40 entries produces 40 copyrightable works; one wins a prize and 39 sit in the platform's storage under
whatever terms the brief set. Per D-02 on the parent those terms are settled in the brief before any
submission exists, and per `03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-cx.md#CX-01` the rights position
constrains what the brief may ask for in the first place.

This is the feature where a community ritual collides with the platform's thesis domain. Per D-10 WeJammin
claims to hold the ownership record end-to-end; fumbling ownership inside its own contest feature would be
a self-inflicted wound in exactly the place it claims authority.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Wins prizes and, far more often, loses — 39 times out of 40. Per personas.md their income is "a portfolio of small income streams", so a contest prize is real money to them, not play money. Per personas.md they are also the persona who "signs a split sheet they don't understand" and "discovers years later they gave away their publishing" — a contest rights grab is that failure with a leaderboard attached. |
| Producer | Full | Organises and funds prizes; enters occasionally. As organiser they are the one accepting a rights position on behalf of a contest they framed — and per personas.md they are the trust broker whose name made people enter, so they carry the reputational weight if the terms turn out to be predatory. |
| Operator | Config | Funds prizes for room-facing contests (a battle night with a cash prize, or free studio time). Per personas.md their inventory is perishable — "free studio Tuesday" is a prize that costs them nothing they could otherwise have sold, which makes them a natural prize funder. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-04). They watch the result; they do not fund, win, or hold rights. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Two things resolve at the verdict:

**Prizes** — cash, gear, studio time, a release, a session. A cash prize drags in domain 23's payout
machinery and, per constraints.md, unresolved KYC/AML and tax-reporting obligations. Per `#CX-04` a contest
can therefore **announce a winner it cannot lawfully pay**.

**Rights** — per 03.08.01 D-02 the default is entrant-retains with a narrow, purpose-limited licence. The
39 losing entries revert cleanly; the winning entry does whatever the brief said, plainly, and the entrant
saw it before they worked.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the licence's exact scope depends on domain 09/11's rights
model.

### Happy Path

1. Verdict issued (03.08.03); disputes resolve **before** money moves (`#CX-04`)
2. The winner's rights position resolves per the frozen brief terms — no surprise, no renegotiation
3. Losing entries' licences lapse; the work reverts entirely to its makers
4. Prize pays out through domain 23's machinery

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Winner cannot be paid** — no KYC, wrong jurisdiction, tax status unknown | Announced but unpaid; per `#CX-04` this destroys the organiser's and the platform's credibility at once | Per constraints.md, KYC/AML and tax reporting are open. Payout eligibility should be checked at *entry*, not at victory |
| Losing entries silently retained under a broad licence | 39 producers quietly lose control of their work on the platform that promised to protect it | 03.08.01 D-02 — the default is where this is won or lost |
| Prize promised, never funded | The scene's trust in the format dies with one incident | Escrow, or the prize is not stated. See DT-02 |
| Result overturned after payout | Practically unrecoverable | Disputes before money (`#CX-04`) |
| Non-cash prize is worthless or vapour ("exposure", "a meeting") | The oldest exploitation in music | See DT-03 |
| Prize is a **release or a deal** | The prize is now a contract in domains 09/12, awarded by a contest | Q-02 — a serious escalation |
| Entry fee charged | Per parent Q-04 this may cross into regulated prize-competition territory | `/create-prd-security` |
| Winner is a minor | Per D-13c statutory duties; payouts and rights grants involving minors are a distinct legal problem | `[PENDING]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Submission rights are a legal detail to sort out at the end | The convenient framing, and what most contest platforms do | ❌ REJECTED → parent D-02. This is **the reconstruction failure**, staged deliberately, inside the product built to prevent it. Per problem-statement.md the root cause is that "no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction" — and a contest that leaves ownership until after the winner is chosen recreates that exact defect on purpose, with the platform as the author of the arrangement. Per personas.md the moment to agree terms is "while everyone is present and friendly" — which for a contest is the brief, before anyone knows whose entry will be brilliant. Once the winning beat exists and is excellent, the negotiation is adversarial. |
| DT-02 | An unfunded prize is a minor problem | Underestimating a mundane failure | ❌ REJECTED: per personas.md the Musician makes a living from "a portfolio of small income streams" — a £200 beat battle prize is not a token, it is a bill paid. An unpaid prize is a small theft, and in a scene it is a story that spreads faster than any marketing. The platform's brand is the frame (03.08.01 DT-03), so the entrant's grievance attaches to WeJammin, not to the stranger who ran the contest. Prizes should be **escrowed at brief-publication** or not stated — the same reasoning that makes deposits standard practice for the Operator persona in domain 17. |
| DT-03 | Non-cash prizes are lower-risk than cash | They avoid payouts, KYC and tax entirely | ❌ REJECTED as stated — they trade financial risk for exploitation risk. "Exposure", "a meeting", "feedback from a name" are the oldest unpaid-work currencies in music, and a contest paying in exposure is asking 40 people to work for free while looking generous. Some non-cash prizes are genuinely valuable and honest (a day in a real studio, actual gear, a release) and per the Operator's Role Lens above, perishable inventory makes a good prize. The distinction the platform must enforce is **specific and deliverable** vs **vague and unenforceable** — "a mastering session with X" is a prize; "exposure to our network" is not, and should not be publishable as one. |
| DT-04 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **payout eligibility must be checked at entry, not at victory** | Reasoning against constraints.md's open KYC/AML and tax questions | ✅ CONFIRMED and it is the practical trap here. Per constraints.md, KYC/AML, marketplace facilitator tax and 1099-K/W-8BEN obligations are all `[PENDING]` and all triggered by paying money to individuals. A contest open to anyone will be won, eventually, by someone the platform cannot lawfully pay — wrong jurisdiction, no tax identity, a minor (D-13c). Discovering that **at the moment of victory** means announcing a winner and then not paying them, which per DT-02 is the worst failure this feature has. The check belongs at entry: if the platform cannot pay you, you should learn that before you spend two days on a beat, not after you win. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Rights are settled in the brief, before submissions exist (parent D-02) | DT-01 — otherwise this is the reconstruction failure staged deliberately, inside the product built to prevent it. |
| D-02 | Prizes are escrowed at brief-publication, or not stated | DT-02 — an unpaid prize is a small theft that attaches to WeJammin's brand, not the organiser's. |
| D-03 | Non-cash prizes must be specific and deliverable; "exposure" is not a prize | DT-03 — the oldest unpaid-work currency in music, and the platform should not publish it as compensation. |
| D-04 | Payout eligibility is checked at entry, not at victory | DT-04 — announcing a winner you cannot lawfully pay is this feature's worst failure. |
| D-05 | Losing entries revert entirely | The default that makes contests safe to enter; 39 of 40 entrants experience only this. |
