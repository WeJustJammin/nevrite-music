## Overview

The scene's competitive rituals — beat battles, remix competitions, production challenges, "flip this
sample". These are a genuine and load-bearing part of music community culture, not a gamification veneer:
a beat battle is how producers in a scene establish standing without a client, and it is one of the few
formats where a newcomer with no credits can be seen (which makes it a partial answer to 03.03.01 DT-02,
the rich-get-richer defect).

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: four capabilities that interact hard and cannot be separated.
The brief constrains eligibility; eligibility constrains judging; judging determines prizes; and **prizes
plus submissions equal rights**, which reaches back and constrains what the brief may ask for. A contest
where a submission's ownership is unresolved is not a contest — it is a dispute waiting to be filed.

**The rights half is why this is more than a fun feature.** A remix competition asks people to create
derivative works from someone else's master. Per D-10 the rights stack is this platform's thesis; running
a contest that fumbles submission ownership would be a self-inflicted wound in the exact domain WeJammin
claims to be authoritative about.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Contests are a sub-domain, not a feature | Brief → eligibility → judging → prizes → rights is a chain where each link constrains the previous one. In particular, submission rights (03.08.04) reach back and constrain what a brief may legally ask for — that is interaction, not decomposition. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | **Submission rights are settled in the brief, before any submission exists** | Per D-18 the platform's thesis is capture-at-source; per personas.md the design must "make the lazy path the correct path". A contest that leaves ownership until after the winner is picked is the reconstruction failure, staged deliberately, in the domain this platform claims authority over. | D-18; D-10; personas.md |
| D-03 | Public voting is **not** the default judging model | Public voting in music contests measures follower count, not the work — it is a promotion mechanic wearing a judging costume, and it systematically defeats the one property that makes contests valuable (a newcomer can be seen). | Deep Think, DT-01 on 03.08.03 |
| D-04 | Fans may **watch and read** contests but not submit, judge or vote | Per personas.md they have no craft to submit and no standing to judge; per D-13a their volume would make any public vote a popularity poll of the entrants' fanbases. Watching is legitimate — a beat battle is genuinely spectator content, and per D-11 fans are first-class users of the things built for them. | personas.md; D-11; D-13a |
| D-05 | Contests stay in domain 03 rather than moving to 04 (Opportunities) or 21 (Promotion) | A beat battle is a **scene ritual** whose purpose is standing within a community, not a job posting and not a marketing campaign. But a brand-sponsored remix competition is genuinely 21's product — the boundary is real and is flagged as Q-01. | Node Classification Gate |
