# Feature: Contest Creation & Briefs

> **Number**: 03.08.01
> **Parent**: [Contests, Challenges & Beat Battles](./03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Where a contest is defined: what the challenge is, who may enter, how it will be judged, what the prize is,
and — per D-02 on the parent — **who will own the submissions**. The brief is a contract offered to
strangers, and per `03.08-contests-challenges-beat-battles-cx.md#CX-01` the organiser's rights position
constrains what it may legally ask for, not the other way around.

The design rule is that everything contestable is settled here, before a single entrant does a minute of
work. That is D-18 (capture at source) applied to a competition.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Runs scene contests and challenges. Per personas.md they are the persona who "signs a split sheet they don't understand" — and as an *organiser* they are now the one writing it, for strangers, which is worse. The brief form must do the understanding for them, because they will not read a rights explainer. |
| Producer | Full | The most credible organiser — per personas.md their name on a brief is what makes people enter, because their attestation carries weight. Also the most likely to run a "flip this sample" challenge, which is the highest-rights-risk format (`#CX-01`). |
| Operator | Full | Organises room-facing contests — a battle night, a residency competition. Their rights exposure is lower (they supply a venue, not a master) and per personas.md their motivation is real: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever, and a battle night fills one. |
| Fan | None | No access (parent D-04). Per personas.md they have no craft and no standing to set a brief. They watch (03.08.03). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A brief declares: the challenge, eligibility (03.08.02), the judging model (03.08.03), the prize, and the
rights terms (03.08.04). Per `#CX-01` publishing **blocks** if the organiser's rights in any supplied source
material do not support the brief — a warning would simply be clicked past.

Per `#CX-01` synthesis (5), terms are **immutable once a submission exists**. An organiser rewriting the
deal after entries arrive is retroactively changing an agreement people already accepted, which is the exact
wrong this platform exists to prevent.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the brief's field set depends on domain 09/11's rights
model, which is not yet drilled.

### Happy Path

1. Organiser defines the challenge and supplies any source material (a master to remix, a sample to flip)
2. System checks the organiser's rights in that material against what the brief asks entrants to do
3. Organiser sets eligibility, judging model, prize, and rights terms — with defaults that are safe rather than maximal (DT-02)
4. Brief publishes; terms freeze on first submission

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Organiser lacks rights in the source material** | A remix brief for a master they do not control invites strangers to create infringing derivatives at scale, on the platform's invitation | Publish blocks (`#CX-01`). Not a warning — a warned organiser publishes anyway |
| Organiser writes a maximal rights grab ("we own all submissions") | Legal, common in brand contests, and corrosive — see DT-02 | Defaults must be safe; a grab should require deliberate action |
| Terms changed after entries arrive | Retroactive rewriting of an accepted deal | Frozen on first submission (`#CX-01`) |
| Brief requires something uncheckable at intake ("must be original") | Schedules a dispute for judging day (`#CX-02`) | Eligibility must be checkable at intake or it is not eligibility |
| Prize is promised but not funded | Winner announced, nobody paid — the credibility failure that kills the format in a scene | See 03.08.04 |
| Scene-scoped brief ("Bristol only") | Inherits the unresolved scene-boundary question (03.06.01 Q-03) | Adjudication is harder than it sounds |
| Sponsored brief with marketing objectives | This is domain 21, not community (parent Q-01) | Boundary leak |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Being written | Editable |
| Blocked | Rights check failed | Cannot publish, with the reason stated specifically |
| Open | Published, accepting entries | Live brief; terms editable **only** until first submission |
| Frozen | First submission received | Terms immutable |
| Closed | Deadline passed | Judging (03.08.03) |
| Error | Write failed | Draft preserved |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A contest brief is a simple form | The obvious reading — it is a description of a challenge | ❌ REJECTED: a brief is a **contract offered to strangers**, covering rights in works that do not exist yet, judged by criteria the organiser sets, with money attached. Per personas.md the organiser is frequently a working musician who does not understand rights — that is a named persona characteristic, not a slur. So the form is doing legal work on behalf of someone who does not know they need it, for entrants who are trusting the platform's frame rather than the organiser's competence. That is a design responsibility, not a form-builder. |
| DT-02 | Rights defaults do not matter much because organisers will set them | Standard product assumption | ❌ REJECTED: defaults are the entire ballgame here, and for the same reason as 03.04.04 DT-04 — the person configuring will not think about it. If the default is "organiser owns all submissions", every scene beat battle silently strips 40 producers of their work, on a platform whose whole thesis (D-18, D-10) is that it protects exactly this. If the default is "entrant retains everything", a legitimate remix competition cannot function without deliberate configuration. The safe default is entrant-retains with a **narrow, explicit** licence for the contest's stated purpose — and anything broader requires the organiser to act deliberately and the entrant to see it clearly. The platform's founding claim is on the line in a default value. |
| DT-03 | What a domain expert would expect that nobody mentioned: **the platform is the frame, so it inherits the trust** | Reasoning about who entrants actually trust | ✅ CONFIRMED: an entrant does not read the brief's terms — they read "this is on WeJammin, so it's probably fine". That is exactly the trust the platform is trying to build (problem-statement.md: the industry's records are unreliable; we are the trustworthy alternative), and it means a predatory contest hosted here damages WeJammin far more than it damages its organiser. The organiser is a stranger; the platform is the brand. So the platform cannot be a neutral host of contest terms in the way a form-builder can — it must constrain what a brief may do. This is the same reasoning that makes the rights check a **block** rather than a warning. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Publishing blocks on a failed rights check | DT-03 — a warning gets clicked past, and the platform's brand carries the entrant's trust. |
| D-02 | Rights defaults are entrant-retains with a narrow purpose-limited licence | DT-02 — the default is the ballgame; a grabby default silently strips the people the platform exists to protect. |
| D-03 | Terms freeze on first submission | `#CX-01` — retroactive rewriting is the wrong this product exists to prevent. |
| D-04 | Everything contestable is settled in the brief | D-02 on the parent; D-18 applied to competition. |
