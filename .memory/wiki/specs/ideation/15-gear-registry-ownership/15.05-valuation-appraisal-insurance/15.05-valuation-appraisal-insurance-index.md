## Overview

What an object is worth, who says so, and the documents that turn that into insurance coverage and
a payout. It spans three genuinely different kinds of claim — an algorithm's estimate from
marketplace comps, a human appraiser's dated opinion, and the evidentiary pack an insurer needs at
the moment of loss — and the sub-domain's whole job is keeping them distinct, because collapsing an
estimate into an appraisal is both a fraud vector and an uninsurable document.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: an estimate, an appraisal and a claim pack interact
constantly — an appraisal supersedes an estimate, a schedule bundles appraisals, a modification
(15.03) invalidates all three — while having different authors, different evidentiary weight, and
different consequences for being wrong.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | An algorithmic estimate is **never** presentable as an appraisal, and never enters a claim pack | They are different claims by different authors with different liability. Insurers require a human appraisal or a receipt; an estimate in a claim pack is a document that gets the claim rejected at best. Recorded as a deliberate severance — see Rejected Pair R-01 in the CX file. | Deep Think DT-01 on 15.05.01 |
| D-02 | Valuation method and sample size are disclosed with every estimate | WeJammin runs both the marketplace and the valuation, so it has a structural incentive to inflate. Undisclosed method makes that incentive invisible. | Deep Think DT-02 on 15.05.01 |
| D-03 | The high-value output of this sub-domain is the **claim pack**, not the schedule | The sweep's candidate named "insurance schedules". A schedule is a low-frequency admin document. The pack assembled at the moment of loss — ownership proof, serial, valuation, photos, theft flag — is the moment the entire domain pays for itself. | Deep Think DT-01 on 15.05.03 |
