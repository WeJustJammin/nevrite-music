## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Appraisal Record ↔ Automated Valuation

**Relationship**: Both produce a number against the same gear record, and the temptation is to
render one "current value". That merge is the failure. An estimate is an algorithm's inference from
comparable sales; an appraisal is a named human's dated opinion with their reputation attached. They
disagree routinely — and when they do, the disagreement is information, not a bug to be resolved by
picking one.

Precedence: an in-date appraisal is authoritative for insurance purposes; the estimate remains
visible as a market signal. Neither overwrites the other.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: usually has estimates only — appraisals cost money and most gear doesn't justify one. Sees the estimate labelled as an estimate.
- **Producer**: has appraisals on a handful of high-value pieces and estimates on everything else. The mixed state is normal and must render coherently.
- **Operator**: appraises room assets for business insurance; estimates are near-irrelevant to them.
- **Fan**: sees only the estimate, on a listing. Never sees an appraisal.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both write value facts to one record. Neither overwrites; the record holds both with provenance. There is no merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: A new appraisal does not delete estimates; it takes precedence for insurance rendering. A modification (15.03) invalidates both — see the domain CX.
3. **Permission intersection**: Estimates are visible to buyers via 13; appraisals are owner-private. A public estimate must never be sourced from a private appraisal.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A large divergence between appraisal and estimate is worth surfacing to the owner — it means either the market moved or the appraisal is stale.
5. **State transition conflict**: An appraisal arriving while a listing is live could change the asking price mid-sale. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

### CX-02: Appraisal Record ↔ Insurance Schedule & Claim Pack

**Relationship**: The pack is only as good as the appraisals in it, and appraisals expire. An
insurer asked to pay out on a schedule backed by a 2019 appraisal of a vintage instrument in a
market that has moved will discount or reject it. The pack must therefore surface appraisal age at
assembly time — *before* the loss, when it can still be fixed — rather than presenting a complete-
looking document that fails at the claim.

This is the sub-domain's only genuinely time-sensitive coupling: everything else here is a
snapshot, but a schedule is a promise about the future.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: rarely schedules items; when they do it's a handful of pieces and they will not track expiry.
- **Producer**: schedules a studio's worth of gear; expiry tracking across dozens of items is a real burden and the platform is well-placed to carry it.
- **Operator**: business insurance with formal renewal cycles — the most structured user of this.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The schedule references appraisals; it does not copy their values. Copying would freeze a number that the appraisal might later be found to have got wrong.
2. **Trigger chain**: Appraisal ages past a threshold → schedule flagged stale → owner notified. Async, and the notification is the entire value: a stale schedule discovered at claim time is worthless.
3. **Permission intersection**: Both owner-private. The pack is generated by the owner and handed to an off-platform insurer — the platform never transmits it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Staleness notifications must reach the owner well before renewal, not at loss.
5. **State transition conflict**: A theft (15.02.01) occurring while a schedule is stale is the worst case, and it is exactly when the owner discovers the problem. The pack must have warned earlier.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 15.05.01 Automated Valuation | 15.05.03 Insurance Schedule & Claim Pack | **Deliberately severed, not merely absent.** The obvious design lets an algorithmic estimate back an insurance schedule — it is free, instant, and covers every item, whereas appraisals are expensive and cover few. It must not exist. Insurers require a human appraisal or a receipt; a schedule backed by estimates produces a claim that is rejected at the moment of loss, when the owner has already lost the gear and believed themselves covered. The platform would have manufactured that false confidence. An estimate may *prompt* an owner to seek an appraisal; it may never substitute for one. Ratified as D-01 in the sub-domain index. |
| R-02 | 15.05.01 Automated Valuation | 15.05.02 Appraisal Record — as an *input* relationship | Considered: should the estimate be shown to the appraiser as a starting point? Rejected — anchoring. An appraiser primed with the platform's number produces a number close to the platform's number, which destroys the independence that makes the appraisal worth more than the estimate in the first place, and it quietly launders the marketplace's conflict of interest (Q-01) into the one document meant to be free of it. The precedence relationship (CX-01) stands; the anchoring one does not. |
