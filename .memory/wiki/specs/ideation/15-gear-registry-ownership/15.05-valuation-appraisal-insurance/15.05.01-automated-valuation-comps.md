# Feature: Automated Valuation from Marketplace Comps

> **Number**: 15.05.01
> **Parent**: [Valuation, Appraisal & Insurance](./15.05-valuation-appraisal-insurance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

An estimate of what a registered object is worth, inferred from comparable sales on the marketplace
(domain 13) and adjusted for condition and originality (15.01.06). It is the number that makes a
collection feel alive and gives an owner a reason to keep the registry current — and it is the one
place in domain 15 where WeJammin has a direct financial interest in the answer, which governs
almost every design decision here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees an estimate per item and a private aggregate across their collection (15.04) — the number that tells them whether they're underinsured. Uses it to price a sale. Sees the sample size behind it, which for anything unusual will be humbling. |
| Producer | Full | Same, on gear where comps are thinnest — a specific vintage compressor may have sold three times in five years, worldwide. The estimate is at its least reliable exactly where the value is highest. |
| Operator | Full | Values room assets for business insurance and for depreciation. Their gear is more commodity-shaped (PA, backline) so comps are healthier and estimates are better. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees an estimate on a marketplace listing as a price signal ("listed above/below estimate"). Never sees an owner's private estimates or any aggregate. |

## Behavior

The estimate is a function of the composite identity (15.01.05), condition, originality (15.01.06),
and comparable sales. It is recomputed when the market moves or when a modification lands — and
recomputation on a mod is **flagged, not silent**, because a number that quietly changes after a
refret is a number nobody can trust.

Every estimate carries its method and its **sample size**. An estimate from four comps and an
estimate from four hundred are different objects, and rendering both as a confident figure is the
feature's easiest and worst mistake.

### Happy Path

1. Musician registers a 2012 American Standard Strat.
2. System finds 60+ comparable sales on domain 13, adjusts for condition and originality.
3. Estimate renders with its range, sample size, and recency: "£850–£980, based on 63 sales in the last 18 months."
4. Musician sees their collection aggregate update privately (15.04) and gets a nudge: they're insured for less than they own.
5. They pursue an appraisal (15.05.02) on the two items that justify it — which is the estimate's most valuable job.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Few or no comps (rare, boutique, one-off) | Estimate withheld rather than fabricated | "Not enough sales data to estimate this" — an honest gap; an estimate on n=1 is noise dressed as information |
| Gear has a provenance premium (15.09) | **Not** auto-priced | Fact surfaced, price not adjusted — see DT-03 |
| Modification lands (15.03) | Revaluation flagged, not silently applied | "This modification may affect value — re-estimate?" |
| Comps are WeJammin-only | The sample is one marketplace's, not the market's | Method disclosure must say so; a WeJammin-only sample in a thin category is a self-referential loop |
| Estimate used as an insurance value | Blocked | See sub-domain D-01 and Rejected Pair R-01 — this is a severed path, not a missing one |
| Market moves sharply (a model gets famous) | Estimate lags | Recency shown; a stale estimate must be legible as stale |
| Owner disagrees with the estimate | No override that changes the displayed estimate | An estimate the owner can edit is not an estimate |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Computing | Skeleton on the value field |
| Empty | Insufficient comps | "Not enough data" — a legitimate terminal state, not a failure |
| Populated | Estimate available | Range + sample size + recency, labelled an estimate |
| Error | Computation failed | Retry; never fall back to a cached number without saying it's cached |
| Partial | Comps exist but condition/originality unknown | Wider range, explicitly wider *because* of what's unknown |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An estimate is basically an appraisal, produced cheaply | The commercially attractive framing, and the one that makes the feature look far more valuable than it is | ❌ REJECTED — they are different claims by different authors with different liability. An insurer will not pay out on an algorithm's guess, so an estimate used as an insurance value produces a *rejected claim at the moment of loss*, after the owner has already lost the gear and spent years believing themselves covered. The platform would have manufactured that false confidence — the same failure shape as 15.02.02's "verified clean". The estimate's legitimate job is to **prompt** an appraisal, never to substitute for one. Ratified as D-01; the path is severed in the CX file as R-01. |
| DT-02 | The platform can value gear neutrally | The unexamined assumption behind offering the feature at all | ❌ REJECTED — WeJammin runs the marketplace whose comps feed the valuation *and* takes a fee on the resulting sales. Higher valuations mean higher asking prices mean higher take. The incentive to inflate is structural and permanent — it does not require anyone to act in bad faith, only for a threshold to be tuned optimistically once. Worse, because WeJammin is both the price source and the price consumer, inflation is self-reinforcing: inflated estimates raise listing prices, which become the comps for the next estimate. Disclosure of method and sample size is the minimum mitigation and is probably not sufficient. Escalated as Q-01 — it is a values decision the owner must make. |
| DT-03 | Provenance premium should be priced automatically | It is the domain's Carfax thesis taken to its conclusion: if the chain makes gear worth more, quantify it | ❌ REJECTED — a provenance premium is real (an instrument played on a famous record is worth a multiple) but it is non-linear, comp-less and reputational. There are no comparable sales for "the Tele on that record" because there is exactly one, and any coefficient the platform invents is fiction with a number attached. Worse, auto-pricing it creates a direct incentive to inflate gear discography claims (15.09) — which is precisely why 15.09 requires Producer attestation rather than owner assertion. **Surface the fact; let the market price it.** The chain's job is to make the fact credible, not to monetise it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Estimates are withheld below a comp threshold rather than widened | An estimate on n=1 is noise wearing the clothes of information. "We don't know" is a better product than a wide range nobody reads the error bars on. |
| D-02 | Sample size and recency are rendered with the estimate, not buried | Per DT-02. It is the only thing that lets a user discount the number appropriately, and the only visible check on the inflation incentive. |
| D-03 | Revaluation after a modification is flagged, never silent | A value that changes quietly after a refret is a value nobody can audit. |
