# Feature: Appraisal Record

> **Number**: 15.05.02
> **Parent**: [Valuation, Appraisal & Insurance](./15.05-valuation-appraisal-insurance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A named human's dated, attributable opinion of what an object is worth, attached to its record.
Unlike an estimate it carries an author with a reputation and (usually) professional liability,
which is exactly why insurers accept it and don't accept algorithms. It is the counter-attestation
mechanic — a qualified second party's word beats a self-claim — applied to value rather than to
ownership or credits.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Commissions an appraisal on the two or three pieces that justify the cost, attaches the resulting document, and mostly forgets it exists until an insurer or a buyer asks. Sees when it has aged out of usefulness — which they would never track themselves. |
| Producer | Full | Appraises high-value outboard and vintage mics. Holds a mixed picture: appraisals on a few pieces, estimates on the rest. Also frequently *is* the qualified opinion for others, informally — a respected engineer's view on a compressor's worth carries real weight even without a formal appraisal. |
| Operator | Full | The most structured user. Business insurance has formal renewal cycles, so appraisals are a recurring operational task across dozens of room assets rather than a one-off. |
| Fan | None | Appraisals are owner-private documents with no public form. A Fan browsing a listing sees the estimate (15.05.01), never the appraisal — publishing "professionally appraised at £14,000" would be a value claim on a public page and a target marker. |

## Behavior

An appraisal attaches to a gear record: the appraiser's identity and credentials, a date, a value or
range, a basis, and the document itself. It supersedes the estimate for insurance purposes without
overwriting it — the two coexist, and their disagreement is information (see `#CX-01`).

**Age is a first-class field, not metadata.** An appraisal is a statement about a market at a point
in time. Vintage gear markets move, and an insurer looking at a 2019 appraisal in 2026 will discount
or reject it. The platform's most useful contribution here is not storing the document — it is
noticing, before the loss, that the document has expired.

### Happy Path

1. Producer has a vintage Fairchild appraised for their studio's insurance.
2. Appraiser produces a document; Producer attaches it to the gear record with the appraiser's details and date.
3. Record shows the appraised value alongside the (lower, thinner-sampled) estimate — the divergence itself is worth seeing.
4. Appraisal joins the insurance schedule (15.05.03).
5. Two years on, the platform flags that the appraisal is approaching the recency window their insurer requires — well before renewal, and long before any loss.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Appraisal ages out | Flagged stale; value retained but marked | "This appraisal is from 2019 — your insurer may not accept it" |
| Appraisal and estimate diverge sharply | Both shown; no reconciliation | The divergence is surfaced — it means the market moved or the appraisal is stale, and both are worth knowing |
| Appraiser is not on WeJammin (the common case) | Document attached; appraiser recorded as free text | Weaker attribution, honestly rendered |
| Gear modified after appraisal (15.03) | Appraisal invalidated — it described a different object | "This was appraised before the pickup swap" |
| Owner uploads a forged appraisal | Undetectable at this layer | No system response; mitigation is the appraiser's attributability, not verification |
| Appraisal commissioned by a seller mid-sale | Conflict — the seller chose and paid the appraiser | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Appraiser later discredited | Their appraisals across the registry are all affected at once | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Attaching document | Upload progress |
| Empty | No appraisal | "Not appraised" with the estimate shown instead, clearly labelled |
| Populated | Appraisal attached and in date | Value, appraiser, date |
| Error | Attach failed | Retry |
| Partial | Attached but aged past the window | Value shown, staleness prominent — this is the state that matters most and it must not be quiet |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An appraisal is a durable fact | It's a signed professional document — it feels permanent, and it's stored like one | ❌ REJECTED — an appraisal is a statement about a **market at a date**, and it decays. Vintage instrument values move considerably over a few years, and insurers enforce recency windows precisely because of it. The failure is silent and perfectly timed: the owner attaches a document, believes themselves covered, and discovers at the moment of loss that the schedule was worthless. Age must be a first-class field driving proactive notification, because **noticing the expiry before the loss is the platform's actual contribution here** — storing a PDF is not a product. |
| DT-02 | The appraiser should be a service-provider category in domain 05, like luthiers | Consistent with 15.03 DT-01's boundary, which worked well there | ⏳ DEFERRED, not confirmed — the boundary logic is identical (05 owns the transaction, 15 owns the record) and it would give appraisers attributable platform identities, which is the whole basis of an appraisal's weight. But the appraiser population is orders of magnitude smaller than luthiers or engineers, and a marketplace category with forty participants worldwide may not sustain itself. Recording the tension rather than forcing a call: escalated as Q-02 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-03 | Appraisals should be public on a listing — "professionally appraised at £14,000" is a strong sales signal | It is a strong sales signal, which is exactly why a seller would want it | ❌ REJECTED — two independent reasons, either sufficient. First, it is a **value claim on a public page** made by a party the seller chose and paid, which is a conflict the buyer cannot see and which would make appraisal-shopping a rational sales tactic. Second, it is a **target marker**: a public "£14,000" attached to a named owner composes with 15.04's exposure hazard, turning the appraisal into precisely the sortable value signal that 15.04 D-02 refuses to publish as an aggregate. Appraisals stay owner-private; a buyer who wants one can commission their own, which is what the appraisal market is for. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Appraisal age drives proactive notification | Per DT-01. Storing the document is table stakes; noticing it expired before the owner needed it is the product. |
| D-02 | Appraisals are owner-private with no public form | Per DT-03. A seller-commissioned value claim on a public listing is a conflict the buyer can't see, and a public high value is a target marker. |
| D-03 | An appraisal never overwrites an estimate | Their disagreement is information. Collapsing to one "value" field erases the distinction the whole sub-domain is built on. |
