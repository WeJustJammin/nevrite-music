# Feature: Insurance Schedule & Claim Pack

> **Number**: 15.05.03
> **Parent**: [Valuation, Appraisal & Insurance](./15.05-valuation-appraisal-insurance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Two documents the registry can produce that nothing else can. The **schedule** is the list of
itemised gear an insurer covers — routine admin. The **claim pack** is what an owner hands an
insurer after a theft: proof of ownership, serials, valuations, photographs, the dated theft flag
and its police reference, assembled in minutes instead of reconstructed from memory across a
fortnight. The pack is the moment the entire domain pays for itself, and the sweep's candidate
("insurance schedules") named the wrong half.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Generates a schedule when they finally get insured, and a claim pack at the worst moment of their professional life — standing next to an empty van. Everything the pack needs already exists in the registry, which is the payoff for years of low-grade admin they had no other reason to do. |
| Producer | Full | Schedules a studio's worth of gear across dozens of appraisals with staggered expiry dates — a tracking burden they cannot carry manually and the platform can. Their claim pack after a break-in is high-value and high-scrutiny. |
| Operator | Full | Business insurance on room assets (15.07), with formal renewal cycles and a broker who asks for the schedule annually. The most routine, least dramatic user — and the one for whom the schedule half actually matters. |
| Fan | None | No surface. Insurance is a professional concern and this persona has no gear stake. |

## Behavior

Both outputs are **documents the owner generates and hands to an off-platform party**. WeJammin
does not transmit them, does not talk to insurers, and takes no position on coverage. The insurer
is not a persona (D-19) — that absence is deliberate and it is why the boundary is a document
rather than an integration.

**The schedule** references appraisals (never copies their values) and flags staleness before
renewal, not at claim.

**The claim pack** is assembled from a theft report (15.02.01) and pulls everything the registry
already knows: identity, ownership claim and its strength, valuations, photographs, service
history, the flag, and its police reference if one exists. The pack is the answer to the domain's
founding observation — the record was captured when it was true, not reconstructed when it became
contested and valuable.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's van is emptied; they file a bulk theft report by case (15.02.01).
2. System offers a claim pack immediately — the prompt arrives at the moment of loss, not weeks later when they think to look.
3. Pack assembles: 12 items, serials, ownership claims with strength, valuations, dated photographs, theft flags, place and date of loss.
4. Musician adds the crime reference when police issue it two days later; the pack updates.
5. They send the pack to their insurer. What would have been a fortnight of hunting for receipts and half-remembering serial numbers took four minutes.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Appraisals in the schedule are stale | Flagged at generation, before renewal | "3 items have appraisals your insurer may reject" |
| Gear has estimates but no appraisals | Estimates **excluded** from the pack | "These items have no appraisal — your insurer will likely want one" — the severed path from R-01, made visible rather than silently substituted |
| Gear was never registered before the theft | Retroactive record (15.02.01 DT-01) with a weak claim | Pack includes it with its evidentiary weakness intact — a pack that overstates its evidence gets a claim rejected and burns the owner's credibility |
| Insurance pays out | Title typically passes to the insurer, who is off-platform | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see 15.02.01 Q-03. The registry has no way to represent an owner who isn't a user |
| Gear recovered after payout | Owner no longer owns it; the insurer does | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Owner has no insurance at all (the common case) | The pack is still useful — police, civil claim, resale evidence | Offered regardless of whether insurance exists |
| Pack used to support a fraudulent claim | The registry's evidence is exactly what a fraudster wants | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Pack assembling | Progress across items |
| Empty | Nothing to schedule | "Register gear to build a schedule" |
| Populated | Document generated | Preview then export |

| Partial | Some items lack appraisals or identity confidence | Pack generates with gaps **shown as gaps**, never silently filled with estimates |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The valuable output here is the insurance schedule | What the sweep's candidate was named — "Valuation & insurance schedules" | ❌ REJECTED, and this reframes the feature. A schedule is low-frequency admin; an owner produces one when they get insured and then not again for years, and a spreadsheet does the job adequately. The **claim pack** is the high-value output, because it lands at the moment of maximum pain and maximum information asymmetry: the owner has just lost their gear and now has to prove to a sceptical insurer that they owned it, what it was, and what it was worth — from memory, with no receipts, while grieving. That reconstruction is *the exact failure the whole platform exists to prevent*, transposed from splits onto objects. The registry can do it in four minutes because it captured the facts when they were true. This is the strongest single argument for registering gear at all, and it should lead the domain's value proposition. Ratified as D-03 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-02 | WeJammin should integrate with insurers or brokers | The obvious ambition — it would be the strongest retention hook in the domain | ❌ REJECTED for this pass — insurers are not on the platform, the insurer is not a persona (D-19), and insurance introduces regulatory surface (broking, distribution) that constraints.md's compliance table doesn't even list yet. The document boundary is honest, achievable, and delivers essentially all of the user value: the owner's problem is *assembling the evidence*, not transmitting it. Retained as Q-03 on the sub-domain index because the ambition is real and the owner may want it. |
| DT-03 | A pack should present the strongest possible case for the owner | Advocacy framing — the platform is on the owner's side, and the owner wants their claim paid | ❌ REJECTED — a pack that overstates its evidence gets the claim **rejected** and torches the owner's credibility with their insurer at the one moment they need it. Insurers are professional evaluators of exactly this and will find the gap. Honest evidence with visible gaps is a stronger claim than confident evidence with hidden ones — and it is the same principle as 15.01.04's "gaps render as gaps" and 15.02.02's "not reported stolen": the registry's value is that it does not overstate what it knows. Estimates stay out of packs; weak claims render as weak. |
