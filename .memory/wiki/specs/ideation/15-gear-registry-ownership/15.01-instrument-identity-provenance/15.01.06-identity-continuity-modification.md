# Feature: Identity Continuity Through Modification

> **Number**: 15.01.06
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Answers whether an instrument is still the same instrument after its parts change — the Ship of
Theseus problem, which in music is not a thought experiment but the normal condition of the
vintage market. A guitar gets a refret, then a new nut, then non-original pickups, then a
refinish, then a replacement neck carrying the serial. Originality is the dominant price driver in
vintage gear, so this is where identity, service history and valuation all collide.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Records what changed and when, at component level rather than as a "modified" checkbox. Sees the originality picture their instrument actually has, and — uncomfortably — sees the value consequence of the refinish they were about to do. |
| Producer | Full | Same, on re-chassised outboard, retubed amps, and recapped consoles. Studio gear is modified as a matter of routine maintenance, and the line between "serviced" and "modified" is genuinely blurry on a 40-year-old compressor. |
| Operator | Full | Room assets get modified constantly and by many hands. Their problem is attribution — who did what to the house Twin, and when. |
| Fan | None | No public surface of its own. A Fan sees the downstream effect on a listing: an originality summary rendered by 15.01.04 and priced by 15.05. |

## Behavior

A modification is a service event (15.03) with identity consequences. This feature owns the
consequences: component-level originality state, and the **fork decision** when the modified
component is the one carrying the serial.

**Originality is a spectrum, not a flag.** "Modified: yes" destroys the information that matters —
a refret is expected maintenance that barely moves value, while a refinish can halve it and
non-original pickups sit somewhere between. The registry must record *which components* are
original, replaced, or refinished, because that is precisely the question a vintage buyer asks.

**The fork.** If the serial lives on a swapped part, the number and the object separate. The
original body and the new assembly both have a defensible claim to the serial. Currently
`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — this is a genuine product decision, not an
implementation detail, and it has fraud consequences either way.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's Strat gets non-original pickups fitted; the tech logs it as a service event (15.03).
2. System recognises the event touches a value-relevant component and prompts for originality detail: which pickups came out, are they retained, are they going back in later?
3. Musician records that the originals are kept in the case — which preserves most of the value and is the single most important fact here.
4. Record's originality state updates at component level; the chain (15.01.04) gains the event.
5. Valuation (15.05.01) is flagged for revaluation rather than silently re-pricing.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Serial-bearing part is swapped | Identity may fork; both records could claim the serial | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see `#CX-05` |
| Original parts retained vs discarded | Materially different value outcome; must be a distinct field | "Are the original parts kept?" — asked at the moment it's known, not reconstructed later |
| Modification later reversed (originals refitted) | Reversal is a new event; originality state returns but the *history* of the mod does not vanish | Chain shows mod and reversal |
| Repair vs modification is ambiguous | On old gear this is genuinely undecidable — is recapping a 1971 console a repair or a mod? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Instrument is a "partscaster" assembled from several donors | Has no single origin; the whole continuity model assumes one | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — this is common and the model does not currently handle it |
| Owner declines to record a value-reducing mod | Undetectable; absence is not evidence | Registry can only render "no modifications recorded" ≠ "unmodified" — see 15.03 DT-04 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Originality recalculating after an event | Pending badge |
| Empty | No modifications recorded | "No modifications recorded" — explicitly not the same as "original" |
| Populated | Component originality known | Component-level breakdown |
| Error | Update failed | Retry |
| Partial | Some components known, most unknown — the normal case for second-hand gear | Honest partial render; unknown components shown as unknown |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | "Modified: yes/no" is sufficient | The obvious minimal model, and what most listing forms use | ❌ REJECTED — it discards the only information anyone wants. Originality is the single largest price variable in vintage instruments, and its components are not interchangeable: a refret is routine and near-neutral, a refinish is catastrophic, replaced pickups are recoverable *if the originals are retained*. A boolean flattens a 3× price range into one bit. Originality must be component-level, and "are the originals kept?" must be captured at the moment the work happens — which is exactly the platform's capture-at-source thesis applied to objects instead of sessions. |
| DT-02 | Modification and identity are independent | Convenient, and true for most gear | ❌ REJECTED — they collide precisely when the serial rides on a removable component, which is the standard Fender-style construction. Swap the neck and the serial goes with it: the original body (with all its history) loses its number, and the new assembly gains one it has no history for. Both can be registered. This is how partscasters get sold as originals, and it is the fraud the registry is most likely to *enable* if it ignores the fork. Recorded as `#CX-05`; the resolution is a real open decision. |
| DT-03 | The registry can enforce honest modification disclosure | Wishful — it is what the feature is *for* | ❌ REJECTED — a seller has a direct financial incentive to omit a refinish, and the platform has no way to detect it. The registry cannot make people honest. What it *can* do is make omission legible: "no modifications recorded" must never render as "original", and a chain with a long service history and no mods is more credible than a chain with nothing at all. The honest framing is that the registry rewards disclosure rather than punishing concealment — which is weaker than it sounds and should not be oversold. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Originality is recorded at component level, not as a flag | Per DT-01. The component breakdown is the thing the vintage market actually trades on. |
| D-02 | "No modifications recorded" never renders as "original" | Per DT-03. Absence of a record is not evidence of absence, and conflating them would let concealment produce a *better*-looking passport than disclosure. |
