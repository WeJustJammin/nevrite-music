# Feature: Gear Record & Serial Identity

> **Number**: 15.01.01
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The durable record for one individual physical object — this Telecaster, not Telecasters — keyed
on `(maker, model, era, serial)` and carrying photos, specs, and the acquisition facts its owner
knows. It is the entity every other capability in domain 15 attaches facts to: theft flags, service
events, valuations, rig membership, and session credits all hang off this record, so it is the
domain's single hard dependency.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Registers each instrument, pedal and amp they own: photographs it, enters or scans the serial, picks maker/model/era from a catalogue, records how and when they got it. Sees their record's identity confidence ("serial confirmed from photo" vs "typed, unverified"). Most of their gear is registered once and touched again only at sale, theft, or service. |
| Producer | Full | Same primitive, different stock: outboard, mics, converters, monitors — much of it old enough to have no serial or an inconsistent one. Registers gear they personally own even when it lives in a room they don't. Their records feed gear discography (15.09) because their gear is what sessions run on. |
| Operator | Full | Registers room assets against their **org entity**, not their person — the studio owns the console, not the engineer. High volume, entered in bulk, and the record's serial is deliberately withheld from the public room listing (see 15.07.03). |
| Fan | Read-only | Never mints a record. Sees the public passport panel on a marketplace listing (domain 13) or on a public collection — maker, model, era, photos, chain summary. Serial is masked. The persona boundary is porous: a fan who buys a guitar and registers it is acting as a Musician. |

## Behavior

A gear record is minted once and then accretes. Minting requires resolving an identity key —
which is where most of the difficulty lives, and is owned by 15.01.05. Everything after minting is
append-only: the record's history is the product, so destructive edits to historical facts are not
offered. Correcting a *typo* in a serial and *changing* a serial are different operations with
different consequences and must not share a control.

### Happy Path

1. Musician taps "Register gear" and photographs the instrument, including the serial.
2. System offers a maker/model/era match from a catalogue and proposes the scanned serial for confirmation.
3. Musician confirms or corrects the model and serial, and states how they acquired it (bought new / bought used / gift / built it / inherited / unknown).
4. System resolves the composite key via 15.01.05, checks it against existing records, and mints the record.
5. Record appears in the Musician's collection (15.04) with an identity-confidence badge and a prompt to strengthen the ownership claim (15.01.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Serial resolves to an existing active record | Mint is blocked pending disambiguation — never a silent duplicate, never a silent merge | "Gear matching this serial is already registered. Is this the same object?" with a disambiguation flow, and no disclosure of who holds the existing record |
| Object has no serial | Falls through to 15.01.05's platform-issued identity token | "This gear has no serial — we'll give it a WeJammin ID bound to your photos" |
| Serial photo is unreadable | Manual entry, identity confidence downgraded, not blocked | Record mints with "serial typed, unverified" |
| Gear is registered but the registrant does not own it | Record exists with a self-asserted claim only; the claim, not the record, carries the weakness | Claim strength shown as lowest tier; see 15.01.02 |
| Model not in catalogue (boutique, DIY, one-off) | Free-text maker/model accepted; identity leans harder on photos and the platform ID | "We don't know this model — tell us what it is" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Catalogue lookup / serial resolution in flight | Skeleton on the match panel; photo already visible |
| Empty | No gear registered yet | Empty collection with a "Register your first piece" CTA and an honest note on why it's worth the effort |
| Populated | Record minted | Passport view: photos, identity, chain, claim strength |
| Error | Key resolution or mint failed | Retry, with the entered data preserved — re-photographing gear is a real cost |
| Partial | Minted with unverified serial or no catalogue match | Record usable, with a visible "identity unconfirmed" state and a path to strengthen it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The serial is the primary key | The obvious design, and what the domain rationale's "serial-keyed instrument identity" implies | ❌ REJECTED — serials are unique only within maker+model+era. Gibson reused serial sequences across decades; Fender's are non-sequential and live on swappable parts. A bare-serial key guarantees collisions and would make point-of-sale screening (15.02.02) unusable through false positives. Key is `(maker, model, era, serial)` — ratified as D-02 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | A gear record is owned by a **person** | Default assumption of a "my collection" feature | ❌ REJECTED — personas.md establishes bands, studios and labels as first-class entities that own things independently of members. The band owns the PA; the studio owns the console. Ownership must point at an entity. Routed to domain 01 as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | Photo-OCR serial scanning is its own node | It is a visible, distinctive capability | ❌ REJECTED — it is an input affordance of this feature, not a capability with its own lifecycle. The ML approach behind it is an architecture decision for `/create-prd`, not a product node. Recorded here so it isn't rediscovered as a candidate later. |
| DT-04 | A rich provenance chain proves an instrument is genuine | Implied by the Carfax framing in the domain rationale | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's most dangerous assumption. Provenance is not authenticity. A counterfeit with a long, honestly-recorded chain of real transfers between real people reads as *more* trustworthy than a genuine instrument with no chain — so the registry, unqualified, is a laundering tool that makes fakes more saleable. The record needs an explicit authenticity-assertion field and the passport must disclose that WeJammin records assertions and does not verify them. Adjudication belongs to domain 24. Escalated as Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The record is append-only for historical facts | The history is the product. Editing the past silently is the failure the domain exists to prevent. |
| D-02 | Correcting a serial typo and changing a serial are distinct operations | One is a data fix; the other changes the object's identity and may fork it (see 15.01.06). Sharing a control would let a fraud look like a typo. |
