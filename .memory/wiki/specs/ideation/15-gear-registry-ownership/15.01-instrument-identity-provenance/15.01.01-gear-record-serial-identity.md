# Feature: Gear Record & Serial Identity

> **Number**: 15.01.01
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The durable record for one individual physical object — this Telecaster, not Telecasters — keyed
on `(maker, model, era, serial)` and carrying photos, secondary identifying numbers, specs, and
the acquisition facts its owner knows. It is the entity every other capability in domain 15
attaches facts to: theft flags, service events, valuations, rig membership, custody and session
credits all hang off this record, so it is the domain's single hard dependency and the only
feature in it proposed `must`.

The record exists to answer two **separate** questions that the rest of the domain keeps trying to
merge: *which object is this?* (identity) and *whose is it?* (ownership). This feature owns the
first and deliberately owns none of the second — minting a record is not claiming it (D-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Registers each instrument, pedal and amp they own: photographs it, confirms the OCR-proposed serial, picks maker/model/era from a catalogue, enters secondary numbers (pot codes, neck date stamps) if the object is in hand, and states how they got it (bought new / bought used / gift / built it / inherited / unknown). Chooses which entity owns it when they hold more than one (their person, their band). Sees identity confidence ("serial confirmed from photo" vs "typed, unverified") separately from claim strength — never fused into one score (DT-08). Most of their gear is registered once and touched again only at sale, theft, or service. Their worst registration session is the one after a van theft, with no object to photograph (DT-05). |
| Producer | Full | Same primitive, different stock: outboard, mics, converters, monitors — much of it old enough to have no serial or an inconsistent one, and much of it re-chassised. Registers gear they personally own even when it lives in a room they don't. Uniquely may mint an `observed` record for a session player's un-registered gear so that gear discography (15.09) has an object to attach to — a record with no claim, no custody, and no public surface. |
| Operator | Full | Registers room assets against their **org entity**, not their person — the studio owns the console, not the engineer. Enters in bulk (a room is 40–200 items), so partial-batch behaviour is their normal case, not an edge case. Not all of that stock mints a record here: commodity items (stands, cables, interchangeable practice amps) go into the org register as **quantity-tracked lines**, which carry a count and no `(maker, model, era, serial)` key at all (15.07.01 D-03, domain D-09). The record's serial is deliberately withheld from the public room listing (15.07.03). Frequently the multi-hyphenate case in its sharpest form: the same human owns some of the room's gear personally and some through the business, and the mint flow must not guess which (DT-11). |
| Fan | Read-only | Never mints a record, in any intent — enforced, not conventional. Sees the public passport panel on a marketplace listing (domain 13) or on a public collection — maker, model, era, photos, chain summary, identity confidence, claim strength, and record age. Serial is masked (Q-03). The persona boundary is porous: a fan who buys a guitar and registers it is acting as a Musician, and the mint flow is what re-lenses them. |

## Behavior

A gear record is minted once and then accretes. Minting requires resolving an identity key —
which is where most of the difficulty lives, and is owned by 15.01.05. Everything after minting is
append-only: the record's history is the product, so destructive edits to historical facts are not
offered. Correcting a *typo* in a serial and *changing* a serial are different operations with
different consequences and must not share a control.

### Minting is not claiming

The mint flow requires a declared **mint intent**. This resolves Q-02 (can you register gear you
don't own?) — yes, but never through a bare "catalogue anything" affordance, and never in a way
that gives the minter a privileged position over the object.

| Intent | Who may use it | What it mints | What the record can do |
|--------|---------------|---------------|------------------------|
| `owned` | Musician, Producer, Operator | Record + an **Asserted** claim (15.01.02) against the chosen entity | Everything: list (13), insure (15.05.03), transfer (15.01.03), flag (15.02.01), value, appear in a collection (15.04) |
| `held` | Musician, Producer, Operator | Record + a **custody** record (15.08); **no claim** | Appears in the holder's register (15.07) / rig (15.06) / manifest (15.10). Cannot be listed, insured, transferred or valued by the holder |
| `observed` | Producer only, from an open session context | Record only; **no claim, no custody** | Carries a gear-discography attestation (15.09) and nothing else. Never appears in any public collection, cannot be listed, insured, valued, flagged or transferred |

Squatting (sub-domain Q-02's stated fear) is defused structurally rather than by policy: because
minting confers nothing, there is nothing to squat. A squatter who mints a record against a famous
serial holds a description; to assert ownership they must still file a claim, and that claim mints
at **Asserted** — the weakest tier, identical to everyone else's, and contestable (15.01.02).

The residual hazard is **denial-of-registration**, not squatting: bulk-minted records against
plausible serials would send honest owners into disambiguation when they later register their own
gear. Two mitigations: (a) `observed` and `held` mints are rate-limited to **5 per account per
24h** (no legitimate user registers more than a handful of objects they neither own nor hold; the
bulk path exists only for `owned`, and the threshold is tunable at `/create-prd-security`);
(b) a claim by the real holder on an **unclaimed** record is the normal path, not a dispute —
there is no competing claimant, so the first claim simply attaches. This is not adjudication and
does not contradict D-07.

### The serial is not one field

A single `serial` field discards the numbers that actually prove an instrument is what it says it
is (DT-06). The record carries a **primary serial** (the key component, per D-05/D-02) and an
open set of **secondary identifying numbers**, each with its location on the object:

- Gibson: headstock serial + ink-stamped factory order number in the control cavity
- Fender: neck-plate serial + neck-heel date stamp + body date + pot date codes
- Amps: chassis serial + transformer date codes + tube-chart stamp

These are not redundant. They are the cross-check that detects a partscaster — an instrument whose
neck-plate serial says 1963 and whose pot codes say 1978 is telling you something, and a registry
that captured only the serial has thrown that fact away at the one moment it was recoverable.
Secondary numbers are **record fields, never key components** — the composite key stays
`(maker, model, era, serial)` per D-05. They feed 15.01.05's open discriminator question (its
Q-01) and they are surfaced to 15.01.06 when they disagree.

### Identity confidence and claim strength are orthogonal

The record renders two independent values and must never fuse them into a single trust score
(DT-08). Identity confidence is supplied by 15.01.05; claim strength is supplied by 15.01.02. The
record's job is to display both, side by side, with neither dominating.

| Identity confidence the record must render | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `serial-confirmed` | Serial legible in a photo the owner confirmed |
| `serial-typed` | Serial entered by hand, unverified against any image |
| `serial-reconstructed` | Serial supplied from memory or a document, object not present (DT-05) |
| `photo-bound (no serial)` | Platform-issued WJ-ID bound to photographs (15.01.05) |
| `era-unknown` | Key degraded to `(maker, model, ?, serial)` |
| `numbers-disagree` | Secondary numbers contradict the primary serial |

15.01.05 owns resolution logic and the authoritative value set; this feature owns the render and
the record's behaviour at each level. The vocabulary above is proposed from the render side and is
flagged for Step 6 synthesis against 15.01.05's.

### Correction vs change

Free correction is bounded by **reliance, not by a clock**. A serial may be corrected in place
while all three hold: (a) the record has exactly one claim, (b) that claim belongs to the minter,
and (c) no third-party fact is attached — no transfer, flag, attestation, listing, service event,
custody record, or valuation. Once any of those exists, someone has relied on the number and the
operation becomes a *change*, which routes to 15.01.06's fork path. A correction after three
untouched years is still free; a correction ninety seconds after a buyer's attestation is not.

Every correction is **appended, not overwritten** (D-01). The chain shows "serial corrected
2026-07-16: `A47291` → `A47921`". A silent edit is precisely the fraud that would let a change
masquerade as a typo (D-02).

### Terminal states

There are none. "Destroyed" is an appended fact asserted by the claim-holder — reversible, because
gear presumed lost in a flood turns up — and it never removes the record. A burned guitar's gear
discography (15.09) is still true, and its serial must stay screenable (15.02.02) because
"destroyed" is also what a thief's buyer is told. Deletion is not offered; erasure obligations are
Q-04's problem, not a product affordance.

### Happy Path

1. Musician taps "Register gear" and photographs the instrument, including the serial.
2. System runs OCR and **proposes** a serial for confirmation — never auto-fills it. A mis-scanned serial the owner never read carries false confidence and is worse than a typed one, and stamped vintage serials are dense with ambiguous glyphs (`0`/`O`, `1`/`I`, `5`/`S`, `8`/`B`).
3. System offers maker/model/era matches from the catalogue (Q-01), ranked, with an explicit "none of these" escape.
4. Musician confirms or corrects the model, era and serial.
5. **If the registrant controls more than one entity** (their person, their band, their studio org), the system requires an explicit ownership-entity choice with **no default and no pre-selection** (DT-11).
6. System asks for what is irrecoverable while the object is in hand and skips what is not (DT-07): secondary numbers and their locations, condition photos, current mods. It does **not** ask for valuation, insurance or rig membership — all recoverable later, from data, without the object.
7. Musician declares mint intent (`owned` here) and states acquisition: bought new / bought used / gift / built it / inherited / unknown.
8. System resolves the composite key via 15.01.05, checks it against existing records, and mints the record plus an Asserted claim against the chosen entity.
9. Record appears in the Musician's collection (15.04) with **two** badges — identity confidence and claim strength — and a single, non-repeating prompt to strengthen the ownership claim (15.01.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Serial resolves to an existing **claimed** record | Mint blocked pending disambiguation — never a silent duplicate, never a silent merge. The existing holder is notified ("someone is registering gear that may be yours"), which is also a theft signal (CX-01) | "Gear matching this serial is already registered. Is this the same object?" with a disambiguation flow, and **no disclosure of who holds the existing record** — that disclosure is the doxxing vector |
| Serial resolves to an existing **unclaimed** record (`held` / `observed` mint) | No dispute. The claim attaches to the existing record — this is the designed path, not a collision | "This has already been catalogued. Is it yours?" → claim attaches at Asserted |
| Two objects genuinely share a composite key (documented manufacturer reuse) | Both records legitimately coexist under one key; secondary numbers discriminate (DT-06). If secondary numbers are absent on both, the records stay distinct and both render `numbers-disagree`-adjacent ambiguity honestly | "Another instrument shares this serial. That's normal for this maker — these numbers tell you which is which" |
| **Two users register the same object simultaneously** (owner and consigning dealer) | Resolves CX-01 synthesis Q5. First mint wins the record; the second becomes a **claim or custody attach** against it, not a second record. Neither user is told they lost a race | Dealer sees "already registered — you're holding it, not owning it?" → custody path (15.08) |
| **Merge strategy on a genuine duplicate** | Resolves CX-01 synthesis Q1. Records are **never auto-merged**. A merge requires both claim-holders to consent, and produces a new record whose chain contains both source chains as explicit, labelled branches. An unconsented merge silently rewrites two people's history | "Merging will combine both histories permanently. Both owners must agree." |
| **Object is gone — registering from memory after a theft** (DT-05) | Mint proceeds with **no photo and no legible serial**. Accepts a serial from a receipt, an insurance schedule, an old social post, or memory. Identity confidence = `serial-reconstructed`, which is honest and weak | "You don't have it any more. Tell us what you can — we'll be clear about how sure we are." Never blocks; this is one of the two highest-intent moments the domain has |
| Serial photo unreadable | Manual entry, identity confidence downgraded to `serial-typed`, not blocked | Record mints with "serial typed, unverified" |
| OCR proposes a serial with ambiguous glyphs | Proposal is shown character-by-character with the ambiguous positions highlighted for confirmation | "Is this an `O` or a `0`?" — asked once, at the only moment the object is in hand |
| Object has no serial | Falls through to 15.01.05's platform-issued identity token | "This gear has no serial — we'll give it a WeJammin ID bound to your photos" |
| Secondary numbers contradict the primary serial | Mint proceeds; identity confidence renders `numbers-disagree`; 15.01.06 is notified (possible partscaster) | "These numbers don't agree. That's worth knowing — it doesn't mean anything's wrong" — stated neutrally, because period-correct replacement is legitimate and an accusation here would be defamatory |
| Key resolver (15.01.05) unavailable | Mint **blocks**, per 15.01.05 D-02. Entered data and uploaded photos are held as a draft for **30 days** — re-photographing gear is a real cost, and the theft-reconstruction user cannot re-photograph at all | "We can't confirm this identity right now. Your entry is saved — try again shortly." |
| User retries a blocked mint repeatedly | Retry is idempotent against the draft; N retries never produce N records | Same draft, same result |
| Mint against a serial already **flagged stolen** | Mint **proceeds** and notifies per CX-01 — see DT-10. Blocking it would destroy the domain's best recovery signal | Innocent buyer sees the flag on the record they just minted, with the dispute path (15.02.04). The flag is a fact on the object, not an accusation against the registrant |
| Registrant controls multiple entities and doesn't choose | Mint blocks on the choice. **No default** — defaulting to person misattributes the studio's console; defaulting to last-used silently misattributes at scale (DT-11) | "Who owns this — you, or {Studio}?" Forced, once, at mint |
| Bulk mint (Operator, 40–200 items), 3 items fail key resolution | Each item mints **independently**. 37 mint, 3 queue as drafts. **Never rolls back the 37** — a partial register is useful; a rolled-back afternoon is not | "37 registered. 3 need your attention." |
| Buyer re-registers gear instead of accepting the transfer handshake | The most common real duplicate cause: the buyer doesn't understand the handshake and just registers "my new guitar". Key resolution catches it and routes to the pending handshake (15.01.03) rather than minting | "This is the guitar {seller} is transferring to you. Accept the transfer instead — you'll keep its whole history." |
| Registrant fabricates a serial outright | Undetectable here, per 15.01.05. No system response; mitigation lives in claim strength (15.01.02), never in key resolution | Nothing |
| Bulk `observed`/`held` minting (squatting attempt) | Rate limit (5/account/24h) trips; the account is surfaced for review. `owned` bulk is unaffected | "You've catalogued a lot of gear you don't own. Tell us why." |
| Model not in catalogue (boutique, DIY, one-off, partscaster) | Free-text maker/model accepted; identity leans harder on photos and the platform ID | "We don't know this model — tell us what it is" |
| Era unknown / instrument undated | Key degrades to `(maker, model, ?, serial)`; confidence renders `era-unknown` | "We're not sure of the year — this weakens identity" |
| Photo upload fails / network drops mid-mint | Draft preserved with whatever uploaded; mint resumable from the failure point, not restarted | "Saved. 3 of 5 photos uploaded." |
| Bulk room photos contain other people's gear or PII in frame | Photos are record-scoped, not published by minting. Publication is 15.07.03's decision and its exposure surface | Nothing at mint — the hazard is publication, not capture |
| Claim-holder deletes the photos that a WJ-ID's identity is bound to | Identity confidence downgrades honestly (the binding *was* the photos); the record persists per D-01. The collision between append-only history and erasure obligations is real and unresolved — Q-04 | "Removing these photos weakens this gear's identity permanently." |
| Claim-holder dies | Record persists; the claim persists and does not decay into unclaimed. Estate transfer has no living counterparty for a two-sided handshake — that one-sided path is 15.01.03's to define, not this feature's | Record unchanged; heirs route through 15.01.03 |
| Gear destroyed (fire, flood, theft write-off) | Appended fact, reversible, never a deletion. Serial stays screenable (15.02.02) | "Recorded as destroyed" — with an undo, because it turns up |
| Account compromised | An attacker's value here is **exfiltration of the collection**, not mint — a stolen collection list plus public tour dates is the targeting package CX-07 names. Mint abuse is self-limiting; read abuse is not | Out of scope for this feature; escalated to Q-05 of the domain index |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Catalogue lookup or 15.01.05 key resolution in flight | Skeleton on the match panel only; the photo the user just took stays visible throughout — it is the thing that reassures them the capture worked |
| Empty | No gear registered yet | Empty collection with a "Register your first piece" CTA and an honest note on why it's worth the effort — insurance, resale, recovery. **Not gamified, not nagged** (DT-12) |
| Populated | Record minted, key resolved, claim attached | Passport view: photos, identity confidence badge, claim strength badge (separate — DT-08), secondary numbers, chain, record age |
| Error | Key resolution or mint failed | Retry, with entered data and photos preserved as a 30-day draft. Idempotent — retrying never duplicates |
| Partial | Minted with `serial-typed`, `serial-reconstructed`, `era-unknown`, `photo-bound`, or `numbers-disagree` | Record fully usable, with a visible, honest identity state and a concrete path to strengthen it. **The majority case, not an exception** — most real gear lands here |
| Unclaimed | Minted `held` or `observed`; no claim exists | Record renders with claim strength absent, not zero. "Nobody has claimed this" ≠ "this is unowned" (D-06) |
| Blocked | Resolver down, entity choice pending, or disambiguation open | Mint held, draft saved, one specific unblocking action offered |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The serial is the primary key | The obvious design, and what the domain rationale's "serial-keyed instrument identity" implies | ❌ REJECTED — serials are unique only within maker+model+era. Gibson reused serial sequences across decades; Fender's are non-sequential and live on swappable parts. A bare-serial key guarantees collisions and would make point-of-sale screening (15.02.02) unusable through false positives. Key is `(maker, model, era, serial)` — ratified as D-02 in the parent index, D-05 at domain level. |
| DT-02 | A gear record is owned by a **person** | Default assumption of a "my collection" feature | ❌ REJECTED — personas.md establishes bands, studios and labels as first-class entities that own things independently of members. The band owns the PA; the studio owns the console. Ownership must point at an entity. Routed to domain 01 as Q-01 of the sub-domain index. See DT-11 for the mint-time behavioural consequence, which this feature *does* own. |
| DT-03 | Photo-OCR serial scanning is its own node | It is a visible, distinctive capability | ❌ REJECTED — it is an input affordance of this feature, not a capability with its own lifecycle. The ML approach behind it is an architecture decision for `/create-prd`, not a product node. Recorded here so it isn't rediscovered as a candidate later. |
| DT-04 | A rich provenance chain proves an instrument is genuine | Implied by the Carfax framing in the domain rationale | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's most dangerous assumption. Provenance is not authenticity. A counterfeit with a long, honestly-recorded chain of real transfers between real people reads as *more* trustworthy than a genuine instrument with no chain — so the registry, unqualified, is a laundering tool that makes fakes more saleable. The record needs an explicit authenticity-assertion field and the passport must disclose that WeJammin records assertions and does not verify them. Adjudication belongs to domain 24. Escalated as Q-03 of the domain index. |
| DT-05 | The registration flow's user always has the object in front of them | Assumed by every step of the original happy path — photograph it, scan it, confirm it | ✅ CONFIRMED as false, and it changes the feature. Gear is registered in bulk at moments of **stress**, not leisure: the two real triggers are getting insured (15.05.03) and having just been robbed (15.02.01). Domain CX-01 already requires that a theft report on unregistered gear mints a record — which means the mint path must work for someone who **cannot photograph the object, cannot read the serial, and is reconstructing from an old Instagram photo and a receipt in their email**. A photograph-first flow dead-ends the highest-intent user the domain has. Requires the `serial-reconstructed` confidence level and a photoless mint path. Nothing in the corpus had noticed this. |
| DT-06 | One `serial` field is sufficient to record identity | The obvious model, and what "serial-keyed" implies | ✅ CONFIRMED as insufficient. A domain expert knows serials are plural and location-dependent: a Gibson carries a headstock serial *and* an ink-stamped factory order number; a Fender carries a neck-plate serial, a neck-heel date stamp, a body date and pot codes; an amp carries a chassis serial and transformer date codes. These are not redundancy — **they are the cross-check that detects a partscaster**, and disagreement between them is the single most informative fact a vintage buyer can have. A one-field model throws it away at the only moment it is recoverable. Secondary numbers are record fields, not key components (D-05 preserved), and they answer 15.01.05's open Q-01 (what discriminates two objects sharing a key). |
| DT-07 | A shorter mint flow is a better mint flow | Standard onboarding instinct, and reinforced by the domain's cold-start problem (domain Q-08) | ✅ CONFIRMED but **only when correctly scoped**, and the naive version is wrong. The mint moment is the only moment the owner will ever have both the object and the motivation in hand — later prompts to "add a photo of the headstock" are ignored, because the guitar is in a case under a bed or gone. This is the platform thesis (D-18, capture-at-source) applied to objects instead of sessions: the split is captured while everyone is in the room; identity is captured while the object is in hand. The resolution is not "ask more" or "ask less" but **ask aggressively for what is irrecoverable** (serial photo, secondary numbers, condition, current mods) and **not at all for what is recoverable from data later** (valuation, insurance, rig membership, catalogue enrichment). |
| DT-08 | The record should render a single trust/confidence score | Every product instinct says one badge; two badges is a worse-looking UI and users don't read nuance | ❌ REJECTED, and this is DT-04's failure mode in a new costume. **Identity confidence and claim strength are orthogonal axes.** A record can be identity-certain and ownership-unknown (a documented famous instrument nobody has claimed), or identity-vague and ownership-witnessed (a no-serial pedal bought on-platform yesterday — the transfer was witnessed; which pedal it was is not provable). Averaging these produces a number that is wrong in both directions and launders the weaker axis behind the stronger — the exact thing D-06 exists to prevent. Two badges, always, neither dominant. |
| DT-09 | Where the record is minted doesn't affect what it means | Implicit; a record is a record | ❌ REJECTED — **record age and mint context are themselves provenance facts.** If domain 13's listing flow mints records as a byproduct, the registry fills with sale-motivated records: minted minutes before listing, by the party with maximum incentive to flatter, with photos chosen to flatter, at the moment of least available history — and that party is gone before anyone could contradict them. This doesn't break the chain (15.01.03 carries the record to the buyer), but it means "record created at listing" and "record created four years ago and maintained since" are different evidence and must not render identically. Record age is a signal the passport must show. |
| DT-10 | Minting a record for gear already flagged stolen should be blocked | Obvious safety instinct — surely you can't register stolen gear? | ❌ REJECTED, and the instinct is exactly backwards. The people most likely to register a flagged instrument are the **innocent buyer who didn't know** and the **recovering owner** — not the thief, who has every reason to stay out of the registry. Blocking the mint destroys the best recovery signal the domain has: a stolen guitar surfacing in someone's registration flow is precisely the event 15.02 exists to catch. Block it and the object goes dark permanently. The mint must proceed and notify, per CX-01's notification synthesis. |
| DT-11 | Entity attribution at mint can be defaulted | The multi-hyphenate registers constantly; forcing a choice every time is friction, and a sensible default (their person, or last-used) seems harmless | ❌ REJECTED — this is the multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once, which personas.md names as the structural centre of the product, and it is where a silent default does the most damage. An Operator "often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves" — so the same human owns some of the room's gear personally and some through the org. **Nobody thinks about attribution at mint, because at mint it doesn't matter. It matters at exit** — when the studio is sold, the band splits, the marriage ends, or the insurer asks whose schedule the console is on. A default is a silent misattribution discovered years later at the worst possible moment. Force the choice when more than one entity exists; never pre-select. |
| DT-12 | Registration should be prompted/gamified to drive coverage | The domain has a real cold-start problem (domain Q-08) and coverage is what makes 15.02.02 and 15.09 work | ❌ REJECTED — it manufactures the exposure hazard. Driving users to register their whole collection maximises the value of a single account compromise and, composed with public tour dates and a home city, builds exactly the targeting package CX-07 names as an unowned platform-level hazard against endemic van and venue theft. The domain wants coverage but must not buy it by nagging people into concentrating a burglary shopping list. The honest incentives already exist and are strong (insurance, resale value, theft recovery) — DT-05 says the users arrive at the two moments those bite. Coverage is earned at those moments or not at all. |

- Touches **Domain 01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity + permission.** The entity that owns the record. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The record is append-only for historical facts | The history is the product. Editing the past silently is the failure the domain exists to prevent. |
| D-02 | Correcting a serial typo and changing a serial are distinct operations | One is a data fix; the other changes the object's identity and may fork it (see 15.01.06). Sharing a control would let a fraud look like a typo. |
| D-03 | **Minting is not claiming.** A record may be minted by a non-owner under a declared intent — `owned`, `held`, or `observed` — and the intent determines what the record can do. Resolves sub-domain Q-02 | Custody (D-04, domain) and gear discography (15.09) both structurally require records for gear the registrant does not own; refusing them would break two ratified features. Squatting is defused structurally rather than by policy: minting confers nothing, so there is nothing to gain by it — the squatter must still file a claim, which mints at the weakest tier like everyone else's. The real residual is denial-of-registration, mitigated by rate-limiting the non-owner intents and by making a real holder's first claim on an unclaimed record an attach rather than a dispute. |
| D-04 | **Free correction is bounded by reliance, not by a time window** | A structural condition (one claim, the minter's, no third-party fact attached) is exactly right where a clock is arbitrary. A correction three untouched years later harms nobody; a correction ninety seconds after a buyer's attestation rewrites evidence someone acted on. Every correction is appended regardless — the value changes, the fact of the change does not disappear. |
| D-05 | **The record carries secondary identifying numbers with their locations, not one serial field** | Per DT-06. The cross-check between a neck-plate serial and a pot code is what detects a partscaster, and mint is the only moment it is recoverable. Secondary numbers are record fields, never key components — the composite key stays `(maker, model, era, serial)` per domain D-05. |
| D-06 | **Identity confidence and claim strength render as two badges, never one score** | Per DT-08. They are orthogonal axes; averaging them launders the weaker behind the stronger, which is domain D-06's prohibition restated at the render layer. |
| D-07 | **The mint path must work with no object present** | Per DT-05. The post-theft registrant is one of the two highest-intent users the domain has and cannot photograph anything. A photograph-first flow that dead-ends them fails at the exact moment the domain is most valuable. Confidence renders `serial-reconstructed` — honest and weak, which is the correct answer, not a blocked one. |
| D-08 | **A mint against a flagged serial proceeds and notifies; it is never blocked** | Per DT-10. The registrant is overwhelmingly the innocent buyer or the recovering owner, not the thief. Blocking sends the object dark and destroys the domain's best recovery signal. |
| D-09 | **Entity attribution is forced at mint when the registrant controls more than one entity; there is no default** | Per DT-11. The multi-hyphenate is the structural centre of the product and this is where a silent default causes damage that surfaces years later, at exit, when it is unfixable. |
| D-10 | **The record has no terminal state.** "Destroyed" is an appended, reversible fact; deletion is not offered | Consistent with D-01 and domain D-06. Gear presumed destroyed turns up; a destroyed guitar's discography is still true; and "destroyed" is also what a thief's buyer gets told, so the serial must stay screenable. |
| D-11 | **Registration is never gamified or nagged** | Per DT-12. Prompting users to concentrate their whole collection into one account builds the targeting package CX-07 names, against a threat (van and venue theft) the domain rationale calls endemic. Coverage is earned at the insurance and theft moments or not at all. |
