## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Gear Record ↔ Non-Serialized & Contested Identity

**Relationship**: 15.01.01 assumes a serial-keyed record. 15.01.05 exists because that assumption
is false for a large share of real gear. The interaction is not "handle the edge case" — 15.01.05
supplies the composite identity key `(maker, model, era, serial)` and the fallback
platform-issued identity token that 15.01.01 mints against. Data flows from 15.01.05's key
resolution into every record 15.01.01 creates.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: hits this constantly — pedalboards are mostly serial-less, and vintage guitars have contested serials.
- **Producer**: hits it on outboard and mics; a lot of studio gear predates consistent serialisation.
- **Operator**: hits it on backline and house gear, which is often old, modified, or assembled.
- **Fan**: no visibility — this is registration machinery, not a public surface.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: 15.01.05 owns the identity key definition; 15.01.01 owns the record. Merge strategy on a collision (two records resolving to one key) is `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
2. **Trigger chain**: 15.01.01 calls 15.01.05's resolver on every mint. Resolver failure must block the mint with a disambiguation prompt, never silently mint a duplicate.
3. **Permission intersection**: None — key resolution is not permissioned.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A near-collision should notify the holder of the existing record ("someone is registering gear that may be yours") — which is also a theft signal. See domain CX.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two users registering the same object simultaneously is real (an owner and a dealer both cataloguing consigned stock). `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

### CX-02: Transfer Handshake ↔ Ownership Claim

**Relationship**: The two-sided transfer is the only ownership event WeJammin actually witnesses.
When it completes, it writes a claim on the receiving side stamped `witnessed-on-platform` — the
top of the strength ladder in D-03. This is the mechanism by which the marketplace (domain 13)
manufactures provenance quality as a byproduct of trade, which is the Carfax model the domain
rationale rests on.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: buys and sells constantly; this is where their chain quality comes from.
- **Producer**: same, plus acquires studio gear that later appears in gear discography (15.09).
- **Operator**: acquires backline; the claim is against their org entity, not their person.
- **Fan**: sees the resulting claim strength on a public listing; never participates.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The gear record's `current_owner` is owned by 15.01.03 during a transfer and by 15.01.02 otherwise. A transfer in flight must lock competing claims.
2. **Trigger chain**: Transfer complete → claim written (sync, same transaction). If the claim write fails the transfer must roll back — a transfer with no resulting claim is a corrupted chain.
3. **Permission intersection**: Only the current claim-holder can initiate a transfer. A contested claim (15.01.02) must block transfer initiation.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both parties notified; the marketplace order (13) is notified so the sale can display "ownership transferred".
5. **State transition conflict**: Seller initiates two transfers of one item to two buyers. Must be prevented at initiation, not detected at completion.

### CX-03: Transfer Handshake ↔ Provenance Chain View

**Relationship**: Every completed transfer appends a link to the chain. The interesting case is
the *incomplete* one: the seller confirms, the buyer never does. The record must show
`transferred — unconfirmed by recipient`, because both silent success (chain lies) and silent
failure (chain loses a real event) are worse than an honest gap.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer / Operator**: see unconfirmed transfers on their own records with a nudge to complete.
- **Fan**: sees the gap on a public chain, rendered neutrally — a gap is normal, not suspicious.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The chain is append-only; the transfer's terminal state is what gets appended. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Async. Handshake timeout `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` writes the unconfirmed state.
3. **Permission intersection**: Chain visibility follows the record's visibility (15.04), not the transfer's participants.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Timeout notifies the initiator.
5. **State transition conflict**: A buyer confirming after the timeout must upgrade the chain link, not create a second one.

### CX-04: Ownership Claim ↔ Provenance Chain View

**Relationship**: This is the cross-cut that decides whether the registry is trustworthy or is a
laundering tool. If the chain renders as a list of events, then thirty self-asserted events beat
two witnessed ones and the incentive is to fabricate volume. The chain must render each link's
attestation strength, and its overall confidence must be bounded by its *weakest* link, not its
length.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer / Operator**: see their own chain's weak points and are prompted to strengthen them (upload the receipt, ask the prior owner to attest).
- **Fan**: as a prospective buyer, sees strength — this is the whole consumer value.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Claims own strength; the chain reads it. Read-only, no conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: A claim upgrade (receipt uploaded, prior owner attests) re-renders the chain. Async is fine.
3. **Permission intersection**: A prior owner may attest but may decline to be **named** — attestation and identity disclosure are separate permissions. See domain CX (exposure).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Prior-owner attestation requests notify a person who may have moved on years ago and has no reason to respond. Response rate is the feature's real risk.
5. **State transition conflict**: A claim downgraded by a dispute (15.02.04) must re-render the chain immediately, including on any live marketplace listing.

### CX-05: Non-Serialized Identity ↔ Identity Continuity Through Modification

**Relationship**: The nastiest interaction in the sub-domain. If the serial-bearing component is
swapped, identity forks: the original body and the new assembly both have a defensible claim to
the serial. This is not hypothetical — it is standard practice on Fender-style instruments where
the serial is on the neck plate, and it is how "partscasters" get sold as originals.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the person doing the swap; usually has no idea they just forked an identity.
- **Producer / Operator**: same exposure on outboard and amps that get re-chassised.
- **Fan**: none directly — but this is precisely the fraud a buyer needs protection from.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Which record keeps the serial? Unresolved — `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. This is a genuine product decision, not an implementation detail.
2. **Trigger chain**: A mod event (15.03) naming a serial-bearing component must trigger a fork decision, not proceed silently.
3. **Permission intersection**: The owner decides the fork, but a future buyer bears the consequence — an asymmetry the platform should surface.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A fork should be visible on the chain of **both** resulting records.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two independent owners (body sold to A, neck to B) both registering against one serial. This is the collision case in CX-01, arrived at legitimately.
