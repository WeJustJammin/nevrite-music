## Overview

The persistent identity of an individual physical object and the dated, attested chain of facts
attached to it — who made it, who has owned it, and what happened to it. Every other capability
in domain 15 (theft flags, valuation, service logs, rig membership, gear discography) is a fact
hung off the record this sub-domain mints, so if identity is wrong here it is wrong everywhere
downstream.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: minting a record, claiming ownership of it,
transferring it to someone else, and rendering the resulting chain are four distinct capabilities
that continuously feed each other — a transfer creates a claim, a claim's strength changes how the
chain renders, and the identity key itself is contested for a large share of real gear.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Sweep candidates 01 (*Instrument Passport & Provenance Chain*) and 02 (*Serial Registry & Ownership Transfer*) MERGED into this one sub-domain | They are the same thing named twice. The "passport" IS the serial-keyed record; the "provenance chain" IS the ownership-transfer history. Keeping both would have produced two nodes competing to own one entity. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | Identity key is `(maker, model, era, serial)`, not `serial` alone | Serials are unique only within a maker+model+era. Gibson reused serial sequences across years; Fender's are famously non-sequential. A bare-serial key produces guaranteed collisions and would make point-of-sale screening (15.02.02) unusable through false positives. | Deep Think DT-02 on 15.01.05 |
| D-03 | Ownership claims are **graded**, never boolean | An on-platform purchase, a prior-owner attestation, an uploaded receipt and a bare self-assertion are wildly different evidence. Collapsing them to `verified: true` destroys the only thing the chain is for. | Deep Think DT-01 on 15.01.02 |
