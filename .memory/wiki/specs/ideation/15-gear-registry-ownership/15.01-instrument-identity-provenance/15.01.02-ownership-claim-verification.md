# Feature: Ownership Claim & Verification

> **Number**: 15.01.02
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The assertion "this object is mine", and the graded evidence behind it. A claim is never a boolean —
an on-platform purchase WeJammin witnessed, a prior owner's attestation, an uploaded receipt, and a
bare self-assertion are radically different facts, and collapsing them into `verified: true` would
destroy the only thing the registry is for. This is where the platform's counter-attestation
thesis (a second party's word beats a self-claim) applies to objects rather than to credits. The
claim's tier is not decoration — it is the input a buyer reads at point of sale (domain 13) and the
signal that decides whether the provenance chain (15.01.04) reads as trustworthy or empty.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Claims gear they registered. Sees their claim's tier and what would raise it — upload the receipt, ask the person they bought it from to attest, or simply wait for their next on-platform sale to prove it. Most of their gear will sit at the weakest tier forever, because it was bought on Gumtree in 2014, and the UI must present that as normal, not as a failure. |
| Producer | Full | Same as Musician, with an asymmetry: a Producer's attestation of *someone else's* claim carries weight, because they're a known trust broker in the room. They are asked to attest more often than they claim. |
| Operator | Full | Claims on behalf of an org entity (studio, venue). Studio purchase records are usually good — it's a business asset with an invoice — so Operator claims reach the Documented tier by default more often than a musician's do. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees a claim's tier on a public listing — this is the consumer-facing point of the whole feature. Never claims, never attests. Explicitly None on the write side: a Fan has no gear-ownership relationship to assert and giving them a claim button would only create noise and squatting surface. |

## Behavior

Claims attach to a gear record (15.01.01) and carry a **strength tier** derived from the strongest
*confirmed* piece of evidence behind them. Strength is the input to how the chain renders (15.01.04)
and to what a buyer sees at point of sale (domain 13). A claim can be contested; a contested claim
is not a demoted claim — it is a *disputed* one, and the platform's job is to surface the contest,
not to decide it (D-01).

A claim always exists in exactly one tier at a time, and the tier is the **maximum** of its
confirmed evidence — never an average, never a sum. Attaching weaker evidence to a claim that
already holds stronger evidence changes nothing. Evidence is additive to the record but the tier is
a ceiling function over it.

**Strength ladder** (order is ratified as D-03 in the parent index; the *count* of tiers and the
exact boundaries between them are a product decision — see Q-01):

1. **Witnessed** — the platform saw the transfer happen (an on-platform sale via 15.01.03). Strongest, because WeJammin was the counterparty's counterparty and the fact is machine-recorded, not remembered.
2. **Counter-attested** — the prior owner, or a Producer/Operator with direct knowledge, confirms it. A second party's word, which is the whole counter-attestation thesis.
3. **Documented** — a receipt, invoice or insurance schedule is attached. Strong but forgeable — which is exactly why it sits below Counter-attested, not above it.
4. **Asserted** — the claimant says so. The default at mint, and the majority of all claims forever.

A claim is minted at **Asserted** the moment a record is created (15.01.01) and rises only when
confirmed evidence arrives. It never rises automatically on unconfirmed input (an uploaded receipt
that has not been processed, an attestation request that has not been answered). The tier the buyer
sees is always backed by something that has actually happened.

### Happy Path

1. Musician registers a guitar (15.01.01); a claim is minted at **Asserted**.
2. System shows the current tier and the cheapest available upgrade — here, "you bought this from Dave, who's on WeJammin. Ask him to confirm?"
3. Musician sends the attestation request to Dave.
4. Dave receives it, and confirms he sold it to them in 2019.
5. Claim rises to **Counter-attested**; the chain (15.01.04) re-renders, and the marketplace listing (domain 13), if live, updates its displayed tier.
6. The claimant sees the new tier badge and the next upgrade path, if any (here, none higher is reachable without an on-platform sale).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Prior owner never responds | Claim stays where it is; no negative inference (D-02) | Request shown as pending, then quietly expires. Silence must not read as a denial — most people simply don't answer. |
| Prior owner is not on the platform | No attestation path exists | Offered the Documented route instead; the gap is stated honestly, not as a failure. (Whether the request becomes an invitation is Q-03.) |
| Prior owner denies the sale | Claim becomes **contested**, not demoted | Both parties enter the dispute path shared with 15.02.04; the record shows "ownership contested" to the owner and to any buyer at point of sale |
| Two users claim the same record | Both claims held; neither auto-wins | Contested state; the higher-tier evidence is displayed, but the platform does not adjudicate title (D-01) |
| Claimant uploads a forged receipt | Undetectable at this tier — which is precisely why Documented sits below Counter-attested | No system response; the tier ordering is the mitigation, not a fraud-detection engine |
| Claimant tries to attest their own claim | Rejected; a claim and its attestation cannot share an identity | "You can't confirm your own ownership" — self-attestation would collapse Counter-attested into Asserted with extra steps |
| Claim raised on a record already flagged stolen (15.02) | The flag is surfaced on the claim; the claim does not auto-verify and cannot reach Witnessed by assertion | Claimant sees the theft flag; any buyer sees "flagged" over the tier. This is DT-02 mitigation (b). |
| Claim on the serial of a famous / high-value instrument (squatting attack) | Claim mints at **Asserted** and stays there — there is no evidence a squatter can produce that raises it, since they never bought it and no prior owner will attest | Public listing shows the weakest tier; the squat gains nothing because the registry never launders the assertion into a verified badge. Deeper adjudication when a *real* owner appears is shared with 15.02.04. Related to 15.01.01 Q-02. |
| Confirmed evidence is later withdrawn (attester recants, receipt retracted) | Tier recomputes to the next-strongest *remaining* confirmed evidence | Tier drops to what still stands; the claimant is told which evidence was withdrawn and the new tier |
| Collusion ring — friends cross-attest each other's false claims | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` — trust-graph weighting of attester reputation is a domain-24 concern; at this level, Counter-attested is honest about being *a* second party, not *a trusted* one | Tier still reads Counter-attested; the mitigation lives in how 15.01.04 renders *who* attested, not in blocking the tier |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Attestation request in flight, or evidence being processed | Pending indicator on the claim |
| Empty | Record minted, no evidence beyond the assertion | Claim at **Asserted** with a visible, non-nagging upgrade path |
| Populated | Confirmed evidence attached or attestation received | Tier badge with a plain statement of what backs it |
| Error | Attestation request failed to send | Retry affordance; the claim tier is unchanged (a send failure is not a denial) |
| Partial | Some evidence confirmed, some pending | Tier reflects the best *confirmed* evidence only; pending evidence is shown as pending and does not lift the tier |
| Contested | A competing or denying claim exists | "Ownership contested" — shown to the owner, and to any buyer at point of sale |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership verification is boolean — verified or not | The obvious design; how most marketplaces render a badge | ❌ REJECTED — a boolean either sets the bar so high nobody clears it (registry stays empty) or so low it's meaningless (every self-assertion shows a tick, which is worse than no badge because it launders assertion into fact). Grading is the only honest render. Ratified as D-03 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | A self-asserted claim on someone else's serial is a theft-adjacent attack | Adversarial reading: what does a thief do with this feature? | ✅ CONFIRMED — a thief registers stolen gear, claims it, builds a plausible record, and now the registry is *helping* them sell it. The mitigations are (a) claims never auto-verify, (b) a claim on a record the platform already knows is flagged must surface the flag, and (c) contested claims and false theft flags need the same dispute path — which is why 15.02.04 exists and is shared, not duplicated. |
| DT-03 | Attestation and identity disclosure are the same permission | Implicit in "prior owner confirms" | ❌ REJECTED — they are separate. A prior owner may be willing to confirm they sold the guitar but not to have their name shown on a public passport forever. Selling gear is often financially embarrassing (people sell in hard times). The chain must be able to say "prior owner confirmed (identity withheld)" or the attestation rate collapses. This is also an exposure issue — see the domain CX. |
| DT-04 | The tier is a property of the *evidence*, so it can only go up | Naive model: evidence accumulates, strength accumulates | ❌ REJECTED — the tier is a property of the evidence *that currently stands*. An attester can recant; a receipt can be exposed as another object's; a Witnessed transfer is evidence of a *past* state that a later off-platform sale silently invalidates (the decay question, Q-02). So the tier must be recomputable **downward**, and "confirmed" must mean "confirmed and not withdrawn", not "was once confirmed". This is why the tier is a live ceiling function over standing evidence, not a high-water mark. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform surfaces contests; it does not adjudicate title | WeJammin is not a court and has no discovery powers. Deciding who owns a disputed instrument would create liability it cannot discharge and would be wrong often. Showing the buyer "two people claim this" lets them decline — which is the actual protection they need. |
| D-02 | Silence from a prior owner is neutral, never negative | Most attestation requests will go unanswered — the counterparty moved on years ago. Treating silence as denial would make the strongest evidence tier unreachable and punish honest owners. |
| D-03 | The tier is the maximum of standing confirmed evidence, recomputable downward | A claim holds one tier at a time; weaker evidence never lowers a stronger claim, and withdrawn evidence recomputes the tier down to what remains (DT-04). "Confirmed" means "confirmed and not withdrawn". |
