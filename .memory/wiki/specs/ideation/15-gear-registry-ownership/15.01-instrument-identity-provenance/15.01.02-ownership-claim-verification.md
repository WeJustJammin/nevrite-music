# Feature: Ownership Claim & Verification

> **Number**: 15.01.02
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The assertion "this object is mine", and the graded evidence behind it. A claim is never a boolean —
an on-platform purchase WeJammin witnessed, a prior owner's attestation, an uploaded receipt, and a
bare self-assertion are radically different facts, and collapsing them into `verified: true` would
destroy the only thing the registry is for. This is where the platform's counter-attestation
thesis (a second party's word beats a self-claim) applies to objects rather than to credits.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Claims gear they registered. Sees their claim's tier and what would raise it — upload the receipt, ask the person they bought it from to attest, or simply wait for their next on-platform sale to prove it. Most of their gear will sit at the weakest tier forever, because it was bought on Gumtree in 2014. |
| Producer | Full | Same, with an asymmetry: a Producer's attestation of *someone else's* claim carries weight, because they're a known trust broker in the room. They are asked to attest more often than they claim. |
| Operator | Full | Claims on behalf of an org entity. Studio purchase records are usually good (it's a business asset with an invoice), so their claims tier higher than musicians' by default. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees a claim's tier on a public listing — this is the consumer-facing point of the whole feature. Never claims, never attests. |

## Behavior

Claims attach to a gear record (15.01.01) and carry a strength tier derived from evidence. Strength
is the input to how the chain renders (15.01.04) and to what a buyer sees at point of sale
(domain 13). A claim can be contested; a contested claim is not a demoted claim — it is a
*disputed* one, and the platform's job is to surface the contest, not to decide it.

**Proposed strength ladder** (order confident; the tier names and exact boundaries are
`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`):

1. **Witnessed** — the platform saw the transfer happen (on-platform sale, 15.01.03). Strongest.
2. **Counter-attested** — the prior owner, or a Producer/Operator with direct knowledge, confirms it.
3. **Documented** — a receipt, invoice or insurance schedule is attached. Strong but forgeable.
4. **Asserted** — the claimant says so. The default, and the majority.

### Happy Path

1. Musician registers a guitar (15.01.01); a claim is minted at **Asserted**.
2. System shows the tier and the cheapest available upgrade — here, "you bought this from Dave, who's on WeJammin. Ask him to confirm?"
3. Musician sends the attestation request.
4. Dave confirms he sold it to them in 2019.
5. Claim rises to **Counter-attested**; the chain (15.01.04) re-renders and the marketplace listing, if live, updates.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Prior owner never responds | Claim stays where it is; no negative inference | Request shown as pending, then quietly expires. Silence must not read as a denial — most people simply don't answer. |
| Prior owner is not on the platform | No attestation path exists | Offered the Documented route instead; the gap is honest, not a failure |
| Prior owner denies the sale | Claim becomes **contested**, not demoted | Both parties enter the dispute path shared with 15.02.04; the record shows "ownership contested" |
| Two users claim the same record | Both claims held; neither auto-wins | Contested state; the higher-tier evidence is displayed but the platform does not adjudicate title |
| Claimant uploads a forged receipt | Undetectable at this tier — which is why Documented sits below Counter-attested | No system response; the tier ordering is the mitigation |
| Claim on the serial of a famous instrument | Squatting attack — high-value target | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Related to 15.01.01 Q-02. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Attestation request in flight | Pending indicator on the claim |
| Empty | Record minted, no evidence beyond assertion | Claim at Asserted with a visible, non-nagging upgrade path |
| Populated | Evidence attached or attestation received | Tier badge with what backs it |
| Error | Attestation request failed to send | Retry |
| Partial | Some evidence, some pending | Tier reflects best *confirmed* evidence, never pending evidence |
| Contested | A competing or denying claim exists | "Ownership contested" — shown to the owner, and to any buyer at point of sale |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership verification is boolean — verified or not | The obvious design; how most marketplaces render a badge | ❌ REJECTED — a boolean either sets the bar so high nobody clears it (registry stays empty) or so low it's meaningless (every self-assertion shows a tick, which is worse than no badge because it launders assertion into fact). Grading is the only honest render. Ratified as D-03 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | A self-asserted claim on someone else's serial is a theft-adjacent attack | Adversarial reading: what does a thief do with this feature? | ✅ CONFIRMED — a thief registers stolen gear, claims it, builds a plausible record, and now the registry is *helping* them sell it. The mitigations are (a) claims never auto-verify, (b) a claim on a record the platform already knows is flagged must surface the flag, and (c) contested claims and false theft flags need the same dispute path — which is why 15.02.04 exists and is shared, not duplicated. |
| DT-03 | Attestation and identity disclosure are the same permission | Implicit in "prior owner confirms" | ❌ REJECTED — they are separate. A prior owner may be willing to confirm they sold the guitar but not to have their name shown on a public passport forever. Selling gear is often financially embarrassing (people sell in hard times). The chain must be able to say "prior owner confirmed (identity withheld)" or the attestation rate collapses. This is also an exposure issue — see the domain CX. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform surfaces contests; it does not adjudicate title | WeJammin is not a court and has no discovery powers. Deciding who owns a disputed instrument would create liability it cannot discharge and would be wrong often. Showing the buyer "two people claim this" lets them decline — which is the actual protection they need. |
| D-02 | Silence from a prior owner is neutral, never negative | Most attestation requests will go unanswered — the counterparty moved on years ago. Treating silence as denial would make the strongest evidence tier unreachable and punish honest owners. |
