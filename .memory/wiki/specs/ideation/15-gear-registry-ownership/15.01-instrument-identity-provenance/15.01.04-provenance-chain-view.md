# Feature: Provenance Chain View

> **Number**: 15.01.04
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The object's life story rendered as a dated, attested timeline: made, acquired, serviced,
modified, transferred, flagged, played on these records. This is the passport page a buyer reads
and the thing the domain rationale means by "every transaction enriches a record that makes the
next one safer and better-priced". Its whole design problem is honesty under incentive — it must
make a thin honest chain look fine and a long fabricated one look thin.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Reads their own gear's chain and sees its weak points with a path to strengthen each. Reads a *prospective purchase's* chain before buying — this is the consumer moment. Controls whether their own chain is public (15.04). |
| Producer | Full | Same, plus their attestations appear in other people's chains — a Producer confirming "this amp was in my room for the Rodrigo sessions" is a chain event worth more than the owner's own say-so. |
| Operator | Full | Reads chains on backline they're acquiring. Their room's assets have chains too, but publishing them is a theft-exposure decision (15.07.03), so the chain is usually internal. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the public chain on a marketplace listing: what it is, how confident the platform is, and where the gaps are. Cannot see prior owners' names unless each consented (see DT-03). This is the only part of domain 15 a Fan meaningfully uses. |

## Behavior

The chain is an append-only sequence of dated events sourced from across domain 15 — it is a
*view*, not a store, and it owns no facts of its own. Its job is rendering, and rendering is where
the integrity lives. Two rules govern it:

**Gaps render as gaps.** A chain that starts "acquired from a pawn shop, prior history unknown" is
the honest majority case. Unknown must render as unknown, neutrally, with no implied suspicion —
otherwise every ordinary owner's gear looks dodgy and nobody uses the feature.

**Confidence is bounded by the weakest link, not the count.** Thirty self-assertions must not
outrank two witnessed transfers.

### Happy Path

1. Fan opens a marketplace listing for a used Rhodes.
2. System renders the chain: "Made 1974 (asserted) → Owned by a WeJammin member since 2019 (witnessed transfer) → Serviced twice, tech-attested → Played on 3 released records."
3. Fan sees a confidence summary and, next to it, an explicit statement of what the chain does *not* prove — that it is not an authenticity check.
4. Fan expands a service event and sees the attesting tech.
5. Fan buys with the chain attached to the order; the sale becomes the chain's next link.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Chain has one event (just registered) | Renders as one event; no penalty implied | "Registered on WeJammin in 2026. Earlier history not recorded." — neutral, not a warning |
| Chain has a gap in the middle | Gap rendered explicitly with its span | "2011–2019: history not recorded" |
| Transfer left unconfirmed (15.01.03) | Rendered as a dangling link, not omitted and not completed | "Transferred to a member in 2022 — recipient did not confirm" |
| Prior owner attested but withheld identity | Event renders, name does not | "Prior owner confirmed this sale (identity withheld)" |
| Record is flagged stolen (15.02) | Flag dominates the render; a contested flag renders as contested, never as "stolen" | See 15.02.04 |
| Long chain of self-assertions only | Confidence stays low despite length | Confidence badge reflects weakest link; the length is visible but doesn't inflate the score |
| Identity forked by a component swap (15.01.06) | Chain renders up to the swap on both resulting records, then splits: shared history above the fork, divergent history below. Neither record inherits the other's post-fork events | "Neck replaced 2021 — history before this point is shared with [other record]" on each; the pre-fork span is marked as shared, not duplicated ownership |
| An event's attester was later banned or deleted their account (15.01.02) | The event survives — the chain owns no facts, and deleting the witness does not un-witness the past — but its attestation strength drops to reflect that the witness can no longer be reached to re-confirm | "Service attested in 2022 by an account no longer active" — event kept, confidence for that link reduced, never silently removed |
| Owner disputes an event on their *own* chain (e.g. a transfer they say never happened) | The chain does not let the subject unilaterally erase a witnessed event — that would defeat the point of witnessing. The dispute is recorded as a contested marker on the event; resolution is a 15.02/24 concern | "This event is disputed by the current owner" — event and dispute both visible; deep resolution `[PENDING — /write-be-spec: dispute state machine]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Chain assembling from multiple sources | Skeleton timeline |
| Empty | Record just minted | Single origin event with an honest note that history starts here |
| Populated | Multiple events | Timeline with per-event attestation strength |
| Error | A source (e.g. flags) unreachable | The chain must **not** render a partial as complete. If flag state is unknown, say so — a chain that silently omits an unreachable flag is the worst failure in the domain |
| Partial | Some events pending attestation | Pending events shown as pending, excluded from confidence |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A chain with gaps signals a problem | Intuitive reading of a provenance record; how a Carfax gap reads for cars | ❌ REJECTED — cars have title registries and mandatory transfer records; guitars have none. The overwhelming majority of real gear was bought second-hand with no paperwork from someone the buyer barely knew. If gaps read as suspicion then honest owners are punished, they stop publishing chains, and the registry only holds the chains of people with something to sell. Unknown must render as unknown, neutrally. This is a *design* decision with adoption stakes, not a copy detail. |
| DT-02 | Chain length is a proxy for trustworthiness | The naive scoring approach, and what a user will assume unless told otherwise | ❌ REJECTED — and it inverts the incentive catastrophically. If length scores, the optimal fraud is to fabricate volume: register early, self-assert a dozen events, and outrank a real instrument with two witnessed transfers. Confidence must be bounded by the weakest link and must show attestation strength per event. Follows directly from D-03 (graded claims). |
| DT-03 | Prior owners can be named freely in a public chain | Implied by "chain of ownership" | ❌ REJECTED — the chain names real people alongside what they owned and when they sold it, on a permanent public page. Selling gear is frequently a financial-distress event; being permanently listed as the person who sold their Les Paul in 2009 is a disclosure they never agreed to. Naming also composes badly with the exposure hazard (a named current owner + a public tour date = a targeting list — see the domain CX). Prior-owner naming requires per-event consent; the default is "confirmed, identity withheld". |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The chain is a view, not a store | Every fact it shows is owned by the feature that produced it. Duplicating them here would create two truths and guarantee drift. |
| D-02 | The chain must state what it does not prove | Per 15.01.01 DT-04: provenance is not authenticity. A chain that implies authenticity while disclaiming it in the terms of service is worse than no chain. |
