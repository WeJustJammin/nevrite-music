# Feature: Non-Serialized & Contested Serial Identity

> **Number**: 15.01.05
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Resolves what "this object" means when the serial is missing, duplicated, unreliable, or attached
to a part that unbolts. This is not an edge case bolted onto the registry — it decides the primary
key, and the domain's headline capability (serial-keyed identity) is simply wrong without it for a
large share of real gear: most pedals have no serial, Gibson reused serial sequences across
decades, and Fender's live on a neck plate that gets swapped.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Registers a pedalboard where nine of twelve boxes have no serial, and a '60s reissue whose serial matches four other instruments. Never sees "identity resolution" as a feature — they see a registration flow that doesn't dead-end, plus an honest identity-confidence badge. |
| Producer | Full | Hits this hardest. Studio outboard, ribbon mics and tube amps routinely predate consistent serialisation entirely, and much of it has been re-chassised or rebuilt. A large share of a Producer's most valuable gear cannot be serial-keyed at all. |
| Operator | Full | Same exposure on house backline and vintage room gear, at higher volume and often registered by staff who don't know the instrument's history. |
| Fan | None | Pure registration machinery with no public surface. A Fan sees only the downstream effect: an identity-confidence badge on a listing, rendered by 15.01.04. |

## Behavior

The feature supplies two things to 15.01.01: a **composite identity key** and a **fallback identity
token**.

**Composite key** — `(maker, model, era, serial)`. A serial is unique only within a manufacturer's
model line and production era. Keying on the bare number produces guaranteed collisions between
unrelated objects, which would be merely untidy in a collection view but is disqualifying for
point-of-sale screening (15.02.02): a false "this is stolen" on an innocent seller's listing is
the worst output the domain can produce.

**Fallback token** — a platform-issued WeJammin gear ID, bound to photographs of the specific
object, for gear with no serial. Its strength is entirely the strength of the photo binding, which
is weak, and the record must say so rather than presenting a WJ-ID as equivalent to a serial.

### Happy Path

1. Musician registers a Klon clone with no serial anywhere on it.
2. System detects no serial, offers a platform-issued WJ-ID and asks for photos that identify *this* box — wear, scratches, knob positions, the underside.
3. Musician photographs it.
4. Record mints with identity confidence "photo-bound, no serial" and is honest that this is weaker than a serial.
5. The pedal is registrable, insurable (15.05.03) and sellable with a passport — which is more than it had before.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Serial matches an existing record on the composite key | Disambiguation, never a silent merge or a silent duplicate | "This may already be registered — same object, or same serial on a different instrument?" |
| Serial matches on the bare number but a different maker | No collision — the composite key separates them | Nothing; this is the key doing its job |
| Two objects genuinely share a serial (documented manufacturer reuse) | Both records legitimately coexist under one composite key. Resolution routes through the disambiguation prompt (same UI as row 1): the registrant asserts "different instrument", and the record is minted with a per-object discriminator so the two are separable downstream. **Which discriminator is authoritative — photo binding, an owner-assigned nickname, or a WJ-ID suffix — is Q-01, deferred to Step 5 Must-depth.** The behavior (coexist + disambiguate, never merge, never block) is settled at Level 1 | "There's already a record with this maker, model, era and serial. Is this the same instrument, or a different one that shares the number?" → choosing "different" mints a second record flagged `shared-serial` |
| Serial-less gear: two owners each mint a WJ-ID for the same physical object | Two WJ-IDs for one object is a duplicate, but there is no serial to detect it on. Photo-binding similarity is the only signal, and it is weak (DT-03). Level 1: the duplicate is **tolerated, not silently merged** — a WJ-ID is declared weak (D-01), so two of them is an acceptable, honest state, reconciled later by transfer or dispute | Each owner sees their own photo-bound record; no false "already registered" is raised on a signal this weak |
| Serial is on a swapped part | Identity may fork — the resolver defers the fork decision to 15.01.06 rather than deciding it here | See `#CX-05` |
| Era unknown (undated instrument) | Key degrades to `(maker, model, ?, serial)` and confidence drops | "We're not sure of the year — this weakens identity" |
| Serial illegible / partially transcribed (worn stamp, OCR miss) | Treated as era-unknown-class degradation: the record mints in the Partial state with a visible confidence downgrade and a path to correct the serial later; it is never guessed or auto-completed | "We couldn't read this serial cleanly — the record is weaker until it's confirmed" |
| Owner enters a fabricated serial | Undetectable here | No system response; mitigation lives in claim strength (15.01.02), not in key resolution |
| Two people register the same object at once (owner and consigning dealer) | A legitimately-arrived-at collision. First mint wins the key; the second registrant hits the row-1 disambiguation path and is told the object may already be registered. Neither is silently dropped and no duplicate is minted behind either's back. The **notification** to the existing holder ("someone is registering gear that may be yours" — also a theft signal) is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / cross-cut with 15.02]`; the collision-handling behavior itself is settled | "This may already be registered — is this the same object you're cataloguing, or a different one?" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Key resolution in flight | Brief pending state during registration |
| Empty | N/A — this feature has no standalone list | — |
| Populated | Key resolved | Identity confidence badge on the record |
| Error | Resolver unavailable | Registration must **block**, not proceed. Minting a record with an unresolved key creates a duplicate that is expensive to unpick later |
| Partial | Serial illegible or era unknown | Record mints with a visible, honest confidence downgrade and a path to improve it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Serial-less gear is a small tail worth deferring | The framing implied by treating this as a sub-feature of a "serial registry" | ❌ REJECTED — it is not the tail, it is a large fraction of the actual population. Almost all effects pedals, most pre-1960s instruments, all DIY and boutique builds, and much studio outboard have no usable serial. A registry that only handles serialised gear silently excludes the pedalboard — the thing a working musician owns most of, loses most often to van theft, and would most want registered. This feature is load-bearing for coverage, which is why it is proposed `should` rather than `could`. |
| DT-02 | Serial alone is a sufficient identity key | Stated implicitly by the domain rationale's "serial-keyed instrument identity" | ❌ REJECTED — serials are namespaced by maker, model line and era, and manufacturers have demonstrably reused them (Gibson's late-'60s/'70s sequences are the canonical case). The consequence is not cosmetic: 15.02.02 screens listings against flagged serials, so a bare-serial key means an innocent seller's Les Paul gets flagged because an unrelated instrument with the same number was stolen. Composite key ratified as D-02 in the parent index. |
| DT-03 | Physical fingerprinting (wood grain, wear pattern, photo hashing) can identify serial-less gear | Attractive — it would solve the problem properly, and grain is genuinely unique | ❌ REJECTED for this pass — no registry demonstrably does this reliably, gear changes appearance with wear/refinishing (which is 15.01.06's whole subject), and lighting/angle variance makes naive matching unreliable. Recording the rejection so it is not rediscovered as an easy win. Retained as Q-02 — it may be worth revisiting, but it cannot be assumed to work. |
| DT-04 | Honestly labelling a weak identity is more valuable than blocking gear that can't be strongly identified | The domain's headline failure is a boolean badge that launders weak evidence into strong-looking certainty; the same failure would recur if serial-less gear were simply refused registration | ✅ CONFIRMED — refusing serial-less gear excludes the pedalboard, the single most-owned and most-stolen category (DT-01). The correct move is to admit the object with a truthful confidence downgrade (Partial state, WJ-ID declared weaker than a serial per D-01) rather than either fabricate certainty or exclude the object. A registrable, insurable, sellable-with-caveats record beats no record. This is why key-resolution *failure* blocks (D-02) but *weak identity* does not — an unresolved key is a data-integrity hazard, a weak-but-resolved key is just an honest fact. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A WJ-ID is presented as weaker than a serial, never as equivalent | Its binding to the object is photographic and therefore contestable. Presenting it as equivalent would launder a weak identity into a strong-looking one — the same failure as a boolean ownership badge. |
| D-03 | **DQ-MG-05:** this file **authors** the canonical identity-confidence value set; `15.01.01` renders it and never defines its own | The tree already names this file as owner of identity-resolution logic and the authoritative value set, so per the ratified author-where-owned rule the enum belongs here and is *referenced* elsewhere — a second definition in a render table is the copy that drifts. The values are the six already in use on disk; relocating them costs nothing because no downstream literal binds to them today. Collapsing the set is forbidden by D-01: any bucketing that renders a photographic WJ-ID as equivalent to a confirmed serial would launder a weak identity into a strong-looking one. A richer set was rejected as three orthogonal fields (serial state, era certainty, non-serial discriminator) wearing one enum's clothes. |
| D-04 | **DQ-MG-06:** two records resolving to one identity key **never auto-merge** — both are retained, both claim-holders are notified, and a merge requires mutual consent | Consistent with the append-only mint and with domain 09's ratified `CQ-08` precedent: exact equality may authorise a merge of unclaimed stubs, but nothing probabilistic ever merges records that assert independent provenance. First-minted-wins privileges timing over evidence; a confidence threshold makes an unpickable mistake at the tail. Note the mechanism precisely: CX-01 **blocks the mint pending disambiguation** — it does not fork. Forking is CX-05's swapped-serial-bearing-part case, a different trigger. |
| D-02 | Key resolution failure blocks the mint | A duplicate record is far more expensive to unpick after facts have accreted onto both halves than a blocked registration is to retry. |
