# Feature: Ownership Transfer Handshake

> **Number**: 15.01.03
> **Parent**: [Instrument Identity & Provenance](./15.01-instrument-identity-provenance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The two-sided confirmation that moves a gear record from one owner to the next, and the only
ownership event WeJammin actually witnesses rather than takes someone's word for. It is the
mechanism by which trading on the platform manufactures provenance quality as a byproduct — the
Carfax model the domain rationale rests on — which means it must fire automatically on an
on-platform sale and must never require the seller to remember to do it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sells a pedal on domain 13 and the transfer just happens — no form, no step. Sells a guitar off-platform to a mate and can send a manual handshake request. Receives gear and confirms with one tap, which mints their Witnessed claim. |
| Producer | Full | Same. Additionally the common source of "I'm giving this to the studio" / "the band's buying my amp" transfers — where there is no sale, so the transfer reason carries the meaning. |
| Operator | Full | Transfers into and out of an org entity — buying backline, selling off a retired console. Their transfers are org-to-person or org-to-org, and staff may initiate on the entity's behalf. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees "ownership transferred on WeJammin, 2024" as the strongest possible line on a public chain. If they buy gear they are acting as a Musician and confirm like anyone else. |

## Behavior

A transfer is a state machine with two participants and one object. It is initiated (by the
current claim-holder, or automatically by a marketplace order), confirmed by the recipient, and
appended to the chain. Its failure mode is the recipient never confirming — which must produce an
honest, visible dangling state rather than a silent success or a silent rollback.

Transfers carry a **reason**: sale, gift, inheritance, band dissolution, org contribution, trade.
This is not decoration — an estate transfer has no living counterparty, a band dissolution has an
entity that is ceasing to exist, and neither can complete a normal handshake.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's marketplace listing (domain 13) sells; the order reaches delivered/complete.
2. System initiates the transfer automatically — the seller does nothing.
3. Buyer is prompted on the order: "Confirm you received this Telecaster" — the same tap that closes the order.
4. Buyer confirms; the record moves, a Witnessed claim is minted (15.01.02), and the chain gains a link.
5. Seller's collection (15.04) moves the item to "formerly owned", keeping their history intact.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recipient never confirms | Timeout writes `transferred — unconfirmed by recipient`; the chain shows the gap explicitly | Seller: "Dave hasn't confirmed — the chain will show this as unconfirmed." Chain reader: an honest gap, rendered neutrally |
| Recipient confirms after the timeout | The existing chain link upgrades; a second link is never created | Chain shows the transfer, confirmed late |
| Seller initiates two transfers of one item | Blocked at initiation, not detected at completion | "This gear already has a transfer in progress" |
| Off-platform sale, buyer not on WeJammin | No handshake possible; seller can mark it transferred-out to an unknown party | Chain shows "sold, recipient unknown" — the honest majority case for off-platform trade |
| Gear sold on-platform but the order is refunded/returned | Transfer must reverse — a reversal is a new chain event, never a deletion | Chain shows transfer and reversal |
| Ownership contested (15.01.02) at initiation | Transfer blocked | "Ownership of this gear is contested — resolve before transferring" |
| Owner has died | No counterparty to initiate | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. See parent Q-04. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Handshake being written | Brief pending state on the order |
| Empty | No transfers on this record | Chain shows acquisition only |
| Populated | Transfer complete | Chain link with Witnessed strength |
| Error | Write failed | Retry; the transfer must be idempotent — a double-tap must not double-transfer |
| Partial | Initiated, awaiting confirmation | "Awaiting confirmation from [recipient]" |
| Unconfirmed | Timeout elapsed | Chain gap, rendered neutrally on both sides |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Transfer should be a form the seller fills in after a sale | The straightforward design | ❌ REJECTED — this is exactly the Producer persona's *accidental* anti-behaviour ("closes a project without capturing splits — we'll do it later") transplanted onto gear. personas.md states the design principle directly: **the lazy path must be the correct path**. A transfer that requires remembering will not happen, the chain will not accrete, and the domain's entire Carfax premise fails. It must be a byproduct of the marketplace order — ideally the same tap that confirms delivery. |
| DT-02 | An unconfirmed transfer should either succeed or roll back | Binary thinking about a two-party protocol | ❌ REJECTED — both are lies. Silent success means the chain says Dave owns it when Dave never agreed. Silent rollback means the chain loses a transfer that really happened. The only honest third option is a visible dangling state, and the chain must be able to express it. This is the same principle as 15.01.04's "gaps are normal" — a registry that cannot say "I don't know" will fabricate. |
| DT-03 | Every transfer is a sale | Marketplace-shaped assumption | ❌ REJECTED — gifts, inheritance, band dissolution (who gets the PA when the band splits?), and contributions to a studio's asset register are all transfers with no money and sometimes no willing counterparty. Transfer reason is a first-class field, and two reasons (inheritance, dissolution) break the handshake premise entirely because one party is gone. Band dissolution in particular is an entity-level event owned by domain 01 that cascades here. |
