# Feature: Rig Spec Sheet & Stage Plot Source Data

> **Number**: 15.06.03
> **Parent**: [Rig Profile & Compatibility](./15.06-rig-profile-compatibility-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Renders a rig as the technical document a venue's crew reads — what's coming, what it needs, where
it goes — and supplies the gear section of a stage plot and a technical rider. Critically, it is
**source data, not the rider**: show advancing is domain 18's job and a rider is far more than a
gear list. This feature's contribution is that the gear section stops being retyped from memory
into a Word document for every show.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Exports their rig as a tech spec once and reuses it for forty shows, instead of rewriting it per venue. The value is that it is *already true* — it comes from the registry rather than from what they remembered while filling in an email. |
| Producer | Full | Exports a session's gear requirements to a studio before a booking. Lower frequency than the live case but higher precision — a session that needs specific outboard needs the studio to confirm it exists and works. |
| Operator | Read-only | The **recipient**. Receives the visiting band's sheet through domain 18's advancing flow and reads it to prepare the room. This is the persona the document exists for, and they never author it — which is exactly why they are read-only across this whole sub-domain. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The sheet is a projection of the rig definition at crew resolution: items, power requirements,
connector types, physical footprint, placement, and — routed from the oracle (15.06.02) — any
requirements the venue needs to satisfy, like a step-down transformer.

It stores nothing. It is a render, and its data quality is entirely inherited from 15.06.01, which
means the sheet will have the same gaps the rig has. Those gaps must render as gaps: a tech spec
that silently omits an unknown power draw reads as a complete document to a crew who will then be
surprised on the day.

**A sent sheet is a snapshot.** If the rig changes afterwards, the venue holds a stale document and
nobody knows. This is unresolved and is the same class of failure as 15.07.03's stale backline
list, pointing the other way.

### Happy Path

1. Musician confirms a booking (domain 17) and the advancing flow (domain 18) asks for their tech spec.
2. They export the "Fly Rig" sheet — no retyping, and it reflects what they actually own now rather than what they owned in 2023.
3. The oracle's finding (needs a 230V step-down) appears as a requirement line, not as a warning buried in the band's own app where it helps nobody.
4. Sheet goes to the venue through domain 18's advancing conversation.
5. Operator reads it, has a step-down in the cupboard, and the mismatch never becomes a problem.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | WeJammin should generate the technical rider here | Tempting: the rider's gear section is the painful part and we have the data | ❌ REJECTED — a rider is a schedule, hospitality, crew, staging, security, load-in times and a gear list, and it is negotiated between band and venue as part of **advancing**, which is domain 18's whole job. Building a rider generator in a gear registry would duplicate 18 for one section of its document and split ownership of a negotiated artefact across two domains. The clean boundary: 15 supplies gear source data; 18 composes, negotiates and delivers the rider. Ratified as D-03 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-02 | The sheet's value is saving retyping | The obvious pitch, and it's true | ✅ CONFIRMED but incomplete — the deeper value is that the sheet is **already true**. A tech spec retyped from memory into an email is a description of the rig the musician *thinks* they have, drifting further from reality with every gear change; a rendered sheet describes the gear that is actually registered. This is the platform's capture-at-source thesis in miniature: the document is a byproduct of a record that was maintained for other reasons, not a reconstruction performed under time pressure. That said — it inherits the rig's gaps (15.06.01 DT-03), so "already true" means "already true about what we know", and the gaps must be visible in the document. |
| DT-03 | A sent spec sheet stays accurate | Unexamined — the export feels like a terminal action | ❌ REJECTED — the sheet is a **snapshot**, and rigs change between the advance and the show. The venue holds a PDF from six weeks ago and neither party knows it's stale. This is structurally identical to 15.07.03's stale-backline-list failure pointing in the opposite direction: the band is now the one publishing rotting data to someone who will act on it. Symmetry noted deliberately — if the fix for the venue's list is "the listing reads the register" (15.07.03 D-01), the fix here may be the same shape: the venue reads a live rig rather than holding a copy. That would put it inside domain 18's advancing flow. Unresolved. |
