# Feature: Compatibility Oracle

> **Number**: 15.06.02
> **Parent**: [Rig Profile & Compatibility](./15.06-rig-profile-compatibility-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Evaluates a rig (15.06.01) against a target environment — a venue's published backline, a studio's
room specs, another country's mains supply — and reports what won't work. It exists because the
Operator persona's named accidental failure is precisely this: "a show that failed on a preventable
technical mismatch (band needed something the room never had)." The whole feature is an attempt to
catch that mismatch weeks earlier, when it is a purchase order rather than a cancelled show.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Checks their rig against a venue before confirming a booking, and against a country before a tour. Wants an answer, not a report: "will this work, and if not what do I need?" |
| Producer | Full | Checks gear against a studio's power and format before a session — will this converter clock to that desk, is there enough clean power for the vintage rack. Their checks are format and impedance, less often mains. |
| Operator | Read-only | The *target*, not the author. Sees what visiting bands need via the spec sheet (15.06.03). Cannot run a check on someone's rig — but is the party who can actually fix the mismatch, which is why CX-03 routes findings into the outgoing sheet. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The oracle is a pure function of `(rig, target)`. Its findings are the mundane, expensive failures
that actually happen:

- **Mains voltage and frequency** — a 120V amp plugged into 230V. The classic international-touring failure, and the most destructive.
- **Connector and plug type** — mains plugs, but also audio connectors and speaker cabling.
- **Physical** — will the rig fit the stage, the van, the load-in, the lift.
- **Format / interconnect** — impedance mismatches, clocking, digital format compatibility.

**It reports what it could not check as prominently as what it did.** Coverage is bounded by two
data sets it does not own: the rig's specs (which 15.06.01 DT-03 says will be sparse) and the
target's specs (which the Operator publishes and lets rot). A check that silently skips the
unknown and returns "compatible" is the false-assurance failure again, in a domain where the
consequence is a cancelled show or a burnt-out amp.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is offered a run of shows in Germany and opens the check against the first venue.
2. Oracle compares the rig to the venue's published backline (15.07.03) and to the region's mains standard.
3. It reports: their amp is 120V and Germany is 230V — a hard mismatch; the venue's published backline includes a suitable amp; two pedals have unknown power draw and could not be checked.
4. Musician routes the finding to the venue as a requirement on their spec sheet (15.06.03): they'll use house backline.
5. The show doesn't fail on a preventable mismatch — which is the entire point.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Rig specs unknown (most items) | Cannot check those items | "8 of 14 items checked — 6 have no power data" as prominently as any pass |
| Target's data is stale | The oracle is confidently wrong | See DT-02 — this is the feature's core hazard, not an edge case |
| Target has no published specs | No check possible | "This venue hasn't published its specs" — honest, not a pass |
| Voltage mismatch | The important find | Hard-fail, prominent — this destroys equipment |
| Target's specs change after the check | Check is a snapshot; the show is later | Re-check on change should notify. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Rig contains placeholders | Placeholders are unspecified by definition | Excluded from the check and reported as excluded |
| Everything checks out | Must not read as a guarantee | "Nothing incompatible found in what we could check" — scoped, per D-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Check running | Progress |
| Empty | No target specs available | "Can't check — this venue hasn't published specs" |
| Populated | Check complete | Findings, plus explicit uncovered items |
| Error | Check failed | Retry; never render a failed check as a pass |
| Partial | The normal state — most checks are partial | Coverage stated with equal prominence to findings |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The interesting compatibility problems are audio-technical — impedance matching, gain staging, clocking | It's what a gear forum argues about, and it's where the domain expertise looks impressive | ❌ REJECTED as the priority — the failures that actually cancel shows and destroy equipment are **mundane**: mains voltage (a 120V amp in Europe), plug and connector type, and physical fit (the rig doesn't get up the stairs, the cab doesn't fit the van). These are boring, entirely checkable from data, and cost thousands when missed. Impedance mismatch is real but rarer and usually survivable. Priority order is voltage → connector → physical → format, which is roughly the inverse of what a gear expert would list first. |
| DT-02 | The oracle's accuracy is a function of how good our matching logic is | The implementer's framing — it makes the problem tractable and internal | ❌ REJECTED — **the oracle is only as good as the target's data, which we don't own and which is known to rot.** The target is a venue's published backline list, and personas.md names the Operator's accidental anti-behaviour exactly: "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show." So the oracle can run flawless logic against wrong inputs and confidently tell a band their rig will work in a room whose amp died last year. That is *worse than no oracle*, because the band would otherwise have phoned to check. Two consequences: the oracle must surface the target data's freshness alongside its verdict, and this feature's value is downstream of 15.07.03 making the register the single source of truth. Escalated as Q-01 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-03 | Full electrical modelling (current draw summation, impedance chains, phantom power budgets) is the end state | It's the technically satisfying version and it would genuinely be useful | ❌ REJECTED for this pass — it requires per-item electrical data that 15.06.01 DT-03 establishes will not exist, so the sophisticated engine would run on data that isn't there. Building the model before the data is available inverts the dependency. Recording the rejection so it isn't rediscovered as an obvious win: the constraint is data availability, not modelling capability. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A pass is scoped to what was checked; it is never a guarantee | Per DT-02. "Nothing incompatible found in what we could check" is true; "compatible" is not, and the domain has already learned this lesson at 15.02.02. |
| D-02 | Uncovered items are reported with equal prominence to findings | A check covering 8 of 14 items that shows only the 8 is a lie by omission, and the 6 unchecked ones are where the surprise lives. |
| D-03 | Priority is voltage → connector → physical → format | Per DT-01. Ordered by frequency × cost of the real failures, not by technical interest. |
