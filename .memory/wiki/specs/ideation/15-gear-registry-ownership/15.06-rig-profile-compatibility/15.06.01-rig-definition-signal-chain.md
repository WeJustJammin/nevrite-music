# Feature: Rig Definition & Signal Chain

> **Number**: 15.06.01
> **Parent**: [Rig Profile & Compatibility](./15.06-rig-profile-compatibility-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A named, ordered grouping of gear that describes a working setup — pedalboard order, stage rig,
studio monitoring path — including the physical and electrical properties a venue or crew needs to
know. It is the functional counterpart to the collection (15.04): a collection is everything you
own, a rig is a subset that does a job together, and it routinely includes gear you don't own at
all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Builds rigs from their collection plus placeholders for what the venue provides. Maintains a couple of standing rigs (the fly rig, the full board) rather than one per gig. The signal chain order matters to them; the power draw doesn't, which is the feature's data problem. |
| Producer | Full | Defines studio signal paths — mic → pre → comp → converter. Specs are better documented on studio gear but the equipment is older, and much of what they'd want to record about routing has no natural home in a "rig" shaped for a stage. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads visiting bands' rigs through the spec sheet (15.06.03), never authors one. Their supply-side equivalent is the asset register (15.07) — the room's gear is inventory, not a rig. |
| Fan | None | Professional tooling with no consumer surface. A public rig would be interesting to fans, but that publication decision belongs to 15.04's visibility controls and carries its exposure hazard. |

## Behavior

A rig references gear records (15.01.01) in an order, with connection and placement information,
plus placeholders for gear the user doesn't own or hasn't registered.

**Rig membership is many-to-many with case membership (15.10).** A rig spans several cases; a case
holds parts of several rigs. Neither is the parent. Both groupings are real, used by different
workflows — compatibility versus transport — and modelling one as a tree over the other breaks the
other.

**Rigs contain gear you don't own.** House drum kits, venue-supplied backline, borrowed amps, hired
PA. A rig restricted to owned, registered gear describes almost nobody's actual setup — which
means placeholders and custody-held items (15.08) are core, not edge cases.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates "Fly Rig" and drags eight registered pedals into a signal-chain order.
2. They add a placeholder for "house amp — Fender Twin or similar" because they don't fly with one.
3. System asks for the properties a venue would need: total power draw, connector types, physical footprint. Most are unknown and stay unknown.
4. Rig saves with visible gaps rather than fabricated defaults.
5. The rig is checkable (15.06.02) to the extent it's specified, and exportable (15.06.03) at whatever resolution it has.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Rig contains unregistered/unowned gear | Placeholders and custody-held items — the normal case | Placeholder items shown as unspecified; the oracle can't check them and says so |
| Same gear in two rigs | Fine — membership is many-to-many | No conflict |
| Gear in a rig is sold (15.01.03) | Rig now references gear the user doesn't own | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — remove, placeholder, or leave? |
| Gear in a rig is stolen (15.02.01) | Rig is a natural bulk-flag unit, like a case | Flag by rig offered |
| Electrical properties unknown | The overwhelmingly common case | Unknown, never defaulted — a defaulted voltage is worse than a blank one |
| Rig spans cases | Expected | Both groupings coexist; neither owns the other |
| Studio signal path vs stage rig | Genuinely different shapes forced into one entity | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rig fetching | Skeleton |
| Empty | No rigs defined | Empty state; a rig only earns its keep if you gig or record out |
| Populated | Rig defined | Ordered chain with per-item spec completeness |
| Error | Save failed | Retry |
| Partial | The normal state — most specs unknown | Gaps visible; completeness is honest, not scored into a false percentage |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A rig is the natural container for gear, so cases (15.10) should be a sub-grouping of rigs — or vice versa | Tidy hierarchy; either direction looks reasonable in isolation | ❌ REJECTED — the relationship is **many-to-many** and neither direction survives contact with reality. A rig spans several cases (the pedalboard in one, the amp in another, cables in a third); a case holds parts of several rigs (the utility case with cables for everything). Making cases children of rigs breaks the van-loading and manifest workflows; making rigs children of cases breaks the compatibility check. Both groupings are first-class over the same gear records, serving transport and compatibility respectively. Ratified as D-02 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-02 | A rig is built from gear you own | It's a feature of a *registry* of gear you own — the assumption is almost invisible | ❌ REJECTED, and it would gut the feature. Real rigs are substantially composed of gear the user does not own: house drum kits, venue backline, hired PA, the amp the guitarist borrows from the other band on the bill. A fly rig is *defined* by what it doesn't include. If a rig can only contain owned registered gear, it describes almost nobody's real setup and every compatibility check runs against a fiction. Placeholders and custody-held items (15.08) are therefore core to the model — which is also why 15.08 exists as a node. |
| DT-03 | Users will fill in electrical and physical specs | Necessary for the oracle (15.06.02) to work, therefore assumed | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the sub-domain's quiet structural problem. A guitarist knows their pedal order and does not know its current draw; nobody has ever needed to record the physical footprint of their amp. The data the oracle needs is data the rig's author has no independent reason to capture, so it won't be there — the classic cold-data failure. Two honest responses: derive specs from a gear catalogue where one exists (15.01.01 Q-01, itself unresolved), and render unknowns as unknown rather than defaulting them. **A defaulted voltage is worse than a blank one** — it produces a confident check against a number nobody supplied. |
