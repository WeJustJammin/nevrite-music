# Feature: Gear Discography

> **Number**: 15.09
> **Parent**: [Gear Registry & Ownership](./gear-registry-ownership-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The link between an individual object and the sessions and records it played on — *this Telecaster
played on these eleven records*. The domain rationale calls it the clearest proof that this is not
a marketplace feature, and the reasoning holds: it is impossible for Reverb (which has gear
identity but no sessions) and impossible for a credits database (which has sessions but no gear
identity). It exists only where both live together, which makes it the sharpest single expression
of the platform thesis anywhere in this domain. It is a **read projection of Domain 02** onto
objects — it stores no session facts of its own, it renders the ones the credit graph already
holds, filtered through the gear's identity.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sees which of their gear appears on which records, and **consents to or declines display** — but cannot assert a link. They have a direct incentive to inflate ("my Tele played on that record"), which is exactly why the assertion isn't theirs to make. Their formerly-owned gear (15.04) keeps its discography: selling the guitar doesn't unmake the session. They may *request* attestation for a link they believe is missing; the Producer confirms it or doesn't. |
| Producer | Full | **The attestation point.** They ran the room, they know the amp was the studio's Deluxe and the bass was the session player's Jazz. They attach gear to sessions as a byproduct of capturing credits (domain 02) — the same moment, the same flow, no extra admin. They can also **correct** a link they attested in error, through the same correction path domain 02 gives credits (D-04). Without this persona the feature has no honest mechanism. |
| Operator | Config | A studio's assets accumulate a discography that is genuinely valuable marketing — "our Neve was on these records" sells rooms. They consent to display of their room's gear; they don't assert session facts they weren't part of. Where the Operator also ran the session as Producer, the Producer lens governs — the personas are lenses, not accounts (personas.md). |
| Fan | Read-only | The audience for it. "The guitar on that solo" is exactly the kind of thing fans care about, and it surfaces on a public passport or a marketplace listing (domain 13). Sees only what everyone consented to publish — never a hidden link, never a hidden-link count. |

> No persona has **None** access: the feature is a four-party surface by construction — Producer
> asserts, Musician and Operator consent to display, Fan consumes. Removing any one collapses either
> the honesty (Producer) or the audience (Fan) of the feature.

## Behavior

A gear-discography link connects a gear record (15.01.01) to a session or credit (domain 02). It is
created by the **Producer as session owner**, not asserted by the gear's owner — the same
counter-attestation logic that makes a producer-confirmed credit worth more than a self-claim.

**The link inherits the credit's visibility and never widens it.** A session may be unreleased, an
NDA'd ghost-production job, or a credit the artist has not disclosed. A gear link that renders "this
amp was used on [unreleased project]" leaks the project through the back door of a gear page —
which is a confidentiality breach the gear owner had no right to cause and possibly no knowledge
of.

**The link also inherits the credit's *status*, not only its visibility.** If the underlying credit
is disputed (domain 02), unverified, or later corrected, the gear link follows it — a disputed
credit yields a suppressed link, a corrected credit yields a corrected link (D-04, D-05). The
discography is never a source of truth the credit graph can contradict; it is a lens on that graph.

**Gear on a session is frequently not the player's.** Studio backline, the producer's own amp, a
borrowed bass. The link is to the *object*, and the object's owner may have had nothing to do with
the record — which is why custody (15.08) is a dependency and why the discography accrues to the
gear, not to the person.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is capturing credits for a finished session (domain 02) — the moment the platform exists to be present for.
2. The same flow asks which gear was used; the studio's Deluxe and the session player's Jazz are already registered and in the room.
3. Producer attaches them. No extra admin, no separate visit, no reconstruction — the lazy path is the correct path.
4. Links inherit the session's visibility. The record is unreleased, so nothing is public yet.
5. Release day: the credits go public and the gear discography goes with them. The Deluxe's passport now says it played on a real record — and the studio's page says so too.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Session is unreleased or NDA'd | Link exists, hidden, inheriting the credit's visibility | Nothing public — see DT-01 |
| Gear owner wants a link the Producer didn't attest | Cannot self-assert | Can *request* attestation; the Producer confirms or doesn't. The request is not itself a link and renders nothing public |
| Gear used wasn't the player's | The normal case | Link is to the object; custody (15.08) explains who had it |
| Gear later sold (15.01.03) | Discography stays with the object; the seller keeps it in their gear CV (15.04) | Both are true — the object played on it, and they owned the object at the time |
| Producer misremembers which amp | Attestation was honest but wrong; the Producer retracts and re-attests through domain 02's correction path (D-04) | Wrong link disappears from the discography; correct object's passport updates. No self-assertion is opened — only the attesting Producer can correct |
| Credit is disputed (domain 02) | Link inherits the dispute and is **suppressed** while unresolved — failing safe toward non-disclosure (D-05) | Public surfaces show nothing for that link until the dispute resolves; on resolution the link reappears or stays gone per the outcome |
| Two Producers on a co-production both attach the same gear→session | Deduplicated to one link; a single object-on-session fact is idempotent regardless of who attests it | One entry, not two. Attestation provenance records both, display shows one |
| Gear record deleted/deregistered while it carries a discography | The session facts live in domain 02, not on the gear record; deletion cannot erase a credit. The gear identity is tombstoned, not vaporised | The credit still names the object; the object's own passport is gone or archived per 15.01 deletion rules |
| Owner sets their collection to private (15.04) after a link went public | Owner privacy narrows *their* surface; it cannot retract an already-public credit — the link never widens but also cannot un-widen a fact the session already published (CX-07) | Gear hidden from the owner's collection view; the public session credit still shows the object played on it |
| Session predates the platform | Nearly all of them | `[PENDING — owner decision, Q-01/Q-02 → /create-prd]` — the cold-start problem in its harshest form; a product decision, not a mechanism gap |
| Famous instrument, inflated claims | The value incentive (15.05.01 DT-03) | Attestation requirement is the mitigation; self-assertion would make this a fabrication engine. Repeated bad-faith attestation is a Domain 24 trust concern |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A gear discography is a harmless "fun fact" surface | It reads as trivia — which record did this guitar play on | ❌ REJECTED — **it is a confidentiality leak vector.** A gear link points at a session, and sessions are routinely confidential: unreleased records, ghost production, NDA'd work, credits an artist hasn't chosen to disclose. A passport page rendering "this amp was used on [unreleased project]" discloses the project's existence, and the gear's owner — who may have had nothing to do with it — caused the leak. Worse, it's a back door: someone could infer an artist's unreleased collaborations by watching gear pages. The link must **inherit the credit's visibility and never widen it**, and the hidden-link count must not be disclosed either, because "3 more sessions, hidden" leaks that undisclosed sessions exist. |
| DT-02 | The gear's owner should attach gear to sessions | They're the one who cares, and it's their gear | ❌ REJECTED — **the owner has a direct financial incentive to inflate.** A provenance premium is real (15.05.01 DT-03), so "my Tele played on that record" is worth money, and self-assertion makes the feature a fabrication engine that inflates the exact claims the valuation is told not to price. The **Producer** is the honest source: they ran the room, they know what was actually used, and their attestation is worth more precisely because they have no stake in the object's resale value. This is the platform's counter-attestation thesis (personas.md: the Producer is a *trust broker*; their attestation beats a self-claim) applied to objects — and it means the feature is structurally dependent on the Producer persona, not merely on domain 02's data. |
| DT-03 | This is the domain's unique-to-WeJammin capability | Asserted by the domain rationale — worth testing rather than accepting | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it survives the test. Reverb has gear identity and no sessions. Jaxsta, Muso.AI and Sound Credit have sessions and no gear identity — they know a person played on a record, not which object did. Neither can build this, and neither can retrofit it: gear discography requires being present when the gear was in the room, which is exactly problem-statement.md's "absence at the point of truth". It is also the causal link the thesis claims — consolidation (13's gear identity + 07/02's sessions) is the *precondition*, and the provenance is what compounds. That said, it inherits the thesis's cold-start problem in its harshest form: it can only ever describe sessions that happened on the platform, so its value is zero at launch and accrues only with time. That's an honest cost of a real moat, but it should not be sold as a launch feature. |
| DT-04 | Correcting a mis-attested gear link risks reopening self-assertion (the door DT-02 closed) | Q-03 asked whether a Producer's honest-but-wrong attestation is fixable without letting owners assert links | ❌ REJECTED as a risk — **correction inherits domain 02's credit-correction path and stays Producer-gated.** Only the attesting Producer (or domain 02's dispute/correction mechanism) can retract or re-point a link; the owner's lever remains a *request*, never an assertion. So the incentive structure DT-02 established is preserved: the party who can change the record is still the party with no stake in resale value. The correction path is not a new mechanism to design here — it is the same one credits already have, projected onto the object. This makes the feature strictly downstream of domain 02's correction semantics (a hard dependency, not a parallel feature). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Links are Producer-attested, never owner-asserted | Per DT-02. The party with the incentive to inflate is not the party who gets to assert. |
| D-02 | The link inherits the credit's visibility and never widens it | Per DT-01. A gear page must not be a back door into an unreleased session. |
| D-03 | The count of hidden links is not disclosed | Per DT-01. "3 more, hidden" leaks the existence of undisclosed sessions, which is the same breach in miniature. |
| D-04 | A mis-attested link is correctable only through domain 02's credit-correction path, and only by the attesting Producer (or 02's dispute mechanism) | Per DT-04 / resolves Q-03. Keeps correction Producer-gated so it never reopens owner self-assertion. |
| D-05 | The link inherits the credit's *status*, not only its visibility: a disputed credit yields a suppressed link, failing safe toward non-disclosure | Per DT-01/DT-04. The discography is a lens on the credit graph and can never contradict or outlive it. |
