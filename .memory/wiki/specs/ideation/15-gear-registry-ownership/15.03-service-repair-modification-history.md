# Feature: Service, Repair & Modification History

> **Number**: 15.03
> **Parent**: [Gear Registry & Ownership](./gear-registry-ownership-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The dated log of work done to an object — setups, refrets, retubes, recaps, repairs and
modifications — attested by the tech who did it where that tech is on the platform. It is the
Carfax service-history analogue: the thing that makes a used instrument's condition legible to a
buyer instead of guessable, and the thing that turns "it's had a hard life" from a suspicion into
a record.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Logs work on their own gear, or — better — has it logged for them when they book a luthier through domain 05 and the completed job writes the entry. Reads a prospective purchase's service history before buying. Uncomfortably, also sees what their planned refinish will do to the record. |
| Producer | Full | Logs work on studio gear. Their history is denser than a musician's: outboard is serviced constantly, and on 40-year-old equipment the repair/modification line is genuinely undecidable. Also a frequent *attester* — a Producer confirming an amp was rebuilt in their room carries weight. |
| Operator | Full | Logs work on room assets, at volume and by many hands. Their real problem is attribution: who did what to the house Twin, and when. Feeds asset condition state (15.07.02). |
| Fan | None | No log surface. A Fan sees the service history summarised on a marketplace listing via the chain (15.01.04) — "serviced 4 times, twice tech-attested" — which is the consumer-facing value, but they never write to it. |

## Behavior

A service event attaches to a gear record: date, what was done, who did it, parts involved, and
whether the work is a repair or a modification. Events are append-only — a corrected entry is a new
event, because a service log that can be quietly rewritten is worth nothing to the buyer it exists
to inform.

Where the tech is a WeJammin service provider (domain 05), the entry is **attested** — the same
counter-attestation mechanic that makes a Producer's credit confirmation worth more than a
self-claim. Where they aren't, the owner's own entry stands unattested, which is honest and still
better than nothing.

Modifications route their identity and value consequences to 15.01.06 and 15.05 — this feature owns
the *log*, not the consequences.

### Happy Path

1. Musician books a fret dress with a luthier through domain 05.
2. Luthier completes the job and marks it done in their own service-provider flow.
3. System offers to write the work to the instrument's record — the luthier confirms what was done.
4. Entry appends, tech-attested, with no extra admin for the owner. The lazy path was the correct path.
5. Chain (15.01.04) shows an attested service event; a future buyer sees the instrument has been looked after by a named professional.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Tech is not on WeJammin (the majority) | Owner logs it themselves, unattested | Entry stands with lower weight; honest, not blocked |
| Work is repair *and* modification | Both, on old gear, constantly | `[PENDING — owner decision, Q-01 → /create-prd]` — the label follows 15.01.06's originality call |
| Owner never logs anything | Undetectable. Most work will never be logged | "No service history recorded" — explicitly not "never serviced" |
| Modification touches a serial-bearing part | Identity may fork | Routed to 15.01.06; see [gear-registry-ownership-cx.md](./gear-registry-ownership-cx.md)`#CX-03` |
| Modification affects value | Revaluation flagged, never auto-applied | Routed to 15.05.01; see `#CX-10` |
| Owner deletes an inconvenient entry before a sale | Not offered — append-only | A correction is a visible new event |
| Tech attests work on gear whose owner disputes it happened | Rare but real (billing disputes) | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security, Q-03]` — a write-permission-over-objection question |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The luthier's work-order flow belongs in this feature | It is where the work happens, so it looks like it belongs here | ❌ REJECTED — a luthier is a **service provider**, and booking, quoting, paying and reviewing them is domain 05's entire job. Building a parallel work-order flow here would duplicate the services marketplace for one trade. Domain 15 owns the *resulting record*; domain 05 owns the transaction that produces it. The boundary is clean and the integration is the interesting part: a completed 05 job should offer to write a 15 entry, which is how the entry gets attested for free. |
| DT-02 | Service history is unambiguously good for the owner | The Carfax framing | ❌ REJECTED — it is good for the *buyer* and often bad for the seller. A guitar with six truss-rod adjustments and two neck resets tells a buyer something the seller would rather they didn't know. The incentive to log is therefore **negative** at exactly the moment the record matters most. This is the feature's central adoption problem and the reason it is proposed `could` rather than `should`: unlike credits (where the contributor wants the credit) there is no party with an incentive to record the inconvenient truth. The mitigations are indirect — attested entries arriving automatically from domain 05 bookings, and rendering absence honestly. |
| DT-03 | Modifications are just service entries with a different label | Structurally true at the log level | ✅ CONFIRMED at the log level, ❌ REJECTED at the consequence level — a modification can fork identity (15.01.06) and can halve value (15.05), while a setup does neither. The log is one thing; the consequences fan out to two other features. This is why 15.01.06 exists as a separate node rather than as a section here: the identity consequences of a mod have nothing to do with logging and everything to do with what "the same instrument" means. |
| DT-04 | The registry can make people disclose modifications honestly | What the feature is nominally for | ❌ REJECTED — a seller has a direct financial incentive to omit the refinish and the platform cannot detect it. What the registry can do is make omission *legible*: "no service history recorded" must never render as "unmodified", and over time a chain with a rich attested history becomes more credible than an empty one — so disclosure is rewarded rather than concealment punished. That is a weaker claim than the Carfax analogy implies and should not be oversold. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Domain 05 owns the work-order; domain 15 owns the record | Per DT-01. Duplicating the services marketplace for luthiers would be a straightforward mistake. |
| D-02 | Append-only; corrections are new events | A service log that can be quietly rewritten before a sale is worth nothing to the buyer it exists to inform. |
| D-03 | "No service history recorded" never renders as "never serviced" | Per DT-04. Absence of a record is not evidence of absence, and conflating them rewards concealment. |
