# Feature: False-Flag Dispute & Flag Lifecycle

> **Number**: 15.02.04
> **Parent**: [Stolen Gear Registry & Recovery](./15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The state machine a theft flag lives inside — from filed, through contested, to a terminal
state — and the path by which someone accused wrongly pushes back. A flag blocks sales and marks a
person's gear in public on the strength of one party's unverified assertion, which makes it a
**weapon**; a flag nobody ever clears rots into a permanent false positive on innocent gear. Both
failures are structural, not incidental, so this feature ships *with* flagging (15.02.01), never
after it — shipping the flag without the lifecycle would make the whole sub-domain net-negative.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two very different experiences. **As reporter**: prompted periodically to reconfirm the gear is still missing, and asked to close the flag on recovery. **As accused**: sees the flag on their gear, sees that it blocks their listing (15.02.02/13), and can contest it with their own evidence (receipt, prior chain, serial photos). The accused experience is the one that has to be right — it is the defence against the weapon. |
| Producer | Full | Same two roles. Exposure skews to the **accused** side: they buy a lot of second-hand outboard whose earlier chain they cannot vouch for, so they are the persona most likely to be caught holding gear someone later flags. |
| Operator | Full | Same, plus the ambiguous case: a flag on backline whose ownership genuinely is unclear after a band dissolution or a departing engineer — not a theft at all, an ownership dispute wearing a theft flag. Routed to domain 24 / 15.01.02, not adjudicated here. |
| Fan | None | No participation in disputes; owns no registered gear in this persona (a fan who owns gear acts as a Musician). Sees only the flag's *state* on a listing, rendered by 15.02.02 — "disputed" is enough for a buyer to walk away, and is true. |

## Behavior

A flag is not a boolean. It has a lifecycle, and only some states justify blocking a sale or
marking a record. The whole design turns on two facts: **the reporter is the one party with no
incentive to ever close the flag** (rot), and **a flag is trivially weaponised as a sale-killer**
(false flag). The lifecycle answers both — it is system-driven, and it never states an allegation
as fact.

### State set — locked at Level 1 (shape D-01/D-02/D-03; exact cadence numbers → Q-02)

| State | Meaning | Blocks sale? | Renders as |
|-------|---------|--------------|------------|
| `reported` | Filed and active; no dispute yet | Yes (screening match blocks/warns) | "reported stolen" |
| `contested` | An accused party has disputed it with evidence | No hard block — renders disputed | "disputed" (never "stolen") |
| `stale` | Reconfirmation window lapsed; reporter unresponsive | No — weight degraded | "reported stolen in {year}, not reconfirmed since" |
| `withdrawn` | Reporter retracted (terminal, recorded as an event) | No | flag closed, retraction visible in chain |
| `recovered` | Gear returned / reporter confirms no longer missing (terminal) | No | theft + recovery both recorded in chain (15.01.04) |
| `resolved` | Dispute reached a terminal external outcome (terminal) | Per outcome | outcome recorded, not adjudicated by platform |

**Transition map:**

- `reported` → `contested` (accused disputes) · → `withdrawn` (reporter retracts) · → `recovered`
  (reporter confirms recovery, incl. via a reconfirmation prompt answered "no longer missing") ·
  → `stale` (reconfirmation window lapses)
- `stale` → `reported` (reporter reconfirms still missing) · → `recovered` (reporter closes) ·
  → `contested` (accused disputes a stale flag)
- `contested` → `recovered` (gear returned mid-dispute) · → `withdrawn` (reporter retracts under
  the dispute) · → `resolved` (external authority settles and a party reports it, or the matter is
  routed to domain 24 and closed there) · → `stale` (reporter stops reconfirming while contested —
  degrades further to "disputed, reporter unresponsive")

`contested` is a **stable long-run state**, not a way-station. The platform is content to render
"disputed" indefinitely, because that is the truth: two parties assert incompatible facts and the
platform cannot know which is right. There is no auto-clear (that would favour the abuser's target)
and no auto-uphold (that would favour the abuser).

**Rot is the default failure, not disputes.** A flag is filed at a moment of crisis and then
abandoned — the gear comes back, or insurance pays and the owner mentally closes it, and the flag
burns forever because nobody returns to clear it. The lifecycle is therefore **system-driven**:
periodic reconfirmation prompts, insurance-payout linkage (15.05.03), and staleness that degrades
weight rather than waiting on the reporter's goodwill. **Proposed default cadence** (numbers
pending user ratification per Q-02): first reconfirm prompt at **30 days**, then every **90 days**;
flag goes `stale` after **2 consecutive missed prompts** (~210 days of silence). Prompts back off,
never nag — the recipient is a theft victim.

**The platform is not a court.** It can hold a flag, collect evidence, and tell a buyer "two people
claim this" — it cannot determine title, and pretending otherwise means being wrong often, with
someone's instrument as the stake. This is why the dispute machinery is *shared with* ownership
claims (15.01.02): a contested claim and a contested flag are the same problem.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's stolen Tele is recovered by police three months later.
2. The scheduled reconfirmation prompt arrives: "Is this still missing?"
3. Musician answers no — recovered.
4. Flag moves to `recovered`; the chain (15.01.04) records the theft **and** the recovery as
   events — both are part of the object's history and neither is deleted.
5. Screening (15.02.02) stops matching; the gear is sellable again with an honest chain that is
   arguably *better* for having the episode in it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reporter never responds to reconfirmation | Flag → `stale` after the cadence window; weight degrades, flag is not cleared | Screening renders "reported stolen in 2026, not reconfirmed since" — the buyer decides |
| Hostile flag filed by an ex-bandmate to kill a sale | The weapon case; accused contests → `contested` | Listing renders as **disputed**, never "stolen"; both parties' positions shown, no verdict |
| Both parties have genuine claims (band split, gear bought jointly) | Not a theft — an ownership dispute wearing a theft flag; routed to domain 24 / 15.01.02 | Flag → `resolved` (routed out); the dispute continues in the ownership-claim surface, not here |
| Accused simply ignores the flag | Flag stands (`reported`); gear stays unsellable on WeJammin | Honest about the asymmetry — silence favours the reporter, which is itself abusable; see Q-04 |
| Reporter withdraws the flag | Flag → `withdrawn`; retraction is recorded as a chain event, not deleted | A re-filed-then-withdrawn-then-re-filed pattern is visible — repeated churn is itself a signal |
| Reporter withdraws under pressure or threat | Withdrawal is not proof of innocence and is not silently trusted | Withdrawal recorded; if a coercion report follows, routed to domain 24 — `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / domain 24 spec]` |
| Insurance pays out; gear later recovered | Title has usually passed to the insurer, who is not a WeJammin persona — the claimant identity changes under the flag | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec, with 15.02.01 Q-03]`: flag survives the payout, claimant reassigned to insurer where represented |
| Bad-faith flagger does it repeatedly across victims | Pattern abuse — beyond any single flag's view | Routed to domain 24 for pattern detection and consequence — `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / domain 24]`; see Q-03 |
| Transition write fails mid-dispute | No partial state; flag holds its prior state, action retried | "Couldn't update — retry"; a dropped transition must never leave a flag in an undefined state |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | A lifecycle transition is in flight | Pending indicator on the flag; prior state still authoritative until commit |
| Empty | No dispute on this flag | Flag view shows `reported` (or `stale`) only — no second party present |
| Populated | Dispute open, evidence attached from both sides | Both parties' positions and evidence, **no verdict** |
| Error | Transition failed | Retry; flag remains in its last committed state, never a half-state |
| Partial | `contested` but unresolved — the long-run normal state | "Disputed" everywhere the flag surfaces; the platform does not resolve it and says so plainly |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A theft flag is a straightforward safety feature | The intuitive read — who could object to flagging stolen gear? | ❌ REJECTED — **a flag is a weapon**. It blocks sales and publicly marks someone's gear on one party's unverified assertion. The obvious abuse is an ownership dispute in disguise: an ex-bandmate, ex-partner, or departing member flags the gear to prevent a sale, and the flag does exactly what it was designed to do while being false. No version of this feature is used only well. It needs a dispute path, an evidentiary standard, and a consequence for bad-faith flagging — shipped *with* the flag. Ratified as D-03 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | Flags will be cleared when gear is recovered | The assumption that makes the lifecycle look trivial | ❌ REJECTED — nobody clears flags. Filing is driven by crisis; clearing is driven by nothing. The owner has the gear back and no reason to think about WeJammin again; the person harmed by the stale flag is a stranger trying to sell that instrument in 2031. Left alone, the registry's false-positive rate climbs monotonically and screening becomes noise. The lifecycle must be system-driven — reconfirmation, insurance linkage, staleness decay — because relying on the reporter's goodwill is relying on the one party with zero incentive. |
| DT-03 | WeJammin should decide who is telling the truth | What both parties will demand, loudly | ❌ REJECTED — the platform has no discovery powers, no subpoena, no way to see a receipt from a 2014 cash sale in a car park. Adjudicating title means being wrong often, with someone's livelihood as the stake, and owning the liability. What the platform *can* do is hold the flag, show the evidence, and render "contested" at the point of sale — giving the buyer exactly what they need (walk away) without the platform knowing something it cannot know. Consistent with 15.01.02 D-01. Ratified as D-04 in the parent index. |
| DT-04 | `contested` should be time-boxed, auto-resolving to a default outcome if a party goes silent | The instinct to force closure so flags don't pile up in limbo | ❌ REJECTED — **there is no safe default.** Auto-clearing on reporter silence rewards the target of a real theft going quiet (and rewards an abuser who out-waits a genuine reporter); auto-upholding on accused silence rewards the false flagger. Both defaults hand a win to a party for doing nothing. The honest terminal is *no* terminal: `contested` renders "disputed" indefinitely, and the platform states that it does not and cannot resolve it. Limbo is not a bug here — it is the accurate representation of an unknowable fact. Staleness still applies (degrading weight over time), but degrading weight ≠ picking a winner. |
| DT-05 | Reconfirmation should be frequent so the registry stays fresh | Freshness is good; more prompts = fresher data | ❌ REJECTED as stated — the recipient of a reconfirmation prompt is a **theft victim**, and "is your stolen instrument still gone?" every week is re-traumatising and will train users to mute the channel, which destroys the freshness it was meant to buy. CONFIRMED in a reshaped form: **low-frequency, escalating-interval, backs-off** (proposed 30d → 90d → stale). Freshness is bought by making the prompt rare, gentle, and one-tap, not by making it loud. Exact cadence is a user decision (Q-02) because the trauma/rot trade-off is a values call, not a technical one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The flag lifecycle is system-driven, not reporter-driven | Per DT-02. The only party who can clear a flag is the only party with no reason to. |
| D-02 | Staleness degrades a flag's weight; it never silently clears it | Per DT-05. A five-year-old unreconfirmed flag is weak evidence, not *no* evidence — some gear is never recovered and the owner never stops wanting it back. Let the buyer weigh it. |
| D-03 | Contested renders as "disputed" at every surface, never as "stolen" | Per DT-01 and 15.02.02 D-01. An allegation stated as fact on a page naming a seller is defamation-shaped. |
| D-04 | `contested` has no forced terminal; the platform renders "disputed" indefinitely | Per DT-04. No auto-clear (favours the abuser's target), no auto-uphold (favours the abuser); "we don't know" is the honest and safe state. |
| D-05 | Withdrawal and re-filing are recorded as chain events, never deletions | Per DT-04/DT-05 reasoning on abuse visibility. A flag that is filed, withdrawn under pressure, and re-filed leaves a pattern that would be lost if withdrawal wiped the record. |
