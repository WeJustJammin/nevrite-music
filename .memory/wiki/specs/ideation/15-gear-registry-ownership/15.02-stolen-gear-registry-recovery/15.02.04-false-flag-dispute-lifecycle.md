# Feature: False-Flag Dispute & Flag Lifecycle

> **Number**: 15.02.04
> **Parent**: [Stolen Gear Registry & Recovery](./15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The flag's state machine — from reported, through contested, to a terminal state — and the path by
which someone accused wrongly can push back. A flag blocks sales and marks a person's gear in
public, which makes it a weapon; and a flag nobody ever clears rots into a permanent false
positive on innocent gear. Shipping flagging (15.02.01) without this feature would make the
sub-domain net-negative, which is why they are proposed together.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Two very different experiences. As a reporter: prompted periodically to confirm the gear is still missing, and asked to close the flag on recovery. As the accused: sees the flag on their gear, sees that it blocks their listing, and can contest it with their own evidence. The second experience is the one that has to be right. |
| Producer | Full | Same. Their exposure skews to the accused side — they buy a lot of second-hand outboard, so they hold gear whose earlier chain they cannot vouch for. |
| Operator | Full | Same, plus the ambiguous case: a flag on a piece of backline whose ownership genuinely is unclear after a band dissolution or a departing engineer. |
| Fan | None | No participation in disputes. They see only the flag's *state* on a listing, rendered by 15.02.02 — "disputed" is enough for a buyer to walk away, and is true. |

## Behavior

A flag has states, and only some of them justify blocking a sale or marking a record.

**Proposed lifecycle** (shape confident; exact states and transitions
`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`):

`reported` → `contested` (a party with a claim disputes it) → `withdrawn` (reporter retracts) |
`recovered` (gear returned; flag closes) | `resolved` (adjudicated or abandoned) | `stale`
(reconfirmation lapsed).

**Rot is the default failure, not disputes.** A flag is filed at a moment of crisis and then
abandoned — the gear comes back, or the insurance pays and the owner mentally closes it, and the
flag burns forever. Nobody returns to clear it because they have no remaining incentive. The
lifecycle must therefore be **system-driven**: periodic reconfirmation, insurance-payout linkage,
and staleness that degrades the flag's weight rather than waiting on the reporter's goodwill.

**The platform is not a court.** It can hold a flag, collect evidence, and tell a buyer "two people
claim this" — it cannot determine title, and pretending otherwise would mean getting it wrong,
often, with someone's instrument as the stake.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's stolen Tele is recovered by police three months later.
2. The scheduled reconfirmation prompt arrives: "Is this still missing?"
3. Musician answers no — recovered.
4. Flag moves to `recovered`; the chain (15.01.04) records the theft and the recovery as events, because both are part of the object's history and neither is deleted.
5. Screening (15.02.02) stops matching; the gear is sellable again with an honest chain that is arguably *better* for having the episode in it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reporter never responds to reconfirmation | Flag goes `stale`; its weight degrades but it is not cleared | Screening renders "reported stolen in 2026, not reconfirmed since" — the buyer decides |
| Hostile flag filed by an ex-bandmate to kill a sale | The weapon case | Accused contests; flag → `contested`; listing renders as disputed, not stolen |
| Both parties have genuine claims (band split, gear bought jointly) | Not a theft at all — an ownership dispute wearing a theft flag | Routed to domain 24 as an ownership dispute; the theft flag is the wrong instrument for it |
| Accused simply ignores the flag | Flag stands; gear stays unsellable here | Honest about the asymmetry — silence favours the reporter, which is itself abusable |
| Insurance pays out; gear later recovered | Title has usually passed to the insurer, who is not a WeJammin persona | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. See 15.02.01 Q-03 |
| Bad-faith flagger does it repeatedly | Pattern abuse | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — routes to domain 24 |
| Reporter withdraws under pressure/threat | Withdrawal is not proof of innocence | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | State transition in flight | Pending on the flag |
| Empty | No disputes on this flag | Flag view shows `reported` only |
| Populated | Dispute open, evidence attached | Both parties' positions, no verdict |
| Error | Transition failed | Retry |
| Partial | Contested but unresolved — the long-run normal state | "Disputed" everywhere the flag surfaces; the platform does not resolve it and says so |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A theft flag is a straightforward safety feature | The intuitive read — who could object to flagging stolen gear? | ❌ REJECTED — **a flag is a weapon**. It blocks sales and publicly marks someone's gear on the strength of one party's unverified assertion. The obvious abuse is an ownership dispute in disguise: an ex-bandmate, ex-partner or departing member flags the gear to prevent a sale, and the flag does exactly what it was designed to do while being entirely false. There is no version of this feature that is only used well. It needs a dispute path, an evidentiary standard, and a consequence for bad-faith flagging — shipped *with* the flag, not after. Ratified as D-03 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | Flags will be cleared when gear is recovered | The assumption that makes the lifecycle look trivial | ❌ REJECTED — nobody clears flags. Filing is driven by crisis; clearing is driven by nothing at all. The owner has the gear back and no reason to think about WeJammin again, and the person harmed by the stale flag is a stranger who will try to sell that instrument in 2031. Left alone, the registry's false-positive rate climbs monotonically forever and screening becomes noise. The lifecycle must be system-driven — reconfirmation prompts, insurance linkage, staleness decay — because relying on the reporter's goodwill is relying on the one party with zero incentive. |
| DT-03 | WeJammin should decide who is telling the truth | What both parties will demand, loudly | ❌ REJECTED — the platform has no discovery powers, no subpoena, and no way to see a receipt from a 2014 cash sale in a car park. Adjudicating title means being wrong often, with someone's livelihood as the stake, and owning the liability for it. What the platform *can* do is hold the flag, show the evidence, and render "contested" at the point of sale — which gives the buyer exactly what they need (walk away) without requiring the platform to know something it cannot know. Consistent with 15.01.02 D-01. Ratified as D-04 in the parent index. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The flag lifecycle is system-driven, not reporter-driven | Per DT-02. The only party who can clear a flag is the only party with no reason to. |
| D-02 | Staleness degrades a flag's weight; it never silently clears it | A five-year-old unreconfirmed flag is weak evidence, but it is not *no* evidence — some gear is never recovered and the owner never stops wanting it back. Let the buyer weigh it. |
| D-03 | Contested renders as contested at every surface, never as "stolen" | Per DT-01 and 15.02.02 D-01. An allegation stated as fact on a page naming a seller is defamation-shaped. |
