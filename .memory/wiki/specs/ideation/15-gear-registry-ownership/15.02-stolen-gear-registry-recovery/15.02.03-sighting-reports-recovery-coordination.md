# Feature: Sighting Reports & Recovery Coordination

> **Number**: 15.02.03
> **Parent**: [Stolen Gear Registry & Recovery](./15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Reporting that flagged gear has been seen — in a pawn shop window, on eBay, on a stage, or in the
hands of someone who bought it in good faith — and coordinating what happens next without the
platform becoming an instrument of vigilantism. This is the "recovery network" half of the sweep's
candidate, and its entire design problem is that the two people it connects are usually **both
victims**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both ends of it. Reports the sighting ("that's my mate's stolen SG on Marketplace"), and is the person who unknowingly bought stolen gear and discovers it when they try to register or sell it. Receives the notification that their own stolen gear has surfaced — the highest-emotion moment in the domain. |
| Producer | Full | Reports sightings of distinctive studio gear, which is highly identifiable in photos — a specific vintage compressor is recognisable to the small community that cares about it. |
| Operator | Full | Sees a lot of gear pass through. Well-placed to spot flagged items arriving with a band or offered as a sale. Also exposed: a venue that has bought stolen backline is the possessor. |
| Fan | Config | May submit an observation if they spot a flagged item in a listing or a photo. Gets no contact details, no case access, and no follow-up beyond an acknowledgement. Their participation ends at the report. |

## Behavior

A sighting attaches evidence to an existing flag — a link, a photo, a place, a date. It does not
mutate the flag, does not accuse anyone, and does not open a channel between the parties.

**All contact is brokered.** The reporter never receives the possessor's details and the owner
never receives the seller's. The platform relays. This is not an over-caution: the alternative is
handing a distressed person the address of someone they believe stole from them, which is a
physical-safety outcome the platform would have caused.

**The possessor is a victim until shown otherwise.** Stolen gear reaches good-faith buyers — that
is what fencing is *for*. The buyer who comes forward is simultaneously the best evidence source
(they know where they bought it, which is the lead that matters) and the person who stands to lose
an instrument they paid for. Treat them as a suspect and they will quietly relist it elsewhere and
the gear is gone for good.

### Happy Path

1. Producer spots a flagged vintage Neve module in a marketplace listing and reports a sighting.
2. System attaches the sighting to the flag; the owner is notified their gear has surfaced.
3. The seller — who bought it in good faith from a studio clearance six months ago — is contacted by the platform, not by the owner, and is told what has been reported and asked what they know.
4. Seller supplies their purchase record; the platform relays between the parties and surfaces the prior link in the chain — the studio clearance, which is the actual lead.
5. Parties reach an outcome off-platform (return, refund, police). The platform records the outcome and drives the flag toward a terminal state (15.02.04).

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The owner and the possessor should be connected directly so they can sort it out | Efficient, and what both parties will demand | ❌ REJECTED — this is a physical-safety decision, not a UX one. Connecting a person who has just been told their stolen gear is in someone's hands with that person's contact details means the platform *caused* whatever happens next. Musicians are not a uniquely calm population when their livelihood has been stolen, and the possessor is usually innocent. All contact is brokered. This also protects the honest possessor from harassment they did nothing to earn. |
| DT-02 | The possessor of stolen gear is a suspect | The intuitive framing, and how a flag hit reads | ❌ REJECTED, and getting this wrong would kill the feature outright. Stolen gear reaching good-faith buyers *is the mechanism of fencing* — the pawn shop, the clearance sale, the too-good eBay listing. A buyer who steps forward is the platform's best evidence source and its most cooperative party, and they are out real money. If the flow treats them as a thief, the rational response is to say nothing, delist, and sell it somewhere with no registry — and the gear is gone. The flow must open with "you're a victim too", because it is true and because it is the only framing that recovers gear. |
| DT-03 | Sightings should be publicly visible to crowdsource recovery | Attractive: more eyes, faster recovery, and the community (domain 03) would amplify it enthusiastically | ❌ REJECTED — a public sighting is a public accusation against a named seller who is probably innocent, broadcast to a community that will not wait for the dispute to resolve. Music scenes are small and reputational damage is immediate and permanent. The amplification that makes crowdsourcing effective is the same thing that makes a false sighting unrecoverable. Sightings are visible to the flag's parties and to moderation only. |
