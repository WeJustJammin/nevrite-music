# Feature: Theft Report & Serial Flagging

> **Number**: 15.02.01
> **Parent**: [Stolen Gear Registry & Recovery](./15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Filing "this was stolen" against a gear identity, producing the flag that every downstream
capability in the sub-domain reads. Van and venue theft is endemic in working music — the whole
back line goes in one night, and the victim is standing in a car park at 2am with a phone and no
serial numbers. The feature's real design constraint is that moment: it must work for someone in
distress, at the worst possible time, who probably never registered the gear.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Files on their own gear, usually all of it at once — a van theft is a *case*, not an item. Flags every piece in one action, ideally by selecting a case (15.10) or a rig (15.06) rather than tapping twelve records. Adds a police reference later if they ever get one. |
| Producer | Full | Files on studio break-ins. Their loss is high-value and often insured properly, so the report feeds a claim pack (15.05.03) more than it feeds recovery hope. |
| Operator | Full | Files on venue theft — which is frequently insider or semi-insider, and where the stolen item may be an asset under someone else's custody (15.08). Who is entitled to file is genuinely ambiguous here. |
| Fan | None | No standing to report gear stolen; they own no registered gear in this persona. A fan who owns gear is acting as a Musician. |

## Behavior

A report attaches to a gear identity and lights a flag. The flag is the product; the report is the
evidence behind it. Reports carry a date/place of loss, an optional police reference, and an
optional case grouping.

**The cold-registry problem is the feature's core:** almost nobody registers gear in anticipation
of theft, so the common case is reporting gear that has no record. A path must exist to mint a
record retroactively — but such a record is minted by someone who no longer has the object, from a
photo and a half-remembered serial, which makes its identity weak and its claim (15.01.02)
unverifiable. That weakness must be visible downstream, not laundered by the flag's urgency.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's van is emptied overnight; they open WeJammin on their phone.
2. They select the flight case (15.10) containing the stolen rig — twelve records flagged in one action.
3. They add date, place, and a note; police reference left blank because they haven't reported it yet.
4. Flags go live; the identities are screened against open marketplace listings retroactively (15.02.02) and any hits surface immediately.
5. A prompt offers the claim pack (15.05.03) for their insurer — the moment this domain is most valuable.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Gear was never registered | Retroactive record mint with weak identity and an unverifiable claim | "You didn't register this — we can still flag it, but tell us what you know" and the flag carries a lower evidentiary weight |
| Serial unknown | Flag on `(maker, model, era, ?)` — a partial key | Flag exists but cannot support point-of-sale screening (needs full composite key per parent D-02); honest that it is close to useless for matching until the serial is added |
| Stolen gear was under someone else's custody (15.08) | Both the owner and the holder may file — the holder often notices first — and the flag records who filed and in which capacity. The platform surfaces the flag; it never adjudicates title (parent D-04). | Flag goes live either way; a second filer on the same identity is folded into the existing flag, not a duplicate. Final standing rules deferred — see Q-01 and 15.08 Q-01. |
| Owner files, then recovers it themselves | Flag must be cleared — but they have no incentive to return and mark it recovered | See 15.02.04; system-driven reconfirmation, not user goodwill |
| Malicious flag on gear the filer doesn't own | The weapon case | Blocked at the claim layer where possible, disputed at 15.02.04 where not; flag carries the filer's identity so the dispute has a named counterparty |
| Duplicate report on already-flagged identity | Not a new flag — the report attaches to the existing flag as corroborating evidence (e.g. a second stolen-from-a-band-van claim) | "This is already reported stolen" with the option to add what you know, not a second competing flag |
| Wrong item swept into a bulk flag (case/rig selection) | An over-broad case selection flags gear that was not actually taken | Per-item review before commit; any item can be de-flagged immediately without a dispute while the report is still fresh (owner-initiated, pre-dispute) |
| Filed offline / no signal at the loss site | The 2am car-park case: the write cannot reach the server | Report is captured locally and retried aggressively (see States → Error); the flag is not considered live until the server confirms, and the user is told which items are still pending |
| Insurance pays out | The gear is still stolen, so the flag persists; the beneficiary of any recovery becomes the insurer once title passes. Level-1 behavior: flag stays live, an insurer-payout note attaches to the report. | Flag unchanged; recovery routing and formal title transfer to the insurer deferred — see Q-03 (user decision) |
| Band-owned gear stolen; band has since split | Filer must hold a role on the owning entity (domain 01) to file on its behalf; the flag records the entity, not just the individual | Flag goes live under the entity; who among ex-members controls the recovered asset is a domain-01 ownership question, and domain 01 has since answered it — **resolved (2026-07-23): the asset follows the band's recorded partnership terms, executed at dissolution; where no terms were recorded — most bands — the platform executes nothing and states the position.** Per [01.04.04](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.04-dissolution-succession.md) Behavior (*"Where the band recorded no terms, the platform executes nothing and states the position: the assets have no recorded disposition and the members must agree or litigate"*) and its D-02 (*"With no recorded terms, the platform states the position and executes nothing"*). Consistent with parent D-04 — the flag surfaces, it never adjudicates title |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Flag being written / retro-screen running | Progress across a bulk flag action |
| Empty | No reports filed | Not a browsable surface — reached from a gear record or a case |
| Populated | Flag live | Flag state on the record and on the chain (15.01.04) |
| Error | Write failed | Retry, aggressively — this is a distress moment and a dropped flag is a real loss |
| Partial | Bulk flag partially applied | Explicit per-item status; a bulk action must never silently half-succeed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Only registered gear can be flagged | Falls naturally out of "flags attach to gear records" | ❌ REJECTED — and accepting it would gut the feature. Nobody registers gear in anticipation of theft; the population of "gear stolen" and "gear registered beforehand" barely overlaps at launch. Requiring prior registration means the registry only ever flags the gear of people who were already diligent, which is a small and unrepresentative set, and it makes the cold-start problem (parent Q-01) permanent rather than temporary. A retroactive mint path is mandatory — with the resulting weak identity made visible rather than hidden. |
| DT-02 | A police report reference should be required to file | Intuitive — it raises evidentiary quality and deters malicious flags | ❌ REJECTED — most gear theft is never reported to police, or is reported days later, or produces a crime number that never arrives. Requiring it filters out the majority of genuine victims to deter a minority of bad actors, and it front-loads bureaucracy onto someone in distress. The right shape is optional-but-strengthening: a flag with a police reference carries more weight in a dispute (15.02.04) than one without, and the reference can be added whenever it materialises. |
| DT-03 | A theft report is a purely private record | The obvious privacy default | ❌ REJECTED, with a sharp edge. A theft report carries **date and place of loss**, and it is filed by someone whose collection value may be public (15.04) and whose tour dates may be public (17/18). The report itself is a signal about a person's whereabouts and losses. Worse, in aggregate, theft reports map where and when musicians get robbed — genuinely useful to musicians, and equally useful to thieves. Report visibility is a real decision, not a default. Feeds the exposure cross-cut at domain level. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Bulk flagging by case or rig is the primary path, not the item flow | Theft is a case-level event. Twelve individual flag actions at 2am in a car park is a flow nobody completes. |
| D-02 | Police reference is optional and strengthens the flag; it never gates it | Per DT-02. Gating on it filters genuine victims to deter a minority. |
