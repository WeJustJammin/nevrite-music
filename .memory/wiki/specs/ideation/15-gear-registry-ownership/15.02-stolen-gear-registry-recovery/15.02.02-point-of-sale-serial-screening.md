# Feature: Point-of-Sale Serial Screening

> **Number**: 15.02.02
> **Parent**: [Stolen Gear Registry & Recovery](./15.02-stolen-gear-registry-recovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Checking a gear identity against the stolen-flag registry at the moment a listing goes up and
again at the moment someone buys. This is the strategic peak of domain 15 and the sharpest proof
of the platform thesis: existing stolen-gear registries fail not because their data is bad but
because **nobody checks them at the point of sale** — and only a platform that is simultaneously
the registry and the marketplace can make the check automatic. Consolidation is what makes the
provenance check possible; the check is what makes the registry worth populating.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As a seller: screening runs on their listing invisibly unless it hits. As a buyer: sees the screening result on the listing and can screen gear they're buying off-platform by entering a serial. The off-platform check is the goodwill feature — it helps people WeJammin isn't monetising. |
| Producer | Full | Same, mostly as a buyer of used outboard where provenance is murky and prices are high enough to be worth a check. |
| Operator | Full | Screens backline they're acquiring, often second-hand from other venues and departing bands — a channel where stolen gear genuinely circulates. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the result on a marketplace listing. **They will over-read a "no match" as "verified safe"** — which is precisely why the wording is a locked decision (D-01) rather than a copy detail. |

## Behavior

Screening resolves the listing's gear identity via the composite key (15.01.05) and matches it
against flags (15.02.01). It runs at listing creation, re-runs when a flag is filed against an
already-open listing, and runs again at purchase — a listing may sit for weeks and the flag state
can change underneath it.

**The result's wording is the feature.** A "no match" says only "not reported stolen in
WeJammin's registry, which currently holds N records and started in 2026". It does not say
"clean", "verified", or "safe". At launch the registry is empty and every check returns no match —
the moment the feature looks most complete is exactly the moment it knows least.

### Happy Path

1. Musician lists a used Jazzmaster on domain 13.
2. System resolves the identity and screens it against the flag registry.
3. No match. Listing publishes with a passport panel showing "Not reported stolen in WeJammin's registry (registry: 41,000 records since 2026)" alongside the chain (15.01.04).
4. Fan browsing the listing reads the statement, understands its scope, and buys.
5. The sale mints a witnessed transfer (15.01.03) — enriching the chain and, over time, the registry that makes the next check worth more.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Flag hit on listing creation | Listing blocked; reporter, seller and moderation notified | Seller: "This gear is reported stolen — your listing is on hold." The reporter's identity is **not** disclosed |
| Flag hit, but the flag is contested (15.02.04) | Rendered as contested, never as "stolen" | "Ownership of this gear is disputed" — a fact; "stolen" would be an allegation stated as fact |
| Flag filed after the listing went live | Retro-screen of open listings | Listing pulled; buyer notified if a purchase is in flight |
| Flag store unreachable | Screening fails **loud** — never silently passes | "We couldn't check this listing against the stolen registry right now." A silent pass is indistinguishable from a clean result and is the domain's worst possible failure |
| Serial-less gear (15.01.05) | Screening cannot match | "This gear has no serial — we can't check it against the registry" — stated plainly rather than showing a hollow green tick |
| Composite key collision (reused serial) | Prevented by D-02's key; a bare-serial match would defame an innocent seller | — |
| Flag **lands (or resolves to active)** mid-checkout | The purchase-time re-screen is the authoritative gate — it runs at payment commit, inside the same transaction that mints the witnessed transfer (15.01.03), so a flag arriving between add-to-cart and commit aborts the commit **before** funds are captured; an authorised-but-uncaptured payment is voided | Buyer: "The status of this gear changed while you were checking out — this listing is now on hold and you have not been charged." |
| Flag moves **active → contested** mid-checkout | Not a block (contested ≠ confirmed stolen, per D-01/15.02.04) but must never complete silently — checkout pauses on an interstitial requiring explicit re-confirmation | Buyer: "Ownership of this gear became disputed during checkout. Review before continuing." Buyer must re-confirm to proceed |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Screen running at listing creation | Listing pends briefly; not published until screened |
| Empty | Registry has few records | Result states the registry's size honestly — small registry, weak signal |
| Populated | Screen returned no match | Scoped, non-absolute statement per D-01 |
| Error | Registry unreachable | Explicit "could not check", never a pass |
| Partial | Identity partially resolved (no era, no serial) | "Limited check — we matched on maker and model only" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A clean screen result can be shown as "verified clean" or a green tick | The natural UI instinct, and what every trust badge on every marketplace does | ❌ REJECTED — this is the feature's central hazard. **The registry starts empty**, so on day one every check returns no match, and the badge that looks most reassuring is the one backed by the least data. Worse than useless: it converts a buyer's own caution — the only protection they actually had — into misplaced confidence, and it hands WeJammin a liability position for an assurance it never had grounds to give. The result must be scoped to what was actually checked: "not reported stolen in WeJammin's registry (N records, since DATE)". Ratified as D-01 in the parent index. |
| DT-02 | Screening should match on serial | The obvious implementation | ❌ REJECTED — serials are unique only within maker+model+era (see 15.01.05 DT-02). A bare-serial match means an innocent seller's instrument is flagged as stolen because an unrelated instrument with a reused number was taken. A false "stolen" on a public listing that names a seller is the worst output this domain can produce — it is defamation-shaped and it destroys a legitimate sale. Must key on the composite identity. Ratified as D-02. |
| DT-03 | Screening catches thieves | The feature's implicit promise, and how it will be marketed | ❌ REJECTED as stated, and this needs saying plainly because the domain's strategic case leans on it. A competent thief will not list stolen gear under its real serial — they will file it off, mistype it, or sell it in a pawn shop for cash. What screening actually catches is **the careless thief and, far more often, the innocent downstream buyer** who is about to unknowingly resell stolen goods. That is still genuinely valuable — it is the intervention point where the honest chain gets broken and the gear surfaces — but the value must be stated as "we surface gear that resurfaces", not "we stop theft". Overselling it is what makes DT-01's false assurance dangerous rather than merely imprecise. |
| DT-04 | Federating with external registries (Stolen911, police databases) is the cold-start fix and should be a node here | Attractive: it solves parent Q-01 at a stroke | ❌ REJECTED as a product node — this is an integration decision, not a capability of this feature. It also imports someone else's data quality directly into a check that can block a seller's listing, and creates data-sharing obligations. Routed to `/create-prd` as integration architecture; retained as parent Q-02 because the *decision* is the owner's, not the agent's. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A screen failure blocks with an explicit "could not check", never a silent pass | Per the error state. A silent pass renders identically to a clean result, which means the one time the check matters is the one time nobody knows it didn't run. |
| D-02 | Off-platform serial lookup is offered even though WeJammin earns nothing from it | It is the highest-goodwill surface in the domain, it drives registry population by giving non-users a reason to search, and gating it would make the registry's value contingent on trading here — which is the hostile-lock-in failure flagged in problem-statement.md Q-02. |
| D-03 | Screening fires at **point-of-transfer, not point-of-display** | Resolves Q-03. Domain 05 (services) and 16 (venues) publish gear identities in backline/spec lists, but publishing gear is not selling it — screening a static backline list would emit flag noise with no transaction to gate and no title moving. Screening runs where title moves (13 marketplace listing + purchase) and where an Operator *acquires* second-hand backline (already in the Role Lens). A studio surfacing flagged gear it merely *uses* is a sighting (15.02.03), not a screen. |
