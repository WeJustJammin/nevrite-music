## Overview

Reporting gear as stolen, flagging its identity, screening that flag at the moment of sale, and
coordinating recovery when it surfaces. The domain rationale calls this "the highest-goodwill
unserved need in the corpus" — van and venue theft is endemic, existing registries are fragmented
and nobody checks them, and only a platform that is *also* the marketplace can check a serial at
listing time. That last clause is the whole argument: this is the domain's clearest expression of
the platform thesis, since consolidation is what makes the provenance check possible at all.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: filing a report, screening a listing, handling a
sighting, and running a flag's lifecycle through dispute are four capabilities that feed each
other constantly — screening reads flags, a screening hit produces a sighting, a sighting produces
a disputant, and every flag needs a terminal state or the marketplace fills with false positives.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Screening renders "**not reported stolen** in WeJammin's registry (N records, since DATE)", never "verified clean" | The registry starts empty. On day one every check returns no match, which reads to a buyer as an assurance the platform cannot possibly give. False assurance is worse than no check: it converts the buyer's own caution into misplaced confidence, and it is a liability position. | Deep Think DT-01 on 15.02.02 |
| D-02 | Screening keys on the composite identity `(maker, model, era, serial)`, per 15.01 D-02 | A bare-serial match would flag innocent sellers whose instrument happens to share a reused number. A false "stolen" on a listing is the worst output this domain can produce — it defames a seller and kills a legitimate sale. | Deep Think DT-02 on 15.02.02 |
| D-03 | Flags require a dispute path and a terminal state, shipped together with the flag itself | A flag with no dispute path is a weapon; a flag with no terminal state rots into a permanent false positive after the gear is recovered. Shipping flagging without 15.02.04 would be net-negative. | Deep Think DT-01/DT-02 on 15.02.04 |
| D-04 | The platform holds and surfaces flags; it does not adjudicate title | Consistent with 15.01.02 D-01. WeJammin has no discovery powers and cannot determine who owns a contested instrument. It can tell a buyer "this is contested" — which is the protection they actually need. | Node Classification Gate + domain 24 boundary |
