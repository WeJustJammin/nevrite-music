# Feature: Cases, Manifests & Carnet Source Data

> **Number**: 15.10
> **Parent**: [Gear Registry & Ownership](./gear-registry-ownership-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The physical grouping of gear into cases and containers, and the exports that grouping enables — a
packing manifest, and the source data an ATA carnet needs to get a touring rig through customs. It
is the transport counterpart to the rig (15.06.01): a rig is what works together, a case is what
travels together, and the two cut across each other. The carnet *process* belongs to domain 18;
what lives here is the registry data it consumes.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Packs gear into cases and knows exactly one useful thing about them: what's in which box. Uses the manifest for load-outs, and — critically — a case is the natural unit for reporting a van theft (15.02.01): the whole case is gone, not twelve individual items. |
| Producer | Read-only | Studio gear mostly doesn't travel, so cases are marginal to them. They see case membership on records they own that are out with someone. |
| Operator | Full | Venue and studio flight cases, hire stock going out, backline touring between rooms. They pack and ship gear that isn't always theirs (15.08). |
| Fan | None | No surface. Pure touring logistics. |

## Behavior

A case is a named container holding gear records. Case membership is **many-to-many with rig
membership** (15.06.01 DT-01): a rig spans several cases; a case holds parts of several rigs.
Neither is the parent.

Two exports:
- **Manifest** — what's in this case, for load-outs, insurance and theft reporting.
- **Carnet source data** — serial, value, weight, and **country of origin** per item, in the shape
  a customs carnet needs. The carnet itself is a legal document with an issuing body and a
  process; domain 18 owns that. This feature owns the data.

**Country of origin and per-item value are carnet-mandatory and nobody records them at
registration time** — which means the export fails at exactly the moment it's needed. Either they
are captured when gear is registered (15.01.01), or this feature is theatre.

### Happy Path

1. Musician packs a European tour: three flight cases, gear assigned to each.
2. System generates a manifest per case for the load-out.
3. For the tour they need a carnet, so they request the source-data export.
4. Export flags that four items have no country of origin and two have no value — the gaps that would have stopped the carnet at the freight agent's desk, surfaced weeks earlier when they're fixable.
5. They fill the gaps once; the data is now on the gear records permanently and the next tour's export is clean.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Country of origin unknown (the normal case) | Carnet export incomplete | Gaps shown explicitly; the export's job is to surface them early, not to produce a document that fails at customs |
| Case contains gear the user doesn't own | Backline, hired, borrowed | Custody (15.08) applies; a carnet on someone else's gear is a real legal question |
| Whole case stolen | The natural bulk-flag unit | Flag by case — see 15.02.01 D-01 |
| Case spans rigs / rig spans cases | Expected — many-to-many | Both groupings coexist |
| Gear repacked between cases constantly | Case membership is far more volatile than rig membership | `[PENDING — /create-prd-architecture, Q-03]` — a model that requires updating after every load-out will not be updated |
| Carnet needs values the owner disputes | Customs value ≠ market estimate ≠ appraisal | `[PENDING — /create-prd + legal review, Q-02]` |
| Nested cases (case inside a road case) | Containers within containers | `[PENDING — /create-prd-architecture]` — a container data-model question, same shape as Q-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | WeJammin should generate the carnet | The sweep candidate's name gestures at it, and it would be a genuinely differentiated touring feature | ❌ REJECTED — an ATA carnet is a legal customs document issued by a national chamber of commerce, with a guarantee, a bond and a process that is not a PDF render. It belongs to **domain 18** (show production and touring) if it belongs anywhere, and even there it is mostly an integration with a freight agent rather than a product surface. What domain 15 uniquely holds is the **source data** — serials, values, countries of origin, weights — which is exactly the part that is painful to assemble and which the registry already half-knows. Clean boundary, same shape as 15.06.03's rider boundary and 15.05.03's insurer boundary: **15 supplies evidence, other domains supply process.** |
| DT-02 | The carnet export is a rendering problem | It looks like one — take the data, shape it for customs | ❌ REJECTED — **it is a capture problem, and the capture happens somewhere else entirely.** A carnet mandates country of origin and per-item value. Nobody records country of origin when registering a guitar; it isn't on the registration form, it isn't interesting, and it has no other use. So the export runs, finds nothing, and the feature is theatre — a button that produces a document full of blanks at the moment someone needed it. The fix has to land upstream in 15.01.01's registration flow, which means this feature's viability depends on a decision made in a different node for a reason that only makes sense here. That is precisely the platform's capture-at-source thesis: **the moment to record the fact is when it's cheap and knowable, not when it's needed and gone.** A carnet assembled the week before a tour is the same reconstruction failure as a split sheet assembled three years late. |
| DT-03 | A case is a rig by another name | Both are groupings of gear records; deduplicating them looks like obvious hygiene | ❌ REJECTED — they answer different questions and their membership genuinely differs. A rig is **functional** (what works together in a signal chain); a case is **physical** (what travels in this box). The utility case with everyone's cables belongs to no rig; the fly rig spans three cases and a placeholder for the house amp. Their volatility differs too — a rig is stable for months while case membership changes at every load-out, which means a model forcing them together would make the stable thing as churny as the volatile one. Many-to-many, both first-class. Consistent with 15.06.01 DT-01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | This feature exports carnet **source data**; domain 18 owns the carnet | Per DT-01. A carnet is a bonded legal instrument, not a render. |
| D-02 | Cases and rigs are both first-class, many-to-many | Per DT-03. Different questions, different volatility, both real. |
| D-03 | The export's primary output is its **gap list** | Per DT-02. Surfacing the four items with no country of origin six weeks early is worth more than a document that looks complete and fails at the freight agent's desk. |
