# Feature: Asset Condition & Availability State

> **Number**: 15.07.02
> **Parent**: [Studio & Backline Asset Register](./15.07-studio-backline-asset-register-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Whether a room's asset actually works right now — the Neve's channel 7 is dead, the house Twin
needs a retube, the piano is a semitone flat and due tuning Thursday. It is the state that decides
whether an asset on a published list (15.07.03) is a promise or a lie, and it is the part of the
register that changes constantly and is observed by people who don't own it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees condition on a published backline list, to whatever depth the Operator publishes. They need "the amp works" before advancing a show, not on the day. |
| Producer | Config | **The person who discovers the fault.** The engineer in the room finds channel 7 dead at 11pm mid-session and is the only one who knows. Logs it against the asset without owning the room. This is why entity-role access (15.07.01 D-02) matters — the fault reporter is usually not the owner. |
| Operator | Full | Owns the state, resolves faults, decides what's exposed publicly. Usually the **last** to know something broke, which is the operational reality this feature is shaped around. |
| Fan | None | No surface. A fan on a venue page might see "Steinway D" but never its tuning state. |

## Behavior

Condition is time-varying state on a register line — per asset on an identity-tracked object and per
count on a quantity-tracked line (D-04): functional, degraded (works with caveats), faulty
(doesn't work), or out for service. It is opened by whoever notices and closed by a service event
(15.03) — condition and service history are the same timeline seen from the two ends.

**Availability here means "does it work", not "is it booked."** Whether the room is free on Tuesday
belongs to domains 16/17. This feature answers whether the asset can do its job when someone
arrives; the booking calendar answers whether anyone may arrive. Conflating them would drag the
booking system into the registry.

**Degraded is the important state and the hardest one.** Most real gear is not binary. "Works but
channel 7 is dead", "works but needs a retube soon", "fine unless you need the reverb tank" — this
is the actual condition of most equipment in most rooms, and a functional/broken model cannot say
any of it.

**Condition now carries two shapes, because the register does (D-04).** On an identity-tracked asset
condition is per object — *this* Neve, *this* channel — exactly as D-01 describes. On a
quantity-tracked line (15.07.01 D-03) condition attaches to the **count**: "4 practice amps — 1
faulty", with a note, and nothing that says which of the four. The grade and the note are the same
model; the referent is not. Everything downstream that pointed at a specific faulty object has to
cope with a line that cannot name one — see Q-04 and Q-05.

### Happy Path

1. Producer, mid-session at 11pm, finds channel 7 on the Neve is dead.
2. They log it against the asset in seconds — the whole feature has to survive that moment or it doesn't work at all.
3. Asset goes `degraded` with a note; the published list (15.07.03) reflects it automatically without the Operator doing anything.
4. Operator sees it next morning and books a tech (domain 05).
5. The repair completes; the service event (15.03) closes the condition and the published list clears itself.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Degraded, not broken (the normal state) | Requires more than a boolean | Condition with a note — "works except channel 7" |
| Nobody logs the fault | Undetectable; the register says fine and the room isn't | The failure this feature is meant to prevent, and it only works if reporting is near-zero effort |
| Fault on an asset with a live booking | Someone booked the room *for* that asset | Must notify the booker (domain 17), not silently update a page they've already read |
| Conflicting reports from two staff | Both are logged; neither auto-wins | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Operator suppresses a fault to keep bookings | Publishing honestly costs money in the short term | See DT-02 — real tension, and the default is the platform's decision even if the choice isn't |
| Asset breaks between advance and show | Both parties' documents were correct when exchanged | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — mirrors 15.06.03 DT-03 |
| Condition never gets closed after repair | Rots exactly like a theft flag (15.02.04 DT-02) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the service-event close is the mitigation |
| One of four identical practice amps is dead (quantity-tracked line) | Condition attaches to the count, not to a unit | "4 practice amps — 1 faulty" plus a note; nothing identifies which one, so nothing downstream can point at the failed unit. See D-04, Q-04, Q-05 |
| Fault on a quantity line that a reservation depends on | The reservation captured a gear dependency from the filter that produced it (16.03.02 D-08); on a count there is no item to match it against | `[PENDING]` — the notification contract must be respecified for quantity lines. See Q-05 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | State updating | Pending on the asset |
| Empty | No condition reported | "No known faults" — explicitly not "verified working". Nobody checked; they just didn't report |
| Populated | Condition known | State + note + who reported it + when |
| Error | Update failed | Retry — a fault report lost at 11pm never gets re-entered |
| Partial | Degraded | The most common real state; needs a note, not a flag |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Condition is binary — working or broken | The simple model, and how a listing form would put it | ❌ REJECTED — **degraded is the normal state of most gear in most rooms** and a boolean cannot express it. "Works except channel 7", "fine but needs a retube within a month", "the reverb tank's dead but nothing else is" — these are the actual conditions, and every one of them is the difference between a session that works and a session that doesn't. Forcing binary means Operators mark everything "working" (because it mostly is) and the visiting band gets the surprise anyway — the exact failure this exists to prevent, reproduced by an over-simplified model. |
| DT-02 | Operators will publish condition honestly | Assumed by the whole publication design | ❌ REJECTED as an assumption — the incentive runs the other way. Publishing "4 of 8 Neve channels are dead" costs bookings *today*; the cost of concealing it lands later, on someone else, as a show that fails. personas.md frames the stale gear list as the Operator's **accidental** anti-behaviour, and mostly it is — but there is an intentional version and the design must not assume it away. The platform cannot force honesty. What it can do is make honesty cheap (auto-derived publication so nothing has to be manually admitted) and concealment effortful. The **default** is the platform's decision even though the choice isn't — and defaults are where this is actually settled. |
| DT-03 | Availability means the booking calendar | The word's ordinary meaning in this context, and the sweep candidate said "availability" | ❌ REJECTED — two different questions with two different owners. *Does this asset work?* is registry state, observed by whoever's in the room, changing on faults and repairs. *Is this room free on Tuesday?* is a booking calendar owned by domains 16/17, changing on reservations. Merging them would pull the booking system into a gear registry and give one state machine two unrelated jobs. They meet at exactly one point — a fault on an asset someone has already booked around must notify the booker — and that is a cross-domain notification, not a shared model. Ratified as D-04 on the sub-domain index. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Condition is graded with a note, not a boolean | Per DT-01. Degraded is the normal state and a boolean forces Operators to round it to "working". **Qualified by D-04** — the graded-with-note model stands, but on a quantity-tracked line its referent is a count rather than an object. |
| D-02 | Fault reporting must be near-zero effort for non-owners | Per the happy path. The person who finds the fault is an engineer at 11pm mid-session; anything more than a few seconds and it never gets logged, and the feature is dead. |
| D-03 | "No known faults" never renders as "verified working" | Nobody inspected; they just didn't report. The same honest-absence principle as 15.03 D-03 and 15.02.02 D-01. |
| D-04 | On a **quantity-tracked line**, condition is a fact about the count — *n of m faulty*, with a note — and cannot identify which unit | Consequence of 15.07.01 D-03 / sub-domain D-06 (owner decision DQ-15.A2), which explicitly degrades D-01: the condition model now carries two shapes. Per-object grading is retained wherever the item is identity-tracked, which is the Operator's choice per item. Published counts inherit the same shape (15.07.03), and the "no known faults" rule (D-03) applies unchanged — a quantity line with no report is not an inspected line. What this cannot do is give a booking a specific failed object to point at; see Q-05. |
