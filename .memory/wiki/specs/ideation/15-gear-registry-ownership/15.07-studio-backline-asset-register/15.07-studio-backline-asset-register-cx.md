## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Org Asset Register ↔ Condition & Availability

**Relationship**: The register is an inventory — a set of assets an entity owns. Condition is
per-asset, time-varying state that decides whether the asset can do its job right now. The
distinction matters because the register is maintained rarely (on acquisition and disposal) while
condition changes constantly and is observed by people who don't own the room.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: owns both. Maintains the register; usually last to know about a fault.
- **Producer**: the engineer in the room is the one who *discovers* the fault. Config access on condition without ownership of the register is the whole reason D-02 puts access on the entity rather than the asset.
- **Musician**: sees the composite effect on a published list.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The register owns the asset; condition owns its state. Multiple staff can report conflicting conditions. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
2. **Trigger chain**: A service event (15.03) resolves a condition; a fault report opens one. Condition and service history are the same timeline seen from two ends.
3. **Permission intersection**: Entity role governs both — see D-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A fault on a booked asset must reach the Operator *and* whoever booked the room expecting it (domain 16/17).
5. **State transition conflict**: An asset marked faulty while a session is mid-booking. Who is told, and when?

### CX-02: Condition & Availability ↔ Backline Publication

**Relationship**: The most consequential pairing in the sub-domain, because it is where the
Operator's named failure mode actually lives. Publishing "Neve 1073 ×8" is technically accurate and
operationally a lie if four channels are dead — the band advances the show believing they have
eight, and the failure surfaces on the day, which is the exact scenario personas.md describes. A
published list must be a function of condition, not merely of existence.

The hard question is how much condition to expose. "Neve 1073 ×8 (4 channels currently faulty)" is
honest and costs the Operator bookings. That tension is real and is not resolvable by the platform
deciding for them — but the *default* is the platform's decision, and defaults are where this gets
decided in practice.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: bears the cost of honesty and the cost of dishonesty, at different times.
- **Musician / Producer**: the visiting party who gets surprised. They need it before advancing, not on the day.
- **Fan**: sees a public venue page; condition detail is professional noise to them.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Publication reads condition; it does not own it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Condition change → published view changes automatically. If it required an Operator action it would not happen — that's the whole failure.
3. **Permission intersection**: Can an Operator publish an asset while suppressing its condition? `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see 15.07.03 Q-01. This is the crux.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A condition change on an asset someone has *already booked around* must notify them (domain 17), not just silently update a page.
5. **State transition conflict**: Asset breaks between advance and show. The band's spec sheet (15.06.03) and the room's list were both correct when exchanged and are both wrong now.

### CX-03: Org Asset Register ↔ Backline Publication

**Relationship**: Publication is a filtered projection of the register — select which assets are
public, at what detail, and the public list is thereafter *derived*. There is no second list to
maintain, therefore no second list to rot. This is the structural answer to the stale-gear-list
problem and the strongest argument for the sub-domain existing at all: without it, domain 16 can
carry a free-text gear list and this whole sub-domain is optional (see Q-01).

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: chooses what's public and at what detail; never maintains a duplicate.
- **Producer**: may configure on the entity's behalf.
- **Musician**: reads the derived list and can trust its freshness in a way they cannot trust a hand-maintained one.
- **Fan**: sees the public slice on a venue page.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None by construction — one store, one derived view.
2. **Trigger chain**: Register change → published view updates. Immediate. An asset sold or disposed of vanishes from the listing without anyone remembering to remove it.
3. **Permission intersection**: Serial is withheld regardless of publication choice, per D-03. That is not the Operator's decision to make — the exposure is created by the platform.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A material change (the piano is gone) should reach anyone with a live booking, not merely update a page nobody re-reads.
5. **State transition conflict**: Asset removed from the register while a booking depends on it. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 15.07.02 Asset Condition | 15.02 Stolen Gear Registry (cross-level, considered here) | Considered because both are "something is wrong with this asset" states on one record, and unifying them looked tidy. Rejected — they are independent state machines with nothing shared. A dead channel is a maintenance fact with a repair path; a theft flag is a legal-adjacent allegation with a dispute path. An asset can be faulty and stolen, faulty and present, fine and stolen. Merging them would force one state machine to model both a repair queue and an ownership dispute. |
| R-02 | 15.07.01 Org Asset Register | 15.06 Rig Profile (cross-level, considered here) | Considered: a room's assets look like a rig — ordered gear in a signal chain. Rejected as a *pairing at this level*: the register is an ownership inventory (what the entity owns) while a rig is a functional configuration (what works together for a job), and the Operator is explicitly read-only across 15.06 because they are the compatibility check's **target**, not its author. The genuine coupling is register → publication → oracle target, which is recorded at the domain level as CX-10. |
