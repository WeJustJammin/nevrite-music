# Feature: Org-Owned Asset Register

> **Number**: 15.07.01
> **Parent**: [Studio & Backline Asset Register](./15.07-studio-backline-asset-register-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The inventory of gear owned by an organisation — a studio, venue, rehearsal room or band — rather
than by a person. An identity-tracked asset here is the same gear record primitive (15.01.01) with
an entity owner; commodity stock instead goes in as a quantity-tracked line, which carries a count
and no serial identity (D-03). It exists because personas.md establishes that studios and bands own things independently of their
members: the studio owns the console, the band owns the PA, and neither is any individual's
personal collection.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads a room's published backline (15.07.03) as a visiting player. Never sees the full register — a room's internal inventory, values and serials are none of a visiting band's business. |
| Producer | Config | The engineer who works in a room but doesn't own it. Adds assets, logs faults, corrects the register — because they're the person who actually knows what's in there — without owning the business. Access comes from their role on the **entity**, not from any relationship to the assets. |
| Operator | Full | Their business inventory. Registers assets in bulk on acquisition, values them for business insurance (15.05.03), disposes of them, and decides what's published. High item count, low per-item drama. |
| Fan | None | No surface at all. |

## Behavior

An org asset register is gear records whose owner is an entity. Everything from 15.01 applies —
serial identity, provenance chain, claim strength — but the *access model* differs: many people
touch these assets and none of them personally own any.

**Access is a role on the entity, not a permission on the asset.** A studio with 200 assets and 6
staff cannot administer per-asset permissions, and would not: the register would be abandoned
within a month. Staff have a role on the studio; the role governs the assets.

The register is maintained rarely — assets arrive and leave, and years pass in between. Condition
(15.07.02) is the part that changes, and it changes constantly.

**Two tracking modes (D-03).** An identity-tracked asset is one object with the full 15.01 record
behind it — serial identity, provenance chain, service history. A quantity-tracked line is a count of
interchangeable commodity stock — 40 mic stands, 200 cables, four practice amps — with no serial
identity, because per-object identity for those buys nothing and costs registration effort that the
Operator will not spend twice. The Operator chooses the mode per item, and keeps identity-tracking
wherever the object is distinguishable, valuable or theft-prone; the platform does not decide for
them. The mode a line is in changes what condition can say about it (15.07.02 D-04) and what
publication states (15.07.03).

### Happy Path

1. Operator sets up their studio entity (domain 01) and bulk-registers the room's gear — identity-tracked wherever the object is distinguishable, valuable or theft-prone, quantity-tracked lines for the commodity stock (D-03).
2. Assets carry serials where they exist; the older half doesn't have them and routes through 15.01.05.
3. Their engineer — a Producer with a role on the studio entity — corrects two entries and adds the outboard the Operator forgot, because they're the one who uses it.
4. Register carries value for business insurance (15.05.03) and condition state (15.07.02).
5. A filtered slice publishes to the room's listing on domain 16 (15.07.03) — model names only, no serials.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Gear in the room is on loan, not owned | Custody ≠ ownership | Routed to 15.08; the register must not silently claim it — the engineer's own mics living in a studio are not the studio's asset |
| Staff member leaves | Their entity role ends; their contributions stay | Register unaffected; entity role revoked |
| Entity dissolves (studio closes, band splits) | The org is never deleted (01.02.02 D-03), so the register survives it; dissolution disposes of the assets per the recorded terms and executes nothing where there are none (01.04.04 D-01/D-02) | Register intact and read-only against a `closed`/`dissolved` entity; assets with no recorded disposition are stated as such, never auto-assigned. See Q-03 |
| Commodity gear (cables, stands, 4 identical amps) | Registered as a **quantity-tracked line** — a count, not per-object identity | A count with count-level condition (15.07.02 D-04); the Operator may still identity-track any individual item that is distinguishable, valuable or theft-prone. See D-03; sub-domain Q-03 resolved |
| Operator has no org entity (sole trader, bedroom studio) | The register's owner is an entity (see Behavior) and access is a role on it (D-02) — there is nothing to own the assets | Blocked: no register, therefore no publication path either (15.07.03). Structural, not an onboarding cost — see sub-domain Q-04 and 16.03.02 DT-12 |
| Bulk registration of 200 assets | Nobody photographs 200 serials | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the onboarding cost may exceed the value; see Q-01 |

| Dealer stock (consignment) | Org-held, not org-owned; and dealers have no persona | See sub-domain Q-02 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An org asset register is just a collection (15.04) with a different owner | Structurally near-true, and the reason the sweep listed them separately looked like duplication | ✅ CONFIRMED at the entity level, ❌ REJECTED as a reason to merge — the *entity* difference drags three things behind it that a personal collection has none of: a multi-person access model (D-02), a publication path to a public room listing (15.07.03), and condition state observed by people who don't own the asset (15.07.02). None of those exist for a musician's collection. The primitive is shared; the sub-domain is not. This is why 15.04 stayed a feature and 15.07 became a sub-domain. |
| DT-02 | Asset access should be permissioned per asset | The precise, secure-looking answer | ❌ REJECTED — unadministerable at real scale. A studio with 200 assets and 6 staff would need 1,200 permission decisions, so the register gets abandoned or everyone gets blanket access and the model was theatre. Access is a role on the **entity** — you work here, so you can touch the room's gear. This matches how studios actually operate and it is the only version anyone would maintain. Ratified as D-02 on the sub-domain index. |
| DT-03 | Every asset deserves serial-keyed identity | Inherited unexamined from the domain's premise | ❌ REJECTED for commodity stock — a rehearsal room with 40 mic stands, 200 cables and four interchangeable practice amps gains nothing from per-object identity, and the registration cost is real while the benefit is zero (nobody flags a stolen cable, insures a stand individually, or cares which XLR played on what). Identity earns its keep where the object is distinguishable, valuable, or theft-prone. The register likely needs a **quantity-tracked** mode alongside the identity-tracked one, which is a genuine model addition rather than a UI concession. Escalated as Q-03 on the sub-domain index. **Ratified 2026-07-22 (DQ-15.A2)**: quantity-tracked mode confirmed, with identity-tracking retained per item where the Operator wants it — D-03 here, D-06 on the sub-domain index, D-09 at the domain level; sub-domain Q-03 resolved. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The register uses the same gear record primitive as personal collections | Per DT-01. A studio's Neve and a musician's Tele are the same kind of object; only the owner and the access model differ. **Qualified by D-03** — the shared primitive governs identity-tracked assets; a quantity-tracked line is a count and carries no `(maker, model, era, serial)` key (domain D-05, D-09). |
| D-02 | Access is an entity role | Per DT-02. Per-asset permissioning is unadministerable and would be abandoned. |
| D-03 | The register has **two tracking modes**: identity-tracked assets and quantity-tracked lines | Per DT-03, ratified by the owner (DQ-15.A2). A quantity-tracked line records a count of interchangeable commodity stock and carries no serial identity; identity-tracking is retained per item wherever the Operator wants it, and is where the object is distinguishable, valuable or theft-prone. The Operator chooses per item — the mode is not derived by the platform. Consequences: condition on a quantity line is a fact about the count, not about a unit (15.07.02 D-04), and publication states counts (15.07.03). |
