# Feature: Custody, Loans & Consignment

> **Number**: 15.08
> **Parent**: [Gear Registry & Ownership](./gear-registry-ownership-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the 14-lens sweep's candidate list

## Overview

Who is physically holding an object right now, as a fact distinct from who owns it. A guitar in a
studio's live room may be owned by the studio, on loan from the engineer, borrowed from a bandmate,
left with a tech for a refret, or sitting on consignment with a dealer — and in every case
"possession" and "ownership" answer different questions with different consequences. The registry
that conflates them gets theft reports, insurance, rig definitions and returns all subtly wrong.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides constantly. Lends an amp to a bandmate and never sees it again; borrows a guitar for a session; leaves a bass with a tech for three weeks. Sees what they own but don't hold, and what they hold but don't own — the second list is the one that surprises people. |
| Producer | Full | Their room accumulates other people's gear. An engineer typically holds several musicians' instruments at any time, plus gear on long-term loan from friends, plus their own in someone else's room. The studio-as-gear-graveyard is a well-known phenomenon and it is entirely a custody problem. |
| Operator | Full | Room backline is routinely a mix of owned, hired, on-loan and abandoned. Their register (15.07.01) is wrong the moment it assumes everything in the room is theirs — and much of it has been there so long nobody remembers whose it is. |
| Fan | None | No gear stake in this persona. Custody is professional bookkeeping with no consumer surface. |

## Behavior

Custody is a state on a gear record: who holds it, since when, why, and when it is expected back.
It is orthogonal to ownership (15.01.02) — either can change without the other.

**Custody reasons** each carry different semantics:
- **Loan** — lent to a person, expected back. The most common and the most likely to be forgotten.
- **Service** — with a tech (domain 05); returns when the work completes (15.03).
- **Consignment** — a dealer holds it to sell it. Interacts with domain 13: who may list it?
- **Hire** — commercially rented in or out.
- **In transit** — sold and shipped; ownership has moved and possession hasn't (15.01.03).
- **Room-resident** — living in a studio or venue that doesn't own it.

**Why this matters, concretely.** The holder is the person who notices the gear is gone — but the
owner is the one with standing to report it stolen (15.02.01). The owner is insured, but the
holder had it when it burned. The rig (15.06.01) contains gear held not owned. The asset register
(15.07.01) lists gear in the room that belongs to someone else. Every one of these is wrong if
custody doesn't exist as a concept.

### Happy Path

1. Musician lends their spare Twin to a bandmate for a tour.
2. They mark it out on loan, to that person, expected back in six weeks.
3. Record shows owned-but-not-held; the bandmate sees held-but-not-owned. Both facts are true and neither is ownership.
4. Six weeks pass. The system asks the obvious question nobody ever asks out loud: is that amp back?
5. It isn't. It's in a lock-up in Leeds. But now everyone knows where it is, which is the entire feature.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Who may set/change custody? | Permission boundary. The owner asserts custody ("I lent this to X"); the holder may confirm or dispute but cannot unilaterally claim custody of gear that isn't theirs | Owner: "Mark out — to whom, why, expected back?". Holder: "[Owner] says you're holding this — confirm / dispute". Unconfirmed custody shows as owner-asserted, not mutually agreed |
| A holds an amp; A marks it returned, but B (owner) still doesn't have it | Concurrent/contested update — the two ends disagree about the physical fact | Both parties see "custody disputed"; the record holds *both* claims rather than silently picking one. Resolution is human, not automatic — the platform's job is to make the disagreement visible, not adjudicate it |
| User marks themselves holder of gear they don't own, to fabricate possession | Abuse — a self-asserted custody claim as a step toward a fraudulent listing or theft cover | Self-asserted custody carries no rights. Listing rights (DT-03) require custody **granted by the owner**, not merely claimed. A one-sided custody claim is inert until the owner confirms |
| Holder notices it's stolen; owner has standing to report | The split-standing problem | `[PENDING — User decision, Q-01]` — see 15.02.01 Q-01. The person who knows can't file; the person who can file doesn't know. Who gets standing (holder / owner / both) is a product call, not an implementation detail |
| Loan with no return date (the normal case) | Most loans are open-ended | Expected-return optional; a nudge after a long silence (see DT-04), not a deadline |
| Holder wants to sell gear they don't own | Consignment (legitimate) or theft (not) | Listing on domain 13 must check custody + granted selling rights, not just ownership — see DT-03 / D-02 |
| Gear on loan is stolen from the holder | Who is liable? Who claims? | `[PENDING — User decision, Q-03 → /ideate-validate]` — the insurance-liability question is genuinely hard; the platform surfaces it and must not pretend to answer it |
| Custody never updated (gear returns, nobody says) | Rots exactly like theft flags (15.02.04 DT-02) | Resolved at Level 1 — see DT-04: a decaying-confidence nudge, no auto-expiry. Deep return-flow detail is `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |
| Sold and shipped | Ownership moved, custody hasn't | Both states shown; "sold, in transit" is a real and distinct state |
| Room-resident gear nobody remembers the owner of | The studio graveyard | Honest unknown-owner state; the register must not silently absorb it as the room's |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Custody updating | Pending on the record |
| Empty | Owner holds their own gear (the default) | No custody state shown — the common case needs no UI at all |
| Populated | Custody differs from ownership | "With [holder] since [date]" on the owner's view; "Held, owned by [owner]" on the holder's |
| Stale | No confirmation/interaction past the decay window (proposed 90d, DT-04) | "With [holder] — last confirmed [date]"; a single nudge to both ends. Never auto-reverts |
| Disputed | The two ends disagree about the physical fact (returned vs still held) | Both claims shown side by side; "custody disputed — confirm who has it". Resolution is human |
| Error | Update failed | Retry |
| Partial | Holder unknown ("it's somewhere") | Honest — "not in my possession, holder unknown" is a real and useful state |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership implies possession, so the registry only needs ownership | The unexamined assumption behind the entire sweep candidate list — which is why this node was missing from it | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection is why this feature exists. **Custody and ownership are orthogonal**, and in working music they diverge constantly: gear lives in studios that don't own it, on loan with bandmates, with techs for weeks, on consignment with dealers, in transit after a sale. Conflating them corrupts every downstream fact. Theft reporting breaks (the holder notices, the owner has standing — 15.02.01 Q-01). Insurance breaks (whose policy covers a guitar that burned in someone else's studio?). Rigs break (they contain gear you don't own — 15.06.01 DT-02). The asset register breaks (a room's gear isn't all the room's — 15.07.01). Each of those was independently discovered in a different feature; that they all trace to one missing concept is the evidence this is a node and not a field. |
| DT-02 | The natural theft-report trigger is the owner | 15.02.01's design assumes it | ❌ REJECTED — **the holder is the person who notices.** If a studio is robbed, the engineer discovers a musician's guitar is gone, and that musician might not know for weeks. If a bandmate's van is emptied, the borrowed amp's owner is at home, unaware. The person with the information and the person with standing to file are routinely different people, and the registry is the only thing that knows they're connected. This is a genuine capability the platform uniquely has — and 15.02.01 currently cannot express it. Recorded there as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | Custody is bookkeeping with no marketplace consequence | It looks like an internal state | ❌ REJECTED — **consignment is custody with selling rights, and it is the case where getting this wrong is expensive.** A dealer holding stock to sell doesn't own it; a thief holding stolen gear also doesn't own it. If domain 13 checks *ownership* to decide who may list, legitimate consignment is blocked; if it checks *possession*, theft is licensed. The listing check must be custody-aware — "held with selling rights granted by the owner" is the state that authorises a listing, and it is neither ownership nor mere possession. Also flags the dealer-persona gap: personas.md Q-01 asks whether a dealer is a distinct persona, and consignment is the strongest evidence that the four-persona model has a real hole here. |
| DT-04 | Custody rot needs hard expiry — a loan that's silently sat 6 months should auto-return | The obvious fix for stale custody (Q-02) | ❌ REJECTED at Level 1 — **auto-expiry manufactures false facts the same way an ownership-implies-possession model does.** The gear has not physically moved just because a timer elapsed; flipping custody back to the owner would assert a location that may be wrong, which is the exact corruption this feature exists to prevent. Instead: custody carries a **confidence that decays with silence** — no interaction, no confirmation, no related event for a threshold window (proposed 90 days, tunable) drops it to "stale, last confirmed [date]" and fires a single low-friction nudge ("Is the Twin still with [holder]?") to *both* ends. Confidence is a display and prompt signal, never an automatic state change. This resolves Q-02: **nudge, not expiry.** Exact decay curve and nudge cadence are `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Custody is a first-class state, orthogonal to ownership | Per DT-01. Four separate features independently produce wrong answers without it. |
| D-02 | Listing rights derive from custody + granted selling rights, not from ownership alone | Per DT-03. Ownership-only blocks legitimate consignment; possession-only licenses theft. |
| D-03 | Custody has no UI in the default case | Most gear is held by its owner. Surfacing a custody state on every record would tax the 95% case to serve the 5%. |
| D-04 | A self-asserted custody claim carries no rights until the owner confirms | Per the abuse edge. Prevents fabricated possession as a route to a fraudulent listing (DT-03) or theft cover. The owner is the authority on who holds their gear; the holder confirms or disputes. |
| D-05 | Stale custody decays confidence and nudges; it never auto-reverts | Per DT-04. Auto-expiry would assert a physical location that may be false — the exact corruption custody exists to prevent. |
