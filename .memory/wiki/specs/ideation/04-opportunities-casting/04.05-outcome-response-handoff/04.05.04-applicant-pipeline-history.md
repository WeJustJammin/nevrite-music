# Feature: Applicant Pipeline & History

> **Number**: 04.05.04
> **Parent**: [Outcome, Response & Handoff](./04.05-outcome-response-handoff-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The applicant's own view of everything they've gone for: what's live, what's waiting, what they won,
what they didn't. It is the mirror of the poster's queue (04.04.01), and its most important property
is that it makes ghosting *visible to the person it happens to* — "applied to 14, heard back from 3"
stops being a feeling and becomes a screen. It also carries the sweep's "outcome & response tracking"
capability from the applicant's side rather than the poster's.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The highest application volume and therefore the most to track. Their pipeline spans roles simultaneously — bass applications, mixing briefs, dep calls, a member-wanted response — which per `personas.md` is one career, not four modes. A pipeline that makes them switch context to see their own applications reproduces the fragmentation the product exists to end. |
| Producer | Full | Fewer, larger applications. Their pipeline is more about scope and deliverables than dates, and a brief they pitched three weeks ago is a real business forecast rather than a hopeful note. |
| Operator | Full | Their own applications (house work, resident slots) — low volume. Mostly they live on the poster side. |
| Fan | None | Never applies, so has no pipeline. |

## Behavior

The pipeline holds one entry per submission, live or historical:

| Entry state | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| Submitted | Waiting. **The rot state if it never changes** (CX-03) |
| Shortlisted | If posters tell (04.04.02 Q-02 — unresolved) |
| Offered | Live offer with a fuse |
| Won | Handed off; entry follows it onward (CX-05) |
| Won · cancelled | Won, but the downstream thing was later cancelled — terminal, still linked (D-04) |
| Not selected | Closed, with a reason if given |
| Filled elsewhere / withdrawn / expired | Closed |

### Happy Path

1. Applicant submits (04.03.01) or pitches (04.03.05). Entry appears.
2. Entry updates as the poster acts — dispositions land here (CX-03).
3. On a win, the entry **links onward** into 17 / 05 / 07 rather than dead-ending (CX-05).
4. History accumulates: what they went for, what they got, over time.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Entry stuck at "submitted" forever** | The ghost, rendered by our own UI. Uncomfortable — deliberately. | "Submitted 6 weeks ago · no response" — see DT-01 |
| Applicant wants to mark it closed themselves | **Refused** (04.05.01 D-04) — it would launder the poster's failure out of the data and out of their reputation (CX R-02) | Can hide, cannot close (D-01) |
| Post deleted by poster | Entry must survive — the applicant's own history is not the poster's to erase (D-03) | Entry persists, post shown as removed |
| Applicant tries to view another user's pipeline | **Refused** — a pipeline is private career telemetry (DT-02), scoped to its owner. No cross-user read path exists | 403 / "not available" — never another person's applications |
| Poster changes disposition while applicant is viewing the entry | Last write wins on the entry's state; the applicant sees the update on next load or via live push (CX-03). No merge conflict — the poster owns the disposition, the applicant only reads it | Entry state refreshes; "Not selected" replaces "Submitted" without applicant action |
| Applicant withdraws while an offer is live | Allowed — withdraw is the applicant's own act, unlike self-close. Entry moves to `withdrawn`; the live offer's fuse is released back to the poster (04.05.03) | "Withdrawn" — and the offer is gone, deliberately irreversible |
| Applicant re-applies to a post they were already `not selected` on | Permitted only if the post reopened/re-listed; otherwise blocked as a duplicate. A second entry never overwrites the first — history is append-only | Either a fresh entry, or "you already applied to this" |
| **Won entry's downstream thing is cancelled** | Entry does not dead-end at "won" and does not point nowhere: it moves to a terminal `won · cancelled` state and links to whatever record documents the cancellation (05/07/17), so the applicant's "what happened with that?" is still answered (CX-05, D-04) | "Won — later cancelled" with a link to the cancelled record. Deeper cancellation-reason surfacing: `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |
| Applicant applied to 40 things (volume) | Pipeline groups by lifecycle — **Live** (waiting) and **Rotting** shown first, **Historical** collapsed by default — and is sortable by age and by poster response rate (CX-04). Live pipeline is never buried under closed history | Triage view: what still needs attention on top, the archive one tap away |
| Pipeline becomes a rejection museum | 30 "not selected" entries is a demoralising screen for a career that is going fine. Level-1 stance: history is honest, not softened (D-02), but it is *collapsed* by default so the default screen is forward-looking. Whether it needs explicit framing is Q-02 | Closed entries live under a "History" fold, not the landing view |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Pipeline being fetched | Skeleton rows grouped by lifecycle band |
| Empty | Never applied | "Nothing yet" + board CTA |
| Live (populated) | Applications outstanding | What's waiting, how long, and the poster's response rate (CX-04) — shown first (D-05) |
| Rotting (populated) | Long-unanswered entries | Age shown plainly — the discomfort is the point (DT-01) |
| Historical (populated) | Closed entries | Outcome record, **collapsed by default** (D-05) |
| Error | Fetch failed | "Couldn't load your pipeline" + retry; never a blank screen that reads as "no applications" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The pipeline is where the platform is held to its own promise.** Every other anti-ghosting mechanism (04.05.01's gate, 04.05.02's reputation) acts on the poster. This one shows the *applicant* exactly how well those mechanisms worked — and a screen full of six-week-old "submitted" entries is the platform failing, visibly, in the user's own hands | Reasoning about who sees the failure; the candidate list frames tracking as a convenience | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it argues **against** softening the display. The temptation is to auto-hide old entries or let the applicant tidy them away, which would make the product feel better while making the problem invisible. Keeping the rot on screen is what keeps 04.05.01 honest. It is the domain's own accountability surface, and it should be uncomfortable when the domain is failing. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would notice this data is **the applicant's own career telemetry, and it belongs to them** — where they applied, what converted, which posters answer, what rate they got offered vs asked. That is exactly the material 22 (Analytics) and 23 (Career/Finance) exist to work with | Reasoning about what accumulates here; `problem-statement.md` Q-02 asks whether lock-in is earned or hostile | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a small, clean piece of evidence for the "earned" answer. A musician who leaves should take their application history with them; it is theirs, it is not commercially sensitive to the platform, and refusing to export it would be exactly the hostile lock-in that Q-02 warns against. This feature is a good test case for the portability decision because there is no argument for withholding it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Applicants **cannot self-close** entries; they can hide them | Self-closing launders ghosting out of the data and out of the poster's reputation (04.05.01 D-04). |
| D-02 | Old unanswered entries are **shown with their age**, not softened | The discomfort is the accountability surface (DT-01). Hiding it makes the product feel better and the problem invisible. |
| D-03 | Entries **survive post deletion** | The applicant's history is not the poster's to erase. |
| D-04 | Won entries **link onward**, never dead-end at "won" | The applicant's question a month later is "what happened with that?" (CX-05). A cancelled win becomes `won · cancelled`, still linked — not silently deleted. |
| D-05 | Closed history is **collapsed by default**, not deleted or softened | Reconciles honesty (D-02) with the rejection-museum problem: the default view is forward-looking (Live + Rotting), history is one tap away. Honesty without a demoralising landing screen. |
| D-06 | Applicants **can withdraw** (unlike self-close), which **releases a live offer's fuse** back to the poster | Withdraw is the applicant's own act about their own participation; it launders nothing out of the poster's record. Self-close (D-01) hides the poster's failure — a different thing. |
| D-07 | Pipelines are **owner-scoped and private** — no cross-user read path | It is personal career telemetry (DT-02); no persona has a legitimate reason to read another's applications. |
