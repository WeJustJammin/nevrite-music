## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Disposition ↔ Response Reputation

**Relationship**: Reputation here is computed from exactly one behaviour — did you answer the people
who applied to you, and how fast. That makes it unusually honest for a reputation signal: it
measures a behaviour the poster fully controls, rather than an opinion about their character or the
quality of a gig. It is also the only reputation signal in the platform that a poster can fix by
simply doing the right thing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: both sides. As poster their response rate is public; as applicant they read it before
  spending effort.
- **Producer**: same. A Producer with a bad response rate is a Producer session players stop applying to.
- **Operator**: same, and it compounds with the venue/studio reputation already accruing in 16/17 —
  which means an Operator's casting behaviour affects their booking business.
- **Fan**: read-only, and only if the board is public (04.02.01 Q-01).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Dispositions are the facts; reputation is derived. The reputation
   store is a cross-cut (sub-domain D-03) — this domain contributes a signal, it does not own a score.
2. **Trigger chain**: Disposition → signal updated. Async is fine; nothing time-critical depends on it.
3. **Permission intersection**: Response rate is public; the individual dispositions are not. The
   aggregate must not leak who was rejected — a small pool makes that reconstruction easy.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A reputation dropping should probably notify the poster before it hurts
   them, or the mechanism is punitive rather than corrective.
5. **State transition conflict**: A poster who bulk-rejects everyone to clear their obligation gets a
   perfect response rate. **The metric is gameable by doing the wrong thing quickly** — see
   04.05.02 DT-02.

### CX-02: Disposition ↔ Won-Opportunity Handoff

**Relationship**: One disposition value — `won` — is a fork in the entire product. Every other value
(rejected, withdrawn, expired, filled-elsewhere) ends the story cleanly. `won` hands off to another
domain and the opportunity stops being an opportunity: it becomes a booking, an engagement, a
session. The parent index calls this "the clean handoff" and it is where the 24-domain consolidation
bet either pays or doesn't.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their won dep call becomes a gig on their calendar with a fee attached.
- **Producer**: their won brief becomes an engagement with a scope and a deliverable.
- **Operator**: their filled slot becomes a booking against perishable inventory.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: **Who owns the entity after handoff?** The opportunity is done; the
   booking/engagement is live elsewhere. A back-reference must survive or the provenance chain
   breaks at exactly the point it becomes valuable — "this credit came from this gig came from this
   dep call" is the story the thesis is made of.
2. **Trigger chain**: Accept (04.04.03) → disposition `won` → handoff creates the downstream entity.
   **If creation fails, someone has accepted a gig that does not exist anywhere.** Compensating
   action is unclear and the failure is silent to both parties.
3. **Permission intersection**: The winner needs access to a thing in another domain they may never
   have used. A dep who accepts a gig must land in 18's advance without onboarding.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Handoff notifies the winner and, per CX-01, everyone else gets their
   disposition at the same moment — the losers' notification is triggered by the winner's success.
5. **State transition conflict**: Winner cancels after handoff. The booking exists, the opportunity
   is closed, and the shortlist was released. **Reopening a closed opportunity is a state nobody has
   modelled** and it is the single most likely real-world sequence in this whole sub-domain.

### CX-03: Disposition ↔ Applicant Pipeline

**Relationship**: The pipeline is the applicant's mirror of the poster's queue, and an
undispositioned application is its rotting state. "I applied to 14 things and heard back from 3" is
the actual lived complaint this domain exists to fix, and the pipeline is where the user can *see*
that complaint — which is precisely why the platform must not be comfortable with it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the highest application volume; the most rot.
- **Producer**: fewer, larger applications.
- **Operator**: mostly the poster side.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The same submission entity, viewed from the applicant's side. Two
   renderings, one truth — which is what makes the ghost visible to both parties.
2. **Trigger chain**: Disposition → pipeline entry closes.
3. **Permission intersection**: The applicant sees their own disposition and reason; never the
   poster's internal notes or scores (04.04.02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every disposition notifies its applicant. This is the fan-out that makes
   the domain worth using.
5. **State transition conflict**: A post closed without dispositions leaves pipeline entries orphaned.
   **The applicant sees an eternal "submitted"** — the exact ghost, now rendered by our own UI.

### CX-04: Response Reputation ↔ Applicant Pipeline

**Relationship**: Reputation's real consumer is the applicant deciding where to spend an hour.
"This poster answers 90% of applications within 3 days" is more decision-useful than any star rating
— it is a prediction about whether the effort will produce an outcome at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the main beneficiary; their time is the scarce resource.
- **Producer**: same, at lower volume.
- **Operator**: mostly on the measured side.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — read-only consumption of a cross-cut signal.
2. **Trigger chain**: None — displayed at decision time.
3. **Permission intersection**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None. Medium confidence pending validation of whether applicants
   actually use this signal or ignore it when the gig looks good enough.

### CX-05: Won-Opportunity Handoff ↔ Applicant Pipeline

**Relationship**: A won application does not end — it leaves. The pipeline entry must follow it into
17 / 05 / 07 rather than dead-ending at "won", because the applicant's question a month later is
"what happened with that?" and the answer lives in another domain now.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their pipeline is their career's application record; a win that vanishes from it is a
  gap in their own history.
- **Producer / Operator**: same, lower volume.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pipeline entry holds a reference to the downstream entity, not a copy.
2. **Trigger chain**: Handoff → pipeline entry links onward.
3. **Permission intersection**: The applicant already has access to the thing they won.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Covered by CX-02.
5. **State transition conflict**: If the downstream entity is deleted or cancelled, the pipeline
   entry points nowhere. Same family as CX-02 synthesis Q5.
