# Feature: Disposition & Close-Out Obligation

> **Number**: 04.05.01
> **Parent**: [Outcome, Response & Handoff](./04.05-outcome-response-handoff-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Every applicant gets an answer. An opportunity cannot be closed while people are still waiting on
one, and a poster who abandons a queue leaves a visible debt rather than a silence. This is the
domain's credibility bet: the parent index names Sonicbids and ReverbNation as the cautionary tale,
and their collapse had two causes — charging desperate artists to submit (killed at 04.01.03) and
never answering them. Every incumbent is still committing the second one daily.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides, and the asymmetry is the point. As applicant they are ghosted constantly — "applied to 14, heard back from 3" is their lived experience. As poster they are the ghoster, usually not from malice but from a maybe pile they never returned to (04.04.01 DT-01). The feature has to work on the same human in both directions on the same day. |
| Producer | Full | Owes dispositions on session calls and briefs. Their queues are smaller, so the obligation is cheap — and their persona already establishes them as someone who defers admin ("we'll do it later" is their named worst accidental behaviour in `personas.md`). This is that tendency, in a domain where the cost lands on someone else. |
| Operator | Full | Owes dispositions on crew and support applications, and theirs compounds — a venue with a bad response rate is one that acts stop pitching to, which directly shrinks their supply of support acts (CX-01). |
| Fan | None | Never applies, never dispositions. |

## Behavior

Disposition values, deliberately few:

| Value | Meaning | Fires handoff? |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Won | Offer accepted | ✅ Yes (CX-02) |
| Not selected | Rejected, with or without a reason | No |
| Filled elsewhere | Position went to someone else / cascade ended | No |
| Withdrawn by poster | Opportunity cancelled | No |
| Withdrawn by applicant | Applicant pulled out | No — no obligation owed |
| Expired | Applicant never answered an offer | No |

### Happy Path

1. Applicant submits (04.03.01). **An obligation now exists.** The poster owes them an answer.
2. Poster triages (04.04.01). Rejects fire dispositions immediately (04.04.01 D-02).
3. Offer accepted (04.04.03). Every remaining live candidate is dispositioned at that moment — the winner's success is what triggers the losers' answers (CX-02 synthesis Q4).
4. Post closes only when nobody is waiting.
5. Response signal updates (CX-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Poster tries to close with people waiting | **The gate.** Blocked, or nagged, depending on Q-01. | "You've got 12 people waiting. Reject all, or answer them." |
| Poster never returns at all | The obligation must reach someone who has left. A gate is useless against absence — that is exactly the abandonment case. | See Q-02. Auto-disposition on expiry is the only remaining lever, and it is a weak one. |
| Poster bulk-rejects to clear the debt | **The gate's own failure mode.** They satisfy the obligation while doing the thing it exists to prevent, and get a perfect response rate for it (CX-01 synthesis Q5). | See 04.05.02 DT-02 |
| Applicant withdrew | No obligation owed | Removed from the count |
| 400 pitches (A&R scale) | The obligation is either the mechanism's integrity or its collapse | See sub-domain Q-02 |
| Rejection reasons required | The thing posters most want to avoid writing, and the thing applicants most want. Requiring free text means bulk-rejecting becomes impossible — which is either the point or the death of the gate. | See Q-03 |
| Post expires by date with people waiting | Auto-closes, obligation survives (04.01.01) | "This closed automatically. You still owe 12 people an answer." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Owed | Submission received | Poster: obligation count. Applicant: "submitted" |
| Answered | Disposition set | Applicant: the outcome, and a reason if given |
| Debt | Post closing with people waiting | Poster: blocked or nagged (Q-01) |
| Abandoned | Poster gone, people waiting | See Q-02 — the case the feature is weakest against |
| Clear | Everyone answered | Post closes; response signal updates |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Ghosting is the incumbents' unfixed failure and the cheapest credibility win available to this domain.** The parent index diagnoses Sonicbids/ReverbNation as "the mechanic is right; charging desperate artists per submission is what destroyed their credibility" — that is half the story. The other half is that applicants paid *and* heard nothing | Reasoning about the cautionary tale the parent index names; `personas.md` Musician success criteria centre on not having to re-prove themselves, which presumes getting answers | ✅ CONFIRMED — and unlike the domain's other differentiators (04.02.03, 04.03.02) this one has **no dependency on the credit graph, no cold-start problem, and costs nothing to build**. It works on day one with an empty platform. That is a rare combination in this domain and it is why it is proposed `must` while more novel features are proposed `could`. |
| DT-02 | **A close-out gate is only honourable if closing out is cheap**, and this feature therefore depends on 04.04.01's queue design more than on its own rules. An obligation imposed on top of an expensive review is a rule people route around by not posting at all — and a poster who doesn't post is worse for the platform than one who ghosts | Reasoning from 04.04.01 DT-01; sub-domain D-01 | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as the sub-domain's dependency on triage. It also bounds the enforcement question (Q-01): a hard gate is only defensible if the queue makes dispositioning a few taps. **Enforce first, make it cheap later** is the wrong order and would kill the domain's supply side. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would immediately point out that **the gate cannot reach the case it most needs to** — abandonment. A poster who never returns cannot be blocked from closing a post they will never close. The gate catches the conscientious poster who was going to answer anyway, and misses the one who vanished | Reasoning about who a gate actually constrains | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real limit. The remaining levers are auto-disposition on expiry (honest but impersonal — "this closed without a decision") and reputation (CX-01), which only works if the poster ever returns to care. ❌ REJECTED as fully solvable — **some ghosting is unreachable by design**, and claiming otherwise would be the over-promise this domain is trying not to make. Logged as Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **A submission creates an obligation.** This is the domain's credibility bet | The half of the Sonicbids failure that every incumbent is still committing (DT-01). No dependencies, no cold start, cheap. |
| D-02 | The winner's acceptance **triggers everyone else's disposition** | One action closes the whole queue. Cheapness is what makes the obligation honourable (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Disposition values are **few and structured**, not free text | Free text makes dispositioning expensive, and expensive obligations get routed around (DT-02). Reasons are optional on top — see Q-03. |
| D-04 | Applicants **cannot self-close** their own entries | It would launder ghosting into a tidy UI and erase the poster's failure from the reputation data (CX R-02). The rot stays visible. |
