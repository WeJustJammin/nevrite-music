# Feature: Response Reputation Signals

> **Number**: 04.05.02
> **Parent**: [Outcome, Response & Handoff](./04.05-outcome-response-handoff-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A poster's answering behaviour, made public: how many applicants they respond to and how fast.
Where the close-out obligation (04.05.01) is the rule, this is the consequence — and it is the lever
that reaches posters a gate cannot, because a poster who abandons a queue can ignore a nag but
cannot ignore that acts stop applying to them. It contributes one signal to the platform's
reputation cross-cut; it does not own a score.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides. As applicant, reads a poster's response rate before spending an hour on an application — this is the most decision-useful number the board can show them (CX-04). As poster, their own rate is public, which for a bandleader who posts twice a year is a thin and possibly unfair sample. |
| Producer | Full | Same. A Producer with a bad response rate is one session players stop applying to, which quietly shrinks their talent pool — a consequence they may never connect to the cause. |
| Operator | Full | Same, and it **compounds across domains**: an Operator's casting behaviour joins the venue/studio reputation already accruing in 16 and 17. A venue that ghosts support acts is a venue with a reputation problem in its core business, which is a bigger stick than this domain could ever wield alone. |
| Fan | Read-only | Only if the board is public (04.02.01 Q-01), and only as an incidental part of a listing. A Fan has no use for a response rate — but if the board is indexed, the number is visible, and that is a decision worth making deliberately rather than discovering. |

## Behavior

Signals contributed to the reputation cross-cut:

| Signal | Why it's honest | Why it's gameable |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Response rate (% dispositioned) | Fully within the poster's control | Bulk-reject scores 100% (DT-02) |
| Median time-to-response | Rewards promptness | Bulk-reject scores instantly |
| Open obligations (people currently waiting) | Real-time, uncheatable — you either owe people or you don't | Not gameable; this is the good one |
| Post-to-hire rate | Shows whether posts are real | Punishes posters whose gig genuinely fell through |

### Happy Path

1. Poster dispositions applicants (04.05.01).
2. Signals compute and feed the reputation cross-cut.
3. Applicants see the signal on the board before applying (CX-04).
4. Posters with good behaviour attract better pools; posters who ghost don't.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Poster bulk-rejects everyone instantly** | Perfect response rate, perfect speed, zero value delivered. **The metric rewards the wrong behaviour done quickly.** | See DT-02 — this is the feature's central defect and it is not fully solved |
| Poster has posted twice, ever | A 50% rate from a sample of two is noise presented as a fact | Suppressed below the sample floor (proposed default: **≥3 posts AND ≥5 dispositioned applicants**, tunable — Q-01). Below the floor: no rate shown, only "Open obligations" if any are live |
| New poster, no history | No signal. Treated as **neutral, not suspicious** — an absent rate is honest, and flagging first-time posters as risky would chill the legitimate supply the board depends on (Q-01) | Nothing where the rate would be — an absent signal, not a bad one |
| Poster's gig genuinely fell through | They withdraw honestly and their post-to-hire rate drops for telling the truth | Withdrawal must be distinguishable from ghosting |
| Reputation drops before the poster notices | Punitive rather than corrective (CX-01 synthesis Q4) | Warn before it bites |
| Small pool lets applicants reconstruct who was rejected | The aggregate leaks the individuals (CX-01 synthesis Q3) | Suppress at small N |
| Applicants ignore the signal for a good enough gig | Likely. People apply to the gig they want regardless of the odds. | The signal may be less load-bearing than it looks — CX-04 is Medium confidence for this reason |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Listing being fetched | Skeleton where the signal will render — never a placeholder zero, which would read as a real bad score |
| No signal (Empty) | Below sample floor / no history | Nothing — not a bad score, an absent one |
| Established (Populated) | Enough history | Rate + median response time on the listing |
| Owing | Open obligations live | "3 people waiting" — the uncheatable signal (D-02) |
| Degrading | Rate falling | Poster warned before it affects them |
| Error | Signal computation unavailable | Signal omitted, listing still renders — a broken reputation signal must never block the board or invent a number |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **This reaches the case the close-out gate cannot.** 04.05.01 DT-03 establishes that a gate cannot constrain a poster who simply never returns — but reputation constrains their *next* post, which is the one moment they do come back | Reasoning from 04.05.01 DT-03 | ✅ CONFIRMED — the two features are a pair: the gate handles the conscientious poster, reputation handles the returning ghost. Neither reaches the poster who ghosts once and never comes back, and nothing can. That residue is the honest limit of the mechanism. |
| DT-02 | **The obvious metric rewards the wrong behaviour.** A poster who bulk-rejects 40 applicants in one tap gets a 100% response rate and a two-second median. They have satisfied the rule and defeated its purpose, and they now look *better* than a poster who agonised for a week and answered everyone thoughtfully | Reasoning about gaming; nothing in the candidate list contemplates reputation at all | ✅ CONFIRMED as the feature's central defect, ⚠️ **only partially mitigated**. "Open obligations" is uncheatable but only measures the present. Requiring rejection reasons (04.05.01 Q-03) makes bulk-rejection expensive, which helps — but a poster can paste the same reason 40 times. **The honest position is that this metric measures responsiveness, not respect, and it must not be presented as the latter.** Logged as Q-02. |
| DT-03 | **Reputation is a cross-cut, not this domain's property.** Seller reputation (13, 14), service-provider reputation (05), venue/studio reputation (16, 17) and trust standing (24) are the same mechanism. A poster is often also a seller and a venue, and three unrelated scores on one human is incoherent | Node Classification Gate cross-cut test | ✅ CONFIRMED — routed to the global CX. This domain contributes a **signal**; the cross-cut owns the **score**. The Operator case proves the point: their ghosting of support acts *should* affect their venue standing, and that composition is only possible if one mechanism owns it (sub-domain D-03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | This domain contributes a **signal**, the reputation cross-cut owns the **score** | Three unrelated scores on one multi-hyphenate human is incoherent (DT-03), and the Operator case requires composition across domains. |
| D-02 | **Open obligations** is the primary signal; rate and speed are secondary | It is the only uncheatable one (DT-02) — you either owe people right now or you don't. |
| D-03 | Signals **suppress below a sample threshold** | 50% from two posts is noise presented as a fact. An absent signal is honest; a noisy one is a lie with a number on it. |
| D-04 | The metric measures **responsiveness, not respect**, and must not be presented otherwise | Bulk-rejection scores perfectly (DT-02). Over-claiming what the number means is worse than a smaller claim. |
| D-05 | A new poster with no history is **neutral, not suspicious**; proposed sample floor **≥3 posts AND ≥5 dispositioned applicants** (tunable, Q-01) | Flagging first-time posters as risky would chill legitimate supply the board depends on. The floor stops a 50%-from-two-posts number being presented as fact (D-03). |
