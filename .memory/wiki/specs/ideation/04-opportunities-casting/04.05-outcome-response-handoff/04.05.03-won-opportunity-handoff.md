# Feature: Won-Opportunity Handoff

> **Number**: 04.05.03
> **Parent**: [Outcome, Response & Handoff](./04.05-outcome-response-handoff-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The moment an opportunity stops being one and becomes real work. The parent index states it
outright: "a won Opportunity CREATES a Services engagement" — and it generalises. A won dep call
creates a gig on a calendar and a line in an advance; a won brief creates an engagement with a fee;
a won session creates a project with contributors. This is where the 24-domain consolidation bet
either pays off or is revealed as 24 products sharing a login.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their won dep call lands as a gig — on the calendar, in the show's advance (18), with the fee tracked (23), and — the part that matters most — as a credit when the show happens (02). They should never re-enter a single fact they already gave the platform when they applied. |
| Producer | Full | Their won brief becomes an engagement (05) or a project (07) with scope, deliverable and fee carried across intact. Their persona's whole pain is reconstructing facts later; a handoff that drops the fee or the contributor list recreates it. |
| Operator | Full | Their filled slot becomes a booking (17) against perishable inventory, with the deposit question arriving immediately. Their double-booking pain (`personas.md`) is fixed here or not at all — the slot must actually become unsellable. |
| Fan | None | Never wins an opportunity. May eventually *see* the result — the show is on sale (19) — but that is 19's flow, not this handoff's. |

## Behavior

Where a won opportunity goes, by type:

| Won type | Becomes | In domain |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Dep / sub call | A gig on the calendar + a lineup change in the advance | 17, 18 |
| Session call | A session with a contributor | 07 |
| Brief / RFP | An engagement with scope + fee | 05 |
| Function gig | A booking | 17 |
| Crew job | An engagement + a call sheet entry | 05, 18 |
| Support slot | A slot on the bill | 17, 18 |
| Member wanted | Membership of a Band **entity** | 01 |
| Camp invitation | A project (the camp) | 07 |

### Happy Path

1. Offer accepted (04.04.03). Disposition = `won` (CX-02).
2. Handoff resolves the target domain by type.
3. Downstream entity created, carrying everything already known — date, venue, fee, scope, contributors, material refs.
4. Winner lands in the downstream domain **without onboarding** — a dep who's never used 18 must still appear in the advance.
5. A back-reference survives in both directions: this booking came from this opportunity.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Downstream creation fails** | Someone has accepted a gig that exists nowhere. Silent to both parties (CX-02 synthesis Q2). | `[PENDING]` — compensating action undecided. See Q-01. This is the feature's most dangerous failure. |
| Winner cancels after handoff | The booking exists, the opportunity closed, the shortlist released. **Reopening a closed opportunity is unmodelled** — and it is the most likely real-world sequence in the sub-domain. | See Q-02 |
| Target domain doesn't exist yet | At launch most of them won't. A won opportunity with nowhere to go is an opportunity that just... ends. | See Q-03 — the honest degradation is a message, not a broken link |
| Winner has never used the target domain | Must land there without onboarding (CX-02 synthesis Q3) | Seamless or the handoff isn't one |
| Facts drift between offer and downstream entity | The fee in the booking must be the fee in the offer, or the platform is the fragmentation it replaced | Handoff carries, never re-asks |
| Handoff crosses a fee boundary | `personas.md` names taking the deal off-platform as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour — the handoff is where that temptation crystallises | See sub-domain Q-03 — monetisation is the owner's call |
| Won, work happens, **no credit captured** | The thesis's failure mode, executed by the domain best placed to prevent it | See DT-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Offer accepted | "Setting up your gig…" |
| Handed off | Downstream entity created | The gig/engagement/booking, linked both ways |
| Failed | Creation failed | `[PENDING]` — see Q-01 |
| No target | Domain doesn't exist yet | Honest message; the opportunity closes as won and stops there |
| Reversed | Winner cancelled | `[PENDING]` — see Q-02 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **This feature is where consolidation is proved or disproved.** D-18 claims consolidation is the platform and provenance the wedge — but consolidation only means anything if the domains actually connect. If a won dep call requires the MD to go and re-enter the gig in 17 and the lineup in 18, then WeJammin is six products behind one login, which is precisely the fragmentation `problem-statement.md` describes with the tools it aims to replace | Reasoning from D-18 and the parent index's "clean handoff" claim | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it makes this feature a **test of the thesis rather than a feature of this domain**. It is also the reason the handoff must carry facts rather than link to them: a link that requires re-entry is not a handoff. |
| DT-02 | **The handoff is the platform's last chance to capture the split before work begins**, and it will walk past it unless designed not to. `personas.md` states the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as closing a project without capturing splits — "the exact failure the platform exists to prevent, committed by the persona best placed to prevent it" — and then says outright: "the design must make the lazy path the correct path" | Reasoning from D-18 and the Producer persona, extending 04.04.03 DT-02 | ✅ CONFIRMED — the handoff *is* the lazy path. Everyone is present, the terms are agreed, nobody has done any work yet and so nobody has an opinion about their own contribution. A provisional split created here, as a byproduct of the handoff, costs one tap; the same split reconstructed after the record does well costs a lawyer. ❌ REJECTED as this domain's to build — the split instrument is 09's. **This domain owns the trigger and the moment; 09 owns the machinery.** Logged as Q-04. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would ask **what happens to the handoff when the target domain doesn't exist yet** — which at launch is nearly all of them. The parent index's clean handoff assumes a 24-domain platform that will not exist for years | Reasoning about launch sequencing against D-03 (coverage over minimalism) | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real sequencing problem. The honest degradation is that a won opportunity closes as `won`, tells the parties, and stops — no broken link, no half-created entity, no promise the platform can't keep. It also means **the domain's MoSCoW `must` for this feature is conditional on at least one target domain existing**, which is a dependency the sweep did not surface. Logged as Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The handoff **carries facts**, never re-asks | A handoff that requires re-entry is not a handoff, and re-entry is the fragmentation the product exists to end (DT-01). |
| D-02 | **Back-references survive in both directions** | "This credit came from this gig came from this dep call" is the provenance chain; breaking it at the handoff breaks it exactly where it becomes valuable. |
| D-03 | The winner lands downstream **without onboarding** | A dep who has never opened 18 must still appear in the advance. |
| D-04 | When no target domain exists, **close as won and stop** | An honest dead-end beats a broken link or a half-created entity (DT-03). |
| D-05 | The split-capture trigger lives here; the instrument lives in 09 | The handoff is the lazy path, and the lazy path must be the correct path (DT-02, quoting `personas.md`). |
