# Feature: Shortlist & Multi-Reviewer Scoring

> **Number**: 04.04.02
> **Parent**: [Triage, Shortlist & Decisioning](./04.04-triage-shortlist-decisioning-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The deliberation stage: the small set that gets listened to properly, and the several people who
argue about it. Casting in music is rarely one person's call — a band picks a member together, a
venue and a promoter agree a support act, a producer defers to the artist and the label. This
feature exists because the sweep's triage candidate assumed a single reviewer, and a single-reviewer
model cannot express the most common decision structure in the industry.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Band decisions are collective, and this is where that becomes real — every member reviews the shortlisted bassists, and the band as an **entity** (domain 01, per `personas.md`) is what decides, not the person who posted. The MD or bandleader may hold a casting vote, or may not, and that is a band-governance fact the platform inherits rather than invents. |
| Producer | Full | Invites the artist and often the label into the shortlist review. Their persona is the session owner but not always the decider — a Producer shortlisting a topline writer usually needs the artist to agree, and their real role here is *convening* the decision. |
| Operator | Full | Venue and promoter review support acts together — routinely two organisations with different interests (the promoter wants a draw, the venue wants a room that empties safely). A shared shortlist across org boundaries is the normal case, not an edge one. |
| Fan | None | Never reviews. |

## Behavior

Shortlisting is expensive by design — this is the stage where audition media actually gets listened
to, evidence gets read properly, and people disagree.

| Capability | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Small set, deep review | Triage was coarse (04.04.01 D-01); this is where the real assessment happens |
| Multiple reviewers | The decision structure is usually collective |
| Scoring / notes | So disagreement is legible rather than a group chat |
| Reviewer invitation | Reviewers may not be able to see the queue — access starts at the shortlist (CX-01 synthesis Q3) |
| Hold semantics | The shortlist is held until acceptance lands (CX-02) |

### Happy Path

1. Poster advances candidates from triage (CX-01).
2. Poster invites reviewers — bandmates, the artist, the promoter.
3. Reviewers assess and score/comment. Blind un-blinds here if enabled (CX-05).
4. The group converges. Offer goes to #1 (04.04.03).
5. Shortlist is **held**, not discarded — #2 matters if #1 declines (CX-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reviewers disagree and don't converge | The normal case. The platform cannot resolve a band's argument and should not try. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — surface the disagreement, don't average it. See Q-01. |
| Scores averaged into a ranking | **Tempting and wrong.** Averaging four opinions produces a number nobody holds and hides the disagreement that matters — the bassist three people love and one hates is a different situation from the one everyone finds fine. | See Q-01 |
| Reviewer sees who else rejected a candidate | In a band, "who rejected me" is socially explosive — and bands leak (sub-domain Q-03) | `[PENDING]` |
| Held candidate takes other work | The hold has no teeth and shouldn't. But the poster's model assumes they're still there (CX-02 synthesis Q5). | Hold should expire loudly rather than rot silently |
| Invited reviewer is outside the org (promoter reviewing at a venue's shortlist) | Cross-org access — normal for Operators, and a permission boundary the domain doesn't own | Routed to 01 (orgs) + `/create-prd-security` |
| Reviewer never reviews | The decision stalls on one person. Common and infuriating. | `[PENDING]` |
| Shortlisted applicant is never told | A shortlist is not an offer; telling raises hope (CX-01 synthesis Q4) | See Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Nothing advanced | "Advance candidates from your queue" |
| Assembled | Candidates advanced | The small set with full evidence + media |
| Under review | Reviewers invited | Per-reviewer progress — including who hasn't looked |
| Diverged | Reviewers disagree | Disagreement surfaced, not averaged (Q-01) |
| Resolved | Group converges | Offer path opens (04.04.03) |
| Held | Offer out to #1 | Shortlist frozen pending acceptance (CX-02) |
| Released | Acceptance landed | Remaining candidates dispositioned (04.05.01) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The decider is usually not the poster, and often not a person.** A band decides as an entity; a venue and promoter decide jointly; a producer defers to the artist. The sweep's triage candidate assumes one reviewer with authority, which is the least common structure in music | Reasoning from `personas.md` — "bands, labels, studios, venues and agencies are first-class entities that book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members", and the Band is modelled as an entity in domain 01 | ✅ CONFIRMED — multi-reviewer is the default, not an enterprise feature. It also means **casting authority is inherited from 01's org/band governance**, not defined here. Who in a band can accept an offer on the band's behalf is a domain-01 fact this feature consumes. Logged as Q-03. |
| DT-02 | **Averaging scores is the obvious design and it destroys the information that matters.** A candidate scoring 4,4,4,4 and one scoring 5,5,5,1 average identically, and they are completely different situations — the second one has a bandmate who will resent this hire for two years | Reasoning about what a score is for in a collective decision | ✅ CONFIRMED — surface disagreement rather than collapse it. The platform's job in a band decision is to make the argument legible, not to settle it. A product that hands a band a ranked list has quietly taken a decision that belongs to them, which is exactly what `decision-classification.md` says not to do — and here it would be doing it to *users*, not just in the spec. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Multi-reviewer is the default**, not an add-on | The collective decision is the common structure in music (DT-01), not the enterprise edge case. |
| D-02 | Disagreement is **surfaced, never averaged** | Averaging destroys the information that matters and quietly takes a decision that belongs to the band (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Casting authority is **inherited from 01**, not defined here | Bands and orgs are first-class entities with their own governance (`personas.md`). This domain must not invent a second, conflicting authority model. |
| D-04 | The shortlist is **held, not discarded**, until acceptance lands | #2 exists for the case where #1 declines (CX-02). |
