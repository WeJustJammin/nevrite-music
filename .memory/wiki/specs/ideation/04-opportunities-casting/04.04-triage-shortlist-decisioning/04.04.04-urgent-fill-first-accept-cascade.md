# Feature: Urgent Fill & First-Accept Cascade

> **Number**: 04.04.04
> **Parent**: [Triage, Shortlist & Decisioning](./04.04-triage-shortlist-decisioning-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The 9pm flow. The drummer just dropped out of tomorrow's show and there is no time to triage, no
shortlist to convene, and no deliberation to be had — the ask goes down a ranked list and the first
yes ends it. This is the parent domain's "dep gigs are won in minutes" made literal, and it is the
flow that most directly displaces the WhatsApp thread the dep circuit currently runs on. It is a
peer of the review queue, not a mode of it: everything the queue is good at is worthless under this
clock.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this exists for, on both sides. As the MD at 9pm: raises the call from the show's context (04.01.01 D-02), watches it cascade in confidence order, and gets a name back in minutes rather than working a phone for two hours. As the dep: an interruption that is worth it, because it carries a gig they can actually play tomorrow. |
| Producer | Read-only | Rarely raises urgent calls — sessions reschedule where shows cannot. Sees the mechanic when depping a session player who dropped out on the day. |
| Operator | Config | Triggers fills for cancelled slots — their inventory is perishable (`personas.md`: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever), so a fast fill is directly revenue. Their cascade is pool-shaped, not name-shaped. |
| Fan | None | Never sees a dep cascade. That the drummer tomorrow is a last-minute sub is the band's information to share or not — the platform must not publish it. |

## Behavior

The cascade consumes two orderings that already exist:

| Input | Source | Contribution |
|-------|--------|--------------|
| The ranked list | 04.01.02 targeting (D-01) | The poster's human judgement — who they'd actually call |
| Material confidence | 04.02.03 | The platform's contribution — who already knows the set |
| Availability | The availability cross-cut | Who could conceivably say yes |

### Happy Path

1. Show exists; drummer drops out. MD raises a dep call **from the show's context** — date, venue, setlist and fee already known (04.01.01 D-02).
2. Cascade order resolves: the poster's ranked list, refined by material confidence.
3. Rung 1 fires — a high-urgency alert whose intrusion is justified by confidence (04.02.04 CX-05).
4. Fuse burns (a per-rung window measured in minutes, platform-defaulted by urgency tier with a hard floor — DT-04). No answer inside the fuse → the next rung fires.
5. First yes wins. Everyone still live is told immediately. Handoff fires (04.05.03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Two people accept within the same second** | The core race (CX-04). One of them was told they had a gig and now doesn't. | "Someone accepted 8 seconds before you." Immediate and honest — see DT-02. |
| Serial cascade is too slow to fill the gig | The trade that makes the race unavoidable — see DT-02 | — |
| Nobody on the list answers | Escalate a rung (04.01.02) — to network, then to the open board | "No answer from your 5 — opening to your network." |
| Nobody at all | The gig doesn't happen. **The stakes are physical.** | The failure the whole feature exists to prevent |
| Accepter is unavailable but says yes anyway | Availability is a hint, not a lock. People move things for a good gig. | Fail open (04.02.02 D-03) |
| No setlist exists | Cascade degrades to the poster's list + geo. Still useful — the ranked list is most of the value. | Honest degradation (04.02.03 D-03) |
| Accepter can't actually play the set | Material confidence overstated (04.02.03 DT-03) — and the failure surfaces on stage | The most expensive possible false positive |
| Quiet hours | Overridden **only** where confidence justifies it (04.02.04 D-02) | 11pm push for a set they know; nothing for one they don't |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Cascading | Call raised | MD: live ladder — who's been asked, who's live, who declined |
| Rung live | Alert delivered | Dep: offer with a visible fuse |
| Declined | Dep says no | Next rung immediately |
| Timed out | Fuse burned | Next rung; the dep learns they missed it |

| Exhausted | List spent | Escalate rung or fail |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **This flow contradicts the review queue and cannot be a setting on it.** First-accept-wins and deliberate comparison are opposite theories of decisioning — one optimises for speed at the cost of selection, the other for selection at the cost of speed. Burying the fast one as a flag on the slow one would hide a conflicting flow inside a feature it negates | Reasoning about candidate 03 (dep calls) against candidate 13 (review queue), which the sweep listed as unrelated peers | ✅ CONFIRMED — modelled as a peer (sub-domain D-02) with an explicit bypass (CX-03). This is also why the domain reorganised by workflow stage rather than by type: the *type* (dep call) is a field, but the *decisioning path* is genuinely forked, and only a stage-based structure can express that a fork exists. |
| DT-02 | **The double-accept race is not an edge case to engineer away — it is a deliberate trade.** Strictly serial cascades (one live offer, wait for the fuse) have no race but may be too slow to fill a gig by tomorrow; parallel rungs fill fast and guarantee that sometimes two people say yes and one is told they didn't get a gig they thought they had | Reasoning about the physics of the flow: a 5-rung serial cascade with 10-minute fuses is 50 minutes, and it's 9pm | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it reframes the problem. The question is not "how do we prevent the race" but "how many people do we ask at once, and how do we tell the loser". A losing accepter told instantly and truthfully has lost 30 seconds; one told nothing has cleared their evening for a gig that isn't theirs. **Honesty is the mitigation; prevention isn't available.** Logged as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | A domain expert would flag that **the platform's confidence can be wrong in the most expensive way here**. Every other feature's wrong answer costs someone a scroll; this one puts a player on stage who doesn't know the set, in front of an audience, at an Operator's venue | Reasoning about consequence asymmetry; 04.02.03 DT-03 established that "knows the set" is a weaker fact than it looks | ✅ CONFIRMED — which argues for confidence being **stated as evidence to a human who decides** (04.02.03 D-02) rather than as an automated ordering the MD never inspects. The MD's ranked list must outrank the platform's confidence, not the reverse. The platform proposes; the bandleader disposes. That is `decision-classification.md`'s principle applied to users rather than to the agent. |
| DT-04 | **Fuse length cannot be free poster input; it must be platform-governed with a floor (resolves Q-03).** A poster under real pressure will set a 60-second fuse and burn their entire list in five minutes, so nobody actually gets the chance to say yes — the very failure a cascade exists to avoid. But a single fixed fuse is also wrong: a 9pm-for-tomorrow call and a same-night 30-minutes-to-doors emergency are genuinely different clocks | Reasoning about the abuse Q-03 named against the physics of DT-02: a fuse below the time-to-see-and-decide guarantees an empty cascade | ✅ CONFIRMED — the platform sets the per-rung fuse from an **urgency tier** (e.g. next-day vs same-night vs imminent), with a **hard floor of ~3 minutes** — the realistic minimum for a dep to see the push, open the offer, and check the date and set before it lapses. The poster may signal *more* urgency (jump a tier), not shorten below the floor. This converts Q-03 from an open product decision into a bounded parameter: what stays open is the exact tier-to-minutes table, not whether the poster can strand their own list. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Urgent fill is a **peer of the review queue**, not a mode | Opposite decisioning theories (DT-01). |
| D-02 | The **poster's ranked list outranks platform confidence** | The wrong answer here puts someone on a stage unprepared (DT-03). The platform proposes; the bandleader disposes. |
| D-03 | The race is **accepted and mitigated by honesty**, not prevented | Serial is too slow to fill the gig (DT-02). A loser told instantly has lost 30 seconds; one told nothing has cleared their night. |
| D-04 | The dep who plays **gets the credit** | The sub is precisely who history forgets, and capturing it is the thesis (D-18) in its cheapest, clearest form. |
| D-05 | **Fuse is platform-set by urgency tier with a ~3-minute floor; the poster can escalate urgency but not shorten below the floor** | A free poster-set fuse strands the whole list (DT-04). The floor is the minimum time to see, open, and check a call before it lapses. |
