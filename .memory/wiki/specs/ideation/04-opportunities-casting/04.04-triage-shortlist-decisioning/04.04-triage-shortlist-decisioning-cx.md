## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Review Queue ↔ Shortlist

**Relationship**: Triage is cheap and fast (in/out/maybe); shortlisting is expensive and deliberate
(listen properly, compare, argue). Separating them is what makes 40 submissions tractable — and
tractability is what makes the close-out obligation (04.05.01) honourable rather than punitive
(sub-domain D-03).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: as bandleader, triages fast on a phone and shortlists properly later at home.
- **Producer**: triages on evidence, shortlists on audition media.
- **Operator**: triages on hard criteria (insurance, certs) which is genuinely binary, then shortlists on fit.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One submission, two stages. Stage is a property of the submission's
   relationship to the post, not of the submission itself — a person can be shortlisted for one
   thing and rejected for another on the same day.
2. **Trigger chain**: Triage → shortlist → offer. A triage-out is a disposition and fires 04.05.01
   immediately; **rejecting early is better for the applicant than being left in a maybe pile**,
   which inverts the usual instinct to defer.
3. **Permission intersection**: Shortlisting may invite additional reviewers (04.04.02) who could
   not see the queue. Their access starts at the shortlist, not before.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Does an applicant learn they were shortlisted? A shortlist is not an
   offer, and telling someone raises hope the poster may not want to raise. Unresolved.
5. **State transition conflict**: Reopening a triaged-out candidate after they were told no. The
   disposition already went out; un-rejecting is awkward and common.

### CX-02: Shortlist ↔ Offer & Acceptance

**Relationship**: The shortlist is not discarded when an offer goes out — it is *held*. The #2
candidate exists precisely for the case where #1 declines, and the poster's real question is "how
long do I hold #2 before I lose them to someone else's gig?" That is a hold, and holds have a cost
borne by the person being held.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: as the held candidate, they are the one paying — a soft "you're on the list" blocks
  their date while carrying no commitment. The Operator's persona note calls the equivalent an
  unreleased hold that blocks a slot they'd happily have sold; the same harm applies to a person.
- **Producer**: holds session players similarly.
- **Operator**: the persona most familiar with holds — their whole booking model is built on them.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: An offer is exclusive; the shortlist is not. Two live offers on one
   slot is the bug.
2. **Trigger chain**: Offer → accept (close everything) or decline (fall to #2). Decline must be
   fast and cheap or the whole chain stalls on someone who is never going to answer.
3. **Permission intersection**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: #1 declining notifies #2 — who may have been told nothing until now,
   and now gets an offer for a date they may have already filled.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Held candidates take other work.** The hold has no teeth and
   should not — but the poster's model assumes they're still there. This is the domain's version of
   the Operator's stale-hold problem, and it argues for holds expiring loudly rather than silently.

### CX-03: Urgent Fill ↔ Review Queue

**Relationship**: A bypass, not a mode. When the drummer drops out at 9pm for a 7pm show tomorrow,
there is no queue, no triage, no comparison — the cascade goes out in confidence order (04.02.03)
and the first yes ends it. Everything the queue is good at is worthless under that clock, and
everything the cascade is good at would be reckless for a member-wanted ad.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona this entire flow exists for. The parent index's "dep gigs are won in
  minutes" is this feature.
- **Producer**: rarely posts urgent calls; sees it when depping session players.
- **Operator**: triggers it for a cancelled slot — their perishable inventory (`personas.md`) makes
  speed worth more than selection.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Same post, same submission entity, entirely different decisioning
   path. The post's type and urgency select which path runs.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cascade rung → offer with a fuse → timeout → next rung. **Failure means the
   gig doesn't happen** — the stakes are physical, not administrative.
3. **Permission intersection**: The cascade order comes from targeting (04.01.02 D-01) and material
   confidence (04.02.03) — so an urgent fill inherits both the poster's judgement and the platform's.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every rung is an interruption, and the quiet-hours override applies
   (04.02.04 D-02). This is the flow that justifies the override existing.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Two people accept simultaneously.** The core race — see 04.04.04.

### CX-04: Urgent Fill ↔ Offer & Acceptance

**Relationship**: Each cascade rung is a real offer with a short fuse. The design question is
whether rungs overlap: strictly serial is slow and safe (one live offer, wait for the fuse); parallel
is fast and creates the double-accept race. Under a 9pm-for-tomorrow clock, serial may simply be too
slow to fill the gig, which means the race is not an edge case to be engineered away but a trade the
product has to make deliberately.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: both sides — cascading and being cascaded to.
- **Producer / Operator**: peripheral.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One slot, potentially several live offers. Whoever accepts first wins;
   the loser was told they had a gig and then didn't.
2. **Trigger chain**: See 04.04.04.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A losing accepter must be told immediately and honestly — "someone
   accepted 8 seconds before you" is survivable; silence is not.
5. **State transition conflict**: **This is the sub-domain's hardest state problem.** Detailed in
   04.04.04 DT-02.

### CX-05: Shortlist ↔ Blind Review Mode _(cross-level — detail lives in 04.03's CX)_

**Relationship**: The un-blind boundary. Blind first round, named final is the flow posters want, and
it means the evidence blind mode stripped must have survived somewhere — which is exactly the
storage trap 04.03.04 DT-03 identifies. The seam is here; the unresolved decision is there.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their anonymity ends at this boundary.
- **Producer / Operator**: as reviewers, gain identity at the moment it most affects the outcome.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Deferred — see [04.03 CX-01](../04.03-submission-audition/04.03-submission-audition-cx.md) and 04.03.04 Q-02. Medium confidence pending that decision.
