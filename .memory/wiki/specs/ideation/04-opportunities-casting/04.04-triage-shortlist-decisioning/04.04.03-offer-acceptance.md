# Feature: Offer & Acceptance

> **Number**: 04.04.03
> **Parent**: [Triage, Shortlist & Decisioning](./04.04-triage-shortlist-decisioning-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The moment a candidate becomes a hire: an offer with terms, an acceptance, and the handoff that
turns an opportunity into real work (04.05.03). It is the smallest feature in the domain and the
most consequential, because acceptance is where the platform stops describing intentions and starts
creating obligations — a booking, an engagement, a session, a fee someone is owed, a calendar that
must actually change.

Deepening found the feature's real subject, and it is not the accept button. **The offer is the
private act that binds, and every guardrail this domain built guards a public one.** 04.01.03's
compensation gate, its six mandatory facets, its versioned downward-edit machinery and its
notify-every-applicant rule all fire at *publication* — an act with many witnesses. The offer is
one-to-one, unwitnessed, and it is the terms of the offer that the candidate actually agrees to.
Everything the gate achieved is undone here unless the gate is re-run here (DT-04). That single
observation produced most of this file.

The second subject is symmetry. An offer costs the poster nothing and costs the candidate their
date (DT-06); a binding acceptance without a binding offer would make the platform extract
commitment from the party with less power (DT-11), which in music is reliably the working musician
this product centres.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides, often the same day — offers a dep gig in the morning, accepts a session in the afternoon. **The persona who pays the fuse** (DT-06): a live offer blocks their Saturday while committing the poster to nothing, which is why an offer they are one of three to receive must say so (D-08). They are also the only persona who can **double-book themselves across their own roles** — offered a dep gig for Saturday 8pm as a Musician and a session for Saturday 2–9pm as a Producer, both offers are theirs and both accepts are hers (DT-08). Accepting is the point where their calendar must actually change, or the platform double-books the person it exists to unfragment (D-03). |
| Producer | Full | Offers session and brief work. Their acceptance **instantiates any points or buyout declared at post time** — this is not this file's invention: 04.01.03 D-07 already ruled that a points/buyout declaration is *intent* at posting and the rights instrument is created **at offer acceptance**, and that re-asking the declaration here would be "the platform admitting it wasn't listening". They are also the persona `personas.md` names as the deferrer ("we'll do it later" is their worst accidental behaviour), and the type of work they offer — sessions, briefs, camps — is the type where a split actually exists, which is why the split prompt is type-scoped to them and not fired at every dep call (D-04, DT-02). |
| Operator | Full | Offers day rates, support slots and crew engagements against **perishable inventory** (`personas.md`: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever), which is why speed of acceptance is directly revenue for them and why the fuse pressure originates on their side. Theirs is the persona whose offers most often carry a **formula rather than a number** — a door split is not a fee, it is a risk position, and what binds is the arithmetic (DT-07). They are also the likeliest **poster-side canceller**: the venue pulls the show, and the dep who accepted has already turned down other work (DT-11). Their non-money terms — load-in, curfew, set length, backline provided — are terms, not colour (D-10). |
| Fan | None | Never offers, never accepts. The exclusion is **structural, not a permission**: a Fan cannot post (domain Role Matrix), so no offer can originate under a Fan account; a Fan cannot submit or pitch (04.03), so no offer can be addressed to one. There is no offer surface to conceal from them and no state to disable. |

## Behavior

The offer carries terms that were declared at post time (04.01.03, 04.01.01) but may have moved — a
negotiated rate, a changed date, an added requirement. **The offer, not the post, is what is
accepted.** That sentence is the whole feature, and it is also the hazard: it means the binding
terms live in the one artefact the domain's publication guardrails never touch.

### The offer's binding term set — and why it is not just money

The breadth pass called the offer's terms "fee shape and value, date, scope, location". That is
thin in a specific and expensive way (DT-10). An offer's terms are:

| Facet group | Contents | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Compensation — six mandatory facets** | shape · amount/basis · unit · currency · expenses · timing | 04.01.03 D-08. Not five. The gate exists because "compensation" reads as "a number" even to people trying hard not to. |
| **The engagement facts** | date + time window · location · duration (set length / session hours) · what the candidate provides (kit, transport, backline) · what the poster provides (backline, parking, food, crew) | 04.01.01's post carries them; the offer binds them. `personas.md`'s Operator names both failure directions — their worst accidental is "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show", and their success criterion is "nobody arrives expecting a backline that doesn't exist". |
| **The rights intent, if declared** | points / buyout scope | 04.01.03 D-07 — declared at post as intent, **instantiated here**, carried forward losslessly. |

**Every facet is delta-checked against the post, not just the fee** (D-10). A dep who accepts £200
and discovers they were supposed to bring a kit has been bait-and-switched as surely as one offered
£150 against a £200 post — and the second is the one 04.01.03 guards.

### The three grants — triage, review, and commit

*This answers the sub-domain CX file's CX-02 synthesis question 3 (permission intersection), which
the breadth pass left open for Step 5 deepening. The answer is resolved here in full and contributed
from this side for Step 6 to fold into the CX map; this file does not edit the CX map itself.*

| Grant | What it permits | Where it comes from | Creates an obligation? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Triage** | See and sort the queue | 01 governance; single-triager by default (04.04.01 D-08) | A reject does (04.05.01) |
| **Review** | See and score the shortlist | **Invitation** (04.04.02) — a promoter, a bandmate, the artist, the label | No |
| **Commit** | **Issue an offer** | **01's casting authority only** — never an invitation | **Yes. This is the only grant that binds the org.** |

**Reviewing is not offering.** The three sets are distinct and none contains another: an invited
reviewer may have no queue access (CX-01 synthesis Q3 — "a promoter invited to pick a support act
has no business seeing who the venue rejected"), and a reviewer with full shortlist access still
cannot commit the venue's money. Commit authority is **inherited from 01, never invented here**
(04.04.02 D-03) and never conferred by an invitation — otherwise inviting a promoter to look at
three support acts would hand them the venue's chequebook. Whether 01 actually models who may
commit a Band entity is 04.04.02 Q-03, and this file has a hole where that answer goes.

### Rules that bound every action

| Rule | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **The offer inherits the gate** | An offer re-runs 04.01.03's publication gate. All six facets mandatory; £0 is not a flat fee (04.01.03 D-09); consideration-gated applicant fees remain unrepresentable (04.01.03 D-02, domain D-03). | D-05 |
| **No silent deltas, no threshold** | Every material delta from the post is shown. Materiality is 04.01.03 D-05's definition, imported verbatim, not re-derived. **No de-minimis threshold** — "a percentage floor invites five 4% edits". | D-01, D-10 |
| **The offer is irrevocable for its fuse** | The poster cannot change their mind inside the window they set. It can still end by a cause outside their discretion — a declared cascade loss, or the opportunity ceasing to exist — and the cause is named. | D-07 |
| **Parallelism is disclosed** | A candidate who is one of N live offers is told so at issue. | D-08 |
| **Accept is never available on incomplete information** | If the delta cannot be computed, accept is disabled. This is 04.04.01 D-09 — "the irreversible call is never available on incomplete information" — applied to the other party. | D-12 |
| **Received, not tapped** | Acceptance is ordered by platform receipt, never by client tap time. | D-13 |
| **Warn, never block** | A calendar conflict at accept time is surfaced and the human decides. Availability fails open (04.02.02 D-03). | D-03 |

### Happy Path

1. **Shortlist resolves** (04.04.02) or the cascade fires (04.04.04) or — R-02's case — a single
   obvious candidate is offered straight from triage, which is a shortlist of one, not a distinct
   path.
2. **Commit authority is checked first**, before the offer is composed. A reviewer without commit
   authority never reaches a compose surface — the control is absent, not disabled, because a
   greyed-out "make offer" teaches a bandmate they nearly had the authority to spend the band's money.
3. **The offer is composed and re-gated** (D-05). All six compensation facets present; the
   engagement facts carried from the post (04.01.01); any declared points/buyout carried forward
   **without re-asking** (04.01.03 D-07).
4. **The delta is computed against the post's version the candidate submitted under** — not against
   the post's current head. A candidate who applied at v1 and is offered against v3 is owed the
   v1 delta, because v1 is what they agreed to be considered for. (Where the post moved downward
   between v1 and v3, 04.01.03 D-05 already re-gated, notified and reconfirmed them at that point;
   this delta is the offer's own drift, not a re-litigation of that.)
5. **The offer is issued with a fuse, and the fuse is disclosed to both sides** — countdown visible
   to poster and candidate alike. If parallel rungs are live (04.04.04), **the candidate is told
   they are one of N** (D-08).
6. **The candidate sees the offer against the post, deltas shown per facet** — money and non-money
   alike, no threshold. "Posted at £200, offered at £150" and "Post said backline provided; offer
   says bring your own" carry equal weight.
7. **The candidate accepts, counters, or declines** (D-06). A counter is an offer in the other
   direction: it re-runs the gate, carries its own fuse, and the other party now holds the decision.
8. **On accept, the conflict check runs before the commit** — if the candidate's calendar already
   holds something in the window, the conflict is named with its time span and they confirm through
   it (D-03). It warns; it never blocks. People move things for a good gig (04.02.02 D-03).
9. **Acceptance commits atomically with the availability update** (D-03). The candidate is busy at
   the instant they are hired, not eventually — otherwise this platform's own matching (04.02.02)
   cascades them a second gig for the same night.
10. **Everything downstream fires from one act**: the shortlist releases; every remaining candidate
    and every untriaged queue card is dispositioned (04.05.01 D-02 — "the winner's acceptance
    triggers everyone else's disposition"); the handoff creates the real thing (04.05.03); a
    declared points/buyout instantiates (04.01.03 D-07); and where the work type has a split and
    none was declared, **this is the last socially free moment to ask** (DT-02, domain D-06).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Terms drifted from the post** | The offer's terms bind (D-01) and the delta is **shown, never silently applied**. This is the bait-and-switch vector arriving one stage after 04.01.03 guards it — and per DT-04 the offer is where the gate stops looking. Materiality is 04.01.03 D-05's definition imported verbatim, **including its no-de-minimis rule**. | "Posted at £200. Offered at £150." Per facet, always, no threshold. |
| **Terms drifted on a facet that isn't money** | Same rule (D-10). A post saying backline provided and an offer saying bring your own is a material delta — it is a cost transferred to the candidate, which is exactly 04.01.03 D-05's materiality test ("increases what they bear"). | "Post said backline provided. This offer says bring your own." |
| **The offer omits a facet the post declared** | Blocked at compose (D-05). The gate does not accept five of six facets at publication and does not accept them here. | "This offer doesn't say who covers expenses. The post did." |
| **Poster offers £0 against a paid post** | £0 is not a flat fee — unpaid is a shape (04.01.03 D-09). The offer re-gates as unpaid, which is legitimate only if type-scoped (04.01.03 D-04), and against a paid post it is the largest possible delta. | "This post was published as paid. An unpaid offer can't be made against it." |
| **Poster attaches a fee to the offer ("£30 to confirm")** | Unrepresentable, not prohibited (04.01.03 D-02, domain D-03). The offer inherits the same absence from the model. **This closes the gate's most obvious workaround**: the applicant-side fee that cannot be published could otherwise be issued privately, one-to-one, to someone who has just been told they got the gig — the moment of maximum leverage over the least powerful party in the transaction. | No surface exists. |
| **The delta can't be computed** — the post's version history is unreachable | **Accept is disabled** (D-12). 04.04.01 D-09's principle applied to the other party: accepting on an uncomputable delta is agreeing to terms nobody could see. Decline stays available — it is recoverable in the only sense that matters, since a candidate who declines has lost a gig, not been bound to one. | "We can't show you how this compares to the post. You can decline, but we won't let you accept blind." |
| **Candidate declines** | Falls to #2 (CX-02) — who may have gone cold. The decline is also the poster's **only repricing signal** (D-11) and their most valuable one. | #2 gets an offer for a date they may have filled. Poster sees the structured reason if given. |
| **Candidate declines on fee** | Structured, optional, and it is the fact 04.01.03's gate most wants and never gets: the market telling the poster their number is wrong. An **upward** re-offer needs no re-gate — it reduces nothing and increases nothing the candidate bears. | Poster: "Declined — fee." Under a cascade: "4 of 5 declined on fee." |
| **Candidate never responds** | The chain stalls on someone who was never going to answer. Decline must be cheap; silence must have a fuse (D-02). | Countdown, then Expired. |
| **Two offers accepted for the same slot** | The urgent-fill race (CX-04, 04.04.04 D-03). **Prevention isn't available — honesty is the mitigation.** Ordering is by platform receipt (D-13), so the winner is the first accept the platform *received*. | Loser, immediately: "Someone accepted 8 seconds before you." Winner: the gig. |
| **A slow connection makes a late tap arrive first** | Cannot happen — D-13. Ordering is receipt, never client tap time. Otherwise a candidate on a bad train connection could retroactively win a race that was decided three minutes earlier, and the platform would have to un-hire someone. | The loser is told the truth, including the margin. |
| **Candidate taps accept while offline; it lands after the offer is gone** | Evaluated at receipt against the current state (D-13). It resolves as Superseded, Expired or Revoked — whichever actually happened — and never as a generic failure, because "it didn't go through" reads as a platform bug and sends them to the phone. | "This was filled 4 minutes ago — your accept arrived after." |
| **Candidate accepts, then cancels** | **Is acceptance a contract?** Unresolved and consequential (Q-01, DT-01). A dep who accepts and pulls out at 5pm has caused real harm and the platform recorded the promise. **What deepening adds is that Q-01 has been asked from one side only** — see the poster-cancels row. | See Q-01. |
| **Poster cancels after the candidate accepted** — the venue pulled the show | The unasked half of Q-01 (DT-11), and **more common than the candidate's cancellation**. The dep has already turned down other work on the strength of it. A design where acceptance binds the candidate and nothing binds the poster is the platform taking the poster's side against the persona it centres. | See Q-01 — which this file has widened to ask whether the obligation runs both ways. |
| **Poster withdraws a live offer because they changed their mind** | **No such button** (D-07). The offer is irrevocable for the fuse the poster themselves chose. A poster who wants the option to change their mind has one available: a shorter fuse. | The control does not exist. |
| **The opportunity itself ceases to exist mid-fuse** — the show is cancelled | The offer is Revoked, **the cause is named**, and the candidate is told the offer ended for a reason that is not about them. This is the only non-cascade exit from a live offer, and it is cause-bound rather than discretionary. | "The show was cancelled. This offer has ended — it wasn't about you." |
| **Poster claims cancellation to escape D-07's irrevocability** | The abuse vector D-07 creates, named rather than ignored. This feature's contribution is that a revocation **records its cause**, so a poster whose shows are repeatedly "cancelled" inside a live fuse produces a pattern. **Detection and consequence are 24's**, not this feature's — but the pattern only exists because the cause is recorded here. | Nothing at the time. The signal goes to 24. |
| **Candidate is one of 3 parallel cascade rungs and doesn't know** | **Foreclosed** (D-08). Without disclosure the candidate believes they hold a fuse-length option they do not hold, which is the platform lying by omission at the exact moment 04.04.04 D-03 says honesty is the whole mitigation. Disclosure also *accelerates* the cascade — a dep who knows two others are looking answers now. | "You're one of 3 people being asked right now. First yes wins." |
| **Candidate counters under a 10-minute cascade fuse** | A counter is a new offer with its own fuse (D-06), and under a cascade clock there is no time for one — **so a counter is a decline in practice** and is presented as one. It is not silently discarded: the counter's number is the decline reason, which is the fee signal (D-11) arriving from the only candidate who bothered to say why. | "There's no time to negotiate this one — countering it means passing. Your £180 will be sent as the reason." |
| **Counter-offers are forbidden and the negotiation moves to DM** | The failure D-06 exists to prevent. A dep call at £150 gets "£180 and I'll bring my own kit" — that is what the WhatsApp thread this domain displaces is *for*. If accept/decline are the only verbs, the negotiation happens off-platform and **the terms that bind are not the terms recorded**, which fails D-18 exactly where it is supposed to work: being where the work happens is the precondition for capturing anything at source. | The counter is a first-class act. |
| **Counter is used to walk the fee down after acceptance interest is shown** | The counter re-runs the gate (D-05, D-06) and every counter's delta is shown against the post — **not against the previous counter**. Otherwise a poster ratchets down in five 4% steps, each individually unremarkable, which is precisely the ladder 04.01.03 D-05's no-threshold rule was written to stop. | Every counter shows its distance from the post, not from the last counter. |
| **Acceptance doesn't update availability** | The platform double-books the person it exists to unfragment. Atomic with acceptance, not eventually-consistent (D-03, DT-03). | — |
| **Candidate accepts against a conflict on their own calendar** | **Warn, never block** (D-03). Availability fails open (04.02.02 D-03) and people move things for a good gig — but the conflict is named with its window, because the one thing the paper diary could never do is tell you. | "You have a session Saturday 2–9pm. This gig is Saturday 8pm. Accept anyway?" |
| **The multi-hyphenate accepts in two roles at once** — a dep gig as Musician, a session as Producer, same Saturday | **The double-book the platform inflicts on itself** (DT-08). Both offers are legitimately theirs; both accept paths are theirs. If availability is scoped to a context or a role rather than to the **human**, neither accept sees the other and the platform hires one person twice. `personas.md`'s central structural fact — "one human routinely occupies three of them in one day" — is a calendar fact here, not a profile fact. | The conflict warning must fire **across roles**, or it is worthless to the persona it exists for. See Q-02. |
| **Band accepts — but who in the band?** | Commit authority is 01's governance (04.04.02 D-03, Q-03), and this file consumes it rather than inventing a second model. What this file adds is that **commit is a strictly narrower grant than review** (the three-grants table): a bandmate invited to score a shortlist has not been handed the authority to accept a booking on the band's behalf. | See 04.04.02 Q-03. |
| **The offer went to a Band; the humans who play are a different set** | Recording only "The Band accepted" loses the fact the platform exists to capture (DT-09). The accepter is the **committing party**; the performing lineup is 18's advance and may legitimately not exist yet for a show six months out. This file's contribution is refusing to conflate them — acceptance never asserts the accepter is the performer. | See Q-05 and DT-09. |
| **Offer accepted, work happens, no credit captured** | The thesis's failure mode at the exact moment it is preventable (DT-02). | See DT-02, Q-03. |
| **A split prompt fires on every dep call** | **The mechanism destroying itself** (DT-02). A dep gig produces a credit, not a composition split. A prompt that fires on 40 dep calls is a prompt nobody reads by the time the writing camp arrives — so over-firing is not merely noise, it is how the platform trains its users to dismiss the single most valuable prompt it has. **Type-scoped** to the work types where a split actually exists (04.01.01's discriminator). | Nothing on a dep call. The prompt appears where a split is real. |
| **Points/buyout was declared at post and the offer re-asks it** | Forbidden. 04.01.03 D-07: re-asking is "the platform admitting it wasn't listening". The declaration carries forward losslessly and **instantiates** here — this is the moment their D-07 names, arriving on schedule. | The declared terms, carried, not a fresh form. |
| **Acceptance of a door split** | Accepting a formula, not a number (DT-07). What binds is the arithmetic — which costs come off the top, whose costs, whether the guarantee floors it. A door deal is the classic live-music dispute precisely because the label ("50% of the door") is agreed and the formula never was. | The formula, stated. If the formula is unavailable, see Q-04. |
| **The platform holds only the fee label, not the formula** | An accepted door deal is then a promise with no arithmetic behind it, and settlement (17) is a fight the platform enabled and cannot adjudicate. **Recorded as a real limit rather than papered over** — see Q-04. | — |
| **Winner never submitted** — the cascade or invite winner | Acceptance is keyed on the **offer**, not on a submission (D-14). 04.04.01 DT-07 established the mirror from the queue's side: the queue's applicants can lose to someone who never applied. The disposition machinery (04.05.01) is keyed on submissions and must tolerate a winner who has none. | Losers: "Filled elsewhere." Nothing implies the winner was in the pile. |
| **Offer expires with no answer, under a cascade** | Next rung immediately (04.04.04). The candidate learns they lost it by not answering — which is information they can act on, unlike silence. | "This expired 20 minutes ago. It's been filled." |
| **Offer expires with no answer, no cascade behind it** | Falls to #2 if the shortlist is held (CX-02, 04.04.02 D-04); if there is no #2, the post is live again with its decide-by unchanged (04.01.01) and the queue's obligation intact (04.05.01). An expiry is not a close. | Poster: "Nobody answered. 6 people are still waiting on you." |
| **The fuse is a hold and the candidate pays it** | **The asymmetry nobody designed** (DT-06). A 24-hour fuse blocks the candidate's Saturday for 24 hours while committing the poster to nothing. D-07 supplies the minimum symmetry — the poster cannot change their mind inside a window they chose — but the *length* of that window is 04.04.04 Q-03, and this file contributes the constraint that bounds it: a poster-set fuse with no ceiling lets a poster impose an unbounded free option on the candidate. | Countdown visible to both sides, from issue. |
| **Poster issues offers to 3 shortlisted candidates "to see who bites"** | Each is a real offer (D-07: each is irrevocable for its fuse), so this is three live commitments to one slot and two people are going to be told they lost a gig they were offered. Under a **declared** cascade this is the accepted trade (04.04.04 D-03, D-08's disclosure). Outside one, it is the same act without the disclosure — so the disclosure is what separates a fast fill from a lie, and D-08 is scoped to any N>1, not to the cascade feature. | Every one of the 3: "You're one of 3 people being asked right now." |
| **Candidate accepts; the handoff fails** — no booking, no engagement, nothing downstream | Someone has accepted a gig that exists nowhere. **04.05.03 Q-01's compensating-action question, and this file must not pre-empt it** — but the acceptance stands: the obligation was created by the acceptance, not by the downstream record, and un-accepting to tidy a failed write would be the platform erasing a promise a human made. | The acceptance holds. See 04.05.03 Q-01. |
| **No target domain exists for the handoff yet** | Close as won and stop — an honest dead-end beats a broken link (04.05.03 D-04). The acceptance and the availability update are still real; only the downstream entity is absent. | "Accepted." No fabricated booking. |
| **Winner cancels after handoff — does the opportunity reopen?** | **04.05.03 Q-02's question, not this file's.** The contribution from this side: the shortlist was released and the losers were told no (04.05.01 D-02), so reopening means re-approaching people who have a disposition on record — and 04.04.01 notes un-rejecting is "awkward and common". Reopening is not free; it spends credibility this domain is built on. | See 04.05.03 Q-02. |
| **Commit authority is revoked mid-fuse** — the poster is removed from the band | The offer stands. It was issued by the **org**, not by the person, and D-07 makes it irrevocable for its fuse — a band cannot un-offer a gig by reshuffling who is in the band. Governance is 01's; this feature consumes the revocation and does not adjudicate it. | Ex-member: "You no longer have access." Candidate: nothing. The offer is unchanged. |
| **The posting org dissolves mid-fuse** — the band splits | The offer's fuse runs to its end and an acceptance against it is real, because the candidate cannot be asked to track the poster's internal politics. Who then owes the fee is an 01 entity-lifecycle boundary this feature cannot assign — the same seam 04.04.01 Q-05 hit from the queue side, one stage later and now with money attached. | See Q-01's dependency on 01. |
| **The candidate's account is suspended mid-fuse** (24) | The offer is Revoked with the cause **withheld from the poster** — "no longer available" and nothing more. A moderation action is not the poster's information, and leaking it through a casting surface would make the offer flow a suspension oracle. | Poster: "This candidate is no longer available." |
| **Offer issued to a candidate who was already rejected in the queue** | Their disposition already fired (04.04.01 D-02). The offer is the un-rejection, arriving as good news, which is the only direction this reversal works in — a rejection reversed *upward* costs the applicant nothing. | "You're back in — here's an offer." |
| **Poster offers to a candidate who has since withdrawn** | Blocked at compose. A withdrawer is owed no disposition (04.05.01) and has removed themselves; offering to them would be the platform re-entering someone into a process they left. | "They withdrew on 3 Aug." |
| **Offer surface fails to load for the candidate** | No action offered on an unloaded offer (04.04.01 D-09's principle). The fuse **keeps running**, which is the honest and uncomfortable answer — the platform cannot pause a real-world slot because its own page broke. | Retry, with the countdown still visible. See Q-06. |
| **The fuse expires during a platform outage** | The candidate lost a gig to a platform failure — the one failure mode here with no honest mitigation available from inside this feature, since the slot may genuinely have been filled by then. Named rather than assumed away. | See Q-06. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Offer opened | Skeleton with **the countdown live immediately** — the fuse is the one fact that must not wait on a render, because it is the fact the decision turns on |
| Empty | Shortlist resolved, no offer issued yet | Poster: "Make an offer to Dave." Candidate: no surface — an offer that hasn't been issued does not exist to its recipient, and a "pending offer" hint would leak a shortlist rank (04.04.02 Q-02's hazard) |
| Offered (Populated) | Offer issued | Terms with **per-facet deltas from the post** — money and non-money alike; the fuse; and the parallelism disclosure if N>1 (D-08) |
| Expiring | Fuse running | Countdown — **visible to both sides**, from issue, not from the last minute |
| Countered | Either party counters | The counter's terms, its own fuse, its delta **from the post** (not from the previous counter), and whose turn it is |
| Conflicted | Accept attempted against a calendar conflict | The conflicting engagement named with its window: "You have a session Saturday 2–9pm." Accept remains available (D-03) |
| Accepted | Candidate accepts, platform receives it first | Availability updates atomically; handoff fires (04.05.03); shortlist releases; everyone else is dispositioned (04.05.01 D-02) |
| Declined | Candidate declines | Falls to #2 (CX-02). Structured reason if given (D-11) |
| Expired | Fuse burned | Same as declined, but the candidate learns they lost it by not answering — which beats silence |
| Superseded | Another accept was received first in a declared cascade | "Someone accepted 8 seconds before you" — immediately, with the margin (04.04.04 D-03) |
| Revoked | The opportunity ceased to exist, **or** the candidate became unavailable via 24 | The **cause**, named — except where the cause is a moderation action, which is withheld from the poster. Never available as a poster's change of mind (D-07) |
| Partial | The post's version history is unreachable, so the delta cannot be computed | The uncomputable element named as uncomputable, never as "no changes". **Accept disabled; decline available** (D-12) |
| Error | The offer itself fails to load, or the accept fails to commit | Retry, countdown still running. **No accept is confirmed until the platform has committed it** — an optimistic "accepted!" that silently lost a race is the worst possible screen in this feature |

Acceptance must not assert the two are the same. Where the lineup is recorded is 18's (shows) and 07's (sessions) — see Q-05.
- Touches **18 Show Production & Touring** ([path](../../18-show-production-touring/)) — **trigger dependency + a boundary.** The offer binds the engagement facts (DT-10); **the advance elaborates them**. The offer is not a rider — but a fee-only offer is how "the room has no backline" becomes a surprise instead of a term. The performing lineup becomes real here, not at acceptance (DT-09).
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission.** Commit authority is inherited, never invented (04.04.02 D-03), and is **strictly narrower than review access** — an invitation confers review, never commit. Two lifecycle boundaries this file can state but not resolve: an offer is issued by the **org**, so revoking the issuer's membership mid-fuse does not revoke the offer (D-07); and when the posting org dissolves mid-fuse, an acceptance against the live offer is real but who owes the fee is 01's — the same seam 04.04.01 Q-05 hit from the queue side, one stage later and now with money attached.

A prompt firing on 40 dep calls is a prompt nobody reads by the time the writing camp arrives — over-firing does not merely annoy, it trains users to dismiss the platform's single most valuable ask. 04.01.01's type discriminator is what keeps the mechanism alive. |
| D-05 | **The offer re-runs 04.01.03's publication gate.** Six mandatory facets; £0 is not a flat fee; applicant-side fees remain unrepresentable | **The file's central decision** (DT-04). Every guardrail 04.01.03 built fires at *publication* — a public act with an applicant pool watching. The offer is private, one-to-one, and it is what binds. The gate guards the shop window and the deal is done in the back room. This also closes their D-02's obvious private workaround: the "£30 to confirm your slot" that cannot be published could otherwise be issued to someone who has just been told they got the gig — the moment of maximum leverage over the least powerful party in the transaction. |
| D-06 | **The counter-offer is a first-class act**, not an absence. A counter is an offer in the other direction: it re-runs the gate, carries its own fuse, and its delta is shown **against the post**, never against the previous counter | Accept/decline moves the negotiation to DM, and then the terms that bind are not the terms recorded (DT-05) — the platform present for the offer and absent for the agreement, which is the problem statement's root cause reproduced inside the fix. This is the same call 04.01.03 D-05 already made about downward edits: they permitted the honest expression because "a banned edit becomes a DM saying 'actually it's £100' — invisible, off-platform, and worse". A banned counter becomes the same DM. The against-the-post rule stops the ratchet — five 4% counters is the ladder their no-threshold rule forecloses, rebuilt one counter at a time. |
| D-07 | **The offer is irrevocable for its fuse.** The poster cannot change their mind inside a window they chose. It ends early only by a cause outside their discretion — a **declared** cascade loss, or the opportunity ceasing to exist — and the cause is named and recorded | The minimum symmetry, and it is available **without pre-empting Q-01** (DT-06). A live offer blocks the candidate's Saturday while committing the poster to nothing; a poster who wants optionality has an honest instrument — a shorter fuse — which transfers the cost back to whoever benefits from it. Whether *acceptance* binds is a user values call with penalties and drafted terms behind it; whether the *offer* binds for its own fuse is the cheap half and is answerable now. The abuse vector (claiming cancellation to escape) is named, its cause recorded, and detection routed to 24. |
| D-08 | **Parallelism is disclosed.** A candidate who is one of N live offers is told so at issue — scoped to **any N>1**, not to the cascade feature | Without it the candidate believes they hold a fuse-length option they do not hold, which is the platform lying by omission at the moment 04.04.04 D-03 says honesty is the entire mitigation. A poster issuing three offers "to see who bites" is running an undisclosed cascade, and the disclosure is what separates a fast fill from a lie. It also **accelerates** the cascade rather than costing it: a dep who knows two others are looking answers now. |
| D-09 | The offer carries the **formula, not the fee label**, wherever the shape has no number | 04.01.03 D-03 already ruled compensation is "a shape with a per-shape schema" — and for half those shapes there is no number, there is arithmetic. Accepting "50% of the door" is accepting a risk position; what binds is which costs come off the top, whose costs, and whether a guarantee floors it (DT-07). The door-deal dispute is live music's classic fight precisely because the label is agreed and the formula never is. Whether 17 holds the arithmetic is Q-04 — this decision states what the offer owes; it cannot state what settlement stores. |
| D-10 | The delta rule is **term-scoped, not fee-scoped**. Boundary: **the offer binds the engagement facts the post declared (04.01.01); the advance elaborates them (18).** The offer is not a rider; a fee-only offer is how a room's limits become a surprise. |
| D-11 | The decline carries an **optional, structured reason** — few values, never required, never free text | The market answering 04.01.03's gate: an honestly-declared fee can still be a wrong fee, and the decline is the only signal that says so (DT-15). Shape borrowed from 04.05.01 D-03. **Optional is load-bearing**: 04.04.01 DT-04's argument mirrors exactly — in a small industry a required, permanent, attributed reason produces lies rather than information. The value concentrates where the volume is: "4 of 5 declined on fee" tells a poster their number is wrong *before the gig fails to happen*. An **upward** re-offer needs no re-gate — D-05's materiality test is one-directional and a raise reduces nothing. |
| D-12 | **Accept is disabled when the delta cannot be computed.** Decline stays available | 04.04.01 D-09's principle — "the irreversible call is never available on incomplete information" — applied to the other party. There the irreversible call was the poster's reject; here it is the candidate's accept. Accepting on an uncomputable delta is agreeing to terms nobody could see, which is D-01 defeated by a loading failure. Decline survives because a candidate who declines has lost a gig, not been bound to one. |
| D-13 | Acceptance is ordered by **platform receipt**, never by client tap time | Otherwise a candidate on a slow connection retroactively wins a race decided three minutes earlier and the platform must un-hire someone. This is the mechanical floor under 04.04.04 D-03's honesty: the loser is told the truth *and the margin* ("8 seconds"), which is only meaningful if the ordering is a fact rather than an artefact of network conditions. A late accept resolves as Superseded, Expired or Revoked — whichever actually happened, never as a generic failure, because "it didn't go through" reads as a bug and sends them to the phone. |
| D-14 | The acceptance event is keyed on the **offer**, not on a submission | The cascade winner and the invite winner never submitted (DT-14, mirroring 04.04.01 DT-07). Keying on the offer makes cascade, invite and shortlist winners one event with one downstream instead of three paths reconciled at the handoff — and it constrains 04.05.01, whose per-submission obligation (D-01) is triggered by an acceptance (D-02) that may come from a non-submitter. The losers' `filled elsewhere` must never imply the winner was in the pile. |
