## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Board & Search ↔ Alert Subscriptions

**Relationship**: A user declares one intent — "bass, London, paid, weekends, £100+". Rendered as a
filter it is a board view; rendered as a standing subscription it is an alert. The sweep treated
these as separate candidates (01 and 02), which would make the user state the same intent twice and
then watch the two drift apart until their board shows work their alerts never mention.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the intent is their working profile in miniature, and the multi-hyphenate reality
  from `personas.md` means they hold *several* simultaneously — "bass, London, paid" and "mixing,
  remote, any" are both live and neither is a mode they switch into.
- **Producer**: fewer, broader intents — brief types and genres rather than dates.
- **Operator**: intents are gap-shaped ("anyone who can fill next Tuesday"), not standing.
- **Fan**: no access — Fans have no opportunity intent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One `SavedIntent` object owns both. No merge problem by construction —
   which is the entire point of collapsing them.
2. **Trigger chain**: Post publishes (after the 04.01.03 gate) → matched against every live intent →
   alert fan-out. If delivery fails, the board still shows it — pull is the fallback for push, which
   is why both must exist.
3. **Permission intersection**: An intent cannot match posts its owner isn't targeted by (04.01.02).
   Targeting is evaluated before matching, not after — otherwise an alert leaks the existence of an
   invite-only post.
4. **Notification fan-out**: This *is* the fan-out. Volume scales with intents × posts.
5. **State transition conflict**: Editing an intent mid-flight can re-match posts already seen.
   Dedup is on (user, post), not on (intent, post) (04.02.04 D-03) — the same post legitimately
   matching two of a multi-hyphenate's intents must not double-alert, so the dedup key is scoped to
   the person and the post, never to the individual intent that surfaced it.

### CX-02: Availability & Geo Matching ↔ Board & Search

**Relationship**: Matching is not a separate screen — it is the board's ranking and its default
filter. The default board view is "things I could actually do", which means availability and radius
are applied before the user asks. A board that leads with a gig on a night you're already booked is
a listings page, not a matching product.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: strongest effect — their availability is dense and their radius is real (travel time
  with gear, not straight-line distance).
- **Producer**: geo is weak (remote work), delivery capacity is the real constraint.
- **Operator**: inverted — they match *others* against their fixed location rather than being
  matched.
- **Fan**: no access; the Fan board view is unranked public listings.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Board owns presentation, matching owns scoring. Availability is owned
   by neither (D-02) — it is a cross-cut.
2. **Trigger chain**: Board load → matching scores → render. A matching failure must degrade to an
   unranked board, not an empty one.
3. **Permission intersection**: Availability is private. Matching reads it; the board must never
   render *why* something ranked low if the reason is another user's private calendar.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None — pull path.
5. **State transition conflict**: Availability changing mid-session restacks the board under the
   user. Probably acceptable; a dep call vanishing as you tap it is not.

### CX-03: Availability & Geo Matching ↔ Alert Subscriptions

**Relationship**: Availability is an alert *suppressor*, and this is the highest-leverage
interaction in the sub-domain. The parent domain justifies itself on "alerts carry money" — but a
channel that alerts you about Friday when you are already gigging Friday teaches you to ignore it,
and once ignored it carries nothing. Suppression is what protects the channel's value.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the whole point. Their calendar is dense and irregular, and the multi-hyphenate case
  means "booked" can mean a session, a gig, a lesson, or a studio hold across four other domains.
- **Producer**: weaker — remote work overlaps in ways gigs cannot.
- **Operator**: inverted — they want alerts *about* their gaps, so their empty calendar is the
  trigger rather than the suppressor.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Availability is not owned here (D-02). This sub-domain is a reader,
   which means a stale read causes a wrongly-suppressed alert — a silent miss with a money cost and
   no error surface.
2. **Trigger chain**: Match → availability check → suppress or deliver. **Fail-open is correct
   here**: if availability is unreadable, deliver the alert. A false positive costs the user a
   glance; a false negative costs them a gig they never knew about.
3. **Permission intersection**: Suppression reveals nothing to the poster — the poster must not
   learn that a candidate was suppressed for being busy, because that is the candidate's private
   calendar.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Suppression is the primary volume control before any digest logic.
5. **State transition conflict**: A gig cancelling should arguably *un*-suppress alerts already
   swallowed — replaying missed alerts on a freed evening is a genuinely novel behaviour and nobody
   asked for it. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

### CX-04: Material-Aware Dep Matching ↔ Availability & Geo Matching

**Relationship**: Material matching composes onto the base match; it never replaces it. The dep who
knows all 25 songs but is booked, or is 300 miles from the venue with a drum kit to move, is not a
match. Material raises confidence within the available-and-reachable pool.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: both the matched dep and the MD doing the matching.
- **Producer**: read-only; rarely the poster of a dep call.
- **Operator**: sees only the outcome — the slot filled.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — separate scoring inputs into one ranking.
2. **Trigger chain**: Base match (availability + geo) → material scoring on the survivors. Ordering
   matters for cost: material scoring is expensive and should never run on people who can't be there.
3. **Permission intersection**: Material knowledge is derived from credits (02) and setlists (18) —
   both have their own visibility rules, and a dep's history of playing a set may be private.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Feeds alert urgency (CX-05).
5. **State transition conflict**: A setlist changing after matching invalidates the material score.
   Setlists change constantly, often on the day.

### CX-05: Material-Aware Dep Matching ↔ Alert Subscriptions

**Relationship**: Material confidence is the only honest justification for an intrusive alert.
"Dep needed Friday" at 11pm is spam; "Dep needed Friday — you've played this exact set twice" at
11pm is the most valuable notification the platform can send, and the parent index's "dep gigs are
won in minutes" says the intrusion is warranted. Urgency should be earned by confidence, not
declared by the poster.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the only affected persona.
- **Producer / Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None.
2. **Trigger chain**: Material score → urgency tier → delivery channel + quiet-hours override.
3. **Permission intersection**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: This is the mechanism that resolves index Q-03 (fatigue vs latency) —
   if confidence gates intrusion, high-latency delivery is reserved for low-confidence matches.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
