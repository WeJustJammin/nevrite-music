# Feature: Alert Subscriptions & Delivery

> **Number**: 04.02.04
> **Parent**: [Discovery, Matching & Alerts](./04.02-discovery-matching-alerts-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The push half of discovery: a standing intent that fires when a matching opportunity publishes. The
parent domain rests its case on this feature — "alerts carry money and dep gigs are won in minutes"
— which makes it the domain's clearest claim on being a daily habit rather than a destination.
Its subscription object is the same one the board saves (CX-01); what this feature owns is the
decision of *whether, when, and how loudly* to interrupt someone.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The highest-value alert consumer on the platform. Holds several simultaneous intents because they hold several simultaneous roles (`personas.md`) — a bass alert and a mixing alert are both live and neither is a mode. Their tolerance for interruption is high *when the alert carries money* and near-zero otherwise, which is the whole design problem. |
| Producer | Full | Brief and session-call alerts. Lower urgency, lower volume — a topline brief is not won in minutes. Digest is the right default for them where it would be a failure for a dep. |
| Operator | Full | Gap-shaped alerts — "someone's free who could fill Tuesday". Inverted trigger: their *empty* calendar is the signal, not a suppressor. Also the persona most likely to be at a loading dock rather than a desk when the alert lands (`personas.md`), which is a mobile-surface fact before it is a product one. |
| Fan | None | Fans get artist and show alerts — but from Fanbase (20) and Promotion (21), not from here. A Fan has no opportunity intent and no professional availability. Keeping this boundary sharp matters: D-13 warns fan-scale notification volume is a different load and moderation problem, and it must not enter this pipe. |

## Behavior

Urgency tiers, because one delivery policy across all types is wrong in both directions:

| Tier | Example | Delivery | Quiet hours |
|------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Critical | Dep call, high material confidence (CX-05) | Immediate push | Overridden — a gig at 11pm for tomorrow is worth the interruption |
| Timely | Dep call, unknown material; support slot | Immediate, respects quiet hours | Respected |
| Standard | Session call, crew job, function gig | Immediate or batched by preference | Respected |
| Digest | Briefs, open calls, member-wanted | Daily/weekly summary | N/A |

### Happy Path

1. User saves an intent on the board (CX-01) — the same object, now standing.
2. A post publishes and clears the gate (04.01.03).
3. Targeting resolves (04.01.02) → matching scores (04.02.02) → material confidence if applicable (04.02.03).
4. Availability suppresses if the user is booked (CX-03).
5. Urgency tier resolves; delivery fires on the appropriate channel.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User is booked that night | Suppressed (CX-03) — the mechanism that protects the channel's signal ratio | Nothing. Still on the board if they look. |
| Availability unreadable | **Fail open** — deliver (04.02.02 D-03) | The alert arrives |
| One post matches two of a multi-hyphenate's intents | Dedup on (user, post), not (intent, post) — one human, one alert | One alert, possibly noting both intents matched |
| Quiet hours vs a dep call for tomorrow | Confidence-gated override (CX-05). Unearned override is how a channel dies. | Push at 11pm *only* if material confidence justifies it |
| Alert delivered, gig already filled | The normal dep case — minutes matter. Alert must reflect state at open, not at send. | "Filled 4 minutes ago." Better than a dead link. |
| Delivery channel fails | Board is the pull fallback (CX-01 synthesis Q2) | Nothing — silent, which is why the board must exist |
| User has 30 intents and gets 30 alerts a day | Fatigue. The channel that carries money becomes the channel they mute. | See Q-01 — the trade-off is unresolved |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No subscriptions | New user | "Save a search to get told when work appears" |
| Active | Intent saved | Intent list with per-intent urgency preference |
| Suppressed | User booked | Nothing — invisible by design, which makes it undebuggable for the user. See Q-02. |
| Delivered | Alert fired | Push/email/in-app with the match reason stated (04.02.01 D-02) |
| Muted | User mutes an intent | Still on the board, no push |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Alert fatigue and alert latency are in direct opposition** and cannot both be won. A dep call is worthless 20 minutes late; a digest is the standard fatigue remedy and is a 24-hour delay. The parent domain's entire justification ("dep gigs are won in minutes") sits on the latency side | Reasoning from the parent index's own claim against standard notification practice | ✅ CONFIRMED as an irreducible tension, resolved by **type-scoped urgency tiers rather than a global policy**. A single delivery policy is wrong for a dep call and wrong for a brief in opposite directions. Whether tiers are also per-user is unresolved (Q-01). |
| DT-02 | A domain expert would expect **suppression to be invisible and therefore undebuggable**. The user cannot tell the difference between "no work matched me" and "work matched me and was suppressed" — and one of those is the platform failing them silently | Reasoning from CX-03; nobody in the candidate list raised suppression at all | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it argues for a visible "suppressed because you were booked" trail rather than pure silence. A user who discovers they missed a gig because the platform decided they were busy, with no record, loses trust permanently. Logged as Q-02. |
| DT-03 | This pipe is a **consumer of a platform-wide notification mechanism, not an owner of one**. Delivery channels, quiet hours, per-channel preferences, digests and unsubscribe are needed identically by 05, 12, 17, 19, 20, 21 and 24 — and D-13 says fan-scale volume makes it a load problem | Node Classification Gate cross-cut test; D-13 in `ideation-index.md` | ✅ CONFIRMED — routed to the global CX as a mechanism. What stays here is the *policy* (which urgency, which suppression, which override); what leaves is the *pipe*. The fan/professional volume separation D-13 warns about is an argument for one pipe with tiers, not two pipes. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Urgency is **type-scoped**, not global | Fatigue and latency are irreducibly opposed (DT-01); one policy is wrong for both a dep call and a brief. |
| D-02 | Quiet-hours override must be **earned by material confidence** (CX-05), never declared by the poster | Every poster believes their gig is urgent. Letting them assert it is how the channel dies. |
| D-03 | Dedup on **(user, post)** | One human, one alert — the multi-hyphenate holds several intents that legitimately match the same post. |
| D-04 | The delivery pipe is a **cross-cut**; only the policy lives here | Seven+ domains need the same pipe (DT-03). |
