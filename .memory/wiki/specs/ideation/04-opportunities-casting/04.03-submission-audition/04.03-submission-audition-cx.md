## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Blind Review Mode ↔ Evidence-Backed Application

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's central problem. Evidence-backed application works by attaching
verified credits — "played on this record, attested by this producer". Blind review works by making
the applicant unidentifiable. A verified credit graph is *the most de-anonymising data on the
platform*: "bass on that album, 2024" identifies one human exactly, and a reviewer who knows the
scene needs one credit, not a name. The two features negate each other, and both are legitimate —
orchestral and house auditions run blind for real fairness reasons, and evidence-backing is the
domain's thesis link.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: loses their strongest asset the moment blind mode engages. A player whose whole case
  is "I've done this" is reduced to whoever they are on the day — which is *precisely the point* of
  blind review, and precisely what makes it costly for them.
- **Producer**: as poster, chooses. As attestor, their attestations are the de-anonymising signal —
  they are the reason the evidence is worth stripping.
- **Operator**: enables blind mode for house auditions; least affected personally.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The submission owns both. Blind mode is a *view* transformation, not a
   different object — which means the evidence still exists and is one access-control mistake from
   being visible. Storing it un-stripped and hiding it at render is the naive design and it will leak.
2. **Trigger chain**: Post has blind mode → submission strips evidence at capture or at render.
   **At capture is safer and lossier** — you cannot un-blind later for the shortlist stage.
3. **Permission intersection**: This is the crux. Blind mode is an access-control rule over the most
   identifying data in the system, which makes enforcement a security-architecture problem and not a
   UI one. Routed to `/create-prd-security`.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A blind submission must not notify the poster in a way that names the
   applicant — the notification is a classic side-channel leak.
5. **State transition conflict**: Un-blinding at shortlist (the normal real-world flow — blind first
   round, named final) means the evidence must survive the blind stage somewhere. That reopens (1).
   Unresolved — see index Q-01.

### CX-02: Evidence-Backed Application ↔ Structured Submission

**Relationship**: The application is not typed, it is assembled. The submission's claims are drawn
from the credit graph (02) as citations — "played on X (attested by Y), played this set twice
(from 18), 14 sessions with 3 of your prior collaborators" — rather than as free text the applicant
writes about themselves. This is D-18's provenance half doing work in a domain that is nominally
about consolidation, and it is why the sweep's framing (a job board) undersells what this can be.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their `personas.md` success criterion is literally "no re-proving themselves to each
  new client" and "credits are cited rather than claimed". This cross-cut *is* that criterion.
- **Producer**: both sides — their attestations make other people's evidence real, and their own
  applications carry the same citations.
- **Operator**: reads evidence when hiring; has fewer credits of their own to cite.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Credits are owned by 02; the submission holds references, never copies.
   A copy would fossilise a credit that later gets disputed or revoked.
2. **Trigger chain**: Submission opened → evidence resolved against the post's requirements →
   pre-filled. If 02 is unreachable, the submission must still be completable by hand — otherwise an
   outage blocks people from applying for work.
3. **Permission intersection**: Some credits are private (02's own visibility rules). An applicant
   may want to cite a private credit to one poster without publishing it — a per-submission
   disclosure that 02 must support.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Citing a credit may notify the attestor. Probably unwanted — an
   attestor does not need to know every time someone cites their attestation.
5. **State transition conflict**: A credit revoked after submission invalidates the evidence. Rare
   but consequential, and it is the disputed-credit case (24) landing in casting.

### CX-03: Audition Tasks ↔ Blind Review Mode

**Relationship**: Audition media is simultaneously the most blind-able artefact (a take can be
judged on its merits) and the leakiest (a voice is a fingerprint, a room has a sound, a player has
a signature). Blind review of audio is the classic case — the orchestral screen — and it is also the
case where anonymisation is hardest to actually achieve.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: submits a take believing it is anonymous. If it isn't, the platform made a promise it
  didn't keep about their livelihood.
- **Producer**: as reviewer, may recognise a player in two bars and cannot un-know it.
- **Operator**: same, for house auditions.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Media owned by the submission; blind mode transforms metadata, not the
   audio. **The audio itself is unanonymisable** — this is a physical fact, not a bug.
2. **Trigger chain**: Blind post → media stripped of filename, EXIF, DAW metadata, session name.
   Every one of those is a real leak vector and DAW-rendered files carry all of them.

4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

### CX-04: Outbound Pitch ↔ Structured Submission

**Relationship**: One object, two anchors. A submission normally hangs off a post; a pitch hangs off
a *target* (a promoter, a label, an act you want to support). Everything downstream — triage,
shortlist, disposition, the close-out obligation — should treat them identically, because a pitch
that can be ignored without consequence is exactly the black hole the industry already has.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: pitches for support slots; the classic "we'd love to open for you" that currently
  goes to an unread inbox.
- **Producer**: pitches production services to artists.
- **Operator**: the recipient, not the pitcher.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: One submission entity, nullable post reference, non-null target
   reference when unanchored. The anchor is a discriminator, same shape as 04.01.01's type.
2. **Trigger chain**: Pitch → target's queue (04.04.01). A target with no queue configured is the
   dead-letter case and the reason Q-02 exists.
3. **Permission intersection**: **The crux** — a post grants implicit permission to be submitted to;
   a target grants nothing. Without an opt-in this is a cold-contact channel pointed at the most
   harassed people in the industry.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Unsolicited by definition; must respect the recipient's controls
   absolutely, or the platform becomes the spam it replaced.
5. **State transition conflict**: A pitch whose target later posts a matching opportunity should
   probably convert rather than duplicate. Unhandled — noted in 04.03.05.

### CX-05: Audition Tasks ↔ Evidence-Backed Application

**Relationship**: Hypothesis worth testing — strong verified evidence should **reduce or remove the
audition**. `personas.md` gives the Musician the success criterion "no re-proving themselves to each
new client", and an audition is precisely re-proving yourself. A player with three attested credits
on the exact repertoire should not be asked to record a take, and a platform that asks anyway has
the credit graph and doesn't use it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the entire benefit. This is the most direct expression of their stated success
  criterion anywhere in the domain.
- **Producer**: as poster, gives up a control they may not want to give up — evidence tells you what
  someone has done, not whether they're right for *this*.
- **Operator**: same.
- **Fan**: no access.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — evidence informs whether the task is required.
2. **Trigger chain**: Evidence resolved → threshold met → audition waived or reduced. Poster-set
   thresholds, since the judgement is theirs.
