# Feature: Load-Out Check & Gear Loss/Damage Log

> **Number**: 18.09.03
> **Parent**: [Backline & Gear Manifest](./18.09-backline-gear-manifest-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Verifying at 01:00, in the dark, that everything that came in is going back out — and recording what
isn't, or what came back broken. Gear disappears on tour constantly and is almost never traced,
because the only record of what was in the van is in someone's head.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own gear) | Confirms their own gear is in the van, and is the one who reports their pedal is missing. **Their gear is theirs** — the loss is theirs, the claim is theirs, and the insurance is theirs. |
| Producer | Full | Runs the check, logs losses and damage, and files what needs filing. The person who discovers at the next load-in that something never made it. |
| Operator | Read-only (claims against them) | Sees any loss or damage claimed to have occurred in their room, with a right of reply. Their room's history of incidents is a real fact about it — and a contestable one. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The date's resolved manifest (18.09.01) is the checklist — so the check costs seconds rather than
being a document someone must first create. A missing item is logged against the date and the venue,
and flows to the registry (15) as a status change, to insurance as a claim, and to the rental
supplier if it wasn't ours.

### Happy Path

1. Load-out begins; the date's manifest opens as a check.
2. Items are confirmed in bulk — by case, not by item, because nobody checks 60 items individually.
3. One item is missing; it is logged against this date and this room.
4. The registry (15) marks it missing; the owner is notified.
5. It turns up in the next city's van; the log closes.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody runs the check | The default outcome; a loss is discovered at the *next* load-in with no idea where | "Not checked out of [venue] — 2 rooms ago" |
| Item found later | Losses are provisional until confirmed; closing the log is normal | "Found in [city] — closed" |
| Damage, not loss | Different claim, different evidence — a photo at the time is worth everything | "Photo attached — damage logged at [venue]" |
| Venue disputes the claim | One report never convicts a room; right of reply, same as 18.18 | Operator: "A loss is claimed against your room — respond" |
| Rented gear damaged | The claim is against the act, not by them — the direction reverses | "You're liable — supplier claim" |
| Theft, not loss | A crime with a police report and an insurance process | "Report to police? Insurance requires a crime number" |
| Check done in the dark at 01:00 by tired people | The whole design constraint: bulk confirm, offline, seconds | Case-level confirm, not item-level |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Check opened | Manifest checklist skeleton |
| Empty | No manifest | "No manifest — nothing to check against" |
| Populated | Check in progress | Case-grouped confirm with a running count |
| Error | Save failed | "Saved locally — will sync" |
| Partial | Some items unconfirmed | "54 of 60 confirmed — 6 unaccounted" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The sweep's backline model has no verification step | Deep Think — domain expert lens | ✅ CONFIRMED, node created — the candidate list has a manifest and sourcing, both about *planning* what gear exists. Nothing checks whether it still does. But gear goes missing at every show, and the loss is typically discovered two cities later with no way to know where it went. **The manifest already exists and the load-out is already happening** — the check is nearly free, and it converts a list into a chain of custody. |
| DT-02 | Item-by-item checking | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — nobody checks 60 items at 01:00 after a show. **Case-level bulk confirmation** is the only thing that will happen, and a check that doesn't happen is worth nothing. Same principle as 18.06.04 D-05: if it takes longer than seconds it will not occur, and the node is worthless. Design for the tired human at the loading dock. |
| DT-03 | This is a small housekeeping feature | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — it's `could` for the tour, but it **feeds the gear registry's core value** (domain 15: ownership and provenance) with real custody events. A registry that knows an instrument was at 40 venues, checked out of each, is a materially better provenance record than one that only knows you bought it. That's the thesis (D-18) applied to a physical object: capture the fact where it happens. |
| DT-04 | Loss is the act's problem alone | Boundary lens | ❌ REJECTED — it happened in someone's room, sometimes to rented gear, sometimes as theft. It touches the venue's reputation (16), the rental supplier (13), the insurer, and possibly the police. But a single unverified claim must not damage a room — right of reply and corroboration, exactly as the post-show report (18.18 D-02). |
| DT-05 | The venue has no interest in this | Adversarial lens | ❌ REJECTED — a venue with a clean custody record has **evidence** they didn't lose someone's amp, which is worth something in a dispute. The incentive is symmetrical, which is unusual and useful — it's one of the few places where the Operator benefits from adopting a check the act runs. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The date's manifest is the checklist — no separate document | The check is nearly free only because the list already exists |
| D-02 | Case-level bulk confirmation, never item-by-item | Nobody checks 60 items at 01:00; a check that doesn't happen is worth nothing |
| D-03 | A single claim never damages a venue — right of reply and corroboration | Same discipline as the post-show report; single-source claims are weaponisable |
| D-04 | Custody events feed the gear registry (15) | A registry that knows an instrument's road history is a better provenance record |
