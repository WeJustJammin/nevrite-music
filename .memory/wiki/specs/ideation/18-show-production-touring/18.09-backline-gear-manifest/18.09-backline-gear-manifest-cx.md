## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Backline Sourcing → Gear Manifest

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's loop closes here. A diff shortfall becomes a rental, and the
rental becomes a manifest entry for one date — an item that is on stage, on the plot, and in the
load-out check, but belongs to nobody on the tour and must go back to its owner at the end of the
night. That last property is what makes it more than a row.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: books it, budgets it, returns it.
- **Operator**: often the supplier, so the item is theirs and never leaves the building.
- **Musician**: plays it, and it isn't theirs — a fact that matters when it breaks.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Owned items reference the registry (15) and belong to a person; rented items belong to a supplier and exist only for a date. **They cannot share an ownership model**, which is 18.09 Q-02 and is unresolved. A naive "owner" field forces a rental to belong to someone on the tour, which is false and breaks the load-out check.
2. **Trigger chain**: Shortfall → rental confirmed → manifest entry (date-scoped) → appears on the plot and in the load-out check → returned. If the rental never arrives (18.09.02 edge case 4), the manifest promised something that doesn't exist and the check will flag a "loss" that was never there.
3. **Permission intersection**: A venue-supplied rental is an item the Operator owns appearing on the act's manifest. They can see it; they cannot see the rest of the list. Item-level visibility scoping inside one document.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The rental supplier needs to know when their item is confirmed on the manifest, and when the load-out says it came back.
5. **State transition conflict**: A rental cancelled after it's on the manifest must remove the item *and* re-open the shortfall — not silently vanish, which would leave the diff thinking the gap was solved.

### CX-02: Gear Manifest → Load-Out Check

**Relationship**: The whole justification for 18.09.03. Checking gear at 01:00 is only viable because
the list already exists as data and can be presented as a case-grouped bulk confirm. If the manifest
were a printout (the industry's version), the check would be a person reading a page by torchlight,
which is why it doesn't happen. The derivation is what converts a list into a chain of custody.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: runs the check against the date's resolved manifest.
- **Musician**: confirms their own cases; their gear, their loss.
- **Operator**: sees only claims against their room.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The manifest owns what *should* be there; the check owns what *is*. Same plan/fact separation as the setlist (18.06 D-01) and the schedule (18.07-cx CX-02) — the domain's recurring structural pattern, and for the same reason: overwriting the plan with the actual destroys the variance that is the whole point.
2. **Trigger chain**: Load-out → check opens pre-populated → bulk confirm → discrepancies logged → registry (15) and venue (16) updated. The chain must survive offline; a loading dock at 01:00 is the worst-connected moment on the tour.
3. **Permission intersection**: A Musician can confirm only their own cases. This is correct but means a check is only complete when several people have acted — and at 01:00 people leave. A partial check is the normal outcome, not an error state.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An unaccounted item notifies its owner immediately — they may still be in the building, which is the only moment recovery is cheap.
5. **State transition conflict**: A "loss" logged at 01:00 that turns out to be in the other van at 09:00 must close cleanly (18.09.03 edge case 2). Losses are provisional by default; a system that treats them as final will train people to skip the check rather than file false alarms.

### CX-03: Load-Out Check → Gear Manifest (reverse mutation)

**Relationship**: Medium confidence — a consequence rather than a designed flow, and one that only
exists because the tour is a multi-date container. A bass rig lost in Manchester is not on the
manifest in Leeds, which means the diff for Leeds now reports a shortfall it didn't have yesterday,
which means sourcing (CX-01) fires again. **A loss propagates forward into every remaining date.**

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: needs to know that tonight's loss is tomorrow's shortfall, immediately.
- **Musician**: their gear, their problem, their remaining tour.
- **Operator**: unaffected downstream.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — remainder deferred to Step 5)_:
2. **Trigger chain**: Loss confirmed → manifest updated for remaining dates → diffs re-run → new shortfalls → sourcing. This is the sub-domain's only *automatic* forward cascade, and it's the kind of thing no TM does at 01:00 — which is exactly why it's valuable.
5. **State transition conflict**: If the loss is provisional (might turn up tomorrow), does the manifest change now or on confirmation? Changing too early creates phantom shortfalls; too late means arriving in Leeds without a bass rig and no time to hire one. Genuinely unresolved.

### CX-04: Load-Out Check → Backline Sourcing (reverse liability)

**Relationship**: Medium confidence. Every other flow in this sub-domain assumes the act is the party
suffering loss. When the damaged item is *rented*, the direction reverses: the act is liable, the
supplier is the claimant, and the evidence the act captured is now evidence against them. The same
mechanism, pointed the other way.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: is now the respondent, not the claimant.
- **Operator**: if they supplied it, they're the claimant — a role reversal from CX-02.
- **Musician**: may have been the one who broke it.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — remainder deferred to Step 5)_:
3. **Permission intersection**: The act's own damage log becomes discoverable evidence against them. That is honest, and it is also a reason someone might not log damage — a real behavioural risk worth naming rather than assuming good faith.
5. **State transition conflict**: A rental damage claim routes to 13/24 and out of this domain entirely, mid-flow. The handover boundary is not specified.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 18.09.02 Backline Sourcing | 18.09.03 Load-Out Check (as a rental-return flow) | Considered: is returning rented gear the same act as checking out owned gear? Rejected as a *cross-cut* — they co-occur at load-out but have no shared state. The return is a transaction event belonging to 13's rental lifecycle; the check is a custody verification. CX-04 covers the one case where they genuinely interact (damage), and that's a liability edge, not a return edge. |
| R-02 | 18.09.01 Gear Manifest | 18.09.02 Backline Sourcing (as manifest-drives-sourcing) | Considered: does the manifest generate sourcing needs? Rejected — **the diff does** (18.03.02), by comparing the rider against the venue. The manifest is what we *have*; the shortfall is what the room *lacks*. Drawing this edge would imply the manifest knows about venues, which it doesn't and shouldn't. The real chain is rider → diff → shortfall → sourcing → manifest, and this pair is the wrong link in it. |

> **Notes for agents:**
> - **CX-01's synthesis question 1 is the sub-domain's live modelling gap** (18.09 Q-02): owned items and rented items cannot share an ownership model, and the manifest currently pretends they can.
> - CX-03's timing question (provisional loss vs manifest mutation) has no good answer yet and has real consequences — a phantom shortfall or an unhired bass rig.
> - R-02 is a reminder that this sub-domain's inputs come from *outside* it. The manifest doesn't find gaps; the diff does.
