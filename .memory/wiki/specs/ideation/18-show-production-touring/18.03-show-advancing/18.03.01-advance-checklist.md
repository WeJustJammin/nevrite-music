# Feature: Advance Checklist & Two-Sided Completion Tracking

> **Number**: 18.03.01
> **Parent**: [Show Advancing](./18.03-show-advancing-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The per-date list of everything that must be settled before the show, with each item owned by a
named side, assigned to a named human, and tracked to explicit two-sided completion. It replaces
the email chain's defining pathology: nobody knows what is still outstanding, because "outstanding"
only exists in the head of whoever last read the thread.

Its second job is evidentiary. A confirmed item is a dated attestation by a named party that a fact
about this show was true — which is the same capture-at-source mechanism D-18 makes the platform's
thesis, applied to live production instead of to a session. The advance is where the operational
facts of a show become true; this is the object that records them while everyone is still friendly.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (contextual — see Q-05) | For a **DIY act** the Musician *is* the advance owner: they hold every artist-side item, answer the venue's questions, and are the freezing party. For a **managed act** they hold only items assigned to them — their own tech needs (18.05), their access provisions (18.04.03), their hospitality — and see one number: their own outstanding count. They never see the venue's item list, the fee, or the other acts on the bill. They are the persona most harmed by an item that silently stays `open`, because they are the one standing on the stage when it turns out to matter. |
| Producer | Full | The primary actor. Opens the advance, reviews the generated items, adds the free-text items the generator could not know, assigns artist-side items to their crew, chases venue-side items, sees both sides' completion state, classifies severity within the limits of D-06, and decides when the advance is settled (18.03.05). Sees the escalation ladder's state per item and the phone number at its terminal rung. |
| Operator | Full (own side) | Works a **cross-date queue of their own dates** (D-12), not one advance at a time: "what does anybody need from me this week?" Within a date they see the venue-side items assigned to them, the artist requirements they must answer, and their own standing questions' replies. They can reassign an item to the colleague who actually knows (D-08), answer, or raise an exception (18.03.03). They **cannot** mark an artist-side item confirmed, cannot see the act's other dates, cannot see fees or crew pay, and — where two acts on the bill contend for the same resource — they are the only party who can see the contention (see edge cases). |
| Fan | None | Entirely internal, with no derived fan-facing artifact. The advance's public consequences — doors, set time, an accessibility provision changing — reach fans only through the schedule's public projection in 19/21, which reads the frozen pack, never this checklist. A fan-visible advance would leak the bill's commercial shape and the venue's operational weaknesses. |

## Behavior

The checklist is **generated, not authored**. Items come from four sources, in descending volume:

| # | Source | What it produces | Owning side |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Venue capability diff** (18.03.02) | Each `shortfall` → an item ("bring, rent, substitute or redline?"). Each `unknown` → a question — and a row reaches `unknown` two ways, which this rule treats identically and the copy must not: **no data** ("do you have 3-phase power?") or **confirmation required**, where the room states a satisfying value but the act flagged the requirement `require-confirmation` and 18.03.02 D-17 holds it at `unknown`, basis `awaiting-confirmation` ("can we use the Steinway?"). Each near-match and each `or similar` judgement call → a judgement item. A `match` produces **no item** (D-04). | Shortfall/judgement → artist. Unknown → venue, on both paths. |
| 2 | **Compliance requirement engine** (18.16) | Permits, licences, insurance certificates, safety documents — each with a statutory lead time declared by the requirement. | Whichever side the requirement names as the responsible party. |
| 3 | **The venue's standing advance questions** (their 16 listing) | The questions this room always needs answered and artists always forget — parking, support-act count, merch cut mechanics, curfew acknowledgement (D-10). | Artist. |
| 4 | **Manual free-text** | What no generator could know: "our drummer's flight lands at 16:00". | Author's choice. |

### The item

Every item carries:

- **Owning side** — `artist` or `venue`. Immutable after creation.
- **Assignee** — a named individual, not a side (D-08). Reassignable by the owning side.
- **Status** — `open → answered → confirmed | self-confirmed`, or `open → waived`. Terminal states are `confirmed`, `self-confirmed` and `waived`.
- **Severity class** — `hard` or `soft`, derived per D-06.
- **Resolve-by date** — `show_date − item_lead_time`, computed in the **venue's** local timezone (D-15). Default lead time is 0, which makes resolve-by the act's freeze target (default T-5d per 18.03.05, act-configurable).
- **Thread** — item-scoped discussion. Only the item's **resolution note** propagates to the pack and the sheets (D-13).
- **History** — every status transition, with author, timestamp, and (for off-platform confirmations) a source note.

### Two-sided completion

**An item marked done by its owner is `answered`, not `confirmed`.** Confirmation requires the
*other* side. This is the whole mechanism: "I told them" ≠ "they know" (D-02).

- Venue answers "yes, we have the 48-channel desk" → `answered`. The Producer confirms → `confirmed`.
- Producer answers "we're bringing our own bass rig" → `answered`. The venue confirms (they need to know it's coming through their dock) → `confirmed`.
- Where the same human holds both sides, the item goes to `self-confirmed` — a distinct terminal state that is honest about being weaker evidence (D-09).
- Where the answer arrives through the account-less link in an emailed sheet (18.03.04), it is `answered` and the answerer's identity is recorded as **weak**. A link answer can never self-confirm and can never `waive`.

### Two numbers, not one

The checklist reports two things that must not be conflated (D-05):

1. **The sort** is urgency: band by resolve-by (`overdue` → `critical` ≤72h → `due` ≤7d → `scheduled` >7d), then severity within band.
2. **The gate** is severity: the **hard-outstanding count** is every `hard` item not in a terminal state, regardless of band. This is the number 18.03.05's freeze reads.

A permit due in 30 days is not urgent and *is* a blocker. A towel count overdue by a week is urgent
and is not. One number cannot carry both facts, and a system that tries produces "80% advanced",
which D-04 of the sub-domain already rejects as meaningless.

### Escalation

Nudges are the feature, because venue silence — not disagreement — is the modal failure (DT-03).
The ladder is per **side**, aggregated, never per item (D-14):

| Rung | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 48h with items `open` and unopened | One in-app + email nudge naming the count, not the items |
| 2 | 5d, or 50% of the nearest item's remaining lead time — whichever is sooner | Second nudge, now naming the `overdue`/`critical` items |
| 3 | 8d, or any hard item entering `overdue` | Escalate to a **different named contact** at that side, not another message to the same one |
| 4 | 10d, or T-14d — whichever is sooner | Terminal: surface the venue's phone number to the Producer, and offer the emailable sheet (18.03.04) |

**Cap**: at most one nudge per side per 48h regardless of item count. A 40-item list must never send
40 nudges — that is how a tool trains its recipients to filter it. The ladder's terminal rung is
"call them", and the call comes back as an off-platform confirmation (D-03) — the system's honest
last move is to hand the human a phone number and then capture the result.

### Happy Path

1. Domain 17's deal confirms → 18.01 creates the event record → Producer moves it to `advancing`.
2. The checklist self-populates within the advance-open flow: the diff (18.03.02) contributes 4 shortfalls, 9 unknowns and 2 judgement items; 18.16 contributes 3 compliance items (one with a 10-working-day statutory lead time, so its resolve-by is already T-14d); the venue's standing questions contribute 5; the 28 diff matches contribute nothing. **23 items, 12 artist-side, 11 venue-side.** Three of the 9 unknowns are rows the room satisfies on paper and the rider flagged `require-confirmation` — the two power items floored there by 18.04.01 D-05, and the house piano — held at `unknown` by 18.03.02 D-17 and therefore questions, not matches. They arrive through source 1's existing `unknown` rule; no new source and no exception to D-04 was needed to produce them.
3. Producer reviews, dismisses one non-applicable generated item as `waived` with the reason "acoustic set — no 3-phase needed", adds 2 free-text items, and assigns the 4 backline items to their PM.
4. Producer sends the advance. The venue's named production contact is notified once, with a count and a link — not 23 notifications.
5. Operator opens their cross-date queue, sees this date among four in flight that week, and works its 11 items. They reassign the console question to the house tech (D-08), answer 9 — including the three confirmations ("yes, the 63A stage-left supply is yours"; "yes, the Steinway is yours for the night") that no amount of listing data could have closed — and raise an exception on the 10th ("no third wedge mix — IEMs or nothing"), which escalates into a redline (18.03.03) rather than sitting `open`.
6. Each answered item is confirmed by the other side. The outstanding count falls; the hard-outstanding count falls to 2 — both compliance items awaiting the venue's certificate upload. The two power confirmations were `hard` on the way in (an `unknown` on a hard requirement is `hard`, D-06) and were counted in that gate until the Operator answered them; had they gone unanswered they would still be sitting in it at T-5d.
7. The venue's answer on the console contradicts their 16 listing ("we sold the M32 last year"). The answer is the truth for this date; the item confirms against the answer, and the Operator is prompted to correct their listing (DT-11). The system never writes to 16 on their behalf.
8. The redline is accepted; the diff re-runs; the item resolves to `waived` with the exception attached, and the affected performers are notified — not just the negotiating parties.
9. The venue uploads the certificates. Hard-outstanding reaches 0.
10. Producer freezes the advance at T-5d (18.03.05). The checklist snapshots into the pack; the event record moves `advancing → advanced`; every later change is a change-control event, not an edit.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue never opens the advance | The 4-rung escalation ladder runs, capped at one nudge per side per 48h; terminal rung surfaces the phone number and the emailable sheet (18.03.04) | Rung 4: "No response in 10 days. Call [name] on [number], or send the sheet by email." |
| Item is disputed, not merely open | Escalates into a redline (18.03.03) rather than sitting `open` forever; the item is the parent, the redline the child | "Venue can't meet this — negotiate an exception" |
| Advance done off-platform (phone call) | Either party records the confirmation with a source note; the record exists and is honest about its channel | "Confirmed by phone — [name], 14 Mar" |
| Both sides edit the same item simultaneously | Last-write-wins is unacceptable on a contested item: the second party is told the first is already answering rather than silently overwriting them, and neither side's answer is discarded without both being visible | Other side: "[Operator] is answering this now" |
| Same human owns both sides (Musician-promoter self-promoting at a room they operate) | Two-sided confirmation collapses — you cannot counter-confirm yourself. Item terminates as `self-confirmed`, badged distinctly, and counts as outstanding for evidentiary purposes in 24 | "Self-confirmed — you hold both sides of this item" |
| Diff re-runs while an item is being answered | Items have stable identity across re-runs, never positional. An in-flight answer is never discarded; a re-run that would change the item's basis queues behind the save | "Venue data changed — 2 new items" (existing answers intact) |
| Venue edits their 16 listing mid-advance | Change notice, never a silent re-run. Items already `confirmed` whose basis changed are flagged `confirmation may be stale` — not silently re-opened, because the confirmation was a real event | "[Venue] updated their spec — 3 confirmations may be stale. Re-check?" |
| Show is 3 days out with 9 items open | Sorted by band then severity; hard-outstanding shown as a separate gate number | "9 open · 2 hard-outstanding · 1 overdue" with a hard-blocker band pinned |
| Item's lead time is already blown at advance open (show booked T-7d, permit needs 10 working days) | Item opens `overdue` on creation. The system says so immediately rather than counting down to a deadline that has passed | "Overdue on creation — this permit needed 10 working days. Show is in 7." |
| Support act items | Scoped per act (18.02). The venue sees every act's items; each act sees only its own. Who advances the support — the headliner's Producer or the support directly — follows the deal, so the support's item ownership is set at bill creation, not assumed | Support act: "Your section — 4 items" |
| Two acts on the bill need the same resource (both want the house kit; one changeover) | **Only the venue can see this.** Contention is a venue-side item showing both acts' requirements; resolution goes back to each act's checklist separately, never cross-act | Operator: "Contended: house kit requested by 2 acts" |
| Date moves (postponement — 18.01) | Advance re-opens. Date-dependent items (curfew, staffing, doors, catering) revert to `open` with prior confirmations kept in history; date-independent items (console model, dock height) stay confirmed with a re-check prompt | Banner: "Date changed — 6 items need re-confirmation, 14 carried over" |
| Venue changes after confirm (show moves rooms) | The counterpart changes: every venue-side item invalidates and the diff re-runs against the new room; artist-side items survive | "New venue — 8 venue items cleared, 12 of yours carried over" |
| Show cancelled mid-advance (18.01) | Checklist freezes read-only and is preserved, not deleted — costs incurred and deposit disputes (17) read it | "Cancelled — advance preserved as record" |
| Generated item doesn't apply | Dismissible as `waived` with a mandatory reason; never deleted. Dismissal reasons feed generator quality | "Waived — acoustic set, no 3-phase needed" |
| Generation fails entirely | Degrades to a manual checklist, loudly. **Never blocks the advance** (CX-01) | "Couldn't generate items — add them manually / retry" |
| Venue-side assignee leaves / their address bounces | Item reassigns to the venue's account owner; if the venue has no account, it routes to the emailable path | Operator: "3 items were assigned to [name] — reassign" |
| Item answered by someone with no authority (the bar manager answers the console question) | Not preventable, only recordable. The confirmation names the attester and their stated role; the record is honest about weak attestation | "Confirmed by [name] — Bar Manager" |
| Answer arrives via the account-less link (18.03.04) | Recorded as `answered` with **weak** identity. Cannot self-confirm, cannot `waive`. The artist side must confirm | "Answered via link — identity unverified. Confirm?" |
| Venue answers, artist never confirms | An `answered` item remains outstanding at its derived severity until a terminal state. A `hard` item therefore continues to count in the clean-freeze gate; a negative answer is an explicit shortfall at the requirement's severity. | "6 answered, awaiting your confirmation"; hard items remain in the clean-freeze count until confirmed, self-confirmed, or waived |
| Redline proposed against an already-`confirmed` item | Item returns to `open` with the prior confirmation intact in history. The redline never deletes its parent (CX-02) | "Re-opened — [venue] can no longer meet this. History: confirmed 14 Mar." |
| Confirmed fact later found false | A confirmation is immutable (D-11). A correction is a **new event** appended, never an edit to the original | "Corrected 02 Apr — supersedes confirmation of 14 Mar" |
| Party types something confidential into an item thread | Thread messages stay in the thread. Only the resolution note propagates to the pack and the sheets (D-13) — but that propagation is not obvious, so the resolution field is labelled at the point of entry | Resolution field: "This appears on the advance sheet" |
| Network failure while answering | The answer is not recorded until it saves. Retry on failure; no optimistic terminal state. Show-day offline (domain Q-06) is out of scope here — advancing runs weeks out, at a desk | "Couldn't save — retry (this isn't recorded yet)" |
| Rider amended mid-advance (18.04.04) | The rider version at advance open is the item basis. An amendment re-runs the diff as a change notice; it does not silently rewrite settled items | "Rider updated to v3 — 2 new items, 1 settled item affected" |
| Festival advance (no venue listing; promoter-facing, shared stage) | The model holds — the counterparty generalises from "venue" to "the production-side counterparty" (DT-13). Generation source 1 degrades to whatever stage spec exists; sources 2–4 are unaffected | "Advancing with [promoter] — no room spec, 6 generated items" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Advance opened; generation in flight | Item list skeleton with side badges and the two counters greyed |
| Empty | `advancing` entered, nothing generated (venue not on platform, no rider, or all sources dry) | "No venue data — build the advance manually" + manual-add CTA + "Invite the venue" |
| Populated | Items generated | Two-sided list, banded by resolve-by, with per-side outstanding counts, the hard-outstanding gate number, and time-to-show |
| Error | Diff or requirement generation failed | "Couldn't generate items — add them manually / retry". The advance is usable; only generation failed |
| Partial | One side engaged, other silent — **or** generation ran with data gaps | "Your side: 0 open. Venue: 7 unopened, last nudged 2d ago." / "Generated from 2 of 4 sources — 18.16 unavailable" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The checklist should be authored by the TM | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — an authored checklist is just a to-do list, and every incumbent has one. The value is that WeJammin *knows* what needs advancing because it holds the rider and the venue's spec. Generated-first, manually-extended. |
| DT-02 | An item marked done by one side is done | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — this is the email chain's exact failure: "I told them" ≠ "they know". Two-sided confirmation is the mechanism, and it's the same counter-attestation pattern the Producer persona makes valuable in the credits domain. Self-assertion is weaker evidence than counter-confirmation. |
| DT-03 | The venue will refuse to engage — the wedge dies on Operator adoption | Adversarial lens | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS RISK — this is the feature's central threat and the reason the emailable fallback (18.03.04) exists. The Operator's own switching trigger (per `personas.md`: a show that failed on a preventable technical mismatch) is the counter-argument, but adoption is not assumed. Escalated to Q-01. |
| DT-04 | Flat lists are fine | Design instinct | ❌ REJECTED — 40 items three days out is not the same as 40 items three weeks out. Severity is risk × time-to-show; a curfew conflict at T-2d and a towel count at T-2d are not peers. Superseded in detail by DT-07. |
| DT-05 | Off-platform advancing must be representable | Domain expert lens | ✅ CONFIRMED — advancing happens on the phone constantly, and a system that can't record "confirmed by phone" pushes users back to the thread. Recording *that a fact was settled and by whom* is worth more than forcing the channel. |
| DT-06 | The venue's unit of work is one advance | Inherited framing | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the adoption hinge nobody designs for. The artist advances one date against many venues; **the Operator advances many artists against one room**, twenty in flight across a month. A per-date item list is the artist's shape imposed on the venue, and it is why every artist-built advancing tool loses the Operator. Their surface is a cross-date queue of their own dates: "what does anybody need from me this week?" This costs nothing to build and is the difference between DT-03 being fatal and being survivable. → D-12. |
| DT-07 | Advancing is one conversation on one clock | Sweep framing | ❌ REJECTED — it is several conversations on different clocks. Hospitality settles in a day. Technical takes weeks. **Compliance has statutory lead times that cannot be compressed at any price** — a permit with a 10-working-day notice period is a blocker at T-14d, not at T-2d, and no amount of urgency at T-3d recovers it. A single outstanding count that mixes a towel item with a licence application is arithmetic that lies. Items carry their own lead time; urgency is computed against that, not against a flat T-minus. → D-05. |
| DT-08 | The advance contact is the person who knows | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the booker takes the email; the house tech knows the console. Today the chain routes through the booker, who forwards, mis-transcribes, or answers from memory, and the artist can't tell which happened. Items must assign to a **named individual**, and the venue side must be able to reassign to the colleague who actually knows without either party losing the thread. The wrong-human problem is a large fraction of what makes advancing take six emails per fact. → D-08. |
| DT-09 | Two-sided confirmation always has two sides | Multi-hyphenate lens (`personas.md`: "one human routinely occupies three of them in one day") | ✅ CONFIRMED AS A REAL CASE — a Musician who self-promotes at a room they also operate holds both sides of the same item. So does a Producer who tour-manages an act into a venue where they are the house engineer. The mechanism collapses: you cannot counter-confirm yourself. The wrong answers are to block it (real, common, legitimate) or to let it silently produce a `confirmed` (a lie, and precisely the self-assertion DT-02 rejects). A distinct `self-confirmed` terminal state is the honest one, and it costs one enum value. → D-09. |
| DT-10 | Generation sources are the rider, the diff and compliance | Sweep framing | ❌ REJECTED as incomplete — it misses the source with the best signal-to-effort ratio in the whole feature. **The venue knows what artists always forget about their room**, and every real venue already has a standing advance form asking it: parking, support count, merch mechanics, curfew acknowledgement. Those questions are the venue's accumulated scar tissue and they generate nothing today. Lifting them off the 16 listing turns the Operator's existing paperwork into a generation source and gives them a reason to fill their listing out. → D-10. |
| DT-11 | Venue data correction is the post-show report's job (18.03.02 DT-04) | Cross-domain lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — 18.18's correction loop is real and load-bearing, but it runs at **T+1d**, which is one show too late for the band it just failed. The advance is a correction loop that runs at **T-14d**: the moment a venue answers "we sold the M32 last year", the platform has learned that its listing is wrong, weeks before anyone gets hurt. This is a second, earlier, cheaper truth loop that 18.03.02 DT-04 didn't see, and it costs one prompt. It must **prompt**, never auto-write — the Operator owns their listing (CX-03: three owners, none may write to another). |
| DT-12 | The checklist is a working document that dies at the freeze | Lifecycle instinct | ❌ REJECTED — it is the **evidence layer under the frozen pack**. 18.03.05 Q-02 asks whether the pack is evidence in a dispute; the pack is a snapshot, but the atom a dispute actually needs is "[named person] confirmed on [date] that the 3-phase existed". That is a checklist item, and it is exactly D-18's capture-at-source thesis applied to live production. Consequence: a confirmation is an immutable dated attestation. A correction appends; it never edits. Overwriting a confirmation destroys both the plan and the fact — domain D-08's rule, one level down. → D-11. |
| DT-13 | The festival case needs a variant of this feature (parent index Q-03) | Boundary lens | ❌ REJECTED — the model holds. Advancing's two sides are "the act" and "the production-side counterparty"; that a festival's counterparty is a promoter with a stage spec rather than an Operator with a room listing changes the *quality of generation source 1*, not the mechanism. Sources 2–4 (compliance, standing questions, manual) are unaffected, and a festival has more compliance items, not fewer. The residue is 16's: whether a festival stage is a listable space. → Q-04. Building a parallel festival checklist would duplicate every rule here to accommodate a different counterparty type. |
| DT-14 | Auto-confirm items the diff reports as `match` | Efficiency instinct | ❌ REJECTED, twice over. First: 31 auto-confirmed matches in a 41-item list is 31 rows of noise that train the TM to stop reading — the alert-fatigue death CX-03 already names. Second and worse: a `match` is a claim about *data*, and that data may be 14 months old (18.03.02 D-03). Auto-confirming it **launders venue data age into an attested fact** — the system would be manufacturing exactly the false confidence DT-11 exists to catch. Matches generate no item and live in the diff, where their data age is visible. Near-matches and `or similar` judgement calls are not matches and do generate items. → D-04. |
| DT-15 | Item threads should be the platform's message threads | Consolidation instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the thread must be first-class (the conversation genuinely happens there), but binding it to a general DM inbox leaks act-confidential context into a venue's messages and vice versa. Threads are item-scoped; notifications route *out* to the messaging/notification cross-cut; the inbox never routes *in*. The related trap: parties assume a thread is private, then a resolution note lands on a sheet a crew member reads. Only the resolution propagates, and the field says so where it is typed. → D-13. |

- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** ([../../17-live-booking-settlement/](../../17-live-booking-settlement/)) — commercial terms are never advance items (18.03.03 D-02). A cancelled advance's record feeds deposit/cost settlement.
- Touches **24 Trust & Safety** ([../../24-trust-safety/](../../24-trust-safety/)) — a confirmed item is the atom a dispute reads (DT-12). `self-confirmed` and weak-identity answers must be distinguishable there, or the evidence over-claims.
- Cross-cut: **Approval, Countersign & Attestation** — two-sided confirmation is the same mechanism as split sign-off (09) and credit counter-attestation (02). `self-confirmed` is a case that mechanism must carry everywhere, not just here.
- Cross-cut: **Notifications & Alerts** — the per-side aggregated escalation ladder with a different-human rung. The 1-nudge-per-side-per-48h cap is a policy the cross-cut should own, not this feature.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Items are generated from the diff/rider/requirements first, manually extended second | An authored checklist is a commodity; a generated one requires holding both datasets |
| D-02 | Completion requires counter-confirmation from the other side | "I told them" ≠ "they know" — the email chain's core failure |
| D-03 | Off-platform confirmations are first-class, with a source note | Forcing the channel pushes users back to the thread |
| D-04 | A diff `match` generates **no item**. Only shortfalls, unknowns, near-matches and judgement items do | Auto-confirming a match launders 14-month-old venue data into an attested fact — and 31 noise rows train the TM to stop reading (DT-14) |
| D-05 | Two numbers, not one: the **sort** is urgency (banded by resolve-by), the **gate** is severity (hard-outstanding count). Resolve-by = show date − the item's own lead time | A permit due in 30d is a blocker and not urgent; a towel count overdue is urgent and not a blocker. One number cannot carry both (DT-07) |
| D-06 | Severity is **derived from source + declared strictness**, not opinion. Compliance-sourced items (18.16) are `hard` and **non-downgradable by anyone**. Diff shortfalls inherit the requirement's strictness. An `unknown` on a hard requirement is `hard`. Hospitality defaults `soft`. The Producer may escalate soft→hard on any item, and may downgrade hard→soft **only** on act-sourced items | Resolves Q-02 (half 1). If a person can call a permit "soft", the gate means nothing; if nobody can adjust, the gate gets bypassed by leaving the tool |
| D-07 | A `hard` **venue-side** item counts in the hard-outstanding gate — so it blocks a **clean** freeze, not the freeze | Resolves Q-02 (half 2). If the venue hasn't confirmed the curfew, there is no plan. But 18.03.05 D-03 makes every freeze overridable with a stated reason, so "block" means "requires an override that goes on the record" — which keeps people inside the tool (18.03.05 DT-04) |
| D-08 | Items assign to a **named individual**, reassignable by the owning side without losing the thread | The booker takes the email; the house tech knows the console (DT-08) |
| D-09 | Where one human holds both sides, the item terminates as `self-confirmed` — a distinct state, never `confirmed` | You cannot counter-confirm yourself. The multi-hyphenate makes this common, not exotic (DT-09) |
| D-10 | The venue's standing advance questions from their 16 listing are **generation source 3** | The venue knows what artists always forget about their room; that knowledge already exists as their paper advance form (DT-10) |
| D-11 | A confirmation is an **immutable dated attestation**. Corrections append as new events; they never edit | The checklist is the evidence layer under the pack (DT-12). Domain D-08 — plan and fact are separate objects — one level down |
| D-12 | The Operator's primary surface is a **cross-date queue of their own dates**; the per-date list is the artist's shape | Twenty advances in flight across a month is the venue's real workload. This is the adoption hinge (DT-06) |
| D-13 | Item threads are item-scoped; only the **resolution note** propagates to the pack and the sheets, and the field says so where it is typed | Binding threads to a DM inbox leaks act-confidential context; silent propagation surprises people onto a printed sheet (DT-15) |
| D-14 | Escalation nudges are **per side, aggregated**, capped at 1 per 48h, and the ladder's rungs escalate to a *different human*, then to a phone number | 40 items must not send 40 nudges. More messages to the same non-responder is not escalation |
| D-15 | Resolve-by dates compute in the **venue's** local timezone | The show is there, and the statutory lead times are that jurisdiction's. A tour crossing timezones must not shift its own deadlines |
