## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Venue Capability Diff ↔ Advance Checklist

**Relationship**: The diff runs first and its output *is* most of the checklist. A `shortfall`
becomes an open item owned by the artist side ("bring, rent, or redline?"); an `unknown` becomes a
question owned by the venue side ("do you have 3-phase power?"). Manually-added items are the
minority. This inversion — generated-first, authored-second — is the sub-domain's whole thesis, and
it is why the checklist can't be lifted from an incumbent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees both, and works the diff *through* the checklist rather than as a separate view.
- **Operator**: never sees the diff as a diff — they see questions about their room and shortfalls raised against it.
- **Musician**: sees neither mechanism; sees the plain consequence on the day sheet.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The diff owns `match/shortfall/unknown`; the checklist owns `open/answered/confirmed/waived`. They are different axes on the same item and must not be collapsed — an item can be a confirmed shortfall (we know the room lacks it, and we've agreed to rent).
2. **Trigger chain**: Diff → item generation is synchronous on advance open, and re-runs on either side's data change. If generation fails, the checklist degrades to manual (18.03.01 Empty state) — it never blocks the advance.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator's write access to their listing in 16 changes what the diff produces for every artist advancing that room. A venue editing their spec mid-advance silently changes another party's checklist — this needs a change notice, not a silent re-run.
4. **Notification fan-out**: New items from a re-run must notify the owning side, or a re-run silently adds work nobody sees.
5. **State transition conflict**: A re-run while an item is being answered must not discard the answer. Items need identity stable across re-runs, not positional identity.

### CX-02: Advance Checklist ↔ Rider Redlines

**Relationship**: An item the venue cannot meet is not "still open" — it is a negotiation. The
checklist must offer the escalation into a redline explicitly, or the item festers as `open` until
show day, which is exactly today's failure. The redline's resolution feeds back as the item's
terminal state.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: accepts or counters; owns the outcome.
- **Operator**: raises the exception with an alternative — the honest half of the negotiation.
- **Musician**: read-only; finds out what was traded on their behalf. For an access item, they must be in the loop (18.03.03 D-03).
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The item is the parent; the redline is a child that resolves it. If a redline is withdrawn, the item reverts to `open` — the redline never deletes its parent.
2. **Trigger chain**: Item → redline is user-initiated, not automatic. An accepted redline sets the item to `waived`; a rejected one returns it to `open` with history.
3. **Permission intersection**: Access-rider items (18.04.03) restrict who can accept — the Producer's general authority does not extend to trading away a person's access provision.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Acceptance notifies the affected performers, not just the negotiating parties. Someone finding out at soundcheck is the failure.
5. **State transition conflict**: A redline accepted after freeze must route through change control (CX-04/05), not quietly resolve the item.

### CX-03: Rider Redlines ↔ Venue Capability Diff

**Relationship**: Accepted redlines are an overlay the diff must consult. Without this, the diff
re-reports a resolved shortfall on every run and the TM learns to ignore it — the alert-fatigue
death of any diff tool. The redline is per-date, so the overlay is per-date: the same rider against
the same venue next tour starts from the rider, not from last year's compromise.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees "shortfall → agreed exception" rather than a permanent red flag.
- **Operator**: sees their room's spec unchanged — the redline is about this date, not their listing.
- **Musician**: sees the agreed reality on the day sheet.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The rider (18.04) is the base and is immutable here; redlines overlay per date; the venue spec (16) is owned by the Operator. Three owners, one computed view — none may write to another.
2. **Trigger chain**: Redline accepted → diff re-runs → item resolves. Async is acceptable; the user must see the diff refresh.
3. **Permission intersection**: A redline cannot change the venue's listed capability. If the room genuinely doesn't have the console, that's a 16 correction (via 18.18), not a redline.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A recurring redline across dates should notify that the *rider* may be wrong (18.03.03 edge case 4) — the aggregate is a signal the per-date view can't see.
5. **State transition conflict**: If venue data changes after a redline is accepted (they bought the M32), the redline should surface as unnecessary rather than silently persist.

### CX-04: Advance Checklist ↔ Advance Freeze

**Relationship**: The checklist's outstanding count is the freeze's precondition, and the freeze is
what makes the checklist's state meaningful. Overriding the freeze with open items records the gap
rather than hiding it — so the pack is honest about what was never settled, which is what the
post-show report (18.18) and any dispute (24) need.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the only role that can freeze or override, and the accountable party.
- **Operator**: sees the frozen pack as authoritative for the night; can request changes, not apply them.
- **Musician**: sees "advanced" as a state; for a DIY act, they *are* the freezing party.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The freeze snapshots the checklist. Post-freeze item changes must go through change control, not edit the snapshot.
2. **Trigger chain**: Freeze → event record moves `advancing → advanced` (18.01) → sheets re-issue. Auto-freeze fires at `day-of` if it never happened.
3. **Permission intersection**: A hard venue-side item blocks a **clean** freeze, not the freeze itself; the Producer may record an override under 18.03.05 D-03. This is resolved by 18.03.01 D-06/D-07 and its resolved Q-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Freeze notifies everyone with a role on the date; it's the "this is the plan" moment.
5. **State transition conflict**: A late answer arriving *during* the freeze must not be lost — it becomes a change-control proposal against v1.

### CX-05: Advance Freeze ↔ Advance Sheet

**Relationship**: The sheet is the pack's projection; the pack's version is the sheet's version.
Every change-control event issues a new sheet and supersedes the old one at its live link. This is
the only mitigation available for the fact that a distributed artifact cannot be recalled.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: controls re-issue and sees delivery/acknowledgement status.
- **Operator**: receives the new version; the old link tells them it's superseded.
- **Musician**: gets the day sheet instead — same mechanism, different projection.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pack is immutable per version; the sheet is derived and never authoritative. If they disagree, the pack wins.
2. **Trigger chain**: Change approved → pack v(n+1) → sheet re-render → re-send to affected scopes only. Blanket re-sends train recipients to ignore them.
3. **Permission intersection**: Recipient scoping is enforced at generation (18.03.04 edge case 5) — a wrong-scope send is a confidentiality incident, not a formatting bug.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Only roles whose slice changed are re-notified.
5. **State transition conflict**: Paper copies cannot be superseded. The version stamp is the only answer, and it is a partial one.

### CX-06: Advance Sheet ↔ Advance Checklist (no-adoption path)

**Relationship**: Medium confidence, and the sub-domain's biggest strategic bet. If an account-less
Operator can answer checklist items through a link in an emailed sheet, the diff's data problem and
the domain's cold-start problem soften at once. If they can only read, the sheet is a nicer email
and the checklist stays one-sided.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: the entire point — engagement with zero adoption cost.
- **Producer**: sees venue answers land as items regardless of channel.
- **Musician**: unaffected.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — deferred per template guidance, except where the risk is structural)_:
3. **Permission intersection**: An unauthenticated link that can write is an authorisation surface. Scope, expiry, and rotation are `/create-prd-security` questions (18.03.04 Q-01), and the answer determines whether CX-06 is real.
2. **Trigger chain**: Link answer → item `answered` → artist confirms. Identity of an account-less answerer is weak evidence — it must be recorded as such, not as a confirmed party.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 18.03.02 Venue Capability Diff | 18.03.04 Advance Sheet | No direct interaction. The sheet renders the *checklist and pack*, not the diff. The diff reaches the sheet only through the items it generated — a transitive path, not a cross-cut. Modelling it as one would imply the sheet re-runs the diff, which it must not. |
| R-02 | 18.03.03 Rider Redlines | 18.03.04 Advance Sheet | Same transitivity. Accepted redlines appear on the sheet *because they are part of the frozen pack* (CX-05), not because the sheet consults redlines. |
| R-03 | 18.03.02 Venue Capability Diff | 18.03.05 Advance Freeze | The freeze reads the checklist's outstanding count, not the diff's output. A shortfall that has been resolved by renting is not a blocker; only its *item* state matters. Coupling the freeze to the diff would block freezes on shortfalls that were dealt with weeks ago. |
| R-04 | 18.03.03 Rider Redlines | 18.03.05 Advance Freeze | No independent interaction. Post-freeze redlines route through the checklist item (CX-02) into change control (CX-04). A direct edge would create a second, competing path into the frozen pack. |

> **Notes for agents:**
> - Rejected pairs here are all **transitivity rejections** — the temptation was to draw an edge for every pair that appears together in a workflow. The discipline is that a cross-cut requires shared state or a direct trigger, not co-occurrence.
> - CX-06 is the only Medium-confidence entry and the only one whose existence depends on an unresolved decision (`/create-prd-security`, Q-01 on 18.03.04).
