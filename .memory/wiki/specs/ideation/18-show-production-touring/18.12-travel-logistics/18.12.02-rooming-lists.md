# Feature: Rooming Lists

> **Number**: 18.12.02
> **Parent**: [Travel, Accommodation & Ground](./18.12-travel-logistics-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who sleeps where, and with whom. It looks like a trivial allocation table and is in fact one of the
more socially loaded objects on a tour: who gets a single, who shares, who cannot share, and who
must not be put next to whom. It also carries personal data that the hotel needs some of and nobody
else needs any of.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own preferences) | Sets their own preferences and constraints — "I don't share", "I need a quiet floor", "I'm up at 05:00". Sees their own room and roommate, not the whole allocation. |
| Producer | Full | Allocates rooms against what was booked, handles the constraints, and takes the blame. On a small tour this is a genuinely delicate five minutes. |
| Operator | **None** | Not their business at all. The hotel isn't on the platform (18.12 D-01), and the venue certainly has no claim to this. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. Where a specific person sleeps is a stalking risk, not a data point. |

## Behavior

Allocates the touring party (18.08.01) across the rooms booked (18.12.01), honouring per-person
constraints that live on the *person* rather than the tour. A mismatch — nine people, four twin
rooms — is detected because both numbers are known. Each person sees only their own row; the
Producer sees the grid.

### Happy Path

1. Hotel booked with 5 rooms; roster has 9 people.
2. Producer opens rooming; the allocation is proposed against known preferences.
3. Constraints are honoured — the singer has a single, two people who don't share are separated.
4. Producer confirms; each person sees their room and roommate on the day sheet.
5. The hotel gets a names list; nobody gets anyone else's preferences.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| More people than beds | Detected because both are known — the point of D-02 | "9 people, 8 beds" |
| Someone cannot share (medical, access, contractual) | A hard constraint, not a preference — and it may be an access requirement (18.04.03) | "[Name] requires a single — access rider" |
| Two people must not share | Real, common, and nobody wants to explain why | Constraint honoured; reason never surfaced |
| Party changes after the hotel is booked | Rooms are fixed, people aren't | "Party grew — 1 person unallocated" |
| Hotel needs names in advance | Some do; that's a data disclosure to a third party | "Hotel requires names — 9 will be shared" |
| Preference conflicts with what's available | Preferences aren't guarantees | "You asked for a single — none available tonight" |
| Local crew hired for a night | Not on the rooming list — they go home (18.08-cx CX-02) | Local labour excluded from the count |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rooming opened | Grid skeleton |
| Empty | No rooms booked | "No accommodation for this date" |
| Populated | Allocated | Producer: full grid. Everyone else: their own row. |
| Error | Save failed | "Couldn't save — retry" |
| Partial | Some unallocated | "7 of 9 allocated" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rooming is part of accommodation and needs no separate node | Classification instinct | ❌ REJECTED — booking four rooms is *logistics*; deciding who shares is *interpersonal*, and it carries person-owned constraints (medical, access, "I won't share with them") that the booking record has no business holding. Different data, different owner, different privacy model. The same reasoning that separates the access rider from hospitality (18.04 D-03). |
| DT-02 | Room preferences are tour data | Ownership lens | ❌ REJECTED — "I don't share" is a fact about a **person**, true across every band and tour they work on, exactly like their dietary requirement (18.04.02 D-03) and their monitor mix (18.05.03 D-03). It belongs on their profile in domain 01. This is the third feature in this domain to land on the same conclusion, which suggests it's a domain-wide pattern rather than three coincidences. |
| DT-03 | The Producer should see everyone's constraints | Authority instinct | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — they must see the *constraint* ("requires a single") to honour it; they should not necessarily see the *reason* (a medical condition, a relationship breakdown). Same disclosure discipline as the access rider (18.04.03 D-03): the requirement, not the reason. |
| DT-04 | This is trivial | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — trivial in mechanics, not in consequence. Get it wrong and someone spends three weeks unable to sleep, which on a tour is a health and performance issue rather than an inconvenience. But it's low-frequency and small-N; a spreadsheet genuinely does work. Honest `could`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Separated from accommodation booking | Logistics vs interpersonal; different data, owner and privacy model |
| D-02 | Preferences and constraints belong to the person (01), not the tour | True across every band they work on — the domain's third instance of this pattern |
| D-03 | The Producer sees the constraint, not the reason | Same discipline as the access rider |
| D-04 | Each person sees their own row; only the Producer sees the grid | The grid is a map of who couldn't be put with whom |
