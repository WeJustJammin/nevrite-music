## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Travel & Accommodation ↔ Rooming Lists

**Relationship**: Booking creates supply (5 rooms, 8 beds); rooming distributes demand (9 people
with constraints). The mismatch is detectable *only because both sides are known* — which is the
single strongest argument for either feature existing, given that 18.12.01's own DT-02 admits it may
be busywork. A spreadsheet holds the rooms and a different spreadsheet holds the people, and nobody
subtracts.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees both sides and owns the mismatch.
- **Musician**: sees their own row and their own booking; sets constraints that shape the allocation.
- **Operator**: **no access to either** — the hotel isn't on the platform and the venue has no claim.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The booking owns room count and type; the roster (18.08.01) owns people; rooming owns the mapping and owns nothing else. Three sources, and the allocation is the only thing this sub-domain actually creates.
2. **Trigger chain**: Party changes (18.08.01) → bed count vs head count re-checked → mismatch flagged. **The trigger originates outside both features** — adding a crew member two sub-domains away creates a rooming problem here. Same pattern as catering quantities (18.04-riders-cx CX-02), and the local-labour exclusion (18.08-cx CX-02) applies identically: a stagehand hired for a night does not need a bed, so `count(roster)` is the wrong number in both places.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Producer sees the grid; each person sees their row. A constraint that is actually an access requirement (18.04.03) carries stronger protections and must not be visible in the grid as a reason — only as a constraint (18.12.02 Q-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A re-allocation should notify only the people whose room changed. Everyone learning everyone's rooming is how the grid's confidentiality leaks by default.
5. **State transition conflict**: A booking change after allocation orphans rooms; a party change after booking orphans people. Both directions break, and the hotel is unmoved by either.

### CX-02: Ground Transport & Driver Hours → Travel & Accommodation

**Relationship**: Medium confidence, and an under-appreciated coupling. When a rest requirement makes
a drive illegal, the fix is usually a night somewhere — which means the compliance constraint
*creates a booking* that wasn't planned and wasn't budgeted. This is the same shape as the routing
fix that costs per diems (18.11-cx CX-03 §5): a legality problem resolves into a cost, two features
away, and nobody currently connects them.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: discovers that "add a travel day" means a hotel, per diems, and a day of not earning.
- **Musician**: sleeps somewhere, or doesn't.
- **Operator**: no visibility — though their late load-out may have caused it (18.12.03 Role Lens).
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — remainder deferred to Step 5)_:
2. **Trigger chain**: Rest breach → routing infeasible (18.11.02) → human adds a travel day → that day needs accommodation → the budget changes (18.13.02). A four-hop chain crossing three sub-domains, initiated by a load-out running late. **No incumbent connects hop 1 to hop 4**, and the platform is the only party that holds every link.
5. **State transition conflict**: The fix is a human decision (18.11-cx CX-03), so the accommodation is created manually. The system can surface that the travel day implies a hotel night; it cannot book it (18.12 D-01).

### CX-03: Rooming Lists → Ground Transport & Driver Hours

**Relationship**: Low confidence — a hypothesis worth recording rather than a designed flow. If the
driver is a band member (18.12.03 D-01, which the tours this platform targets guarantee), then their
sleep is a safety input, not a comfort preference. A driver put in a twin with someone who came in
at 04:00 is a fatigue risk that nobody models as one, because rooming is filed under "logistics" and
fatigue is filed under "compliance".

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: could, in principle, prioritise the driver's room.
- **Musician**: is the driver, and is also the person who didn't sleep.
- **Operator**: no visibility.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Low confidence — deferred per template guidance)_:

Not answered at this pass. The hypothesis is that the driver-tomorrow should be a rooming
constraint, in the same way "I don't share" is (18.12.02 D-02). It is recorded because the
connection is invisible in both feature files and is the kind of thing that only appears at an
intersection — but it may be over-engineering a five-person decision that a human makes correctly by
instinct. Flagged for Step 5, not designed now.

> **Notes for agents:**
> - **CX-02 is the finding worth carrying forward**: a late load-out (18.07) → an illegal drive (18.12.03) → an infeasible route (18.11.02) → an unplanned hotel and per diems (here, 18.13). Four sub-domains, one causal chain, and the platform is the only party holding every link. That chain is a better argument for this sub-domain's existence than any of its three features make alone.
> - Both rejected pairs are **splits from the sweep's bundling**, not merges. The sweep grouped by subject matter ("travel stuff"); the classification gate groups by owner, privacy model and stakes.
