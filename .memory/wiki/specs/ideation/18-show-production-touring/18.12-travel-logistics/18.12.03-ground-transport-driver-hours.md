# Feature: Ground Transport, Vehicles & Driver Hours

> **Number**: 18.12.03
> **Parent**: [Travel, Accommodation & Ground](./18.12-travel-logistics-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The van, the bus, who drives, and whether they are legally and physically able to. Driver hours are
the one item in this sub-domain with genuinely serious stakes: fatigue kills touring musicians with
grim regularity, and the person driving at 03:00 after a 01:00 load-out is usually the bass player
who also loaded the van.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own licence) | Declares whether they can drive and what they're licensed for — because on a small tour, **the band is the driver**. Sees whether tonight's drive is legal and whether it's them. |
| Producer | Full | Assigns drivers, tracks hours across the run, and sees when a leg breaks the rules. On a bus tour this is a hired driver's problem; on a splitter tour it's everyone's. |
| Operator | **None** | Not their business — though a late load-out from their room is what causes the breach (18.07.03 DT-05). They never see the consequence they created. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Vehicles are records (type, capacity, height, weight class) and drivers are roster members with
licences. Hours accumulate across legs and are checked against the applicable rule set — which is
the hard part, because "the applicable rule set" varies by territory, vehicle weight and whether
anyone is being paid. Hours **bind routing feasibility** (18.11.02), which makes this the most
consequential feature in the sub-domain.

### Happy Path

1. Producer records the vehicle: 9-seat splitter with a trailer.
2. Band members declare their licences; two can drive it.
3. Routing (18.11.02) checks each leg against drive time plus mandatory rest.
4. Leg 4→5 fails: 480 miles after a 01:00 load-out with one driver.
5. Producer adds a travel day, or a second driver — and the cost of that lands in the budget.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Load-out runs late | **Tonight's overrun is tomorrow's illegal drive** — the cross-day cascade | "Load-out 00:30 → 09:00 departure breaks rest" |
| Rules don't apply (small van, unpaid driver) | Genuinely exempt in many cases — and claiming otherwise is crying wolf | "Under 3.5t, not for hire — tachograph rules don't apply" |
| Rules apply and nobody knows | The dangerous case; the band assumes they're exempt | "Over 3.5t with a trailer — this is regulated" |
| Driver is also the bassist | Normal, and the reason fatigue is a music problem | "[Name]: bass, driver — on stage until 22:30, driving at 23:45" |
| Vehicle too tall for a ferry/tunnel | A real routing constraint the platform could know | "Trailer height — check ferry deck" |
| Hired bus with a professional driver | Their hours are the bus company's compliance problem, not ours | "Bus driver — hours tracked by [operator]" |
| Nobody logs actual driving | The rules are about actuals, not plans | "Planned only — actual hours unknown" |

### States

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Driver hours are a compliance nicety, low priority | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED as framing — **fatigue kills touring musicians**, and the mechanism is entirely predictable: play until 22:30, load out until 01:00, drive 300 miles. The platform is the only party that can see the load-out time and the next load-in and do the arithmetic. That said, it is `wont` for now (see MoSCoW) because of Q-01, not because it doesn't matter — which is an uncomfortable but honest position. |
| DT-02 | The platform should tell people when a drive is illegal | Product instinct | ⚠️ DANGEROUS — and it's Q-01. Getting the rule set wrong is worse than silence: a false "this is legal" is a liability we would have manufactured, and a false "this is illegal" trains people to ignore the check. EU/UK tachograph, US DOT HOS, and the sub-3.5t not-for-hire exemption are three different regimes with different answers for the same van. Without confidence in the rule set, the honest feature is drive *time* and rest *gaps* as facts, with no legality claim. |
| DT-03 | Drivers are hired professionals | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — **on the tours this platform is for, the band drives.** The multi-hyphenate (`personas.md`) is the bassist who loads the van, sells the merch and drives it home. That's exactly why the risk concentrates here: a professional driver has rest rules and hasn't just played a show. Modelling for hired drivers models the wrong tour. |
| DT-04 | Hours are a travel concern only | Boundary lens | ❌ REJECTED — they **bind routing feasibility** (18.11.02 D-02). The gap between load-out and next load-in minus mandatory rest is what makes a route legal or not, which makes this feature a hard input to a check in another sub-domain. It also receives the slippage cascade (18.07.03 DT-05): a late show tonight is an illegal drive tomorrow. |
| DT-05 | Planned hours are enough | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the rules govern **actual** driving, and actuals require logging that nobody does at 03:00. A plan-only check is advisory at best, which further undermines any legality claim (DT-02). This is the same "who updates it?" problem as live slippage (18.07.03 DT-03), with legal consequences. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Model for band-as-driver, not hired professionals | The multi-hyphenate bassist who loaded the van is who this risk lands on |
| D-02 | Rest gaps bind routing feasibility | Load-out → rest → load-in is what makes a route legal, and it lives here |
| D-03 | Without a confident rule set, report facts (drive time, rest gap) and make no legality claim | A wrong "this is legal" is a liability we manufactured; a wrong "illegal" trains people to ignore it |
