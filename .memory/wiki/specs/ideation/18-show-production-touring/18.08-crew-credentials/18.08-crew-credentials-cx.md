## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Crew Roster → Credentials & Passes

**Relationship**: A pass exists because a person is on the roster, and its access level is a
function of their role rather than a decision. Backline tech → stage + backstage. Merch → foyer +
backstage. The derivation is what keeps this from being a form someone fills in nine times per
date, and it means a roster change silently changes who can go where.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: reviews derived levels and handles the few genuine exceptions.
- **Operator**: supplies the area map their building actually has, and enforces at the door.
- **Musician**: sees their own pass; notices it only when it fails.
- **Fan**: no visibility — a guest pass is a 19 object, not a credential (18.08.02 Role Lens).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The roster owns people and roles; the venue (16) owns the area map; the pass is derived from both and owns neither. If the venue's area map changes, every issued pass's meaning changes without the pass changing.
2. **Trigger chain**: Roster entry added → level derived → pass issuable. Removing a person revokes the pass **in the system only** — a physical laminate is beyond recall (18.08.02 edge case 6). The revocation is honest about its own limits.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Producer can issue; the Operator can override at their door. Our system is advisory (18.08.02 D-02), which means a "valid" pass here can be refused there, and the product must never imply otherwise.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A pass issued to a late hire must reach them on a phone at a loading dock, in seconds.
5. **State transition conflict**: A person on the roster for dates 3–9 has a pass valid for dates 3–9. If their range changes, the pass's scope must follow — and a laminate already printed cannot.

### CX-02: Local Crew Hire → Crew Roster

**Relationship**: The hire is the *acquisition*; the roster is the *record*. Once confirmed, a local
stagehand is indistinguishable from touring crew for scheduling purposes — they get a call time
derived from the schedule and they appear in the head count. But they differ in exactly one
consequential way: they don't eat the catering, sleep in the rooms, or take a per diem, so they must
**not** inflate the party-size derivations that hospitality, rooming and finance consume.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: hires and rosters in one flow.
- **Operator**: often the source of the hire, and sees them on site.
- **Musician**: unaffected.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The hire owns the commercial terms (rate, hours); the roster owns presence and call. One person, two records, one identity in domain 01.
2. **Trigger chain**: Hire confirmed → roster entry created → call derived → pass issuable → after the show, hours confirmed → payment. The chain crosses into 05 (booking) and the payouts cross-cut, and back.
3. **Permission intersection**: **The important one.** A local hire is on the roster but must not be in the party-size roll-up that drives catering (18.04.02 D-01), rooming (18.12.02) and per diems (18.13.01). A naive `count(roster)` would cater for three extra stagehands and book them hotel rooms. The roster's head count needs a *type* dimension — touring party vs local labour — or 18.08.01 D-04's downstream derivations break the moment this feature is used. **This is a real modelling requirement neither feature file states alone.**
4. **Notification fan-out**: The venue should know who is arriving and when; the hire needs their call and their pass.
5. **State transition conflict**: A hire cancelling at 06:00 on show day leaves a hole in the load-in with no recovery time. The roster shows the gap; nothing can fill it.

### CX-03: Local Crew Hire → Credentials & Passes

**Relationship**: Medium confidence, and the case that justifies 18.08.02's existence. Nine people
who know each other don't need a credential system. A stagehand hired at 15:00 whom nobody at the
door has ever seen absolutely does — and they need it issued to a phone, at a dock, in under a
minute, scoped to load-in only so they aren't wandering the building at 23:00.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: issues in seconds from a phone.
- **Operator**: receives an updated door list, or refuses because their own system doesn't know this person.
- **Musician**: unaffected.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — remainder deferred to Step 5)_:
2. **Trigger chain**: Hire confirmed → pass issued scoped to their hours, not the full date. A load-in stagehand with an all-day AAA is a small security failure the derivation prevents.
3. **Permission intersection**: Where the venue runs its own credential system (18.08.02 D-02), this chain terminates at a list we hand over — the pass we issue is advisory and the door may not honour it. That caps the feature's value at exactly the moment it matters most, and it is the same Operator-adoption bet as 18.03.01 DT-03.
