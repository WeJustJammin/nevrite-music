# Feature: Credentials, Passes & Access Levels

> **Number**: 18.08.02
> **Parent**: [Crew, Call Times & Credentials](./18.08-crew-credentials-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who may go where: laminates, day passes, AAA, working passes, guest wristbands. The access level is
not a decision so much as a derivation — a backline tech needs the stage, a merch seller needs the
foyer, a guest needs neither. Today it is a sheet of stickers and a bouncer's judgement.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (own) | Sees what their pass gets them, which matters mostly when it doesn't — being stopped at your own show is a genuinely common indignity. |
| Producer | Full | Issues passes, sets access levels, and manages the guest list allocation against the venue's. The person the door calls when someone's pass doesn't work. |
| Operator | **Config (venue access rules)** | Their building, their rules — which areas exist, which are restricted, whether the venue's own credential system takes precedence. Enforces at the door. |
| Fan | None | A fan with a guest pass holds a **ticket-domain** object (19), not a credential. The distinction matters: a guest is admitted to the audience; a credential holder is admitted to work. |

## Behavior

Access levels derive from the roster's role (18.08.01) against the venue's area map — a backline
tech gets stage + backstage, a merch seller gets foyer + backstage. Passes are scoped by date. The
mechanism is the same as a ticket (issue → present → scan → admit), which is why it is recorded as
a cross-cut with 19 rather than built twice.

### Happy Path

1. Roster is set; access levels derive from each role.
2. Producer reviews, adjusts exceptions, and issues passes for the date or the tour.
3. The venue receives the credential list as part of the advance.
4. People present passes at the door and are admitted to their areas.
5. A local hire added at 15:00 gets a pass immediately, scoped to load-in only.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue runs its own credential system | Ours is advisory; theirs enforces. Do not pretend otherwise | "This venue issues its own passes — this list is for their door" |
| Person added at 15:00 | Must be issuable instantly, on a phone, at a loading dock | "Pass issued — show this at the stage door" |
| Guest list vs rider allowance vs box office allocation | Three numbers, three owners (18.04.02 Q-02) | "Rider says 10, box office allocated 6" |
| Pass lost / person turns up without a phone | Paper and a name on a list must always work — the failure mode is a person outside in the cold | "Door list — check by name" |
| Access needed for an assistant/PA | An access rider (18.04.03) implies a credential the head count didn't plan for | "[Name]'s access rider requires an assistant pass" |
| Someone leaves mid-tour but keeps a laminate | Revocation is meaningless on a physical laminate | "Revoked in the system — the laminate still exists" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Credentials opened | Pass list skeleton |
| Empty | No passes issued | "No passes" + issue-from-roster CTA |
| Populated | Passes exist | Per-person pass with access level and date scope |
| Error | Issue failed | "Couldn't issue — retry" |
| Partial | Some roster entries without passes | "3 people have no pass" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Credentials and tickets are different systems | Domain boundary instinct | ❌ REJECTED — both are "this identity may enter this area during this window", presented at a door and checked. The **mechanism is identical**; only issuance differs (a ticket is bought, a pass is granted by role). Recorded as the **Event Access Control & Credentialing** cross-cut with 19 (D-04 at sub-domain level). Building two scanners for one door would be an obvious duplication. |
| DT-02 | We control access | Ownership instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **it's the venue's building and the venue's door staff.** Our credential list is at best an input to their process; many venues have their own laminate system and a security contractor who has never heard of us. Designing as if we enforce would produce a system that is confidently wrong at the one moment it's tested. Advisory by default, enforcing only where the Operator adopts. |
| DT-03 | Access levels are configured per person | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — they derive from role against the venue's area map, and the exceptions are few. Asking a TM to configure nine people's area access per date is how the sticker sheet won. |
| DT-04 | This is a low-value convenience feature | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — for the touring core, yes: nine people who know each other don't need a system to know they're allowed backstage. Its real value is at the **edges** — the local hired at 15:00, the dep nobody at the door recognises, the assistant an access rider requires (18.04.03). Those are exactly the people who get stopped, and they're the ones a sticker sheet fails. Genuine but narrow — hence a `could`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Same mechanism as tickets; recorded as a cross-cut with 19 | Both are "this identity may enter this area now", scanned at the same door |
| D-02 | Advisory by default — the venue's door enforces | It's their building and their security contractor; pretending otherwise fails at the door |
| D-03 | Access levels derive from role against the venue's area map | Per-person configuration per date is how the sticker sheet won |
