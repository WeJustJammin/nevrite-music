# Feature: Crew Roster, Roles & Call Times

> **Number**: 18.08.01
> **Parent**: [Crew, Call Times & Credentials](./18.08-crew-credentials-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Who is on the tour, in what role, and what time they're called on each date. It is a small object
with a surprisingly wide blast radius: the head count it produces silently determines how many
people the venue caters for, how many hotel rooms are booked, and how many per diems are paid.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (own call) | Sees their own call time and where to be. Band members are roster entries too — the multi-hyphenate reality means the bassist is often also the merch seller and the driver. |
| Producer | Full | Owns the roster. Adds people and roles, sets which dates each person is on, and sees the call sheet resolve from the schedule. Is themselves a roster entry. |
| Operator | Read-only (who's on site) | Sees the touring party size and roles for their date — they need it for catering, credentials and knowing how many people are in their building. Not the personal data behind it. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

A person is on the roster for a date range, with a role. Call times **derive** from the run of show
(18.07.01) by role — a backline tech is called at load-in, a merch seller at doors — rather than
being typed per person per day. Party size rolls up and is consumed by hospitality, rooming and per
diems, which means a roster edit has consequences elsewhere.

When one person's derived calls collide — the bassist called at 14:00 for backline load-in and
14:00 for merch load — the roster **surfaces the conflict, it does not block it**. The person, not
the system, decides which role wins on the day: a double-call is often legitimate (they load
backline first, merch after), and a hard block would fight the multi-hyphenate reality the model
exists to serve. The conflict is shown as a flag on both call entries and on the day sheet, and
clears when a call is manually overridden or the schedule moves one of them apart.

### Happy Path

1. Producer builds the roster: 4 band, 1 FOH, 1 monitor, 1 backline, 1 TM, 1 merch.
2. Roles are assigned; call rules derive per role from the schedule.
3. Party size (9) flows to hospitality quantities, rooming and per diems.
4. Each person sees their own call time on the day sheet.
5. Schedule slips 90 minutes; calls shift automatically and everyone is re-notified.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Roster changes after the advance | Party size changed → catering, rooming, per diems all stale | "Party grew to 9 — catering says 6, 4 rooms booked" |
| Person is on some dates, not others | Date-ranged membership, not tour-wide | "[Name]: dates 3–9 only" |
| One person, several roles | The multi-hyphenate reality — the bassist drives the van and sells merch | "[Name]: Bass · Driver · Merch" — three call times, possibly conflicting |
| Dep covers for a member | The roster changes for one date; the *performance* record needs it too (18.06.04) | "[Dep] covering bass — 14 Mar only" |
| Local crew hired for a date | Appears on the roster via 18.08.03 with a scoped date and access | "3 local stagehands — load-in only" |
| Call time conflict across roles | One person cannot be called at load-in and at doors simultaneously | "[Name] is called at 14:00 (backline) and 14:00 (merch load)" |
| Crew member leaves mid-tour | Roster, credentials, rooming and per diems all detach from that date forward | "Removing [name] from date 6 — affects 3 bookings" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Roster opened | Person list skeleton |
| Empty | No crew | "Just the band" + add-crew CTA |
| Populated | Roster exists | People with roles, date ranges, derived calls, party size |
| Error | Save failed | "Couldn't save — retry" |
| Partial | Roles without call rules | "2 roles have no call rule — their times won't derive" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Call times are typed per person per day | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — a call time is a *function of the schedule* ("backline: load-in minus 15"). Typed calls are stale the second the truck is late, which is exactly when a call time matters. Derive by role and they follow slippage (18.07.03) for free. Same discipline as the input list and day sheet: derive, don't transcribe. |
| DT-02 | The roster is a list of names | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it is the **head count that silently governs three other sub-domains**. Adding a merch seller changes what the venue must cater (18.04.02), how many rooms are booked (18.12.02), and how much cash the float needs (18.13.01). The roster is the domain's most quietly load-bearing object, and the "catering for 5, party of 9" failure is entirely caused by nobody connecting them. |
| DT-03 | One person, one role | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — `personas.md` is explicit that the multi-hyphenate is music's defining identity characteristic, and it is at its most extreme on a small tour: the bassist drives, sells merch, and techs their own rig. Modelling one role per person forces the exact "are you an artist OR a seller?" failure the persona doc warns kills the product. Several roles, several call times, and the conflicts between them are real (edge case 6). |
| DT-04 | Crew are a persona | Persona lens | ❌ REJECTED per D-19 — but not comfortably. A TM and a monitor engineer are Producers by the persona doc's own definition ("owns the room, coordinates contributors, de-facto project manager"). A **local stagehand** is none of the four. The model holds for the touring core and strains at the edges — escalated as sub-domain Q-01 rather than papered over. |
| DT-05 | The roster is the same as the band lineup | Boundary lens | ❌ REJECTED — the band (an *entity* in domain 01, per `personas.md`) is who the act is; the roster is who is on this tour, which includes deps, crew and locals, and excludes the member who sat this one out. Conflating them corrupts both, and it's precisely why performance personnel must be captured per-show (18.06.04 D-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Call times derive from the schedule by role | A typed call is stale the moment the truck is late — precisely when it matters |
| D-02 | One person may hold several roles with several call times | The multi-hyphenate is the persona model's central fact; one-role-per-person breaks it |
| D-03 | The roster is not the band lineup | The band is an entity in 01; the roster is who's on this tour, deps and locals included |
| D-04 | Party size is a published input, and edits to it must be visible downstream | Adding one person silently changes a rider obligation two sub-domains away |
| D-05 | Multi-role call conflicts are **surfaced, not blocked** — the person decides which role wins on the day | Resolves Q-02. A hard block fights the multi-hyphenate reality; a double-call is often legitimate. The system flags both entries and the day sheet; the flag clears on manual override or schedule move |
