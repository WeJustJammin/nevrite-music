# Feature: Local Crew & Stagehand Hire

> **Number**: 18.08.03
> **Parent**: [Crew, Call Times & Credentials](./18.08-crew-credentials-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Hiring the people you don't travel with: stagehands for load-in, a local monitor engineer, a driver,
a merch seller. Every touring act needs bodies in every city, and the current process is asking the
venue "do you know anyone?" — which works until it doesn't, at 08:00, in a city where you know
nobody.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not their job — the TM hires. A musician who *works* as local crew engages through the services marketplace (05), where they're the seller, not through this. |
| Producer | Full | Posts the need, picks people, sets the rate and the call, and pays them. The hiring party. |
| Operator | **Full (often supplies them)** | Venues frequently *are* the source of local crew — they have a house crew list and a relationship with the local stagehand pool. They can propose or supply crew against the act's need. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

A need is a scoped brief: role, date, hours, rate, skills. It resolves either through the venue
(who usually knows someone) or through the services marketplace (domain 05) — deliberately **not**
through a hiring rail built here. A confirmed hire becomes a roster entry (18.08.01) with a call
time and a scoped credential (18.08.02).

### Happy Path

1. Advance reveals the act needs 3 stagehands for load-in at a date.
2. Producer posts the need — role, hours, rate.
3. The venue proposes their house crew; alternatively, local providers respond via 05.
4. Producer confirms; the hires land on the roster with derived calls and scoped passes.
5. Hours are confirmed after the show; payment runs through the platform's payouts rail.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No local supply in this city | The cold-start reality for most of the map | "No providers in [city] — ask the venue?" |
| Venue mandates their own crew | Common and contractual — the act cannot bring or hire their own | "This venue requires house crew — 4 stagehands, £X" |
| Hire doesn't show up at 08:00 | The whole point of the load-in; there is no recovery time | "1 of 3 hasn't checked in — call time was 30 min ago" |
| Hours overrun | Rate is hourly; the settled amount differs from the booked one | "Booked 4h, worked 6.5h — approve £X?" |
| Union/collective agreement rules apply | Real in many territories, with rates and break rules the platform doesn't know | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Employment status of a one-night stagehand | Worker vs contractor is a legal question with tax consequences | Routed — see Q-02 |
| Same person hired repeatedly across tours | They accumulate a work history — exactly the thesis (D-18) applied to crew | "Worked 6 shows for you — rehire?" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Local crew hire needs its own hiring rail | Naive scoping | ❌ REJECTED — it is **structurally identical to a services marketplace booking** (domain 05): a scoped brief, a rate, a person, a delivery, a payment. Building a parallel rail here would fork the marketplace, duplicate payouts and reviews, and split one person's work history in two. This feature is a *demand generator* for 05, not a marketplace. |
| DT-02 | The venue is a competitor for this supply | Boundary lens | ❌ REJECTED — the venue is the **best source**. They know the local pool, they've worked with them, and they often mandate house crew contractually (edge case 2). Modelling the Operator as a supplier of crew rather than an obstacle is both realistic and a reason for them to engage — which matters, because Operator adoption is the domain's central risk (18.03.01 DT-03). |
| DT-03 | This is a small convenience | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — small for the act, but it is a **genuine income stream for working musicians**, who are exactly the Musician persona. The drummer who techs, drives and stagehands between their own gigs is the multi-hyphenate the whole product is built for, and this is one of the portfolio income streams `personas.md` describes. It's `could` for the tour, but it feeds 05 and 23 with real work. |
| DT-04 | The platform can just facilitate introductions | Simplification instinct | ❌ REJECTED — an introduction that ends in a cash payment off-platform captures nothing: no work history, no payout record, no reputation. The thesis says the fact must be captured where the work happens. An introduction rail is the Musician anti-persona's off-platform dodge, designed in. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Uses the services marketplace rail (05); does not build a hiring rail | Identical shape — a fork would duplicate payouts, reviews and split one person's work history |
| D-02 | The Operator is modelled as a crew *source*, not an obstacle | They know the local pool, often mandate it, and it gives them a reason to engage |
| D-03 | Payment runs on-platform, not as an introduction | An off-platform cash payment captures no history, no reputation, no proof of work |
