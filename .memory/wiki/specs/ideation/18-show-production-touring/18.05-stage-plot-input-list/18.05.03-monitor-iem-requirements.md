# Feature: Monitor & IEM Requirements

> **Number**: 18.05.03
> **Parent**: [Stage Plot & Input List](./18.05-stage-plot-input-list-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who hears what, and how: how many discrete mixes, wedges or in-ears, which channels are in each
mix, and whether the room can send that many. Monitors are the single most common cause of a bad
night for a performer, and the requirement is usually a sentence in a rider ("4 mixes please")
that no diff can check.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own mix) | Declares their own mix — "mostly my vocal, a bit of kick and bass, no guitars" — and whether they're on a wedge or IEMs. This is the one production document that directly determines whether they can perform. |
| Producer | Full | Owns the monitor spec, sets total mix count, and diffs it against the room's monitor provision. Often *is* the monitor engineer on a small tour. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads it to know how many sends and wedges to prepare, and whether they need a monitor desk and an engineer at all. Answers via redline if they can't meet the mix count. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

A mix is defined as a set of input list channels (18.05.02) sent to a position on the plot
(18.05.01) — which is why it can only exist here. Mix count is diffed against the room's aux sends
and wedge inventory; IEM use shifts the requirement from wedges to RF and a split. Per-person mixes
persist across tours as a member's default.

### Happy Path

1. Input list exists; Producer creates mixes and assigns positions from the plot.
2. Each member sets their own mix contents and wedge/IEM preference.
3. Mix count is diffed — 6 mixes against a room with 4 aux sends: shortfall.
4. Producer redlines to 4 shared mixes, or the venue hires in a monitor desk.
5. The agreed spec is in the frozen pack; the monitor engineer works from it at soundcheck.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| More mixes than the room has sends | Shortfall; requires consolidation or hire | "6 mixes, 4 sends — merge or hire a desk?" |
| Act on IEMs, venue assumes wedges | Different requirement entirely — RF, split, no wedges needed | "IEMs — venue supplies split, no wedges required" |
| Venue has a monitor desk but no engineer | Two separate facts; both needed | "Desk yes, monitor engineer no — who's mixing?" |
| Shared bill, different monitor needs | Changeover implications; mixes must be re-patched between acts | "3 acts, 3 monitor setups — 20 min changeover is tight" |
| Member's default mix conflicts with this act | Defaults are a starting point, not authority | "Your usual mix references channels this act doesn't have" |
| Stereo IEM mixes double the send count | Counting error that fails at soundcheck | "4 stereo mixes = 8 sends, not 4" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Monitor view opened | Mix list skeleton |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The sweep's model is incomplete without monitors | Deep Think — domain expert lens | ✅ CONFIRMED, node created — the candidate list has a stage plot and an input list, which describe what the *audience* hears getting into the console. Nothing describes what the *band* hears, and that is what determines whether they can play. "4 mixes" as rider prose is undiffable; as structured mixes it's an integer against the room's aux sends. It's the third document of the three that real productions maintain, and the sweep had two. |
| DT-02 | Monitor spec belongs in the technical rider | Placement lens | ❌ REJECTED — a mix is *defined* as channels (input list) sent to a position (plot). It cannot be authored where those two don't exist. Putting it in the rider makes it prose again, which is the problem. |
| DT-03 | Mix count is the whole requirement | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — stereo IEM mixes consume two sends each (edge case 6), a wedge and an in-ear are entirely different provisions, and a desk without an engineer is not a monitor system. "4 mixes" hides at least three distinct facts, each of which fails differently. |
| DT-04 | Per-person mixes are act-scoped | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — a drummer's mix preference is *theirs* and travels across the three bands they're in, exactly like the multi-hyphenate pattern in `personas.md`. Person-level defaults, act-level overrides. This is the same person-owns-it structure as the access rider, for a much lower-stakes reason. |

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
