## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Expense & Receipt Capture ↔ Per Diems & Float

**Relationship**: The float is cash out of an envelope; receipts are the only account of where it
went. Without capture, "the float has £860 left" is a measurement of an envelope, not a financial
record — and the £140 gap is a feeling the TM has rather than a fact anyone can act on. This edge is
why 18.13.03 exists at all: it is not an expenses feature, it is the float's reconciliation
mechanism.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: carries the float, captures the spend, owns the reconciliation.
- **Musician**: occasionally spends float cash and photographs a receipt; sees their own reimbursements.
- **Operator**: no access — though a buyout they paid is float income (18.13.01 Role Lens).
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The float owns the balance; receipts own the explanations. Neither is authoritative about the other — the reconciliation is precisely the act of discovering they disagree, and the **variance must survive as a first-class fact** rather than being absorbed to make the numbers tie. A system that silently balances is lying.
2. **Trigger chain**: Receipt captured → float balance drops → variance recomputed. **The chain must complete offline** (18.13.03 D-01), because the capture happens at a petrol station and the whole feature dies if it needs signal at the moment of spend.
3. **Permission intersection**: A Musician capturing a receipt against the float is spending the TM's money — a Config-level action in one feature mutating a balance the Producer owns. That needs to be visible to the Producer, not silent.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A reimbursement owed to someone should tell them. Being owed £24 and forgetting is the small, common failure this closes.
5. **State transition conflict**: A receipt captured offline and synced days later lands in a float that has since been reconciled. Does it reopen the reconciliation? Unresolved — and real, because offline capture guarantees late arrivals.

### CX-02: Expense & Receipt Capture ↔ Tour Budget vs Actual

**Relationship**: 18.13.02's central claim (DT-02) is that the P&L is a **byproduct** — actuals
accrue from data the platform already holds. That claim is true for settlement, merch and rentals,
which arrive automatically. It is **false for cash costs**, which arrive only if a human
photographs a receipt. This edge is therefore where the consolidation dividend either lands or
doesn't, and it depends on a tired person at a pump.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the person whose diligence determines whether the P&L is real.
- **Musician**: sees a band-scoped P&L whose accuracy they can't assess.
- **Operator**: no access.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Receipts own individual costs; the budget owns categories and totals. An uncategorised receipt is a cost the budget can count but not place — countable, unattributable.
2. **Trigger chain**: Receipt → categorised → budget line accrues → variance updates. If categorisation is deferred (it will be), the budget is accurate in total and wrong per line, which is the worse of the two failures because per-date is the actionable grain (18.13.02 D-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Band members see band-scoped totals (18.13.02 D-04) that include costs captured by others. They inherit a number's accuracy without any visibility into its provenance — which is uncomfortable when they're profit partners.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A budget line blowing out mid-tour should surface while there's still time to act — that's the entire value over an end-of-tour reconstruction.
5. **State transition conflict**: A late-synced receipt changes a variance the Producer already acted on. The budget is provisional throughout, and should say so rather than presenting a settled number.

### CX-03: Per Diems & Float ↔ Tour Budget vs Actual

**Relationship**: Per diems are one of the very few tour costs knowable **in advance and exactly**:
rate × days × people, all derived (18.13.01 D-01). That makes them the budget's most reliable line
and a useful anchor — everything else (fees, merch, rentals, fuel) is an estimate. The float's
unaccounted variance is the mirror image: the least knowable cost on the tour, sitting in the same
P&L.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees both the certainty and the gap.
- **Musician**: sees their own per diem, and a band-scoped total that includes everyone's.
- **Operator**: no access.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The roster (18.08.01) owns people and dates; per diems compute from it; the budget consumes the total. A roster change re-derives the per diem line **and** the catering quantities (18.04-riders-cx CX-02) **and** the rooming allocation (18.12-cx CX-01) — one edit, three sub-domains. The local-labour exclusion (18.08-cx CX-02) applies here too: stagehands don't get per diems.
2. **Trigger chain**: Roster change → per diem total re-derives → budget line moves. Adding one person for six days is a £150 budget change nobody typed.
3. **Permission intersection**: Per diems are self-scoped (18.13.01 D-03) but their *total* appears in a band-scoped P&L. The aggregate is visible where the individual amounts are not — which is correct, and only works if the P&L never breaks the line down by person.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None distinct; the budget's variance alerts cover it.
5. **State transition conflict**: Per diems are paid daily; the budget is a running total. They're consistent by construction, which makes this the sub-domain's only edge that can't drift.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 18.13.01 Per Diems & Float | 18.13.03 Expense & Receipt Capture (per diems as expenses) | Considered: is a per diem payment just another float expense with a receipt? Rejected — a per diem is a **derived entitlement** (rate × days × person) paid to a known person on the roster, not a discretionary spend needing justification. Modelling it as an expense would require a "receipt" for every person every day and would bury the one cost line the budget knows exactly (CX-03). They both reduce the float; that is co-occurrence, not shared state. |
| R-02 | 18.13.02 Tour Budget vs Actual | 18.13.01 Per Diems & Float (budget setting the PD rate) | Considered: should the budget constrain the per diem rate — "you can only afford £18/day"? Rejected — the rate is a **commitment to people**, often contractual and always a morale fact, not a variable the budget gets to squeeze mid-tour. The budget reports what per diems cost; it does not set them. Letting it would model a tour where the spreadsheet cuts the crew's food money, which is both wrong and the kind of thing a product should decline to make easy. |

> **Notes for agents:**
> - **CX-02 is the honest weak point of this sub-domain.** 18.13.02's "byproduct P&L" claim — the clearest expression of the consolidation thesis (D-18) in this domain — is only true for the costs that arrive automatically. Cash costs depend on a human with a phone at a petrol station. That dependency deserves to be visible rather than assumed away.
> - CX-03's synthesis question 1 records the domain's widest silent coupling: **one roster edit changes per diems, catering and rooming across three sub-domains.** The roster (18.08.01 D-04) is the quietest load-bearing object in domain 18.
