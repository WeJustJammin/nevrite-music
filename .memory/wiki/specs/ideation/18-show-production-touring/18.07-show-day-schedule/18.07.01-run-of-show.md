# Feature: Run of Show & Day Schedule Builder

> **Number**: 18.07.01
> **Parent**: [Show Day Schedule & Timing](./18.07-show-day-schedule-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The show day as one ordered timeline: load-in, build, soundchecks in reverse bill order, dinner,
doors, sets, changeovers, curfew, load-out. Most of it is not typed — it is *computed* from facts
the platform already holds (set durations from the setlist, changeover from the bill, load-in and
curfew from the venue) and adjusted by the two parties who own the day.

It exists because every number on the show day is arithmetic on data that is currently scattered
across a PDF, a WhatsApp thread and a TM's memory — and because the one document every person on
site works from is the one nobody owns. It is the object the crew roster (18.08.01), the day sheet
(18.10), the curfew check (18.07.02) and the whole of show-day slippage (18.07.03) derive from.
Get this wrong and eight other features inherit the error.

> **Merged**: this node consolidates the sweep's candidate 08, which listed "Run of Show", "Set
> Times", "Changeover" and "Load-in/Soundcheck Scheduling" as one candidate. They are one timeline,
> not four documents.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Read-only** | Needs four facts and no more: when they load in, when they soundcheck, when they're on, and how long they have. Reaches them via the day sheet (18.10) — a Musician rarely opens this object. A **support act's** Musician sees the shared bill timeline (doors, every act's stage time, changeovers) but never items scoped private to another act (D-09). **Cannot edit — including their own set time**: set length belongs to the bill (18.02), and a Musician who could stretch their own set could move the venue's curfew margin without ever seeing the constraint behind it (CX-03 §3). They build the day they live in *through* the setlist, not through this. |
| Producer | **Full (act-side items) · propose on venue-side** | Owns soundcheck order, set times, changeovers, catering placement, band call, dep/bus calls and every act-side item. Adjusts derived values, sets buffer, and reads the curfew margin as they work. Changes to venue-side items are **proposals, not edits** (D-06). The person who knows whether the day is achievable. **The DIY case**: access follows the lens held on the event record (18.01), not the human — on a four-piece tour where the drummer is the TM, that one human holds the Producer lens here and Full access, and simultaneously holds the Musician lens on the setlist. `personas.md`'s multi-hyphenate is not an edge case in this feature; it is most of the market. |
| Operator | **Full (venue-side items) · propose on act-side** | Owns load-in access, doors, curfew, load-out, permitted hours and the occupancy windows their own calendar imposes (D-05). These are building facts and — **where the room's regime profile declares an instrument that imposes them** (`16.01.06` D-05) — statutory conditions, not preferences: the act does not negotiate them and the Operator bears a breach (18.07.02 D-06/DT-05). **Where the profile declares none, as the US launch profile does**, they are the Operator's own claims (`16.02.03` D-01/D-13) — still venue-owned on this timeline, but theirs to vary rather than an authority's. Cannot set an act's set length or soundcheck order; can propose. **At a festival the promoter is the Operator and authors the entire timeline** — the act receives it read-only and layers private items on top (D-13). |
| Fan | **None** | The run of show is operational: a fan has no use for load-in, and the encore's existence is deliberately a surprise. Doors and stage times reach fans as a *derived public listing* through 19/21 (sub-domain D-04), which is why publishing is a marked, one-way act on specific items (D-12) rather than a visibility setting on the document. |

## Behavior

The timeline is an ordered set of typed items scoped to the **event record** (18.01), not to a
calendar day (D-08). Item types: `load-in`, `build`, `line-check`, `soundcheck`, `catering`,
`reset`, `doors`, `set`, `changeover`, `encore` (conditional), `curfew`, `load-out`, `buffer`,
`promo`, `press`, `meet-and-greet`, `settlement`, `bus-call`. Every item carries an **owning
side** (act / venue), a **duration in whole minutes**, an **anchor type**, and a **visibility
scope**.

**Anchor type is the modelling primitive** (D-07). Three kinds:

| Anchor | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `pinned` | Fixed by an authority outside the timeline; moving it is an event, not an edit | `doors` (published to 19), `curfew` (licence), `load-in` start (access window), occupancy boundaries (16) |
| `derived` | Computed from data held elsewhere; editable, but the edit is recorded as an override (D-10) | `set` (duration from 18.06.01), `changeover` (from 18.02), `catering` (from 18.04.02) |
| `floating` | Placed by the item's owner within the constraints the others impose | `soundcheck`, `buffer`, `band call`, `promo` |

Without this distinction the timeline cannot answer the only question anyone asks at 17:30 —
*which of these two colliding items is the one that has to move?*

**Derivation, then adjustment.** On entering `advancing` (18.01) the timeline is pre-built from
four sources: the venue's access window, permitted hours, curfew set and occupancy boundaries
(16); the bill's act order, set lengths and changeover budgets (18.02); the setlist's rolled-up
durations (18.06.01); the production model — carried PA/lights vs house rig — which decides
whether `build` is 40 minutes or 4 hours (18.09, 18.04). Derived items show their source. A
Producer may override any derived value; the override does not break the link (D-10) — the item
displays as `52 min (override · setlist says 45)` and the divergence stays visible, because a band
that plays 58 every night is a fact the platform should learn (18.06.04), not one it should
silently lose.

**Buffer is derived too, and it is not optional** (D-06). The pre-built skeleton inserts labelled
`buffer` items at the four places every experienced TM already leaves slack and never writes down:
after `build`, between `soundcheck` and `doors` (the room `reset`), between the last support's set
and the headliner's changeover, and before `curfew`. A timeline with 0 minutes of buffer is
arithmetically perfect and physically impossible, and derivation is what makes that failure easy
to produce.

**Two authors, one document, no edits across the line** (D-02, D-06). Each side edits the items it
owns and *proposes* changes to the other side's, using the same propose/approve mechanism the bill
uses for venue-originated support acts (18.02) and the advance uses for post-freeze changes
(18.03.05). A proposal names its author, its reason and its blast radius. **Who wins when a
proposal is refused is not decided** — see Q-01.

**A third author nobody named** (DT-09). Promo commitments originate in 21 — an in-store at 15:00,
a radio session at 13:00, a photo call at 17:30 — booked by people who have never seen a load-in
schedule. They arrive here as **proposals against the act's side**, requiring Producer approval
(D-15), because a promo commitment accepted silently is the domain's most common invisible
collision. Same for 19: selling a VIP early-entry or meet-and-greet ticket product creates a real
timeline item with a duration and an artist obligation (D-14), and the act currently finds out on
the day.

**Time is venue-local, 24-hour, whole minutes** (D-16). `21:00`, never `9pm` — a 12-hour clock on
a run of show is a rookie tell and an ambiguity on an object where 00:30 is a normal load-out. A
TM in London looking at a Berlin date sees Berlin time.

**Validation never blocks.** The conflict checker (18.07.02) runs continuously against every
version and reports a banded margin; a failing check must be impossible to miss and must never
prevent the edit (18.07-cx CX-01 §2). The TM must be able to build an impossible day and then fix
it — a blocking builder gets worked around by not using the builder.

**Freeze and after.** At advance freeze (18.03.05) the timeline is snapshotted into the pack. It
does not become immutable — it becomes change-controlled: every later change is authored,
blast-radius-previewed and notified. **Freeze cannot immunise against a licence condition**
(18.07-cx CX-01 §5): if the venue corrects curfew or occupancy in 16 afterwards, the frozen
timeline is retroactively wrong and must say so loudly rather than silently recompute.

**Tour shape, per-date derivation** (D-11). Night 12 looks exactly like night 11 with different
numbers. A tour holds the timeline's *shape* — item set, offsets, the TM's house style — and each
date derives from it against that venue's facts. Without this, 40 dates become 40 hand-built days
that drift into 40 truths, which is exactly the failure 18.04.04 records for riders.

### Happy Path

1. Domain 17 confirms the deal → event record created (18.01) → Producer moves it to `advancing`.
2. The timeline **pre-builds** in venue-local time: `load-in 14:00` (venue access window, 16,
   `pinned`), `build 60 min` (house rig — the act carries no PA), `soundcheck` and `line-check`
   slots (`floating`), `catering 17:00, 60 min for 9` (from the hospitality rider, 18.04.02,
   `derived` from the roster's party size), `doors 19:00` (`pinned`), three `set` items and two
   `changeover` items (`derived` from the bill, 18.02), `curfew 23:00 music-off` (`pinned`, from
   16), `load-out` (`pinned` end at the load-out noise window, 23:45).
3. The skeleton includes four labelled `buffer` items totalling 45 minutes and a `reset` item
   between soundcheck and doors — none of which anyone typed.
4. Producer sets **soundcheck order: headliner 15:30 (60 min), mid 16:45 (30 min), opener line
   check 17:20 (15 min)** — reverse bill order, offered as a one-click default (D-03), because the
   headliner's kit stays up and everyone works around it.
5. Producer confirms set times from the derived durations: `opener 19:30–20:05 (35)`,
   `changeover 20:05–20:25 (20)`, `mid 20:25–21:10 (45)`, `changeover 21:10–21:35 (25 — no shared
   kit)`, `headline 21:35–22:35 (60)`, `encore 22:35–22:44 (9, conditional)`.
6. Operator confirms their side: load-in access, doors 19:00, curfew 23:00, load-out ends 23:45,
   and the occupancy boundary their calendar imposes — **club night, room access from 23:15**
   (D-05).
7. Checker (18.07.02) validates the bill against doors→curfew: last note at 22:44 with encore, 16
   min margin, banded — and against permitted hours at the *other* end: soundcheck 15:30 clears the
   room's 14:00 noise start (DT-05).
8. Operator's occupancy boundary and the load-out window collide: load-out must finish 23:45 but
   room access releases at 23:15. Producer sees the 30-minute load-out and the truck's position;
   it fits.
9. Producer freezes the advance (18.03.05); the timeline snapshots into v1 of the pack.
10. Call times resolve from the timeline by role (18.08.01 D-01) — backline at load-in minus 15,
    merch at doors minus 60 — and nine people are notified of a time nobody typed.
11. The timeline renders onto the day sheet (18.10), venue-scoped for the Operator, person-scoped
    for each Musician, whole for the Producer.
12. Doors and the headline stage time are **published** to 19/21 (D-12); both items are marked
    `published` and their movement is now a confirmed change, not an edit.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Sets + changeovers overrun curfew | Checker fails at the bill level, not per act (18.07.02); the edit is never blocked | "Bill overruns curfew by 12 min — trim a set, a changeover, or accept the fine" |
| Set duration is an estimate | Uncertainty propagates from 18.06.01 D-02 into every downstream time on the day | "Set times are estimates — 7 of 14 songs unmeasured (±8 min)" |
| **Derived set duration is wrong** — setlist says 45, the band plays 58 every night | Override accepted without breaking the link (D-10); the divergence stays visible and feeds 18.06.04 | "Headline set 58 min (override · setlist rolls up to 45) — measure at rehearsal?" |
| Venue has an event before/after | Load-in and load-out are hard-constrained by 16's occupancy boundaries, consumed as **times only** (D-05) | "Room releases 13:30 · Next occupancy 23:15 — load-out must fit in 30 min" |
| **Load-out is impossible tonight** — club night turns the room around at 23:15 | Legitimate and common: load-out moves to a next-morning item on the same event (D-08), which starts the driver's rest clock later (18.12.03) | "No load-out window tonight. Morning load-out 09:00 — this delays tomorrow's departure by 2h" |
| Occupancy data absent in 16 | `unknown`, never "the room is free" — the same discipline 18.07.02 applies to curfew | "No calendar data for this room — confirm load-out access with the venue" |
| **Soundcheck would start before permitted hours** (the early end) | Checked at *both* ends of the permitted-hours window, not just curfew (DT-05) | "Room can't make noise before 15:00 — soundcheck at 14:30 breaches permitted hours" |
| **Load-in noise at 08:00 breaches a neighbour agreement** | The venue's access window and its *noise* window are different facts; a tail-lift at 08:00 is the complaint that starts a licence review | "Access from 08:00 · No amplified or loading noise before 10:00" |
| No soundcheck possible (festival, tight day, late arrival) | `line-check` replaces `soundcheck` — a distinct item type with its own duration, not a shortened soundcheck | "Line check only — 15 min, inside the changeover" |
| Reverse bill order broken (headliner flying in at 17:00) | The default is a default, not a rule (DT-11); breaking it is one click and is not an error state | "Soundcheck order: custom — headliner arrives 17:00" |
| **Two acts share a kit** | Changeover duration is a fact of the bill's sharing model, not a constant: shared kit ~10 min, full swap ~25 | "Changeover 2: 25 min (no shared kit) · Changeover 1: 10 min (shared drums)" |
| **Derived skeleton has zero slack** | The failure derivation creates: an arithmetically perfect, physically impossible day. Buffer is inserted by default (D-06) and its removal is explicit | "0 min buffer between soundcheck and doors — the stage clears at the last note?" |
| **Setlist reordered while the TM is dragging doors** | A derived input moved under an active editor (CX-03 §3). Derived changes never silently move an item the user is holding — they arrive as a visible delta | "[Jo] changed the setlist — headline set +4 min. Apply?" |
| Doors already published; Producer moves it | Not an edit: a change to a promise made to ticket-holders (D-12). Confirm step names the count; the change emits to 19 | "Doors is published — 340 ticket-holders were told 19:00. Move to 19:30 and notify?" |
| Venue changes curfew in 16 **after** the advance is frozen | Freeze cannot immunise against the room's live constraint (CX-01 §5) — **whatever its provenance class** (18.07.02 D-06): a statutory change where the profile declares an instrument, an Operator restatement where it does not. Notify, never silently recompute | "Venue changed curfew 23:00 → 22:30 after freeze v1. Frozen pack now overruns by 26 min." |
| Venue changes occupancy in 16 after freeze | Identical treatment to curfew — same source, same authority, same blast radius | "Club night added at 23:15 after freeze — load-out window gone" |
| **Load-out crosses midnight** | The timeline is event-scoped, not day-scoped (D-08). 00:30 belongs to this show, not to tomorrow | Timeline continues past a `00:00` divider — no second document |
| **Build spans the previous day** (theatre, production show) | Same event, earlier items. A "day schedule" that assumes one day is wrong above club level | "Build day: Mon 08:00 · Show day: Tue" — one timeline, two dates |
| **DST clock-change night** — 02:00 happens twice | Real: October festival load-outs. Display disambiguates rather than guessing (Q-05 covers representation) | "Load-out 01:30 (BST) → 01:00 (GMT) — clocks go back tonight" |
| **Festival slot** — the act does not build the day | Received mode (D-13): the promoter (Operator) authors; the act's copy is read-only with private items layered on | "Stage 2 · 16:40–17:25 · 20 min changeover · line check only — set by [promoter]" |
| **Promo item booked in 21 collides with soundcheck** | Arrives as a proposal against the act's side, never applied silently (D-15) — the domain's most common invisible collision | "[Publicist] proposed: radio session 15:00–16:00. Conflicts with headline soundcheck 15:30." |
| **VIP early-entry sold in 19 creates a doors-minus item** | A ticket product silently created 30 min of artist obligation and moved 'room ready' earlier (D-14) | "19 sold 24 VIP early-entry — M&G 18:00–18:30 added. Room must be ready 45 min earlier." |
| **Catering collides with soundcheck** | The catering window is simultaneously a timeline item and a rider obligation (18.04.02); a collision breaks a contract, not just a plan | "Dinner 17:00 (rider: hot meal for 9) overlaps opener's line check — and the kitchen closes 18:00" |
| Venue not on WeJammin — nothing to derive from | Falls back to the tour's shape (D-11), then to a generic club-show template. Every venue-sourced item is `unset`, never assumed | "No venue data — timeline from tour shape. 5 items need real times." |
| Room `reset` gap unset | Never assumed to be zero — an assumed-zero reset produces an impossible day silently | "Reset (soundcheck → doors): duration unset — how long to clear the stage?" |
| Item with genuinely unknown duration ("load-in: until it's done") | Open-ended items are legal; they make everything downstream `floating` and the margin unknown | "Load-in has no duration — 6 downstream items can't be timed" |
| **Multi-hyphenate: the bassist is also TM and merch seller** | This timeline owns item times; 18.08.01 owns call rules and detects the person-level collision it creates | "[Sam] called 14:00 (backline) and 14:00 (merch load) — from soundcheck 15:30" |
| **Support's contractual set length ≠ their setlist** | The bill's number is contractual (18.02/17); the setlist's is planned. Divergence is a real fight and must be visible before the night, not at 20:04 | "Opener: contracted 35 min · setlist rolls up to 42" |
| Support act drops out day-of | Slot vacated; the timeline recomputes and *offers* the freed time rather than absorbing it | "Removing [act] frees 55 min — later doors, longer changeover, or earlier curfew margin?" |
| **Operator moves curfew earlier after the deal is signed** | Bad-faith case (Operator anti-persona). The system does not adjudicate; it records who changed what, when, and against which frozen version — which is the evidence | "Curfew changed 23:00 → 22:15 by [venue] on 12 Mar, 4 days after freeze v1" |
| **Settlement item on a shared bill timeline** | The item's *existence* is commercial. Item-level visibility, bill-visible by default, explicit private scope (D-09) | Support act's view: the 22:00 settlement item is simply absent |
| Two parties edit the same item simultaneously | Per-item ownership (D-02) makes this rare by construction — the sides cannot edit each other's items at all. What remains is same-side concurrency | "[Name] is editing load-in — 1 change since you opened" |
| **Freeze lands at the same instant as an Operator edit** | The freeze snapshots; the in-flight edit lands *after* and becomes a post-freeze change-control event (18.03.05) — not a lost write, not a blocked one | "Frozen v1. [Venue]'s load-in change arrived after freeze — review as a change?" |
| An item is dragged before its predecessor ends | Overlap is permitted (soundcheck and catering genuinely overlap) but never silent; only *impossible* overlaps flag | "Opener's line check overlaps dinner — same 9 people" |
| Doors deleted | Pinned, published items cannot be deleted — only moved or unpublished. Deleting doors means the show has no doors | "Doors is published and can't be removed. Unpublish first?" |
| Nobody updates it once the day starts | Not this feature's failure — handed to 18.07.03, where staleness is always visible (D-02 there) | Handled live |
| Show runs late on the night | Handed to slippage (18.07.03): the plan is preserved, actuals annotate it | Handled live |
| Offline edits conflict on sync | Show-day concern — handed to 18.07.03 Q-01, the domain's hardest constraint | Handled live |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Timeline opened | Item-row skeleton with the day's span; no times fabricated while loading |
| Empty (a) | Event `confirmed`, venue **is** on WeJammin, nothing confirmed | Pre-built skeleton from venue windows + bill + setlist, every derived item badged with its source, plus "Confirm the day — 6 items need your side" |
| Empty (b) | Event `confirmed`, venue **not** on WeJammin (18.01 edge case) | Timeline from the tour's shape (D-11); venue-sourced items shown `unset`, never guessed: "No venue data — 5 items need real times" |
| Empty (c) | Event earlier than `advancing` | "The day builds when the advance opens" — no timeline, no fabricated skeleton |
| Populated | Timeline built, both sides confirmed | Ordered timeline in venue-local 24h, per-item owner badge, anchor badge (`pinned`/`derived`/`floating`), source on every derived value, live curfew margin from 18.07.02 |
| Populated (received) | Festival slot (D-13) | The promoter's timeline, read-only, with the act's private items layered and editable: "Set by [promoter] — your items only" |
| Error | Save failed | "Couldn't save — retry." The local edit is retained; a lost run of show at 17:00 is not an acceptable failure |

| Partial | Venue-side items unconfirmed | "Your side is set. Venue hasn't confirmed doors, curfew or load-out." |
| Partial | Some durations estimated | "Set times are estimates — 7 songs unmeasured. Margin ±8 min." |
| Partial | Occupancy unknown | "No calendar data for this room — load-out window unverified" |
| Partial | Frozen, then a venue constraint changed underneath (CX-01 §5) | "Frozen v1 — venue changed curfew after freeze. This pack is now wrong by 26 min." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The schedule is typed by the TM | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — most of it is *computable*. Set length is in the setlist, changeover is in the bill, load-in and curfew are the venue's. A TM typing "21:00–21:45 headline set" is transcribing data the platform already holds — and transcription is where the errors come from. Derive first, adjust second. |
| DT-02 | It's the artist's document with venue input | Ownership instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the venue *owns* doors, curfew and load-in; those are the room's operating conditions, not the act's to author. A model where the act "asks" for a curfew is fiction. **⚠️ Refined (owner ratification, DQ-14.4 → 18.07.02 D-06, 16.02.03 D-13):** this annotation said "their **licence** conditions", which is one (now unauthored) profile's vocabulary asserted as the model. Ownership is regime-independent and the rejection is unchanged; what varies is only whether the value is statutory-derived and read-only to the Operator, or their own claim (`16.01.06` D-05). Per-item ownership on one shared timeline is the honest structure (D-02), and it's genuinely unusual — this and the input list patch column (18.05.02 D-02) are the domain's only co-authored documents. |
| DT-03 | Soundcheck order is arbitrary | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it is reverse bill order for a hard physical reason: the headliner's kit stays up, so they check first and everyone else works around it. This is domain knowledge with a real default, and a system that asks the TM to type it every night has learned nothing. |
| DT-04 | Load-out is a footnote | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED — load-out is frequently the hardest constraint on the day. Noise curfews for the street, the venue's next event, and crew hours all bite after the show, and it's the moment everyone is tired and the truck is double-parked. It is a scheduled item with constraints, not the end of the timeline. |
| DT-05 | The permitted-hours window has one end | Domain expert lens — **the check everyone omits** | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it changes the checker's inputs — the whole industry models the *late* end (curfew) because that is where the fine arrives, and nobody models the early end. But a room in a residential street has a time before which it cannot make noise, and a tail-lift plus flight cases at 08:00 on a Sunday is the complaint that starts a licence review. A soundcheck at 14:30 can breach permitted hours exactly as a 23:10 encore can. **The timeline's start is as constrained as its end.** 18.07.02 D-02 already models curfew as several distinct constraints; this says the same set exists, unmodelled, at dawn. |
| DT-06 | Derivation makes the day better | The obvious reading of D-01 — **and the trap in it** | ✅ CONFIRMED WITH A CONDITION, and the condition is load-bearing: derivation from exact durations produces a day with **zero slack**, which is arithmetically perfect and physically impossible. A 45-min set + 20-min changeover with no gap assumes the stage clears at the last note. Every experienced TM already builds contingency and never labels it, so it is invisible to a system learning from documents. **Without first-class buffer, D-01 actively makes the day worse than a TM's guess** — because the TM's guess contains slack and the derivation doesn't. This is why 18.07.02's margin must be banded (18.07.02 D-01) rather than compared, and it answers 18.07.02 Q-01 from the other side: a "tight" margin is one with no buffer left to spend. |
| DT-07 | Items are all the same kind of thing — they have a time | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — some are `pinned` by an authority outside the timeline (doors, once published; curfew, by licence; load-in, by access), some are `derived` (set, changeover, catering), some `float` (soundcheck, buffer, promo). A uniform model cannot answer the only question anyone asks at 17:30 — *which of these two colliding items is the one that has to move?* — and so it makes the user do the reasoning the platform exists to do. Anchor type is the primitive that makes the timeline a constraint system rather than a list. |
| DT-08 | The show day is a day | Naming instinct — the sub-domain is literally called "Show Day Schedule" | ❌ REJECTED, three independent ways. (a) Load-out routinely ends at 00:30 and belongs to *this* show, not to tomorrow — a day-scoped model either truncates the timeline or files load-out under the wrong event. (b) Theatre and production shows build the day before; festivals build for a week. (c) The clock-change night gives 02:00 twice, and an October load-out sits inside the ambiguous hour. The timeline is anchored to the **event record** and spans whatever it spans, in venue-local time. |
| DT-09 | The timeline has two authors — the act and the venue | The sub-domain's own D-01 | ❌ REJECTED — **a third author exists and nobody in the corpus has named them.** Promo (21) books an in-store at 15:00, a radio session at 13:00, a photo call at 17:30 — commitments made by people who have never seen a load-in schedule and are not in the advance. This is the show day's most common invisible collision, and no incumbent catches it because it is cross-domain by construction: the publicist's tool and the TM's tool have never met. It needs no new persona (the author holds the Producer or Musician lens); it needs the timeline to *accept items from 21 as proposals*. |
| DT-10 | Ticketing consumes the timeline and never writes to it | Boundary lens — the obvious reading of sub-domain D-04 | ❌ REJECTED — **a ticket product creates a schedule item.** VIP early entry and meet-and-greet are things 19 *sells*, and each is a timeline item with a duration, a room requirement and an artist obligation. Selling one silently commits 30 minutes of the artist's time before doors and moves "room ready" earlier — and the act finds out on the day. The flow is bidirectional: the timeline publishes doors and stage times *to* 19, and 19's inventory creates items *in* the timeline. |
| DT-11 | Reverse bill order is a rule | Over-reading D-03 | ❌ REJECTED (refines D-03) — it is a strong default with real, frequent exceptions: a headliner flying in at 17:00 checks last or not at all; a shared kit is checked once for everyone; a support whose kit must strike before the headliner builds checks first; a festival has no soundcheck at all, only a line check inside the changeover. The default must be one click to break and breaking it must not be an error state — the system that argues with the TM about their own soundcheck is the system that gets closed. |
| DT-12 | Both parties see the whole timeline | The shared-document reading | ❌ REJECTED at the edges, where it is dangerous. The timeline *is* one of the bill's genuinely shared objects — everyone must know doors, and hiding stage times from a support act would be absurd. But some items are commercial or personal in their *existence*: a 22:00 settlement meeting, a private M&G, a band call. 18.02 D-02 sets the default confidentiality posture as private-per-act with explicit sharing, and a timeline that ignores it re-creates the leak on the one document everyone reads. Item-level visibility, **bill-visible by default**, with an explicit private scope. |
| DT-13 | Every show's timeline is built the same way | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED — at a festival the act does not build the day; they **receive** a slot. The promoter (Operator, per domain D-02's mapping) authors the timeline and hands down "Stage 2, 16:40–17:25, 20 min changeover, line check only". 18.01 DT-03 established that a festival slot is the same record with a different `role_on_bill`; the consequence for *this* feature — a received, read-only authoring mode with the act's private items layered on top — has never been stated. Without it, a festival date forces the act to hand-copy a timeline they don't own, which is transcription: exactly what D-01 exists to kill. |
| DT-14 | The derived number is the right number | Optimism about D-01 | ❌ REJECTED — the derivation is only as good as its source. A band with a 45-minute setlist that plays 58 every night turns the platform into the confident author of a wrong number, and a confidently wrong schedule is worse than a TM's guess, because the TM's guess carries their knowledge of their own band. Derived values must be **overridable without breaking the link**, the override must display as an override with its source visible, and the measured actual (18.06.04) must flow back into the estimate. Derivation without correction is just transcription with extra confidence. |
| DT-15 | The timeline is per-date | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED — night 12 of a tour is night 11 with different numbers, and a TM has a house style they apply every night. The tour holds the *shape* (item set, offsets, buffer policy); each date derives from it against that venue's facts. This is 18.04.04's rider-templating argument reaching a second object for the same reason: without a shape, 40 dates become 40 hand-built days that drift into 40 truths. It also gives the venue-not-on-platform case (Empty (b)) something honest to fall back to. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Timeline items derive from held data; the TM adjusts rather than types | Transcription is where the errors come from, and the data is already here |
| D-02 | Per-item ownership on one shared timeline | The venue owns curfew as an operating condition of the room, not as a term the act may author. Its **provenance class** — statutory-derived or Operator claim — is profile-resolved and does not change the ownership (18.07.02 D-06; `16.02.03` D-01/D-13) |
| D-03 | Reverse bill order is the soundcheck **default**, one click to break (DT-11) | The headliner's kit stays up — real domain knowledge with a real default, and real exceptions |
| D-04 | Load-out is a scheduled, constrained item | Street noise curfew, the venue's next event, and crew hours all bite after the show |
| D-05 | **The venue's calendar (16) is consumed as occupancy *windows* only — times, never the other event's identity or content** (resolves Q-02, and mirrors sub-domain Q-03) | The constraint is purely temporal: load-in cannot start before the previous occupancy releases plus the room's reset, and load-out must finish before the next occupancy's access. The times are all the schedule needs. The other event may be a confidential hold or a private hire, and 16 must remain its owner. The venue may attach an optional label ("club night") at their discretion — a courtesy, not a required fact. An occupancy change after freeze behaves exactly like a curfew change: notify, never silently recompute (CX-01 §5). |
| D-06 | **Cross-side changes are proposals, never edits** | The honest implementation of D-02. Each side edits what it owns and proposes to the other, reusing the mechanism the bill (18.02) and the freeze (18.03.05) already use. It does **not** decide who wins a refused proposal — that is Q-01, and pretending otherwise would be fiction. |
| D-07 | **Every item has an anchor type: `pinned` / `derived` / `floating`** | Without it the timeline cannot say which of two colliding items has to move — the only question anyone asks at 17:30 |
| D-08 | **The timeline is scoped to the event and spans whatever it spans**, in venue-local time — it is not a day | Load-out ends at 00:30; theatre builds start the day before; festivals build for a week. A day-scoped model files load-out under the wrong event. |
| D-09 | **Item-level visibility: bill-visible by default, explicit private scope** | The timeline is genuinely shared (everyone needs doors), but a settlement meeting's *existence* is commercial. 18.02 D-02's posture must survive onto the one document everyone reads. |
| D-10 | **Derived values are overridable without breaking the link; the override is visible and the divergence feeds back** | A setlist that says 45 for a band that plays 58 makes the platform the confident author of a wrong number. Derivation without correction is transcription with extra confidence. |
| D-11 | **The tour holds the timeline's shape; each date derives from it** | Night 12 is night 11 with different numbers. Same argument as 18.04.04's rider templates, same failure without it: 40 dates drift into 40 truths. |
| D-12 | **Publishing marks an item; moving a published item is a confirmed change that emits to 19** | Doors is a promise to ticket-holders, not a field. Whether that promise creates an *obligation* is Q-04 — but the flagging, the confirm step and the emitted event are behaviour this feature owns either way. |
| D-13 | **Received mode for festival slots** — the promoter authors, the act layers private items on a read-only timeline | 18.01 DT-03 made the festival slot the same record; without received mode the act hand-copies a timeline they don't own, which is the transcription D-01 exists to kill |
| D-14 | **A ticket product can create a timeline item** (VIP early entry, M&G) | 19's inventory silently commits the artist's time and moves "room ready" earlier. The relationship with 19 is bidirectional, not just publish-out. |
| D-15 | **Promo commitments from 21 arrive as proposals against the act's side, requiring Producer approval** | The publicist has never seen a load-in schedule. Auto-applying their booking puts a radio session on top of soundcheck; rejecting the integration puts it there anyway, invisibly. Same mechanism as D-06. |
| D-16 | **Venue-local time, 24-hour clock, durations in whole minutes** | "9pm" on a run of show is an ambiguity on a document where 00:30 is normal — and a TM in London must see a Berlin date in Berlin time |
