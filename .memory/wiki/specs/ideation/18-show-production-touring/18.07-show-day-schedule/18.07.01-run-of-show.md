# Feature: Run of Show & Day Schedule Builder

> **Number**: 18.07.01
> **Parent**: [Show Day Schedule & Timing](./18.07-show-day-schedule-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The show day as one ordered timeline: load-in, build, soundchecks in reverse bill order, doors,
sets, changeovers, curfew, load-out. Most of it is not typed — it is *computed* from facts the
platform already holds (set durations from the setlist, changeover from the bill, curfew from the
venue) and adjusted by the two people who own the day.

> **Merged**: this node consolidates the sweep's candidate 08, which listed "Run of Show", "Set
> Times", "Changeover" and "Load-in/Soundcheck Scheduling" as one candidate. They are one timeline,
> not four documents.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees when they load in, when they soundcheck, when they're on. Reaches them via the day sheet (18.10), not by opening this. They don't build the day; they live in it. |
| Producer | Full | Builds the artist side — soundcheck order, set times, changeovers — and negotiates the rest with the venue. The person who knows whether the day is achievable. |
| Operator | **Full (venue-side items)** | Owns load-in access, doors, curfew and load-out — these are their licence conditions and their building. Co-authors the same timeline; cannot set the act's set length. |
| Fan | None | The run of show is operational. Doors and stage times reach fans as a *derived public listing* through 19/21 (D-04 at sub-domain level). |

## Behavior

Items are typed (load-in, soundcheck, doors, set, changeover, load-out) with an owning side and a
duration. Durations derive where they can — set length from the setlist (18.06.01), changeover from
the bill (18.02), load-in window from the venue (16) — so the schedule inherits real numbers rather
than a TM's guesses. The whole timeline is validated continuously by the conflict checker (18.07.02).

### Happy Path

1. Event enters advancing; the timeline is pre-built from venue windows, bill and setlist.
2. Producer sets soundcheck order (headliner first, then reverse bill order) and set times.
3. Operator confirms load-in access, doors and curfew from their side.
4. The checker validates: sets + changeovers fit inside doors→curfew. It fits.
5. The timeline is frozen with the advance and renders onto the day sheet.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Sets + changeovers overrun curfew | Checker fails at the timeline level (18.07.02) | "Bill overruns curfew by 12 min" |
| Set duration is an estimate | The whole timeline inherits the uncertainty | "Set times are estimates — 7 songs unmeasured" |
| Venue has an event before/after | Load-in and load-out windows are hard-constrained by 16's calendar (Q-03) | "Club night at 23:00 — load-out by 22:30" |
| No soundcheck possible (festival, tight day) | Line check replaces soundcheck; a real and common item type | "Line check only — 15 min" |
| Support act arrives late | Slippage (18.07.03), not a rebuild | Handled live |
| Two parties edit the same item | Per-item ownership prevents most of it; genuine conflicts need a rule (Q-01) | "[Operator] owns doors — propose a change?" |
| Curfew and load-out conflict | Load-out is often *after* curfew and constrained by neighbours and noise | "Curfew 23:00, load-out must finish by 23:45" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Schedule opened | Timeline skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing scheduled | Pre-built skeleton from venue defaults + "confirm the day" |
| Populated | Timeline built | Ordered timeline with per-item owner badges and validation status |
| Error | Save failed | "Couldn't save — retry" |
| Partial | Venue-side items unconfirmed | "Your side is set. Venue hasn't confirmed doors or curfew." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The schedule is typed by the TM | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — most of it is *computable*. Set length is in the setlist, changeover is in the bill, load-in and curfew are the venue's. A TM typing "21:00–21:45 headline set" is transcribing data the platform already holds — and transcription is where the errors come from. Derive first, adjust second. |
| DT-02 | It's the artist's document with venue input | Ownership instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the venue *owns* doors, curfew and load-in; those are their licence conditions, not their opinions. A model where the act "asks" for a curfew is fiction. Per-item ownership on one shared timeline is the honest structure (D-01), and it's genuinely unusual — this and the input list patch column (18.05.02 D-02) are the domain's only co-authored documents. |
| DT-03 | Soundcheck order is arbitrary | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it is reverse bill order for a hard physical reason: the headliner's kit stays up, so they check first and everyone else works around it. This is domain knowledge with a real default, and a system that asks the TM to type it every night has learned nothing. |
| DT-04 | Load-out is a footnote | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED — load-out is frequently the hardest constraint on the day. Noise curfews for the street, the venue's next event, and crew hours all bite after the show, and it's the moment everyone is tired and the truck is double-parked. It is a scheduled item with constraints, not the end of the timeline. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Timeline items derive from held data; the TM adjusts rather than types | Transcription is where the errors come from, and the data is already here |
| D-02 | Per-item ownership on one shared timeline | The venue owns curfew as a licence condition, not as a preference |
| D-03 | Reverse bill order is the soundcheck default | The headliner's kit stays up — real domain knowledge with a real default |
| D-04 | Load-out is a scheduled, constrained item | Street noise curfew, the venue's next event, and crew hours all bite after the show |
