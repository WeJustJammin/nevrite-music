## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Run of Show ↔ Curfew & Conflict Checking

**Relationship**: The checker is not a gate on the timeline — it is a continuous readout of whether
the day is possible. It runs on every change, in both directions: change the timeline and the
margin moves; discover a curfew you didn't know about and the timeline is retroactively broken. The
checker's inputs come from three other domains (16's licence data, 18.06's set durations, 18.02's
bill), so the timeline is validated against facts its author cannot see.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees margin and cause together, and decides what gives.
- **Operator**: supplies the constraints and bears the penalty. Sees conflicts raised against their room.
- **Musician**: sees only the consequence — "you have 45 minutes, not 52".
- **Fan**: no visibility; feels it as a hard stop mid-encore.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The timeline owns times; the venue (16) owns constraints; the setlist (18.06) owns durations. The checker owns nothing and writes nothing — it is a pure function of three sources it cannot mutate. That's what keeps it honest.
2. **Trigger chain**: Timeline change → check re-runs → margin updates. A failing check **never blocks** the edit (the TM must be able to build an impossible day and then fix it) but the failure must be impossible to miss. A blocking checker would be worked around by not using the tool — the same reasoning as 18.03.05 D-02.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator writes constraints in 16, not here — and that write silently re-validates every act's timeline for that room. A venue correcting their curfew from 23:00 to 22:30 breaks somebody's frozen schedule. That must notify, not just re-compute.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A newly-failing check must reach the Producer *and* the Operator, because the penalty is the Operator's. This asymmetry (18.07.02 DT-05) means the venue must be told when an act's plan puts their licence at risk — even though it's the act's plan.
5. **State transition conflict**: A frozen advance holds a timeline that was valid when frozen. If the venue's constraint data changes afterward, the frozen pack is now wrong. Freeze cannot immunise against a licence condition — this must surface as change control (18.03.05), not silently.

### CX-02: Live Slippage → Run of Show

**Relationship**: Slippage doesn't replace the timeline; it annotates every item with an actual
alongside its plan, and shifts downstream items by default. This produces the planned-vs-actual
variance that the post-show report (18.18) pre-fills from — which is why the report can be honest
without anyone typing it at 01:00. The timeline is the plan; slippage is what makes it a record.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: slips items and previews the cascade before committing.
- **Operator**: slips their own items — the delay is frequently theirs.
- **Musician**: read-only; gets told.
- **Fan**: public times only, if published.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The plan is preserved; actuals are a separate layer. Overwriting the plan with actuals would destroy the variance — the same plan/fact discipline as the setlist (18.06 D-01), for the same reason.
2. **Trigger chain**: Item slipped → downstream items shift → check re-runs → notifications fan out → day sheet stales. The cascade must be *previewed* before commit, because at 17:30 nobody can trace it mentally and the whole value is that the system can.
3. **Permission intersection**: Both parties hold Full access to one timeline at high tempo (sub-domain D-01). Per-item ownership prevents most conflicts; genuine simultaneous slips of the same item need a rule that doesn't exist yet (Q-01).
4. **Notification fan-out**: The largest in the domain — crew, band, support acts, venue, and potentially hundreds of ticket-holders. Scoping matters: not everyone needs to know that soundcheck moved 15 minutes.
5. **State transition conflict**: Offline slips queue and may conflict on sync. Two people slipping the same item on separate devices in a basement is a real scenario with no clean answer — flagged in 18.07.03 Q-01.

### CX-03: Live Slippage ↔ Curfew & Conflict Checking

**Relationship**: The same check as CX-01 with the fuse burned down. Weeks out, a curfew conflict is
a planning problem with several solutions. At 21:40 with the support still on, it is a decision with
two options and both are bad. The margin stops being a report and becomes a live gauge — and the
platform is the only party in the building doing the arithmetic continuously.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: watches the margin shrink and decides what gives — a trimmed set, a dropped encore.
- **Operator**: the same number, but it's their fine. They may need to force the decision.
- **Musician**: told "you have 40 minutes, not 45", ideally before they walk on.
- **Fan**: feels the consequence.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None new — the checker still owns nothing. But its inputs are now moving in real time, so it must be cheap to re-run and stable enough not to flap.
2. **Trigger chain**: Slip → re-check → margin update → escalation if breached. Unlike CX-01, this chain has a deadline: a warning that arrives after the band walked on is worthless.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator may need authority to force a decision here that they don't have weeks out — it's their licence, and by 22:50 there is no time to negotiate. Whether the platform models that authority is Q-01 at sub-domain level, and it bites hardest right here.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A live breach warning must reach the Producer, the Operator, and plausibly the stage — but a notification to a performer mid-set is both useless and hostile. The escalation path has a physical limit the system must respect.
5. **State transition conflict**: The margin depends on setlist durations that are estimates (18.07.02 D-03). Live, that uncertainty is at its most expensive: "±8 minutes" on a 4-minute margin means nobody actually knows. This is the strongest argument in the domain for measuring durations at rehearsal (18.19 DT-02) — the cost of not doing it lands here, at 22:50, on the venue's licence.
