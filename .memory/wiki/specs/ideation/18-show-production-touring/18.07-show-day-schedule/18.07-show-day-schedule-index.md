## Overview

The show day as a timeline: load-in, soundchecks in bill order, doors, set times, changeovers,
curfew, load-out. It is the object every person on site works from and the one most likely to be
wrong by 19:00, because it was agreed by email three weeks ago and reality has been renegotiating
it since the truck was late.

**Why this is a sub-domain**: three capabilities that interact continuously. The schedule builder
produces a timeline; the conflict checker validates it against constraints the venue's licence
imposes (curfew, sound limits, load-in windows) and fails it if the arithmetic doesn't work; and
slippage mutates the timeline in real time, which re-runs the checker with a much shorter fuse. A
schedule without a checker is a wish; a checker without slippage validates a fiction.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | The schedule is jointly owned, not artist-owned with venue input | The venue owns doors, curfew, load-in access and the room's operating conditions — statutory-derived where the regime profile declares an instrument, the Operator's own claims where it does not (18.07.02 D-06; `16.02.03` D-01/D-13); the act owns set length and soundcheck order. Modelling it as one party's document with the other commenting reproduces the email chain. Per-item ownership on a shared timeline is the honest model. | Agent, Operator persona analysis |
| D-02 | Curfew checking is a distinct capability, not a validation rule | It draws on licence data the act cannot see, and its failure has legal and financial consequences (fines, licence review) that no other schedule error has. It also validates against the setlist's duration, which lives in another sub-domain. That's a checker with its own inputs, not a form validator. | Agent classification, 2026-07-16 |
| D-03 | Slippage is a first-class feature, not an edit | The industry's schedules are PDFs, and a PDF cannot slip. The moment the truck is late, the distributed schedule is fiction and everyone reverts to shouting. Modelling slippage as a live mutation with propagation is what makes the platform useful *on the day* rather than *before it*. | Agent Deep Think, 2026-07-16 |
| D-04 | Set times reach fans through 19/21; the schedule is not a fan surface | Doors and stage times are public facts derived from this timeline, but the run of show is an operational document. The derived public listing is 19's. | Agent, Role Matrix analysis |
