# Feature: Stage-Ready Setlist Output & Performer View

> **Number**: 18.06.02
> **Parent**: [Setlist & Show Files](./18.06-setlist-show-files-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The setlist as it exists on stage: a large-type sheet taped to the floor, or a per-performer view
with their own cues, tuning and instrument changes. The design constraint is unusual and absolute —
it is read from two metres away, in the dark, by someone holding an instrument, who cannot tap
anything.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their own view: the running order in huge type, their own cues, their key and tuning per song, their instrument change on the next track. This is the only feature in the domain used *while performing*. |
| Producer | Config | Configures what each performer's view shows and generates the printable outputs. Doesn't use it on stage — the MD or the band does. |
| Operator | None | No access. The venue does not get the setlist (18.06 D-03), and the stage-ready output is the setlist in larger type. |
| Fan | None | Never. A published performed setlist (18.06.04) is a different artifact entirely. |

## Behavior

Renders per performer from the setlist plus their cues, at print sizes that actually work on a
stage floor, and as an on-device view for those who use a tablet. The output must survive the
venue's realities: no signal, no free hands, and stage lighting that renders a phone unreadable.
Print is not a fallback here — it is the dominant medium.

### Happy Path

1. Setlist is finalised for the night.
2. Producer generates outputs — one printable floor sheet plus per-member views.
3. Members open their view on a tablet, or print theirs.
4. The view caches fully; the show runs with no connectivity.
5. A last-minute set change re-issues and flags who is still on the old version.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No signal on stage | Must work fully offline once cached — non-negotiable | Cached view with a version stamp |
| Set changes after printing | Paper cannot be recalled; version stamp is the only mitigation | "v3 printed at 18:40 — 2 people still on v2" |
| Performer's hands are full | No interaction can be required; auto-advance or foot control only | View advances on its own or not at all |
| Screen too dim / stage lighting | High-contrast, huge-type rendering is a hard requirement, not a preference | Dark-optimised, minimal-chrome view |
| Different members need different info | Per-member render from shared cues | Drummer sees tempos; guitarist sees tunings |
| Tablet dies mid-show | Paper is the only real backup and must always be generatable | "Print floor sheets" always available |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | View opened | Song list skeleton (should be near-instant from cache) |
| Empty | No setlist | "No setlist for tonight" |
| Populated | Setlist exists | Huge-type running order + own cues |
| Error | Sync failed | Cached version + "showing v2 — couldn't check for updates" |
| Partial | Cues missing for this member | Running order without cues (never blocks the list) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a print stylesheet of 18.06.01 | Classification instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the constraints are so different that they produce a different product. Offline-mandatory, zero-interaction, two-metre legibility, and paper as the *primary* medium rather than the fallback. A view that requires a tap has failed; a view that requires signal has failed. That's a distinct feature, not a stylesheet. |
| DT-02 | Everyone sees the same setlist | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the drummer needs tempos, the guitarist needs tunings, the MD needs cues, and the singer needs the words in the right order and nothing else. A shared sheet forces everyone to visually filter for their own information in the dark, which is why per-member cues get written on hands. |
| DT-03 | Paper is a legacy fallback | Modernisation instinct | ❌ REJECTED — paper is the **dominant medium** and will remain so. It has no battery, no glare, no lock screen, and survives beer. The product's job is to make the paper *correct and versioned*, not to replace it. Designing the tablet view first and printing second gets this exactly backwards. |
| DT-04 | Offline is an architecture concern, not this feature's problem | Boundary lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the *mechanism* is architecture (routed to `/create-prd`), but offline is a **product requirement** here in a way it isn't elsewhere: a setlist view that needs signal is not a setlist view. It is this domain's strongest argument in the mobile-surface question (`meta/constraints.md`). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Paper is the primary medium; the device view is secondary | No battery, no glare, no lock screen — the product's job is to make the paper correct and versioned |
| D-02 | Zero interaction required during performance | The performer's hands are full; any view needing a tap has failed |
| D-03 | Fully functional offline once cached — a product requirement, not an NFR | A setlist view that needs signal is not a setlist view |
