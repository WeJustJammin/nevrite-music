# Feature: Performed Setlist Capture & Live Performance Log

> **Number**: 18.06.04
> **Parent**: [Setlist & Show Files](./18.06-setlist-show-files-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What was **actually played**, by **whom**, on **that stage**, on **that date** — captured as a fact
at the time it becomes true, rather than reconstructed later from a photo of a floor sheet. This is
the domain's direct expression of the platform thesis: the platform is already in the room running
the show, so the record of the performance is a byproduct rather than an act of admin.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Confirms what was played and who played it — including the dep who covered for the regular bassist, which is the fact that evaporates first. Accumulates a performance history that is *cited* rather than claimed: "1,400 shows, 312 venues, verified". |
| Producer | Full | Confirms deviations from the plan and the personnel who actually performed. The same counter-attestation role they hold in the studio — their confirmation is worth more than a self-claim. |
| Operator | Read-only + occurrence attest | Sees *that* a show happened in their room and can **counter-attest the occurrence** — a disinterested third party who was there, the strongest available witness that the event took place on this date at this venue (DT-06). Does NOT see or attest the setlist songs, personnel splits, or deps — those stay private to the act (D-03, sub-domain index). A venue's history of who has played there is an asset to them. |
| Fan | Read-only (published only) | If the act publishes it, fans see the setlist for a show they attended — the artifact fans actually want and go to setlist.fm for. Never sees personnel splits, deps, or unpublished sets. |

## Behavior

The planned setlist (18.06.01) pre-fills the performed one; the human confirms or corrects it, which
takes seconds rather than minutes. Personnel is captured per performance, not per band — because
who was on stage on a given night is not who is in the band. Confirmation follows the same
counter-attestation pattern as studio credits: the act's assertion plus the Producer's or another
member's confirmation.

Two attestation layers exist and must not be conflated (DT-06). The act plus a Producer or member
counter-attest **content** — what was played and who played it, including the dep. The venue
(Operator), as the disinterested third party who was in the room, can counter-attest **occurrence** —
that the show happened here on this date — without ever seeing the setlist or personnel, which stay
private to the act (D-03). Capture is offline-tolerant: venues have poor signal, so the record is
written against a local timestamp and synced later, and a locally-timestamped capture still counts
as captured-at-the-moment rather than reconstructed.

### Happy Path

1. Show hits `performed`; the planned setlist is offered as the presumed performed one.
2. Producer corrects it — two songs dropped, one added, encore played.
3. Personnel is pre-filled from the tour's lineup; the dep on bass is corrected.
4. Members confirm; the performance is logged as a counter-attested fact.
5. It accrues to every performer's history and (if enabled) publishes to fans.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody captures it | Falls back to the planned setlist marked **unconfirmed** — never presented as fact | "Planned setlist — not confirmed as performed" |
| A dep played instead of a member | The fact that most needs capturing and evaporates fastest | "Bass: [dep name] — not [member]" |
| Captured days later | Allowed, but marked as reconstructed, not captured | "Logged 6 days after the show" |
| Members disagree about what was played | Dispute on a factual claim; both positions held | "2 confirmations, 1 correction pending" |
| Guest performer sat in | A performance credit for someone not on the tour | "Add guest performer" |
| Act inflates their performance history | Counter-attestation is the defence — a self-claim is weaker evidence | Unconfirmed entries visibly weaker in the history |
| Cover songs / songs not in the catalogue | Breaks the work linkage that royalty reporting needs | "Not linked to a work — can't be reported" |
| No connectivity at the venue (the common case — load-in areas and stages have terrible signal) | Capture happens offline against a locally-timestamped record; syncs when signal returns. Because the timestamp is at-the-moment, it still counts as captured, NOT reconstructed (contrast the "captured days later" row) | "Saved offline — will sync. Captured [time], not yet uploaded." |
| Venue counter-attests the occurrence | Operator confirms the show happened in their room on that date — the strongest witness because they are disinterested (DT-06). Confirms the *event*, never the songs or personnel | "Venue confirmed: show took place here, [date]" |
| Two acts on one bill both claim the same slot/room | Occurrence attestations and setlists are per-act; the venue's occurrence attest can disambiguate who actually played | Each act's log is independent; no shared setlist object |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Capture opened | Pre-filled list skeleton |
| Empty | Show performed, nothing captured | Planned setlist offered for confirmation |
| Populated | Captured + confirmed | Performed setlist with personnel and confirmation status |
| Error | Save failed | "Couldn't save — retry" |
| Partial | Captured, awaiting confirmations | "You confirmed. Waiting on 2 of 4 members." |
| Offline | Saved without connectivity | "Saved offline — will sync. Captured [time]." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The sweep's setlist model has no place for what was actually played | Deep Think — thesis lens (D-18) | ✅ CONFIRMED, node created — the candidate list has setlist *management* and setlist *output*, both about the plan. Nothing captures the performance. But **the performance is the fact**, and the thesis says the platform's whole justification is being present when facts become true. A live show is the single most attended, most witnessed, most evidenceable event in a musician's working life, and the industry's record of it is a fan wiki. This is the domain's provenance node and the sweep missed it entirely. |
| DT-02 | Who performed is known from the band lineup | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — **this is the whole point.** The bassist was ill and a dep played. The regular drummer's tendonitis meant three months of covers. Band lineup is a slowly-changing dimension; *who was on stage tonight* is a per-event fact, and it is exactly what nobody records and everybody later disputes. Per-performance personnel is the feature's core, not an attribute. |
| DT-03 | Live performance credits belong in domain 02 | Boundary lens | ⚠️ TENSION — 02 owns the credit *record*; this is the *capture point*, which only exists because the show ran here. Same structure as the studio: 07 runs the session, 02 holds the credit. The clean split is that 18 captures and 02 records — but the boundary needs owner ratification (sub-domain Q-02). |
| DT-04 | Live performance royalties are a domain 10 concern | Boundary lens | ✅ CONFIRMED, with a large caveat — PRO live performance royalties are **routinely unclaimed** by working musicians because setlist submission is manual admin nobody does. A platform that already holds the confirmed setlist, the date, and the venue can make that submission a byproduct. That is arguably the strongest *money* argument in this entire domain, and it belongs to 10. Escalated (sub-domain Q-01). |
| DT-05 | Capturing it is more admin, so it won't happen | Adversarial lens — the Producer anti-persona | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS RISK, and the design answer is pre-fill. `personas.md` warns the Producer's worst accidental behaviour is closing a project without capturing splits — "the design must make the lazy path the correct path". Here the lazy path is confirming a pre-filled list in five seconds. If it takes longer than that it will not happen, and the node is worthless. |
| DT-06 | A venue (Operator) can counter-attest what happened, making it the strongest attestation available | Q-03, deferred to this step (Step 5) | ✅ CONFIRMED — but scoped. The Operator is the only **disinterested** party who was in the room, so their word is the strongest evidence the *event occurred* (this act, this venue, this date). But the split must respect D-03: the setlist is private to the act, and the Role Lens already hides songs/personnel/deps from the Operator. So the venue attests **occurrence, not content** — it cannot confirm the dep on bass or the dropped encore because it never sees them. Result: two attestation layers — Operator confirms the show *happened* (unforgeable third-party witness); act + Producer/member counter-attest *what* was played and *who* played it (DT-05). This makes the log stronger than either layer alone and does not leak private setlist data. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The performed setlist is a separate object from the plan, never an overwrite | Overwriting destroys both the intent and the fact; provenance requires capture, not editing |
| D-02 | Personnel is captured per performance, not inherited from the band lineup | The dep who covered is the fact that evaporates first and gets disputed later |
| D-03 | Counter-attestation, not self-claim | A self-reported performance history is exactly the inflation the Musician anti-persona describes |
| D-04 | Uncaptured shows fall back to the plan marked unconfirmed — never presented as fact | A plan is not evidence |
| D-05 | Capture must take seconds — pre-filled, confirm-only | If it's admin it won't happen, and then the node is worthless |
| D-06 | Occurrence and content are separate attestation layers; the venue attests occurrence only | The venue is the strongest witness that the show happened but must never see private setlist/personnel data (D-03). Splitting the layers gets the third-party strength without leaking the act's private plan (DT-06) |
| D-07 | Capture is offline-tolerant; a locally-timestamped capture counts as captured-at-the-moment | Venues have poor signal and the whole value is capture-at-source — a flow that requires connectivity at the stage would fail exactly where it matters most |
