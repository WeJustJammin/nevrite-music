## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Visas & Work Permits ↔ Carnets & Gear Customs

**Relationship**: One trigger — the tour crosses a border on date 5 — fires two entirely separate
chains: the people chain (nationality × territory, lead times in weeks or months) and the gear chain
(manifest × serials × values, issued by an agent). They share the trigger, the deadline discipline
and the "your date cannot happen without this" stakes, but nothing else. That shared trigger is what
makes them one sub-domain rather than two unrelated forms.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: runs both, and is the person at the border at 04:00 for either.
- **Musician**: holds their own documents (18.14.01) and their own gear's serials (18.14.02) — **both are person-owned**, which is unusual and consistent with the domain's recurring pattern.
- **Operator**: sponsor duties on visas; nothing on carnets.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — disjoint data. Visas derive from the roster (18.08.01); carnets derive from the manifest (18.09.01). They share only the tour's territories and dates (18.11.01). Two projections of two different lists onto one border.
2. **Trigger chain**: Crossing added → both requirement sets derive → deadlines computed against *lead times*, not the travel date. **The lead times differ by months**, so they cannot share an alert cadence: a visa needed at T-12 weeks and a carnet needed at T-2 weeks are different conversations.
3. **Permission intersection**: A promoter sponsoring a visa (18.14.01 Role Lens) sees person data; they see nothing of the carnet. The Operator's access is asymmetric across the sub-domain, which the permission model must not flatten.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A missed visa affects **one person**; a missed carnet affects **the whole tour's gear**. Same trigger, radically different blast radius — and the alerts should say so rather than treating both as "a border requirement is outstanding".
5. **State transition conflict**: A person removed from the roster removes a visa requirement; an item removed from the manifest *breaks* an already-issued carnet (18.14.02 DT-04). One is subtraction, the other is corruption. The carnet is far less forgiving of change, and the domain's change-control instinct (18.03.05) has no reach here — a chamber of commerce does not accept a version bump.

### CX-02: Visas & Work Permits ↔ Withholding Tax

**Relationship**: Medium confidence, and conceptually the sharper edge. The work permit is the
document asserting that you are **legally working** in a territory — and being legally at work there
is precisely the fact that makes your fee taxable there. The two are causally linked through the
same underlying event, and acts routinely obtain the first without ever learning the second exists.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees the permit deadline and, ideally, the tax consequence in the same view.
- **Musician**: gets the permit, and finds out about the 30% when the money arrives short.
- **Operator**: **frequently both the sponsor and the withholder** — the only place in the domain where the venue/promoter carries two legal duties at once.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Medium confidence — remainder deferred to Step 5)_:
2. **Trigger chain**: Territory + performance + fee → both a permit requirement and a withholding exposure. They should surface **together**, because the act getting a permit is the moment they're most receptive to learning what it costs. Today they're discovered months apart, in that order, by different means.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator's dual role is unusual and worth flagging to whoever specs the permission layer. As sponsor they hold the act's personal data; as withholder they hold the act's money. Two duties, one party, different data.
5. **State transition conflict**: Relief deadlines (18.14.03) and permit deadlines are both months out and independent. Missing the permit stops the date; missing the relief just costs 30%. Both are silent failures — nothing happens on the deadline day, which is exactly why they're missed.

### CX-03: Carnets & Gear Customs ↔ Withholding Tax

**Relationship**: Low confidence — recorded as a hypothesis, not a designed flow. Both are
declarations of value to arms of the same state: the carnet declares what your gear is worth
(bonding against duty), and withholding declares what your performance is worth (taxing income). The
speculative connection is that they are the same tour, valued twice, for the same government, with no
reconciliation between them.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the only role that would see both.
- **Musician**: their gear's value, their income's value.
- **Operator**: withholder only; no carnet visibility.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered** _(Low confidence — deferred per template guidance)_:

Not answered at this pass. The hypothesis is that the two value declarations are related in the eyes
of a single tax authority and that an inconsistency between them could matter. It may be entirely
spurious — customs and revenue are usually separate agencies with no shared view, and a carnet's
bonded value has no relationship to a fee's taxable base. Recorded because dismissing it required
thinking about it, and a later specialist may see something here that this pass does not.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 18.14.02 Carnets & Gear Customs | 18.14.01 Visas & Work Permits (as one document flow) | Considered merging into a single "border pack": one crossing, one set of paperwork. Rejected — **people and things fail differently.** A visa problem strands one person while the tour continues; a carnet problem impounds every instrument. Their lead times differ by months, their data sources are disjoint (roster vs manifest), their issuers are unrelated (immigration authority vs chamber of commerce), and only one of them has an Operator duty. A merged "border pack" would average two things that need opposite treatment. |
| R-02 | 18.14.03 Withholding Tax | 18.14.02 Carnets & Gear Customs (via merch) | Considered: merch crossing a border is dutiable (18.15 DT-04) and its sale is taxable — does that bridge these two? Rejected — merch is explicitly **not** carnet gear (18.14.02 D-04), so the carnet has no view of it, and merch VAT/duty is a separate customs matter from performer withholding. The temptation was to connect them because both involve "tax at a border", which is not a relationship. Merch's customs exposure is its own gap, flagged at 18.15. |

> **Notes for agents:**
> - **CX-01's synthesis question 5 is the finding worth carrying**: the carnet is the only object in this domain that **cannot be versioned or change-controlled**. Every other artifact here (riders, schedules, advance packs, manifests) handles change by issuing a new version. A carnet issued by a chamber of commerce does not — an item added mid-tour is simply not covered, and no amount of good software fixes that. The domain's core change-control instinct hits a hard external wall here, and that's worth knowing before anyone designs it.
> - CX-03 is Low confidence and may be spurious. It is recorded per the template's guidance that a considered-and-dismissed interaction has value.
