# Feature: Carnets & Gear Customs

> **Number**: 18.14.02
> **Parent**: [Border, Visas & Carnets](./18.14-border-visas-carnets-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The document that lets you take your gear into a country and — critically — back out again without
paying import duty on your own instruments. An ATA Carnet is a serial-numbered, valued list of every
item, stamped at every crossing. It is the gear manifest (18.09.01) in a customs format, and today
it is typed from scratch by someone reading serial numbers off the back of an amp.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own gear) | Their gear, their serials, their values — from their registry (domain 15). Their instrument is on this list because it's theirs, and it's the thing that gets impounded if the list is wrong. |
| Producer | Full | Assembles the carnet from the manifest, works with the carnet agent, and is the person at the border at 04:00 explaining a discrepancy. |
| Operator | **None** | Nothing to do with the venue. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

A carnet is generated from the tour manifest: every item with a serial, a value, a country of
manufacture and a weight. The list must match **exactly** what's in the van at every crossing — an
item missing, added or sold breaks it. The platform's realistic role is to produce a correct list and
track the crossings; the carnet itself is issued by a chamber of commerce through an agent.

### Happy Path

1. Tour crosses a border; a carnet requirement is flagged with its lead time.
2. The carnet list generates from the manifest — serials, values, origins, weights.
3. Gaps are flagged: 6 of 24 items have no serial (18.09.01 edge case 1).
4. The completed list goes to a carnet agent, who issues the document.
5. Each crossing is stamped and recorded; the return crossing reconciles against the list.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| An item isn't on the carnet | Duty payable, or the item is impounded — no discretion at a border | "Pedal added after the carnet — not covered" |
| An item is sold or lost mid-tour | The carnet is now wrong and won't discharge on the way out | "Item lost in Berlin — carnet won't reconcile" |
| Missing serials | The most common blocker, and it originates in 15's data model | "6 items have no serial — carnets need one" |
| Merch is on the list | **Merch is goods for sale, not temporary import** — carnets don't cover it (18.15 DT-04) | "Merch isn't carnet gear — declare separately" |
| Consumables (strings, batteries) | Not carnet items either; they're consumed, not returned | "Consumables excluded" |
| Rented backline crosses a border | Whose gear is it, and whose carnet? (18.09 Q-02) | `[PENDING — /write-architecture-spec, Q-03]` |
| Values are guessed | The carnet's value is the duty bond's basis — a guess is a financial exposure | "Values from your registry — 4 items unvalued" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A carnet is a customs document — pure compliance, no product | Node Classification Gate | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the gear manifest in a different format.** The platform already holds (or references, via 15) every item with its serial, value and origin. Today someone types that list from scratch off the backs of amplifiers. This is the most literal instance of the consolidation dividend in the entire domain: the hardest document in touring is a projection of a list you already have. |
| DT-02 | The platform should issue carnets | Product ambition | ❌ REJECTED — carnets are issued by chambers of commerce via bonded agents, with a duty bond. That is a regulated financial and legal role, not a software one. **Generate the list, broker the agent** (18.14 Q-03). The list is where the pain is; the issuance is where the liability is. |
| DT-03 | The manifest is enough | Optimism | ❌ REJECTED — the carnet needs **serial, value, country of manufacture and weight**, and domain 15's registry may hold none of the last three (18.09 Q-01). The requirement originates here and lands on 15's data model — a real, actionable cross-domain finding that neither domain would discover alone. |
| DT-04 | The carnet is a one-time document | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it is **stamped at every crossing and must discharge on the final one**. An item lost, sold, or added anywhere in between breaks it, and the failure surfaces at the last border when you're trying to go home. Modelling it as a static document misses that it's a running reconciliation against the manifest, which is precisely what the load-out check (18.09.03) produces. |
| DT-05 | Merch travels on the carnet | Common misconception | ❌ REJECTED — a carnet covers **temporary import of goods that leave again**. Merch is imported *to be sold*, which is a customs and VAT matter entirely (18.15 DT-04). Acts get this wrong constantly and get stopped for it. The distinction is worth encoding because it's a real, common, expensive error. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The carnet list generates from the manifest; we don't issue carnets | The list is where the pain is; issuance is a bonded, regulated role |
| D-02 | Requires serial, value, country of manufacture and weight from 15 | Fields the registry may not hold — the requirement originates here |
| D-03 | A carnet is a running reconciliation, not a static document | Stamped at every crossing; a mid-tour loss breaks the discharge |
