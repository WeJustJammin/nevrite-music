# Feature: Foreign Withholding Tax & Treaty Relief

> **Number**: 18.14.03
> **Parent**: [Border, Visas & Carnets](./18.14-border-visas-carnets-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The tax a country takes off a foreign performer's fee before they ever see it. The US withholds up
to 30% at source; the UK's FEU scheme withholds at the basic rate; most of Europe does something
similar. Treaty relief and expense deductions can reduce it, sometimes to nothing — but only if
somebody applies in advance, and almost nobody at working-musician level knows it exists.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (own income) | Sees that a £2,000 fee will arrive as £1,400 and why — which is more than they know today. Sees what has been withheld across the year, because it's creditable against their home tax. |
| Producer | Full | Sees the withheld amount as a budget line before the tour rather than a shock after it, and tracks the certificates that make it reclaimable. |
| Operator | **Config (often the withholder)** | In most schemes **the payer is legally obliged to withhold** and remit. The promoter is not a bystander — they carry the duty, and getting it wrong is their liability, not the act's. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

A show in a foreign territory carries a withholding rate against the fee (17). The platform surfaces
the expected deduction *before* the date, tracks whether relief was applied for and when, and holds
the withholding certificate — the document that makes the deduction creditable at home. It computes
nothing definitive and files nothing.

### Happy Path

1. Tour includes a US date; the fee is $3,000.
2. The expected withholding is surfaced at routing: up to 30% — the net is $2,100.
3. The relief deadline (a CWA must be applied for well in advance) is flagged with its lead time.
4. Relief is applied for by a specialist; the rate drops.
5. The withholding certificate is captured and flows to 23 as a foreign tax credit.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody knew this existed | The normal case, and the whole reason the feature is proposed | "US dates withhold up to 30% — you'd net $2,100" |
| Relief deadline passed | The full rate applies; relief was available and is now gone | "CWA deadline was 45 days out — full rate now" |
| Certificate never obtained | The deduction can't be credited at home — taxed twice | "No certificate — this can't be claimed against UK tax" |
| Withholding applies to the band, split per member | It's per-performer income, not a band-level fee — a real modelling problem | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Promoter withholds incorrectly | Their liability, the act's money | "Withheld 30% — treaty rate was 0%" |
| Expenses reduce the base | The relief mechanism in several schemes is expense-based — which is what 18.13.03 captures | "Tour expenses may reduce the base — see receipts" |
| Rate/rules change | Per-territory, changes constantly (18.14 Q-02) | Rate set and date shown |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The sweep's border model is missing the money | Deep Think — domain expert lens | ✅ CONFIRMED, node created — the candidate list has visas (the right to work) and carnets (the right to bring gear), which are the two things people *know* about crossing a border. It has nothing about the third: **the destination country taxes the fee at source.** A UK act plays New York, invoices $3,000, and receives $2,100 with no explanation. It is the single most surprising financial fact in international touring and the sweep didn't see it, because it isn't a document you carry — it's money that doesn't arrive. |
| DT-02 | This belongs in 23 (Career, Finance) or 17 (Settlement) | Boundary lens | ⏳ DEFERRED to sub-domain Q-01 — genuinely contested, and the **third boundary question of this exact shape** in this domain (with 18.13 Q-01 on the tour P&L and 18.15 Q-01 on merch). The pattern is consistent: an operational fact produced by touring, consumed by a finance domain. Placed here because the *trigger* is the border crossing and the *deadline* precedes the show — but the argument for 17 (it's a settlement deduction) is strong. |
| DT-03 | The platform should compute the withholding | Product instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **tax advice is regulated**, rates and treaty positions are per-territory and per-circumstance, and a confident wrong number leads to a wrong filing. The honest feature is: *this territory withholds, here's the ballpark, here's the deadline for relief, here's a specialist.* An estimate flagged as an estimate is enormously valuable; a computed figure is a liability. |
| DT-04 | It's niche and can wait | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — `wont` for phasing, but note **the asymmetry**: the information is worth the most to the people who know the least about it. A working musician doing their first US dates is exactly the persona (`personas.md`) who loses 30% of a fee they'd already spent. The value isn't the tracking — it's *knowing this exists before you sign the deal*, which makes the real home for the warning arguably 17, at the moment of the offer. |
