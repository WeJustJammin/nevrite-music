# Feature: Immigration, Visas & Work Permits

> **Number**: 18.14.01
> **Parent**: [Border, Visas & Carnets](./18.14-border-visas-carnets-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The right to work in the country you're playing in. Every crossing has its own answer — some
territories need nothing for a short visit, some need a permit obtained months ahead by a sponsor
who must be local, and getting it wrong means a musician turned around at a border while the rest of
the tour goes on without them.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own documents) | Holds their own passport, visas and permits — **these belong to the person**, across every band and tour, exactly like their access rider. Sees which upcoming dates they're not yet cleared for. |
| Producer | Full | Tracks who needs what for which date, chases documents, and is the person who discovers at the airport that the new drummer's passport expires in four months. |
| Operator | **Read-only (sponsor duties)** | In several territories the **promoter is the visa sponsor** — a legal duty, not a courtesy. They see what they must sponsor and confirm and are genuinely on the hook. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Requirements derive from the roster's nationalities crossed with the tour's territories and dates —
a matrix of person × border. Each cell resolves to `not required`, `required` (with a document,
deadline and validity window), or `unknown`. Lead times are the point: a permit needed in twelve
weeks is actionable, and the same permit needed on Friday is not.

### Happy Path

1. Tour crosses into a territory on date 5; requirements derive per person.
2. The matrix resolves: 6 nationals need nothing; 1 needs a permit with a 10-week lead.
3. Deadline alerts fire against the *lead time*, not the travel date.
4. The permit is obtained (by a specialist) and recorded with its validity window.
5. Every affected date shows cleared; the passport expiry check runs against the last date, not today.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Passport expires mid-tour | Many countries require 6 months' validity beyond entry — checked against the date, not today | "[Name]'s passport expires 12 Aug — 3 dates need 6 months' validity" |
| One person needs a visa, the rest don't | The normal case, and the one that breaks a band | "8 cleared · 1 needs a permit (10 weeks)" |
| Requirement unknown for a territory | `unknown`, never "not required" — the difference is a deportation | "No data for [territory] — get advice" |
| Rules change (they do, constantly) | Encoded rules go stale silently; that's the liability (18.14 Q-02) | Rule-set date shown on every requirement |
| Member joins mid-tour | Their clearance is per-person; the tour's status doesn't transfer | "[Name] joined — 4 dates unchecked" |
| Deadline already missed | The date may be undeliverable for that person | "Permit lead time was 10 weeks — date is in 3" |
| Dep covers because the member can't cross | Then the *performance* personnel changes (18.06.04 D-02) | — |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should tell people which visa they need | Product instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **immigration advice is regulated in most territories** and getting it wrong gets a person refused entry or banned. The platform tracks the *requirement, the document, the deadline and the validity*; it does not advise, and it must never phrase an output as advice. Same posture as 18.16 D-01, with much higher stakes: a wrong compliance certificate delays a show, a wrong visa answer ends someone's tour at a border. |
| DT-02 | It's a tour-level requirement | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **person × border matrix**. Nationality decides everything, and a band is rarely one nationality. The classic failure is exactly this: eight people cleared, one turned around, and the whole tour discovers it at the airport. Modelling at tour level makes the actual failure invisible. |
| DT-03 | The lead time is the product | Domain expert lens | ✅ CONFIRMED — the information is worthless on the day and transformative twelve weeks out. Everything here is a **deadline that precedes the travel date by weeks or months**, and the platform is the only party that knows the dates, the people and their nationalities at the moment the tour is routed. The alert is the feature; the tracking is scaffolding. |
| DT-04 | This is niche | Scope lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a caveat — it's `wont` because it serves the minority who cross borders. But post-Brexit it is **the reason a great many UK acts stopped touring Europe at all**, so its absence is not neutral: it's a barrier the platform could measurably lower, for the persona it's built for. `wont`-for-now is a phasing call, not a judgement that it doesn't matter (18.14 D-04). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No immigration advice — track requirements, documents, deadlines and validity only | Regulated advice; a wrong answer ends someone's tour at a border |
| D-02 | Modelled as a person × border matrix | Nationality decides everything, and a band is rarely one nationality |
