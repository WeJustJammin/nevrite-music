# Feature: Routing Feasibility & Constraint Checking

> **Number**: 18.11.02
> **Parent**: [Tour Container & Routing](./18.11-tour-container-routing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Answering whether a sequence of dates is actually possible: can you physically get from last night's
load-out to tonight's load-in, legally, with a driver who is allowed to drive? It checks the route
against drive times, curfews, load-in windows, border crossings and contract terms — and reports
what breaks, not what's optimal.

> **Merged**: consolidates the sweep's candidate 18 ("Route Optimization & Drive-Time Feasibility").
> The optimisation half is rejected — see DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the consequence in the only terms that matter: "you leave at 06:00 and arrive at 16:00 — that's a nine-hour drive after a 01:00 load-out". |
| Producer | Full | Runs it while sequencing, sees which legs break and why, and uses it to argue for dropping a date or adding a travel day. The person who currently does this with a map and instinct. |
| Operator | **None** | The route is not their business (sub-domain Role Matrix note). They see their own date's load-in window as a constraint the act must meet — never where the act came from or is going. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

For each pair of consecutive dates the check computes the gap between load-out (18.07.01) and next
load-in, compares it against drive time plus mandatory rest (18.12.03), and flags what fails.
Contract constraints from 17 (radius clauses, avails) and border crossings (18.14) participate.
It reports **infeasible / tight / fine**, never a recommended route.

### Happy Path

1. Producer sequences six dates including holds.
2. Feasibility runs across each leg automatically.
3. Leg 4→5 flags: 480 miles, load-out 01:00, load-in 11:00 — infeasible with legal rest.
4. Producer adds a travel day, or drops the hold.
5. The route clears; the tour book (18.11.03) regenerates.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Drive is legal but brutal | Feasible ≠ humane; a 9-hour drive after a 01:00 load-out is a real cost | "Legal, but: 9h drive after a 01:00 load-out" |
| Radius clause forbids a date | A contract term (17) makes a geographically fine date illegal | "Radius clause: no show within 100 mi of [city] before 12 Apr" |
| Border crossing on a leg | Adds hours, and requires documents (18.14) | "Crossing adds 2–4h — unpredictable" |
| Traffic/season variance | A drive time is a distribution, not a number | "6h typical · 9h on a Friday" |
| Fly date | Different constraints entirely — airports, freight, baggage weight | "Fly date: 240 kg over allowance (18.09.01)" |
| Venue's load-in window is narrow | The constraint is the venue's, not the road's | "Load-in 10:00–12:00 only — you arrive 16:00" |
| Route has holds in it | The check runs on speculation, which is the point (18.11 D-03) | "Provisional — 4 of 6 dates are holds" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should optimise the route (the sweep's "Route Optimization") | Candidate 18; TSP/VRP instinct | ❌ **REJECTED — the domain's second clearest scope rejection** (after the playback engine, 18.06.03 D-01). Real routing is dominated by what the platform cannot see: which venues will *offer*, which agent owes whom a favour, which market is worth playing at a loss, radius clauses, avails, and a promoter's willingness. A solver optimising over distance would confidently produce a route no agent would book. **Feasibility is checkable; optimality is a fantasy.** The check is the useful half and it's the half nobody does. |
| DT-02 | Drive time is the constraint | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the constraint is **load-out to load-in with legal rest in between**, which is a different and much tighter number. A 6-hour drive between two shows 20 hours apart is fine; the same drive with a 01:00 load-out and an 11:00 load-in is not, because the driver legally cannot. The interesting arithmetic is the *gap*, not the distance. |
| DT-03 | Feasible means fine | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — legal and humane are different thresholds, and the gap between them is where tours break people. A route that is technically legal but requires nine-hour drives after every show has a cost the platform can *see* and a TM under pressure will discount. Reporting "legal, but" is more honest than a green tick. |
| DT-04 | Contract terms are out of scope | Boundary lens | ⚠️ TENSION — radius clauses and avails are 17's data but they are **routing constraints**, and a route that ignores them is wrong in a way that costs money. The check needs them structured; if 17 stores contracts as PDFs, this check cannot exist (Q-02). Flagged, unresolved. |
| DT-05 | Drive time is a number | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — it's a distribution. Friday afternoon on the M6 is not Tuesday at 06:00, and a route planned on the optimistic number breaks in reality. Presenting a range is honest; presenting a point estimate on a legal constraint is the false-precision failure that also afflicts curfew margins (18.07.02 D-03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **No route optimisation.** Feasibility checking only | Real routing is dominated by avails, offers and relationships a solver cannot see; it would be confidently wrong |
| D-02 | The constraint is load-out → load-in with legal rest, not distance | The gap is the arithmetic that breaks; the distance is not |
| D-03 | Report "legal, but" — feasible is not the same as humane | The gap between legal and survivable is where tours break people |
| D-04 | Drive times are ranges, not points | A route planned on the optimistic number breaks on a Friday |
