# Feature: Itinerary & Tour Book

> **Number**: 18.11.03
> **Parent**: [Tour Container & Routing](./18.11-tour-container-routing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The compiled book of the whole run: every date, venue, address, contact, hotel, travel leg and set
time, in one document people carry. It is the day sheet's tour-scoped sibling — same render
mechanism, wider scope, and the same core problem: the moment it is printed it starts being wrong.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Carries it. Wants to know where they'll be in ten days and which hotel has the good coffee. Sees their own per diem and rooming, not other people's. |
| Producer | Full | Generates it, scopes it per recipient, and re-issues when the route changes. The person who currently maintains it in a Word document. |
| Operator | **None** | Not a recipient. Their date's information reaches them as the advance sheet (18.03.04) — the tour book would show them the whole route, which is not theirs to see (sub-domain Role Matrix note). |
| Fan | None | Never. Announced dates reach fans through 20/21. |

## Behavior

Compiles from the tour container: dates, venues, contacts, travel, hotels, schedules. Role-scoped —
the band's book omits crew pay, everyone's omits the deal terms. Versioned with changed-page
summaries, exportable as PDF, and cached for offline reading, because the tour book's readers are
by definition in transit.

### Happy Path

1. Tour is sequenced; travel and hotels are booked.
2. Producer generates the book and reviews the band-scoped and crew-scoped versions.
3. It is distributed and cached on everyone's device; PDFs are printed for the van.
4. A date moves; the book re-issues with a changed-pages summary.
5. Everyone is re-notified; the printed copies carry a version stamp and a short link.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Route changes after printing | Unrecallable; version stamp + live link is the only mitigation | "v4 — printed copies are v2" |
| Hotel not yet booked | Renders as an explicit gap, never a blank (18.10 D-03) | "Hotel: NOT BOOKED" |
| Reading it on a plane | Must work fully offline | Cached, with a staleness stamp |
| Mostly holds | The book is largely speculative | "Provisional — 6 of 11 dates are holds" |
| Someone joins mid-tour | They get the current version scoped to their role and dates | "Your book covers dates 6–11" |
| Book contains personal data (rooming, dietary, phone numbers) | A tour book is a PII document that gets left in dressing rooms | See Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Book opened | Page skeleton |
| Empty | No dates | "Nothing to compile" |
| Populated | Tour has dates | Compiled book with per-role preview and version |
| Error | Generation failed | "Couldn't generate — retry" |
| Partial | Sources incomplete | "Compiled with 7 gaps — see the list" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The tour book is a distinct document worth authoring | Incumbent behaviour (every TM maintains one in Word) | ❌ REJECTED as an authored object — it is the **third instance of the same render primitive** (advance sheet 18.03.04, day sheet 18.10, tour book). All three compile held data, scope by recipient, version, and distribute; only scope and audience differ. Recorded as the **Document Generation & Distribution** cross-cut rather than three separately-built documents. |
| DT-02 | The tour book and the day sheet are redundant | Near-duplicate check | ❌ REJECTED — different scope and moment. The book is the whole run, read in advance and in transit ("where am I on the 14th?"); the day sheet is today, read this morning ("what time is the bus?"). One is a reference, the other is an instruction. Merging them gives you a 40-page document to answer "when's soundcheck?". |
| DT-03 | Offline is a nice-to-have | Scope lens | ❌ REJECTED — the tour book's readers are *definitionally* in transit: on a plane, in a van, in a country with no data plan. Of the domain's three offline arguments (this, the setlist view 18.06.02 Q-01, and live slippage 18.07.03 Q-01), this one is the most obviously unarguable. |
| DT-04 | It's a convenience document, so low stakes | Privacy lens | ❌ REJECTED — a tour book contains **home addresses, phone numbers, rooming preferences, dietary and medical requirements, and passport details** for a dozen people, in a PDF that gets printed and left in a dressing room. It is quietly the highest-PII-density artifact in the domain, and the industry treats it as a handout. That deserves naming (Q-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A render, not an authored document — third instance of one primitive | A hand-typed tour book is wrong by date 3 |
| D-02 | Distinct from the day sheet | Reference vs instruction; a 40-page answer to "when's soundcheck?" is not an answer |
| D-03 | Offline is mandatory | Its readers are definitionally in transit |
| D-04 | It is a high-PII artifact and must be treated as one | Addresses, phone numbers, dietary/medical data and passports, printed and left in dressing rooms |
