# Feature: Access Rider & Performer Accessibility

> **Number**: 18.04.03
> **Parent**: [Riders](./18.04-riders-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What a disabled performer or crew member needs in order to work: step-free stage access, a seated
position, an accessible toilet within reach of the stage, a BSL interpreter for the advance, low
stimulus space, medication refrigeration, an assistant's credential. The access rider is an
established instrument in the UK live sector (Attitude is Everything's work made it standard), and
it is the one rider item that is not a preference — without it, the person cannot do the job.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Full (own only)** | **Authors their own access rider — nobody else can.** Controls what is disclosed, to whom, and how much detail. Sees whether each venue on the tour can meet it before they get there, which is the thing that does not exist today. |
| Producer | Read-only (scoped) | Sees the *requirements* they must advance and provide for — not the diagnosis behind them. Ensures venues meet them; cannot author, edit, or trade them away (18.03.03 D-03). |
| Operator | Read-only (need-to-know) | Sees only what their room must provide, framed as venue requirements ("step-free stage access required"), not as a named person's medical information. Answers whether they can meet it — honestly. |
| Fan | None | This is performer-side. **Fan-facing venue accessibility is a different thing entirely** — audience accessibility belongs to 16/19 and must never be conflated with this. A venue with a step-free audience entrance may still have a stage reachable only by ladder. |

## Behavior

Owned by the individual, referenced by the act's rider, and disclosed on a need-to-know basis per
requirement. Each item is diffable against a venue's *performer-side* accessibility data in domain
16 — data most venues do not currently hold, which is itself the finding. Because the person owns
it, it travels with them across bands, tours and employers rather than being re-disclosed each time.

### Happy Path

1. Musician authors their access rider once on their own profile.
2. They join a tour; the requirements attach to every date without re-disclosure.
3. Each date's advance diffs them against the venue's performer accessibility data.
4. A venue that can't meet a requirement surfaces *weeks out*, not on arrival.
5. Producer resolves it — a different room, a ramp hire, or an informed conversation with the person.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue has no performer-accessibility data at all | `unknown`, never `match` — and this is the common case | "No performer access data for this venue — ask before you commit" |
| Producer wants to redline an access item | Blocked without the affected person's confirmation | "This is [name]'s access requirement — they must confirm" |
| Person leaves the band mid-tour | Their access rider leaves with them; the requirement is theirs, not the act's | Requirement detaches from remaining dates |
| Person doesn't want the band to know why | Requirement disclosed, reason never | Producer sees "step-free stage access", not a diagnosis |
| Venue is genuinely inaccessible | The show may be undeliverable for this person; surfaced early enough to matter | "3 dates can't meet [requirement] — flagged at booking, not on the day" |
| Access need arises mid-tour (injury) | Rider is live, not a signup-time artifact; re-diffs all remaining dates | "Added — 4 remaining venues need re-checking" |
| Fan-side accessibility confused with performer-side | Explicitly different data on different objects | Venue: "Audience access ≠ stage access — both are asked" |
| Recipient forwards/retains disclosed access data beyond the show | Disclosure is scoped to the date and the requirement; misuse is a 24 Trust & Safety matter, retention bounded per Q-02 | "Access data is shared for this date only — misuse is reportable" |
| Two producers on the same act both try to advance the same access item | Item is owned by the person, not the seat; both see identical read-only requirement, neither can diverge it | "Single source — [name] owns this; you both see the same thing" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Access rider opened | Requirement skeleton |
| Empty | No access requirements | "No access rider" — offered neutrally, never as an omission to fix |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Access requirements are a section of the hospitality rider | Industry precedent (where they've historically been buried) | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the feature's reason to exist as a node. Burying access in hospitality is precisely what makes it invisible and tradeable: it sits next to the towels, so a TM negotiating a buyout treats it like towels. Different owner, different privacy model, different legal weight. Separation is a design position, not taxonomy. |
| DT-02 | The TM owns the act's riders, so the TM owns this | Authority inheritance | ❌ REJECTED — **the only place in this domain where the Musician outranks the Producer.** The requirement belongs to the person who has it. A TM authoring someone's access needs, or trading them away in a negotiation, is a dignity failure and plausibly an unlawful one. Ownership must invert. |
| DT-03 | Venue accessibility data already exists in 16 | Data availability lens | ❌ REJECTED — what exists (where it exists at all) is **audience** accessibility, because that's what licensing and consumer law ask about. Almost no venue publishes whether a wheelchair user can get *onto the stage*. This feature therefore creates a demand for data domain 16 does not yet model — a real, actionable finding for 16 that the sweep missed. |
| DT-04 | Disclosure is binary — the venue sees the rider or doesn't | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the venue needs the *requirement*; almost never the reason. Per-requirement, per-recipient disclosure with the person controlling it is the only posture that gets people to fill this in at all. Over-disclosure means non-disclosure. |
| DT-05 | This is a compliance checkbox — low priority | Scope instinct | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **differentiator with a constituency**. Attitude is Everything's Next Stage research documents disabled artists routinely discovering inaccessibility on arrival, because the information does not exist anywhere in the booking chain. A platform holding both the person's requirements and the room's truth is the first thing that could close that loop. It is also directly thesis-aligned: capture the fact at the point it's true, carry it across contexts, stop re-proving it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The individual owns their access rider — not the act, not the TM | The requirement belongs to the person who has it; TM authorship is a dignity failure |
| D-02 | Separated from the hospitality rider | Burying it next to the towels is what makes it invisible and tradeable |
| D-03 | Per-requirement, per-recipient disclosure controlled by the person | Over-disclosure produces non-disclosure |
| D-04 | Performer accessibility ≠ audience accessibility | A room with a step-free entrance can still have an unreachable stage; 16 models only the latter today |
