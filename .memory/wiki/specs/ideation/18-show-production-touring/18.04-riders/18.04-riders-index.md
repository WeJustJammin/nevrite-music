## Overview

The act's standing requirements, authored once and carried across every date: what the show
technically needs, what the humans need, and what any performer requires to work at all. The whole
domain's wedge depends on these being **structured data rather than a PDF** — an unstructured rider
cannot be diffed against a room, and a rider that can't be diffed is just an attachment.

**Why this is a sub-domain**: four interacting capabilities. The three rider types are authored and
consumed differently (technical diffs against gear, hospitality against amenities, access against
accessibility — with a materially different privacy model), and the template/version/override
capability governs all three, because one rider serves forty dates and each date may need a variant.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Riders are structured data; the PDF is an export, never the source | The entire advancing wedge (18.03.02) requires a machine-comparable rider. If the rider is a PDF, WeJammin is a file host and the domain has no differentiator. This is the single most load-bearing decision in the sub-domain. | Agent, from domain rationale + D-18 |
| D-02 | Riders live at the act/tour level; dates get overrides, never edits | One rider serves 40 dates. Per-date needs are handled by overrides (18.04.04) and redlines (18.03.03), which are different things: an override is *our* variation for this date, a redline is a *negotiated* exception with the venue. | Agent, 2026-07-16 |
| D-03 | Access rider is separated from the technical and hospitality riders | It has a different owner (the person, not the TM), a different privacy model (health-adjacent data, need-to-know), and a different legal weight (accessibility obligations). Folding it into hospitality — where the industry historically buried it — is what makes it invisible and tradeable. | Agent Deep Think, 2026-07-16 |
| D-04 | Three rider types, not one document with sections | They diff against different venue data, carry different confidentiality, and are authored by different people. A single document forces one privacy model onto all three. | Agent classification, 2026-07-16 |
