# Feature: Technical Rider

> **Number**: 18.04.01
> **Parent**: [Riders](./18.04-riders-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The act's structured technical requirements — PA and coverage, console, monitors, backline, power,
stage, lighting, and the crew the venue must supply. Authored once per act (or per tour) as typed
data rather than prose, because this is the object the capability diff (18.03.02) compares against
the room, and prose cannot be compared.

It exists to end a specific failure: the act needs something, the venue can't provide it, and
**nobody finds out until load-in**. The rider is the act's half of the only comparison that can
catch that in advance. Everything about its design follows from being a *diff input* rather than a
document — it is structured because the diff needs structure, and it carries strictness flags
because a diff without them is either uselessly strict or dangerously loose.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own needs) | Authors their own section — "bass rig with a DI, 2 wedges on my side, power stage left". Sees the resolved rider for tonight and what was traded away, never the version history. Cannot set another member's section, cannot cut a version, cannot change another member's flags. For a DIY act with no TM, the Musician *is* the Producer here and authors the whole document — the access path is identical, only the scope differs. |
| Producer | Full | Owns the rider. Adds and removes items, sets each item's three flags (substitution / negotiability / verification) and its supply expectation, resolves conflicts between members' sections, and is the party the venue negotiates with. Cannot override a category floor (D-05) and cannot author or trade away an access requirement (18.04.03 D-01) — the two limits on an otherwise total authority. |
| Operator | Read-only | Receives the date's **resolved version** as part of the advance — never the live document, never the version history, never other dates' overrides. Sees each item's flags, because knowing what is tradeable before asking is the point (D-04). Their answer is a redline (18.03.03) or a correction to their own listing in 16 — never an edit of the act's rider. The one exception in the sub-domain is the input-list patch column (18.05.02 D-02), which is not this file's data. |
| Fan | None | Never visible, at any lifecycle state, in any export. A rider is professional data whose only public-facing history is as tabloid copy. Fan-visible consequences (an accessibility provision changing, a stage-time shift) reach the Fan through 16/19/21 as published facts, never as rider content. |

## Behavior

An item is a typed requirement in one of eight categories — **PA & coverage, console, monitors,
backline, power, stage, lighting, crew** — carrying attributes from a gear taxonomy (Q-01), a
**supply expectation**, **three independent flags**, and **authoring provenance**. Items are
grouped into sections that individual Musicians own; the Producer owns the document.

The rider is authored at act or tour level and inherited by every date. A date never edits it — a
per-date need is an override (18.04.04) and a negotiated exception is a redline (18.03.03). Every
date consumes a resolved *version*, never the live document (CX-01).

### The three flags

Modelling strictness as one axis was the breadth pass's error (DT-05). Three different downstream
consumers each need a different question answered, and they are orthogonal:

| Axis | Values | Question it answers | Consumer |
|------|--------|--------------------|----------|
| **Substitution** | `exact` · `equivalent` · `category` | What counts as meeting this? "M32 or nothing" vs "any 32-ch digital desk" | Diff equivalence model (18.03.02 D-02) |
| **Negotiability** | `hard` · `negotiable` · `nice-to-have` | May this be traded away entirely? | Redlines (18.03.03) |
| **Verification** | `trust-listing` · `require-confirmation` | Does the venue's listing data alone satisfy this, or must a human confirm? | Freeze gate (18.03.02 DT-03) |

They are independent in both directions: an item can be `exact` + `negotiable` (we want precisely
an M32, but if you haven't got one we'll rent — no substitute interests us) or `category` +
`hard` (any 32-channel desk will do, but we cannot play without one). Collapsing them loses a real
distinction the diff and the negotiation each depend on.

**Authoring burden is real and is answered by defaults, not by discipline.** Three dropdowns across
a 40-item rider is 120 decisions, which guarantees defaults get used unexamined. Each taxonomy
category ships a default triple; the author overrides only where their act differs. An item whose
flags are category defaults is marked as such, so the Producer can see at a glance which flags were
actually *decided* and which were merely inherited.

### Category floors

The act sets flags, but **a category can set a floor the act cannot lower** (D-05). Power is the
archetype: voltage, frequency and phase are not preferences, and an act cannot declare 120V/60Hz an
acceptable substitute for 230V/50Hz because physics does not negotiate. Power items are floored at
`exact` on voltage/frequency/phase, `hard` on negotiability, and `require-confirmation` on
verification. The equivalence model is **disabled** for floored attributes — a near-match on power
is not offered, because a near-match on power is a fire.

### Supply expectation

Every item declares who satisfies it: `venue-supplied` · `act-brings` · `rented` ·
`provided-by-production`. This is the single decision that 18.09.01 D-04's "one decision with two
records" refers to, and this is where it is made. **Only `venue-supplied` items are diffed** — an
`act-brings` item is not a requirement on the room, it is a manifest line (18.09.01), and it
appears on the rider as context only. Changing an item from `venue-supplied` to `act-brings`
removes it from the diff and adds it to the manifest in one action.

### Provenance

Every item records **who added it, when, and a free-text rationale**. This is D-18's thesis applied
to the rider itself: the person who knows why the rider asks for a specific thing is the engineer
who wrote it, and that engineer will leave (DT-07). The rationale costs one sentence at authoring
and is unobtainable at any price two years later, when the act is defending a requirement nobody
present understands.

### Happy Path

1. Producer creates the act's technical rider and selects the categories that apply. Categories not
   selected produce no items and no diff comparisons.
2. Producer adds items from the gear taxonomy; each arrives with its category's default flag triple
   and a `venue-supplied` default, both visibly marked as inherited.
3. Producer overrides flags where the act differs — the console goes to `exact` + `negotiable`
   ("M32 or we rent"); the wedges go to `category` + `hard` ("any 4 wedges, but we need 4").
4. Producer writes a one-line rationale on the items whose reason is not self-evident.
5. Band members are invited to their sections; the bassist adds "bass rig, DI, `equivalent`" and
   "power stage left, `hard`" to theirs.
6. System detects that two members have claimed stage-left power and surfaces the conflict to both
   plus the Producer, at authoring, with both claims shown.
7. Producer resolves the conflict; the rider is attached to the tour and a version is cut
   (18.04.04).
8. Each date's advance opens and diffs that date's resolved version against that room (18.03.02),
   comparing only `venue-supplied` items.
9. Shortfalls become advance items; `hard` + `unknown` items block the freeze until confirmed;
   `require-confirmation` items block the freeze even on an automatic match.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Act arrives with a PDF rider | Import is assistive and never silently trusted (Q-01). Parsed items enter `unconfirmed`, are **excluded from the diff**, and count as `unknown` — never `match` (D-09) | "We read 23 items — confirm each before we diff against venues. 0 of 23 confirmed." |
| Requirement has no taxonomy entry | Free-text item, explicitly undiffable, counted as `unknown` (D-11) | "Custom item — won't be checked against venues" |
| Rider says "or similar" / "TBC" | Cannot be structured; the flag model is the correct expression of the intent | "'Or similar' can't be diffed — set substitution to `equivalent` instead" |
| Rider is aspirational (asks for more than the act needs) | System cannot detect it; aggregate redline volume across dates is the only signal (DT-03) | "You've redlined this on 7 of 9 dates" (via 18.04.04) |
| Two members claim the same resource (stage-left power) | Conflict surfaced at authoring to both members and the Producer, not at soundcheck | "Two people claimed stage-left power — [name] and [name]" |
| **Festival / production-supplied date** | No rider variant exists (D-10). A date override sets PA/console/monitors/lighting/crew to `provided-by-production`; those items leave the diff entirely rather than resolving to match or shortfall | "Festival date — 18 items supplied by production, 6 still yours" |
| **Support act on a shared bill** | The headliner's production constrains what's actually available, and the support act cannot see the headliner's rider or plot (permission boundary, 18.02). Their diff is knowingly incomplete and says so | "Shared bill — your diff can't see the headliner's setup. 4 items need a human call." |
| **Power spec mismatch (international date)** | Category floor forbids a near-match; hard shortfall regardless of the act's flags (D-05) | "230V/50Hz required, room is 120V/60Hz — this is not a substitution" |
| **Venue has the item but won't supply it** (house piano, house kit) | `match` means "exists in the room", not "you may use it" (DT-11). Resolved only by the venue's answer — a redline, or a `require-confirmation` flag catching it pre-freeze | "Room lists a Steinway — venue must confirm you can use it" |
| **Member edits their section while a date is freezing** | The edit lands on the live rider (and the next version); the frozen date keeps its version. The member is told explicitly — they believe they have told everyone (CX-01) | "Saved to the rider — **this does not apply to tonight**. [Date] froze on v1." |
| Two members edit the same section concurrently | Conflict is item-level, not document-level; both edits survive, the collision surfaces | "[Name] changed this item while you were editing — keep yours or theirs?" |
| Version cut while a member is mid-edit | The edit continues against the head; on save the member is told which version it landed in (CX-01 Q) | "Saved to v3 — v2 was cut while you were editing" |
| **Access or medical content typed into a technical free-text item** | Detected at authoring and routed to 18.04.03, not stored here. This is CX-04's conflation failure arriving through the back door, and the technical rider's privacy model would export it to every venue | "This looks like an access requirement — those belong to [name], not the rider. Move it?" |
| Free-text item containing a phone number or personal detail | Same routing check; free text is the leak surface, because it is exported into a document a venue opens (DT-09) | "Free text is sent to every venue — remove personal details?" |
| Channel count typed into a rider free-text item | Refused — the count is the input list's and is derived, never restated (D-06). Two copies of one number is two truths | "Channel count comes from your input list (28) — don't restate it here" |
| Soundcheck duration or load-in window typed into the rider | The rider declares the *requirement*; 18.07 owns where it is satisfied. Structured as a timing requirement, referenced by the schedule, not duplicated | "Timing requirements feed the show-day schedule — set it once" |
| **Rider asks for venue crew** (2 loaders, house engineer, lighting op) | Diffed against venue-supplied labour, which 16 may not model — `unknown`, never `match`. Data requirement flowing to 16 (Q-04) | "No data on venue crew — this is a question for the room" |
| Requirement has no counterpart field in the venue schema | `unknown`, never `match` (18.03.02 D-01) | "No data on 3-phase power at this venue" |
| **An act member owns the venue** (multi-hyphenate, both roles at once) | Both roles execute normally — of course you can play your own room. The self-attestation is recorded and **visible**, not blocked: the same identity appears as the rider's author and the confirming Operator | "Confirmed by [name], who also authored this rider" |
| Item is deleted while a date is advanced against that version | Version immutability protects the advanced date; the deletion applies to upcoming dates only (18.04.04 D-02) | "Removed from v3. 3 advanced dates keep it." |
| `hard` + `unknown` at freeze time | Blocks the freeze (18.03.02 DT-03). This is the flag's purpose | "Can't freeze — 2 hard requirements have no answer" |
| `require-confirmation` item matched automatically | Still blocks the freeze until a human at the venue confirms | "Room's data says yes — you asked for confirmation. Awaiting [venue]." |
| **Every item set to `exact` + `hard`** | The diff becomes all-shortfalls and the act has weaponised it into alert fatigue against itself. Not blocked — nudged, with the aggregate shown | "38 of 41 items are hard. Most riders have 5–10 — is that right?" |
| Stage load / flying / rigging requirement | Structural limits are not diffable against a capacity number; judgement item requiring the room's rigging plot | "Rigging needs the venue's plot — human call" |
| Rider author (the FOH engineer) leaves the act | Provenance is retained, not orphaned; the act keeps the *why* even after the who is gone (DT-07) | "Added by [name], Mar 2024 — 'we carry no desk'" |
| Act has no rider at all (most of the target market) | The diff cannot run. Templates are the on-ramp (18.04.04 DT-04) | "Build a rider first — there's nothing to compare" |
| Save fails | Not saved, not agreed, not diffed. No optimistic display of an unsaved requirement | "Couldn't save — retry. This item isn't on your rider yet." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rider opened | Category skeleton |
| Empty | New act, no items | "No rider yet" + start-from-template / import-PDF CTAs (18.04.04 DT-04) |
| Populated | Items exist | Categorised list; per item: flags (inherited vs decided), supply expectation, diffability, provenance |
| Error | Save failed, or version resolution conflict | "Couldn't save — retry" / "Can't resolve this date's rider — conflicting overrides" |
| Partial — undiffable | Some items free-text or unconfirmed | "18 of 23 items can be checked against venues" |
| Partial — unconfirmed import | PDF import pending confirmation | "0 of 23 confirmed — none of these are being diffed yet" |
| Partial — sections outstanding | Members invited, not all contributed | "3 of 5 members have added their needs" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A rider is a document, and the product is a nicer document editor | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — this is the sub-domain's hinge. Every incumbent stores a rider PDF; storing prose makes WeJammin a file host. **Structured-first is what makes the diff exist**, and the diff is the domain's only defensible claim. The PDF is an export for the venue that wants one. |
| DT-02 | Strictness is a property the rider can omit | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — without per-item strictness the diff must choose between reporting every substitution as a failure (alert fatigue) or accepting everything (useless). The act is the only party who knows whether an X32 is fine. Strictness is what makes the equivalence model usable rather than a guess. |
| DT-03 | Riders are aspirational and therefore untrustworthy input | Adversarial lens | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS TENSION — riders routinely ask for more than the act needs, as a negotiating position. The system cannot detect this, but the **aggregate redline pattern can**: an item redlined on 7 of 9 dates is an item the act doesn't need. That signal exists only because the platform sees every date — no incumbent has it. A genuine emergent capability. |
| DT-04 | Band members shouldn't touch the rider | Authority instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the multi-hyphenate reality (`personas.md`) is that the bassist knows their rig better than the TM. Section-level contribution with Producer ownership matches how riders are actually assembled, and surfaces member conflicts at authoring rather than at soundcheck. |
| DT-05 | Strictness is one axis | Breadth pass's own model | ❌ REJECTED — **it is three, and they are orthogonal.** Three siblings independently asked for a different one: 18.03.02 Q-03 wants substitution tolerance for the equivalence model; 18.03.03 Q-02 wants negotiability so venues know what's tradeable before asking; 18.03.02 DT-03 needs a criticality signal to block the freeze on `unknown`. That three consumers each asked separately is the evidence they are real and distinct — an item can be `exact`+`negotiable` (M32 or we rent) or `category`+`hard` (any desk, but we must have one). One axis cannot express either. **Resolves 18.03.02 Q-03 and 18.03.03 Q-02.** |
| DT-06 | The act is free to set any flag on any item | Authority lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — **the category sets a floor the act cannot lower.** An act cannot declare 120V/60Hz an acceptable substitute for 230V/50Hz; the equivalence model must be *disabled* on power's electrical attributes rather than merely strict. Getting a console wrong is inconvenient; getting power wrong destroys gear or kills someone, and touring acts already carry transformers because this failure is routine. The general principle: **where the consequence of a near-match is physical rather than commercial, preference does not get a vote.** |
| DT-07 | The rider is self-explanatory to whoever holds it | Continuity lens | ❌ REJECTED — **riders are written by the FOH engineer, and the FOH engineer leaves.** The act inherits requirements nobody present can explain or defend, which is why riders calcify: no one dares remove an item they don't understand. Per-item provenance (who, when, why) costs one sentence at authoring and is unobtainable two years later. This is D-18's thesis — capture at source rather than reconstruct — applied to the rider itself, and it is what makes DT-03's amendment signal *actionable*: "redlined 7 times, added by an engineer who left in 2024" is a decision; "redlined 7 times" alone is an argument. |
| DT-08 | The rider is the container for all of the act's technical requirements | Scope instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **it is a requirement layer, not a container.** The temptation is to put the channel count, the soundcheck duration and the gear list in the rider because a paper rider has all three. But the input list owns channels (18.05.02 D-01), the schedule owns durations (18.07), and the manifest owns gear (18.09.01 D-01) — and a restated number is a second truth that will drift, which is the exact defect the industry has now. The rider references and derives; it never transcribes. **Resolves Q-02.** |
| DT-09 | Free text is a harmless safety valve for requirements the taxonomy misses | Scope lens | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS RISK — free text is necessary (no taxonomy covers everything) and is simultaneously the feature's **only leak surface**. Two failures, one of them serious: it is exported into a document every venue opens, and it is where someone types "our drummer is diabetic, needs fridge access" because they have never heard of an access rider. That is CX-04's conflation failure arriving through the back door, into a document with the *act's* privacy model and a fan-out to forty venues. Free text must be routed, not merely stored. |
| DT-10 | A festival needs a rider variant | Sweep's framing (18.03 Q-03) | ❌ REJECTED for the rider half — **a variant is a copy, and copies drift into forty truths** (18.04.04 DT-01). What actually changes at a festival is not the rider but the *counterparty*: the PA, console, monitors, lighting and crew belong to the festival's production, are shared across twenty acts, and are not negotiable by anyone. That is expressible as a date override (18.04.04) setting those items to `provided-by-production`, which removes them from the diff rather than resolving them to match or shortfall. No new object is needed. The genuinely open half — whether 16 models a festival's production spec at all, given a festival is a field rather than a room — is not mine and is recorded as Q-03. |
| DT-11 | A `match` means the act will get the item | Diff-consumer instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **capability ≠ availability ≠ consent.** House pianos, house kits and the house console are routinely present and routinely gated ("we have a Steinway; you're not touching it"). The diff compares against a *listing*, which records what exists in the room, not what the venue will hand over. This is not a defect in the diff — it is why the verification axis in DT-05 must exist: the act declaring "this item is too important to trust your data on" is the only mechanism that catches it before load-in. It also means a match on house gear is structurally weaker than a match on a spec attribute, which the default flags should reflect. |
| DT-12 | The multi-hyphenate is a persona-model concern, not a rider concern | Persona lens | ❌ REJECTED — it produces a concrete conflict of interest here. `personas.md` notes the Operator "often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves", so an act playing a venue owned by one of its own members is unremarkable, and that person is simultaneously the rider's author and its confirming recipient. They can answer their own rider's unknowns from the venue side, producing a self-attested match with no second party. Blocking it would be absurd; the correct response is that the identity collision is **recorded and visible** on the attestation. The general rule: where one human occupies both sides of an attestation, the platform's job is disclosure, not prevention. |
| DT-13 | "Crew the venue must supply" is just another rider category | Taxonomy lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — it is a category here, but it diffs against something 16 probably does not model: **venue-supplied labour**. Real riders ask for 2 loaders, a house engineer and a lighting op, and no venue listing schema in the corpus holds that. It also lands on the domain's known persona gap — the local stagehand hired for four hours is not a Musician, Producer, Operator or Fan (domain Q-03). That gap is now corroborated from a **third** independent direction (personas.md Q-01 flagged the dealer; domain 18 Q-03 flagged the stagehand; this feature needs to *ask for* them by count and skill). Recorded as a data requirement flowing to 16 (Q-04) rather than resolved here. |
