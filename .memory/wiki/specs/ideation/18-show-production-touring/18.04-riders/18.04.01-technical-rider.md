# Feature: Technical Rider

> **Number**: 18.04.01
> **Parent**: [Riders](./18.04-riders-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The act's structured technical requirements — PA and coverage, console, monitors, backline,
power, stage size, lighting, crew the venue must supply. Authored once per act (or per tour) as
typed data rather than prose, because this is the object the capability diff (18.03.02) compares
against the room, and prose cannot be compared.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own needs) | Declares their own instrument's needs — "I need a bass rig with a DI, 2 wedges on my side, power stage left". Contributes their slice; doesn't own the document. For a DIY act they author the whole thing. |
| Producer | Full | Owns the rider. Authors and maintains it, sets per-item strictness (hard vs substitutable), and is the person the venue negotiates with over it. The FOH/production lens is the primary author. |
| Operator | Read-only | Receives it as part of the advance. Reads it against their room; can't edit it. Their answer to it is a redline (18.03.03) or a capability correction to their own listing — never an edit of the act's rider. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. A rider is professional data and, when leaked publicly, historically becomes a tabloid story rather than information. |

## Behavior

Items are typed by category (PA / console / monitors / backline / power / stage / lighting / crew)
with structured attributes and a strictness flag. Each item is diffable against the venue's spec
in domain 16. The rider is authored at act or tour level and inherited by dates; a per-date need is
an override (18.04.04), never an edit.

### Happy Path

1. Producer creates the act's technical rider, choosing categories that apply.
2. Items are added from a gear taxonomy with attributes (channel count, wattage, quantity).
3. Each item gets a strictness flag — "M32 or nothing" vs "any 32-ch digital desk".
4. Band members add their own instrument-level needs to their sections.
5. The rider is attached to the tour; every date's advance diffs it against that room.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Act arrives with a PDF rider | Import path; parsing is assistive and never silently trusted (18.04 Q-01) | "We read 23 items — confirm each before we diff against venues" |
| Requirement has no taxonomy entry | Free-text item, explicitly marked undiffable | "Custom item — won't be checked against venues" |
| Rider says "or similar" / "TBC" | Cannot be structured; flagged as a judgement item | "'Or similar' can't be diffed — set a strictness rule instead" |
| Rider is aspirational (asks for more than the act needs) | System can't know; the redline volume across dates is the signal | "You've redlined this on 7 of 9 dates" (via 18.04.04) |
| Different band members contradict each other | Conflict surfaced at authoring, not at soundcheck | "Two people claimed stage-left power" |
| Festival/shared-bill context | Some items become the promoter's, not per-act | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — festival rider variants unresolved (18.03 Q-03) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A rider is a document, and the product is a nicer document editor | Incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — this is the sub-domain's hinge. Every incumbent stores a rider PDF; storing prose makes WeJammin a file host. **Structured-first is what makes the diff exist**, and the diff is the domain's only defensible claim. The PDF is an export for the venue that wants one. |
| DT-02 | Strictness is a property the rider can omit | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — without per-item strictness the diff is forced to choose between reporting every substitution as a failure (alert fatigue) or accepting everything (useless). The act is the only party who knows whether an X32 is fine. Strictness is what makes the equivalence model usable rather than a guess. |
| DT-03 | Riders are aspirational and therefore untrustworthy input | Adversarial lens | ⚠️ CONFIRMED AS TENSION — riders routinely ask for more than the act needs, as a negotiating position. The system cannot detect this, but the **aggregate redline pattern can**: an item redlined on 7 of 9 dates is an item the act doesn't actually need. That signal exists only because the platform sees every date — no incumbent has it. Recorded as a genuine emergent capability. |
| DT-04 | Band members shouldn't touch the rider | Authority instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the multi-hyphenate reality (`personas.md`) is that the bassist knows their rig better than the TM. Section-level contribution with TM ownership matches how riders are actually assembled, and it surfaces member conflicts at authoring rather than at soundcheck. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Structured data first; PDF is an export | A prose rider cannot be diffed, and the diff is the domain's differentiator |
| D-02 | Per-item strictness flags are required, not optional | Without them the diff is either uselessly strict or dangerously loose |
| D-03 | Band members contribute sections; the Producer owns the document | Matches how riders are actually assembled and catches member conflicts early |
