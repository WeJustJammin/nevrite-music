# Feature: Hospitality Rider

> **Number**: 18.04.02
> **Parent**: [Riders](./18.04-riders-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What the humans need to get through the day: catering and dietary requirements, dressing rooms,
towels, water, parking, wifi, guest list allowance, buyout terms. Structurally identical to the
technical rider but diffed against a room's *amenities* rather than its gear — and carrying dietary
and medical-adjacent data that the technical rider does not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config (own needs) | Declares their own dietary requirements and personal needs. This is the rider that most directly affects their day, and the one they most often discover was traded away. |
| Producer | Full | Owns and maintains it; negotiates buyouts (cash instead of catering) and handles the head count that drives every quantity in it. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads it to cater the show. Sees dietary requirements as *requirements*, not as named people's medical information wherever a count suffices. Proposes buyouts and substitutions via redlines. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. Leaked hospitality riders are the genre of music journalism this feature must never feed. |

## Behavior

Quantities derive from the party size (band + crew from 18.08.01), so a rider written for a
five-piece doesn't silently under-cater a nine-person touring party. Dietary requirements are
attached to people but exposed to the venue as aggregated counts wherever possible. Buyouts convert
an in-kind item to cash and hand the amount to tour finance (18.13).

### Happy Path

1. Producer authors the hospitality rider at tour level with categories and quantities.
2. Band members add dietary requirements to their own profiles; the rider aggregates them.
3. Party size resolves from the crew roster; quantities auto-scale.
4. Venue reads it in the advance and confirms, or proposes a buyout via redline.
5. Accepted buyout amount flows to the tour float (18.13.01) and appears on the day sheet.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Party size changes after the advance | Quantities go stale in a frozen pack; change control flags it | "Party grew to 9 — catering says 6" |
| Dietary requirement is medical (coeliac, severe allergy) | Distinct from preference; cannot be quietly dropped in a buyout | "1 severe allergy — buyout requires explicit confirmation" |
| Venue can't cater at all | Buyout redline; amount must cover the local reality, not a fixed rate | "No kitchen — £20/head buyout proposed" |
| Rider written for the band, but crew eat too | The classic under-count; party size derivation exists to prevent it | "Rider covers 5 — the touring party is 9" |
| Guest list allowance in the rider vs ticketing's allocation | Two systems, one number — must reconcile with 19 | "Rider says 10 guests; box office allocated 6" |
| Dietary data crosses a border / GDPR | Health-adjacent personal data shared with a third party | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — routed to `/create-prd-security` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Hospitality is the trivial rider — towels and beer | Cultural stereotype | ❌ REJECTED — it carries **dietary and medical-adjacent personal data about named individuals**, shared with a third party (the venue) in a different jurisdiction. That is the most privacy-sensitive object in the domain and it is the one everyone assumes is a joke. A coeliac's requirement is not a preference and cannot be handled like a towel count. |
| DT-02 | Quantities are typed by the TM | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the single most common hospitality failure is catering for the band and forgetting the crew. Party size already exists in the crew roster; deriving quantities from it eliminates the error class entirely rather than reminding someone to remember. |
| DT-03 | Dietary requirements should be sent to the venue with names attached | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED — the venue needs to know *what to cook*, not who is allergic. Aggregate counts by default; named disclosure only where the requirement is severe enough that the individual must be identifiable on site, and then with the person's knowledge. Default-aggregate is the correct privacy posture. |
| DT-04 | Buyouts are a settlement concern | Boundary lens | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the buyout *amount* is money and touches 17/18.13, but the *decision* to convert catering to cash is a rider negotiation and belongs in the redline flow. The item is authored here, negotiated in 18.03.03, and paid through 18.13. Three nodes, one fact — recorded in the CX rather than duplicated. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Quantities derive from party size, never typed | Catering for the band and forgetting the crew is the domain's most repeated error |
| D-02 | Dietary requirements are exposed to venues as aggregate counts by default | The venue needs to know what to cook, not who is allergic |
| D-03 | Dietary data belongs to the person's profile (01), referenced here | Authored once, carried across every act and tour that person works on |
