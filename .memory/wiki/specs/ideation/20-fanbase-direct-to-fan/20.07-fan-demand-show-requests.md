# Feature: Fan Demand & Show Requests

> **Number**: 20.07
> **Parent**: [Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan](./fanbase-direct-to-fan-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The inverse of the gig alert: instead of telling fans where an artist is playing, it tells the artist where
their fans are. Fans ask for a show in their city; the artist sees a map of demand built from **their actual
owned audience** — ticket buyers, purchasers, members, followers — not from streaming numbers they cannot
verify or act on. It answers the question every touring musician asks and nobody can answer: *where is it
worth playing?*

> **Feature, not sub-domain.** The request and the demand map are one loop, not two interacting capabilities:
> a request with no map is a suggestion box, and a map with no requests is [20.02.01](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.01-segment-builder.md)
> with a geo filter. Its neighbours are cross-domain (18 routing, 17 booking), not intra-domain — which is why
> it sits directly under the domain rather than inside a sub-domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees fan density by city/region, weighted by fan quality rather than headcount ([20.02.02](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.02-superfan-score.md)). Sees explicit requests. Takes it into routing (18) and booking (17). Can convert a demand cluster into a target: *"420 fans within 40 miles of Leeds, 60 have been to a show"*. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. |
| Operator | Reports | **The under-appreciated half.** A venue seeing which artists have fan demand in *their* catchment is a booking tool with no incumbent — the Operator's stated pain is a perishable calendar, and this is the only signal that says which act would fill a Tuesday. See DT-03. |
| Fan | Full (requests) | Asks for a show in their city. Sees others asking. **Must never be led to believe a request is a commitment from the artist** — a request board that implies promises is a disappointment machine. |

## Behavior

Two halves, one loop:

**Passive demand** — computed from the fan graph: where the artist's fans are, weighted by fan quality. This
is the honest, always-available half, and it is the one no competitor can build: streaming platforms know
where you are *streamed* (unverifiable, unactionable, and Spotify will not give the artist the addresses);
we know where fans **bought tickets and walked through doors** (domain 19).

**Active requests** — fans explicitly asking. Higher intent, much sparser, and vulnerable to the classic
demand-board failure: a small organised group looks like a city.

The two must be presented together and never conflated. 400 quiet fans in Leeds is a better booking signal
than 40 loud requests from Bristol, and an artist who cannot see the difference will route their tour wrong.

### Happy Path

1. Musician plans a UK tour; opens demand
2. Map: Manchester 890 fans (140 have attended a show), Leeds 420 (60 attended), Bristol 180 (12 attended, but 60 explicit requests)
3. Musician sees Leeds is worth a room and Bristol is loud rather than large — **the distinction that decides whether the tour loses money**
4. Converts the Leeds cluster into a routing target (18); finds a room (16); books (17)
5. Announces; the alert fires ([20.06.02](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md)) to exactly the 420
6. Attendance is scanned; the fan record deepens; the next demand map is sharper. **The loop closes and compounds**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Most fans have no location** | Cripples the map — the same problem as [20.06.02](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md). The map is only as good as the location data, and location is the least-supplied field the domain has. | The gap named: "620 of your 1,400 fans have no location." |
| Requests are brigaded | 60 requests from Bristol from one WhatsApp group of 12 people. Classic demand-board failure. | Weight requests by fan quality; show attendance alongside. |
| **Fan requests a show, artist never comes** | Inevitable and the emotional failure mode. A request board that implies a promise turns fans into people who were let down. | Framing is the whole design: an artist listening, not a petition that succeeds. See DT-02. |
| Demand exists where the artist cannot economically play | 400 fans in Sydney; a UK band. Real demand, unactionable. | Show it — it is still true, and it is the thing an artist most wants to know exists. |
| New artist, no fan graph | Empty map. This feature is worthless until there is an audience — it is downstream of the whole domain. | Empty state that says so. |
| Requests from fans who never buy | Loud, free, and uncorrelated with attendance. | Weighted by [superfan score](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.02-superfan-score.md). |
| Demand map reveals individuals in a sparse region | "3 fans in Inverness" is close to naming them, and it is a real disclosure of a person's location to an artist. | k-anonymity floor on the **map**, not on the fan record. Ties to [20.02.01](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.01-segment-builder.md) DT-03. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is an analytics feature and belongs in domain 22 | Node Classification Gate — it looks like a dashboard | ❌ REJECTED — 22 is **aggregate market intelligence**; this is **the artist's own identified audience**, per-fan, weighted by attendance and spend that this domain owns. The boundary was drawn deliberately when the domain was renamed (Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan, to kill the collision with the analytics domain). More decisively: this is not a report, it is a **loop** — requests come in, the map informs routing, the show is booked, the alert fires, the scan deepens the graph, the next map is sharper. 22 does not close loops; it observes. |
| DT-02 | Fan requests are the valuable half | The candidate name leads with "Show Requests"; it is the visible, interactive part | ❌ REJECTED — **passive demand is the valuable half.** Requests are sparse, self-selecting, brigadable, and free to give; 40 loud requests from Bristol are worth less than 400 quiet ticket-buyers in Leeds. But the map is only computable because we hold verified purchase and **door-scan** data (19) — which is precisely what Spotify's "where you're streamed" cannot be and what Bandsintown cannot see. The request board is the feature that *looks* like the product and the demand map is the product. Worth stating loudly, because the request board is also the half that manufactures disappointment (see the edge cases) — **building the loud half first would be actively harmful.** |
| DT-03 | This is an artist-side feature | The framing throughout — it is about where to tour | ⏳ **Under-scoped, and I think this is a real miss.** The **Operator** may want it more. `meta/personas.md` says a venue's inventory is "time and space… perishable — an unsold Tuesday is gone forever", and their pain is a calendar that is the business. A venue seeing *which artists have real fan demand in this catchment* is a booking tool with no incumbent product and a direct line to their core pain. It also inverts the domain's usual direction: normally an artist brings their audience to a room; here the room discovers which artist's audience is already outside its door. That may be a stronger product than the artist-side one and it is currently a `Reports` cell. Flagged — I am not confident enough to restructure on it. Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Feature, not sub-domain | Request and map are one loop; its real interactions are cross-domain (17/18/19), not intra-domain. |
| D-02 | Passive demand leads; requests are secondary | See DT-02. The map is the product; the request board is the part that looks like it and manufactures disappointment. |
| D-03 | Requests are never framed as commitments | An artist listening, not a petition that can succeed. The alternative converts fans into people who were let down. |
| D-04 | The no-location gap is always named | A map silently missing 40% of the audience routes a tour into an empty room. |
| D-05 | k-anonymity floor on the map | "3 fans in Inverness" names people. The floor belongs on the map, not on the fan record ([20.02.01](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.01-segment-builder.md) DT-03). |
