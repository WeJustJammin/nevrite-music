# Feature: Digital Music Sales, Name-Your-Price & Bundles

> **Number**: 20.04.03
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-22

## Overview

Selling the music itself directly to fans: downloads, albums, name-your-price, and the bundle that made D2F
work — *buy the vinyl, download it now*. NYP is not a pricing gimmick; it is the mechanic that let Bandcamp
pay artists on the order of $1.5B, because fans routinely pay a multiple of the ask when they know the money
reaches the person who made it — this file's own happy path takes £20 on a £5 minimum (4× the floor, 2.5× the
£8 suggestion). It is also the mechanic that breaks the split's basis
(`-cx.md` CX-03), which is a problem nobody has had to solve before, because no storefront ever knew the split
— **now solved**: one platform-wide constant, paid-net, disclosed at split capture (20.04.04 D-10/D-11).

> **Merged** — the sweep listed "Digital Music Sales, Name-Your-Price & Bundles" (11) as one candidate and I
> have kept it as one. Bundling is inseparable from digital sales: the bundle exists *because* the download is
> instant and the record is not.

**Boundary with [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md)**: this file owns the **buy-side** — pricing,
NYP mechanics, format delivery, entitlement, the bundle's two-lifecycle order. The **pay-side** — how the
money is divided and routed to collaborators once collected — lives in 20.04.04. The two meet at exactly one
seam: the **NYP basis** (`-cx.md` CX-03), and that seam is now much thinner than it was. The basis is a
**platform-wide constant — paid-net — stated as a term of service and printed on the split-capture screen**
(20.04.04 D-10/D-11, ratified DQ-19.1/19.2/19.3). It is **not a listing field**, so this file no longer
captures or locks anything: the storefront's whole remaining obligation is to **display** the rule (on the
product page and the receipt) and to keep the fan-facing promise honest; 20.04.04 applies it at payout.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Lists a track/album/download. Sets a fixed price **or** NYP (minimum ≥ £0, optional suggested price ≥ minimum). Picks delivery formats from {MP3 320 kbps, FLAC, WAV}. Bundles a digital download with a physical pre-order, setting each component's notional value at listing (drives refund apportionment — see edge cases). Sees, per product, what fans *actually paid over the ask* — the median and total over-payment, the number no streaming dashboard will ever surface. When they are **also a payee** on the same record (multi-hyphenate: they drummed on their bandmate's release), they see both the seller view here and the payee view in [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md); the two are never conflated. |
| Producer | None (on this feature) | No catalogue or pricing access — they do not run the shop. **But this is the product type whose sale pays out their session split** — see [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md), the one cell in domain 20 where the Producer persona appears. Explicit absence here is deliberate: pricing a record is the releasing artist's decision, not the engineer's, even though the engineer is paid from it. |
| Operator | None | Venues do not sell recordings. Explicit absence — a venue's shop ([20.04.01](./20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md)) is merch-only. A venue that presses a live-show record sells it *as the artist entity*, through that artist's D2F shop, not as the venue. |
| Fan | Read-only | Buys, pays more than asked if they want to (NYP), picks a format, downloads immediately, keeps it forever in [20.06.03](../20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md). Sees who played on it (domain 02) on the product page — where a credit stops being a database row and becomes a reason to care. Sees the platform's cut stated plainly (D-02). A fan who is *also* a collaborator on the record (multi-hyphenate) buys through the normal fan flow; their purchase is a sale like any other and their separate payee entitlement is untouched by it. |

## Behavior

**Name-your-price**: the artist sets a **minimum** (may be £0) and an optional **suggested price** (≥ minimum).
The fan enters any amount ≥ minimum. The over-payment is the point. Fans overpay when the line from their money
to the artist is short and visible — so the platform's job is to make that line visible (D-02: state our cut),
not to optimise a checkout. The entered amount is validated **server-side** against the minimum on every
submission; a client that posts below the minimum is rejected (see malicious-input edge cases), never silently
floored.

**The NYP basis is a platform-wide constant, not a property of the product** (20.04.04 D-10). It is
**paid-net** — the amount the fan actually paid, net of the platform cut and processor fees — stated as a term
of service and printed on the **split-capture screen** beside the percentages, which is where the payee sees it
*before they agree the split* (`-cx.md` CX-03). The listing flow has **no basis control at all**: nothing to
choose, nothing to capture, nothing to lock, and no way to vary it for one record. The fan sees, on the product
page, the plain line "your payment is shared with the people who made this record" (amounts hidden — DT-04 on
20.04.04) and the basis rule stated plainly alongside the platform's cut (D-02); the receipt repeats it. The
constant is **versioned as a platform term** (20.04.04 D-11), so a change binds only splits agreed after its
effective date — but that versioning lives entirely on the pay-side and never reaches this file's listing flow.

**Bundles** (`-cx.md` CX-01): one purchase, two lifecycles. Digital entitles instantly and irreversibly;
physical ships in a lead time the artist states at listing (the happy path's is 12 weeks) and may slip past it.
The digital entitlement **stands regardless** of the
physical's fate (D-01) — you cannot unhear a record and should not try. At listing the artist records a
**notional value per component** (digital £X, physical £Y, bundle price £Z where typically Z ≤ X+Y); these
captured values — not an after-the-fact fraction — govern any partial refund, making apportionment deterministic
rather than arbitrary (resolves `-cx.md` CX-01 Q5; D-06).

**Formats**: MP3 320 kbps, FLAC, WAV. Fans who buy downloads rather than stream disproportionately want FLAC,
and they are exactly the superfans this domain serves — so format is not a footnote. The fan chooses format
**at or after purchase**; the entitlement is format-agnostic (one purchase entitles all offered formats — you
bought the record, not a file), and re-downloading in a different format later is free and unlimited.

**Delivery** is CX-M10 (Media Handling); **entitlement** is CX-M24; **the library** is
[20.06.03](../20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md). Delivery failure never
revokes entitlement (see Error state).

### Happy Path

1. Musician lists the album: NYP, minimum £5, suggested £8; formats MP3/FLAC/WAV enabled. There is no basis step — the platform rule (paid-net) is stated on the listing screen as a fact about how splits pay, not offered as a choice (20.04.04 D-10).
2. System resolves the rights record from the session (domain 09) and binds the payout plan: artist 70%, producer 20%, drummer 10% (`-cx.md` CX-02, via [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md)). The musician **confirms a fact, not fills a form**.
3. Musician adds a vinyl pre-order bundled with the instant download: bundle price £20, notional digital £8, notional physical £15, pressing of 300, stated lead time 12 weeks.
4. Fan opens the product: player preview, credits (domain 02), the "shared with the people who made this" line, formats, NYP field pre-filled at the £8 suggestion, our cut stated.
5. Fan raises the amount to **£20** — four times the minimum, because they love the record and know where the money goes — and pays once.
6. At payment settlement: digital entitlement granted **immediately and irreversibly**; fan picks FLAC and downloads; the physical enters CX-M14 fulfilment with a "ships in ~12 weeks" state.
7. Payout runs in [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md): platform cut off the top, split applied on the **platform basis rule** (paid-net, at the terms version bound to that split agreement), drummer/producer paid, statements name the session. Nobody chased anybody.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **NYP over-payment — what is the split's basis?** | **Settled: paid-net, as a platform-wide constant** (20.04.04 D-10/D-11, ratified DQ-19.1/19.2/19.3). Not a listing field and not a product property — a term of service, printed on the split-capture screen where the payee sees it before agreeing (`-cx.md` CX-03), shown on the product page, and repeated on the fan's receipt. The over-payment is therefore shared, which is the only reading consistent with the promise the product page makes (DT-04 on 20.04.04). The storefront neither captures nor locks it; it displays it. | Fan: receipt "£20 · shared with the 3 people who made this record". Payee (in 20.04.04): "paid on: amount received, net of platform cut and fees". |
| **Artist wants a per-listing basis** (charity release paying on the ask, a one-off arrangement) | **Not expressible.** There is no basis field on a listing, a product or a split — one platform constant governs every sale (20.04.04 D-10). Supporting per-record variation means reopening DQ-19.1, not toggling a setting. The lister's levers are the price rule (floor/suggested) and the split percentages (domain 09). | Lister: no basis control appears anywhere in the listing flow. |
| **NYP minimum £0 on a work owned by more than one party** | Setting minimum £0 can give away a collaborator's percentage. The storefront **gates it on consent**: a multi-owner work (rights record shows >1 payee) rejects a £0 minimum unless every payee has consented to zero-floor pricing at listing. The listing is refused as co-owner-consent-required; the lister gets a one-tap "request consent" flow. Whether the platform should *ever* permit it is Q-02 (user); the *gate* is specified. | Lister: "This record has 3 rights-holders. A £0 minimum needs their OK — request it?" |
| **NYP minimum £0 on a sole-owner work** | Fully allowed and valuable. A £0 download still creates a fan record ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) and an email — worth more than £0. A 100%-of-£0 payout is £0 with no collaborator to object. | Fan: "Name your price (from £0)". Downloads free; enters library. |
| **Bundle: pressing cancelled after the download was taken** | Digital stands (D-01). The physical refund equals the **captured physical notional** from listing (£15 in the happy path), never a re-derived fraction of the bundle price. If bundle price £20 < notional sum £23, the physical notional is scaled proportionally (£15 × 20/23 = £13.04) so refunds can never exceed what was paid. Deterministic; resolves `-cx.md` CX-01 Q5 (D-06). Routes through CX-M01 refund + CX-M14 cancellation. | Fan: "Your download is unaffected. £13.04 refunded for the cancelled vinyl." |
| **Fan re-downloads years later, or in a new format** | Must work. A download bought is a download owned — the promise separating this from streaming. Entitlement is format-agnostic and has no expiry; re-download and format-switch are free and unlimited. | Library, indefinitely, all offered formats. |
| **Artist removes a track from sale after fans bought it** | Existing entitlements survive removal, always (D-01). New sales stop; the product page 404s or shows "no longer available"; buyers keep it. | Buyers: stays in their library. New visitors: unavailable. |
| **Uncleared sample in a sold track (negligent/malicious upload)** | The platform is in the distribution chain. On a valid takedown/dispute (domain 24 / CX-M11 DMCA), **new sales halt immediately**, the listing is suspended, and existing entitlements **survive** (D-01) unless a legal order requires revocation — a domain 24 decision, not a storefront one. Buyers are notified per DMCA obligation. Whether the sample *is* cleared is a domain 09/11 question (upstream); the storefront's halt-and-preserve behaviour is specified here. | Fan (buyer): notice if legally required. Visitor: "unavailable". Lister: suspension notice + 24 case link. |
| **Fan buys the same album twice (forgot they own it)** | The storefront checks the fan's existing entitlement (CX-M24) at add-to-cart. If found, it warns before charging and offers a gift purchase instead. Taking a superfan's money twice for one download is a small theft against the person who supports them most. | "You already own this — buy it as a gift instead?" |
| **Track is partly owned; artist tries to list it unilaterally** | At listing the storefront resolves the rights record (`-cx.md` CX-02). If the work is multi-owned and the lister lacks unilateral sale authority (domain 09 usually forbids it), the **Buy button cannot go live**: listing is blocked as sale-authority-required until co-owner consent or the domain-09 authority is present. The *authority model* is domain 09 (upstream); the *listing gate* is specified here. Consistent with 20.04.04 CX-02. | Lister: "You own 40% of this recording. Selling it needs the other owners' OK." |
| **Track has credits (domain 02) but no rights record (domain 09)** — the launch-catalogue common case | Sale is allowed but the absence is **loud and asymmetric** (20.04.04 DT-02): "No split on file — you're taking 100%. Is that right?" plus a one-tap "add the people who played on this". The product page shows credits where they exist and their absence where they do not. Q-02 (user) decides whether this stays permitted; the storefront behaviour (allow + loud acknowledgement + easy honest path) is specified. | Lister: 100%-acknowledgement interstitial + "add collaborators". Fan: credits shown, or their absence shown. |
| **Concurrency — two fans buy the last unit of a 300-pressing bundle at the same instant** | Physical stock is decremented **atomically** at payment settlement. The first commit wins; the second's physical component fails as sold-out. The fan is offered the **digital-only** purchase at its notional price (the download never sells out); no charge is captured for the unavailable physical. | Second fan: "The vinyl just sold out — buy the download alone for £8?" |
| **Race — fan double-submits the NYP form (double-click, retried request)** | Purchases carry an **idempotency key** (CX-M01); duplicate submissions within the 24 h window collapse to a single charge and a single entitlement. The second response returns the first purchase's result, not a new one. | One charge, one download. No duplicate in library. |
| **Network failure — payment succeeds but download delivery (CDN) fails** | Entitlement is granted at **payment settlement**, before delivery is attempted. A failed download is a retry, not a lost purchase — **never lose a paid entitlement to a CDN failure**. Delivery retries with backoff; the library link is durable. | "Payment complete. Preparing your download… retry." Entitlement already in library. |
| **Network failure — fan closes the tab mid-payment** | Idempotent (CX-M01). Either the charge settled (entitlement appears in library; a resumed session shows it) or it did not (no charge, cart intact). Never a charge without an entitlement or an entitlement without a charge. | On return: "You already bought this" **or** cart unchanged. |
| **Malicious input — tampered request posts an amount below the minimum (or negative)** | Rejected server-side. Amount must be a positive value ≥ the product's minimum; violations are rejected as below-minimum. The minimum is re-read from the product, never trusted from the client. | "Minimum for this record is £5." |
| **Malicious input — absurdly high NYP amount (e.g. £1,000,000, card-testing / laundering)** | Amounts above a per-item confirmation threshold (default **£1,000**) require an explicit "yes, I meant to pay this" interstitial and pass through CX-M01 fraud screening before capture. This catches both fat-finger and abuse without punishing a genuine £200 super-fan payment. | "You're paying £1,000 — confirm?" then fraud check may hold/decline. |
| **Multi-hyphenate — the record's paid collaborator buys their own record as a fan** | Handled as a normal fan sale: their purchase is charged in full and creates an entitlement. Their **separate payee share** (they drummed on it) is paid by 20.04.04 from the sale like any other — they are paid their percentage *of their own purchase's revenue* along with every other buyer's. No netting, no "you can't buy your own record", no double-counting: the buy-side and pay-side are independent ledgers meeting only at the split. | Buyer view: normal purchase + download. Payee view (20.04.04): the sale, including their own, appears in their statement. |
| **Split changes between add-to-cart and settlement (a dispute resolves mid-checkout)** | The payout plan **version** is bound at **payment settlement**, not at add-to-cart, and the entitlement records that version. Sales before the change pay the old plan, after pay the new (20.04.04). Retroactivity is domain 09/10 and is not improvised here. | Fan: unaffected (they pay one price). Payee statement: names the plan version that governed the sale. |
| **Partial data — bundle where the digital is ready but the master is still encoding** | The product cannot go live for sale until CX-M10 confirms all offered formats are encoded and playable. A listing with a pending encode shows a **draft** state to the artist and is not purchasable; it never sells a download it cannot deliver. | Artist: "Encoding FLAC… not yet on sale." Fan: product not visible. |
| **Cover song sold D2F (statutory mechanical owed)** | Selling a recording of someone else's composition owes a mechanical (domain 11 / CX-M11). The storefront flags a work marked as a cover at listing and blocks sale until the licensing obligation is acknowledged/resolved upstream; it does not compute the mechanical itself. | Lister: "This is a cover — a mechanical licence is required before selling." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Product opened | Player + price/NYP skeleton; credits placeholder |
| Empty | Artist has no digital products listed | For the artist: their released records from domain 12, offered as one-tap products — the platform already knows what they made. For the fan: nothing (the shop tab is hidden until ≥1 product exists). |
| Populated | ≥1 product | Player, NYP field (min + suggested) or fixed price, format selector {MP3/FLAC/WAV}, credits (domain 02), the "shared with the makers" line, stated platform cut, the platform basis rule (paid-net), Buy |
| Error | Media delivery unavailable at/after purchase | Purchase completes and entitlement stands; download retries with backoff. **Never lose a paid entitlement to a CDN failure.** For a pre-purchase encode failure, the product is not purchasable (see Partial). |
| Partial | Bundle: digital delivered, physical pending (or physical sold out mid-order) | Two states in one order, clearly separated — "Download ready · Vinyl ships ~12 weeks" — never one confusing "partially complete". If the physical sold out at settlement, the order shows the digital granted and the physical declined with no charge for it. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | NYP works because fans are generous | The folk explanation | ❌ REJECTED — too vague to design on. NYP works because **the line from the fan's money to the artist is short and visible**. The fan knows £20 reaches the person who made the thing, unlike a stream worth £0.003. That is a mechanism with a design consequence: the platform must **show the line** — that money goes to the artist and the drummer, show the cut, show the split's existence. A platform that obscures its cut destroys the exact mechanism NYP depends on. This is the strongest argument in the domain for radical transparency about our own fee (index Q-01). |
| DT-02 | The bundle is a convenience feature | It looks like buying two things at once | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **cashflow instrument**. The fan pays in November for a record pressed in March; the artist gets the money now and presses with it. That mechanism finances independent vinyl, and it is functionally identical to [crowdfunding](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.04-crowdfunding-preorder-campaigns.md) — so it carries the **same PledgeMusic hazard**: fan money spent on something else, record never pressed. A pre-order bundle is a promise with a supply chain attached, and it should sit under the same fund-segregation policy as a crowdfunding campaign ([20.05.05](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)). **Nobody in the candidate list connected pre-orders to the crowdfunding hazard** — this file's most important finding. |
| DT-03 | Digital sales are dying — streaming won; this is nostalgia | The market-share read, true in aggregate | ❌ REJECTED for **this** audience. Aggregate digital sales collapsed; D2F digital did not, because it is a different transaction. A fan buying a FLAC on Bandcamp Friday is not buying access to music (they have Spotify) — they are **paying an artist they love and receiving a token of it**. It is patronage wearing a purchase's clothes. That reframing matters for design: the receipt, the credits, the thank-you and the visible split are not e-commerce hygiene, they *are* the product. |
| DT-04 | Refund apportionment on a failed bundle component is inherently arbitrary and must be a manual/support decision | `-cx.md` CX-01 Q5 framed it as unsolved | ❌ REJECTED — it is only arbitrary if you derive the fraction *after* the failure. Capture each component's **notional value at listing** (digital £X, physical £Y) and apportionment becomes deterministic arithmetic: refund the failed component's captured notional, scaled proportionally if the bundle discount means notionals exceed the price paid. This turns the "unsolved" partial-refund case into a specified rule (D-06) and removes a whole class of support tickets. The cost is one extra field per bundle component at listing — cheap for a deterministic, auditable refund. |
| DT-05 | The format choice (MP3/FLAC/WAV) should be fixed at purchase — the fan buys "a FLAC" | Mirrors how iTunes/most stores model a file sale | ❌ REJECTED — the fan buys **the record**, not a file. Binding the entitlement to a single format re-download is a streaming-era mistake: it means a fan who bought MP3 in 2026 and wants FLAC in 2029 has to re-buy. Format-agnostic entitlement (buy once, download any offered format forever) costs nothing extra to store and directly reinforces DT-03's "you own this" promise. It also collapses a would-be edge case ("fan wants a different format") into normal behaviour. |
| DT-06 | The multi-hyphenate buying their own record needs special handling to avoid paying themselves | Naive fraud/netting instinct | ❌ REJECTED — netting is the bug, not the fix. Buy-side and pay-side are independent ledgers (see boundary note). A collaborator who buys the record is a fan making a sale like any other; their payee share of *that* sale's revenue is theirs, exactly as it would be for any buyer. Trying to "net out" self-purchases would special-case the multi-hyphenate — the persona model explicitly warns against that (`meta/personas.md`: roles are lenses, not account types). The correct design has no special case at all. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The digital entitlement survives everything | Physical fails, artist delists, pressing cancelled — the download stands. You cannot unhear a record, and trying poisons the one promise that separates buying from streaming. Only a legal revocation order (domain 24) can override, and that is not a storefront decision. |
| D-02 | Show the line from fan to artist, including our cut | See DT-01. NYP's mechanism is visible short distance; obscuring the cut breaks the mechanism we depend on. Argues strongly for fee transparency (index Q-01). |
| D-03 | Pre-order bundles carry the same fund-segregation policy as crowdfunding | See DT-02. A pre-order is a promise with a supply chain; PledgeMusic died on exactly this and took artists' money with it. |
| D-04 | Warn on duplicate purchase, offer gift instead | Taking a superfan's money twice for one download is a small theft against your best customer. Checked against CX-M24 entitlement at add-to-cart. |
| D-05 | Credits appear on the product page | The commercial payoff of domain 02, and the moment a credit becomes useful rather than recorded. |
| D-06 | Partial refunds use component notional values captured at listing, scaled to the price paid | Resolves `-cx.md` CX-01 Q5 (see DT-04). Deterministic, auditable, refund never exceeds what was paid. One extra field per bundle component; eliminates a class of support tickets. |
| D-07 | ~~The NYP basis is captured and locked at listing and shown to every payee before they agree the split~~ — **SUPERSEDED by D-11 here and 20.04.04 D-10/D-11** (owner ratification of DQ-19.1/19.2, 2026-07-22). Row retained so the reversal is auditable | The half that survives is the obligation: a basis discovered on a statement is a dispute (24); a basis shown before agreement is a contract. The **mechanism is withdrawn**, and it was disqualified rather than merely outvoted: 20.04.04's own happy path agrees the split in 2023 and lists the record in 2024, so a listing-time capture is a year too late to have been shown before agreement, and no per-listing payee *consent* flow exists in any file (`-cx.md` CX-02 is notification, not consent). A platform-wide constant printed at split capture satisfies the obligation by construction. |
| D-08 | Download entitlement is format-agnostic and never expires | See DT-05. Buy the record, not a file; re-download any offered format forever. Reinforces the "you own this" promise at zero storage cost. |
| D-09 | Buy-side and pay-side are independent ledgers; a collaborator's own purchase gets no special-casing | See DT-06. Netting a self-purchase would violate the persona model (roles are lenses, not account types) and create a bug where the honest design has no special case. |
| D-10 | A listing cannot go live without: ~~resolved basis,~~ encoded formats, and (for multi-owner works) sale authority — **the basis gate is REMOVED by D-11**; the other two stand unchanged | Fail closed at listing time, where it is cheap, rather than at payout, where it is a dispute.
