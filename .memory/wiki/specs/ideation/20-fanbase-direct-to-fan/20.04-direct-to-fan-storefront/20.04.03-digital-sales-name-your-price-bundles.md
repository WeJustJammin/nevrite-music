# Feature: Digital Music Sales, Name-Your-Price & Bundles

> **Number**: 20.04.03
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Selling the music itself directly to fans: downloads, albums, name-your-price, and the bundle that made D2F
work — *buy the vinyl, download it now*. NYP is not a pricing gimmick; it is the mechanic that let Bandcamp
pay artists on the order of $1.5B, because fans reliably pay several times the asking price when they know
the money reaches the person who made it. It is also the mechanic that breaks the split's basis
(`-cx.md` CX-03), which is a problem nobody has had to solve before, because no storefront ever knew the split.

> **Merged** — the sweep listed "Digital Music Sales, Name-Your-Price & Bundles" (11) as one candidate and I
> have kept it as one. Bundling is inseparable from digital sales: the bundle exists *because* the download is
> instant and the record is not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sells a track, an album, a download. Sets fixed price or NYP with a minimum. Bundles digital with physical. Sees what fans actually paid over the ask — the number that tells them what their audience is worth, which no streaming dashboard will ever tell them. |
| Producer | None | No catalogue access. **But this is the product type where their split from the session pays out** — see [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md). |
| Operator | None | Venues do not sell recordings. Explicit absence — a venue's shop ([20.04.01](./20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md)) is merch-only. |
| Fan | Read-only | Buys, pays more than asked if they want to, downloads immediately, keeps it forever in [20.06.03](../20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md). Sees who played on it (domain 02) on the product page — which is where a credit stops being a database row and becomes a reason to care. |

## Behavior

**Name-your-price**: a minimum (possibly zero) and a suggested price; the fan chooses. The over-payment is
the point. Fans overpay when the line from their money to the artist is short and visible — which means the
platform's job is to make that line visible, not to optimise a checkout.

**Bundles** (`-cx.md` CX-01): one purchase, two lifecycles. Digital entitles instantly and irreversibly;
physical ships in twelve weeks and may slip. The digital entitlement **stands regardless** of the physical's
fate — you cannot unhear a record and should not try.

**Formats**: MP3/FLAC/WAV. Fans who buy downloads rather than stream are disproportionately the ones who care
about FLAC, and they are exactly the superfans this domain exists to serve — so format choice is not a
footnote.

**Delivery** is CX-M10 (Media Handling); **entitlement** is CX-M24; **the library** is
[20.06.03](../20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md).

### Happy Path

1. Musician lists the album: NYP, minimum £5, suggested £8
2. Binds automatically to the rights record from the session: artist 70%, producer 20%, drummer 10% (`-cx.md` CX-02)
3. Adds a vinyl pre-order bundled with the instant download; pressing of 300
4. Fan buys, pays **£20** — four times the minimum, because they love the record and know where the money goes
5. Download available immediately; vinyl ships in March
6. Payout: the drummer is paid their percentage of the £20 (or of the £5 — this is `-cx.md` CX-03 and **it is genuinely unresolved**). The producer is paid. Nobody chased anybody

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan pays £20 for a £5 record — what is the split's basis? | `-cx.md` CX-03. Nobody agreed a percentage of a tip. Must be deterministic and stated **on the split**, before the payee agrees to it — not discovered on a statement. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) Q-01. |
| NYP minimum zero; fan pays nothing | Legitimate and valuable — a free download is a fan record ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) and an email address, which is worth more than £0. But a 10% split of £0 is £0, and a collaborator may reasonably object to their work being given away. **Whose decision is that?** | `[PENDING]` — a real rights question, not a UI one. |
| Bundle: pressing cancelled, download already taken | Digital stands. Refunding the physical portion of a single price is arbitrary apportionment (`-cx.md` CX-01 Q5). | `[PENDING]` |
| Fan re-downloads years later | Must work. A download bought is a download owned — that is the promise separating this from streaming, and it is the entire emotional basis of the purchase. | Library, indefinitely. |
| Artist removes a track from sale after fans bought it | Existing entitlements survive removal. Always. | Stays in their library. |
| Uncleared sample in a sold track | The platform is in the distribution chain. Musician anti-persona: *"uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream."* | `[PENDING]` — 24 + domain 09/11. |
| Fan buys the same album twice (forgot) | Detect and warn. Taking a superfan's money twice for the same download is a small theft against the person who supports them most. | "You already own this — buy it as a gift?" |
| Track partly owned; artist lists unilaterally | Domain 09 usually forbids it. The Buy button is where that becomes concrete. | `[PENDING]` |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Product opened | Player + price skeleton |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | NYP works because fans are generous | The folk explanation | ❌ REJECTED — too vague to design on. NYP works because **the line from the fan's money to the artist is short and visible**. The fan knows £20 reaches the person who made the thing, unlike a stream worth £0.003. That is a mechanism, and it has a design consequence: the platform must **show the line** — show that the money goes to the artist and to the drummer, show the cut, show the split. A platform that obscures its cut destroys the exact mechanism NYP depends on. This is the strongest argument in the domain for radical transparency about our own fee (index Q-01). |
| DT-02 | The bundle is a convenience feature | It looks like buying two things at once | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **cashflow instrument**. The fan pays in November for a record pressed in March; the artist gets the money now and presses with it. That is the mechanism financing independent vinyl, and it is functionally identical to [crowdfunding](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.04-crowdfunding-preorder-campaigns.md) — which means it carries the **same PledgeMusic hazard**: fan money spent on something else, record never pressed. A pre-order bundle is a promise with a supply chain attached, and it should probably sit under the same fund-segregation policy as a crowdfunding campaign ([20.05.05](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)). **Nobody in the candidate list connected pre-orders to the crowdfunding hazard.** That connection is this file's most important finding. |
| DT-03 | Digital sales are dying — streaming won; this is nostalgia | The market-share read, and it is true in aggregate | ❌ REJECTED for **this** audience. Aggregate digital sales collapsed; D2F digital did not, because it is a different transaction. A fan buying a FLAC from Bandcamp on Bandcamp Friday is not buying access to music (they have Spotify) — they are **paying an artist they love and receiving a token of it**. It is patronage wearing a purchase's clothes. That reframing matters for design: the receipt, the credits, the thank-you and the visible split are not e-commerce hygiene, they are the product. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The digital entitlement survives everything | Physical fails, artist delists, pressing cancelled — the download stands. You cannot unhear a record, and trying poisons the one promise that separates buying from streaming. |
| D-02 | Show the line from fan to artist, including our cut | See DT-01. NYP's mechanism is visible short distance; obscuring the cut breaks the mechanism we depend on. Argues strongly for fee transparency. |
| D-03 | Pre-order bundles carry the same fund-segregation policy as crowdfunding | See DT-02. A pre-order is a promise with a supply chain; PledgeMusic died on exactly this and took artists' money with it. |
| D-04 | Warn on duplicate purchase | Taking a superfan's money twice for one download is a small theft against your best customer. |
| D-05 | Credits appear on the product page | The commercial payoff of domain 02, and the moment a credit becomes useful rather than recorded. |
