# Feature: D2F Revenue Split & Collaborator Payout

> **Number**: 20.04.04
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

When a fan buys a record directly from an artist, everyone who made it gets paid — automatically, per the
split captured at the session, without anyone chasing anyone. **This is the platform's thesis at its most
concrete.** Every other storefront pays 100% to the account holder and leaves the artist to remember, months
later, that they owe their drummer 10% — which mostly means the drummer is never paid, because the split was
verbal and everyone has moved on.

WeJammin can do this and no competitor can, for exactly the reason stated in `meta/problem-statement.md`:
they were not in the room. We were, so we hold both the split and the money, and the payout is arithmetic
rather than a negotiation.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list, which enumerated the storefront (merch, POD,
> digital, NYP, bundles) and never asked **who gets paid**. It is the single most thesis-aligned feature in
> domain 20 and it was invisible to a sweep tuned for coverage, because it lives at the join between two
> domains rather than inside either.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees, per product, who gets paid and how much — pulled from the rights record, not typed. Sees per sale where the money went. Authors merch splits explicitly (`-cx.md` CX-04). Cannot unilaterally alter a recording split here — that is domain 09, and a store that can quietly edit a split is a store that has broken the platform's only real promise. |
| Producer | **Reports (as payee)** | **The persona's payoff.** Sees D2F income from every record they worked on, per sale, with the split that produced it and the session it came from. They do not manage the shop, do not see the fan list, and do not need to — money simply arrives from a session they ran in 2023, because they captured the split on the day. `meta/personas.md` names them "the capture point"; this is the cell where capture pays. |
| Operator | Reports | Venue merch cut only (index Q-04) — the three-party arrangement at physical shows. |
| Fan | None | No visibility into splits, and should have none — it is commercially sensitive between collaborators. **But see DT-04**: the *existence* of a split may be exactly what a fan wants to know, and it may be worth money. |

## Behavior

Every product binds a payout plan at listing (`-cx.md` CX-02). For a recording, the plan is **referenced**
from domain 09's rights record — never copied, because a copy drifts and a drifted split is precisely the
reconstruction failure this platform exists to prevent. For merch, no rights record exists and the split is
authored here (`-cx.md` CX-04).

On sale: the platform's cut comes off, then the split applies, then CX-M01 routes money to each payee's payout
account. Each payee sees what they got, from what, under which split.

Three things make this hard rather than obvious:

1. **The NYP basis** (`-cx.md` CX-03) — a £5 record sold for £20. Is the drummer's 10% worth 50p or £2? Nobody agreed a percentage of a tip.
2. **The unpayable payee** — the drummer has no payout account, or is dead, or has vanished. The money exists and belongs to someone who cannot receive it.
3. **The missing rights record** — most tracks predate the platform. If no split exists, what is sellable?

### Happy Path

1. Producer runs a session in 2023; captures the split while everyone is in the room and friendly (domain 09 — the platform's founding act)
2. Musician releases the record in 2024 and lists it in their D2F shop ([20.04.01](./20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md))
3. System binds the payout plan from the rights record: artist 70%, producer 20%, session drummer 10%. **The musician confirms a fact; they do not fill in a form**
4. 2026: a fan buys it for £15
5. Platform cut off the top; split applied; CX-M01 routes to three payout accounts
6. The drummer — who has not spoken to this artist in two years and has no idea the record is selling — receives £1.35 and a statement saying which session it came from

**Step 6 is the product.** Not the shop. The shop is how you get to step 6.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **No rights record** (track predates the platform / imported) | The common case for years, and the sharpest tension in the sub-domain. Hard-block is honest and empties the launch catalogue. 100%-to-lister with a recorded acknowledgement is pragmatic and quietly makes the split optional — which erodes the thing that makes us different. See DT-02. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| **Payee has no payout account** | Money is owed to someone who cannot receive it. Must be **held, not absorbed by the platform and not silently given to the artist**. Held funds are a regulated liability (KYC/AML, escrow — `meta/constraints.md` flags both as PENDING) and there must be a claim path: the drummer signs up in 2027 and finds £340 waiting. That moment is the single best acquisition story this platform has. | Payee: "£340 is waiting for you from 4 records." Artist: "£340 held for a collaborator." |
| **NYP over-payment** | `-cx.md` CX-03. Basis must be deterministic and agreed at split time, not discovered on a statement. | `[PENDING]` — Q-01. |
| **Refund after payout** | Money already left to three people. Clawback across parties is the standard marketplace nightmare, and here the payees are strangers to the transaction. | `[PENDING]` — reserve vs clawback is a CX-M01 question with a real answer. |
| **Split changes after sales** | Prior sales pay the old plan; later sales the new. Retroactivity belongs to domain 09/10 and must not be improvised in a store. | Statement names the plan version. |
| **Split disputed while product is live** | `[PENDING]` — hold payouts (fair to the disputer, punishes the innocent) vs pay the recorded split and settle later (fast, may pay the wrong person). Interacts with 24. | `[PENDING]` |
| **Payee is a minor** | Session players are sometimes 16. Payouts to minors have KYC and safeguarding consequences (CX-M31). | `[PENDING]` |
| **Merch inherits a recording split by accident** | Pays the drummer for a t-shirt. `-cx.md` CX-04 — confidently wrong, which is worse than absent. | Must be impossible by construction, not by validation. |
| **Sub-penny amounts** | 2% of a £3 sale is 6p; payout rails have minimums and per-transaction costs. Accrue to a threshold — but an accrual that never reaches the threshold is a silent debt the platform holds forever. | Accrued balance, visible, with the threshold stated. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Payout panel opened | Skeleton |
| Empty | No sales | Artist: the plan, before any money moves. Payee: "nothing yet from this work" |
| Populated | ≥1 sale | Per-sale breakdown: gross, platform cut, each payee's share, status |
| Error | Payout rail failure | Money **held and visible**, never silently lost. Every party sees the same state — divergent views of who owes whom is how disputes start. |
| Partial | Some payees paid, one unpayable | Explicit held state, named amount, claim path |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is just CX-M01 (Payments, Escrow & Payouts) and should be dissolved into the cross-cut | Node Classification Gate — "is it a mechanism serving many domains?" | ❌ REJECTED — CX-M01 is the rails: it moves money to accounts and it serves twelve domains. It has no opinion about *who* should be paid. What lives here is the **application of a rights record to a D2F sale**, which exists nowhere else on the platform: the marketplace domains (13/14) pay a seller, licensing (11) pays under a licence, royalties (10) reconciles DSP statements. **Only here does a fan's direct purchase resolve against a split captured at a session.** CX-M04 (Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer) is the closest relative and is about transferring rights *in exchange for* money — the reverse direction. The rails are shared; the product is unique. |
| DT-02 | Sales should hard-block without a rights record — the thesis demands it | Consistency with D-18: provenance must have consequences | ⏳ **Genuinely unresolved, and it is the domain's sharpest product tension.** Hard-block is thesis-pure and empties the launch catalogue: every track recorded before WeJammin existed — i.e. every track — becomes unsellable, so nobody opens a shop, so the store never captures a fan, so the domain fails. 100%-to-lister-with-acknowledgement fills the catalogue and quietly makes splits optional, which erodes the only thing that makes us different from Bandcamp. **My reading**: allow the sale, but make the absence *loud and asymmetric* — "no split on file: you are taking 100%; is that right?" plus a one-tap path to add the collaborators, and a public product page that shows credits where they exist and their absence where they do not. Make the honest path the easy path rather than making the dishonest path impossible. That is the same lesson `meta/personas.md` draws about the Producer: **"the design must make the lazy path the correct path."** Owner call — Q-02. |
| DT-03 | The killer moment is the artist seeing their collaborators paid | Artist-centric framing; the artist is the buyer of this platform | ❌ REJECTED — the killer moment is **the drummer receiving money they had forgotten they were owed, from a session they barely remember, without having done anything.** That is the moment that produces the sentence "wait, how did you get paid for that?" — and that sentence is the platform's entire distribution strategy. It works because it reaches someone who is *not* our user, in a moment of pure benefit, with no ask attached. **The unpayable-payee claim path is therefore not an edge case — it is the acquisition funnel**, and it should be resourced as one. This inverts the priority of the "payee has no account" row above from an edge case to a headline feature. |
| DT-04 | Splits are commercially sensitive and must be hidden from fans | Obvious and correct as a default | ⏳ **Confirmed for amounts, questioned for existence.** The percentages are nobody's business. But DT-01 on [20.04.03](./20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md) establishes that NYP works because *the line from the fan to the artist is short and visible*, and a fan paying £20 for a record is buying the feeling that their money reaches the people who made it. "**Your £20 was shared with the 4 people who made this record**" — no amounts, no percentages — may be the strongest purchase argument in the entire product, and it is a fact only we can state. It is also a fact fans can verify against the credits (domain 02). Not confident: it could equally read as a platform boasting with someone else's money. Worth testing. Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Splits are referenced from domain 09, never copied into the store | A copied split drifts. A drifted split is the reconstruction failure the platform exists to prevent — committing it in our own storefront would be self-refuting. |
| D-02 | Unpayable amounts are held, never absorbed and never redirected to the artist | It is not our money and it is not theirs. It is also, per DT-03, the acquisition funnel. |
| D-03 | The claim path for held funds is a headline feature, not an edge case | See DT-03. "£340 is waiting for you" reaching a non-user in a moment of pure benefit is the best distribution this platform has. |
| D-04 | Merch splits never inherit from recordings | `-cx.md` CX-04. Confidently wrong is worse than absent. |
| D-05 | Payees see the split that produced their money and the session it came from | Money with no explanation is a support ticket. Money with a story is the thing they tell other musicians about. |
