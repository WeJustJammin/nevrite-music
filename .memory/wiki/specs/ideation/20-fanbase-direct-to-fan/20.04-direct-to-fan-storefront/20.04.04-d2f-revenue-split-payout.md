# Feature: D2F Revenue Split & Collaborator Payout

> **Number**: 20.04.04
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-22

## Overview

When a fan buys a record directly from an artist, everyone who made it gets paid — automatically, per the
split captured at the session, without anyone chasing anyone. **This is the platform's thesis at its most
concrete.** Every other storefront pays 100% to the account holder and leaves the artist to remember, months
later, that they owe their drummer 10% — which mostly means the drummer is never paid, because the split was
verbal and everyone has moved on.

WeJammin can do this and no competitor can, for exactly the reason stated in `meta/problem-statement.md`:
they were not in the room. We were, so we hold both the split and the money, and the payout is arithmetic
rather than a negotiation.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list, which enumerated the storefront (merch, POD,
> digital, NYP, bundles) and never asked **who gets paid**. It is the single most thesis-aligned feature in
> domain 20 and it was invisible to a sweep tuned for coverage, because it lives at the join between two
> domains rather than inside either.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees, per product, who gets paid and how much — pulled from the rights record (domain 09), not typed. Sees per sale where the money went: gross, platform cut, each payee's share, the 10% rolling reserve held, and any amount held because a payee is unpayable. Authors merch splits explicitly (`-cx.md` CX-04). Cannot unilaterally alter a recording split here — that is domain 09, and a store that can quietly edit a split is a store that has broken the platform's only real promise. When they are *also* a payee (artist-who-also-drummed), their multiple shares are attributed distinctly on the statement but land as one disbursement. |
| Producer | **Reports (as payee)** | **The persona's payoff.** Sees D2F income from every record they worked on, per sale, with the split that produced it, the platform basis rule it was applied to (and the terms version they agreed under — D-11), and the session it came from. They do not manage the shop, do not see the fan list, and cannot reach either — a store or fan-list request is refused as outside the payee role. Money simply arrives from a session they ran in 2023 because they captured the split on the day. `meta/personas.md` names them "the capture point"; this is the cell where capture pays. |
| Operator | Reports | Venue merch cut only (index Q-04) — the three-party arrangement at physical shows. Sees the venue's cut per merch sale; never sees recording splits, which venues have no claim on. |

That is a fact about existence, never amounts. |

## Behavior

Every product binds a payout plan at listing (`-cx.md` CX-02). For a recording, the plan is **referenced**
from domain 09's rights record — never copied, because a copy drifts and a drifted split is precisely the
reconstruction failure this platform exists to prevent. For merch, no rights record exists and the split is
authored here (`-cx.md` CX-04).

**On sale**, in strict order — and either every step below completes for a purchase or none of it does,
so a sale never lands half-applied: (1) the platform's cut comes off the top; (2) the split percentages
apply to the **basis** in force for that split agreement (see below); (3) each payee's share accrues **immediately** to a
per-payee, per-currency running balance, visible to that payee the moment the sale clears; (4) CX-M01
disburses accrued balances to external payout accounts on the disbursement schedule below. A retried or
duplicated payment confirmation for the same purchase never accrues twice — a given sale pays its payees
once and only once.

**Accrual vs disbursement are deliberately separated** (DT-05). Accrual is instant and always visible — the
magic of "you got paid" happens the moment the sale clears, on-screen, per DT-03. Disbursement to a real
payout rail batches, because per-transaction rail costs make paying out 6p on every micro-sale ruinous:

| Parameter | Default | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Accrual** | Immediate, per sale | The visible moment (DT-03); costs nothing, it is a ledger row |
| **Disbursement trigger** | Accrued balance ≥ **£20** (or currency equivalent), **or** a monthly sweep on the 1st for any balance ≥ the rail minimum — whichever comes first | Threshold caps rail-fee waste; the monthly sweep stops small balances being held forever (the "silent debt" hazard) |
| **Rail minimum** | **£1** | Below this, a payout costs more than it moves; accrues and shows as "pending payout" |
| **Rolling reserve** | **10%** of each disbursement held **90 days**, released if no refund/chargeback/dispute touches the originating sale | Covers post-payout refunds and chargebacks without clawing debt from strangers (see Refund and Chargeback rows) |

> Whether disbursement is per-sale (magical, expensive) or batched (sane, less magical) is a product/cost
> call for the owner — Q-04. The behavior above is the specified default; the magic is load-bearing (DT-03),
> so accrual is instant regardless of how disbursement is tuned.

**Basis for the split** is the amount **actually paid, net of platform cut and payment-processing fees**
("paid-net"), so under name-your-price the drummer shares in a fan's generosity rather than being cut out of
it — consistent with the fan-facing promise "your £20 was shared with the people who made this record"
(DT-04). Ratified: **paid-net**, and no longer a product question (Q-01 closed).

**The basis is a platform-wide constant, not a field** (D-10). No rights record, split agreement, product or
listing carries a basis property, and none may be added: **domain 09 owns the split percentages and the
payout-plan source and defines no basis field**, so nothing here authors one there. The rule is stated as a
**term of service** and printed on the **split-capture screen beside the percentages**, which is how the
locked obligation — *the payee must be told the basis before the split is agreed* — is satisfied **by
construction** rather than by a per-agreement disclosure step. It is also shown on the product page and on
every statement.

**The constant is versioned as a platform term** (D-11). It is fixed at launch; any later change is a new
**terms version** with an effective date, applies **only to splits agreed after that date**, and every split
agreed earlier keeps the terms version that was live when it was agreed. There is consequently **no
per-record lock trigger** — there is no per-record field to lock — and no payee is ever paid on a rule they
were not shown, including across a change. The payout engine therefore resolves **two version dimensions**
per sale: the payout **plan version** live at payment authorization (D-01, 20.04.01 D-02) and the **basis
terms version** bound to the split agreement's agreement date.

The accepted costs are stated in D-10 and D-11: a payee's effective take moves with the platform cut and the
processor fee structure, no per-record variation can be expressed, and the rule lives in platform terms
rather than in an artefact each payee individually signed.

**Chargeback and refund cover order**: reserve first → the payee's future accruals second → platform absorbs
the residual last. A collaborator who is a stranger to the transaction is **never** issued a debt demand;
the worst case for them is a reduced future statement, never a bill.

### Happy Path

1. Producer runs a session in 2023; captures the split while everyone is in the room and friendly (domain 09 — the platform's founding act). The split records artist 70%, producer 20%, session drummer 10%; the capture screen prints the platform basis rule (paid-net) beside those percentages, and the agreement binds the **basis terms version** live that day. Nobody fills in a basis field — there is none (D-10)
2. Musician releases the record in 2024 and lists it in their D2F shop ([20.04.01](./20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md))
3. System binds the payout plan by reference from the rights record. **The musician confirms a fact; they do not fill in a form**
4. 2026: a fan buys it for £15 under name-your-price (£5 minimum). Fees are £1; platform cut £1.40; basis (paid-net) = £12.60
5. Split applied at payment authorization against the plan version live at that instant, on the basis terms version bound to the 2023 agreement: artist £8.82, producer £2.52, drummer £1.26 — each accrues immediately, each with a 10% reserve held for 90 days
6. The drummer — who has not spoken to this artist in two years and has no idea the record is selling — sees £1.26 accrue instantly, and receives it once their balance crosses £20 or on the next monthly sweep, with a statement naming the session, the split, and the basis rule (with the terms version they agreed under)

**Step 6 is the product.** Not the shop. The shop is how you get to step 6.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **No rights record** (track predates the platform / imported) | Sale is **allowed**, but the absence is made loud and asymmetric (DT-02, my reading; owner may switch to hard-block — Q-02). At listing, a blocking interstitial fires before the product can go live; a one-tap "Add collaborators" deep-links to domain 09 capture; "Yes, I own 100%" writes a timestamped, attributed acknowledgement to CX-M19. The public product page shows credits where they exist and an explicit "credits not recorded" state where they do not — never a fabricated 100%. The basis needs no handling in this path: there is no split agreement to carry a field (D-10), no second payee to disclose a rule to, and the platform constant applies to the lister's 100% exactly as it does everywhere else. | Lister: *"No split on file — you will receive 100%. Is that correct?"* + [Add collaborators] / [Yes, I own 100%]. Fan: honest "credits not recorded" state. |
| **Payee has no payout account** | Money is owed to someone who cannot receive it. It is **held, never absorbed by the platform and never silently redirected to the artist**. Held funds are a regulated liability (KYC/AML, escrow — Q-05) with a claim path: the drummer signs up in 2027 and finds the balance waiting. That moment is the single best acquisition story this platform has (DT-03). | Payee (on signup): *"£340 is waiting for you from 4 records."* Artist: *"£340 held for a collaborator."* |
| **NYP over-payment — what basis?** | **Settled: paid-net, as a platform-wide constant** (D-10, ratified DQ-19.1/19.3). It is not a field on the product, the listing or the split — it is a term of service printed on the split-capture screen beside the percentages, so the payee saw it before agreeing (`-cx.md` CX-03). Deterministic, identical for every payee on every sale, never discovered on a statement. | Statement line: *"split applied to £12.60 (£15 paid − £1.40 platform − £1 fees) · your 10% = £1.26."* |
| **Platform basis terms change after a split was agreed** | The constant is versioned (D-11). A change is a new terms version with an effective date and binds **only splits agreed after it**; every earlier agreement keeps the version live when it was agreed, and its sales keep paying on that version however long the record sells for. No payee is ever moved onto a rule they were not shown, and there is no per-record lock to trigger because there is no per-record field. | Payee statement names the basis rule **and** its terms version. A payee on two records agreed either side of a change sees two rules, each attributed. |
| **Artist wants a different basis for one listing** (a charity release paying on the ask, a one-off arrangement) | **Not expressible, by design.** The basis is one platform constant; there is no per-listing, per-product or per-agreement basis field to vary (D-10). Supporting a per-record basis would require reopening DQ-19.1, not a configuration change. The lister's available lever is the price rule and the split percentages, not the basis. | Lister: no basis control anywhere in the listing flow; the platform rule is stated, not offered. |
| **Refund after payout** | Reversed against each payee's **rolling reserve pro-rata** first. If the reserve is released or insufficient, the shortfall is recovered from the payee's **future accruals**; if none exist, the **platform absorbs** it. No stranger is ever chased for a debt. | Payee: *"Refund −£1.26 reversed from reserve · reserve now £X."* |
| **Chargeback after reserve released** | Same cover order as refund (reserve → future accruals → platform absorbs). The 90-day reserve window is sized to the typical card chargeback window so most land inside it. | Payee: statement reversal line; balance updates; no debt demand. |
| **Split changes after sales** | Prior sales pay the plan version live at their payment authorization; later sales pay the new. Each sale is locked to the split version live at the moment it is authorized — a domain 09 edit mid-checkout does not re-bind an in-flight sale. Retroactivity belongs to domain 09/10 and is never improvised here. | Statement names the plan version applied to each sale. |
| **Split disputed while product is live** | Sales **continue** (fans keep buying), but **disbursement for that work is held**: shares accrue into a hold against the disputed split and nothing pays out to any payee on that work until domain 24 resolves it (D-06). Holding is the lesser evil — paying the recorded split during a live dispute risks disbursing hard-to-reclaim money to the wrong stranger. On resolution, held funds disburse per the resolved split (retroactivity per domain 09/10). | Every party sees the same *"held pending dispute #NNNN"* state with the reference. |
| **Payee is a minor** | Accrual is real and visible, but disbursement requires a verified guardian-linked custodial arrangement (CX-M31). Until it exists, funds accrue and hold — the platform never disburses to a minor's bare account (KYC + safeguarding). | Minor/guardian: *"£X held — a parent or guardian must complete verification to release."* |
| **Merch inherits a recording split by accident** | Impossible by construction, not by validation. Merch has no domain 09 rights record to reference; its split is authored in this feature (`-cx.md` CX-04) and defaults to 100% to the listing entity. There is no code path that reads a recording split for a merch product. | N/A — cannot occur. |
| **Sub-penny / sub-minimum amounts** | 2% of a £3 sale is 6p; rails have per-transaction minimums. Accrues to the ledger, disburses only once the balance crosses £20 or the monthly sweep clears it above the £1 rail minimum. The monthly sweep prevents an accrual that never reaches the threshold becoming a silent debt held forever. | Accrued balance, visible, with the threshold and next sweep date stated. |
| **Concurrent sales / payment-confirmation retry** | A retried or duplicated payment confirmation for the same purchase never accrues twice — the same sale pays its payees once and only once. Two simultaneous sales each lock to the split version live at their own authorization instant and accrue independently. | No duplicate lines; each sale one row. |
| **Stale plan version on a late confirmation** | A late confirmation carrying a superseded plan version does not re-bind the sale; the accrual applies the split version that was live when the sale was authorized. | Internal; payee sees the correct authorized version. |
| **Partial rights record** (percentages sum <100%, or a payee unresolved) | The unallocated remainder is **held unallocated**, never given to the lister. The gap is surfaced at listing so it can be fixed before selling. | Lister at listing: *"Split covers 90% — 10% will be held unallocated until you complete it."* |
| **Malicious split override in the store payload** | The store references domain 09 read-only for recordings (D-01). Any request to write/override a recording split from the store is rejected as not store-authored. Merch is the only store-authored split, and even there negative or >100% allocations are rejected as an invalid split allocation. | Rejected before the product goes live. |
| **Malicious held-funds claim** (someone claims a payee's held balance) | The claim path matches against the domain 02/09 payee **identity**, not a display-name string; an unmatched claim is denied as a payee-identity mismatch and logged to CX-M19. | Claimant: *"We couldn't verify you as a payee on this work."* |
| **Payout rail failure / ambiguous timeout** | Funds stay in an explicit "payout in progress" state; a retry after an ambiguous timeout never pays the same money twice. Money is **held and visible**, never silently lost. | Every party sees the same *"payout in progress"* state; nothing vanishes. |
| **Lister is also a payee** (multi-hyphenate: artist who also drummed) | The two shares are attributed **distinctly** on the statement (provenance is per-role) but land as **one** disbursement to the one human. The 100%-acknowledgement logic keys on the rights record, not the account, so self-overlap never suppresses the credit trail. | Statement: two attributed lines (*"as artist £8.82 · as drummer £1.26"*), one payout. |
| **Payee buys their own record** (artist test-purchase) | Allowed; the sale accrues normally, but is flagged a self-purchase and excluded from fan-count and audience metrics so it never inflates the fan graph ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)). | Flagged internally; excluded from audience stats. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Payout panel opened | Skeleton |
| Empty | No sales yet | Artist: the bound plan, before any money moves. Payee: *"nothing yet from this work"* |
| Populated | ≥1 sale | Per-sale breakdown: gross, platform cut, basis (the platform rule + the terms version bound to the agreement), each payee's share, 10% reserve held, disbursement status |
| Error | Payout rail failure / ambiguous timeout | Money **held and visible** in a "payout in progress" state, never silently lost; a retry never pays the same money twice. Every party sees the same state — divergent views of who owes whom is how disputes start |
| Partial | Some payees paid, one unpayable / minor / disputed | Explicit held state, named amount, and the claim or resolution path (signup, guardian verification, or dispute #) |
