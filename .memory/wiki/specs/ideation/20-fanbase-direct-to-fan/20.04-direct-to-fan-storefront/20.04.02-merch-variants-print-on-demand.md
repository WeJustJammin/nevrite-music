# Feature: Merch Products, Variants & Print-on-Demand

> **Number**: 20.04.02
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Physical merch: t-shirts in six sizes and three colours, posters, tote bags, the limited pressing of 300 —
either printed on demand with no capital risk, or held as real stock the artist paid for up front. Merch is
where independent artists make the money that streaming does not pay them, and it is the product type with
the most ways to lose money: dead stock in a spare room, a POD provider whose print quality embarrasses the
artist, and a supply chain nobody in the band understands.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Uploads a design, picks products and variants, chooses POD or own-stock, sets prices, sees margin per unit **after** POD cost and shipping (the number that decides whether the shirt is worth selling). Authors a merch split explicitly — never inherited from a recording (`-cx.md` CX-04). Sees stock, sell-through, and what is dying in a box. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. **Explicitly not a payee on merch** unless separately agreed — the drummer on the record has no claim on a t-shirt. Stated as an absence because the obvious implementation (reuse the split on file) gets this wrong and pays the wrong people. |
| Operator | Full | Venue merch: the room's t-shirt, posters. Selling an artist's merch on the artist's behalf — the standard merch-cut arrangement at physical shows — is index Q-04 and is a three-party split. |
| Fan | Read-only | Buys. Sees real availability, real variants, and an honest shipping cost and lead time **before** paying — a fan who buys a shirt and learns at checkout that shipping costs more than the shirt does not come back. |

## Behavior

The core split is **POD vs stock**, and it changes the meaning of everything downstream (`-cx.md` CX-05):

| | Print-on-demand | Own stock |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | none | paid up front |
| Margin | lower | higher |
| Availability | never sells out | defined by selling out |
| Lead time | days–weeks | immediate |
| Quality control | the provider's | the artist's |
| Risk | provider dependency | dead stock |

Scarcity is a **product** here, not a constraint: "3 of 100 left" drives D2F purchase behaviour hard, and
limited runs are a legitimate and central music mechanic. Which means overselling a limited run is not a
data-integrity bug — it is breaking the specific promise the fan bought.

Fulfilment, rates, labels and tracking are **CX-M14** (Shipping, Fulfilment & Logistics), which the domain
rationale created specifically so this feature would not reimplement them.

**Variant taxonomy.** A merch listing is a *product* (e.g. "Tour Tee 2026") that owns one or more *variant
axes*; a *variant* is one combination of axis values and is the unit that carries price, stock, SKU, and
POD provider mapping. Two axes cover the overwhelming majority of music merch — **size** (XS–XXXL, or a
free-size sentinel for posters/totes) and **colour** — with occasional third axes (format for a vinyl
variant colour, cut for fitted vs unisex). Rules at Level 1:

- Stock, price and availability live on the **variant**, never the product (DT the per-variant edge cases below). "Black / L sold out" while "Black / M" is live must be representable and shown.
- POD and own-stock are set **per variant, not per product** — an artist can run a POD long-tail of sizes while holding own-stock of the one size that sells at the show.
- Every variant maps to exactly one fulfilment source: a POD provider SKU **or** an own-stock quantity. A variant with no valid source is unpublishable, not a silent zero.

POD provider **integration depth** (one provider vs many — DT-03, Q-01) and whether the platform ever holds
stock in a fulfilment centre (Q-02) are business-terms and architecture calls, deferred to `/create-prd`
and `/ideate-validate` respectively — not fabricated here.

### Happy Path

1. Musician uploads artwork
2. Picks a POD t-shirt; system shows per-variant cost, suggested price, and the margin they actually keep after shipping
3. Sets £22; margin shows as £9.40 after POD and average shipping — the number that matters and that most artists never calculate
4. Authors the merch split: 100% to the band entity (default), or a share to the designer who drew it
5. Publishes; fans buy; CX-M14 routes each order to the POD provider; tracking flows back
6. Musician never touches a box

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| POD provider quality is poor | The artist's name is on a bad shirt. The platform chose the provider; the fan blames the artist. **A reputational risk the platform imposes on the artist and cannot fully control.** Fan opens a quality complaint on the order; it routes through CX-M14 (fulfilment) and, if contested, 24 (disputes) — reprint or refund at no cost to the artist, since the artist did not choose the provider. | Fan: "Report a problem with this order" → photo + reason → reprint/refund options. Artist: a quality-complaint rate surfaced on the provider so a persistently bad provider is legible before it becomes their reputation. |
| POD provider outage / discontinues a line | Orders taken cannot be fulfilled. Refund + notice; the artist looks unreliable through no fault of theirs. | Refund + notice + a substitute line if one exists. |
| Limited pressing races at the last unit | Must not oversell. Scarcity is the product being sold; breaking it breaks the specific promise. | "Sold out" — truthfully. |
| Shipping costs more than the item | Common for a £5 poster shipped internationally, and the single biggest D2F cart-abandon cause. | Honest cost early, never at the last step. |
| International order where POD has no local facility | Long lead, high shipping, possible customs charge on delivery — a fan billed on the doorstep for a shirt is furious at the artist. | Cost and lead time before payment, including duty where knowable. |
| Artist uploads artwork they do not own | Sample-clearance's merch cousin: an uncleared photo or a traced logo on a shirt. Platform prints it and is in the chain. On upload the artist affirms ownership/licence of the design; a valid rights-holder complaint takes the line down and halts new orders, routing to 24 and CX-M23/DMCA. In-flight orders already at the POD provider are the hard case — `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` for the exact cut-off and refund/reprint rule. | Artist at upload: an explicit "I own or have licence to this artwork" affirmation. On a takedown: line pulled, affected buyers notified and refunded. |
| Dead stock — 200 shirts in a spare room | The own-stock failure. Not the platform's problem to solve, but it is the platform's job to make the risk legible **before** they order 300. | Sell-through + stock ageing. |
| Variant mismatch (size sold out, colour not) | Per-variant stock, never per-product. | Per-variant availability. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Merch section opened | Product/variant skeleton |
| Empty | No merch | POD path offered — no capital, no risk, live today. The right first product for a broke artist. |
| Populated | ≥1 product | Variant grid, stock/scarcity, margin, sell-through |
| Error | POD provider unreachable | Products stay visible; **new orders must not be accepted for lines that cannot be fulfilled**. Silent acceptance is a refund and a broken promise later. |
| Partial | Some variants out of stock | Per-variant state, sold-out variants visible not hidden (fans want to know a size existed) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | POD is strictly better for independent artists — no capital, no risk | The standard pitch, and half true | ⏳ **Partially confirmed.** POD is right for a long tail and for testing designs. But it is *worse* precisely where merch matters most: **at the show**. A band on tour sells shirts from a table for cash, and POD cannot put a shirt in a room in Sheffield on a Tuesday. The highest-margin, highest-volume merch moment in independent music is physical, immediate and inventory-based — which means own-stock is not a legacy path but the tour path, and this feature must serve both. Connects directly to 18 (touring) and to index Q-04 (venue merch cut). |
| DT-02 | Merch inherits the recording's split — it is the split on file | The obvious implementation | ❌ REJECTED, emphatically. `-cx.md` CX-04: the drummer on the record has no claim on a t-shirt; the designer might. Merch has **no rights record** in domain 09 — nothing to inherit from. Inheriting silently is worse than having no split, because it is confidently wrong and pays the wrong people from a system whose entire credibility is being right about who gets paid. **Merch is the one product type where the platform's provenance advantage does not apply**, and pretending otherwise damages the thing that does work. |
| DT-03 | The platform should pick one POD provider and integrate deeply | Simpler; better margins through volume | ⏳ **Unresolved, leaning against.** Deep integration means the platform owns the quality problem: a bad print carries the artist's name and the artist's reputation, and they had no say. Multi-provider is more work and lets an artist choose someone they trust — which matters, because merch quality *is* the artist's brand in a way a shipping delay is not. I do not have the evidence to call this; it is partly a business-terms question. `/create-prd`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Merch splits are authored, never inherited from a recording | See DT-02. The obvious implementation pays the wrong people. |
| D-02 | Both POD and own-stock are first-class | See DT-01. POD is not a strict improvement — the tour needs stock, and the tour is where merch money is made. |
| D-03 | Margin after POD cost and shipping is shown at pricing time | Artists routinely price merch without knowing what they keep. The platform knows; showing it is nearly free and changes decisions. |
| D-04 | Shipping cost and lead time shown before payment, including duty where knowable | The doorstep customs charge is the worst D2F experience available, and the fan blames the artist. |
| D-05 | Per-variant stock; sold-out variants stay visible | Fans want to know the size existed. Hiding it reads as never having made it. |
| D-06 | **Tour/van inventory and cash-at-the-table sales are owned by 18 (Show Production & Touring), not this feature.** This feature owns the D2F storefront product/variant/POD model; 18 owns physical tour-inventory movement and merch-table settlement. The two share the same product+variant catalog so stock is one number across the online store and the merch table. | Resolves Q-03. Own-stock is first-class here (D-02, DT-01) so the *product* lives here; the *place it is sold on tour* is 18's workflow. Splitting ownership prevents this feature from reimplementing van logistics while keeping the shirt one entity. |
