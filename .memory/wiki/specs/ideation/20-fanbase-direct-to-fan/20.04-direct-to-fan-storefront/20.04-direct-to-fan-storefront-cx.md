## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Digital Sales ↔ Merch & POD (the bundle)

**Relationship**: A bundle is one purchase containing objects with incompatible lifecycles. The download is
entitled instantly and irreversibly; the vinyl ships in twelve weeks and may never ship at all if the pressing
plant slips. Refunding the bundle means clawing back a download the fan already has — which is impossible —
and the fan's expectation ("I bought a record") does not decompose into line items.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sells one thing. Should not have to think about the fact that half of it is instant and half is a supply chain.
- **Fan**: buys the record, listens tonight, receives the object in spring. This is the mechanic they love.
- **Operator**: applies to a venue's own merch bundles; rarer.
- **Producer**: not affected directly — but see CX-02/CX-04, because the payout differs per component.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The bundle is one order with per-component fulfilment state. The order is not "complete" until the physical ships, but the digital entitlement is granted at payment. Neither component owns the order.
2. **Trigger chain**: Payment → digital entitlement immediately → physical enters CX-M14 fulfilment. If the physical fails (plant slips, POD provider dies), the digital entitlement **stands** — you cannot unhear a record and should not try.
3. **Permission intersection**: The digital entitlement is checked by [20.06.03 Fan Library](../20.06-fan-experience-discovery/20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md) and is independent of the physical's fate.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Two streams from one purchase — "your download is ready" now, "your vinyl shipped" later. Fans must not read the second as a duplicate of the first.
5. **State transition conflict**: **Partial refund is the unsolved case.** `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. A cancelled pressing means refunding the physical portion while the digital stands — but the fan paid one price for one thing, and apportioning it after the fact is arbitrary. Interacts with [20.05.05](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md), where the same problem is worse.

### CX-02: Catalog ↔ Revenue Split & Payout

**Relationship**: The split is a property of the product. When an artist lists a track, the platform already
holds who played on it (domain 02) and who owns what percentage (domain 09) — captured at the session, which
is the entire point of the platform. The catalogue must bind to that record at listing time, so the payout is
determined before the first sale rather than argued after.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees, when listing, who else gets paid and how much. Ideally as a fact they merely confirm, not a form they fill.
- **Producer**: sees they are on the payout plan for a product they may not have known was being sold. **This is the moment the provenance promise becomes visible to the persona that made it possible.**
- **Fan**: no visibility.
- **Operator**: only for venue-sold merch (index Q-04).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The rights record (domain 09) is authoritative; the catalogue **references** it and never copies it. A copy would drift, and a drifted split is exactly the reconstruction failure the platform exists to prevent.
2. **Trigger chain**: List → resolve rights → bind payout plan → sellable. **No rights record ⇒ the product is not sellable** — or sellable with 100% to the lister and an explicit, recorded acknowledgement. See [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) DT-02; this is the sharpest product tension in the sub-domain.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is significant — **can an artist sell a track they only partly own?** Domain 09 holds the answer, and it is usually "not unilaterally". The store is where that abstraction meets a Buy button.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Collaborators should be told when a work they are on goes on sale. They should not learn from the fan.
5. **State transition conflict**: The split changes (a dispute resolves) while the product is live. Sales before the change pay the old plan; after, the new. Retroactivity is a domain 09/10 question and must not be invented here.

### CX-03: Digital Sales ↔ Revenue Split (name-your-price)

**Relationship**: NYP is the mechanic that made Bandcamp work — fans routinely pay several times the asking
price. It also breaks the split's basis. A £5 record bought for £17: is the drummer's 5% worth 25p or 85p?
The 25p reading says the £12 over-payment was a tip to the person the fan loves; the 85p reading says revenue
is revenue and the split governs it. **Nobody agreed a percentage of a tip**, because when the split was
signed at the session no one imagined the price would be decided by the buyer.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: benefits from the tip reading. Also the person who has to explain it to the drummer.
- **Producer**: the payee. Whichever way this resolves, they must be told *before* they agree to the split, not after the first payout.
- **Fan**: overpaying *for the artist*, in their mind. They are not thinking about the drummer, and would probably be surprised either way.
- **Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The split percentage is fixed; the basis is not. The basis is a **product decision the split sheet never contemplated**.
2. **Trigger chain**: NYP payment → basis determined → split applied → payout. Must be deterministic and stated on the product, or the first surprise payout becomes a dispute (24).
3. **Permission intersection**: Arguably the collaborator must consent to the basis rule, which makes it part of the split agreement rather than a store setting. That is the cleanest reading and the most work.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Payees should see the basis in their statement, not just an amount.
5. **State transition conflict**: None technically. The conflict is normative, and it is genuinely unresolved — see [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) Q-01.

### CX-04: Merch & POD ↔ Revenue Split

**Relationship**: The recording split does not govern a t-shirt. The drummer on the record has no claim on
merch; the artist who drew the design might have one; the band's own internal agreement might be different
again. Applying the recording split to merch — the obvious implementation, since it is the split on file —
pays the wrong people and is worse than having no split at all, because it is confidently wrong.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets a merch split explicitly, or takes 100%. Must not inherit one silently.
- **Producer**: **explicitly not a payee on merch** unless separately agreed. Their `Reports` access in the Role Matrix covers recordings, not shirts.
- **Fan**: no visibility.
- **Operator**: the venue merch cut (index Q-04) is a third split model again.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Merch has **no rights record** in domain 09 — there is nothing to reference. The split must be authored here, which makes merch the one product type where the platform's provenance advantage does not apply.
2. **Trigger chain**: List merch → author split (default 100% to the entity) → sellable. Never inherit from a recording.
3. **Permission intersection**: A band entity's merch revenue is the band's; member shares are a domain 01/09 matter, not a store matter.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Payees told on addition.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-05: Catalog ↔ Merch & POD

**Relationship**: Variants and stock model are catalogue structure. POD changes the meaning of availability —
a POD shirt is never out of stock and never sells out; a limited pressing is defined by selling out, and
scarcity is the product. The catalogue must express both without pretending they are the same.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: picks POD (no risk, lower margin, slower) or stock (capital up front, better margin, sells out).
- **Fan**: sees "3 left of 100" or nothing at all, depending — and scarcity signals drive D2F purchase behaviour hard.
- **Operator**: same for venue merch.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Deferred — Medium confidence, well-trodden commerce modelling. The
music-specific part (scarcity as a product, limited pressings) is covered in
[20.04.02](./20.04.02-merch-variants-print-on-demand.md).
