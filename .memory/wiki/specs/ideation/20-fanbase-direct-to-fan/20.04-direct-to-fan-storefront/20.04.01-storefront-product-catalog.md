# Feature: Storefront & Product Catalog

> **Number**: 20.04.01
> **Parent**: [Direct-to-Fan Storefront](./20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The artist's shop and the products in it: what is for sale, at what price, in what quantity, to whom. It is
the commercial spine of the sub-domain — merch, records, downloads, bundles and members-only items all live in
one catalogue — and it is the point at which every product must bind to a payout plan
(`-cx.md` CX-02) before it can be sold.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates products, sets prices and stock, orders the shop, marks items members-only or presale-gated ([20.02.03](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.03-perk-entitlements-presale-eligibility.md)). At listing time sees who else gets paid, pulled from the rights record rather than typed. Sees sales, and sees who bought — because a buyer is a fan record ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)), not an anonymous order. |
| Producer | None | No visibility on the catalogue. Their stake appears only at [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) as a payee. |
| Operator | Full (venue merch) | A room's own shop: tote bags, posters, the venue t-shirt. Narrower than an artist's — no recordings (see the Role Matrix on the parent index). Selling an artist's merch on their behalf is index Q-04, unresolved. |
| Fan | Read-only | Browses and buys. Sees members-only items they cannot access as **visible but locked** — a locked door they can open by joining is a conversion surface; a hidden one is nothing. Sees scarcity honestly ("3 of 100 left") because in D2F scarcity is part of the product. |

## Behavior

The catalogue holds heterogeneous products under one roof: a POD t-shirt that never runs out, a pressing of
300 that is defined by running out, a download that is infinite, a bundle that is half instant and half a
supply chain (`-cx.md` CX-01). It must express all of them without flattening them into a `product` row with
forty nullable columns — the same failure D-14 rejected at domain scale.

Every product binds to a payout plan at listing (`-cx.md` CX-02). For a recording, that plan comes from the
rights record (domain 09) — already captured, already agreed, at the session. For merch, there is no rights
record and the split must be authored (`-cx.md` CX-04). **A product whose payout cannot be resolved is not
sellable**, or is sellable only with an explicit recorded acknowledgement — see
[20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) DT-02.

The public rendering — the page, its SEO, its embeds — is **CX-M23**, not this (index D-03). Cart, checkout,
payments and tax are **CX-M01/M02** (index D-04). What lives here is the catalogue and the commercial rules.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — product type taxonomy, pricing model detail, shop layout.

### Happy Path

1. Musician finishes a record; the rights record already exists from the session (domain 09)
2. Opens the shop, creates a product: *Digital album — name-your-price, minimum £5*
3. System shows the payout plan pulled from the split: artist 70%, producer 20%, session drummer 10% — **as a fact to confirm, not a form to complete**
4. Musician confirms; adds a vinyl pre-order bundled with an instant download (`-cx.md` CX-01); sets a pressing of 300
5. Publishes. CX-M23 renders it publicly. [20.03.01](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.01-campaign-composer.md) can announce it to a segment
6. First sale: the drummer's 10% is routed to the drummer without anyone doing anything. **The split they signed at the session paid them, silently, three years later.** That is the product

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Product has no rights record (track predates the platform, or was imported) | The common case for years. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — hard-block is honest and blocks most of the catalogue at launch; 100%-to-lister with a recorded acknowledgement is pragmatic and quietly makes the split optional. See [20.04.04](./20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) DT-02. | `[PENDING]` |
| Artist partly owns a track and lists it unilaterally | Domain 09 usually says they may not. The store is where that abstraction meets a Buy button, and it will be the first place anyone notices. | `[PENDING]` |
| Limited pressing oversells (race at the last unit) | Must not. Overselling a limited run is a promise broken to the fans who queued for exactly the scarcity being sold. | "Sold out" — and it must be true. |
| POD provider drops a product line mid-sale | Orders taken cannot be fulfilled. [20.04.02](./20.04.02-merch-variants-print-on-demand.md). | Refund + notice. |
| Members-only item viewed by a non-member | Visible, locked, joinable. | "Members only — join to unlock." |
| Price changed while items are in carts | Cart price holds for a bounded window. Changing the price under a fan mid-purchase is a trust loss out of all proportion to the money. | Held price + expiry. |
| Split changes (dispute resolves) while a product is live | Sales before pay the old plan, after pay the new. Retroactivity is a domain 09/10 question and must not be improvised here. | Payout statement shows which plan applied. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Shop opened | Product grid skeleton |
| Empty | No products | For the artist: what they could sell, drawn from what the platform already knows they have (a released record, a design, a tour). For the fan: not an empty shop — no shop at all. |
| Populated | ≥1 published product | Grid with price, stock/scarcity, members-only badges |
| Error | Catalogue unavailable | Explicit. A shop that silently shows nothing looks like an artist with nothing to sell. |
| Partial | Draft products exist | Artist sees drafts; fans see nothing |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The catalogue should reuse domain 13's listing model | Both sell things; obvious reuse instinct | ❌ REJECTED — settled at domain level by D-14 and it holds at feature level. A gear listing is qty=1, non-fungible, condition-graded, in a make/model catalogue. A merch product is qty=300, fungible, variant-dimensioned, with no catalogue at all. The union is a listing entity with forty nullable columns. The genuinely shared parts — cart, payments, shipping, tax — are **already cross-cuts** (CX-M01/M02/M14), which is precisely why the merge buys nothing. |
| DT-02 | The storefront's differentiator is a better shop | The obvious framing; Bandcamp/Shopify/Big Cartel all compete here | ❌ REJECTED — we will never out-shop Shopify and should not try. The differentiator is **the split binding** (`-cx.md` CX-02): this is the only storefront on earth that knows who played on the record it is selling, because it was in the room when they played. Every competing store pays 100% to the account holder and leaves the artist to Venmo their drummer, which mostly means the drummer is never paid. Framing this as "a better shop" loses; framing it as "the shop that pays your collaborators automatically" is the thesis (D-18) at its most concrete. |
| DT-03 | Fans buying merch are anonymous orders | Standard e-commerce assumption | ❌ REJECTED — **a buyer is a fan record.** That is the join no other store can make: the shop knows this person also came to three shows and has been on the list since 2019. It means the store is simultaneously the domain's revenue engine **and** its highest-quality fan-acquisition surface (the domain rationale says exactly this: "the store captures the fan"). It also means a guest checkout is a data-quality decision, not just a conversion one — see [20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Products bind a payout plan at listing time | `-cx.md` CX-02. Determined before the first sale, not argued after — the platform's own principle applied to its shop. |
| D-02 | The split is referenced from domain 09, never copied | A copied split drifts, and a drifted split is the exact reconstruction failure this platform exists to prevent. |
| D-03 | A buyer is a fan record, not an order | See DT-03. The store is the domain's best fan-acquisition surface, and only because it is not anonymous. |
| D-04 | Heterogeneous product types, not a flattened `product` row | See DT-01. Same reasoning as D-14, one level down. |
| D-05 | Locked members-only items are visible | A locked door converts; a hidden one does nothing. |
