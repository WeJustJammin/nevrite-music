# Feature: Listening History Import & Follow Bootstrap

> **Number**: 20.06.04
> **Parent**: [Fan Experience & Discovery](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Turning "who do you listen to?" from a question a fan answers by typing forty band names into a question they
answer in one tap — by connecting Spotify/Apple Music, or importing a Bandcamp collection or a Bandsintown
follow list. Without it a new fan follows nobody, so the alerts have nothing to fire on, so the product is
empty at exactly the moment it must prove itself. **This is the fan-side cold-start fix, and it is the mirror
of [20.01.04](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md)** — that one is the
artist's adoption gate, this one is the fan's.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list, despite being **step 1 of the Fan persona's
> stated workflow**: *"Follows an artist (or imports who they already listen to)"*. The sweep enumerated the
> artist's list import and missed the fan's, which is a good illustration of why a coverage sweep needs a
> persona re-read.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Fan | Full | Connects a streaming service or uploads an export; sees a proposed follow list drawn from what they actually listen to; **confirms or prunes** — nothing is followed silently. Understands what they handed over. |
| Musician | None | No visibility into the import. They simply gain followers they did not earn on this platform — the acquisition flywheel. Whether they see "47 followers from imports today" is Q-03. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. |
| Operator | None | No visibility. Venue follows are unlikely to be importable from anywhere. |

## Behavior

Connect or upload → resolve artist names to platform entities (CX-M17) → **propose** follows → fan confirms →
follows created ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md)).

Two hard properties:

1. **Never auto-follow** (DT-02). Import is an *inference*; a follow is an *instruction*. A silently-created follow is a subscription the fan never asked for, and it poisons the alert that follows it.
2. **Most of a fan's artists will not be here** (DT-03). A fan listens to 200 artists; 6 are on WeJammin. The import's honest output is mostly a list of people we do not have — and how that is presented decides whether the fan sees a small product or a broken one.

Re-import must not resurrect follows the fan removed — the same suppression logic as
[20.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-cx.md) CX-06, one level down. An
unfollow is an instruction, not a preference.

### Happy Path

1. Fan signs up after buying a ticket; follows one band; the product is otherwise empty
2. Offered: *connect Spotify and we'll find the artists you already listen to* — offered **here**, at the empty state, not buried in settings
3. Fan connects; system reads top artists; resolves them (CX-M17)
4. Proposes: *14 of the artists you listen to are on WeJammin* — pre-ticked, prunable
5. Fan confirms 12. Twelve follows created; the alert product now has something to fire on
6. **And the honest part**: *186 of your artists aren't here yet.* Handled as information, not as failure

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Most artists are not on the platform** | The normal case (DT-03), not an edge. 14 of 200. | See DT-03 — the presentation is the whole design problem. |
| Name resolution ambiguity ("Nirvana", "The Sound", any band called Prophecy) | CX-M17. A wrong match creates a follow to a stranger and an alert about a gig they do not care about. | Conservative matching; propose, never assume. |
| Fan connects Spotify, then disconnects | Follows already created **stay** — they were confirmed. The connection was a source, not a lease. | Follows persist. |
| Fan's taste is embarrassing | Listening history is inference-rich and revealing. Handing it over is a real disclosure, and a fan who feels surveilled at signup does not come back. | Explicit about what is read and what is kept. |
| Re-import resurrects removed follows | Must not. Unfollow is an instruction. | "3 skipped — you unfollowed these." |
| Streaming API access changes/withdrawn | Spotify has repeatedly narrowed third-party API access, and a fan-data integration is a dependency on a competitor's goodwill. | `[PENDING]` — export-upload fallback is the resilient path. |
| Import proposes 200 follows; fan confirms all | Alert volume becomes noise ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md) Q-03), and the product they just set up starts failing immediately. Confirming all is a legitimate choice, so the follows are created — but the alert side must default the fan into a digestible cadence, not one-alert-per-artist firehose from minute one. | Follows created; alert preferences seeded conservatively (digest/near-me default). Exact cap/cadence `[PENDING — /write-be-spec, coordinated with 20.06.01 Q-03]`. |
| **Fan imports; artists gain followers who never chose them** | Wait — no: they did choose, at confirm. This is exactly why DT-02 matters, and why auto-follow would have made it a real problem. | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Service connected | "Reading your artists…" |
| Empty | Nothing imported yet | **The fan's first real screen.** The offer, stated plainly. Not a settings toggle. |
| Populated | Resolution complete | Proposed follows, pre-ticked, prunable + the honest not-here count |
| Error | Service unavailable / auth failed | Manual search fallback. Never a dead end at the fan's first meaningful action. |
| Partial | Some resolved, some ambiguous | Confident matches proposed; ambiguous ones surfaced for the fan to pick, never guessed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a convenience feature — a nice onboarding shortcut | Why the sweep omitted it | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the fan-side adoption gate**, exactly as [20.01.04](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md) is the artist's. A fan who follows nobody gets no alerts, so nothing ever happens, so they never return, so the largest population on the platform (D-13) churns at the empty state. `meta/personas.md` puts it in the Fan's workflow at **step 1** and the sweep still missed it. The symmetry is worth noting: **both sides of this domain have an import as their adoption gate**, and both were under-weighted — the artist's as an unremarkable utility, the fan's as nothing at all. |
| DT-02 | Import should auto-follow — that is the point of a bootstrap | Frictionless onboarding; every growth playbook | ❌ REJECTED — an import is an **inference**, a follow is an **instruction**, and silently converting one into the other creates subscriptions the fan never asked for. The consequences compound: the first alert from an artist they never chose to follow reads as spam, they mute everything, and the alert product — the whole fan-side value proposition — is dead before it fired once. It is also the exact mistake [20.01.04](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md) refuses on the artist side (import proposes, never manufactures consent). **Same principle, both directions: the platform does not decide what you agreed to.** |
| DT-03 | The import's value is the artists it finds | Obvious — that is what it is for | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the finding that matters. **The import's dominant output is the artists we do NOT have.** A fan listens to 200 artists; at launch perhaps 6 are here. So this feature is, unavoidably, the moment a fan learns how small the platform is — and how that is framed decides everything. Framed as failure ("only 6 matches"), the fan leaves. Framed as **information** — *"186 of your artists aren't here yet. Want to be told if they arrive?"* — the same fact becomes a standing intent, a demand signal ([20.07](../20.07-fan-demand-show-requests.md)), and an artist-acquisition list: **"340 of your listeners are already on WeJammin waiting for you"** is the single strongest cold-outreach message this platform could send an artist. The cold-start problem, honestly presented, converts into the acquisition channel. Nobody in the candidate list saw this because nobody listed the feature. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Import proposes; the fan confirms. Never auto-follow | See DT-02. Inference is not instruction — the same principle as the artist-side import, in the other direction. |
| D-02 | The "not here yet" list is information, not failure | See DT-03. The honest framing turns the cold-start problem into a demand signal and an acquisition channel. |
| D-03 | Offered at the empty state, not buried in settings | The fan's first screen is where this matters; a settings toggle nobody opens is the same as not building it. |
| D-04 | Unfollow survives re-import | An unfollow is an instruction. Same suppression logic as the artist-side list ([20.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-cx.md) CX-06). |
| D-05 | Export-upload works without an API connection | Streaming APIs are a competitor's goodwill; a file is not. |

This is a consent-scope and data-retention model question — what is read, how long it is held, what the fan is told and can revoke — sitting on CX-M18 (Privacy, Consent & Data Portability), which this file already consumes. | User | `/create-prd-security` |
