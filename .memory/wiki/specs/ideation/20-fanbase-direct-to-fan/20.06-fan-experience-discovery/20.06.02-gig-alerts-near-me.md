# Feature: Gig Alerts & Near-Me Show Notifications

> **Number**: 20.06.02
> **Parent**: [Fan Experience & Discovery](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Telling a fan that an artist they follow is playing near them, **before the show rather than after it**. This
is the Fan persona's entire stated problem — *"Finds out an artist they love played their city — after the
show"* — and their entire stated success criterion — *"Never misses a show by an artist they follow"*,
measured as alert→attendance conversion. It is the Bandsintown/Songkick job, and the reason it can be done
better here is that the show is announced *on the same platform the fan follows the artist on*: no scraping,
no lag, no wrong dates.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Fan | Full | Sets where they are and how far they will travel; gets told when a followed artist books within that radius; sets frequency and channel ([20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md)). Acts: buys a ticket (19). |
| Musician | Reports | Sees alerts sent and **alert→attendance conversion** — the one number that proves this domain's fan-side value, and the one no other platform can compute because no other platform holds both the alert and the door scan. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. |
| Operator | Reports | Followers of the room told what is on. A venue's own alert product, structurally identical. |

## Behavior

Announcement (17/19) → resolve followers ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md)) → filter by
geography (CX-M13) and preference → alert (CX-M07). Simple to describe; three hard problems inside it:

1. **The fan's location.** Most fans never state one. No location ⇒ no geo filter ⇒ either no alert (the product silently fails for them) or every alert (noise, then mute, then the product fails louder).
2. **The channel.** This is a phone-shaped, time-sensitive product and `meta/constraints.md` declares web as the only surface. See DT-01 — this is the domain's single strongest piece of evidence for the mobile question.
3. **Comprehensiveness.** Bandsintown wins by knowing about *every* show; we only know about shows booked here. A fan whose favourite band books elsewhere gets nothing, and blames us. See DT-03.

The alert must **carry its justification** ("you follow this band") — a follow never expires and the alert may
fire three years later, at which point an unexplained notification is a spam report.

**Resolved defaults (Step 5, Level 1):**

- **Radius.** Default **25 miles / 40 km** from the fan's stated location — metro-scale, the distance a fan
  will cross for a band they follow without it feeling like a road trip. Fan can widen (up to a stated max,
  `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`) or narrow. Too wide manufactures noise the fan learns to mute; too narrow
  misses the show the product exists to catch. The number is a starting default, not a claim of optimality
  (D-06).
- **Timing.** One alert, fired **at on-sale** (D-01 / DT-02), not at announcement. Superfans with a presale
  entitlement ([20.02.03](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.03-perk-entitlements-presale-eligibility.md))
  are alerted at **presale open**, earlier — the timing *is* the perk (DT-02).
- **Digest vs immediate.** A **single-gig** alert is **immediate** — it is one time-critical event a fan can
  act on now. A **multi-artist single event** (a festival, a bill where the fan follows several acts) collapses
  to **one aggregate alert** keyed on the event, never one-per-artist (D-02, D-07). There is no daily/weekly
  digest of unrelated gigs — that would be a recommendation feed, which is index Q-03 and a different product.

### Happy Path

1. Fan follows a band; sets "Manchester, will travel 50 miles"
2. Eighteen months pass. Fan forgets
3. Band books a show 30 miles away; announced in 17; on-sale set in 19
4. Alert fires at on-sale — **not at announcement** (an alert about tickets you cannot buy yet is a tease, and a second alert later is noise)
5. Fan taps through, buys, attends; the scan lands on their fan record and proves the whole loop worked
6. Artist sees: 340 alerted, 47 attended. **The only platform on earth that can measure that sentence**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Fan has no location** | The common case, and the product silently fails for them. Multi-moment capture (D-08): ask at follow time (the only moment they care), infer from a ticket purchase, or ask on the first alert-worthy event — never let them find out by missing a show. | A one-tap prompt — "use my location" or type a city + radius — at follow, and again the first time a followed artist books *anywhere* (so the fan sees "we have a show for you, tell us where you are" rather than silence). |
| **Fan's artist books a show elsewhere** (not on WeJammin) | We know nothing; the fan hears nothing; the fan blames **us**, correctly. See DT-03. **The comprehensiveness problem is the product's existential issue.** | Nothing — which is the failure. |
| Fan moved cities and never updated | Alerts for the wrong city. Silent and irritating. A ticket purchase or scan in a new metro is a strong signal the stored location is stale. | A low-friction "still in Manchester?" confirm — triggered by a purchase/scan in a different metro, or a long idle period — that updates the radius in one tap without opening settings. |
| Show announced, then cancelled after alerts fired | The correction must reach the **exact** set who got the original, harder than the original did. | Cancellation, prominently. |
| Fan follows a band and its solo project; both announce the same gig | Two alerts, one night. Dedupe must key on the **event** ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md) DT-03), not on the artist entity. | One alert. |
| Festival with 40 artists a fan follows | 40 alerts, or one? One, obviously — but the "one" has to be assembled, and it is a different object from a gig alert (D-07): an aggregate keyed on the event, listing the followed artists on the bill. | One alert: "12 artists you follow are playing Green Man" — the count is the hook, the list is the justification (D-04). |
| Alert arrives after tickets sold out | Worse than no alert. It tells the fan they missed something they were told about too late — the failure they came here to avoid, delivered by the product that promised to fix it. | Timing is not a detail; it is the feature. |
| Fan in a rural area, 50-mile radius, nothing ever | The product is empty for them forever, through no fault of theirs — a supply-density problem (DT-03), not a fan behaviour one. | The honest empty state (see States → Empty): "nothing near you yet" + the levers — widen the radius, follow more artists — never a fake feed to hide the emptiness. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Alerts page opened | Upcoming skeleton |
| Empty | No upcoming shows from followed artists | Honest: "nothing near you yet" + the levers (follow more, widen the radius). Not a recommendation feed — that is index Q-03 and a different product. |
| Populated | ≥1 upcoming | Shows near them, dated, with on-sale state |
| Error | Alert dispatch fails | **The fan misses the show.** Not a retryable background job — it is time-critical, unrepeatable, and the single job the product exists to do. Needs an alerting SLO, not a queue. |
| Partial | Following, no location | Following works, geo alerts silently do not. **Must be surfaced loudly** — this is the most dangerous partial state in domain 20 because it looks like success. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Gig alerts work fine on a responsive web surface | Implied by `meta/constraints.md` — web is the only declared surface | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the strongest evidence in domain 20 for the mobile-surface question.** This is a time-critical push product for a persona who lives on a phone. Email is where a tour announcement dies among 200 other emails; SMS costs money per fan and carries TCPA exposure ([20.03.02](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.02-channel-routing-bulk-delivery.md) DT-03); iOS web push effectively requires the fan to add the site to their home screen, which approximately nobody does. So the channel this feature needs is the one the declared surface cannot reliably provide. **D-13 already predicted this** ("fan gig alerts and show discovery are push-notification and phone-shaped workflows"); this file is the concrete case. Escalated to `/ideate-validate` as index Q-01. |
| DT-02 | The alert should fire when the show is announced | Obvious — tell them as soon as we know | ❌ REJECTED — **fire at on-sale.** An alert about a show whose tickets are not yet purchasable is a tease that converts nothing and trains the fan to ignore alerts; then a second alert at on-sale is noise from a product already being ignored. One alert, at the moment the fan can act, is the whole discipline. It also aligns with [20.02.03](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.03-perk-entitlements-presale-eligibility.md): superfans get a *presale* alert earlier, which makes early notification a **perk** rather than a default — turning what looks like a timing detail into the mechanism that makes the superfan tier worth something. |
| DT-03 | We can beat Bandsintown because our data is first-party | The natural competitive claim: no scraping, no lag, correct dates | ⏳ **Half true, and the false half is existential.** Our data is better *for shows booked here*, and we know nothing about the rest. Bandsintown's product is not accuracy — it is **comprehensiveness**: a fan following 40 artists expects to hear about all 40, and a service that covers 3 of them is worse than useless, because the fan cannot tell "no show" from "we don't know". A fan who misses a gig *while using our alert product* is worse off than one who never installed it. **This is a chicken-and-egg that quality cannot solve**: the alert product only works when a critical mass of shows are booked on the platform, which means it depends entirely on domains 16/17/19 winning the supply side first. That is a **sequencing fact with MoSCoW consequences**, and it is the strongest argument in this domain for a supply-side-first beachhead (`meta/problem-statement.md` Q-03). |
| DT-04 | Alert→attendance conversion is a nice metric | It appears in the Fan persona's success criteria | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is more than nice — **it is the only quantitative proof of this domain's fan-side value, and only we can compute it.** It requires the alert and the door scan to be in the same system: Bandsintown sends the alert and never learns if you went; the box office sees the scan and never knew about the alert. We hold both (domain 19). This means the metric is not just reporting — it is the argument the platform makes to an artist about why the fan graph is worth having, and it should be a first-class artifact rather than a dashboard line. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Fire at on-sale, not at announcement | See DT-02. One alert, at the moment the fan can act. Makes early notice a superfan perk rather than a default. |
| D-02 | Dedupe on the event, not the artist | One human's several entities announcing one gig produces one alert ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md) DT-03). |
| D-03 | The no-location partial state must be surfaced loudly | It looks like success and silently withholds the product's only real feature. The most dangerous partial state in the domain. |
| D-04 | Alerts carry their justification | A follow never expires; an unexplained alert in 2029 is a spam report. |
| D-05 | Alert dispatch has an SLO, not a queue | A missed alert is not a retryable job — the show happened without them, which is the exact failure the product exists to prevent. |
| D-09 | **DQ-04.07:** alerts fire for **announced first-party shows at on-sale** — never for confirmed-but-unannounced shows, and not for third-party listings | "Confirmed" would contradict `17.01.04`, which gives the Fan no visibility until announce: alerting on a confirmed show would leak an embargoed date. Third-party listings were rejected because the locked on-sale trigger (D-01) **cannot fire for them** — no external listing produces an on-sale instant the platform can observe — so "verified partner listings" is not constructible from any source. Resolves Q-02. |
| D-06 | Default radius 25 mi / 40 km, fan-adjustable | Metro-scale — far enough to catch the show, near enough to stay signal. A starting default, not a claim of the optimal number; the max and the tuning are `/write-be-spec`. See Behavior → Resolved defaults. **Radius conflict recorded (the actual `r-79[0]` finding):** `20.01.03` D-05 uses 80 km / 50 mi with a 10–500 km range for a different purpose. The two are not reconciled and neither cites the other. They are not necessarily the same number — `20.06.01` DT-02 locks that *follow grants the alert; it does not grant marketing* — so this needs an explicit owner ruling on whether alert radius and the 20.01.03 radius are one setting or two. See Q-05. |
| D-07 | A multi-artist single event is one aggregate alert, not one per artist | Resolves Q-04. A festival/shared bill collapses to one event-keyed alert listing the followed acts — a distinct object from a single-gig alert. Extends D-02 (event-keyed dedupe) from "one human's several entities" to "several artists, one event". |
| D-08 | Location is captured at multiple moments, never via a settings page | Resolves Q-03. Ask at follow, infer from purchase/scan, ask on the first alert-worthy event. A fan will never open a settings page to set a radius; the capture must ride moments they already care about. Pairs with D-03 (surface the no-location partial loudly). |
