## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Artist Tracking & Follow ↔ Gig Alerts

**Relationship**: This pair *is* the fan product. Everything else in the sub-domain exists to make it work.
The fan's stated success criterion in `meta/personas.md` is "never misses a show by an artist they follow",
measured as "alert → attendance conversion" — which is a metric that spans both children and belongs to
neither.

The subtlety: a follow is a **standing instruction with no expiry**, and the alert that matters may fire three
years later. A fan who followed a band in 2026 and gets an alert in 2029 must find that alert welcome, not
mysterious. That means the alert has to carry its own justification ("you follow this band") — a bare
notification from a platform they half-remember signing up to is a spam report.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: follows, then forgets, then is glad. That is the whole experience and it is the correct one.
- **Musician**: sees follower counts, and — via [20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md) — that a follower attended. The **alert→attendance conversion** is the one metric that proves this domain's fan-side value.
- **Operator**: same for venue followers.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The follow is owned by `20.06.01`; alert *preferences* are owned by the fan ([20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md)). Following does not consent to marketing — see `20.06.01` DT-02, which is the sharpest distinction in this sub-domain.
2. **Trigger chain**: Show announced (17/19) → resolve followers → filter by geography (CX-M13) and preference → alert (CX-M07). If the alert fails, the fan misses the show and the product has failed at its single job — this is not a retryable background task, it is time-critical and unrepeatable.
3. **Permission intersection**: **Yes, and it is the important one.** A follow grants a *gig alert*, not a *marketing email*. Conflating them means an artist can spam anyone who followed them, which converts the follow button into a liability the fan learns not to press.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An announcement fans out to potentially thousands of followers at once, geo-filtered. This is a load spike (D-13: fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude), and it is bursty and unpredictable — announcements cluster.
5. **State transition conflict**: Show cancelled after alerts fire. The correction must reach the **exact** set who got the original, and it must reach them harder than the original did.

### CX-02: History Import & Bootstrap ↔ Artist Tracking & Follow

**Relationship**: The cold-start fix. A fan arrives following nobody; the product has nothing to say; they
leave. Import turns "who do you listen to?" from a question the fan has to answer by typing 40 band names
into an act they complete in one tap.

`meta/personas.md` puts this in the Fan's workflow as step 1 and the sweep's candidate list missed it
entirely — which is a good illustration of why breadth sweeps need a persona re-read.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: connects a service or uploads an export; gets a proposed follow list; confirms or prunes.
- **Musician / Operator**: gain followers they did not have to earn on this platform. **This is also how an artist gets followers before they have done any work here** — the acquisition flywheel.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Import **proposes** follows; the fan disposes. Never auto-follow — see `20.06.04` DT-02. A silently-created follow is a subscription the fan did not ask for, and it poisons the alert.
2. **Trigger chain**: Import → entity-resolve artist names to platform entities (CX-M17) → propose → fan confirms → follows created. Resolution failure is the interesting one: **most artists a fan listens to will not be on WeJammin**, which is the real problem here (`20.06.04` DT-03).
3. **Permission intersection**: Importing listening history is a significant privacy disclosure — it reveals taste, and taste is inference-rich. The fan must know what they are handing over.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Does an artist learn "47 people followed you from Spotify imports today"? Nice for them; possibly an inference channel about individuals if counts are small.
5. **State transition conflict**: Re-import must not resurrect follows the fan explicitly removed — the same suppression logic as [20.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-cx.md) CX-06, one level down. An unfollow is an instruction, not a preference.

### CX-03: Fan Library ↔ Artist Tracking & Follow

**Relationship**: A purchase is a stronger statement than a follow, and asking a fan to press Follow after
they just spent £20 is asking them to declare something they already proved. Auto-follow on purchase is the
obvious move — and it collides directly with CX-02's "never auto-follow", which is not a contradiction: the
fan **acted** here, whereas import is an inference. A purchase is consent to a relationship in a way that
"you streamed this once in 2019" is not.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: buys; is followed-up appropriately; is not asked to do bookkeeping about their own affection.
- **Musician**: buyers become followers, which is the conversion that makes the store a fan-acquisition surface ([20.04.01](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md) DT-03).
- **Operator / Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Purchase creates a fan record ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)); the follow is a separate, weaker object over the same relationship.
2. **Trigger chain**: Purchase → fan record → **auto-follow, undoable, visible.** Not auto-consent-to-marketing — that remains [20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md)'s to grant and the fan's to give.
3. **Permission intersection**: The line that must not blur: purchase → transactional basis + follow (gig alerts); purchase ≠ marketing consent. A store that harvests marketing consent from a checkout is the thing every fan has learned to fear.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The artist sees a buyer; the fan sees "following" appear with an obvious off switch.
5. **State transition conflict**: A fan unfollows but keeps the library. Correct and must work — owning a record is not a relationship, and the library survives everything ([20.06.03](./20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md) D-01).

### CX-04: Fan Library ↔ History Import & Bootstrap

**Relationship**: A Bandcamp collection export is one file doing two jobs — it populates the library with
things the fan already owns *and* bootstraps follows from purchase history, which is the best signal in the
sub-domain because every row is money someone spent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: their collection arrives, and the follows come free.
- **Musician**: gains followers who have *already bought their record elsewhere* — the highest-value follower there is.
- **Operator / Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Deferred — Medium confidence. Turns on whether the library holds
externally-purchased items at all, which is [20.06.03](./20.06.03-fan-library-collection.md) Q-01 and a real
scope question: a library of things we did not sell is a different product from a library of things we did.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |

| R-01 | 20.06.02 Gig Alerts | 20.06.03 Fan Library | Considered: does owning a record make a gig alert more relevant? That is precisely the auto-follow failure `20.06.04` DT-02 rejects, and drawing the edge would legitimise it. |
