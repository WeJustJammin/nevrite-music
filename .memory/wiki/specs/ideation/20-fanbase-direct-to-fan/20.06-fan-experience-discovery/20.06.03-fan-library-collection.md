# Feature: Fan Library & Purchase Collection

> **Number**: 20.06.03
> **Parent**: [Fan Experience & Discovery](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Where a fan's stuff lives: the records they bought, the downloads they own, the memberships they hold, the
tickets they have, the vault items they can open. It is the fan's half of every transaction in this domain,
and it is the reason to come back when nothing is being sold — **the only screen in domain 20 that exists
purely for the fan's benefit**, with nothing asked of them.

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list, which enumerated the artist's vault
> ([20.05.02](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.02-exclusive-content-vault.md)) and never the
> fan's collection. Every purchase in this domain is a fan getting a thing, and the sweep specified the
> selling and not the having.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Fan | Full | Everything they own, in one place: downloads (re-downloadable forever), memberships, tickets, vault access, physical orders and where they are. Plays their music in-browser (CX-M10), re-downloads in the format purchased, exports the whole collection. **Their library is theirs and is nobody else's business** ([20.06 index](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md) D-03). |
| Musician | None | No visibility into any fan's library. An artist sees that *this fan bought my record* ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) — never what else is in their collection. The cross-artist view is the exact privacy exposure [20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md) DT-01 rejected the global fan record to prevent. A Musician who is *also* a Fan sees their own library through the Fan lens; records they played on are their **catalog** (domain 02/01), never surfaced here — see Edge Cases. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. Same boundary as Musician — a Producer who is also a Fan sees only their own purchases here, never anyone else's, and never the sessions they engineered (that is domain 07/02). |
| Operator | None | No visibility. An Operator who is also a Fan has a personal library on the same terms as any other fan; the venue/studio entity has no library. |

## Behavior

The library is the **entitlement store made human**. Underneath it is CX-M24 (entitlements) and CX-M10 (media);
on the surface it is "my records".

Its governing promise: **a download bought is a download owned.** Re-downloadable indefinitely, in the formats
purchased, surviving the artist delisting the track, the pressing being cancelled, the campaign failing, and —
ideally — the artist leaving the platform. That promise is the entire emotional difference between buying and
streaming, and it is what a fan paying £20 for a £5 record ([20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md))
believes they are buying. Breaking it once, publicly, would cost more than the feature is worth.

Note the honest tension with
[20.05.02](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.02-exclusive-content-vault.md) DT-02: purchases are
owned forever, membership content may not be. Those are different transactions and a fan will not naturally
see the difference — both look like "things in my library". The library resolves this **visually, not by
hiding it** (see D-06): owned items and membership-gated items are labelled and grouped distinctly, so the
fan can see at a glance which of their things are permanent and which depend on a live membership.

### Organisation

- **Default view**: reverse-chronological by acquisition date — the newest thing the fan got is the first
  thing they see, because the most common reason to open the library is "I just bought something, show me".
- **Type filters** (single-select chips, all-selected by default): `Music` · `Memberships` · `Tickets` ·
  `Merch & Physical` · `Vault`. A fan with 400 downloads and one ticket must be able to isolate the ticket in
  one tap.
- **Ownership grouping within Music**: two labelled bands — **Owned** (permanent; purchases and downloads)
  and **Membership access** (contingent on a live membership). The label is always visible on the item, never
  only in a settings screen the fan won't open.
- **Physical orders** carry a fulfilment badge — `Processing`, `Shipped (tracking)`, `Delivered`,
  `Cancelled — refunded` — sourced from [20.04.02](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.02-merch-variants-print-on-demand.md).
- **Search**: free-text over title and artist name, scoped to the fan's own library only. Empty query =
  the default reverse-chronological list.

### Playback & Download Depth

- **In-browser playback** via CX-M10: gapless album playback, a persistent mini-player, and per-track
  play. Credits (domain 02) are one tap from the now-playing track — "who played on it" made useful rather
  than merely recorded.
- **Download**: the fan re-downloads the **original purchased formats** (e.g. FLAC, WAV, MP3-320,
  ALAC — whatever the release offered and they bought), individually or as a per-release ZIP. No expiry,
  no download-count cap on owned items — "re-downloadable indefinitely" is D-01 and it is literal.
- **Abuse ceiling**: re-download is unmetered *in intent* but rate-limited in *mechanism* — a soft cap of
  **20 release-downloads per rolling hour per fan** exists solely to stop a compromised account being used to
  scrape the catalogue; a genuine fan re-downloading their own collection to a new laptop never hits it, and
  hitting it queues rather than denies (see Edge Cases). This is a security control, not a re-download limit —
  the two must never be confused in copy.
- **No DRM**: the downloaded file is a plain audio file the fan owns. Offline listening *is* the downloaded
  file on their disk — there is no DRM wrapper, no "authorised device" count, nothing that can later revoke a
  file already on the fan's machine. In-browser streaming playback requires a connection; a PWA offline-cache
  of streamed items is explicitly **out of scope for v1** (Q-05).

### Ticket & Vault Surfacing

- **Tickets** ([19 Ticketing](../../19-ticketing-box-office/)) appear in the library because a fan will not
  accept two places for "things I bought". The library shows the ticket; opening it hands off to domain 19 for
  the QR/wallet artefact and transfer/refund flows — the library **surfaces** the ticket, it does not own the
  ticketing logic.
- **Vault access** ([20.05.02](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.02-exclusive-content-vault.md))
  appears under `Membership access`, clearly labelled as membership-gated so its impermanence is legible
  before a lapse, not discovered after one.

### Cross-Domain Effects

- A purchase **creates a follow** ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md), `-cx.md` CX-03): buying is
  the strongest possible declaration of interest, stronger than any Follow button. **Auto-follow, never
  auto-consent** — the follow is created; marketing consent is not (that stays with [20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)).
- The library is **bootstrappable** from an external import ([20.06.04](./20.06.04-listening-history-import-bootstrap.md),
  `-cx.md` CX-04) *if and only if* Q-01 resolves in favour of holding externally-purchased items.

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys an album ([20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md)), paying £20 for a £5 ask
2. It appears in their library **instantly** (realtime insert; no refresh needed); the vinyl line shows as *Shipped — expected March*, the download as immediately playable
3. They play it gapless in the browser (CX-M10), tap a track to see who played on it (domain 02) — the credits made useful rather than recorded
4. A purchase-created follow ([20.06.01](./20.06.01-artist-tracking-follow.md)) means the band's March show now reaches this fan through gig alerts ([20.06.02](./20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md)) — the loop closes
5. Two years later, new laptop: they re-download the FLACs from the `Owned` band. **It just works, and that is the promise**
6. The library also holds their ticket to that March show, their membership, and the vault they can open — one place for everything they have

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Artist delists a track after fans bought it | Entitlement survives permanently ([20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md) D-01). Delisting stops *future* sales; it never touches an existing entitlement. | Still there, still playable, still re-downloadable. No change of any kind. |
| Artist leaves the platform | Downloads survive — the fan paid **us**, so we hold and serve the file after the artist has gone. Departure removes the storefront, not the fan's ownership. The residual conflict is the *artist's* wish to have their files gone — a policy call, not a UI one (Q-02). | Item stays in `Owned`; playback and re-download work identically. No "artist unavailable" banner on owned items — that would read as revocation. |
| Rights dispute; a track pulled for an uncleared sample | The one legal case that can override ownership. Product behaviour is specified even though the legal policy is not: the item moves to a distinct **`Withheld — rights claim`** treatment (not deleted, not silently gone), playback/download disabled for that track only, and the fan is offered a **credit or refund** because they did nothing wrong. Never rendered as a plain revoked entitlement. Legal policy = Q-03. | Track shows `Unavailable — rights claim` with a plain-language note and a refund/credit CTA; the rest of the release is unaffected. |
| Membership lapses; vault items in the library | Owned purchases **never** lock. Membership-gated items are already grouped under `Membership access`, so the change is legible: on lapse, per [20.05.02](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.02-exclusive-content-vault.md) DT-02's leaning, items **released while the fan was paying stay accessible** and future items lock. Locked items remain *listed* (greyed, `Renew to access`) so the fan sees exactly what renewing restores rather than experiencing a silent disappearance. Final keep-vs-lose policy = Q-04. | A `Membership access` item greys with `Renew to access`; owned items in the same view are visibly untouched. Nothing vanishes without a trace. |
| Bundle: download owned, vinyl never pressed | Split fate, made honest ([20.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-cx.md) CX-01). The download line stays permanent in `Owned`; the physical line moves to `Cancelled — refunded` and the physical allocation of the price is refunded. The two were always shown as separate line items so neither surprises. | Download unchanged and playable; vinyl line reads `Cancelled — refunded £X`; a one-line note explains the pressing was cancelled. |
| Fan bought as a guest, no account | The entitlement is created against the purchase email and held **claimable**. The confirmation email carries a **claim link** that provisions or links an account; downloads work immediately via a **signed, expiring email link** (7-day expiry) so the guest is never stranded, and once claimed the items land permanently in the library. A guest checkout that produces no library is just a download link that gets lost ([20.04.01](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.01-storefront-product-catalog.md) Q-02) — this is the fix. | Email: "Your download is ready — [Download now] · [Claim to your library]". After claiming, everything appears in the library retroactively. |
| Two devices/tabs re-download the same release at once | Both succeed — downloads are idempotent reads of an owned file, not a transactional decrement. The rolling-hour abuse counter is shared across the account, so simultaneous devices count together. | Both downloads complete. No lock, no "in use elsewhere" error. |
| Fan hits the 20/hour abuse ceiling legitimately (bulk re-download to new machine) | The request is **queued, not denied** — excess downloads are throttled and released as the window rolls, with a clear explanation. Ownership is never the thing being limited; throughput is. | "Preparing your downloads — large re-downloads are paced to keep your account secure. They'll continue automatically." No error, no data loss. |
| Tampered / forged download URL, or a request for another fan's entitlement | Every entitlement fetch re-checks ownership server-side (CX-M24) — a signed URL is never trusted as authorisation on its own. A request for an item the fan does not own returns **404** (not 403 — existence of another fan's item is itself private, [20.06 index](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md) D-03). | `Not found` — indistinguishable from an item that does not exist. No leak that the item belongs to someone. |
| Fan is also the **artist** who released the record (multi-hyphenate) | They can buy their own release like any fan; it appears in their **library** as an owned purchase. It does **not** merge with their artist catalog — catalog (records they made/played on) is domain 02/01 and is a different surface. Library = "things I got as a fan"; catalog = "work I did". The two never blend. | The self-bought record sits in `Owned` exactly like any purchase; their catalog is unaffected and unshown here. |
| Fan credited on a record they did **not** buy | Being a credited performer is **not** an entitlement — credits (domain 02) grant identity, not a download. A drummer who played on an album they never bought sees it in their catalog/credits, never in their library. | The record is **absent** from the library. If they want the download, they buy it (or the artist gifts an entitlement — [20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md)). |
| Payment later refunded / charged back after download | Refund revokes the entitlement going forward; the item leaves `Owned` and moves to a `Refunded` state (no longer playable in-app). The already-downloaded no-DRM file cannot be recalled — accepted and honest, same class as any digital sale. | Item shows `Refunded — access ended`; in-app playback stops; the fan keeps whatever they already downloaded to disk. |
| Media/CDN unavailable for an owned item | The item **stays listed and owned**; only playback/download is momentarily degraded with a retry. Never render a transient CDN failure as a revoked entitlement (D-04) — that is the one way the fan can see the promise break, and it would be a lie told by infrastructure. | Item present, tagged `Temporarily unavailable — retry`; ownership never in doubt; retry succeeds when the CDN recovers. |
| Fan wants their whole collection out | Bulk export of everything they bought — files plus a manifest (titles, artists, purchase dates, formats). This is `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02 (earned vs hostile lock-in) pointed at the fan, and DT-05's only honest mitigation. Consumes CX-M18. | `Export my collection` produces a downloadable archive + manifest; it just works. |
| **WeJammin dies** | Every library on the platform evaporates and every ownership promise made here becomes retrospectively a lie — same class as [20.05.05](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md) DT-03 and just as far outside a schema. No in-product state can fix this; the mitigations are non-architectural: **bulk export that actually works** (D-05) and **not over-claiming** permanence we cannot structurally guarantee (DT-05). | No UI. The honest defence is the export button existing and being used, and copy that never promises more than the platform's survival can back. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Library opened | Shelf skeleton (typed placeholders per section), not a full-screen spinner |
| Empty | Owns nothing | **The most important empty state for the Fan persona** — the first screen of a product that has not yet given them anything. Not a shop pitch: the artists they follow, releases coming, a route to import an existing collection ([20.06.04](./20.06.04-listening-history-import-bootstrap.md)). Something worth being here for. |
| Populated | ≥1 entitlement | Their collection: play, re-download, view orders, open vault, export. Reverse-chronological, type-filtered, ownership-grouped. |
| Error | Media unavailable | The item **stays listed** and owned; only the media action is degraded with a retry. A CDN failure must never look like a revoked entitlement (D-04). |
| Partial | Bundle half-delivered, or a claim/import mid-flight | Delivered half is playable and permanent; pending half (unpressed vinyl, unclaimed guest item, in-progress import) is shown as clearly distinct and in-progress — never conflated with the owned half. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The library is a receipts page — an order history with a play button | The obvious e-commerce framing, and why the sweep never listed it | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the fan's home.** Bandcamp's collection is the reason people return to Bandcamp when nothing is on sale; it is where the purchase becomes an identity ("these are my records"). It is also the **only screen in domain 20 built purely for the fan's benefit** — everything else extracts something from them (a consent, a purchase, an attention). `meta/personas.md` says the fan's surface "must be a different product wearing the same brand"; this is that product's front door, and treating it as an order history guarantees the fan never comes back between purchases. |
| DT-02 | Owning-forever is a nice promise we can make cheaply | It costs storage; storage is cheap | ❌ REJECTED — it is cheap in storage and **expensive in obligation.** "Forever" means: surviving the artist leaving, surviving a rights dispute, surviving a delisting, surviving format drift, surviving us. Every one of those is a real scenario with a real cost, and each has a party who will want the file gone. Making the promise is one sentence; keeping it is a permanent liability. It is still worth it — the promise is the entire difference between buying and streaming, and it is what makes a fan pay £20 for a £5 record — **but it must be made deliberately, not casually**, and the delisting/dispute cases must be answered *before* it is made, not after the first takedown. |
| DT-03 | Owned and membership-gated items should live in separate screens so the fan never confuses permanent with contingent | It is the safest way to prevent the "I lost my library" complaint | ❌ REJECTED — **separation by hiding trades one confusion for a worse one.** Two screens teach the fan that some of their things are second-class and hide the impermanence until they go looking for it. The library must hold *everything* the fan has ("a fan will not accept two places for the things they bought"), so the honest design is **one library, two visible labels** (`Owned` / `Membership access`) — the impermanence is legible *before* a lapse rather than discovered *after* one. Confirmed as D-06. |
| DT-04 | Re-download should be uncapped because ownership is unconditional | D-01 says re-downloadable indefinitely, literally | ✅ CONFIRMED with a security caveat — ownership is unconditional, but an **account** is a compromise target, and an uncapped download endpoint on the whole catalogue is a scraping tool waiting for a stolen session. The resolution is to cap the *mechanism* (a soft 20 releases/rolling-hour, **queued not denied**) while leaving the *right* uncapped — a genuine fan never notices, a thief is slowed, and the copy must call it a security pace, never a re-download limit, or it silently breaks the promise. |
| DT-05 | Library permanence is a storage problem | It looks like one | ❌ REJECTED — it is the same problem as [20.05.05](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md) DT-03 (backer funds in insolvency), wearing different clothes. **If WeJammin dies, every library evaporates and every ownership promise made here becomes retrospectively a lie** — for fans who paid over the odds precisely because they believed they were buying rather than renting. The honest mitigations are not architectural: bulk export that actually works (which is also `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02, pointed at the fan), and not over-claiming permanence we cannot structurally guarantee. **The platform that exists because records get lost should be unusually careful about promising to keep them.** |
| DT-06 | A credit or an import row is the same as an entitlement — if the platform knows you're connected to a record, it should be in your library | The consolidation thesis makes everything feel adjacent | ❌ REJECTED — **being connected to a record is not owning a download.** A drummer credited on an album (domain 02) has *identity* in it, not an *entitlement* to it; an imported Bandcamp row (Q-01) is evidence of a past purchase we did not fulfil. Collapsing credits/imports/purchases into one "library" would mean a fan's catalog, their listening history, and their receipts all grant the same download — which is wrong three different ways. The library is strictly **entitlements**: things the fan got a right to *from us*. Catalog is domain 02/01; import is [20.06.04](./20.06.04-listening-history-import-bootstrap.md) and only becomes library if Q-01 says so. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A download bought is a download owned — re-downloadable indefinitely, in the purchased formats, with no expiry or count cap | The entire difference between buying and streaming, and what a fan paying 4× the ask believes they are paying for. |
| D-02 | The library is the fan's home, not an order history | See DT-01. The only screen in the domain that asks nothing of them, and therefore the only reason to return between purchases. |
| D-03 | Entitlements survive delisting, artist departure, and campaign failure | The fan paid us. Their ownership cannot be contingent on someone else's later decision. |
| D-04 | Never render a media/CDN failure as a revoked entitlement | The one way the fan can see the promise break, and it would be a lie told by infrastructure. Failed items stay listed and owned. |
| D-05 | Bulk export must actually work — files plus a manifest | DT-05's only honest mitigation, and `meta/problem-statement.md` Q-02 applied to the fan. |
| D-06 | One library, two visible ownership labels (`Owned` / `Membership access`) — never two screens | See DT-03. Impermanence must be legible before a lapse, not discovered after one; and everything the fan has lives in one place. |
| D-07 | Downloads are DRM-free plain audio files; offline = the downloaded file | No authorised-device count, nothing that can revoke a file already on the fan's machine. |
| D-08 | Re-download is unmetered as a right but paced as a mechanism (soft 20 releases/hour, queued not denied) | DT-04 — an account is a compromise target; the security pace must never be described as a re-download limit. |
| D-09 | The library is strictly entitlements — credits are catalog (02/01), not library | DT-06. Being connected to a record is not owning a download. |
| D-10 | A request for an item the fan does not own returns 404, not 403 | [20.06 index](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md) D-03 — the existence of another fan's item is itself private. |
