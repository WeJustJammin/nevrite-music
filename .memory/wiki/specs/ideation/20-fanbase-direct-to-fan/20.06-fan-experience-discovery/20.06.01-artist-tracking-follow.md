# Feature: Artist Tracking & Follow

> **Number**: 20.06.01
> **Parent**: [Fan Experience & Discovery](./20.06-fan-experience-discovery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A fan saying "tell me when this artist does something". It is one button and it is the load-bearing act of the
entire fan-side product: the follow is the alert subscription (`-cx.md` CX-01), the fan record's origin, and
the standing instruction that fires three years later when the band finally plays their city.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Fan | Full | Follows and unfollows artists and venues. Sees who they follow, and tunes what each one may tell them ([20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md)). Unfollowing is instant, complete, and asks no questions — a "are you sure?" on an unfollow is a dark pattern aimed at the person with no professional stake. |
| Musician | Reports | Sees follower **count** and growth. Sees followers as fan records ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) — but never a cross-artist view of who else that fan follows (index D-03). |
| Producer | None | No visibility. Producers do not have followers in this domain; a producer with an audience is using the Musician lens. |
| Operator | Reports | Venue followers — people who want to know what is on at this room. Genuinely valuable and structurally identical to an artist follow. |

## Behavior

Following consumes **CX-M28** (Follow, Save & Watchlist) — the mechanism serves 8 domains (saving a gear
listing, watching an opportunity). What is domain-owned is what following an **artist** *means* (index D-04):
it creates a fan record, grants gig-alert eligibility, and starts a relationship. Saving a used pedal does
none of those things.

The critical boundary, and the one every incumbent gets wrong: **a follow is not marketing consent**
(`-cx.md` CX-01 Q3). Follow → gig alerts about that artist's shows. Follow ≠ permission to sell the fan a
hoodie. The moment those merge, the follow button becomes a thing fans learn not to press, and the whole
fan-side product dies with it.

A follow has **no expiry** and must age well: the alert that matters may fire in 2029, and it must carry its
own justification, because a bare notification from a half-remembered platform is a spam report.

**Level-1 defaults** (owner ratification pending — see Open Questions):
- **A follow ships with gig alerts ON, not a blank subscription.** The default is: alert me to this artist's
  shows within my geo radius ([20.06.02](./20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md)). A follow that grants *eligibility*
  but no actual alerts unless the fan then hunts through settings is the load-bearing act quietly failing —
  the fan believes they will be told and they will not be. Per-artist granularity (release alerts, this-artist-only
  frequency) is tunable in the preference centre ([20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md)),
  but the useful default is on.
- **Follower identity is private to the fan; only the aggregate count is artist-visible** (Role Lens, index D-03).
  A fan's list of who they follow is theirs, never a cross-artist lookup. Whether a *public* "2.4k followers"
  badge renders at all is a separate product call (Q-02) — public counts are social proof but are actively
  harmful to a new artist with 3 followers and are a vanity-gaming target (DT-01).

### Happy Path

1. Fan sees an artist's page (rendered by CX-M23) after buying a ticket
2. Taps Follow. One tap; no account wall in front of it if avoidable
3. Fan record created/updated ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)); gig-alert eligibility granted; **no marketing consent granted**
4. Fan forgets entirely
5. Eighteen months later the artist announces a show 12 miles away; the alert fires ([20.06.02](./20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md)) and says *you follow this band*
6. Fan goes. **That is the product, and steps 4–5 are the hard part**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan follows without an account | Should work — an account wall in front of the load-bearing act is where fan products die. Needs an anonymous-to-identified path (email-only, then upgrade). | Follow registers instantly. Fan is then asked for an email *to keep the follow durable* — framed as "so we can tell you when {artist} plays near you", not "create an account". No password wall. A follow with no durable contact point is a follow that can never fire an alert. |
| Artist assumes a follow means they can email marketing | The predictable, universal misunderstanding, and it is the artist's honest expectation because every other platform works that way. Enforced by [20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md), not by explanation. | Artist sees followers ≠ contactable, with the gap named. |
| Fan follows a band **and** its members' solo projects | Three follows, three fan records ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) — and three alerts about what may be one gig. | See DT-03. |
| Band splits; followers follow… what? | [20.01.05](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.05-fan-list-ownership-transfer.md) — the follow graph transfers or forks with the list. The fan followed *that band*, and that band no longer exists. | Notice + choice. |
| Fan follows 400 artists | Alert volume becomes noise, which destroys the one thing the product is for. Frequency control lives in the preference centre — but a fan who muted everything to stop the noise has also lost the alerts they wanted. | Alerts are geo-gated by default, so 400 follows ≠ 400 alerts — only the ones playing near you surface. Beyond a threshold the fan is offered a digest and per-artist muting (never a single global mute that silently kills every alert). Concrete volume ceiling deferred — see Q-03. |
| Artist deletes their account | Followers dangle — the entity they subscribed to no longer exists. | The follow is retired from the fan's list; the fan is not spammed about a deletion they did not initiate. Fan record ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md)) is retained (it may hold purchases), but the dead follow stops being a live alert subscription. `[PENDING — /write-be-spec: retention window + whether a departing artist can hand off followers]` |
| Follower count as vanity/gaming | Followers are cheap to fake and artists will buy them. Unlike the [superfan score](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.02-superfan-score.md), a follow costs the fan nothing — which is exactly what makes it a weak signal. | See DT-01. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Page opens | Follow button in an indeterminate state — **never render it as "Follow" if they already do**; a fan who follows twice thinks the product is broken |
| Empty | Fan follows nobody | The cold start. [20.06.04](./20.06.04-listening-history-import-bootstrap.md) is the answer, and it must be the first thing offered, not a settings-page afterthought |
| Populated | ≥1 follow | Their artists; what each may tell them |
| Error | Follow write fails | Must not appear to succeed. A fan who thinks they are following and gets no alerts blames the artist for not playing their city. |
| Partial | Following, but no location set | Following works; **geo alerts do not** ([20.06.02](./20.06.02-gig-alerts-near-me.md)) — the single most valuable alert type is silently unavailable. This must be surfaced, not discovered by missing a show. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The follow is the fan-side product's core object | It is the obvious centre — everything hangs off it | ⏳ **True structurally, misleading strategically.** A follow costs the fan nothing, which makes it a weak signal, gameable, and a vanity metric artists will inflate. The *valuable* objects are the ones with cost attached — a scan, a purchase, a membership ([20.02.02](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.02-superfan-score.md) D-01). The follow's real job is not to *measure* fandom but to be the **cheapest possible commitment that turns an anonymous human into an addressable one**. That reframing matters: optimise the follow for friction (make it free, one tap, no account), and let the costly signals do the measuring. |
| DT-02 | Following an artist implies consent to hear from them | What every artist believes and every social platform trained them to believe | ❌ REJECTED — **the sharpest distinction in this sub-domain.** A follow is "tell me when you play near me"; it is not "sell me things". Merging them is how Bandsintown-style alerting becomes a marketing list and how the follow button becomes a thing fans learn not to press. Keeping them separate is the harder product (artists will complain that their 4,000 followers are not contactable) and it is what makes the alert credible enough to be worth having. This is the fan-side mirror of [20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md)'s whole argument. |
| DT-03 | One follow per artist is sufficient modelling | Obvious | ❌ REJECTED — **music's multi-hyphenate fact runs the other way too.** `meta/personas.md` opens with it: one human is a drummer in two bands, a solo artist, and a producer. A fan who loves that human follows the band, the solo project, and maybe the side project — three entities, three follows, three fan records ([20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md) D-01). Then one gig gets announced by two of them and the fan gets alerted twice about the same night. **The persona doc's structural insight about professionals has a fan-side consequence nobody traced**, and dedupe cannot key on the artist entity — it has to key on the *event*. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A follow grants gig alerts, never marketing consent | See DT-02. The distinction that keeps the follow button pressable. |
| D-02 | Optimise the follow for zero friction; let costly signals measure fandom | See DT-01. A follow's job is addressability, not measurement. |
| D-03 | Unfollow is instant and asks nothing | A retention prompt aimed at the persona with no professional stake is a dark pattern, and they will remember it. |
| D-04 | Alert dedupe keys on the **event**, not the artist | See DT-03. One human's three entities announcing one gig must produce one alert. |
| D-05 | Alerts carry their justification | A follow never expires; the alert may fire in 2029 and must explain itself or be reported as spam. |
| D-06 | A follow ships with **geo-gated gig alerts ON by default** | Eligibility without a live default subscription is the load-bearing act failing silently — the fan believes they'll be told and won't. Geo-gating is what keeps 400 follows from becoming 400 alerts. |
| D-07 | Follower **identity** is private to the fan; only aggregate **count** is artist-visible | The fan's follow list is theirs, never a cross-artist lookup (index D-03). Whether a public count *badge* renders is a separate call (Q-02). |
