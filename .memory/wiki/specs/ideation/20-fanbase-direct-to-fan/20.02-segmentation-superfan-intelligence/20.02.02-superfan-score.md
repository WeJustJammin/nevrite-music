# Feature: Superfan Score

> **Number**: 20.02.02
> **Parent**: [Segmentation & Superfan Intelligence](./20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A per-fan, per-artist ranking of how much of a fan someone actually is — built from spend, attendance, recency,
tenure and membership rather than from email opens. It exists to answer the one question every independent
artist has and no tool can answer today: **who are my 200 real fans?** Kevin Kelly's "1,000 true fans" is the
economic model most independents are actually running; nothing in their stack tells them who those people are.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees each fan's band/tier on their record, filters on it in the [builder](./20.02.01-segment-builder.md), sees the distribution of their audience. Cannot edit a score. May be able to tune the *formula* — unresolved, see parent Q-02. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. No fan graph. |
| Operator | Read-only | Same, for the venue's repeat attendees. Arguably **more reliable here** — a venue's score is built almost entirely on door scans, which are the hardest signal on the platform, whereas an artist's blends spend, follows and softer data. |
| Fan | None | Never sees it (D-02). Experiences only consequences — an early-access invite, a perk. Never a rank, never a tier name, never a number. |

## Behavior

Scoring is **per (fan, artist-entity)**, following [20.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md) D-01. There is no global "this person is a superfan" — that is meaningless. Someone can be an artist's most valuable fan and a stranger to everyone else.

Signals, strongest first:

| Signal | Why it ranks here | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Ticket scanned at the door** | Attendance is costly, physical, and unfakeable. The strongest fan signal that exists. | 19 (scan) |
| Money spent D2F | Costly, and directly the thing the artist cares about. | [20.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md) |
| Membership tenure / active tier | Recurring commitment. | [20.05.01](../20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns/20.05.01-membership-tiers-benefits.md) |
| Recency | A fan from 2019 who has done nothing since is a memory, not a fan. | derived |
| Tenure | Depth of relationship; counterweight to recency. | derived |
| Email opens/clicks | Weakest. Included for completeness and because it is all a new artist has. Apple Mail Privacy Protection has made opens near-meaningless since 2021. | [20.03](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md) |

Recency **decay** is intrinsic, not a separate feature — a score without decay is a lifetime-value tally, and an
artist asking "who are my fans" means *now*.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — weights, decay half-life, banding, and cold-start handling.

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys a ticket, attends (scan), buys a shirt at the merch table, joins the mailing list
2. System accretes signals onto the fan record as each occurs
3. Score recomputes; fan lands in the top band for this artist
4. Artist announces a tour; builds "top 5%" and gives them a 48-hour presale ([20.02.03](./20.02.03-perk-entitlements-presale-eligibility.md))
5. Fan receives an early-access invite. They never see a score, a tier name, or a rank — they experience being remembered

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| New artist, no purchase/scan history | Score degrades to open/click — i.e. to Mailchimp. **The domain's headline claim is unavailable at exactly the moment of adoption.** See DT-02. | `[PENDING]` — the honest answer may be to withhold scoring until there is signal, rather than show a confident number built on nothing. |
| Wealthy fan vs devoted broke fan | Spend-weighted scoring ranks a rich casual above a devoted teenager who came to nine shows on a bus. That is arguably **wrong for music** in a way it is not wrong for retail. See DT-01. | `[PENDING]` |
| Fan attends every show but never buys | Attendance-weighted scoring must catch them. If it does not, the score is a revenue tally with a friendly name. | Should rank highly. |
| Fan of the band, not the solo project | Separate scores per entity. Correct — but it collides with parent Q-03 (cross-entity segments). | Two records. |
| Score recompute fails | Segments resolve on last-good (CX-01). Never null. | Stale indicator. |
| Fan buys 40 tickets (a scalper — Fan anti-persona) | Scores as a superfan. **Bulk purchase is a scalping signal, not a devotion signal**, and this scoring model reads it exactly backwards. | Nothing — which is the bug. See DT-04. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fan record / segment opened | Skeleton band indicator |
| Empty | Insufficient signal | An explicit "not enough signal yet" — not a zero, not a low band. A confident-looking wrong number is worse than an honest gap. |
| Populated | Score computed | Band on the record; distribution in aggregate |
| Error | Recompute pipeline down | Last-good with timestamp |
| Partial | Some signal sources unavailable (e.g. 19 not integrated) | Score shown with the missing dimension named — the artist must know their score has no attendance in it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Spend should be the dominant signal — it is what artists care about | Standard e-commerce RFM practice, transplanted | ❌ REJECTED — an RFM model imported from retail ranks a rich casual above a devoted fan who cannot afford the vinyl, and music is one of the few markets where **that is a category error the customer will notice**. The devoted broke fan brings friends, fills the room, and is the person the artist means when they say "my fans". **Attendance should outweigh spend**, which is the opposite of every CRM's default and is only possible because we hold scans. This is the sharpest music-specific finding in the sub-domain. |
| DT-02 | The score works from day one | Implicit in the domain rationale | ❌ REJECTED — cold-start is severe and structural. A brand-new artist has no scans, no D2F sales, no memberships; the score collapses to email opens, which Apple MPP has rendered noise since 2021. So the domain's headline capability is **weakest precisely when a new artist is deciding whether to stay**. Two honest responses: withhold scoring until signal exists (recommended — an honest gap beats a fabricated rank), and lean on [20.01.04 import](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md) to backfill purchase history from Bandcamp exports. Raised to MoSCoW. |
| DT-03 | Fans should see their own score — gamification drives engagement | Every loyalty programme ever; Spotify Wrapped's success | ❌ REJECTED, and this is a values call the platform should make deliberately. Telling a person their loyalty rank does two bad things: it gamifies a relationship that is not a game, and it tells someone they are a *bad* fan. Spotify Wrapped works because it is celebratory and self-comparative; a superfan rank is competitive and other-comparative. It also invites score-farming, which corrupts the artist's data. Recorded as D-02 on the parent index. |
| DT-04 | Bulk ticket purchase reads as devotion when it is scalping | Adversarial read against the Fan anti-persona ("bulk-buys tickets to scalp") | ✅ CONFIRMED — a genuine defect in the naive model. 40 tickets bought, ~2 scanned to that identity, resold to strangers: maximal spend, near-zero attendance. **The scan-over-spend weighting from DT-01 fixes this for free** — a scalper's tickets are scanned by other people. That is a strong convergent argument for attendance-dominant scoring: it is both more musically honest *and* structurally scalper-resistant. Coordinate with 19 and 24. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attendance (scans) outweighs spend | See DT-01 and DT-04. Musically honest and scalper-resistant — the two arguments converge, which is the best evidence available that it is right. |
| D-02 | Score is never shown to the fan | See DT-03. A values decision, taken deliberately. |
| D-03 | Recency decay is intrinsic to the score, not a separate feature | A score without decay answers "who *was* my fan". Merged the sweep's implied lapse/win-back concern into the score's decay rather than creating a node for it. |
| D-04 | Withhold the score rather than show a low-confidence one | Cold-start (DT-02). An artist who trusts a fabricated rank makes worse decisions than one who knows they lack data. |
