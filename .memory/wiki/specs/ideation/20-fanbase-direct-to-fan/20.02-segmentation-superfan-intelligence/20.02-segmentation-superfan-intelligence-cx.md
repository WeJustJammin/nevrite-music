## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Superfan Score ↔ Segment Builder

**Relationship**: The builder filters on observed facts (bought X, attended Y, lives within Z miles) plus one
derived fact: the score. Observed facts are auditable — the ticket was scanned or it was not. The score is a
model output, and if the artist cannot see why it says what it says, they will not trust a segment built on it,
and will fall back to "everyone" — which is Mailchimp with extra steps.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: filters on score bands ("top 5%", "lapsed superfans"). Needs to believe the number. See parent Q-01.
- **Operator**: same, for a venue's repeat attendees — where the score is arguably *more* reliable, being built almost entirely on scans.
- **Fan**: no visibility (D-02).
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The score is owned by `20.02.02` and is read-only to the builder. No merge — one writer.
2. **Trigger chain**: Score recompute → live segments re-resolve on next evaluation. Async and eventually consistent. If recompute fails, segments must resolve on the **last good score**, not on a null that silently empties an audience.
3. **Permission intersection**: None beyond entity scope — anyone who can build a segment can filter on score.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None. A score change is not an event anyone is told about, by design (D-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: A fan crossing a score band mid-send is benign for a broadcast, and **not** benign for an entitlement — which is CX-04, the real problem.

---

### CX-02: Segment Builder ↔ Perk Entitlements

**Relationship**: The builder produces an audience; the entitlement attaches a promise to it (early access, a
discount, a members-only vault item, a presale window). Domain 19 then enforces that promise at the box office
without needing to understand the fan graph at all — it receives an eligibility set and a window, not a query.
That boundary is what keeps 19 from having to reimplement segmentation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: defines "top 200 fans get 48 hours early" and never thinks about it again.
- **Operator**: same for the room's own presales.
- **Fan**: experiences it as an invite arriving before the general on-sale. Never sees the criterion.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The segment is owned by `20.02.01`; the entitlement grant is owned by `20.02.03`. The grant **copies** the membership at issue time (CX-04) — deliberate denormalisation, because the promise must outlive the query.
2. **Trigger chain**: Segment → entitlement issued → codes/eligibility handed to 19 → fan notified via CX-M07. If 19 rejects the set (sale cancelled, show pulled), the entitlement must be revoked *and* the fan told, or they will show up with a code that does nothing.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. Whoever may create an entitlement is committing the artist to inventory they may not have — a presale promise is a promise about tickets. Delegated authority (CX-M09) must scope this separately from "can send email".
4. **Notification fan-out**: Yes — entitlement issue is one of the few genuinely welcome messages this domain sends.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two entitlements from overlapping segments (top-5% *and* bought-the-vinyl) hit the same fan. Must be idempotent per fan per sale — one code, best window. Otherwise a fan gets two codes and a second fan gets none.

### CX-03: Perk Entitlements ↔ Superfan Score (the feedback loop)

**Relationship**: Redemption is a fan signal — a strong one. Feeding it back into the score means fans who got
a perk score higher, so they get the next perk, so they score higher. The ratchet is real and it does not need
malice to appear; it is the default behaviour of the loop.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees a stable top-tier and reads it as loyalty rather than as an artefact of their own past choices.
- **Fan**: a fan who joined last month can never catch a fan who has been getting presales since 2019 — regardless of how much they now spend.
- **Operator / Producer**: Operator same as Musician; Producer not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — one-way write.
2. **Trigger chain**: Redemption → signal → score recompute. Async.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: Not a race — a **drift**. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Options: exclude perk redemption from scoring entirely (clean, discards a real signal); weight it low; or hold back a slice of every perk for fans outside the top tier so the ladder stays climbable. The third is the most interesting and the least standard.

### CX-04: Segment Builder ↔ Perk Entitlements (live vs snapshot)

**Relationship**: Two correct requirements in direct conflict. D-01: segments are live, so a revoked consent
leaves every segment instantly. CX-02: an entitlement must be stable, or a fan promised a presale loses it
overnight because they drifted out of the top 5% when someone else bought a t-shirt.

**Resolution**: they are different objects. The **segment** stays live and is re-evaluated every time it is
used. The **entitlement** snapshots its membership at grant time and is revoked only by an explicit act
(fraud, or the fan's own consent revocation — which must still pull the *notification*, though arguably not
the *entitlement itself*: a fan who unsubscribed from email should still be allowed into the presale they
earned, they just cannot be emailed about it).

That last distinction is subtle and is exactly the sort of thing that gets implemented wrong by default —
consent governs **contact**, not **entitlement**.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: promises hold. No support tickets from fans whose code stopped working.
- **Fan**: what they were promised is what they get, even if their behaviour changed after.
- **Operator**: same as Musician.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Entitlement wins for its own lifetime. The segment is not its master after issue.
2. **Trigger chain**: Grant → snapshot → immutable until expiry/revocation. No recompute path.
3. **Permission intersection**: Consent revocation suppresses the *message* about the entitlement, not the entitlement. Needs explicit confirmation — see Q-01 below.
4. **Notification fan-out**: On grant only.
5. **State transition conflict**: Resolved by making entitlement and segment distinct objects with distinct lifecycles rather than trying to make one behave as both.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 20.02.02 Superfan Score | 20.02.03 Perk Entitlements | Direct pairing rejected — the score never grants an entitlement by itself. It is always mediated by a segment (CX-01 → CX-02), because an entitlement needs a defined population, not a threshold. The reverse direction (redemption → score) is real and is CX-03. Modelling score → entitlement directly would double-count the path that already exists. |
| R-02 | 20.02.01 Segment Builder | 20.02.01 (self — nested segments) | "Segments of segments" is tempting and is a real ESP feature. Rejected at this level: it is a capability *within* the builder, not an interaction between children. If it becomes complex enough to have its own behaviour, that is a signal to promote the builder to a sub-domain — noted, not acted on. |
