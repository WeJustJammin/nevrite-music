# Feature: Consent, Legal Basis & Proof-of-Consent

> **Number**: 20.01.02
> **Parent**: [Fan Graph & Owned Audience](./20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The record of *why* an artist is permitted to contact a fan, captured at the moment the fan grants it and
held as evidence rather than as a boolean. Contactability is resolved per (fan, owning entity, channel,
purpose) — a fan who bought a ticket can be told the doors time but not sold a t-shirt; a fan who signed up
at the merch table can be sold the t-shirt but has not consented to SMS. This is the platform's own thesis
turned on consent: **capture the fact at the moment it is true, because reconstructing it later is worthless**
(D-18, problem-statement.md). Consent is a leaf feature and not a sub-domain because it is one capability —
the derivation of a per-tuple contactability answer from an append-only event chain — even though four other
children of this sub-domain bend around it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Selects, per channel (email / SMS / push), which purposes (marketing / transactional / service) they intend to rely on, and sees per segment the **contactable count** — always ≤ segment size, usually far smaller, which is the point of the feature. Can open any single fan's **evidence chain** (every grant/revoke event: timestamp, owning entity, channel, purpose, exact wording shown, source surface, provenance strength, captured IP/user-agent where the platform witnessed it). **Cannot create, edit, upgrade, restore, or delete any consent event.** There is no owner-side write path — see D-03. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. A Producer's stake in domain 20 is as a payee on D2F splits ([20.04.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md)), never as a holder of someone else's fan list. A Producer who runs their own audience is exercising the Musician lens on their own artist entity, not the Producer lens (parent index, Role Matrix note). |
| Operator | Config | Identical to Musician, scoped to the venue/studio entity. Called out separately because a venue's lawful basis for ticket holders is usually **transactional-only** (they sold an entry, not a marketing relationship), yet venues habitually market to that list anyway — so the contactable count for a venue's "everyone who ever bought a ticket" segment will be dramatically smaller than the venue expects, and the feature must make that gap legible rather than let them assume the whole list is marketable. |
| Fan | Full | Grants and revokes per artist entity, per channel, per purpose, through the Preference Centre ([20.01.03](./20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md)) — the only fan-side writer onto this state (CX-03). Sees every basis every artist relies on for them and the evidence behind each. **The fan's write always wins over the artist's** and always wins the race (CX-03, D-05 below). A fan is also a first-class user (D-11), so a fan who is themselves a Musician sees both their own artist entity's contactability panel (Config) and their personal fan record (Full) — two different lenses on one human, never merged. |

## Behavior

Consent is **append-only**. A grant is an event; a revocation is an event; the current state is *derived* by
replaying events per (fan, owning entity, channel, purpose) and taking the one with the latest **fan-authored
timestamp**. No row is ever updated in place, because the evidentiary value of consent is entirely in the
record of the moment — a boolean `subscribed = true` proves nothing to a regulator, a mailbox provider, or a
complaint handler. This is deliberately the identical shape as split capture: the fact is worth capturing only
at source (DT-02, D-01 below).

Each consent event permanently records the full circumstance of the moment: **who** the fan is, **which
owning entity** the consent runs to, **which channel** it covers (email, SMS, or push), **which purpose** it
authorises (marketing, transactional, or service), whether the fan **granted or revoked**, the **moment the
fan acted** — held separately from the moment the platform durably recorded it, because the two can diverge and
only the first is evidentiary — the **exact wording the fan saw**, the **surface it happened on** (store
checkout, preference centre, an imported list, a ticket scan), the **legal basis** relied on (the fan's
consent, a contract they entered, or the owner's legitimate interest), and the **provenance strength** —
whether the platform itself witnessed the act or an owner merely asserted it. Provenance strength is **decided
by the platform, never by the owner**: an event counts as platform-witnessed only when the platform originated
it, and every owner-supplied event is treated as artist-asserted (see [20.01.04](./20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md),
CX-01). An owner has no way to claim a stronger provenance; any provenance an owner supplies is **ignored**,
because provenance is not an owner-authorable fact at all.

Purposes are **not interchangeable and not hierarchical**. Revoking marketing must never suppress
transactional — a fan who unsubscribes from a newsletter and consequently stops receiving their own ticket QR
is a support catastrophe wearing a compliance costume (CX-03). A contactability resolution for `marketing`
never falls back to a `transactional` grant, and vice versa.

**Capture is opt-in by construction (D-06 below).** No consent surface anywhere on the platform ships a pre-ticked
marketing box, for any fan, in any jurisdiction. This is the **strictest-global default**: the platform
applies GDPR/PECR-style affirmative opt-in everywhere rather than geo-varying the capture UI, because a
pre-tick is unlawful in some jurisdictions and merely unnecessary in the rest — offering it nowhere is the
only design that is lawful everywhere and needs no jurisdiction lookup at the point of capture. Whether the
owner *wants* to trade the list growth a US-style pre-tick would lawfully win in permissive jurisdictions is a
values-and-revenue decision, not a capture-UI decision, and is held open as Q-01 — but the buildable default
is fixed: never pre-tick.

**Contactability is resolved fresh at send time, not cached (D-07 below).** A segment's contactable count is a
point-in-time snapshot; the authoritative check happens per recipient at dispatch, so an unsubscribe that
lands after a campaign is composed but before a given recipient is dispatched still suppresses that recipient
(CX-03 synthesis Q2). The consent state is the hard gate on [20.03 Broadcast & Fan Messaging](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md) —
no send resolves without it.

### Happy Path

1. Fan enters their email into an artist's store checkout, alongside an **unticked** box reading exactly "Also email me about {artist}'s new releases and shows".
2. Fan ticks it and completes the purchase.
3. System records **two** consent events at the moment of purchase — a `transactional`/`contract` grant (they bought a thing) and a `marketing`/`consent` grant (they ticked) — the transactional grant inseparable from completing the order, the marketing grant existing only because the fan ticked. Each is platform-witnessed, each carries the exact wording shown and the checkout as its source surface, and both are authored at the submit moment.
4. Artist later builds a campaign to "everyone who bought the vinyl"; the system resolves contactability per fan and includes this fan on the marketing channel because a live `marketing` grant exists with no later `revoke`.
5. Fan clicks unsubscribe in that campaign → a marketing revoke is recorded, sourced to the preference centre → the fan **still** receives the vinyl's shipping notification, because that is a transactional message resolved against the still-live transactional grant.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Artist attempts to re-subscribe a fan who opted out (UI or API) | Blocked — there is **no** owner-side write path to consent, enforced by absence of the capability, not by a runtime check. Any owner-initiated consent write is **rejected as forbidden**; the capability does not exist for an owner principal. | "Only the fan can restore this. You can ask them in person; you cannot do it here." |
| Fan is in an opt-in jurisdiction (EU/UK) and hits a checkout | No pre-tick is ever offered — strictest-global default applies identically to a US fan. The `marketing` grant only exists if the fan affirmatively ticked. There is no geo-branch in the capture UI. | Standard unticked opt-in box; identical everywhere. |
| Consent granted 24+ months ago, fan never engaged | Consent does **not** auto-expire — the event chain is permanent (append-only). The platform surfaces a **staleness advisory** on any segment whose newest event for a fan is older than 24 months with zero opens/clicks, and downgrades that fan's deliverability tier ([20.03.04](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)), but **blocks no send on age alone**. Whether the platform should hard-expire stale consent, and at what age, is a legal/values call held as Q-02. | Segment shows "N fans: consent >24 months old, low engagement — re-permission recommended". No send blocked. |
| Consent evidence requested during a spam complaint | Artist (or Trust & Safety, domain 24) exports the full immutable event chain for one fan: every grant/revoke with wording, timestamps, source, basis, provenance strength, and captured IP/user-agent. | A single-fan evidence pack (read-only). |
| Fan grants SMS consent but the artist has configured no SMS sender | Consent is recorded and **dormant** — it never expires on non-use and is available the instant a sender is configured. | "N fans consented to SMS — no SMS sender configured." |
| Two fan records merged with **disagreeing** consent (ticket buyer = transactional-only, merged with newsletter signup = marketing) | **Intersection of permissions, union of identities** (CX-02). The merged record holds every identity but only the narrower contactability; a transactional-only record is never silently upgraded to marketable by the merge. An unmerge restores each side's pre-merge state, not the intersected one (append-only makes this recoverable). | Artist sees the narrower state and the reason ("merged with a transactional-only record — marketing not inherited"). |
| Owner-supplied import claims the strongest, platform-witnessed provenance to smuggle weak records into a strong tier | Downgraded to artist-asserted; the claimed strength is **rejected, never trusted** (CX-01). | Import completes; records land in the artist-asserted (unmarketable-until-re-permissioned) tier. |
| Fan revokes marketing in the same window a campaign is resolving contactability for them (concurrency) | The revoke carries the earlier fan-authored moment; the per-recipient check re-runs at **dispatch**, not only at compose, so the revoke suppresses this recipient. If dispatch and revoke are genuinely simultaneous, the **fan write wins** and the send is suppressed (fail toward not-sending). | Fan: no email. Artist: recipient shown as "suppressed — unsubscribed before dispatch" in the campaign report. |
| Two events for the same tuple arrive out of order (revoke recorded before an earlier-authored grant, or a legitimate re-grant after a revoke) | Derived state uses the **latest fan-authored moment**, never the order in which the platform received the events. A grant the fan authored after a revoke is a lawful self-re-subscription and correctly re-enables contactability; a late-arriving grant the fan authored *before* a live revoke does not resurrect contactability. | Correct current state; evidence chain shows the true chronology regardless of arrival order. |
| Consent read times out or is unreachable at send-resolution | **Fail closed** — contactability resolves to "not contactable" and the send is blocked. The per-recipient check is time-bounded; if it cannot obtain a definitive answer within its window, it returns not-contactable rather than guessing. Blocking a campaign is recoverable; an unlawful send is not (D-04). | "Consent service unavailable — sends paused. No fan was contacted." |
| Marketing-grant write fails at checkout (network/service error) while the purchase itself completes | The **purchase still completes** — the transactional grant is captured as an inseparable part of buying the thing. The marketing grant is retried; if it cannot be durably recorded, the fan is **not** marked marketing-consented — an unrecorded consent is no consent (fail closed). | Fan: normal order confirmation, no error surfaced. Artist: fan appears transactional-only until a durable grant exists. |
| Consent event missing the exact wording the fan saw (legacy/partial import) | Retained but flagged as evidence-incomplete; contactability still resolves from the recorded action, but the evidence pack shows "wording not captured — imported assertion" and the fan sits in the artist-asserted, deliverability-downgraded tier. | Artist: "N records have incomplete consent evidence." |
| Consent event attributed to a known minor | **Not consent** — a consent event whose fan record is flagged under age assurance (D-13, CX-M31) resolves to not-contactable for `marketing` regardless of the recorded action; the event is retained as evidence but never gates a send. | "Contactability withheld — age assurance." (Detail routed to CX-M31.) |
| Multi-hyphenate: the same human's email is both a fan of Artist A and on Venue V's ticket list, and that human also *operates* Venue V | Consent is scoped per **owning entity**, so the tuple `(fan, Artist A, email, marketing)` and `(fan, Venue V, email, marketing)` are independent states — a revoke against Artist A never touches Venue V, and the operator cannot see or alter their own contactability *as a fan* of Artist A from the venue's Config panel. | Each entity sees only its own scoped state for this fan; no cross-entity leakage. |
| Malicious mass-unsubscribe: attacker tries to forge or replay preference-centre links to nuke a rival's list | A revocation is only honoured when it proves it came from a real message the platform actually sent to *that* fan for *that* owner; a forged, replayed, or cross-recipient attempt cannot make that proof and is **rejected as invalid**. An attacker cannot unsubscribe addresses they were never legitimately sent a preference link for. | Attacker: rejected. Legitimate fan: unaffected. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Contactability panel or evidence chain opened | Skeleton; per-channel/purpose counts resolve after the segment resolves against the ledger |
| Empty | No consent events for this (fan, entity) | "Not contactable" **with the reason** ("no consent on record") — never a blank cell |
| Populated | ≥1 event exists | Current derived state per (channel, purpose) plus the full evidence chain behind it |
| Error | Consent ledger unreachable / read timeout | **Fail closed** — resolves to "not contactable", sends blocked, "Consent service unavailable — sends paused" |
| Partial | Contactable on some (channel, purpose) tuples only, or evidence flagged incomplete | Per-tuple state, each with its own basis and provenance strength; incomplete-evidence badge wherever the exact wording was never captured |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This duplicates CX-M18 (Privacy, Consent & Data Portability) and should be dissolved into it | Node Classification Gate — "is it a mechanism serving many domains?" | ❌ REJECTED — CX-M18 is the general ledger, subject-rights machinery (DSAR, erasure), and portability; this feature *consumes* it. What is domain-owned is **marketing** consent: which artist may sell to which fan, on which channel, under which basis. Nothing else on the platform has that surface — Community (03) is peer-to-peer professional contact, Promotion (21) addresses strangers, platform Notifications (CX-M07) are the platform's own transactional messages. The mechanism is shared; the product is not (D-02, parent index). |
| DT-02 | Consent should be stored as current state (a boolean per channel) with history as a nice-to-have | Simpler; what every basic ESP (Mailchimp `subscribed` flag) does | ❌ REJECTED — the entire value of consent is evidentiary, and the evidence is the event, not the state. A boolean cannot answer "what exactly did she agree to, on what date, in what words, from what surface" — the only question ever asked about consent when it matters. This is the *identical* failure the platform exists to fix for splits: a reconstructed record is worthless at the moment it becomes contested. Append-only events; derived state (D-01). |
| DT-03 | The hardest problem here is unlawful-marketing risk from artists, not from the platform | Fan anti-persona (unexpected email) + the import path (CX-01) | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a **multi-tenant** risk, not a per-artist one. On a shared sending domain, one artist blasting a bought list generates complaints that degrade deliverability for **every other artist** (see [20.03.04](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)). The platform therefore has a **commercial** interest in strict consent, not merely a legal one — a far more durable enforcement motive than compliance alone. This is why weak-provenance records are throttled/tiered rather than trusted (D-08 below). |
| DT-04 | Purposes can be modelled as a hierarchy (transactional ⊂ service ⊂ marketing) so one grant implies the lesser ones | Tidy; matches how engineers instinctively model permission scopes | ❌ REJECTED — they are **orthogonal**, not nested. A `marketing` grant does not imply a right to send `service` messages, and — critically — a `marketing` revoke must *not* touch `transactional` (the ticket-QR catastrophe, CX-03 Q3). Treating them as a hierarchy is exactly how a newsletter unsubscribe silently kills a fan's order receipts. Each (channel, purpose) tuple is resolved independently with its own basis. Textually distinct from DT-03: that is about *who* creates risk; this is about *how the permission model itself* manufactures it if mis-shaped. |
| DT-05 | Fail-open on a ledger outage is acceptable because blocking sends harms artists and a brief outage is low-risk | Availability instinct — "don't let infra break the product" | ❌ REJECTED — an unlawful send is irreversible and multi-tenant-damaging (a complaint hurts every sender on the shared domain), whereas a blocked campaign is fully recoverable once the ledger returns. The asymmetry is total: the cost of wrongly-not-sending is a delay; the cost of wrongly-sending is a regulatory complaint and deliverability damage that is not undoable. Fail closed (D-04). Distinct from DT-02/DT-03: this is about the *default under uncertainty*, not storage shape or threat source. |

> Every Deep Think hypothesis is logged — confirmed and rejected — as the audit trail that the obvious cheaper designs (boolean state, purpose hierarchy, fail-open) were considered and refused for concrete reasons.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Consent is append-only events; current state is **derived** by latest fan-authored timestamp | See DT-02. The evidence *is* the product; a boolean is worthless when consent is contested. Enables lossless unmerge (CX-02). |
| D-02 | Contactability is resolved per **(fan, owning entity, channel, purpose)** — never per list or per boolean | A single flag cannot express "tell me about the show, don't sell me a hoodie" — a distinction fans genuinely hold. Scoping by owning entity is what keeps a multi-hyphenate's fan/operator states independent. |
| D-03 | There is **no owner-side write path** to consent | Enforced by absence of the capability, not a runtime permission check. An artist cannot grant, upgrade, or restore consent under any circumstance, including support escalation. Owner-initiated consent writes are rejected as forbidden. |
| D-04 | **Fail closed** on ledger unavailability | An unlawful send is irreversible and damages every tenant on the shared domain; a blocked campaign is recoverable (DT-05). Resolve to not-contactable on read timeout. |
| D-05 | The **fan write always wins** the race against an owner action | An unsubscribe landing between compose and dispatch suppresses that dispatch; simultaneity resolves toward not-sending. Owner writes cannot participate in the race at all (they do not exist — D-03). |
| D-06 | **No pre-ticked marketing box, anywhere, for anyone** — strictest-global capture default | Opt-in-only is lawful in every jurisdiction and needs no geo-branch at capture. The revenue trade-off of *not* pre-ticking where it would be lawful is a values decision (Q-01), separate from the buildable default. |
| D-07 | Contactability is re-resolved **per recipient at dispatch**, not cached at compose | Closes the compose→dispatch window in which a fan revokes; the segment count is advisory, the dispatch check is authoritative (CX-03). |
| D-08 | **Provenance strength is server-assigned**; purposes are **orthogonal**, never hierarchical | `platform-witnessed` is stamped only on platform-originated events; owner-supplied events are coerced to `artist-asserted` (DT-03, CX-01). A marketing revoke never touches transactional (DT-04). |
