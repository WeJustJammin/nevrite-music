## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Import & Hygiene ↔ Consent & Legal Basis

**Relationship**: Import creates fan records the platform has never observed consenting. The artist asserts
a source ("gig signup sheet", "old Mailchimp list", "Bandcamp buyers") and the platform must record that
assertion as *provenance metadata on the consent state* — not convert it into equivalent standing with a
consent event it actually witnessed. Downstream, `20.03.04` (Deliverability) reads this provenance to
decide send throttling, because imported lists are where spam complaints come from, and complaints on a
shared sending domain damage every other artist on the platform.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: asserts the source at import; sees each segment's consent-strength composition; is throttled or blocked on the weakest tier.
- **Operator**: identical, for a venue list.
- **Fan**: does not see the import happen — which is exactly the hazard. Their first signal is an unexpected email.
- **Producer**: not affected — no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The consent state is owned by `20.01.02`; import may only *propose* a state. If an address already exists with a stronger, platform-witnessed consent, import must not downgrade it, and if it exists with an explicit revocation, import must not touch it (see CX-06).
2. **Trigger chain**: Import → consent-state proposal → provenance stamped → deliverability tier assigned. If the provenance assertion is missing, the import completes but the records land in an unmarketable tier rather than failing — an artist should never lose data because they couldn't answer a compliance question.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. Import permission does not confer send permission. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` on whether weak-provenance records are sendable-with-warning or hard-blocked until re-permissioned.
4. **Notification fan-out**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether a re-permission campaign to imported records is itself lawful is the recursive version of this problem and needs counsel.
5. **State transition conflict**: Import is a bulk write racing the Preference Centre's single-record writes. Revocation must always win regardless of arrival order — it is the only state transition here with a statutory consequence.

### CX-02: Unified Fan Record ↔ Consent & Legal Basis

**Relationship**: `20.01.01` resolves several observations of a human into one record. Each observation may
carry a different lawful basis: a ticket purchase gives transactional/legitimate-interest standing (you may
send them their ticket, and arguably a "doors at 7" reminder); a newsletter signup gives marketing consent.
The merged record must hold **the union of identities and the intersection of permissions** — never the union
of permissions.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Operator**: sees one fan, one contactability state, and the reason for it. Must not be able to widen the state by merging.
- **Fan**: sees one record of themselves per artist and every basis the artist relies on.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: `20.01.02` owns consent; `20.01.01` owns identity. Merge strategy for permissions is intersection-with-provenance, and it is not user-overridable.
2. **Trigger chain**: Merge → recompute contactability → if narrowed, no notification; if a pending send targeted the old record, it re-evaluates against the merged state before dispatch.
3. **Permission intersection**: This cross-cut *is* the permission intersection. Contactability is per (fan, owner, channel, purpose) — not per record.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A merge must not fan out to the fan. Telling someone "we worked out that your two email addresses are the same person" is unsettling and is itself a privacy event. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: An unmerge (false-positive resolution) must restore the pre-merge consent states, not the intersected one. This implies consent history is append-only — consumes CX-M19 (Audit Log & Provenance Ledger).

### CX-03: Preference Centre ↔ Consent & Legal Basis

**Relationship**: The Preference Centre is not a settings page; it is the fan's write access to a legal record.
Every toggle emits an immutable consent event with timestamp, source IP/user-agent, and the exact wording shown.
That evidence is what defends the artist in a complaint, and it is the same shape of "capture the fact at the
moment it is true" that the platform thesis is built on — applied to consent instead of splits.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan**: full control; can act without an account (signed token from any message) — a fan who must log in to unsubscribe will hit "report spam" instead.
- **Musician / Operator**: read-only. Cannot re-subscribe a fan who opted out. The absence of this write is the feature.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: `20.01.02` owns the state; the Preference Centre is the only fan-side writer. Fan writes beat owner writes, always.
2. **Trigger chain**: Toggle → consent event → contactability recompute → in-flight campaign re-evaluation. An unsubscribe during a send-in-progress must suppress remaining sends, not merely apply to the next campaign.
3. **Permission intersection**: An unsubscribe from marketing must not suppress transactional messages (order shipped, ticket QR, membership receipt). Conflating them means a fan who opts out of a newsletter stops receiving their ticket — a support catastrophe dressed as compliance.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Confirmation to the fan; no notification to the artist per-event (a mail per unsubscribe trains artists to resent their fans). Aggregate only, via CX-M30.
5. **State transition conflict**: Last-write-wins by fan-authored timestamp; owner-side writes cannot participate in the race at all.

### CX-04: Ownership & Transfer ↔ Unified Fan Record

**Relationship**: `20.01.05` changes the owning entity on a set of records — on band dissolution, label
signing, or a manager's departure. This is a re-parent, not a copy: the fan does not become two fans.
Where both successors claim the list, the outcome may be a genuine fork (both get a copy) and that is a
product/legal decision, not an engineering default.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: initiates and consents to transfer as a member of the band entity (domain 01). Multi-party approval likely required.
- **Operator**: same for a venue sale/handover.
- **Fan**: read-only, but must be *told* — see CX-05.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The band entity (domain 01) owns the list, not the members. Merge strategy on a contested split is `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`; the plausible options are unanimous-consent, majority-of-members, or fork-with-notice.
2. **Trigger chain**: Transfer → re-parent records → re-evaluate consent (CX-05) → notify fans → re-baseline superfan scores (they are owner-scoped). A partial failure must not leave records orphaned; the pre-transfer owner retains until the whole set commits.
3. **Permission intersection**: Delegated authority is enforced by CX-M09. A manager with send rights does not thereby have transfer rights.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Yes — to every fan on the transferred list, and to co-owners of the source entity.
5. **State transition conflict**: A transfer racing a campaign send must block the send. Mail going out mid-transfer has ambiguous provenance and an ambiguous sender identity.

### CX-05: Ownership & Transfer ↔ Consent & Legal Basis

**Relationship**: A fan consented to hear from a specific act. Whether that consent survives the act becoming
a different legal entity is unsettled and jurisdiction-dependent — under GDPR a change of controller is at
minimum a transparency event and may invalidate the basis; US practice treats lists as assignable business
assets. The platform cannot paper over that difference.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: may find their transferred list is legally smaller than the one they were promised.
- **Fan**: gains a notice, and possibly a re-permission prompt.
- **Operator / Producer**: Operator same as Musician; Producer not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Deferred — Medium confidence entry. This turns on legal advice, not design preference. See `20.01.05` Q-01.

### CX-06: Preference Centre ↔ Import & Hygiene

**Relationship**: Suppression must be enforced at the address level per owning entity and must be immune to
re-import. The failure mode is concrete and common: artist exports a list in January, a fan unsubscribes in
March, artist re-uploads the January CSV in June, and the fan gets mail they revoked — which is both unlawful
and, on a shared sending domain, a complaint that damages other artists (see `20.03.04`).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Operator**: sees "N records skipped — previously unsubscribed" at import, with no way to override.
- **Fan**: unaware, correctly — the system defended them silently.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Suppression list is owned by `20.01.02` and is append-only. Import reads it and can never write to it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Import → suppression check per address → skipped records reported, not created. No compensating action needed because nothing was written.
3. **Permission intersection**: No import permission overrides suppression, including admin.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None to the fan. A summary to the importer.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — suppression is terminal until the fan themselves reverses it.
