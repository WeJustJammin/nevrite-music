# Feature: Fan Preference Centre & Unsubscribe

> **Number**: 20.01.03
> **Parent**: [Fan Graph & Owned Audience](./20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The fan's control surface over what every artist and venue they follow may send them, and through which
channel and at what frequency. It is the only fan-side write path onto the consent ledger
([20.01.02](./20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md)), and it must work **without a login** — a fan forced to
authenticate in order to unsubscribe will press "report spam" instead, which converts one fan's irritation
into a deliverability tax levied on every artist sharing the platform's sending domain
([20.03.04](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)). Its second job
is to sell "less" as an alternative to "never", because a permanent unsubscribe is unrecoverable and a
frequency down-shift is not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Fan | Full | Sees every artist and venue holding a marketing basis for them, on one page, with per-channel toggles (email / SMS / push) and a per-entity frequency tier. Can turn frequency down instead of leaving (Highlights / Shows near me / New releases / Snooze). One-click global opt-out is always present, always above the fold, and always honoured. Reachable from any message via a signed link with no login challenge. Their write always beats any owner-side state. |
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the **aggregate** preference distribution for their own list — counts per tier ("412 on All, 88 on Highlights, 31 Shows-near-me only"), counts per muted channel, and net churn as a single number. Has **no write path** and **no per-fan toggle history** as an editable object. Cannot re-subscribe, cannot restore, cannot see a per-fan feed of who rejected what — only the roll-up. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. A Producer owns sessions and provenance, not a fan list; there is no fan population that holds a marketing basis for a Producer, so there is nothing here for them to see or configure. If a human who is a Producer also operates as a Musician/Operator, they see this surface **through that persona's list**, never as a Producer. |
| Operator | Read-only | Identical to Musician, scoped to the venue/studio entity's own list. Relevant because a venue's lawful basis over ticket buyers is frequently transactional-only, so the aggregate they see here is often much smaller than their raw address count — which is the intended, honest number. |

## Behavior

The Preference Centre inverts the incumbent pattern: it is organised **by the fan**, not by the artist. A fan
who follows nine artists sees one page listing all nine, each with its own controls — not nine separate
unsubscribe pages reachable only by excavating nine old emails. This is only possible because the platform is
consolidated; a per-artist ESP (Mailchimp/Klaviyo/Bandzoogle) structurally cannot offer a cross-sender view
because it can only see its own tenant.

Every control is a **consent-ledger event**, never an in-place settings update. A toggle emits an immutable
event stamped with timestamp, the exact wording shown to the fan, the source surface, and the fan-authored
IP/user-agent; current state is derived (see [20.01.02](./20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md) D-01, and `-cx.md`
CX-03). This is the platform thesis — capture the fact at the moment it is true — applied to consent instead
of splits.

### Frequency Tiers (see D-05)

Frequency is a **per-(fan, entity)** setting, orthogonal to the per-channel toggles and to the global opt-out.
The tiers are:

| Tier | Admits | Suppresses | Notes |
|------|--------|-----------|-------|
| **All** (default) | Every marketing send the entity is otherwise permitted to make | — | The unchanged relationship. |
| **Highlights** | At most **1 marketing email per calendar month** from this entity | The 2nd+ marketing send in a month is **dropped, not queued** | "Monthly digest". Cap is per-entity, not global. |
| **Shows near me** | Only campaigns tagged `live-event-geo` whose event is within the fan's radius (default **80 km / 50 mi**, fan-adjustable 10–500 km) | All `general` and `release` campaigns | Requires a resolvable fan location (see partial-data edge case). |
| **New releases** | Only campaigns tagged `release` | All `general` and `live-event-geo` campaigns | Release-announcement sends only. |
| **Snooze 30 / 60 / 90 days** | Nothing, until the timer expires | All marketing from this entity for the window | Auto-resumes to the tier that was active before the snooze. Transactional is unaffected. |
| **Off** (per entity) | Nothing marketing from this entity | All marketing from this entity, permanently, until the fan reverses it | Equivalent to a per-entity unsubscribe; transactional still flows. |

**Send-time resolution** (this feature writes the state; [20.03](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md)
reads it): each campaign carries a purpose-type — `transactional`, `general`, `release`, or `live-event-geo`,
tagged by the sender at compose time. Resolution per fan, in order: (1) `transactional` always passes,
untouched by any tier or channel mute — a ticket QR is never suppressed by a marketing preference; (2) marketing
passes only if the entity is not globally opted out, **and** the campaign's channel is enabled for this fan,
**and** the active tier admits this campaign's purpose-type, **and** the tier's frequency cap is not already
exhausted for the period. A campaign left **untagged defaults to `general`**, which only "All"-tier fans
receive — the safe direction is fewer sends, and tagging discipline is enforced upstream in 20.03.

The global one-click opt-out sets every entity to **Off** on every marketing channel in a single event, and is
the action wired behind the RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header.

### Happy Path

1. Fan receives an email from an artist and taps "manage preferences" in the footer.
2. The signed link opens the Preference Centre with **no login challenge**; the sending artist is anchored at
   the top of the roster.
3. Fan sees all nine artists/venues they hear from, each row showing three channel toggles and the current
   frequency tier.
4. Fan sets this artist to **Shows near me** (radius left at the 80 km default), and mutes the **SMS** channel
   **globally** across all nine.
5. Fan confirms; the system writes the consent events (one tier-change event scoped to the artist, one
   global-SMS-mute event), shows an inline confirmation, and no page reload loses the other
   rows' state.
6. The artist's next `general` campaign resolves this fan **out**; a `live-event-geo` campaign for a show in
   the fan's city resolves them **in**; every artist's next SMS campaign resolves this fan **out** on SMS but
   leaves their email state intact.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Signed link is expired/old | The full-roster view is gated behind login, but the **single-sender opt-out for the entity that sent the link never fails** — that action stays available on an expired token. | Working opt-out for that one sender + "log in to manage everyone who emails you". The cross-sender roster is refused as an expired-token error; the single-sender opt-out still works. |
| Forwarded email — a third party clicks unsubscribe | The opt-out is applied to the **original recipient** (the token's subject), because that is who the token identifies — the accepted industry trade-off, since requiring login causes far more harm. The hole is closed by a **transactional confirmation email** to the true recipient carrying a **one-click "undo — this wasn't me" link valid 7 days**; that link re-grants the prior state as a fresh consent event. (see D-06.) | Clicker: a plain confirmation. True owner: an "unsubscribed you — undo?" email; one click restores. |
| Fan unsubscribes mid-send | The in-flight campaign must **suppress remaining sends** to this fan, not merely apply next time. Revocation wins any race with an active send. | Nothing — silence is the correct outcome. |
| Fan opts out of marketing, then buys a ticket | Still receives the ticket, the QR, the doors time — a different **purpose** (`transactional`), unaffected by the marketing opt-out. | Their ticket, exactly as expected. |
| Fan has no WeJammin account (imported address) | The centre works via the signed token and shows only entities holding a basis for that address. No account is created by visiting. | Same page, narrower roster. |
| Native `List-Unsubscribe` header click (Gmail/Apple) | Honoured as a full **Off** for that sender via RFC 8058 POST, with no page load. | The mail client's native "unsubscribed" confirmation. |
| Two tabs / two devices toggle the same fan at once | Both are append-only events; **last write wins by fan-authored timestamp**; nothing is lost because state is derived, not overwritten. | Whichever change is chronologically later; a stale tab that re-submits an older timestamp does not resurrect a superseded state. |
| Global opt-out (tab A) races a per-artist "Shows near me" (tab B) | Different scopes on the same fan; resolved by fan-authored timestamp per entity. If global-Off is later, the entity is Off; if the per-artist tier is later, that entity is Shows-near-me and the rest stay Off. | The later intent, per entity — no silent merge that widens contactability. |
| Import bulk-write races a fan's unsubscribe here | **Revocation always wins**, regardless of arrival order — it is the only transition here with a statutory consequence (`-cx.md` CX-01 q5, CX-06). Import can read suppression but can never write over a revocation. | Fan stays suppressed; importer later sees "N skipped — previously unsubscribed". |
| Owner attempts a write to the fan-side endpoint | There is **no owner write path**; a forged owner-authenticated request is rejected. | Rejected — "Only the fan can change this." |
| Ledger write fails (network / ledger down) | The opt-out **must not appear to succeed**. The event is queued to a durable fallback opt-out sink and the fan is shown explicit failure + retry; the RFC 8058 POST path is the fallback of last resort. Fail-safe direction is suppression, never a false success. | Explicit failure + retry; never a green "you're unsubscribed". |
| Signed link prefetched by an email security scanner (GET) | A **GET must never mutate state** — scanners (Gmail/Outlook Safe Links) prefetch links. The one-click action is an RFC 8058 **POST**; the page renders on GET and requires a confirming POST. (see DT-03.) | Nothing changes from the prefetch; the fan's real click performs the POST. |
| "Shows near me" chosen but no resolvable location | The tier **cannot resolve**, so it **holds** (suppresses) the entity's sends rather than defaulting to "send everything" — fail-safe is fewer sends. The fan is prompted inline for a city/postal code (coarse, not precise). | "Add a city so we can filter to shows near you" — until then this entity is effectively paused. |
| Endpoint hit with a mass/automated submission on a leaked token | Harm is capped (it can only alter the token subject's own state), but the token endpoint is still rate-limited. | Throttled on abuse; normal fans never hit it. |
| Token-enumeration attempt to harvest a person's artist roster | Tokens are unguessable and single-fan-scoped; a forged/invalid token yields **only** the single-sender opt-out affordance, **never** the cross-artist roster. The roster requires a token that genuinely resolves to that fan (privacy exposure tracked in Q-02). | Forged token: opt-out for the one claimed sender at most; no roster ever leaks. |
| Multi-hyphenate — a Fan who is also a Musician/Operator | They act here **purely as Fan** — the roster shows entities that market **to** them, including their own band if they follow it. Managing their **own** outbound list is a different surface (20.01.02 config), never merged into this inbound view. Unsubscribing themselves from their own band's marketing is honoured like any fan's. | One inbound preference page; their artist dashboard is elsewhere and read-only here. |
| A subset of sender rows fails to load | The page renders the rows it has, marks the rest "couldn't load — retry", and the **global opt-out still works** even when individual rows are degraded. | Partial roster + explicit per-row error; global control unaffected. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Centre opened | Skeleton list of senders; the anchored sender resolves first so the most-likely action is available soonest. |
| Empty | Fan holds no marketing relationships | "Nobody is emailing you" + how it happens (following an artist, buying a ticket, signing up at a show) — never a blank page. |
| Populated | ≥1 sender | Grouped-by-sender rows (channel toggles + frequency tier) with global controls pinned above the fold. |
| Error | Ledger write fails | **The opt-out must not appear to succeed.** Explicit failure + retry + the RFC 8058 fallback path. A false "you're unsubscribed" is the single worst outcome available on this surface. |
| Partial | Signed token identifies one sender only, or some rows failed to load | That sender's controls fully functional; the remaining roster sits behind an optional login, or degraded rows show a retry — the global opt-out never degrades. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A cross-artist preference centre is the single fan-facing advantage consolidation buys that no incumbent can copy | Thesis (D-18): consolidation is the platform | ✅ CONFIRMED — Mailchimp/Klaviyo/Bandzoogle each see only their own tenant, so "manage everyone who emails me" is structurally impossible for them and near-free here. It is also the rare consolidation dividend that accrues to the **Fan**, who otherwise receives mostly obligations from this domain. Worth more than its file size suggests. |
| DT-02 | Frequency down-shift meaningfully reduces permanent unsubscribes | Standard CRM practice; the asymmetric cost of losing a fan forever vs annoying them monthly | ⏳ HYPOTHESIS, honestly unproven — the practice is well-established in generic CRM, but there is no music-specific evidence, and parasocial attachment could cut either way (a fan may forgive volume from an artist they love, or resent it more). Recorded as a thing to measure (alert→attendance and tier-change→retention), not a fact. Cheap to build, so the option value is high regardless — which is itself the argument for shipping it. |
| DT-03 | A one-click GET unsubscribe link is safe to implement | The naive "just put a link in the footer" approach | ❌ REJECTED — email security scanners (Gmail Safe Links, Outlook ATP) issue **GET prefetches** on every link, which would auto-unsubscribe recipients who never clicked, silently shredding lists. The correct mechanism is RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (a **POST** one-click) for the header, and a page that only **renders** on GET and mutates only on a confirming POST. This is a concrete, well-documented production failure, not a theoretical one. |
| DT-04 | "Shows near me" is a pure convenience with no privacy cost | Feature framing as a nicety | ❌ REJECTED — the tier requires storing a fan's **location**, which is new PII created by this feature. Mitigation: store only **coarse** location (city/postal centroid), collected from the fan, purpose-limited to send-filtering, never precise geolocation, and never resolved server-side from IP without consent. The tier's value is real but it is not free — it manufactures a data-protection obligation that the geo default must respect. |
| DT-05 | An unsubscribe-confirmation email to a fan who just opted out is annoying spam and should be suppressed | Fan-experience instinct ("don't email someone who asked you to stop") | ❌ REJECTED — exactly one confirmation, containing **no marketing content**, is **transactional**: it concerns the fan's own consent action, and it is the mechanism that closes the forwarded-email hole (DT-linked to D-06) by giving the true recipient a one-click undo. Suppressing it would leave forwarded-email unsubscribes silently unrecoverable. Send precisely one; never a follow-up. |
| DT-06 | Re-enabling consent from the same no-login link is trivially safe | Symmetry with the unsubscribe link | ✅ CONFIRMED with a caveat — allowing a fat-fingered fan to undo without logging in is correct UX, **but a re-grant is a consent grant**, so it must capture wording/timestamp/source like any grant (not silently flip a boolean), and in opt-in jurisdictions the fan's affirmative click **is** the required affirmative act. The link therefore carries both directions, and both directions write real ledger events. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

_These feed Step 6 cross-cut consolidation. Each is a concrete, directional dependency._

- **Writes to [20.01.02 Consent, Legal Basis & Proof-of-Consent](./20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md)** — this
  feature is the *only* fan-side writer onto the consent ledger; every control here is a ledger event, not a
  settings toggle. Shared-entity + state-write dependency. See `-cx.md` CX-03.
- **Feeds [20.01.04 Import & Hygiene](./20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md) suppression** — an Off/unsubscribe
  written here must survive re-import forever; import reads suppression and can never overwrite a revocation
  (`-cx.md` CX-06). State-race dependency: revocation always wins the import race.
- **Gates [20.03 Broadcast & Fan Messaging](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md)**
  — every send resolves against tier + channel + global state written here, and depends on 20.03 tagging each
  campaign with a purpose-type (`transactional`/`general`/`release`/`live-event-geo`). Trigger-dependency both
  ways: this feature defines the resolution rules; 20.03 must supply the campaign tag or default to `general`.
- **Consumes CX-M18 Privacy, Consent & Data Portability** — sits adjacent to the platform's DSAR/erasure
  surface; a fan reaching for "stop emailing me" and a fan reaching for "delete me" arrive from the same
  impulse and must be able to find each other from this page.
- **Consumes CX-M21 Accessibility** — this is a legal control surface; an inaccessible unsubscribe is an
  unlawful one. Keyboard/screen-reader operation of every toggle and the global opt-out is a compliance
  requirement, not a nicety.
- **Consumes CX-M20 Localization** — jurisdiction affects whether re-grant needs an affirmative act (opt-in
  regimes) and shapes the wording captured on each event.
- **Touches CX-M07 Platform Notifications** — the global opt-out's scope against *platform* (non-artist)
  notifications is unresolved and fans will not distinguish the two (Q-01).
- **Touches 24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** — a "report spam" that a broken/absent unsubscribe provokes lands
  there and reads this feature's evidence chain; the whole point of the no-login design is to keep volume out
  of that queue.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No login required to unsubscribe | A login wall converts unsubscribes into spam complaints, and complaints are a shared cost across every artist on the sending domain ([20.03.04](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)). |
| D-02 | Organised by fan (all senders on one page), not by artist | See DT-01. This is a consolidation dividend and the fan's payoff for the domain existing. |
| D-03 | Offer "less" alongside "never" | A permanent unsubscribe is unrecoverable; a frequency reduction is not. Marked a hypothesis (DT-02), not a proven win — but cheap enough that option value justifies it. |
| D-04 | Artists have no write path and see churn only in aggregate | Per-fan unsubscribe feeds train artists to take it personally and to chase fans off-platform (see Role Lens, and 20.01.02 D-03). |
| D-05 | Frequency tiers are **All / Highlights (≤1 email/month) / Shows near me (geo, 80 km default) / New releases / Snooze 30-60-90 / Off**, orthogonal to per-channel toggles, resolved at send-time against a campaign purpose-type | Concrete tiers let 20.03 resolve deterministically; an untagged campaign defaults to `general` so the safe direction is fewer sends. |
| D-06 | Forwarded-email unsubscribes are applied to the token subject, then made recoverable by exactly one **transactional** confirmation email carrying a 7-day one-click undo | Applying to the token subject is the accepted trade-off (login would harm more); the confirmation+undo closes the third-party-click hole without adding friction to the common case. See DT-05. |
| D-07 | The one-click action is an RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` **POST**; GET never mutates state | Email security scanners prefetch links with GET (DT-03); a GET mutation would auto-unsubscribe fans who never clicked. |
| D-08 | "Shows near me" stores only **coarse, fan-provided** location, purpose-limited to send filtering | The tier creates new PII (DT-04); coarse + purpose-limited is the minimal footprint that still makes the tier work. |
