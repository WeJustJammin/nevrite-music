# Feature: Fan List Ownership, Band Splits & Roster Transfer

> **Number**: 20.01.05
> **Parent**: [Fan Graph & Owned Audience](./20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who owns the fan list, and what happens to it when the relationship that built it ends — the band splits, the
label deal expires, the manager leaves and takes their laptop, the venue is sold. The audience is now the most
valuable asset an independent act holds (Bandcamp and Patreon prove direct fan monetization exceeds streaming
for most independents), and the platform is about to become the place it is custodied. **A custodian with no
answer to "whose is it?" is not a custodian.**

> **Deep Think addition** — this node was not in the sweep's candidate list. It is the ownership question
> applied to the fan graph, and it is the exact class of failure the platform exists to prevent for splits and
> credits: a fact nobody recorded while everyone was friendly, litigated later when they are not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees who owns each list they can act on (themselves, their band entity, their label) and on what terms. Initiates a transfer or a fork on dissolution. As a band member, votes/consents on a transfer of the band's list. Sees, at the moment they join a band or sign a deal, **what happens to the fans if it ends** — stated up front, not discovered afterwards. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. Producers do not own artist fan lists. Flagged as a genuine boundary: a producer who develops an act and drives its audience may believe otherwise, but this domain does not model that claim — see Q-03. |
| Operator | Config | Configures ownership of the venue's list at the entity level. Relevant on venue sale/handover, where the mailing list is frequently the most valuable thing being sold and nobody documents it. |
| Fan | Read-only (notified) | Cannot control who owns their record — but is **told** when it moves, by whom, to whom, and is given the same one-click exit they always had. See CX-05: notification may be a legal obligation, not a courtesy. |

## Behavior

The list is owned by an **entity** (domain 01) — an artist, a band, a label, or a venue — never by a natural
person acting for it. A manager with send rights holds delegated authority (CX-M09), not ownership; when the
manager leaves, nothing about the list changes except who can act on it. That distinction alone prevents the
single most common real-world theft: the person who had the login treating the list as theirs.

Transfer is a **re-parent, not a copy** (`-cx.md` CX-04). A fork — both successors get a copy — is possible
but is a distinct, deliberate outcome with different consent consequences, not a default.

The design principle is the one the platform already believes: **capture the fact while everyone is friendly.**
Ownership and dissolution terms should be set when a band entity is created or a label signs the act — at the
moment nobody is fighting — not derived from a support ticket two years later.

### Happy Path

1. Three Musicians form a band entity (domain 01); the fan list is created under the band
2. At creation, the platform asks the question nobody asks: **if this band ends, what happens to the fans?** Options presented with plain-language consequences; the answer is recorded as a term of the entity
3. Band builds a list over four years
4. Band dissolves; a member triggers dissolution on the entity
5. System applies the recorded term — e.g. "fork: every member's successor project gets a copy, and every fan is told" — rather than adjudicating a fight
6. Fans receive a notice naming the successor entities and offering one-click exit; consent is re-evaluated (CX-05)

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Fan-list ownership is a domain-01 (Identity/Organizations) concern and should not exist here | Node Classification Gate — entities and their terms live in 01 | ❌ REJECTED — 01 owns the *entity* and its membership. What is unowned anywhere else is the fan-specific consequence: consent does not assign like property (CX-05), fans must be notified, superfan scores are owner-scoped and must re-baseline, and a fork produces two lists where one existed. None of that is expressible as a generic entity term. The entity relationship lives in 01; **what happens to the fans** lives here. |
| DT-02 | This is the same failure the platform exists to fix, one layer up | Thesis (D-18): provenance is the wedge; `meta/problem-statement.md` — "the reconstruction is the failure" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the strongest argument for the node's existence. The problem statement says facts get "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." Fan-list ownership on a band split is *precisely* that shape — verbally assumed, never recorded, catastrophic at dissolution. A platform that captures splits at the session and then loses the fan list to whoever had the password is not internally consistent. **The fix is the same fix: ask at formation, while everyone is friendly.** |
| DT-03 | Export scoping matters more than ownership rules | Follow-through on the manager-leaves case | ✅ CONFIRMED — ownership is a claim; export is a capability. If any delegate can export 40,000 emails to a CSV, the ownership model is decorative. This collides head-on with parent Q-01 (if artists cannot export, lock-in is hostile; if anyone can export, ownership is unenforceable). **The resolution is scope + audit + notice, not prohibition** — the owning entity may export, delegates may not, every export is logged and the owner is told. Raised to `/create-prd-security`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The list is owned by an entity, never by a person acting for it | Prevents the login-holder-owns-it failure, which is the common real-world theft. |
| D-02 | Dissolution terms are captured at entity formation, not at dissolution | The platform's own thesis applied to itself. A term agreed while everyone is friendly costs one conversation; the same term reconstructed at a breakup may be unobtainable at any price. |
| D-03 | Transfer is a re-parent; fork is a distinct deliberate outcome | Copying by default silently doubles a consent surface and misrepresents what the fan agreed to. |
| D-04 | Fans are notified on transfer | Partly legal (CX-05), partly the only honest thing to do. A fan list that is silently traded is a mailing list, not an audience. |
