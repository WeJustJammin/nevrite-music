# Feature: Unified Fan Record & Identity Resolution

> **Number**: 20.01.01
> **Parent**: [Fan Graph & Owned Audience](./20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

One durable record per fan per artist-entity, assembled from every observation the platform makes of that
human: their WeJammin account (D-11), tickets they bought, merch they ordered, shows they scanned into,
follows they made, and addresses the artist imported. This is the thing no competitor can assemble —
Mailchimp has the email, Bandsintown has the follow, the box office has the scan, and none of them can join.
WeJammin can, because all three events happened on it. The record is the substrate every other child of
domain 20 stands on; its correctness is the domain's entire competitive claim (DT-02), and a wrong merge on
it is a privacy incident (D-02), not a data-quality blemish.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full (own artist-entity's list) | Opens a fan's record and sees: every purchase, every ticket scanned, every show attended, membership tier, first-seen date and arrival source (gig signup / store / follow / import). Can **merge** two records they believe are the same person and **split** a bad merge — both gated by a `fan:merge` delegated scope (CX-M09), not conferred by mere `fan:view`. Cannot see the fan's activity with *other* artists — the record is per-entity (D-01). Cannot edit an observed fact (the fan bought the ticket; that is true) — only merge/split identity and act on contactability. |
| Producer | None | No visibility, no fan list. A Producer's only stake in domain 20 is as a payee on [20.04.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md). A Producer who has their own audience is exercising the **Musician** lens on their own artist-entity, not a Producer capability — the two lenses never blend within one record. |
| Operator | Full (own venue-entity's list) | Same record shape, scoped to the venue entity (D-03): who bought tickets to shows at this room, who is on the venue list, who returns. Same `fan:merge` gate. **Cannot** see an artist's fan record for the same human even when the observation happened at their venue — two entities, one event, two isolated records (Q-02). |
| Fan | Read-only (own record) | Sees what each artist/venue they follow holds about them and how it was obtained (per-observation provenance). Cannot edit the facts, but acts on permissions via [20.01.03](./20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md) and exercises erasure via CX-M18. Never sees the resolution machinery, merges, or that two of their addresses were joined — a merge is silent to the fan (CX-02). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A fan record is created by the **first observation**, not by a signup (D-03). Buying a ticket creates one;
following creates one; an import creates one. Every subsequent observation attaches to the existing record
if it resolves, or creates a new one if it does not — and resolution is deliberately conservative (D-02),
because a false merge exposes one human's purchase history to a record they do not control.

**Resolution keys, strongest first**: platform account ID (exact, when the fan is logged in) → verified
email (platform-witnessed a confirmed click) → payment-instrument fingerprint → asserted email from import.
Name and city are **not** resolution keys — "Sarah Jones, London" is not an identity. Auto-merge fires only
on the top two keys; the lower two produce a *suggestion* an owner must confirm.

**Write-then-resolve (D-05)**: an observation is persisted immutably first, then resolved. Resolution never
sits synchronously on the write path — the highest-throughput event on the platform (a door scan, CX to
domain 19) must not fail closed because the resolver is slow. An unresolved observation is queued, visible as
a pending-identity record, and re-resolved when the resolver returns. Losing an attendance signal is worse
than briefly holding an unresolved one.

**Merge permission holds the union of identities and the intersection of permissions** (CX-02) — never the
union of permissions. Merging a transactional-only ticket buyer with a marketing-consented signup yields a
record contactable only where *both* bases agree, i.e. transactional, until a fresh marketing consent exists.

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys a ticket to a show as a logged-in WeJammin user
2. System writes a ticketing observation and resolves it to identity = platform account; creates a fan record under the artist entity seeded with first-seen = purchase timestamp, source = `ticketing`, consent basis = transactional only
3. Fan later signs up to the artist's list at the merch table using the same email
4. System writes the signup observation, resolves it to the existing record on verified-email match, and attaches a marketing-consent event — the record now carries two bases; contactability widens to marketing, governed entirely by [20.01.02](./20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md)
5. Artist opens the record and sees **one person, two touchpoints, one contactable state**, with the provenance of each observation shown on the timeline

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two records, same human, different emails (work + personal) | Not auto-merged — email is the resolution key and they differ. Surfaced as a *merge suggestion* on a weaker signal (shared payment fingerprint or shared account). | Artist sees "possible duplicate" with the evidence; merge is a deliberate act, never automatic on weak signals. |
| Shared email (family/band account) merges two humans | Wrong merge — both purchase histories now sit on one record. | Artist can **split**; split restores pre-merge consent states (CX-02). The split path ships *before* the merge path — never expose merge without its inverse. |
| **Fan deletes their WeJammin account** | Account closure ≠ Art.17 erasure (D-04). Closure erases the platform-account identity key and every observation whose *sole* provenance is that account (in-app follows, on-platform browsing). Observations with an independent lawful basis are handled by provenance: (a) transactional observations (ticket, merch) are de-identified and retained for the statutory financial-retention window (tax/accounting) — the sale survives as an anonymized line, the artist can no longer contact the person; (b) an email the artist independently imported (20.01.04) under the artist's own asserted basis is **de-linked** from the deleted account and reverts to a standalone unverified imported record with its own consent/suppression state — the fan's account closure does not reach data the fan never gave the platform. A full erasure that *does* reach imported copies is a separate CX-M18 DSAR flow, whose ultimate reach over artist-held copies is Q-03 (values/legal, `/create-prd-security`). | **Fan sees**: confirmation the account and account-linked activity are removed; a note that transactional records are retained de-identified as legally required, and that lists an artist independently holds are governed separately and erasable via a data-erasure request, not account closure. **Artist sees**: the record collapses to its remaining provenance — if the only provenance was the account, the record leaves the fan list; if the artist imported the address, the record remains as an unverified imported record minus the account-derived enrichment (attendance timeline, follows). |
| Anonymous/guest purchase, no account | Record created keyed on payment-instrument fingerprint (+ payment email if provided). Weaker; resolves later if that human ever logs in with the same address/instrument. | Artist sees the record marked **unverified-identity**; marketing disabled until a stronger key confirms. |
| Guest checkout with processor-hidden email (Apple Pay Hide-My-Email at purchase) | No usable email returned. Record created on payment-instrument fingerprint only; contactable = false, marketable = false. | Artist sees an anonymous attendee record — a headcount and a scan, not a contact. |
| Import asserts an email that already exists with stronger, platform-witnessed provenance | Import must not downgrade. Existing record wins; import is a no-op on that address (R-02 / CX-01). | "N records already known — not modified". |
| Import row with name + city only, no email | Rejected at import — name/city is not a resolution key. No record created. | "N rows unresolvable — no contactable identifier". |
| Fan uses Apple Private Relay / per-service alias | Resolution silently fails on email; the same human accretes as several thin records unless a stronger key (account, payment fingerprint) later joins them. | Nothing — which is the problem (DT-03). Under-scored superfan until a strong key resolves. |
| **Concurrency** — two delegated staff on the same artist click **merge** on the same suggested pair | The pair's state at review time is re-checked at the moment of commit: the first commit wins, the second is rejected because the pair is no longer in a mergeable state (it was already unified by the first). Never a double-merge. | Second staffer sees "this suggestion was already actioned — re-review", not a double-merge. |
| **Race** — a ticket-scan observation arrives for record A while A is mid-merge into B | The observation is written immutably (D-05) and queued against the in-flight merge transaction; after the merge commits it re-resolves onto the surviving record B. Never attaches to the soon-to-be-tombstoned record. | Artist sees the scan on the merged record; no lost signal, no orphan. |
| **Race** — a **split (unmerge)** runs while a campaign send targets the merged record | Send is blocked and re-evaluated against the post-split records before dispatch (CX-02 §5, CX-03 §2). Mail with ambiguous recipient identity never leaves. | Send shows "paused — recipient identity changed"; resumes against the corrected records. |
| **Permission** — staffer with `fan:view` but not `fan:merge` attempts a merge | Denied before any mutation. Merge/split require the delegated `fan:merge` scope (CX-M09); viewing never confers it. | "You can view this list but not merge records — ask an owner for merge rights." |
| **Permission** — Operator opens an artist's record for a human scanned at the Operator's venue | Denied — per-entity isolation (D-01). The venue holds its *own* record for that human; it may not read the artist's. | The venue's own record for that human, only. (Q-02 governs whether both entities may legitimately hold a record from one scan — resolved yes, isolated.) |
| **Network failure** — resolver unreachable when a new observation is written | Observation persisted as **unresolved/queued** (D-05); never dropped, never force-created as a duplicate to paper over the timeout. Resolved asynchronously when the resolver returns. | New records that day may show a transient pending-identity badge; merge/split disabled with an explicit "resolution unavailable", not a silent no-op. |
| **Malicious input** — attacker probes a public follow/signup endpoint with crafted emails to learn whether a given address is already a fan (enumeration oracle) | Blocked (D-06). Resolution state is never exposed to an unauthenticated caller; signup is idempotent and **response-invariant** — identical response whether the address resolved to an existing record or created a new one. Rate-limited per IP + per address (unauthenticated: stricter, per `security-first`). | Fan sees a normal "check your inbox to confirm" regardless; an attacker learns nothing. |
| **Malicious input** — import CSV with homoglyph/confusable email crafted to false-merge onto a high-value fan | Imported emails are normalized and screened for visually-confusable / mixed-script look-alikes before resolution; more importantly, import can never *auto-merge* (D-02) — it only proposes, and the account/payment keys it would need to auto-merge are not supplyable via CSV. The attack degrades to a suggestion an owner must manually confirm. | Owner sees a suggestion flagged "addresses visually similar — verify"; no silent merge. |
| **Malicious input** — fan-supplied or import-supplied strings rendered in the record UI | All fan/import-supplied fields escaped on output; no field is rendered as raw HTML (`security-first`). Stored, never executed. | Names/notes render as inert text. |
| **Partial data** — record exists but every resolved key is unverified (all guest/import) | Record renders; marketing send is hard-disabled at the record level until a stronger key verifies. | Explicit unverified badge, reduced contactability, "verify to enable marketing" affordance where applicable. |
| **Multi-hyphenate in two roles** — the human is a **Fan of artist A** and is themselves a **Musician (artist B)** on WeJammin | Their fan record under A shows them as a fan of A only. It must **not** reveal that they are artist B or expose any of B's data. Conversely B's own fan list never contains B's fan-of-A relationship. One human, two isolated contexts (D-01). | Artist A sees an ordinary fan. Artist B, viewing their own fan list, sees no trace of their private fandom of A. |
| **Multi-hyphenate across owning entities** — a human who is a **Musician** *and* an **Operator** (owns a venue) observes the same fan through both an artist show and a venue booking | Two owning entities → two isolated records (D-01 + D-03), never auto-joined even for the same human. The fan appears on the artist list and the venue list independently. | Each entity sees only its own record; no cross-entity join surface (Q-02). |
| Self-merge (record merged with itself) or merge of two records already unified | Rejected before any mutation — the merge is a no-op and changes nothing. | "These are already the same record." |
| Merge where one side is suppressed/unsubscribed | Suppression is terminal and wins the intersection (CX-02, CX-06); the merged record inherits the suppressed state on that channel. Merge can never resurrect a suppressed address. | Merged record shows the channel as unsubscribed; owner cannot re-enable. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fan record opened | Skeleton of the timeline; identity header resolves first |
| Empty | Artist has no fans yet | Empty state with the three ways fans arrive: share your store, import a list, play a show |
| Populated | Record has ≥1 resolved observation | Identity header + provenance + chronological observation timeline |
| Pending-identity (queued) | Observation written but resolver not yet run or unreachable (D-05) | Record shown with a transient "identity resolving" badge; excluded from marketable counts; merge/split disabled |
| Error | Resolution service unavailable on read | Record renders from last-known state; merge/split disabled with an explicit "resolution unavailable" rather than a silent no-op |
| Partial | Record exists but identity unverified (guest purchase, import, hidden email) | Record shown with an explicit unverified badge and reduced contactability |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The fan record should be global (one fan, many artists) rather than per-artist | Simpler schema; matches how the fan experiences themselves | ❌ REJECTED — a global record cannot express per-artist consent, per-artist ownership on band split ([20.01.05](./20.01.05-fan-list-ownership-transfer.md)), or per-artist scoring, and it creates an inadvertent cross-artist data-sharing surface: "who else does my fan follow" becomes queryable, which is a privacy exposure nobody asked for. Per-entity, per D-01. |
| DT-02 | Identity resolution is what makes segmentation ([20.02](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence-index.md)) sharper than any standalone email tool | Domain rationale claims the platform holds ticket + merch + attendance data | ✅ CONFIRMED — and load-bearing: if resolution fails, the superfan score degrades to "opened an email", which is exactly Mailchimp. The domain's entire competitive claim rests on this feature working; that warrants building the split path before the merge path. |
| DT-03 | Email-as-identity is durable enough to resolve on | Standard industry practice | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient — Apple Private Relay, Gmail `+` aliases and per-service masked addresses are normal among exactly the young, phone-native population that constitutes a fanbase. Email-only resolution silently fragments the most valuable fans into thin records and under-scores them. Needs payment-fingerprint and account-linked keys. Flagged for `/create-prd-architecture`. |
| DT-04 | Account deletion should cascade-erase *everything* about the fan, including artist-imported copies (maximal-privacy default) | Intuitive reading of "delete my account" | ❌ REJECTED — this conflates account **closure** with an Art.17 **erasure request**. It would let any fan destroy an artist's lawfully, independently held list by closing a free account they may never have paid for, erasing data the fan never gave the platform. Account closure severs account-keyed data and de-identifies statutorily-retained transactions; a true erasure is a distinct CX-M18 DSAR (D-04). The remaining question — whether a DSAR reaches artist-imported copies — is a values/legal call escalated as Q-03. |
| DT-05 | Resolution should run synchronously on the observation write so a record is always correct the instant it is written | Correctness-first intuition | ❌ REJECTED as the default — coupling every ticket scan to a live resolver call makes the platform's highest-throughput event (door scans) fail closed when the resolver is slow, losing attendance signal that is unrepeatable. ✅ CONFIRMED alternative (D-05): write the observation immutably, resolve asynchronously, expose a pending-identity state. A briefly-unresolved record is recoverable; a dropped scan is not. |
| DT-06 | A public follow/signup endpoint is a fan-enumeration oracle | Adversarial review of unauthenticated surfaces | ✅ CONFIRMED — an attacker who can tell from the response whether an address is already a fan has a membership oracle over an artist's audience. Resolution state must never be observable unauthenticated; signup must be idempotent and response-invariant, rate-limited per address and IP (D-06). This is the backbone of the malicious-input row. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Consumes **CX-M17 Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution** — the resolution *mechanism* is platform-wide (it also resolves gear makes/models and venue entities). What is domain-owned is the *policy*: which keys are strong enough to auto-merge a human, and the asymmetric-cost default (D-02).
- Consumes **CX-M19 Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** — merges, splits, consent changes and observation writes are append-only history; required for the unmerge-restores-state guarantee (CX-02 §5) and for the write-then-resolve queue (D-05).
- Consumes **CX-M18 Privacy, Consent & Data Portability** — erasure and DSAR. The account-closure-vs-erasure distinction (D-04) is drawn here but its full reach over imported copies escalates to `/create-prd-security` (Q-03).
- Consumes **CX-M09 Delegated Authority & Roles** — the `fan:merge` scope that gates merge/split independently of `fan:view` (permission edge cases).
- Touches **19 Ticketing & Box Office** ([../../19-ticketing-box-office/](../../19-ticketing-box-office/)) — a ticket **scan** (not just a purchase) is the strongest attendance signal on the platform and the input that makes the superfan score real. The highest-value cross-domain edge in domain 20, and the reason resolution must be write-then-resolve (DT-05) rather than block a door queue.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — the artist/venue entity that owns the record, the band entity for split/transfer (20.01.05), and the Fan's own D-11 account.
- Touches **13/14 marketplaces** — a fan who buys the artist's used pedal is arguably a fan observation; whether marketplace purchases feed the fan record is Q-01.
- Feeds **20.01.02 Consent & Legal Basis** — the merge produces the intersection-of-permissions its consent model depends on (CX-02); resolution never widens contactability.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Record is per (fan, artist-entity) | See DT-01. Rejected the global-record simplification on consent, ownership and privacy grounds. |
| D-02 | Auto-merge only on strong keys (account ID, verified email); weak signals produce *suggestions* | A false merge is a privacy incident, not a data-quality issue. Asymmetric cost ⇒ conservative default. |
| D-03 | First observation creates the record; signup is not required | The fan does not "join a fan list" — they buy a ticket. Requiring an explicit join loses the majority of the audience. |
| D-04 | Account **closure** ≠ Art.17 **erasure**; closure severs account-keyed data and de-identifies statutorily-retained transactions; erasure of artist-imported copies is a separate CX-M18 DSAR | Prevents a free-account closure from destroying an artist's independently, lawfully held list, and separates a statutory retention obligation (tax) from a marketing-contact right. The residual "does a DSAR reach imported copies" is a values/legal call, not an engineering default — see Q-03. |
| D-05 | Observations are written immutably first and resolved asynchronously (write-then-resolve); never dropped or force-duplicated on resolver timeout | A lost attendance scan is unrepeatable; a briefly-unresolved record is recoverable. Resolution must not sit on the door-scan write path (DT-05). |
| D-06 | Resolution state is never exposed to unauthenticated callers; public follow/signup endpoints are idempotent and response-invariant, rate-limited per address and IP | Closes the fan-enumeration oracle (DT-06). An attacker must learn nothing from a signup response about who is already on a list. |
