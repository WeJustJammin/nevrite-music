# Feature: Unified Fan Record & Identity Resolution

> **Number**: 20.01.01
> **Parent**: [Fan Graph & Owned Audience](./20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

One durable record per fan per artist-entity, assembled from every observation the platform makes of that
human: their WeJammin account (D-11), tickets they bought, merch they ordered, streams they attended, follows
they made, and addresses the artist imported. This is the thing no competitor can assemble — Mailchimp has
the email, Bandsintown has the follow, the box office has the scan, and none of them can join. WeJammin can,
because all three events happened on it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Opens a fan's record and sees: every purchase, every ticket scanned, every show attended, membership tier, first-seen date and how they arrived (gig signup / store / follow / import). Can merge two records they believe are the same person, and split a bad merge. Cannot see the fan's activity with *other* artists — the record is per-entity (D-01). |
| Producer | None | No visibility. A Producer has no fan list; their stake in this domain is as a payee on [20.04.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md). A producer with their own audience is acting through the Musician lens. |
| Operator | Full (own venue's list) | Same record shape, scoped to the venue entity: who bought tickets to shows at this room, who is on the venue's list, who attends repeatedly. Cannot see an artist's fan record for the same human even when the observation happened at their venue — see Q-02. |
| Fan | Read-only (own record) | Sees what each artist they follow holds about them, and how it was obtained. Cannot edit the facts (they bought the ticket; that is true) but can act on the permissions via [20.01.03](./20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md) and exercise erasure via CX-M18. |

## Behavior

A fan record is created by the **first observation**, not by a signup. Buying a ticket creates one. Following
creates one. An import creates one. Every subsequent observation attaches to the existing record if it
resolves, or creates a new one if it does not — and resolution is deliberately conservative, because a false
merge exposes one human's purchase history to a record they do not control, which is a privacy incident, not
a data-quality blemish.

Resolution keys, strongest first: platform account ID (exact, when the fan is logged in) → verified email →
payment-instrument fingerprint → asserted email from import. Name and city are **not** resolution keys —
"Sarah Jones, London" is not an identity.

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys a ticket to a show as a logged-in WeJammin user
2. System creates a fan record under the artist entity, seeded with: identity = platform account, first-seen = purchase date, source = ticketing, consent basis = transactional only
3. Fan later signs up to the artist's list at the merch table using the same email
4. System resolves the new observation to the existing record on verified-email match, and attaches a marketing-consent event — record now has two bases, and contactability widens to marketing (governed by [20.01.02](./20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md))
5. Artist opens the fan's record and sees one person, two touchpoints, one contactable state, with the provenance of each

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two records, same human, different emails (work + personal) | Not auto-merged — email is the resolution key and they differ. Surfaced as a *merge suggestion* on weaker signals (same payment fingerprint, same account). | Artist sees "possible duplicate" with the evidence shown; merge is a deliberate act, never automatic on weak signals. |
| Shared email (family/band account) merges two humans | Wrong merge. Both humans' purchase histories now sit on one record. | Artist can split; split restores pre-merge consent states (see `-cx.md` CX-02). The split path must exist before the merge path ships. |
| Fan deletes their WeJammin account | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — does the artist keep the imported email they lawfully held, or does erasure take the whole record? This is Q-03 on the parent index and it is a values decision, not a technical one. | `[PENDING]` |
| Anonymous/guest purchase, no account | Record created keyed on the payment email. Weaker; resolves later if that human ever logs in with the same address. | Artist sees the record marked as unverified-identity. |
| Import asserts an email that already exists with stronger provenance | Import must not downgrade. Existing record wins; import is a no-op on that address. | "N records already known — not modified". |
| Fan uses Apple Private Relay / alias-per-service | Resolution silently fails; the same human accretes as several records forever. | Nothing — which is the problem. Flagged as DT-03. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fan record opened | Skeleton of the timeline; identity header resolves first |
| Empty | Artist has no fans yet | Empty state with the three ways fans arrive: share your store, import a list, play a show |
| Populated | Record has ≥1 observation | Identity header + provenance + chronological observation timeline |
| Error | Resolution service unavailable | Record renders from last-known state; merge/split disabled with an explicit "resolution unavailable" rather than a silent no-op |
| Partial | Record exists but identity unverified (guest purchase, import) | Record shown with an explicit unverified badge and reduced contactability |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The fan record should be global (one fan, many artists) rather than per-artist | Simpler schema; matches how the fan experiences themselves | ❌ REJECTED — a global record cannot express per-artist consent, per-artist ownership on band split ([20.01.05](./20.01.05-fan-list-ownership-transfer.md)), or per-artist scoring. It also creates an inadvertent cross-artist data-sharing surface: if the record is global, "who else does my fan follow" becomes queryable, which is a product nobody asked for and a privacy exposure. Per-entity, per D-01. |
| DT-02 | Identity resolution is the thing that makes segmentation ([20.02](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence-index.md)) sharper than any standalone email tool | Domain rationale claims the platform holds ticket + merch + attendance data | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is load-bearing: if resolution fails, the superfan score degrades to "opened an email", which is exactly Mailchimp. **The domain's entire competitive claim rests on this one feature working.** That warrants a `must` proposal and warrants building the split path before the merge path. |
| DT-03 | Email-as-identity is durable enough to resolve on | Standard industry practice | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient — Apple Private Relay, Gmail `+` aliases, and per-service masked addresses are now normal among exactly the young, phone-native population that constitutes a fanbase. A resolution strategy keyed on email will silently fragment the most valuable fans into several thin records and under-score them. Needs a payment-fingerprint and account-linked path. Flagged for `/create-prd-architecture`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Record is per (fan, artist-entity) | See DT-01. Rejected the global-record simplification on consent, ownership and privacy grounds. |
| D-02 | Auto-merge only on strong keys (account ID, verified email); weak signals produce *suggestions* | A false merge is a privacy incident, not a data-quality issue. Asymmetric cost ⇒ conservative default. |
| D-03 | First observation creates the record; signup is not required | The fan does not "join a fan list" — they buy a ticket. Requiring an explicit join loses the majority of the audience. |
