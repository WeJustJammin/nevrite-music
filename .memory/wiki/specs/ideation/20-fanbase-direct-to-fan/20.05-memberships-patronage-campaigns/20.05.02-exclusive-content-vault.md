# Feature: Exclusive Content Vault & Entitlement Gating

> **Number**: 20.05.02
> **Parent**: [Memberships, Patronage & Campaigns](./20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What members actually get: demos, unreleased takes, session video, the acoustic version, the thing from the
tour bus. The vault is the room behind the membership door — and its content is a byproduct of work the artist
is already doing, which is what makes it sustainable. **Every session on this platform generates vault
material as exhaust**: rough mixes, takes that did not make it, video from the room. Nothing else in the
artist's stack is standing next to that material when it is created.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Puts things in; sets the minimum tier per item; sees what members opened and what they ignored. Ideally posts **from the session itself** — a rough mix goes to the vault with one tap from the project (domain 07), not via an export/upload/describe ritual at midnight. |
| Producer | None | No visibility on the vault. **But see DT-03** — the producer is often the person holding the material the vault wants, and they are not modelled as a contributor to it. That may be a genuine gap. |
| Operator | None | No vault. Tied to index Q-04. |
| Fan | Read-only (own entitlements) | Sees everything in the vault; opens what their tier permits; sees locked items **as locked, with the tier that opens them** — a locked door converts, a hidden one does nothing. |

## Behavior

Each item declares a minimum tier. Entitlement checking is **CX-M24**; media handling and playback are
**CX-M10**. What is domain-owned is the vault as a *place* — an accumulating body of work-adjacent material
with an access ladder over it.

The interesting design question is not access control. It is **where the content comes from**. Asking a
touring musician to produce exclusive content monthly is asking them to become a content creator, which is a
different job that most of them do not want and will quietly stop doing by month four (the churn cause in
[20.05.01](./20.05.01-membership-tiers-benefits.md) DT-02). The consolidation thesis (D-18) offers a way out:
the platform is *already in the room where the material exists*. A rough mix in a project (domain 07) is one
tap from the vault. That is not a convenience — it is the difference between a vault that stays alive and one
that dies in the spring.

### Happy Path

1. Producer bounces a rough mix in a session (domain 07)
2. Musician taps "send to vault", picks the £3+ tier, adds one line: *drums went down today, listen to the fills*
3. Entitled members notified (CX-M07)
4. Fan opens it, plays it in the browser (CX-M10) — a thing that did not exist publicly and never will
5. The fan stays a member for another year because of a 20-second act by the artist

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Member lapses — do they keep what they had?** | The genuinely hard one (`-cx.md` CX-01 Q2). Patreon: no. Fan intuition: yes, obviously — they paid for that month. A fan who loses a year of demos feels robbed and says so publicly. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-02. |
| Artist stops posting | The membership dies (`20.05.01` DT-02). The vault is where the death is visible first. | Artist nudged; fan sees the last-updated date, which is brutally honest and probably right. |
| Vault item leaks (a member rips and posts it) | Inevitable. Any content a fan can play, a fan can capture. DRM is hostile and defeated in an afternoon. | See DT-01. |
| Uncleared sample in a vault demo | A rough mix is *more* likely to contain uncleared material than a release, not less — that is what rough means. Musician anti-persona: *"uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream."* | `[PENDING]` — 24 / domain 09. |
| Item's minimum tier raised after members accessed it | Retroactive removal. Feels like theft. | Existing access should hold. |
| Session material posted without collaborator consent | The producer's rough mix, the session drummer's isolated take — posted by the artist. **The people on the recording did not agree to their unfinished work being published to 300 strangers.** Real, common, and unmodelled. See DT-03. | `[PENDING]` |
| Large video from a phone at a rehearsal | CX-M10. Musicians shoot vertical video on bad wifi in a basement; the upload path must survive that. | Progressive upload + retry. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Vault opened | Item skeleton |
| Empty | Nothing in it | Fan: "nothing here yet" — a bad first impression that must never precede a payment; do not let a tier go live with an empty vault. Artist: what they could post, drawn from their actual sessions. |
| Populated | ≥1 item | Chronological feed, locked/unlocked, minimum tier per item |
| Error | Media unavailable | Item listed, playback retries. Never make an entitlement look revoked because a CDN blipped. |
| Partial | Fan's tier opens some items | Locked items visible with the tier that opens them — the conversion surface |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The vault needs DRM / leak protection to be worth paying for | The instinct behind every "exclusive content" product | ❌ REJECTED — DRM on a demo is hostile to the exact fans paying you, and it is defeated by a phone pointed at a screen. More importantly it misreads the transaction: **a fan is not paying for access to a file, they are paying to be close to the artist.** The leak is not the failure — the leak is marketing, and a demo circulating with the artist's name on it costs nothing and reaches people who might join. Watermarking per-member is the most that could ever be justified, and even that treats members as suspects. This is the same reframing as [20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md) DT-03: patronage wearing a purchase's clothes. |
| DT-02 | Lapsed members should lose everything — that is what makes membership valuable | Patreon's model; the standard answer | ⏳ **Genuinely unresolved and I lean against the standard answer.** "You paid for twelve months and now have nothing" is how a fan learns memberships are a trick, and they take that lesson to every other artist on the platform — which makes it a **platform** problem, not an artist's pricing choice. The alternative (keep what was released while you paid; lose future items) matches the fan's intuition, matches how buying anything else works, and makes rejoining psychologically easy rather than a fresh loss. The counter: it weakens the reason to stay subscribed continuously. My read is that continuous-membership pressure is worth less than fan trust, but I have no evidence, and it is a product decision. Q-01. |
| DT-03 | The vault's content problem is solved by making it easy to post | Follows from the consolidation thesis — the material is already here | ⏳ **Half right, and the other half is a real gap.** Easy posting from a session (domain 07) is the sustainability answer and it is good. But it walks straight into a consent problem nobody listed: **the material is not the artist's alone.** A rough mix has the producer's work on it; an isolated take is the session drummer's unfinished performance, published to 300 strangers without them being asked. `meta/personas.md` names the Producer as the trust broker whose attestation matters — and here they are, `None` on a feature that publishes their room's work. The platform that captures splits at the session should presumably capture *publishing consent* at the session too, in the same breath, while everyone is friendly. **That is the same pattern as [20.01.05](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.05-fan-list-ownership-transfer.md) D-02 and domain 09's whole approach.** Not confident about the mechanism; confident it is a gap. Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No DRM | See DT-01. Hostile to paying fans, trivially defeated, and it misreads what the fan is buying. |
| D-02 | Locked items are visible with their tier | The conversion surface. A hidden benefit is not a benefit. |
| D-03 | Content flows from sessions, not from a content calendar | See DT-03. The consolidation thesis's most practical payout — and the only version of this that survives month four. |
| D-04 | Do not let a paid tier go live with an empty vault | A fan's first post-payment experience being an empty room is unrecoverable. |
| D-05 | A raised minimum tier does not retroactively remove access | Taking something back from someone who had it is the most expensive small act available. |
