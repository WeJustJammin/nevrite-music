# Feature: Backer Funds, Escrow & Reward Fulfilment

> **Number**: 20.05.05
> **Parent**: [Memberships, Patronage & Campaigns](./20.05-memberships-patronage-campaigns-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Where fan money lives between the pledge and the record, and what has to be true before the artist can touch
it. **PledgeMusic collapsed in 2019 owing artists millions because it commingled fan money with operating
funds** — the money fans paid for records was spent running the company, and when the company died, both the
artists and the fans lost. Segregation is the response, and it has to be structural rather than a promise,
because a promise is exactly what PledgeMusic also made.

> **Deep Think addition** — the sweep had "(segregated funds)" as a parenthetical inside the crowdfunding
> candidate. It is not a qualifier on a feature; it is the feature. Promoted to its own node because a policy
> buried inside the thing it constrains is a policy that gets negotiated away when the constrained thing is
> under pressure.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Read-only** | Sees how much is held, what releases each tranche, and what they may draw today. **Cannot release their own funds** (index D-02). Will find this frustrating, and the frustration is the feature — it is what stands between an honest artist under cashflow pressure and becoming the next cautionary tale. |
| Producer | None | No visibility — though they are frequently the invoice the money is meant to pay. See Q-03. |
| Operator | None | Not applicable. |
| Fan | Read-only (own backing) | Sees their money is held, not spent; sees the fulfilment milestone the campaign is on; sees the refund condition. **This visibility is what makes backing credible** — it is the difference between "trust me" and "look". |

## Behavior

Backer money is **segregated on arrival** and is not the artist's until a milestone releases it. Milestones are
tranches, not a single gate: masters delivered → release the pressing cost; plant confirms → release
fulfilment; shipped → release the remainder.

The tension is real and must not be papered over (`-cx.md` CX-02): **the artist needs the money to make the
thing.** A policy that holds everything until delivery makes crowdfunding useless — you cannot press a record
with money you cannot spend. The design is not "hold it all"; it is "release it against evidence that the
thing is progressing".

Which surfaces the unsolved problem (index Q-02): **who verifies a milestone?** Self-attested is gameable.
Carrier-confirmed works for shipping only. And nothing on earth verifies "I made the record" — masters can be
delivered and be bad, or be delivered and never pressed.

Two hard questions I cannot resolve here:
- **Does the platform hold the money itself?** That is likely a regulated activity (money transmission / e-money). `meta/constraints.md` flags KYC/AML `[PENDING]`; with [20.04.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.04-d2f-revenue-split-payout.md) Q-05 this may be the largest regulatory consequence in the domain. Index Q-01.
- **What happens on platform insolvency?** If WeJammin dies, segregated funds must be *legally* the backers' — not an asset of the estate. That is the actual lesson of PledgeMusic and it is a corporate-structure question, not a schema one.

### Happy Path

1. Campaign funds: £14,200 from 400 backers, segregated on arrival
2. Artist sees £14,200 held, £0 available, and the first milestone: *deliver masters*
3. Masters delivered → tranche 1 releases £6,000 → pressing paid
4. Plant confirms shipment to fulfilment → tranche 2 releases £5,000
5. Records ship (CX-M14, carrier-confirmed) → remainder releases
6. Backers got records; the artist got paid at each real step; **nobody had to trust anybody**

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Fund segregation is a compliance detail — an implementation note on crowdfunding | Why the sweep left it as a parenthetical | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the product.** A crowdfunding campaign is a promise, and the promise is worth exactly as much as the mechanism behind it. PledgeMusic had campaigns, tiers, updates and a lovely UI; it lacked segregation, and everything else it built became worthless in a week. It also has real product surface — what the artist may draw and when, what the fan can see, what evidence releases money. Burying it inside [20.05.04](./20.05.04-crowdfunding-preorder-campaigns.md) would put the constraint inside the thing it constrains, which is how constraints get negotiated away under pressure. Its own node, its own policy, its own owner. |
| DT-02 | Holding all funds until delivery is the safe design | The intuitive fix for PledgeMusic | ❌ REJECTED — it makes crowdfunding **pointless**. The artist raises money *to make the thing*; if they cannot spend it until the thing exists, they needed the money they did not have, and the campaign was theatre. `-cx.md` CX-02 is right that the campaign and the funds node must be allowed to disagree — but the funds node cannot simply win, or the feature dies. The design is tranches against evidence, and the first tranche is the hard one (see the edge cases): the artist genuinely needs studio money *before* there are masters, and that is precisely the moment the policy has nothing to verify. **I do not have a good answer** and I am not going to invent one. |
| DT-03 | Segregation protects fans from artists | The obvious framing — fans paid, artists might not deliver | ⏳ **Half right, and the more important half is the reverse.** Segregation also protects **artists from the platform**: PledgeMusic's artists lost money because *the company* spent it, not because artists absconded. If WeJammin holds fan money in its own accounts and dies, artists and fans both lose exactly as before, and every word in this file will have been decoration. **The protection that matters most is legal and structural — funds that are not ours in insolvency** — and that is a corporate-structure decision above the pipeline's pay grade, made once, early, and cheaply, or not at all. This is the single most important thing in this file and it is not a software feature. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Backer funds are segregated on arrival and artist-inaccessible until a milestone | Index D-02. The anti-PledgeMusic structure. |
| D-02 | Tranches against evidence, not a single gate at delivery | See DT-02. Hold-everything makes the feature pointless; the artist needs the money to make the thing. |
| D-03 | Fans see that their money is held | The visibility *is* the credibility. "Look" beats "trust me", and it is the only honest answer to PledgeMusic's ghost. |
| D-04 | Refunds on artist disappearance are automatic | A refund requiring the artist to act will not happen; the artist is gone. That is the scenario. |
| D-05 | Artist and backer see the same ledger | Divergent views of the same money is how disputes start and how bad faith hides. |
