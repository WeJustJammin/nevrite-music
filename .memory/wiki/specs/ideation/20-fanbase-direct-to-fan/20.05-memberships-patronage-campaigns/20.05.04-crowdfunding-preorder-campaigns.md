# Feature: Crowdfunding & Pre-order Campaigns

> **Number**: 20.05.04

> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Fans paying for a record before it exists so that it can exist. A goal, a deadline, a ladder of reward tiers,
and a promise — which is the entire product, because everything the fan receives is in the future. This is how
independent vinyl gets made, and it is also the mechanic that killed PledgeMusic in 2019, taking millions of
artists' money with it. The campaign is where the promise is made; [20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)
is what makes it credible.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets a goal, a deadline, reward tiers; runs the campaign; posts updates. Sees backers as fan records, pledged totals, and — the number they will not like — **how much of the goal is actually theirs to spend and when** ([20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)). |
| Producer | Reports (as split payee) | If the campaign funds a work they hold a split in, they are a payee. Also frequently the person whose invoice the campaign exists to pay — the money is often literally for the studio time. |
| Operator | None | Venues do not crowdfund through this. |
| Fan | Read-only (backs, tracks) | Backs at a tier; watches progress; receives updates; gets the thing, or their money back. Sees the fulfilment state honestly — including "the pressing plant has slipped by three months", which is the update that never gets sent and whose absence destroys everything. |

## Behavior

A campaign takes money now against delivery later. The gap between those two is where every crowdfunding
disaster lives, and the gap in music is unusually long and unusually unpredictable: vinyl lead times ran to
9+ months post-2020 and remain volatile, so an artist promising a record "in the spring" is making a promise
about someone else's factory.

The **campaign** optimises for raising; **[20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)** optimises for
keeping the promise. They are two nodes because they must be allowed to disagree (`-cx.md` CX-02) — a
campaign that owned its own money would always find a reason to release it.

Reward tiers look exactly like membership tiers and are a different object (`-cx.md` CX-04): a reward is a
one-off promise with a delivery obligation; a membership is a recurring entitlement with no delivery date.

**D-03 on the index applies here in reverse**: a vinyl pre-order in
[20.04.03](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04.03-digital-sales-name-your-price-bundles.md) is this
feature wearing a Buy button. Same money-now-object-later hazard, therefore the same fund policy.

### Happy Path

1. Musician needs £12,000 to press a record; opens a campaign with a 30-day deadline
2. Reward tiers: £15 digital, £30 vinyl + digital, £75 signed + test pressing, £250 a house show
3. Announces to their fan list ([20.03.01](../20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging/20.03.01-campaign-composer.md)) — **the reason a campaign works here and not on Kickstarter: the audience is already ours and already known**
4. 400 backers, £14,200. Funds held ([20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md))
5. Milestone: masters delivered → tranche released → pressing paid
6. Milestone: plant confirms shipment → tranche released → fulfilment via CX-M14
7. Backers get records. The campaign closes. Nobody lost anything

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Campaign funds; artist spends it on rent; record never made** | The PledgeMusic failure, at individual scale. Prevented structurally by [20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md) — the artist cannot reach the money — or not prevented at all. | Artist: "£8,400 held, releases when the masters land." |
| Pressing plant slips 4 months | Normal, not exceptional. Post-2020 vinyl lead times made this the default. The failure is not the slip — **it is the silence**. | Update prompted and expected. Slipping is forgivable; going quiet is not. |
| Campaign misses the goal | All-or-nothing (refund all) vs keep-what-you-raise (£8k cannot press a record but the fans still paid). Both are defensible; only one is honest about a half-funded pressing. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Q-01. |
| Artist dies / band splits mid-campaign | Not hypothetical. Funds held; refund path; someone must decide. Overlaps [20.01.05](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.05-fan-list-ownership-transfer.md). | `[PENDING]` |
| Costs rise after funding (plant raises prices, freight spikes) | Common. The £12,000 raised no longer presses the record. The artist is contractually stuck between fans and a factory. | `[PENDING]` — no good answer exists; the platform should at least surface it early. |
| Backer's card fails at collection | Depends on pledge-now-charge-later vs charge-now. Charge-later means the goal was partly fiction. | `[PENDING]` |
| Campaign cannibalises the membership | Index Q-03 — a fan with £10/month may not also back £50. The platform can see both sides; the artist cannot. | `[PENDING]` |
| Stretch goals | Every stretch goal is a new promise made while the artist is excited and the money is arriving. The mechanism most reliably converts a fundable campaign into an unfulfillable one. | `[PENDING]` — see DT-03. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This competes with Kickstarter/Indiegogo and must match their features | The obvious competitive read | ❌ REJECTED — it does not compete with them, because **the audience is the product**. A Kickstarter campaign's hardest problem is finding backers; it has no list, so artists spend the campaign begging on social media. Here the backers are already known, already segmented, already scored ([20.02](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence-index.md)), and reachable in one act. The campaign is downstream of the fan graph, which is the one thing a generic platform can never have. **Feature parity with Kickstarter is irrelevant; a superfan segment is the whole advantage.** |
| DT-02 | The risk is fraudulent artists taking the money | The intuitive read of PledgeMusic | ❌ REJECTED — the common failure is **an honest artist who under-costed, got slipped by a plant, and spent the money on the thing they needed today.** No malice, same outcome: the record never ships and 400 fans are out £30. This matters because it changes the fix entirely. Fraud is solved by vetting; optimism and cashflow pressure are solved by **not letting the money be reachable** ([20.05.05](./20.05.05-backer-funds-fulfilment.md)). It is the same insight as `meta/personas.md` on the Producer — *"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"* — because the failure is committed by the person best placed to prevent it, and they do not think they are doing anything wrong. |
| DT-03 | Stretch goals increase revenue and are a straightforward win | Standard crowdfunding practice, present on every platform | ❌ REJECTED — a stretch goal is a **new promise made at the worst possible moment**: the artist is euphoric, money is arriving, and they have no idea what the plant will charge in four months. It is the mechanism that most reliably converts a fundable campaign into an unfulfillable one, because it adds obligations without adding schedule. Every music crowdfunding horror story has a stretch goal in it. If offered at all, a stretch goal must be **costed before it is offered**, not celebrated. Nothing in the candidate list touched this. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The campaign cannot move its own money | `-cx.md` CX-02. Separation of raising from releasing is the whole anti-PledgeMusic structure. |
| D-02 | Design for the honest, optimistic, cash-pressed artist | See DT-02. Not for the fraudster. The common failure has no villain, which is exactly why policy must be structural rather than moral. |
| D-03 | Updates are prompted and expected, especially bad ones | Slipping is forgivable; silence is not. Silence is what killed trust in music crowdfunding, and it is free to prevent. |
| D-04 | Stretch goals, if offered, must be costed before being offered | See DT-03. The euphoria mechanism that breaks fulfilment. |
| D-05 | The advantage is the fan graph, not feature parity | See DT-01. Do not build Kickstarter. Build the thing Kickstarter cannot: a campaign that opens with 400 known superfans already listening. |
