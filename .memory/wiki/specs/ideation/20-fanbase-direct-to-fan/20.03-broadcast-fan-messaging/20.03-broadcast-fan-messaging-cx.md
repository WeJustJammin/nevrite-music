## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Composer ↔ Channel Routing

**Relationship**: The composer must know the channel or it will produce content that cannot be delivered.
The tempting design — write once, auto-adapt to email/SMS/push — is where most multi-channel tools become
useless: the SMS is a mangled truncation, the push is the subject line, and the artist stops trusting the
tool. The honest design is channel-aware composition with **shared intent**, not shared body.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: writes the announcement once, then sees and edits what each channel will actually say. Not three unrelated drafts, not one auto-mangled one.
- **Operator**: same, for programme announcements.
- **Fan**: receives one coherent message on the channel they chose, not the same message three times — which is the failure everyone has experienced.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The campaign owns the intent (what is being announced, the link, the audience). Each channel owns its rendering. Edits to a channel rendering do not propagate back to intent.
2. **Trigger chain**: Compose → per-channel render → per-fan channel resolution ([20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md) consent decides which channels are permitted) → dispatch. If one channel fails, others must still go — a broken SMS sender must not block the email.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the important one: a fan consented to email but not SMS receives only email, regardless of what the artist composed. Consent is per-channel, so the composer's channel set is a *proposal*, and per-fan resolution is authoritative.
4. **Notification fan-out**: This *is* the fan-out. The deduplication rule matters — a fan consented on all three channels must not receive the same announcement three times. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: channel priority per fan, or artist-set primary channel per campaign?
5. **State transition conflict**: Editing a campaign mid-send. Must be impossible — a campaign in flight is immutable, or half the audience gets a different message and the send log means nothing.

### CX-02: Deliverability ↔ Scheduling & Throttling

**Relationship**: Reputation state sets the maximum rate. A fresh sender, or one that just imported 4,000
unwitnessed addresses ([20.01.04](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.04-fan-list-import-hygiene.md)), is
throttled to a warm-up ramp. An artist whose complaint rate crosses a threshold is throttled further or
stopped. This is the mechanism by which index D-02 ("reputation is a platform asset") stops being a slogan.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees "your 4,000-person announcement will take 6 hours to go out, because this list is new" — and cannot override it. Will hate this. Must be told **why**, in terms of their own interest (landing in inboxes) rather than the platform's.
- **Operator**: same.
- **Fan**: unaware. Benefits — throttled sends are the ones that arrive.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Reputation is owned by `20.03.04`, read-only to scheduling. One writer.
2. **Trigger chain**: Reputation change → throttle recalculated → in-flight sends **re-rated mid-flight**, not just future ones. If complaints spike at 10% of the send, the remaining 90% must slow or stop immediately — that is the entire value of the circuit breaker.
3. **Permission intersection**: No role, including admin, may lift a throttle. See Q-01 — I am not fully confident admin should be excluded, but a bypassable circuit breaker will be bypassed under commercial pressure.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The artist is told when throttled and when stopped. Silent throttling makes the platform look broken and generates support load that teaches artists to distrust it.
5. **State transition conflict**: A reputation collapse during a send must halt it. Mid-flight halt leaves a partially-sent campaign — which is correct and must be legible in the send log, not hidden.

### CX-03: Channel Routing ↔ Deliverability

**Relationship**: Every dispatch produces telemetry — delivered, bounced (hard/soft), complained, opened,
clicked, unsubscribed, blocked. That telemetry is the sole input to reputation. Hard bounces and complaints
also write suppression back to
[20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md), permanently.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Operator**: sees per-campaign delivery outcomes; sees list health degrade if they behave badly.
- **Fan**: a complaint (hitting "spam") is a consent revocation by another name, and must be honoured as one.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Routing emits; `20.03.04` aggregates. Suppression writes go to the consent ledger, which is authoritative — `20.03.04` never holds its own copy.
2. **Trigger chain**: Bounce/complaint → suppress address → reputation recompute → throttle change (CX-02). Async, but the suppression write must be synchronous-enough that a hard-bounced address is not retried in the same campaign.
3. **Permission intersection**: A complaint from a fan overrides everything an artist believes about their consent. Terminal.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Aggregate only to the artist. A per-complaint feed makes artists resent individual fans, and there is nothing useful they can do with it.
5. **State transition conflict**: Telemetry arrives asynchronously from providers, sometimes hours late. Reputation is eventually consistent; throttling must therefore be conservative, because acting late on a complaint spike means the damage is already shared out.

### CX-04: Composer ↔ Deliverability

**Relationship**: Content decisions are deliverability decisions. An image-only email, a shortened link, a
subject line that reads like a promotion, a missing List-Unsubscribe header — each raises spam placement for
the sender, and on shared infrastructure the sender is everyone.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: gets pre-send feedback on content risk. Advisory, mostly — but structural requirements (unsubscribe link, List-Unsubscribe header, plain-text alternative) are not optional and are injected rather than requested.
- **Operator**: same.
- **Fan**: benefits invisibly.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — one direction, advisory plus injection.
2. **Trigger chain**: Compose → risk check → warn or block. Blocking should be reserved for structural violations, not stylistic ones; a platform that rejects an artist's subject line for tone is a platform artists leave.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Pre-send warnings to the composer only.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-05: Scheduling ↔ Channel Routing

**Relationship**: "9am local" requires knowing the fan's local. The platform knows a stated city (often),
a purchase geo (sometimes), a phone country code (for SMS). These disagree. For email a mistake is invisible;
for **SMS a mistake is a 3am buzz**, which is a complaint, a TCPA exposure, and a fan lost.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets "9am local" once and assumes it works.
- **Fan**: the person woken at 3am.
- **Operator**: lower risk — a venue's audience is geographically concentrated by definition.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Fan location is owned by [20.01.01](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.01-unified-fan-record.md) and is frequently absent or stale.
2. **Trigger chain**: Schedule → resolve per-fan timezone → bucket → dispatch. On unresolvable timezone, fall back to the artist's timezone for email; for SMS, **hold to a globally-safe window** rather than guess. Asymmetric cost justifies asymmetric caution.
3. **Permission intersection**: Quiet-hours enforcement for SMS is statutory in some jurisdictions, not a preference.
4. **Notification fan-out**: N/A.
5. **State transition conflict**: A fan whose timezone changes mid-schedule (they are on tour, or travelling) may be double- or zero-bucketed. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.
