# Feature: Campaign Composer & Templates

> **Number**: 20.03.01
> **Parent**: [Broadcast & Fan Messaging](./20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Where an artist writes the thing their fans will read: an announcement, a tour, a release, a thank-you. It is
channel-aware by design — the email, the SMS and the push are three messages that share one intent, not one
message rendered three ways (`-cx.md` CX-01). Its real advantage is that the platform already knows what is
being announced: the show exists in domain 17, the release in 12, the product in
[20.04](../20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront/20.04-direct-to-fan-storefront-index.md) — so the composer can
assemble most of the message rather than asking a musician to write marketing copy at midnight.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Picks what they are announcing (a show, a release, a product — pulled from the platform, not retyped), picks an audience ([20.02.01](../20.02-segmentation-superfan-intelligence/20.02.01-segment-builder.md)), sees each channel's version, edits any of them, previews, sends a test to themselves, sends. Sees the honest number before sending: *this reaches 1,203 of your 1,847 Manchester fans*. |
| Producer | None | No visibility — no audience to address. |
| Operator | Full | Same, for programme announcements: this month's shows, a room reopening, a residency. A venue's message is nearly always "here is what's on", which is a strong argument for a calendar-driven template rather than a blank page. |
| Fan | None | Never sees the composer. Receives its output — and receives it once, on their chosen channel, not three times. |

## Behavior

The composer's centre of gravity is **the object being announced**, not the blank page. A musician announcing
a tour should pick the shows and get a message with dates, cities, venue names, on-sale times and ticket links
already assembled and correct — because the platform holds all of that in domain 17. Requiring them to retype
it is both a worse product and an error source (wrong door time in a mailout is a real and costly mistake).

Structural email requirements are **injected, not requested**: unsubscribe link, List-Unsubscribe header,
plain-text alternative, physical sender address where required. An artist cannot forget them, because they are
never asked to remember them.

### Happy Path

1. Musician taps "tell my fans" from a tour they just announced in domain 17
2. Composer opens pre-filled: subject, the six dates with cities and venues, on-sale times per timezone, links
3. Musician picks the audience: "everyone within 40 miles of a date" — a segment the platform can build and no email tool could
4. Sees per-channel versions: full email; SMS reading *Tour dates are up. Manchester 12 Mar. Tickets: [link]*; push as a title
5. Sends a test to themselves; it looks right
6. Sends. Sees: 4,102 targeted, 3,340 contactable, throttled over 90 minutes because the SMS leg is new ([20.03.03](./20.03.03-send-scheduling-throttling.md))

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Artist edits a campaign mid-send | Blocked. In-flight campaigns are immutable (`-cx.md` CX-01 Q5) — otherwise half the audience gets a different message and the send log is fiction. | "This one's already going out. You can stop it, or send a follow-up." |
| Announced show is cancelled after the campaign sent | The message is gone; it cannot be recalled. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — does the platform prompt a correction send to the same audience? It should; that is the one follow-up nobody regrets. | `[PENDING]` |
| Artist writes an SMS at newsletter length | Segments into multiple billable SMS. Must show the cost and the segment count **before** send, not on the invoice. | "This is 4 SMS per fan — £X across 3,340 fans." |
| Image-only email | Warned (`-cx.md` CX-04). Not blocked — a poster *is* the announcement for many acts, and blocking that is culturally wrong. | "Add some text — image-only emails often land in spam." |
| Missing unsubscribe | Cannot happen. Injected. | Nothing. |
| Artist sends to "everyone" every time | Not blocked here. The lever is pricing (index Q-04) and reputation ([20.03.04](./20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)), not a nag in the composer. | Contactable count + unsubscribe trend. |
| Test send to self, from an address on the list | Must not consume/alter their own fan record state. | Test badge in the message. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Composer opened from an object | Pre-fill assembling; audience count resolving |
| Empty | New blank campaign | Not a blank page — the things they could announce: shows, releases, products, all pulled from the platform |
| Populated | Content + audience set | Per-channel previews + targeted/contactable counts + cost where SMS is involved |
| Error | Pre-fill source unavailable (17 down) | Composer still works, manual entry — never blocked by a dependency being unavailable |
| Partial | Draft saved, not sent | Draft list with the audience definition preserved (live — the count will differ when they return, which must be visible, not silent) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The composer's differentiator is a better editor | The obvious read — everyone competes on editors | ❌ REJECTED — the editor is commodity and we will lose that fight to Mailchimp forever. The differentiator is that **the platform already holds the thing being announced**. A tour email that assembles itself from the shows in domain 17 — correct dates, correct door times, correct on-sale, correct links, per-timezone — is something no standalone tool can build, because it does not have the tour. This is consolidation (D-18) paying out in the most mundane possible place, which is where it is most credible. The composer should be **object-first**, not page-first. |
| DT-02 | Compose once, auto-adapt to every channel | Standard multi-channel marketing pitch | ❌ REJECTED — auto-truncation produces a mangled SMS and a push that is just the subject line, and artists stop trusting it within two sends. Shared **intent**, separate **renderings**, each editable, is the honest model. Costs more UI; is the difference between a tool used twice and a tool used weekly. |
| DT-03 | The real risk here is artists writing bad marketing copy | Reasonable-seeming — musicians are not marketers | ❌ REJECTED — the real risk is **artists sending too often to everyone**, because that is what destroys a list, and no composer feature prevents it. Nagging in the UI is ignored. The only levers that work are economic (charge per send — index Q-04) and mechanical (reputation throttling — [20.03.04](./20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md)). Worth stating plainly so nobody builds a "send score" nag and believes the problem is handled. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Object-first, not blank-page-first | See DT-01. The composer's whole advantage is that the platform holds the announcement. |
| D-02 | Channel-aware composition: shared intent, separate renderings | See DT-02. Auto-adaptation is the failure mode that kills trust in multi-channel tools. |
| D-03 | Structural compliance elements are injected, never requested | An artist cannot forget an unsubscribe link they were never asked for. |
| D-04 | Costs (SMS segments) shown pre-send | A per-message cost discovered on an invoice is a support ticket and a trust loss. |
| D-05 | In-flight campaigns are immutable | Editing mid-send makes the send log meaningless and splits the audience's experience. |
