## Overview

The artist-to-fan send path: compose once, resolve an audience, deliver across email, SMS and push, at
volume, lawfully, and without landing in spam. This is the sub-domain that turns an owned audience from a
list into a channel — the mailing list is the thing artists have been told for a decade is the only asset
nobody can take away from them, and it is worthless if it does not arrive.

**Why this is a sub-domain (2+ interacting capabilities)**: composition, channel routing, scheduling and
deliverability are one machine. What you compose constrains which channel can carry it (160 characters is
not a newsletter); when you send changes whether it arrives; and deliverability reputation — shared across
every artist on the platform — decides whether any of it lands, which in turn throttles what scheduling may
do. They cannot be specified independently.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | Bulk artist→fan marketing is **distinct** from CX-M07 (Notifications & Alerts) | CX-M07 is the platform speaking to its users transactionally: "your order shipped", "you were invited to a session". This is an artist speaking to their audience at volume, under consent, with a marketing purpose, on shared sending infrastructure. Different consent model, different deliverability profile, different cost model, different failure modes. Shared transport, different product. Not a duplicate. | Node Classification Gate |
| D-02 | Sender reputation is a **platform asset**, not an artist's | On a shared sending domain, one artist's complaint rate degrades every other artist's inbox placement. It follows that artists cannot be trusted to manage it and must not be allowed to spend it. This makes deliverability enforcement mechanical rather than advisory. | Deep Think — see [20.03.04](./20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md) |
| D-03 | No send resolves without [20.01.02](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.02-consent-legal-basis.md) | A hard gate, not a filter, and it fails closed. Sending unlawfully during a ledger outage is worse than not sending. | `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-04 | Fan **replies** to broadcasts route to CX-M06 (Messaging & Contextual Inbox), not here | A reply is a 1:1 conversation, which is a mechanism that already exists platform-wide. But it is also a harassment surface (Fan anti-persona) and an artist-overwhelm surface. Routed out with the hazard flagged to 24 and CX-M06 — not silently dropped. See Q-02. | Node Classification Gate |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | Does the platform send from a **shared** domain (`wejamm.in`), or per-artist authenticated domains? Shared is trivial to onboard and creates the multi-tenant contagion in D-02. Per-artist is isolated, dramatically better for deliverability, and demands DNS competence from musicians — most will not do it. This is the single highest-leverage decision in the sub-domain and it is partly a product decision, not purely architectural. | User | `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Q-02 | Can fans reply to a broadcast at all? Reply-to is expected by fans and is an unbounded inbox plus a harassment vector for the artist. | User | `/ideate-validate` |
| Q-03 | Is SMS in scope for v1? It is where D2F engagement actually is (Community.com, Laylo), carries a real per-message cost, and brings TCPA exposure with statutory damages per message that dwarf email's risk profile. | User | MoSCoW |
