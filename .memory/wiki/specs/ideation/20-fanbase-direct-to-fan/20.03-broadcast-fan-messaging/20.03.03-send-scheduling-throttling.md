# Feature: Send Scheduling, Throttling & Timezone-Aware Delivery

> **Number**: 20.03.03
> **Parent**: [Broadcast & Fan Messaging](./20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

When a message actually leaves, and how fast. Three jobs in one mechanism: let an artist schedule a send;
deliver at a civilised local hour for each fan wherever they are; and enforce the rate that
[deliverability](./20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md) permits — which the artist cannot override,
because the reputation being spent is shared with every other artist on the platform (index D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Schedules for a date/time, or "9am local to each fan", or now. Sees the honest completion estimate: *this will take 6 hours, because this list is new*. Can cancel a scheduled send and stop one in flight. **Cannot lift a throttle** — and must be told why in terms of their own interest, not the platform's. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. |
| Operator | Config | Same. Timezone matters less (a room's audience is local by definition) and **urgency matters more** — "the support act dropped, doors are at 8 not 7" is a send that cannot wait for a warm-up ramp. See Q-02. |
| Fan | None | Never sees it. Experiences it as messages arriving in the morning rather than at 3am, and as not being buzzed during the night. |

## Behavior

**Scheduling** is ordinary. **Timezone-aware delivery** is not: "9am local" requires a fan's local, and the
platform's evidence is a stated city (often absent), a purchase geo (sometimes), and a phone country code
(SMS only). These disagree, and for email a mistake is invisible while for SMS it is a 3am buzz — a complaint,
a TCPA exposure, and a fan gone (`-cx.md` CX-05). The asymmetry justifies asymmetric caution: **guess for
email, hold for SMS.** Where a timezone is unresolvable, email falls back to the artist's timezone; SMS waits
for a globally-safe window rather than gambling.

**Throttling** is the enforcement arm of reputation (`-cx.md` CX-02). New sender, freshly-imported list, or a
complaint spike → the ramp slows. Crucially the rate is re-rated **mid-flight**: if complaints spike at 10% of
the send, the remaining 90% must slow or halt immediately. A circuit breaker that only applies to the next
campaign is not a circuit breaker.

Quiet hours for SMS are statutory in several jurisdictions — not a courtesy setting.

### Happy Path

1. Musician schedules a tour announcement: "9am local"
2. System resolves each fan's timezone from their strongest location evidence, and buckets
3. Rate ceiling is computed from sender reputation: this artist is warm on email, new on SMS
4. Buckets release at 9am in each timezone; SMS trickles within its ramp; email flows at full rate
5. Musician watches it roll west across the map over the day, seeing delivery per timezone
6. Fans wake up to a message at a sensible hour, wherever they are

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan timezone unknown | Email → artist's timezone. SMS → held to a globally-safe window. Asymmetric by cost. | "412 fans have no location — sending at your local 9am." |
| Fan is travelling; stated city ≠ actual phone | Wrong local time, and unfixable — we have no device GPS and do not collect it (a privacy line we will not cross for a send-timing nicety). Mitigation, not a fix: SMS never uses the stated-city guess at all — it uses the **phone country code** as the only signal tied to the physical number, and where that still leaves ambiguity it holds to a conservative window. Residual mistimed-buzz risk is accepted and documented, not engineered away. | Nothing — a timing detail, invisible to the fan by design. |
| Artist schedules 9am local, then the show sells out overnight | Message goes to Sydney before the artist wakes up in London and is stale for the rest. Scheduled sends must be **cancellable mid-roll**, per remaining bucket. | Cancel + per-timezone progress. |
| Throttle makes a time-critical send too slow | Resolved (D-06). There is no artist-set "urgent" flag — it would be on everything within a month. Urgency is **object-derived**: a show cancellation or on-sale/door-time change originating in [17 Live Booking](../../17-live-booking-settlement/) carries a platform-recognised urgency the artist cannot self-assert. Such a send takes a priority lane that bypasses the warm-up ramp but still respects statutory quiet hours and the hard deliverability circuit-breaker — speed, not a licence to spam. | Object-triggered sends show "urgent — bypassing warm-up" with the reason (e.g. "show cancelled"). |
| Complaint spike mid-send | Rate drops or halts immediately. Partially-sent campaign is a legitimate end state and must be legible. | "Stopped at 1,204 of 3,340 — complaint rate hit the limit." + what to do. |
| Scheduled send whose audience emptied (all unsubscribed) | Resolves at send, not at schedule (segments are live). Sends to nobody, reports honestly. | "Audience is now 0." |
| DST transition on the scheduled day | Resolved: buckets store an IANA timezone id (e.g. `Europe/London`), never a cached fixed offset, and the local dispatch time is recomputed against DST rules at release. "9am local" is 9am after the clocks move, not 8am. | Correct local hour; no visible artefact. |
| Artist tries to lift or raise the throttle | Denied — the throttle is not artist-overridable (D-02, index D-02). The reputation being spent is shared across every artist on the platform. | Explained in the artist's own interest: "Sending faster now burns inbox placement you'll need for the next tour — here's why." Not a platform-scold. |
| Two campaigns scheduled to overlapping segments in the same window | Both would buzz the same fan. The per-fan **frequency cap** that governs this is the fan's own **frequency tier** — owned by [20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md) D-05, per-(fan, entity), resolved at send-time against the campaign's purpose-type. A fan on **Highlights** admits at most one marketing email per calendar month from this entity and the second is **dropped, not queued** (20.01.03 Frequency Tiers); a fan on the default **All** tier is uncapped and receives both. This node applies the resolution at dispatch; it does not own the tiers and cannot widen them. | "1,090 fans are on a reduced-frequency tier and have already had this month's message — not sent to them." |
| Cancel issued while a bucket is mid-dispatch | Messages already handed to the email/SMS provider cannot be recalled; cancel stops everything not yet dispatched. The count is honest about the gap. | "Stopped. 2,140 already sent and cannot be recalled; 1,200 cancelled." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Timezone-aware sending is a polish feature | It looks like scheduling with extra steps | ❌ REJECTED — for **SMS it is a safety feature**. Getting it wrong is a 3am buzz: a complaint, a TCPA exposure with per-message statutory damages, and a permanently lost fan. Music audiences are internationally distributed at exactly the moment an artist most wants to send (a tour announcement, a release day), so the "everyone's basically local" simplification fails for precisely the highest-stakes sends. The fallback asymmetry — guess for email, hold for SMS — follows from the cost asymmetry, not from taste. |
| DT-02 | Artists should be able to override a throttle for urgent sends | Reasonable — some sends genuinely are urgent | ❌ REJECTED as an artist-set capability; ✅ CONFIRMED as an **object-derived** one (D-06, resolving Q-02). Any artist checkbox becomes always-on within a month, and the reputation is shared (index D-02), so a self-asserted override is one artist spending everyone else's inbox placement. The Operator case is real — "support act dropped, doors moved" tonight cannot wait 6 hours and is a message fans actively want. The resolution: urgency attaches to the **object**, not a flag. A show cancellation or door-time change in [domain 17](../../17-live-booking-settlement/) is urgent *by nature*; the platform knows this without trusting anyone's self-assessment. That send bypasses the warm-up ramp but never the statutory quiet hours or the hard circuit-breaker. What stays deferred to `/create-prd-architecture`: the exact catalogue of which object events qualify. |
| DT-03 | Throttling and scheduling are the same feature | They both decide when a message leaves | ✅ CONFIRMED — deliberately kept in one node. Scheduling sets intent; reputation sets the ceiling; the two resolve to one dispatch plan. Splitting them into separate nodes would create two owners of the same queue and guarantee they disagree. The alternative structure (throttling inside deliverability) is worse: it puts the enforcement mechanism away from the queue it enforces on. |
| DT-04 | Per-fan frequency capping belongs in this node too | It is also a "should this message leave now?" decision | ❌ REJECTED — it is a *different* question with a *different* owner. Throttling protects **shared platform reputation** (a fleet-wide ceiling, artist-blind); frequency capping protects **an individual fan from one artist's over-sending** (a per-fan, per-artist budget). Folding them together would let a high-reputation artist send fast *and* often and call it fine. This node **consumes** the frequency cap as a gate at dispatch (see the overlapping-campaigns edge case) but does not own the policy — the budget is the fan's own frequency tier, written in [20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md) D-05 and only *read* here ("this feature writes the state; 20.03 reads it"). Keeping the ceiling and the budget separate is what stops "warm sender" from meaning "may fatigue fans." |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Throttling and scheduling are one node | See DT-03. One queue, one owner. |
| D-02 | Throttle is not artist-overridable | Index D-02 — shared reputation is not the artist's to spend. Narrow object-level urgency remains open (Q-02). |
| D-03 | Timezone fallback is asymmetric: guess for email, hold for SMS | Cost asymmetry. A wrong-hour email is invisible; a wrong-hour SMS is a 3am buzz and a statutory exposure. |
| D-04 | Mid-flight re-rating is mandatory | A circuit breaker that only affects the next campaign has already let the damage out. |
| D-05 | Scheduled sends are cancellable per remaining timezone bucket | A rolling send that becomes wrong at 4am must be stoppable for everyone it has not reached. |
| D-06 | Urgency is object-derived, never artist-asserted; urgent sends bypass the warm-up ramp but not quiet hours or the circuit-breaker | Resolves Q-02. An artist flag becomes always-on; a platform-recognised cancelled/changed show (domain 17) is urgent by nature and needs no self-report. See DT-02. |
| D-07 | Buckets store IANA timezone ids and recompute local time at dispatch; fixed offsets are never cached | DST correctness at scale — "9am local" must survive a clock change. |
| D-08 | Frequency capping is a separate policy this node consumes, not owns — the owner is [20.01.03](../20.01-fan-graph-owned-audience/20.01.03-fan-preference-centre.md) D-05's per-(fan, entity) frequency tiers | Throttling guards shared reputation; frequency capping guards the individual fan. Different question, different owner. The tiers are fan-authored, so the cap the artist meets at dispatch is the fan's own setting, not a platform budget the artist can negotiate. See DT-04, and Q-04 for the part that is genuinely unspecified. |
