# Feature: Send Scheduling, Throttling & Timezone-Aware Delivery

> **Number**: 20.03.03
> **Parent**: [Broadcast & Fan Messaging](./20.03-broadcast-fan-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

When a message actually leaves, and how fast. Three jobs in one mechanism: let an artist schedule a send;
deliver at a civilised local hour for each fan wherever they are; and enforce the rate that
[deliverability](./20.03.04-deliverability-sender-reputation.md) permits — which the artist cannot override,
because the reputation being spent is shared with every other artist on the platform (index D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Schedules for a date/time, or "9am local to each fan", or now. Sees the honest completion estimate: *this will take 6 hours, because this list is new*. Can cancel a scheduled send and stop one in flight. **Cannot lift a throttle** — and must be told why in terms of their own interest, not the platform's. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. |
| Operator | Config | Same. Timezone matters less (a room's audience is local by definition) and **urgency matters more** — "the support act dropped, doors are at 8 not 7" is a send that cannot wait for a warm-up ramp. See Q-02. |
| Fan | None | Never sees it. Experiences it as messages arriving in the morning rather than at 3am, and as not being buzzed during the night. |

## Behavior

**Scheduling** is ordinary. **Timezone-aware delivery** is not: "9am local" requires a fan's local, and the
platform's evidence is a stated city (often absent), a purchase geo (sometimes), and a phone country code
(SMS only). These disagree, and for email a mistake is invisible while for SMS it is a 3am buzz — a complaint,
a TCPA exposure, and a fan gone (`-cx.md` CX-05). The asymmetry justifies asymmetric caution: **guess for
email, hold for SMS.** Where a timezone is unresolvable, email falls back to the artist's timezone; SMS waits
for a globally-safe window rather than gambling.

**Throttling** is the enforcement arm of reputation (`-cx.md` CX-02). New sender, freshly-imported list, or a
complaint spike → the ramp slows. Crucially the rate is re-rated **mid-flight**: if complaints spike at 10% of
the send, the remaining 90% must slow or halt immediately. A circuit breaker that only applies to the next
campaign is not a circuit breaker.

Quiet hours for SMS are statutory in several jurisdictions — not a courtesy setting.

### Happy Path

1. Musician schedules a tour announcement: "9am local"
2. System resolves each fan's timezone from their strongest location evidence, and buckets
3. Rate ceiling is computed from sender reputation: this artist is warm on email, new on SMS
4. Buckets release at 9am in each timezone; SMS trickles within its ramp; email flows at full rate
5. Musician watches it roll west across the map over the day, seeing delivery per timezone
6. Fans wake up to a message at a sensible hour, wherever they are

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan timezone unknown | Email → artist's timezone. SMS → held to a globally-safe window. Asymmetric by cost. | "412 fans have no location — sending at your local 9am." |
| Fan is travelling; stated city ≠ actual phone | Wrong local time. Unfixable without device signal we do not have. For SMS this is a 3am buzz to someone who did nothing wrong. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Artist schedules 9am local, then the show sells out overnight | Message goes to Sydney before the artist wakes up in London and is stale for the rest. Scheduled sends must be **cancellable mid-roll**, per remaining bucket. | Cancel + per-timezone progress. |
| Throttle makes a time-critical send too slow | The genuinely hard case (Q-02). A cancelled show tonight cannot wait 6 hours. But "urgent" as an artist-set flag will be set on everything by everyone. | `[PENDING]` |
| Complaint spike mid-send | Rate drops or halts immediately. Partially-sent campaign is a legitimate end state and must be legible. | "Stopped at 1,204 of 3,340 — complaint rate hit the limit." + what to do. |
| Scheduled send whose audience emptied (all unsubscribed) | Resolves at send, not at schedule (segments are live). Sends to nobody, reports honestly. | "Audience is now 0." |
| DST transition on the scheduled day | Off-by-one-hour. Boring, real, and embarrassing at scale. | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Schedule confirmed | Bucketing + rate estimate |
| Empty | Nothing scheduled | Scheduled-sends list, empty |
| Populated | Send scheduled or rolling | Per-timezone buckets, completion estimate, live progress |
| Error | Scheduler failure | **Must not silently skip a send.** An announcement that never went is invisible until the show is empty. Alert the artist. |
| Partial | Some timezones delivered, some pending | The map/timeline view — genuinely the clearest presentation of this |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Timezone-aware sending is a polish feature | It looks like scheduling with extra steps | ❌ REJECTED — for **SMS it is a safety feature**. Getting it wrong is a 3am buzz: a complaint, a TCPA exposure with per-message statutory damages, and a permanently lost fan. Music audiences are internationally distributed at exactly the moment an artist most wants to send (a tour announcement, a release day), so the "everyone's basically local" simplification fails for precisely the highest-stakes sends. The fallback asymmetry — guess for email, hold for SMS — follows from the cost asymmetry, not from taste. |
| DT-02 | Artists should be able to override a throttle for urgent sends | Reasonable — some sends genuinely are urgent | ❌ REJECTED as a general capability, ⏳ **unresolved as a narrow one.** Any artist-set urgency flag becomes always-on within a month; the reputation being spent is shared (index D-02), so an override is one artist spending everyone else's inbox placement. **But** the Operator case is real and sympathetic: "the support act dropped, doors moved" tonight cannot wait 6 hours, and it is a message fans actively want. My tentative read: urgency should attach to the **object** (a show change in domain 17, a cancellation) rather than to a checkbox — the platform knows a cancelled show is urgent and does not have to trust anyone's self-assessment. Not confident. Q-02. |
| DT-03 | Throttling and scheduling are the same feature | They both decide when a message leaves | ✅ CONFIRMED — deliberately kept in one node. Scheduling sets intent; reputation sets the ceiling; the two resolve to one dispatch plan. Splitting them into separate nodes would create two owners of the same queue and guarantee they disagree. The alternative structure (throttling inside deliverability) is worse: it puts the enforcement mechanism away from the queue it enforces on. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Throttling and scheduling are one node | See DT-03. One queue, one owner. |
| D-02 | Throttle is not artist-overridable | Index D-02 — shared reputation is not the artist's to spend. Narrow object-level urgency remains open (Q-02). |
| D-03 | Timezone fallback is asymmetric: guess for email, hold for SMS | Cost asymmetry. A wrong-hour email is invisible; a wrong-hour SMS is a 3am buzz and a statutory exposure. |
| D-04 | Mid-flight re-rating is mandatory | A circuit breaker that only affects the next campaign has already let the damage out. |
| D-05 | Scheduled sends are cancellable per remaining timezone bucket | A rolling send that becomes wrong at 4am must be stoppable for everyone it has not reached. |
