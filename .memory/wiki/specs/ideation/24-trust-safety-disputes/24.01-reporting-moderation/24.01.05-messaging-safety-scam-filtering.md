# Feature: Messaging Safety & Scam Filtering

> **Number**: 24.01.05
> **Parent**: [Reporting, Moderation & Notice-and-Action](./24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Detection and interruption of the scams that run through the platform's own inbox — the fake booking
agent, the overpayment cheque on a gear sale, the "send me your stems" harvest, the fake sync offer,
the deposit-first rehearsal-room fraud. Music's scam surface is distinctive because the lure is the
platform's own promise: the aspiration WeJammin sells (a tour, a placement, a session) is exactly what
the scammer offers. **Scope note**: the *disintermediation* half of the original candidate — detecting
"let's take this to WhatsApp" — is a take-rate concern and is routed to the
`Messaging & Contextual Inbox` cross-cut, which already claims it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees an inline warning on a suspicious thread ("this pattern matches a known booking-agent scam"), can report the thread, can block. Never sees a score. **The most-targeted persona** — the fake-agent scam is aimed precisely at a working musician who wants the gig badly enough to pay a deposit. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees warnings on stem-harvesting approaches and fake client briefs ("I'll pay you to mix — here's a cheque for more than we agreed"). Their exposure is unusual: handing over stems *is* the job, so "don't share files with strangers" is not usable advice. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees warnings on fraudulent booking enquiries and deposit scams targeting the room. Their enquiry inbox is public by necessity, which makes it the most-abusable surface they own. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees warnings on artist-impersonator approaches ("DM me for VIP tickets") and ticket-resale scams. Least equipped to evaluate the warning; the warning must therefore be concrete about the *pattern*, not a generic "be careful". |

## Behavior

The design constraint is that **private messages are not public content**. Scanning them carries a
different lawful basis, a different user expectation and a different retention posture, and the
Privacy cross-cut already establishes that consent behaviour must be regional rather than uniform.
The default posture is therefore: pattern and metadata signals (new account + first message contains a
payment instruction + off-platform contact + urgency), producing an **in-thread warning to the
recipient** and a reportable prompt — not an unsolicited enforcement action against the sender.

Content-level inspection escalates only on report. A reported thread is evidence
([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)); an unreported thread is not.

Grooming and adult↔minor contact restriction are **not** owned here — they belong to the
`Safeguarding & Minor Protection` cross-cut, which already claims restricted adult↔minor messaging.

### Happy Path

1. A message is sent in a context-bound thread (listing, brief, enquiry, gig offer)
2. Metadata/pattern signals evaluate the thread — sender age, prior history, structure, payment language
3. On a match, the recipient sees an inline, specific warning naming the pattern
4. Recipient may report the thread; the report carries the thread as evidence
5. Case enters the queue (24.01.03); the sender may enter the risk engine (24.03.01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Overpayment cheque scam on gear ("I'll send £3,000 for the £2,000 amp, wire back the difference") | Pattern match on amount-mismatch + off-platform payment | Recipient: warning naming the overpayment pattern and why it works |
| Fake booking agent ("I'm booking a UK tour, £200 admin deposit") | Pattern match on deposit-request-from-new-account | Recipient: warning + link to how legitimate booking works on-platform (17) |
| Legitimate producer asking for stems | **No warning.** Sending stems to a stranger is the job | Nothing |
| Legitimate off-platform contact (an existing collaborator sharing a phone number) | No safety action; the take-rate concern is the Messaging cross-cut's, not this feature's | Nothing |
| Warning shown on a legitimate high-value negotiation | False positive damages a real sale | Warning must be dismissible and never block the send |
| Scam moves to a channel the platform can't see | Out of reach — this is the ceiling of the feature and must be stated, not papered over | Warning includes "we cannot protect you off-platform" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Clean | No signals | Normal thread |
| Warned | Pattern matched | Inline, dismissible warning naming the specific pattern |
| Reported | Recipient reports | "Reported — case {id}"; thread preserved as evidence |
| Restricted | Sender sanctioned (24.02.01) | Recipient: thread marked; sender: per the sanction |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Private messages can be moderated like public content | The path of least resistance | ❌ REJECTED — different lawful basis, different expectation, different retention. Building one pipeline for both is not an efficiency, it is a privacy incident with a shared codebase. The defensible design is metadata/pattern signals + recipient warning + report-triggered content review. This must be settled at architecture time because the Privacy cross-cut's regional-consent requirement makes it a schema decision, not a toggle. |
| DT-02 | The highest-value music scam is the gear overpayment scam | Marketplace precedent (it is the classic) | ❌ REJECTED — the **fake booking agent / fake A&R** scam is worse, and it is music-native. It targets the exact aspiration the platform sells, its victim pays willingly, and the lure is the platform's own credibility ("I found you through your credits on WeJammin"). A gear scam costs one amp. A fake-tour scam costs a musician their savings and costs the platform its founding promise. It also cannot be caught by transactional signals, because **no transaction occurs on-platform** — the money leaves by bank transfer. |
| DT-03 | "Don't share files with strangers" is usable safety advice here | Generic anti-phishing guidance | ❌ REJECTED — for the Producer persona, sharing multi-GB stem packs with people they have never met **is the job** (the Messaging cross-cut notes 5–20 GB stem exchange as a core requirement). Generic advice inverts the product. The music-correct control is not "don't share" but "share through the platform so the transfer is recorded" — which converts a safety feature into a provenance feature, and vice versa. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Disintermediation detection is **split out** and routed to the `Messaging & Contextual Inbox` cross-cut | It is a take-rate concern wearing a safety costume. The cross-cut already names it as "the primary defence against off-platform leakage, the existential threat to take rate". Same classifier, different owner, different escalation — and conflating them would let a revenue metric drive a safety intervention. |
| D-02 | Default posture is metadata + recipient warning; content inspection only on report | DT-01. |
| D-03 | Grooming and minor-contact restriction are the Safeguarding cross-cut's, not this feature's | Already claimed there; duplicating it produces two inconsistent minor-protection regimes. |
