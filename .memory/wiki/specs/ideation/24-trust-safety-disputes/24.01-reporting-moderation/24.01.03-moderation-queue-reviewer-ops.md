# Feature: Moderation Queue Routing & Reviewer Operations

> **Number**: 24.01.03
> **Parent**: [Reporting, Moderation & Notice-and-Action](./24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The work-allocation layer of adjudication: which case goes to which reviewer, in what order, against
what clock, with what second opinion, and with what exposure limits on the human doing it. The console
*shell* it renders inside is the `Admin, Back-Office & Support` cross-cut — what this feature owns is
the routing logic, the SLA model, reviewer quality assurance, and the wellbeing controls that decide
what a human is made to look at and how often. It is the throughput governor for every enforcement
action in domain 24: a case that intake (24.01.01) accepts and a classifier (24.01.02) scores does
nothing until this feature puts it in front of a competent, unconflicted, un-burned-out reviewer
before its statutory clock runs out.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | No queue visibility, ever. Experiences the feature only as time-to-decision on a case that may be about their livelihood — an impersonator taking deposits under their name is a revenue event with an SLA, not a support ticket. When they are the *subject* of a case, routing decides whether a skilled reviewer or a generalist judges "is this really their mix", but they never see which queue they landed in. |
| Producer | None | No queue visibility. The persona whose cases most need *skill-routed* review: "is that a sample, an interpolation, or a re-play" is not a generalist's call, and mis-routing their case to a content-policy queue produces a confidently wrong Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) rather than a correct silence. They feel routing quality as decision *accuracy*, invisibly. |
| Operator | None | No queue visibility. Their cases are time-critical in a way others are not — a fraudulent booking reported Friday and reviewed Monday is a lost Saturday that cannot be re-sold (`personas.md`: "an unsold Tuesday is gone forever"). Severity-to-SLA mapping is the only part of this feature that touches them, and only as elapsed time. |
| Fan | None | No queue visibility. Generates the highest case volume (D-13: fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude) and therefore sets the queue's throughput floor and the capacity-reservation math. A fan reporting harassment is also frequently the *subject* of a harassment case — routing must never let the two cases about the same fan land with the same reviewer in the same session. |

## Behavior

A case arrives from one of four sources — a user report (24.01.01), a classifier hit (24.01.02), a
trusted-flagger notice (24.01.04), or a message-safety escalation (24.01.05) — carrying a reason code,
a severity, a jurisdiction, and (sometimes) a classifier score. **Routing selects a queue by required
skill, not by content type**: the music-domain queues (sample clearance, credit disputes, gear
authenticity, mix-ownership) demand a reviewer who knows the domain, and that expert requirement is in
direct tension with staffing capacity under the small-team assumption (DT-02, Q-06). Severity sets the SLA; the SLA clock starts at
*intake*, not at claim (CX-01), because the DSA measures "timely" from receipt, not from when a human
got around to it.

**Severity tiers** (structure is fixed here; the committed minute/hour value per tier is set with
counsel at `/create-prd-security` — see Q-04, and 24.01.01 Q-02):

| Tier | Meaning | Routing rule | Clock |
|------|---------|--------------|-------|
| S0 | Illegal / imminent-harm (CSAM, credible threat, self-harm) | **Bypasses this queue entirely** — hard-routed to 24.08.01 / 24.06.03; never enters a general lane (DT-04) | Immediate handoff, measured in minutes |
| S1 | Critical (statutory clock, financial harm in progress, active impersonation) | Skill-routed, priority lane, two-reviewer concurrence required before sanction | Shortest human SLA |
| S2 | Standard policy violation or factual dispute | Skill-routed by reason family | Standard SLA |
| S3 | Low-severity / high-false-positive (lyric content, style disputes) | Classifier-scored, deprioritised, may age within policy | Longest SLA |

**SLA breach is itself an alert, not a backlog entry** (D-03) — a missed statutory clock fires an
operator alert, and the case does **not** auto-decide to beat the clock (DT-03). **High-severity (S1)
decisions require a second reviewer to concur before the sanction lands.** A configurable sample of
all decisions (default 5%, see Q-05) is re-reviewed *blind* for inter-rater agreement, because without
a measured agreement rate there is no evidence the policy is being applied consistently — and
consistency is exactly what an appeal (24.02.02) tests.

**Queue-starvation protection** (resolving 24.01.04 Q-03): priority lanes get elevated SLA but **not
unbounded capacity**. A reserved-capacity floor guarantees that safety-of-person queues (harassment,
threats) always retain a minimum share of reviewer time regardless of how large a trusted-flagger
back-catalogue sweep is — a label submitting 5,000 copyright notices cannot delay a harassment case.
This is weighted-fair scheduling with a hard floor, not strict priority ordering.

**Reviewer exposure controls are product configuration, not HR policy** (DT-01, D-02): graphic media
blurred by default with click-to-reveal, audio muted by default, per-session exposure caps that force
a break, forced rotation off high-harm queues on a timer, and the hard structural rule that CSAM
(24.08.01) can never be *routed into* a general queue at all. These controls must be **encoded in the
queue regardless of team size** — an exposure limit that lives only in an HR document does not exist at
the moment a reviewer opens a case, so if it is not in the routing configuration it is not enforced.
(The launch moderation team size is an untested staffing assumption, not a locked constraint — see
Q-06 — and this conclusion deliberately does not depend on it: the controls are mandatory whether the
team is one person or fifty.)

### Happy Path

1. Case is created upstream (report, classifier hit, trusted-flagger notice, or message-safety escalation) and arrives with reason code, severity tier, jurisdiction, and optional classifier score; its SLA clock is already running from intake.
2. Router evaluates the reason family against the skill-map: S0 reasons are hard-routed out to 24.08.01 / 24.06.03 and never touch this queue; all others select a skill-scoped queue (e.g. `sample-clearance`, `credit-dispute`, `gear-authenticity`, `harassment`, `general-policy`).
3. Router applies conflict-of-interest exclusion: any reviewer who shares an identity, band, label, or transaction with the target (across *all* the target's persona contexts) is removed from the eligible pool for that case.
4. Case enters its skill-scoped queue at a priority derived from severity tier and time-remaining-on-clock (not from reporter count — DT-06); the reserved-capacity floor for safety queues is honoured.
5. An eligible reviewer claims the case; the claim is an **exclusive, time-boxed lease** (default 30 min, Q-05) — one case, one reviewer, no double-claim.
6. Reviewer opens the evidence bundle ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) rendered inside the admin console shell, with graphic media blurred and audio muted by default; the exposure meter for the session ticks up.
7. Reviewer decides against a **cited rule** from the policy library (24.02.04); an S1 decision is held in `Awaiting concurrence` until a second, independent, unconflicted reviewer concurs.
8. On decision (or concurrence), the case emits a sanction (24.02.01) and a Statement of Reasons (24.02.03); the reviewer's claim lease is released; if the case was in the blind-sampling set, a shadow copy is enqueued for independent re-review.
9. Outcome is written back to any originating trusted-flagger's accuracy ledger (CX-03) and, when the case originated from a report, becomes labelled training data for the classifier (CX-05).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Concurrency** — two reviewers open the same case and both hit "claim" | The claim is an exclusive lease: the first claim wins, the second is **rejected as already-claimed** and the case leaves the losing reviewer's list on refresh. The wire-level rejection contract (status code, error shape) is deferred to `/write-be-spec`. | Reporter: nothing. Losing reviewer: "This case was just claimed by someone else." |
| **Claim abandoned** — reviewer claims a case then disconnects or walks away | Lease expires at 30 min (Q-05); the case returns to the queue with its original priority and time-remaining **preserved, not reset** — the clock never stopped | Reporter: nothing (still "Under review") |
| **Race — SLA expires during active review** | Breach alert fires to the operator *while the reviewer still holds the case*; the reviewer is not interrupted and the case does **not** auto-decide — a breach is a missed deadline, an auto-decision is a fabricated statutory statement (DT-03) | Reporter: "the original response window has passed; your case is still open" |
| **Race — concurrence disagreement** | Two reviewers reach opposite conclusions on an S1 case; the disagreement is logged, the case escalates to a senior/tie-break reviewer, and it is recorded as a **policy** defect signal, not a reviewer defect signal (DT-05) | Reporter: "Under review" (unchanged) |
| **Blind re-review disagreement** — sampled re-review contradicts the original | Logged and escalated as an inter-rater-agreement failure; feeds the policy-clarity metric, never punishes an individual reviewer (DT-05) | Nothing |
| **Permission** — reviewer lacks the skill scope for a queue | Reviewer cannot see or claim cases outside their granted skill scopes; a `sample-clearance` case is invisible to a general-policy-only reviewer | Nothing — the case simply is not in their list |
| **Permission / conflict of interest** — reviewer is a party to the case | Conflict-of-interest block removes them from the eligible pool at routing time; on a sufficiently small team (potentially one person — Q-06) the conflict can become *unavoidable*, and the case must then escalate outward (external reviewer / disclosed-conflict path) rather than be silently self-adjudicated | Nothing — but see Q-02 |
| **Multi-hyphenate subject in two roles** — case is filed about someone acting as Producer, but the same identity also books as Operator and sells as Musician | The case attaches to the *acting context*; conflict-of-interest and any resulting sanction (24.02.01) must resolve to the acting context, and CoI exclusion must span *all* the identity's contexts (a reviewer who is that person's bandmate in *any* role is conflicted) — a sanction on the Producer context must not silently disable their Operator bookings unless the sanction explicitly says so | Nothing at this layer; the scoping surfaces in the SoR |
| **Malicious input — collusive self-claim** | A reviewer who is secretly a party tries to claim a case to bury it; CoI graph exclusion blocks the claim, and every claim/decision is written to the admin-action audit (Audit Log cross-cut) so a bypass is detectable after the fact | Nothing |
| **Malicious input — brigading arrives as volume** | Volume is stripped at intake (24.01.01 dedups to one case with N reporters); the queue receives *one* case and **priority is never derived from reporter count** — a fanbase cannot escalate a case by mass-reporting (DT-06) | Each reporter: same single outcome |
| **Network failure — decision write fails mid-commit** | The decision is transactional: sanction + SoR + ledger write-back either all commit or none do; on failure the case stays `Claimed` with the draft decision preserved and the reviewer is prompted to retry — a half-applied sanction with no SoR is never left on disk | Reviewer: "Save failed — your decision is preserved, retry." Reporter: nothing |
| **Partial data — evidence bundle incomplete** | If the 24.09 snapshot is missing an asset (deleted-at-source, storage lag), the case enters `Partial`; the reviewer sees exactly which evidence is unavailable and may decide only on what is present, or route to `Escalated` if the missing piece is dispositive — never guesses | Reviewer: "1 of 4 evidence items unavailable" |
| **Partial data — classifier score missing** | Classifier failure degrades the case to an *unscored* item at **default (S2) priority** (CX-02); it is never blocked and never auto-removed for lack of a score | Nothing |
| **CSAM routed into a general queue** | Structurally impossible, not merely unlikely: S0-illegal reasons are hard-routed to 24.08.01 before a general queue is ever selected; there is no code path from an S0 reason to a general lane (DT-04) | N/A — no reviewer is ever exposed |
| **Empty queue** — no cases pending in a skill scope | The scope shows its empty state; the reviewer is offered eligible cases from adjacent scopes they *are* skilled for, respecting the reserved safety floor | Reviewer: "No cases in {scope}. 3 harassment cases need attention." |
| **Queue depth exceeds capacity** — projected clearance time for a tier exceeds its SLA | Behaviour is specified: (a) a **capacity alert** fires to the operator the moment projected-clearance crosses the SLA threshold for a tier; (b) **severity-preserving triage** — S0/S1 and statutory-clock cases hold priority, S3 ages within its policy window; (c) the system **never auto-decides and never auto-drops** a case to relieve pressure. The *response* to a sustained capacity alert (triage further, or hire) is a business decision, not a routing algorithm — see Q-03 | Reporter of an aging S3 case: the standard "response by {date}" window, honestly set to reflect the backlog |
| **Bulk trusted-flagger notice floods the priority lane** | Weighted-fair scheduling with a reserved floor (see Behavior) prevents starvation; the bulk lane drains at its allocated share while the safety floor is untouched | Nothing — the harassment case in the safety floor is unaffected |

### States

The persona-facing "user" of this feature is the Admin/Moderator (not a persona — Q-02); the states
below are that reviewer's console states, paired with the case-lifecycle states the reporter observes
indirectly through 24.01.01.

**Reviewer console states**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Queue view opening / claiming a case | Skeleton queue rows; on claim, a spinner while the exclusive lease is acquired |
| Empty | No cases in the reviewer's skill scope | Empty state + an offer of eligible adjacent-scope or safety-floor cases |
| Populated | Cases present in scope | Priority-ordered list (severity × time-remaining), each row showing reason family, SLA countdown, and classifier confidence (shown so it can be *distrusted*) |
| Error | Lease acquisition or decision write failed | Inline error with retry; draft decision preserved; no partial state committed |
| Partial | Evidence bundle incomplete for a claimed case | The case rendered with explicit "N of M evidence items unavailable" markers |

**Case-lifecycle states** (what the reporter perceives via 24.01.01)

| State | Trigger | What Reporter Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Queued | Case routed into a skill queue | "Received — response by {date}" |
| Claimed | Reviewer takes an exclusive lease | "Under review" |
| Awaiting concurrence | S1 single decision pending a second reviewer | "Under review" (concurrence is invisible) |
| Decided | Concurrence reached or not required | Outcome; target receives a Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) |
| Escalated | Conflict, disagreement, novel policy question, or dispositive missing evidence | "Under review" |
| Breached | SLA clock elapsed | "The response window has passed; your case is still open" — **no auto-decision** |
| Dead-letter | Routing itself failed (no skill match, malformed case) | "Under review" — the dead-letter queue is itself SLA'd (CX-01), a lost case is a statutory breach not a dropped job |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Reviewer wellbeing is an HR concern, not a product concern | The usual framing, and the reason it is usually absent from the queue | ❌ REJECTED — exposure caps, blur-by-default, and forced rotation are **routing configuration**. If they are not encoded in the queue they do not exist: an exposure limit that lives only in an HR handbook is unenforced the instant a reviewer opens a case. This holds at **any team size** — one moderator or fifty, the control is only real if the queue enforces it, so it is a product requirement, not an HR perk. (The launch staffing shape is an untested assumption — Q-06 — and this rejection deliberately does not depend on it.) |
| DT-02 | Music adjudication is generalist work | Cost-driven assumption behind every outsourced moderation contract | ✅ CONFIRMED as a *requirement*, which is itself the problem — "is this a sample, an interpolation, or a re-play", "is this a refin or an original", "is this NSBM or Nordic folk" are expert calls. Skill-routed queues are therefore mandatory and, under the working assumption of a small launch team (uninterviewed — Q-06), directly conflict with staffing capacity. The resolution is scope: **only launch the domains whose queues you can staff**, which makes the beachhead question (`problem-statement.md` Q-03) a Trust & Safety question, not only a growth one. The skill-routing requirement itself is team-size-independent; only the number of launchable queues depends on the (pending) team answer. |
| DT-03 | Beating the SLA by auto-deciding is better than breaching it | Plausible-sounding compliance logic | ❌ REJECTED — an auto-decision emits a Statement of Reasons asserting facts nobody checked, which is *affirmative evidence* of a defective process, worse than the absence of a decision. A breach is a missed deadline; a fabricated decision is a false statutory statement. Breach, alert, and escalate — never auto-decide. |
| DT-04 | Preventing illegal content from reaching a general queue is a matter of careful routing rules | Reasonable first instinct | ❌ REJECTED as *insufficient* — "careful rules" fail eventually. S0-illegal (CSAM, credible threat, self-harm) must be **structurally** unreachable from a general lane: there is no code path from an S0 reason code to a general-queue assignment, so no misconfiguration can expose a general reviewer. Structural impossibility, not vigilance. Confirms the hard-route in the severity table. |
| DT-05 | A reviewer whose decision is overturned on blind re-review or in concurrence made an error to be corrected | Standard QA framing that treats the reviewer as the defect | ❌ REJECTED — disagreement between two competent reviewers is overwhelmingly a **policy-clarity** defect, not an individual-competence defect. Attributing it to the reviewer creates the incentive to guess the "house answer" rather than apply the rule, which destroys the value of a second opinion. Disagreement routes to policy (24.02.04) as a clarity signal; it never lands on a scorecard for the individual. |
| DT-06 | Higher reporter volume on a case is a useful priority signal | The default queue-prioritisation heuristic | ❌ REJECTED — volume is dedup'd away at intake (24.01.01), and D-13 makes fan volume overwhelming; letting count drive priority hands any fanbase a suppression lever against an artist. Priority is derived from **severity tier × time-remaining-on-clock only**. This is the same finding as 24.01.01 DT-03, enforced at the queue rather than at intake — the two layers must agree or the guarantee leaks. |
| DT-07 | A priority lane should always be served before the general queue | The literal meaning of "priority" | ❌ REJECTED — strict priority ordering lets a trusted-flagger back-catalogue sweep (24.01.04) starve the harassment queue indefinitely. The correct model is **weighted-fair scheduling with a reserved capacity floor** for safety-of-person queues: priority accelerates a lane, it never grants it the whole team. This directly answers 24.01.04 Q-03 and is why starvation protection lives here, in the router, not in the flagger feature. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **[24.01.01 Report Intake](./24.01.01-report-intake-notice-and-action.md)** (CX-01) — every accepted report becomes a queue item carrying its own clock; the reason code chosen at intake selects the queue, the SLA, and the required skill. Dead-letter handling for unroutable cases is shared.
- Touches **[24.01.02 Automated Classification](./24.01.02-automated-content-classification.md)** (CX-02) — classifier output is a *priority signal only*, shown with its confidence so a reviewer can distrust it; a missing score degrades to default priority, never blocks or removes.
- Touches **[24.01.04 Trusted Flagger](./24.01.04-trusted-flagger-priority-channel.md)** (CX-03) — the priority lane feeds this queue at elevated SLA; decisions here write back to the flagger accuracy ledger; **this feature owns the starvation protection** that answers 24.01.04 Q-03.
- Touches **[24.01.05 Messaging Safety](./24.01.05-messaging-safety-scam-filtering.md)** — message-safety escalations enter the queue under a *different lawful basis* than public content, which the routing metadata must carry so a reviewer knows the governance regime of what they are looking at.
- Touches **[24.02.03 Statements of Reasons](../24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy/24.02.03-statements-of-reasons.md)** — the decision record must be *structured at this step* (cited rule, evidence referenced, reviewer/concurrence recorded), or the SoR cannot be generated from it.
- Touches **[24.02.04 Policy Library](../24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy/24.02.04-policy-library-versioned-terms.md)** — every decision cites a versioned rule; concurrence and re-review disagreements feed back here as policy-clarity signals (DT-05).
- Touches **[24.06.03 Crisis & Welfare Escalation](../24.06-personal-safety-threat-response/24.06.03-crisis-welfare-escalation.md)** — the S0 self-harm hard-route terminates here (out of the queue) and executes there; no sanction ever.
- Touches **[24.08.01 CSAM](../24.08-illegal-content-legal-process/24.08.01-csam-detection-preservation-reporting.md)** — the hard, structural exclusion from every general queue (DT-04).
- Touches **[24.09 Case Evidence Locker](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)** — the reviewer's entire view *is* the bundle; the snapshot is what lets a case survive its target being deleted, and its incompleteness is the `Partial` state.
- Consumed by the **Admin, Back-Office & Support** cross-cut — owns the console shell, PII masking, break-glass, and admin-action audit; this feature owns only what goes *in* the shell (the routing, the SLA countdown, the exposure meter, the claim lease).
- Consumed by the **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — every claim, decision, concurrence, re-route, and CoI-block is an audited moderation action; "moderation decisions" is named in its own scope.
- Consumed by the **Notifications & Alerts** cross-cut — SLA-breach alerts and capacity alerts to the operator are notification fan-outs, not in-queue state.
