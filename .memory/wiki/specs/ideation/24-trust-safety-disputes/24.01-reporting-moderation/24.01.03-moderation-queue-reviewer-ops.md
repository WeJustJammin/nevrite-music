# Feature: Moderation Queue Routing & Reviewer Operations

> **Number**: 24.01.03
> **Parent**: [Reporting, Moderation & Notice-and-Action](./24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The work-allocation layer of adjudication: which case goes to which reviewer, in what order, against
what clock, with what second opinion, and with what exposure limits on the human doing it. The console
*shell* it renders inside is the `Admin, Back-Office & Support` cross-cut — what this feature owns is
the routing logic, the SLA model, reviewer quality assurance, and the wellbeing controls that decide
what a human is made to look at and how often.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | No visibility. Experiences it only as time-to-decision on a case that may be about their livelihood — an impersonator taking deposits under their name is a revenue event with an SLA. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. The persona whose cases most need *skill-routed* review: "is that a sample or an interpolation" is not a generalist's call. |
| Operator | None | No visibility. Their cases are time-critical in a way others are not — a fraudulent booking on Friday reviewed on Monday is a lost Saturday that cannot be re-sold (`personas.md`: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever). |
| Fan | None | No visibility. Generates the highest case volume and therefore sets the queue's throughput requirement. |

> **All four personas are `None`.** This row is the clearest evidence in the ideation for `personas.md`

## Behavior

A case arrives with a reason code, a severity, a jurisdiction and (sometimes) a classifier score. Routing
selects a queue by **required skill**, not by content type — the music-domain queues (sample clearance,
credit disputes, gear authenticity) need a reviewer who knows the domain, and that is in direct tension
with a small team. Severity sets the SLA; SLA breach is itself an alert, because a missed statutory clock
is a breach, not a backlog.

High-severity decisions require a second reviewer before the sanction lands. Sampled decisions are
re-reviewed blind for inter-rater agreement — without it there is no way to know the policy is being
applied consistently, and consistency is what an appeal (24.02.02) tests.

**Reviewer exposure controls** are product configuration, not HR policy: blur-by-default on graphic media,
audio muted by default, per-session exposure caps, forced rotation off high-harm queues, and a hard rule
that CSAM never enters a general queue (24.08.01).

### Happy Path

1. Case is created (report, classifier hit, or trusted-flagger notice)
2. Router selects queue by reason family + required skill + jurisdiction
3. Severity assigns SLA and priority; the clock is already running from intake
4. Reviewer claims the case; the claim is exclusive and time-boxed
5. Reviewer sees the evidence bundle ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) with graphic media blurred
6. Reviewer decides against a cited rule (24.02.04); high-severity requires a second reviewer to concur
7. Decision emits a sanction (24.02.01) and a Statement of Reasons (24.02.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Reviewer claims a case and disappears | Claim expires; case returns to queue with priority preserved, not reset | Nothing |
| Reviewer is a party to the case (small scene, small team) | Conflict-of-interest block on assignment | Nothing — but see Q-02; on a one-person team the conflict is unavoidable and must escalate outward |
| Two reviewers reach opposite conclusions on a sampled re-review | Disagreement is logged, escalated, and is a **policy** defect signal, not a reviewer defect signal | Nothing |
| SLA breached | Alert fires; the case does **not** auto-decide — an auto-decision to beat a clock is the worst possible outcome | Reporter sees the original window has passed |
| CSAM routed into a general queue | Must be structurally impossible, not merely unlikely | N/A |
| Queue depth exceeds capacity | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the honest options (triage by severity and let low-severity age, or hire) are a business decision, not a routing algorithm |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Queued | Case routed | Reporter: "Received — response by {date}" |
| Claimed | Reviewer takes it | Reporter: "Under review" |
| Awaiting concurrence | High-severity single decision | Reporter: "Under review" |
| Decided | Concurrence reached or not required | Reporter: outcome; target: Statement of Reasons |
| Escalated | Conflict, disagreement, or novel policy question | Reporter: "Under review" |
| Breached | SLA elapsed | Reporter: response window passed; no auto-decision |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Reviewer wellbeing is an HR concern, not a product concern | The usual framing, and the reason it is usually absent | ❌ REJECTED — exposure caps, blur-by-default, and rotation are **routing configuration**. If they are not in the queue they do not exist. This platform makes the argument unusually sharp: `meta/constraints.md` describes no platform team, so **the first moderator is the owner** — a single point of failure who cannot be rotated out by hiring. Wellbeing controls here are business-continuity controls. |
| DT-02 | Music adjudication is generalist work | Cost-driven assumption behind every outsourced moderation contract | ✅ CONFIRMED as a *requirement*, which is a problem — "is this a sample, an interpolation, or a re-play", "is this a refin or original", "is this NSBM or Nordic folk" are expert calls. Skill-routed queues are therefore mandatory and directly conflict with a small team. The resolution is likely scope: **only launch the domains whose queues you can staff** — which makes the beachhead question (`problem-statement.md` Q-03) a Trust & Safety question, not only a growth one. |
| DT-03 | Beating the SLA by auto-deciding is better than breaching it | Plausible-sounding compliance logic | ❌ REJECTED — an auto-decision produces a Statement of Reasons asserting facts nobody checked, which is affirmative evidence of a defective process rather than the absence of one. A breach is a missed deadline; a fabricated decision is a false statutory statement. Breach, alert, and escalate. |
