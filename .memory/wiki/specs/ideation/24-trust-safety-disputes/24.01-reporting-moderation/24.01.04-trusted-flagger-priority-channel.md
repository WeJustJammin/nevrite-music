# Feature: Trusted Flagger & Rights-Holder Priority Channel

> **Number**: 24.01.04
> **Parent**: [Reporting, Moderation & Notice-and-Action](./24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A privileged reporting lane for entities whose notices are expected to be more accurate than the
average user's — DSA Art 22 trusted flaggers (awarded by Digital Services Coordinators), and the lane
that actually matters commercially: labels, publishers, PROs, brands and estates submitting takedown
notices in bulk, by API, at a priority SLA. It is also the domain's sharpest values decision, because
the same lane that makes rights enforcement efficient makes it asymmetric against the platform's own
core persona.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees that a notice against them came from a flagged channel, and who the flagger is. **The lane's primary exposure for this persona is as a target, not a user** — an independent artist is not a trusted flagger anywhere in the industry today. Whether they can be one here is the open question below. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same as Musician. Frequently the party a label bot claims against — their own mix, uploaded to their own session, matched to a master the label controls. |
| Operator | None | Not applicable. Venues and studios neither hold flagger status nor are typical targets of bulk notices. |
| Fan | None | Not applicable. A fan cannot obtain flagger status and is not a bulk-notice target. |

## Behavior

Flagger status is granted per-entity and is a **permission that changes the SLA of a report**. Notices
may arrive individually or in bulk via API; bulk submission must be idempotent per item so a retried
5,000-item notice does not create 10,000 cases. Each per-item decision writes back to the flagger's
**accuracy ledger**, and sustained inaccuracy throttles or suspends the lane — DSA Art 22(6) requires
suspension after frequent insufficiently-precise or inadequately-substantiated notices, so the write-back
is not optional bookkeeping, it is the statutory mechanism.

The lane grants **priority on reports about public content only**. It never reaches into private
messages (24.01 CX R-01) and never bypasses the target's right to a Statement of Reasons or an appeal.

**Scale gate**: DSA Art 22 sits in Section 3 of Chapter III, which Art 19 disapplies for micro and small
enterprises. This feature is therefore not a launch obligation — a fact worth knowing before building it.

### Happy Path

1. An entity applies for (or is awarded) flagger status; the platform records the basis and scope
2. Flagger submits a notice — UI or bulk API — with the required substantiation
3. Each item becomes a case at elevated priority with a shortened SLA
4. Reviewer decides; the target receives a Statement of Reasons naming the flagged origin
5. Outcome writes back to the flagger's accuracy ledger
6. Accuracy below threshold triggers a status review — itself an enforcement action against an entity

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Bulk notice retried after partial failure | Idempotent per item; no duplicate cases | Flagger sees per-item status, not a single batch status |
| Flagger claims content the platform's own provenance graph shows the target created | **The platform has evidence the flagger does not.** The notice does not win by default — see 24.05.02 DT-03 | Target: the claim is contested with the platform's own record cited |
| Flagger accuracy falls below threshold | Lane throttled, then suspended, with a reasoned decision and an appeal | Flagger sees the accuracy record and the decision |
| Flagger status weaponised against a competitor or a critic | Same accuracy machinery; plus abuse is itself reportable | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Target's listing edited between notice and review | Snapshot at case creation ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) fixes what was actually claimed against | Nothing |
| Bulk notice arrives at a volume exceeding review capacity | Priority lane cannot be allowed to starve the general queue — a label's back-catalogue sweep must not delay a harassment case. **Resolved in the router** ([24.01.03](./24.01.03-moderation-queue-reviewer-ops.md) D-05 / DT-07): weighted-fair scheduling with a reserved capacity floor for safety-of-person queues — "the bulk lane drains at its allocated share while the safety floor is untouched" | Flagger: per-item status as the lane drains at its allocated share. Everyone else: nothing — the harassment case in the safety floor is unaffected |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not a flagger | Default | No lane; reports go through 24.01.01 |
| Applied | Application submitted | "Under review" |
| Active | Status granted | API credentials, bulk UI, accuracy record |
| Throttled | Accuracy below threshold | Reduced priority + the reasoned decision |
| Suspended | Sustained inaccuracy (Art 22(6)) | Status revoked with appeal rights |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A trusted-flagger lane is neutral compliance machinery | The framing every platform uses | ❌ REJECTED — a priority lane plus a repeat-infringer ladder (24.05.01) is a **lever pointed at the platform's core persona**. Labels acquire the lane; working musicians do not. On a general marketplace that asymmetry is background noise; on a platform whose thesis is that musicians should own the proof of their work (D-18), building the industry's standard asymmetry by default contradicts the product. This is a values decision that must be made deliberately, not inherited. |
| DT-02 | Art 22 binds from launch | Reasonable reading if you stop at Art 22 | ❌ REJECTED — Art 19 disapplies Section 3 (which contains Art 22) for micro and small enterprises. Art 16 notice-and-action (Section 2) has no such carve-out and *does* bind from user one. The distinction reorders MoSCoW materially: intake is Must, this is Could. **Flagged for counsel — this reasoning drives a scope decision and must not be taken on an agent's word.** |
| DT-03 | The platform must defer to a rights-holder's notice because it cannot know the truth | The structural assumption behind every takedown regime | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the domain's most thesis-aligned finding. WeJammin *does* know: it holds the session record showing who uploaded which stem, when, into whose project, with whose counter-attestation (D-18). A label bot claiming a producer's own mix is contradicted by the platform's own provenance graph. That does not resolve the legal question, but it means the platform is the first intermediary in history with a *positive* record to weigh against a notice — rather than only the notice. No competitor can retroactively manufacture that. |
