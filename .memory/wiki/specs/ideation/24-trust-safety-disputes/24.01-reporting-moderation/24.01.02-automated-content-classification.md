# Feature: Automated Content Classification & Proactive Detection

> **Number**: 24.01.02
> **Parent**: [Reporting, Moderation & Notice-and-Action](./24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Machine pre-triage of user-supplied content — listing text and photos, profile copy, audio, video,
reviews, public posts — producing a label and a confidence that **reorders the moderation queue and
nothing else**. It exists to make a small review team survive consumer-scale volume (D-13), not to make
decisions. Its most important property is what it is forbidden to do: on a music platform, automated
removal of creative work is a category of failure the product cannot recover from.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Invisible. They experience it only as review latency, and as the absence of the thing every musician has been burned by elsewhere: their own track auto-flagged and removed by a machine with no human in the loop. |
| Producer | None | Invisible. The persona most exposed to false positives — they upload the most raw audio (stems, rough mixes, unmastered comps) and the most explicit working material. |
| Operator | None | Invisible. Their exposure is gear/room photos, where the classifier's only high-value job is EXIF stripping and address leakage — a control, not a moderation call. |
| Fan | None | Invisible. Generates the highest volume of classified content (comments, posts, gig videos) and the highest true-positive rate. |

## Behavior

Content is classified asynchronously on create and **re-classified on edit** — a stale score on an edited
listing is a lie, and edit-after-review is a known fraud pattern (24.03.03). Output is written to
dedicated fields on the queue item; the classifier never mutates the reporter's stated reason and never
triggers a notification. Modalities differ sharply in maturity: text and image classification are
commodity; audio classification for policy purposes is close to useless and is scoped out of this
feature except as metadata (loudness, silence, duration anomalies).

Exact-hash matching for known illegal content is **not** this feature — it lives in
[24.08.01](../24.08-illegal-content-legal-process/24.08.01-csam-detection-preservation-reporting.md)
and [24.08.02](../24.08-illegal-content-legal-process/24.08.02-tvec-removal.md), because a hash match is
a categorically different confidence regime and carries a preservation duty this feature does not.

EXIF stripping on upload is treated here as a **safety control**, not an optimization — the same position
the Media Handling cross-cut takes.

### Happy Path

1. User publishes a listing, profile, post, or upload
2. Content is queued for classification; publication is **not** blocked
3. Classifier returns label + confidence; queue item is enriched
4. If confidence exceeds a review threshold, the item enters the human queue (24.01.03) proactively
5. A human decides; the decision becomes a training label (CX-05)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Violent or hateful **lyrics** in a track description, lyric sheet, or title | Flag for human review only; never auto-remove. Drill, murder ballads, death metal and gangsta rap all trip generic hate/violence classifiers | Nothing — content stays live pending review |
| Gear photo carries EXIF GPS of the seller's home | EXIF stripped on ingest, unconditionally, before storage | Nothing (silent control) |
| Classifier unavailable | Item enters the queue **unscored at default priority** — fail open, never fail closed onto removal | Nothing |
| Nudity classifier fires on an album cover | Human review; music has a long history of legitimate artistic nudity on covers | Content stays live pending review |
| User edits a listing after it was scored | Re-classify; the prior score is retained on the case as evidence of what was scored | Nothing |
| Non-English content | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on the Localization cross-cut's language coverage; a classifier that only works in English silently under-enforces everywhere else |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unscored | Content just published, or classifier down | Nothing — content is live |
| Scored | Classification returned | Nothing |
| Proactively queued | Confidence over threshold | Nothing — content remains live during review |
| Stale | Content edited after scoring | Nothing — internally re-queued |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | General-purpose content classifiers transfer to a music platform | Standard practice on every UGC platform | ❌ REJECTED — lyrics are the single highest false-positive surface in music moderation. Hate, violence, drugs and self-harm classifiers are trained on assertions, and a lyric is not an assertion. A platform whose users are the artists cannot afford to auto-remove art. **The rule is: music content defaults to human review; automated removal is reserved for exact hash matches of known illegal content.** This constraint is cheap to state now and impossible to retrofit once auto-removal is wired into the enforcement ladder. |
| DT-02 | EXIF stripping is a performance/storage optimization | Common framing in media pipelines | ❌ REJECTED — it is a safety control with a named victim. A seller photographing a bass in their living room publishes their home address to anyone who downloads the image, and this platform pairs that with a *public gig calendar telling a stalker when the seller is out of the house* (24.06.01). No other marketplace has that pairing. The Media Handling cross-cut already reaches the same conclusion independently, which is corroboration. |
| DT-03 | Audio classification can detect policy violations in tracks | The obvious expectation for a music platform | ❌ REJECTED for this pass — there is no reliable general audio policy classifier, and the music-specific ask ("is this hate music?") is a genre-adjacent judgment call that defeats it (24.08.02 DT-02). What *is* tractable on audio is exact matching (24.05.02 fingerprinting, 24.08.01 hashing) and metadata anomaly. Claiming audio classification here would be a confident fabrication. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Classifier output is advisory only — it reorders the queue and cannot remove, restrict, or notify | DT-01. This is the constraint that keeps the platform's relationship with its own users intact. |
| D-02 | Fail open, never closed | An unavailable classifier must produce unscored items at default priority. Failing closed on a music platform means the outage removes art. |
