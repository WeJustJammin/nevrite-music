# Feature: Law Enforcement & Legal Process Portal

> **Number**: 24.08.03
> **Parent**: [Illegal Content & Legal Process](./24.08-illegal-content-legal-process-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The channel through which the state and civil litigants reach the platform: subpoenas, warrants,
preservation requests, emergency disclosure requests, and civil process including DMCA 512(h) subpoenas to
unmask an alleged infringer. Most platforms build this late and reluctantly. **WeJammin is a police-facing
product by construction**: a stolen-gear registry (domain 15) whose entire value proposition is that
police and buyers can use it to identify stolen instruments will generate law-enforcement contact as a
success metric, not as an exception.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | No visibility. But they are the **subject** of the most consequential request type: a 512(h) subpoena unmasking an anonymous alleged infringer is a lever rights-holders use against individual users, and the person unmasked is a working musician. They are also the beneficiary — their stolen Rhodes is found because police could query the registry. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. Subject of the same unmasking exposure via their uploads. |
| Operator | None | No visibility. Occasionally the *originator* of a police matter — an incident at their venue — but they contact police directly, not through this portal. |
| Fan | None | No visibility. Subject of requests in harassment and threat cases, where the platform's data may be what identifies a stalker. |

## Behavior

Requests arrive, are **verified**, are scoped, and are answered — or refused. Verification is the feature,
not the form (DT-02). Each request type has a different legal standard and a different response: a
preservation request freezes data (24.08 CX-04) without disclosing it; a subpoena compels subscriber
information; a warrant compels content; an emergency disclosure request compels speed and is the most
dangerous of all.

**A legal hold overrides everything else in the product**, including a user's erasure right — the one place
where the platform defeats a user's rights by design rather than by defect. Gag orders mean the platform
often cannot tell the user, and must not accidentally tell them through a UI state.

**Overbroad requests are refused or narrowed.** A request for "all data on this user" when the case is a
stolen pedal is not a request the platform should satisfy just because it arrived on letterhead.

The retention and legal-hold machinery is `/create-prd-security`'s (24.08 D-04); this feature owns the
decision to place a hold and the response to the request.

### Happy Path

1. A request arrives through a defined channel
2. **Verification**: is this a genuine authority, genuinely making this request?
3. Classification: preservation, subpoena, warrant, EDR, civil process
4. Scoping: what is actually compelled, versus what was asked for
5. Response: preserve, produce, narrow, or refuse — with the reasoning recorded
6. Notify the user, unless prohibited
7. The whole exchange is retained; it will be scrutinised

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Police query the **stolen-gear registry** for a serial | The registry exists for this. It is the product working | Nothing — the owner may later learn their instrument was found |
| **Fake emergency disclosure request** from a compromised police email domain | The top social-engineering vector against platforms, and it works because EDRs demand speed | Nothing — the failure is silent and the data is gone |
| 512(h) subpoena unmasking a musician accused of infringement | A rights-holder lever aimed at an individual user (24.05.01 DT-02, again) | User notified unless prohibited — and the notification is what gives them a chance to quash |
| "All data on this user" for a stolen-pedal case | **Overbroad.** Narrow it or refuse | User: notified |
| Preservation request, then nothing for a year | The hold persists and the data cannot be deleted. Erasure requests silently fail | 24.08 CX-04 |
| Gag order plus a pending GDPR erasure request | The platform must not reveal the hold via a UI state ("your deletion is pending") | The deletion appears to complete |
| Request from a jurisdiction with no authority over the platform | Not automatically valid because it looks official | Refused, with the reasoning recorded |
| A one-person team receives a warrant at 2am | `meta/constraints.md` describes no legal team | `[PENDING]` — see Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | Default | Nothing |
| Received | Request arrives | Nothing yet |
| Verifying | Authority being confirmed | Nothing — **the step EDRs are designed to skip** |
| Held | Preservation placed | Nothing; deletion silently does not happen |
| Produced | Data disclosed | User notified, unless prohibited |
| Narrowed | Scope reduced | User notified of what was actually produced |
| Refused | No valid basis | Requester: the reasoning |
| Released | Hold lifted | Ordinary retention resumes |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Law-enforcement requests are rare — build the portal when the volume justifies it | The universal sequencing | ❌ REJECTED — **this platform is police-facing by design.** Domain 15 is a stolen-gear registry whose entire value proposition is that a stolen instrument can be identified: that means police querying serials, and it means the platform is *inviting* the contact because the contact is the feature working. Add a marketplace with in-person meetups (24.06.02), a stalking-relevant public location graph (24.06.01 DT-01), and a minors population (D-13), and law-enforcement contact is a **designed-in workload**, not an exception. Deferring the portal means an ad hoc email process — which is precisely the condition DT-02 exploits. |
| DT-02 | An EDR is a simple fast-path form — the whole point is speed | What an EDR is designed to make you think | ❌ REJECTED — **the emergency disclosure request is the top social-engineering vector against platforms**, and it works by weaponising exactly the urgency it claims. Fraudulent EDRs sent from compromised or spoofed police email domains have been used repeatedly and successfully against major platforms to extract user data, and the attackers targeting a music platform have a clear motive: unmask an artist, locate a person, or find a stalking target. **The portal is itself an attack surface**, and the verification step — the one an EDR pressures you to skip — is the feature. A fast path with no verification is a data-disclosure API with a police-themed skin. |
| DT-03 | The portal is a compliance obligation with no product consequence | It looks like plumbing | ❌ REJECTED — **it is where the platform decides what kind of company it is, and the decision is visible.** A 512(h) subpoena unmasking an anonymous musician accused of infringement is a rights-holder lever pointed at an individual user, and the platform chooses whether to notify them (giving them a chance to quash), whether to demand proper process, and whether to narrow an overbroad request. Those choices are the same choice as 24.05.01 DT-02 (the counter-notice trap) and 24.01.04 DT-01 (the trusted-flagger asymmetry): **every one of them is a place where the industry default points at the platform's own core persona**, and inheriting the default is a decision even when it is made by not deciding. This portal is the third instance of one pattern, which is itself the finding. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Verification is the feature; the form is not | DT-02. A fast path without verification is a data-disclosure API with a police skin. |
| D-02 | Users are notified unless prohibited | DT-03. Notification is what makes quashing possible, and it is a choice about what kind of company this is. |
