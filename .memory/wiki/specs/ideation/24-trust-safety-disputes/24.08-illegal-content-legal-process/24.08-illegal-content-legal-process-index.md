## Overview

The children where the platform's counterparty is a **state** rather than a user: mandatory CSAM detection
and reporting, terrorist and violent extremist content removal, the portal through which law enforcement
and civil process arrive, and the risk-assessment register that governs which safety measures the platform
selects and can prove it selected. These duties do not scale with the business — 18 U.S.C. 2258A binds a
provider from its first user, with no micro-enterprise carve-out anywhere in sight.

**Why a sub-domain, not features**: four capabilities chained by a preservation duty and one escalation
path. A hash match (24.08.01) triggers preservation and a report; the report may bring a law-enforcement
request back through the portal (24.08.03); the portal's requests impose legal holds that override
deletion; and the risk register (24.08.04) is the document that says which of these the platform assessed
and why.

**The music-specific fact that makes this domain-relevant rather than boilerplate**: extremist *music* is a
real, organised, distributed category — NSBM, white-power rock, and their label networks — and a
marketplace selling physical media is a distribution channel. Every major music platform has had a public
incident. See 24.08.02 DT-01.
