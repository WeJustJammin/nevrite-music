## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: CSAM ↔ Law Enforcement Portal

**Relationship**: A confirmed match triggers report-and-preserve. The report goes out; a request may come
back. The two are one workflow with a gap of weeks in the middle, and the material must survive that gap
intact — which means the preservation hold has to be placed at report time, automatically, and cannot
depend on anyone remembering.

**Role scoping**:
- **All four personas**: no visibility, by design and by law. A user must not learn that their content
  triggered a report.
- **Admin/Moderator** *(not a persona — Q-02)*: minimal exposure by design (24.08 D-02).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the report owns the submission; the portal owns inbound requests. The
   **preservation hold** is shared state and it must be written by the report, not by the portal — by the
   time a request arrives, an ordinary retention job may already have deleted the evidence.
2. **Trigger chain**: match → preserve → report → (weeks) → request → produce. If preservation fails, the
   whole chain fails silently and nobody notices until a warrant arrives for material that no longer
   exists. **This chain must fail loudly.**
3. **Permission intersection**: access to preserved material must be narrower than ordinary operator
   access — a break-glass path the `Admin, Back-Office & Support` cross-cut's model already contemplates.
4. **Notification fan-out**: **none to the user, deliberately.** This is the one place in domain 24 where
   the platform acts against a user and tells them nothing, and that is correct.
5. **State transition conflict**: a user deletes their account between report and request. Deletion must
   not win. The `Privacy` cross-cut has already concluded that deletion means anonymise-and-retain; this
   is the case that proves it.

### CX-04: Law Enforcement Portal ↔ Case Evidence Locker

**Relationship**: A preservation request or a legal hold freezes a bundle against every other rule in the
system — including the user's own right to erasure. The locker's retention clock is not one clock; it is
several conflicting ones, and legal hold outranks all of them.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer / Operator / Fan**: affected invisibly. A user who exercises their deletion right
  while a hold is in place has their data retained, and generally cannot be told why. The `Privacy`
  cross-cut's answer — anonymise-and-retain — is what makes this survivable rather than a straight
  contradiction.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the locker owns the bundle; the portal writes a hold onto it. Only the portal
   (or the court) may lift it.
2. **Trigger chain**: request → verify (see 24.08.03 DT-02 — the request itself may be fraudulent) →
   hold → produce → (later) release. A hold placed on a fraudulent request is a privacy incident caused by
   compliance.
3. **Permission intersection**: a hold overrides the *user's* rights, which is the only place in the
   product where that happens by design rather than by defect.
4. **Notification fan-out**: often prohibited. A gag order means the platform cannot tell the user, and
   must not accidentally tell them through a UI state ("your deletion is pending").
5. **State transition conflict**: **yes, and it is the sharpest in the product** — a GDPR erasure request
   and a legal hold on the same data, with statutory clocks running in opposite directions. The `Privacy`
   cross-cut is right that this must be decided at architecture time rather than under a live DSAR clock.
