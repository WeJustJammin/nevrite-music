# Feature: CSAM Detection, Preservation & Mandatory Reporting

> **Number**: 24.08.01
> **Parent**: [Illegal Content & Legal Process](./24.08-illegal-content-legal-process-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Hash-matching of uploaded images and video against known child sexual abuse material, preservation of
matched content and its metadata, and the mandatory report to NCMEC. It is the only feature in domain 24
whose obligation is **criminal law with no size carve-out anywhere** — 18 U.S.C. 2258A binds a provider
from its first user, and unlike the DSA's Section 3 duties it does not wait for scale, revenue, or an
enterprise classification. It is a Must from user one, on a platform that lets people upload photographs
of amplifiers.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | No visibility. They upload gear photos, profile images and press shots — the same pipeline. They must never learn that a report was made, which is the one place in domain 24 where the platform acts and says nothing. If actioned, they see only the generic suspension notice from [24.02](../24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy/24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md) — never a CSAM-specific reason. |
| Producer | None | No visibility. Same pipeline: project artwork, reference images, session photos. Identical actioning behavior — generic notice, no disclosed basis. |
| Operator | None | No visibility. Uploads room photos, stage shots and crowd images — **crowd photographs at all-ages shows are a real upload category** and it is worth stating plainly that the platform's exposure here is not hypothetical. |
| Fan | None | No visibility, and the population that matters: D-11 makes fans first-class users, D-13 acknowledges minors are among them, and the `Safeguarding` cross-cut confirms that music teaching is overwhelmingly a minors market. |

> **The entire column is `None`, and that is the complete and correct answer.** No persona sees, configures,
> or appeals this feature. The actors are the platform itself (automatic), a single break-glass reviewer for
> the audio path only, and NCMEC. None are among the four personas. **Role is irrelevant to behavior** (DT-04):
> there is no verification tier, entity type, or "trusted uploader" status that exempts an upload from matching.

## Behavior

Hash matching runs on image and video at ingest. A match is **near-certainty against known material** —
categorically unlike classification (24.08 CX R-02) — and it triggers, automatically and without a human
decision: content blocked from ever being served, preservation hold placed, report filed with NCMEC,
account actioned, and a narrow break-glass path opened for the small number of people who may ever look at
it. The uploaded bytes are stored before they are servable; matching runs against the stored copy; content
becomes publicly reachable **only after** matching clears. On a match it is never served and is moved to a
preservation hold instead of the public store.

**Preservation is written by the report, not by the portal** (24.08 CX-01) — a request may arrive weeks
later, and by then an ordinary retention job may have deleted the evidence. The hold overrides deletion,
including a user's own erasure right; the `Privacy` cross-cut's "anonymise-and-retain, not DELETE CASCADE"
is what makes that coherent rather than contradictory.

**The report cannot be silently dropped.** NCMEC submission is a durable, retrying obligation with a
statutory "as soon as reasonably possible" standard: on transient failure it retries with persistence and
alerts a human; it **fails loud**, never silent. Each distinct upload event of matching material is its own
reportable incident — the platform does not suppress a second report because it has already seen the same
hash (DT-05).

**Access to hash sets and to the NCMEC pipeline is a vetted vendor relationship with strict handling
rules** (24.08 D-03) — it is not an API key you sign up for.

### Happy Path

1. An image or video is uploaded anywhere on the platform (gear photo, avatar, project art, cover image)
2. The bytes are stored but not yet publicly servable; hash matching runs at ingest against known material
3. **No match** → content is released to normal processing and becomes servable (the outcome for essentially all traffic)
4. **Match** → in one automatic transaction, with no human decision in the loop: content is blocked and never served, a preservation hold is written, an NCMEC report is filed, and the account is actioned
5. The preservation hold survives deletion, account closure and erasure requests until a court or the statutory clock releases it
6. A follow-up legal request, if it comes, arrives through [24.08.03](./24.08.03-law-enforcement-legal-process-portal.md) to material that still exists

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Match (image/video) | Automatic: block, preserve, report, action, in one transaction. **No reviewer sees it in a general queue** (24.08 D-02) | Nothing — deliberately |
| **Audio** file containing abuse material | **No hash set exists for audio CSAM.** Handled by user report + a single break-glass human review only; the audio path is the documented coverage gap (D-03, 24.08 Q-01) | Nothing during upload; the honest coverage gap is recorded in 24.08.04's risk register |
| **False positive** (perceptual hash is a similarity match, not a cryptographic identity) | Report and preservation still fire and are **not retracted by the platform** — the platform is not the adjudicator, NCMEC and law enforcement are. The account action is reversible; the content block is not lifted onto the public surface. **There is no self-service CSAM appeal** — exposing one would confirm detection to a true offender and force reviewer exposure. Reinstatement, if warranted, runs through the standard 24.02 suspension-review path without disclosing the CSAM basis | The generic 24.02 suspension notice — never a CSAM-specific reason. Residual counsel question → Q-04 |
| Reviewer exposure during the **audio** human step | Blur/caps/rotation do not exist on a one-person team. Concrete mitigation available now: access is single-viewer through a break-glass path with a logged justification; the platform builds **no** review queue and **no** thumbnail/preview surface for this material. The psychological safeguarding of a solo reviewer is a genuine open question | Nothing (user sees nothing) — reviewer safeguarding is Q-03 (24.01.03 Q-02 at its most serious) |
| Matching provider unavailable | **Fail closed on the upload path.** Publishing unscanned content is worse than delaying it; the only other place in domain 24 that fails closed is 24.02.05, for the same reason | "Upload temporarily unavailable — please try again shortly" |
| **NCMEC submission fails** (transient network/API error) | The report is queued and retried durably with backoff; a human is alerted if it cannot be delivered. **The report is never dropped** — the block/preserve/action already stand independently of submission success | Nothing |
| User **deletes their account** after a match | Preservation wins. Deletion is anonymise-and-retain, not DELETE CASCADE | Their deletion "succeeds"; the preserved data does not go |
| **Race**: match fires while the user is mid-deletion / a retention job is mid-sweep | The preservation hold is authoritative and is placed atomically with the match; a concurrent deletion or retention sweep must observe the hold and skip the held bytes. The chain **fails loud** if a hold cannot be confirmed — it must never assume success | Deletion appears to complete; held bytes are silently retained |
| **Concurrency**: the same matching file uploaded twice at once, or by two accounts | Each upload is matched independently and each generates its own incident and report (DT-05). Both accounts are actioned; neither report is suppressed by the other | Each affected account: generic 24.02 notice |
| **Re-upload** of already-reported material | Treated as a new incident — a fresh report and a fresh hold. The platform does not dedupe reports to reduce noise; under-reporting a criminal-law duty is not an acceptable optimisation | Nothing |
| GDPR erasure request over preserved material | Legal hold overrides; the platform generally cannot explain why (24.08 CX-04). The erasure is honoured for everything **except** the held bytes, which are retained | Erasure appears to complete; the platform is generally silent on the exception |
| **Empty / unhashable input** (zero-byte, truncated, or corrupt file that cannot be hashed) | Cannot be matched. It is treated as unscanned and **not released** to the public surface — fail closed, not fail open. The upload is rejected as invalid rather than published unscanned | "This file could not be processed — please re-upload" |
| **Partial data** (resumable/multipart upload not yet complete) | Matching runs only on the fully assembled file. No partial content is servable and no partial content is matched-and-cleared; an abandoned partial upload is never published | Normal upload-in-progress indicator; nothing CSAM-specific |
| **Malicious input**: adversary crafts a file to trigger a false positive to get a rival reported | The report still fires — the platform does not adjudicate — but the **reporter/uploader identity and full provenance are preserved with the material**, so a weaponised false report is itself evidence. This does not change the automatic behavior; it is why provenance is preserved alongside the bytes | Nothing (the targeted account is actioned; see false-positive row for reinstatement) |
| **Malicious probing**: adversary uploads then instantly deletes to test whether detection fired | Detection and preservation are synchronous with ingest and precede any deletion; an instant delete cannot outrun the hold, and the delete does not remove held bytes | Deletion appears to complete |
| **Multi-hyphenate in two roles** (same human uploads as Musician *and* as an Operator entity's admin) | Behavior is **role-blind** (DT-04): the automatic path does not consult persona, entity membership, or verification tier. The same human in any role, and any entity they act for, is matched and actioned identically | Nothing / generic 24.02 notice, identical across roles |
| **Novel (not previously hashed) material** | Hash matching finds nothing — it only knows *known* material. Classification of novel CSAM is a specialised capability the platform will not have; this is a stated gap, not a silent one | The gap, recorded in 24.08.04 |

### States

> The standard state set, mapped to this invisible pipeline. Because no persona has a surface here, most
> "What User Sees" cells are deliberately empty — the honest answer, not an omission.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | File received; hash matching in progress at ingest | Normal upload progress — must not perceptibly block the user-visible path, and must not hint that scanning occurs |
| Empty | No user-facing surface exists for this feature | Nothing — this pipeline has no empty state by nature; it is invisible plumbing, and that N/A is stated rather than faked |
| Populated | Match confirmed; block + preserve + report + action committed | Nothing (the user is never told a match occurred) |
| Error | Matching provider unavailable, or input unhashable/corrupt | "Upload temporarily unavailable" (provider down) or "This file could not be processed" (unhashable) — **fail closed**, never publish unscanned |
| Partial | Resumable/multipart upload incomplete; only part of the bytes present | Normal upload-in-progress indicator; no partial content is matched-and-cleared or served |

> **Internal states** (never surfaced to any persona, retained for the audit trail): `Scanning` (upload) →
> `Clear` (no match, servable) or `Matched` (hash hit) → `Preserved` (hold placed) → `Reported` (NCMEC
> submission durable/retrying) → `Held` (legal hold active; deletion silently does not happen). A user's
> deletion or erasure attempt while `Held` transitions nothing — it is silently a no-op on the held bytes.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A music platform's CSAM exposure is limited to images, and hash-matching covers it | The comfortable scoping | ❌ REJECTED — **there is no equivalent hash set for audio**, and this platform's primary medium is audio. The image/video path is covered by mature, industry-shared matching; the audio path has no matcher, no reference set, and no industry programme, so it relies entirely on someone reporting it and a human listening. On a platform built around audio upload, with a minors population (D-13, Safeguarding), **that is a genuine coverage gap and it should be written down as one** rather than left implicit for someone to discover during an incident. Stating it is also the honest input to 24.08.04's risk register: you cannot assess a risk you have not named. |
| DT-02 | This is scale-gated — small platforms deal with it when they get big | The instinct that governs every other duty in this domain, and it is wrong exactly here | ❌ REJECTED — **and the contrast is the point.** Nearly everything else in domain 24 has a size carve-out: DSA Art 19 disapplies Section 3 (appeals, trusted flaggers) for micro/small enterprises; Art 15(2) exempts them from transparency reporting. It is easy to absorb the general lesson "small platform, deferred duties" and apply it here. **18 U.S.C. 2258A has no such carve-out** — the reporting duty attaches to any provider, from the first user, and it is criminal law rather than administrative. The general lesson does not apply, and the mistake is a natural one to make. This is the single clearest Must in domain 24 and the reasoning that gets you there is *not* the same reasoning that got you to the others. |
| DT-03 | Detection is the hard part | Where attention naturally goes | ❌ REJECTED — **preservation is**, and it is the part that silently fails. Detection is a vendor call. Preservation must be written by the report, survive deletion, survive account closure, survive an erasure request, survive an ordinary retention job, and still be there when a warrant arrives weeks later (24.08 CX-01). Every one of those is a different subsystem that has no idea this hold exists, and the failure is invisible — nobody discovers that the retention job deleted the evidence until law enforcement asks for it. The `Privacy` cross-cut independently reached the same architectural conclusion from the erasure side, which is corroboration: **this collision must be decided at architecture time, not under a live clock.** |
| DT-04 | The automatic path should exempt trusted or verified uploaders to reduce false-positive load | The efficiency instinct — treat established users as lower-risk | ✅ CONFIRMED that it must **not** — the path is role-blind by design. The reporting duty attaches to the provider regardless of *who* uploaded, so a "trusted uploader" exemption buys nothing legally and creates exactly the hole an offender seeks: obtain trust, then upload under it. Verification tiers, entity membership, and persona are all irrelevant to matching. This is textually distinct from DT-02 (which is about *provider* scale, not *user* trust): DT-02 rejects deferring the duty by platform size; DT-04 rejects narrowing it by user standing. Both roads lead to "the duty is universal," from opposite directions. |
| DT-05 | Re-uploads of already-seen material can be deduplicated so the platform reports each hash only once | The noise-reduction instinct — one incident, one report | ❌ REJECTED — **each upload event is a distinct reportable incident.** Deduping reports to cut volume would systematically under-report a criminal-law duty and would erase the fact that the material is being *re-distributed*, which is itself the thing law enforcement needs to see. The platform stores the hash to *detect* faster, not to *report* less. This is distinct from DT-03 (preservation of the bytes): DT-05 is about the report count and the distinctness of incidents; DT-03 is about the durability of the evidence for a single incident. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Fully automatic on match: block, preserve, report, action — no human decision | DT-03 + 24.08 D-02. A decision point is a place where it does not happen. |
| D-02 | Preservation is written by the report, not by the request | DT-03. By request time the evidence may be gone. |
| D-03 | The audio coverage gap is **documented, not papered over** | DT-01. An unnamed risk cannot be assessed, and 24.08.04 needs it named. |
| D-04 | The automatic path is **role-blind** — no persona, entity, or verification tier exempts an upload | DT-04. A trust exemption is the hole an offender seeks and buys nothing legally. |
| D-05 | Each upload is a **distinct incident**; reports are never deduped, and the NCMEC report **fails loud, never silent** | DT-05 + the criminal-law standard. Under-reporting to cut noise is not an available optimisation, and a dropped report is a compliance failure. |
| D-06 | **No self-service CSAM appeal.** A false positive surfaces only as the generic 24.02 suspension notice; reinstatement runs through the standard suspension-review path without disclosing the CSAM basis | Exposing a CSAM-specific appeal confirms detection to a true offender and forces reviewer exposure. Fail-closed on unhashable/empty input for the same fail-closed reason as the provider-down path. |
