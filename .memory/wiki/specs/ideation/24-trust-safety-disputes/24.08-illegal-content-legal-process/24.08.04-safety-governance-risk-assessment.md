# Feature: Safety Governance & Illegal-Harms Risk Assessment

> **Number**: 24.08.04
> **Parent**: [Illegal Content & Legal Process](./24.08-illegal-content-legal-process-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The living register that records which harms the platform assessed, what it concluded, which measures it
selected as a result, and what evidence exists that the measures work — re-run whenever the service changes
materially. The UK Online Safety Act's illegal-harms and children's-access duties require an assessment
that is recorded and drives measure selection; the DSA has adjacent expectations.

**This feature is flagged honestly**: it may be a `/create-prd-security` compliance artifact rather than a
product node (24.08 Q-03). It is retained here because the domain's own rationale names OSA illegal-harms
duties as a discriminator that justifies the domain existing, and because a register that binds
*assessment → measure → evidence* has an admin surface and real product consequences.

|------|--------|-------------------|

| Producer | None | No visibility. Same. |

| Fan | None | No visibility — while being the population that **triggers** most of the duties. D-13 says it explicitly: statutory duties scale with consumer reach, and children's-access assessment exists because of them. The persona that causes the obligation cannot see it, which is the correct answer and an odd one. |

## Behavior

The register enumerates harms in scope, the platform's exposure to each, the measures selected, and the
evidence for them. Its value is the **binding**: a measure with no assessed harm is unexplained, and an
assessed harm with no measure is an accepted risk that must be recorded *as accepted* rather than left
implicit.

**Re-assessment on material change** is the part with product consequence. Launching a domain changes the
risk surface: shipping the gear marketplace adds in-person meetings; shipping lessons adds minors at scale;
shipping ticketing adds a fan population and a public location graph. Each is a material change, and the
register is what says the platform noticed.

Domain 24's own honest gaps belong here as recorded, accepted risks: the audio CSAM gap (24.08.01 DT-01),
the absence of on-platform triangulation signals (24.08 — see 24.03.04 Q-01), the ceiling of messaging
safety (24.01.05 DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. Harms in scope are enumerated for the service as it currently exists
2. Exposure is assessed per harm, with reasoning
3. Measures are selected and bound to the harms they address
4. Gaps are recorded as **accepted risks**, with the reason, not omitted
5. A material change (a new domain launches) triggers re-assessment
6. The register is retained immutably — what it said in 2027 is the question later

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A new domain launches and changes the risk surface | Re-assessment. **This is where the register earns its place** — nobody re-runs a compliance document unless something makes them | Nothing |
| An assessed harm has no measure (the audio CSAM gap) | Recorded as an accepted risk with the reason. **An unrecorded gap is indistinguishable from an unnoticed one**, and the difference is everything | Nothing |
| The register says one thing and the product does another | The register is worse than useless — it is evidence of knowledge without action, the same defect as 24.05.01 DT-01's unimplemented repeat-infringer policy | Nothing |
| The assessment is written once and never touched | The modal failure of every compliance document ever produced | Nothing — which is the problem |
| A harm nobody thought of | The register cannot find unknown unknowns; it can record that the enumeration method was thin | `[PENDING]` |
| The assessment is unflattering | It is an internal record, not a publication — unlike the transparency report (24.02.06) | Nothing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Absent | Pre-launch | Nothing — and the duty may already attach |
| Current | Assessed against the service as it is | Nothing |
| Stale | Service changed materially; not re-assessed | Nothing — the dangerous state, and it is invisible |
| Re-assessed | Change processed | Nothing |
| Archived | Superseded | Prior versions retained; what it said then is the question later |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a document, not a product feature — route it to `/create-prd-security` | The Node Classification Gate's not-product test, correctly applied | ⏳ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and retained with the tension stated.** The assessment itself is a compliance artifact and no persona opens the app to use it (the Role Lens is four `None`s, which is a strong not-product signal). **But** the register has two properties a document does not: it *binds* assessment → measure → evidence, which makes it queryable and auditable; and it must **re-run on material change**, which makes it an event-driven thing with an admin surface rather than a file. The domain map's own rationale also names OSA illegal-harms duties as a discriminator justifying the domain. The honest position: this is the weakest product claim in domain 24, it is flagged as such (24.08 Q-03), and the owner may well route it out. **Recording the uncertainty is more useful than resolving it confidently in either direction.** |
| DT-02 | The assessment is a launch checkbox — do it once, file it | How risk assessments are actually treated everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — **re-assessment on material change is the entire value**, and on this platform material change is the roadmap. Twenty-four domains launching over time means the risk surface changes repeatedly and radically: gear adds in-person meetings between strangers; lessons add minors at scale; ticketing adds a consumer population and a public location graph (24.06.01 DT-01); digital goods add instant-fulfilment card testing (24.03.04 DT-01). **Each of those is a different platform from a safety standpoint**, and a register assessed against the launch version describes a service that no longer exists. This is the one argument that makes the register product-shaped rather than documentary: it is coupled to the release process. |
| DT-03 | An assessment with gaps is a failure — better to omit what you cannot address | The instinct to only write down what looks good | ❌ REJECTED, and inverted — **a recorded, accepted risk is a far stronger position than an unrecorded one**, because the difference between "we assessed this and accepted it for these reasons" and silence is the difference between judgment and negligence. Domain 24 has produced several honest gaps that belong in the register precisely *because* they are gaps: no hash set for audio CSAM (24.08.01 DT-01); no on-platform triangulation signal (24.03.04 DT-02); messaging safety cannot reach off-platform (24.01.05 DT-02); the block does not unpublish the gig listing (24.06.01 DT-01). **Every one of those is a finding, and the register is the only place in the product where a finding can be stored as a finding** rather than lost in a spec's prose. |
