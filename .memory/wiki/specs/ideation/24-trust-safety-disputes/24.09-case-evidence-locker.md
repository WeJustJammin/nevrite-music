# Feature: Case Evidence Locker & Chain of Custody

> **Number**: 24.09
> **Parent**: [Trust, Safety & Disputes](./trust-safety-disputes-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The shared spine under every adjudication surface in domain 24: a case-scoped, immutable bundle of the
evidence **as it existed at the moment that mattered**, with a chain of custody. It sits directly under the
domain rather than inside a sub-domain because every one of them depends on it — moderation appeals, split
disputes, chargeback representments, DMCA counter-notices, ownership disputes, leak findings, and law-
enforcement productions all reach for the same bundle ([24 CX-02](./trust-safety-disputes-cx.md)).

Its defining property is **snapshotting**. Referencing a live object loses the case: a seller who edits a
listing after the sale has destroyed the evidence unless it was captured at transaction time. This is the
product's own thesis (D-18) applied to disputes — the fact must be captured at the moment it is true,
because reconstruction at the moment of contest is exactly what does not work.

The chain of custody is the second half of the feature. A snapshot with no provenance is a screenshot —
forgeable, deniable, worthless in an adversarial process. Every item in a bundle carries: what it is, the
content hash it was sealed under, the event that produced it, the timestamp of capture (system clock, not
user-supplied), and the immutable ordering position that lets an adjudicator prove a message posted *after*
a dispute opened did not exist *before* it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only — own case only | Sees the bundle for **their own case, scoped to their own case** — the listing as it was, the thread, the delivery proof, the session record. Cannot add to it retroactively, which is the point and will occasionally feel unfair to someone who genuinely forgot to photograph the amp. When the Musician is a **party in one case and a named collaborator in someone else's** (e.g. a leak finding, 24.05.04), they see their own case in full and see only the **accusation surface** of the case that names them — the item that concerns them plus the chain of custody proving it, never the reporter identity or unrelated parties' PII. |
| Producer | Read-only — own case only | Same, and the persona whose bundle is richest: the brief, the revision thread, the delivered versions, the approval, the session provenance. Their disputes are the ones the platform can actually adjudicate on facts (24.04.01 DT-03), and this is why. As the **session owner** they frequently appear in bundles for cases between two *other* contributors; there they see only their own attestations and the items they authored, scoped by the same rule. |
| Operator | Read-only — own case only | Same. Their bundles are the **thinnest** — a booking has no delivery signature and no item, so the evidence for "the band didn't turn up" is a hold record, a deposit event, and a message thread, which is why their chargebacks are the hardest to defend (24.04.04 DT-03). |
| Fan | Read-only — own case only | Same, scoped to their own case. Sees an order snapshot and a thread; the simplest bundle in the product. A Fan who is **also a hobbyist Musician** (the porous boundary in personas.md) sees each of their cases through whichever persona lens the case's object belongs to — a gear purchase dispute is the Musician-as-buyer bundle, a ticket refund is the Fan bundle — because scope follows the case's object, not the human's self-description. |

> **None has write access — this is the entire design.** Evidence is written by the system at the moment of
> the event, never by a party at the moment of the dispute. There is no "upload evidence" affordance anywhere
> in this feature. A party who wants to add context does so through the **messaging thread** (a captured
> source), where their contribution is timestamped and ordered — visible as the after-the-fact statement it
> is, not laundered into contemporaneous evidence. The explicit absence of a write path is the load-bearing
> control and every downstream spec must preserve it.

## Behavior

The locker assembles, per case, from sources it does not own: the `Audit Log & Provenance Ledger`
cross-cut (who did what, when, on what basis), the `Messaging & Contextual Inbox` cross-cut (the thread
bound to the object), media as it was at the time, the listing/booking/brief snapshot, the delivery proof,
and the executed contracts (`Contracts & E-Signature` cross-cut).

**Snapshots, not references.** A snapshot of the listing photo at transaction time is evidence; a link to
the current listing photo is a link to whatever the seller changed it to. For **small structured data**
(a listing's fields, a split table, an order line) the snapshot is a full serialized copy captured
transactionally with the event. For **large media** (audio stems, session material) the snapshot is a
**content-addressed reference** — the SHA-256 hash of the bytes as they were at the event — pointing at the
immutable blob the `Media Handling` cross-cut already stores; the bundle copies the *hash and manifest*, not
the gigabytes. This defeats mutation (a changed file has a different hash, so substitution is detectable)
without duplicating multi-GB payloads per case. The economic question of who pays to *retain the underlying
blob* long enough for a case is real and unresolved — see Q-02 — but the **behavior** is fixed: the bundle
holds a hash, the media store holds the bytes, and a mismatch or a purged blob is surfaced explicitly, never
silently.

**Capture is durable, not best-effort.** The *intent* to capture a snapshot is recorded **atomically with the
event itself** — the event and the "capture this" instruction either both take effect or neither does, so a
crash in between can never leave an event on the record with its evidence silently un-captured. The actual
capture then runs asynchronously and retries on failure (5 attempts over ~1 hour). If capture ultimately
fails, the bundle records a `capture-failed` marker for that item with the reason code — the gap is made
**explicit**, because a bundle that silently omits an item it should have held is worse than one that admits
the hole.

**Retention is not one clock.** CSAM preservation, chargeback windows, tax retention, open disputes, legal
holds and GDPR erasure all impose conflicting requirements on the same bundle. The reconciling rule is
**retain until the maximum of all applicable clocks expires, subject to override by legal hold**:

- A bundle's purge-eligible date is the **latest** expiry among every clock that applies to it.
- A **legal hold** sets that date to infinity until the hold is released (24.08 CX-08) — it outranks every
  other clock, including a user's own erasure right. This is the one place the platform defeats a user's
  right by design, and it does so **silently** (the held party is not told a hold exists).
- A **GDPR erasure request** against a bundle still inside another clock's window resolves to
  **anonymise-and-retain, not a blanket hard-delete of the whole bundle** (the `Privacy` cross-cut's named
  rule): PII fields are tombstoned, financial and evidentiary records are kept. The user is told the bundle is retained; they are
  **not** told why when the reason is a legal hold (disclosing the hold could itself be unlawful).

The **exact numeric value** of each statutory clock (CSAM preservation period, card-network representment
window, tax retention years) is a legal fact, not a product decision, and is deferred to counsel at
`/create-prd-security` — see Q-03. What is decided here is the *arithmetic* (max-of-clocks, hold = infinity,
erasure = anonymise) so the schema can be built around it.

**Both parties see the same bundle** (24.04.02) — asymmetric evidence is what makes people fight. Neither
party can see items the other's PII-scoping rule hides, but the *evidentiary core* (the object, the thread,
the delivery proof) is identical for both.

### Happy Path

1. A consequential event occurs — a listing publishes, an item ships, a mix is delivered, a message is sent,
   a deposit is taken, a split is signed.
2. The system snapshots what is capturable **at that moment**, as a byproduct of the event: structured data
   copied in full, large media captured by content hash, each item stamped with capture time and the event id.
3. Capture is committed transactionally with the event; on failure it retries and, if exhausted, records an
   explicit `capture-failed` marker.
4. Later, a case opens (a claim, a report, a notice, a chargeback, a request).
5. The locker assembles the relevant sealed snapshots into a **case-scoped bundle** with a chain of custody
   — every item ordered, hashed, and attributed.
6. Both parties and the adjudicator open the same bundle; each party's view is PII-scoped, the evidentiary
   core is identical.
7. The case is decided; the bundle is **sealed** — frozen as it was at decision.
8. The bundle survives the case, retained until the maximum applicable clock expires, unless a legal hold
   pins it indefinitely, then purged.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Mutation** — seller edits the listing after the sale | The snapshot holds; the live edit is a new object with a new hash. The single most common evidence-destruction move in marketplaces, defeated by construction | Both parties see the listing as it was at purchase; the edit appears on the live listing only |
| **Empty** — nothing was captured at event time | The bundle is two people's memories; the case is unwinnable on facts. Bundle enters `Incomplete` and says so | The honest answer (24.03.03 Q-03): "No contemporaneous record exists for this item" — never a blank that implies completeness |
| **Permission** — a party requests their bundle while account-suspended | Read access to **their own open case** persists — sanctions act on privileges, never on the record (index line 42); due process requires the accused can see the case against them | Bundle opens read-only despite suspension; other product surfaces stay gated |
| **Permission** — a party tries to view a case they are only *named* in (leak finding) | Scope rule returns only the accusation surface + its chain of custody; reporter identity and unrelated PII are withheld | The item that concerns them and the custody proving it — nothing else |
| **Network / storage failure** at capture time | Capture retries (5×, ~1h backoff); on exhaustion the item is marked `capture-failed` with a reason code. The event itself is **not blocked** — a shipment does not fail because evidence capture failed | If that case later opens, the bundle shows `capture-failed` for that item, not a silent gap |
| **Malicious input** — a party posts a forged document into the thread after the dispute opened | The thread is captured with true system timestamps and immutable ordering; the forged item is stamped *after* the dispute-open event, so its post-hoc nature is visible to the adjudicator | Adjudicator sees the item's real capture time relative to the case timeline; fabrication is not hidden |
| **Malicious input** — a party edits or deletes their own earlier message to rewrite history | Messaging cross-cut treats edits/deletes as **new events**; the version captured at the time is what the bundle holds | Bundle shows the message as it was when snapshotted; the later edit is a separate, later event |
| **Partial data** — a thread moved off-platform mid-negotiation | The bundle has the on-platform half; the transition point is timestamped so the gap is a visible fact, not a pretence of completeness | The gap is explicit in the timeline — better than pretending the record is whole |
| **Partial data** — a snapshot references media the media store already purged under a shorter clock | The bundle shows a **tombstone**: the content hash and manifest survive (proving something of that exact byte-content existed), the bytes are gone | "Content retained by reference only — original media purged on {date}" with the hash |
| **Concurrency** — a case opens at the instant an event's snapshot is mid-write | Assembly sees only **fully-captured** snapshots; an item whose capture is still in flight is either fully present or absent-and-retrying, never a half-written fragment | Bundle is internally consistent; a still-capturing item shows as pending, then resolves |
| **Race** — two cases open over the same underlying object (a listing in both a transaction dispute *and* a DMCA claim) | One shared immutable snapshot feeds **two separate case-scoped bundles**; closing/purging one case never affects the other's retention | Each case shows the same object identically; neither case's lifecycle leaks into the other |
| **Race** — legal hold lands on a bundle that another process has just marked purge-eligible | Hold wins; purge re-checks hold status **at the moment of deletion** and abandons the delete if a hold now exists, so a hold that arrives any time before the bytes are actually removed always saves the bundle | Nothing user-facing; the bundle simply does not purge |
| **Malicious volume** — a party opens frivolous cases to force repeated bundle assembly (resource abuse) | Case-opening is rate-limited upstream (24.04 concern); bundle assembly is idempotent and cached per case, so re-opening the same case does not re-assemble from scratch | Handled at the case-open surface, not here; the locker does no unbounded work |
| **Scale** — a bundle references a tracked album session (stems 5–20 GB, sessions to hundreds of GB) | Bundle holds hashes + manifest, not copies (see Behavior); the cost is *keeping the underlying blobs alive* for the retention window, which is the open economic question | No user-facing difference; the cost lands on architecture — see Q-02 |
| **Multi-hyphenate in two roles in one case** — a user is the seller (Musician-as-seller) *and* the session owner (Producer) whose attestation is also in evidence | The locker scopes by **case object and party role in that case**, not by the human. The same human sees the seller-side view and, separately, their own Producer attestation; they never gain the counterparty's PII by virtue of wearing two hats | One case, one bundle, one PII-scoped view — the extra role adds their own attestation, nothing of the other party's |
| **Leak finding names a collaborator** (24.05.04) | The bundle *is* an accusation and will be contested by someone with a lot to lose; the chain of custody is what makes it defensible | The named collaborator sees the accusation surface + custody; the finding stands or falls on provenance, not assertion |
| **Legal hold on a bundle** | Overrides every other clock, indefinitely and silently (24.08 CX-08) | Nothing — the held party is not told the hold exists |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Accruing | Consequential events occurring; snapshots being captured as a byproduct | Nothing — capture is invisible; there is no case yet |
| Assembling | Case opened; locker gathering committed snapshots into the bundle | Brief pending indicator while committed items are collected (bounded — reads committed data only, no external calls) |
| Assembled | Assembly complete; case active | Both parties + adjudicator: the same PII-scoped bundle, scoped to the case |
| Partial | One or more items `capture-failed` or reference purged media | The bundle plus an explicit missing-item indicator per gap, with reason code — never an implied-complete view |
| Incomplete | No contemporaneous record was captured for the disputed fact | Explicit statement that no record exists — the honest empty, never a blank implying completeness |
| Error | Assembly could not read a source store (transient) | Retryable error with retry affordance for the adjudicator; parties see "evidence loading" not a failure |
| Sealed | Case decided | The bundle frozen as it was at decision; no further items accrue to it |
| Held | Legal hold placed | Nothing user-facing; retention pinned to infinity |
| Retained | Case closed, no hold, clocks running | Nothing; bundle exists, purge-eligible date = max of applicable clocks |
| Purged | Every applicable clock expired and no hold present | Nothing; PII anonymised-and-retained where a financial/evidentiary clock still applies, fully removed only when all clocks have expired |

> **Transition guards.** Accruing → Assembling only on a real case open. Any state → Held on hold placement,
> and Held → (prior state) only on hold *release* by an authorized process. Retained → Purged only when
> `now ≥ max(all applicable clocks)` **and** no hold — checked atomically at delete time (see the legal-hold
> race above). Sealed is terminal for accrual: a sealed bundle never gains items, even if the underlying
> object keeps changing.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The audit log **is** the evidence locker — build one thing | The tempting consolidation; both are cross-cut-shaped | ❌ REJECTED — they are different objects with different properties. The `Audit Log & Provenance Ledger` cross-cut is a **system-wide, append-only stream of actions**: who did what, when, on what basis. The locker is a **case-scoped bundle of snapshots**: the listing photo as it was, the mix as delivered, the thread as it stood. The audit log records that the listing was edited; **it does not contain the old photo**. In a used-gear dispute, the old photo *is* the case. The locker reads the audit log and adds what the log structurally cannot hold — content at a point in time. Merging them produces either an audit log bloated with media or a locker with no evidence in it. |
| DT-02 | Evidence can be gathered when a case opens | The way every dispute system works | ❌ REJECTED — **this is the product's thesis, and the reason this feature sits at domain level rather than inside disputes.** `problem-statement.md`: "the facts of the work are never recorded... then reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." A case *is* that moment. The condition photo, the serial, the delivery signature, the brief, the approval, the session record — all capturable as a byproduct of the event and none at filing. And it is not one sub-domain's problem: 24.03.03 DT-02 (returns), 24.04.01 DT-02 (claims) and 24.04.04 DT-01 (chargebacks) each reached this conclusion independently, from different directions. **Three independent derivations of the same principle is the signal that it belongs one level up.** |
| DT-03 | Retention is one policy — pick a number of years | The way it gets specified | ❌ REJECTED — **the same bundle is subject to several clocks running in opposite directions.** CSAM preservation demands retention (24.08.01); chargeback windows demand months; tax demands years; an open dispute demands indefinite; GDPR erasure demands deletion; a legal hold demands everything stays. The `Privacy` cross-cut names this collision explicitly and reaches the right answer — "delete my account" must mean anonymise-and-retain-financials, not a blanket hard-delete — and adds the crucial point that it is **far cheaper to decide at architecture time than under a live DSAR clock**. The resolution is arithmetic: retain to the **max** of applicable clocks, legal hold = infinity, erasure = anonymise. The *numbers* are legal facts (Q-03); the *rule* is decided here. |
| DT-04 | Parties should be able to upload their own evidence into the bundle | The obvious usability instinct — "let people make their case" | ❌ REJECTED — **a write path is the reconstruction the product exists to prevent (D-02).** The moment a party can add to the bundle at dispute time, the bundle stops being a record of what was true and becomes a record of what each side *claims* was true — which is exactly the memory-vs-memory failure the thesis attacks. The usability need is real, so it is **redirected, not denied**: a party adds context through the captured messaging thread, where their contribution is timestamped and ordered as the after-the-fact statement it is. The distinction between contemporaneous evidence and post-hoc argument is preserved by construction rather than by asking an adjudicator to guess. |
| DT-05 | The bundle should copy every referenced media file so it is self-contained | The archival instinct — a bundle you can hand over whole | ❌ REJECTED — **at multi-GB-per-case, per-case copying is economically ruinous and buys nothing over content-addressing.** A tracked album session is 5–20 GB of stems; hundreds of cases would multiply that. A SHA-256 hash in the bundle pointing at the immutable blob in the `Media Handling` store gives the same tamper-evidence (a changed file mismatches its hash) at kilobytes of overhead. The genuine risk this introduces — the underlying blob being purged before the case needs it — is made **explicit** via the tombstone (hash survives, bytes gone) rather than hidden. Self-containment is an illusion anyway once bundles reference contracts, threads, and audit rows that also live elsewhere. |
| DT-06 | Capture can be best-effort — a missed snapshot is a rare, acceptable loss | The pragmatic shortcut ("don't let evidence capture block a shipment") | ❌ REJECTED as stated, **but its valid half is preserved.** It is true that capture must **not block** the primary event — you cannot fail a shipment because a snapshot write timed out. But "best-effort fire-and-forget" silently loses evidence, and a silently-empty bundle is the worst outcome for a provenance product. The synthesis separates the two: the *intent* to capture is guaranteed — recorded atomically with the event so it is never lost — while the *capture itself* is asynchronous and retried so it never blocks the event, and terminal failure is recorded as an explicit `capture-failed` marker so a gap is admitted, not hidden. **Guaranteed intent, best-effort delivery, admitted failure.** |

This is [24 CX-02](./trust-safety-disputes-cx.md) and it is why the feature exists at this level. **Step 6 note**: every adjudication surface consumes the *same* bundle contract — the read API and PII-scoping rule must be defined once, here, and reused, not re-invented per sub-domain.
- Touches **Domain 13 (Gear Marketplace)** — the listing schema is what makes the snapshot worth having (24.04.04 DT-01). **Step 6 note**: the listing snapshot must capture condition, serial, and photos as structured fields, not a rendered HTML blob, or the bundle cannot be queried in a dispute.
- Touches **Domain 07 (Projects)** — the session record that makes creative-service disputes decidable (24.04.01 DT-03). **Step 6 note**: the "moment that matters" capture in Projects (credit + split confirmed on the day) is the *event* the locker snapshots; the two features share one capture trigger.
- Touches **Domain 17 (Live Booking)** — the hold record + deposit event are the *only* evidence in an Operator no-show chargeback (24.04.04 DT-03). **Step 6 note**: because these bundles are the thinnest, the booking/hold/deposit events must be captured with extra care — there is no delivery signature to fall back on.
- Consumed by the **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — the locker reads it; DT-01 explains why it is not the same thing. Its own line — "in a dispute the question is never what the record says now but what it said in 2027 and who changed it" — is this feature's specification.
- Consumed by the **Messaging & Contextual Inbox** cross-cut — the thread bound to the object is evidence, and DT-04 makes the thread the *only* sanctioned path for a party to add context to a case. **Step 6 note**: message edits/deletes must be modelled as new events, or the "post-hoc fabrication is visible" guarantee breaks.
- Consumed by the **Media Handling & Audio Playback** cross-cut — snapshots of media are content-addressed hashes into its immutable blob store (DT-05), at multi-GB scale (Q-02). **Step 6 note**: media blobs referenced by an open or held bundle must be exempt from the media store's own shorter purge clock, or bundles tombstone prematurely.
- Consumed by the **Privacy, Consent & Data Portability** cross-cut — DT-03's clock collision is its named conflict, arriving in one concrete place; the anonymise-and-retain rule is shared. **Step 6 note**: the erasure handler must know how to anonymise a bundle (PII tombstoning) without breaking its chain-of-custody hashes.
- Consumed by the **Contracts & E-Signature** cross-cut — its tamper-evident executed archive is a sibling of this bundle; the signed split/agreement is a first-class evidence item.

|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Snapshots, never references | DT-02. A reference to a live object is a reference to whatever the other party changed it to. |
| D-02 | No party may write to a bundle | DT-04. Evidence is written by the system at the event. A party adding evidence at dispute time is the reconstruction the product exists to prevent. Context is redirected to the captured messaging thread. |
