# Feature: Deceased & Incapacitated Account Succession

> **Number**: 24.07.03
> **Parent**: [Identity Abuse & Ownership Disputes](./24.07-identity-abuse-ownership-disputes-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's candidate list

## Overview

What happens to an account, an entity and a catalogue when the person behind them dies or becomes
incapacitated. On a social network this is memorialisation — a respectful freeze. **Here it cannot be a
freeze**, because the account is an operating business: royalties keep arriving, sync requests keep coming,
splits keep paying, and a band with a dead member cannot make decisions that require unanimous consent.
Music's economics are posthumous by design, and a rights registry with no succession path becomes useless
to the estate at the exact moment the record it holds is most valuable.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both as the person who dies and as the surviving bandmate. Their catalogue is an inheritable asset — often the only one they have — and their splits become someone else's property. The surviving band faces a governance deadlock: unanimous decisions with a member who cannot consent. |
| Producer | Full | Their points on records keep earning, and their account holds other artists' unreleased material. A producer's death freezes six projects belonging to people who are not their heirs — the estate inherits the points and has no business holding the stems. |
| Operator | Full | Ordinary commercial succession of a business — the least exceptional case here, and the one existing law handles well. A venue outlives its owner routinely. |
| Fan | Read-only | A deceased fan's account is memorialised; the standard social answer is the correct one, because there is no asset. They also *experience* an artist's memorialised presence — and are the population most likely to interact with it, which is why the impersonation exposure matters (24.07 CX-03). |

## Behavior

Notification of death is verified (death certificate, probate, or an equivalent). Consequential actions
freeze — **accruals do not.** A frozen account that stops earning inflicts a financial loss on a grieving
family via a data model, and it would be the platform's most avoidable cruelty.

Succession **transfers control**; it does not lock the door. An executor is a mandate holder of a kind
domain 01's model may not have — someone who acts *for* a person rather than being one — and once
established they need real capability: redirect payouts, license a sync, approve a release, respond to a
claim.

Incapacity is harder than death: no certificate, no probate, a power of attorney that may or may not
cover digital assets, and a person who may recover.

**The bandmate case is the one nobody plans for**: a band entity requiring unanimity has a member who
cannot consent, and every decision stops until succession resolves it.

### Happy Path

1. A death or incapacity is reported by a family member, a bandmate, or a co-owner
2. Verification: certificate, probate, letters of administration, or an equivalent
3. Consequential actions freeze; **royalties, splits and accruals continue**
4. The estate's standing is established against domain 01's entity model
5. Control transfers to the executor with real capability, not read-only access
6. Co-owners and collaborators whose splits touch the deceased's works are notified

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Death is an edge case — rare, low-volume, defer it | The scoping instinct, and it is precisely backwards for this industry | ❌ REJECTED — **music's economics are posthumous by design.** Catalogues are *the* asset class in this business; estates are among its largest and most active actors; a musician's rights outlive them by seventy years and frequently earn more afterwards than before. A platform whose thesis is that it holds the ownership record end-to-end (D-10, D-18) and which has no succession path becomes **unusable to the estate at the exact moment the record it holds is most valuable** — which is the moment the entire thesis is finally proved. It is not an edge case; it is the destination the whole product is pointed at. Volume is low and stakes are total. |
| DT-02 | Memorialisation means locking the account — freeze it respectfully | Every social platform's answer, and it is the wrong product | ❌ REJECTED — **this account is an operating business.** A locked account cannot receive royalties, cannot license a sync, cannot approve a compilation, cannot respond to a copyright claim, cannot correct a wrong split. Locking it does not preserve the estate's asset; it strands it — and every one of those events will happen, because a catalogue's whole nature is that things keep happening to it. Succession must **transfer operating control**, not seal the room. The memorial (a profile a fan sees) and the business (a catalogue that earns) are two different things sharing one account, and the model must separate them. |
| DT-03 | Succession is a legal formality — take the probate documents and hand over the keys | It looks procedural | ❌ REJECTED — **succession collides with the platform's other absolutes in ways nothing else does.** The family wants the account deleted; a co-writer's 25% split lives in the same record, so deletion is impossible (24.02.01 DT-01; the Privacy cross-cut's "anonymise-and-retain, not DELETE CASCADE"). The estate inherits the producer's points but has no business holding six other artists' unreleased masters. The surviving band needs the seat filled to decide anything, and the executor is a stranger with a legal right and no relationship. **This is the one feature where the rights model, the privacy model, the entity model and human grief all meet in the same case** — and it is a design problem, not a paperwork problem, which is why it needed to be a node rather than an edge case in someone else's file. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Succession transfers **operating control**; it never merely locks the account | DT-02. The catalogue is a business and it does not stop. |
| D-02 | Accruals continue throughout the freeze | Stopping a dead musician's royalties while paperwork clears is a loss inflicted on a grieving family by a data model. |
| D-03 | Incapacity succession must be **reversible** | People recover. An irreversible transfer is a theft with paperwork attached. |
