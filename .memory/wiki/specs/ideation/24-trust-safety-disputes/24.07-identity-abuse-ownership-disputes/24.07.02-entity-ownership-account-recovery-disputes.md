# Feature: Entity Ownership & Account Recovery Disputes

> **Number**: 24.07.02
> **Parent**: [Identity Abuse & Ownership Disputes](./24.07-identity-abuse-ownership-disputes-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Adjudication when two or more humans both claim an account or an entity: a band that split up and both
halves want the profile, a manager who created the account and kept the password after being fired, a
studio's departed engineer, a venue page a promoter made and will not hand over. The domain map named the
motivating case exactly — "a band account with five members and a departed manager" — and routed it here
because it is a **property dispute requiring evidence, both parties heard, a reasoned decision and an
appeal**, not a support ticket.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The band member whose band broke up. The account holds the credits, the fan list, the masters and the money — and the person holding the password may be the one who left. This is not an account recovery; it is a divorce over the only asset that matters. |
| Producer | Full | The studio equivalent: an engineer or partner departs, the account and its project history stay. Their exposure is heavier because their account owns *other people's* unreleased material — a contested producer account freezes six artists' projects. |
| Operator | Full | **The most common version in live music and the one nobody anticipates**: a venue page created by an ex-promoter, or by a booker who has moved on. The room is a real business with a real address, and its page belongs to someone who no longer works there. |
| Fan | None | No entity, no mandate, nothing to dispute (24.07 Role Matrix). A fan's account has one claimant by construction. |

## Behavior

The dispute resolves against **domain 01's membership and mandate graph**, not against the credential.
Holding the password is evidence of nothing — the manager was *given* it, which is the whole problem.
The graph says who is a member, with what mandate, granted by whom, and whether it was revoked. Where the
graph is complete, the case is nearly mechanical. Where it is not — which is most of the time, because
bands do not file paperwork — the case is a judgment call on inference.

**Freezing consequential actions while the case is open is probably mandatory** (24.07 CX-01): payouts,
releases, fan-list export, deletion. The `Audit Log` cross-cut already names "a manager exporting the fan
list on their way out" as a real incident type, and the open-dispute window is exactly when it happens.

The forgot-password flow and credential mechanics are **not** here (24.07 D-04). This is the case where
recovery is *contested* — where the flow would work perfectly and give the account to the wrong person.

### Happy Path

1. A claimant asserts that an entity's account is controlled by someone without a mandate
2. Consequential actions on the entity are frozen; accruals continue
3. Both parties are heard; evidence is gathered (mandate records, audit log, contracts, minutes, splits)
4. The case resolves against domain 01's graph
5. Decision: mandate change requested in domain 01 → access changes via the Roles cross-cut
6. All members of the entity are notified, not just the parties — the outcome changes their governance

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Manager holds the password and a mandate nobody ever revoked | **They are the authorised operator.** The band's problem is governance, not impersonation | The uncomfortable, correct answer — and the reason 24.07 CX-01 exists |
| Band splits; both halves claim the name | This is trademark and partnership law, not a platform decision | `[PENDING]` — 24.07 Q-01; freeze and route to counsel may be the only honest answer |
| The mandate graph is empty because nobody ever filled it in | **The modal case.** Bands do not do paperwork | Judgment on inference: who posted, who was paid, who the credits attest |
| Contested account holds six other artists' unreleased masters | The freeze harms uninvolved third parties who did nothing | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Losing party's own splits sit inside the entity | **Losing the account cannot lose their share** — the same constraint as 24.02.01 DT-01 | Their ownership records persist and remain exportable |
| A party exports the fan list while the case is open | The named incident type. The freeze must include export, or the freeze is decorative | Export blocked; the attempt is logged |
| Both parties are honest and both genuinely believe they own it | **The common case.** Bands remember differently, and both are sincere | Adjudication on the record, not on sincerity |
| An estate claim races an ownership dispute | The worst case; both claims may be legitimate (24.07 CX-02) | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncontested | Default | Nothing |
| Disputed | Claim filed | Both parties: case; entity: consequential actions frozen, accruals unaffected |
| Evidence gathering | Both heard | Requests for mandates, contracts, records |

| Deadlocked | No factual basis in the graph | `[PENDING]` — 24.07 Q-01 |
| Frozen indefinitely | Legal proceedings elsewhere | The account persists, earns, and does nothing else |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership follows the credential — whoever can log in owns it | The implicit model of every account-recovery flow | ❌ REJECTED — **the credential is evidence of nothing here, and that is the entire problem.** The manager holding the password was *given* it, deliberately, by the band. Domain 01's model says bands, labels and studios are **entities** that exist independently of their members (`personas.md`: "a persona is not always a person"), so an entity's account belongs to the entity, and who may act for it is a mandate question. A recovery flow that returns the account to whoever proves control of the email would hand a band's masters, fan list and royalties to a person they fired. **The platform's own ontology is what makes the right answer computable — and it is also the only reason the wrong answer is avoidable.** |
| DT-02 | This is customer support with extra steps | Where it lands on every other platform | ❌ REJECTED — the disputed asset is **property**: a catalogue, a chain of title, a fan list, a royalty stream, co-owned masters. The domain map's own reasoning routed it here for this reason, and it is right: this needs evidence, both parties heard, a reasoned decision and an appeal, because the losing party is losing an asset and will say so to a lawyer. Staffing it as support produces decisions that cannot survive contact with one. It is also the case that most needs the freeze — support tickets do not have a window during which the respondent can export the disputed asset and leave. |
| DT-03 | The platform should decide who owns the band | The user expectation, and the tempting answer | ⏳ **DEFERRED — genuinely uncertain, and the honest position is that it probably should not.** Who owns "The Band Name" is trademark and partnership law, decided by what the members agreed (usually nothing), what they registered (usually nothing), and who has used it in commerce. A platform adjudicating it is making a legal determination it has no standing for, and getting it wrong hands one half of a band the other half's career. **What the platform *can* legitimately do is narrower and still valuable**: freeze, preserve everything (the audit log means neither party can rewrite history), keep royalties accruing to the entity rather than to either claimant, and wait for an agreement or a court. That is an unsatisfying answer to give a user, and it may be the only defensible one. The alternative — the platform as band-breakup arbiter — is a role no other company has ever wanted. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The dispute resolves against domain 01's mandate graph, never against the credential | DT-01. |
| D-02 | Consequential actions freeze on dispute; accruals do not | 24.07 CX-01. The fan-list export happens in exactly this window, and a frozen account that stops earning punishes everyone. |
| D-03 | The losing party keeps their ownership records and their share | Consistent with 24.02.01 D-01. Losing an argument is not forfeiting property. |
