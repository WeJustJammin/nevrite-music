# Feature: Meetup Safety & Safe Exchange

> **Number**: 24.06.02
> **Parent**: [Personal Safety & Threat Response](./24.06-personal-safety-threat-response-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Safety product for the in-person meetings the platform arranges between strangers: local gear pickups,
band auditions in someone's garage, sessions at a private home studio, lessons in a living room. The
platform does not merely permit these — the marketplace, community and casting domains **produce** them,
and a high-value cash gear pickup is a robbery-bait transaction by construction. Safe-exchange location
suggestions, a check-in/check-out timer, sharing a meetup with a trusted contact, and in-person incident
reporting.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The persona this exists for. Sells a £3,000 guitar for cash to a stranger who wants to collect from their flat; auditions for a band in a garage they have never been to; goes to a producer's home studio at 10pm. `personas.md` describes someone who moves constantly between rooms owned by people they barely know — that is the job, and it is also the exposure. |
| Producer | Full | Invites strangers into a room they own, or goes to one they do not. Their exposure is bidirectional and largely invisible to them: hosting feels safe until it isn't. |
| Operator | Full | Their staff meet strangers at a loading dock at 1am, handle cash, and the venue's address is public. **Their room is frequently the safest exchange location in a city** — which is a genuinely interesting product idea: the venue as a safe-exchange point. See Q-03. |
| Fan | Full | Meets sellers when buying gear or resold tickets; meets artists at shows. Lowest situational awareness of the four, and the most likely to arrange something in a stranger's home without thinking about it. |

> **No persona has None.** Every persona physically meets strangers the platform introduced them to,
> so the safety layer is universal by construction — there is no role for whom it does not apply. The
> variation is in *default posture*, not access: the Fan gets the layer surfaced more assertively (D-04)
> because they have the lowest situational awareness (D-05); the others can dismiss it in one tap.

## Behavior

Any on-platform arrangement that implies a physical meeting can carry a safety layer: a suggested public
exchange location (police-station safe-trade zones, staffed public places, and possibly partner venues),
an optional check-in/check-out timer, and share-my-meetup with a trusted contact.

**The layer must be offered at the moment the meeting is arranged**, not buried in settings — that is the
only moment the user is thinking about the meeting at all.

The record of an arrangement is evidence if something goes wrong (24.06 CX-01). A restricted party must be
unable to *arrange* a meeting, not merely unable to message (24.06 CX-04).

An in-person incident is **not a dispute** (24.06 CX R-02): there is no remedy set and no counterparty to
negotiate with. It is a safety case, and possibly a police matter (24.08.03).

### Happy Path

1. Two parties agree a local pickup or an in-person session on-platform
2. At the moment of arrangement, the safety layer is offered: suggested location, timer, share-with-contact
3. Parties meet; the buyer checks in
4. Transaction completes; both check out
5. No incident; the record persists as evidence that never gets used

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| £3k cash pickup at the seller's flat | The modal high-value local gear sale, and it is robbery bait. The safety layer's whole reason for existing | Suggested public alternative, offered at arrangement time |
| Audition in a stranger's garage, no transaction, no money | **Higher risk than the gear sale and there is no payment trail to trace.** Community (03) and Opportunities (04) generate these | The layer must attach to non-transactional arrangements too — which means it cannot live in checkout |
| Seller insists on home collection ("the amp's too heavy to move") | Often entirely true. Vintage amps are 40kg and the seller is not being sinister | The layer informs; it must not moralise or block |
| Check-out missed | Weak signal — phones die, people forget, the meeting ran long. **Heavy response, weak signal** | `[PENDING]` — 24.06 CX-03 and 24.06.03 Q-01 |
| Assault or robbery at a meetup | Not a dispute. Safety case, evidence preservation, and probably police | Incident report — never a refund flow |
| Lesson in a private home with a minor | Not this feature's — the `Safeguarding & Minor Protection` cross-cut owns chaperone and recording norms explicitly | Routed |
| Party arranges off-platform after matching on-platform | Out of reach, and it is the majority case for auditions | The layer's ceiling, stated honestly |
| Restricted party attempts to arrange | Blocked at arrangement, not at message (24.06 CX-04) | Purchase/booking unavailable, without a reason |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Local meetups are the users' own business — the platform is not responsible for what happens off its surface | The Craigslist position | ❌ REJECTED — **the platform arranges the meeting.** A £3,000 cash gear pickup at a stranger's address is a robbery-bait transaction, and it is not an edge case: it is the modal high-value local sale in gear, because buyers of vintage instruments want to play the thing before paying and neither party wants to ship a £3k guitar. OfferUp and Facebook built safe-exchange zones after real deaths, not as a courtesy. The duty of care follows the arrangement, and this platform arranges more of these than a generic marketplace does — because music also produces auditions, home studios and lessons. |
| DT-02 | This is a gear-marketplace feature — attach it to checkout | The obvious placement | ❌ REJECTED — **the highest-risk meeting on this platform has no transaction at all.** "Come to my garage Thursday at 8pm and audition" is generated by Community (03) and Opportunities (04) between strangers with no money, no order, no escrow and no payment trail — so if something happens there is no transaction record to trace, and the arrangement may exist only as a message. The gear pickup at least has an order behind it. Attaching the safety layer to checkout covers the *lower*-risk case and misses the higher one, which is a precisely inverted design. It must attach to **arrangements**, wherever they are made. |
| DT-03 | The safety layer should discourage private-address meetings | The protective instinct | ❌ REJECTED — it would be wrong most of the time and would be ignored the rest. A vintage amp genuinely weighs 40kg and genuinely cannot be carried to a car park; a home studio genuinely is where the session happens; an audition genuinely happens in the drummer's garage because that is where the drums are. **A safety feature that moralises about the normal conduct of the profession gets dismissed as noise, and then it is not there for the one meeting that mattered.** It should inform, offer alternatives, and make the timer effortless — and then get out of the way. |
| DT-04 | The safety record can be discarded once the meeting completes without incident | The "no incident, nothing to keep" instinct — most meetups are fine | ✅ CONFIRMED as a value, INVERTED as a rule — the record's whole worth is that it exists *before* anyone knows whether it will be needed. The arrangement, the check-in/check-out log, the chosen contact and the suggested location are the evidence a police officer or a harassment case (24.06.01, CX-01) asks for, and by definition you cannot know at check-out time whether the incident surfaces a week later (a stalker who met the seller once, a delayed assault report). So the safety record must be **immutable and preserved on the same footing as a dispute record**, not deleted on a clean check-out — while staying subject to the platform's retention policy and the arranging party's data rights (Q-01, and the Data Portability / Retention cross-cut). "Kept just in case, never used" is the *successful* outcome, not waste. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The layer attaches to **arrangements**, not to checkout | DT-02. The riskiest meeting has no transaction. |
| D-02 | Inform and offer; never moralise or block | DT-03. Private-address meetings are normal professional conduct in music. |
| D-03 | An in-person incident is a safety case, never a dispute | 24.06 CX R-02. There is no remedy set for an assault. |
| D-04 | The safety record is immutable and preserved on a clean check-out, not deleted | DT-04. The incident can surface after the meeting; the record's value is that it predates knowing it was needed. |
| D-05 | The Fan gets the layer surfaced more assertively than the professional personas | Role Lens. Lowest situational awareness; the others can dismiss in one tap. |
