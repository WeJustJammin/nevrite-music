# Feature: Harassment, Stalking & Doxxing Enforcement

> **Number**: 24.06.01
> **Parent**: [Personal Safety & Threat Response](./24.06-personal-safety-threat-response-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — the sweep produced no harassment feature at all

## Overview

Policy and adjudication for one person deliberately harming another: sustained unwanted contact,
coordinated pile-ons, threats, stalking, and the publication of private information. The *mechanism* of
restriction — block, mute, contact ban — is a cross-cut (24.06 D-03), because it must be enforced at every
call site. What is owned here is the policy, the case, the evidence, and one product problem no other
marketplace has: **WeJammin publishes, on purpose, exactly the information a stalker needs.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The primary stalking target. Their gig calendar is their income *and* a schedule of their physical whereabouts; their gear listings carry photos of their home; their credits map their professional network. Reports harassment, requests restriction, and — if the platform builds it — controls who can see where they will be. Also faces the industry's power-asymmetry harassment: the session player who cannot report the producer who books them. |
| Producer | Full | Both a target and, structurally, the persona best placed to abuse power — they control who gets booked, who gets credited, and who is in the room. Music's #MeToo reckoning was largely about this exact asymmetry, and a platform that formalises the session relationship formalises the asymmetry with it. |
| Operator | Full | Their venue's address is public by necessity and is sometimes their home. Their staff face strangers at a loading dock at 1am. They also need to bar an individual from a *physical* space, which no software restriction achieves — the gap between a platform block and a door is real. |
| Fan | Full | Both directions, and `personas.md` names the bad one explicitly: "harasses artists through open contact surfaces". Also a victim — of other fans, and occasionally of artists' fanbases. The persona with the least power and the most numbers. |

## Behavior

Harassment is claim-driven: it is adjudicated on evidence, not detected by a score (24.06 CX R-03).
A case assembles the thread, the posts, the pattern and the timeline; a decision produces a restriction
(via the cross-cut) and, where warranted, a sanction (24.02.01).

**The location problem is the feature's centre.** A gig listing is a public, timestamped, geolocated
commitment that a named human will be at a known address at a known hour. Domains 17, 18, 19 and 21 exist
to broadcast it — that is their job, and the artist's livelihood depends on it working. A stalker needs
nothing else. Blocking them does not unpublish the show.

Doxxing has a music-specific inventory that the Privacy cross-cut already names: EXIF in gear photos
(24.01.02 DT-02), a venue operator's home address, and **rider dietary and medical data** — health-adjacent
information the touring domains collect as a matter of routine.

### Happy Path

1. A target reports harassment from the object where it happened (24.01.01)
2. The case assembles the pattern — not a single message, but the timeline
3. A reviewer with the relevant context decides against a cited rule
4. A restriction is applied through the cross-cut and propagates to **every** surface (24.06 CX-04)
5. Sanction where warranted (24.02.01); the target is told what protection is now in place
6. Where the harm is location-based, the target is offered whatever location controls exist

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Blocking is sufficient protection | The assumption on every social and marketplace platform | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the finding that justifies the whole sub-domain.** A block stops contact. It does not unpublish a **gig listing**, which is a public, timestamped, geolocated commitment that a named human will stand at a known address at a known hour, announced in advance to maximise attendance. Domains 17, 18, 19 and 21 broadcast it deliberately — the artist's income depends on it. No other marketplace has this: Reverb never tells you where the seller will physically be next Tuesday at 9pm; Airbnb hides the address until booking. WeJammin's core value proposition *is* telling people where a specific person will be. **Protecting a stalking target therefore means degrading the promotion machinery that pays their rent**, and the platform cannot have both. This is a genuine, unresolved product conflict and it belongs to the owner (24.06 Q-01), not to an agent. |
| DT-02 | Harassment belongs to Community (domain 03) — it is a social feature | The natural home if you think in surfaces | ❌ REJECTED — the *surface* is Community's, but the adjudication, the evidence and the restriction are Trust's, and **the stalking vector does not come from Community at all**: it comes from Live (17/18/19), Promotion (21), and the gear listings' photographs. A harassment feature built inside Community would protect the comment thread and leave the tour schedule, the loading dock and the seller's living room untouched — which is where the actual harm is. The surface is not the domain. |
| DT-03 | Music's harassment profile is a fan-to-artist problem | The visible one, and the one `personas.md` names | ❌ REJECTED as incomplete — the **power-asymmetry** harassment is worse and quieter. A session player, a student, or an artist depends on a producer, a teacher, or an engineer for work, credits and access. Music's #MeToo reckoning was overwhelmingly about that asymmetry, and it is the one this platform *formalises*: WeJammin makes the booking, holds the money, records the credit and hosts the room. Reporting the person who books you is a career decision, and the platform now holds the evidence that you did. This changes what the feature must be — anonymous reporting, pattern-matching across multiple victims who each think they are alone, and a route that does not require the victim to confront the person controlling their income. **A report button does not address it.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Self-serve restriction requires **zero** process — instant, private, no case | A victim should never have to file a report to stop contact. Reporting is optional and separate. |
| D-02 | Harassment is claim-driven, never scored | 24.06 CX R-03. A per-person "harassment risk score" is a defamatory artifact built on signals that fire on enthusiasm. |
| D-03 | Restriction propagation failure is a correctness bug, not a UX issue | A block that silently missed the ticketing surface is worse than no block: the target believes they are safe. |
