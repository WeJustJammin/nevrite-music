## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Meetup Safety ↔ Harassment

**Relationship**: The platform arranges meetings between strangers as a normal part of its product — a
gear pickup, an audition, a session at a home studio, a lesson. Each one transfers a large amount of
information the platform's privacy model otherwise protects: a face, a real name, a voice, a home address,
and a routine. When a meeting produces a harassment or stalking case, the case begins with an adversary
who already has everything.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: buys and sells gear locally and auditions with strangers. Both directions of exposure.
- **Producer**: invites people into a room they own, or goes to one they do not.
- **Operator**: their staff meet strangers at a loading dock at 1am; the venue's address is public by
  necessity, and their home may be the venue.
- **Fan**: meets artists at shows and merch tables. The persona whose named anti-persona behaviour is
  exactly this escalation.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the meetup owns the arrangement; the harassment case owns the adjudication.
   A meetup record becomes evidence in the case (24.09) — which is the reason to have a record at all.
2. **Trigger chain**: meetup → (incident) → report (24.01.01) → harassment case → restriction. The
   restriction must then reach back and prevent future arrangements (CX-04).
3. **Permission intersection**: yes, and it is the point — a restricted party must lose the ability to
   arrange, not merely to message.
4. **Notification fan-out**: an incident report notifies nobody automatically. Telling the reported party
   "you have been reported" before a restriction is in place is how retaliation happens.
5. **State transition conflict**: a meeting can be arranged and a block applied in the same hour. The
   arrangement must be voided, and the other party told the meeting is off — without disclosing why.

### CX-04: Harassment ↔ Meetup Safety

**Relationship**: A restriction that only stops messages is not a restriction. On this platform the
restricted party can still buy the target's gear (creating a pickup obligation), book the target's studio,
buy a ticket to their gig, or apply to their casting call. **The restriction must reach every surface
where a transaction creates a real-world proximity** — which is precisely the argument for block/mute
being a cross-cut (24.06 D-03) rather than an inbox feature.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: a restricted party buying their listed amp and expecting collection at their home is the
  concrete failure case.
- **Operator**: a restricted party booking their room, or buying a ticket to a show at their venue —
  where a *ticket* restriction collides with a public event.
- **Fan**: the restricted party in most instances.
- **Producer**: a restricted party applying to a session they are casting.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the restriction is a single fact enforced in many places. One writer
   (Trust), many readers (every domain). Duplicating the state is how it drifts.
2. **Trigger chain**: restriction → propagate → block at every call site. **Propagation failure is
   invisible and dangerous** — a block that silently did not reach the ticketing surface is worse than no
   block, because the target believes they are protected.
3. **Permission intersection**: this *is* a permission — it is a negative one, scoped to a pair of actors
   rather than to a role. Generic RBAC cannot express "these two people, never".
4. **Notification fan-out**: the restricted party must not learn the restriction exists from a mysterious
   failure at checkout, or they will test until they find the boundary.
5. **Existing-obligation containment**: when a restriction lands after an obligation has been accepted
   (an amp already bought, a ticket already sold, or a booking already confirmed), that obligation becomes
   `safety-mediated` immediately. Direct contact, address/coordinate disclosure, and automated
   arrangement, collection, or check-in prompts stop; neither party is told that a restriction caused the
   change. The containment state does **not** itself void, transfer, refund, or force an in-person
   completion. A terminal money or fulfilment route is selected only by the category-specific policy in
   24.06 Q-04; until that policy exists, no transaction surface may improvise one.
