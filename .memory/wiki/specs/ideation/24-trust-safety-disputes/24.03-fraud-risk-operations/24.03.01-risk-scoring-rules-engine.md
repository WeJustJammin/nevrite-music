# Feature: Risk Scoring & Rules Engine

> **Number**: 24.03.01
> **Parent**: [Fraud & Risk Operations](./24.03-fraud-risk-operations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The evaluation layer that scores an *action* — list, buy, pay out, message, book, review — against
signals and rules, and proposes friction: a verification step, a hold, a review, a block. It has no
authority to sanction (24.03 D-02); it produces a proposal that
[24.02.01](../24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy/24.02.01-enforcement-ladder-sanctions.md) applies with a
statement of reasons. Its defining problem on this platform is that **the user it is most likely to flag
is the one the product exists for.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Invisible — and the persona at greatest risk of false positives. `personas.md`: their roles are *simultaneous, not sequential*. A drummer who lists a cymbal, invoices a session, joins a project and books a room within an hour trips every generic velocity and role-consistency rule ever written. They experience the engine only as an unexplained hold on money they have earned. |
| Producer | None | Invisible. High-value, low-frequency actions (large escrow releases, multi-party splits) sit in the tail where generic thresholds are worst calibrated. |
| Operator | None | Invisible. Their risk profile inverts the marketplace default: they are simultaneously buyer and seller (`personas.md`), and their inventory is perishable, so a 48-hour review hold on a Friday booking is not friction, it is a destroyed weekend. |
| Fan | None | Invisible. Highest volume, lowest value, most homogeneous — the only persona a generic engine is actually well-calibrated for. |

## Behavior

Risk is scored **per action, not per user**. A trusted seller can be a risky payout; a clean buyer can be
a risky ticket purchase (bulk-buying to scalp is the Fan's named anti-persona behaviour). Each evaluation
carries: the action, the actor, the counterparty, the object, the graph context (24.03.02), and the
declared outcome — `allow | step-up | hold | review | block`.

**Rules before models.** At cold start there is no history, no labels and no corpus; a model has nothing
to learn from and the only labels the platform will ever get come from human adjudications (24.03 CX-04).
Rules are also explicable, which matters because every consequence must ship with a statement of reasons.

Screening hits (24.03.05) are **hard gates**, never score contributions — a legal determination must not
be averaged away by a good reputation (24.03 CX-05).

### Happy Path

1. An action is attempted
2. The engine evaluates rules against signals and graph context
3. Below threshold → allow, with the evaluation recorded
4. Above threshold → propose friction (step-up verification, hold, or review)
5. 24.02.01 applies the consequence with an SoR; the user has an appeal route
6. The eventual adjudication becomes a label (24.03 CX-04)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Multi-hyphenate performs 5 unrelated role-actions in an hour | **Must not flag.** Role diversity is the platform's thesis, not an anomaly | Nothing |
| Two musicians on the same studio Wi-Fi both list gear | Shared-IP linkage must not fire | Nothing — see 24.03 Q-02 |
| Genuine first-time high-value sale (a £12k vintage bass, seller's first listing) | New account + high value + no history = the highest-scoring pattern in any generic engine, and also **the platform's best possible transaction** | Step-up verification, not a block — and the reason must be honest |
| Engine unavailable | Fail open with rules aware they are blind; do not silently score everyone clean | Nothing |
| Rule change causes mass false positives | Rules are versioned and revertible; a rule is a config change with blast radius | Affected users get an SoR they will not deserve — hence shadow-mode first |
| Score is exposed in an API response or an error message | Teaches the adversary the threshold; must be structurally impossible | N/A |
| Sanctioned action is later reversed on appeal | The label must flow back as a **negative** label, or the engine learns from its own errors | Nothing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Allow | Below threshold | Nothing — the correct outcome for ~all traffic |
| Step-up | Medium risk | An identity or payment verification step, framed as a step, not an accusation |
| Hold | High risk, reversible action | A delay with a reason and an ETA (via 24.02.01) |
| Review | High risk, human needed | "Under review" with a real deadline |
| Block | Rules-certain (card testing, screening hit) | Blocked with a reason |
| Shadow | Rule in evaluation | Nothing — scores recorded, no action taken |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Risk is a property of a user — one score per account | The framing behind most fraud dashboards | ❌ REJECTED — risk is a property of an **action in a context**. The same account is trustworthy listing a cymbal and untrustworthy requesting a £4,000 payout to a bank account changed nine minutes ago. A per-user score also collides directly with `personas.md`'s central claim: this platform's user *is* several users. A single score for a human who is a session drummer, a mixer, a band member and a gear flipper is not a summary — it is a category error. |
| DT-02 | Generic marketplace velocity and role-consistency heuristics are a sensible starting point | Every fraud vendor ships them as defaults | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the sub-domain's most important finding. **The multi-hyphenate is indistinguishable from an account farm under generic rules.** "New account performing several unrelated commercial actions across categories within an hour, at multiple price points, in several roles" is textbook farming — and it is also a Tuesday for the exact user `personas.md` calls the structural centre. Shipping vendor defaults means the platform's ideal user is flagged in week one, discovers their earnings are held, and leaves. The rules must be built inverted: **role diversity is a trust signal here, not a risk signal.** No vendor default encodes that, because no other platform's user looks like this. |
| DT-03 | Start with a model; rules are a stopgap | The modern instinct | ❌ REJECTED at cold start — there is no history, no labels, no corpus, and `meta/constraints.md` describes no platform team. The only label source is human adjudication (24.03 CX-04), which does not exist until there are decisions. Rules are also **explicable**, which is not a soft benefit: every consequence must ship with an Art 17 statement of reasons naming the facts relied on, and "the model scored you 0.87" is not a statement of facts. A model here would be simultaneously untrainable and unexplainable. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Score the action, never the user | DT-01. The multi-hyphenate makes a per-user score incoherent, not merely imprecise. |
| D-02 | Role diversity is a **trust** signal on this platform, not a risk signal | DT-02. The single most important inversion of vendor defaults, and it must be stated before anyone configures a fraud vendor. |
| D-03 | Rules before models; every new rule ships in shadow mode first | DT-03 + the mass-false-positive edge case. |
