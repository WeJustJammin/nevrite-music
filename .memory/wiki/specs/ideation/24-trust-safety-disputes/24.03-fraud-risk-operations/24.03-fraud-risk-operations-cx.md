## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: ATO/Ring Detection ↔ Risk Scoring

**Relationship**: Linkage produces the graph; scoring consumes it. At cold start the platform has no
transaction history, no chargeback history and no labels, so graph proximity to a known-bad entity is
close to the only real signal available. That makes CX-01 the sub-domain's load-bearing edge — and its
biggest liability, because the linkage substrate is exactly the one music breaks (24.03 Q-02: studios and
rehearsal rooms are shared-device, shared-IP environments).

**Role scoping**:
- **All four personas**: no visibility. They experience only the friction the score produces, delivered
  as a sanction with reasons (24.02.01/24.02.03).
- **Musician** specifically: the multi-hyphenate is the persona most likely to be *falsely* linked —
  several accounts, several roles, one laptop, one studio Wi-Fi.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: linkage owns the graph; scoring owns the score. Neither may write the other's.
   A score must never be fed back as a graph edge — that is a feedback loop that manufactures its own
   evidence.
2. **Trigger chain**: signal → fingerprint → edge → proximity → score → rule → *proposed* friction →
   24.02.01 applies it. Detection has no sanctioning power (24.03 D-02). If the graph is unavailable,
   the score degrades to attribute-only and rules must be aware they are running blind, not silently
   score everyone as clean.
3. **Permission intersection**: none. The graph is operator-only, and it is the most privacy-sensitive
   store in the domain — it links humans by device.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none from detection. All user-facing consequence flows through 24.02.
5. **State transition conflict**: a linkage discovered *after* a payout has been made is the expensive
   case; it converts a detection into a recovery problem (24.04.04).

### CX-03: Risk Scoring ↔ Triangulation & Card Testing

**Relationship**: The two named schemes sit at opposite ends of what a rules engine can do. Card testing
is high-velocity, low-value, on-platform — a generic engine catches it with a rate rule. Triangulation is
low-velocity, high-value, and **the on-platform half is legitimate by construction**: real goods, real
delivery, a happy buyer, a clean transaction. The fraud happened at a third-party retailer with a stolen
card, and the platform sees none of it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Fan / Musician** (as buyers): receive real goods and are unaware. They are the fraud's *cover*, not
  its victim.
- **Musician / Operator** (as honest sellers): harmed indirectly — the triangulator undercuts them with
  goods they never paid for.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — different detectors, one score surface.
2. **Trigger chain**: for card testing, detect → throttle → block, in milliseconds, before the score is
   even relevant. For triangulation, there is no on-platform trigger at all; the signal arrives weeks
   later as an external chargeback or a police enquiry. **The two cannot share a response model.**
3. **Permission intersection**: none.
4. **Notification fan-out**: card testing notifies nobody. A triangulation finding may need to notify a
   buyer that goods they legitimately received are stolen property — a genuinely awful notification with
   no good version.
5. **State transition conflict**: a triangulation seller may have an excellent reputation score at the
   moment of detection. The score must not veto the finding.
