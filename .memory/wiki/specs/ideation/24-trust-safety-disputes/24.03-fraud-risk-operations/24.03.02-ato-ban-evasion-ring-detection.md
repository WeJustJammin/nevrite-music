# Feature: Account Takeover, Ban Evasion & Fraud Ring Detection

> **Number**: 24.03.02
> **Parent**: [Fraud & Risk Operations](./24.03-fraud-risk-operations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

The linkage layer: device, network and behavioural fingerprinting that answers three questions with one
substrate — is this the account's real owner (ATO), is this a banned user returning (evasion), and are
these accounts one operation (rings). Credential architecture (MFA, session, device binding) is **not**
here — it is routed to `/create-prd-security` (24.03 D-03). What is here is detection and response.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Invisible. Their account is the **highest-value ATO target on the platform**: a catalogue, a credit graph that is the proof their next job depends on (D-18), a payout method, and a fan list. Losing it is not losing an account, it is losing a career's evidence. |
| Producer | None | Invisible. ATO on a Producer reaches *other people's* unreleased masters — the blast radius extends to every contributor in every project they own (→ 24.05.04). |
| Operator | None | Invisible, and the persona whose environment **breaks this feature**: a studio or rehearsal room is a shared-device, shared-IP space where a dozen unrelated musicians log in from one machine on one connection. Naive linkage manufactures a fraud ring out of a Tuesday. |
| Fan | None | Invisible. Highest ATO volume, lowest per-account value, and the population where ban evasion is most common and least consequential. |

## Behavior

Signals are collected at authentication and at consequential actions: device characteristics, network,
behavioural cadence, payout-method changes, and the graph of shared attributes across accounts. Linkage
produces edges; edges produce a graph; graph proximity to known-bad is the highest-value feature in the
score (24.03 CX-01) — and, at cold start, close to the only one that exists.

**Response, not sanction**: a suspected ATO triggers a payout freeze and a step-up, not a ban — banning a
victim compounds the theft. A suspected evasion triggers a review, because the false-positive population
is "musicians who share a studio".

The ownership-dispute case ("this is my band's account and he changed the password") is **not** here — it
is a claim with evidence and it belongs to [24.07.02](../24.07-identity-abuse-ownership-disputes/24.07.02-entity-ownership-account-recovery-disputes.md).
The line is 24.03 D-01: signals here, claims there.

### Happy Path

1. Signals are captured at auth and at consequential actions
2. Fingerprints are computed and linkage edges written
3. An anomaly (new device + payout change + unusual value) raises a score (24.03.01)
4. Response is proposed: freeze payouts, step up verification, notify the real owner out-of-band
5. Owner confirms or denies; a denial escalates to recovery (24.07.02)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Twelve musicians log in from one studio's Wi-Fi and one desktop | **Must not link.** Shared professional space is the norm, not a ring | Nothing — the honest answer to *how* is 24.03 Q-02, unresolved |
| Band members legitimately share one band account | Shared credentials are endemic in music and the Roles cross-cut names it: getting permissions wrong "forces the password sharing the industry already does" | Nothing — and the real fix is delegated authority, not detection |
| ATO'd artist account releases a fake track under the artist's name | Payout freeze + release hold; the credit graph is the recovery evidence | Owner: out-of-band alert |
| ATO'd account exports the fan list | Named explicitly by the Audit Log cross-cut as a real incident type ("a manager exporting the fan list on their way out") — export is a consequential action and must be gated and logged | Owner: alert; the export is in the audit log either way |
| Banned user returns with a new email, same laptop, same studio | Linkage fires — and so does the false-positive case above. Same signal, opposite meanings | Review, not auto-ban |
| Ring of accounts, one operator, different devices via VPN | Device linkage fails by design; the residual signals are behavioural cadence and the **transactional graph** — shared payout instruments, circular payments between the accounts, coordinated listing/review timing (→ 24.03.06). These are weaker and slower than device linkage, so the ring surfaces on **accumulated behaviour over time**, not at signup, and is routed to review rather than auto-action | Nothing for the accounts; operators see a linked cluster in review queue with the transactional edges that raised it |
| Burst signup: dozens of new accounts created in minutes off one payment instrument / referral chain to farm a promo or seed a review ring | **Velocity + shared-instrument linkage fires.** New-account clusters sharing a funding source or an invite chain, then acting in concert, are a ring signature that shared-studio confounding (DT-01) does **not** explain away — a Tuesday at a studio does not create 40 accounts in 4 minutes | Accounts: new-account limits and step-up before consequential actions; operators: cluster flagged for review |
| Legitimate multi-account user (personal + band + label entity) | **Not a ring.** Domain 01 models these as entities with a membership graph — the platform's own data says these accounts are legitimately linked | Nothing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Clean | No anomaly | Nothing |
| Anomalous | Signal deviation | Step-up at the next consequential action |
| Suspected ATO | Strong signal | Payouts frozen, out-of-band owner alert; account stays usable |
| Confirmed ATO | Owner denies the session | Recovery flow (24.07.02); sanctions do not apply to a victim |
| Suspected evasion | Linkage to a banned entity | Review; **never** auto-ban |
| Linked | Graph edge exists | Nothing — visible only to operators |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Device/network fingerprinting is a reliable linkage substrate | It is, on consumer platforms | ❌ REJECTED for this platform's core environments. **Music is played in shared rooms.** A studio, a rehearsal space, a venue office and a college practice room are shared-device, shared-IP by definition — the Operator persona *runs* such a space, and `personas.md` describes musicians moving between several of them weekly. Naive linkage will connect a dozen unrelated professionals into a "ring" whose only shared attribute is a room. This is not a tuning problem; it is a substrate that is wrong for the domain, and it must be surfaced now because every fraud vendor ships it as the default. |
| DT-02 | An ATO'd music account is low-value — there's no money sitting in it | Plausible if you think of it as a social account | ❌ REJECTED — it is one of the highest-value ATO targets in any consumer marketplace, for four distinct reasons: **redirect the payout method** (money that is about to arrive), **export the fan list** (a directly saleable asset the Audit Log cross-cut already names as an incident type), **release a fake track under the artist's name** to steal streaming revenue (a documented, active attack pattern against distributors), and **reach every collaborator's unreleased material** through the projects the account owns (→ 24.05.04). The last one is unique to this product: an ATO here is a supply-chain compromise, not an account compromise. |
| DT-03 | Ban evasion detection and ATO detection can share a response | They share a substrate, so it feels natural | ❌ REJECTED — they share signals and must **not** share responses. An ATO victim is a victim: freeze payouts, alert out-of-band, protect them, sanction nothing. A ban evader is an adversary: review and re-ban. Same linkage edge, opposite conclusions — and the population that trips both is identical (musicians on a shared studio machine). Wiring one response to the shared substrate guarantees that the platform eventually bans a victim, which is the worst outcome available to it. |
| DT-04 | If device/IP linkage is unusable here (DT-01), the platform has a **stronger** native substrate than a generic fraud vendor does — the transactional and provenance graph it already owns | The product's own data model: payouts, splits, membership, listings, reviews | ✅ CONFIRMED — the money graph (shared payout instruments, circular transfers) and the collaboration graph (who splits with whom, who is credited on what per D-18) are attributes a shared studio Wi-Fi does **not** manufacture. Two musicians on one desktop share a room; they do not share a bank account or route royalties in a loop. This substrate is weaker at **cold start** (it needs transactions to exist) but it is the correct primary signal for a music platform and it degrades gracefully where device linkage produces false rings. The architecture of that graph is Q-01 → `/create-prd-architecture`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | ATO response is protective, never punitive | DT-03. Banning a victim compounds the theft. |
| D-02 | Ban-evasion linkage triggers review, never auto-ban | DT-01. The false-positive population is "musicians who share a studio", and it is large. |
| D-03 | Legitimate multi-entity accounts are resolved from domain 01's membership graph, not inferred | Otherwise every band, label and studio is a detected ring. The platform's own ontology is the disambiguator. |
