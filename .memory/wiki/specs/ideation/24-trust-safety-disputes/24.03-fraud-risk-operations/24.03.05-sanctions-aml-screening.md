# Feature: Sanctions, AML & High-Value Transaction Screening

> **Number**: 24.03.05
> **Parent**: [Fraud & Risk Operations](./24.03-fraud-risk-operations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Screening of parties and payouts against sanctions lists, and monitoring of high-value and structured
transaction patterns. The **list-matching mechanism** belongs to the `Payments, Escrow & Payouts`
cross-cut, which already owns connected-account onboarding and KYC/KYB tiers. What is owned here is the
**adjudication half**: the false-positive review queue, the case, the decision, and the platform's own
duty. The music-specific reason this is not boilerplate: a '59 Les Paul is a portable, six-figure,
internationally-shipped store of value — economically the same object regulators reached for when they
brought high-value art into scope.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Invisible. Experiences it as a payout hold at onboarding or on a large sale. **False positives are brutal here**: name-matching against sanctions lists misfires on common names, and the persona's income is a portfolio of small payments (`personas.md`) — a two-week hold on a £900 session fee is rent. |
| Producer | None | Invisible. Cross-border client work (a UK studio invoicing a US client, per the Localization cross-cut) puts them in scope more often than they would expect. |
| Operator | None | Invisible. Hit at connected-account onboarding, and again if they sell high-value gear internationally. Their business entity, not just their person, is screened — a KYB question the Payments cross-cut owns. |
| Fan | None | Invisible. Effectively never in scope — they buy, they do not receive payouts. Listed to record the absence: the screening burden falls entirely on the paid side of the platform. |

## Behavior

Screening runs at connected-account onboarding, at payout, and on high-value transactions. A hit is a
**hard gate**, never a score contribution (24.03 CX-05) — a legal determination must not be averaged away
by a good reputation. Hits enter a review queue where a human resolves true match vs name collision; the
decision and its basis are retained as evidence.

**Timing is the whole feature.** You cannot retroactively screen a payout you have already made — the
identical logic the `Tax & Fiscal Compliance` cross-cut states about TINs ("you cannot retroactively
obtain a TIN from a seller who has left"). If screening is required at all, it is required from the first
payout, not from the first audit.

The applicable obligations follow the **money-transmission posture**, which `meta/constraints.md` already
records as an unresolved **legal constraint** determining whether several product concepts are viable.
This feature cannot be scoped until that is decided.

### Happy Path

1. A party onboards for payouts; the Payments cross-cut runs KYB/KYC and screening
2. No match → cleared; the screening decision is recorded with its basis
3. Payout and high-value transactions re-screen against updated lists
4. A hit gates the payout and opens a review case
5. Human resolves: true match → escalate per obligation; collision → clear, with the basis recorded
6. Rescreening on list updates catches parties added after onboarding

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Name collision with a listed individual | Payout gated pending human review. **The most common outcome by far** | A hold with a reason that cannot say why — see Q-02 |
| A cleared party is added to a list later | Rescreening on list update catches it; screening once at onboarding is not screening | Payouts gate at the next attempt |
| £180k vintage guitar sold internationally, paid in three instalments | Structuring pattern or a normal instalment plan? In this market, both are ordinary | Review, not a block |
| A band entity's payout splits across five members | Every recipient is screened, not just the account holder — the Payments cross-cut's "single payment routinely splits across a band" is a screening multiplier |
| Screening provider unavailable | **Fail closed on payouts.** Delaying money is recoverable; paying a sanctioned party is not | "Payout delayed" |
| Buyer (not seller) is sanctioned | Sale gated. The obligation is not payout-only | Order blocked |
| Platform decides AML does not apply, and is wrong | Discovered at an audit, retroactively, with no screening history to show | N/A — this is Q-01 and it is the real risk |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not screened | Pre-onboarding | No payouts available |
| Cleared | No match | Nothing |
| Gated | Potential match | Payout held with a reason (via 24.02.01) |
| Under review | Case open | "Under review" — deliberately uninformative; see Q-02 |
| True match | Confirmed | Per obligation; the user-facing behaviour is constrained by law, not by product taste |
| Rescreen pending | List updated | Nothing until a payout is attempted |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A marketplace is not a financial institution, so AML does not apply | The comfortable assumption | ⏳ **DEFERRED — genuinely unresolved and it must not be guessed.** Obligations follow the money-transmission posture, which `meta/constraints.md` already flags as an open legal constraint. What can be said honestly: **sanctions screening is not conditional on AML status** — sanctions obligations bind everyone, and paying a listed party is a strict-liability problem regardless of what kind of business you are. So the two halves of this feature have different answers, and collapsing them into one "does AML apply?" question hides the half that is already settled. |
| DT-02 | High-value gear is just expensive retail | It looks like a big Reverb order | ❌ REJECTED — a '59 Les Paul is a **portable, six-figure, internationally-shipped, hard-to-value store of value with an opaque price discovery mechanism**. That is the same economic profile that put high-value art dealers into AML scope in the EU. Vintage instruments sit in that category and the analogy is close enough that "we're a marketplace" is not a defence anyone should build on. D-05 puts this inventory at the centre of the product, and the high-value tail is where the margin is (24.04.03 DT-01) — so the exposure grows exactly as the business succeeds. |
| DT-03 | Screening can be added once volume justifies it | The standard sequencing instinct | ❌ REJECTED — **you cannot retroactively screen a payout you already made.** The `Tax & Fiscal Compliance` cross-cut makes the identical argument about TINs and reaches the identical conclusion: it changes what is collected at onboarding, and the party you needed data from has left. If screening is required at all, the *hook* is required from payout one — even if the list check is a vendor call the Payments cross-cut makes. Deferring the decision defers the hook, and the hook is the part that cannot be back-filled. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The mechanism is the Payments cross-cut's; the **case and the decision** are this feature's | Splitting it prevents domain 24 from rebuilding a payout rail, and prevents Payments from owning an adjudication surface it has no console for. |
| D-02 | Sanctions and AML are **separate questions** with separate answers | DT-01. Sanctions bind regardless of money-transmission status; AML follows the posture. Merging them defers a settled obligation behind an unsettled one. |
| D-03 | Fail closed on payouts | A delayed payout is recoverable. A payout to a sanctioned party is not. |
