# Feature: Triangulation & Card-Testing Defense

> **Number**: 24.03.04
> **Parent**: [Fraud & Risk Operations](./24.03-fraud-risk-operations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Defence against two named payment-fraud schemes that sit at opposite extremes of detectability.
**Card testing** is high-velocity, low-value, entirely on-platform, and trivially detectable — and
D-06's digital goods marketplace is the ideal target for it. **Triangulation** is the opposite: a
fraudster lists cheap gear here, and when someone buys it, buys the same item from a legitimate retailer
with a stolen card and ships it to the buyer, pocketing clean money. The on-platform transaction is
*perfect by construction* — real goods, real delivery, a delighted buyer, a five-star review.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Invisible. As a **buyer** they are the triangulator's unwitting cover: they get the amp, they are happy, and they may later learn they are holding stolen property. As an honest **seller** they are the victim — undercut by someone whose cost of goods is zero. |
| Producer | None | Invisible. Their exposure is the digital-goods side (D-06): a plugin licence bought with a tested card. |
| Operator | None | Invisible. Same buyer/seller duality as the Musician, with higher transaction values. |
| Fan | None | Invisible. Highest volume of low-value card-present-absent transactions (merch, tickets) — the population card testing hides in. |

## Behavior

**Card testing**: velocity rules on authorisation attempts — many small charges, many cards, one device
or session, in minutes. Response is throttle-then-block in milliseconds, before any score is consulted
(24.03 CX-03). Digital goods (D-06) are the preferred vector because they are instant, cheap, need no
address, and confirm the card works without a human ever being involved. This is a **launch-day exposure
for domain 14**, not a scale problem.

**Triangulation**: there is no on-platform trigger. Signals are indirect and weak — a new seller offering
inventory below market with no acquisition history, a dropshipped tracking number originating from a
retailer's fulfilment centre rather than the seller's address, mismatched packaging, and (weeks later)
an external chargeback or a police enquiry. The response model cannot be shared with card testing: one is
a millisecond block, the other is a weeks-late investigation.

### Happy Path

1. Authorisation attempts are evaluated for velocity per device, session, and card BIN spread
2. Card testing pattern → throttle → block, with no human in the loop
3. Separately, listings are evaluated for triangulation indicators (below-market + no history + dropship signals)
4. Indicators → payout hold + review, never an auto-block (the transactions are legitimate on their face)
5. Confirmed triangulation → enforcement (24.02.01) + buyer notification + law-enforcement handoff (24.08.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 4,000 £0.99 plugin authorisations in 6 minutes across 300 cards | Blocked at the rail; digital fulfilment must **never** precede settlement confirmation | Attacker: failures. Nobody else notices |
| Triangulator's on-platform record is spotless — 5-star reviews, fast shipping, happy buyers | **The reputation score must not veto the finding** (24.03 CX-03) | Nothing until the hold |
| Buyer legitimately receives goods bought with a stolen card | The goods are stolen property. The buyer did nothing wrong and may lose the item | A notification with no good version — see Q-02 |
| Honest seller dropships (a dealer with a distributor account) | Dropship signals fire on legitimate Trader behaviour | Must not block; the Trader flag (Tax cross-cut) is the disambiguator |
| Genuine below-market listing (a musician who needs rent money by Friday) | **Extremely common in music** and not a signal on its own | Nothing |
| Chargeback arrives ~60 days later at the retailer, not at WeJammin | The platform never sees the fraud in its own data; the only inbound signal is an external party reaching in — a retailer fraud team, a card network, or police (24.08.03). Until then the seller's record stays clean | On-platform: nothing. Detection is exogenous by construction (DT-02) |
| Card testing shifts to low-value physical goods | Slower and costlier for the attacker (needs an address, waits for settlement); still possible | Velocity rules are goods-agnostic — device/session/BIN-spread signals fire regardless of what's being bought |
| Distributed card testing — one card per device, one attempt each, across a botnet | Per-device velocity sees nothing; the pattern only exists in aggregate at the BIN/merchant level | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec: cross-session aggregation window and thresholds]` |
| Legitimate flash sale or artist drop spikes authorisation volume | High velocity from many distinct real buyers, distinct devices, distinct funding sources — the inverse of the card-testing fingerprint (few cards, one device) | Nothing; must not throttle a real sales spike |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Clean | Normal | Nothing |
| Throttled | Velocity threshold | Attacker: slowdown |
| Blocked | Card-testing certain | Attacker: hard failure |
| Held | Triangulation indicators | Seller: payout delayed with a reason (via 24.02.01) |
| Under investigation | Review opened | Seller: "under review"; buyer: nothing yet |
| Confirmed | Investigation concludes | Enforcement + buyer notification + 24.08.03 |

The state machine above is the feature's substance; there is no consumer-facing surface (all personas are None). The only rendered surface is the **internal review queue** (Admin, per personas.md Q-02) that the `Held` state feeds, and its standard UI states map as: **Loading** — case detail fetching; **Empty** — no held cases in the queue (the healthy default); **Populated** — a list of triangulation-indicator cases awaiting a human decision; **Error** — signal-source (PSP/shipping) fetch failed, case shown with a degraded-data banner rather than an auto-decision.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Card testing needs physical goods and a shipping address | The pattern most people picture | ❌ REJECTED — **digital goods are the preferred card-testing vector**, and D-06 puts a plugin marketplace in scope. Instant fulfilment, low price, no address, no human. A £0.99 sample pack that delivers a download link on authorisation is a perfect card-validity oracle. This is a **launch-day exposure for domain 14** — not a scale-gated concern — and it produces a specific product rule: *digital fulfilment must never precede settlement confirmation*, which is a checkout-design constraint, not a fraud-tool setting. |
| DT-02 | Fraud detection works because fraudsters behave badly on-platform | The premise under every behavioural model | ❌ REJECTED for triangulation, which is why it deserves its own feature rather than a rule in 24.03.01. The triangulator's on-platform conduct is **exemplary by construction**: they ship fast (a retailer does it for them), they never miss a delivery, their buyers are genuinely happy, and they accumulate real five-star reviews. Every reputation signal the platform has says they are a model seller. The fraud is entirely off-platform, at a retailer WeJammin has no relationship with, and it surfaces weeks later as someone else's chargeback. **The platform's own data cannot detect it** — which is worth stating plainly rather than assuming a rules engine will cover it. |
| DT-03 | Below-market pricing is a reliable triangulation indicator | It is the standard first heuristic | ❌ REJECTED as a standalone signal in this market. Musicians sell gear below market **constantly** and for reasons that are structural rather than suspicious: rent is due Friday, the tour needs a van, the amp must go before the move. `personas.md` describes a persona living on "a portfolio of small income streams" — distress pricing is a normal Tuesday, not an anomaly. Using it alone would flag the platform's most sympathetic sellers at their most vulnerable moment. It is only meaningful combined with dropship-origin signals and an absent acquisition history. |
