# Feature: Audio Fingerprinting & Content Matching

> **Number**: 24.05.02
> **Parent**: [Copyright & Authenticity Enforcement](./24.05-copyright-authenticity-enforcement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Acoustic matching of uploaded audio against reference recordings, producing `(asset, reference, confidence,
offset)`. On YouTube this powers Content ID, the most-hated system in music. Here it must be scoped
severely: a match is **a signal into a claim, never a takedown** (24.05 CX-01), because the false-positive
set in a professional music context is enormous and consists almost entirely of legitimate work. The
engine and vendor are routed to architecture (24.05 D-04); what is owned here is what a match *means*.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees a match only if it becomes a claim against them. Every musician arrives at this platform having been burned by an automated matcher elsewhere — a live version flagged, a cover taken down, their own song claimed. Their expectation is hostile and it is earned. |
| Producer | Read-only | The persona this feature is most dangerous to. **Their entire working method produces matches**: a rough mix matches the master, a stem matches the mix, a comp matches the take, an alternate matches the release. Every one is legitimate, and every one is acoustically identical to infringement. |
| Operator | None | Not applicable — they do not upload audio catalogues. |
| Fan | None | Not applicable, except a gig video matching a live performance. Even then, the match reaches them only as a claim. |

## Behavior

Matching runs against a reference set, asynchronously, on upload. It **never** removes, restricts, or
notifies. A match is weighed against the platform's own provenance graph before anything happens
(DT-03) — which is the inversion this product makes possible.

The engine has a hard architectural problem: fingerprint extraction is heavy DSP requiring a reference
database, and it **will not run in the Cloudflare Workers execution model** — the identical conflict the
`Media Handling & Audio Playback` cross-cut already flags for transcode. That is an architecture decision
(24.05 D-04), and it is the reason this feature is a Could-have rather than a Should.

There is a second, cleaner use of the same machinery: leak matching (24.05 CX-02), where the reference is
the platform's own unreleased file and the question is unambiguous.

### Happy Path

1. Audio is uploaded to a project, a listing, a portfolio, or a catalogue
2. Fingerprint extraction runs asynchronously, outside the request path
3. A match returns with confidence and offset
4. The match is weighed against the provenance graph for that asset
5. Contradicted by provenance → nothing happens, and the contradiction is recorded
6. Not contradicted → available as evidence if a rights-holder files a claim (24.05.01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A producer's stem matches the master they made | Provenance contradicts the match — the upload predates the release, in their own session | Nothing |
| A licensed sample (bought on Splice) matches thousands of other tracks | Match is meaningless. **Two tracks sharing a royalty-free loop are not related** | Nothing |
| A licensed cover matches the original | Match is correct and irrelevant — mechanical licensing exists and the platform cannot see it | Nothing |
| A live version matches the studio recording | Match with low confidence, legitimate | Nothing |
| A DJ mix matches 40 references | 40 matches, all correct, all possibly licensed | Nothing automated; a human question if anyone claims |
| An interpolation (re-played, not sampled) | **Acoustically different, legally infringing.** The matcher misses exactly the case that matters most in modern music litigation | Nothing — a real coverage gap, honestly stated |
| Matcher unavailable | Nothing happens; no claims generated. **Failing open is correct** — the statutory path (24.05.01) does not depend on the matcher | Nothing |
| A match on an unreleased asset | Different question entirely — routed to 24.05.04, where it is nearly unambiguous | Owner: leak alert |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unmatched | No reference hit, or matcher unavailable | Nothing |
| Matched | Reference hit | Nothing — a match is not a user-facing event |
| Contradicted | Provenance says the uploader made it | Nothing; the contradiction is retained as evidence for later |
| Evidence | A claim was filed and the match is relevant | The match appears inside the claim (24.05.01), never alone |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An acoustic match indicates infringement | The premise of Content ID and every matcher built since | ❌ REJECTED — in a **professional** music context the false-positive set is not an edge case, it is the bulk of the data: your own stem matching your own master; a licensed Splice loop shared by ten thousand tracks; a licensed cover; a live version; an alternate mix; a DJ set; a remix with a signed licence. Content ID's failure mode is exactly this, and it is why musicians hate it. **Worse, the matcher misses the case that matters most**: an interpolation is re-played rather than sampled, so it is acoustically different and legally infringing — most modern music litigation is about interpolation, melody and feel, none of which fingerprinting sees. So the tool has a huge false-positive rate on legitimate work *and* a blind spot on the real infringement. A match is a weak signal into a human claim, and nothing more. |
| DT-02 | Fingerprinting fits the platform's architecture | The assumption if you only read the feature list | ❌ REJECTED — fingerprint extraction is heavy DSP against a large reference database, and **it does not fit the Cloudflare Workers execution model**, which `meta/constraints.md` locks. The `Media Handling & Audio Playback` cross-cut already flags the identical conflict for transcode ("a hard architecture conflict"), reaching the same conclusion independently. Combined with vendor cost (Audible Magic / Pex / ACRCloud are not cheap) and DT-01's poor signal quality, this feature is expensive, architecturally awkward, and weak — which is precisely why it is a Could and not the "obvious" copyright feature it appears to be. |
| DT-03 | Fingerprinting is the best available evidence about who made a recording | It is, for everyone else | ❌ REJECTED — **WeJammin has better, and this is the domain's strongest wedge-aligned finding.** Fingerprinting infers a relationship between two audio files from acoustic similarity. The platform's provenance graph *records* the relationship: this stem was uploaded by this producer at this timestamp into this project, and these three contributors counter-attested it (D-18). That is contemporaneous, counter-attested, first-party evidence — categorically stronger than acoustic inference, and it exists precisely because the platform was in the room (`problem-statement.md`: "absence at the point of truth" is the root cause everyone else suffers from). **No competitor can retroactively manufacture it.** The strategic consequence is that the platform should invest in making the provenance graph queryable as evidence *before* it invests in a matcher — the cheap thing is better than the expensive thing here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A match never removes, restricts, or notifies | DT-01. It is evidence, and weak evidence at that. |
| D-02 | A match is weighed against the provenance graph before it becomes a claim | DT-03. The platform has better evidence than the matcher produces. |
| D-03 | Fail open — an unavailable matcher generates no claims | The statutory path does not depend on it, and a matcher that fails closed would remove art. |
