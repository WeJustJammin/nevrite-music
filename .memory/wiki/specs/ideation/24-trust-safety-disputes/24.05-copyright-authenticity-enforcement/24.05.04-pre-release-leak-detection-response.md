# Feature: Pre-Release Leak Detection & Response

> **Number**: 24.05.04
> **Parent**: [Copyright & Authenticity Enforcement](./24.05-copyright-authenticity-enforcement-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's candidate list

## Overview

The forensic response when unreleased material escapes: per-recipient watermarking applied at delivery,
access-log forensics that answer "who had this", and the response path when a leak is reported. WeJammin
will store unreleased masters (D-10 puts the rights stack at the centre; the `Media Handling` cross-cut
already names "leak protection for unreleased material" and the `Audit Log` cross-cut already names "leak
investigation") — **and nothing in the candidate list owned it.** If a track leaks, the platform's access
log is the only record of who had it. The platform is the forensic authority whether it plans to be or not.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The artist whose record leaked. A pre-release leak is a career and commercial event measured in a lost first week, a burned campaign, and a label relationship. They want one answer — who — and they want it in hours. |
| Producer | Full | Both the party most exposed and the party most often blamed. They hold the stems, they invited the contributors, they run the room (`personas.md`: the session owner). When a leak happens, the finger points at their project first, and the access log is either their defence or their indictment. |
| Operator | None | Not applicable. Venues and studios do not hold unreleased catalogues on this platform — though a studio's *staff* may appear in the access log of a project, which is a Q-02 problem. |
| Fan | None | Not applicable — and structurally so. A fan who has access to an unreleased master is already the incident. |

## Behavior

Protection is **forensic, not preventive** (DT-02). Every delivery of an unreleased asset is watermarked
per-recipient at the point of transfer, so the leaked file identifies its own source. Access is logged as
a consequential action (the Audit Log cross-cut's own definition). A leak report triggers: identify the
asset, match the leaked copy (24.05 CX-02 — the clean version of matching, because the reference is the
platform's own file and its unreleased status is the platform's own fact), extract the watermark, trace
the access log, and produce a finding.

The **response** is mostly external: the leak is on Telegram, YouTube, a Discord, a private tracker.
WeJammin does not host it. What the platform contributes is the evidence to support notices elsewhere
(24.05 CX-04) and the internal enforcement if the leaker is a user.

The asset's **release state changes the meaning of an identical signal** — the same match, before release,
is a crisis; after release, a non-event.

### Happy Path

1. An asset is marked unreleased in its project (domain 07 / 12)
2. Every delivery to a recipient is watermarked with that recipient's identifier
3. Access is logged: who, when, what, from where
4. A leak is reported (by the artist, the label, or an external match)
5. The leaked copy's watermark is extracted → recipient identified
6. The access log corroborates → a finding, with evidence (24.09)
7. Enforcement against the leaker if a user; notices to external platforms; the artist is told **who**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Leaked file is a phone recording of a playback in a room | **No watermark survives, no log entry exists.** The analogue hole defeats everything | The honest answer: "we cannot trace this" |
| Leaked file is a re-encoded, pitched, or trimmed copy | Watermarking must survive transcoding — that is what forensic watermarking is for, and it is why DRM is not the answer | Trace succeeds |
| Watermark identifies a recipient who was themselves ATO'd (24.03.02) | The access log names an innocent person. **The tool produces an accusation, and the accusation may be wrong** | See Q-02 — this is the feature's ethical core |
| Producer's own account compromised; every project they own is exposed | An ATO here is a supply-chain compromise, not an account compromise (24.03.02 DT-02) | Owner alerted; all their projects' assets are in scope |
| Leak traced to a label's own A&R | The platform has evidence against a commercial counterparty who may also hold a trusted-flagger lane | `[PENDING]` — a real conflict with no comfortable resolution |
| Leak occurs after release | Non-event. Same signal, different meaning | Nothing |
| Nobody reports it; the platform never learns | Most leaks are found by the artist on Twitter, not by the platform | The report path (24.01.01) must have a leak reason code |
| Artist demands the leaker's identity | The platform has it. Handing it over is a disclosure decision, not a technical one | `[PENDING]` — Q-02 |
| A contributor (not the owner) tries to open the project's access log | **Denied.** The access log names every collaborator; exposing it to a co-contributor turns a forensic tool into a surveillance one. Read access to the log is owner-scoped (Roles cross-cut) | "Only the project owner can view access records" |
| Recipient downloads their watermarked copy, then re-shares it off-platform (WeTransfer, DM) | The design working as intended: the file they downloaded carries **their** per-recipient watermark, so an extracted copy still points at them regardless of where it travelled next | Trace succeeds; recipient identified |
| Two recipients' watermarks collide / cannot be distinguished at extraction | Watermark payload space and robustness must guarantee per-recipient uniqueness under expected transcoding; a collision produces an ambiguous, non-actionable finding rather than a false accusation | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`; finding marked inconclusive, never names one of two |

### States

**Asset lifecycle** (drives whether the machinery is armed):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unreleased | Asset marked pre-release | Delivery is watermarked; access is logged; the project shows who has access |
| Delivered | Recipient receives it | Recipient: the file. Owner: the access record |
| Leak reported | Report filed or external match | Owner: "investigating" |
| Traced | Watermark extracted, log corroborates | Owner: the finding |
| Untraceable | Analogue hole, or no watermark applied | Owner: the honest answer |
| Released | Asset goes live | Watermarking and tracing stop being meaningful |

**Investigation surface** (the owner's access-log / trace view — standard UI lifecycle):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Owner opens the access log or a trace is running | Skeleton rows; watermark extraction shows a determinate progress indicator (extraction is compute, not instant) |
| Empty | Project has unreleased assets but zero deliveries yet | "No one has been given this asset yet — nothing to trace" with the access-grant CTA |
| Populated | Access records / a completed finding exist | The access ledger (who, when, what, from where) and, if traced, the finding with its evidence bundle (24.09) |
| Error | Trace fails to run (watermark decode error, log unavailable) | "We couldn't complete the trace" with retry; the finding is never partially rendered as if complete |
| Untraceable-result | Trace completes but resolves nothing | The honest answer, explicitly stated — **not** an Error state; "we cannot trace this" is a valid, complete outcome (see DT-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Leaks are an external problem — the platform hosts files, it does not police the internet | The comfortable position, and the one that leaves this feature unbuilt | ❌ REJECTED — **the platform is the distribution point.** If a producer shares stems with four contributors through WeJammin and the track appears on Telegram, the platform's access log is the *only* record of who had it. The artist will ask. The label will ask. Refusing the role does not make the question go away; it makes the answer unavailable — and a label that cannot get an answer will not let its artists store unreleased material here, which forecloses the professional tier the rights stack (D-10) is aimed at. This is the mirror image of the product's thesis: WeJammin is present at the moment the file moves, so it can capture the fact. Nobody else can, because nobody else is in the room. |
| DT-02 | Leak protection means DRM — stop the file from being copied | The instinct, and a 25-year-old dead end | ❌ REJECTED — **the analogue hole is undefeated**, and music's leak surface is worse than most: a phone in a room during playback bypasses every technical control, and playback in rooms is how music is made. Every leak-protection system that has ever worked in music is **forensic** (trace after the fact) rather than preventive: per-recipient watermarking plus an access log. DRM would also break the actual workflow — a Producer's job is to send stems to people (24.01.05 DT-03), and a control that makes the job harder gets routed around via WeTransfer, which is the exact off-platform leak the feature exists to prevent. **The correct design makes the traceable path the easy path.** |
| DT-03 | Tracing a leak is a technical problem | It looks like one | ❌ REJECTED — it is an **accusation machine pointed at a project's own members**, and that is a product problem with no technical answer. The access log for a session contains the artist's closest collaborators. A trace names one of them. That person may be guilty; they may have been compromised (24.03.02); they may have played it for someone they trusted. The platform will hold a finding that can end a working relationship or a career, and it will be asked to hand it to a label. **Who receives the finding, and with what certainty attached, is a values decision that must be made before the first leak, not during one** — because during one, everybody involved is at their least reasonable. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Forensic, not preventive: per-recipient watermarking + access log, never DRM | DT-02. The analogue hole is undefeated and DRM breaks the Producer's actual job. |
| D-02 | The traceable path must be the **easy** path | DT-02. A control that adds friction pushes stems to WeTransfer, which is the leak. |
| D-03 | The platform accepts the forensic-authority role | DT-01. Refusing it does not remove the question; it removes the answer, and with it the professional tier. |

| Q-03 | **[OWNER]** What does the platform do when the trace leads to a **label's own staff** — a commercial counterparty who may hold a trusted-flagger lane?
