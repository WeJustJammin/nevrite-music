# Feature: Chargeback Management & Representment

> **Number**: 24.04.04
> **Parent**: [Transaction Disputes & Protection](./24.04-transaction-disputes-protection-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

Handling disputes that arrive through the card network instead of the platform: intake of the chargeback,
reconciliation to the platform's own case, assembly of the representment evidence, submission within the
network's deadline, and reconciliation of the outcome. The platform's win rate here is **not determined by
how well it argues** — it is determined by what evidence was captured at listing and dispatch, weeks
before anyone disputed anything.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | As a seller: sees a chargeback against them and the representment the platform filed on their behalf. The money is usually already gone from their balance, which for a persona living on a portfolio of small income streams (`personas.md`) is a cash-flow event, not a line item. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, less often. A services chargeback ("I didn't authorise this") on a mix that was delivered and approved is defended with the approval record — one of the few places the platform's session data is directly load-bearing. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees chargebacks on deposits and bookings, which are the hardest to defend: the "service" was a slot held open, and there is no delivery signature for a Saturday. |
| Fan | None | The Fan is the party who **filed** the chargeback, with their bank. They never see this feature — they experience it entirely through their card issuer. Listed as `None` deliberately: the platform has no surface for the disputing buyer, which is exactly why the chargeback exists as a venue at all. |

## Behavior

A chargeback arriving must **link to the platform's own case** by transaction reference (24.04 CX-03), or
the platform can refund and have the bank reverse the same transaction, and the seller pays twice.
Mediation halts on arrival (24.04 CX R-01) — the venue has changed.

Representment assembles the evidence bundle ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) into the
network's required shape and submits it inside the network's window, which is short and non-negotiable and
overrides every clock the platform sets.

**Chargeback ratio is an existential PSP risk**, not a loss line: exceed the network's threshold and the
platform's ability to accept cards is at stake — which would end the marketplace domains outright.
That makes suppression (protection, 24.04 CX-04; clear descriptors; fast refunds) more valuable than
winning.

The rails, the ratio, and the PSP relationship belong to the `Payments, Escrow & Payouts` cross-cut.
This feature owns the case, the evidence and the argument.

### Happy Path

1. A chargeback notification arrives from the PSP
2. It links to the transaction and to any open platform claim; mediation halts
3. The evidence bundle is assembled: listing snapshot, condition photos, serial, delivery proof with
   signature, the message thread, the approval record
4. Representment is submitted within the network's deadline
5. The network decides; the outcome reconciles to the case and to the seller's balance
6. The result feeds the risk record (24.03.01) — the seller's, and, if the buyer is a serial
   filer, the buyer's own risk profile — and the chargeback counts toward the platform's ratio
   regardless of who won

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer filed a platform claim **and** a chargeback | Must reconcile to one case, or the seller pays twice (CX-03) | Seller: one case; the bank's clock governs |
| Platform already refunded before the chargeback landed | "Already refunded" is generally the strongest representment argument available | Seller: representment filed |
| "Item not as described" on subjective condition, no condition photos at listing | **Effectively indefensible.** There is nothing to show | Seller: loss — and DT-01 explains why it was decided weeks earlier |
| Same case, condition photos + serial + signed delivery captured at listing | Defensible on facts | Seller: representment with the record |
| Friendly fraud — the buyer received the amp and says they didn't | Delivery proof decides it; the Shipping cross-cut's signature data is the evidence | Seller: representment with signed delivery; usually winnable |
| Chargeback on a booking deposit | No goods, no delivery, no signature. The evidence model is *substitutive* (DT-03): the hold record (slot removed from sale, timestamped), the accepted deposit/cancellation terms at checkout, the confirmation, and the message thread stand in for a delivery signature. The argument is "the service — an exclusively held slot — *was* rendered, and the buyer agreed the deposit was non-refundable." Weaker than shipping proof; wins turn on whether the terms were shown and accepted, not reconstructed | Operator: representment built from the hold + accepted terms; the persona least able to absorb a loss if it fails |
| Chargeback lands after the payout already reached the seller | The platform is out of pocket; whether it claws back from a seller who has spent the money or absorbs it is a money-transmission decision, not a T&S one | Seller: a debit or clawback attempt against balance — mechanics owned by Payments. `[PENDING — /create-prd]`; ties to 24.02.01 Q-02 |
| Chargeback ratio approaches the network threshold | Existential. Suppression beats winning | N/A |
| Triangulation chargeback lands at a **retailer**, not at WeJammin | The platform never sees it (24.03.04 DT-02) | Nothing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | Normal transaction | Nothing |
| Received | PSP notification | Seller: notified, funds already held or debited |
| Linked | Reconciled to a case | Seller: one case view |
| Representing | Evidence submitted | Seller: what was submitted on their behalf |
| Won | Network found for the platform | Funds restored |
| Lost | Network found for the buyer | Loss allocated per the protection policy (24.04.03) |
| Not contested | No evidence worth submitting | The honest state — an unwinnable representment wastes a submission and worsens the ratio |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Representment is a post-hoc job — assemble the best argument once the chargeback arrives | How chargeback teams are staffed everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — **the win rate was decided at listing and dispatch time.** The representment is an assembly job, not an argument job: either the condition photos, the serial, the signed delivery and the thread exist, or they do not, and nothing can create them retroactively. This is *precisely* the product's own thesis (D-18) applied to money: "a split captured on the day costs one signature; the same split reconstructed three years later may cost a lawsuit, and may be unobtainable at any price." Substitute "condition photo" for "split" and the sentence is unchanged. **Domain 13's listing schema is a chargeback control** — which means a Trust & Safety requirement lands on a marketplace domain's data model, and only gets there if it is stated now. |
| DT-02 | Winning chargebacks is the goal | The obvious metric | ❌ REJECTED — **suppressing them is.** The chargeback *ratio* is a network threshold with an existential consequence: breach it and the platform's card acceptance is at risk, which ends D-05, D-06 and D-07 simultaneously. A won chargeback still counts toward the ratio in most schemes. That inverts the priorities: clear descriptors, fast voluntary refunds, and protection (24.04 CX-04) are worth more than a good representment team. The cheapest chargeback is the one the buyer never files because the platform already made them whole. |
| DT-03 | Chargebacks are a gear-marketplace concern | The instinct — physical goods, physical fraud | ❌ REJECTED — the **hardest** chargebacks on this platform are the ones with no goods at all. A booking deposit has no delivery signature; the thing sold was a slot held open, and "I didn't receive anything" is literally true. A ticket has a scan record, if the Ticketing domain captures it. A service has a deliverable but no shipment. Each needs its *own* evidence model, captured at its own moment — the venue's hold record, the door scan, the approval timestamp. A chargeback strategy built around shipping proof covers one of four transaction types (24.04.01 DT-01) and silently loses the rest. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Chargebacks and platform claims reconcile to one case | CX-03. Two records is how a seller pays twice. |
| D-02 | Suppression over win rate | DT-02. The ratio is existential; a win still counts toward it. |
| D-03 | An unwinnable representment is not filed | It wastes a submission and worsens the ratio. "Not contested" is an honest state. |
| D-04 | Non-goods transactions use a **substitutive evidence model**, not a stubbed shipping one | DT-03/Q-01. A booking deposit represents on the hold record + accepted deposit terms; a ticket on the door scan; a service on the approval timestamp. Each captured at its own moment, per DT-01. |
