# Feature: Buyer & Seller Protection Programs

> **Number**: 24.04.03
> **Parent**: [Transaction Disputes & Protection](./24.04-transaction-disputes-protection-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

The published promise about who eats the loss when a transaction fails and neither party can be made
whole by the other: what is covered, up to what value, under what conditions, and what the claimant must
have done to qualify. It is the feature that decides whether a stranger will send £12,000 to another
stranger for a guitar — which is the whole gear marketplace (D-05), because **the high-value tail is
where the trust deficit actually blocks the sale**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the terms before deciding whether to buy a £12k bass from someone they don't know — and before deciding whether to *list* one. On the sell side, seller protection against a fraudulent INAD claim is what makes them willing to ship a vintage instrument at all. This persona is on both sides of the same programme, which is why it cannot be one-sided. |
| Producer | Read-only | Reads it for services. Their loss shape is different: they delivered work that cannot be un-delivered, and the client has the stems. Protection for delivered creative labour is a genuinely unsolved problem — see Q-02. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads it for gear flips and, more importantly, for **bookings — where it probably does not apply**. Protection programmes cover goods; a no-showed Saturday is an uncoverable loss because the "item" was time (`personas.md`). |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads it for merch and tickets, and is the persona a standard programme actually fits: low value, objective failure (it didn't arrive), complete remedy (money back). |

> **No persona has `None`.** Every persona transacts and therefore every persona reads the promise before committing money — there is no user of the marketplace to whom coverage terms are irrelevant. The **write/config side does not belong to any persona**: setting caps, tier boundaries, and payout authority is an Admin/Trust-Ops function (Q-02, `personas.md`), which is why the four persona rows are uniformly `Read-only` rather than one holding `Config`. Adjudication and payout are performed by Admin against the captured record, not by a party to the trade.

## Behavior

Protection pays only on an **adjudicated** loss meeting the policy — never on a mediated settlement
(24.04 CX-02), or failing mediation becomes a route to the platform's money. Coverage is conditioned on
the claimant's own conduct: shipped through the platform, paid through the platform, evidence captured at
listing and dispatch, claim filed within the window. Those conditions are not bureaucracy — they are the
same capture-at-source conditions that make the case decidable at all (24.03.03 DT-02).

**Caps are the whole design problem.** Used-gear value is a fat-tailed distribution: most transactions are
£50–£500 pedals, and the ones that matter are £5k–£50k instruments. A flat cap set where the volume is
protects nothing where the value is.

Protection is also the cheapest chargeback suppressant available (24.04 CX-04) — a buyer made whole
in-platform has no reason to call their bank, and chargeback ratios are a PSP-relationship risk.

### Happy Path

1. A buyer transacts within the covered conditions (on-platform payment, on-platform shipping)
2. Something fails; a claim is filed (24.04.01) and mediation is attempted (24.04.02)
3. Mediation fails; the case is adjudicated and a covered loss is established
4. Protection pays the claimant up to the cap; the platform pursues recovery from the at-fault party
5. The outcome feeds the at-fault party's risk record (24.03.01)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| £12,000 vintage bass never arrives; cap is £2,000 | Buyer loses £10,000 and the programme is worthless exactly where it was needed | The cap, stated **before** purchase — see DT-01 |
| Buyer paid off-platform because the seller asked | Not covered. This is also why disintermediation is a safety problem and not only a revenue one | Terms stated at the moment of the off-platform ask (24.01.05) |
| Seller shipped a real amp; buyer claims INAD fraudulently | Seller protection must exist, or sellers do not list. Serial + condition evidence decides it (24.03.03) | Adjudication on the captured record |
| Producer delivered a mix; client refuses to release escrow | The work cannot be un-delivered and the client has the files. Standard protection has no answer | `[PENDING]` — see Q-02 |
| Venue no-show on a booked Saturday | Almost certainly uncoverable: no item, no return, and the loss is a night that no longer exists | `[PENDING]` — 24.04 Q-02 |
| Adjudicated loss, at-fault party has no money | The platform pays and recovers what it can. Protection is a **platform-funded promise**, not an insurance product — unless it is; see Q-03 | — |
| Mediated settlement that leaves the buyer short | Not a covered loss. They agreed to it | Terms make this explicit before they accept |
| Counterfeit discovered a year later (a refin sold as original) | The window has closed, and authenticity fraud surfaces late by nature | `[PENDING]` — the vintage market's defining timing problem |

### States

Two surfaces have states: the **coverage indicator** shown pre-purchase and at the transaction, and the **payout lifecycle** after a claim is adjudicated. Both are covered.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Coverage eligibility being computed for a transaction (payment method, shipping route, item value read against tier table) | Skeleton on the coverage indicator — "Checking protection…"; checkout is not blocked while it resolves |
| Empty | Persona has no active transactions and no claims (e.g. a Fan who has never bought, viewing the programme page) | The programme terms rendered as a plain read: what's covered, caps by tier, conditions — no per-transaction state |
| Covered | Transaction meets the conditions | A coverage indicator at checkout, with the cap and the conditions |
| Not covered | Off-platform payment/shipping, or over cap | Stated **before** payment, not after the loss |
| Claim eligible | Adjudicated loss | "Covered — payment initiated" |
| Paid | Payout made | Confirmation; recovery pursued separately |
| Partially covered | Loss exceeds the cap | The gap, stated plainly |
| Denied | Conditions not met | The specific unmet condition, with an appeal route |
| Error | Eligibility check fails (tier table unreachable, valuation service down) | Coverage shown as **indeterminate**, never silently as "covered" — a retry, and a fail-safe note that protection is confirmed at payment capture, not at page render (never over-promise on a failed read) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A flat coverage cap works | It works on Amazon and mostly works on eBay | ❌ REJECTED — used-gear value is **fat-tailed**, and the tail is the business. A cap calibrated to the median transaction (£50–£500 pedals and cables) protects nothing on the £5k–£50k instruments, and those are exactly the transactions where the buyer's fear is what blocks the sale. **The trust deficit and the value are in the same place**, which is why a flat cap is not a conservative choice but a self-defeating one: it protects the transactions that did not need protecting and abandons the ones that did. The likely answer is tiered coverage plus escrow-with-inspection above a threshold — which couples this feature to the Payments cross-cut's escrow release triggers, not to a policy page. |
| DT-02 | Protection is a cost centre — a loss the platform absorbs to keep buyers happy | The standard accounting | ❌ REJECTED — it is **the gear marketplace's enabling condition**, and it is also the cheapest chargeback suppressant available (24.04 CX-04). Nobody wires £12,000 to a stranger for a guitar without one; Reverb's own high-value handling exists for this reason. Framed as a cost, it gets a low cap and it fails. Framed as the thing that makes the high-value market exist at all, it gets priced into the take rate for that segment — and the take rate on a £12k instrument can carry real coverage. |
| DT-03 | Protection can be extended to services the way it extends to goods | The consolidation instinct — one programme, all domains | ❌ REJECTED — delivered creative labour has **no return path and no objective failure test**. If a Producer delivers a mix and the client refuses to approve, nothing can be un-delivered: the client has the files, the Producer has spent the hours, and "not as described" is a matter of taste. Goods protection works because non-delivery is objective and returns are possible. Neither holds here. The honest answer is that services need a different instrument — probably escrow with milestone release and the platform's own session record as the fact base (24.04.01 DT-03) — not this programme with a services flag. Claiming otherwise would promise a Producer something the platform cannot deliver. |
| DT-04 | The coverage terms must be presented **before** the money moves, not surfaced only when a claim is filed | Every edge case in this file resolves to "the cap/condition was stated up front" — a promise disclosed only after the loss is not a protection programme, it is a denial letter | ✅ CONFIRMED — the coverage indicator (cap, tier, conditions) renders at the transaction, and the off-platform / over-cap / disintermediation cases all disclose their exclusion at the decision point (checkout, or the moment of the off-platform ask, 24.01.05). This is what lets a buyer knowingly accept an uncovered £50k transaction rather than discover the £2k cap after non-delivery. The promise is a pre-commitment disclosure, not a post-loss adjudication artefact. |
| DT-05 | A single programme can serve both buyer and seller without conflict of interest | The instinct to build one "protection" surface | ✅ CONFIRMED, but conditionally — buyer protection (non-delivery, INAD) and seller protection (fraudulent INAD claim) are the **two sides of the same adjudication**, decided on one captured record (serial + condition evidence, 24.03.03). They are not separate products; they are the two outcomes of one decision. The conflict is managed structurally: the platform is neither party, adjudicates against evidence captured at listing/dispatch, and pays the side the record supports — which only works *because* capture-at-source makes the record exist. Without that record, "protecting both sides" collapses into arbitrating memory. |
