# Feature: Mediation & Resolution Center

> **Number**: 24.04.02
> **Parent**: [Transaction Disputes & Protection](./24.04-transaction-disputes-protection-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Structured party-to-party negotiation before anyone adjudicates: a shared view of the same evidence, a
menu of concrete proposals (partial refund, return, repair, re-do, price adjustment, extra revision), a
deadline, and no third party naming a loser. On a generic marketplace this is a cost-saving step that
deflects tickets. Here it is something else — **music is a small scene, and the parties to a dispute are
almost always repeat counterparties who will need each other again.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Proposes and counter-proposes. The persona for whom the *relationship* usually outweighs the amount: the producer they are disputing over £400 is someone they may need on a record next year, and the scene is smaller than the disagreement. |
| Producer | Full | Same, more so — they are the hub `personas.md` describes as the de-facto project manager. A producer who wins a public adjudication against a session player has won £600 and lost a player, and everyone in their address book will hear about it. |
| Operator | Full | Proposes deposit splits, re-books, credit against a future night. **The most creative remedy space of the four** — an Operator can offer next Thursday, which costs them a perishable slot they might not have sold anyway. Nothing in a refund model can express that. |
| Fan | Full | Proposes and accepts refunds and replacements. The only persona with no ongoing relationship at stake, and therefore the one for whom mediation is genuinely just a step before a refund. |

## Behavior

Both parties see the same evidence bundle ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) — an asymmetry of
evidence is what makes people fight. Proposals are **structured objects**, not free text, so accepting one
executes it: a partial refund moves money, a return generates a label, a re-do reopens the engagement.

**Remedies extend beyond money**, and this is where music-specific value sits. An Operator can offer a
future slot. A Producer can offer a revision. A seller can offer a partial refund and let the buyer keep
the pedal. A generic refund-or-return model cannot express any of these, and they are frequently what both
parties actually want.

Mediation **stops** the moment a chargeback is filed (24.04 CX R-01) — the venue has changed and two
authorities on one dispute produce contradictory outcomes.

Settlements are the parties' agreement, not a platform finding, and therefore **do not trigger protection**
(24.04 CX-02) — otherwise both parties are incentivised to fail mediation and claim on the platform's money.

### Happy Path

1. A claim opens; both parties get the same evidence view
2. The filer states a desired remedy from the object's remedy set
3. The counterparty accepts, rejects, or counter-proposes
4. Agreement → the proposal executes automatically (money moves, label generates, engagement reopens)
5. The claim closes as settled; no adjudicator, no loser, no reputation event

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Counterparty ignores mediation | Timeout resolves in the filer's favour (24.04.01 D-03) | "No response — resolved in your favour" |
| Parties agree something the system can't execute (a swap, a favour, a future session) | The system must not pretend to execute it. Record it as an off-system settlement and close the claim | "Agreed outside the platform — closing" |
| Party mediates in bad faith to run out the chargeback clock | The card clock is not the platform's to extend; the seller is exposed | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Power asymmetry — a first-time session player vs an established producer | Structured proposals reduce it (nobody has to argue); they do not remove it | `[PENDING]` — see Q-02 |
| Entity party (a band, a studio) — who may bind it? | A settlement is a binding act; domain 01's mandate graph decides | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Settlement moves money held in escrow for five people | The band's and the producer's funds are affected by two people's agreement | Third parties notified — and they may object |
| Chargeback filed mid-mediation | Mediation **stops**; the network decides | "This is now with your bank" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Open | Claim filed | Shared evidence + proposal composer |
| Proposed | A party proposes | The proposal with its concrete effect stated ("you receive £180; the pedal is yours") |
| Countered | Counter-proposal | Both proposals, side by side |
| Settled | Accepted | Execution confirmation |
| Timed out | Window elapsed | Resolved in the filer's favour |
| Escalated | Refusal or impasse | "Under review" — adjudication |
| Halted | Chargeback filed | Mediation closed; venue changed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Mediation-first is a cost-saving deflection step | Why every marketplace builds it | ❌ REJECTED — on this platform it is a **network-health control**, and that changes what it should optimise for. `personas.md` describes a world of repeat counterparties: the Producer runs sessions with the same players, the Operator books the same bands, the Musician sits in three bands and works with everyone. Music scenes are small and reputational; an adjudication that names a loser burns a working relationship the platform itself depends on for supply. eBay can afford to name a loser between two strangers who will never trade again. WeJammin cannot, because the "loser" is someone the "winner" will need on a record in eighteen months. **Mediation should be optimised for a face-saving settlement, not for deflection volume** — and those two goals produce different products. |
| DT-02 | The remedy set is refund / return / replace | Inherited from retail | ❌ REJECTED — the most valuable remedies here are **non-monetary and music-specific**, and they are frequently what both parties actually want. An Operator can offer next Thursday (a perishable slot they might not have sold anyway — near-zero cost to give, real value to receive). A Producer can offer an extra revision (an hour of work versus a £600 refund). A seller can refund £80 and let the buyer keep the pedal. None of these are expressible in a refund model, and all of them settle disputes that a refund model escalates. Structured proposals must include them or mediation is just a slow refund. |
| DT-03 | Symmetric structured proposals neutralise power asymmetry | The hopeful assumption behind every resolution centre | ❌ REJECTED — the asymmetry is real and the product cannot fix it with UI. A first-call session player disputing with an established producer is negotiating with someone who can cost them a career's worth of future work, and both parties know it. Structure helps (nobody has to compose an angry paragraph; the proposals are concrete), but a player who accepts a bad settlement to protect a relationship has not been helped by a fair interface. This is worth stating rather than assuming away, because the platform's own thesis is that it protects the working musician (D-18) — and mediation is where that promise is quietly tested. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Optimise for face-saving settlement, not deflection volume | DT-01. The metric matters: deflection rate rewards grinding people down; settlement quality rewards keeping both parties on the platform. |
| D-02 | The remedy set includes non-monetary music-specific proposals | DT-02. A future slot and an extra revision are cheap to give and valuable to receive. |
| D-03 | A settlement is the parties' agreement, not a platform finding, and never triggers protection | CX-02. Otherwise failing mediation becomes a route to the platform's money. |
