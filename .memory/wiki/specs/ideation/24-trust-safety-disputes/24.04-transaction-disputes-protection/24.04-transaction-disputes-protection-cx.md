## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Claims ↔ Mediation

**Relationship**: A filed claim opens a structured negotiation between the parties with a deadline and a
menu of proposals (partial refund, return, repair, re-do, price adjustment). Only on timeout or refusal
does it escalate to adjudication. The state machine is claim → mediation → (settled | escalated) →
(adjudicated) → (protection).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: on both sides — buyer of an amp, seller of a cymbal, provider of a session. The same
  human may be mediating two claims from opposite chairs in one week.
- **Producer**: mediating a subjective service dispute where "not as described" has no objective test.
- **Operator**: mediating over perishable inventory, where no proposal restores what was lost.
- **Fan**: mediating a merch or ticket claim; the only persona for whom a refund is a complete remedy.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the claim owns case state; mediation owns the proposal thread. A settlement
   transitions the claim; the claim never transitions itself.
2. **Trigger chain**: file → notify counterparty → mediation window opens → settle or escalate. If the
   counterparty never responds, timeout must resolve *in favour of the filer* by default, or silence
   becomes a winning strategy.
3. **Permission intersection**: only the two parties may propose. For an entity party (a band, a studio),
   a recorded `communicate` mandate permits evidence and non-binding messages, while an independent
   `settle` mandate is required to make or accept a binding settlement. No delegate is inferred from
   membership; absent a qualifying delegate, the entity follows the ordinary non-response path (24.04.01 D-05).
4. **Notification fan-out**: both parties, plus any third party whose money is held in escrow pending the
   outcome — on this platform a single payment routinely splits across a band and a producer (Payments
   cross-cut), so a two-party dispute can freeze five people's money.
5. **State transition conflict**: a chargeback can land mid-mediation (CX-03). The card network's clock
   overrides the platform's, and the mediation must yield rather than race.

### CX-03: Claims ↔ Chargebacks

**Relationship**: The same underlying dispute, two venues. The buyer can file in both, deliberately or
not — most buyers do not know a chargeback and a platform claim are alternatives rather than a belt and
braces. If the platform refunds and the bank also reverses, the seller pays twice.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Operator (as sellers)**: the party who eats the double loss. For a working musician, a
  double loss on a £2,000 amp is not an accounting event.
- **Fan (as buyer)**: usually the filer, and usually unaware they have done both.
- **Producer**: rarely — services chargebacks exist but are less common than goods.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: one case, two venues. The chargeback must **link** to the claim on arrival,
   by transaction reference. Two unlinked records is how a platform pays twice without noticing.
2. **Trigger chain**: chargeback arrives → link → freeze the platform claim → represent with the evidence
   bundle → network decides → reconcile. If the platform already refunded, the representment's argument is
   "already refunded", which is generally the strongest one available.
3. **Permission intersection**: none — the buyer's bank does not ask the platform's permission.
4. **Notification fan-out**: seller (their money is at risk), and the escrow holder if funds are still held.
5. **State transition conflict**: **yes, and it is the defining one.** The network's deadline is short and
   non-negotiable; the platform's mediation window is its own. The card clock always wins, and the design
   must assume mediation gets interrupted rather than treating it as an exception.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 24.04.02 Mediation | 24.04.04 Chargebacks | No interaction, deliberately. Once a chargeback is filed the venue has changed — the card network decides, and continuing to mediate creates two authorities on one dispute with contradictory outcomes. Mediation must *stop*, not run alongside. Recorded because "keep talking while we represent" is the intuitive design and it produces a settlement the network then overrides. |
| R-02 | 24.04.03 Protection | 24.04.01 Claims | Considered making protection a claim *type* ("file a protection claim"). Rejected: protection is not a thing you file, it is what the platform does when an adjudicated loss meets a policy. Making it filable teaches users to route around mediation and creates a queue of speculative claims on the platform's own money. |
| R-03 | 24.04.01 Claims | Domain 09 split disputes | Explicitly rejected as an in-sub-domain pair (24.04 D-02). They share intake and evidence but not remedy — a songwriting share cannot be refunded, returned, or repaired. Putting the product's core asset in a returns queue would be the single worst structural error available here. |
