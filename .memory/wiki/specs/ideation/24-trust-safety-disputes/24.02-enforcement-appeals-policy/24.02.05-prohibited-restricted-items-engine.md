# Feature: Prohibited & Restricted Items Policy Engine

> **Number**: 24.02.05
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The machine-readable projection of the commerce rulebook, evaluated at listing time and again at
checkout: what may be sold, by whom, to where. On a generic marketplace this is a banned-keywords list.
On a music marketplace it is a genuinely hard, genuinely music-specific problem, because the single most
valuable category of goods on the platform — vintage instruments — is materially entangled with
**CITES-protected species**: Brazilian rosewood (*Dalbergia nigra*, Appendix I), the wider *Dalbergia*
genus, ivory nuts and inlay, tortoiseshell picks. A pre-1992 Martin is legal to own, legal to sell
domestically, and a customs seizure if it crosses a border without paperwork.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Hits the engine as a **seller** (D-05: multi-vendor physical marketplace; the multi-hyphenate flips gear). Sees a block with a specific reason at listing time and a destination-dependent warning at checkout: "this guitar's fretboard is Brazilian rosewood — it can ship within the UK; export needs a CITES certificate." Also hits it as a buyer whose order is blocked at their address. |
| Producer | Read-only | Rarely lists physical goods; encounters the engine mainly on digital goods (D-06) — cracked plugins, leaked sample packs, resold licences that the licence terms forbid transferring. |
| Operator | Full | Lists gear alongside rooms and is the persona most likely to be a **Trader** rather than a private seller (the Tax cross-cut holds that classification on the Identity entity), which changes what they may sell and what they must disclose. Sees the block at listing time, on a phone, at a loading dock. |
| Fan | Read-only | Encounters it as a buyer whose order is blocked at the border, and as a reporter of listings that should have been blocked. Never lists regulated goods, but does list resold tickets — where the restriction is jurisdictional resale law, not a species. |

## Behavior

Rules are `(category | attribute | material | serial-state) × (seller-class) × (destination) → allow |
warn | require-evidence | block`, projected from the policy library (24.02.04) so a blocked listing cites
the same rule ID a moderator would cite for the same goods.

**Restriction is destination-dependent**, so the engine runs twice: at listing (is this listable at all?)
and at checkout/shipping (is this shippable *there*?). A listing gate alone is wrong — the same guitar is
a legal domestic sale and an illegal export.

Three music-specific rule families carry the weight:
- **Protected materials** — CITES/Lacey exposure on rosewood, ivory, tortoiseshell, ebony. Documented
  instruments are sellable with paperwork; undocumented ones are export-blocked.
- **Stolen goods** — a serial number on the stolen register (domain 15) must block the listing. This is
  the engine's tightest cross-domain coupling and it makes the Gear Registry a fraud control, not a
  nice-to-have.
- **Illegitimate digital goods** — cracked plugins, leaked sample packs, non-transferable licence resale.

Plus the ordinary hazards a music platform actually has: counterfeit mics and pedals (→ 24.05.03),
pyrotechnics and lasers for show production (domain 18), replica firearms as stage props.

### Happy Path

1. Seller creates a listing; the engine evaluates category, attributes, declared materials, serial
2. Clean → publishes
3. `require-evidence` → seller is asked for the specific document (CITES certificate, proof of purchase)
4. Buyer checks out to a destination; the engine re-evaluates against that destination
5. Clean → order proceeds. Restricted → the buyer is told before payment, not after

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 1968 Martin D-28, Brazilian rosewood, no paperwork, buyer in the US, seller in the UK | Listing allowed; **export blocked at checkout** | Seller at listing: "listable domestically". Buyer at checkout: "cannot ship to your country without a CITES certificate" |
| Same guitar, seller has the CITES certificate | `require-evidence` satisfied; export allowed | Certificate attached to the listing as a selling point |
| Seller does not know their fretboard is rosewood | The engine cannot know either — material is a **declared** attribute and sellers will get it wrong | Guided prompt by model/era from the canonical catalog, not a free-text field |
| Serial matches the stolen register (domain 15) | Listing blocked; the block is itself a safety event, not just a gate refusal | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — telling the lister *why* tips off a thief; not telling them is opaque. Real tension, unresolved |
| Cracked plugin listed as "full version, no licence needed" | Blocked; escalates to 24.05.03 and the seller's risk record | Specific reason, not "violates our terms" |
| Rule changes; live listings become non-compliant | Re-evaluate existing listings on rule publish — a gate that only runs on create leaves a legacy of violations | Seller notified with the specific rule and a remedy |
| Ticket resale above face value where jurisdiction forbids it | Destination/jurisdiction rule, same engine | Blocked at listing with the jurisdiction named |
| Engine unavailable | **Fail closed** — the one place in domain 24 where failing closed is correct, because publishing an illegal listing is worse than delaying a legal one | "Listing temporarily unavailable — try again shortly" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Allowed | Clean evaluation | Listing publishes |
| Warned | Restricted but permitted | Publishes with a disclosure the buyer sees |
| Evidence required | `require-evidence` rule | Upload prompt naming the exact document |
| Blocked (listing) | Prohibited outright | Specific reason + rule ID + correction path |
| Blocked (destination) | Legal here, not there | At checkout, before payment |
| Re-evaluation pending | Rule changed under a live listing | "Action needed by {date}" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A generic prohibited-items list (weapons, drugs, counterfeits) covers a music marketplace | Every marketplace's policy page | ❌ REJECTED — music's signature restricted-goods problem is **CITES/Lacey exposure on the platform's most valuable inventory**. Brazilian rosewood is Appendix I; the whole *Dalbergia* genus was listed in 2017 (with a later carve-out for finished instruments that itself needs encoding); Gibson was raided under the Lacey Act in 2011 over rosewood and ebony. No generic list encodes "pre-Convention Brazilian rosewood with documentation, else export-blocked". This is not an edge case — it is a large share of the vintage market, which is the high-value tail the whole gear domain depends on (24.04.03 DT-01 makes the same point from the protection side). |
| DT-02 | The prohibited-items check is a listing-time gate | The obvious design | ❌ REJECTED — restriction is **destination-dependent**, so the engine must run at checkout too. The same instrument is a legal domestic sale and an illegal export. A listing-only gate either blocks legal domestic sales (destroying the market) or permits illegal exports (destroying the platform). This forces a coupling to the Shipping and Localization cross-cuts that a listing-time-only design would never surface. |
| DT-03 | Material and provenance attributes can be self-declared free-text | The cheap build | ❌ REJECTED — sellers **do not know**. A working musician selling a '68 Martin has no idea the fretboard is *Dalbergia nigra*, and a free-text "wood: rosewood" is unenforceable and unsearchable. The engine only works if material is a structured attribute inferred from the canonical catalog (make/model/year → likely materials → confirm), which makes this feature dependent on the `Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution` cross-cut. Without a catalog, this feature is theatre. |
