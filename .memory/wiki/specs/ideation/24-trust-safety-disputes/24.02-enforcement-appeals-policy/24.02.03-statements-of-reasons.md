# Feature: Statements of Reasons

> **Number**: 24.02.03
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The structured notice that must accompany every restriction the platform imposes on a user's content,
visibility, monetisation, or account. DSA Art 17 enumerates what it must contain — the facts relied on,
whether automated means were used, the legal or contractual ground, the territorial and temporal scope,
and the redress options — and Art 17 sits in Section 2, which has **no micro/small-enterprise carve-out**.
This is a launch obligation, and its field list is effectively a schema specification for the decision
record that produces it. The SoR is a *projection* of a structured decision, never a hand-typed letter:
that inversion — schema before sanction — is the feature's single load-bearing consequence.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (recipient) | Receives the SoR when sanctioned. For this persona it is the document that says why their income stopped — the specificity of the **facts-relied-on** field determines whether they can mount an Art 20 appeal or are reduced to guessing. A vague SoR is functionally a denial of the appeal right (24.02.02 CX-06). Because Musicians are multi-hyphenate, the SoR must name the **exact object** (this listing / this credit / this stem), not "your account", or the recipient cannot tell which of their five roles was hit. |
| Producer | Read-only (recipient, often collateral) | Receives it as the sanctioned party for a takedown on a project they own — but the takedown frequently has **one addressee and many victims**: contributors who also lost access to the shared artifact and are *not* recipients because they were not sanctioned. The Producer is the one persona who will notice the SoR is scoped to a person when the harm is scoped to an artifact (DT-03, Q-02). |
| Operator | Read-only (recipient) | Receives it. Cares most about the **duration** and **territorial-scope** fields — "how long is my room unlistable, and everywhere or just in {territory}" is the only question, and the answer is money and perishable inventory (personas.md). An SoR with an open-ended or missing duration is, to an Operator, unbounded confiscation. |
| Fan | Read-only (recipient) | Receives it and will not read the statutory body. The design consequence is real: the plain-language summary at the top must state what happened and the single next action in one line (D-03), or the obligation is satisfied while the user is not informed. The Fan population is also the one that generates the highest *volume* of account-level sanctions, so the SoR pipeline must sustain bulk emission without degrading (see Edge Cases). |
| Admin/Moderator *(not a persona — index Q-02)* | Producer of the source record | Not a recipient — fills the **structured decision record** (rule version, facts, automated-means flag, scope, duration) from which the SoR is generated. Cannot free-type the SoR body. Never sees or edits the emitted notice as prose; only the record. Listed here because they are the sole author of the SoR's inputs and the boundary between "typed" and "generated" lives at their interface. |

> No persona has Full/Config access: the SoR is machine-generated and immutable once issued (it is evidence).
> Every persona is a read-only recipient; the only write path is the moderator's structured decision, which
> is a different feature surface (24.02.01).

## Behavior

The SoR is **generated from the structured decision record, never typed**. Because Art 17 fixes the required
fields, the decision record's schema is fixed on day one, before the first sanction — retrofitting structure
onto a year of free-text moderation decisions is impossible, because the facts were never captured in a form
that can be projected (DT-01; same capture-at-source logic as the product thesis D-18).

A sanction and its SoR land **atomically** (24.02 CX-02): they take effect as a single all-or-nothing unit —
either both land or neither does. If SoR generation fails validation — any Art 17 required field is empty,
null, or malformed — the whole unit is refused and the **sanction never applies**. The correct compensating action for a post-commit emission failure
is to **roll back the sanction, not to retry silently** (DT-05): a restriction with no notice is the exact
failure Art 17 exists to prevent.

Two projections come from the one record with two different cadences:
1. **User notice** — delivered to the subject synchronously on sanction commit; blocking on the regulator's
   endpoint is not permitted.
2. **Transparency Database submission** — Art 24(5) requires SoRs to be submitted to the Commission's
   **public** Transparency Database. This is a public, mineable feed (DT-02), so it is a reputational surface,
   not merely a filing. It is submitted asynchronously "without undue delay"; a target of **within 24 hours**
   of the sanction is used, and its failure never blocks the user's notice.

An issued SoR is **immutable**. An appeal that reverses the decision does not edit or delete the SoR — it is
retained as evidence and marked `superseded`, and a **correction** is submitted to the Transparency Database
(the public feed must not continue to assert a reversed action).

### Happy Path

1. Reviewer completes a structured decision on object `X`: rule version pinned by ID (24.02.04), facts-relied-on
   populated, automated-means flag set to an explicit `true`/`false`, territorial scope set, duration set (fixed
   term or `permanent`), redress route resolved.
2. On commit, as a single all-or-nothing unit: the sanction takes effect, object `X`'s status becomes
   `restricted`, and the SoR is generated from the decision record. If any part fails, none of it lands.
3. The subject receives the SoR: **plain-language summary first** (what happened + the one next action), the
   full Art 17 statutory detail below (D-03). Delivered as its own notice, never batched into a digest
   (Notifications cross-cut).
4. The SoR carries the **redress route** (Art 17 mandates it), which is the entry point to the Art 20 internal
   appeal (24.02.02) and, where applicable, the out-of-court settlement pointer.
5. Within 24 hours the SoR is submitted to the Commission's Transparency Database (Art 24(5)) with the
   statutory-field payload; its transmission state is tracked independently of the user notice.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Required Art 17 field empty at commit (e.g. facts-relied-on blank, automated-means null) | SoR generation validation fails → the all-or-nothing unit is refused → **sanction never applies**. The moderator is shown a blocking error that names the exact missing field and cannot commit until it is filled | Subject sees nothing (the sanction never landed); moderator sees the blocking validation error |
| SoR emission fails *after* the sanction committed (infra fault between write and notice) | Compensating reversal: the sanction is undone and the object returns to its prior unrestricted state. No user-visible restriction persists without a notice (DT-05) | Nothing (the sanction is undone) |
| Two moderators sanction the **same object** concurrently | The platform admits only one sanction per object at a time; the first to complete wins, the second is refused and re-evaluated against the now-restricted object, and its moderator is told the object was already actioned. Never two SoRs asserting contradictory scope on one object | Subject receives at most one SoR per state change |
| Cited rule has since changed | SoR pins the **version in force at the act time** by ID (CX-01), not the current text | The rule as it applied to them, with its version ID and effective date |
| Automated means contributed | Flag is mandatory and honest — a classifier that *reordered a queue* is not "automated means"; a classifier that *decided* is. No null permitted | "This decision was made [with / without] automated means" |
| Facts contain the reporter's identity or reporter-supplied PII | Reporter identity is withheld where the report was permitted anonymously (24.01.01 D-02); reporter-supplied free-text is not passed through verbatim. The *facts themselves* are still stated | Facts without the reporter's identity |
| Moderator pastes defamatory / injected free-text or oversized content into a facts field | Facts field is length-capped at 4 000 characters and stored/rendered as escaped plain text — never interpreted as HTML/markup (security-first). Over-limit input is refused at decision time and the moderator must shorten it before committing | A bounded, escaped facts statement |
| Restriction is geo-scoped | Territorial-scope field populated; content stays live outside those territories | "Restricted in {territories}" (never a bare "restricted") |
| Multi-hyphenate sanctioned in one role while active in others | SoR is scoped to the **specific object and role context** (this marketplace listing), and the restriction and its notice apply only to that object. The same human's unrelated Operator venue listing or Producer projects are untouched and get no SoR | An SoR naming the exact object; other role surfaces continue to function |
| Producer takedown removes access for 6 collaborators | Art 17 SoR issued only to the **sanctioned addressee**; collaborators are not statutory recipients. Whether to send them a non-statutory courtesy notice is an open product-values question (DT-03, Q-02) | Sanctioned party sees the SoR; collaborators see loss of access with no SoR (pending Q-02) |
| Thousands of routine listing-gate refusals (24.02.05) | **Not** issued as Art 17 SoRs — a listing never published was never "restricted". It still needs a *specific* block reason citing the same rule ID a moderator would cite, but not a statutory instrument (24.02 CX R-03) | A specific block reason at listing time, not a statutory notice |
| Transparency Database endpoint unavailable at submission | User notice already delivered; the DB submission is queued and retried with progressively longer delays for up to 72 hours, after which it raises an internal alert rather than being dropped. The user is never blocked on the regulator's endpoint | Nothing user-facing; internal `transparency_submission` state = `pending`/`failed` |
| Appeal reverses the decision while its SoR is mid-submission to the Transparency DB | The original SoR is retained and marked `superseded`; a **correction/withdrawal** record is submitted to the DB so the public feed does not keep asserting a reversed action (CX-06) | The original SoR marked "reversed"; downstream feed corrected |
| Recipient's locale is unknown or unsupported | SoR is delivered in the platform default language with a language-availability note; the set of mandatory SoR languages is an open cross-cut question (Localization; Q-03) | SoR in default language + a note offering the appeal route |
| High-volume account-sanction burst (Fan-scale) | SoR generation and user-notice delivery must sustain bursts of at least 10 000 account-level sanctions per hour, with each subject's notice delivered within 60 seconds of commit; Transparency-DB submissions are batched under the 24h window. Generation is never dropped or coalesced — one sanction, one SoR | Each sanctioned user receives their own SoR |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not applicable | No restriction on the subject | Nothing |
| Generating | Sanction in flight (decision recorded, SoR being projected) | Nothing yet (internal only; not a user-visible spinner) |
| Issued (Populated) | Sanction and its SoR landed together (all-or-nothing) | Plain-language summary + full statutory detail + redress route |
| Error | SoR validation failed pre-commit, or emission failed post-commit | Nothing — the sanction is blocked (pre-commit) or rolled back (post-commit); no restriction persists without a notice |
| Partial | User notice delivered, but Transparency-DB submission still `pending` or `failed` | Full SoR (user side complete); the outstanding regulator submission is internal only |
| Superseded | Appeal reversed the underlying decision | The original SoR retained and marked "reversed" (it is evidence); a correction submitted to the DB |
| Submitted | SoR accepted by the Transparency Database | Nothing user-facing |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A Statement of Reasons can be a well-written email template | How almost every platform started | ❌ REJECTED — Art 17 enumerates required fields; a free-text notice cannot reliably contain them and cannot be machine-submitted to the Transparency Database. **The real consequence is upstream**: because the SoR is a projection of the decision, the decision record must be structured from the first sanction. This is the same "capture at source" logic as the product's own thesis (D-18) — a fact not captured at the moment it is true cannot be reconstructed later, and a year of free-text moderation notes cannot be back-filled into Art 17 fields. |
| DT-02 | The Transparency Database is a filing obligation with no product consequence | It reads like paperwork | ❌ REJECTED — the database is **public and mineable**. A music platform's SoR feed publicly reveals its takedown politics: how often it acts on label notices versus artist counter-claims, what it removes, whose claims win. For a product whose thesis is that it protects musicians (D-18), that feed is an externally-auditable scorecard of whether it does. It should be treated as a reputational surface the platform's own users will read back to it. |
| DT-03 | One SoR per action is sufficient | One action, one notice | ❌ REJECTED — a takedown on a Producer's project mix has **one addressee and many victims**: contributors lose access to a shared artifact and none receive an SoR, because none were sanctioned. Art 17 does not require notifying them. The product should, or the collateral damage of enforcement is invisible to everyone except the people it lands on. This is a music-specific consequence of collaborative artifacts and it needs a deliberate answer (Q-02). |
| DT-04 | One document can serve both a lawyer and a Fan | The obligation is to inform, and both are informed by the same text | ✅ CONFIRMED (with structure) — the statutory body serves the appeal; a Fan will never read it. Rather than *two* documents (a divergence risk, and a second thing to keep in sync), a **single ordered document**: plain-language summary first (what happened + one next action), full Art 17 detail below (D-03). Ordering, not duplication, serves both audiences and keeps one source of truth. |
| DT-05 | On SoR emission failure, retrying the emission is the safe default | Retries are the usual answer to a transient write failure | ✅ CONFIRMED that the safe default is the **opposite** — roll the sanction back, do not retry-in-place. A silent retry loop leaves a live restriction with no notice for the duration of the retries, which is precisely the Art 17 violation. The atomic guarantee is "sanction ⇔ SoR"; if the SoR cannot be delivered, the sanction must not stand. (The Transparency-DB *submission* — a separate, non-user projection — is the one thing that *is* safe to retry, because the user is already notified.) |
| DT-06 | A sanction on a multi-hyphenate should notify them at the account level | Simpler: one human, one notice | ✅ CONFIRMED that it must be **object-scoped, not account-scoped** — a Musician who is also an Operator and Producer cannot tell which of their livelihoods was hit by an account-level "you were restricted". The SoR must name the exact object and the role context, and the restriction must not bleed into unrelated role surfaces. This is a direct consequence of the personas being lenses on one human, not separate accounts (personas.md). |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **[24.02.01 Enforcement Ladder](./24.02.01-enforcement-ladder-sanctions.md)** — atomic (all-or-nothing) pairing of sanction and SoR; the SoR's field list *is* the decision record's schema (CX-02). Admitting only one sanction per object at a time is what serialises concurrent sanctions.
- Touches **[24.02.02 Appeals](./24.02.02-appeals-internal-complaints.md)** — the SoR is the appeal's entry point (carries the redress route) and its subject matter (the appellant contests the stated facts/grounds) (CX-06). A reversal marks the SoR `superseded`, never deletes it.
- Touches **[24.02.04 Policy Library](./24.02.04-policy-library-versioned-terms.md)** — the cited rule version is pinned by ID from there, in force at act time (CX-01).
- Touches **[24.02.05 Prohibited Items Engine](./24.02.05-prohibited-restricted-items-engine.md)** — listing-gate refusals are explicitly **not** Art 17 SoRs (R-03); they need a specific reason citing the same rule ID, not a statutory instrument.
- Touches **[24.02.06 Transparency Reporting](./24.02.06-transparency-reporting.md)** — same source decision record, different aggregation and a different legal regime (Art 24 has a micro/small carve-out; Art 17 does not — D-01, Q-01).
- Touches **[24.01.01 Reporting intake](../24.01-reporting-moderation/)** — reporter-anonymity rule (D-02) governs what identity may appear in the facts field.
- Consumed by the **Notifications & Alerts** cross-cut — delivery, channel, and the hard rule that an SoR is **never** batched into a digest.
- Consumed by the **Localization, Currency & Timezone** cross-cut — the SoR must be in a language the recipient understands (a stated Art 17 expectation, not a nicety); the mandatory language set is undecided (Q-03).
- Consumed by the **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — the SoR is immutable and retained even when superseded; it is evidence.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Split from Transparency Reporting (the sweep merged them) | Different legal status → different MoSCoW. Art 17 has no micro/small carve-out; Art 15(2)/Art 24 do. Merging buries a launch obligation inside a deferrable one. |
| D-02 | The SoR is generated from the structured decision record, never typed | DT-01. Fixes the decision-record schema on day one. |
| D-03 | Plain-language summary above the statutory text, one document not two | DT-04. The Fan persona will not read a legal notice; the obligation is to inform, not to file. One ordered document keeps a single source of truth. |
| D-04 | Sanction and SoR are one atomic transaction; failure rolls back the sanction rather than retrying the notice | DT-05. A live restriction with no notice is the Art 17 violation. |
| D-05 | SoRs are immutable; a reversal marks `superseded` and submits a correction to the Transparency DB, never edits or deletes | The SoR is evidence for the appeal and the audit ledger; the public feed must not keep asserting a reversed action. |
| D-06 | The SoR is object-and-role-scoped, not account-scoped | DT-06. A multi-hyphenate must be told which object/role was restricted; the restriction must not bleed into unrelated role surfaces. |
| D-07 | User notice is synchronous on commit; Transparency-DB submission is async within 24h and never blocks the user | The user's right to a notice cannot depend on the regulator's endpoint being reachable. |
