# Feature: Statements of Reasons

> **Number**: 24.02.03
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The structured notice that must accompany every restriction the platform imposes on a user's content,
visibility, monetisation, or account. DSA Art 17 enumerates what it must contain — the facts relied on,
whether automated means were used, the legal or contractual ground, the territorial and temporal scope,
and the redress options — and Art 17 sits in Section 2, which has **no micro/small-enterprise carve-out**.
This is a launch obligation, and its field list is effectively a schema specification for the decision
record that produces it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Receives it. For this persona it is the document that says why their income stopped — and the specificity of the "facts relied on" field determines whether they can mount an appeal or are reduced to guessing. A vague SoR is functionally a denial of the appeal right. |
| Producer | Read-only | Receives it, often on behalf of a project with contributors who also lost access and who are *not* recipients — an SoR has one addressee and a takedown can have six victims. |
| Operator | Read-only | Receives it. Cares most about the **duration and scope** fields: "how long is my room unlistable" is the only question, and the answer is money. |
| Fan | Read-only | Receives it. Will not read it. The design consequence is real: the statutory text must be present and complete, but the top of the notice has to say what happened and what to do in one line, or the obligation is satisfied and the user is not informed. |

## Behavior

The SoR is **generated from the structured decision record, never typed**. That inverts the usual build
order: because Art 17 fixes the required fields, the decision record's schema is fixed on day one, before
the first sanction. Retrofitting structure onto a year of free-text moderation decisions is not possible —
the facts were never captured in a form that can be projected.

A sanction and its SoR land atomically (24.02 CX-02). If SoR emission fails, the correct compensating
action is to roll back the sanction, not to retry silently — a restriction with no notice is the exact
failure the article exists to prevent.

Art 24(5) requires SoRs to be submitted to the Commission's **public** Transparency Database. That makes
the platform's enforcement record externally readable and mineable — a reputational surface, not just a
filing.

### Happy Path

1. Reviewer completes a structured decision: rule version pinned, facts, automated-means flag, scope, duration
2. Sanction is applied and the SoR is generated from that record in the same transaction
3. Subject receives the SoR — plain-language summary first, statutory detail below
4. The SoR carries the redress route (Art 17 requires it), which is the entry to 24.02.02
5. The SoR is submitted to the Commission's Transparency Database (Art 24(5))

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| The decision cites a rule that has since changed | The SoR pins the **version in force at the time of the act**, not the current text | The rule as it applied to them, with its version and effective date |
| Automated means contributed to the decision | The flag is mandatory and must be honest — a classifier that reordered the queue is not "automated means"; a classifier that *decided* is | "This decision was made [with / without] automated means" |
| SoR generation fails after the sanction applied | Roll back the sanction | Nothing (the sanction never lands) |
| Facts include information that would identify the reporter | Reporter identity is withheld where the report was permitted anonymously (24.01.01 D-02); the *facts* still have to be stated | Facts without the reporter's identity |
| Restriction is geo-scoped | Territorial scope field is populated; the content stays live elsewhere | "Restricted in {territories}" |
| Thousands of routine listing-gate refusals (24.02.05) | **Not** issued as Art 17 SoRs — a listing never published was never restricted; it still needs a specific reason | A specific block reason, not a statutory instrument (see 24.02 CX R-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not applicable | No restriction | Nothing |
| Issued | Sanction applied | Plain-language summary + statutory detail + redress route |
| Superseded | Appeal reversed the decision | The original SoR is retained (it is evidence) and marked reversed |
| Submitted | Sent to the Transparency Database | Nothing user-facing |
| Submission failed | DB unavailable | Nothing user-facing; retried — the user's notice is not blocked on the regulator's endpoint |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A Statement of Reasons can be a well-written email template | How almost every platform started | ❌ REJECTED — Art 17 enumerates required fields; a free-text notice cannot reliably contain them and cannot be machine-submitted to the Transparency Database. **The real consequence is upstream**: because the SoR is a projection of the decision, the decision record must be structured from the first sanction. This is the same "capture at source" logic as the product's own thesis (D-18) — a fact not captured at the moment it is true cannot be reconstructed later, and a year of free-text moderation notes cannot be back-filled into Art 17 fields. |
| DT-02 | The Transparency Database is a filing obligation with no product consequence | It reads like paperwork | ❌ REJECTED — the database is **public and mineable**. A music platform's SoR feed publicly reveals its takedown politics: how often it acts on label notices versus artist counter-claims, what it removes, whose claims win. For a product whose thesis is that it protects musicians (D-18), that feed is an externally-auditable scorecard of whether it does. It should be treated as a reputational surface the platform's own users will read back to it. |
| DT-03 | One SoR per action is sufficient | One action, one notice | ❌ REJECTED — a takedown on a Producer's project mix has **one addressee and many victims**: six contributors lose access to a shared artifact and none of them receive the SoR, because none of them were sanctioned. Art 17 does not require notifying them. The product should, or the collateral damage of enforcement is invisible to everyone except the people it lands on. This is a music-specific consequence of collaborative artifacts and it needs a deliberate answer. |
