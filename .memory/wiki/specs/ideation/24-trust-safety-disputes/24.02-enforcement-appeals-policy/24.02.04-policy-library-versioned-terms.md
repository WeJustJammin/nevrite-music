# Feature: Policy Library & Versioned Terms Acceptance

> **Number**: 24.02.04
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The rulebook, versioned, with effective dates, plus the record of which version each user accepted and
when. It is the feature nothing else in domain 24 works without: a sanction that cannot cite a rule that
was in force at the time of the act is arbitrary, a report taxonomy that is not bound to rules produces
reports nobody can decide, and an appeal against an unpublished rule wins automatically.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the current rules; on a sanction, reads the *specific version* cited against them with its effective date and their own acceptance date. The music-native rules matter most to them: what counts as a credit claim, what evidence a split needs, what an uncleared sample obliges them to disclose. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, plus the rules that govern their session-owner role: what they may assert about contributors, what a counter-attestation means, what happens when they close a project without splits (`personas.md` names this as their worst accidental behaviour — the rulebook is where the platform says whether that is merely unwise or actionable). |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads listing rules, cancellation/deposit policy boundaries, and the prohibited-items rules their gear flips hit. Their acceptance record is the one most likely to be an *organisation's* acceptance, not a person's — which is a domain 01 entity question. |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads the community rules and the ticket-resale rules. Will not read them. Their acceptance record still has to exist, because it is what a resale ban is enforced against. |

## Behavior

Rules are versioned objects with text, effective dates, and stable IDs. **Sanctions pin a version by ID;
they do not reference "the current rule"** — a later edit must not retroactively change what a past
sanction said. Acceptance records the (user, terms-version, timestamp, method) tuple. A material change
re-triggers acceptance; an immaterial one does not, and deciding which is which is a judgment the record
must preserve.

The library has three consumers and each is a projection of it: the report taxonomy (24.01.01), the
prohibited-items engine (24.02.05), and the sanction's cited ground (24.02.01). A rule that no consumer
projects is decorative.

**Seam with domain 01**: Identity owns *who accepted* (the entity/person model and the membership graph).
This feature owns *what was accepted, in which version, and whether it was in force at time T*. The line
is the same one the domain map already drew between Identity's entity model and the Roles cross-cut's
enforcement.

### Happy Path

1. A rule is authored, reviewed, and published with an effective date
2. Publication propagates to the taxonomy (24.01.01) and the items engine (24.02.05)
3. Material changes notify users and re-trigger terms acceptance
4. A user acts; the act is timestamped
5. A report is filed; a reviewer decides, and the ladder pins the rule version in force at step 4
6. The SoR (24.02.03) states the rule, its version, its effective date, and the user's acceptance date

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Act under v3, reported under v4, decided under v5 | Resolves against **v3** | The v3 text, with dates |
| A rule is tightened; old conduct becomes a violation | **Not retroactively enforceable.** The pin resolves to the rule in force at the act | Nothing — no sanction |
| A user never accepted the version being enforced | The contractual ground is weak; the statutory ground may still hold. These are different grounds and the SoR must say which | The actual ground, not a generic one |
| Terms accepted by a band's manager, enforced against a member | Whose acceptance binds whom? A domain 01 mandate question this feature cannot answer alone | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Rule publish succeeds; items-engine rebuild fails | Keep the old engine, alert. **Never fail open** — a gate that stops enforcing is worse than a stale gate | Sellers see the old rules until it is fixed |
| Rule text differs across languages | The authoritative version must be named, or two users are held to two different rules | `[PENDING]` — Localization cross-cut |
| A rule is deleted | Never deleted — superseded. Past sanctions cite it and must remain readable | Superseded rules remain retrievable by version |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Authored, unpublished | Nothing — unenforceable |
| Published | Effective date reached | Current rulebook |
| Superseded | A newer version took effect | Historical version, retrievable, marked with its date range |
| Acceptance pending | Material change | Acceptance prompt gating relevant actions |
| Accepted | User accepted | Recorded (version, timestamp, method) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Point-in-time rule versioning is a nice-to-have | It looks like documentation hygiene | ❌ REJECTED — without it, **every appeal wins on "that rule didn't exist when I did it"**, and the platform cannot prove otherwise. Retroactive rule-making is also the fastest way to lose the trust of a professional user base that is being asked to store its life's work here. The cost of versioning is trivial at day one and unrecoverable later: you cannot reconstruct what your rules said in 2027 from a wiki that has been edited since. This is the identical failure mode the product's own thesis describes (D-18) — a record reconstructed after the fact, at the moment it becomes contested. |
| DT-02 | Versioned terms acceptance belongs to Identity/onboarding | Acceptance happens at signup, so it feels like signup's | ❌ REJECTED, with a seam. Identity owns the actor; this feature owns the **enforcement-grade question**: "which version was in force at time T, and had this actor accepted it?" That question is only ever asked by an adjudicator, and the answer is evidence. Putting it in onboarding produces a boolean (`accepted: true`) which is worthless in a dispute. The line matches the one already drawn between Identity's entity model and the Roles cross-cut's enforcement. |
| DT-03 | One rulebook covers the platform | The default | ❌ REJECTED — 24 domains produce genuinely different rule *kinds*: a content policy (what may be posted), a commerce policy (what may be sold — 24.02.05), a conduct policy (how people treat each other), and a **provenance policy** (what a credit claim asserts and what evidence it needs). The last one has no analogue on any other platform and is where the product's differentiator gets written down as a rule. They can share machinery; they cannot share a document, because the fourth one is the only rulebook in the industry that says what a credit *means*. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Rules are pinned by version ID on every sanction, never referenced live | DT-01. |
| D-02 | Rules are never deleted, only superseded | Past sanctions cite them; a deleted rule makes an old SoR unreadable and an old appeal unanswerable. |
| D-03 | Four rulebook kinds share machinery, not a document | DT-03. The provenance policy is the one with no precedent, and it is the one that matters most. |
