# Feature: Policy Library & Versioned Terms Acceptance

> **Number**: 24.02.04
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The rulebook, versioned, with effective dates, plus the record of which version each actor accepted and
when. It is the feature nothing else in domain 24 works without: a sanction that cannot cite a rule that
was in force at the time of the act is arbitrary (24.02.01), a report taxonomy that is not bound to a rule
produces reports nobody can decide (24.01.01), a Statement of Reasons has no ground to state (24.02.03),
and an appeal against an unpublished rule wins automatically (24.02.02). Its job is to answer one
enforcement-grade question deterministically: **"for actor A acting at time T, which rule version was in
force, and had A accepted the terms version that governed that act?"** Every other consumer is a
projection of that answer.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the current rules; on a sanction, reads the *specific version* cited against them with its effective-date range and their own acceptance date and method. The provenance rules matter most to this persona: what counts as a credit claim (domain 02), what evidence a split needs (domain 09), what an uncleared sample obliges them to disclose. As a multi-hyphenate they hold **two acceptance records at once** — a personal one (their solo acts: gear listings, session credits) and, through any band they are in, an *entity* acceptance they did not personally sign. Which one governs an act is decided by the actor-identity the act was performed under. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, plus the rules governing their session-owner role: what they may assert about contributors, what a counter-attestation means, and — per `personas.md`, their worst accidental behaviour — whether closing a project without splits is merely unwise or actionable. The rulebook is where that boundary is written; this feature is where the version that was in force when they closed the project is retrievable. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads listing rules, cancellation/deposit-policy boundaries, and the prohibited-items rules their gear flips hit (24.02.05). Their acceptance record is the one most likely to be an **organisation's** acceptance (a studio LLC, a venue company) rather than a person's — which routes the "whose acceptance binds whom" question to the domain 01 entity/mandate model (Q-01). |
| Fan | Read-only | Reads the community rules and the ticket-resale rules. Will not read them (`personas.md`: the only persona with no professional stake). Their acceptance record still has to exist and be complete, because it is what a resale ban is enforced against and what a Statement of Reasons cites. |
| Admin/Moderator | **Author** *(not a persona — `personas.md` Q-02; listed because it is the actor the four personas never are)* | Authors, reviews, and publishes rule versions; sets effective dates; classifies a change as material or immaterial; can never sanction against, or pin, a Draft version. Authorship is the highest-privilege action in the domain (CX-01 synthesis Q3) and every author action is written to the Audit Log cross-cut. |

> No persona has `None` access: the rulebook is public-readable by design (a rule nobody can read cannot
> bind anyone), so all four personas are `Read-only`. The write role is not a persona — that asymmetry is
> the defining shape of the whole sub-domain (parent Role Matrix note).

## Behavior

Rules are **versioned objects** with immutable text, a stable rule ID, a version number, an
`[effective_from, effective_until)` half-open interval, and a `material | immaterial` change
classification. **Sanctions pin a version by ID; they never reference "the current rule"** — a later
edit must not retroactively change what a past sanction said (D-01). Published text is immutable: an edit
does not mutate a version, it mints a new one (D-04).

Acceptance records the tuple **(actor-identity, terms-version-id, timestamp, method, authoritative-locale)**,
append-only and tamper-evident (the Contracts & E-Signature cross-cut's archive is the model). `method`
is one of `signup-gate | material-change-reacceptance | api-agreement | imported-legacy`. A **material**
change re-triggers acceptance and gates the actions it governs until re-accepted; an **immaterial** change
(a typo fix, a clarified example, a formatting change) does not, and the record must preserve *which*
classification was made and by whom, because that judgment is itself contestable in an appeal.

The library has three machine consumers and each is a projection of it: the report taxonomy (24.01.01),
the prohibited-items engine (24.02.05), and the sanction's cited ground (24.02.01). **A rule that no
consumer projects is decorative** and is flagged at publish time.

**Time resolution is the core algorithm.** Given an act's server timestamp T and a rule ID, the in-force
version is the single version whose `[effective_from, effective_until)` interval contains T. The lower
bound is inclusive, the upper exclusive, so a version boundary is unambiguous even for an act at the exact
switchover millisecond. Effective dates are stored and compared in UTC (the Localization cross-cut owns
display); an `effective_from` earlier than the version's publish timestamp is rejected (no backdating —
D-05, DT-01).

**Seam with domain 01**: Identity owns *who* an actor is (the entity/person model and the membership /
mandate graph). This feature owns *what was accepted, in which version, in which authoritative locale, and
whether it was in force at time T*. The line is the same one the domain map already drew between Identity's
entity model and the Roles cross-cut's enforcement (CX-01, DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. An Admin authors a rule version; it is reviewed and published with `effective_from` set to a future
   or present UTC instant (never earlier than the publish timestamp)
2. The change is classified `material` or `immaterial`; the classification and its author are recorded on
   the version
3. Publication propagates atomically-with-alerting to the three consumers: the report taxonomy (24.01.01),
   the items engine (24.02.05), and the sanction ground index (24.02.01). Any consumer that fails to
   rebuild keeps its prior projection and raises an alert — it never adopts a partial one
4. If the change is `material`, all governed actors are notified and terms acceptance is re-triggered; the
   actions the changed rule governs are gated behind re-acceptance
5. An actor acts under a specific actor-identity; the act is stamped with a UTC server timestamp T
6. A report is filed (24.01.01); a reviewer decides (24.02.01); the ladder resolves the rule version whose
   interval contains T and pins it by ID onto the sanction record
7. The Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) states the rule text of that pinned version, its version number,
   its effective-date range, the authoritative locale, and the actor's acceptance date and method for the
   terms version that governed the act

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| # | Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|---|----------|-------------|-----------|
| E-01 | Act under rule v3, reported under v4, decided under v5 | Resolves against **v3** — the version whose interval contains T (DT-01, CX-01 Q5) | The v3 text, with its effective-date range and dates |
| E-02 | A rule is tightened; conduct that was legal becomes a violation | **Not retroactively enforceable.** The pin resolves to the version in force at the act; the new stricter version has no interval covering T | Nothing — no sanction |
| E-03 | Act at the exact millisecond a new version takes effect (boundary race) | Deterministic: the lower bound `effective_from` is inclusive, so an act at exactly `effective_from` is governed by the **new** version; one millisecond earlier, the old | The single version that owns that instant; never two |
| E-04 | The actor never accepted the version being enforced | The **contractual** ground is weak; a **statutory** ground may still hold. These are different grounds and the SoR (24.02.03) must state which one is being relied on, not a generic one | The actual ground relied on, and their acceptance record showing no acceptance of that version |
| E-05 | Terms accepted by a band's authorised representative; a member is sanctioned for a **personal** act | The **entity** acceptance binds the entity's acts (a band release under the band identity); a member's **personal** act (a solo listing) is governed by that member's **personal** acceptance record. The pin selects the record keyed to the act's actor-identity. *Which* representative may accept for the entity is a domain 01 mandate question (Q-01) — this feature records and applies whichever mandate domain 01 asserts; it does not invent one | The acceptance record that matches the actor-identity of the sanctioned act — personal or entity, never conflated |
| E-06 | A multi-hyphenate acts in two roles in one day: a band-release act and a private gear listing | Two acts, two actor-identities, two acceptance records consulted independently. The band-release act is governed by the entity acceptance; the gear listing by the personal acceptance. Neither borrows the other's version or acceptance | Each sanction cites the record for *that* act's identity |
| E-07 | Rule text differs across locales (translations diverge) | Each published version names **one authoritative locale**; translations are stored as non-authoritative renderings tagged to the same version ID. An actor is bound to the authoritative text, shown their locale's translation with a "translation of the authoritative {locale} version" notice. *Which* locale is authoritative is a user + counsel decision (Q-03); the mechanism (one authoritative locale per version, translations non-binding) is fixed | Their translation plus the notice; on a sanction, the authoritative text is what is cited |
| E-08 | Admin attempts to edit the text of a **Published** version | Rejected — published text is immutable. `returns 409 CONFLICT {error:'version-immutable', code:'POLICY_VERSION_LOCKED'}`; the API offers to fork a new Draft version instead | (Author) "This version is published and locked. Create v{n+1}?" |
| E-09 | Admin sets `effective_from` earlier than the publish timestamp (backdating) | Rejected — no retroactive enforceability. `returns 422 {error:'effective-date-in-past', code:'POLICY_BACKDATE_FORBIDDEN'}` | (Author) "Effective date cannot precede publication." |
| E-10 | Two Admins publish concurrent edits of the same rule (concurrency) | Optimistic concurrency on the rule's version counter. The first write wins and increments; the second sees a stale version number and `returns 409 CONFLICT {error:'stale-version', code:'POLICY_VERSION_CONFLICT'}` and must rebase its draft onto the new head | (Author) "This rule changed while you were editing. Review v{n} and re-apply." |
| E-11 | A sanction or the items engine tries to pin/evaluate a **Draft** (unpublished) version | Rejected — a draft has no effective interval and cannot be in force. `returns 422 {error:'rule-not-in-force', code:'POLICY_DRAFT_UNENFORCEABLE'}` | Nothing — the sanction cannot be built against a draft |
| E-12 | Rule publish succeeds; the **items-engine** rebuild (24.02.05) fails | Keep the old engine projection, alert on-call. **Never fail open** — a listing gate that stops enforcing is worse than one enforcing stale rules (CX-04, CX-01 Q2) | Sellers are gated against the previous rule set until the rebuild is fixed |
| E-13 | Rule publish succeeds; the **report-taxonomy** rebuild (24.01.01) fails | Keep the old taxonomy; new reason codes are not offered until reconciled. A report may cite the prior taxonomy; the reviewer sees a banner that a taxonomy update is pending | Reporters see the previous reason list until reconciled |
| E-14 | Empty state: no rulebook of the relevant kind has ever been published (fresh platform, or a new rule *kind* — DT-03) | Enforcement cannot proceed — there is no version to pin. A sanction attempt in that rule kind is blocked, not defaulted. Readers see "no rules published in this category yet" | (Reader) empty rulebook; (Admin attempting a sanction) "No published rule to cite — publish the governing rule first" |
| E-15 | Permission: any of the four personas attempts to author or edit a rule | Rejected — all four are `Read-only` by the parent Role Matrix. `returns 403 {error:'not-authorised', code:'POLICY_AUTHOR_FORBIDDEN'}`; the attempt is logged | The read-only rulebook, no edit affordance |
| E-16 | Malicious: an author backdates effective dates or edits published text to manufacture a violation | Structurally impossible — E-08 and E-09 both reject at write time, and every author action is append-only in the Audit Log. There is no code path that mutates a pinned version's text or its `effective_from` after publish | (Attacker) the same 409/422 as E-08/E-09; (Auditor) the attempt is visible in the log |
| E-17 | Malicious: a forged acceptance record is injected, or acceptance is claimed for a non-existent version | Rejected — acceptance is append-only against an existing published version ID; a write referencing an unknown or draft version `returns 422 {error:'unknown-terms-version', code:'ACCEPTANCE_VERSION_INVALID'}`. Records are tamper-evident; a mismatch is detectable on read | (Adjudicator) the acceptance ledger shows no valid record; the contractual ground falls to E-04 |
| E-18 | Malicious: rule text authored with embedded HTML/script, served to readers | Rule text is stored as data and escaped on render; no author-supplied markup executes in a reader's context (security-first). Rich formatting is a constrained allowlist, not raw HTML | Formatted text, never executed markup |
| E-19 | Duplicate acceptance: the same (actor, version) accepted twice (double submit, retry) | Idempotent — the **first** timestamp is authoritative; a duplicate `returns 200` with the existing record, never a second row. The earliest genuine acceptance is the one that governs | (User) "already accepted on {date}"; no duplicate created |
| E-20 | Partial data: a legacy-imported acceptance has (actor, version, timestamp) but no `method` | Stored with `method:'imported-legacy'` and a completeness flag; the SoR must **disclose** the gap ("acceptance imported from a prior system; capture method unknown") rather than assert a method it does not have | (Adjudicator) the record marked incomplete; (User) an honest SoR, not a fabricated method |
| E-21 | A rule is deleted | Never deleted — **superseded** (D-02). Its interval is closed by the successor's `effective_from`; past sanctions still cite it and it stays retrievable by version ID forever | Superseded rules remain retrievable, marked with their date range |
| E-22 | An `immaterial` change is later contested as having been material | The classification, its author, and its timestamp are on the version record; an appeal (24.02.02) can challenge the classification itself. If overturned, re-acceptance is triggered retroactively-forward (from the overturn date), never backdated into past enforcement | (Appellant) the recorded classification and who made it; the outcome of contesting it |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Rulebook or acceptance record being fetched | Skeleton of the rule list / acceptance panel |
| Empty | No published rule of the requested kind exists (E-14) | "No rules published in this category yet" — no enforcement possible |
| Populated (Published) | A version's `effective_from` reached and not yet superseded | The current rulebook; this is the in-force text |
| Draft | Authored, unpublished | Nothing to a reader — unenforceable, uncitable (E-11) |
| Superseded | A newer version took effect; interval closed | Historical version, retrievable, marked with its `[from, until)` range |
| Acceptance pending | A `material` change published for a governed actor | Acceptance prompt gating the governed actions until accepted |
| Accepted | Actor accepted a version | Recorded (actor-identity, version, timestamp, method, authoritative-locale) |
| Partial | An acceptance record is missing `method` or an in-force version has no successor projection reconciled (E-13/E-20) | A completeness/pending indicator; the record is usable but flagged |
| Error | Fetch failed, or a consumer projection is out of sync (E-12/E-13) | Retryable error to a reader; an on-call alert to Admin — never silent, never fail-open |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Point-in-time rule versioning is a nice-to-have | It looks like documentation hygiene | ❌ REJECTED — without it, **every appeal wins on "that rule didn't exist when I did it"**, and the platform cannot prove otherwise. Retroactive rule-making is also the fastest way to lose the trust of a professional user base being asked to store its life's work here. The cost of versioning is trivial at day one and unrecoverable later: you cannot reconstruct what your rules said in 2027 from a wiki edited since. This is the identical failure mode the product's own thesis describes (D-18) — a record reconstructed after the fact, at the moment it becomes contested. |
| DT-02 | Versioned terms acceptance belongs to Identity/onboarding | Acceptance happens at signup, so it feels like signup's | ❌ REJECTED, with a seam. Identity owns the actor; this feature owns the **enforcement-grade question**: "which version was in force at time T, and had this actor accepted it?" That question is only ever asked by an adjudicator, and the answer is evidence. Putting it in onboarding produces a boolean (`accepted: true`) which is worthless in a dispute — it has no version, no method, no locale, no time-resolution. The line matches the one already drawn between Identity's entity model and the Roles cross-cut's enforcement. |
| DT-03 | One rulebook covers the platform | The default | ❌ REJECTED — 24 domains produce genuinely different rule *kinds*: a content policy (what may be posted), a commerce policy (what may be sold — 24.02.05), a conduct policy (how people treat each other), and a **provenance policy** (what a credit claim asserts and what evidence a split needs). The last has no analogue on any other platform and is where the product's differentiator (D-10, D-18) becomes an enforceable rule. They share machinery — versioning, effective dates, acceptance — but not a document, because the fourth one is the only rulebook in the industry that says what a credit *means*. |
| DT-04 | An acceptance record is a boolean flag with a timestamp | The universal "I agree" checkbox | ❌ REJECTED — a boolean is exactly the reconstruction failure the thesis names. The record must carry **actor-identity, version ID, method, and authoritative locale** or it cannot answer any question an adjudicator actually asks: *which* version, accepted *how*, in *which* text, by *which* identity (personal vs entity). E-05/E-06 (multi-hyphenate and band) are unanswerable without actor-identity on the record; E-04 (weak contractual ground) is undecidable without the version; E-07 (locale divergence) is undecidable without the authoritative locale. The boolean produces `accepted:true` and a dispute the platform loses. |
| DT-05 | The platform is free to define "material vs immaterial change" however is convenient | It is an internal editorial call | ✅ CONFIRMED as a first-class, contestable, recorded decision — **not** a free internal call. The classification decides whether re-acceptance is triggered and therefore whether the contractual ground survives an appeal (E-04, E-22). If the platform could reclassify silently, it could quietly strip users of a re-acceptance right or manufacture one. So the classification, its author, and its timestamp are stored on the version and are themselves appealable (E-22). This is the same "capture the judgment at the moment it is made" discipline as the provenance thesis — the material/immaterial call is a fact that cannot be reconstructed later without the platform marking its own homework. |
| DT-06 | Rule authorship can be a normal admin CRUD screen | It is just editing text | ❌ REJECTED — authorship is the **highest-privilege action in the domain** (CX-01 Q3): it changes what every user may do, retroactively frames nothing but prospectively frames everything, and a malicious or careless author can gate or un-gate the entire platform. It therefore needs (a) immutable published versions (E-08), (b) forbidden backdating (E-09), (c) optimistic concurrency between authors (E-10), (d) an append-only Audit Log entry per action, and (e) a decorative-rule check at publish (a rule no consumer projects is flagged). None of that is normal CRUD; it is closer to a signed release process. |

- Touches **[24.02.05 Prohibited Items Engine](./24.02.05-prohibited-restricted-items-engine.md)** — the engine is the machine-readable projection of the commerce rulebook; a rule publish must rebuild the engine or the two disagree and a seller gets contradictory reasons (CX-04). **Feeds Step 6**: rebuild failure keeps the old projection and alerts (E-12) — never fail open here, unlike the engine's own fail-*closed* posture at listing time.
- Touches **[24.01.01 Report Intake](../24.01-reporting-moderation/24.01.01-report-intake-notice-and-action.md)** — the reason taxonomy is a projection of the library; an unbound reason cannot be cited. **Feeds Step 6**: taxonomy rebuild failure keeps the old reason set (E-13).
- Touches **[24.02.03 Statements of Reasons](./24.02.03-statements-of-reasons.md)** — the SoR cites this feature's pinned version, effective range, authoritative locale, and the actor's acceptance record; a `method:'imported-legacy'` gap (E-20) must be disclosed in the SoR, not hidden.
- Touches **Domain 01 (Identity)** — owns *who* accepted and the mandate graph that answers "which representative may accept for a band" (Q-01, E-05). This feature records and applies the mandate; it does not define it.
- Touches **Domain 02 (Credits)** and **Domain 09 (Rights)** — the provenance policy (DT-03) is where their semantics (what a credit asserts, what evidence a split requires) become enforceable rules; **Q-02** flags who authors that rulebook.
- Consumed by the **Contracts & E-Signature** cross-cut — terms acceptance is adjacent to, but not the same as, an executed agreement; the cross-cut's tamper-evident, append-only archive is the model for this feature's version store and acceptance ledger (E-16, E-17).
- Consumed by the **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** cross-cut — rule authorship is the highest-privilege action in the domain (DT-06); every author action and every material/immaterial classification is an audit-log row.
- Consumed by the **Localization, Currency & Timezone** cross-cut — one authoritative locale per version, translations non-binding (E-07); effective dates stored/compared in UTC, displayed per the user's timezone.
- Consumed by the **Notifications & Alerts** cross-cut — a `material` change fans out a re-acceptance prompt to every governed actor (Happy Path 4); consumer-rebuild failures fan out an on-call alert (E-12/E-13).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Rules are pinned by immutable version ID on every sanction, never referenced live | DT-01. A later edit must not change what a past sanction said. |
| D-02 | Rules are never deleted, only superseded; superseded versions stay retrievable forever | Past sanctions cite them; a deleted rule makes an old SoR unreadable and an old appeal unanswerable (E-21). |
| D-03 | Four rulebook kinds share machinery, not a document | DT-03. The provenance policy is the one with no precedent and the one that matters most (D-10, D-18). |
| D-04 | Published rule text is immutable; an edit mints a new version, never mutates the old | DT-06, E-08. Enables reliable point-in-time resolution and blocks retroactive rewriting. |
| D-05 | `effective_from` may not precede the publish timestamp; effective intervals are half-open `[from, until)` in UTC | DT-01, E-03, E-09. No backdating; boundary instants resolve to exactly one version. |
| D-06 | Acceptance is keyed to **actor-identity** (personal or entity), append-only, and records (version, timestamp, method, authoritative-locale) | DT-04, E-05, E-06. A boolean is worthless in a dispute; actor-identity is what distinguishes personal from entity acceptance. |
| D-07 | Each version names one **authoritative locale**; translations are non-binding renderings tagged to the same version ID | E-07. Prevents two users being held to two different rules; the *choice* of authoritative locale is Q-03. |
| D-08 | The material/immaterial classification is a recorded, appealable decision — not a free internal call | DT-05, E-22. Whether re-acceptance triggers decides whether the contractual ground survives. |
| D-09 | Consumer-projection rebuild failures keep the prior projection and alert; they never adopt a partial one and never fail open | E-12, E-13, CX-01 Q2. A stale gate beats a broken one. |
