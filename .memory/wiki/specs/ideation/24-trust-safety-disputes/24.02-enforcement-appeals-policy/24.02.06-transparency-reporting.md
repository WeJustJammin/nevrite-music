# Feature: Transparency Reporting

> **Number**: 24.02.06
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The periodic, public, aggregate account of what the platform did: notices received and acted on, by
category and source; own-initiative actions; sanctions by type; appeals filed, upheld and reversed;
median time-to-decision; automated-means usage. DSA Arts 15 and 24 require it — and both carve out micro
and small enterprises (Art 15(2)), which makes this the clearest Could-have in the sub-domain and a
deliberately deferred one.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Reads the public report. The number that matters to this persona is the one no incumbent publishes: **how often a rights-holder's claim beat an artist's counter-claim**. On a platform whose thesis is protecting them (D-18), that ratio is the scorecard. |
| Producer | Reports | Reads it for the same reason, plus the takedown-category breakdown — whether the platform is a place unreleased work can be stored without becoming enforcement fodder. |
| Operator | Reports | Low interest. Their trust question is about disputes and settlement, which this report barely touches. |
| Fan | Reports | Essentially no interest. Included for completeness: the report is public and unauthenticated, so a Fan can read it; none will. |

## Behavior

The report is a **projection of the decision record** (24.02 CX-05), never a separately-maintained tally.
That constraint is the whole feature: a hand-assembled transparency report is a fiction, and the only way
to produce a true one is to have structured every decision at the moment it was made — the same
capture-at-source logic the SoR imposes (24.02.03 DT-01) and the same logic as the product's own thesis.

Two audiences, one source: the statutory aggregate (Art 15/24 fields, machine-readable) and the human
report the platform publishes for its users.

**The disclosed process description projects from the sanction row too, so it moves with the row.**
Every sanction carries a `concurrence` field that is optional-with-a-reason (24.02.03 D-08): a
concurring reviewer's ID, or `below-concurrence-threshold`, or `s0-s1-clock-override`. Which control
actually gated a decision is therefore a **fact in the record**, not an internal detail, and the report
states it: below the concurrence threshold, S1 and rung ≥ 6 sanctions were decided by a **single**
reviewer under compensating controls (24.01.03 D-09, 24.02.01 D-07 as amended), with the disclosed
substitutes named and counted — the 100% blind re-review rate on S1, the cooling-off re-affirmation on
the rung-≥-6 path, and the guaranteed appeal (24.02.02 D-04) with its uphold/reverse split. Publishing
"two-reviewer concurrence" as the process while operating a one-reviewer process is not a rounding
error; it is the restatement failure DT-03 rejects, arriving a period early and by construction rather
than by accident. "We suspend accounts on one person's say-so, and here is what checks it" is a weak
sentence honestly published; the same sentence discovered later is a credibility event.

Note the relationship to the Transparency Database: individual Statements of Reasons are submitted there
continuously under Art 24(5) (24.02.03), which is a *different* obligation from this periodic aggregate.
The database submission is per-decision and is not carved out the way this report is.

### Happy Path

1. Every decision is persisted as a structured record with its category, source, means and outcome
2. At period end, the report is generated from those records
3. Statutory fields are produced in the required machine-readable shape
4. A human-readable report is published alongside
5. Both are archived immutably — a restated transparency report is a credibility event

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Decisions were free-text before the schema existed | The period cannot be reported truthfully. **This is why the schema must precede the first sanction** | A gap the platform must disclose rather than estimate |
| An appeal reverses a decision after the period closed | The reversal counts in the period it occurred; the original stands in its own period. Never restate | Both periods, honestly |
| Numbers are embarrassing (e.g. label claims overwhelmingly beat artist counter-claims) | Publish anyway. A report that only publishes flattering numbers is not a transparency report | The real ratio |
| Volume is so low the report identifies individuals | Suppress below a threshold rather than publishing pseudo-anonymous rows | Aggregates only, with suppression noted |
| The whole period ran below the concurrence threshold — every S1 and every rung ≥ 6 sanction decided by one reviewer | Disclosed as such, with the counts and the compensating controls named: the 100% S1 blind re-review rate and its disagreement rate, the cooling-off re-affirmations on the rung-≥-6 path, and the appeals filed and upheld against those sanctions. It is a real number the report publishes, not a footnote or an omission | The real process, including that it was one person, and what checked them |
| Report generation fails | Not user-facing; retried | Nothing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not applicable | Micro/small carve-out applies (Art 15(2)) | No report — and, honestly, a statement that none is published and why |
| Accruing | Decisions being recorded | Nothing |
| Generated | Period closed | Draft, internal |
| Published | Released | Public report + machine-readable aggregate |
| Archived | Superseded by a later period | Prior reports remain retrievable, never restated |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Transparency reporting is a launch obligation because the DSA requires it | Reading Art 15 without Art 15(2) | ❌ REJECTED — Art 15(2) exempts micro and small enterprises from the Art 15 reporting obligation, and Art 19 disapplies Section 3 (which contains Art 24) for the same class. This report is genuinely deferrable. **The thing that is not deferrable is the structured decision record it projects from** — and that lives in 24.02.03, which has no carve-out. Getting this distinction right moves one feature from Must to Could without losing anything. Flagged for counsel; an agent's reading of the DSA is not a compliance decision. |
| DT-02 | The report is paperwork with no product consequence | It reads like a filing | ❌ REJECTED — for this product it is a **thesis-verification instrument**. WeJammin's claim is that it protects musicians (D-18). The number that tests the claim is the one no incumbent publishes: how often a rights-holder's notice beat a creator's counter-claim. Publishing it invites the platform to be held to its own promise, which is exactly what a platform asking musicians to store their life's work here should want. Choosing to publish early — before it is required — would be a differentiator that costs almost nothing and cannot be faked retroactively. |
| DT-03 | A restatement is fine if the numbers were wrong | Standard reporting practice | ❌ REJECTED — a restated transparency report destroys the only thing a transparency report has. Archive immutably, correct forward, disclose gaps. This mirrors the Audit Log cross-cut's own reasoning: "in a dispute the question is never what the record says now but what it said in 2027 and who changed it." |
