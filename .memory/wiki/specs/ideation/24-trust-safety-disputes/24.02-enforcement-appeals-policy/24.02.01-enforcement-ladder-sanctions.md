# Feature: Enforcement Ladder & Sanctions

> **Number**: 24.02.01
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The catalogue of consequences and the rules for escalating through them: warning, content removal,
feature restriction, listing suspension, demonetization, payout hold, geo-scoped removal, account
suspension, permanent ban, entity-level action. On a normal marketplace this is a proportionality
matrix. Here it is the feature with the domain's hardest constraint attached: **WeJammin holds
co-owned ownership records (D-10, D-18), so a sanction must be able to remove a person's access
without touching anyone's property — including the sanctioned person's own share.** A sanction acts
on *what a party may do*, never on *what a party owns or is owed*.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Subject, never operator. Sees the sanction, its scope, its duration, the rule version cited (24.02.04), and the appeal route (24.02.02). The stakes are livelihood: a listing suspension is lost income, a payout hold is rent, a ban is a career built on this platform's credit graph becoming inaccessible. Their **splits, credits and co-owned masters survive every sanction** — that is D-03 of this sub-domain and it is non-negotiable. Because a Musician is routinely a multi-hyphenate (session player + seller + band member), a sanction scoped to one facet (e.g. a marketplace-listing suspension) must not touch the others (their session credits, their band's bookings) — see the multi-hyphenate edge case. |
| Producer | Read-only | Subject. Their exposure is distinctive: a takedown on a project mix affects every contributor to that project, none of whom were sanctioned. Sanctions on a Producer have blast radius by default, which is why scope must be an explicit field, not an implication — a content sanction targets the specific infringing artifact where separable, never the shared project container. |
| Operator | Read-only | Subject. Sanctions on a venue/studio hit perishable inventory — a listing suspension over a weekend destroys value that cannot be restored on appeal (`personas.md`: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever). Duration matters more here than anywhere else, which is why any suspension touching a calendar surface must carry an explicit end timestamp and default to the shortest rung that addresses the harm. |
| Fan | Read-only | Subject. The most-sanctioned persona by volume (D-13) and the one with the least at stake — an account, some tickets, some follows. Their sanctions are the ones where a rigid, low-discretion ladder is actually appropriate, because volume makes per-case discretion unaffordable and the downside of a wrong low-severity call is small and fully reversible. |

> **Note on the missing operator**: sanctions are *applied* by an Admin/Moderator, which is **not one
> of the four personas** (`meta/personas.md` Q-02). All four personas appear here only as *subjects*
> and (via 24.02.02) *appellants*. No persona has write access to this feature; that asymmetry is the
> sub-domain's defining shape and is why every row above is Read-only.

## Behavior

A sanction is a record `(subject, act, rule-version-pinned, scope, severity, duration, reversibility,
statement-of-reasons-ref)`. Every field is mandatory; a sanction missing any field cannot be applied
— it is rejected before it reaches the subject. Scope is explicit and **narrow by default** —
its allowed values form a containment order: `object ⊂ feature ⊂ domain ⊂ account ⊂ entity`. The
narrowest scope that addresses the reported harm is chosen; widening scope is an explicit escalation,
never an implication. Duration is explicit; `indefinite` is a distinct, higher-severity value, not a
default, and cannot be selected on the same rung as a time-boxed sanction.

**The ladder rungs, lowest to highest severity:**

| Rung | Sanction | Acts on | Reversible? |
|------|----------|---------|-------------|
| 0 | Warning | Nothing (notice only) | N/A — no state changed |
| 1 | Content removal / restriction | A specific object | Fully (object restored) |
| 2 | Feature restriction | One capability on the account | Fully |
| 3 | Listing / booking suspension | A marketplace or calendar surface | Nominally (perishable inventory may be lost — see 24.02.02 D-02) |
| 4 | Demonetization | Payout *eligibility on future earnings* | Fully forward; does not touch accrued balances |
| 5 | Payout hold | Withholding of *owed* money | **Gated — see the Payout-hold rule below** |
| 6 | Account suspension (time-boxed) | Login-gated action; data stays readable + exportable | Fully |
| 7 | Permanent ban | All access; ownership records preserved + exportable | By appeal only |
| 8 | Entity-level action | An entity's platform privileges | Fully for the entity; must not touch co-owners |

**Two ladders, one process.** The general ladder (rungs 0–8) is proportionate and discretionary. The
DMCA repeat-infringer ladder (24.05.01) is **rigid on purpose** — 512(i) safe harbour is conditioned on
a policy that is *reasonably implemented*, and BMG v. Cox and UMG v. Grande were lost precisely by
exercising discretion around a written policy. Discretion is the feature in one ladder and the liability
in the other. An act routes to the rigid ladder whenever the citing rule is a copyright-strike
rule and to the general ladder otherwise; a single act may land on both (a repeat-infringer strike *and*
a content removal), and the two are recorded as two sanctions, not merged. See
[24 CX-05](../trust-safety-disputes-cx.md).

**Entity vs member resolution.** Sanctioning an entity (band, label, studio) must resolve to the
*acting member* wherever the act was performed by an identifiable member under a personal mandate,
because an entity sanction punishes co-owners who did nothing. The resolution queries domain 01's
membership/mandate graph (01.03) and governance record (01.04.01):

1. If the act maps to a **single acting member** under a personal mandate → sanction *that member's*
   account (rung ≤ 7). The entity is untouched.
2. If the act was a **governance decision of the entity** (a recorded band vote / a decision the entity's
   own thresholds in 01.04.01 authorised) → the entity itself acted; an entity-level action (rung 8) is
   permitted, but it restricts only the *entity's* platform privileges (its ability to list, book,
   transact under the entity name) and **never** reaches any co-owner's personal account, share, credits
   or balance.
3. If the acting member **cannot be resolved** from the graph → entity-level application is **blocked**
   for an unresolved actor, and the moderator is told "no acting member identifiable; entity-level
   action requires either an identified actor or evidence the entity itself acted." The default when
   resolution fails is **no sanction on the entity**, escalated for actor identification — never a
   blanket entity ban.

**Payout-hold rule (rung 5).** Withholding *owed* money is a legal act with consumer-protection and
money-transmission consequences (`meta/constraints.md` flags the money-transmission posture as an
unresolved legal constraint). Until that posture is decided (Q-02), rung 5 is **disabled** for direct
moderator application: a rung-5 sanction is rejected as sanction-class-gated, with the
reason "payout holds are not an available enforcement sanction pending the money-transmission
decision." Fraud-driven withholding is not blocked — it is routed to the **Payments, Escrow & Payouts**
cross-cut's risk-reserve mechanism (a risk control with its own consumer-law footing), which is a hold
on *undisbursed escrow*, not a punitive sanction on *owed* balance. The distinction is deliberate: a
risk reserve pauses money still in flight; a payout-hold sanction withholds money already owed, and only
the latter carries the money-transmission exposure.

**Atomicity.** A sanction and its Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) are one atomic act (CX-02). If SoR
generation fails, the sanction is rolled back rather than left standing without its notice. A sanction
never lands alone, and an SoR never describes a sanction that did not apply.

### Happy Path

1. A decision is reached (24.01.03) citing a rule version as it stood at the time of the act (24.02.04, CX-01)
2. The subject scope is resolved: if the subject is an entity, entity-vs-member resolution (above) runs first
3. The ladder proposes a rung from prior sanction history on this subject + severity of the cited rule + the narrowest scope that addresses the harm
4. If the proposed rung ≥ 6 (suspension or above) **or** `duration = indefinite`, a second moderator's concurrence is required before application; the proposal sits in `proposed` state until concurred or withdrawn
5. On concurrence (or immediately, for rungs ≤ 5), the sanction is applied **atomically** with its Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) — neither lands without the other
6. Subject is notified with scope, duration, the cited rule version, and the appeal route (24.02.02) — target notification is the SoR itself, delivered at application time
7. Affected counterparties are notified where the sanctioned object has one — a suspended listing with a buyer mid-negotiation, a booking with a confirmed hold (CX-02 fan-out)
8. The sanction is written as one row to the decision record that transparency reporting (24.02.06) projects from (CX-05) — no separately-maintained tally exists
9. The sanction expires automatically on its `duration` end timestamp, or is reversed on appeal (24.02.02) with a restoration cascade (CX-03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Empty history** — first-ever sanction on a subject, no prior record | Ladder proposes the lowest rung consistent with the rule's severity floor; no escalation multiplier applies | Subject: the specific low rung, with the rule version and appeal route |
| **Banned musician holds 20% of a released master and is owed royalties** (property vs access) | **Access is removed; the share is not.** The ledger entry stands, the royalty accrues; whether the *payout* follows is Q-01, but the *ownership* never changes | Subject: "your account is closed; your ownership records, credits and balances are unaffected and remain exportable" |
| **Sanctioned Producer's takedown removes a mix six contributors depend on** (blast radius) | Scope targets the separable infringing act, not the shared artifact, wherever separability exists; where it does not, the shared asset is restricted and every contributor is notified | Contributors: "a shared asset is restricted, and why" — without exposing the Producer's case detail |
| **Multi-hyphenate sanctioned in one role bleeds into another** — a Musician suspended as a *seller* (rung 3, marketplace scope) who is also an active *Producer* on live projects | Scope is pinned to the marketplace surface; the sanction touches no object outside `scope`. The producer's project access, session credits and band bookings are unaffected | Subject: "your marketplace listings are suspended until {date}; your projects and credits are unaffected" |
| **Concurrency — two moderators apply a sanction to the same object at the same instant** | Enforcement status is tracked per `(subject, object)` pair; the first write wins and moves the object out of `none`, the second is rejected as already-sanctioned and shown the existing active sanction | Second moderator: the existing sanction, not a duplicate |
| **Race — sanction proposed while an appeal is open on the same object** | Double-application is blocked; the object will not accept a new sanction while an appeal is pending on it. A genuinely new, independent violation is recorded against the *account*, not the appealed object | Subject sees one active sanction per object; independent sanctions are distinct rows |
| **Race — sanction expires while its appeal is under review** | Expiry lifts the *access* effect but does **not** close the appeal; a subsequently upheld appeal still decrements the repeat-infringer counter and clears the record, because "expired" is not "vindicated" | Subject: "sanction expired; your appeal continues" |
| **Network / partial failure — sanction applied but SoR emission fails** | Atomic rollback (CX-02): the sanction is reverted; nothing is shown to the subject; the moderator sees the failure and retries. An un-noticed sanction is never left standing | Moderator: "sanction not applied — Statement of Reasons could not be generated; retry" |
| **Partial data — cited rule version cannot be resolved** (24.02.04 lookup fails) | Application is refused as rule-version-unresolved: a sanction against an unresolvable rule version is arbitrary by construction (CX-01) | Moderator: "cannot apply — the cited rule version could not be pinned" |
| **Permission — moderator applies a rung above their authority** (e.g. a rung-7 ban without concurrence) | Rejected as concurrence-required; the proposal is held in `proposed` until a second, distinct moderator concurs. One person cannot self-concur | Moderator: "this sanction requires a second moderator's concurrence" |
| **Malicious input — subject mass-triggers reports to manufacture a self-favouring record, or submits forged evidence** | Report-brigading is a 24.01 concern, not the ladder's; the ladder acts only on a *decided* case. Forged evidence surfaced later is grounds for the decision's reversal via appeal, not a ladder bypass | No ladder-level surface; handled upstream at 24.01/24.09 |
| **Band entity sanctioned for one member's act** | Entity-vs-member resolution runs (see Behavior): resolve to the acting member; entity-level action only where the entity itself acted via a recorded governance decision (01.04.01); if the actor is unresolvable, block for an unresolved actor and escalate — never a blanket entity ban | Acting member (if resolved): sanctioned individually. Co-owners: untouched, not notified of a personal sanction they do not have. Moderator (if unresolved): the block and its reason |
| **Sanction applied while an appeal is open on the same object** | Enforcement status is tracked per object; double-application is blocked | Subject sees one active sanction |
| **Payout hold requested as a sanction** | **Blocked** — rung 5 is gated (sanction-class-gated) pending the money-transmission posture (Q-02). Fraud-driven withholding is routed to the Payments risk-reserve mechanism instead (a hold on undisbursed escrow, not on owed balance) | Moderator: "payout holds are unavailable as an enforcement sanction; use the Payments risk reserve for in-flight funds" |
| **Repeat-infringer strike would be softened by proportionality** | Blocked. The rigid 512(i) ladder does not accept discretion; the strike stands at full weight | Subject: the strike stands; the general appeal route (24.02.02) still applies |
| **Sanctioned user is the sole admin of an entity that owes an open deliverable** (escrow, delivery) | The individual sanction **proceeds** and restricts the individual's account. The entity is **not** dissolved and its obligation is **not** seized: the entity is flagged `orphaned-obligation`; the open deliverable persists as the entity's debt; counterparties awaiting it are notified; any escrow tied to the obligation is **not** released to the sanctioned admin while the obligation is open. The genuine choice — force admin succession, appoint an interim, or freeze the entity — is a product decision (Q-04), routed to 01.04.04 succession machinery | Counterparty: "the party owing your deliverable is under restriction; the obligation stands and is being handled." Sanctioned admin: their personal restriction, with the entity's obligation noted as unaffected |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | Default — no sanction on the object/account | Nothing |
| Proposed | Rung ≥ 6 or indefinite duration awaiting a second moderator's concurrence | (Subject sees nothing yet — not applied) |
| Warned | Rung 0 applied | Notice; no capability change |
| Restricted | Rung 1–4 (object/feature/listing/monetization sanction) | The specific capability is unavailable, with the reason inline at the point of failure |
| Suspended | Rung 6 — account-level, time-boxed | Login permitted, action blocked, data readable — **exportable**, per the Privacy cross-cut's portability commitment |
| Banned | Rung 7 — terminal | Access closed; ownership records, balances and credits preserved and exportable |
| Reversed | Appeal upheld (24.02.02) | Sanction lifted + restoration cascade (24.02 CX-03), reported per-item |
| Expired | `duration` end timestamp elapsed | Automatic restoration of access; any open appeal continues |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A ban removes the user and their content — the standard terminal sanction | Every platform's ladder | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's most consequential finding. WeJammin holds **co-owned** rights records: a banned bassist's 20% share belongs to them, the split sheet is evidence for four other people, and the master's chain of title is the product (D-18: "the reason they cannot leave"). A DELETE CASCADE on ban would destroy other people's property and the platform's own evidentiary value in one action. **Sanctions act on access and privileges; never on the record.** The Privacy cross-cut reaches the identical conclusion from the deletion side ("anonymise-and-retain, not DELETE CASCADE"), which is independent corroboration. This is a data-model constraint, not a policy preference. |
| DT-02 | The enforcement ladder is one proportionality matrix | The natural design | ❌ REJECTED — there are **two ladders with opposite virtues**. The general ladder rewards discretion (a first-time Fan who posted an insult is not a repeat offender). The DMCA repeat-infringer ladder punishes discretion: Sony Music v. Cox produced a ~$1B verdict and UMG v. Grande $46.7M because they had a policy and exercised judgment around it, which courts read as *not reasonably implemented*. A written policy you do not execute is worse than none — it is evidence of knowledge. So one engine must host one discretionary ladder and one rigid, automatic, auditable counter. Building a single "proportionate" ladder is how the safe harbour is lost. |
| DT-03 | A payout hold is a cheap, obviously-available sanction | It looks like a flag on a row | ❌ REJECTED — holding money you owe is a legal act with consumer-protection and money-transmission consequences, and `meta/constraints.md` already flags the money-transmission posture as an unresolved legal constraint. The resolution here is not "defer the whole feature" but "**gate the specific rung**": rung 5 is disabled and rejected as sanction-class-gated, while fraud-driven withholding routes to the Payments risk-reserve (a hold on *in-flight* escrow, which has a different and firmer legal footing than withholding *owed* balance). This lets the rest of the ladder ship while the one legally-loaded rung waits on counsel. |
| DT-04 | An entity sanction is just an account sanction pointed at an entity account | Entities are first-class parties (personas.md), so they have accounts, so they can be sanctioned like one | ❌ REJECTED — an entity is co-owned, so an entity sanction is a *collective punishment* unless it is proven the entity acted. The resolution is a required **entity-vs-member resolution step** that consults the 01.03 mandate graph and 01.04.01 governance record: resolve to the acting member by default, allow entity-level action *only* where a recorded governance decision shows the entity itself acted, and **block** (rather than default-widen) when the actor is unresolvable. The failure mode this avoids — banning a four-piece band because one member listed a counterfeit pedal — is exactly the co-owner-harm DT-01 forbids, reached from the actor side instead of the record side. |
| DT-05 | Applying a sanction is a single write, so idempotency and concurrency are not concerns | The naive model of "set a flag" | ❌ REJECTED — the object's enforcement status moves through **distinct states with defined transitions**, not a single flag, precisely because three real races exist: two moderators sanctioning the same object at once (first-write-wins, second rejected as already-sanctioned), a sanction proposed against an object with an open appeal (blocked until the appeal resolves), and a sanction expiring mid-appeal ("expired" ≠ "vindicated", so the appeal must survive expiry). A single-flag model silently loses one of the two concurrent writes or lets an expiry close an appeal that would have decremented a repeat-infringer counter — a wrong strike that ratchets the rigid 512(i) ladder. Concurrency is not incidental; it is where the ladder's correctness is decided. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Sanctions act on access and privileges, never on the ownership record | DT-01. The single hardest constraint in the domain and unique to a provenance platform. |
| D-02 | Two ladders in one engine: discretionary general (rungs 0–8), rigid repeat-infringer (24.05.01) | DT-02. Cox/Grande are the evidence. A single act may land on both as two distinct sanctions. |
| D-03 | A banned user retains export rights over their data, ownership records and credits | The Privacy cross-cut's argument — "promising easy exit is what makes storing masters here acceptable" — has no bad-actor exception, or the promise is conditional and worthless. |
| D-04 | Scope is a mandatory field with a containment order (`object ⊂ feature ⊂ domain ⊂ account ⊂ entity`); the narrowest that addresses the harm is chosen | Sanctions on Producers and multi-hyphenate Musicians have blast radius by default; explicit narrow scope is the only guard. Widening is an escalation, never an implication. |
| D-05 | Entity sanctions require an entity-vs-member resolution step; unresolvable actors block application rather than defaulting to entity-level | DT-04. The default-to-entity path is collective punishment of co-owners, which D-01 forbids from the record side and this forbids from the actor side. |
| D-06 | Rung 5 (payout hold) is gated off pending the money-transmission decision; fraud withholding uses the Payments risk-reserve instead | DT-03. Withholding *owed* money differs legally from reserving *in-flight* escrow; only the former carries the money-transmission exposure. |
| D-07 | Rung ≥ 6 or `indefinite` duration requires a second moderator's concurrence; no self-concurrence | The highest-impact sanctions (suspension, ban, entity action) are exactly where a single wrong call is most expensive and least reversible. |
| D-08 | Object enforcement status moves through distinct states per `(subject, object)` pair, not a boolean flag | DT-05. First-write-wins under concurrency; appeals survive expiry; double-application is blocked. |
