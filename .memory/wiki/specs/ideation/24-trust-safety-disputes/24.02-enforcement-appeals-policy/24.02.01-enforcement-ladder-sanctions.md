# Feature: Enforcement Ladder & Sanctions

> **Number**: 24.02.01
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The catalogue of consequences and the rules for escalating through them: warning, content removal,
feature restriction, listing suspension, demonetization, payout hold, geo-scoped removal, account
suspension, permanent ban, entity-level action. On a normal marketplace this is a proportionality
matrix. Here it is the feature with the domain's hardest constraint attached: **WeJammin holds
co-owned ownership records (D-10, D-18), so a sanction must be able to remove a person's access
without touching anyone's property — including the sanctioned person's own share.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Subject, never operator. Sees the sanction, its scope, its duration, the rule version cited, and the appeal route. The stakes are livelihood: a listing suspension is lost income, a payout hold is rent, a ban is a career built on this platform's credit graph becoming inaccessible. Their **splits, credits and co-owned masters survive every sanction** — that is D-03 of this sub-domain and it is non-negotiable. |
| Producer | Read-only | Subject. Their exposure is distinctive: a takedown on a project mix affects every contributor to that project, none of whom were sanctioned. Sanctions on a Producer have blast radius by default, which is why scope must be an explicit field, not an implication. |
| Operator | Read-only | Subject. Sanctions on a venue/studio hit perishable inventory — a listing suspension over a weekend destroys value that cannot be restored on appeal (`personas.md`: an unsold Tuesday is gone forever). Duration matters more here than anywhere else. |
| Fan | Read-only | Subject. The most-sanctioned persona by volume (D-13) and the one with the least at stake — an account, some tickets, some follows. Their sanctions are the ones where a rigid ladder is actually appropriate. |

## Behavior

A sanction is `(subject, act, rule-version-pinned, scope, severity, duration, reversibility)`. Scope is
explicit and narrow by default: an object, a feature, a domain, an account, an entity. Duration is
explicit; indefinite is a distinct, higher-severity choice, not a default.

**Two ladders, one engine.** The general ladder is proportionate and discretionary. The DMCA
repeat-infringer ladder (24.05.01) is **rigid on purpose** — 512(i) safe harbour is conditioned on a
policy that is *reasonably implemented*, and BMG v. Cox and UMG v. Grande were lost precisely by
exercising discretion around a written policy. Discretion is the feature in one ladder and the liability
in the other. See [24 CX-05](../trust-safety-disputes-cx.md).

Entity-level action is a separate class: sanctioning a band, label, or studio entity must resolve to the
*acting member* wherever possible, because an entity sanction punishes co-owners who did nothing. The
membership and mandate graph that makes that resolvable is domain 01's.

### Happy Path

1. A decision is reached (24.01.03) citing a rule version (24.02.04)
2. The ladder proposes a sanction from prior history + severity + scope
3. High-severity requires concurrence before application
4. Sanction is applied atomically with its Statement of Reasons (24.02.03) — neither lands alone
5. Subject is notified with scope, duration, the cited rule version, and the appeal route
6. Affected counterparties are notified (a suspended listing has a buyer mid-negotiation)
7. Sanction expires on its own, or is reversed on appeal (24.02.02)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Banned musician holds 20% of a released master and is owed royalties | **Access is removed; the share is not.** The ledger entry stands, the royalty accrues, payout follows the money-transmission posture — see Q-02 | Subject: "your account is closed; your ownership records and balances are unaffected" |
| Sanctioned Producer's takedown removes a mix six contributors depend on | Scope must target the infringing act, not the shared artifact, wherever separable | Contributors: notified that a shared asset is restricted, and why, without exposing the Producer's case |
| Band entity sanctioned for one member's act | Resolve to the acting member; entity-level action only where the entity itself acted | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on domain 01's mandate model |
| Sanction applied while an appeal is open on the same object | Enforcement state is a state machine; double-application is blocked | Subject sees one active sanction |
| Payout hold as a sanction | **Treated as a legal act, not a UI state** — withholding owed money has consumer-law and money-transmission consequences | `[PENDING]` — gated on Q-02 |
| Repeat-infringer strike would be softened by proportionality | Blocked. The 512(i) ladder does not accept discretion | Subject: the strike stands; the general appeal route still applies |
| Sanctioned user is the sole admin of an entity that owes a deliverable | Entity continuity vs individual sanction — unresolved | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | Default | Nothing |
| Warned | Lowest rung | Notice; no capability change |
| Restricted | Feature/object sanction | The specific capability is unavailable, with the reason inline at the point of failure |
| Suspended | Account-level, time-boxed | Login permitted, action blocked, data readable — **exportable**, per the Privacy cross-cut's portability commitment |
| Banned | Terminal | Access closed; ownership records, balances and credits preserved and exportable |
| Reversed | Appeal upheld | Sanction lifted + restoration cascade (24.02 CX-03) |
| Expired | Duration elapsed | Automatic restoration |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A ban removes the user and their content — the standard terminal sanction | Every platform's ladder | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's most consequential finding. WeJammin holds **co-owned** rights records: a banned bassist's 20% share belongs to them, the split sheet is evidence for four other people, and the master's chain of title is the product (D-18: "the reason they cannot leave"). A DELETE CASCADE on ban would destroy other people's property and the platform's own evidentiary value in one action. **Sanctions act on access and privileges; never on the record.** The Privacy cross-cut reaches the identical conclusion from the deletion side ("anonymise-and-retain, not DELETE CASCADE"), which is independent corroboration. This must be settled at architecture time — it is a data-model constraint, not a policy preference. |
| DT-02 | The enforcement ladder is one proportionality matrix | The natural design | ❌ REJECTED — there are **two ladders with opposite virtues**. The general ladder rewards discretion (a first-time Fan who posted an insult is not a repeat offender). The DMCA repeat-infringer ladder punishes discretion: Cox lost ~$1B and Grande $46.7M because they had a policy and exercised judgment around it, which courts read as *not reasonably implemented*. A written policy you do not execute is worse than none — it is evidence of knowledge. So one engine must host one discretionary ladder and one rigid, automatic, auditable counter. Building a single "proportionate" ladder is how the safe harbour is lost. |
| DT-03 | A payout hold is a cheap, obviously-available sanction | It looks like a flag on a row | ❌ REJECTED — holding money you owe is a legal act with consumer-protection and money-transmission consequences, and `meta/constraints.md` already flags the money-transmission posture as an unresolved **legal constraint** determining whether several product concepts are viable at all. A hold is also the sanction most likely to be *right* (it stops fraud losses) and most likely to be *challenged*. It cannot be specified until the posture is decided. |
