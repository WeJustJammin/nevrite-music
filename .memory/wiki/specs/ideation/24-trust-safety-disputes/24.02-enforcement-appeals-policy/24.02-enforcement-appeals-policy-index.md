## Overview

The consequence half of the adjudication machine: the rulebook, the sanctions that cite it, the notice
that explains the sanction, the appeal that can reverse it, and the aggregate report that discloses all
of it. Where [24.01](../24.01-reporting-moderation/24.01-reporting-moderation-index.md) decides what gets
looked at, this sub-domain decides what happens — and, critically for a platform that holds ownership
records, **what a sanction is forbidden to touch**.

**Why a sub-domain, not a feature**: six capabilities that are mutually constitutive. A sanction is only
defensible if it cites a rule that was in force when the act occurred (24.02.04), it is only lawful if it
ships with a Statement of Reasons (24.02.03), it is only fair if it can be reversed (24.02.02), and the
reversal is only real if the sanction was designed to be undone. The prohibited-items engine (24.02.05)
is the machine-readable half of the same rulebook, evaluated at listing time rather than after a report.

Merging them buries that. | `/ideate-discover` Step 3 classification |
| D-02 | **Prohibited & Restricted Items placed here**, not in the marketplace domains | It is the machine-readable projection of the policy library, and its enforcement path (block → appeal → reasoned decision) is this sub-domain's. | D-03 | **Sanctions act on access and privileges — never on the ownership record** | The platform holds co-owned rights records (D-10, D-18). Banning a bassist cannot void their 20% share or destroy the band's chain of title. This is the hardest constraint in the domain and it is unique to a provenance platform. See 24.02.01 DT-01. | `/ideate-discover` Deep Think |
