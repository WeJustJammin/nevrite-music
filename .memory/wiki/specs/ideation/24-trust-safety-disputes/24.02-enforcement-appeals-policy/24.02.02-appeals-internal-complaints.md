# Feature: Appeals & Internal Complaint Handling

> **Number**: 24.02.02
> **Parent**: [Enforcement, Appeals & Policy](./24.02-enforcement-appeals-policy-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-18

## Overview

The reverse edge of enforcement: a sanctioned party contests a decision, a human who was not the
original decider reviews it, and the sanction is upheld or reversed. DSA Art 20 describes this as an
internal complaint-handling system, free of charge, open for six months, decided under human supervision
rather than solely by automated means. The feature's real difficulty is not the review — it is what
"reversed" has to mean, because **undoing a sanction is much harder than applying one**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Appeals a takedown, a listing suspension, a credit restriction, a ban. Contests the *facts and grounds stated in the SoR* — which is why a vague SoR makes their appeal unanswerable. For this persona the appeal is often about income already lost: the gig is gone, the buyer bought elsewhere, the release shipped with the wrong metadata. |
| Producer | Full | Appeals on behalf of a project that has contributors — and hits the first real question: can they appeal a takedown of a *shared* mix, when the sanction hit an artifact six people co-own? Their appeals are the most time-sensitive: a release date does not move for a review queue. |
| Operator | Full | Appeals a listing suspension or a booking restriction. **Reversal is least meaningful for this persona** — perishable inventory cannot be restored. A room suspended over a Saturday is a Saturday that is gone, and an upheld appeal on Monday returns a calendar, not the money. |
| Fan | Full | Appeals an account suspension or a removed post. The highest-volume appellant (D-13), the lowest stakes, and the persona for whom reversal genuinely does restore everything. |

## Behavior

Any sanction with a Statement of Reasons carries a redress route inside it (Art 17 requires it), so the
SoR is the entry point rather than a separate hub. The reviewer must not be the original decider, and the
review must not be solely automated — a model that reverses model decisions is not supervision.

The **restoration cascade** is the substance of the feature. Reversal must: reinstate the object,
re-open the listing, release the hold, restore the credit, decrement the repeat-infringer counter,
requeue the payout, and un-notify the counterparties who were told the thing was gone. Each step has its
own window, and some windows close. Where restoration is impossible, the feature must say so plainly
rather than reporting success.

**New appeal evidence is post-decision, not a retroactive bundle write.** Filing creates one immutable
`appeal_submission` supplement containing the appellant's reason and any attached material. The system
seals it with the submitting party, system timestamp, immutable sequence, and content hashes; it is
read-only after filing and remains visibly later than the original decision evidence. It is case-scoped:
the appellant receives a read-only receipt, the appeal reviewer receives the supplement with the original
bundle, and any other party receives only the ordinary case-scoped projection. The supplement inherits the
case's retention and legal-hold clocks, but never appends to or reorders the original 24.09 bundle.

**Scale gate**: Art 20 sits in DSA Section 3, which Art 19 disapplies for micro and small enterprises.
The *legal* driver for this feature is therefore absent at launch. The *architectural* driver is not —
see D-01.

**The carve-out inside the scale gate.** Below the concurrence threshold — the launch state of exactly
one human moderator — the S1 gate (24.01.03 D-09) and the rung-≥-6 gate (24.02.01 D-07 as amended) are
scale-gated too, which means the single reviewer's call is final at the point of application. For those
sanctions the appeal is no longer the second check; **it is the primary one**, and it is therefore
**guaranteed**: offered on every S1 and every rung ≥ 6 sanction regardless of whether the appeal
*surface* is otherwise deferred under Art 19 (D-04). This inverts the load the Art 19 reading carries.
Deferring the surface was a scoping convenience while a second moderator was still notionally checking
the decision; with that check gated away, the reading is what stands between a single unreviewed
suspension and any redress at all — so it is a counsel question now, not a scoping one (Q-02).

### Happy Path

1. Subject receives a Statement of Reasons containing the redress route
2. Subject files an appeal with a reason and any new evidence; the system seals one `appeal_submission` supplement rather than modifying the original bundle
3. Case is assigned to a reviewer who was not the original decider
4. Reviewer reviews the original evidence bundle ([24.09](../24.09-case-evidence-locker.md)) plus the immutable, visibly post-decision appeal supplement
5. Reviewer upholds or reverses, with reasons
6. On reversal: sanction lifts, restoration cascade runs, counterparties are re-notified
7. Outcome is recorded and feeds the policy-consistency signal (24.01.03 QA)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Appeal upheld but the listing's buyer already bought elsewhere | Listing is reinstated; the sale is not. Restoration is nominal | "Your listing is live again" — and nothing about the lost sale, which is the honest failure |
| Appeal upheld after the release date passed | The mix is restored; the release window is not | Explicit: "restored — note that {date} has passed" |
| Appeal upheld after escrow auto-released to the other party | Money has moved. If funds are still traceable on-platform, a **clawback** is attempted; if already withdrawn to an external account, clawback may be impossible and the item drops into *Partially restored* pending the Q-01 values decision (compensate / apologise / nothing) | "Reversed. Funds already released — {clawback status}." The definitive clawback mechanics defer to `/write-be-spec` and the **Payments, Escrow & Payouts** cross-cut, which may have no answer |
| Repeat-infringer strike reversed | Counter must decrement, or the rigid 512(i) ladder ratchets on a decision that was wrong | Subject: strike count corrected |
| Entity sanction appealed (band/label/studio) — which member may appeal? | Any member holding an appeal-capable mandate in the entity's mandate graph ([domain 01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations)) may file; the appeal binds the entity, not the individual. Conflicting filings by co-owners resolve to a single case, first-filed | "Appealing on behalf of {entity}." Exact mandate-to-appeal mapping and co-owner conflict rules defer to `/write-be-spec` against domain 01 |
| Appellant appeals repeatedly on the same facts | Rate-limited after one full review on unchanged facts; a second appeal needs an `appeal_submission` item whose content hash is not already in the reviewed supplement | "This decision has been reviewed. Submit genuinely new evidence?" |
| Original decider is the only reviewer (one-person team) | **Narrowed 2026-07-30, not closed.** Two halves, and only one is now settled. *Availability* is settled: below the concurrence threshold the appeal is **guaranteed** on every S1 and every rung ≥ 6 sanction (D-04), so the redress route always exists and is never scale-gated away — which matters more than before, because 24.01.03 D-09 and 24.02.01 D-07 make this the primary check on those decisions rather than the second one. *Independence* is **not** settled and remains structurally unavailable: **D-03 is untouched** — the reviewer must not be the original decider and must not be a model — so on a team of one there is no compliant reviewer at all, and the only shapes available are a named external reviewer or a disclosed-conflict self-review, neither of which an agent may choose | "You can appeal this decision" — always, and on the guaranteed path within a stated window. Who reviews it, and on what disclosure, is still open: 24.01.03 Q-02 (unchanged) and Q-04 below |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available | A sanction exists and is within the appeal window | Redress route inside the SoR |
| Filed | Appeal submitted | "Under review — response by {date}" |
| Under review | Assigned to an independent reviewer | Status only |
| Upheld | Reviewer agrees with the original | Reasons; the sanction stands; onward options (Art 21 out-of-court settlement, if applicable) |
| Reversed | Reviewer disagrees | Sanction lifted; restoration cascade with a **per-item** result, not a blanket "restored" |
| Partially restored | Cascade could not complete | Explicit list of what was restored and what could not be |
| Expired | Window elapsed (Art 20: six months) | No appeal route |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Reversal is un-deletion — flip the flag back | The way appeals get built when they get built late | ❌ REJECTED — reversal must restore **derived state**, and derived state on this platform is unusually deep: an escrow that auto-released, a payout that ran, a listing whose buyer left, a release that shipped with the wrong metadata, a repeat-infringer counter that ratcheted, a reputation score that already moved (Reviews cross-cut). Some cannot be restored at all. **The consequence is that reversibility is a property every sanction must be designed with (24.02.01), not a capability this feature can add later.** If the appeal is built after the ladder, the appeal is theatre. |
| DT-02 | Art 20 makes appeals a launch obligation | Reading Art 20 without Art 19 | ❌ REJECTED — Art 19 disapplies Section 3 (Arts 20–28) for micro and small enterprises, and Art 20 sits in Section 3. The legal driver is absent at launch. **But this changes only the MoSCoW of the appeal *surface*, not of sanction reversibility**, which is architectural and belongs inside 24.02.01's scope regardless. Flagged for counsel: this reasoning drives a scope decision. |
| DT-03 | An appeal is a customer-service function | The cheap framing | ❌ REJECTED — on this platform an appeal contests a decision about **property and livelihood**, not a refund. The appellant may be contesting the removal of a master they co-own, a payout that is their rent, or a credit that is the proof their next job depends on (D-18: "the accumulated proof is what wins the next job"). Staffing it as support produces decisions that cannot survive an appeal to a regulator or a court — and it is the one surface where the platform is adjudicating against its own users, so getting it wrong is a thesis failure, not a CSAT failure. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The appeal *surface* is scale-gated; sanction *reversibility* is not | DT-01 + DT-02. Reversibility is a data-model property that must ship with the ladder. Splitting these two lets the surface be deferred honestly without deferring the thing that cannot be retrofitted. |
| D-02 | Reversal reports per-item results, never a blanket "restored" | DT-01. Some restorations are impossible; claiming otherwise is a lie the user can see through immediately. |
| D-03 | The reviewer must not be the original decider, and must not be a model | Art 20 requires human supervision; a model reviewing a model is not supervision. |
| D-04 | Below the concurrence threshold, the appeal is **guaranteed** on every S1 and every rung ≥ 6 sanction, regardless of the otherwise scale-gated appeal *surface* | 24.01.03 D-09 and 24.02.01 D-07 (as amended) gate the pre-decision check away on exactly those sanctions, which makes this appeal the only post-decision check that is not the decider re-reading their own rationale. D-01's split holds and is extended by one clause: the surface is scale-gated for *ordinary* sanctions, never for the ones a single reviewer applied alone. Note what this does and does not fix — it guarantees *availability*, not *independence*, which D-03 still forbids on a team of one and which stays open at 24.01.03 Q-02. Ratified 2026-07-30 with 24.01.03 D-09. |
| D-05 | New appeal evidence is captured as one immutable, case-scoped `appeal_submission` supplement; it is visibly post-decision and never writes to the original 24.09 bundle | The appeal must accept new material without laundering it into contemporaneous evidence. System capture, hash, timestamp, sequence, scoped read access, and inherited retention preserve its chain of custody. |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | **[OWNER]** What is the answer when restoration is **impossible** (escrow released, date passed, sale lost)? Compensation, apology, or nothing? This is a values decision with a cost attached. D-02 fixes only the *disclosure* ("per-item results, never a blanket restored") and deliberately not the *remedy*; no later stage can choose the platform's liability posture for a harm it caused and cannot undo. *Re-pointed from the completed `/ideate-validate`.* | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-02 | Confirm the Art 19 carve-out with counsel before treating the appeal surface as Should rather than Must. **Escalated 2026-07-30 — this is no longer a scoping convenience.** DT-02 and D-01 defer the appeal *surface* on the Art 19 micro/small reading; 24.01.03 D-09 and 24.02.01 D-07 (as amended) now gate the pre-decision concurrence check away on S1 and rung ≥ 6, so the same reading is what sits between a single unreviewed suspension and any redress at all. D-04 hard-carves the guaranteed appeal out of the deferral, which contains the exposure but does not test the reading. Counsel must confirm (a) that Art 19 genuinely disapplies Art 20 here, and (b) whether a one-reviewer decision process changes that answer — an agent's reading of the DSA is not a compliance decision. | User + counsel | `/create-prd-security` |
| Q-03 | Is Art 21 out-of-court dispute settlement in scope, and at what threshold does it start applying? | User + counsel | `/create-prd-security` |
| Q-04 | **[OWNER]** Who reviews a guaranteed appeal against a sanction the same person decided alone? D-04 guarantees the appeal *exists*; D-03 forbids the original decider and forbids a model, and below the concurrence threshold there is nobody else — so on the primary check for S1 and rung ≥ 6 the compliant reviewer set is **empty**. The available shapes are a named external reviewer (cost, an outside dependency, and a PII path into the 24.09 evidence locker for a non-employee) or a disclosed-conflict self-review with the conflict stated to the appellant in the SoR. This is the *independence* half of the one-person-team edge case above and the appeals-side twin of 24.01.03 Q-02; answer them together. **D-03 is not up for amendment** — the question is which compliant shape is bought, not whether the rule bends. | User | `/create-prd` |
