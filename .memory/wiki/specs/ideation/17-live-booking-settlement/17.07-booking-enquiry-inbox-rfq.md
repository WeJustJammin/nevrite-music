# Feature: Booking Enquiry Inbox & RFQ

> **Number**: 17.07
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The top of the booking funnel — structured inbound enquiries ("can you play our room on the 14th?",
"what would it cost to book this act?"), triaged, qualified, and converted into a hold or an offer.
It replaces the email inbox that `meta/personas.md` records as the Operator's current front door, and
its real job is **disqualification**: most enquiries are not deals, and the value is in the ones you
don't have to read.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Receives enquiries for their own act ("can you play our wedding / our bar / our festival") and sends them to rooms they want to play. The self-booking working musician in `meta/personas.md` has no agent — this inbox *is* their booking business. |
| Producer | None | No role in live booking enquiries. Studio enquiries are domain 16's. |
| Operator | Full | Receives enquiries for their room, triages against auto-qualification rules (date open? fee in range? genre fit? capacity plausible?), auto-declines what fails, and converts what survives into a hold (17.01.02) or an offer (17.02.01). |
| Fan | None | **Deliberately none, and this is a design position.** An open enquiry surface addressed to artists is a harassment vector — `meta/personas.md` names "harasses artists through open contact surfaces" as the Fan's worst intentional behaviour. Fan-to-artist contact belongs in domain 20 under its own moderation model, not in a booking inbox. See DT-03. |

## Behavior

An enquiry is a **structured pre-offer artefact**, not a message. It carries the fields that decide
whether it is worth a reply — date or window, act, indicative fee or budget, expected capacity, event
type — and those fields are what make triage possible. A free-text "hey are you free in March" is
exactly the thing this feature exists to stop being the input format.

The enquiry's canonical fields (the shape this feature owns, per DT-01):

- **Target window** — a specific date or a date range the sender wants to play/book.
- **Act** — the artist/band being offered or requested (carries its identity from domain 01).
- **Indicative fee / budget** — a number or a range; the axis auto-qualification scores first.
- **Expected capacity / draw** — plausibility-checked against the room's stated capacity.
- **Event type** — headline / support / private hire / festival slot / etc. — sets the genre-fit and format expectation.
- **Context note** — a bounded free-text field for the one thing structure cannot capture (see DT-04). Not the payload; a footnote to it.

The flow is: **capture → qualify → convert or decline**. Conversion is the point; an enquiry that
converts becomes a hold or an offer and the enquiry's job is over.

Auto-qualification rules do the work. An Operator taking 40 enquiries a week cannot read 40
enquiries a week, and the ones they should read are buried.

The **messaging transport** underneath is a platform cross-cut, not this feature's property. What is
domain-owned is the enquiry's *shape* and its *conversion path*.

### Happy Path

1. An agent (or a self-booking Musician) finds a room and sends a structured enquiry against a window.
2. System checks it against the Operator's auto-qualification rules — date open, fee within the
   room's stated range, capacity plausible.
3. Enquiry that passes lands in the Operator's inbox, ranked, with the act's draw history (17.11.01)
   attached so the decision has a basis.
4. Operator converts it — places a hold (17.01.02) or composes an offer (17.02.01).
5. The enquiry closes, having done its only job.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Enquiry fails auto-qualification | Auto-declined, and this is the feature working | Sender gets a real reason ("this room's dates open 6 months out"), not silence |
| Sender submits with a required field missing (no date, no fee/budget) | Rejected at submission — an unstructured enquiry defeats the feature's whole purpose (D-02) | Inline validation on the missing field; the enquiry never sends |
| Room fills the target date between submission and triage (race) | The enquiry is now moot; auto-qualification re-checks avails at the moment of triage, not only at submit | Sender sees "this date was booked after you enquired"; it leaves the Operator's ranked inbox |
| Operator has set no auto-qualification rules yet (empty config) | Nothing to disqualify against — every enquiry falls through to the inbox unranked rather than being dropped | Operator sees an unfiltered inbox plus a prompt to set rules; no enquiry is silently lost |
| Sender withdraws an enquiry before the Operator acts | Enquiry closes as withdrawn; it disappears from the Operator's inbox and cannot be converted | Operator sees "withdrawn by sender" if the row was already open; otherwise it silently leaves the queue |
| Context note carries abuse / spam / off-platform contact ("DM me, we'll skip the fees") | The free-text field is a moderation surface and an off-platform-leakage vector (Musician anti-persona) | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` — routes to domain 24 moderation; the structured fields still triage normally |
| Operator ignores an enquiry | The industry's cheapest way to say no without saying no (17.01.02 Q-03) | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / User Q-01]` — whether enquiries expire or auto-decline is a product decision, undecided |
| Enquiry arrives for a date that is open but unpublished (invite-only avails) | The Operator's visibility scoping said no; the enquiry says otherwise | `[PENDING — User Q-02]` — interacts with 17.01.01 Q-02 |
| Bulk enquiries from one sender across every room in a city | Legitimate (shopping a date) at small scale, spam at large | `[PENDING — User Q-03]` — same shape as the hold-count question (17.01.02 Q-01) |
| Enquiry converts to an offer that is then countered | The enquiry is done; the thread lives in 17.02.02 | Enquiry closed with a pointer to the deal |
| Enquiry converts to a hold that later expires unconverted | The enquiry stays closed; expiry is 17.01.02's concern, not this feature's — it does not re-open | Sender sees the hold's expiry state in 17.01.02, not a re-opened enquiry |
| Fan sends a booking enquiry to an artist | Not supported (DT-03) | Fan contact routes to domain 20's surface and moderation model |

### States

Two layers of state: the **inbox UI** (Loading/Empty/Populated/Error) and the **enquiry lifecycle**
(the states a single enquiry moves through).

Inbox UI states:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Inbox opened, enquiries fetching | Skeleton rows in the ranked list |
| Empty | No enquiries (or all disqualified away) | Empty inbox with the room's qualification rules summarised + a CTA to tune rules |
| Populated | Qualified enquiries present | Ranked list, each row with act + draw history + fee-vs-range indicator |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error banner with retry; the ranked list is not partially/misleadingly rendered |

Enquiry lifecycle states:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Received | Enquiry submitted | In the sender's sent list; pending for the recipient |
| Auto-declined | Failed qualification | Sender sees the reason; recipient never sees it (or sees it in a filtered view) |
| Qualified | Passed rules | In the Operator's inbox, ranked, with draw history attached |
| Withdrawn | Sender retracts before conversion | Leaves the queue; marked withdrawn if already opened |
| Converted | Became a hold or an offer | Closed with a link to the hold/deal |
| Declined | Operator said no | Closed with a reason |
| Stale | No action after a period | `[PENDING — User Q-01]` — expiry-vs-auto-decline is an undecided product choice |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a cross-cut — a structured RFQ inbox serves 05 (services), 06 (lessons), 16 (room hire) and 17 identically | Node Classification Gate, cross-cut test | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — split the candidate.** The **transport** (an inbound structured request that routes to an owner, gets triaged, and converts to a quote) is genuinely generic and is recorded as a cross-cut. The **enquiry's shape** is not: a booking enquiry's qualifying fields are date/window, fee, capacity, genre fit and routing — none of which mean anything to a lesson request or a mixing brief. Keeping the shape here and the transport as a cross-cut is the honest split; folding the whole thing into a cross-cut would produce a generic "request" entity with a bag of nullable fields, which is precisely the anti-pattern D-14 rejected for marketplaces. |
| DT-02 | The inbox's value is organising enquiries | The candidate's name — "Inbox" | ❌ REJECTED: the value is **disqualification**. An Optimist builds a nice inbox; an Operator taking 40 enquiries a week needs 34 of them to never arrive. The current workaround is email precisely because email at least has a delete key. A prettier inbox is not an improvement over email — *auto-declining the 34 with a real reason* is. This reframes the feature from a list to a rules engine, which changes what gets built. |
| DT-03 | Fans should be able to send booking enquiries to artists ("play my town", "play my bar") | Fan-as-first-class-user reasoning (D-11); a fan running a small venue is a real person | ❌ REJECTED **as a booking-inbox capability**, with a caveat. `meta/personas.md` names open artist contact surfaces as the Fan's worst intentional abuse vector, and the Fan persona is explicitly the one who "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity". An open enquiry pipe from a consumer-scale population (D-13: fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude) into a professional inbox would destroy the inbox — which is DT-02's point exactly. **The legitimate demand behind this is real, but it is 17.13's aggregated demand signal, not a per-fan message.** A fan who genuinely runs a venue is an Operator and gets the Operator's surface. |
| DT-04 | The enquiry should be fully structured — no free-text field at all, since free text is what DT-02 said to kill | Purity of the rules-engine framing (D-02) | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — **structure decides, but a bounded note survives.** Killing free text entirely is over-correction: a real enquiry carries one thing the fields can't ("we want you to close, not open"; "this is a memorial show"). The honest design is **structured fields are the payload and the only thing triage reads; the note is a bounded footnote that is never scored and is a moderation surface** (see the abuse edge case). What DT-02 actually rejected was free text *as the input format* — a single unstructured message. A footnote to a structured artefact is not that. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The enquiry's shape stays in this domain; the transport is a cross-cut | A generic request entity with nullable fields is the anti-pattern D-14 rejected for marketplaces (DT-01). |
| D-02 | The feature is a rules engine, not an inbox | The value is auto-declining what should never arrive, not organising what does (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Auto-decline gives a real reason, never silence | Silence is what email already does. A reason is the only thing that makes the sender's next enquiry better. |
| D-04 | Fans cannot send booking enquiries | Open artist contact at consumer scale destroys the inbox and is a named abuse vector (DT-03). The real demand is 17.13's. |
| D-05 | The enquiry carries structured fields plus one bounded context note; only the fields are triaged, the note is never scored and is a moderation surface | Killing free text entirely over-corrects DT-02; a bounded footnote to a structured artefact is not the unstructured input format DT-02 rejected (DT-04). |
