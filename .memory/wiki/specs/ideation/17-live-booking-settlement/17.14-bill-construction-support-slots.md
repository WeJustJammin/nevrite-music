# Feature: Bill Construction & Support Slot Offers

> **Number**: 17.14
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Who else is on the show. A bill is headliner plus direct support plus opener, each booked on their own
deal, sometimes by different people — and sometimes the support **pays to be there**. The economics
invert: support acts take flat fees with no versus, often no merch cut, and buy-ons run money the
opposite direction from every other deal in this domain.

**Deep Think addition** — none of the sweep's 18 candidates mention support acts, bills, or buy-ons.
The domain modelled a show as one artist and one promoter.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Both sides of this.** A headliner has a view on who opens for them — sometimes contractual approval. A support act receives a flat offer with terms they mostly cannot negotiate, and for a working musician the opening slot is how a career starts. The same person occupies both roles across different shows, which is the multi-hyphenate reality. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Constructs the bill and books the supports — or doesn't, when the headliner's agent brings their own. Who has the right to fill the slot is a negotiated term and a routine argument. |
| Fan | None | Never visible here. The Fan sees the bill only once it is announced (17.01.04), which is exactly why announcing before supports are locked is a real problem — the lineup they bought changes after they bought it. |

## Behavior

A bill is an **ordered set of bookings against one show**, each with its own deal:

- **Headliner** — the main deal (17.02, 17.03), full versus/door economics.
- **Direct support** — usually a flat fee, no versus, often a reduced or zero merch cut.
- **Opener** — flat, small, sometimes local, sometimes nothing.
- **Buy-on** — the support **pays** for the slot. Money runs backwards through the entire domain.

Who controls the slot is negotiated: the headliner's agent may have approval or may own it outright;
the promoter may insist on a local opener to sell tickets. This is a routine argument and it is a
deal term.

Support economics come **off the top** of the show (17.10.01) before the headliner's split.

**Bill construction is booking; running order and set times are domain 18.**

### Happy Path

1. Headline deal confirms (17.01.04).
2. The party who controls the slot — per the deal — proposes a support act.
3. Support receives a flat offer with its own terms; approval runs their own entity's chain (17.02.03).
4. Support confirms; the bill locks; the announce precondition is satisfied (17.01.04).
5. Support's fee comes off the top at settlement (17.09.01); their own draw record is recorded **as
   support**, not as a headliner (17.11.01 D-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show announced before supports are locked | Fans bought a lineup that then changes — a refund-rights and trust problem, not an embarrassment | Supports-locked should be an announce precondition (17.01.04 Q-01) |
| Buy-on: support pays for the slot | **Money runs backwards** — the artist is the payer, the promoter the payee | Every payment-direction assumption in the domain inverts (DT-02) |
| Headline show cancels | Every support's booking dies with it — a cascade the cancellation ladder does not model | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` (17.05.03 Q-03) |
| Support drops out days before | The bill must be refilled fast, after announce | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Headliner and promoter both claim the slot | Routine argument; it is a deal term | Slot control must be an explicit term, not an assumption |
| Support draws more than expected | Their flat fee is unchanged; they made the promoter money | Correct, normal, and why the draw record's slot qualifier matters (17.11.01 DT-01) |
| Local opener added at the last minute for £50 | The long tail; must not require the full deal machinery | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — see DT-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Headliner only | Main deal confirmed | Bill with open slots and who controls each |
| Proposed | A support proposed | Pending that act's approval |
| Partial | Some slots filled | Outstanding slots; announce blocked if it is a precondition |
| Locked | All slots filled and confirmed | Announce precondition satisfied |
| Broken | A support dropped after announce | Bill changed post-announce; fan-facing consequence in 19/21 |
| Cascaded | Headline show cancelled | All supports' bookings die |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A show is one artist and one promoter | The model implied by **all 18 sweep candidates** — not one mentions supports, bills or buy-ons | ❌ REJECTED: **most shows have two or three acts on separate deals.** The entire domain — offers, contracts, settlement, splits — was modelled around a single artist. Support fees come off the top before the headliner's split (17.10.01), which means the sweep's settlement model was missing a deduction that exists on the majority of shows. This is not a gap at the edge; it is a wrong assumption at the centre, and it would have surfaced in `/write-be-spec` as "why does settlement have one artist?". |
| DT-02 | Money always flows promoter → artist | The assumption embedded in every payment feature in this domain (17.05, 17.09, 17.10) | ❌ REJECTED: **buy-ons run it backwards.** A support act paying £500 to open a tour is a real, common, and much-criticised practice — and it inverts the deposit direction (17.05.01 D-02 already had to allow for this on venue-hire), the settlement direction, and the payout direction. A model hard-coding promoter→artist cannot represent it. Note this is the *second* place the direction inverts, which suggests direction is a property of a deal rather than an exception to it — a structural insight the sweep's flat candidate list could not have surfaced. |
| DT-03 | Support booking is the headline flow with smaller numbers | Reuse instinct — it is a deal, so use the deal machinery | ❌ REJECTED: **the ceremony does not survive the scale.** A £50 local opener added on Tuesday for Friday will not go through offer versioning, an entity approval chain, a contract, a deposit schedule and a settlement signoff. The people involved will text each other and the platform will never know — which loses the platform the bill data it needs for the announce gate, the settlement deduction, and the support act's own draw record. **The support flow needs a genuinely lighter path**, and designing it is a real product question rather than a configuration of the headline one. Getting this wrong quietly excludes the exact population `meta/personas.md` describes: working musicians whose careers are made of £50 opening slots. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A show is a bill of several bookings, not one artist's deal | The majority of shows have 2-3 acts on separate deals; the sweep modelled one (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Payment direction is a property of a deal, not a global assumption | Buy-ons and venue-hire both invert it; two inversions is a pattern, not an exception (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Slot control is an explicit deal term | Who fills the support slot is a routine argument between headliner and promoter; leaving it implicit picks a side silently. |
| D-04 | The support flow needs a lighter path than the headline flow | A £50 opener will not survive versioning, approval chains, contracts and signoff — and excluding them excludes the platform's core persona (DT-03). |
| D-05 | Bill construction is booking; running order is domain 18 | Who is on the show vs when they play — a clean boundary worth keeping. |
