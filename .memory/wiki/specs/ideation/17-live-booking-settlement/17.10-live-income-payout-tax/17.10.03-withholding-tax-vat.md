# Feature: Withholding Tax & VAT on Live Income

> **Number**: 17.10.03
> **Parent**: [Live Income Payout & Tax](./17.10-live-income-payout-tax-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Why a £10,000 international date pays £7,000. Most territories require the payer to withhold tax from
a foreign performer's fee at source and remit it to the state — US FDAP withholding, the UK's Foreign
Entertainers Unit, and equivalents almost everywhere. The artist is taxed in a country they visited
for one night, at a rate applied to gross, and reclaiming the excess is a separate fight that needs
evidence this feature is the only source of.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what was withheld and why, and **needs the evidence to reclaim it** — the reclaim is frequently worth more than the show's profit. They cannot alter, waive, or negotiate it. The most common real experience: discovering at settlement that the fee they agreed is not the money they get. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | **The payer is the withholding agent** — legally obliged to deduct and remit, and liable to the state if they don't. This is the one feature in the sub-domain where the Operator is the actor and the Musician is a spectator to their own money. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Withholding applies **before the money moves** (CX-03) — the withheld amount never enters the payment
rail; the payer remits it to the state directly.

Two things make this genuinely hard, and neither is arithmetic:

1. **Sequencing** (CX-02). Some regimes withhold at entity level on the gross paid to the act; others
   require withholding per individual payee. A sideman on a flat £200 is untouched by the first and
   directly reduced by the second. Same show, same law, different money.
2. **It is applied to gross, not profit.** An act that loses money on a date still has tax withheld
   on the fee. The reclaim mechanism exists precisely because the withheld amount routinely exceeds
   the actual liability — and reclaiming needs evidence, deadlines, and often a local agent.

Reduced-rate mechanisms exist (US central withholding agreements, UK FEU applications, treaty rates)
and they all require applying **before** the show, not at settlement.

**The platform must never advise on tax.** It applies a rate, records what it did, and produces the
evidence.

### Happy Path

1. A booking is for a territory where the artist is foreign; the regime is identified from the deal.
2. The regime's rate and sequencing are applied to the settlement (17.09.01) — before or after the
   split per CX-02.
3. The Operator withholds and remits to the state; the disbursement (17.10.02) carries the remainder.
4. The artist sees exactly what was withheld, under which regime, at which rate.
5. The withholding evidence flows into the statement (17.09.07) and the artist's finances (23), where
   the reclaim happens.

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Withholding is a settlement-time calculation | The candidate's placement — it appears next to settlement in the sweep's list | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's most useful insight. **The decisions that matter happen months before the show.** Reduced-rate mechanisms — US central withholding agreements, UK FEU applications, treaty claims — must be applied for *in advance*; at settlement the rate is simply a fact and the money is gone. The platform knows the date, the territory and the fee the moment the offer is agreed (17.02), which means it is uniquely positioned to prompt at the only moment the prompt is worth anything. Modelling this as settlement arithmetic would build the feature at the point where it can no longer help. |
| DT-02 | The platform should compute the correct withholding | The obvious ambition, and what the sweep's candidate implies | ❌ REJECTED: **computing a rate is giving tax advice**, and getting it wrong exposes the artist to liability and the platform to a claim. Rates depend on treaty status, residence, entity structure, and reduced-rate applications the platform does not control. The defensible position is narrow: **flag that withholding applies, prompt the reduced-rate deadline, record what was actually withheld, and produce the evidence.** That is genuinely valuable and does not require the platform to be right about tax. The gap between "we told you it applies" and "we told you it's 30%" is the whole liability question (Q-02). |
| DT-03 | Withholding and VAT are one feature ("Withholding Tax & VAT on Live Income") | The sweep's candidate name, which bundles them | ❌ REJECTED — **they are different taxes with opposite mechanics.** Withholding is deducted *from* the artist's fee by the payer and remitted on the artist's behalf. VAT is charged *on top of* the fee by the artist, collected by them, and remitted by them — and whether it applies at all depends on the artist's own registration status, which has nothing to do with the show. Bundling them models one thing that does not exist. VAT on live income is arguably domain 23's (the artist's own tax position) rather than a settlement operation at all. **The sweep's candidate is a false merge**, and unpicking it is what this rejection is for. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform flags, prompts, records and evidences — it never advises | Computing a rate is tax advice; being wrong exposes the artist and the platform (DT-02). |
| D-02 | The reduced-rate prompt fires at offer time, not settlement | The mechanisms have pre-show deadlines; at settlement the money is gone (DT-01). |
| D-03 | **Withholding and VAT are separated**; VAT is escalated as probably domain 23's | Opposite mechanics — one is deducted from the fee by the payer, the other is charged on top by the artist (DT-03). The sweep's candidate was a false merge. |
| D-04 | Expected withholding is surfaced at offer time | A £10,000 fee that pays £7,000 is a materially different offer and the artist should read it that way. |
