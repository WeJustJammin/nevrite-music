# Feature: Disbursement Execution & Payment Status

> **Number**: 17.10.02
> **Parent**: [Live Income Payout & Tax](./17.10-live-income-payout-tax-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Moving the money. Once the settlement is signed and the split evaluated, each party's share has to
actually arrive — and everyone has to be able to see where theirs is. This is where the platform's
promise stops being arithmetic and becomes a bank transfer, and where the whole domain's credibility
is either earned or lost.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Recipients track their own money — the share, what was withheld, when it left, when it lands. `meta/personas.md` names "an unpaid session" as the Musician's switching trigger; the live equivalent is a settled show whose money never arrived and nobody can say why. Visibility is the feature. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees that **their obligation was discharged** — not who got what. Once they have paid the act, how it divides is not theirs to see (17.10.01). |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Disbursement executes the split (CX-01) against whatever survives withholding (CX-03), on the
platform's payments cross-cut. The rail is not owned here; **who is owed what** is.

The defining question is index Q-01: does the platform pay **individuals** or the **band entity**?
Paying individuals is what musicians actually want and makes the platform a mass payout facilitator —
KYC per recipient, tax reporting per recipient, and a compliance surface that `meta/constraints.md`
flags as entirely `[PENDING]`. Paying the entity is trivial and delivers almost none of the value:
the drummer is back to chasing the band's treasurer, which is the fragmentation the product exists to
end.

**Partial disbursement is normal, not an error.** One member without a payout method must not stop
the other four being paid.

**A refusal is not a stop — it is a smaller number.** Full bilateral signoff releases the full
computed payable, but it is not the only release event. A refused settlement releases its
**undisputed floor** — the sheet re-evaluated with every disputed line conceded to the counterparty —
and holds only the contested delta
([17.09.05](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md) D-09;
[17.09.06](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.06-settlement-audit-trail-disputes.md) D-08).
Refusal blocks **finality**, not **payment**: the settlement stays open, the floor moves on the
normal per-recipient path below, and the delta disburses later on the amendment's signoff (D-14
there). Two consequences land here and nowhere else: the floor disburses **without a second
signature**, so this feature cannot treat "signed" as its only trigger; and because the floor is by
construction the amount that cannot shrink when the dispute resolves, paying it carries no clawback
exposure — the clawback risk sits entirely in the held delta, which is why the delta is held.

### Happy Path

1. Settlement signs off (17.09.05); the number is final.
2. Split evaluates (17.10.01); withholding applies (17.10.03).
3. Each share disburses on the payments rail.
4. Every recipient sees their own share move — sent, in flight, landed — with the withholding and
   commission deductions itemised.
5. The agency's commission lands alongside (17.08.02); the show is closed.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One recipient has no payout method | Partial disbursement — the others are paid; the blocked share is held, not lost | The recipient sees "Held — add a payout method to release your share"; the others see theirs move normally; it is not surfaced as an error on the show |
| Settlement is **refused** rather than signed | The undisputed floor disburses immediately; the contested delta is held ([17.09.05](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md) D-09). Symmetric — an Operator refusal releases the floor of what they do not dispute. The settlement is not final, so nothing here closes the show | Each recipient sees their share of the floor move normally, and a separate held line: "Held — {n} lines disputed, {amount} pending resolution". The floor is never labelled as the payout; the recipient must be able to tell a part-payment from a settled one |
| Disputed lines resolve after the floor was paid | The amendment is a new signoff ([17.09.05](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md) D-14), and the delta disburses on it. This is **not** the clawback case — the floor could only ever be topped up, never reduced | "Dispute resolved — {amount} released" against the same show; the floor payment and the delta payment both appear in the recipient's history rather than being restated as one |
| Settlement is signed twice / disbursement re-triggered | Idempotent **per release event** — each release (the undisputed floor on a refusal, the full payable on signoff, a delta on an amendment) disburses exactly **once**; a repeat trigger against a release already executed is a no-op, never a double payment. Idempotency cannot key on the settlement alone, because a refused-then-resolved settlement legitimately releases twice ([17.09.05](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md) D-09, D-14) | "Already disbursed on {date}" — no second transfer initiated |
| A share is sent but the rail bounces it back (closed/invalid account) | That share reverts to Held; the rest of the settlement is unaffected | "Payment returned — check your payout details and we'll retry"; per-recipient status flips In flight → Held with the rail's reason |
| Operator tries to see the per-recipient split | Denied — the Operator sees only that their obligation was discharged (D-03, 17.10.01) | "Paid in full on {date}" — a single discharge line, never the breakdown |
| Money paid in cash at the venue | The most common live payment; the platform sees an assertion, not a transfer | Recordable as an assertion — the show reads "Settled in cash (recorded, not disbursed)"; the platform moves nothing |
| Band entity has no bank account | Extremely common; the "band" is four people and a WhatsApp group — bears directly on index Q-01 | Members are prompted to add individual payout methods so shares can route to people, not a missing entity account |
| Settlement amended after disbursement | **Clawback from people who have spent it** (17.09.06) — across more parties than the commission case | Behavior complete at Level 1: the obligation is recorded and each affected recipient is notified of a balance owed. Enforcement mechanics `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth]` — the sub-domain's hardest problem |
| Show inside an open cross-collateralized run | The number is provisional; disbursing it invites clawback (17.03.03) — advances against an open run are standard | The share shows "Provisional — held until the run closes"; the recipient sees why. Advance modelling `[PENDING — Step 5 / /write-be-spec]` |
| Currency differs from the recipient's | FX on the way out, unmodelled (17.03.01 DT-03) | The recipient sees the amount and a note that conversion applies; the FX basis/timing `[PENDING — Step 5 / /write-be-spec]` |
| Recipient is a minor | Real in live music; changes what a payout account can be | The share is Held with "A guardian must complete payout setup"; the eligibility rules `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Blocked | Settlement unsigned **and unrefused** — still awaiting an answer, so no line has been contested and no floor exists | Nothing moves; a proposal is not a payment |
| Floor ready | Settlement **refused**; the undisputed floor is evaluated and payable, the contested delta held ([17.09.05](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md) D-09) | Each share of the floor with its deductions itemised, beside the held delta and the count of disputed lines. Explicitly labelled a part-payment on an open settlement |
| Ready | Settlement final, split evaluated | Each share with its deductions itemised |
| Partial | Some recipients blocked | Others paid; blocked shares held with reasons |
| In flight | Payment initiated | Per-recipient status |
| Returned | Rail bounced a sent share | That share reverts to Held with the rail's reason; a retry is offered |
| Complete | All shares landed | Show closed |
| Asserted | Paid in cash off-platform | Recorded, not executed |
| Clawback | Post-disbursement amendment | Obligation recorded; enforceability uncertain |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Disbursement is the payments cross-cut and has no domain content | Cross-cut test — moving money serves 05, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19 | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the rail is a cross-cut; the shape is not.** Live disbursement is **one settlement fanning out to many heterogeneous recipients** — members on shares, sidemen on day rates, crew on flat fees, an agency on commission, a state on withholding — each with a different basis, some inside the band entity and some not. That is not "charge a customer"; it is a mass payout with a per-recipient tax and compliance profile. The rail is shared with 13's marketplace payouts; the fan-out shape is domain-specific. |
| DT-02 | Paying individuals directly is obviously better | Musician-centred instinct — it is exactly what `meta/personas.md` describes wanting | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a cost the sweep never surfaced.** It *is* what musicians want, and it converts the platform into a **mass payout facilitator**: KYC/AML per recipient, tax reporting per recipient (1099-K / W-9 / W-8BEN), money-transmission exposure — every one of which `meta/constraints.md` flags as `[PENDING]` and unresolved. The alternative (pay the entity) is trivial and delivers almost nothing: the drummer chases the band's treasurer, which is the fragmentation the product exists to end. **There is no cheap option here**, and the sweep's flat candidate "Payout Splits & Disbursement" hides that this single choice may be the most expensive compliance decision in the entire 24-domain map. |
| DT-03 | The platform disburses the money | The premise of the feature | ❌ REJECTED as the common case, at least initially. **The overwhelmingly normal live payment is cash, in a back room, at 1am** — the balance is handed over while the settlement sheet is being agreed (17.05.02 D-03). If the platform can only add value when it moves the money, it adds no value at the exact moment the domain is about it. The honest position: the platform's *first* value is the computation and the record (17.09), and disbursement is what it earns the right to do later. **That is a MoSCoW insight**, not a defeat — but it argues against disbursement being the domain's entry point. |
