## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Live Income Split Definition ↔ Disbursement Execution

**Relationship**: The split is the instruction; disbursement is the execution. Settlement finalises
(17.09.05), the split divides the pool, and disbursement moves each share. An undefined split is
money sitting in a pool with nowhere to go — which is a real state, because bands routinely do not
have a live split written down anywhere.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the band entity defines the split; each recipient tracks their own share.
- **Operator**: read-only on disbursement — they see their obligation discharged, not who got what.
  How the act divides its money is not the promoter's business.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The split is owned by the band entity under its own authority rule
   (01). Disbursement reads it. If the split changes between settlement and payment, the version in
   force at the show governs — the same pinning problem as commission terms (17.08.01 D-02).
2. **Trigger chain**: Settlement final → split → withhold → disburse. If disbursement fails for one
   recipient (no payout method), the others must still be paid — a partial disbursement is normal,
   not an error.
3. **Permission intersection**: A band member can see their own share. Whether they can see everyone
   else's is a real governance question (17.10.01 Q-02) — transparency is healthy and knowing what
   the other guitarist earns has ended bands.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Each recipient, plus the agent whose commission is deducted alongside
   (17.08.02).
5. **State transition conflict**: A settlement amendment (17.09.06) after disbursement means clawing
   back from people who have spent it. Same structural problem as commission clawback, across more
   parties.

### CX-02: Withholding Tax & VAT ↔ Live Income Split Definition

**Relationship**: **Sequencing is the whole issue.** Some regimes withhold at the entity level on the
gross paid to the act; others require withholding per individual payee. These produce different
results — a sideman on a flat £200 day rate is unaffected by entity-level withholding (the band
absorbs it) but is directly reduced by per-payee withholding. Same show, same law, different money
depending on which order the operations run.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the band absorbs entity-level withholding out of its own share; sidemen may or may
  not be touched.
- **Operator**: the withholding agent — they carry the liability for getting the sequence right.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — but the *order* is state, and it must be an explicit property of
   the regime rather than an implementation accident.
2. **Trigger chain**: Regime determines order → withhold → split, or split → withhold per payee. The
   branch must be modelled, not chosen once.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator applies withholding to money the artist owns. That is a
   legal obligation, not a permission — and it is why the Role Matrix inverts here.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every affected payee needs the withholding evidence to reclaim
   (17.10.03).
5. **State transition conflict**: A split changed after withholding was computed at entity level.
   `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

### CX-03: Withholding Tax & VAT ↔ Disbursement Execution

**Relationship**: Withheld money **never reaches the payee** — the payer remits it to the state
directly. The disbursement is therefore smaller than the split says, and the gap is not an error, a
fee, or a deduction the artist agreed to. It is the state.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: receives less than the contract says and must be told exactly why, with the evidence
  needed to reclaim it.
- **Operator**: remits, and is liable if they don't.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — withholding is computed before the rail is touched.
2. **Trigger chain**: Withhold → remit to state → disburse the remainder. If remittance fails, the
   Operator is liable, not the platform — but the platform recorded that it happened.
3. **Permission intersection**: Nobody may waive withholding, including by agreement.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The withholding evidence must reach the payee, or they cannot reclaim —
   and the reclaim is often worth more than the show's profit.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — Step 5]`.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Disbursement Execution | Live Income Split Definition (write direction) | Rejected. A payment never defines a split. The tempting move is "pay these people this way and remember it as the split" — which would let a one-off convenience become the band's standing agreement without anyone approving it under the entity's authority rule (17.02.03). The split is a governance artefact; disbursement is its consequence. |
| R-02 | Withholding Tax & VAT | Disbursement Execution (rate configuration) | Rejected as an interaction. Withholding **rates and regimes are determined by law and territory**, never by the payment rail or by what the disbursement mechanism can do. Modelling an edge here would invite regime selection to follow payment convenience, which is exactly backwards and is how a platform ends up advising on tax. The only real edge is CX-03: withheld money simply never enters the rail. |
