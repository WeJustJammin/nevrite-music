# Feature: Live Income Split Definition

> **Number**: 17.10.01
> **Parent**: [Live Income Payout & Tax](./17.10-live-income-payout-tax-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a band's gig money divides — equal shares, unequal shares, sidemen on day rates, the support act
paid off the top, the crew paid before anyone. **This is not the recording split.** The sideman
drummer on a £15,000 tour gets £200 a night and no points; the same drummer on the record may own 5%
of the master. Modelling live payout as the split sheet applied to gig money would pay the wrong
people the wrong amounts, confidently.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The **band entity** defines the split under its own authority rule (domain 01) — the same rule that binds its deals (17.02.03). **Members** see their own share; whether they see everyone else's is a real governance question (Q-02). **Sidemen** see their day rate and nothing else, which is correct and is also exactly how a sideman discovers they are not in the band. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | None | How the act divides its money is not the promoter's business. Their obligation is discharged when they pay the act. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The split divides the settled pool (17.09.05) among parties who are **not all the same kind of
party**:

- **Members** — share the pool, equally or not, per the band's agreement.
- **Sidemen** — a fixed day rate off the top. Not a share. They get paid the same whether the show
  grossed £2,000 or £20,000, and that is the deal they took.
- **Crew** — tour manager, FOH engineer, backline tech. Usually flat, usually off the top, usually
  before the band eats.
- **Support acts** — paid off the top per their own deal (17.14).

The order of operations decides who absorbs a bad night. When a show underperforms, the sidemen and
crew still get their flat rates and the members split what is left — which is sometimes nothing.
**That is the actual economics of being in a band** and a model with a flat percentage table cannot
express it.

Commission is **not** part of the split (index D-04) — it is a deduction from the artist's share under
a separate agreement.

### Happy Path

1. Band entity defines its live split — member shares, sideman rates, crew rates — approved under its
   authority rule.
2. A show settles (17.09.05); the pool is known.
3. Split evaluates in its stated order: support and crew off the top, sidemen at their rates, members
   share the remainder.
4. Withholding applies before or after depending on the regime (CX-02).
5. Disbursement executes each share (17.10.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show underperforms; flat rates exceed the pool | Sidemen and crew are owed their rates and the members get nothing — or the band owes money | The real economics; must be representable, not an error |
| Band has no written live split | The overwhelmingly common case — most bands have never written one down | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a default is required and choosing it is a product decision (Q-01) |
| A dep plays one show for an absent member | Is the dep a sideman? Does the absent member still take their share? | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — real, frequent, and bitterly argued |
| Member sees another member's share | Transparency is healthy; knowing what the other guitarist earns has ended bands | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (Q-02) |
| Split changed between settlement and payment | The version in force at the show governs (CX-01) | Split is versioned and pinned per show |
| Lineup changed mid-tour | Shares must follow the lineup that played, not the current one | Pinned per show, not per band |
| Recording split accidentally applied | Would pay a sideman points they don't have and stiff them their day rate | Structurally prevented — different model (index D-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Undefined | Band never wrote one | Prompt with the consequence of not having one; **the common case** |
| Defined | Band entity approved a split | Members, rates, order of operations |
| Pinned | A show settled under this version | That show's split is frozen at the version in force |
| Evaluated | Pool divided | Each party's share with its derivation |
| Underwater | Flat rates exceed the pool | Members get nothing; representable, not an error |
| Superseded | Split changed | New version live; settled shows retain theirs |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The live split is the recording split applied to gig money | Reuse instinct — the platform already models splits in domain 09, and it is the same band | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the reason the feature exists. **They are different agreements between different parties over different money.** The sideman drummer gets a day rate live and no points on the record; the songwriter who does not tour gets points on the record and nothing live. Reusing 09's model would pay a sideman a percentage they never agreed to and deny them the rate they did. It would also be *invisibly* wrong — the numbers would compute and be plausible. A domain expert would catch this instantly; a schema designer looking for reuse would not. |
| DT-02 | A split is a percentage table | The obvious model, and how 09's split sheet works | ❌ REJECTED: **the parties are not all the same kind of party.** Sidemen and crew take flat rates off the top; members share what remains. A percentage table cannot express "the drummer gets £200 whether we gross £2,000 or £20,000", and that is most people on most tours. The order of operations is the substance: flat rates first, members last, which is why the members absorb a bad night and the sidemen don't. That asymmetry *is* what being in a band means, financially. |
| DT-03 | Bands have a live split that the platform records | The premise of the whole feature | ❌ REJECTED: **most bands have never written one down.** They have a verbal understanding that has never been tested by a night that lost money, and it is exactly the class of fact `meta/problem-statement.md` says gets reconstructed years later at the moment it becomes contested. This is the **provenance thesis on live income** — and it means the feature's real job is not recording an existing agreement but getting one made *before* the money arrives, when everyone is still friendly. That reframes the design: the prompt matters more than the form. It also means the empty state is the main state at launch (Q-01). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The live split is a distinct model from the recording split | Different agreements, different parties, different money — and reusing 09 would be *invisibly* wrong (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Flat-rate parties and share parties are different kinds, with an explicit order | A percentage table cannot express a day rate, and the order is what decides who absorbs a bad night (DT-02). |
| D-03 | The split is pinned per show at the version in force | Lineups change mid-tour; shares must follow the lineup that played. |
| D-04 | The feature's job is to get a split agreed before the money arrives, not to record an existing one | Most bands have never written one down; this is the provenance thesis applied to live income (DT-03). |
