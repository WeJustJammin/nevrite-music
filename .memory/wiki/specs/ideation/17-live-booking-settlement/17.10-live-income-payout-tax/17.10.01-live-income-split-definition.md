# Feature: Live Income Split Definition

> **Number**: 17.10.01
> **Parent**: [Live Income Payout & Tax](./17.10-live-income-payout-tax-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

How a band's gig money divides — equal shares, unequal shares, sidemen on day rates, the support act
paid off the top, the crew paid before anyone. **This is not the recording split.** The sideman
drummer on a £15,000 tour gets £200 a night and no points; the same drummer on the record may own 5%
of the master. Modelling live payout as the split sheet applied to gig money would pay the wrong
people the wrong amounts, confidently.

The feature's real job is not recording an agreement that exists. **Most bands have never written one
down** (DT-03) — they have a verbal understanding untested by a night that lost money. That is exactly
the class of fact `meta/problem-statement.md` says gets reconstructed years later, at the moment it
becomes contested and valuable. So the feature exists to get a split *agreed before the money
arrives*, while everyone is still friendly, and then to hold it as evidence. Provenance, applied to
live income.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The **band entity** defines the split under its own governance rule (01.04), consumed — not redefined — here, the same rule that binds its deals (17.02.03 D-03). Approval binds to a **version hash**, not to "the split". **Visibility follows derivation (D-08)**: a party sees every input their own number depends on, and nothing else. A member on a share of the residual therefore sees every other residual line, every flat rate off the top and the band fund — because each of those changes their number. A **sideman on £200 flat** sees their own line and nothing else, because nothing else moves their number — which is correct, and is also exactly how a sideman discovers they are not in the band. A **former member** retains permanent read access to the pinned splits of shows they played (DT-13), and no access to shows they did not. |
| Producer | None | No role. Live payout has no Producer lens (sub-domain index Role Matrix). A Producer who plays the gig is acting as a **Musician** and appears in the split as a party like any other — personas are lenses on behaviour, not account types (`meta/personas.md`). |
| Operator | None | How the act divides its money is not the promoter's business. Their obligation is discharged when they pay the act (17.10.02 D-03). This holds **even inside a settlement dispute** (17.09.06 / domain 24): the Operator contests the pool, never its division. The one place an Operator touches a per-payee number is withholding (17.10.03), and that is a legal obligation imposed on them, not a permission granted to them. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

The split divides a settled pool (17.09.05) among parties who are **not all the same kind of party**,
in a **stated order** that decides who absorbs a bad night.

### The party kinds

| Kind | Basis | Paid | Absorbs a bad night? |
|------|-------|------|---------------------|
| **Flat-rate, off the top** | A fixed amount per show or per day | Before anything is shared — sidemen, deps, crew (tour manager, FOH, backline tech), and bought-on support (17.14) | **No.** £200 whether the show grossed £2,000 or £20,000 — that is the deal they took |
| **Band fund** | A percentage or fixed amount off the top, payable to the **band entity itself** (DT-10) | Off the top, alongside flat rates, in the order the split states | No |
| **Share of the residual** | A weight against whatever is left | Last | **Yes.** Members eat the variance — sometimes to zero, sometimes below it |

**A party is a `(payee × basis)` pair, not a person** (DT-04). One human routinely holds two lines:
the bassist who also mixes FOH takes the FOH flat fee off the top *and* a member share of the
residual. Both lines are real, both are paid, and the flat one pays first.

### The order of operations is the substance

`pool → flat-rate lines (in stated order) → band fund → residual → share weights → per-member deductions`

Commission is **not** in this chain (index D-04) — it is a deduction from one party's share under a
separate agreement with a separate counterparty (17.08.02). Withholding sequences before or after
the residual depending on the regime, which changes everyone's number (CX-02); the split states its
own order and the regime states its own, and neither may silently reorder the other.

### What the split governs — and what it does not

The split governs **the performance pool for a given show**. It does not govern:

- **Live performance royalties.** Setlist returns to the PRO pay the **writers** (10.06), not the
  band, and never touch this split (DT-06). The drummer on an equal live split does not share the
  PRS the guitarist earns from the same night's setlist. This is invisible inside this feature and it
  is a real source of resentment; the feature must not imply it divides money it never sees.
- **Merch net.** Merch is a distinct pool with its own venue cut (17.09.04) and frequently its own
  rule (DT-05). The split **must state explicitly** whether its rule governs merch net or whether
  merch has its own; a split silent on merch blocks merch evaluation rather than assuming.
- **Per-member deductions.** A float advance drawn before the tour, per diems taken (18.13.01),
  damage charged back — these hit **one member's share after the division**, never the pool before
  it (DT-07). This is the mechanism by which a member ends a tour owing the band money.

### Determinism and exact sum

Evaluation is **deterministic and reproducible**: re-evaluating a pinned split against the same pool
must produce identical results forever.

- All allocations round to the **minor unit of the pool's currency**.
- `sum(flat lines) + band fund + sum(member shares) + sum(withheld) == pool`, **exactly**. A non-zero
  difference **blocks evaluation** — it is never absorbed silently.
- Rounding residue is allocated by **largest remainder**, ties broken by a stable party order fixed at
  pin time. £1,000 across three equal members is £333.33 × 3 = £999.99; the remaining 1p goes to a
  named party by a rule the band can read, not to nobody (DT-15).

### The platform never invents a fact about someone's money

Two consequences, and they are the feature's spine:

1. **Propose, never apply** (D-05). A band with no split gets a *pre-filled proposal* clearly labelled
   as a platform suggestion that nobody has agreed. It is inert until approved under the band's
   governance rule. The platform never evaluates a split no human ratified.
2. **Never renormalize** (D-11). Removing a member does not silently rescale the survivors' shares —
   that would change every remaining member's number with nobody approving it. Removal yields a split
   that no longer allocates the pool, which **blocks evaluation** and demands a new approved version.

### Pinning

The split is **pinned per show**, at the instant the settlement becomes final (17.09.05 `Signed`),
stamped with the version hash then in force. Lineups change mid-tour; shares must follow the lineup
that played, not the current one. A version approved after that instant — even by milliseconds —
never applies to that show, and its approvers are told so explicitly rather than left to assume.

### Happy Path

1. The band entity opens its live split. The platform pre-fills a **proposal**: every current member
   at equal weight, no flat-rate parties, no band fund — labelled "**Proposed — nobody has agreed
   this yet**" (D-05, Q-01).
2. A member edits it into the real agreement: adds the sideman drummer at £200/show off the top, the
   tour manager at £150/show off the top, a 10% band fund, and moves the two writers to weight 1.25
   against the other two at 1.0. Each edit renders as a **consequence**, not a field diff — "this pays
   Dave £200 before anyone shares" (17.02.01 D-02 pattern).
3. They set the two required policies the platform will not choose for them: the **absence rule**
   (what happens to a member's share on a show they did not play — D-09) and the **loss rule** (how a
   negative residual is funded — D-10).
4. They submit. The band's governance rule (01.04) determines who must approve; approvals accumulate
   against the **version hash** (17.02.03 D-01).
5. Rule satisfied → the split is `Defined`. Every party is notified of the lines their own number
   depends on (D-08). Non-members with flat lines are notified of their own line only.
6. A show settles (17.09.05); the pool is final. The split **pins** at the version in force.
7. Evaluation runs in the stated order. Withholding sequences per the regime (CX-02). Every party's
   number carries its full derivation — pool, each deduction above them, their basis, their result.
8. Disbursement executes each share (17.10.02, CX-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show underperforms; flat rates exceed the pool | Sidemen and crew are owed their rates in full; the residual is negative and the **loss rule** (D-10) allocates it among members | `Underwater`. The real economics, stated: "Dave, Sam and the TM are paid £550. The pool was £400. The band owes £150, split by your loss rule: £50 each." Representable, not an error |
| Band has no written live split | The overwhelmingly common case. The platform proposes equal member shares, labelled as **unagreed**, and evaluates nothing until it is ratified (D-05) | `Empty`: "You have never written down how gig money divides. Here is a starting point — nobody has agreed it." Money holds; nothing is invented |
| Pool settles while the split is still unapproved | Evaluation blocks; the pool is held. **This is CX-01's "money with nowhere to go"** | `Blocked — no agreed split`, naming the outstanding approvers under the band's rule. Not an error state — a governance state |
| A dep plays one show for an absent member | The dep is a **flat-rate party** engaged by the band (they took a rate, not a share). The absent member's share is decided by the split's **absence rule**, which is a required field with three modelled options (D-09) | The rule as the band chose it: band absorbs / member absorbs / member forfeits. The platform never picks |
| Split has no absence rule (legacy or imported) and a member misses a show | Evaluation **blocks** on that show; the split is not evaluable without the policy | `Blocked — undefined absence rule`, naming the absent member and the show. Prompted to choose once, applies onward |
| Member sees another member's share | **Structural, not a setting** (D-08). A residual-share member's number depends on every other residual line, so they see them. An approver cannot approve what they cannot see | Full derivation of everything above and beside them. Transparency is not offered here; it is forced by the arithmetic (Q-02) |
| Sideman tries to view the band's split | Their £200 does not depend on anyone else's number, so they see nothing else (D-08) | Their own line, its basis, its status. Nothing about the members |
| Operator asks who got what — including inside a settlement dispute | Refused. Their obligation is discharged at the pool (17.10.02 D-03) | Nothing. The dispute contests the pool, never its division |
| A member with edit rights adds a flat line payable to **themselves** off the top | The `meta/personas.md` "worst intentional" pattern, at band scale. Not blocked — it is sometimes legitimate (the bassist really is mixing FOH) — but a flat line payable to a member requires the **same approval as a share change**, and every affected member sees it because it changes their number (D-08) | The consequence rendering: "This pays Dave £300 before anyone shares. Your share falls from £250 to £175." Visible, not silent |
| The multi-hyphenate: bassist is also FOH engineer | Two lines, one human. The flat line pays first; the share line pays last (DT-04) | Both lines, both derivations. They are paid twice on two bases and that is correct |
| The multi-hyphenate: a member is also the band's booking agent | Commission is **not in the split** (index D-04). It is deducted from the band's share under 17.08's separate agreement — with the acute wrinkle that the counterparty is a bandmate | The split is unaffected. The commission appears where commission appears (17.08.02) — deliberately not laundered into the split, which would make an agent look like a band member |
| Split changed between settlement and payment | The version pinned at settlement-final governs (CX-01) | The pinned version, with the newer one visibly marked "does not apply to this show" |
| Split version approved in the same instant a settlement finalises | The pin is taken atomically at settlement-final. The later version loses, by any margin | Approvers are told explicitly: "Your v4 was approved after Leeds settled. Leeds paid on v3." Never left to assume |
| Two members approve different versions concurrently | Approval binds to a version hash (17.02.03 D-01); an approval of a superseded version is invalidated and its approver re-notified — never silently dropped | "Your approval of v2 no longer applies — the split is now v4. Here is what changed." |
| Two shows settle against the same split simultaneously | Both pin the same hash; evaluations are independent. No conflict — a pin is a read, not a lock | Nothing. This is normal |
| Lineup changed mid-tour | Shares follow the lineup that played, because the pin is per show (D-03) | Each show's split as it was on the night, not as it is today |
| Member leaves; a show they played settles afterwards | They are owed. The pinned split names them, and it pays them | The ex-member sees and receives their share for that show. Leaving does not forfeit money already earned |
| Ex-member's access to a split they were part of | **Permanent read access** to pinned splits of shows they played; none to shows they did not (DT-13) | Their history survives their departure — which is the entire point, since departure is precisely when it becomes contested |
| Member dies mid-tour | Party identity is not stable for the life of a split (DT-17). The share is owed to the estate (01.10) | The pinned split still names them; the payee resolves to the estate. The band cannot edit them out of a show they played |
| Member's payee changes mid-tour (personal → limited company, for tax) | The party persists; the **payee** changes. It is not a new party and it does not restart approvals | A payee change, not a split change |
| Band edits a sideman's £200 down to £150 inside its own split | **Blocked** (D-07). The sideman's rate is a two-party engagement; the split's non-member flat lines are **references**, not editable numbers. Editing it would change someone else's contract by editing your own document | "Dave's rate is £200 under his engagement for this run. Change it with Dave, not here." |
| Flat line references an engagement with no agreed rate ("we said about two hundred") | Evaluation blocks with the party named. This is the reconstruction failure the product exists to prevent, caught before the money moves rather than years later | `Partial — 1 party unpriced`: "Dave's rate was never agreed. The pool cannot divide." |
| A party has no platform identity (the dep who never signed up) | Representable. The split must not require every payee to have an account — a band cannot wait for its dep to register before agreeing how it pays people. Identity resolves later (01.09) | Party present, payee unresolved. The split is valid; **disbursement** is what blocks (17.10.02 `Partial`) |
| Member on a zero share (new member on 6-month probation) | Valid and must be representable. Weight 0 is a real agreement, not a validation error | A member with a £0 share and a visible reason. Not an error |
| Share weights are all zero, or there are no members at all | Blocked. The residual has nowhere to go | `Invalid — the residual allocates to nobody` |
| Solo artist trading as an entity | One member at weight 1.0. The feature must not impose band ceremony on one person | A one-line split, approved in one action. No governance theatre |
| Support act on a **buy-on** (pays for the slot) | A **negative flat line** — income into the pool, not a deduction from it (DT-12) | Labelled as income, not as a negative expense. The arithmetic is identical; the statement is only readable if the sign is named |
| Promoter pays the support directly | The support is **not a party to this split at all**. 17.14's deal decides whether the headliner is the payer | Support absent from the split entirely — correct, not an omission |
| Show sits inside an open cross-collateralized run | The pool is provisional (17.03.03), so **residual shares are provisional** — but flat-rate parties are paid on the night regardless (DT-11). **Two settlement grains inside one split** | Flat parties: paid, final. Share parties: provisional, with the run's close date. This is domain Q-02 landing here (Q-05) |
| Split changed after entity-level withholding was already computed and remitted | Withholding was computed against the **pinned** split. A later version cannot retroactively change what the state already took; the change applies to future shows only | The pinned show's withholding stands. The new version is marked as not applying to it |
| Settlement amended after the split evaluated and disbursed | Re-evaluation runs against the **same pinned version** with the new pool — never against the current version. Clawback obligations fall out per party (17.09.06, 17.10.02 Q-02) | Each party's before/after with the derivation of the delta. The clawback is 17.10.02's problem; the recomputation is this feature's |
| Recording split accidentally applied | Would pay a sideman points they never had and deny them the rate they did — and would compute plausibly while doing it | Structurally prevented — a different model (index D-03, DT-01) |
| Draft edit lost to a network failure mid-edit | A lost draft is a lost draft. Evaluation never reads drafts, so nothing is at risk beyond retyping | Draft state lost; the approved version is untouched. No partial application is possible |
| Merch settles but the split is silent on merch | Merch evaluation blocks; the performance pool is unaffected (DT-05) | `Blocked — this split does not say whether it governs merch.` On a bad night merch **is** the income (17.09.04), so this is not a corner case |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Split or pinned evaluation being fetched | Skeleton of the party table. Numbers **never** render partially — a half-drawn split is a wrong split, and this is money |
| **Empty / Undefined** | Band never wrote one. **The main state at launch** (DT-03) | The consequence stated plainly ("a night that loses money will decide this for you"), plus the unagreed pre-filled proposal (D-05) |
| **Proposed** | Platform pre-fill exists; nobody has approved | Labelled "Proposed — nobody has agreed this yet". Inert. Evaluates nothing |
| **Pending approval** | Version submitted under the band's rule (01.04) | Named approvers, who has responded, what is outstanding, against the version hash |
| **Invalidated** | A newer version superseded one already approved | Approvers re-notified naming the version and the delta; chain restarts against the new hash |
| **Defined / Populated** | Governance rule satisfied for this version | Parties, bases, order of operations, absence rule, loss rule, merch scope |
| **Pinned** | A settlement went final under this version | That show's split frozen at the version in force, with the pin timestamp |
| **Evaluated** | Pool divided | Every party's number with its full derivation |
| **Underwater** | Flat rates exceed the pool | Negative residual allocated by the loss rule. Representable, not an error |
| **Partial** | Pool known; ≥1 flat line unpriced, or a payee unresolved | The parties that *can* be computed, and the exact party blocking the rest, named |
| **Blocked** | No agreed split, no absence rule, or sum ≠ pool | The specific reason and the exact action that clears it. Money holds |
| **Error** | Evaluation failed the exact-sum assertion | Nothing is disbursed. The discrepancy shown to the penny with its derivation. **Never** absorbed into someone's share |
| **Superseded** | Split changed | New version live; settled shows retain theirs, visibly marked as not affected |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The live split is the recording split applied to gig money | Reuse instinct — the platform already models splits in domain 09, and it is the same band | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the reason the feature exists. **They are different agreements between different parties over different money.** The sideman drummer gets a day rate live and no points on the record; the songwriter who does not tour gets points on the record and nothing live. Reusing 09's model would pay a sideman a percentage they never agreed to and deny them the rate they did. It would also be *invisibly* wrong — the numbers would compute and be plausible. A domain expert would catch this instantly; a schema designer looking for reuse would not. |
| DT-02 | A split is a percentage table | The obvious model, and how 09's split sheet works | ❌ REJECTED: **the parties are not all the same kind of party.** Sidemen and crew take flat rates off the top; members share what remains. A percentage table cannot express "the drummer gets £200 whether we gross £2,000 or £20,000", and that is most people on most tours. The order of operations is the substance: flat rates first, members last, which is why the members absorb a bad night and the sidemen don't. That asymmetry *is* what being in a band means, financially. |
| DT-03 | Bands have a live split that the platform records | The premise of the whole feature | ❌ REJECTED: **most bands have never written one down.** They have a verbal understanding that has never been tested by a night that lost money, and it is exactly the class of fact `meta/problem-statement.md` says gets reconstructed years later at the moment it becomes contested. This is the **provenance thesis on live income** — and it means the feature's real job is not recording an existing agreement but getting one made *before* the money arrives, when everyone is still friendly. That reframes the design: the prompt matters more than the form. It also means the empty state is the main state at launch (Q-01). |
| DT-04 | A party to the split is a person | The naive model, and how every split UI is drawn | ❌ REJECTED: a party is a **`(payee × basis)` pair**. `meta/personas.md` names the multi-hyphenate as music's *defining* identity characteristic — and it lands here as arithmetic, not just UX. The bassist who mixes FOH takes the FOH flat fee off the top **and** a member share of the residual: two lines, one human, paid on two bases, in two positions in the order. A model of one-person-one-share cannot express it and will either pay them their fee or their share, never both. This is the persona model's structural fact showing up as a money bug. |
| DT-05 | The show has one pool, and this split divides it | The sub-domain index's framing — "the pool" throughout | ❌ REJECTED: **there are at least two pools and they routinely divide differently.** The performance fee is one; **merch net (17.09.04) is another**, and it is frequently governed by a different rule — the entity's, or the person who stood at the table's, or an even split when the fee split is not. On a bad night merch **is** the artist's income (17.09.04's own words), so this is not a corner. The split must state its pool scope explicitly; a split silent on merch must block merch rather than assume it governs money nobody said it governed. |
| DT-06 | The live split governs the money the show generates for the band | The feature's apparent scope | ✅ CONFIRMED **only for the performance pool — and the exclusion is the finding.** Live performance royalties do **not** flow through this split: the setlist returns to the PRO (10.06) and pays the **writers**, directly, bypassing the band entity entirely. The drummer on an equal live split does not share the PRS the guitarist earns from the same night's setlist. This is invisible inside the feature and it is one of the most reliable sources of resentment in working bands — the non-writing member who thinks "equal split" means equal. A domain expert expects it; nobody in this corpus has mentioned it. The feature must not imply it divides money it never sees. |
| DT-07 | Deductions happen to the pool | The order-of-operations model in DT-02 | ❌ REJECTED as complete: **per-member deductions exist and they land after the division, not before it.** The float advance drawn before the tour, per diems taken (18.13.01), a damage charge-back — these hit **one member's share**, not the pool. The distinction is not cosmetic: a pool deduction costs every member proportionally; a per-member deduction costs one member entirely. It is also the mechanism by which **a member ends a tour owing the band money** — a real, common, and completely unmodelled outcome that a pool-only model renders arithmetically impossible. |
| DT-08 | A negative residual just means the members get nothing | The `Underwater` state as originally written | ✅ CONFIRMED **and materially incomplete.** Getting nothing is the *floor* case; the real case is the band **owes**. That obligation must be allocated, and — the part nobody designs for — **the loss rule is not necessarily the profit rule.** Bands that split profit unequally (writers on 1.25) very often split losses equally, because the felt logic is different: profit tracks contribution, loss tracks membership. A model that inverts the profit weights to allocate a loss will produce a number the band never agreed and cannot argue with. The loss rule must be its own required field. |
| DT-09 | The band edits its own split, so it may edit every line in it | It is the band's document, under the band's authority rule | ❌ REJECTED, and this is a real integrity hole. **Flat-rate lines for non-members are references to two-party engagements, not editable numbers.** If the band can lower the sideman's £200 to £150 by editing its own split, it has changed someone else's contract by editing its own document — under its own governance rule, with the sideman having no vote and (per D-08) no visibility of the version that did it. The split *references* the engagement's rate; changing it is a conversation with that person, elsewhere. The band's authority is over the band's agreement, not over its counterparties'. |
| DT-10 | The band entity is not a party to its own split | Splits divide money among people | ✅ CONFIRMED as **wrong** — the entity is routinely a party to itself. The **band fund** — a percentage off the top into the band account for the van, the rehearsal room, the record — is not an expense and it is not anyone's share. It is the entity paying itself, and it must be a first-class party whose payee is the band. `meta/personas.md` establishes bands as first-class entities that "book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members"; this is that fact meeting the split table. It also collides with index Q-01 head-on: if the platform disburses only to individuals, the band fund has no payee (Q-06). |
| DT-11 | Every party to a split settles at the same time | One pool, one settlement, one payout event | ✅ CONFIRMED as **false — there are two grains inside one split.** The sideman is paid **on the night**; that is what a day rate means. But if the show sits in a cross-collateralized run (17.03.03), the pool is provisional until the run closes, so the **members' shares are provisional while the sidemen's are final**. Same split, same show, two settlement grains — flat parties settle per show, share parties settle per run. This is the domain's Q-02 (settlement grain) arriving inside a feature that cannot answer it, and it means a split cannot be modelled as one evaluation producing one set of final numbers. |
| DT-12 | The support act is a deduction from the pool | The Behavior section as originally written | ❌ REJECTED as a general rule, twice over. (1) Where the **promoter** pays the support directly, the support is not a party to this split at all — 17.14's deal decides who the payer is, and the split must reflect that rather than assume the headliner pays. (2) On a **buy-on**, the support pays *for* the slot: it is **income into the pool**, a negative flat line. The arithmetic of a negative deduction is trivially correct and the statement is unreadable unless the sign is named — "Support: -£500" is a line that has started arguments in real settlement rooms. |
| DT-13 | Access to a split follows membership — you leave, you lose it | The obvious permission model | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the thesis at its sharpest. **A former member must retain permanent read access to the pinned splits of shows they played.** Departure is *precisely* the moment the record becomes contested and valuable — `meta/problem-statement.md`'s "reconstructed years later, usually at the exact moment they become contested". A platform that revokes the evidence when the relationship ends has built the reconstruction failure into its permission model, and has done so at the one moment its entire value proposition is being tested. They see the shows they played. They see nothing after. |
| DT-14 | The platform should propose a *fair* split — from credits, contribution, or who wrote what | It has the data (02, 09), and the empty state is the main state (DT-03) | ❌ REJECTED, firmly. **There is no fair answer, and proposing one is the platform deciding someone's money.** A contribution-weighted proposal encodes a contested theory of what live money is *for* — labour, or authorship, or membership — and bands hold all three views sincerely. Worse, a proposal carrying the platform's authority becomes the anchor the band argues from, so the platform's opinion silently wins by being first. The defensible pre-fill is the one that asserts nothing: equal member shares, visibly labelled as unagreed (D-05). `decision-classification.md` is unambiguous — this is a product decision about the user's money, and it is theirs. |
| DT-15 | Rounding is an implementation detail for `/create-prd` | It looks like arithmetic hygiene | ❌ REJECTED: it is **behaviour, and it is a class of bug that presents as the band's money vanishing.** £1,000 across three equal members is £333.33 each — £999.99, and a penny with no home. Unspecified, that penny is dropped, or double-paid, or silently absorbed into whoever the loop hit last. Across a 30-date run with five parties it is a discrepancy the band will find and will not be able to explain, in the one artefact whose entire value is being unarguable. The exact-sum assertion (`sum == pool`, or block) and a **stated, readable** allocation rule are behaviour the band must be able to read, not an implementation choice. |
| DT-16 | Removing a member rescales the remaining shares to fill the gap | It is what every percentage UI does, and it looks helpful | ❌ REJECTED: **silent renormalization changes every remaining member's number with nobody approving it.** Four members at 25% become three at 33.3% — the platform just gave three people a raise, under no authority, and it will be discovered at settlement. It is D-05's failure with a friendlier face: the platform inventing a fact about money. Removal must produce a split that no longer allocates the pool, which **blocks evaluation** and forces a new version through the governance rule (01.04). The block is the feature. |
| DT-17 | A party's identity is stable for the life of the split | Implicit in pinning | ❌ REJECTED — party identity moves under the split constantly, and pinning must survive it. A member **dies** mid-tour and their share is owed to their estate (01.10) — and the band cannot edit them out of a show they played. A member incorporates mid-tour and their **payee** becomes their limited company for tax reasons — that is a payee change, not a split change, and it must not restart approvals or invalidate a pin. A dep has **no platform identity at all** and the split must still be definable, or a band cannot agree how it pays people until its dep signs up (01.09). The party persists; the payee resolves. Conflating them breaks all three. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The live split is a distinct model from the recording split | Different agreements, different parties, different money — and reusing 09 would be *invisibly* wrong (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Flat-rate parties and share parties are different kinds, with an explicit order | A percentage table cannot express a day rate, and the order is what decides who absorbs a bad night (DT-02). |
| D-03 | The split is pinned per show, atomically, at the version in force at settlement-final | Lineups change mid-tour; shares must follow the lineup that played. A version approved a millisecond later never applies, and its approvers are told so. |
| D-04 | The feature's job is to get a split agreed before the money arrives, not to record an existing one | Most bands have never written one down; this is the provenance thesis applied to live income (DT-03). |
| D-05 | **Propose, never apply.** A band with no split gets a pre-filled proposal, labelled unagreed, inert until ratified under the band's rule | The platform must never invent a fact about someone's money. The pre-fill makes the lazy path the correct path (`meta/personas.md`) without the platform deciding anything. |
| D-06 | **A party is `(payee × basis)`, not a person** — one human may hold multiple lines in different positions in the order | The multi-hyphenate is the persona model's defining structural fact (`meta/personas.md`) and it lands here as arithmetic. The FOH-engineer-bassist is paid twice, on two bases (DT-04). |
| D-07 | Flat lines for **non-members** are references to two-party engagements — the band cannot edit their amounts | Otherwise the band changes someone else's contract by editing its own document, under its own rule, with the counterparty having no vote and no visibility (DT-09). |
| D-08 | **Visibility follows derivation**: a party sees exactly the inputs their own number depends on | This dissolves the transparency question rather than answering it. A residual-share member's number depends on every other line, and an approver cannot approve what they cannot see — so member-to-member visibility is *forced*, not chosen. A flat-rate sideman's number depends on nothing else, so they see nothing else. The rule is derivable, explicable, and not a preference. |
| D-09 | The **absence rule** is a required field, with three modelled options and **no platform default**: band absorbs the dep / the absent member absorbs the dep / the member forfeits and the residual splits among the players | Real, frequent, bitterly argued. Any default the platform picks pays or docks a specific human being. A split without one blocks at the first show with an absence, rather than guessing. |
| D-10 | The **loss rule** is a required field, separate from the profit rule | Profit tracks contribution; loss tracks membership. Bands that split profit unequally routinely split loss equally. Inverting the profit weights produces a number nobody agreed (DT-08). |
| D-11 | **Never renormalize.** Removing a member yields a split that no longer allocates the pool, blocking evaluation until a new version is approved | Silent rescaling gives every survivor a raise under no authority (DT-16). The block is the feature. |
| D-12 | Evaluation is deterministic and **must sum to the pool exactly**; rounding residue allocates by largest remainder with a stated tiebreak, and a non-zero difference blocks | Unspecified rounding presents as the band's money vanishing, in the one artefact whose value is being unarguable (DT-15). |
| D-13 | The split states its **pool scope** explicitly; merch net is a separate pool and a split silent on merch blocks merch rather than assuming | Merch is frequently governed by a different rule, and on a bad night it is the only income (17.09.04, DT-05). |
| D-14 | **Former members retain permanent read access to pinned splits of shows they played** | Departure is precisely when the record becomes contested. Revoking it builds the reconstruction failure into the permission model (DT-13). |
| D-15 | The platform never proposes a *fair* split from contribution or credit data | There is no fair answer; a proposal carrying the platform's authority becomes the anchor and silently wins by being first (DT-14). A product decision about the user's money is the user's (`decision-classification.md`). |
