# Feature: Force Majeure Declaration & Resolution

> **Number**: 17.05.04
> **Parent**: [Deposits, Balances & Cancellation](./17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The path for a show that dies through nobody's fault — illness, weather, a licensing failure, a
pandemic. Force majeure **overrides** the cancellation ladder rather than occupying a rung on it
(CX-03): it typically voids obligations in both directions and returns the deposit regardless of
proximity to the date, because the ladder's whole logic (price the fault by how late it came) has no
subject when there is no fault.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | May declare — the singer has laryngitis, the van died, the drummer's visa was refused. Their declaration is a **claim**, not an outcome; the Operator may not accept it, and "the singer is ill" is precisely the claim promoters have heard as a lie before. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | May declare — the roof failed, the licence was pulled, the city cancelled the event. Also the party who accepts or contests the artist's declaration. |
| Fan | None | Never visible here. The fan's experience of an FM cancellation is a refund and an apology, both owned by domain 19 (17.05.03 D-03 applies identically). |

## Behavior

FM is a **two-step, inherently adversarial** mechanic: one side declares, the other accepts or
contests. Declaring FM is not the same as FM applying — and the gap between those two things is where
every real FM argument lives.

If accepted: obligations void both ways, the deposit returns, the date releases, and 18 and 19 stand
down.

If contested: the matter leaves this sub-domain entirely and becomes a dispute (domain 24). This
feature does not adjudicate. It records the declaration, its grounds, and its timestamp — which is
the provenance artefact that a later adjudication will actually need.

What counts as force majeure is a **contract term** (17.04), not a platform judgement. The platform
must never decide whether a pandemic is covered.

### Happy Path

1. A party's show becomes impossible for a reason the contract's FM clause covers.
2. They declare FM against the booking, stating grounds and attaching evidence.
3. The counterparty is notified urgently and accepts.
4. Obligations void both ways; the deposit returns (17.05.01); the cancellation ladder is bypassed
   (CX-03).
5. Date returns to avails; 18 stands production down; 19 handles fan refunds; the declaration is
   recorded permanently and does **not** count against either party's reliability record (D-03).

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Force majeure means the show is cancelled | The framing of the sweep's candidate ("Cancellation/Force Majeure Terms") and of this feature's own name | ❌ REJECTED: **the overwhelmingly common FM outcome is postponement, not cancellation.** The date moves, the deal survives, the deposit rolls forward, and the tickets stay valid. Post-2020 this is the dominant path by a wide margin — and it is unmodelled everywhere in domain 17. A postponed show is not a cancelled show and not a new booking; it is the same deal on a different date, which means the hold ladder, the contract, the schedule, and the ticket manifest all have to move together. **This is a genuine structural gap the sweep did not surface** and it is escalated to the domain index. |
| DT-02 | FM declarations can be validated by the platform | Fraud-prevention instinct — the abuse (fake laryngitis on a slow-selling show) is obvious | ❌ REJECTED: the platform must **never** decide whether a pandemic, an illness, or a storm is covered — that is contract interpretation with legal consequences, and getting it wrong once would be catastrophic for trust and possibly for liability. What the platform can legitimately do is make the declaration **expensive to fake**: record it permanently with grounds, evidence, and timestamp; make the counterparty's contest cheap; and let a pattern of FM declarations be visible in 17.12's relationship record. Deterrence through provenance, not adjudication — which is the platform's thesis applied to a legal edge it must not enter. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform records FM declarations; it never adjudicates them | Contract interpretation with legal consequence is not a product function (DT-02). Deterrence comes from permanent provenance, not judgement. |
| D-02 | FM overrides the cancellation ladder rather than being a tier on it | The ladder prices fault by lateness; FM has no fault to price (index D-02). |
| D-03 | An accepted FM does not damage either party's reliability record | Penalising a genuine FM would push both sides to lie about why a show died — destroying the record's value. A *pattern* remains visible (DT-02). |
| D-04 | Postponement is escalated as a domain-level structural gap, not absorbed here | It is neither FM nor cancellation, and it moves the hold, contract, schedule and ticket manifest together (DT-01). |
