## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Deposit Invoice & Collection ↔ Cancellation Tiers & Forfeit

**Relationship**: The deposit is what makes cancellation terms self-executing. A forfeit clause with
no money held is a **claim**, not a remedy — the wronged party must chase, and chasing is the
workaround `meta/personas.md` documents as the current state. With money held, forfeit is
arithmetic; without it, forfeit is litigation.

This is why index Q-01 (escrow vs direct payment) is the sub-domain's highest-consequence question:
it determines whether this whole sub-domain computes an outcome or merely records an opinion.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the deposit is their protection against a promoter who vanishes — the artist has
  already turned down other dates.
- **Operator**: the deposit is their exposure; cancelling after paying it is what the tier ladder
  prices.
- **Producer**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected — fan refunds are domain 19 (17.05.03 D-03).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The deposit record is the shared state. If the platform holds it,
   ownership is unambiguous; if paid directly, the platform holds only an *assertion* that it was
   paid, which either side can dispute.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cancel → evaluate tier → allocate the held deposit. If nothing is held, the
   chain terminates in an invoice nobody has to honour.
3. **Permission intersection**: Neither party may unilaterally release a held deposit — which is the
   point, and which is exactly what makes the platform a money-transmitter for regulatory purposes.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Allocation notifies both principals and, when commission is affected,
   the agent (17.08.02).
5. **State transition conflict**: A cancellation landing while a deposit payment is in flight. Rare,
   real, money-relevant; `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

### CX-02: Balance Schedule & Reminders ↔ Cancellation Tiers & Forfeit

**Relationship**: Forfeit needs two facts the schedule owns: **how much has been paid** and **how
close to the date we are**. A cancellation 30 days out after two instalments allocates completely
differently from the same cancellation with only a deposit down. The tier ladder reads the schedule;
it does not keep its own record.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: their payment history against the schedule is their standing when things go wrong.
- **Musician**: sees what has landed and what has not — and a missed instalment is itself a signal
  the show may be in trouble.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The schedule is the single record; cancellation reads it. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cancel → read paid-to-date + days-to-show → tier → allocation. Deterministic.
3. **Permission intersection**: None beyond the principals.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A missed instalment should arguably notify the *artist*, not just remind
   the Operator — it is early warning that the promoter is in trouble. Unmodelled (17.05.02 Q-01).
5. **State transition conflict**: A show cancelled on the same day an instalment falls due. Which
   applies is a real question with a real answer that nobody has written down.

### CX-03: Force Majeure ↔ Cancellation Tiers & Forfeit

**Relationship**: FM is an **override**, not a tier. A normal cancellation allocates forfeit by
proximity to the date; FM typically voids obligations in both directions and returns the deposit
regardless of proximity. Modelling FM as "tier 0" would force it through allocation logic it
structurally rejects (index D-02).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician** / **Operator**: either may declare, and the declaration is adversarial in exactly the
  cases that matter — one side's force majeure is the other side's excuse.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both sides may declare FM over the same show, for different reasons,
   with different desired outcomes. There is no merge — this is a dispute (domain 24).
2. **Trigger chain**: Declare → (accepted → void obligations | contested → dispute). The contested
   branch leaves the sub-domain entirely.
3. **Permission intersection**: Declaring FM is not the same as FM applying. The declaration is a
   claim; acceptance is the counterparty's.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Urgent both ways, plus 19 (fan refunds) and 18 (production standing
   down) if accepted.
5. **State transition conflict**: A cancellation already processed under the tier ladder,
   subsequently reclassified as FM. Money has moved; unwinding is a compensating action.

### CX-04: Deposit Invoice & Collection ↔ Balance Schedule & Reminders

**Relationship**: The deposit is **instalment zero**. "50% on signature, balance on the night" is one
schedule with two rows, not a deposit plus a separate arrangement. Modelling them as different
mechanisms would put the same money in two places and let them disagree.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: sees one obligation, not two systems.
- **Musician**: sees one expected inflow.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: High confidence on the relationship. The remaining detail —
whether the deposit's collection mechanics differ from a later instalment's (they often do; the
deposit gates announce and later instalments do not) — is `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5
deepening]`.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Force Majeure | Deposit Invoice & Collection | Rejected as a direct edge. FM does not interact with the deposit *mechanism* — it changes the **allocation rule**, and allocation is 17.05.03's job (CX-03 → CX-01). A direct edge would imply FM can return a deposit without passing through forfeit computation, which would let the two produce contradictory allocations for the same show. |
| R-02 | Force Majeure | Balance Schedule & Reminders | Rejected. FM stops the schedule as a **consequence** of voiding the obligations, not as an interaction with the schedule itself. The schedule has no FM-specific behaviour to model — it simply has nothing left to collect. Modelling this edge would add a state to the schedule that duplicates 17.05.04's outcome. |
| R-03 | Deposit Invoice & Collection | Balance Schedule (as separate money records) | Rejected as a *distinction*, which is the inverse of a rejected pair but belongs here: they are not two things that interact, they are **one schedule** (CX-04). Recorded so a later reader does not "discover" the deposit and the balance as separate ledgers and reintroduce the split. |
