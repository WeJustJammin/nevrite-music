# Feature: Balance Schedule & Payment Reminders

> **Number**: 17.05.02
> **Parent**: [Deposits, Balances & Cancellation](./17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The full payment timeline of a booking — deposit, any interim instalments, and the balance due on the
night — plus the reminders that stop it being chased by phone. The schedule is the single record of
what has been paid and when, which makes it the input cancellation forfeit reads (CX-02) and the raw
material of the payment-reliability record (17.12).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what is owed and when, and what has actually landed. Chases via their agent. **A missed instalment is early warning that the promoter is in trouble** — arguably the most valuable signal in this feature, and currently it only notifies the person who missed the payment (Q-01). |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Owns the schedule and the payment obligation. Receives the reminders. In the standard deal they are the payer; on venue-hire deals they are the chaser (17.05.01 Q-02). |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

One schedule, one ledger. The deposit is row zero (CX-04). Terms come from the contract (17.04); the
money moves on the payments cross-cut.

The balance is characteristically **due on the night** — which is what makes live different from
almost every other commerce flow the platform has. The final payment happens in a back room, in
person, often against a settlement sheet being computed at the same moment (17.09.01). The schedule
does not close in a billing system; it closes at 1am with a handshake.

Reminders are the feature's actual work. The Operator persona's documented reality is chasing
deposits manually; the artist's is not knowing whether the money is coming.

### Happy Path

1. Contract executes (17.04) with a payment schedule — e.g. 50% on signature, balance on the night.
2. System writes the schedule; the deposit becomes due (17.05.01).
3. Reminders fire ahead of each due date to the paying side.
4. Each payment lands and is recorded against its row.
5. The balance row closes at settlement (17.09.01), where the number owed may be computed on the spot
   rather than known in advance.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Instalment missed | The show is now at risk and the artist does not know | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — notifying the artist is arguably the point (Q-01) |
| Balance due "on the night" against a settlement still being computed | The normal case, not an edge case — the amount is not knowable until the count is | Row closes from 17.09.01's output, not from a pre-set figure |
| Balance paid in cash at the venue | Extremely common and invisible to the platform | Must be recordable as an assertion; see 17.09.01 |
| Show cancelled mid-schedule | Remaining rows void; paid rows feed forfeit (CX-02) | Handled by 17.05.03 |
| Cancellation on the same day an instalment falls due | Which applies is a real question nobody has written down | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (CX-02) |
| Schedule amended after contract amendment (17.04) | Terms changed; the schedule must follow | Amendment chain; `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Scheduled | Contract executed | All rows with amounts and due dates |
| Partially paid | Some rows settled | Paid-to-date and outstanding |
| Overdue | A row passed its due date unpaid | Escalating reminders; show flagged at risk |
| Open balance | All rows paid except the on-the-night balance | Awaiting settlement to compute the final figure |
| Closed | Balance settled | Complete payment history; feeds 17.12 |
| Voided | Show cancelled or FM declared | Remaining rows void; paid rows feed 17.05.03 / 17.05.04 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The balance is a known amount with a due date — this is invoicing | Standard billing modelling | ❌ REJECTED: **the balance due on the night is usually not knowable in advance.** On a versus or door deal the final figure is computed at settlement from the count, in a back room, at 1am (17.09.01). A schedule that requires an amount per row cannot represent the single most important row in live music. The row must be able to say "whatever settlement computes" — which is a genuinely different data model from an invoice. |
| DT-02 | Reminders are a notification feature | Feature-classification instinct | ❌ REJECTED as *merely* that. The reminder's real value is **asymmetric and currently mis-aimed**: reminding the Operator they owe money is polite; telling the *artist* that an instalment was missed is materially valuable, because it is early warning that the show may collapse while there is still time to react. The sweep's candidate list, and the obvious design, both aim the reminder at the payer only. Aiming it at both sides converts a chore-reducer into a risk signal — and feeds 17.12 (Q-01). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A schedule row may defer its amount to settlement | The on-the-night balance on a versus or door deal is not knowable in advance; requiring an amount per row cannot model live music's most important payment (DT-01). |
| D-02 | One schedule includes the deposit as row zero | Splitting deposit and balance into two ledgers lets the same money be recorded twice and disagree (CX-04). |
| D-03 | Off-platform and cash payments must be recordable as assertions | The balance is routinely paid in cash at the venue; a model that cannot represent this is a model of a different industry. |
