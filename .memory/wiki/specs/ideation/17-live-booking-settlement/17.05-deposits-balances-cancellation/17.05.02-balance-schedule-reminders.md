# Feature: Balance Schedule & Payment Reminders

> **Number**: 17.05.02
> **Parent**: [Deposits, Balances & Cancellation](./17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The full payment timeline of a booking — deposit, any interim instalments, and the balance due on the
night — plus the reminders that stop it being chased by phone. The schedule is the single record of
what has been paid and when, which makes it the input cancellation forfeit reads (CX-02) and the raw
material of the payment-reliability record (17.12). It replaces the Operator's documented reality of
chasing deposits manually and the artist's reality of not knowing whether the money is coming.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what is owed and when, and what has actually landed against each row. Cannot edit the schedule (its terms come from the contract, 17.04). Chases via their agent, not by editing rows. **A missed instalment is early warning that the promoter is in trouble** — arguably the most valuable signal in this feature; whether it reaches the artist and not only the payer is the live product question (Q-01). |
| Producer | None | No role. Studio/session payment schedules, where they exist, belong to domain 16's room hire, not to a live-booking settlement schedule. |
| Operator | Full | Owns the schedule and the payment obligation. Receives the reminders. In the standard deal they are the payer; on venue-hire deals they are the chaser (17.05.01 Q-02), so which side receives which reminder follows the deal's payment direction, not a fixed assumption. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. The fan's money is ticket revenue (domain 19) and has no relationship to the settlement schedule. |

## Behavior

One schedule, one ledger. The deposit is row zero (CX-04). Terms come from the contract (17.04); the
money moves on the payments cross-cut. Each row carries a due date and either a fixed amount or a
marker that its amount defers to settlement (D-01).

The balance is characteristically **due on the night** — which is what makes live different from
almost every other commerce flow the platform has. The final payment happens in a back room, in
person, often against a settlement sheet being computed at the same moment (17.09.01). The schedule
does not close in a billing system; it closes at 1am with a handshake.

Reminders are the feature's actual work. Each pre-night row generates reminders on a fixed cadence
ahead of its due date and escalating reminders once it passes due unpaid. The on-the-night balance
row is the exception — it cannot be dunned on a schedule because its amount is not knowable in advance
and it is settled in person; it is handed to settlement (17.09.01) rather than reminded (DT-03).

A reminder is aimed by the deal's payment direction: it goes to the side that owes. The unresolved
product question is whether a *missed* row also notifies the counterparty — telling the artist their
promoter defaulted (Q-01, DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. Contract executes (17.04) with a payment schedule — e.g. 50% on signature, balance on the night.
2. System writes the schedule; the deposit becomes due (17.05.01) as row zero.
3. Reminders fire ahead of each due date to the paying side on the standing cadence.
4. Each payment lands and is recorded against its row; that row's pending reminders are suppressed.
5. The balance row closes at settlement (17.09.01), where the number owed may be computed on the spot
   rather than known in advance.
6. With all rows closed, the schedule closes and feeds the payment-reliability record (17.12).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Instalment missed (row passes due unpaid) | Row moves to Overdue; escalating reminders fire to the payer; the show is flagged at risk | Payer sees escalating reminders; the show carries an at-risk flag. Whether the artist is also notified is Q-01 — the recommended design (DT-02) is yes |
| Payment lands while a reminder for that row is in flight / just before it fires | The row is recorded paid; any queued reminder for that row is cancelled so nobody is dunned for money already received | No spurious reminder; the row shows Paid (D-04) |
| Balance due "on the night" against a settlement still being computed | The normal case, not an edge case — the amount is not knowable until the count is | Row shows "amount at settlement"; it closes from 17.09.01's output, not from a pre-set figure |
| Balance (or any row) paid in cash / off-platform at the venue | Extremely common and invisible to the platform's money rails | Recordable as an assertion by either side, disputable, unenforceable (D-03); see 17.09.01 |
| Contract has no explicit schedule (single payment on the night) | Valid; the schedule is deposit row (if any) plus one settlement row | Minimal schedule, not an empty/error state |
| Show cancelled mid-schedule | Remaining unpaid rows void; paid rows feed forfeit (CX-02) | Remaining rows show Voided; paid rows flow to 17.05.03 |
| Reminder fatigue / payer opts out of nudges | Pre-due nudges are suppressible per payer preference; the overdue risk escalation is **not** suppressible because it protects the counterparty | Payer controls pre-due reminders only; overdue escalation always fires |
| Cancellation and an instalment due date fall on the same day | Which applies — the forfeit tier or the instalment obligation — is a genuine ordering question | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` (Q-02, CX-02) |
| Contract amended (17.04) after rows are already paid | Terms changed; the schedule must follow without losing the paid history | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` — amendment chain must reconcile paid rows against new terms (Q-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Scheduled | Contract executed | All rows with amounts (or "at settlement") and due dates |
| Partially paid | Some rows settled | Paid-to-date and outstanding, per row |
| Overdue | A row passed its due date unpaid | Escalating reminders; show flagged at risk |
| Open balance | All rows paid except the on-the-night balance | Awaiting settlement to compute the final figure |
| Closed | Balance settled | Complete payment history; feeds 17.12 |
| Voided | Show cancelled or FM declared | Remaining rows void; paid rows feed 17.05.03 / 17.05.04 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The balance is a known amount with a due date — this is invoicing | Standard billing modelling | ❌ REJECTED: **the balance due on the night is usually not knowable in advance.** On a versus or door deal the final figure is computed at settlement from the count, in a back room, at 1am (17.09.01). A schedule that requires an amount per row cannot represent the single most important row in live music. The row must be able to say "whatever settlement computes" — a genuinely different data model from an invoice. |
| DT-02 | Reminders are a notification feature | Feature-classification instinct | ❌ REJECTED as *merely* that. The reminder's real value is **asymmetric and currently mis-aimed**: reminding the Operator they owe money is polite; telling the *artist* that an instalment was missed is materially valuable, because it is early warning that the show may collapse while there is still time to react. The obvious design aims the reminder at the payer only. Aiming the *miss* at both sides converts a chore-reducer into a risk signal — and feeds 17.12 (Q-01). |
| DT-03 | Reminders should escalate in frequency as the due date nears — standard dunning | SaaS billing best practice | ❌ REJECTED as a whole-schedule model. Dunning fits **pre-night instalments** (fixed amount, fixed date, on-rail collection). It does **not** fit the on-the-night balance: no fixed amount to dun, settled in person, and a "you owe X, pay now" nudge at 1am is meaningless. The night row is a **handoff to settlement (17.09.01)**, not a reminder target. Applying one cadence uniformly would model the wrong industry — the schedule needs two reminder regimes, not one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A schedule row may defer its amount to settlement | The on-the-night balance on a versus or door deal is not knowable in advance; requiring an amount per row cannot model live music's most important payment (DT-01). |
| D-02 | One schedule includes the deposit as row zero | Splitting deposit and balance into two ledgers lets the same money be recorded twice and disagree (CX-04). |
| D-03 | Off-platform and cash payments must be recordable as assertions | The balance is routinely paid in cash at the venue; a model that cannot represent this is a model of a different industry. |
| D-04 | A payment against a row suppresses that row's pending reminders | A payer dunned for money already received loses trust in the reminder; the reminder must reflect current ledger state, not the state when it was queued. |
| D-05 | Overdue risk escalation is not suppressible even when pre-due nudges are | Pre-due nudges serve the payer's convenience and may be muted; overdue escalation protects the counterparty's show and must fire regardless of the payer's preference. |
