# Feature: Cancellation Tiers & Forfeit Computation

> **Number**: 17.05.03
> **Parent**: [Deposits, Balances & Cancellation](./17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Who keeps what when a show dies. A tiered ladder priced by proximity to the date — cancel six months
out and it costs little; cancel on the day and the deposit is gone and more may be owed — computed
against what was actually paid and when (CX-02). It turns "we'll sort it out" into arithmetic.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Cancelling artist forfeits; cancelled-on artist collects. Sees the cost of cancelling **before** confirming the cancellation — an artist who takes a better offer needs to know the old date costs £800 to walk away from, not discover it later. |
| Producer | None | No role here. Studio deposits exist but belong to domain 16's room hire, not this live-booking ladder (parent index Role Matrix). A Producer only touches cancellation forfeit when acting *as* an Operator (renting their room) — and is then wearing the Operator lens, per the multi-hyphenate model (personas.md). |
| Operator | Full | Symmetrically exposed. A promoter cancelling a slow-selling show pays the ladder. `meta/personas.md` names their worst intentional behaviour as "keeps deposits on cancellations outside the stated policy" — a computed, contract-derived ladder is the direct countermeasure. |
| Fan | None | **Fan refunds are domain 19's product, not this feature's** (D-03). This computes what the principals owe each other; the fan's ticket money is a different pool with different rules and different statutory protection. Conflating them is the most consequential boundary error available here. |

## Behavior

Cancellation terms come from the contract (17.04) as a tiered ladder. Forfeit computation needs two
facts, both owned by the schedule (17.05.02): **how much has been paid** and **how many days to the
show**. It reads them; it does not keep its own copy.

The ladder is symmetric in structure and asymmetric in practice — who cancelled decides which
direction money flows.

Force majeure is **not** a tier on this ladder (index D-02). It overrides the ladder entirely
(CX-03).

The deposit is what gives this feature teeth (CX-01). Without money held, a forfeit is a claim to
chase, and chasing is the status quo the platform exists to replace.

### Happy Path

1. A party cancels a confirmed show.
2. System reads the contract's tier ladder, days-to-show, and paid-to-date from the schedule.
3. Forfeit is computed and shown to the cancelling party **before** they commit — with the number,
   the tier that produced it, and the contract clause it came from.
4. Cancellation is confirmed; allocation executes against the held deposit (subject to index Q-01).
5. Both principals are notified; the date returns to avails (17.01.01); 19 and 18 stand down; the
   event feeds 17.12's reliability record.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Permission** — a non-principal (booking agent, support act, or fan) tries to trigger cancellation | Only the two named principals on the contract may cancel. An agent acting *for* a principal inherits that principal's authority; anyone else is refused | Action unavailable; if attempted via a stale link, "Only a party to this booking can cancel it" |
| **Empty** — nothing has been paid yet when a party cancels | The ladder still computes a forfeit from days-to-show, but there is no held deposit to allocate against, so the figure is a claim to invoice, not a transfer (CX-01) | Forfeit shown with an explicit "unfunded — this is owed, not yet held" flag; enforceability depends on index Q-01 |
| **Concurrency** — both principals hit cancel within the same window | "Who cancelled first" must be resolved by an authoritative server timestamp, not UI ordering; a dead heat routes to the agreed-cancellation path (D-04) rather than charging one side by accident | The later click is told "the other party has already opened a cancellation"; simultaneous → agreed-cancellation prompt |
| Cancellation after announce, tickets sold | Two separate money problems: principal forfeit (here) and fan refunds (domain 19). **They must not be conflated** | Both surfaced, computed separately, from different pools |
| Cancelled show sits inside a cross-collateralized run (17.03.03) | Its economics are entangled with 11 other dates; independent forfeit may be meaningless | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` (index Q-02) |
| Deposit was paid off-platform | Forfeit is an invoice nobody must honour (CX-01) | Computed and asserted; enforceability depends on index Q-01 |
| Both parties want to cancel and each blames the other | Whoever formally cancels first pays the ladder — a perverse race (DT-03). The mitigation is D-04: either side can propose agreed cancellation instead of racing to cancel | Offered the agreed-cancellation path as the escape from the standoff; ladder-cancel remains available but priced |
| Mutual cancellation by agreement | Common and sensible; the ladder does not apply. Handled by the explicit agreed-cancellation path (D-04), which settles on agreed terms — deposit returned, split, or forfeited as the parties decide | An agreed-cancellation flow, not a ladder computation; both principals must accept the terms |
| Cancellation reclassified as FM afterwards | Money already moved; unwinding is compensating, not rollback | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` (CX-03) |
| Support act's booking dies when the headline show cancels | Downstream cascade the ladder does not model | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth]` — see 17.14 (index Q-03) |
| Abuse — Operator cancels then stalls allocation by pre-emptively disputing | The forfeit is computed and asserted regardless; contesting the computation moves the case to domain 24, but does not let the cancelling party escape the ladder by manufacturing a dispute | "Forfeit stands pending dispute resolution"; funds held, not released to either side, until 24 rules |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Cancellation forfeit and fan refunds are the same problem | Superficially both are "money back when a show dies" | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most consequential rejection in the sub-domain. They are **different pools, different rules, different law**. Principal forfeit is contract between two businesses; fan refunds are consumer protection with statutory force (`meta/constraints.md` flags consumer protection and distance selling as `[PENDING]`). A promoter can forfeit a deposit and still owe every fan a full refund, funded from ticket revenue that may already be spent. Modelling them together would produce a system that nets a consumer's statutory refund against a commercial forfeit — which is both wrong and unlawful. Domain 19 owns fan refunds. |
| DT-02 | The tier ladder is priced by proximity to the date | Standard industry structure and the obvious model | ✅ CONFIRMED, but **incomplete**. Proximity is the ladder's axis, and the reason it works is that proximity proxies for *irrecoverable spend* — at 6 months nothing is committed; at 2 weeks the marketing is spent, the crew is booked, and the fans have made plans. The proxy breaks on shows with unusual cost curves (a festival slot's costs land differently from a club date's). Proximity is right for v1 and will be wrong for someone; noting it rather than over-engineering. |
| DT-03 | The ladder is a fair, symmetric mechanism | Its structural symmetry | ❌ REJECTED as *fair*: it creates a **perverse race**. When a show is clearly dying and both sides know it, whoever formally cancels first pays the ladder — so both sides stall, hoping the other blinks, while the fans hold tickets and the crew holds the date. A domain expert would recognise this standoff immediately; it is why "mutual cancellation by agreement" exists as an industry norm. **The product must offer an explicit agreed-cancellation path**, or it will faithfully automate a game of chicken. Nothing in the sweep's candidates suggests this. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Forfeit is previewed before the cancellation is committed | A party deciding whether to cancel must know the price at the moment of the decision, not after it. |
| D-02 | The ladder reads the schedule; it keeps no separate payment record | Two records of the same money will disagree, and will disagree exactly when contested (CX-02). |
| D-03 | **Fan refunds are domain 19's, not this feature's** | Different pool, different rules, statutory force (DT-01). Conflating them nets a consumer's statutory refund against a commercial forfeit — wrong and unlawful. |
| D-04 | An explicit agreed-cancellation path exists outside the ladder | Without one the product automates a game of chicken (DT-03). |
