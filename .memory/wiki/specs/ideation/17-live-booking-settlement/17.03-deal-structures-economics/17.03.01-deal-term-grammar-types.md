# Feature: Deal Term Grammar & Deal Types

> **Number**: 17.03.01
> **Parent**: [Deal Structures & Economics](./17.03-deal-structures-economics-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The typed vocabulary of live money: flat guarantee, door deal, guarantee-versus-percentage,
plus-bonus, walkout potential, buyout, and the qualifying terms (expense cap, comp allowance, merch
cut, deduction order) that decide what "net" actually means. **This grammar is the settlement
formula.** Everything the domain claims about automating settlement rests on this file being typed
data rather than a paragraph in an email.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Selects and parameterises a structure when self-booking; reads their deal rendered as outcomes rather than as an expression. Does **not** author new deal types — the grammar is platform vocabulary. The Musician's real need here is comprehension: `meta/personas.md` names "signs a split sheet they don't understand" as their worst accidental behaviour, and a versus deal is the live equivalent. |
| Producer | None | No role in live deal economics. |
| Operator | Read-only | Selects a deal type and fills its parameters — guarantee amount, split percentage, expense cap, bonus thresholds, deduction order. Cannot mint new deal types (D-02). Their leverage is in the qualifying terms, not the headline: the expense cap and the deduction order decide the money far more often than the percentage does. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. Deal terms are commercially confidential between the principals. |

## Behavior

A deal is an **expression**, not a description. `$2,000 versus 85% of net after $6,000 expenses,
whichever is greater` parses into an evaluable structure with a defined deduction order awaiting one
input: the ticket count. That is the entire domain thesis in one line.

The grammar's hard part is not the headline deal types — those are a short list. It is the
**qualifying terms and their order of operations**: what counts as gross, which expenses are
deductible, whether the cap is per-line or aggregate, whether comps reduce the count, whether the
support act's fee comes off the top or off the promoter's side. Two parties can agree on "85% of net
after $6,000" and still disagree by four figures because they resolve the order differently — and
that disagreement is exactly what the 1am back-office settlement argument is made of.

### Happy Path

1. Operator selects a deal type from the grammar while composing an offer (17.02.01).
2. System presents that type's required parameters and its qualifying terms with an explicit default
   deduction order.
3. Operator fills the parameters; the deduction order is shown, not hidden, because it is where the
   money is.
4. System compiles the terms to an evaluable expression stored on the offer.
5. That same expression is later evaluated against real counts by 17.09.01 — no re-entry, no
   re-interpretation, no calculator.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two parties resolve deduction order differently | The classic four-figure settlement argument | Order is explicit on the offer and rendered as worked outcomes (17.02.01 D-02), not left implicit |
| A term is written as free text instead of grammar | Settlement silently cannot see it — an Operator writes "plus £500 if it sells out" in the notes and the formula never knows | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the sharpest unresolved risk (17.02.01 Q-02) |
| Deal type exists in the industry but not in the grammar | Users fall back to free text, and the domain's automation claim quietly fails for that deal | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — this is the cost of a closed vocabulary (index Q-01) |
| Territory-specific convention (US door deal vs UK versus vs EU buyout) | Different vocabularies, not different parameters | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (index Q-02) |
| Currency differs from the settlement currency | A €-denominated guarantee settled in £ at the show — the FX rate and its date are deal terms nobody writes down | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — see DT-03 |
| Bonus threshold references a count the box office defines differently | Grammar says "sellout"; the box office has a manifest, comps, and holds | Requires 19's count definitions; `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The hard part is enumerating the deal types | Surface reading of the domain index's list (guarantee, door split, versus, breakeven, bonus, walkout) | ❌ REJECTED: the headline types are a short, well-known list. The hard part is the **order of operations on the qualifying terms** — what counts as gross, which expenses deduct, whether the cap is per-line or aggregate, whether the support fee comes off the top. Two parties agreeing on "85% of net after $6,000" routinely differ by four figures because they resolve the order differently. **The 1am settlement argument is almost never about the deal type.** |
| DT-02 | Users should be able to define their own deal types | Long-tail coverage instinct; the industry has endless bespoke structures | ❌ REJECTED **for now**, with discomfort. An open expression language covers the long tail and is a spreadsheet with extra steps — it destroys the ability to compare deals, to model reliably, and to reason about settlement, which is the entire product. A closed vocabulary is safe and will not cover every deal. This is genuinely a product decision (index Q-01) and the agent should not make it: it sets the ceiling on the domain's automation claim. |
| DT-03 | The grammar is currency-agnostic | Implicit assumption in every deal-type description | ❌ REJECTED: an international booking has a €-denominated guarantee settled in £ on the night, and **the FX rate and its fixing date are deal terms that nobody writes down** — they are argued about at settlement instead. A grammar that assumes one currency will silently produce wrong numbers on exactly the deals where the stakes are highest. A domain expert would expect the rate basis to be an explicit term. Nothing in the sweep's 18 candidates mentions FX. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The deal structure is an evaluable expression, not a description | This is the domain's founding insight (domain index D-01): settlement is only computable if the offer was structured data. |
| D-02 | Neither principal authors new deal types; the grammar is platform vocabulary | Open authorship is an expression language, which is a spreadsheet and destroys comparison, modelling and settlement (DT-02). Recorded as a decision to be **re-opened** by the owner via index Q-01, not as a closed matter. |
| D-03 | Deduction order is explicit and surfaced, never implicit | It is where the money actually is (DT-01), and hiding it recreates the 1am argument the domain exists to prevent. |
