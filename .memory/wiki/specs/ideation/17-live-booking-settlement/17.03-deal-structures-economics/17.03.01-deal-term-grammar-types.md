# Feature: Deal Term Grammar & Deal Types

> **Number**: 17.03.01
> **Parent**: [Deal Structures & Economics](./17.03-deal-structures-economics-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The typed vocabulary of live money: the deal types (flat guarantee, door deal,
guarantee-versus-percentage, guarantee-plus-percentage, buyout, plus-bonus), and the qualifying terms
(gross basis, expense cap, promoter profit, comp allowance, deduction order, merch cut, FX basis) that
decide what "net" actually means.

**This grammar is the settlement formula.** Everything domain 17 claims about automating settlement —
the 1am back-office argument dissolved, the artist arriving with the same arithmetic as the promoter —
rests on this file being typed data rather than a paragraph in an email. It is the domain's keystone:
17.03.02 can only model what this can express, and 17.09.01 can only compute what this compiled.

The problem it solves is not "what is the fee". It is that **two parties can agree on a sentence and
disagree by four figures**, because the sentence never said what "gross" meant, in what order things
came off, or which count "sellout" referred to.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Read-only | **Selects and parameterises** a structure when self-booking their own date (the pub gig with no agent — 17.02.01 Role Lens); **receives** it, otherwise. Never authors a new deal type — the grammar is platform vocabulary (D-02). Their view of a deal is deliberately not the expression: they see the deduction order as a worked ladder and the outcome at attendance levels (17.02.01 D-02). This is the countermeasure to `meta/personas.md`'s named worst-accidental behaviour — "signs a split sheet they don't understand" — whose live equivalent is agreeing to "85% of net after $6,000" without knowing that the facility fee, the promoter profit and the support cost all come off before the 85% is applied to anything. They **cannot** see the Operator's private cost assumptions (17.03.02 D-02), and the Operator **cannot** see their agent commission term (17.08). |
| **Producer** | None | No role in live deal economics. The Producer persona owns the studio room, not the stage (domain index, Role Matrix note 1); a Producer negotiating a live fee is acting as a **Musician**, and a Producer booking a room is acting as an **Operator**. Neither lens is "Producer" here. Studio-time pricing is domain 16 and has a different grammar entirely. |
| **Operator** | Read-only | Selects the deal type and fills its parameters — guarantee, split percentage, gross basis, expense cap, promoter profit, bonus thresholds, comp allowance, merch rates, deduction order. Cannot mint new deal types (D-02); can file a **grammar gap report** when their structure is not expressible (D-06). Room templates (17.02.01 D-03) pre-fill the territory profile and the house-standard qualifying terms, so the hundredth deal costs minutes. Their leverage is in the qualifying terms, not the headline: **the gross basis and the deduction order decide the money far more often than the percentage does** (DT-01, DT-05). |
| **Fan** | None | Never visible, at any state, including after settlement. Deal terms are commercially confidential between the principals, and a leaked guarantee damages the artist's next negotiation in every other market. The Fan is nonetheless the origin of every number in the expression — they buy the ticket that is the gross — but they never see the arithmetic (domain index, Role Matrix note 2). Nothing in this feature has a public surface. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None
>
> **Why both principals are Read-only**: "Read-only" scopes the **grammar**, not the deal. Both sides
> write deal *values* freely; neither writes deal *vocabulary*. Full authorship would mean users
> defining arbitrary formulas, which is a spreadsheet (D-02, sub-domain R-01).

## Behavior

A deal is an **expression**, not a description. `$2,000 versus 85% of net after $6,000 of expenses,
whichever is greater` parses into an evaluable structure with a defined deduction order, awaiting one
input: the count. That is the entire domain thesis in one line, and it is why booking and settlement
are one domain (domain index D-01).

### What the grammar is made of

A compiled deal expression has exactly five parts. Every one of them is required; there is no
"unspecified" state that reaches an offer.

| Part | What it fixes | Why it cannot be omitted |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Deal type** | The shape of the payout function | Selected from the closed vocabulary (D-02) |
| **2. Parameters** | The numbers in that function — guarantee, split %, thresholds | Meaningless without the type |
| **3. Gross basis** | What the percentage is a percentage **of** (DT-05) | "85% of net" names no number until this is fixed |
| **4. Deduction ladder** | Which lines come off, in what order, subject to what caps | Where the money actually is (DT-01, D-03) |
| **5. Scope + basis terms** | Show or run (17.03.03 CX-02); currency, FX basis, territory profile, grammar version | A term with no scope has no meaning on a 12-date run |

### The deal types — the closed vocabulary

These are the shapes. Every one is parameterised, not free-form.

| Type | Payout function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat guarantee** | `guarantee` | Independent of the count. Settlement still runs — deposits, deductions and merch land on it. |
| **Door deal** | `door_percentage × gross_basis` | No floor. The artist carries the risk. Ubiquitous at the working-musician tier. |
| **Guarantee vs percentage** | `max(guarantee, split_share)` | The classic. **The artist gets one or the other, never both.** |
| **Guarantee plus percentage** | `guarantee + split_share` | Materially different from *versus* and routinely confused with it in prose (DT-11). |
| **Buyout (deal)** | `all_in_fee` | Artist covers their own costs from it. EU/festival convention. **Not the same word as a rider buyout** (DT-06). |
| **Plus-bonus** | `base + Σ(bonus_i where threshold_i met)` | Thresholds are count references, never adjectives (D-07). |
| **Percentage of overage (backend)** | `artist_share × (adjusted_gross − nut − promoter_profit)` | The US split-point structure. Requires the promoter-profit term (DT-07). |

Composition is bounded: a deal is one type plus its qualifying terms. **A deal is not a formula the
user builds** — that is the open-vocabulary design D-02 rejects.

### The qualifying terms — where the arguments actually live

| Term | Values | Default source |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross basis** | Which of {face value, sales tax/VAT, facility fee, ticketing/service charge, VIP/upsell premium, per-scale carve-outs} sit **before** the percentage applies (DT-05) | Territory profile |
| **Expense cap** | Amount + `per_line` \| `aggregate` — these are not interchangeable and produce different numbers on identical expenses | Deal, no default |
| **Promoter profit** | Amount or % of the nut. US convention: 15% of the nut, deducted before the split point (DT-07) | Territory profile |
| **Comp allowance** | `N` comps free; comps above `N` count as sold at a named scale price (D-07) | Room template (16) |
| **Support cost** | `off_the_top` \| `promoter_side` — decides whether the artist funds their own opener | Deal, no default |
| **Merch cut** | Its own sub-grammar: soft rate, hard rate, with/without seller, exemptions, basis (17.09.04) | Room template + territory profile |
| **Deduction order** | The ordered ladder below; every deal carries a concrete one | Territory profile |
| **FX basis** | Denomination currency, settlement currency, rate basis (D-05) | Territory profile |
| **Curfew / overtime penalty** | Amount + trigger — a logistics term with money attached | Room template (16) |
| **Radius clause** | No money; recorded as a term, enforced by 17.06 | Deal |

### The default deduction ladder

Every deal carries a **concrete, ordered, editable** ladder. It is written onto the deal at compile
time from the territory profile — never inherited by reference (D-04), because a profile that changes
in 2028 must not move a number signed in 2026.

The US-style versus/overage default, which is the most-argued ladder in live music:

1. Gross ticket sales at face value, **summed per scale** (not price × count — scales differ)
2. Less sales tax / VAT, **if** the gross basis says net-of-tax
3. Less facility fee, ticketing/service charges, per-scale carve-outs → **adjusted gross**
4. Less the nut — documented show expenses, each subject to the cap and its `per_line`/`aggregate` mode
5. Less promoter profit (convention: 15% of the nut)
6. = **Overage**, split at the deal's percentages (convention: 85% artist / 15% promoter)
7. Artist figure = the deal type's payout function applied to the above
8. Less support cost, **if** `off_the_top`
9. Less deposits already paid (17.05.02) and any forfeit (17.05.03)
10. → the settlement figure, **signed** (D-08)

Steps 9–10 sit outside the deal's own arithmetic but inside the sheet's. Withholding (17.10.01) and
agent commission (17.08.02) are sequenced **after** this, on the artist's side, and the Operator never
sees step 11.

> **Every step is rendered, always.** Collapsing the ladder to a total is the status quo with better
> typography (17.09.01 DT-02) and recreates exactly the 1am argument this domain exists to prevent.

### The escape hatch, and what the grammar does about it

Free text cannot be banned — real offers carry genuinely non-economic bespoke terms ("no support act
may use the house drum kit"), and banning them drives users back to email and loses the whole deal
(17.02.01 DT-03). So the grammar does not ban it. It **detects economic content in it and forces an
acknowledgement** (D-09).

The detector is deliberately over-sensitive. **A false positive costs one click; a false negative
costs a settlement.**

### Grammar versioning

The platform will add deal types under a closed vocabulary — that is what D-06's gap-report loop is
for. Therefore: **a compiled expression pins the grammar version it was compiled under, and is always
evaluated under that pinned version, forever** (D-10). A settlement run in 2029 against a deal signed
in 2026 uses 2026 semantics. Grammar evolution is a settlement-correctness problem, not a product
roadmap problem.

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens an offer against a held date (17.02.01 step 1). The room template (16) supplies the
   territory profile, the house merch rates, the comp allowance and the curfew penalty.
2. System resolves the territory profile and **writes its defaults as concrete values onto the deal** —
   the deduction ladder, the gross basis, the promoter-profit convention, the denomination currency.
   Every one is shown as an editable line, flagged `from profile`.
3. Operator picks a deal type from the vocabulary. Each is presented in plain language with its
   payout function stated — **"versus: you get the guarantee *or* the percentage, whichever is
   greater, never both"** — because *versus* and *plus* are the pair users confuse (DT-11).
4. System presents that type's required parameters. Operator fills them: `guarantee = £2,000`,
   `artist_share = 85%`, `expense_cap = £6,000 aggregate`, `promoter_profit = 15% of nut`.
5. Operator sets the gross basis. The picker enumerates the room's actual pre-gross lines from 19 —
   this room's £2.50 facility fee, its 10% service charge, its 20% VAT — and the Operator ticks which
   sit before the percentage. **This step cannot be skipped**; "85% of net" names no number without it.
6. Operator adds a bonus: types "sellout"; the system refuses the word and offers the explicit count
   definitions from 19 with the number pre-filled from the room's manifest. Operator picks
   `tickets_sold_paid ≥ 400 → +£500` (D-07).
7. System renders the deduction ladder as a worked ladder at three attendance levels, with each step
   named and the versus comparison shown flipping. Operator drags step 8 (support cost) above step 4
   — the ladder is editable and the change is visible on the numbers immediately (D-03).
8. Operator writes "artist requires Tuesday load-in; no support act may use the house kit" in
   additional terms. The detector finds no economic content and does not interrupt.
9. Operator sends. System compiles the five parts to an evaluable expression, pins the grammar version,
   and stores it on the offer (D-01). Version v1 enters the negotiation thread (17.02.02).
10. Musician opens the offer. They see the same ladder, worked, at attendance levels — never the
    expression. Their agent commission term (17.08) appears on their view only.
11. Musician counters the merch cut; the counter recompiles a **new** expression at v2. v1 is retained
    (D-11).
12. Months later the show plays. 17.09.01 evaluates **that same pinned expression** against the real
    count. No re-entry, no re-interpretation, no calculator.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Two parties resolve deduction order differently** | Cannot occur post-compile — the order is a concrete ordered list on the deal, not a convention either party carries in their head | The ladder is rendered on the offer, worked, before either side agrees (D-03, 17.02.01 D-02) |
| **Economic term smuggled into free text** ("plus £500 if it sells out") | Detector fires at send: any free text containing a currency symbol, a number adjacent to `%`, or a money keyword (`fee`, `bonus`, `pay`, `cut`, `deduct`, `plus`, `less`, `owed`, `split`, `guarantee`) **blocks the send** | Blocking prompt: *"This note looks like it contains money. Settlement cannot see it."* → **[Move it into the deal]** (opens the term picker seeded with the matched text) or **[It's not economic — send anyway]**, which records an attributed, timestamped acknowledgement on the offer (D-09) |
| **Free text acknowledged as non-economic, but it was economic** | The leak survives — by the parties' own explicit choice. It is no longer silent | Settlement sheet's derivation carries a standing banner: *"This deal has 2 acknowledged free-text terms this computation does not include, acknowledged by [party] on [date]."* The sheet is honest about its own incompleteness (D-09) |
| **Detector false positive** ("no support act may use the house kit — £0 value") | Fires. One click to dismiss | The over-sensitivity is deliberate and is stated in the prompt copy |
| **Deal type exists in the industry but not in the vocabulary** | Offer is **not** blocked. Operator composes with the nearest type plus acknowledged free text, and files a structured **grammar gap report** naming the structure they wanted | *"My deal isn't here"* action in the picker → captures the attempted structure, the room, the territory, and what it should have paid. Gap reports are the vocabulary's roadmap (D-06) |
| **`Other` chosen as a deal type** | Not reachable — there is no `Other` type. `Other` is free text wearing a type's clothes and would convert a closed vocabulary into an open one by the back door (D-02, sub-domain R-01) | The picker has no such row |
| **Territory convention differs (US door vs UK versus vs EU buyout)** | Not different vocabularies — **one vocabulary, different defaults** (D-04). The types are near-universal; what differs is the gross basis, whether promoter profit is a standard line (US: yes; UK: usually not), tax treatment, hard-goods merch convention, and the currency | Territory profile named on the deal; every default it wrote is visible, flagged `from profile`, and editable |
| **Territory profile updated after a deal is signed** | The signed deal does not move. Profiles write **concrete values** at compile time and are never referenced at evaluation (D-04) | Nothing. The absence of an event is the requirement |
| **Currency ≠ settlement currency** (€ guarantee settled in £) | FX basis is a first-class term: denomination currency, settlement currency, and rate basis ∈ {fixed rate stated in the deal, reference rate fixed on a named date, rate on settlement date}. Unset → defaults to settlement-date reference rate, **rendered as an explicit line, never silently applied** (D-05) | Offer outcome view shows the artist's take in **both** currencies, naming the rate and its date. "We didn't discuss FX" is how a 6% swing becomes an argument |
| **Rate moves between offer and settlement** | Expected. If rate basis is `settlement_date`, the number moves and both sides agreed it would | The offer states the basis. The sheet names the applied rate and date in the derivation |
| **Bonus threshold says "sellout"** | Rejected at authoring. **"Sellout" is not a grammar token** — sellout of manifest, or of capacity less kills and holds? Paid, or including comps? It is precisely the word that produces the argument | The word is refused; explicit count definitions from 19 are offered with the number pre-filled from the room's manifest (D-07) |
| **Count reference has no 19-owned definition** (external box office) | The threshold must still name a definition, and that definition becomes an **asserted input** at settlement, not a platform-owned one | Threshold renders as *"per [Operator]'s reported count"*; the sheet marks the line asserted (17.09.01 DT-03) |
| **Gross basis left unset** | Blocking. The deal cannot compile: `85% of net` names no number | Blocking validation at step 5: *"Set what the 85% applies to. This deal cannot be settled without it."* |
| **Expense cap below the fixed expense lines already on the room template** | Not an error — an over-cap expense is captured-but-not-deductible (17.09.01), not rejected. But it is almost always a mistake at authoring time | Non-blocking warning naming the shortfall: *"Cap £4,000 is below this room's £5,200 of standard fixed costs. £1,200 will not be deductible."* |
| **Cap mode not stated** (`per_line` vs `aggregate`) | Blocking. The two produce different numbers on identical expenses and there is no safe default | Blocking validation naming both readings with the number each produces at the room's standard costs |
| **Promoter profit set with no nut** (flat guarantee + promoter profit) | Incoherent — promoter profit is a percentage of the nut and there is no split point on a flat deal | Blocking validation: *"Promoter profit applies to overage deals. This deal has no split point."* |
| **`versus` authored where `plus` was meant** | Silently produces a materially smaller number for the artist and nothing detects it — the terms are both valid | Mitigated, not solved: the picker states the payout function in plain language (Happy Path 3), and the ladder renders both at attendance levels so the difference is visible before agreeing (DT-11) |
| **Deal is part of a cross-collateralized run** | Terms carry a **scope** dimension. Run-scoped terms ("85% of the aggregate across 12 dates") have no per-show meaning at all; a grammar assuming every term resolves within one show is wrong (17.03.03 CX-02) | Scope is a required field on every term of a run deal; per-date renderings are labelled provisional (17.03.03 D-02) |
| **Run-scoped term on a single-show deal** | Blocking. `aggregate` has no referent | Blocking validation: *"This term needs a run. This deal is one date."* |
| **Settlement figure is negative** — artist owes the promoter | **Permitted and required.** A forfeited deposit (17.05.03), a 4-wall rental, consignment chargebacks, or expense over-recovery on a door deal can all net negative. A grammar typing the fee as unsigned is arithmetically wrong (D-08) | The ladder resolves to a signed figure; the sheet states direction explicitly (*"Artist owes Operator £340"*), never an unlabelled number |
| **Multi-hyphenate: Operator's own band takes the support slot** | Permitted; the deal is between two **parties**, not two personas (D-12). But the support-cost term now names a party who is the counterparty's own principal, and `off_the_top` means the headliner funds the promoter's band | The support-cost term names the party. The offer's outcome view shows the headliner what the support slot costs them, by name |
| **Multi-hyphenate: Musician self-books their own room** — both sides are one identity | Permitted (17.02.01 Role Lens). The grammar is unaffected; the *confidentiality* properties become vacuous — one person holds both sides' private cost assumptions (17.03.02 D-02) | Nothing hidden that the same identity already sees. Noted so downstream permission specs do not build a rule that cannot hold |
| **Operator edits deal terms after the offer is sent** | Cannot mutate v1. Any term change **recompiles a new expression** at a new version; the prior expression is retained in the negotiation thread (D-11, 17.02.02) | Prior version read-only in history; the counterparty sees a diff of terms, not a replaced document |
| **Terms amended post-signature via contract amendment (17.04)** | Recompiles against the **pinned grammar version** of the original, not the current one (D-10) | Settlement derivation names the contract version and the grammar version applied (17.09.01 edge case) |
| **Platform ships grammar v3 adding a facility-fee basis** | Every expression compiled at v1/v2 continues to evaluate at v1/v2 semantics, unchanged, forever (D-10) | Nothing moves. Legacy deals render with a `grammar v1` marker; new offers cannot be authored at a retired version |
| **Concurrent parameterisation** — Operator and their GM edit the same draft offer's terms simultaneously | Last-write-wins on a **deal expression** is a silently wrong number, not a lost paragraph | The draft is edit-locked to one editor at a time; the second sees who holds it. Escalated to 17.02.01's draft model as a cross-cut, since the draft is its entity |
| **Percentage of the bar take** ("if the bar takes £1,500 you get £200") | Expressible as a threshold, but the input — the bar take — comes from no box office and is asserted by the Operator with **zero verification**. This is a worse asymmetry than the count (DT-08) | Open Question Q-04. If admitted, the line must be labelled an unverified assertion on the sheet |
| **Ticket consignment / pay-to-play** ("here are 50 tickets — sell them or pay for them") | Structurally expressible (a negative bonus against `tickets_sold_by_artist`). Whether the platform *should* make it easy to express is a values decision, not a modelling one | Open Question Q-03 |
| **Split percentages sum above 100%** | Blocking | Blocking validation naming the sum |
| **Guarantee of zero** | Valid — a pure door deal with an explicit zero is more honest than an absent guarantee, and settles identically | Accepted without warning |
| **Malicious input: a 3,000-character "additional term"** | Detector runs over the whole field; length is not a bypass. Terms are typed values, never evaluated strings — there is no expression to inject into under a closed vocabulary (D-02) | Normal detector behaviour |
| **Rider buyout confused with deal buyout** | Two entirely different things sharing one industry word — an all-in £5,000 fee vs £150 cash in lieu of catering. A single `buyout` token conflates them (DT-06) | Namespaced in the UI as **Deal buyout** and **Rider buyout**; the rider buyout is a production term (18), not a deal type, and lands as a settlement line |
| **Deal references a capacity above the room's real capacity** | The potential gross is a lie and settlement will disagree with the offer | Validated against domain 16's room record (17.02.01 edge case) |
| **All four personas: Fan attempts to view deal terms** | No surface exists. Not a permission check that could be misconfigured — there is no rendering path | 404 / not reachable |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Empty / Unselected** | Offer composition started, no type chosen | Deal type picker; each row states its payout function in plain language. No parameters shown |
| **Loading** | Territory profile + room template + 19's pre-gross lines resolving (Happy Path 2, 5) | Skeleton of the parameter form. **The gross-basis picker does not render until 19's lines have resolved** — an empty basis picker would invite an unset basis, which is the blocking error at step 5 |
| **Partial / Parameterised** | Type selected, some parameters filled | Parameters with `from profile` flags on defaults; the deduction ladder rendered live and editable; unfilled required parameters listed by name, not counted |
| **Error / Invalid** | A parameter contradicts another (cap mode unstated, gross basis unset, promoter profit with no nut, splits >100%, run-scoped term on a single show) | Named blocking validation stating **both readings and the number each produces**, not a generic "invalid" |
| **Populated / Compiled** | All five parts valid | Evaluable expression stored on the offer; grammar version pinned; the ladder rendered as worked outcomes at attendance levels; the versus comparison shown flipping |
| **Compiled with acknowledged gaps** | Free-text economic content acknowledged as non-economic at send (D-09) | Compiled, **plus** a standing incompleteness marker carried onto the offer and inherited by the settlement sheet, naming the acknowledger and date |
| **Blocked at send** | Detector found economic content, not yet resolved | Modal: move it into the deal, or acknowledge. No third option; no dismiss-by-navigating-away |
| **Superseded** | A counter changed any term (D-11) | New expression at a new version; prior retained read-only in the thread with a term-level diff |
| **Locked** | Contract executed (17.04) | Terms read-only. Change requires a contract amendment, which recompiles against the **pinned** grammar version |
| **Legacy grammar** | Deal pinned to a retired grammar version | Renders normally with a `grammar v1` marker. Evaluates at v1 semantics forever (D-10). Not authorable at that version |
| **Provisional scope** | Deal is inside an open cross-collateralized run | Run-scoped terms shown at run scope; per-date renderings explicitly labelled provisional (17.03.03 D-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The hard part is enumerating the deal types | Surface reading of the domain index's list (guarantee, door split, versus, breakeven, bonus, walkout) | ❌ **REJECTED**: the headline types are a short, well-known list. The hard part is the **order of operations on the qualifying terms**. Two parties agreeing on "85% of net after $6,000" routinely differ by four figures because they resolve the order differently. **The 1am settlement argument is almost never about the deal type.** |
| DT-02 | Users should be able to define their own deal types | Long-tail coverage instinct; the industry has endless bespoke structures | ❌ **REJECTED for now**, with discomfort. An open expression language covers the long tail and is a spreadsheet with extra steps — it destroys comparison, modelling and settlement, which is the entire product. Closed is safe and will not cover every deal. Genuinely a product decision (Q-01); the agent should not make it. **But DT-12 finds a third option the original framing missed.** |
| DT-03 | The grammar is currency-agnostic | Implicit assumption in every deal-type description | ❌ **REJECTED**: an international booking has a €-denominated guarantee settled in £, and **the FX rate and its fixing date are deal terms nobody writes down** — they are argued about at settlement instead. A grammar assuming one currency silently produces wrong numbers on exactly the deals where the stakes are highest. Nothing in the sweep's 18 candidates mentions FX. **Resolved** → D-05. |
| DT-04 | The settlement figure is always ≥ 0 — money flows promoter → artist | Universal implicit assumption across 17.03, 17.09 and 17.10; every worked example in the domain has the artist being paid | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is structural.** Four ordinary paths produce a negative figure: a **4-wall rental** (the artist rents the room and pays for it); **ticket consignment** (unsold tickets charged back); a **forfeited deposit** on a cancellation (17.05.03) landing against a settled show; and **expense over-recovery** on a door deal where the nut exceeds the gross. A grammar typing the fee as an unsigned amount cannot express any of them, and — worse — 17.10's disbursement has no concept of a payment running the other way. Nobody in domain 17 has said the word "negative". → D-08. |
| DT-05 | "Gross" is the ticket price × tickets sold | The word's plain meaning; the reading every worked example in the domain silently assumes | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is more important than DT-01.** "Gross" is the most contested word in the vocabulary. Between the price the fan paid and the number the percentage applies to sit: sales tax/VAT, the **facility fee**, the ticketing/service charge, VIP/upsell premiums, and per-scale carve-outs. The industry has a specific term — *net gross potential* — for exactly this, which is itself an admission that "gross" is ambiguous. **The deduction order arranges the deductions; the gross basis decides what they are subtracted from.** Get the order right and the basis wrong and you are precise about the wrong number. → D-03 extended, and the blocking validation at Happy Path 5. |
| DT-06 | "Buyout" is one term | Its appearance as a single item in the domain index's deal-type list | ❌ **REJECTED**: it is **two entirely different things sharing one industry word**. A *deal buyout* is an all-in fee (£5,000, artist covers their own costs). A *rider buyout* is cash in lieu of a rider item (£150 instead of catering — domain 18). Both land on a settlement sheet. A grammar with one `buyout` token silently conflates them, and the conflation is invisible until someone settles a £5,000 catering line or a £150 headline fee. Cheap to namespace now; expensive after the first hundred deals. → D-01 note, edge-case row. |
| DT-07 | Promoter profit is not a deal term — it is the promoter's business, not the deal's | The 85/15 split reads like the whole bargain | ❌ **REJECTED**: in the standard US versus/overage structure it is **explicitly a line in the deal** — the promoter recoups the nut *plus* a promoter profit (conventionally 15% of the nut) before the overage splits 85/15. Without the term, US deals **cannot be expressed at all**, and the 85% the artist thinks they agreed applies to a different number than the one the promoter is computing. This is the surface file's abstract worry — "deal type exists in the industry but not in the grammar" — made concrete on the single largest live market. |
| DT-08 | Working-musician-tier deals are small versions of the same deals | Scope instinct; the domain's worked examples are all agency-tier ($2,000 versus 85%) | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the persona gap.** `meta/personas.md` describes a musician making a living from a portfolio of small income streams, not a stadium act. Their deals are structurally different and frequently adversarial: **percentage of the bar take**, **ticket consignment** ("here are 50 tickets — sell them or pay for them"), and **pay-to-play**. Two consequences the domain has not faced: (a) percentage-of-bar needs an input no box office supplies and the Operator asserts with zero verification — **a worse asymmetry than the count** (17.09.01 DT-03 at least concerns a number the box office produces); (b) making consignment expressible is a values decision — the platform would be making a predatory structure a first-class citizen with a nice picker. → Q-03, Q-04. |
| DT-09 | A closed vocabulary can be judged complete at launch | The implicit promise of D-02 — "we enumerated the deal types" | ❌ **REJECTED**: it cannot be judged complete at launch or ever, and the honest consequence is that **the vocabulary must be instrumented**. Under D-02 the platform is asserting it knows every deal in live music; it does not. The cost of a closed vocabulary is not paid by pretending — it is paid by seeing what the vocabulary fails to express and closing the gap. Without gap reports, D-02's failure mode is invisible: users quietly fall back to free text, the detector fires, they acknowledge, and the automation claim degrades silently, one deal at a time, with nobody able to see it happening. → D-06. |
| DT-10 | Grammar evolution is a product-roadmap concern | Framing instinct — "we'll add deal types in v2" | ❌ **REJECTED — it is a settlement-correctness concern.** D-06 guarantees the vocabulary changes. If a v3 that adds a facility-fee basis also changes a default deduction order, every historical expression silently re-derives to a different number the next time it is evaluated — and long-lived deals *are* re-evaluated (amendments, count corrections, run recomputation across dates months apart, disputes). A settlement in 2029 of a deal signed in 2026 must produce 2026's number. ✅ **The corollary is CONFIRMED**: the expression pins its grammar version at compile time and is evaluated under that version forever. → D-10. This is a genuine cross-cut — any long-lived computed statement (10 royalties, 11 licences) has it. |
| DT-11 | *Versus* and *plus* are distinct enough that users will not confuse them | They are different words | ❌ **REJECTED**: they are the single most-confused pair in live-money prose, they are one keystroke apart in an email, and **the grammar cannot detect the error** — both are valid deals. `£2,000 versus 85%` and `£2,000 plus 85%` are both well-formed and differ by the entire guarantee. Mitigation is presentational, not validational: state the payout function in plain language in the picker ("you get one or the other, never both") and render both at attendance levels. **Honest limit: this is mitigated, not solved.** A grammar cannot know what you meant. |
| DT-12 | Q-01 is a binary — closed vocabulary or open expression language | The framing inherited from the sub-domain index and carried into domain index Q-08 | ❌ **REJECTED as a binary.** DT-09 produces a third option the original framing missed: **closed vocabulary + instrumented gap reporting + acknowledged free text**. It has the closed option's settlement guarantees (every typed term computes; comparison and modelling work), and it makes the closed option's cost *measurable* rather than silent — you can see exactly which deals you cannot express, how often, and in which territory. It does not cover the long tail today; it tells you what the long tail actually is, which the open option never does because everything is expressible and nothing is legible. **The decision remains the owner's** (Q-01), but it should be made against three options, not two. |
| DT-13 | The grammar should warn a party when a deal is bad for them | Follows temptingly from `meta/personas.md`'s "signs a split sheet they don't understand" and from the domain's fairness framing | ❌ **REJECTED**: the grammar's job is to express what was agreed, not to judge it — and it would be wrong constantly. A low guarantee with a strong backend at a room that reliably sells out is an excellent deal; a high guarantee at a room that will not is how an artist burns a relationship they need. The platform has no basis for the judgment and taking one puts it on one principal's side of a two-sided deal. **Comprehension is the answer, not judgment**: render outcomes (17.03.02), render the ladder (D-03), name the payout function (DT-11). The uncomfortable caveat is DT-08 — this rejection is least defensible on consignment, where the structure is adversarial by construction and comprehension may not be enough. That tension is Q-03's, not this rejection's. |
| DT-14 | A deal has exactly two parties, matching the domain's two-sided model (D-11) | Domain index D-11 — "the deal has exactly two sides" | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the sides are two; the parties are not.** A term routinely names a **third** party: the support act (17.14), whose fee comes `off_the_top` or off the promoter's side; the agent (17.08), paid from the artist's side and invisible to the Operator. And the multi-hyphenate collapses them — the Operator whose own band takes the support slot puts a party on both sides of one term. ✅ **The safe corollary**: terms bind to **parties and sides**, never to personas. A persona is a lens on behaviour (`meta/personas.md`); a deal term needs a payee. → D-12. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The deal structure is an **evaluable expression**, not a description | The domain's founding insight (domain index D-01): settlement is only computable if the offer was structured data. |
| D-02 | Neither principal authors new deal types; the grammar is **platform vocabulary** | Open authorship is an expression language, which is a spreadsheet, and destroys comparison, modelling and settlement (DT-02). Recorded as a decision to be **re-opened** by the owner via Q-01 — with three options now, not two (DT-12) — not as a closed matter. |
| D-03 | **Deduction order is explicit, concrete and rendered** — never implicit, never collapsed to a total | It is where the money actually is (DT-01), and hiding it recreates the 1am argument the domain exists to prevent. Every deal carries an ordered ladder as data, not a convention either party holds in their head. |
| D-04 | **One vocabulary, territory profiles supply defaults** — profiles write concrete values at compile time and are never referenced at evaluation | Resolves the "which territory is baseline" framing: the deal *types* are near-universal; what differs is the gross basis, promoter-profit convention, tax treatment, merch convention and currency. Reference-at-evaluation would let a 2028 profile edit move a 2026 signed number — the same failure D-10 prevents for grammar versions. |
| D-05 | **FX basis is a first-class deal term**: denomination currency, settlement currency, rate basis ∈ {fixed, reference-fixed-on-date, settlement-date}. The default is applied **visibly**, never silently | An unwritten FX assumption is how a 6% swing becomes a settlement argument on exactly the deals with the highest stakes (DT-03). A silently-applied default is indistinguishable from no term at all. |
| D-06 | The closed vocabulary is **instrumented**: a "my deal isn't here" gap report is a first-class action that does not block the offer | D-02's cost must be measurable, not silent (DT-09). Without gap reports, users fall back to free text one deal at a time and the automation claim degrades where nobody can see it. Gap reports are the vocabulary's roadmap. |
| D-07 | **Count references resolve to 19-owned named definitions. "Sellout" is not a token** | It is precisely the ambiguous word that produces the argument — sellout of manifest, or capacity less kills and holds? Paid, or including comps? A threshold is `tickets_sold_paid ≥ 400`, never an adjective. |
| D-08 | **The settlement figure is signed**; the grammar and the ladder must express artist-owes-Operator | Four ordinary paths produce it — 4-wall rental, consignment chargeback, deposit forfeit, expense over-recovery (DT-04). An unsigned fee type cannot express any of them, and the failure surfaces in `/write-be-spec` as a wrong entity, not in ideation. |
| D-09 | Free text is **not banned; economic content in it is detected and must be acknowledged**, and the acknowledgement is carried to the settlement sheet | Banning drives users to email and loses the deal (17.02.01 DT-03); silence loses the settlement. The third path: the leak survives only by the parties' explicit, attributed, timestamped choice, and the sheet is then honest about its own incompleteness. Detector is deliberately over-sensitive — a false positive costs one click, a false negative costs a settlement. |
| D-10 | **An expression pins its grammar version at compile time and is evaluated under that version forever** | Grammar evolution is a settlement-correctness problem, not a roadmap one (DT-10). D-06 guarantees the vocabulary changes; long-lived deals are re-evaluated years later via amendments, count corrections, run recomputation and disputes. 2026's deal must produce 2026's number in 2029. |
| D-11 | A term change **recompiles a new expression**; prior versions are retained, never mutated | A deal expression is not a document — last-write-wins on it is a silently wrong number. Version history is the negotiation's evidence (17.02.02). |
| D-12 | **Terms bind to parties and sides, never to personas** | A deal has two sides but routinely three parties — support act, agent — and the multi-hyphenate puts one party on both sides of a term (DT-14). A persona is a lens on behaviour (`meta/personas.md`); a term needs a payee. This is what makes 17.08's commission invisibility structural rather than a UI convention. |
