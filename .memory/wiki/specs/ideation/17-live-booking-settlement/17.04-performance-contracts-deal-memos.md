# Feature: Performance Contracts & Deal Memos

> **Number**: 17.04
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The executed document that turns an accepted offer into a contract — generated from the agreed deal
version rather than typed fresh, executed by both entities, and amendable when the show changes.
Generation-from-the-deal is the whole point: a contract retyped from an offer is a second source of
truth that will disagree with the first, and the disagreement will be discovered at settlement.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The artist entity executes — bound by the same authority rule that approved the offer (17.02.03). Receives the contract with its terms rendered as the outcomes they already agreed, not as fresh legal prose they must re-read. Attaches their rider (owned by domain 18). Their real interest is that the contract says what the offer said. |
| Producer | None | No role in live performance contracts. |
| Operator | Full | Generates the contract from the accepted deal version, executes it, countersigns, and issues amendments when the show changes. Works from their own house paper — a room with a legal template will not abandon it for a platform's generic one. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The contract is **generated from the accepted offer version** (17.02.02) — the deal terms flow
through as structured data, and the legal prose wraps them. It is not a document someone writes and
hopes matches.

Execution runs through the platform's **document generation and e-signature cross-cut**, not through
machinery owned here. That mechanism serves split sheets (09), licences (11), service SOWs (05) and
consignment (13) identically; building it inside this feature would be a mechanism wearing a
product costume.

The deal memo / long-form distinction is real: the industry treats a signed deal memo as binding and
the long-form contract as ceremony that often arrives late or never. Modelling only the long-form
would model a document that frequently does not exist by showtime.

Amendments matter more than they look. Shows change — the support act drops, the curfew moves, the
capacity is reduced by a licensing condition — and each change is a term change with settlement
consequences.

### Happy Path

1. An offer version is accepted with the artist entity's authority satisfied (17.02.03).
2. System generates the contract from that exact version — deal terms as structured data, wrapped in
   the Operator's template.
3. Both entities execute via the e-signature cross-cut, each under its own authority rule.
4. Executed contract becomes an announce precondition (17.01.04) and the reference document for
   settlement (17.09.01).
5. If the show changes, an amendment is generated, executed, and versioned against the original.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No accepted offer version exists yet | Generation is gated on an accepted version (17.02.02); there is nothing to generate from | "Generate contract" is unavailable; the state is Not-generatable with the reason "offer not yet accepted" |
| Contract terms diverge from the accepted offer | Should be impossible by construction — generated, not typed (D-01) | Divergence is a bug, not a state |
| Someone without the entity's authority tries to execute | Execution re-checks the entity authority rule at signing time (same rule that approved the offer, 17.02.03) — a non-authorised signer is refused | "You are not authorised to sign for {entity}"; the outstanding-signature indicator names who can |
| Operator edits the generated contract's prose to change an economic term | The escape hatch: the document now disagrees with the formula settlement will use — the same free-text hazard as 17.02.01 DT-03, and here it has legal force. Level-1 stance: economic terms are structured fields, not free prose, so an economic change must route back through the offer/amendment path rather than a prose edit | Economic fields are locked in the generated document; a prose edit that touches them is either blocked or flagged as an off-formula override. Exact detect/block/accept policy is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / /write-be-spec]` (Q-02) |
| Offer is re-opened or countered after the contract is generated but before execution | The generated contract is bound to a specific accepted version; a superseding offer invalidates it | Draft is marked stale against the newer version; regeneration required before signing |
| Both entities sign against different versions (race) | Execution binds to one version only; a countersignature against a superseded draft is refused | "This draft is no longer current — regenerate from the accepted version" |
| Show reaches showtime with a signed deal memo and no long-form | Extremely common; the deal memo is binding | Must be a valid, complete state — not a warning |
| Amendment changes a term with settlement consequences | Settlement must use the amended terms; the original is history | Amendment versioned against the original; formula recompiled (17.09.01) |
| Amendment back-dated to change settlement retroactively (abuse) | Amendments carry their own execution timestamp and authority; settlement uses the terms in force, not a rewritten past | Amendment chain is append-only; each entry shows who executed it and when |
| Artist entity's authority rule changed between offer approval and contract execution | Execution validates against the authority rule **in force at signing time**, not the rule captured at approval — so a tightened rule is honoured | Whichever rule is current governs the signature; the deep case (signer lost authority mid-flight) is `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Counterparty demands their own paper | Universal above a certain size — the room will not use the platform's template | Templates are Operator-owned (D-03); the platform binds the data into their template |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not generatable (empty) | No accepted offer version yet | "Generate contract" disabled with the reason "offer not yet accepted" |
| Not generated | Offer accepted, contract not yet issued | "Generate contract" available with the source version named |
| Generated | Contract issued | Draft with the deal terms flowed through, awaiting execution |
| Stale | Offer re-opened/countered after generation | Draft flagged out-of-date against the newer version; regeneration required before signing |
| Partially executed | One entity signed | Whose signature is outstanding, under which authority rule |
| Executed | Both entities signed | Announce precondition satisfied (17.01.04); settlement reference set |
| Amended | Change executed post-signature | Amendment chain against the original; recompiled formula |
| Superseded | Deal memo replaced by long-form | Both retained; the long-form governs from execution |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This should be a sub-domain — it has generation, signature, and amendment | Node Classification Gate, first pass | ❌ REJECTED: **once e-signature is correctly extracted as a cross-cut, what remains is one lifecycle** — generate → execute → amend — with no two capabilities that interact independently of that line. Amendment is the same generation and the same signature applied again. It is "Password Reset with edge cases", not "AI Assistant". Classified as a feature. |
| DT-02 | The long-form performance contract is the artefact to model | Legal-completeness instinct | ❌ REJECTED: the industry binds on the **deal memo** and treats the long-form as ceremony that routinely arrives after the show or never at all. Modelling only the long-form would make the normal case — showtime with a signed memo and no contract — look like a failure state. A domain expert would model the memo as first-class and the long-form as optional. |
| DT-03 | The platform should supply the contract template | Product-completeness instinct | ❌ REJECTED: any room doing real volume has house paper and a lawyer who approved it, and will not adopt a generic template. **The platform's value is that the terms flow through as structured data, not that it wrote the prose.** Owning the prose would be a legal liability the product does not need and a reason for the Operator to say no. Templates are Operator-owned; the platform owns the data binding. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The contract is generated from the accepted offer version, never typed | A retyped contract is a second source of truth that will disagree with the first, and the disagreement surfaces at settlement — the exact failure the domain exists to prevent. |
| D-02 | Deal memo is first-class; long-form is optional | The industry binds on the memo (DT-02); modelling only the long-form makes the normal case look broken. |
| D-03 | Contract templates are Operator-owned; the platform owns the data binding | Rooms have house paper and lawyers (DT-03). Owning the prose adds liability and gives the Operator a reason to refuse. |
| D-04 | Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain | With e-signature extracted as a cross-cut, one lifecycle remains (DT-01). |
