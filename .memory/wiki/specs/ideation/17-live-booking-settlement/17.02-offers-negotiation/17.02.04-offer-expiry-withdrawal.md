# Feature: Offer Expiry & Withdrawal

> **Number**: 17.02.04
> **Parent**: [Offers & Negotiation](./17.02-offers-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The clock on an offer and the offering side's right to pull it before acceptance. Without expiry an
offer is an indefinite free option — the artist shops it against every other room in the city while
the promoter holds a date they cannot sell. "Good until Friday 5pm" is a term of the bargain, not a
system setting.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the clock and cannot extend it unilaterally — which is exactly why the pressure works. Receives escalating warnings as it runs down, and those warnings must reach **inside the band entity**, not stop at the agent: the agent is not the bottleneck, the band's group chat is. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Sets the expiry when composing (CX-04), may extend it as a concession, and may withdraw the offer any time before acceptance. Withdrawal is a real move — a better act appeared, or the room needs the date back. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Expiry is authored on the offer sheet as a **term** and lives in the version history like any other
term (CX-04). Countering does **not** reset the clock — a new expiry is a deliberate term change on
the new version, not an automatic side effect. Auto-reset would let either side stall forever by
countering trivially, destroying the mechanic.

Withdrawal is available to the offering side until acceptance completes. After acceptance it is a
cancellation, which is a different and much more expensive thing (17.05.03).

The important collision is with approval (CX-03): an offer expiring mid-approval is the **normal**
path for the working musicians this platform serves, not an edge case.

### Happy Path

1. Operator sets the expiry as a term when composing (17.02.01).
2. Clock runs; both sides see it on the offer.
3. Warnings escalate as it approaches — to the agent, and inside the artist entity to whoever is
   holding up approval.
4. The artist side accepts before expiry (17.02.03), or the offer dies.
5. On death, the thread closes and the underlying hold's fate is decided separately (17.01.03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Approval completes the same instant the offer expires | Real, money-relevant, and routine. Deterministic ordering required; the tie-break is a **product decision**, not an implementation detail (Q-02) | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth / /write-be-spec]` (CX-03) |
| Operator withdraws while approval is in flight | Permitted, but the band has already spent its decision-making on it. Withdrawal wins the race — the offer dies even if an approval vote lands microseconds later | Approvers told the offer was withdrawn, not that they were too slow |
| Musician tries to accept an offer that already expired | Rejected — there is no live offer to accept. Acceptance acts on a dead object; the click resolves to nothing bookable | "This offer expired at {time}" — accept action disabled/blocked; thread shown as dead |
| Non-owner (Musician / agent) attempts to withdraw | Denied — withdrawal is the offering side's move only (Role Lens). The artist side can decline or let it expire, but cannot *withdraw* the Operator's offer | No withdraw control surfaced; only decline / counter / accept |
| Operator sets expiry in the past or below a sane minimum when composing | Validation failure at compose time (17.02.01) — a zero/negative clock is not a term, it is a mistake | Inline error on the offer sheet; offer not sendable until fixed |
| Offer expires but the hold survives | Common and correct — the date is still held, there is simply no live offer | Hold unaffected; thread dead |
| Offer expiry set beyond the hold's expiry | Incoherent (CX-04); nothing currently detects it — the offer promises terms on a date the room may already have released | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth]` — see Q-03 |
| Withdrawal after acceptance | Not withdrawal — this is cancellation | Routed to 17.05.03 cancellation terms |
| Serial extension by the Operator | Expiry becomes theatre; the artist learns the clock is fake and stops treating deadlines as real | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth]` — see DT-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Live | Offer sent, clock running | Time remaining on the offer |
| Expiring soon | Below warning threshold | Escalating warnings, including inside the artist entity |
| Expired | Clock ran out | Thread dead; timestamp recorded; hold unaffected |
| Withdrawn | Operator pulled it | Dead with reason; approvers notified |
| Extended | Operator conceded time | New expiry as a term on a new version |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Offer expiry duplicates hold expiry (17.01.03) and should be merged | Consolidation instinct during classification | ❌ REJECTED: they expire **different things on different clocks**. A hold is a claim on a date; an offer is a proposal of terms. An offer can die while the hold lives (the promoter still wants the act, at a different price), and a hold can die while an offer stands (the room sold the date). Merging them would make "we're still holding you but the money's off the table" — an entirely normal state — unrepresentable. They do interact incoherently though, which is a real unmodelled gap (CX-04). |
| DT-02 | An offer expiry is a hard commitment | Face-value reading of the term | ❌ REJECTED: promoters extend routinely, and a domain expert knows the Friday deadline is usually soft. **The product hazard is that a system-enforced hard expiry is stricter than the industry it models** — kill a deal both parties still wanted and the users will stop putting real dates in the box, at which point the clock is decorative and settlement loses its structured input. The tension between enforcing the term and matching real behaviour is unresolved (Q-01). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Expiry is a term of the offer, not a system setting | It is negotiated, appears in version history, and extending it is a concession (CX-04). |
| D-02 | Countering does not reset the clock | Auto-reset lets either side stall indefinitely via trivial counters, destroying the mechanic. A new expiry must be an explicit term on the new version. |
| D-03 | Offer expiry and hold expiry stay separate | They expire different objects on different clocks; merging makes "still held, no live offer" unrepresentable (DT-01). |
| D-04 | Expiry warnings escalate inside the artist entity, not only to the agent | The agent is not the bottleneck — the band is (CX-03). |
