# Feature: Offer Approval Chain & Entity Authority

> **Number**: 17.02.03
> **Parent**: [Offers & Negotiation](./17.02-offers-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Who may bind the band to a Tuesday in Leeds, and how that authority is proven. Bands are first-class
entities that book and get paid independently of their members (`meta/personas.md`), so an offer is
accepted by an **entity**, not a person — and the entity's rule for granting that authority is a
thing the band must actually decide.

**Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's 18 candidates. The sweep modelled offers and contracts
but never modelled authority, which leaves the industry's most common relationship-ending failure
(the agent who accepted a deal the band never approved) entirely unmodelled.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The artist/band entity is what authorises. Members approve per the band's own configured rule — unanimous, majority, or a delegated authority (manager or agent below a fee threshold). Sees **which exact version** they approved, and is told when a later counter invalidates it. A sideman with no equity in the band may have no vote and still needs to see the date. |
| Producer | None | No role in live booking authority. |
| Operator | Config | Configures **their own side's** internal sign-off — a talent buyer may commit up to £5,000 and needs the GM above that. Sees only that the artist side's approval is outstanding or granted; **never** the artist's internal politics. Who objected in the band's group chat is not the promoter's business. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

**Approval binds to a version hash, not to an offer** (CX-02). This single property is the feature's
reason to exist. The canonical failure: the band approves v2, the agent keeps negotiating, and
accepts v4 — a deal the band never saw. Modelling approval as "the band said yes to this deal"
instead of "the band said yes to *this exact version*" reproduces the failure faithfully in software.

Two authorities are distinct and are constantly conflated in the industry: authority to **negotiate**
and authority to **bind**. An agent almost always has the first and frequently not the second.

Both are read from domain 01 as a **conjunction, never a single flag**: an act is authorised iff its
verb is in the edge's `activities` and its commercial domain is in the edge's `domains`
(`01.03.02` D-05). Binding a live date needs `book` **and** `live booking` — an agent granted `book`
for `recording` only cannot accept a Tuesday in Leeds, and the refusal names the missing axis rather
than a generic permission error. Two flat lists, at most 7 + 5 = 12 plain-language statements per
edge, never a 7×5 grid (`01.03.03` DT-02). A **membership** edge carries the activity axis alone and
resolves to every domain, so `01.03.03` D-07's peer seed still binds live dates below the USD 1,000
ceiling with no domain for the band to configure.

Both sides have chains. They are structurally different: the artist side is an entity governance
question (what does this band's own rule say); the buy side is a corporate spend-threshold question.

### Happy Path

1. Agent reaches a version they recommend (17.02.02).
2. Agent submits it to the artist entity's approval chain; the band's configured rule determines who
   must approve. In parallel, the buy side's spend-threshold chain routes the same version to whoever
   can commit at that fee (talent buyer below their cap, GM above it).
3. Each approver sees the version rendered as consequences (17.02.01 D-02) plus what changed since
   any version they previously approved. Neither side sees the other side's internal chain (D-04).
4. Approvals accumulate against the version hash on each side independently until **both** the entity
   rule and the buy-side threshold are satisfied for the same hash.
5. Acceptance unlocks only when both chains are green. The agent (or whichever party holds bind
   authority) accepts; the deal record is created against that exact hash and the contract (17.04)
   can be generated.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| New counter arrives after partial approval | Approvals bound to superseded versions are invalidated; approvers re-notified naming the version (CX-02) | "Your approval of v2 no longer applies — the deal is now v4" |
| Agent accepts without the chain satisfied | Blocked. This is the failure the feature exists to prevent | Blocked at accept with the outstanding approver named |
| Offer expires mid-approval | **DQ-04.03 (D-08):** the offer expires. There is **no implicit grace window** — an extension must be granted **explicitly, before expiry, by the offering side, as a new version**, which is exactly how domain 17 already handles the analogous clock in `17.01.03`. Recorded approvals are preserved as audit evidence; they do not carry into the new version by themselves. An automatic window was rejected because Q-02 already names it an obvious stalling vector. | "This offer expires at 18:00 — extend it?" to the offering side; on expiry, "Expired. 2 of 3 approvals were recorded." |
| Band has never configured a rule | **DQ-04.02 (D-07):** this feature does not invent one. Per D-03 the governance rule is **consumed from domain 01**, which already defines the model — decision thresholds of unanimity / majority / any-one-member (`01.04.01`) plus the mandate-and-ceiling enforcement half (`01.03.03`). With no rule configured, domain 01's fail-closed posture governs: no offer is treated as approved until the applicable rule exists. | "This band hasn't set how offers get approved — set it once and it applies from here" |
| Band member unreachable (on tour, no signal) | **Resolved (Q-01 / D-03, D-07, D-08).** In the **default** state there is no deadlock at all: `01.03.03` **D-07** peer-seeds the full seven-activity set — `book` included — onto every `confirmed` `permanent` band membership edge, capped at **USD 1,000 per act** (`01.03.03` **D-08**), so one reachable member can bind below the ceiling and an over-ceiling act escalates to the uncapped owning-mandate holder rather than dead-ending. Deadlock is reachable **only where the band itself configured unanimity** (`01.04.01`) — and per D-03 this feature consumes that rule and invents no relief from it. If the clock runs out first, D-08 governs: the offer expires with no implicit grace, recorded approvals are kept as audit evidence, and the only relief is an explicit pre-expiry extension by the offering side as a new version | Below the ceiling: a single reachable permanent member approves outright. Above it: "This is above your limit. Sent to {name} to approve." Under a band-configured unanimous rule: Pending with the unreachable approver named, and on expiry "Expired. 2 of 3 approvals were recorded" |
| Member approves, then the band's lineup changes before the show | `[PENDING — Q-03 → /write-architecture-spec]` | — |
| Approval lands the same instant a counter arrives | The counter wins; the approval is consumed by nothing and the approver must be told | Approver sees their approval was superseded, not silently dropped |
| A revision pushes the fee above the delegated threshold that let it bind directly | The delegated authority is no longer sufficient; the version now requires the higher chain and cannot be accepted on the old delegation. This is the buy-side twin of the v2/v4 failure — a talent buyer who could commit £5,000 cannot commit £6,000 just because £5,000 was fine (CX-02) | "This version now needs GM sign-off — your authority covers up to £5,000" |
| Approver wants to withdraw an approval before the chain is satisfied | Allowed while the version is still Pending/Partially approved; the approval is retracted and the entity rule is re-evaluated against the remaining approvals | Approver can revoke; others see the count drop back and the chain return to outstanding |
| Delegated authority (manager/agent) approves, but a member with a vote objects before satisfaction | **Resolved (Q-01 / D-03).** Precedence is domain 01's and domain 01 already states it: an act taken **within** a granted mandate stands — `01.03.03` Edge Cases, *"Member acts within mandate but against the band's wishes → Not a platform failure — the mandate was granted. Governance (01.04.01) is the remedy, and the audit trail is the evidence."* A member's objection is therefore **not a veto** over an in-mandate approval; it is a governance matter under `01.04.01`, with the attributed audit trail as the evidence. **Outside** the mandate — above its value ceiling (`01.03.03` D-08), or with `book` missing from the edge's `activities` or `live booking` missing from its `domains` (`01.03.02` D-05) — the delegation is insufficient and the act escalates instead of binding, which is this file's "revision pushes the fee above the delegated threshold" row | The approval stands, attributed and logged, visible to every member (`01.03.03` D-07's accepted cost). The objecting member is pointed at governance (01.04.01), not at an override control. Above the ceiling: "This is above your limit. Sent to {name} to approve." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not configured | Band has no governance rule yet (first offer at onboarding) | Prompt to set the rule inline before the chain can run — cannot submit until a rule (or the default per Q-01) is in place |
| Not required | Version below a delegated threshold | Accept available directly |
| Pending | Version submitted for approval | Named approvers, who has responded, what's outstanding |
| Partially approved | Some approvers responded | Progress against the entity's rule |
| Satisfied | Entity rule met for this version hash | Accept unlocked |
| Invalidated | A newer version superseded the approved one | Approvers re-notified; chain restarts against the new hash |
| Blocked | Approver rejected | Rejection with reason; thread returns to negotiation |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The agent has authority to accept on the artist's behalf | Standard industry assumption; how it usually works in practice | ❌ REJECTED: they usually have authority to **negotiate**, not to **bind** — and the industry conflates these two constantly, which is precisely why the failure is so common. `meta/personas.md` lists "signs a split sheet they don't understand" as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour; the live-booking equivalent is discovering you're playing Leeds on your daughter's birthday because your agent said yes. Modelling one authority instead of two reproduces the bug. |
| DT-02 | Approval attaches to the offer | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED: it attaches to a **version hash**. The v2/v4 failure — band approves v2, agent accepts v4 — is the single most consequential edge in this sub-domain, and it is invisible unless approval is version-bound. This is the same structural insight as the platform's thesis: capture the fact at the moment it is true, against the exact thing that was true. |
| DT-03 | A unanimous default is the safe, fair choice for bands | Fairness instinct | ❌ REJECTED: unanimous **deadlocks exactly when the band is working** — one member on tour with no signal kills every offer, and the 48h clock (CX-03) does not wait. The safe-looking default is the one that loses the band money. But the alternative (majority, or delegated authority) means a member can be bound to a date they never agreed to, which is the DT-01 failure at a smaller scale. There is no default that is both safe and fast; this is a **product decision the owner must make**, not an agent call (Q-01). |
| DT-04 | The artist-governance chain and the buy-side spend-threshold chain are two separate features needing two separate models | They *look* different — one is band politics, one is corporate procurement | ✅ CONFIRMED as **one mechanism, two rule shapes**: both are "accumulate approvals against a version hash until a rule is satisfied." The rule differs — an entity-governance rule (who in the band) vs a spend-threshold rule (who up to what fee) — but the engine is identical, and it is the same engine that must serve splits (09) and licensing (11). Modelling them as two features would fork the exact cross-cut this file exists to name (D-03). One chain primitive, pluggable rule. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **the band entity and its governance rule live in 01, not here.** This feature *consumes* that rule; it does not define what a band is or who its members are. The boundary matters: the same rule should govern split approval (09) and any other binding act. The scope this feature reads from 01 has **two flat axes** — `activities` (the seven verbs) and `domains` (the five commercial domains) — ANDed at the moment of the act and never stored here (`01.03.02` D-05, `01.03.03` D-01); a membership edge supplies the activity axis and resolves to all domains (`01.03.02` D-05).
- Touches **17.02.02 Counteroffer Thread & Versions** ([./17.02.02-counteroffer-thread-versions.md](./17.02.02-counteroffer-thread-versions.md)) — supplies the version hash approval binds to (CX-02).
- Touches **17.02.04 Offer Expiry & Withdrawal** ([./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md](./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md)) — approval races the clock (CX-03).
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([../../09-rights-ownership/](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — structurally identical problem: who may bind the entity to a split. If the two are solved differently, the platform has two contradictory authority models for the same band.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Entity Approval Chains & Binding Authority** — serves 01 (orgs/bands), 09 (splits), 11 (licensing), 17 (deals). Recorded in the domain CX file.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Approval binds to a version hash, not to an offer | The v2/v4 failure is invisible under any other model (DT-02). |
| D-02 | Authority to negotiate and authority to bind are separate permissions | The industry conflates them and that conflation is the failure mode (DT-01). Each is read as the conjunction of the edge's two scope axes — `activities` and `domains` — never as one flag (`01.03.02` D-05), so a scope narrowed on either axis removes bind authority without ending the edge. |
| D-03 | The band's governance rule is consumed from domain 01, not defined here | The same rule must govern splits (09) and deals (17), or the platform holds two contradictory authority models for one band. |
| D-04 | The Operator never sees the artist entity's internal approval detail | Who objected is not the counterparty's business; exposing it would make bands route around the tool. |
| D-05 | Acceptance requires **both** the artist-governance chain and the buy-side threshold chain satisfied against the same version hash | Either side binding alone reproduces the failure the feature prevents — a band bound with no promoter authority, or vice versa, is a deal that unravels. |
| D-06 | The chain is one primitive with a pluggable rule (entity-governance or spend-threshold), not two features | The same primitive must serve splits (09), licensing (11) and deals (17), or the platform forks its authority model (DT-04). |
