## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Offer Sheet Composition ↔ Counteroffer Thread & Versions

**Relationship**: Composition emits v1. Every counter emits a complete new version rather than
mutating fields, because live deals are **traded as bundles** — a promoter drops the guarantee by
£500 and simultaneously gives back the merch cut. Field-level diffing would present that as two
unrelated changes and destroy the trade's meaning.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: composes v1 and most counters; sees the full ladder of versions.
- **Musician**: counters via their agent; their interest is in seeing *what was traded for what*,
  not which fields changed.
- **Producer**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected — offers are never public.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The thread is shared; drafts are not (Q-03, index). Both sides can
   compose a counter simultaneously — the resulting versions are siblings, and the product must not
   pretend one is authoritative.
2. **Trigger chain**: Compose → v1 → counter → v2 → … → accept. If acceptance fails on authority
   (17.02.03), the thread does not roll back — it stays open at the latest version.
3. **Permission intersection**: Composing does not grant accepting. The composer's own side may
   still need internal sign-off above a threshold.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Each version notifies the counterparty and, on the artist side, anyone
   in the approval chain whose prior approval was just invalidated (CX-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: Simultaneous counters race. Server-assigned version ordering; both
   survive as siblings rather than one overwriting the other.

### CX-02: Counteroffer Thread & Versions ↔ Offer Approval Chain

**Relationship**: **Approval binds to a version hash, not to an offer.** This is the single most
important edge in this sub-domain. The canonical real-world failure: the band approves v2, the agent
keeps negotiating, and accepts v4 — which the band never saw and would not have agreed to. Modelling
approval as "the band said yes to this deal" instead of "the band said yes to *this exact version*"
reproduces the failure in software.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the band entity's approvers see exactly which version they approved and are notified
  when a new counter invalidates it.
- **Operator**: sees only that the other side's approval is outstanding or granted — never the
  internal politics of who objected.
- **Producer**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Approval state is owned by the artist entity and is private to it. The
   Operator sees a boolean, not the chain.
2. **Trigger chain**: New version → invalidate approvals bound to prior versions → re-notify
   approvers. If the counter is trivial (a typo fix), full re-approval is punitive — but auto-judging
   triviality is how the v2/v4 failure sneaks back in. Unresolved (Q-01 below).
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, sharply. An agent may hold full authority to *negotiate* and no
   authority to *bind*. These are different permissions on the same object and the industry conflates
   them constantly.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Invalidation must notify every approver who already said yes, or they
   will believe the deal they approved is the deal that happened.
5. **State transition conflict**: An approval landing at the same moment as a counter is the race.
   The counter must win — approving a superseded version is meaningless — but the approver must be
   told their approval was consumed by nothing.

### CX-03: Offer Approval Chain ↔ Offer Expiry & Withdrawal

**Relationship**: The clock does not care about the band's group chat. A 48h offer window against a
five-member band with a day job each is a routine collision, not an edge case. Expiry mid-approval
is arguably the **normal** path for the artists this platform is for — `meta/personas.md` describes
working musicians with portfolio careers, not stars with full-time management.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: bears the cost — loses deals to their own slowness, which is the fragmentation pain
  the platform exists to fix.
- **Operator**: sets the clock and benefits from the pressure it creates.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — expiry is the Operator's term; approval is the Musician's
   process. They collide without sharing state.
2. **Trigger chain**: Expiry fires → offer dies → any in-flight approval is orphaned. Whether a
   completing approval can beat the clock is the question; a grace window is the obvious answer and
   the obvious abuse vector.
3. **Permission intersection**: Only the offering side may extend. An artist cannot buy themselves
   time, which is exactly why the pressure works.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Approaching expiry must escalate *inside* the artist entity, not just
   to the agent — the agent is not the bottleneck, the band is.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes. Approval-completes and offer-expires in the same instant is
   real and money-relevant. Deterministic ordering required; the tie-break rule is a product
   decision, not an implementation detail.

### CX-04: Offer Sheet Composition ↔ Offer Expiry & Withdrawal

**Relationship**: Expiry is authored on the offer sheet as a term ("good until Friday 5pm"), not
configured as a system preference. It is part of the deal's content and appears in the version
history like any other term — a counter that extends the window is a real negotiation move.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: authors the window; shortening it is a pressure tactic and lengthening it is a
  concession.
- **Musician**: reads it as a term.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — High confidence
on the relationship, but the interaction with hold expiry (17.01.03) is unmodelled: an offer good
until Friday on a hold that expires Thursday is incoherent, and nothing currently detects it.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Offer Sheet Composition | Offer Approval Chain | Considered a direct edge and rejected. Composition never requires the *counterparty's* approval chain, and the composer's own internal sign-off (talent buyer → GM) is a different mechanism operating on the buy side. Every real path from composition to approval passes through a version (CX-01 → CX-02). A direct edge would imply an offer can be approved before it exists as a version, which is the v2/v4 bug in a different costume. |
| R-02 | Counteroffer Thread & Versions | Offer Expiry & Withdrawal | Rejected. Countering does not reset the clock — that is a **term change** requiring an explicit new expiry on the new version (CX-04), not an automatic side effect of countering. Modelling counter → clock-reset would let either side stall indefinitely by countering trivially, destroying the mechanic. The two are related only through composition. |
| R-03 | Offer Approval Chain | Offer Sheet Composition (artist-side authoring) | Rejected as a distinct interaction. When an artist composes an offer for their own direct-booked date, the approval chain applies identically — it is the same edge as CX-01 → CX-02, not a new one. Modelling artist-side authoring separately would duplicate the whole sub-domain for a persona swap. |
