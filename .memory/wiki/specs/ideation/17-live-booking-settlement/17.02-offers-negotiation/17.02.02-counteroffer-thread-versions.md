# Feature: Counteroffer Thread & Version History

> **Number**: 17.02.02
> **Parent**: [Offers & Negotiation](./17.02-offers-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The negotiation itself — an ordered thread of complete offer versions, where each counter is a whole
new document rather than a field edit, and every version is preserved. The version history is not an
audit convenience: it is the evidentiary record of what was actually agreed, which is the exact class
of fact that `meta/problem-statement.md` says currently gets reconstructed from memory years later at
the moment it becomes contested.

The feature exists for two users with one shared problem. The agent needs to know **what was traded
for what** across forty versions and three weeks. The promoter needs to know **why this room gave up
its merch cut** long after the person who conceded it has forgotten. Neither question is answerable
from a change log; both are answerable from a thread of whole documents rendered as consequences.

Everything downstream of acceptance — the contract (17.04), the settlement formula (17.09.01), the
dispute record (17.09.06) — is generated from **one version in this thread**. If the thread cannot say
which version, with certainty, the rest of the domain is built on a memory.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Full | Counters via their agent, or directly when self-booking their own pub gig. Sees every version rendered **as outcomes at attendance levels** (D-08), diffed against a baseline they choose — the parent version, any earlier version, or *the version they last approved*. Their real question is never "which fields changed" but "did we give up the merch cut to get the guarantee up, and was that worth it?" — so the diff answers in money at 150 / 300 / sell-out, not in fields. **Approvers see the whole thread**, not the agent's summary (D-11): approval binds to a version (CX-02) and an approver who cannot see what the version replaced cannot judge it. A non-approving member (a sideman with no equity, per 17.02.03) sees the **accepted version's date and logistics only** — never the negotiation. |
| **Producer** | None | No role. Live deal negotiation has no Producer lens (`meta/personas.md`; 17.02 index Role Matrix). A Producer negotiating a room is acting as an **Operator**; a Producer negotiating their own gig is acting as a **Musician**. **Boundary for spec writers**: booking a studio session is domain 16 and does **not** reuse this thread — a studio booking has no bundled-trade structure and no entity approval chain, so reusing the offer thread there would import a negotiation model the Producer does not have. |
| **Operator** | Full | Counters, compares versions, sees the whole thread. Uses version history to remember why a term is where it is — three weeks and forty offers later, nobody remembers why this room gave up its merch cut. **Never** sees the artist side's internal approval detail (17.02.03 D-04) and **never** sees the agent's commission (17.08) — so the artist-side outcome rendering and the buy-side outcome rendering are two different numbers over the same version (Q-04). Sees only that a version is approved, pending, or superseded. |
| **Fan** | None | Never visible, at any state, including after acceptance. An offer is a proposal, not a show — leaking a live thread announces a show that may never happen and exposes the terms of one that does. Both damage both principals (17.02.01 Role Lens). The Fan learns of the show at announce (17.01.04), from a confirmed date, never from this thread. |

## Behavior

### 1. A counter is a document, not an edit

Each counter produces a **complete new version**, not a diff applied to a mutable object. This is
deliberate and non-obvious: live deals are traded as bundles — a promoter drops the guarantee by £500
and simultaneously gives back the merch cut. Field-level mutation would present that as two unrelated
edits and destroy the trade's meaning (CX-01, DT-01).

It also destroys the evidence. "What did we actually agree" needs the **document**, not a change log
replayed against a mutable head that no longer exists.

Every version is immutable once sent and permanent. The thread survives the deal, the tour, and the
band (D-02).

### 2. A version is compared as outcomes, not as fields

The trade is surfaced by rendering **both versions' economic consequences at the same attendance
levels** (17.02.01 D-02) and presenting the delta, grouped by direction of benefit:

> **v2 vs v1, for you:** at 150 → **−£60**; at 300 → **+£240**; at sell-out (450) → **+£610**
> **Moved toward you:** guarantee £2,000 → £2,500 (+£500)
> **Moved away from you:** merch cut 90% → 80% (−10 pts)
> **Unchanged:** curfew, comp allowance, deposit terms, expiry

This is not a UI preference. It is the **only** comparison that works, because versions are frequently
not field-comparable at all: v1 is a £2,500 guarantee and v2 is 85% of net after £6,000 expenses.
There is no field diff between those two documents — there is only an outcome diff (DT-04).

The baseline is chooseable. The default is the parent version. Two other baselines are first-class
because they answer the questions people actually ask: **"what changed since I last looked"** (any
prior version) and **"what changed since I approved"** (the approver's own last-approved version,
which is what 17.02.03's invalidation notice must show).

### 3. Materiality is computed, not judged

**Approval carries forward to a new version if and only if the new version is not worse for the
approving side on any term** (D-05). Otherwise every approval bound to the superseded version is
invalidated (CX-02).

This replaces the "is the counter trivial?" question, which is unanswerable — and dangerous, because
auto-judging triviality is exactly how the v2/v4 failure returns wearing a helpful costume. The
directional question is answerable, because 17.02.01 D-01 makes every economic term **typed data**:

| Change in the new version | Materiality | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A typed economic term moves **in the approver's favour**, all else identical | **Non-material** — approvals carry forward | The approver already said yes to a strictly worse deal. Nobody can be bound to anything they did not already accept. |
| A typed economic term moves **against** the approver | **Material** — all approvals invalidated | This is the v2/v4 failure. |
| A typed term with **no defined direction of benefit** changes (set length, load-in time, support slot identity) | **Material** — invalidated | "Longer set" is not obviously better; "different support act" is not comparable at all. Undefined polarity is treated as adverse. Safe by default. |
| **Any free-text term changes** | **Material** — invalidated | Free text is the automation escape hatch (17.02.01 DT-03) and is therefore an *economic* escape hatch. "Plus £500 if it sells out" is prose. Prose cannot be proven benign, so it is never assumed benign — including a typo fix. |
| **Any attachment changes** (rider, stage plot, tech spec) | **Material** — invalidated | An attachment is part of the document and is a smuggling vector identical to free text. |
| The offer's own **expiry** is extended | **Non-material** for the artist side (more time is a concession); **material** for the buy side | Expiry is a term (CX-04). Direction of benefit is defined and asymmetric. |
| The offer's own **expiry** is shortened | **Material** | Pressure is adverse. |

**The rule is safe in one direction only, and that is the point.** A false "material" costs a
re-approval. A false "non-material" binds a band to a deal it never saw. The rule never trades the
second for the first.

**Hard dependency, stated plainly**: this rule requires a **polarity** — a defined direction of
benefit per side — for each term in 17.03's grammar. If 17.03's grammar is an *open expression
language* rather than a closed vocabulary (domain index Q-08), polarity is undecidable for arbitrary
expressions and this rule **degrades safely to "every counter invalidates every approval"** — correct,
punitive, and the honest cost of an open grammar. See Q-01.

### 4. Drafts are private; the thread is shared; siblings are real

Drafts are private per side (17.02 index Q-03, D-04). Only sent versions enter the thread.

Both sides may compose a counter simultaneously, producing **sibling versions** — the product must not
pretend one is authoritative (D-03). Server-assigned ordering breaks display ties; it does not
anoint a winner. A counter **names its parent** explicitly, so a fork is visible as a fork.

**A version can only be accepted while it is the sole live leaf** (D-09). If two leaves are live,
acceptance is refused and names the sibling. Without this rule, both sides can accept different
siblings in the same second and the platform holds two contradictory binding deals for one Tuesday —
which is worse than having no platform at all.

### 5. The phone call is captured, not pretended away

The decisive moves happen on the phone (DT-03). A version may therefore be entered as a
**transcription** — a version one side composes and attributes to a verbal agreement (D-06).

A transcription enters the thread as **Unconfirmed**. It is a *claim about a conversation*, and it is
displayed as one. Until the counterparty confirms it:

- it **cannot be approved** (17.02.03),
- it **cannot be accepted**,
- it **cannot be the parent of a counter by its own author** (you may not build a ladder on your own
  unconfirmed claim),
- it **is** visible to the counterparty, permanently, and its non-confirmation is itself part of the
  record.

Confirmation converts it into an ordinary version, authored jointly. A counter to it is a normal
counter and implicitly declines the transcription. This is the platform thesis applied to
negotiation: **capture the fact while everyone is present and friendly**, and never let one party's
memory silently become a term.

### 6. Nothing auto-closes

A thread with no activity is **dormant, not dead** (D-07). Dormancy is a **display state with no
state effect** — the promoter who goes quiet for three weeks and returns is normal practice, and
auto-closing his thread destroys a live negotiating position to tidy a list.

What actually kills a thread is expiry, withdrawal, rejection, or acceptance — all owned by
17.02.04 and 17.02.03. Auto-close would be a second, competing death mechanism for the same object.
A dead thread does **not** release the underlying hold: "offer expires but the hold survives" is
common and correct (17.02.04 edge cases), and hold release is 17.01.03's decision, not this feature's.

### Happy Path

1. Agent opens v1 (emitted by 17.02.01) against the artist's targets. The thread shows one version;
   the diff panel is empty because there is no baseline.
2. System renders v1 as outcomes at attendance levels — "at 300 you take £2,400" — not as terms.
3. Agent composes a counter in a **private draft** (D-04). The draft names v1 as its parent. Nothing
   is visible to the Operator.
4. Agent raises the guarantee £2,000 → £2,500 and concedes the merch cut 90% → 80%. The draft shows,
   live, the net effect of the bundle at each attendance level, so the agent is trading against
   money, not against fields.
5. Agent sends. **v2 enters the thread**, immutable, ordered by the server, notifying the Operator.
   The expiry does **not** reset (17.02.04 D-02; R-02) — the clock is v1's term until a version
   changes it deliberately.
6. Operator opens v2. System renders **v2 against v1 as a trade** — "+£240 at 300; guarantee +£500,
   merch −10 pts" — not as two unrelated field edits.
7. Operator counters with v3, naming v2 as parent: guarantee back to £2,200, merch cut restored to
   90%. Thread continues.
8. Agent submits v3 to the artist entity's approval chain (17.02.03). Approval binds to **v3's
   version hash**, not to "the offer" (CX-02).
9. Operator sends v4: guarantee £2,200 → £2,400, nothing else changed. **Materiality is computed**
   (D-05): strictly in the artist's favour, no free text, no attachment → **approvals carry forward**.
   Approvers are told what improved; they are not asked to vote again.
10. v4 is the sole live leaf and its approvals satisfy the band's rule. Acceptance unlocks (17.02.03).
11. Agent accepts v4. Thread freezes. The deal record is created; the contract (17.04) and the
    settlement formula (17.09.01) are generated **from v4 specifically**.
12. Every prior version — v1, v2, v3, and every unconfirmed transcription — remains permanently
    readable, in order, with its author, timestamp and parent intact.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Both sides counter simultaneously** | Server-assigned ordering for display; both survive as **siblings**, neither overwrites (D-03) | Fork banner: both versions shown as concurrent, each with its parent named; "neither side has responded to the other" |
| **Both sides accept different siblings in the same instant** | Refused. Acceptance requires the version be the **sole live leaf** (D-09). The catastrophe — two contradictory binding deals on one date — is unreachable by construction | "Cannot accept v3 — v4 is also live and unresolved. One branch must be withdrawn first." |
| **Fork never resolved; both leaves sit live** | Thread stays open and un-acceptable indefinitely. This is correct: the parties genuinely have not agreed | Both leaves shown; accept is disabled on both with the sibling named. Either author may withdraw their own leaf to unblock |
| **Counter arrives after an approval was granted, and is adverse** | Prior approval invalidated (CX-02) — the v2/v4 failure this design exists to prevent | Approvers notified naming the version: "Your approval of v2 no longer applies — the deal is now v4", with **v4 diffed against v2**, not against v3 |
| **Counter is strictly in the approver's favour, nothing else changed** | **Approvals carry forward** (D-05). Not judged trivial — computed non-adverse | "The promoter improved the guarantee by £500. Your approval still stands." No re-vote requested |
| **Counter fixes a typo in a free-text term** | **Material.** Invalidates approvals. Punitive and deliberate — prose cannot be proven benign, and a "correction" label is trivially gameable (DT-06) | Warned **before send**: "This changes a free-text term and will require re-approval by 3 people. Send anyway?" |
| **Counter changes a term with no defined polarity** (set length 45 → 60 min) | **Material.** Undefined direction of benefit is treated as adverse | "This will require re-approval — we can't tell whether a longer set is better for you." |
| **Counter changes only an attached rider PDF** | **Material.** An attachment is part of the document and an economic smuggling vector identical to free text | Re-approval required; the attachment change is named in the diff |
| **17.03's grammar is an open expression language** (domain Q-08) | Polarity undecidable → rule degrades to **every counter invalidates every approval** (D-05). Safe, correct, punitive | Approvers re-vote on every counter. The cost of an open grammar is visible here, by design |
| **v1 is a guarantee; v2 is a door split — no comparable fields** | Field diff is impossible; **outcome diff is the only diff** (DT-04). Rendered at shared attendance levels | "At 150 you take £2,000 → £1,150. At 450 you take £2,000 → £3,600." The structures differ; the money is comparable |
| **A version's deal structure cannot be evaluated** (references a capacity the room no longer has, per domain 16) | Version renders in **Partial** state — terms readable, outcome block unavailable. The version is not corrupted; the world moved | "Outcomes unavailable — this version assumes a capacity of 450; the room record now says 380." Terms shown; diff shows fields only, flagged as incomplete |
| **17.03's grammar changes after v2 was sent** | Every version renders under **the grammar as at its own send time** (D-12). A version means what it meant when it was agreed | Historic versions render normally. If the grammar's meaning changed, the version carries a note naming the grammar version it was authored under |
| **Negotiation moves to a phone call and returns with "agreed"** | Either side enters a **transcription** (D-06). It enters as **Unconfirmed** — a claim about a conversation, not a term | "Agent logged this from a call on 14 Mar. Not confirmed by the promoter." Cannot be approved or accepted while unconfirmed |
| **Transcription is never confirmed** | It stays Unconfirmed permanently. Non-confirmation is itself part of the record and is visible in a dispute (17.09.06) | Displayed in the thread, greyed, marked "unconfirmed", excluded from the live leaf set |
| **Counterparty disputes a transcription** ("I never said that") | Counterparty declines it. The declined transcription is **not deleted** — the claim and the denial are both facts, and deleting either is exactly the reconstruction failure the platform exists to prevent | Both the claim and the denial are shown, with timestamps and authors |
| **A party counters their own unconfirmed transcription** | Refused. You may not build a ladder on your own unconfirmed claim | "Confirm or withdraw this transcription before countering from it." |
| **Counter offered after the offer expired** | Refused; expiry killed the thread (17.02.04) | "This offer expired on {date}. Ask for a new one." |
| **Counter sent while a withdrawal is in flight** | Server ordering decides. If the withdrawal lands first, the counter is refused against a dead thread; if the counter lands first, the withdrawal still succeeds — withdrawal is available until **acceptance**, not until the next counter (17.02.04) | Counter author sees "The promoter withdrew this offer while you were composing. Your draft is saved." Draft preserved, never silently discarded |
| **Counterparty is composing against a stale parent** (v3 arrived mid-draft) | The draft is **not** rebased — rebasing silently changes what the author thought they were countering | Banner in the draft: "v3 arrived while you were writing. You are countering v2. Review v3, or send anyway and create a fork." |
| **Byte-identical resend of a counter** (network failure, double submit) | No new version. A resend whose complete document content matches the current leaf **authored by the same side** collapses onto that leaf | "Already sent — this is v3." No v4 is created. The thread never shows a phantom counter |
| **Send fails mid-flight** (connectivity lost at a loading dock — `meta/personas.md`, Operator) | Nothing enters the thread. The draft is preserved locally and unsent. A version half-in-the-thread is not a state that exists | "Not sent — no connection. Your draft is saved." Explicit unsent state; never an optimistic "sent" |
| **Thread abandoned with no accept or reject** | **Nothing auto-closes** (D-07). Dormant is a display state with no state effect | After 14 days with no counterparty response and no expiry, the thread shows "Dormant — no response since {date}". Everything still works. Threshold is display-only and safe to tune |
| **Same human on both sides** — a Musician who owns the venue books their own band (`meta/personas.md`: Operators "often run the business on the side of performing themselves") | **Flagged on the version, not blocked.** It is legitimate and common. But an approval satisfied by the person who authored the offer is not an arm's-length approval, and the band's other approvers must know | Version carries a **self-dealing flag** naming the shared actor, visible to both sides, to the artist entity's other approvers, and permanently in the thread for 17.09.06 |
| **One agent counters for two acts on the same bill against one promoter's budget** | Out of scope here. This feature owns **one thread's integrity**, not budget arithmetic across threads (DT-05) | Each thread behaves normally. The linkage is 17.14's problem and is recorded as a cross-cut, not silently modelled |
| **Two live offer threads exist against the same date** (promoter runs two acts, takes whoever says yes first) | Real, routine, and **unmodelled by this feature**. Accepting one version must kill the other thread — but this thread does not know its siblings exist. Acceptance is gated on the date still being available (17.01), so an accepted version **can fail to create a deal** | The losing side sees the offer die by withdrawal (17.02.04), never by silent disappearance. See Cross-Cut Notes → 17.01 |
| **A party's account is erased** (GDPR right to erasure) | Versions are **preserved**; the actor's identity is tombstoned to a role label ("the promoter's talent buyer") while the document content and its ordering are untouched. Deleting a version would delete the counterparty's evidence of their own deal | "Author no longer on WeJammin." Terms, timestamps, order intact. **Legal basis needs ratification — Q-03** |
| **A 40-version thread** | Readable by construction: the thread never requires reading 40 documents, because every question is a diff against a chosen baseline (D-08) | Collapsed list; "what changed since I last looked" and "what changed since I approved" are first-class baselines |
| **Two counters land in the same millisecond** | Deterministic total order assigned by the server; ordering is never derived from a client clock (a client clock is attacker-controlled and a laptop at a venue is frequently wrong) | Both appear as siblings in a stable, identical order for both sides. No user ever sees the thread in a different order from their counterparty |
| **A party claims a version was sent that is not in the thread** | Unreachable. Only server-ordered sends exist; there is no client-asserted version. The transcription mechanism is the *only* way a claim enters the thread, and it enters marked as a claim (D-06) | The thread is the record. Nothing else is |

### States

#### Thread lifecycle states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Open** | v1 sent | Latest version rendered as outcomes + full history; whose turn it is; expiry clock |
| **Countered** | A new version sent | Diff against parent with the **trade surfaced** as money at attendance levels |
| **Forked** | Simultaneous counters | Two sibling leaves, neither authoritative; accept disabled on both, each naming the other |
| **Transcription pending** | A verbal agreement logged (D-06) | Unconfirmed version, greyed, attributed to a call; cannot be approved or accepted |
| **Dormant** | No counterparty response for 14 days, offer not expired | Display-only badge. **No state effect** — every action still available (D-07) |
| **Pending approval** | Version submitted to an entity's chain (17.02.03) | Version locked from further countering **by that side**; the counterparty may still counter, which is the CX-02 race |
| **Accepted** | Sole live leaf, chain satisfied, accepted (D-09) | Thread frozen; deal record created; history permanently readable |
| **Dead** | Expired, withdrawn, or rejected (17.02.04) | Read-only history retained permanently, with cause and timestamp. The hold is **unaffected** (17.01.03 owns that) |

#### View states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Thread opening | Skeleton of the version ladder. The **latest version's terms render before the outcome computation completes** — terms are the document; outcomes are derived. Never a blank screen holding the deal hostage to arithmetic |
| **Empty** | Offer composed but not yet sent (17.02.01 Draft) | No thread exists. "No offer has been sent yet." The private draft is the only artefact; the counterparty sees nothing at all |
| **Populated** | Two or more versions | Version ladder with authors, timestamps, parents; diff panel against the chosen baseline; live leaf highlighted; fork and self-dealing flags inline |
| **Error** | Thread cannot be loaded | "Can't load this negotiation." Retry. **Never** a partial ladder presented as complete — a thread missing v3 is a lie about what was agreed, and is worse than no thread |
| **Partial** | A version's outcomes cannot be computed (stale room capacity, unevaluable structure) | Terms shown; outcome block replaced by a named reason; diff degrades to fields with an explicit "money comparison unavailable" marker — never a silently field-only diff presented as a trade |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A counteroffer is an edit to the offer | Naive CRUD modelling | ❌ **REJECTED**: it is a new document. Deals are traded as bundles — guarantee down, merch cut back — and field-level mutation renders that as two unrelated edits, destroying the negotiation's meaning (CX-01). It also destroys the evidence: "what did we actually agree" needs the document, not a change log. |
| DT-02 | Version history is an audit nicety | Feature-priority instinct | ✅ **CONFIRMED as thesis-critical**, not a nicety. `meta/problem-statement.md`: the defining failure is that facts get reconstructed from memory at the moment they become contested. A live deal's terms are exactly such a fact, and the version thread is provenance-at-source applied to booking. This is why the feature is `must` despite looking like table stakes. |
| DT-03 | The thread captures the negotiation | Optimistic modelling | ❌ **REJECTED**: the decisive moves happen on the phone. An agent and a promoter agree the real deal in a two-minute call and the thread receives only the ratified outcome — a version with no visible antecedent, which is exactly where a later dispute lives. **Resolved by D-06**: the hole is not accepted and not pretended away. Logging a call is made cheap, and a logged call enters as an *unconfirmed claim* until the counterparty confirms — never as a term. Residual legal question: Q-02. |
| DT-04 | Versions can be compared field-by-field | The diff instinct — every tool does this | ❌ **REJECTED**, and this is the sharpest new finding. Live versions are routinely **not field-comparable at all**: v1 is a £2,500 guarantee, v2 is 85% of net after £6,000 expenses. There is no field correspondence between those documents. A domain expert compares them the only way they *are* comparable — **as money at attendance levels**. This turns D-01's "surface the trade" from an aspiration into arithmetic, and it makes the same machinery available to compute materiality (D-05). A field diff is not a worse version of this feature; it is a different feature that does not work. |
| DT-05 | Threads are economically independent of each other | One-thread-at-a-time modelling | ❌ **REJECTED as a factual claim, accepted as a scope boundary.** Domain index D-10 already records that "a show is one artist and one promoter" is wrong on the majority of shows. A promoter packaging a bill (17.14) has **one talent budget for the night**: a £500 concession to the headliner's thread is £500 the support slot's thread cannot have. The threads are economically coupled and this feature models them as independent. That is a real, knowing simplification — not an oversight. Modelling the coupling here would drag bill economics into a negotiation primitive and make every counter a portfolio optimisation. **This feature owns one thread's integrity; 17.14 owns the budget across threads.** Recorded as a cross-cut so 17.14 inherits a stated problem rather than discovering a silent one. |
| DT-06 | A "correction" is a distinct kind of counter and should be modelled | Sympathy for the user who mistyped a date | ❌ **REJECTED**: instantly gameable. If "correction" exempts a version from re-approval, every material change becomes a correction, and the v2/v4 failure returns wearing the friendliest possible costume. Corrections are versions. **D-05's directional rule handles them correctly without a type**: a corrected date is adverse-or-undefined → re-approval; a corrected guarantee in the artist's favour → approval carries forward. Worth noting the *cause*, though: 17.02.01 D-03 makes room templates first-class, which makes **template-inherited errors in v1 a predictable failure, not a rare one** — last month's date, last month's curfew. The thread must expect v1 to be wrong sometimes; it must not make being wrong cheap. |
| DT-07 | Everyone on the artist side sees the same thread | "The thread is shared" (CX-01), read too broadly | ❌ **REJECTED**: CX-01's "shared" means *across the deal*, not *within an entity*. Two different questions collapse if you conflate them. **Approvers must see the whole thread** — approval binds to a version (CX-02), and an approver shown only the agent's summary is being asked to ratify a document they cannot read, which is the v2/v4 failure with extra steps and `meta/personas.md`'s "signs a split sheet they don't understand" as the outcome. **A non-approving member must not**: a sideman with no equity (17.02.03) needs the date, not the fee history — and showing every hired player what the band negotiated is a band-destroying disclosure the product has no business forcing. Membership and vote come from domain 01; this feature consumes both (D-11). |
| DT-08 | The version thread is neutral evidence — it helps everyone equally | The platform's own thesis, assumed to be universally welcome | ✅ **CONFIRMED as a real hazard, and it is the failure mode nobody designs for.** A thread is **discoverable**. A promoter who opened at £3,000 and settled at £2,000 has created a written record of his own opening position, timestamped, against himself. Provenance is an asset to the platform and to the *weaker-information* party — and a **liability** to the party with more information, who is usually the promoter. The rational move for a sophisticated Operator is therefore to negotiate the real deal off-platform and post only the ratified outcome — which is DT-03's hole, arrived at deliberately rather than by accident. **This is the thesis's own shadow**: `meta/problem-statement.md` argues consolidation earns the right to capture provenance because the platform is already where the work happens. That argument holds only while staying is worth more than the record costs. The mitigation is not technical and cannot be — it is that template speed (17.02.01 D-03), computed settlement (17.09.01) and the diff must make the thread **more useful than it is dangerous**. Any feature that increases the thread's evidentiary teeth without increasing its daily utility pushes users to email. Recorded as a standing constraint on this sub-domain, not as a solvable problem. |
| DT-09 | "Immutable and permanent" is a design choice with no external constraints | D-02, taken at face value | ❌ **REJECTED**: it collides head-on with the right to erasure. `meta/constraints.md` still lists GDPR/CCPA as unresolved (pending a compliance interview at `/ideate-validate`), and a version thread is dense with personal data — names, and what a named individual was paid. **The collision is structural, not incidental**: the platform's thesis *requires* the permanent record, and erasure requires its destruction. The behavioural resolution (D-02, extended): erase the **actor**, never the **document** — tombstone the identity to a role label, preserve terms, order and hashes. A counterparty's evidence of their own deal cannot be deleted by the other party's account closure, or "delete your account" becomes a litigation tactic. The legal basis for retention (contractual necessity / legal claims) is **not an agent call** — Q-03. |
| DT-10 | The two sides of a version render the same numbers | Symmetry instinct — one document, one truth | ❌ **REJECTED**: the artist's outcome and the promoter's outcome over the *same version* are different numbers, and must be. 17.08 makes the agent's commission **invisible to the Operator** by design (domain index Role Matrix: 17.08 is `None` for Operator — "a promoter who could see it would negotiate against it"). So "at 300 you take £2,400" is gross on the buy side and net-of-commission on the artist side — if the artist side wants it net at all, which is a genuine product question (Q-04). The hazard is precise: **one shared document rendering two different numbers is exactly the divergent-reading failure 17.02.01 DT-02 says causes settlement disputes** — solved there by making both sides read the same consequences. This feature must therefore be explicit about which number is being shown to whom, and never let the artist-side rendering leak into a view the Operator can reach. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **17.02.01 Offer Sheet Composition** ([./17.02.01-offer-sheet-composition.md](./17.02.01-offer-sheet-composition.md)) — **trigger dependency + inherited hazard**. Composition emits v1 (CX-01). Two inheritances matter: (a) its D-01 "every economic term is typed data" is the **precondition for D-05's computed materiality** — untyped terms cannot have polarity; (b) its DT-03 free-text escape hatch propagates into every version, which is why any free-text change is material here (D-05). Its D-03 first-class templates make **v1 errors predictable** (DT-06).
- Touches **17.02.03 Offer Approval Chain** ([./17.02.03-offer-approval-chain.md](./17.02.03-offer-approval-chain.md)) — **permission + notification fan-out**. Approval binds to a **version hash**, not to the offer (CX-02) — the most important edge in the sub-domain. This feature supplies the hash, the parent link, and **the diff against the approver's own last-approved version**, which is what 17.02.03's invalidation notice must render. D-05 (computed materiality) resolves 17.02.03's carry-forward question from this side; its Q-01 (default band rule) is unaffected and stays theirs.
- Touches **17.02.04 Offer Expiry & Withdrawal** ([./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md](./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md)) — **shared entity (the expiry term) + state race**. Countering does **not** reset the clock (R-02); a new expiry is a term change on the new version (CX-04). D-05 gives expiry a defined, **asymmetric** polarity — extension is a concession to the artist and adverse to the buy side. Withdrawal-vs-counter is a live race resolved by server order.
- Touches **17.03 Deal Structures & Economics** ([../17.03-deal-structures-economics/](../17.03-deal-structures-economics/)) — **hard dependency, two ways**. (a) Its grammar must define a **polarity per term per side** or D-05 degrades to "always invalidate" — this is a direct consequence of domain index **Q-08** (closed vocabulary vs open expression language) and is the sharpest place that decision bites. (b) Versions render under **the grammar as at their own send time** (D-12), so grammar evolution is a versioning problem 17.03 must own.
- Touches **17.01 Availability, Holds & Confirmation** ([../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/](../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/)) — **state race across threads, currently unmodelled**. A promoter routinely runs **two live offer threads against one date** and takes whoever says yes first. This thread does not know its siblings exist, so **acceptance of a version can fail to create a deal** if the date went. Also: a dead thread does **not** release the hold (17.02.04) — release is 17.01.03's call, and this feature must never touch it.
- Touches **17.14 Bill Construction & Support Slot Offers** ([../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md](../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md)) — **shared entity: the promoter's talent budget**. Threads on one bill are economically coupled; this feature models them as independent (DT-05). 17.14 inherits a **stated** simplification, not a hidden one.
- Touches **17.04 Performance Contracts & Deal Memos** ([../17.04-performance-contracts-deal-memos.md](../17.04-performance-contracts-deal-memos.md)) — **trigger dependency**. The accepted version — one specific version hash, not "the offer" — is what the contract is generated from.
- Touches **17.09.01 Settlement Sheet Computation** ([../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.01-settlement-sheet-computation.md](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.01-settlement-sheet-computation.md)) — **shared entity**. The accepted version's deal structure **is** the settlement formula (domain D-01). The outcome-rendering engine this feature uses for diffs is the same evaluation this feature's accepted version later feeds — if a version rendered "£2,400 at 300" during negotiation and settlement computes something else at 300, the platform has contradicted itself in writing.
- Touches **17.09.06 Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes** ([../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.06-settlement-audit-trail-disputes.md](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.06-settlement-audit-trail-disputes.md)) — **evidence**. When a settlement is contested, the version thread is the evidence of what was agreed — including unconfirmed transcriptions, declined transcriptions, and self-dealing flags, each of which is a fact about the negotiation.
- Touches **17.08 Agency Representation & Commission** ([../17.08-agency-representation-commission/](../17.08-agency-representation-commission/)) — **permission**. The agent's commission is invisible to the Operator by design (domain Role Matrix). The artist-side and buy-side outcome renderings of one version are therefore different numbers (DT-10, Q-04), and the artist-side number must never reach an Operator view.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission**. Who is an approver and who is a member is domain 01's; this feature consumes both to decide **who inside the artist entity sees the thread versus only the accepted version** (D-11, DT-07). It does not define membership.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **evidence consumer**. The dispute machinery reads this thread; it does not live here. Whether an *unconfirmed* transcription carries weight there is Q-02.
- Touches **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([../../16-venues-studios-spaces/](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) — **shared entity: room capacity**. A version's outcomes are computed against the room record; when the room record changes, historic versions degrade to **Partial** rather than silently re-computing to a number nobody agreed to.
- **Not-product — routed to `/create-prd-architecture`**: content-addressed version identity, immutability enforcement, and server-assigned deterministic total ordering. Behaviourally required throughout (a client clock is attacker-controlled and a laptop at a venue is frequently wrong); mechanically not this file's business.
- **Not-product — routed to `/create-prd-security`**: the erasure-vs-immutability collision (DT-09) and the retention basis for personal data inside permanent versions.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every counter is a complete new version, never a field mutation | Deals are traded as bundles; mutation destroys the trade's meaning and the evidentiary record (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Versions are immutable and permanent; the thread outlives the deal. **Erasure removes the actor, never the document** | The thread is the provenance artefact for the deal's terms (DT-02) — the same argument that makes split capture the platform's wedge. A counterparty's evidence of their own deal cannot be deleted by the other party closing their account, or account deletion becomes a litigation tactic (DT-09). Legal basis: Q-03. |
| D-03 | Simultaneous counters produce siblings, not an overwrite | Neither side's counter is authoritative; pretending otherwise silently discards a real negotiating position. |
| D-04 | Drafts are private per side; only sent versions enter the thread | Private drafting is universal practice; a shared-draft model would be a fantasy nobody would use. Resolves 17.02 index Q-03. |
| D-05 | **Materiality is computed directionally, never judged as "trivial"**. Approval carries forward iff the new version is not worse for the approver on any term; free text, attachments and undefined-polarity terms are always material | Resolves the Step-5 Q-01. "Is this trivial?" is unanswerable and auto-judging it is how the v2/v4 failure returns. "Is this worse for you?" is arithmetic over typed terms (17.02.01 D-01). The rule is deliberately asymmetric: a false *material* costs a re-approval; a false *non-material* binds a band to a deal it never saw. Degrades safely to always-invalidate if 17.03's grammar is open (domain Q-08 → Q-01). |
| D-06 | **A verbal agreement enters as an unconfirmed transcription** — visible, permanent, attributed, and unable to be approved or accepted until the counterparty confirms | Resolves the Step-5 Q-02. The phone-call hole (DT-03) is neither accepted nor pretended away: logging is made cheap, and the log is marked as a *claim about a conversation* rather than a term. Confirming is the platform thesis exactly — capture the fact while everyone is present and friendly. A declined transcription is retained: the claim and the denial are both facts. |
| D-07 | **Threads never auto-close.** Dormancy is a display state with no state effect; a dead thread never releases the hold | Resolves the Step-5 Q-03. The promoter who goes quiet for three weeks and returns is normal practice — auto-close destroys a live position to tidy a list. Expiry, withdrawal, rejection and acceptance already kill threads (17.02.03/17.02.04); auto-close would be a second competing death mechanism for one object. Hold release is 17.01.03's decision (17.02.04 edge cases). |
| D-08 | **A version is diffed as outcomes at attendance levels against a chooseable baseline**, not as fields | Versions are frequently not field-comparable at all — a guarantee and a door split share no fields (DT-04). Outcomes are the only comparison that exists, and they make D-01's "surface the trade" computable. Baselines: parent (default), any prior version, and the approver's own last-approved version. |
| D-09 | **A version can only be accepted while it is the sole live leaf** | Without it, both sides can accept different siblings in the same second and the platform holds two contradictory binding deals for one Tuesday — worse than having no platform. Makes the catastrophe unreachable rather than unlikely. |
| D-10 | **Same-human-on-both-sides is flagged on the version, never blocked** | The multi-hyphenate is the product's structural fact (`meta/personas.md`) and a musician who owns a venue booking their own band is legitimate and common. But an approval satisfied by the offer's own author is not arm's-length, and the band's other approvers and any later dispute (17.09.06) must be able to see that. Visibility, not prohibition. |
| D-11 | **Every approver sees the whole thread; a non-approving member sees only the accepted version's date and logistics** | Approval binds to a version (CX-02) — an approver shown a summary is ratifying a document they cannot read. A sideman with no equity needs the date, not the band's fee history (DT-07). Membership and vote come from domain 01. |
| D-12 | **Every version renders under the deal grammar as at its own send time** | A version means what it meant when it was agreed. Re-rendering a two-year-old version under a changed grammar silently rewrites the evidence — the precise failure `meta/problem-statement.md` exists to prevent. |
| D-13 | **Version ordering is server-assigned and deterministic; no ordering is ever derived from a client clock** | Both sides must see the thread in the same order or they are reading different documents. A client clock is attacker-controlled, and a laptop at a loading dock is frequently just wrong. Mechanism routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. |
