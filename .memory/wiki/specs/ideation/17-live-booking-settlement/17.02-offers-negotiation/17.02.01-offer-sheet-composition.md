# Feature: Offer Sheet Composition

> **Number**: 17.02.01
> **Parent**: [Offers & Negotiation](./17.02-offers-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Authoring the offer — the document that proposes a date, a deal structure, and the logistics that
qualify it. This is the promoter's primary instrument and the moment the deal first becomes
**structured data** rather than prose in an email. That structuring is what makes settlement
computable later (17.09.01); an offer written as a paragraph is a settlement that must be done by
hand with a calculator at 1am.

The feature's whole job is to get every term that will later change a number **into the grammar**
(17.03.01) at the only moment anyone is willing to type it — while they still want the deal. Every
term that escapes the grammar here is a term settlement cannot see, and the platform's central claim
about automating the 1am back-office argument fails exactly one term at a time.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Composes** in the two cases where the artist side is the party committing money: a self-promoted show (they hire the room and offer the support slot), and offering a support slot their own deal gives them control of (17.14 D-03). Pitching themselves to a room is an **enquiry** (17.07), not an offer — see DT-09. **Reads** every offer made to them, rendered as outcomes at attendance levels (D-02) and **net of their agent's commission** (D-09) — not as the formula. `meta/personas.md` names "signs a split sheet they don't understand" as this persona's worst accidental behaviour; a guarantee-versus-percentage deal is the live equivalent, and the render is the mitigation. Sees the hold position and hold expiry backing the offer (D-07, D-08) — including the fact that they are 2nd on the ladder, which an offer that omits it conceals. |
| Producer | None | No role in live deal composition. A Producer hiring a room to run a session is acting as an **Operator** (domain 16); a Producer playing a gig is acting as a **Musician**. Neither lens is "Producer" here, per `meta/personas.md`. |
| Operator | Full | Composes: date, room + **capacity configuration** (D-11), deal structure from 17.03's grammar, ticket scaling assumptions, curfew, set length, support slots, merch cut, comp allowance, deposit terms, currency + FX basis (D-12), and the offer's own expiry (CX-04). Works from **room templates** (D-03) so the hundredth offer costs minutes — with template-derived terms marked as such, and any term whose source room record has changed since the template was cut surfaced at compose (DT-08). Sees the deal as **cost to the room**, never the artist's net (D-09). Blocked at send above their internal authority threshold (17.02.03). **Never** sees the artist's radius exposure (D-19) or approval politics. |
| Fan | None | Offers are never public and never rendered to a Fan in any state, including expired and withdrawn. A proposed show is not a show; leaking one damages both principals, misinforms ticket buyers about a lineup that does not exist, and hands a rival promoter the room's price. The Fan sees a bill only at announce (17.01.04). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An offer is a **structured document**: a deal structure drawn from 17.03's grammar, plus the
logistics that are actually part of the bargain — set length, curfew, comp allowance, merch cut,
soundcheck time, backline provision, deposit terms, and the offer's own expiry (CX-04).

The critical design property: **every economic term is typed data, not prose.** "$2,000 versus 85% of
net after $6,000 expenses, whichever is greater" must be a formula the system can later evaluate
against real counts, not a sentence a human re-reads at 1am.

### The Term Taxonomy (resolves Q-01)

Every term on an offer sheet falls into exactly one of three tiers. The tier decides whether
settlement can see it.

| Tier | Definition | Contents | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Economic** | Changes a number at settlement | Deal type + parameters (guarantee, split %, versus basis, bonus thresholds, walkout potential, buyout); expense cap and per-line vs aggregate; **deduction order**; merch cut (soft/hard, with/without seller); comp allowance; support-fee treatment (off the top vs promoter's side); payment direction (17.14 D-02); deposit amount + due date; currency + FX basis and fixing date (17.03.01 DT-03); agent commission basis (17.08.01) | **MUST be structured.** Never expressible as free text. A T1 term in free text is a hard stop (D-06). |
| **T2 — Qualifying** | Does not change a settlement number, but is a term of the bargain and is machine-checkable | Date; room + **capacity configuration**; hold position and hold expiry (D-08, D-07); slot (headline / direct support / opener); set length; curfew; load-in and soundcheck times; backline provision (provided / hired / artist-supplied); rider **reference** (D-18); radius clause reference (17.06); offer expiry (CX-04) | **MUST be structured**, because they are validated against the room record (16) and consumed by advancing (18) and the announce gate (17.01.04). |
| **T3 — Bespoke** | A genuinely non-economic, non-checkable condition | "No support act may use the house drum kit"; "artist requires the Tuesday load-in"; "the room's piano must be tuned to A=440" | **Free text, permitted, and stamped `NOT SETTLED`** (D-05). Carried onto the settlement sheet as an unsettled-terms panel requiring explicit acknowledgment at signoff (17.09.05). |

The escape hatch (DT-03) is not closed — it cannot be. It is made **visible at the moment it costs
money**. A promoter who writes "plus £500 if it sells out" in the notes still gets no automatic
computation of it, but both parties are shown that unsettled term on the settlement sheet, in the
room, at the moment of signing — which is exactly when someone says "hang on, what about the £500?".
The design principle throughout this feature: **do not pretend to prevent human behaviour the
industry has; be present when it becomes contested.** That is the platform's thesis
(`meta/problem-statement.md`) applied to a text box.

### The Economic-Signal Lint (resolves Q-02)

Free text is scanned on blur and again before send. Two outcomes, deliberately asymmetric:

| Signal | Verdict | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Free-text figure **contradicts or duplicates a structured T1 field** (notes say "guarantee is actually £2,500"; structured guarantee is £2,000) | **Hard stop.** Structured terms are authoritative (D-06); a document that says two numbers is a dispute in a costume | Send blocked. "Your notes say £2,500 but the guarantee term says £2,000. Fix the term or remove the note." |
| Free text carries an **economic signal** with no structured counterpart — a currency symbol or ISO code adjacent to a number (`£500`, `EUR 750`); a number followed by `%`; an economic noun/verb within 40 characters of a number (`pay, plus, bonus, deduct, cut, fee, split, extra, additional, waive, cover, reimburse, per head, per ticket, comp`); a conditional near a number (`if`, `once`, `provided`, `subject to`, `in the event`) | **Warn and require acknowledgment** | "This looks like an economic term. Terms in notes are **not settled by the system**. Convert to a structured bonus?" → **Convert** (opens the grammar picker) / **Send anyway** (records a per-term acknowledgment on the version, visible to the counterparty) |

The lint is tuned to **over-flag**: target ≥95% recall on T1 terms; precision is explicitly not
optimised. A false flag costs one click; a false pass costs a settlement dispute and a relationship.
Whether that trade survives contact with a promoter composing their 200th offer is a real risk and is
logged honestly as Q-01.

### Composition Mechanics

Templates matter more than they appear to. An Operator running 200 shows a year composes from a room
template; the marginal offer must cost minutes (D-03). But pre-fill has a hazard — the hundredth
offer is composed **without being read**, and a stale template term becomes a real deal term nobody
chose (DT-08). Therefore: template-derived terms are visibly marked as template-derived, and any term
whose source room record (16) changed since the template was cut is surfaced at compose, unmissably,
before send.

**Drafts are entity-owned, not personal** (DT-04). A venue's talent buyer drafts and the GM approves —
both are the buy side. A draft is therefore visible to the composing entity and claimed by one editor
at a time; the claim releases after 5 minutes of inactivity or on explicit release. A second editor
sees the draft read-only with the holder named and a "request edit" action. Last-write-wins is not
acceptable on a document whose fields are money.

**Sending is atomic and idempotent.** A retry after a timeout produces the same v1, never two live
offers. Drafts autosave on field blur and every 10 seconds of active editing; a network failure
mid-compose loses nothing, because the composition is durable and only the *send* is the commitment.

**A sent version is an immutable snapshot** (17.02.02 D-02). The room record's referenced values —
capacity in the named configuration, curfew, backline — are captured onto the version at send. If the
room record later changes (a licence change drops capacity, the piano is sold), the sent version does
**not** mutate: a notice is raised on the thread naming the changed term and both sides decide whether
it warrants a counter (D-13). Silently mutating a sent offer would destroy the evidentiary property
that makes the version thread worth having (17.02.02 DT-02).

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens a held date (17.01.02) or a target date and starts an offer. Composing on a past
   date is blocked; composing on a date already **confirmed** to another act is blocked (17.01.01).
2. System pre-fills from the room template — capacity configuration, standard merch cut, curfew,
   house comp allowance, backline — each marked template-derived, and flags any term whose source
   room record changed since the template was cut (DT-08).
3. System states the date's backing: the Operator's hold position on the ladder and the hold's expiry
   (17.01.02, 17.01.03) — or, with no hold, that the date is unheld and can be taken (D-07).
4. Operator names the **capacity configuration** (seated / standing / balcony closed). Capacity is per
   configuration, not per room (D-11); the offer carries which one.
5. Operator selects a deal structure from 17.03's grammar and fills its parameters — guarantee, split
   %, expense cap, bonus thresholds — with the **deduction order shown, not hidden** (17.03.01 D-03).
   Currency is required explicitly and never inferred from the room's country (D-12).
6. System renders the deal's **consequences at attendance levels**: breakeven always, plus 50% / 75%
   / 100% of capacity-in-configuration, plus the act's verified-draw median in this market where
   ≥3 verified draws exist within 24 months (17.11.01, D-10). The render is role-scoped — the Operator
   sees cost to the room (D-09).
7. Operator adds any T3 bespoke terms as free text. The lint runs on blur: contradictions hard-stop;
   economic signals warn and require Convert or an on-the-record acknowledgment.
8. Operator sets the offer's expiry (CX-04) — pre-filled to the hold's expiry where a hold exists,
   with no pre-fill where none does (D-07). Setting it beyond the hold's expiry is permitted and
   discloses the gap on the offer.
9. Operator sends. If the deal's value is above the Operator's internal authority threshold, send is
   blocked with the named approver (17.02.03). Send is idempotent.
10. Version v1 enters the thread (17.02.02) and the expiry clock starts (17.02.04). The counterparty
    is notified — including a recipient with no platform account, who receives a readable link to the
    outcomes render but cannot accept without claiming the entity (D-16).
11. The artist side reads it net of their agent's commission (D-09), with the hold position and hold
    expiry disclosed (D-07, D-08), and with any radius conflict surfaced **to them only** (D-19).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Offer composed with no hold on the date | **Permitted** — cold offers are real practice. Resolves index Q-02: an offer may exist without a hold; the hold is a claim on a date, the offer a proposal of terms (17.02.04 DT-01) | Warning that the date is unheld and could be taken; the unheld status is a **disclosed term on the offer**, not just a composer-side warning |
| Offer expiry set beyond the underlying hold's expiry | **Warn and disclose — never block** (D-07). Blocking is incoherent while cold offers are permitted: an offer with no hold is strictly weaker than an offer on a hold that dies Thursday, and forbidding the stronger while allowing the weaker is arbitrary | Operator: "Your hold expires Thu 18:00; this offer runs to Fri 17:00. Extend the hold?" (one-click, executes in 17.01.03). Musician: the offer renders "good until Fri 17:00 — promoter's hold on this date expires Thu 18:00" |
| Hold expires **while** the offer is being composed | Send proceeds — cold offers are permitted — but the disclosure silently changing from "held" to "unheld" between compose and send is not acceptable | Re-checked at send; if the backing changed, send pauses once with "This date is no longer held. Send as a cold offer?" |
| Deal structure references a capacity above the room's real capacity for the named configuration (domain 16) | **Hard stop.** The potential gross is a lie and settlement will disagree with the offer. Below the configuration's capacity is **permitted** — offering against a reduced config is a real move | Blocked: "The Main Room seats 450. You've scaled 600 — that's the standing configuration." |
| Room is not on the platform (independent promoter renting a room they don't own) | **Permitted** — independent promoters are a large share of the market. The room's terms become Operator-asserted rather than room-verified, and are stamped `unverified` | Artist sees which terms are verified against a room record and which the promoter asserted. Unverified capacity does not feed 17.11's comparables |
| Room record incomplete (no curfew on file) | The term cannot pre-fill; it is entered manually and marked offer-asserted, not room-verified | Partial state: the missing terms named, with a link to complete the room record (16) |
| Room record changes **after** the offer is sent (licence change drops capacity) | The sent version does **not** mutate (D-13). A notice is raised on the thread naming the changed term | Both sides: "The Main Room's standing capacity changed from 600 to 550 after this offer was sent. This offer still says 600." Neither side is forced to counter |
| Room template changed after offers were composed from it | Old offers unaffected — the template is a **source, not a link** | No change to live offers; the next composition uses the new template |
| Free-text term contradicts a structured term ("notes: guarantee is actually £2,500") | **Hard stop.** Structured is authoritative (D-06); two numbers in one document is a dispute pre-loaded | Blocked at send, naming both figures |
| Free-text term carries an economic signal with no structured counterpart ("plus £500 if it sells out") | Lint warns; Operator may Convert to a structured bonus or Send anyway with a recorded acknowledgment. If sent, the term is stamped `NOT SETTLED` and surfaces on the settlement sheet at signoff (D-05, 17.09.05) | "Terms in notes are not settled by the system." → Convert / Send anyway. Counterparty sees the `NOT SETTLED` stamp too — the acknowledgment is bilateral, not a private confession |
| Operator composes an offer they lack internal authority to make | Blocked at send (17.02.03). Composition is never blocked — drafting a deal you cannot sign is normal work | Blocked at send with the named approver; the draft survives |
| Operator composes a second offer on a date that already has a live offer to a different act | **Permitted** — this is how the hold ladder is worked, offering the 2nd hold as a contingency. But an offer that does not disclose the recipient's ladder position is a lie of omission (D-08) | Operator warned that two acceptances would double-book the room. The 2nd-position offer **renders its hold position as a term**: "You are 2nd on the ladder for this date" |
| Both acts accept their competing offers | The ladder decides (17.01.02); this feature's contribution is that the 2nd act was **told** they were 2nd before they spent their approval chain on it | The losing act sees they were 2nd from the offer they read, not from the rejection |
| Two Operators at the same venue compose offers on the same date simultaneously | Both drafts exist privately; both may send; both become live offers | Second sender warned at send: "{colleague} has a live offer to {act} on this date" |
| Two Operators edit the same draft concurrently | Single-editor claim (DT-04); the second sees read-only with the holder named. Claim releases after 5 min idle or explicit release | "{name} is editing. Request edit?" |
| Double-send (double click, or retry after a timeout) | Idempotent — one v1, never two live offers on one draft | No duplicate; the thread shows a single v1 |
| Network failure mid-compose | Draft is durable (autosave on blur + every 10s active). Only *send* is the commitment | Draft intact on return, with an unsent indicator |
| **Composer is a member of the receiving artist entity** (venue owner books their own band — the multi-hyphenate acting as Operator and Musician at once) | **Permitted** — this is normal and common. Stamped a **related-party offer**; the composing member is excluded from their own side's approval on it (D-15), and the deal is excluded from 17.11's comparables because a self-dealt price is not a market price | Both sides see the related-party stamp. The band's remaining approvers see that the composer cannot approve their own offer |
| An Operator's own act is on a competing offer for the same date | Same related-party machinery; the conflict is disclosed rather than prevented | Related-party stamp on the competing offer |
| Offer sent to an act with no platform account | Reaches them as a readable link — outcomes render, terms, expiry (D-16). **Acceptance requires claiming the entity** (17.02.03 needs an entity to run a chain against) | Recipient reads the full offer without an account; accepting prompts entity claim |
| Recipient forwards the offer to their agent by email (universal behaviour) | The link is what travels; the render is the document. An exported PDF is a **view, never a source** (D-17), carrying a back-link and a version stamp | Forwarded reader sees the live version and whether it is still the current one |
| Artist's radius clause (17.06) conflicts with the offered date | Surfaced **to the artist only** on receipt. Showing it to the Operator would disclose the artist's other bookings — the promoter learning "this act is restricted in Manchester until March" learns who else booked them (D-19) | Musician: "This date is inside your radius exclusion from {show}." Operator: nothing |
| Operator's **own** radius clause from a prior act at their room conflicts | Surfaced to the Operator at compose — it is their own term, on their own room | "You granted {act} a 60-mile / 90-day exclusion covering this date" |
| Support-slot offer at £50 (17.14 DT-03) | Composition offers a **short form**: deal type = flat, fee, date, slot, set length, expiry. Everything else inherits from the room template unedited. Six fields, not sixty (D-20) — the full ceremony does not survive a £50 opener booked on Tuesday for Friday | Short form by default when slot ≠ headline; "add full terms" available |
| Rider pasted into free text | The rider is 18's structured artifact. Embedding it duplicates 18 and creates two contradicting sources — and riders carry **economic** obligations (hospitality buyout, backline hire) that belong in T1, not in a paragraph (DT-10) | "Reference your rider instead" → rider version picker; economic rider lines routed to the grammar |
| Currency differs from the room's country (€ guarantee, UK room) | **Permitted and required to be explicit** (D-12). Currency is never inferred; FX basis and fixing date are T1 terms (17.03.01 DT-03) | Currency is a required field with no country-derived default |
| Malicious input in free text (markup, script, oversized paste) | Escaped and length-bounded; never rendered as markup. The interesting attack here is not XSS — it is **a term hidden by volume**: 4,000 words of boilerplate with one economic line buried in it | Free text is bounded and the lint runs over the whole block regardless of length; flagged terms are surfaced individually, not as a wall |
| Offer composed against a date the act has already declined at this room | Permitted — re-offering at a better price is the job | Prior declined version surfaced to the composer: "{act} declined v3 on this date on {date}" |
| Act has no verified draw record in this market (D-10) | The draw-anchored attendance level is omitted; **breakeven is always rendered** — it is the number neither side can spin (DT-07) | Render shows breakeven + capacity percentages; no draw anchor, stated plainly rather than silently absent |
| Draft deleted before send | Permitted. Drafts are private and unsent — there is nothing evidentiary to preserve. Sent versions are immutable forever (17.02.02 D-02) | Confirm-and-delete; no thread entry is created |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Composition opened; room record and template being resolved | Skeleton of the offer sheet with the template's field structure; the deal-structure picker is disabled until 17.03's grammar resolves — an offer composed against a half-loaded grammar is how a term goes missing |
| **Empty** | Operator has no room template yet (their first offer) | Empty state: "Compose this one from scratch, and we'll save it as this room's template" — the template is a byproduct of the first offer, not a setup chore. The lazy path must be the correct path (`meta/personas.md`, Producer anti-pattern) |
| **Populated** | Template resolved and pre-filled | Full sheet: template-derived terms marked as such, structured T1/T2 terms with the deduction order shown, T3 free text, outcomes render, hold backing, expiry |
| **Partial** | Room record incomplete, or the room is off-platform | Missing terms named and required manually; each marked offer-asserted vs room-verified; unverified terms flagged to the counterparty on send |
| **Error** | Send failed (network, or a validation the composer cannot see) | Named error with retry; the draft is never lost. Send remains idempotent across retries — a retry after an ambiguous timeout resolves to the same v1 |
| **Stale** | A referenced room record changed since the template was cut (pre-send) or since the version was sent (post-send) | Pre-send: changed terms flagged before send. Post-send: thread notice naming the term; the version does **not** mutate (D-13) |
| **Draft** | Composition started | Private to the composing **entity** (index Q-03), not to the composing person (DT-04). Single-editor claim; not visible to the counterparty in any form |
| **Blocked (lint)** | A free-text term contradicts a structured term | Send blocked naming both figures (D-06) |
| **Pending internal sign-off** | Deal value above the composer's authority threshold | Send blocked with the named approver (17.02.03); the draft survives intact |
| **Sent** | Offer issued | v1 in the thread; expiry clock running; immutable snapshot taken of every referenced room term |
| **Superseded** | A counter arrived | Read-only; visible in version history (17.02.02) |
| **Withdrawn / Expired** | See 17.02.04 | Dead; reason and timestamp recorded; the thread's history is retained permanently |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An offer is primarily a fee | Naive modelling — "how much are you paying" | ❌ REJECTED: the fee is often the *least* contested term. Merch cut, comp allowance, curfew, expense cap and support-slot control routinely decide whether a deal is good, and a headliner will trade £1,000 of guarantee for the merch cut without hesitating. An offer modelled as fee + notes cannot express the trade, and cannot settle. |
| DT-02 | Offers should be rendered as terms | Document-fidelity instinct | ❌ REJECTED: a domain expert renders an offer as **outcomes at attendance levels**, because "85% of net after $6,000" means nothing to the artist who has to decide. Both sides misreading the same document differently is a primary source of settlement disputes (17.09.06) — and the artist in `meta/personas.md` is explicitly someone who signs things they don't understand. Rendering consequences is a provenance safeguard, not a UI nicety. |
| DT-03 | Structured terms are sufficient; free text can be banned | The domain's own automation thesis, taken to its conclusion | ❌ REJECTED: real offers always carry bespoke terms ("no support act may use the house drum kit"). Banning free text drives users back to email and loses the whole deal. The honest problem is that free text is an automation escape hatch that **will** be abused to smuggle economic terms out of the formula. **Now resolved** (D-05, D-06): the hatch is not closed — it is stamped `NOT SETTLED`, linted at compose, and surfaced on the settlement sheet at signoff. Do not prevent the behaviour; be present when it becomes contested. |
| DT-04 | An offer is composed by one person | Implicit single-author assumption in the breadth pass | ❌ REJECTED: the buy side is routinely two people — a talent buyer drafts, a GM signs off above a threshold (17.02.03). The draft is therefore an **entity artifact, not a personal one**, and two colleagues editing one draft is ordinary Tuesday work at any venue with more than one booker. Modelling drafts as personal makes the GM unable to see what they are being asked to approve until it is already sent, which is backwards. Last-write-wins on a money document is not acceptable → single-editor claim, 5-minute idle release. |
| DT-05 | The room's capacity is a fact | Every capacity reference in the sweep and in the breadth pass | ❌ REJECTED: **a room has capacities, plural.** The same room is 450 seated, 600 standing, 380 with the balcony closed, and a different number again under a licence variation. An offer that says "capacity 600" without naming the configuration has already created the dispute: the promoter settles against 450 seated and the artist modelled 600 standing, and both are reading the same document honestly. A domain expert would never quote a capacity without the configuration; nothing in the corpus models it. **Configuration is a T2 term** (D-11), and it must reach 19 too — the manifest is per-configuration or the count means two things. |
| DT-06 | The offer is between a promoter and an artist | The two-sided framing in the domain index (D-11) and this sub-domain | ❌ REJECTED at the **render** level, which is where it bites. In a large share of real shows the counterparty is an *agent*, and the agent's commission (17.08) comes off the artist's side, invisible to the promoter (domain Role Matrix: 17.08 Operator = `None`, by design). So the offer's fee is **gross to the artist side, not net to the artist** — and rendering "if 300 people come, you get £2,400" to a Musician without deducting their 10% is a lie in **exactly the direction the platform claims to fix**. It is the same failure as an unrecorded split: a number the musician believes, which is not the number they receive. The render must be **role-scoped** (D-09): Operator sees cost to the room; Musician sees their net after commission. This is the sharpest intersection break found in this feature. |
| DT-07 | Rendering outcomes at attendance levels is neutral | The framing of D-02, taken at face value | ❌ REJECTED: **the choice of levels is persuasion.** A promoter who renders 300/450/600 on a room that realistically does 200 has made a bad offer look good using arithmetic the platform supplied — and the platform, by choosing the levels, took a side. The fix is to anchor the levels to evidence rather than to hope: the act's **verified draw record** in that market (17.11.01) where ≥3 records exist within 24 months (one draw is weather; three is a pattern; a draw decays past two years), and **breakeven always**, because breakeven is the one number neither side can spin. Where no draw record exists, say so plainly rather than silently falling back to capacity percentages that flatter the offerer. |
| DT-08 | Template pre-fill is a convenience | D-03's framing | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary — **with a hazard the framing hides.** Pre-fill means the hundredth offer is composed *without being read*, so a stale template term (a merch cut from two years ago, a curfew that changed with the licence) silently becomes a real deal term nobody chose. `meta/personas.md` names the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as taking the lazy path, and demands the design make the lazy path the correct path. Applied here: template-derived terms are marked as template-derived, and any term whose **source room record changed since the template was cut** is surfaced at compose. The template must not be a way to stop looking. |
| DT-09 | A Musician composing an offer to a venue is an offer | The Role Matrix's "composes offers when selling their own date direct" | ❌ REJECTED as stated, and the Role Lens is sharpened rather than contradicted. **An offer runs from the party committing money.** A musician approaching a room to ask for a gig is an **enquiry** (17.07) — it has no expiry, no approval chain, and no binding force, and routing a speculative pitch into machinery designed to *bind* an entity would be a category error with real consequences. The Musician composes a genuine offer in two cases, both of which make them the money: a **self-promoted show** (they hire the room and offer the support slot), and **offering a support slot their own deal gives them control of** (17.14 D-03 — slot control is an explicit deal term). `Full` is earned through those paths. The distinction matters because it decides whether an artist's approach carries an expiry clock and an approval chain — and it should carry neither. |
| DT-10 | The rider belongs in the offer's free text | Where it goes today, in the email the offer is a paragraph in | ❌ REJECTED, twice over. First, the rider is domain 18's structured artifact; embedding a copy in an offer creates two sources that will contradict, and the one that is a paragraph will win the argument nobody can settle. Second and worse, **riders carry economic obligations** — hospitality buyout, backline hire, ground transport, room nights — that are T1 terms wearing T3 clothing. A rider pasted into notes is the escape hatch (DT-03) at industrial scale: not one smuggled £500 bonus but a page of them. The offer **references** a rider version (D-18); economic rider lines are routed into the grammar. |

- Touches **17.03.01 Deal Term Grammar & Deal Types** ([../17.03-deal-structures-economics/17.03.01-deal-term-grammar-types.md](../17.03-deal-structures-economics/17.03.01-deal-term-grammar-types.md)) — **the T1 tier of this feature's taxonomy is exactly 17.03.01's vocabulary.** Q-01's resolution here is only as good as that grammar's coverage: every deal type the industry has and the grammar lacks becomes a T3 free-text term, i.e. an unsettled one. **This feature's automation claim is capped by domain Q-08** (closed vocabulary vs open expression language). Currency + FX basis are T1 per that file's DT-03.
- Touches **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([../../16-venues-studios-spaces/](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) — **shared entity**: room capacity, curfew and backline are validated against the room record, not asserted in the offer. **This feature requires 16 to model capacity per configuration** (DT-05), which it may not; and it requires the room record to carry a change history, because a sent offer snapshots it (D-13). Off-platform rooms make every room term Operator-asserted and `unverified` — a real and common case (independent promoters).
- Touches **19 Ticketing & Box Office** ([../../19-ticketing-box-office/](../../19-ticketing-box-office/)) — ticket scaling assumptions in the offer become the actual scaling at on-sale; a mismatch is a settlement dispute in waiting. **DT-05 propagates here**: the manifest is per capacity-configuration, or the offer's capacity and the box office's count are two different numbers wearing one name. Bonus thresholds referencing "sellout" must resolve to 19's count definitions (17.03.01).
- Touches **17.09.01 Settlement Sheet Computation** ([../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.01-settlement-sheet-computation.md](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.01-settlement-sheet-computation.md)) — **the deal structure authored here IS the settlement formula.** This is the domain's core insight and the reason booking and settlement are one domain.
- Touches **17.09.05 Settlement Signoff & Variance** ([../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md](../17.09-settlement-reconciliation/17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md)) — **trigger dependency created here**: D-05 requires `NOT SETTLED` T3 terms to render on the settlement sheet and be explicitly acknowledged at signoff. Without that, the escape-hatch resolution is a stamp nobody ever sees, and Q-02 is not actually resolved. **This is a hard dependency, not a nice-to-have.**
- Touches **17.08 Agency Representation & Commission** ([../17.08-agency-representation-commission/](../17.08-agency-representation-commission/)) — **DT-06, the sharpest break.** The offer's fee is gross to the artist side; the agent's commission comes off it and is invisible to the Operator by design (domain Role Matrix). The outcomes render must be **role-scoped** or it tells the musician a number they will not receive. Requires 17.08.01's commission terms to be readable from a live offer, artist-side only.
- Touches **17.11.01 Verified Draw Record** ([../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/17.11.01-verified-draw-record.md](../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/17.11.01-verified-draw-record.md)) — **DT-07**: attendance levels anchor to the act's verified draw in that market (≥3 records / 24 months), not to capacity. Also **outbound**: related-party offers (D-15) and unverified off-platform rooms must be excluded from what feeds 17.11 — a self-dealt or unverified price is not a market price.
- Touches **17.11.02 Market Comparables** / **17.11.03 Offer Guidance** ([../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/](../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/)) — draw data informs what to offer. **Permission tension**: D-10's draw-anchored render shows the Operator an evidence-based attendance number for this act. Whether the Operator may see the artist's draw at all is domain **Q-07** ("whose settlement data may inform whose decision") landing squarely inside this feature's render.
- Touches **17.01.02 Hold Ladder & Priority** ([../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.02-hold-ladder-priority.md](../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.02-hold-ladder-priority.md)) — **D-08**: an offer against a 2nd-position hold must render that position as a term. An offer that conceals the recipient's ladder position is a lie of omission, and the artist spends a 48h approval chain (CX-03) on a date they may never get.
- Touches **17.01.03 Challenge, Release & Expiry** ([../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md](../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md)) — **CX-04's unmodelled gap, now resolved** (D-07): offer expiry may exceed hold expiry; it warns, discloses on the offer, and offers a one-click hold extension (executed there, not here). Blocking is incoherent while cold offers are permitted.
- Touches **17.01.01 Availability Calendar** ([../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md](../17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md)) — composing on a date already **confirmed** to another act is blocked; composing on a merely held or contested date is not.
- Touches **17.02.02 Counteroffer Thread & Versions** ([./17.02.02-counteroffer-thread-versions.md](./17.02.02-counteroffer-thread-versions.md)) — composition emits v1 (CX-01). Send is idempotent: an ambiguous timeout must not fork the thread. The immutable snapshot (D-13) is what makes the thread evidentiary.
- Touches **17.02.03 Offer Approval Chain & Entity Authority** ([./17.02.03-offer-approval-chain.md](./17.02.03-offer-approval-chain.md)) — **permission**: composition is never blocked, only *send* is (above the buy side's internal threshold). Drafting a deal you cannot sign is normal work. **D-15**: a related-party composer is excluded from their own side's approval on that offer. **D-16**: a non-platform recipient can read but cannot accept — acceptance needs an entity to run a chain against.
- Touches **17.02.04 Offer Expiry & Withdrawal** ([./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md](./17.02.04-offer-expiry-withdrawal.md)) — expiry is authored here as a term (CX-04), pre-filled from the hold's expiry where one exists and left blank where none does.
- Touches **17.06 Radius Clause & Exclusivity** ([../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md](../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md)) — **permission asymmetry (D-19)**: the artist's radius conflicts surface to the artist on receipt only; showing them to the Operator discloses the artist's other bookings. The Operator's own granted exclusions surface to the Operator at compose — their term, their room.
- Touches **17.14 Bill Construction & Support Slot Offers** ([../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md](../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md)) — **D-20** contributes this feature's half of 17.14 Q-01's lighter path: a six-field short form for slot offers. Slot control is a term authored here; whether the full lighter path is only a short form is 17.14's call, not this file's.
- Touches **18 Show Production & Touring** ([../../18-show-production-touring/](../../18-show-production-touring/)) — **DT-10**: the offer *references* a rider version; it never embeds one. Economic rider lines (hospitality buyout, backline hire, room nights) are T1 terms and must be routed into the grammar, not left in a paragraph.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity**: the composing side is an entity, not a person (DT-04), and related-party detection (D-15) needs 01's membership graph to know that this Operator is a member of that band. Entity claim by a non-platform recipient (D-16) is 01's flow.
- Touches **17.07 Booking Enquiry Inbox & RFQ** ([../17.07-booking-enquiry-inbox-rfq.md](../17.07-booking-enquiry-inbox-rfq.md)) — **boundary, per DT-09**: an artist approaching a room is an enquiry, not an offer. The line is who commits money. An enquiry has no expiry clock and no approval chain, and it must not acquire them by being modelled here.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Structured Economic Terms → Computed Statement engine** — the same "typed terms compile to an evaluable formula" pattern serves 10 (royalty statements), 11 (licence fees) and 05 (service invoices). Recorded in the domain CX file.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Unsettled-Term Disclosure** (D-05) — "free text is permitted, stamped, and surfaced at the moment of money" generalises to any structured agreement with a bespoke escape hatch: 09 (split sheets with side letters), 11 (licence special conditions), 05 (SOW scope notes). Proposed for the domain CX file.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Multi-Currency & FX** (17.03.01 DT-03) — explicit currency, never country-inferred (D-12). Serves 05, 13, 14, 17, 19, 23.
- Not-product: **free-text lint execution**. Whether the economic-signal scan runs client-side, server-side, or both is an architecture concern, not a behaviour. Routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. The **behaviour** locked here: it fires on blur and again before send, and send cannot complete without it.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every economic term is typed data, never prose | Settlement is only computable if the offer was structured. This is the domain's founding insight (domain index D-01). |
| D-02 | Offers render as outcomes at attendance levels, not only as terms | Both sides must read the same document the same way; divergent readings are a primary settlement-dispute source (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Room templates are first-class, not a convenience | An Operator composing 200 offers a year is the target user; the marginal offer must cost minutes or they stay in email. |
| D-04 | **Three-tier term taxonomy: T1 economic (must be structured) / T2 qualifying (must be structured) / T3 bespoke (free text)** | Resolves Q-01. The tier test is mechanical: *does this term change a number at settlement?* → T1. *Is it machine-checkable against the room record or consumed by advancing/announce?* → T2. Otherwise T3. Without a stated test, "which terms are structured" is re-litigated per offer and the grammar erodes. |
| D-05 | **Free text is permitted, stamped `NOT SETTLED`, and carried onto the settlement sheet as an unsettled-terms panel requiring explicit bilateral acknowledgment at signoff (17.09.05)** | Resolves Q-02. The hatch cannot be closed (DT-03) and pretending otherwise drives users to email. So make the leak **visible at the moment it costs money** — the smuggled "plus £500 if it sells out" is shown to both parties in the room at signing, which is exactly when someone raises it. This is the platform's own thesis (`meta/problem-statement.md`: be present when the fact becomes contested) applied to a text box. |
| D-06 | **Free text is linted for economic signals: a contradiction with a structured term is a hard stop; an unmatched economic signal warns and requires Convert-or-acknowledge. The lint over-flags by design (≥95% recall; precision not optimised)** | A false flag costs one click; a false pass costs a settlement dispute and a relationship. Structured terms are authoritative because a document stating two numbers is a pre-loaded dispute. The acknowledgment is **bilateral** — the counterparty sees the stamp too, so it is not a private confession. |
| D-07 | **An offer may outlive its hold: warn, disclose the hold's expiry as a rendered term on the offer, offer a one-click hold extension — never block** | Resolves Q-03 and CX-04's gap. Blocking is **incoherent while cold offers are permitted**: an offer with no hold is strictly weaker than an offer on a hold that dies Thursday, so forbidding the stronger while allowing the weaker is arbitrary. Disclosure puts the fact where the decision is made — with the artist deciding whether to spend a 48h approval chain on it. |
| D-08 | **Hold position is a rendered term on the offer** | An offer against a 2nd-position hold that does not say so is a lie of omission. The artist spends their approval chain (CX-03, a scarce and slow resource for a five-member band) on a date they may never get. Disclosure costs the promoter negotiating leverage; concealment costs the platform its reason to exist. |
| D-09 | **The outcomes render is role-scoped: the Operator sees cost to the room; the Musician sees their net after agent commission (17.08)** | DT-06. The offer's fee is gross to the artist *side*; the agent's cut comes off it. Rendering "you get £2,400" to a musician who will receive £2,160 is a lie **in exactly the direction the platform claims to fix** — a number they believe that is not the number they get. Commission remains invisible to the Operator (domain Role Matrix: 17.08 Operator = `None`). |
| D-10 | **Attendance levels anchor to the act's verified draw record in that market (≥3 records within 24 months); breakeven is always rendered** | DT-07. Choosing the levels is persuasion, and a platform that lets the offerer choose them has taken a side. Three records because one draw is weather; 24 months because a draw decays. Breakeven always, because it is the one number neither side can spin. Where no draw record exists, say so — do not silently fall back to capacity percentages that flatter the offerer. |
| D-11 | **Capacity is per configuration, and the configuration is a T2 term** | DT-05. A room is 450 seated / 600 standing / 380 balcony-closed. "Capacity 600" without the configuration is the settlement dispute already written down, with both parties reading honestly. Requires 16 to model configurations and 19 to make the manifest per-configuration. |
| D-12 | **Currency is explicit and required at compose; never inferred from the room's country** | 17.03.01 DT-03. A €-denominated guarantee settled in £ is normal on international bookings, and a country-derived default is silently wrong on exactly the deals with the highest stakes. FX basis and fixing date are T1 terms. |
| D-13 | **A sent version snapshots every referenced room term; later room-record changes raise a thread notice and never mutate the version** | Silent mutation of a sent offer destroys the evidentiary property that makes the version thread worth having (17.02.02 DT-02) — the thread is provenance-at-source applied to booking. A capacity that changed after sending is information both sides need, not a correction to apply behind their backs. |
| D-14 | **Drafts are entity-owned with a single-editor claim (5-min idle release); only send is the commitment** | DT-04. The buy side is two people (talent buyer + GM). Personal drafts leave the GM approving something they never saw. Last-write-wins on a money document is not acceptable. Drafts autosave; a network failure loses nothing. |
| D-15 | **Related-party offers (composer is a member of the receiving artist entity) are permitted, stamped, exclude the composer from their own side's approval, and are excluded from 17.11's comparables** | The multi-hyphenate makes this ordinary, not exotic — a venue owner booking their own band is normal and blocking it would be absurd. But a self-dealt price is not a market price, and a member approving their own offer is the 17.02.03 authority failure with the mask off. Disclose and fence, do not prohibit. |
| D-16 | **An offer reaches a non-platform recipient as a readable link; acceptance requires claiming the entity** | A platform that only works if both sides already joined cannot win the first side. Read without an account; bind only with one — the approval chain (17.02.03) needs an entity to run against. Read-free / bind-gated is the honest split. |
| D-17 | **Exported PDF is a view, never a source; it carries a version stamp and a back-link** | Agents forward offers by email — universal, and not worth fighting. But a PDF is the prose this feature exists to eliminate, so it must never be the authority. The stamp lets a forwarded reader know whether they are reading the live version or a superseded one. |
| D-18 | **The offer references a rider version; it never embeds one, and economic rider lines are routed into the grammar** | DT-10. Embedding duplicates 18's structured artifact and the paragraph copy will win the argument. Worse, riders carry real economic obligations (hospitality buyout, backline hire, room nights) — a pasted rider is the escape hatch at industrial scale. |
| D-19 | **The artist's radius conflicts surface to the artist only; the Operator's own granted exclusions surface to the Operator** | Showing an Operator "this act is restricted in Manchester until March" discloses **who else booked them** — the artist's confidential pipeline, handed to a rival buyer. Same structural logic as 17.08 being invisible to the Operator (domain index Role Matrix note 3). |
| D-20 | **Slot offers (slot ≠ headline) default to a six-field short form: deal type = flat, fee, date, slot, set length, expiry** | 17.14 DT-03/D-04. A £50 local opener booked Tuesday for Friday will not survive sixty fields, and excluding them excludes the exact population `meta/personas.md` is built around — working musicians whose careers are made of £50 opening slots. Everything else inherits from the room template unedited. |

## Open Questions

|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | **Does the over-flagging lint (D-06) survive the 200th offer, or does it train Operators to click through?** The recall/precision trade is deliberately lopsided, and warning fatigue is the classic way a well-designed safeguard becomes a rubber stamp — at which point the acknowledgment is worse than nothing, because it looks like consent. Options: keep it lopsided; tune precision once real free-text corpus exists; or drop the compose-time warn entirely and rely solely on the settlement-sheet disclosure (D-05), which fires later but cannot be habituated. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-02 | **Are related-party offers (D-15) permitted at all, or blocked?** Disclose-and-fence is this file's call, but it is a values decision: self-dealing is real and ordinary for the multi-hyphenate, and also the cleanest way to manufacture a fake market price. The fence (exclusion from 17.11 comparables) protects the data; it does not protect a band member from a bandmate who owns the room. | User | `/ideate-validate` |
| Q-03 | **Do non-platform recipients (D-16) get the full outcomes render, or a gated preview?** Full render is the honest product and the strongest possible advert; a gated preview converts better and makes the artist's first experience of the platform a signup wall placed between them and their own deal. A growth decision with a values edge. | User | `/create-prd` |
