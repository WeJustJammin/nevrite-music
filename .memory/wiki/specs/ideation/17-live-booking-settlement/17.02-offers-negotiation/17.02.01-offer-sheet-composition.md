# Feature: Offer Sheet Composition

> **Number**: 17.02.01
> **Parent**: [Offers & Negotiation](./17.02-offers-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Authoring the offer — the document that proposes a date, a deal structure, and the logistics that
qualify it. This is the promoter's primary instrument and the moment the deal first becomes
**structured data** rather than prose in an email. That structuring is what makes settlement
computable later (17.09.01); an offer written as a paragraph is a settlement that must be done by
hand with a calculator at 1am.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Composes offers when selling their own date direct — the working musician booking their own pub gig has no agent and is their own promoter's counterparty. Receives and reads offers made to them, with the deal structure rendered as plain consequences ("if 300 people come, you get £2,400") rather than as the formula. |
| Producer | None | No role in live deal composition. |
| Operator | Full | Composes the offer: date, deal structure (17.03), ticket scaling assumptions, curfew, support slots, merch terms, comp allowance, deposit terms. Works from room templates so the hundredth offer takes minutes, not an hour of copy-paste from an old email. |
| Fan | None | Offers are never public. A proposed show is not a show, and leaking one damages both principals. |

## Behavior

An offer is a **structured document**: a deal structure drawn from 17.03's grammar, plus the
logistics that are actually part of the bargain — set length, curfew, comp allowance, merch cut,
soundcheck time, backline provision, deposit terms, and the offer's own expiry (CX-04).

The critical design property: **every economic term is typed data, not prose.** The domain's thesis
is that settlement is only automatable if the offer was structured — "$2,000 versus 85% of net after
$6,000 expenses, whichever is greater" must be a formula the system can later evaluate against real
counts, not a sentence a human re-reads at 1am.

Templates matter more than they appear to. An Operator running 200 shows a year composes from a room
template; the marginal offer must cost minutes.

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens a held date (17.01.02) or a target date and starts an offer.
2. System pre-fills from the room template — capacity, standard merch cut, curfew, house comp
   allowance — so only the act-specific terms need thought.
3. Operator selects a deal structure from 17.03's grammar and fills its parameters (guarantee,
   split %, expense cap, bonus thresholds).
4. System renders the deal's consequences at several attendance levels — the offer is shown as
   outcomes, not just as terms, so both sides read the same thing.
5. Operator sets the offer's expiry and sends. Version v1 enters the thread (17.02.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Offer composed with no hold on the date | Permitted — cold offers are real practice (Q-02, index) | Warning that the date is unheld and could be taken |
| Offer expiry set beyond the underlying hold's expiry | Incoherent — an offer good until Friday on a hold that dies Thursday | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`; currently nothing detects this (CX-04) |
| Deal structure references a capacity above the room's real capacity (domain 16) | Should be caught — the potential gross is a lie, and settlement will disagree with the offer | Validation against domain 16's room record |
| Operator composes an offer they lack internal authority to make | Buy-side sign-off threshold (talent buyer → GM) | Blocked at send with the named approver |
| Free-text terms bolted onto a structured offer | The escape hatch that destroys settlement automation — and it will be used | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — see DT-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Composition started | Private to the composing side; not visible to the counterparty (Q-03, index) |
| Pending internal sign-off | Above the composer's authority threshold | Blocked with named approver |
| Sent | Offer issued | v1 in the thread; expiry clock running |
| Superseded | A counter arrived | Read-only; visible in version history |
| Withdrawn / Expired | See 17.02.04 | Dead; reason and timestamp recorded |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An offer is primarily a fee | Naive modelling — "how much are you paying" | ❌ REJECTED: the fee is often the *least* contested term. Merch cut, comp allowance, curfew, expense cap and support-slot control routinely decide whether a deal is good, and a headliner will trade £1,000 of guarantee for the merch cut without hesitating. An offer modelled as fee + notes cannot express the trade, and cannot settle. |
| DT-02 | Offers should be rendered as terms | Document-fidelity instinct | ❌ REJECTED: a domain expert renders an offer as **outcomes at attendance levels**, because "85% of net after $6,000" means nothing to the artist who has to decide. Both sides misreading the same document differently is a primary source of settlement disputes (17.09.06) — and the artist in `meta/personas.md` is explicitly someone who signs things they don't understand. Rendering consequences is a provenance safeguard, not a UI nicety. |
| DT-03 | Structured terms are sufficient; free text can be banned | The domain's own automation thesis, taken to its conclusion | ❌ REJECTED: real offers always carry bespoke terms ("no support act may use the house drum kit", "artist requires the Tuesday load-in"). Banning free text drives users back to email and loses the whole deal. **The honest design problem is that free text is an automation escape hatch that will be abused to smuggle economic terms out of the formula** — an Operator will write "plus £500 if it sells out" in the notes and settlement will not see it. Unresolved (Q-02); this is the sharpest tension in the sub-domain. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every economic term is typed data, never prose | Settlement is only computable if the offer was structured. This is the domain's founding insight (domain index D-01). |
| D-02 | Offers render as outcomes at attendance levels, not only as terms | Both sides must read the same document the same way; divergent readings are a primary settlement-dispute source (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Room templates are first-class, not a convenience | An Operator composing 200 offers a year is the target user; the marginal offer must cost minutes or they stay in email. |
