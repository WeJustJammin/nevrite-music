# Feature: Radius Clause & Exclusivity Tracking

> **Number**: 17.06
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The contractual promise not to play nearby, near the date. A radius clause is a geofence plus a time
window — "no performance within 150 miles, 60 days either side" — attached to a deal, checked against
everything else on the artist's calendar, and occasionally waived. It is one of the few live terms
that constrains dates the artist has not booked yet, which is exactly why it is forgotten until it is
breached.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Bound by clauses on their own dates, and — critically — **warned before booking a date that would breach one**. This is the feature's entire value to them: the breach is normally discovered by an angry promoter, not by the artist, because nobody remembers a clause they signed eight months ago. Requests waivers. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Imposes clauses when their deal warrants it, sees breaches against their own shows, and grants or refuses waivers. A promoter who has spent money marketing a show does not want the act playing 20 miles away three weeks earlier. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. The fan experiences a radius clause only as an artist mysteriously not playing their city — which is the point of it. |

## Behavior

A radius clause is a **deal term** (17.03.01 grammar) with three parameters: distance, time window
either side of the date, and any carve-outs (festivals, private shows, support slots, and radio
sessions are commonly excluded).

Its work happens at two moments:

1. **Prospectively** — when the artist considers a new date, the system checks it against every live
   clause and warns *before* the hold, not after the contract. This is where the value is.
2. **Retrospectively** — when a booking is confirmed, existing clauses are re-evaluated in case a
   breach was created by someone else's action.

Waivers are routine: a promoter will often say yes to a festival 100 miles away because refusing
costs them goodwill and gains them nothing.

### Happy Path

1. Operator includes a radius clause in the offer (17.02.01) — 150 miles, 60 days either side.
2. Deal is agreed and the contract executes (17.04); the clause becomes live on the artist's calendar.
3. Later, the artist's agent considers a date 90 miles away, 30 days before.
4. System warns at the point of the hold (17.01.02), naming the clause, the show it protects, and the
   contract it comes from.
5. Agent requests a waiver; the protecting Operator grants or refuses; the decision is recorded
   against the clause.

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | No clauses on the artist | Feature invisible |
| Live | Clause active from an executed contract | Clause visible on the calendar with its protected show |
| Warned | A candidate date would breach | Warning at hold time naming clause, show and contract |
| Breaching | A confirmed date breaches a live clause | Flagged to both the artist and the protecting Operator |
| Waived | Protecting Operator granted a waiver | Recorded against the clause with who granted it and when |
| Expired | Time window passed | Clause dormant; retained as history |

| DT-01 | This is a sub-domain — it has clause definition, breach detection, and waiver management | Node Classification Gate, first pass | ❌ REJECTED: it is **one constraint with a lifecycle**. Definition is a deal term (17.03.01 owns the grammar), detection is a check, and a waiver is an exception to the check. None of the three has independent internal features that interact — this is "Password Reset with edge cases", not "AI Assistant". Classified as a feature. |
| DT-02 | The value is enforcement — catching breaches | The obvious reading of "tracking" in the candidate's name | ❌ REJECTED: the value is **prospective warning**. | DT-03 | A radius clause binds the artist | The plain reading of every radius clause ever written | ❌ REJECTED as underspecified, and this is the interesting one. `meta/personas.md` establishes the multi-hyphenate as the structural centre: **one human, three bands**. Does a clause on Band A's show stop the same drummer playing the same city with Band B? The contract says "the Artist", and the Artist is Band A — but the promoter's actual concern is audience cannibalisation, which the drummer's other band may or may not cause. **The industry has never had to answer this precisely because no system ever knew.** This platform will know, and will therefore be asked. ## Decisions
