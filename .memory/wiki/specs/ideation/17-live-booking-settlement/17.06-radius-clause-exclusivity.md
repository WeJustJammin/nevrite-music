# Feature: Radius Clause & Exclusivity Tracking

> **Number**: 17.06
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-30

## Overview

The contractual promise not to play nearby, near the date. A radius clause is a geofence plus a time
window — "no performance within 150 miles, 60 days either side" — attached to a deal, checked against
everything else on the artist's calendar, and occasionally waived. It is one of the few live terms
that constrains dates the artist has not booked yet, which is exactly why it is forgotten until it is
breached.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Bound by clauses on their own dates, and — critically — **warned before booking a date that would breach one**. This is the feature's entire value to them: the breach is normally discovered by an angry promoter, not by the artist, because nobody remembers a clause they signed eight months ago. **Raises waiver requests** (D-04) — the agent may request; only the **artist principal in person** may break glass on a blocked confirm, and only under a live challenge clock (D-05). |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Imposes clauses when their deal warrants it, sees breaches against their own shows, and **grants or refuses waiver requests inside a clock, on an escalating channel ladder** (D-04). A promoter who has spent money marketing a show does not want the act playing 20 miles away three weeks earlier. Told **at the moment** a break-glass override is used against their clause, not at announce (D-05) — which is the only reason an override is defensible at all. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. The fan experiences a radius clause only as an artist mysteriously not playing their city — which is the point of it. |

## Behavior

A radius clause is a **deal term** (17.03.01 grammar) with three parameters: distance, time window
either side of the date, and any carve-outs (festivals, private shows, support slots, and radio
sessions are commonly excluded).

Its work happens at three moments:

1. **Prospectively** — when the artist considers a new date, the system checks it against every live
   clause and warns *before* the hold, not after the contract. This is where the value is.
2. **At confirm, as a permission** — a breach against an existing confirmed booking is a **hard
   confirmation precondition** ([17.01.04](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md)
   C-06/D-13). The obligation is breached the moment the second date is confirmed, so this is the last
   moment a refusal is still cheaper than a lawsuit. The block is not absolute — it has a door, and the
   door is the waiver-request instrument below (D-04).
3. **Retrospectively** — when a booking is confirmed, existing clauses are re-evaluated in case a
   breach was created by someone else's action. This is the case the artist did not cause and cannot be
   debited for (D-06).

Waivers are routine: a promoter will often say yes to a festival 100 miles away because refusing
costs them goodwill and gains them nothing. That is exactly why a block absent a waiver is defensible
and a block *without a way to ask* is not — the whole weight of D-04 is that the ask has to be an
instrument with a clock, not an email the artist sends and hopes about.

### The waiver-request instrument

A waiver is a **permission held by the constraining Operator**, and the request for one is a
first-class in-app action, not a message. It exists because C-06's door has to be openable inside the
window of the decision it blocks (D-04).

| Parameter | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Who may request** | The artist side — the same authority that may place a hold and negotiate the offer (17.02.03's negotiating authority). Requesting a waiver binds nobody, so it needs no principal. |
| **Who decides** | The **constraining Operator**: the party whose clause it is, named on the contract the clause came from (17.04). Nobody else, and never the platform. |
| **Clock** | The request carries its own expiry. **Hard cap**: it may never outlive the deadline of the decision it exists to unblock — under a challenge ([17.01.03](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md)), the request expires no later than the challenge deadline, on the same offer-expiry-cap logic. A waiver granted after the date is lost is not a door. The default duration outside a live clock is open (Q-04). |
| **Escalation** | The same channel ladder [17.01.03](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md) already specifies, aimed at the constraining Operator: T+0 in-app + email to their registered contact; **25% elapsed, unacknowledged** — SMS/push per their escalation contract; **50%** — their principal directly, bypassing the delegate; **75%** — the *requester* is told the request is likely to lapse, so a human can make a commercial judgement instead of the clock. Acknowledgement stops the escalation, not the clock. |
| **Grant** | Recorded against the clause with who granted it, when, and its scope (which date, which venue). The clause moves to `Waived` **for that date only** — a waiver is never blanket. C-06 then passes. |
| **Refuse** | Recorded with the refusal and any stated reason. C-06 still blocks. A refusal is a legitimate answer and the artist's remedy is commercial: move the date, or accept losing it. |
| **Lapse** | Silence grants nothing. **A lapsed request is not a waiver** — C-06 still blocks, and the only remaining path is the break-glass, which exists only while a challenge clock is live (17.01.04 C-06, 17.01.03 D-15). |
| **Repeat requests** | One live request per clause per candidate date. A refused request may not be immediately re-sent — pestering the party who holds the permission is how a routine yes becomes a policy no. |

**Break-glass**, and only under a live challenge clock: the confirming party may override C-06 if and
only if all four hold — (1) a 17.01.03 challenge clock is live on the hold, (2) the **artist
principal** acts in person and never a delegate, (3) the constraining Operator is notified **at the
moment of use**, not after, and (4) the confirm writes a named `radius-break-glass` breach event
attributed to the confirming party into [17.12](./17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md).
This is priced, not permitted: the injured party learns of it in the same instant, and the record
follows the person who chose it (D-05).

### Happy Path

1. Operator includes a radius clause in the offer (17.02.01) — 150 miles, 60 days either side.
2. Deal is agreed and the contract executes (17.04); the clause becomes live on the artist's calendar.
3. Later, the artist's agent considers a date 90 miles away, 30 days before.
4. System warns at the point of the hold (17.01.02), naming the clause, the show it protects, and the
   contract it comes from.
5. Agent raises a **waiver request** against the protecting Operator (D-04) — a timed, escalating
   in-app instrument, not an email.
6. The protecting Operator grants or refuses inside the clock; the decision is recorded against the
   clause with who decided and when.
7. Granted: the clause reads `Waived` for that date and confirmation proceeds (17.01.04 C-06).
   Refused or lapsed: confirmation stays blocked, and the artist's remedy is commercial — move the
   date, or lose it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Breach discovered after both dates are confirmed | The expensive case — one show must move or a waiver must be begged | Warning must fire at hold time, not at contract time (D-01) |
| Confirm attempted with a live conflict and no waiver on file | **Blocked** (17.01.04 C-06). The block names the constraining contract and Operator and offers the waiver request inline — a block with no door is a block the industry routes around, and an off-platform date is the platform's worst outcome | "This breaches your radius clause on {show} held by {Operator}. Request a waiver — you cannot confirm until it is granted." |
| Constraining Operator never answers the waiver request | The request lapses on its own clock. **Silence is not consent** — C-06 still blocks. The escalation ladder has already tried in-app, email, SMS and their principal by then (D-04) | Requester at 75% elapsed: "{Operator} has not responded. This request expires {T}." At lapse: "No waiver. This date cannot be confirmed." |
| Waiver refused, and the artist's challenge clock expires in four hours | **Break-glass** — the single exception in 17.01.04's precondition set, and only here (D-05). Artist principal in person; the constraining Operator is told in the same instant; a `radius-break-glass` event is recorded against the confirming party and feeds 17.12 | Principal: "{Operator} refused. Confirming breaches {contract}. They will be told the moment you do, and this is permanent." The agent sees the action disabled and the principal who must act |
| Agent tries to break glass on the principal's behalf at 2am | **Refused.** Booking authority is not breach authority — this is a third authority slot alongside offer-binding (17.02.03 D-03) and settlement-signing (17.09.05 D-07), owned by domain 01 | "Only {principal} can do this. They have been notified." |
| Break-glass attempted on an ordinary confirm with no live challenge | **Refused** — there is no clock, so there is no latency argument, so there is no exception. Wait for the waiver or move the date | "No override exists here. This date can only be confirmed with a waiver from {Operator}." |
| The artist's clause is breached by someone *else's* later confirm | Not the artist's act and not attributable to them. Distinguished from break-glass in the States table and **excluded from the 17.12 signal** (D-06) — debiting a party for a third party's write is the failure 17.01.03 D-12 names | Both the artist and the protecting Operator see the breach flagged with its cause: "created by {other party}'s confirmation on {date}" |
| Carve-out ambiguity: is a festival 80 miles away covered? | Festivals, private shows and support slots are commonly carved out — and the carve-out is usually vague | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — carve-outs are where clauses actually get argued (Q-01) |
| Artist plays under a different band name within the radius | Multi-hyphenate reality: the drummer's other band plays the same city. Does the clause bind the *human* or the *entity*? | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — genuinely unresolved and structurally important (Q-02) |
| Clause on a show that is later cancelled | Does the clause die with the show? Usually yes; not always | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Two clauses from different promoters overlap and contradict | Both are live; the artist is squeezed between them | Both surfaced; no automatic resolution |
| Waiver granted verbally | The most common way a waiver happens, and it leaves no record. **Now materially worse under C-06**: a waiver that exists only on a phone call is not "on file", so the confirm still blocks and the artist is refused a date both parties already agreed to. D-04 makes the on-platform path fast enough to be the lazy path, which is the only lever the product has — it cannot make a promoter type | The block names what is missing: "{Operator} has not recorded a waiver. Ask them to confirm it here." Whether the *artist* may attest to a verbal grant, and what that attestation is worth against a hard block, is the open half — the same capture hole as 17.02.02 DT-03, and the same question its Q-02 asks about unconfirmed transcriptions |
| Radius measured how — driving distance or straight line? | Materially different at 150 miles | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a real, unglamorous specification gap |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | No clauses on the artist | Feature invisible |
| Live | Clause active from an executed contract | Clause visible on the calendar with its protected show |
| Warned | A candidate date would breach | Warning at hold time naming clause, show and contract |
| Blocked | Confirm attempted against a live clause with no waiver on file | 17.01.04 C-06 refuses the commit; the constraining contract and Operator are named and the waiver request is offered inline |
| Waiver requested | Waiver request raised against the constraining Operator | Both sides see the request, its deadline and where it sits on the escalation ladder (D-04) |
| Waiver refused | Constraining Operator declined inside the clock | Recorded with who refused, when, and any stated reason. The block stands |
| Breaching (break-glass) | The artist principal overrode the block under a live challenge clock (D-05) | **Attributable.** Flagged to the artist and the protecting Operator **at the moment of use**, with the named `radius-break-glass` event carried into 17.12 against the confirming party |
| Breaching (third-party) | A confirmed date breaches a live clause through **another party's** later confirm, not the clause-bound artist's act | Flagged to both the artist and the protecting Operator with its cause named. **Not attributable to the artist and excluded from the 17.12 signal** (D-06) |
| Waived | Protecting Operator granted a waiver | Recorded against the clause with who granted it, when, and for which date — never blanket |
| Expired | Time window passed | Clause dormant; retained as history |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a sub-domain — it has clause definition, breach detection, and waiver management | Node Classification Gate, first pass | ❌ REJECTED: it is **one constraint with a lifecycle**. Definition is a deal term (17.03.01 owns the grammar), detection is a check, and a waiver is an exception to the check. None of the three has independent internal features that interact — this is "Password Reset with edge cases", not "AI Assistant". Classified as a feature. |
| DT-02 | The value is enforcement — catching breaches | The obvious reading of "tracking" in the candidate's name | ❌ REJECTED: the value is **prospective warning**. A detected breach is already a disaster — two contracts exist and one must break. The artist in `meta/personas.md` is a portfolio worker with dates across three bands; they will not remember a clause signed eight months ago, and nobody currently tells them. Warning at hold time is nearly all the value; detection after the fact is a nicer way to receive bad news. |
| DT-03 | A radius clause binds the artist | The plain reading of every radius clause ever written | ❌ REJECTED as underspecified, and this is the interesting one. `meta/personas.md` establishes the multi-hyphenate as the structural centre: **one human, three bands**. Does a clause on Band A's show stop the same drummer playing the same city with Band B? The contract says "the Artist", and the Artist is Band A — but the promoter's actual concern is audience cannibalisation, which the drummer's other band may or may not cause. **The industry has never had to answer this precisely because no system ever knew.** This platform will know, and will therefore be asked. Genuinely unresolved (Q-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The warning fires at hold time, not at contract time | A breach found at contract time is already a disaster with two live contracts (DT-02). Prospective warning is nearly all the value. |
| D-02 | Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain | One constraint with a lifecycle; definition/detection/waiver have no independent interacting internals (DT-01). |
| D-03 | Whether a clause binds the entity or the human is escalated, not assumed | The multi-hyphenate is the platform's structural centre, and this is the first place the ambiguity bites (DT-03). |
| D-04 | The **waiver request is a first-class instrument**, not a message: named decider, its own clock capped by the decision it unblocks, escalation on 17.01.03's channel ladder, and recorded grant / refuse / lapse outcomes | Owner ratification, run-3 D6. 17.01.04 C-06 makes a breach a hard block; a block whose only door is a third party's response time is a block the industry routes around, and an off-platform date is the platform's worst outcome. The original counter-argument for warn-and-override (17.01.03 D-15 as first written) was about **latency**, not permission — "consent is obtainable, just not within 48h". This is the fix for the latency, which is why the permission rule gets to stand. Silence grants nothing: a lapsed request is not a waiver. |
| D-05 | The **break-glass is narrow and priced**: available only while a 17.01.03 challenge clock is live, exercisable only by the **artist principal in person**, notifying the constraining Operator **at the moment of use**, and recorded as a named `radius-break-glass` breach event | Owner ratification, run-3 D6. Without it, a block whose door is an unreachable promoter loses a real date and pushes the deal to the phone. With it unpriced, it is a click-through the artist taps at 1am under a 48h clock. All four conditions are conjunctive and each removes one way to make it cheap: no clock means no exception; principal-only means booking authority is not breach authority (a **third** authority slot alongside 17.02.03 D-03 offer-binding and 17.09.05 D-07 settlement-signing, for domain 01 to model); immediate notice means the injured party is never surprised at announce; the record means the choice follows the chooser. |
| D-06 | A breach the artist **caused** and a breach a **third party's later confirm** created are structurally distinct states, and only the first debits 17.12 | The States table previously carried one `Breaching` row, which read as an accusation against whoever the clause was on regardless of who acted. 17.01.03 D-12 already establishes that fault-free outcomes must never debit a reliability record; retrospective breach detection (moment 3) is exactly that case, and break-glass is exactly not. Collapsing the two is the most likely downstream implementation error in this feature. |

The run-3 D6 ratification licenses "a short clock" and no number, so no number is asserted here. | User | `/create-prd` |
