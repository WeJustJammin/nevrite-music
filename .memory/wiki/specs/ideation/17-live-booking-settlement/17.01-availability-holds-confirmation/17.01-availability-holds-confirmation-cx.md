## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Availability Calendar ↔ Hold Ladder

**Relationship**: The avail is the durable resource; the hold is a soft, prioritised claim layered
over it. Critically, **a hold does not consume the avail** — multiple holds coexist on one date in
ranked order, and the date continues to appear as holdable to a fourth party. This is
counter-intuitive to anyone modelling this as a normal booking system and is the most likely place
for an implementer to get it wrong.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: sees the full ladder on their room — who is in each position, when each expires.
- **Musician**: sees that a hold exists on their date and its position; identity visibility is
  contested (Q-02 in the index).
- **Producer**: not affected — no visibility into stage-date availability.
- **Fan**: not affected — holds are never public. A held date is not a show.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The avail is owned by whichever side published it (Operator for a
   room-date, Musician for an artist-date). Holds are owned by the placer. Both sides can hold the
   *same* logical date from opposite directions — the merge strategy is unresolved (Q-03).
2. **Trigger chain**: Placing a hold reads the avail but does not write it. If the avail is
   withdrawn while holds exist, the holds must be notified and voided — a compensating action, not
   a rollback.
3. **Permission intersection**: Publishing an avail grants no authority over holds placed on it.
   An Operator can refuse a hold but cannot reorder positions arbitrarily without breaking trust.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A new hold notifies the avail publisher and every superior position
   holder ("someone is behind you") — the latter is a genuine behavioural lever, not just an FYI.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two holds placed in the same instant race for position. Ordering
   must be server-assigned and monotonic; ties resolved by receipt timestamp, never by client clock.
   Every mutation carries a ladder version; a stale version rejects before mutation and is re-offered
   against the authoritative current ladder. No merge, automatic replay, lock, queue-behind, or
   last-write-wins path exists. This is the ratified DQ-04.06 / `r-78[0]` contract.

### CX-02: Hold Ladder ↔ Challenge, Release & Expiry

**Relationship**: Position N can challenge position N−1, starting a fixed countdown (24–48h) in
which the superior holder must confirm the date on real terms or drop it. This is the ladder's only
enforcement mechanism — without it, a 1st hold is a free indefinite option and the ladder is theatre.
Expiry is the passive twin: when a hold's clock runs out, everything below promotes by one.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: issues and adjudicates challenges; sets the window length per room policy.
- **Musician**: is the party who must actually answer a challenge — via their agent — and whose
  money is at risk if they confirm defensively.
- **Producer**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ladder is a single ordered list owned by the Operator. A challenge
   and a voluntary release can target the same position concurrently — release must win (the
   challenge is moot). The winning mutation advances the ladder version; the losing or stale action
   is rejected and re-offered against current state, never merged or replayed automatically.
2. **Trigger chain**: Challenge → countdown → (confirm | drop | timeout). Timeout is treated as
   drop. If the confirm path fails downstream (e.g. the deal is never agreed), the confirmation must
   be compensated by returning the ladder to its prior shape — which is lossy if others promoted
   meanwhile. **This is the sub-domain's hardest consistency problem.**
3. **Permission intersection**: Only a position holder may challenge, and only the position directly
   above. Skipping is forbidden — otherwise position 5 could destabilise position 1.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A challenge notifies the challenged party urgently (this is a
   time-critical, money-relevant event) and the Operator. On resolution, every position on the
   ladder is notified because everyone's position may have changed.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — expiry and challenge can fire simultaneously on adjacent
   positions, producing an ambiguous ladder. Resolution order must be deterministic and logged.

### CX-03: Challenge, Release & Expiry ↔ Confirmation & Announce Gate

**Relationship**: A won challenge, or the collapse of all superior holds, promotes a party to a
confirmable position. Confirmation is a hard, near-irreversible transition: it destroys every
inferior hold on the date and commits both sides to a deal.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: executes the confirmation; must notify the destroyed holds.
- **Musician**: must approve confirmation of their own date — an agent confirming without artist
  authority is a real and common failure.
- **Producer**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected until announce (see 17.01.04).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Confirmation writes to the ladder, the avail, and creates the booking
   record consumed by 17.02/17.04. Three writes that must be atomic — a partial confirm leaves a
   date that is neither held nor bookable.
2. **Trigger chain**: Confirm → destroy inferior holds → write avail → create booking → (deferred)
   announce. If deposit collection later fails, the booking reverts — but the destroyed holds do
   not come back. Compensating action only; no true rollback.
3. **Permission intersection**: Holding position 1 does not grant confirm authority on the artist
   side — that requires entity-level approval (see `17.02.03`).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Large. Every destroyed hold, both principals, the agent, and — at
   announce — domains 19 and 21.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two Operators confirming the same artist-date from different rooms
   is the canonical double-booking. Detection must be artist-scoped, not room-scoped.

### CX-04: Confirmation & Announce Gate ↔ Availability Calendar

**Relationship**: Confirmation is the only transition that hard-writes the calendar. It also blocks
adjacent dates via travel/routing windows, which is why a confirmed London date can make a Glasgow
date the next night unbookable.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their calendar loses the date plus a routing buffer they may not have expected.
- **Operator**: their room-date is gone; the perishable inventory is now sold.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the routing
buffer rule is owned by domain 18 (Touring) and cannot be specified from inside 17.

### CX-05: Availability Calendar ↔ Challenge, Release & Expiry

**Relationship**: Auto-release is the mechanism that protects the Operator from their own documented
accidental failure mode. When a hold expires unchallenged, the slot returns to avails with no human
in the loop.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: recovers perishable inventory automatically — an unsold Tuesday is gone forever.
- **Musician**: may lose a date they still wanted but forgot to renew — the symmetric harm.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — deferred to Step 5. The open tension: silent
auto-release is good for the Operator and hostile to the Musician. A warn-then-release ladder is the
likely answer but has not been validated with the owner.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Availability Calendar | Confirmation & Announce Gate (announce half only) | The *confirmation* half writes the calendar (CX-04); the *announce* half does not. Announce is a publicity event with no calendar consequence — a confirmed-but-unannounced date is already blocked. Modelling announce → calendar would imply an unannounced show is still sellable, which is false. |
| R-02 | Hold Ladder | Confirmation & Announce Gate | Considered a direct edge and rejected: the ladder never confirms anything by itself. Every path from ladder to confirmation passes through challenge/release/expiry (CX-02 → CX-03). A direct edge would let position 3 confirm over a live 1st hold, which is precisely the abuse the ladder exists to prevent. |
| R-03 | Hold Ladder | Availability Calendar (write direction) | Rejected as a write relationship. Holds deliberately do **not** consume avails — see CX-01. The read direction is real; the write direction is a modelling error that would collapse the ladder into a queue. |
