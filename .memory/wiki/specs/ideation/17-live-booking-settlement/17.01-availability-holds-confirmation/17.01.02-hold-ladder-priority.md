# Feature: Hold Ladder & Priority Positions

> **Number**: 17.01.02
> **Parent**: [Availability, Holds & Confirmation](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The prioritised queue of soft claims on a single bookable slot — 1st hold, 2nd hold, 3rd hold — that
lets a room entertain several possible shows on one night without committing to any of them, and lets
an artist entertain several possible rooms without committing to any of them. This is the most
industry-specific mechanic in the corpus and is invisible to anyone who has not worked in live music;
modelling it correctly is the strongest credibility signal this product has available.

The problem it solves is the one `meta/personas.md` records for the Operator: a calendar that "lives
in three incompatible places — email enquiries, a paper diary or Google Calendar, and a phone."
Today the ladder exists only in the talent buyer's head and an email thread. Nobody except the buyer
knows the ladder's shape, so a 2nd hold is a rumour, and the buyer's own documented accidental
failure — "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold" — is invisible until
the date has passed and the perishable Tuesday is gone.

**Thesis alignment** (D-18): the ladder is a consolidation feature that earns a provenance right.
Being the place where the position is assigned is what lets the platform capture — at the moment it
is true, from the party who said it — the indicative fee, the reason for a demotion, and who
authorised a hold in an act's name. Every one of those facts is today reconstructed from memory
inside an argument. That is the same failure the platform exists to prevent, applied to booking.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Full on artist-side ladders · Read-only on room-side ladders | **Two distinct positions, and this is the feature's central subtlety (D-04).** On a *room-side* ladder (several acts contending for one room-date) they are read-only: they see that their date carries a hold, its position, its expiry, the ladder's depth, and the age of every position above them — "you are 2nd hold at The Leadmill, 14 March; 1st hold has been sitting 62 days." They cannot place, reorder, or promote themselves; that ladder is the Operator's instrument. On an *artist-side* ladder (several promoters contending for one artist-date) they are the ranking party: their agent assigns 1st/2nd/3rd across competing promoters exactly as an Operator does, with the identical rules mirrored. Whether they see the *identity* of a superior room-side position is the recommended default's open half (Q-02). Their honest interest: 3rd hold on a Saturday behind two 90-day-old zombie positions is worth nothing, and they need to know that before they stop pitching the date. Per the domain index, "Musician" here covers the artist, the band entity, their agent, manager and tour manager. |
| **Producer** | None | No role in stage-date contention. A Producer's bookable resource is a studio room, whose contention lives in domain 16; a Producer who books a venue is acting as an **Operator** in that moment, and one who plays the gig is acting as a **Musician** (personas are lenses on behaviour, not account types). There is no Producer lens on a stage date. |
| **Operator** | Full on room-side ladders · Read-only on artist-side ladders | Places and ranks holds on their own room's slots, sees the whole ladder with every party, every indicative fee, every expiry and every position's age. Decides who gets 1st — a judgement trading a known-safe act against a bigger maybe, and weighing bar spend against guarantee, which is why it must not be automated away (DT-02). Sees the multiplicity disclosure ("this act is held on 4 rooms for this date") and the requester's hold→confirm conversion rate from 17.12. On an artist-side ladder they are the requester and are read-only, symmetric to the Musician's room-side position. Covers promoter, venue talent buyer and festival booker per the domain index. Much of this happens on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk. |
| **Fan** | None | Holds are **never** public and never enter a public aggregate — not the identity, not the count, not the fact that a slot is contended. A 1st hold is not a show. Announcing one, or letting a discovery surface infer one from a "3 holds" badge, is a real commercial harm to both principals and creates fan expectations that routinely collapse. The Fan's relationship to this feature is that it silently decides which shows they will one day be told about. |

## Behavior

A hold is a **soft, ranked, expiring claim on a bookable slot**. Position determines who gets the
slot if everyone wants it and — critically — who may challenge whom (17.01.03). Multiple holds
coexist on one slot by design; the ladder is not a queue that drains, it is a live ranking that
mutates as positions expire, release, get reordered, or get challenged.

Three properties define the mechanic, and each is a place an implementer will get it wrong:

1. **Placing a hold does not consume the avail** (D-02, CX-01). The slot stays holdable and continues
   to attract further positions. A date under three holds is still displayed as holdable.
2. **Position is assigned, not earned by arrival** (D-01). The Operator ranks by commercial judgement.
   A later request routinely takes 1st over an earlier one.
3. **The ladder's unit is a slot, not a date** (D-05). One room-date is one slot *by default*, but a
   room with two stages, a matinee plus an evening, or a headline plus two support positions has
   several — and acts contending for *different* slots on the same date are not in contention at all
   and never share a ladder.

### The Hold Request

A request names, and cannot omit:

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Act** | An entity (domain 01), never free text. Actless "date TBC act" speculative holds are refused (D-08). |
| **Slot** | A specific bookable slot, resolved from the Operator's room model (domain 16) and its billing positions (17.14). |
| **Billing intent** | headline · co-headline · support. Determines which ladder the request joins. |
| **Indicative fee** | A number and a currency, captured immutably at request time (D-06). Not binding; the formal offer is 17.02. |
| **Requesting party** | The human, and the entity they act for. |
| **Routing-block link** | Optional. Names other holds this one is contingent on (D-09). |
| **Authority** | The requester must hold delegated booking authority for the named act (17.08 / domain 01). Unauthorised requests are refused at source (D-07). |

The request is **pre-ladder**: it occupies no position and blocks nothing.

### Position Assignment

Positions are **dense contiguous integers, 1..N, server-assigned**. The Operator chooses an *insert
point* ("place this act above The Wytches"), never a number — so 999, 0, negative, non-integer and
duplicate positions are unrepresentable rather than validated. Every write carries the ladder's
version; a write against a stale version is rejected and the current ladder re-rendered.

Ladder depth is **uncapped**. A cap would force an Operator to refuse rather than rank honestly,
which is a worse outcome — a refusal carries no information and a 4th hold at least tells the agent
where they stand. But a grant at position ≥ 4 requires the Operator to explicitly confirm the depth,
and the requester is told their position and the depth at grant.

### What the Ladder Discloses, and What It Does Not

| Disclosed to everyone on the ladder | Withheld |
|---|---|
| Own position and expiry | Identity of other position holders (recommended default — Q-02) |
| Ladder depth (N) | Other positions' indicative fees, always |
| Age in days of every position above yours | The Operator's reasoning, unless they choose to give it |
| Whether a superior position is **in-house** (D-10) | Anything at all, to a Fan or a Producer |
| Whether a superior position is stale (≥ 30 days) | |

**Age is disclosed; identity is not.** Age is not a competitive fact — it is the honest description of
the thing you are queued behind, and without it "3rd hold" is a number with no meaning (DT-07). This
split is what makes the recommended default on Q-02 tenable: the artist learns what they actually need
(is this ladder real?) without learning what the promoter is entitled to guard (who else wants my
Saturday).

### The Two Ladders (D-04)

A show requires **two ladders to resolve, not one**. The promoter takes a hold on the artist from the
agent; the promoter takes a hold on the room from the venue. A promoter who is 1st on the artist and
2nd on the room cannot confirm anything. The platform is the first system positioned to show both,
and the pairing is the single most useful thing it can tell either side. Both ladders are the same
primitive from opposite ends of a two-sided deal — the identical argument, and the identical answer,
as 17.01.01 D-01 for avails.

Where the room-side Operator is the owner-operator of the venue *and* the act is their own in-house
promotion, both ladders collapse into one party. That is not an error; it is a disclosure obligation
(D-10).

### Happy Path

1. Agent finds an open avail (17.01.01) and submits a hold request naming the act, the slot, a
   billing intent of *headline*, and an indicative fee of £4,000. The system verifies the agent holds
   booking authority for that act (17.08) and that the act is not already held on the same date by
   another entity the same human plays in (D-11).
2. System records the request and notifies the Operator. The request occupies no position; nothing on
   the slot is blocked. A lapse clock starts (7 days default).
3. Operator opens the request on their phone. Beside it the system shows, as decision support and
   never as a rank (D-03): the act's verified draw history for this market (17.11.01), the requester's
   hold→confirm conversion rate (17.12), the multiplicity count — "this act is held on 4 rooms for
   14 March" — and any radius conflict this hold would create (D-12).
4. Operator decides a position — 1st if they want it, 2nd or 3rd if something better is already
   pending — by choosing an insert point in the current ladder.
5. System writes the hold at the assigned position with a server-assigned expiry (17.01.03), assigns
   the request's immutable indicative fee to the position, and renumbers the ladder densely.
6. System notifies: the requester (position, expiry, depth, and whether any superior position is
   in-house or stale); every existing position holder that the ladder has deepened; and the named
   act's booking authority, always, so that no claim can be made about an act without the act knowing
   (D-07).
7. Agent sees the position and expiry on the artist's merged calendar, alongside the age of every
   position above them.
8. The ladder persists, disclosing position ages as they grow, until a challenge (17.01.03), an
   expiry, a release, a reorder, an avail withdrawal, the date passing, or a confirmation (17.01.04)
   mutates it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two hold requests arrive in the same instant | Position is server-assigned and monotonic; ties break on server receipt time, never on client clock | Both requesters see their assigned position; the tie is never exposed |
| Two of the venue's bookers assign a position concurrently from different devices | Optimistic concurrency on the ladder version. The second write is rejected — not merged, not last-write-wins, which would silently demote a live party | "While you were deciding, {booker} placed {act} at 1st. Here is the current ladder." The rejected assignment is re-offered against the new shape |
| Operator inserts a new hold **above** an existing one | Permitted — it is their inventory and their judgement (D-01) — but never silent. The Operator must name a reason from {a superior offer arrived · better fit for the room · at the artist's request · correcting an error} plus optional free text | Demoted holder sees "You have moved from 1st to 2nd hold at {room}. {Operator}: 'a superior offer arrived'" with the timestamp. The reason is recorded as *what was said*, not as adjudicated truth |
| Operator reorders live positions with no new hold arriving | Permitted, same rules. A pure reorder is the loudest trust event on the ladder and is always attributed, reasoned, and written to the position's immutable audit trail visible to affected parties | Every affected position is notified with the Operator named and the reason given |
| Agent places holds on six rooms in one city for one artist-date | **No cap** (D-13). Instead, every hold discloses the multiplicity count to each Operator, and the requester's hold→confirm conversion rate travels with every request via 17.12 | Operator sees "This act is held on 6 rooms for 14 March. {Requester} converts 8% of holds to confirmed shows (37 holds / 3 shows, 24 months)." They can then rank accordingly, refuse, or take it at 3rd — an informed commercial choice, which a cap would have replaced with a blunt one |
| Operator ghosts a hold request | **Requests lapse; holds expire — they are different clocks** (D-14). Default 7 calendar days, Operator-configurable 1–30 days per room, Operator warned at day 5. On lapse the request closes with reason `no-response` and is recorded to 17.12 as a responsiveness fact | Requester sees "No response from {room} within 7 days. Request closed." Never "declined" — the Operator never declined, and recording a decision they did not make is a lie about a counterparty |
| Operator declines a request outright | Distinct from lapse. Closes as `declined`, optional reason, does not count as unresponsiveness | "{Room} declined this hold. Reason: {given, or 'none given'}" |
| A hold names an act that never authorised it | The room ladder is otherwise a mechanism for making claims about third parties. The named act's booking authority is notified of **every** hold in their name and may repudiate it, which voids the position immediately and records the repudiation to 17.12 | Act's agent sees "{Promoter} has taken 1st hold on you for 14 March at {room}." Actions: acknowledge · repudiate. Repudiation notifies the Operator with the reason |
| Requester has no booking authority for the named act | Refused at request time; never reaches the ladder | "You do not hold booking authority for {act}. Request representation (17.08) or ask their agent to place this hold." |
| Hold request names an act with no slot's billing intent set | Refused; billing intent is mandatory because it selects the ladder | "Is this a headline, co-headline or support hold? Support holds queue on a different ladder and do not compete with the headline." |
| An act holds the headline slot and another act requests support on the same date | **Not contention.** They join different ladders and may both get the date (D-05). The naive room-date model would have ranked them against each other and destroyed a normal bill | Both see their own ladder. The support requester is told the headline is pending, not that they are behind it |
| Room has two stages, or a matinee and an evening | Each is a distinct slot with its own independent ladder (D-05) | Ladder is labelled by slot, not by date: "2nd hold — Main Room, 14 March (evening)" |
| Same human held on the same date via two different bands | Detected as a **body conflict** on the individual's merged calendar and disclosed **only** to that human (D-11). Neither Operator is told: which other band the drummer plays in is not their business, and disclosing leaks one band's negotiation into another's room. Holds are soft, so nothing has yet been promised twice | Musician sees "You are held on 14 March by two acts: {Band A} 1st at {room}, {Band B} 2nd at {room}. Only one can be confirmed." Neither band is told about the other |
| A body conflict survives to confirmation | Out of scope here. A soft double-hold is not a lie; a double *confirmation* is, and it is 17.01.04's hard block (its D-04, artist-scoped detection) | — |
| Same artist held on two dates that cannot both be played (routing) | Detected artist-scoped, not room-scoped — the canonical double-booking precursor. Feasibility model is owned by domain 18 | Conflict warning on the artist's merged calendar naming both slots and the travel gap |
| A hold would breach a live radius clause on a confirmed date | **Warned at hold time, not blocked** (D-12). The clause binds a confirmation, not a soft claim — but an expert expects to learn this now, not at confirmation after they have spent two weeks on a routing | Both the Operator and the requester see "This date sits inside a 150-mile / 60-day radius clause on {act}'s confirmed {date} at {room}. A waiver (17.06) is required before this can confirm." |
| Routing-block hold loses its anchor (Bristol drops; Cardiff and London were contingent on it) | **No automatic cascade drop** (D-09). Auto-cascade would let one Operator's decision silently destroy two other Operators' inventory decisions without either of them being consulted. The agent is prompted to review the block | Agent sees "Bristol (14 Mar) is gone. Cardiff (15 Mar) and London (16 Mar) were held as one routing block. Keep · Release both · Decide individually." The two Operators see nothing until the agent acts |
| Superior position is the venue's own in-house promotion | The owner-operator case, and it is common. **Disclosed as in-house to every inferior position** (D-10). Concealing it makes 2nd hold a fraud on the holder: they will never win a challenge against the person adjudicating it | "1st hold: in-house — {venue}'s own promotion." The identity is disclosed here even under Q-02's default, because the conflict of interest, not the competitor's name, is the disclosed fact |
| Ladder fills with zombie positions nobody cleaned up | **Ladder rot — the failure nobody designs for** (DT-07). Every position carries its age from day 1; at 30 days since placement or last counterparty contact (whichever is later) it is flagged stale to everyone on the ladder and to the Operator's own board | 3rd hold reads "3rd of 3 — 1st hold placed 94 days ago (stale), 2nd hold placed 61 days ago (stale)". The number now means something. Operator's board surfaces stale positions as a cleanup queue |
| Avail withdrawn while holds are live (17.01.01) | **The whole ladder is voided; nobody promotes.** This is not an expiry and must never be counted as one — position 2 did not win, the Operator withdrew the inventory | Every position sees "{Room} has withdrawn 14 March. All holds on this date are void." Recorded against the Operator in 17.12, not against the holders |
| Slot's date arrives with the ladder unresolved | At 00:00 in the room's local timezone on the slot's date, every live position voids with reason `date-passed`. The room went dark with a ladder on it — a real and recordable fact | All positions: "14 March has passed. This hold is closed." Recorded to 17.12 for the Operator: a slot that expired contended is inventory they lost while holding a queue |
| Party places and voluntarily releases a hold on the same slot repeatedly | **Hold flapping.** Each new hold notifies superior positions ("someone is behind you") — the ladder's real behavioural lever (DT-13) and therefore its abuse vector: repeated place/release pressures 1st hold with no intent. On the 3rd place-and-release of the same slot by the same party within 30 days, further requests on that slot require explicit Operator approval, and the pattern is recorded to 17.12 | Flapper: "Your last two holds on this slot were released within days. {Room} must approve any further request." Operator sees the pattern with dates |
| Requester attempts to buy 1st with an inflated indicative fee, then offers lower | Permitted — an indicative fee is not binding and the industry behaves this way — but **captured immutably at request time** and the delta surfaced when the real offer lands (D-06). This is the platform thesis applied to booking: the fact is recorded when it is true, not reconstructed inside the argument | Operator sees on the offer: "Held at £4,000 on 3 Feb. Offered £2,500." The pattern accrues to 17.12 |
| Operator assigns a position while offline at a loading dock | Position assignment is server-authoritative. The action queues locally and is submitted on reconnect; if the ladder changed meanwhile the write is rejected exactly as a concurrent write is | Queued action shows as pending, not as done. On rejection: "Your assignment could not be applied — the ladder changed. Here is the current shape." A position is never shown as granted until the server granted it |
| Ladder loads but expiry countdowns cannot be resolved | Positions render **without** countdowns. A wrong countdown on a money deadline is worse than no countdown — the entire challenge mechanic is priced off these clocks | "Positions are current. Expiry times are temporarily unavailable." No countdown is displayed rather than a stale one |
| Agent's representation of the act ends while holds are live | **Holds belong to the act, not to the agent who placed them** (D-15). Positions survive and transfer with booking authority to the new representative | New agent sees the inherited ladder positions on the roster board. Operators are notified of the change of counterparty contact, not of the ladder's shape changing — because it did not |
| Act entity is deleted or merged (domain 01) | Positions follow the surviving entity on a merge. On deletion, positions void with reason `act-withdrawn` and every Operator is notified | Operator: "{Act} is no longer on the platform. Their hold on 14 March is void." |
| Room or venue is delisted (domain 16) | Every ladder on every slot of that room voids with reason `room-withdrawn`, distinct from `avail-withdrawn` | Every position on every affected slot is notified and the reason named |
| A ladder is requested by a party with no position and no ownership | Refused. The ladder is visible to its Operator, its position holders (scoped per the disclosure table), and the named acts' booking authorities. Nobody else — no Fan, no Producer, no unrelated professional | 404, not 403 — the existence of contention on a slot is itself the sensitive fact |
| A public surface tries to derive "this date is busy" from hold counts | Refused by design. Holds never enter a public aggregate, including counts, badges and heatmaps. A "3 holds" badge on a discovery surface leaks contention to a rival room and to fans (Role Lens: Fan = None) | Fans and unauthenticated visitors see the slot exactly as 17.01.01 shows it: holdable or not. Never contended |

### Position Lifecycle States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Requester submits a hold request with authority verified | Pending badge; no position yet; lapse clock (7d default) shown to both parties |
| Lapsed | Lapse clock ran out with no Operator action | "No response from {room} within 7 days." Closed as `no-response`, never as declined |
| Declined | Operator refused the request | "{Room} declined this hold" with the reason if given |
| Held | Operator assigned a position | Position number, expiry countdown, ladder depth, and the age + in-house/stale flags of every superior position |
| Repudiated | Named act disowned a hold placed in their name | Position void; Operator notified with the act's reason; recorded to 17.12 |
| Challenged | Directly inferior position challenged this one (17.01.03) | Urgent countdown — confirm or drop — with the terms on the table |
| Promoted | A superior position expired, released, or was repudiated | "You are now 1st hold at {room}, 14 March" with the reason the position above vacated |
| Demoted | Operator inserted above, or reordered | Position change with the Operator named and their stated reason |
| Stale | 30 days since placement or last counterparty contact | Flagged to the holder, to every inferior position, and on the Operator's cleanup board |
| Expired | The hold's own clock ran out (17.01.03) | Position gone; ladder promoted; all positions notified |
| Released | Holder voluntarily dropped | Position gone; ladder promoted; all positions notified |
| Voided | Avail withdrawn, room delisted, or act withdrawn | Whole ladder gone; **nobody promotes**; reason named; recorded against the withdrawing party |
| Date-passed | 00:00 local on the slot's date with the ladder unresolved | All positions closed; the room went dark contended |
| Destroyed | Slot confirmed to another party (17.01.04) | "This date is gone" with the reason. Irreversible — see 17.01.04 DT-02 |

### View States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Ladder opened | Skeleton showing the known position count from the calendar; no numbers rendered until authoritative, because a briefly-wrong position is a money-relevant lie |
| Empty | Slot has avails and zero holds | "No holds on this date." Operator sees "Open to holds"; a Musician viewing their own open date sees the same |
| Populated | 1+ positions on the slot | Ordered ladder scoped by the disclosure table, with ages, expiry countdowns, stale flags and in-house flags |
| Partial | Positions resolved, expiry substrate unavailable | Positions and ages render; countdowns replaced by "Expiry times temporarily unavailable." No stale countdown is ever shown |
| Error | Ladder cannot be read | "Can't load the ladder for {slot}." Retry. **No cached ladder is shown as current** — a stale ladder would misprice a challenge decision |
| Stale-write | Write rejected on a version conflict | Current ladder re-rendered with the conflicting change named and the rejected action re-offered against the new shape |
| Forbidden | Requester has no relationship to the slot | Rendered as not-found; contention on a slot is itself sensitive |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The hold ladder is a FIFO queue | Naive modelling of "first come first served" | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **merit ranking assigned by the Operator**, not an arrival order. A talent buyer routinely gives 1st hold to a later request because the act is bigger. Modelling it as FIFO would strip out the Operator's core commercial judgement and produce a product no talent buyer would use. |
| DT-02 | Holds should be automatically ranked by expected value (fee × draw) | Optimisation instinct; draw data exists in 17.11 | ❌ REJECTED: a domain expert would find this offensive and wrong. The 1st-hold decision encodes relationship, genre fit, room-building strategy, bar spend and favours owed — none of which are in the data. A 200-cap room makes its money on the bar, so 150 drinkers can beat a higher guarantee. Automating it removes the Operator's reason to trust the tool. **Decision support (showing draw history next to the request) is welcome; automated ranking is not.** |
| DT-03 | Position visibility is symmetric — everyone sees the whole ladder | Transparency instinct | ⏳ PARTIALLY RESOLVED at Step 5, remainder is Q-02. The identity question is genuinely a product decision and is not an agent call. But it was masking a resolvable one: the ladder can disclose **age and shape** without disclosing **identity**, and age is the fact that makes a position meaningful (DT-07). Recommended default now specified in Behavior; identity remains Q-02. |
| DT-04 | The ladder is venue-side only — Operators rank, Musicians queue | The [SURFACE] pass's implicit model; sub-domain index Q-03 | ✅ CONFIRMED as wrong, and resolved: **there are two ladders, and both must resolve for a show to exist.** The promoter holds the artist with the agent; the promoter holds the room with the venue. A promoter 1st on the artist and 2nd on the room can confirm nothing. The agent's board *is* the artist-side ladder — which is why 17.08.03 Roster Booking Pipeline exists and needs this feature to feed it. This is the identical argument as 17.01.01 D-01 (artist avails and venue avails are one primitive from opposite ends), and the identical answer. **Consequence**: the sub-domain index Role Matrix row for 17.01.02 ("Musician 👁️ Read-only") is true only of room-side ladders and understates the Musician; the domain index has Musician ✅ Full at 17.01. Flagged for Step 6 reconciliation. |
| DT-05 | A hold attaches to a room-date | Naive booking-system modelling; sub-domain index Q-01 | ❌ REJECTED: **the unit is a bookable slot.** A room with two stages, a matinee plus an evening, or a headline plus support positions has several slots on one date. The break this exposes is severe and nobody has named it: under the room-date model, a headliner holding 14 March and a support act holding 14 March are ranked *against each other* — but they are not competing, they would share the bill. The ladder assumes every position wants the same exclusive thing, and support slots violate that. Default remains one room-date = one slot, so the simple case is untouched; the Operator declares more when their room has more. |
| DT-06 | The indicative fee on a hold request is decorative | It is non-binding, so modelling it looks like noise | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **real and routine hazard**. Indicative fees are inflated to buy a position and then negotiated down at offer. A domain expert expects this specific move. The response is not to police it — it is **capture**: record the fee immutably at request time and surface the delta when the real offer arrives. This is the platform's thesis (D-18) applied to booking: the fact is captured at the moment it is true, from the party who said it, instead of being reconstructed from an email thread once it is contested. **This is the strongest provenance hook in the sub-domain and it was one field away from being missed.** |
| DT-07 | A hold ladder, once built, stays meaningful | The mechanic's own premise | ❌ REJECTED — **this is the failure mode nobody designs for**. Holds are free to place, expiry is soft, and `meta/personas.md` documents the Operator forgetting to release. So ladders rot: an agent offered "3rd hold" assumes two live competitors when both are 90-day zombies the buyer never cleaned. A rotted ladder is worse than no ladder — it *misprices the challenge decision*, which is where real money moves (17.01.03). The system knows every position's age and currently does not say. Surfacing age converts the ladder from theatre into a tool, and costs one field. |
| DT-08 | Speculative actless holds ("I want that Saturday, act TBC") should be supported | They are real at the top of the market | ❌ REJECTED for v1, with a live open question (Q-05). Three reasons: the Operator cannot rank "TBC" against a known act, so DT-02's decision support has nothing to show; there is no counterparty, so the hold notifies nobody and repudiation (D-07) is impossible; and the actless holder is a promoter with no room and no act, who does not map onto the four-persona model (D-19). An actless hold is pure inventory blocking with zero information — the abuse of the mechanic in its purest form. Rejecting it for v1 costs the arena tier, which is not this product's user base. |
| DT-09 | Routing-block holds should cascade-drop automatically when the anchor is lost | Consistency instinct; the routing is dead anyway | ❌ REJECTED: auto-cascade lets one Operator's decision silently destroy **two other Operators' inventory decisions** without either being consulted, and it is wrong on the facts — an agent who loses Bristol may well keep Cardiff and re-route around it. The routing is the agent's asset and its collapse is the agent's call. Prompt to review; never cascade. But the *link itself* is confirmed real and nobody had modelled it: an agent holds a run as one object ("the whole run or none of it"), and a ladder that only knows single dates cannot see that dropping the Tuesday kills the week. |
| DT-10 | The Operator is always a disinterested adjudicator of their own ladder | Implicit in D-01's "the Operator's judgement is the product" | ❌ REJECTED: **the owner-operator running an in-house promotion holds a position on their own ladder**, and `meta/personas.md` says this is normal ("often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves"). They can assign themselves 1st and never lose a challenge, because they adjudicate it. That does not make it illegitimate — it is their room. It makes **non-disclosure** illegitimate: a 2nd hold behind an undisclosed in-house 1st is being sold a lottery ticket in a draw the seller decides. Disclose "in-house", and the position below prices itself correctly. This is the one case where identity is disclosed regardless of Q-02, because the disclosed fact is the conflict, not the competitor. |
| DT-11 | Contention is between acts, so per-act detection is sufficient | The ladder's own framing | ❌ REJECTED: the multi-hyphenate breaks it. `meta/personas.md`: "a drummer who also mixes, sits in three bands." Two of those bands can hold the same night at different rooms, both ladders clean, both Operators satisfied, and **one body**. Neither ladder can see it; only the human's merged calendar can. Detection confirmed, at the person layer not the act layer. **Disclosure to the Operators rejected** — which band a drummer also plays in is not a rival room's business, and telling them leaks one negotiation into another. Holds are soft: a double-hold is a warning, not a lie. The lie would be a double *confirmation*, and that is 17.01.04's hard block. |
| DT-12 | Radius clauses bite at confirmation, so the ladder need not know about them | Clean layering instinct — 17.06 owns clauses, 17.01.04 owns confirmation | ❌ REJECTED as a product answer, though correct as an ownership answer. An expert expects to learn at **hold** time that the act is holding a room 40 miles away the night before — not after two weeks of routing work, at confirmation, when the block lands. 17.06 itself says the clause "is forgotten until it is breached" and that warning-before-booking is its entire value to the Musician. A ladder that stays silent reproduces exactly that failure. Warn at hold; block at confirm. |
| DT-13 | Notifying superior positions that someone is behind them is a courtesy FYI | Notification-design instinct | ❌ REJECTED: CX-01 already calls it "a genuine behavioural lever." What follows and had not been drawn: **a lever is an abuse vector.** A rival can place and pull holds on the same slot to keep pressuring 1st hold with no intent to take it. Free to do, unlimited today, and it works — it is a denial-of-attention attack on the person you are competing with. Rate-limited at 3 place-and-releases of the same slot in 30 days, and recorded. |
| DT-14 | Hold requests and holds share one clock | The [SURFACE] pass left this undecided (old Q-03) | ❌ REJECTED — they are different objects with different clocks. A **hold** expires because it blocks a decision; a **request** lapses because it is an unanswered question. Conflating them either gives requests the weight of holds (blocking nothing, but nagging like something) or gives them no clock at all — which is today's state, and today an ignored request is the Operator's cheapest way to say no without saying no. The critical detail: a lapse must close as `no-response`, **never** as `declined`. The Operator never declined. Recording a decision they did not make is a fabricated fact about a counterparty, in a product whose whole thesis is that recorded facts are true when captured. |
| DT-15 | A hold belongs to the party who placed it | Possession instinct | ❌ REJECTED: **a hold belongs to the act.** When an artist changes agency — routine, and often acrimonious — the positions their old agent built are the artist's inventory, not the agent's leverage. An ownership model that let a departing agent walk off with, or burn, a roster's ladder positions would make the platform an instrument in exactly the dispute it should be evidence in. Positions transfer with booking authority (17.08 / domain 01). |
| DT-16 | Hold hoarding is fixed by a cap on concurrent holds (old Q-01) | The obvious lever; the [SURFACE] pass framed it as "hold-count limits" | ❌ REJECTED: a cap cannot tell a legitimate multi-market routing from inventory abuse, so it breaks the honest agent and merely inconveniences the abusive one (who splits across entities). Two better instruments already exist in the corpus and neither had been connected: **multiplicity disclosure** — an artist can play one room per date, so six concurrent room-holds means five are dead and the Operator is entitled to know the count (never the identities, which would leak the routing plan 17.01.01 DT-03 says agents guard); and **17.12's derived reliability** — hold→confirm conversion rate is precisely a derived signal computed from facts the platform already holds, which is 17.12's stated purpose. A promoter who takes 37 holds and confirms 3 should carry that number into every request. Price the behaviour; do not ban it. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([../../16-venues-studios-spaces/](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) — **shared entity, upstream**. The room and its slot decomposition (stages, matinee/evening) are owned there; the ladder's unit resolves from 16's room model (D-05). Room delisting voids every ladder on it. This feature publishes contention against 16's resource; it does not own the resource.
- **17.01.01 Availability Calendar & Avails** ([./17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md](./17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md)) — **shared entity, read-only (CX-01, R-03)**. The avail is what a hold attaches to; the hold never writes it. **Reconciliation needed at Step 6**: 17.01.01's avail is a "window with constraints" and this feature's unit is a slot (D-05); the two must agree that a window decomposes into slots and that a ladder hangs off a slot, not off the window. Avail withdrawal voids the ladder without promotion — a distinct outcome from expiry that 17.12 must not conflate.
- **17.01.03 Challenge, Release & Expiry** ([./17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md](./17.01.03-challenge-release-expiry.md)) — **trigger dependency, bidirectional (CX-02)**. The ladder supplies the ordering that decides who may challenge whom; 17.01.03 supplies the clocks and the mutations. **New dependency from Step 5**: position *age* and *stale* flags (DT-07) are what make a challenge decision priceable — a challenge against a 90-day zombie is a different decision from one against a 3-day-old hold, and 17.01.03 currently has no input carrying that. Also: 17.01.03's expiry clock and this feature's request-lapse clock (D-14) are different objects and must not be unified.
- **17.01.04 Confirmation & Announce Gate** ([./17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md](./17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md)) — **no direct edge (CX R-02)**; every path from ladder to confirmation runs through 17.01.03. But D-04's two-ladder model means confirmation requires **both** ladders resolved, which its current single-ladder happy path ("a hold survives to a confirmable position") does not state. Body conflicts (D-11) are tolerated as holds and must be blocked at confirm — an input to its artist-scoped detection (its D-04).
- **17.02 Offers & Negotiation** ([../17.02-offers-negotiation/](../17.02-offers-negotiation/)) — **trigger dependency + data handoff**. A hold travels with an indicative fee; the formal offer follows and is a different object. **New from Step 5 (D-06)**: the immutable indicative fee must be surfaced against the offer as a delta ("held at £4,000, offered £2,500"). 17.02 is the consumer of that comparison and does not currently know the field exists.
- **17.06 Radius Clause & Exclusivity** ([../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md](../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md)) — **trigger dependency, new at Step 5 (D-12)**. Radius conflict evaluation must fire at hold time as a **warning** (block stays at confirmation). 17.06 owns the clause and the check; this feature owns the moment the check is surfaced. Its stated value — "warned before booking a date that would breach one" — is unachievable if the first evaluation happens at confirm.
- **17.08 Agency Representation & Commission** ([../17.08-agency-representation-commission/](../17.08-agency-representation-commission/)) — **permission, load-bearing**. Booking authority gates every hold request (D-07) and carries positions across a representation change (D-15). 17.08.03 Roster Booking Pipeline is the artist-side ladder's primary read surface (D-04) — the agent's board is a ladder view across the roster, not a per-date view.
- **17.11 Draw History & Market Intelligence** ([../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/](../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/)) — **decision support, never rank (D-03, DT-02)**. Verified draw for this market renders beside a hold request. Read-only, advisory, and must never be wired into position assignment.
- **17.12 Counterparty Relationship & Payment Reliability** ([../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md](../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md)) — **derived-signal producer, new and load-bearing at Step 5 (D-13, DT-16)**. This feature emits five derived facts 17.12 does not yet know about: hold→confirm conversion rate; request lapse rate per Operator (`no-response`, distinct from `declined`); repudiation rate (holds placed in acts' names without authority); flapping incidents; and slots that expired contended. These are exactly 17.12's stated model — "derived, not asserted... facts the platform already holds because it computed them" — and they are the enforcement instrument that replaces the rejected hold cap. **This is the sub-domain's most under-modelled edge.**
- **17.14 Bill Construction & Support Slot Offers** ([../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md](../17.14-bill-construction-support-slots.md)) — **boundary, newly sharp (D-05)**. Billing intent (headline/co-headline/support) selects the ladder; support and headline holds never contend. 17.14 owns how a bill is constructed and how supports are offered; this feature owns contention ranking *within* a slot. The boundary is: 17.14 decides what the slots are, the ladder ranks who is in them.
- **18 Show Production & Touring** ([../../18-show-production-touring/](../../18-show-production-touring/)) — **model dependency**. Travel feasibility between held dates is 18's model, consumed here for routing conflict warnings. Routing-block holds (D-09) are the ladder's projection of 18's routing object.
- **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity**. The hold's subject is an entity, never free text; delegated booking authority is 01's model; the body-conflict detection (D-11) runs at the person layer across entities, which only 01 can resolve.
- **24 Trust & Safety** ([../../24-trust-safety/](../../24-trust-safety/)) — **report surface**. Repudiated holds, flapping, and mass-hoarding are abuse reports with a professional (not consumer) profile. The ladder produces the evidence; 24 owns the report and the sanction.

- **Leak boundary — 19 / 20 / 21**: holds must never reach a Fan-facing surface, **including in aggregate**. No counts, no badges, no "busy date" heatmaps derived from contention. The only fan-visible artefact in this chain is an announced show (17.01.04 → 19/21).

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Position is Operator-assigned, not arrival-ordered | The 1st-hold decision is the Operator's core commercial skill (DT-01). FIFO would model a different, non-existent industry. |
| D-02 | Holds do not consume avails | A date under three holds is still holdable. The defining property of the mechanic and the most likely implementer error (CX-01, R-03). |
| D-03 | Draw data informs but never ranks | DT-02 — automated ranking removes the Operator's judgement and their reason to trust the tool. |
| D-04 | **Two ladders, both must resolve** — the room-side ladder (Operator-owned) and the artist-side ladder (Musician-side) are one primitive from opposite ends | Resolves sub-domain index Q-03. Precedent and reasoning are identical to 17.01.01 D-01 for avails. A promoter 1st on the artist and 2nd on the room can confirm nothing; showing both is the most useful thing the platform can tell either side. **Requires reconciling the sub-domain index's "Musician 👁️ Read-only" row (Q-06).** |
| D-05 | **The ladder's unit is a bookable slot, not a room-date.** Default: one room-date = one slot | Resolves sub-domain index Q-01. Two stages, matinee/evening, and headline-vs-support all break the room-date model, and the support case breaks it *silently* — it would rank two acts against each other who were never in contention (DT-05). Defaulting to one slot leaves the simple case untouched. |
| D-06 | The indicative fee is captured immutably at request time and its delta against the real offer is surfaced | DT-06. Non-binding, routinely inflated to buy position, and today unrecorded — so the inflation is unprovable. Capturing it is the platform thesis (D-18) applied to booking. |
| D-07 | Every hold names an act, requires the requester's delegated booking authority, is disclosed to the act, and may be repudiated by them | Without this the room ladder is a mechanism for making claims about third parties — a promoter could pencil an act that never agreed, to block a rival or to bluff the artist. Disclosure matches practice (the conversation was with the agent anyway) and costs one notification. |
| D-08 | Actless speculative holds are refused in v1 | DT-08 — unrankable, counterparty-less, unrepudiable, and outside the four-persona model. Pure inventory blocking with zero information. Re-openable (Q-05). |
| D-09 | Routing-block holds are linked but **never** cascade-drop; the agent is prompted to review | DT-09. Auto-cascade lets one Operator's decision silently destroy two other Operators' inventory decisions, and is wrong on the facts — agents re-route around a lost anchor. |
| D-10 | An in-house position is disclosed as in-house to every inferior position, regardless of Q-02 | DT-10. The owner-operator holding their own ladder is legitimate; concealing it is not, because the inferior position will never win a challenge the holder adjudicates. The disclosed fact is the conflict of interest, not the competitor's name — so it does not pre-empt Q-02. |
| D-11 | Body conflicts (one human held twice via two acts) are detected at the person layer and disclosed **only** to that human | DT-11. The multi-hyphenate is `meta/personas.md`'s structural fact and defeats act-layer detection. Disclosing to Operators would leak one band's negotiation into a rival's room. | D-12 | Radius conflicts are **warned at hold time**, blocked at confirmation | DT-12. 17.06's entire value to the Musician is warning before booking; a ladder that stays silent until confirm reproduces the failure 17.06 exists to prevent. |
| D-14 | **DQ-04.01:** a held date **stays available**, and only an **aggregate** hold state is disclosed — never counterparty identities | Confirms what D-13 and CX-01 already establish: a hold attaches to an avail without removing it, and multiplicity travels while identities never do. Removing the date at first hold would convert a soft hold into an exclusive reservation and contradict the multi-position ladder model outright; showing nothing would delete locked disclosure text and leave a counterparty unable to judge contention before entering the flow. **Audience is scoped, not universal** — the aggregate is disclosed to the parties the ladder already exposes it to, and the pre-ladder request state (which occupies no position) is excluded from the count. |
| D-13 | **No cap on concurrent holds.** Multiplicity count (never identities) is disclosed to the Operator, and hold→confirm conversion rate travels with every request | DT-16. A cap cannot distinguish legitimate shopping from abuse, breaks the honest agent, and is trivially evaded by splitting entities. Disclosure prices the behaviour instead of banning it — and is the same "derived, not asserted" instrument 17.12 already exists to provide. Resolves old Q-01. |
| D-14 | **Requests lapse; holds expire.** Different objects, different clocks. A lapse closes as `no-response`, never as `declined` | DT-14. Resolves old Q-03. Recording a decision the Operator never made would fabricate a fact about a counterparty inside a product whose thesis is captured truth. Default 7 days, Operator-configurable 1–30, warned at day 5. |
| D-15 | A hold belongs to the act, not to the agent who placed it; positions transfer with booking authority | DT-15. Otherwise a departing agent could walk off with or burn a roster's ladder, making the platform an instrument in the dispute it should be evidence in. |
| D-16 | **Position age is disclosed to everyone on the ladder; identity is not** | DT-07 + DT-03. Age is what makes a position number mean anything, and it is not a competitive fact. This split is what lets the ladder be honest without pre-empting Q-02. Stale threshold: 30 days since placement or last counterparty contact, whichever is later. |
| D-17 | Reorders and demotions are permitted but never silent — always attributed, always reasoned, always written to a trail the affected party can read | D-01 says the judgement is the product, so the ladder must not constrain it; CX-01 says arbitrary reordering breaks trust. | D-18 | Hold flapping is rate-limited at 3 place-and-releases of the same slot by the same party in 30 days | DT-13. The "someone is behind you" notification is a lever, and an unlimited free lever is an attack. The 30-day/3× threshold is a platform default, not an observed industry figure — flagged in Q-07. |
| D-19 | Hold-ladder mutations use **optimistic per-ladder version checks**: server-assigned dense ordering, receipt-time ties, stale-write rejection, and re-offer against the authoritative current ladder | Owner ratification for DQ-04.06 / `r-78[0]`, 2026-07-20. No stale mutation may merge, replay automatically, queue behind, lock out another booker, or apply last-write-wins. Successful mutation preserves version, ordering, required attribution/reason, and audit/notification intent atomically. Release or expiry promotes lower positions; avail/room/act withdrawal, date-passed, and confirmation void without promotion; release wins over a simultaneous challenge. |
