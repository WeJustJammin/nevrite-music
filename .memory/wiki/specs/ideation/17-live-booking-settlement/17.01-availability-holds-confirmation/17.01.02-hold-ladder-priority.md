# Feature: Hold Ladder & Priority Positions

> **Number**: 17.01.02
> **Parent**: [Availability, Holds & Confirmation](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The prioritised queue of soft claims on a single date — 1st hold, 2nd hold, 3rd hold — that lets a
room entertain several possible shows on one night without committing to any of them. This is the
most industry-specific mechanic in the corpus and is invisible to anyone who has not worked in live
music; modelling it correctly is the strongest credibility signal this product has available.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees that their date carries a hold and what position it occupies — "you are 2nd hold at The Leadmill, 14 March". Cannot place, reorder, or promote their own position; the ladder is the Operator's instrument. Whether they see *who* holds the superior position is contested (Q-02). Their real interest is honest: position 3 on a Saturday is worth nothing and they need to know that before they stop pitching the date. |
| Producer | None | No role in stage-date contention. |
| Operator | Full | Places holds, assigns positions, sees the whole ladder with every party and expiry. Decides who gets 1st — a judgement call trading a known-safe act against a bigger maybe. This is the Operator's core commercial skill and the feature must not automate it away. |
| Fan | None | Holds are never public. A 1st hold is not a show and announcing one is a real commercial harm. |

## Behavior

A hold is a **soft, ranked, expiring claim**. Position determines who gets the date if everyone wants
it, and — critically — who may challenge whom (17.01.03). Multiple holds coexist on one date by
design; the ladder is not a queue that drains, it is a live ranking that mutates as positions expire,
release, or get challenged.

The counter-intuitive property that implementers get wrong: **placing a hold does not consume the
avail**. The date stays holdable and continues to attract further positions (CX-01).

### Happy Path

1. Agent finds an open date (17.01.01) and requests a hold, stating the act and an indicative fee.
2. Operator receives the request and decides a position — 1st if they want it, 2nd/3rd if something
   better is already pending.
3. System writes the hold at the assigned position with a server-assigned expiry, and notifies every
   existing position holder that the ladder has deepened.
4. Agent sees their position and expiry on the artist's calendar.
5. The ladder persists until a challenge (17.01.03), an expiry, a release, or a confirmation
   (17.01.04) mutates it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two hold requests arrive in the same instant | Position is server-assigned and monotonic; ties break on server receipt time, never on client clock | Both agents see their assigned position; no ambiguity is exposed |
| Operator wants to insert a new hold **above** an existing one | Permitted but demoting a live position is a trust event — the demoted party is notified explicitly | Demoted holder sees "you have moved from 1st to 2nd hold" with the Operator named |
| Same artist held on two dates that cannot both be played (routing) | Detected artist-scoped, not room-scoped — the canonical double-booking precursor | Conflict warning on the artist's merged calendar |
| Agent places holds on six rooms in one city for one date | Legitimate practice (shopping a date) but abusive at scale — it locks up other people's inventory | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — hold-count limits are a live design question |
| Operator ghosts a hold request | The request itself has no expiry, unlike the hold | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — unclear whether requests expire |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Agent asks for a hold | Pending badge; no position yet |
| Held | Operator assigns a position | Position number, expiry countdown, ladder depth |
| Challenged | Superior position challenged (17.01.03) | Urgent countdown — confirm or drop |
| Promoted | A superior position expired or released | "You are now 1st hold" notification |
| Demoted | Operator inserted a hold above | Position change with Operator named |
| Destroyed | Date confirmed to another party (17.01.04) | "This date is gone" with the reason |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The hold ladder is a FIFO queue | Naive modelling of "first come first served" | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **merit ranking assigned by the Operator**, not an arrival order. A talent buyer routinely gives 1st hold to a later request because the act is bigger. Modelling it as FIFO would strip out the Operator's core commercial judgement and produce a product no talent buyer would use. |
| DT-02 | Holds should be automatically ranked by expected value (fee × draw) | Optimisation instinct; draw data exists in 17.11 | ❌ REJECTED: a domain expert would find this offensive and wrong. The 1st-hold decision encodes relationship, genre fit, room-building strategy, and favours owed — none of which are in the data. Automating it removes the Operator's reason to trust the tool. **Decision support (showing draw history next to the request) is welcome; automated ranking is not.** |
| DT-03 | Position visibility is symmetric — everyone sees the whole ladder | Transparency instinct | ⏳ DEFERRED (Q-02): a promoter treats "who else is holding my Saturday" as commercially sensitive; an artist believes it is their date. Both are right. This is a product decision, not an agent call. Flagged rather than assumed. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Position is Operator-assigned, not arrival-ordered | The 1st-hold decision is the Operator's core commercial skill (DT-01). FIFO would model a different, non-existent industry. |
| D-02 | Holds do not consume avails | A date under three holds is still holdable. This is the defining property of the mechanic and the most likely implementer error (CX-01). |
| D-03 | Draw data informs but never ranks | DT-02 — automated ranking removes the Operator's judgement and their reason to trust the tool. |
