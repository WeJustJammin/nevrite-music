# Feature: Availability Calendar & Avails Publishing

> **Number**: 17.01.01
> **Parent**: [Availability, Holds & Confirmation](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The publishable record of **what is being offered for a show, and when** — an Operator's open
room-dates, an act's open touring windows. This is the resource every hold (17.01.02) attaches to
and the thing the Operator's business actually is: perishable inventory where an unsold Tuesday is
gone forever. It replaces the "email + Google Calendar + a spreadsheet + phone calls" workaround
documented in [`meta/personas.md`](../../meta/personas.md).

The word is the industry's own. **"Avails" is an agent's term before it is a venue's** — it names the
list an agent sends promoters: *"here's who I have open in the Midlands in Q2."* That origin is
load-bearing and the breadth pass missed it: avails are **pushed to named counterparties**, not merely
published and searched (DT-08). A venue's equivalent — "what's open in March" — is the same primitive
seen from the buy side, which is why one feature serves both (D-01).

**What this feature is not**: it is not the room's calendar. [16.06.01](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/16.06-space-booking-reservations/16.06.01-availability-calendar-holds.md)
owns whether a room is physically free. This feature owns whether the Operator will **entertain a show
offer** on a slot that is free. Those are different facts and conflating them is the single largest
modelling error available here (DT-04, D-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | ✅ Full | Publishes **routing windows**, not dates — *"North of England, 12–19 March, we want two mid-week dates, £800–1,500, no radius conflict with Manchester."* The window carries a **quota** (2), a **pattern** (mid-week), a **territory**, and a **fee floor** — an act knows its own price, which the buy side cannot know in advance (DT-06, DT-07). Publishes as the **band entity**, not as themselves (01); an agent publishes on the act's behalf only where a representation record grants it, scoped by territory (17.08). Sends the window to a named promoter list, or a tokenised link for off-platform promoters — and sees who opened it. Sees which of their dates are open, held, or confirmed **across every band and project they belong to at once**: the multi-hyphenate's merged calendar (D-11). **Never has an open/public tier** — a routing window is an unannounced tour's plan and is containment-bounded (D-09). |
| **Producer** | ❌ None | No stage-date inventory. A Producer's bookable resource is a studio room, whose calendar and holds are [16.06.01](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/16.06-space-booking-reservations/16.06.01-availability-calendar-holds.md) — a different domain, not a missing column here. A Producer who offers a venue date is acting as an **Operator** in that moment; one who plays the gig is acting as a **Musician**. Personas are lenses on behaviour (`meta/personas.md`), and neither lens is "Producer" on a stage date. |
| **Operator** | ✅ Full | Publishes **room-date avails** over intervals that [16.06.01](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/16.06-space-booking-reservations/16.06.01-availability-calendar-holds.md) already says are free — never over its blackouts, bookings or cascade-blocks. Sets what is act-independent and therefore publishable: **deal posture** ("door split, 70/30 after costs"), a **budget ceiling** ("we cannot consider over £600 on a Tuesday" — a ceiling, never a minimum, DT-06), **bill shape** (headline / support-slot only), and **visibility**. Does **not** re-type capacity, curfew or load-in — those are the room record's (16.02) and are referenced with their freshness (D-04). Withdraws an avail, seeing exactly which holders that voids first. Gets exactly one reconfirm prompt per avail, at the on-sale boundary, where the date stops being an advance date and becomes a **fill** date (D-08). |
| **Fan** | ❌ None | Never, at any visibility tier, in any aggregate. An avail is not a show. This is not commercial squeamishness — it is a **containment boundary** (16.06.01 D-20, extended here): an artist's published routing window states *in words* what a hold pattern only implies. A scraper wearing a Fan account reading avails would reconstruct an unannounced tour from the primary source, blowing the on-sale (19), the announce (17.01.04) and the artist's leverage at once. Consolidation means this platform holds every book, which makes this a harm **only WeJammin could cause** (D-10). |

## Behavior

An avail is a **commercial intention layered over a physical availability**, not a boolean on a date.
Its defining property: **publishing it commits nothing**. It is an invitation to hold (17.01.02), and
it survives having holds placed on it — a date carrying three holds is still displayed as holdable
(CX-01, 16.06.01 D-01). This is the property implementers get wrong, and it is why an avail cannot be
modelled as inventory that depletes.

### The boundary with domain 16 — the load-bearing distinction

| Fact | Owner | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Is the room physically free at this wall-clock interval? | **16.06.01** | "Sat 14 Mar 19:00–23:00 `Europe/London` is `available`" |
| Will the Operator entertain a **show** offer on it? | **17.01.01** (this) | "Yes — door split, ≤£600, support-slot only, visible to invited agents" |
| What are the room's capacity, curfew, load-in? | **16.02** (spec), decayed by **16.05.05** | "300 cap · 23:00 curfew · verified 14 months ago" |
| Who holds it, in what rank, until when? | **17.01.02 / 17.01.03** — *see the seam note in Cross-Cuts and Q-03* | "1st hold, expires 31 Jan" |

Consequences, each of which a naive model gets wrong:

- **An avail is published over a 16.06.01 interval**, at its 15-minute resolution (16.06.01 D-18) —
  never over "a date". This resolves the sub-domain's Q-01 from the avail side: a room with a matinee
  and an evening publishes **two** avails over two intervals; a venue with two stages has **two rooms**
  (16.01.02) with two calendars and therefore two independent avails. No new grain is invented here.
- **An avail cannot exist where 16 says the slot is not available.** Publishing over a `booked`,
  `blocked` or `cascade-blocked` interval is refused, carrying 16's reason verbatim ("Live Room is
  booked"), never a bare failure (16.06.01 D-05).
- **Time semantics are inherited wholesale** from 16.06.01 D-11/D-12: wall-clock + IANA zone, duration
  as elapsed real time, room facts in room time, personal deadlines in viewer time. This feature adds
  no time machinery and must not invent any.
- **Availability is a fact; an avail is an intention.** The room being free on Tuesday is not an offer
  to put a show on it (DT-04). Auto-deriving avails from the calendar would commit the Operator to
  offers they never made and flood discovery with dates nobody will sell.

### Constraints are referenced, never copied

The breadth pass had this wrong. An avail does **not** carry capacity, curfew, load-in or gear — it
**references the room record** (16.02), which carries per-field provenance (16.05.03) and per-field
decay (16.05.05 DT-01). Copying those onto every avail would create one independently-rotting duplicate
per published date, which is the Operator's named accidental anti-persona — *"publishes a stale gear
list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show"* — committed by the platform, at machine
speed, on every date at once (DT-05).

What the avail **does** own is everything that is date-specific or commercial, and act-independent:

| Field | Side | Why it is avail-owned, not room-owned |
|---|---|---|
| **Deal posture** | Operator | "Door split · 70/30 after costs" — a property of what the room does, not of the room |
| **Budget ceiling** | Operator | The buy side has a **ceiling**, not a floor (DT-06). "≤£600 on a Tuesday" lets an agent for a £2,000 act filter out the room in one pass |
| **Bill shape** | Operator | Headline / support-only / co-headline — decides whether the date is even the right conversation (17.14) |
| **Date-level deviation** | Operator | "Curfew is 22:00 *this* night — private hire after." A deviation from the room record, valid for this interval only, never a write to 16.02 |
| **Fee floor** | Musician | An act knows its price; the room cannot, because the room's price depends on the act (17.11). The asymmetry is real, not an oversight |
| **Quota + pattern** | Musician | "Two mid-week dates in this range" (DT-07) |
| **Territory** | Musician | The window's geography — the routing radius the act will actually travel |
| **Visibility** | Both | Who may see this at all (below) |

### Visibility and distribution

Three tiers. The audience of the widest tier is **asymmetric between the two sides**, and that asymmetry
is derived, not a preference (DT-09):

| Tier | Audience | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Invited** | Named entities only | The professional norm at the top of the market. Agents guard routing plans; a venue publicly advertising an open Saturday signals weakness (DT-03) |
| **Linked** | Anyone holding a per-recipient token | The avails *email*, made live. Necessary for the same reason 16.06.01 D-14 is: most of the book is people with no account. Tokens are **per recipient** (so opens are attributable and revocation is individual) and die with the avail |
| **Discoverable** | **Room-date**: any account. **Artist window**: only entities with a claimed, verified room (16.05.02) | There is **no professional account type** — personas are lenses (D-19) — so "open" on an artist window means *public*, and a public routing window is the D-10 leak. The claimed-verified-room entity is the only real credential in the data, so it is the widest an artist window can go |

**A sent avails list is live, not a snapshot** (DT-08). The industry failure it replaces: an agent mails
avails on Monday; by Wednesday two dates are held; a promoter replies Friday on a date that is gone. A
recipient opening the list reads **current state at read time**. A date that has died since sending shows
that it died and why — *"no longer available"* — and **never who killed it**: competing-holder identity is
the Operator's alone (16.06.01 D-15), and the same reasoning binds here.

### Lifecycle — an avail dies on the same physics as a hold

Avails have the Operator's other named failure built into them: *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a
slot they'd happily have sold"* has a twin — *publishes an open Tuesday and forgets it for a year*. Sync
(16.06.02) cannot fix it, because sync keeps **availability** true and can say nothing about
**intention** (DT-12).

- **Auto-lapse**: an avail lapses within **≤60s** of its underlying 16.06.01 interval ceasing to be
  `available` — the Operator booked it in Google Calendar, blacked it out, or an exclusive sibling
  cascaded. Same granularity as 16.06.01's expiry sweep, for the same reason: the holder must not act on
  a date that no longer exists.
- **The fill boundary — exactly one reconfirm**: when an avail reaches `slot_start − on_sale_lead`
  (**42 days** default, per 16.06.01 D-07, per-room), it crosses from an **advance date** to a **fill
  date**. This is not a threshold this feature invented: it is 19's physics — a show that cannot be
  announced, marketed and put on sale cannot be sold, however free the room is. A fill date is a
  **different product** with different economics (usually a door deal, no guarantee, often a support
  slot), and presenting it as an ordinary avail lies to both sides (DT-11). At the boundary the avail
  fires **exactly one** confirm-or-withdraw prompt — mirroring 16.06.01 D-09's "no hold ever expires
  without being asked exactly once" — and, if confirmed, reclassifies and is labelled as a fill for the
  rest of its life.
- **Artist windows lapse** when their date range passes, or when their **quota fills**. Quota is soft: a
  filled window stops accepting **new** holds and flags the live ones; it never destroys a hold —
  destruction is 17.01.03/17.01.04's mechanism and this feature must not reach into it. The agent may
  raise the quota.

### Happy Path

1. The Leadmill's Operator has claimed the room and set opening hours in 16.06.01 (Tue–Sat 18:00–02:00,
   `Europe/London`). They open the avails view; it shows only intervals 16 already reports `available`.
2. They publish avails over **Tue 10 / Wed 11 / Tue 17 March 2026, 19:00–23:00** — 240 days out. Per
   avail they set posture (**door split 70/30 after costs**), ceiling (**≤£600**), bill shape
   (**headline**), visibility (**discoverable**). They type no capacity, no curfew, no load-in: the avail
   renders the room record's values with their age — *"300 cap · 23:00 curfew · verified 14 months ago"*
   (16.05.05 D-02).
3. An agent (Musician lens, acting for the entity **Pale Blue Machine**) publishes a routing window:
   **North of England, 12–19 March, quota 2, mid-week, £800–1,500, visibility invited**. The system
   checks it against the act's live contract terms and warns: *"Radius clause on your 14 Mar Manchester
   date — 60 mi / 30 days. Sheffield is 38 mi. 3 of the 6 dates in this window are unsellable."*
   (17.06, extended to publish time — DT-10). The agent narrows the window to 17–19 March.
4. The agent sends the window to a promoter list of 14 — 9 on-platform entities (invited), 5 by
   per-recipient link (off-platform). The window is one live object; the 14 recipients read its current
   state, not a copy.
5. The Leadmill's Tue 17 March avail matches. The match is presented to the agent with the room's spec
   and its freshness attached, and its **budget ceiling (£600) below the window's floor (£800)** shown
   as the reason it is a weak match, not hidden.
6. A promoter on the list places a hold on the Leadmill avail (17.01.02). **The avail does not change
   state** — it stays published, keeps its visibility, and continues to attract further positions. Only
   its contention indicator moves: *"1 hold · still open."*
7. On **3 February** — 42 days out — the Leadmill's unheld Wed 11 March avail crosses the fill boundary
   and fires its single reconfirm: *"11 March can no longer be announced and put on sale as a normal
   show. Keep it as a fill date, or withdraw it?"* The Operator keeps it; it relabels as **fill**, and
   drops out of advance-date matching.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Publish over an interval already `booked` / `blocked` / `cascade-blocked` in 16 | Refused. 16's reason is carried verbatim; a bare "unavailable" is useless (16.06.01 D-05) | "The Live Room is booked that night." |
| Publish over a date with a **confirmed show** | Hard-blocked. Confirmation is the only hard write back to the calendar (CX-04) | "This date is confirmed for {show}. Cancel the booking to reopen it." |
| Publish over an interval whose **exclusive sibling** has a live hold (`hold-blocked`, 16.06.01 D-17) | **Permitted** — an avail commits nothing, so it cannot freeze a building. The avail is labelled; a *booking* attempt downstream auto-challenges the sibling's hold, which is 16's mechanism, not this one | "Open — but the Live Room has a hold that could take this room. They have until Mon 09:00." |
| Room spec **field is stale** (16.05.05) | Avail publishes. The field shows with its age and **drops out of confident filtering** — a match on a rotten fact is worse than no match (16.05.05 D-03) | "PA: d&b Y-Series · verified 2 years ago" · absent from filtered results for that field |
| Room spec is **missing a minimum-publishable field** (capacity, or the room's address) | Publish blocked **on that field only**, naming it with a link to fix it. Not a spec-completeness gate — everything else is optional and its absence costs filterability, not publishability (D-05, resolving the breadth pass's Q-01) | "Add a capacity before publishing — agents filter on it first." |
| Room spec **changes after publish** (curfew moves, capacity re-rated) | The avail is a reference, so it re-renders automatically. Every **list recipient** and every party holding it is notified with the delta; 16.07 conformance re-runs for anyone who matched on the changed field | "The Leadmill's curfew moved to 22:00 — you have 17 March on your list." |
| A room's **statutory-slot declaration has expired** (16.01.06 D-06 — occupancy ceiling, liability cover, electrical/fire-safety record, performing-rights licence status, hirer requirements) | Expired is not stale — it is a harder state (16.05.05). Publish is **warned, not blocked** (it is their premises and declarations renew), but the avail drops out of confident filtering until renewed. **Which slots exist, and what instrument fills each, comes from the room's regime profile** (16.01.06 D-05) — this feature reads the slot and its expiry and never names the instrument. Only the US profile is authored and its **instrument names are a live `[PENDING]` on 16.01.06 D-06**, deferred to `/create-prd-security`; a slot the profile does not declare cannot expire and produces no warning | Operator: "Your {profile-declared liability-cover record} expired 4 Jan. This date won't be matched until it's renewed." |
| Operator's calendar is **unproven** — paper diary, no sync (16.06.01 D-22) | Avails still publish. D-22 gates instant-**booking**, which is a commitment; an avail is not. The soft hold absorbs the error — the Operator answers the request against their diary. **This is the feature working as designed**, not a gap | "Open (we'll confirm against our book)" |
| Sync degraded (16.06.02 D-04) | Existing avails shown with a staleness banner and last-synced time. Loud, never a green tick over a frozen calendar | "Availability may be out of date — last synced 6 days ago" |
| Underlying interval stops being available (Operator books it in Google Calendar) | Avail auto-lapses within **≤60s**; list recipients and any holders are notified. A hold on a lapsed avail is voided as a compensating action, never silently | Holder: "The Leadmill sold 17 March. Your hold is released." |
| **Withdraw an avail carrying live holds** | Permitted, but it **cannot silently swallow a hold** (mirrors 16.06.01's blackout rule). The Operator is shown exactly what they would void, by name, and must confirm | "This voids 3 holds: Rough Trade (1st), {…}. Release them?" |
| **Withdraw during a live challenge** (17.01.03) | Withdrawal wins; the challenge is voided as moot, both parties notified, and the challenger's rate-limit consumption is **credited back** — they spent a challenge on a date that ceased to exist (mirrors 16.06.01's challenger-withdrawal credit) | Challenger: "17 March was withdrawn. Your challenge didn't count against your limit." |
| **Withdraw races an incoming hold** | The two are resolved in a definite order, never applied together. If the hold lands first, withdrawal degrades into the named-release flow above — it never silently drops a hold that legally existed for an instant | "A hold arrived while you were withdrawing. Release it explicitly?" |
| **Radius clause intersects a published window** | Warned at **publish**, not only at hold (extends 17.06 CX-14 upstream). Never blocked — the clause may be renegotiated or the date may be outside it on inspection | "3 of 6 dates breach your Manchester radius (60 mi / 30 days)." |
| **Radius clause created later** by a new confirmed show | Previously-clean published windows now breach. Re-check fires on the confirmation; the agent is warned; **avails are not auto-withdrawn** — the clause is a term the act may renegotiate, and silently retracting their dates is the platform deciding a commercial matter | "Confirming Manchester makes 3 dates in your March window unsellable." |
| **Two agents hold the same avail in the same instant** | Not a conflict. The ladder absorbs it (16.06.01, CX-01); the avail is unchanged either way — it does not deplete | Both see their assigned position |
| **Quota fills** — 2 of 2 confirmed, 3rd hold live | Window lapses to **new** holds; the live 3rd hold survives and is flagged. Destroying it is 17.01.03/04's mechanism, not this feature's | Agent: "Window full. You still hold 19 March — keep or release?" · Holder: "This act's window is full; your hold stands." |
| Window's range passes with quota unfilled | Lapses silently; recorded, not celebrated | "North of England, 12–19 Mar: 0 of 2 filled" |
| Avail crosses the **42-day on-sale boundary** unheld | Reclassifies to **fill date** after exactly one confirm-or-withdraw prompt (D-08) | "11 March can't be announced and put on sale as a normal show now. Keep as a fill date, or withdraw?" |
| **Agent publishes avails for an act they don't represent** | Refused. Avails are entity-attributed (01) and publishing authority comes from a representation record (17.08). Claiming to represent an act you don't is a real and current industry harm | "You don't have a representation record for Pale Blue Machine." |
| **Act has a UK agent and a US agent** (territorial split — the norm) | Publishing authority is scoped to the representation's **territory**. A UK agent may not publish a US window | "Your representation covers UK & Ireland. This window is in Texas." · **Depends on 17.08 modelling territory — Q-04** |
| **Representation ends mid-window** (agent dropped) | Live avails **freeze**, they do not auto-withdraw. Auto-withdrawal would let a representation dispute nuke a tour in flight, and would let a false claim clear a rival's dates (mirrors 16.06.01's suspension rule) | Act: "Your ex-agent published 3 live windows. Take them over, or withdraw them?" |
| **The same human's two bands publish overlapping windows** | Legitimate and correct: avails are entity-attributed, so two bands are two publishers. The **bands are never told about each other** — that is the human's business, not theirs. The human is warned on their merged calendar | "You're in two windows covering 17 March. You can only play one." |
| **The same human is a Producer holding a studio and a Musician in a window on the same night** | The cross-domain multi-hyphenate conflict. Same treatment, across 16 and 17 | "You're booked at Konk on 17 March and in a window that covers it." |
| **Avails link forwarded to an unintended recipient** | Tokens are per-recipient, so a leak is attributable and revocable individually — but a forwarded token is indistinguishable from its owner at read time, and pretending otherwise would be false assurance. Anomalous read patterns on one token (many geographies) auto-revoke it and notify the publisher | Publisher: "Your link to {promoter} was opened from 6 countries. Revoked." |
| **Scraper reconstructs an unannounced tour from avails** | The sharpest harm available in this feature (D-10). Artist windows have no public tier; the discoverable tier is gated to claimed-verified-room entities (16.05.02); reads are rate-limited; breadth anomalies are a 24 signal. **Not fully closable** — a determined actor with a claimed room reads what a promoter reads | Anon/Fan: no avails surface exists at all |
| **Fan account with a claimed, verified room reads artist avails** | Legitimate. The gate is the claimed room, not the persona — a Fan who claims a venue is acting as an Operator, which is exactly what `personas.md` says personas are | Normal Operator-lens view |
| **A spurious room claim used as an avails-reading credential** | The claim gate (16.05.02) **is** the avails gate. If claiming is cheap, this leak is cheap. This feature imports 16.05.02's strength and cannot exceed it — recorded as a dependency, not solved here | — |
| **Off-platform promoter follows a link and wants the date** | They cannot self-hold — a hold has a counterparty on a ladder (16.06.01 D-14 is the symmetric case). Their reply lands as a structured enquiry in 17.07's inbox, and the Operator/agent places the hold on their behalf | "Reply to this list — {agent} will place the hold." |
| **Entity floods discovery with avails** | Bounded structurally: the Operator side cannot publish more avails than they have claimed rooms × available intervals; the artist side cannot publish outside its representation. Volume anomalies surface to 24; this feature reports, it does not punish | Rate-limit message with reset time |
| Operator **ghosts every hold request** on a published avail | Out of scope here — the request lifecycle is 17.01.02's (its Q-03). What this feature contributes is the signal: a published avail with a high request-to-no-response ratio is a 24 reputation input | — |
| Artist window spans a territory crossing **two time zones** (UK + IE) | No new machinery. A window is a **range of local dates in its territory**; matching compares against the room's own local date (16.06.01 D-11). An avail is on an interval, not an instant, so DST is already handled upstream | — |

### States

#### Avail states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Not publishable** | Room unclaimed, or no opening hours in 16.06.01 | Operator: "Set your opening hours before you can offer dates" → 16.06.01 onboarding |
| **Published (advance)** | Avail live, more than 42 days out (16.06.01 D-07) | Open date with posture, ceiling, bill shape, and the room record's specs **with their age** |
| **Published (fill)** | Crossed the on-sale boundary and was reconfirmed | Same, labelled **fill date** — different economics, out of advance matching (D-08) |
| **Contended** | 1+ holds live on the avail | Hold count and ladder depth; **still marked holdable** — the avail does not deplete (CX-01) |
| **Frozen** | Publisher's representation ended (17.08) or account suspended (24) | Visible, not holdable; the act is asked to adopt or withdraw it |
| **Lapsed** | Underlying interval ceased to be available (≤60s), range passed, or quota filled | "No longer available" — never *why* it died to a competing holder (16.06.01 D-15) |
| **Withdrawn** | Publisher withdrew | Withdrawn with a timestamp; every voided hold notified by name |
| **Blocked** | Date confirmed (CX-04) | The confirmed show; not holdable, not republishable |

#### View states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Avails fetch in flight | Skeleton; **nothing rendered optimistically** — a guessed "open" is a wasted hold request and a wasted phone call |
| **Empty (no avails)** | Claimed room, hours set, nothing offered | "Open dates to start receiving holds" |
| **Empty (no matches)** | Window searched, nothing fits | "No rooms match 12–19 March at 300+ cap under your radius" + the **loosest failing constraint named** — "3 rooms fit except your £800 floor" |
| **Populated** | Avails resolved | Calendar / list; room facts in room time, viewer's zone secondary when it differs (16.06.01 D-12) |
| **Partial (stale spec)** | One or more referenced room fields past their decay window (16.05.05) | Values shown **with age**, never hidden; excluded from confident filtering |
| **Partial (sync degraded)** | 16.06.02 sync failing | Staleness banner + last-synced time over the affected avails |
| **Partial (radius warning)** | Published window intersects a live radius clause (17.06) | Window shown with breaching dates marked, not removed |
| **Error** | Fetch failed | "Couldn't load availability" + retry. **Never renders a fallback grid** — availability must fail closed (16.06.01's rule) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Avails are per-date booleans | Naive booking-system modelling | ❌ REJECTED: an avail without deal posture, budget ceiling and bill shape is useless — those are what let an agent filter, and their absence is exactly why the current email workflow requires a phone call per date. A boolean avail rebuilds Google Calendar, which the Operator already has and already hates. |
| DT-02 | Artist-side avails and venue-side avails are different features | Two personas, two surfaces | ❌ REJECTED: they are one primitive from two ends. Both are "a window I will entertain offers on, with constraints". Splitting them duplicates the hold ladder, and worse, prevents matching an artist window against a room window — which is the actual product. **Sharpened at depth**: the two ends are not symmetric (DT-06, DT-07, DT-09), but the asymmetries are *fields and audiences*, not *mechanics*. One primitive, two field sets. |
| DT-03 | A published avail must be publicly visible to be useful | Marketplace-liquidity instinct | ❌ REJECTED: a domain expert would expect **invite-only avails** to be the norm at the top of the market. Agents guard routing plans; a venue publicly advertising an open Saturday signals weakness. Visibility scoping is not a nice-to-have — an always-public model would make the product unusable for exactly the professionals whose participation gives it liquidity. |
| DT-04 | Avails should auto-derive from the room calendar — if the room is free, it's available | Consolidation instinct; the data is right there | ❌ **REJECTED — and this rejection defines the feature's boundary.** A free Tuesday is not an offer to put a show on it. The Operator may want it for a private hire, may not want a gig that night, may not want *that kind* of gig ever. Auto-publishing every free interval commits them to offers they never made, floods discovery with dates nobody will sell, destroys the invite-only norm (DT-03), and makes the platform's avails untrustworthy exactly once — after which agents go back to email. **16 owns a physical fact; 17 owns a commercial intention.** |
