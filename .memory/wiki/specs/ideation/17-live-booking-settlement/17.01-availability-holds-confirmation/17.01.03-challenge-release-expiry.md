# Feature: Challenge, Release & Expiry

> **Number**: 17.01.03
> **Parent**: [Availability, Holds & Confirmation](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-30

## Overview

The three mechanisms that give the hold ladder teeth: a **challenge** forces a superior position to
confirm on real terms or drop within a fixed window (24h–14d, room default 48h); a **release** is a
voluntary drop; an **expiry** is the automatic drop when a hold's clock runs out. Without these, a
1st hold is a free indefinite option and the ladder is theatre.

All three are **destructive acts against someone else's commercial position**, executed by a clock
rather than a person. The feature's whole design problem is that the party being destroyed is
frequently not the party holding the phone: the hold belongs to the artist, the notification goes to
their agent, and the money at risk is the artist's. Every rule below follows from that asymmetry.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | **Configures the machinery that decides their losses, and answers challenges.** Sets the auto-release warning schedule and whether auto-release is enabled per avails window; sets their **escalation contract** — which channels a challenge may use (in-app, email, SMS, push) and at what point the platform may **bypass their agent and notify them directly** (default: 50% of the window elapsed with no acknowledgement). Sets their declared timezone, which is the authority for every deadline placement on their holds (D-06). Answers a challenge with **confirm** (→ 17.01.04), **drop**, **release-to-position**, or **request extension**. Cannot issue challenges, cannot see competing terms, cannot reorder the ladder. |
| Producer | None | No role in stage-date contention. The Producer persona's bookable resource is a studio room (domain 16), which has no hold ladder — studio time is booked, not held in ranked contention. A Producer who books a venue is acting as an **Operator** here; one whose band is on the ladder is acting as a **Musician**. Neither lens is "Producer". |
| Operator | Full | Issues challenges (and **may decline to issue one** — D-07), sets the room's default challenge window and hold durations, sets per-challenge window length within the 24h–14d bounds, releases a hold unilaterally, grants or declines extension requests, and grants or declines release-to-position requests. **Certifies** that a challenge is backed by a qualifying offer without disclosing its terms (D-08). Sees every countdown across their whole calendar — this is the Operator's early-warning surface for which dates are about to free up. When the Operator is themselves a party to the ladder (they own the room and their band wants the date), every challenge they issue on that date carries a **conflict-of-interest disclosure** to all positions (D-10). |
| Fan | None | Never visible, at any point, including after resolution. A challenge is a private commercial negotiation; a lost 1st hold is a show that never existed. The Fan's first contact with any of this is announce (17.01.04), which is downstream of a *confirmation*, never of a challenge. |

## Behavior

**Challenge** is the active mechanism. Position N asks the Operator to challenge position N−1. The
Operator decides whether to issue it; if issued, the superior holder gets a fixed countdown to
confirm the date or lose it. Skipping positions is forbidden — position 5 may not challenge
position 1, or the ladder would be destabilised from the bottom (D-02).

A challenge is **not free for the challenger**. Issuing one requires a live, qualifying offer
(17.02) from the challenging position, and that offer is **locked for the duration of the window**
— the challenger cannot withdraw or reduce it while the clock runs (D-09). You put someone else on
a clock only by putting your own money on the table for the same period. This is the single rule
that separates a challenge from a bluff.

The challenge's only two outcomes are **confirm** or **drop**. A timeout is treated as a drop
(D-01). Silence is an answer.

**Release** is voluntary and immediate: everything below promotes by one. Because a release under
challenge is the moment an agent most wants a face-saving option — and because a costly drop is
what drives the defensive confirm (DT-02) — the released party may request **release-to-position**:
exit the top of the ladder but request a lower position rather than leave entirely. The Operator
grants or declines, consistent with positions being Operator-assigned (17.01.02 D-01).

**Expiry** is passive and exists specifically to protect the Operator from their own documented
accidental failure (`meta/personas.md`) — "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd
happily have sold." An unsold Tuesday is gone forever; a forgotten hold is how it gets lost. Expiry
is **warn-then-release, never silent** (D-04), and a warning that was not *delivered* is not a
warning (D-05).

### Clock rules — these are the feature

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Window bounds** | Room policy sets a default (platform default 48h). Per-challenge override permitted within **24h ≤ W ≤ 14d**. No override below 24h under any circumstance, including with the challenged party's consent — a consent-based floor is coercible by the party who controls the ladder. |
| **Deadline placement** | The clock is **wall-clock**, not business-hours-elapsed — 48h means 48h. But the deadline **may not land** outside 09:00–18:00 on a business day in the challenged party's declared timezone and jurisdiction. If it would, it rolls forward to **12:00 on the next business day**. A 48h challenge issued Friday 17:00 therefore expires Monday 12:00, not Sunday 17:00. |
| **Timezone authority** | The **challenged party's** declared timezone (they must act). Fallback chain: challenged party's declared TZ → their artist entity's registered TZ (domain 01) → the room's TZ. Never the challenger's, never the server's, never a client clock. |
| **Grace** | The countdown displayed to the user ends at T. Answers received in **(T, T+5min]** are still honoured; the promotion executes at T+5min and is not broadcast before then. The grace is not advertised (an advertised grace becomes the deadline) and is never extended. |
| **Extension** | The challenged party may request **one** extension of **+24h**, only while a live offer from the challenger exists and negotiation is active (a counter-offer has moved in the last 24h, per 17.02). The Operator grants or declines within the remaining window; an undecided request does **not** pause the clock. A second extension request is refused without reaching the Operator. |
| **Offer-expiry cap** | If the challenger's locked offer expires before T, the challenge window is capped at the offer's expiry. The clock cannot outlive the money backing it. |
| **Escalation ladder** | T+0: in-app + email to the hold's registered contact (usually the agent). **25% elapsed, unacknowledged**: SMS/push per the Musician's escalation contract. **50% elapsed, unacknowledged**: notify the artist directly, bypassing the agent (Musician-configurable, default on). **75% elapsed, unacknowledged**: notify the Operator that this challenge is likely to time out, so a human can make a commercial judgement instead of the clock. Acknowledgement stops the escalation; it does **not** stop the clock. |
| **Auto-release warning schedule** | Hold ≥ 14d: warn at T−7d and T−48h. Hold 48h–14d: warn at T−48h and T−12h. Hold < 48h: warn at 50% elapsed and T−6h. Every warning uses the escalation contract, not an in-app badge alone. |
| **Delivery gates expiry** | A hold **may not expire** unless ≥1 warning was **delivery-confirmed** ≥12h earlier. If none was, expiry is deferred 24h and retried — **maximum 2 deferrals (48h total)**, after which expiry proceeds and is recorded with reason `expired-without-confirmed-warning`. The record is the honest residue: the harm is at least evidenced (D-05). |

### Deterministic resolution order

The existing model required this to be "deterministic and logged" without saying what it is. It is:

1. Every same-instant event is ordered by **server receipt timestamp**, monotonic, never a client clock.
2. **Scheduled events use their scheduled time as their receipt time.** An expiry due at 14:00:00 and
   a confirm received at 13:59:58 are ordered by those values — the confirm wins, no special-casing.
3. **Exact ties** (identical timestamps) break by precedence class, highest first:
   **(1)** avail withdrawal → **(2)** confirmation → **(3)** voluntary release → **(4)** expiry →
   **(5)** challenge resolution → **(6)** challenge issuance.
4. The ladder is **recomputed once, after all same-instant events are applied**, and **one**
   reshuffle notification is emitted per affected party — never one per event. No intermediate
   ladder is ever observable.

Release outranking expiry and challenge resolution is D-03 restated as arithmetic: the position is
voluntarily gone, so nothing downstream of it has anything to resolve.

### Happy Path

1. 2nd hold's agent has a real, signed-off offer on the table (17.02) and wants the date. They
   request a challenge against 1st hold.
2. System verifies the request: the requester is directly inferior to the target (D-02); a live
   qualifying offer exists; the requester and the target are not controlled by the same entity
   (D-11); the avail still exists.
3. System **locks the challenger's offer** for the proposed window. The challenger is told
   explicitly what they can no longer do: withdraw or reduce this offer until the challenge closes.
4. Operator receives the request with the ladder and the challenger's offer in full. They decide to
   issue, and set the window (room default 48h; bounded 24h–14d).
5. System computes the deadline T, rolls it forward if it would land outside business hours in the
   1st hold's declared timezone, and starts the countdown. 1st hold sees: an urgent countdown to a
   named date and time, the confirm / drop / release-to-position / request-extension actions, and
   **that the Operator has certified a qualifying offer exists** — not its terms (D-08).
6. Escalation fires on schedule: in-app + email now, SMS at 25%, artist-direct at 50%, Operator
   warned at 75%. Acknowledgement silences escalation but not the clock.
7. 1st hold's agent escalates to the artist. This is a money decision and the artist is the body
   that has to be in the room.
8. Artist authorises. Agent selects **confirm**. Before the action is available, the system runs the
   radius check (17.06) and routing feasibility (18); both pass.
9. Confirmation hands off to 17.01.04, which owns the atomic transition and the entity approval
   chain (17.02.03). The challenge closes with outcome `confirmed`.
10. The challenger's locked offer is released. Their hold is destroyed by the confirmation. They are
    notified with the reason class `confirmed-to-another-party` — the winning party's identity is
    **not** disclosed here; it arrives with the public announce, if one ever happens (17.01.02 Q-02
    remains the authority on identity visibility).
11. Every position on the ladder receives exactly one reshuffle notification reflecting the final
    ladder.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Challenge window expires with no response | Treated as a drop (D-01). Promotion executes at T+5min | Dropped party: "You lost 1st hold at {room} — no response by {deadline, their TZ}", with the full escalation trail (what was sent, to which channel, when, delivered y/n). The trail is the point: it is the evidence in the argument that follows |
| Challenged party confirms defensively, then cannot agree a deal | Booking reverts (17.01.04). Destroyed holds **do not come back** — compensating action only, no true rollback (CX-03). What the platform does instead: former holders receive a **first-refusal notice in their former ladder order**, each with 48h to re-place a hold at their former position before the date is republished to the open market. The collapse is recorded against the confirming party and feeds 17.12 (D-13) | Former 2nd hold: "The date you lost at {room} on {date} is available again. You have first refusal until {T+48h}. Your previous position was 2nd." Explicitly **not** framed as a restoration — the original hold and its expiry are gone, they may have re-routed, and the notice is a courtesy with priority, not a rollback |
| Is a remedy owed to a destroyed holder? | **No financial remedy.** A hold is a soft claim with no consideration paid; attaching damages converts holds into priced options and changes the entire mechanic. The remedy is **the record** (D-13) — thesis-consistent: evidence, not damages | Nothing is offered or claimed. The confirming party's reliability record carries the collapse; the market prices it |
| Expiry and challenge fire on adjacent positions simultaneously | Resolved by the deterministic order above: expiry (class 4) before challenge resolution (class 5) on exact ties, otherwise by receipt time. Ladder recomputed once; one notification per party | Both parties see a consistent final ladder. The ordering rule is not surfaced — only the outcome |
| Voluntary release arrives during a challenge on the same position | Release wins (D-03); the challenge closes as `won-by-release` | Challenger: "Position released — you are now 1st hold." Their locked offer is released from lock |
| Position 5 attempts to challenge position 1 | Refused — only the directly superior position may be challenged (D-02) | "You can only challenge the hold directly above you. You are 5th." |
| Challenger withdraws their offer mid-challenge | **Impossible by construction** — the offer is locked for the window (D-09). The attempt is refused | "This offer is locked until {T} because it is backing your challenge on {date}. Withdraw the challenge to release it." |
| Challenger withdraws the **challenge** itself mid-window | Permitted. Challenge closes `withdrawn-by-challenger`; the challenged party keeps their position and their original expiry (the challenge never touched it); the offer unlocks. The withdrawal is recorded — repeated issue-then-withdraw is challenge spam and is 24's problem | Challenged party: "The challenge on your 1st hold at {room} was withdrawn. Your hold stands, expiring {original expiry}." |
| Challenger's locked offer expires before T | Window is capped at the offer's expiry; challenge closes `offer-lapsed` at that moment | Both parties told the challenge ended because the offer behind it lapsed. The challenged party's position stands untouched |
| Operator declines to issue a requested challenge | Permitted (D-07). No clock starts, nothing is consumed, the offer never locks | Requester: "{Operator} declined to challenge the 1st hold on this date" with the Operator's stated reason if given. The decline is visible and recorded — an Operator who declines to challenge on behalf of real money is holding the challenger's inventory hostage to someone else's option, and the requester's remedy is to withdraw their hold and say why |
| Operator never responds to a challenge request | The request itself carries a **7-day expiry**, then closes `request-lapsed`. Nothing else is affected | Requester: "Your challenge request on {date} expired without a decision from {Operator}." (Consistent with 17.01.02 Q-03's shape — an ignored request is the Operator's cheapest way to say no. Here it is at least timeboxed and named) |
| Challenge issued on a date whose avail was withdrawn | The avail is the resource the whole ladder hangs from; withdrawing it voids every hold (17.01.01, CX-01). The ladder ceases to exist, so any live challenge dies with it, closing `voided-by-withdrawal`. **This is not a drop and not a timeout** — no party's reliability record is debited (D-12) | Challenged party: "The date was withdrawn by {Operator}. Your hold is void — you did not lose it." Challenger: same, plus their offer unlocks. Reason-code distinctness is load-bearing: a party voided by withdrawal must never be recorded as having gone silent |
| Avail withdrawn in the same instant a challenge is requested | Withdrawal wins (precedence class 1) — a challenge against a non-existent ladder is meaningless | Requester sees "this date is no longer available", **not** "challenge declined". Distinct reason code |
| Auto-release drops a date the Musician still wanted | Cannot happen silently (D-04). Warn-then-release per the schedule above, and expiry is **gated on delivery confirmation** (D-05) — the position cannot die unless the platform proved a warning reached them ≥12h earlier, up to 2×24h deferrals | Warning: "Your 1st hold at {room}, {date} expires {T}. Renew, confirm, or it drops automatically." At expiry: "{date} at {room} has been released — your hold expired." If it expired after 2 failed deferrals: the record carries `expired-without-confirmed-warning`, and both the holder and the Operator see that flag |
| Musician has auto-release disabled and never acts | Their hold does not auto-expire. The Operator's unilateral release (Role Lens) is the backstop — the Operator's perishable inventory is not hostage to another party's config. The Operator's release is a human decision, notified, and recorded as `released-by-operator` | Operator sees the hold flagged as opted-out-of-expiry in their calendar, so the choice is visible before it costs them a Tuesday |
| Agent drops by mistake at 1am | **60-second undo window** on release/drop. The reshuffle is not broadcast during it; no other party can observe or act on the drop. After 60s: irreversible, no exceptions | "Dropped 1st hold at {room}. Undo (57s)" |
| Cascading challenge: 3 challenges 2 while 2 challenges 1, and 1 drops | The challenge attaches to **the party**, not the position number, and **survives promotion**. Party-B promotes 2nd→1st still carrying party-C's challenge, on the original clock, un-reset. C, now 2nd, is still directly superior-adjacent to B — D-02's invariant holds throughout (DT-07). Resetting on promotion would be a free 48h option, obtainable by colluding with position 1 to drop | B: "You are now 1st hold. Your challenge deadline is unchanged: {T}." The clock visibly does not move — that is the message |
| Operator inserts a new hold **between** a challenger and the party they are challenging | The D-02 invariant breaks through no act of the challenger's. Challenge is **voided** (`voided-by-insertion`), the offer unlocks, the challenged party's position stands. The challenger may re-challenge the newly adjacent party | Challenger: "Your challenge was cancelled — {Operator} placed a hold above you." The Operator is named, mirroring 17.01.02's rule that demotion is a trust event, not a silent write |
| Same agency controls both the challenging and the challenged position | **Refused.** The agent controls both sides and would simply pick one; a challenge here is a nonsense state that would produce a real, recorded "loss" against their own act. Detection is entity-graph-scoped (domain 01), not account-scoped — an agency shopping two of its own acts at one room is normal practice, and the refusal must name it as such, not accuse | "You represent both the 1st and 2nd hold on this date. Release one to proceed." |
| Operator is a party to their own ladder (venue owner's band wants the date) | **Disclosed, not prohibited.** Venue owners book their own bands legitimately and constantly (`meta/personas.md`: Operators frequently perform and engineer too). But a challenge issued by an Operator against a rival for a date the Operator's own act sits behind is self-dealing, and the ladder is already Operator-assigned. Every challenge on that date carries a conflict disclosure to all positions (D-10) | All positions: "{Operator} is also a party to this date via {act}." Shown at hold placement, at challenge issue, and on the record afterwards |
| Confirming under challenge would violate a radius clause (17.06) | The check runs **before** the confirm action is offered, and it is a **hard block** — 17.01.04 C-06 applies unchanged under a clock (D-15). The block's door is the **waiver-request instrument** (17.06 D-04): an in-app request against the constraining Operator, on its own clock, escalating on the ladder above and capped by the challenge deadline it exists to beat. A lapsed request grants nothing — silence is not consent, and C-06 still blocks | "Confirming this date breaches your radius clause on {show}, {date}, {venue}, held by {Operator}. **Request a waiver** — {Operator} has until {waiver deadline, their TZ}. You cannot confirm until it is granted." |
| The waiver lapses or is refused and the challenge deadline is hours away | **Break-glass**, and only here: an override exists **only while a challenge clock is live** (this is the sole exception to C-06's hard set). It requires the **artist principal, never a delegate** (17.02.03's binding authority, not its negotiating authority); it notifies the constraining Operator **at the moment it is used**, not after; and it writes a named breach event (`radius-break-glass`) attributed to the confirming party, feeding 17.12. D-12's fault-free exclusions do **not** cover it — this is counterparty behaviour, not a platform failure | Principal only: "{Operator} has not granted a waiver. Confirming breaches {contract}. {Operator} will be told the moment you do, and this is recorded against you permanently." An agent sees the same screen with the action disabled and the named principal who must act |
| Confirming under challenge creates an impossible routing (18) | Warn + override, never block — **unlike radius (D-15)**, which is a contractual permission and not a feasibility opinion. An artist may genuinely fly; 18 owns feasibility, not permission | "London 14 March → Glasgow 15 March: routing flagged infeasible by your touring profile. Confirm anyway?" |
| Challenged party confirms without artist authority | Not this feature's gate — 17.01.04 D-02 and the 17.02.03 approval chain own it. But **this is where it happens**: a 48h clock is the exact pressure that makes an agent skip the approval. The challenge surface must therefore display the approval chain's live status *inside* the countdown, not behind a link | Countdown shows "Artist approval: outstanding — {named approver} notified {time}" beside the confirm action |
| Operator goes dark entirely mid-challenge (sells the venue, quits) | Nothing stalls. The clock is scheduled, the timeout is automatic, the promotion executes without a human. The mechanism is Operator-absence-tolerant by construction (DT-13) | No user-visible difference. The absence surfaces only as unanswered extension/release-to-position requests, which lapse |
| Two challenges on one ladder at different position pairs (5v4 and 2v1) | Both permitted and independent — they touch disjoint pairs. Each resolves on its own clock; the ladder recomputes once per resolution | Each pair sees only their own countdown. Neither is told about the other's |
| Challenged party requests an extension with no live negotiation | Refused before it reaches the Operator — there is nothing to negotiate, so the clock stands. "I need more time" without an offer moving is the indefinite option the ladder exists to destroy | "Extensions require an active negotiation. No counter-offer has moved on this date in 24h." |
| Party who dropped under challenge later climbs back and re-challenges the winner | **Refused for the life of that ladder.** Otherwise the drop is free: A drops (clock stops), regroups, then puts B on a clock — converting A's own challenge into B's at zero cost | "You dropped under challenge from {act} on this date. You cannot challenge them on it again." Climbing past *other* positions remains permitted — that requires actually beating them |
| Challenged party answers 3 seconds after the deadline | Honoured — within the 5-minute grace; the promotion has not broadcast | Nothing unusual. The grace is invisible by design |
| Challenged party answers 6 minutes after the deadline | Refused. The promotion has executed and been broadcast; the ladder has moved and other parties have acted on it | "This date is gone. Your 1st hold expired at {T} without a response." |
| Notification channel hard-bounces (dead agent email) throughout the window | Escalation walks the ladder: SMS at 25%, **artist-direct at 50%**, Operator warned at 75%. If nothing is delivery-confirmed by T, the timeout still stands — but is recorded `timed-out-without-confirmed-delivery`, and that flag is visible to both parties and excluded from the reliability signal (17.12). A challenge is not a warning: the challenged party had a live commercial position and the clock is the mechanic. But an undelivered clock must not become evidence against them | Both parties see the flag and the full channel trail. The Operator sees it before the promotion is final, at 75% |
| Malicious challenge spam: repeated issue-and-withdraw to burn a rival's attention | Each cycle requires a real, lockable offer, which is the throttle (D-09). Beyond that, the pattern is a Trust & Safety concern (domain 24) and out of scope here. The record exists; 24 decides what to do with it | Nothing here. The withdrawal record is the artefact 24 consumes |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Idle** | Hold live, no contention | Position, expiry countdown, next warning time |
| **Challenge requested** | Inferior position requested a challenge; Operator has not decided | Requester: pending badge + their offer now shows as locked. Challenged party: **nothing** — an undecided request must not leak, or the Operator loses the ability to decline privately |
| **Challenged** | Operator issued the challenge | Urgent countdown to a named date/time in their TZ; confirm / drop / release-to-position / request-extension; certified-offer-exists badge (no terms); live approval-chain status; radius/routing flags if any |
| **Extension requested** | Challenged party asked for +24h | Countdown continues, unpaused, with "extension requested — awaiting {Operator}" beside it |
| **Confirming** | Challenged party chose confirm; checks passed | Handoff to 17.01.04 |
| **Dropped** | Drop, timeout, release, or release-to-position | Position gone (or reduced); reason code and timestamp recorded; escalation trail attached |
| **Expiring soon** | Clock crossed a warning threshold | Warning via the escalation contract naming the exact expiry moment and the actions available |
| **Expiry deferred** | Expiry due but no warning delivery-confirmed ≥12h prior | Holder: nothing (they are unreachable — that is the state). Operator: "This hold could not be expired — {holder} has not received a warning. Deferred to {T+24h}. 1 of 2 deferrals used." |
| **Expired** | Clock ran out with warning delivery satisfied, or after 2 deferrals | Position gone; ladder promoted; all parties notified once with the final ladder |
| **Voided** | Avail withdrawn, or Operator inserted between challenger and challenged | Position or challenge gone **without fault**; reason code distinguishes it from a drop; no reliability debit |
| **Loading** | Countdown surface opened | Position and deadline render first from last-known state; the offer-certification badge and approval-chain status stream in. **The countdown never renders a skeleton** — a blank clock on a 48h money deadline is worse than a stale one |
| **Empty** | No holds on any of the user's dates | Operator: "No active holds. Countdowns appear here as holds are placed." Musician: "No holds on your dates." |
| **Populated** | 1+ live countdowns | Countdowns sorted by urgency, not by date — the thing about to expire is the thing that matters |
| **Error** | Countdown state cannot be fetched | **Last-known deadline shown with an explicit staleness stamp** ("as of 14:02 — reconnecting"), never a blank state and never a spinner over a live deadline. Actions are disabled while stale, because acting on a stale ladder is worse than not acting. Retry is automatic |
| **Partial** | Ladder readable but the challenger's offer certification is unavailable | Countdown and actions remain live; the certification badge shows "unverified — {Operator} has not certified an offer". **Confirm stays available**: a certification outage must not force a drop by making the answer unanswerable |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A challenge is a notification — the challenged party can just ignore it | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED: silence **is** an answer, and it means drop. This is the entire point of the mechanic. Modelling a challenge as a dismissible notification would recreate the indefinite free option the ladder exists to destroy. The timeout path is the most important path, not an edge case. |
| DT-02 | Confirming under challenge is safe because the deal follows | Sequencing instinct | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **real hazard**, not a safe path. An agent under a 48h clock will confirm to protect the date and negotiate afterwards — and the confirmation has already destroyed the inferior holds irreversibly. Resolved not by blocking the confirm (D-14) but by attacking its three causes: the clock can bend when a deal is live (extension), the drop is made cheap (release-to-position), and the collapse is evidenced (D-13). |
| DT-03 | Auto-release is unambiguously good | It solves the Operator's documented accidental failure (`meta/personas.md`) | ❌ REJECTED as unambiguous: it is good for the Operator and **hostile to the Musician**, who loses a date they still wanted without a human ever deciding to take it. The failure modes are symmetric and the personas file only documents one of them. Resolved as warn-then-release with delivery-gated expiry (D-04, D-05); the schedule's configurability remains Q-02. |
| DT-04 | A challenger can force a drop, then walk away — the challenge costs them nothing | Adversarial reading of the mechanic | ✅ CONFIRMED as **the strongest abuse vector in the feature**. Challenge position 1 with a soft offer, watch them drop, withdraw the offer, take 1st hold for free. Nothing in the sweep's model prevents it. Fixed by locking the challenger's offer for the window (D-09): you put someone on a clock only by putting your own money on the table for the same period. This is also why the industry's "don't challenge unless you're real" norm exists — the software must encode the norm, not just the mechanic. |
| DT-05 | A 48h window means 48 hours | Literal reading | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient: a 48h window issued Friday 17:00 expires Sunday 17:00, when nobody in this industry is at a desk. That is not a challenge, it is a trap, and experienced agents issue Friday challenges **on purpose**. A domain expert would expect business-day handling and nobody has mentioned it. Fixed by the deadline-placement rule (D-06) — the duration stays wall-clock (48h means 48h) but the deadline may not land where the party cannot act. |
| DT-06 | The challenged party should see the competing offer's terms so they can decide informedly | Transparency instinct | ❌ REJECTED: it hands position 1 the number they must beat, and a bad-faith position 1 confirms at exactly that number. It also breaks the domain's confidentiality norm (17.08's entire Operator column is `None` for the same reason). But the challenge cannot be unaccountable either — "challenge me on what?" is a fair question. Resolved: the Operator **certifies** a qualifying offer exists; the challenged party sees the certification, never the terms (D-08). The Operator is already the trust broker who issues the challenge. |
| DT-07 | A challenge dies, or resets, when the challenged party is promoted | Positional modelling of the challenge | ❌ REJECTED: the challenge attaches to a **party**, not a position number, and survives promotion on its original clock. "Confirm or drop" is the same question at 1st as at 2nd — the information changed, the question did not. Resetting on promotion is a free 48h option obtainable by colluding with position 1 to drop. D-02's adjacency invariant survives the promotion automatically, since everyone below moves in lockstep. |
| DT-08 | The Operator must issue any challenge a position holder requests | Fairness instinct — otherwise the ladder is hostage to the Operator | ❌ REJECTED: an automatic challenge lets the bottom of the ladder compel the talent buyer, undoing 17.01.02 D-01 (position is Operator-assigned commercial judgement) from below. Two new couplings this file adds and Step 6 must reconcile: (a) an Operator **insertion between** a challenger and their target breaks the adjacency invariant and voids the challenge — the ladder's write is my feature's void; (b) **release-to-position** requires the ladder to accept a re-entry at an Operator-assigned lower position, which is a new ladder mutation the ladder file does not model.
- Touches **17.01.04 Confirmation & Announce Gate** ([./17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md](./17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md)) — **trigger dependency** (CX-03). A won challenge is the most common path into confirmation. **Boundary declared explicitly**: 17.01.04 owns the confirm transition, its atomicity, and the revert trigger. This file owns what the *ladder* does about it — the first-refusal notice and its ordering (D-13) are ladder semantics fired by 17.01.04's revert. Their Q-03 and this file's Q-01 are **the same question** asked from both ends and must be answered once.
- Touches **17.01.01 Availability Calendar** ([./17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md](./17.01.01-availability-calendar-avails.md)) — **shared entity + trigger** (CX-05). Auto-release returns the slot to avails with no human in the loop. Inverse direction: avail withdrawal voids the ladder and therefore every live challenge, with a fault-free reason code (D-12).
- Touches **17.02 Offers & Negotiation** ([../17.02-offers-negotiation/](../17.02-offers-negotiation/)) — **state race, and the strongest coupling in this file.** A challenge requires a live qualifying offer, and **locks it for the window** (D-09). 17.02 must therefore model a lock it does not own and cannot clear: offer withdrawal and reduction are refused while a challenge holds the lock, and an offer's own expiry **caps** the challenge window. If 17.02's offer lifecycle does not admit an externally-held lock, this feature's central anti-abuse rule does not exist.
- Touches **17.02.03 (entity approval chain)** — **permission**. Confirming under challenge still requires artist authority (17.01.04 D-02). The 48h clock is the specific pressure that makes agents skip it, so the approval chain's live status must render **inside** the countdown, not behind a link. **New with D-15**: the radius break-glass is a *strictly narrower* permission than confirm — the **artist principal in person, never a delegate**, which is a third authority slot alongside offer-binding (17.02.03 D-03) and settlement-signing (17.09.05 D-06) and is recorded for domain 01 to model. An agent with full booking authority may confirm and may not break glass.
- Touches **17.06 Radius Clause & Exclusivity** ([../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md](../17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md)) — **permission dependency** (DT-12, D-15). The radius check must run before the confirm action is offered under challenge, and a breach is a **hard block** absent a waiver on file (17.01.04 C-06). Two new requirements this file places on 17.06: (a) the **waiver-request instrument** must be answerable inside a challenge window — its clock is capped by the challenge deadline, since a waiver granted after the date is lost is not a door; (b) 17.06 must model the **break-glass breach** as a distinct state from a breach a third party's later confirm created, because only the first is attributable.
- Touches **18 Show Production & Touring** ([../../18-show-production-touring/](../../18-show-production-touring/)) — **trigger dependency**. Routing feasibility warns and may be overridden with the override recorded. This is deliberately *not* the radius treatment: 18 owns feasibility, it does not own permission, and a flight the artist is willing to take is nobody else's contract.
- Touches **17.12 Counterparty Relationship & Payment Reliability** ([../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md](../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md)) — **derived record; the thesis lands here.** This feature is a *producer* of reliability facts: defensive-confirm collapses (D-13), challenge-request declines (D-07), issue-and-withdraw spam, timeouts. **Hard constraint on 17.12**: `timed-out-without-confirmed-delivery`, `voided-by-withdrawal`, `voided-by-insertion` and `expired-without-confirmed-warning` are **excluded from the reliability signal** (D-12, DT-15) — they are platform failures or third-party acts, not counterparty behaviour.

Account-scoped checks would miss every real instance of both.
