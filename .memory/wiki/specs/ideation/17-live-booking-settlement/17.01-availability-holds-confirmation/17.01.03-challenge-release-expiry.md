# Feature: Challenge, Release & Expiry

> **Number**: 17.01.03
> **Parent**: [Availability, Holds & Confirmation](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The three mechanisms that give the hold ladder teeth: a **challenge** forces a superior position to
confirm on real terms or drop within a fixed window (typically 24–48h); a **release** is a voluntary
drop; an **expiry** is the automatic drop when a hold's clock runs out. Without these, a 1st hold is
a free indefinite option and the ladder is theatre.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sets auto-release preferences ("drop any hold I haven't acted on in 30 days") and how urgently they want to be reached when challenged. **Answers challenges via their agent** — the artist is the one whose money is at risk if they confirm defensively on a date they didn't really want, so the notification must actually reach them, not die in an agent's inbox. |
| Producer | None | No role in stage-date contention. |
| Operator | Full | Issues challenges, sets the challenge window length as room policy, sets default hold durations, and can release a hold unilaterally. Sees every countdown across their whole calendar — this is the Operator's early-warning surface for which dates are about to free up. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. A challenge is a private commercial negotiation. |

## Behavior

**Challenge** is the active mechanism: position N asks the Operator to challenge position N−1. The
superior holder gets a fixed countdown to confirm the date on real, agreed terms or lose it.
Skipping positions is forbidden — position 5 may not challenge position 1, or the ladder would be
destabilised from the bottom.

**Release** is voluntary and immediate: everything below promotes by one.

**Expiry** is passive and, per `meta/personas.md`, exists specifically to protect the Operator from
their own documented accidental failure — "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily
have sold." An unsold Tuesday is gone forever; a forgotten hold is how it gets lost.

The challenge's only two outcomes are **confirm** or **drop**. A timeout is treated as a drop.

### Happy Path

1. 2nd hold has a real offer and wants the date; their agent requests a challenge.
2. Operator issues the challenge to the 1st hold, starting the room's standard window (e.g. 48h).
3. 1st hold's agent is notified urgently and escalates to the artist — this is a money decision.
4. 1st hold either confirms on agreed terms (→ 17.01.04) or drops.
5. If they drop, 2nd hold promotes to 1st; the whole ladder is notified of the reshuffle.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Challenge window expires with no response | Treated as a drop; ladder promotes | Dropped party sees "you lost 1st hold at {room} — no response within 48h" with the timestamp trail |
| Challenged party confirms defensively, then cannot agree a deal | Booking reverts — but the holds destroyed by the confirmation **do not come back**. Compensating action only; no true rollback. **This is the sub-domain's hardest consistency problem** (CX-03). | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — what the reverted-to party is told is undecided |
| Expiry and challenge fire on adjacent positions simultaneously | Resolution order must be deterministic and logged, or the ladder is ambiguous | Both parties see a consistent final ladder; the ordering rule itself is not surfaced |
| Voluntary release arrives during a challenge on the same position | Release wins; the challenge is moot and is closed as won | Challenger sees "position released — you are now 1st hold" |
| Position 5 attempts to challenge position 1 | Refused — only the directly superior position may be challenged | "You can only challenge the hold directly above you. You are 5th." |
| Auto-release drops a date the Musician still wanted | The symmetric harm to the Operator's protection — silent auto-release is hostile to the artist | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a warn-then-release ladder is the likely answer, unvalidated |
| Challenge issued on a date whose avail was withdrawn | `[PENDING — Step 5]` | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | Hold live, no contention | Position and expiry countdown |
| Challenged | Challenge issued | Urgent countdown, confirm/drop actions, the terms on the table |
| Confirming | Challenged party chose confirm | Handoff to 17.01.04 |
| Dropped | Drop, timeout, or release | Position gone; reason and timestamp recorded |
| Expiring soon | Clock below a warning threshold | Warning to the holder before silent loss |
| Expired | Clock ran out | Position gone; ladder promoted; all parties notified |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A challenge is a notification — the challenged party can just ignore it | Naive modelling | ❌ REJECTED: silence **is** an answer, and it means drop. This is the entire point of the mechanic. Modelling a challenge as a dismissible notification would recreate the indefinite free option the ladder exists to destroy. The timeout path is the most important path, not an edge case. |
| DT-02 | Confirming under challenge is safe because the deal follows | Sequencing instinct | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **real hazard**, not a safe path. An agent under a 48h clock will confirm to protect the date and negotiate afterwards — and the confirmation has already destroyed the inferior holds irreversibly. A domain expert would expect this exact failure. The product must either require agreed terms *before* confirm is available, or make the revert path explicit about what it cannot undo. Currently unresolved (Q-01). |
| DT-03 | Auto-release is unambiguously good | It solves the Operator's documented accidental failure (`meta/personas.md`) | ❌ REJECTED as unambiguous: it is good for the Operator and **hostile to the Musician**, who loses a date they still wanted without a human ever deciding to take it. The failure modes are symmetric and the personas file only documents one of them. A warn-then-release ladder is the likely answer but is a product decision, not an agent call. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Timeout equals drop | Silence is an answer. Any other reading restores the indefinite free option the ladder exists to eliminate (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Only the directly superior position may be challenged | Skipping would let the bottom of the ladder destabilise the top, which is the abuse the ranking prevents. |
| D-03 | Release beats a concurrent challenge on the same position | The challenge is moot once the position is voluntarily gone; the challenger should win cleanly rather than wait out a countdown against nobody. |
