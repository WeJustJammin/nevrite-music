# Feature: Commission Accrual & Deduction at Settlement

> **Number**: 17.08.02
> **Parent**: [Agency Representation & Commission](./17.08-agency-representation-commission-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Turning a settled show into the agency's money — evaluating the commission formula against the
settlement, deducting it from the artist's side, and disbursing it. This is where an agency actually
gets paid, and where an artist finds out what representation cost them on this particular night.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The **artist** sees commission deducted from their own money, with the basis and the line it was computed from — they must be able to check it, because historically they could not. The **agency** sees the accrual land and the money arrive. This is the settlement information asymmetry appearing inside the artist's own team, and it is the same asymmetry the domain exists to close. |
| Producer | None | No role — a session/mixing engineer is not a party to a live booking commission agreement. If a producer happens to also be the represented performer, they experience this feature through the **Musician** lens, not this one. |
| Operator | None | Commission comes off the artist's side after the promoter's obligation is discharged. The promoter pays the deal; what happens to that money inside the artist's team is not theirs to see. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The commission terms (17.08.01) compile to a formula; this feature evaluates it against the finalised
settlement (17.09.01) and deducts.

The order of operations is the whole game. Commission on **gross** is taken before the artist's costs;
commission on **net** is taken after — and *which* net depends on which settlement line the basis
names. Stack a manager's percentage on top and the order between them decides the artist's take
(17.08.01 Q-03).

Deduction at source versus invoicing afterwards (index Q-02) is unresolved and material: at-source
makes the platform a party to the agency's money and drags in the same KYC/AML questions as escrow;
invoicing is safer and reduces the feature to bookkeeping.

The hazard that defines this feature: **a settlement can be amended after commission is paid**
(17.09.06). That is a clawback from a third party who has already been paid.

### Happy Path

1. Settlement finalises (17.09.05) with signed-off numbers.
2. System evaluates the commission formula against the settlement line the basis names.
3. Commission accrues; the artist sees the deduction with its full derivation, not just a number.
4. Deduction executes as part of disbursement (17.10.02).
5. The accrual leaves the agency's pipeline (17.08.03) — a projection becomes a fact.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Settlement amended after commission disbursed | **Clawback from a third party who has already been paid** — the platform holds an obligation it cannot execute | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-02 |
| Show settles at a loss or the artist takes nothing | Commission on gross is still owed; commission on net is not | Basis decides; the artist paying commission on a show that lost money is real and legal |
| Date booked under superseded terms | The pinned version governs, not the current one (17.08.01 D-02) | Derivation names the terms version |
| Cross-collateralized run (17.03.03) with provisional numbers | Commission on a provisional number is itself provisional | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — compounds the run's finality conflict |
| Agent booked the date but was fired before the show | The sunset rule governs (17.08.01 DT-02) — commission is usually still owed | Derivation names the sunset rule applied |
| Agent and manager both commission the same gross | Order of operations decides the artist's take | Must be explicit; `[PENDING — Step 5]` (17.08.01 Q-03) |
| Artist disputes the commission computation | Not a settlement dispute — a dispute with their own agent | Routed to 24, but the counterparty is inside the artist's own team |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Commission is a payout split and belongs in 17.10 | Locality instinct — it is money coming out of the settlement alongside the band's shares | ❌ REJECTED: a split divides **one pool among its owners** under one agreement; commission is a **deduction from one party's share** under a *separate agreement with a separate counterparty*. Folding commission into the split would make the agent look like a band member — with a share of the pool rather than a claim on the artist's share — and would apply the band's authority rule to an agreement the band is not party to. Two instruments, two homes (index D-03). |
| DT-02 | Commission is final once paid | The obvious reading — the show is over, the money moved | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's hardest problem. **Settlements get amended** (17.09.06) — a miscounted comp, a disputed expense, a box office correction — and an amendment after disbursement means clawing money back from an agency that has already been paid and has already spent it. The platform ends up holding an obligation it has no mechanism to execute. A domain expert would expect this; nothing in the sweep's candidate suggests it was considered. The options (hold commission until an amendment window closes / accept clawback risk / net against future accruals) are all product decisions with real costs. |
| DT-03 | Commission on a loss-making show is an edge case to prevent | Fairness instinct — surely you don't pay commission on a show that lost money | ❌ REJECTED: on a **gross** basis it is entirely normal, legal, and exactly what the agreement says. The agent's work was booking the date, not selling the tickets. Designing this away would be the platform silently rewriting an agreement between two parties — which is not the platform's business. What the platform **can** do is make it visible *before* it happens: 17.03.02's modeller should show the artist that this deal, at this attendance, pays their agent and not them. Transparency, not paternalism. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Commission is a deduction from a party's share, not a split of the pool | A split divides one pool under one agreement; commission is a separate agreement with a separate counterparty (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Every deduction shows its full derivation, not just a number | The artist must be able to check what representation cost them — this is the settlement asymmetry appearing inside their own team. |
| D-03 | The platform does not design away commission on loss-making shows | It is what the agreement says; rewriting it silently is not the platform's business. Make it visible in modelling instead (DT-03). |
