# Feature: Representation & Commission Terms

> **Number**: 17.08.01
> **Parent**: [Agency Representation & Commission](./17.08-agency-representation-commission-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The economic terms of being represented — the rate, the basis, the scope, and what happens when the
relationship ends. The relationship itself belongs to domain 01 (an agency is an organization;
representation is a relationship between an org and an artist); what lives here is the **formula that
turns a live settlement into the agency's money**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both parties to the agreement are Musician-side (index Role Matrix note). The **artist** agrees the rate and basis, and must be able to read what they signed in terms of consequences — 20% of gross and 20% of net are different careers. The **agency** sets its standard terms and varies them per signing. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | None | Correct, not an oversight. What an act pays its agent is deliberately invisible to the promoter — a promoter who could see it would negotiate against it. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Commission terms compile to a formula evaluated at settlement (CX-01) — the same structural pattern
as the deal grammar → settlement formula edge that defines the domain.

The parameters that matter:

- **Rate** — the headline percentage. The least interesting term.
- **Basis** — gross or net, and *which* net. This is where the money is.
- **Scope** — territory and work type. A US agent does not commission a UK festival (CX-03).
- **Sunset** — what happens to dates booked before the relationship ended. This is the hard part.

The sunset problem is the feature's real subject: an agent who books a date in January and is fired
in March is usually still owed commission on the show in June, because they did the work. Every
agency agreement addresses it and no two address it identically.

### Happy Path

1. An agency and an artist establish representation (the relationship is created in domain 01).
2. The agency proposes commission terms — rate, basis, scope, sunset.
3. Artist entity approves under its own authority rule (the same rule that binds deals, 17.02.03).
4. Terms compile to a formula and become live; dates booked from now on accrue against it (17.08.02).
5. Terms are read at every settlement for the life of the relationship — and beyond it, for dates
   booked while it was live.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Terms change mid-relationship | Already-booked dates are governed by the terms **in force when they were booked**, not the current ones (CX-01) | Terms are versioned; each booking pins the version it was made under |
| Relationship ends with dates still to play | The sunset problem — the fired agent is usually still owed on shows they booked | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see DT-02 |
| Artist has three agents across three territories | Each commissions only its own scope (CX-03) — the multi-hyphenate problem on the representation axis | Scope must be structured, or nothing can be filtered (Q-02) |
| Basis says "net" without defining which net | The same ambiguity as the deal grammar's deduction order (17.03.01 DT-01), one level up | Basis must reference a defined settlement line, not a word |
| Scope written as free text in a PDF agreement | The platform cannot read it, so it cannot filter or compute | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (Q-02) |
| Manager commission (percentage of everything) alongside agent commission (live only) | Two different instruments stacked on the same money | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (index Q-03) |
| Double commission — agent and manager both take a cut of the same gross | Real and normal; the artist's take is what is left | Stacking order must be explicit, like the deal grammar's deduction order |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | Artist unrepresented | Feature invisible; artist self-books |
| Proposed | Agency proposed terms | Terms rendered as consequences at realistic fee levels |
| Live | Artist entity approved | Formula active; new bookings pin this version |
| Versioned | Terms changed | New version live; existing bookings retain their pinned version |
| Sunsetting | Relationship ended, dates outstanding | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the sunset rule governs |
| Ended | Relationship over, no dates outstanding | Historical; retained for the settlement record |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The representation relationship belongs in this domain — it is how bookings happen | Locality instinct; the agent is the main actor in domain 17 | ❌ REJECTED: an agency is an **organization** and representation is a **relationship between an org and an artist** — that is domain 01's model, and the same relationship governs non-live work (recording, sync, endorsements). Duplicating it here would give the platform two contradictory records of who represents whom, which is exactly the fragmentation `meta/problem-statement.md` says the product exists to end. What is genuinely domain-owned is the commission **computed from a live settlement**. |
| DT-02 | Commission ends when the relationship ends | The intuitive reading | ❌ REJECTED: **the agent who books a date in January and is fired in March is still owed on the June show** — they did the work, and every agency agreement in the industry says so in some form. A model where commission stops at termination would silently underpay a party who is legally owed, and the platform would be the instrument. The sunset rule is not an edge case; it is a term every agreement has and no two write identically. Its absence from the sweep's candidate ("Agency Roster & Commission Tracking") is a real gap. |
| DT-03 | Commission is a percentage — the rate is the term that matters | The headline number | ❌ REJECTED: **the basis is what matters.** 20% of gross and 20% of net differ by thousands on the same show, and "net" is undefined until it names a settlement line — the same ambiguity that makes the deal grammar's deduction order load-bearing (17.03.01 DT-01), one level up. An artist reading "20%" and an agent computing "20%" can be describing different money, and the artist has no way to know. This is the settlement information asymmetry appearing *inside* the artist's own team. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The representation relationship is consumed from domain 01, never duplicated | Two records of who represents whom is the fragmentation the product exists to end (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Terms are versioned; each booking pins the version it was made under | Terms change mid-relationship and already-booked dates must not silently reprice (CX-01). |
| D-03 | The basis must reference a defined settlement line, not the word "net" | "20% of net" is not a formula until "net" names a line (DT-03). |
| D-04 | The sunset rule is a first-class term, not an edge case | Every agency agreement has one; omitting it would make the platform underpay parties who are owed (DT-02). |
