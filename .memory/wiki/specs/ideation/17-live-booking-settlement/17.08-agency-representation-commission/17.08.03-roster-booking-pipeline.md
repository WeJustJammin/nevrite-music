# Feature: Roster Booking Pipeline

> **Number**: 17.08.03
> **Parent**: [Agency Representation & Commission](./17.08-agency-representation-commission-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The agency's forward view of its own book — every act on the roster, every date from enquiry through
hold and offer to confirmed and settled, and the commission each will earn. An agency's solvency is a
function of this projection, which is what separates it from a CRM board: the pipeline is the
forecast, not a to-do list.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The **agency** sees its whole roster's pipeline and its projected commission. An **individual artist** sees only their own dates — never the agency's book, and never another act's. The multi-hyphenate complication: an artist with three agents across three territories appears in three pipelines, each seeing only its own scope (CX-03) — and the same holds for an artist with a live agent and a recording agent, because the split can run on either axis, territory or domain (`01.03.02` D-05). |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | None | An agency's book is competitively sensitive. A promoter seeing which of an agency's acts are struggling to place dates would negotiate against it. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The pipeline is a **derived view that owns nothing** (sub-domain R-01). It projects across the stages
this domain already models — enquiry (17.07), hold (17.01.02), offer (17.02), confirmed (17.01.04),
settled (17.09) — filtered to the dates within the agency's representation scope: territory **and** the
edge's `domains`, which for a booking pipeline means the edge must carry `live booking` (CX-03;
`01.03.02` D-05).

Its value is the money column. A booking agent does not need a kanban board of dates; they need to
know what their book is worth, when it lands, and what just fell out of it.

The honest limitation: it is a **projection built on projections**. A confirmed date's commission
depends on a settlement that has not happened, from a deal whose outcome depends on ticket sales
nobody can predict — and inside a cross-collateralized run (17.03.03), on numbers that are themselves
provisional.

### Happy Path

1. Agency opens its pipeline; every roster act's dates are listed by stage.
2. Each date carries its projected commission from the deal terms and an attendance assumption.
3. A date confirms (17.01.04) and its projection firms up.
4. The show settles (17.09.05); the accrual becomes a fact (17.08.02) and leaves the pipeline.
5. A cancelled date drops out and the agency's forecast moves — which is how they discover it was
   wrong.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Date cancels | The agency's forecast moves, not just the artist's calendar — arguably the agency should know before the date is even released | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` (CX-02) |
| Date is inside an open cross-collateralized run | Projecting from provisional numbers — compounding uncertainty | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — projection confidence must degrade visibly |
| Artist has three agents in three territories | Three pipelines, each scoped; none sees the whole artist | Correct behaviour, but it means no pipeline shows the artist's real book |
| Representation scope is free text | The pipeline cannot filter, and this feature partly collapses | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (17.08.01 Q-02) |
| Projected commission assumes attendance nobody knows | The projection launders a guess into a forecast | Draw history (17.11.01) should seed it; otherwise confidence must be shown |
| Artist leaves the agency with dates outstanding | Sunset rule governs (17.08.01) — those dates stay in the pipeline | Marked as sunsetting, not removed |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No roster or no dates | Empty pipeline with a prompt to add representation |
| Populated | Dates across stages | Roster × stage view with projected commission and confidence |
| Firming | Dates confirming | Projections tighten as attendance assumptions get real inputs |
| Provisional | Dates inside open runs | Explicitly degraded confidence on those rows |
| Realised | Show settled | Accrual becomes fact and exits the pipeline |
| Sunsetting | Artist left, dates outstanding | Retained under the sunset rule |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The pipeline is a CRM board — a place to track deals through stages | The candidate's framing ("Agency Roster & Commission Tracking") and every SaaS instinct | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **revenue forecast**. The stages already exist as first-class state elsewhere in this domain (hold, offer, confirmed, settled) — the pipeline does not track them, it *sums* them. Building a kanban board would duplicate the domain's own state machine in a second place where it can drift, and would miss the only column that matters. An agent does not need to be told a date is on hold; they need to know what their book is worth and when it lands. |
| DT-02 | The pipeline's projections are useful from day one | Build-order instinct — it is a view over data that already exists | ⏳ DEFERRED, leaning negative — and this is the same problem as 17.03.02 DT-03. **A projection is only as good as its attendance assumption**, and attendance comes from draw history (17.11.01), which is a settlement byproduct that does not exist at cold start. A pipeline built before the settlement corpus exists projects from guesses and presents them with the authority of a forecast — worse than showing nothing, because the agency will run its business on it. The feature is weaker than it looks until the data underneath it accumulates, which is a MoSCoW consequence the owner should see (Q-02). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The pipeline is a derived view that owns no state | The stages are first-class elsewhere in this domain; a second copy would drift (DT-01). |
| D-02 | The money column is the feature, not the stage board | An agency needs to know what its book is worth; the stages are already visible on each date (DT-01). |
| D-03 | Projection confidence is shown, never hidden | A projection from a guessed attendance presented as a forecast is worse than no forecast, because the agency will run its business on it (DT-02). |
| D-04 | An agency sees only its own roster's dates | Cross-agency visibility would be a competitive disaster (CX-02). |
