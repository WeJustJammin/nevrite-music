# Feature: Counterparty Relationship & Payment Reliability

> **Number**: 17.12
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-30

## Overview

The history between two parties who do business — every show they did together, and whether it went
well. What makes this different from a review is that **the signals are derived, not asserted**: did
the deposit arrive on time, did the balance get paid on the night, did the settlement get contested,
did the count match. These are facts the platform already holds because it computed them.

The domain index calls settlement "the source of payment-reliability reputation". This is that.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their history with a room before agreeing to play it, and their own record as a counterparty. `meta/personas.md` names "an unpaid session" as their switching trigger — the live equivalent is a promoter who pays late, or not at all, and whom nobody warned them about. This is the warning. |
| Producer | None | No role in live counterparty history. |
| Operator | Full | Symmetrically exposed and symmetrically served — sees whether an act cancels, whether they show up, whether they contest every settlement. Also sees their own record, which is an **asset if they are honest** (17.09.06 DT-03). |
| Fan | None | Never visible. This is B2B reputation between professionals; a fan has no stake in whether a promoter pays on time and no business seeing it. |

## Behavior

Two layers, and only one is novel:

1. **The relationship timeline** — every deal, show and settlement between these two parties. A
   record, not a judgement.
2. **Derived reliability signals** — computed from facts the platform holds: deposit paid on time
   (17.05.01), balance paid on the night (17.05.02), settlement contested or signed under protest
   (17.09.05), count disputed (17.09.02), cancellations and their tier (17.05.03), an accepted force
   majeure (which explicitly **does not** count — 17.05.04 D-03), confirm-then-revert rate
   (17.01.04 DT-11), and **radius break-glass usage** (D-06).

The **generic reputation and reviews mechanism** is a platform cross-cut serving 05, 13, 14, 16 and
17. What is domain-owned is that live settlement produces *derived* signals — provenance applied to
reputation, and the same argument as the verified draw record (17.11.01).

### Happy Path

1. Two parties do a show; it settles (17.09.05); the money moves (17.10.02).
2. System derives reliability facts from what it already computed — no survey, no prompt, no review.
3. Facts accrue to both parties' records and to the timeline between them.
4. Next time either considers the other — at enquiry (17.07) or hold (17.01.02) — the history is there.
5. It informs the decision; it never makes it (same principle as 17.01.02 DT-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Force majeure accepted | Must **not** damage reliability (17.05.04 D-03) — penalising genuine FM makes both sides lie about why a show died | The show appears on the timeline flagged as an accepted FM cancellation and is **excluded from every derived reliability signal** — it counts toward neither "on-time" nor "late/unpaid" |
| Artist contests every settlement — legitimately | Per D-04, the platform surfaces the **raw fact and its outcome** — "contested 3 of 4 settlements; 3 resolved in the contestor's favour" — never a bare "difficult" label. Whether contest-rate should feed a *negative* reliability inference is the product decision that punishes vigilance | The dated facts and their resolution outcomes; **no character judgement**. Whether a contest-rate signal exists at all is deferred (Q-01) |
| Cash paid on the night, off-platform | The platform saw no on-platform payment and could wrongly derive "balance unpaid" from an assertion gap | Silence is **not** derived as non-payment — an unpaid signal fires only from a schedule the platform tracked (17.05.02); an off-platform settlement reads as "not recorded on platform", never "unpaid" |
| A promoter's one bad year during a genuine crisis | Facts are permanent and timestamped; the timeline shows recency so a distant bad run does not read as current | Dated facts on the timeline; the counterparty may attach a **context note** to a settled fact (it never alters the fact). Whether the derivation *weights* recency numerically is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` |
| New counterparty, no history | The cold-start state; also the state of every legitimate new venue (D-03) | Explicit "no history yet" — absence reads as absence, **never** as a negative signal |
| An artist breaks glass on a blocked confirm and plays inside someone's radius | **It debits** (D-06). A `radius-break-glass` event is a principal's deliberate choice to breach a contract the platform showed them, made in person under a live challenge clock ([17.06 D-05](./17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md), [17.01.04 C-06](./17.01-availability-holds-confirmation/17.01.04-confirmation-announce-gate.md)) — counterparty behaviour, not a platform failure, so 17.01.03 D-12's fault-free exclusions do not reach it | The dated fact and the clause it broke, on the confirming party's record and on the timeline with that Operator. Per D-04, the fact and its context — never a "breaches contracts" label |
| The same artist's clause is breached by a **third party's** later confirm | **It does not debit** — nobody's act, from the clause-holder's side. 17.06 keeps `Breaching (break-glass)` and `Breaching (third-party)` as distinct states precisely so this derivation can tell them apart (17.06 D-06) | The breach appears on the timeline with its cause named, and is **excluded from every derived signal** — the same treatment as an accepted force majeure |
| Party disputes a derived signal | The signal is derived from their own settled shows, so there is nothing new to dispute | To move the signal they must dispute the underlying settlement (17.09.06); the signal is held pending that resolution |
| Signal used to justify a worse deal | "Your reliability is low, so the deposit is bigger" (17.05.01 Q-03) | Foreseeable and **cuts both ways** — makes the honest party's record a commercial asset (D-05) and a weak record commercially consequential. Whether reliability sizes the deposit ask is deferred (Q-02) |
| Counterparty is an entity, not a person (band, venue, agency) | The reliable party is a first-class entity (personas.md structural fact / domain 01), not the individual who signed | The record accrues to the **entity**, so a promoter cannot shed a bad history by trading under a new personal login; entity identity is the anchor |
| A party deletes their account or leaves | Both parties co-created the settled facts; one leaving cannot erase the other's evidence | The counterparty still sees the historical timeline of shows that happened; the departed party is shown as inactive, the derived facts persist (they are the surviving party's evidence) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No history | First time with this counterparty | Explicit absence — **not** a negative signal |
| Thin | One or two shows together | Timeline with visibly low confidence |
| Established | Several shows | Timeline plus derived signals with confidence |
| Flagged | Signals indicate a pattern | Surfaced with the specific facts behind it, never a bare score |
| Contested | A source settlement is disputed | Signal held pending resolution (17.09.06) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a reviews feature and should be routed out as the reputation cross-cut | Node Classification Gate, cross-cut test — reviews serve 05, 13, 14, 16, 17 | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — split it.** The generic reputation mechanism (leave a rating, aggregate it, display it) **is** a cross-cut and is recorded as one. But live settlement produces something reviews cannot: **derived facts**. "Paid the balance on the night" is not an opinion — it is arithmetic the platform already did. That is `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis applied to reputation: the fact is captured because the platform was in the room, not reconstructed from someone's feelings afterwards. A star rating is a memory; a payment record is evidence. The derived half is domain-owned. |
| DT-02 | Derived signals are objective and therefore fair | The appeal of derivation — no opinions, just facts | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's real problem. **The facts are objective; the derivation is a judgement.** "Contested 3 of 4 settlements" is a fact. Reading it as *unreliable* is an inference — and the artist who contests every settlement may simply be the only one reading the numbers, which is **exactly the behaviour this platform exists to enable**. A reliability signal that punishes vigilance would quietly train artists to stop checking, destroying the domain's central value while appearing rigorous. The derivation's design is a product decision with a perverse incentive hiding in it (Q-01). |
| DT-03 | Reliability history is primarily an artist-protection feature | The persona framing — the artist is the party who gets stiffed | ❌ REJECTED as one-sided. It is **the honest Operator's asset**, and that is the stronger commercial argument. `meta/personas.md` gives the Operator's switching trigger as a costly failure and their anti-persona as keeping deposits outside policy — meaning the honest operator currently has no way to distinguish themselves from the dishonest one except reputation and hearsay. A permanent record of shows that settled cleanly is worth more to a good promoter than to anyone else, and it is the reason an Operator would adopt a tool that otherwise reduces their information advantage. **Frame it as protection and Operators resist; frame it as their asset and they volunteer.** Same data, opposite adoption dynamics — the same inversion as 17.11.01 DT-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Signals are **derived** from settled facts, never solicited as reviews | A star rating is a memory; a payment record is evidence. This is the platform thesis applied to reputation (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Accepted force majeure never damages reliability | Penalising genuine FM makes both sides lie about why a show died, destroying the record (17.05.04 D-03). |
| D-03 | No history reads as absence, never as a negative | Every legitimate new venue starts here; a cold-start penalty would make the platform unjoinable. |
| D-04 | Signals surface the specific facts, never a bare score | A score hides the judgement inside the derivation (DT-02). |
| D-05 | Framed as the honest Operator's asset, not as artist protection | Same data, opposite adoption dynamics (DT-03). |
| D-06 | **Radius break-glass usage is a derived fact**, attributed to the confirming party — and a breach created by a **third party's** later confirm is not | Owner ratification, run-3 D6. The break-glass override on 17.01.04 C-06 is only defensible because it is priced, and this record is half the price ([17.06 D-05](./17.06-radius-clause-exclusivity.md)); without it, the override is a free click at 1am under a 48h clock. It sits deliberately **outside** 17.01.03 D-12's fault-free exclusion set — a principal choosing in person to breach a contract the platform put in front of them is counterparty behaviour, which is exactly what this feature exists to derive. The mirror case is the test of the rule: a breach someone else's confirm created is excluded, because debiting a party for a third party's write is the injustice D-12 was written to prevent (17.06 D-06 keeps the two states distinct so the derivation can). Rendered under D-04 as the dated fact and the clause it broke, never as a character label. |
