# Feature: Verified Draw Record

> **Number**: 17.11.01
> **Parent**: [Draw History & Market Intelligence](./17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What this artist actually drew, where, when, and in what slot — derived from signed settlements rather
than claimed on a one-sheet. This is domain 17's clearest expression of the platform thesis: a fact
that became true in a room the platform was in, that no competitor can retroactively manufacture,
and that the artist accumulates simply by working.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **It is their record and their strongest asset.** `meta/personas.md`: their pain is that "every new client requires re-proving who they are"; a verified draw record is the live-music answer — evidence instead of assertion. It also contains their bad nights, which they cannot delete, and that is precisely what makes it worth anything. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads a prospective act's record when deciding a hold (17.01.02) or sizing an offer — decision support, **never an automated rank** (17.01.02 DT-02). Whether they may read it without asking is a real question (Q-02). |
| Fan | None | Never visible — but the Fan is every number in it. |

## Behavior

A draw record is emitted by settlement (17.09.05) and carries far more than a number:

- **Scan count, not paid count** (17.09.02 DT-02) — how many people came, which is not how many paid.
- **Market and room** — 340 at a 400-cap club is a different fact from 340 at a 1,200-cap hall.
- **Slot** — **headliner, direct support, or opener.** This is the qualifier that makes or breaks the
  record's meaning and the one most likely to be dropped.
- **Night and season** — a Tuesday in February is not a Saturday in June.
- **Ticket price** — 340 at £8 is not 340 at £30.
- **Confidence** — one show is an anecdote; six across three years is a fact.

The record is **append-only and unflattering by design**. An artist who could delete a soft night
would hold a record that proves nothing, and a promoter reading it would be right not to believe it.

### Happy Path

1. Show settles; both parties sign (17.09.05).
2. System emits a draw record — scan count, room, market, slot, night, price, against that artist.
3. Record accumulates with the artist across every band and project they play in.
4. An Operator considering a hold reads it alongside the enquiry (17.07) — decision support, not a rank.
5. The record seeds attendance ranges in modelling (17.03.02) and guidance (17.11.03), replacing a guess.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Support slot recorded without the qualifier | 600 as an opener for a huge act reads as a 600-draw headliner — **the most damaging possible error** | Slot is mandatory, never inferred (D-02) |
| Artist's bad night is permanently in their record | Correct and uncomfortable; it is what makes the record credible | No deletion; append-only (D-03) |
| Show settled but never scanned (no ticketing) | No scan count — a settled show with no draw fact | Record marked incomplete rather than imputed |
| Multi-hyphenate: drummer plays in three bands | Whose draw is it? **The band's** — the drummer did not draw those people | Record attaches to the performing entity, not the human (Q-01) |
| Festival slot with 8,000 in the field | The artist did not draw 8,000; the festival did | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — festival draw is not draw |
| Settlement amended after the record emitted | The record moves; anything computed from it is stale | Recomputation event |
| Cold start — no records exist | The main state at launch | Feature is empty and everything downstream is weaker (index Q-02) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No settled shows | Nothing — the launch state |
| Accumulating | Settlements landing | Records with low confidence |
| Established | Enough shows in a market | Records with meaningful confidence per market |
| Incomplete | Settled show without a scan count | Record present, draw absent, not imputed |
| Stale | Source settlement amended | Recomputed; downstream notified |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A draw record is an attendance number per show | The obvious model, and the domain index's shorthand ("draw intelligence") | ❌ REJECTED: **the qualifiers are the record.** 600 people as the opener for a huge act is *not* a 600 draw — it is proof someone else drew 600 and you played to them. Without the slot qualifier the record is not merely imprecise, it is **actively false in the direction that most benefits the artist**, which is exactly the self-serving inflation `meta/personas.md` names as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour ("games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation"). A bare number would automate that abuse. |
| DT-02 | The record's value is helping promoters decide | The obvious commercial reading — it is decision support for the buy side | ❌ REJECTED as primary: **its value is to the artist**, and that inversion is the whole point. `meta/problem-statement.md` says the Musician's pain is re-proving themselves to every new client and their success criterion is "credits are cited rather than claimed". A verified draw record is that, for live. The promoter benefits too — but if the feature is built as a promoter tool, artists will resist it, and if it is built as the artist's asset, promoters get it anyway because the artist will show them. **Same data, opposite adoption dynamics.** |
| DT-03 | A verified record is strictly better than a claimed one | The thesis, applied without examination | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — with an adoption cost nobody has priced.** Verified means *unflattering*: the artist cannot delete the Tuesday that drew 40, and their one-sheet currently can and does. An artist choosing between a platform that records their bad nights permanently and a PDF they control will feel the pull of the PDF — **the incentive to defect is strongest for exactly the mid-tier artists this platform needs.** The counter is that a record only has value *because* it cannot be edited, so an artist with a good history gains more than a bad one loses, and the bad-history artist was never going to win that room anyway. But this is a real objection to the thesis, at its strongest point, and it should be tested with the owner rather than assumed away. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Only signed settlements emit records | The record's entire value is that every number came from an event both parties agreed happened. |
| D-02 | Slot (headliner / support / opener) is mandatory and never inferred | Without it the record is false in the direction that flatters the artist, automating the abuse it should prevent (DT-01). |
| D-03 | Records are append-only; artists cannot delete their bad nights | A deletable record proves nothing, and a promoter reading it would be right to ignore it (DT-03). |
| D-04 | The record attaches to the performing entity, not the human | The drummer did not draw those people; the band did. |
| D-05 | The record is built as the **artist's** asset, not the promoter's tool | Same data, opposite adoption dynamics — artists resist a promoter tool and volunteer their own asset (DT-02). |
