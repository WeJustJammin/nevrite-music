## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Verified Draw Record ↔ Market Comparables & Benchmarks

**Relationship**: Comparables are built by aggregating draw records **belonging to other people**.
This is the sub-domain's defining edge and it is not a technical one: the promoter whose settlement
informed the benchmark did not agree to size a rival's offer, and the artist whose soft Tuesday is in
the denominator did not agree to be a cautionary data point.

Aggregation thresholds (never show a cell with fewer than N shows, never attribute) mitigate the
identifiability problem. They do not answer the consent problem — and in a thin market, "the average
guarantee for this genre at 400-cap rooms in Manchester" *is* three promoters, threshold or no
threshold.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their own record is theirs (Full); their contribution to everyone else's benchmark is
  invisible to them.
- **Operator**: reads benchmarks; their own deal terms are in someone else's.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The record's owner and the benchmark's beneficiary are **different
   parties with opposed interests**. There is no merge — there is a policy, and it is index Q-01.
2. **Trigger chain**: Settlement → record → (gate) → aggregate. If the gate says no, the chain stops
   and 17.11.02 does not exist for that data.
3. **Permission intersection**: The sharpest in the domain. Contributing data is not the same as
   consuming it, and a reciprocity model ("see benchmarks only if you contribute") is the obvious
   design and a coercive one.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None — and that is itself the problem. Nobody is told their data
   informed a decision.
5. **State transition conflict**: A settlement amended (17.09.06) after it fed a benchmark. The
   benchmark is already spent — someone made an offer on it.

### CX-02: Verified Draw Record ↔ Offer Guidance & Guarantee Sizing

**Relationship**: An artist's own history is the honest basis for guidance. "You drew 340 here last
year, mid-week, as a headliner" needs no permission from anyone and is the single most useful fact
either side of a negotiation can have. **No gate applies** — this is the sub-domain's clean edge.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their record is their strongest asset in a negotiation, and for the first time it is
  evidenced rather than claimed.
- **Operator**: reads the prospective act's record when sizing an offer — with the act's visibility
  permission (17.11.01 Q-02).
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — one artist, their own data.
2. **Trigger chain**: Record → guidance. Guidance is a pure read; it never writes back.
3. **Permission intersection**: Whether an Operator may read an act's record without asking is a real
   question (17.11.01 Q-02) — the record is the artist's, and a soft night is in it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Arguably an artist should know when a promoter reads their draw history.
   Unmodelled.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-03: Market Comparables & Benchmarks ↔ Offer Guidance & Guarantee Sizing

**Relationship**: Comparables are what let guidance say anything at all about an act with **no own
history** — the artist who has never played this city, which is the majority of interesting bookings.
This is where the gate matters most, because it is where the value is.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician** / **Operator**: both consume aggregate guidance for an unknown act.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence — the whole edge depends on 17.11.02 existing,
which depends on index Q-01 and Q-02. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Offer Guidance & Guarantee Sizing | Verified Draw Record (write direction) | Rejected. Guidance is a **pure read**; a prediction must never become a record. The tempting move is caching a computed expectation onto the artist's record — which would pollute a corpus of *settled facts* with *model output*, and the next model would train on its own guesses. The record's entire value is that every number in it came from a signed settlement (17.11.01 D-01). |
| R-02 | Market Comparables & Benchmarks | Verified Draw Record (write direction) | Rejected for the same reason, more sharply. A benchmark must never be written back onto an artist's record as an imputed draw ("acts like you draw 300 here"). That would manufacture the exact thing the domain exists to eliminate: a plausible number with no event behind it. An artist's record must contain only shows they actually played. |
| R-03 | Offer Guidance & Guarantee Sizing | Market Comparables & Benchmarks (as one feature) | Rejected as a **merge**, recorded here because it is the tempting simplification. They sit on opposite sides of the confidentiality gate: guidance from an artist's own record needs no consent (CX-02); guidance from comparables needs a policy nobody has written (CX-01). Merging them would hide the gate inside a single node and let the unresolved consent question ship by accident. |
