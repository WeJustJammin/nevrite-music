# Feature: Fan Demand Signals & Routing Requests

> **Number**: 17.13
> **Parent**: [Live Booking & Settlement](./live-booking-settlement-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Fans telling an artist where they want to be played, and that demand arriving where booking decisions
are actually made. "1,400 people in Manchester want this act" is a booking input no promoter has ever
had and every promoter would want — and it is the **only place in domain 17 the Fan persona touches
the product**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees where their audience is asking for them, and uses it to pitch a room they could not otherwise justify — "1,400 people in Manchester want this, give me the Tuesday". For a working musician with no agent this is leverage they have never had. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads aggregate demand for an act when deciding a hold (17.01.02) or sizing an offer. It is a **signal, not a promise** — see DT-02. |
| Fan | Full — **and this is their only touchpoint in domain 17** | Requests an artist in their city. `meta/personas.md`: their entire pain is finding out after the show, and their success criterion is never missing one. This is the upstream of that — asking for the show to exist at all. It must be one tap and nothing more; the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity". |

## Behavior

A demand signal is a fan expressing intent — "play my city" — aggregated by market and surfaced to
the artist and, at their discretion, to promoters.

**Split from the sweep's candidate 18** ("Event Listing Syndication & Fan Demand Signals"), which
bundled two unrelated things. Syndication is an *outbound announce* concern belonging to domain 21;
demand signals are an *inbound booking input*. They share nothing but a bullet.

The critical property, and the reason it is not a fan inbox: this is **aggregate, one-way, and
unaddressed**. `meta/personas.md` names open artist contact surfaces as the Fan's worst intentional
abuse vector, and D-13 notes fans outnumber professionals by orders of magnitude. A per-fan message
to an artist is a harassment pipe at consumer scale (17.07 DT-03). A counter by city is not.

The fan-facing surface lives in domain 20; the **booking-facing consumption is here**.

### Happy Path

1. A fan follows an artist (domain 20) and requests them in their city — one tap.
2. Signals aggregate by market, with no individual identity reaching the artist.
3. Artist sees a demand map: 1,400 Manchester, 900 Glasgow, 200 Cardiff.
4. Artist or their agent uses it to pitch a room, or to route (domain 18).
5. Operator reads the aggregate beside an enquiry (17.07) or hold request as one input among several.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Demand is high; the show flops | **Requesting costs nothing; attending costs £20 and a Tuesday** | The gap is the feature's central weakness (DT-02) |
| Signals gamed — an artist's fans brigade a city | Cheap to do; the incentive is obvious | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a signal that can be manufactured is a signal (Q-02) |
| Artist plays a requested city; it does not sell | The signal has now cost the artist real money | Signal must never read as a forecast (D-02) |
| Fan requests, artist never plays there | The overwhelmingly common outcome | Expectation must be managed at the point of asking; the fan is told nothing was promised |
| Demand map used to justify a lowball | "Only 200 want you in Cardiff" — demand as a negotiating weapon | Foreseeable; same shape as 17.11.03 DT-03 |
| Cold start — no fans on the platform | The launch state; the feature is empty | Depends entirely on domain 20's adoption, not on 17's |
| Fan requests a city the artist cannot legally play (visa) | Demand does not know about immigration | Out of scope; domain 18 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No signals | Nothing — **the launch state** |
| Accumulating | Signals arriving | Demand map with low confidence |
| Meaningful | Signals above a threshold in a market | Aggregate count with its confidence |
| Shared | Artist chose to expose it to promoters | Visible beside enquiries and holds |
| Converted | A show is announced in a requested market | Requesting fans notified (domain 20's alert) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This belongs in domain 20 (Fanbase), not 17 | Boundary test — it is a fan feature, and 20 owns the fan relationship | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — split by direction.** The **fan-facing surface** (the request button, the follow, the notification when it converts) is unambiguously domain 20's; that is the fan relationship. But the **booking-facing consumption** — demand as an input to a hold decision or an offer — is a booking tool that only makes sense next to the avails, the ladder and the deal. Putting the whole thing in 20 would leave the promoter's view of demand orphaned in a fan-marketing domain. This is the domain's only Fan touchpoint precisely *because* the split is real. |
| DT-02 | Demand signals predict attendance | The feature's implicit promise, and the reason anyone would use it | ❌ REJECTED — **requesting costs nothing; attending costs £20 and a Tuesday night.** The gap between a tap and a ticket is enormous and unmeasured, and every band who has toured on Bandsintown "trackers" knows a city that "wanted" them and did not come. A signal presented as a forecast would cause artists to book rooms on the strength of clicks and lose real money — the platform would have manufactured a confident number with nothing behind it, which is the precise failure `meta/problem-statement.md` says the industry already has. **The verified draw record (17.11.01) is the fact; this is a wish.** They must never be rendered as the same kind of thing. |
| DT-03 | This is a nice-to-have — the domain's smallest feature | Its size, its lateness in the candidate list, and its cold-start weakness | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED as low priority, but it is doing structural work worth naming.** It is the **only Fan touchpoint in a 24-domain map's live-booking domain** — the one place D-11's "fans are first-class users" reaches the commercial spine of a show. If it does not exist, the Fan's relationship to live music on this platform is purely consumptive: they hear about shows and buy tickets, and their preferences never reach the people who decide what gets booked. That is a coherent product, but it is a smaller claim than D-11 makes. Worth flagging to the owner as a values question rather than deleting quietly (Q-01). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Signals are aggregate, one-way and unaddressed — never a message | A per-fan pipe to an artist is a harassment vector at consumer scale (17.07 DT-03, `meta/personas.md`). |
| D-02 | A demand signal is never rendered as a forecast | Requesting costs nothing; attending costs £20 and a Tuesday. Presenting a wish as a fact would lose artists real money (DT-02). |
| D-03 | **Split from candidate 18**: syndication is domain 21's; demand is a booking input | The sweep bundled an outbound announce concern with an inbound booking input. They share nothing. |
| D-04 | The fan-facing surface is domain 20's; the booking-facing consumption is here | Splitting by direction keeps the promoter's view of demand next to the decision it informs (DT-01). |
| D-05 | Whether the artist exposes demand to promoters is the artist's choice | It is leverage when it is high and a weapon against them when it is low. |
