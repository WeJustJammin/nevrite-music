# Feature: Settlement Sheet Computation

> **Number**: 17.09.01
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The flagship. Evaluating the deal's compiled expression (17.03.01) against the show's real counts and
expenses to produce the number the promoter owes the artist — automatically, transparently, and with
every step shown. This is the work that currently happens in a back office at 1am with a calculator,
and it is only possible because the offer was structured data.

The feature's product is **the derivation, not the total**. A number handed over is what the artist
has always been handed. A worked derivation, from terms both parties authored months earlier, read by
both of them off the same screen, is what has never existed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the computed sheet with every line's derivation visible — which count, which deduction, which clause, which contract version. **They cannot alter inputs they do not own** (the count is the Operator's, the expenses are the Operator's) but they can contest any line (17.09.06). Sees the versus comparison worked **both ways** — guarantee branch and percentage branch — not only the branch that won, because the losing branch is what tells them whether the deal was any good. Sees the deposit already paid netted off as an explicit line (D-08), so the number on the sheet is the number that will actually move. On a multi-artist bill they see the shared lines their own deal's expression references, and nothing else (D-10). This is the first time in the industry's history the artist arrives at settlement with the same arithmetic the promoter has. |
| Producer | None | No role in live settlement. The Producer persona owns the studio room, not the stage (domain index Role Matrix note 1). A Producer who plays the show is acting as a **Musician**; one who promotes it is acting as an **Operator**. Neither lens is Producer, and the explicit absence is the finding. |
| Operator | Full | Supplies the counts (17.09.02) and expenses (17.09.03); the sheet computes from the deal they already agreed. Notably they **do not compute the number** — the formula does, from terms they authored months ago. That is the point and it is also what they may resist. "Full" here is inherited from owning the *inputs*, not from write access to the sheet: **nobody writes a computed line** — every line is derived, and the only writable things in this feature are un-evaluated terms (D-05) and their counter-attestation. |
| Fan | None | Never sees the arithmetic — but their ticket purchase is the gross that drives every line of it. Every number on this sheet is an aggregate of individual Fans buying, being comped, or not turning up. |

> **The multi-hyphenate case is not exotic here.** One human routinely holds both lenses on the same
> show — a venue owner who books their own band, or (far more common at this platform's tier) an
> artist self-promoting their own night. The Role Lens above describes **parties to a deal**, not
> humans. When one principal holds both sides, the sheet computes identically and the settlement is
> valid — but it is **self-attested**, and everything downstream that borrows credibility from
> counterparty attestation must be able to tell (DT-12, D-12).

## Behavior

**The deal structure IS the settlement formula.** The expression compiled at offer time (17.03.01) is
inert until a count arrives (CX-01); settlement is the moment it evaluates. No re-entry, no
re-interpretation, no calculator.

The evaluator is **the same one the modeller used** (17.03.02 DT-02). If they differed, the platform
would have quoted a number at offer time it does not honour at settlement.

The sheet's real product is not the number — it is the **derivation**. Both sides seeing the same
worked arithmetic, from terms they both agreed, is what dissolves the 1am argument. A settlement
system that outputs a total is a calculator; one that outputs a defensible derivation is the product.

Critically: **a computed number is a proposal, not a fact** (CX-03). It becomes real at signoff.

### What the derivation is made of

The sheet is an ordered sequence of **lines**, each with exactly one of three provenances, and the
provenance is always visible:

| Line provenance | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Derived** | Computed from the deal expression against an input. The line shows its operands, the operation, and the clause of the deal that authorised it. | `Gross (paid) = Σ tiers = 312 × £15 + 44 × £18 = £5,472` — clause: "gross means paid ticket revenue net of nothing" |
| **Asserted** | An input supplied by a party rather than established by the platform. Carries the asserting party and the time. | `Security = £800 — asserted by Operator, receipt attached, 2026-03-14 19:40` |
| **Agreed** | An un-evaluated term (D-05) that the formula cannot see. Has no derivation; carries two attestations and a basis. | `Sellout bonus = £500 — agreed manually by Operator + Musician, basis: "plus £500 if it sells out" (deal note, un-modelled)` |

A sheet with **zero** Agreed lines is fully automated. A sheet's Agreed-line count is therefore the
honest, per-settlement measure of the grammar's ceiling — which is what makes 17.03.01 Q-01 (closed
vocabulary vs open expression language) a **measurable** question rather than a philosophical one
(D-06).

### Un-evaluated terms — the resolution of the domain's sharpest risk

The failure this feature could most easily commit is **silent incompleteness**: an Operator writes
"plus £500 if it sells out" in a free-text note, the grammar never sees it, the formula computes a
defensible-looking number that is £500 wrong, and the derivation — the whole product — is a
confident lie.

The resolution (D-05):

1. Any term the grammar cannot compile is **retained verbatim** on the deal as an un-evaluated term.
   It is never discarded and never silently folded into a note.
2. At computation, each un-evaluated term renders as a line marked `no derivation — must be agreed`.
3. Either party may propose an amount with a basis; the **other party must counter-attest** before
   the line resolves. Either party may instead mark it `not applicable to this show`, which also
   requires counter-attestation.
4. Until every un-evaluated term is resolved, the sheet's state is **Incomplete** and its total is
   never rendered bare. It renders as `£8,400 + 1 un-agreed term`.
5. **Signature is refused against an Incomplete sheet** (17.09.05 binds a signature to a
   computation; an Incomplete sheet has no complete computation to bind to). This is not a blocker —
   resolving a term is two taps and an attestation — it is a forcing function, and it is the
   difference between a system that knows what it does not know and one that does not.

This makes a closed vocabulary **safe**: a closed grammar that names what it cannot see is honest;
a closed grammar that silently drops is dangerous. It does not answer 17.03.01 Q-01 — it removes the
catastrophic downside from one of its two answers.

### Never default a missing input to zero

An absent count is not `0`. An absent expense pool is not `0`. A line whose input has not arrived is
**un-evaluated**, and the sheet is Incomplete — because a zero produces a number that looks computed
and is wrong in the artist's favour or the promoter's, silently, depending on which side of the
expression the input sits (D-07).

### The number on the sheet is not the number received

The sheet computes what **this show's deal** owes. It is not the artist's income. The derivation must
therefore continue past the deal's own arithmetic and show, as explicit lines:

- **Less deposit already paid** (17.05.02) — a deposit is an advance against this settlement. A sheet
  that omits it states a figure nobody will pay, and the balance-on-the-night is the number the room
  actually cares about (D-08).
- **Merch disposition** — either an on-sheet line or an explicit `settled separately — see 17.09.04`.
  Never silently absent (D-09, resolves this feature's half of CX-05).
- **FX conversion**, where the deal's currency differs from the settlement currency — as a line at a
  stated position in the deduction order, with rate and fixing date (DT-06).

It must **not** show: agent commission (17.08.02), withholding (17.10), or platform fees. Those are
operations on the artist's side of the money and are invisible to the Operator by design (domain
index Role Matrix note 3 — "what an act pays its agent is deliberately invisible to the promoter").
A commission line on a shared sheet would leak the act's agency terms to the party negotiating
against them (D-11).

### Determinism

The same expression version + the same input set produces the same sheet, always. This is what makes
a signature bindable to a computation version (17.09.05 D-01), what makes a recomputation
attributable to a specific input change rather than to the system, and what lets the trail (17.09.06)
answer "why is this number different from the one I saw" with an input diff rather than an apology.

### Happy Path

1. Show plays. The box office closes; the reconciled count arrives (17.09.02) — manifest, sold by
   tier, comps against allowance, holds, walk-ups. Expenses are captured (17.09.03), categorised
   against the deal's schedule, and checked against its cap.
2. System evaluates the deal expression compiled at offer time (17.03.01) — **the same expression,
   never re-entered** — against those inputs.
3. Sheet renders top-to-bottom in the deal's own stated deduction order: gross summed across price
   tiers; each deduction as its own line with the clause that authorised it and its receipt where one
   exists; the expense cap and the headroom it left; the **versus comparison worked both ways** —
   `guarantee £2,000` vs `85% of net £8,400` — with the winning branch named and the losing branch
   still shown.
4. Derivation continues past the deal: less the deposit already paid (17.05.02), merch's disposition
   stated, FX converted at the stated position if applicable, rounding residual assigned. The result
   is the **balance due tonight**.
5. Both parties read the same sheet in the same room, with the same arithmetic, each line traceable
   to a count they can check and a clause they signed.
6. Both sign (17.09.05); the number becomes final; disbursement (17.10.02), commission (17.08.02),
   the draw record (17.11.01) and the reliability record (17.12) all fire from it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Deal contains free-text terms outside the grammar | Term is retained verbatim as an **un-evaluated term**; the formula never pretends to see it (D-05) | Line marked `no derivation — must be agreed`; sheet state **Incomplete**; total renders as `£8,400 + 1 un-agreed term`; signature refused until both parties attest an amount or mark it not-applicable |
| An input has not arrived (count, expense pool) | The dependent line is **un-evaluated**, never zero (D-07) | Sheet Incomplete, naming the missing input and who owes it. A total is never shown for an Incomplete sheet |
| Show is inside an open cross-collateralized run | **The number is not final and cannot be** (17.03.03 DT-02) | Every figure carries a provisional marker on every view, not only the first (17.03.03 D-02). Signature signs a provisional — a different act (17.09.05 Q-03). Contradicts this sub-domain's per-show finality (index Q-01, this file Q-04) |
| Balance paid in cash on the night | Extremely common; the platform sees an assertion, not a payment | Recordable as an assertion (17.05.02 D-03), visibly distinguished from a platform-executed disbursement |
| Count corrected after computation | Everything downstream recomputes, including disbursed commission | Recomputation is an **event**, not a silent job (17.09.06). The sheet's version increments; both parties see the input diff that caused it; any signature bound to the prior version invalidates (17.09.05 Invalidated) |
| No connectivity in the back room at 1am | The moment the feature exists for is the moment it may not work | Last-computed sheet is readable **read-only** with a staleness marker naming its computation time and any input known to be unsynced. A signature cast against a stale computation binds to **the version the signer saw**; on reconnect it is validated — if the canonical computation has moved, it invalidates per 17.09.05, exactly as a mid-room recomputation would. Whether a surface exists that can do this at all is Q-02 |
| Deal terms amended by contract amendment (17.04) | Settlement uses amended terms; the original is history | Derivation names the contract version applied on every line it authorised |
| Expense exceeds the deal's cap | Not an error — simply not deductible | Shown as captured-but-not-deductible with headroom exhausted, not as a rejection (17.09.03 D-02) |
| Multi-artist bill; a shared line (gross, expense pool) changes after the support already signed | One show, one gross, one expense pool, **N sheets** (D-10). A shared-line change recomputes every sheet that references it | Support's signature invalidates and they are re-notified with the input diff — the same mechanism as any recomputation, applied across sheets |
| Support act's deal references net; headliner's deal deducts the support fee | The support fee is a line in the **headliner's** derivation, not the support's. No leak occurs | Support sees the shared lines their own expression references (gross, expense pool) and never the headliner's guarantee or fee (D-10) |
| Settlement computes to a negative artist figure | Real: a pure door/net deal with backline recharges or a buy-on can leave the artist owing. A **guarantee is a floor** and clamps to it; a deal with no floor does not | Rendered as a negative balance with its full derivation — **never clamped to zero**. Clamping would silently transfer the loss to whichever party the clamp favours. Whether the platform can *collect* artist→Operator is Q-03 |
| Deal currency ≠ settlement currency | Converting gross then deducting local expenses ≠ deducting then converting — the two differ by real money | Conversion is an explicit line at a stated position in the deduction order, with rate and fixing date named (DT-06). If the deal did not state the basis, the FX term is an **un-evaluated term** (D-05) — not an agent guess |
| Rounding: 85% of an odd net leaves a fraction | Computed to the settlement currency's minor unit (2dp GBP/USD/EUR, 0dp JPY). Per-line rounding and total-only rounding give different answers and both are defensible | The convention is stated on the sheet and the **residual is assigned to a named party as its own line**, never absorbed. An absorbed residual makes the sum of disbursements differ from the settled total and costs an accountant an hour (DT-05) |
| Both parties have the sheet open and a count lands | The sheet's version increments for both simultaneously | Neither party's number changes underneath them silently — the version change is surfaced with its input diff. A number that moves without an event is the trust failure (17.03.03 D-03) |
| Comps exceed the deal's allowance | The excess is capacity the artist arguably paid for | The excess renders as its own derivation line against the allowance, attributed to whoever issued the comps (17.09.02 D-03) — not buried in a gross figure |
| A line dispute holds a line out (17.09.06) | The sheet is **Incomplete**, not wrong | The held line is visible with its dispute; no final total is rendered; signature refused pending resolution |
| A negative expense is captured (a refund, or a manufactured credit) | A legitimate credit exists (a returned backline deposit). A negative deduction that silently increases the artist's net without a deal basis does not | Credits render as their own explicitly-categorised line, never as a negative netted invisibly into another line |
| One human holds both sides (self-promoted show; venue owner books own band) | Computes identically and is a valid settlement. But both signatures are the same principal | The sheet records that the two sides are **not independent principals**, and every downstream consumer of the settlement as evidence (17.11.01, 17.12, 02) reads that flag (DT-12, D-12) |
| Merch settled in cash before the show settlement starts | Common; merch has its own grammar (CX-05) | The sheet states merch's disposition explicitly — on-sheet line, or `settled separately — see 17.09.04`. **Never silently absent** (D-09) |
| Agent (17.08) reads the artist's sheet | They must, to compute commission | Sheet carries no commission line; commission is computed on the artist's side (17.08.02) and is invisible to the Operator (D-11) |
| A signature is cast while a recomputation is in flight | The classic race, and it is real at 1am | Signature binds to the version presented; if that version is superseded before the second signature, it invalidates (17.09.05) rather than silently signing a number nobody read |
| Post-signoff amendment after commission was disbursed | Clawback from a third party already paid (17.08.02 DT-02) | New computation; trail records the obligation; all signers and everyone whose money moved are re-notified. The sub-domain's hardest downstream problem — amendment window is index Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Sheet requested | Deal terms and the input inventory render first — what is in, what is outstanding, who owes it. The skeleton is informative because the deal is already known; only the actuals are pending |
| Awaiting inputs (Empty) | Show played, no actuals yet | Sheet skeleton with the deal terms, the expense cap and headroom, the comp allowance, and an explicit list of what is outstanding and from whom. **No total** |
| Projected (Partial) | Some actuals present — e.g. advance sales in, show not played | The sheet computed against actuals-so-far, labelled **Projected** on every figure. This is not a settlement; it is the modeller (17.03.02) called from this site so that nobody arrives at 1am surprised. Cannot be signed (DT-10) |
| Computed (Populated) | All referenced inputs present; no un-evaluated terms; no held lines | Full derivation, both versus branches, deposit netted, balance due. A **proposal**, not a fact (D-03) |
| Incomplete (Partial) | An un-evaluated term is unresolved, an input is missing, or a line is held out by a dispute | Every complete line renders normally; incomplete lines are named with what they need and who owes it; total renders as `£X + N unresolved`. Signature refused |
| Provisional | Show inside an open cross-collateralized run | Full derivation with a provisional marker **on every figure, on every view** — not a one-time banner (17.03.03 D-02) |
| Stale (degraded) | The canonical computation is unreachable | Last-computed sheet, read-only, with its computation time and any known-unsynced input named. Signature is permitted but binds to the seen version and validates on reconnect |
| Error | The expression cannot be evaluated — e.g. an input contradicts the grammar's type, a cap references a term that no longer exists after an amendment | Named failure identifying the clause and the input that conflict. **Never a partial total.** Routed to the parties, not to a silent retry |
| Signed | Both parties signed (17.09.05) | Final; downstream fires |
| Superseded | A recomputation replaced this version | The prior version remains readable in the trail with the input diff that replaced it; signatures bound to it are invalid |
| Amended | Post-signoff change | New computation; trail records it; clawback obligations may arise |
| Contested | A line disputed (17.09.06) | The line is held out; the sheet returns to Incomplete, or escalates to domain 24 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Settlement is a computation problem | The domain index's framing — "settlement is only computable if the offer was structured data" | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the gap matters.** The arithmetic is the easy half. The hard half is that **settlement is a negotiation over a computation** (CX-03) — a promoter and a tour manager in a back room, one of whom has all the information, arguing about comps and an expense line. A system that computes perfectly and cannot represent "signed under protest", a contested line, or a number both parties know is wrong but accept to get out of the building, has modelled the arithmetic and missed the event. |
| DT-02 | The output is the number | The obvious reading of "settlement sheet" | ❌ REJECTED: **the output is the derivation.** The number alone is what a calculator produces and is exactly what the artist has always been handed and always had to accept. What has never existed is both parties reading the same worked arithmetic from terms they both agreed. A sheet showing a total is the status quo with better typography; a sheet showing *why* is the product. |
| DT-03 | The platform computing settlement closes the information asymmetry | The domain index's claim — asymmetry "runs one direction" and automating it fixes that | ❌ REJECTED as stated, and this is uncomfortable. **The platform only closes the asymmetry over inputs it owns.** The formula is symmetric — both sides see it. But the *count* comes from the Operator's box office and the *expenses* come from the Operator's phone. If the platform is not also the box office (domain 19), it has made the arithmetic transparent while leaving both inputs asserted by the party who benefits from them. That is a real improvement and it is **not** what the domain index claims. The asymmetry closes fully only when 19 owns the count — which is a strong argument for their coupling and a MoSCoW consequence the owner should see. |
| DT-04 | A show has one artist, so a show has one settlement sheet | The implicit model of this file's `[SURFACE]` pass, and of the sub-domain index throughout | ❌ REJECTED — and this is a **grain error as sharp as the cross-collateralization one** (17.03.03 DT-01). Domain index D-10 already established that "a show as one artist and one promoter" is "an assumption wrong on the majority of shows", and created 17.14 for it — but the correction never propagated here. A bill has a headliner, a support, often a local opener, each with **their own deal** against **one gross and one expense pool**. The consequence is not cosmetic: the headliner's net deal may deduct the support's fee, so the two settlements are coupled through a shared pool, and a shared line changing after one artist signed invalidates their signature. One show → **N sheets over one pool** (D-10). A single sheet with N artist sections would require N+1 signatures on one artefact and would leak every act's terms to every other. |
| DT-05 | Rounding is an implementation detail | The instinct that fractions of a penny are beneath a product spec | ❌ REJECTED, on a specific consequence. 85% of an odd net leaves a fraction; per-line rounding and total-only rounding produce different, both-defensible answers. Alone that is pennies. But commission (17.08.02) is a percentage **of a percentage**, and a run (17.03.03) aggregates twelve of these — so the residual compounds and **the sum of the disbursements stops equalling the settled total**. That is not a rounding curiosity; it is a reconciliation failure that lands on the artist's accountant, who is 17.09.07's actual consumer, and it makes the statement look wrong at exactly the moment its credibility is the product. A domain expert would expect the rounding convention and the residual's destination to be stated, not discovered. |
| DT-06 | FX is 17.03.01's problem, already flagged | 17.03.01 DT-03 identifies the rate and fixing date as unwritten deal terms | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the un-flagged half is this feature's.** 17.03.01 correctly says the rate basis is a deal term. What it does not say is that **where the conversion sits in the deduction order changes the number**: converting a € gross to £ and then deducting £-denominated local expenses gives a different answer from deducting €-expenses and converting the result. Both are defensible; they differ by real money on exactly the international bookings where the stakes are highest. The rate is the grammar's; **the position is the derivation's**, and if it is not an explicit line the sheet is silently taking a side. |
| DT-07 | A missing input is a zero | Nothing states this — which is the danger; it is what an implementer does by default | ❌ REJECTED as the single most dangerous default available to this feature. `0` propagates through the expression and produces a fully-formed, defensible-looking, wrong number — and which party it robs depends on which side of the expression the input sits, so it is not even consistently biased. The whole product is a derivation that can be trusted; a derivation built on a silent zero is a confident lie with a receipt attached. A missing input makes a line **un-evaluated** and the sheet **Incomplete**. |
| DT-08 | The sheet's number is the money that moves | The plain reading of "what the promoter owes the artist" | ❌ REJECTED: **the deposit breaks it.** A deposit paid at contract (17.05) is an advance against this settlement, so what moves tonight is fee-minus-deposit. The room does not care about the fee; it cares about the balance. A sheet that states a fee nobody will pay is arithmetically correct and practically useless, and it will be immediately "corrected" with a calculator — reinstating the exact 1am artefact this feature exists to abolish. The deposit is a derivation line, not an afterthought elsewhere. |
| DT-09 | The sheet should flag lines that look wrong — "this security cost is 3× this venue's average" | Anomaly-detection instinct; the platform has 17.11's comparables and could | ❌ REJECTED, and the reason is commercial, not technical. **The sheet's authority comes from being neutral arithmetic both parties already agreed.** The moment it editorialises about a line it becomes a party to the negotiation — and the Operator, whose adoption is already the harder sell (17.09.06 DT-03: they are being asked to adopt a tool that reduces their information advantage), correctly reads it as the platform taking the artist's side. It also spends 17.11's comparables, which are governed by an unresolved consent problem (domain Q-07). What the sheet may do without becoming a party is **state facts adjacently**: "this category qualifies under the schedule; this line is £800; cap headroom is £200". The human draws the conclusion. Neutrality is not timidity here — it is the only reason both sides will accept the output. |
| DT-10 | The sheet is computed at settlement time | The feature's name and its position after the show in every description | ❌ REJECTED: the evaluator is free (it is 17.03.02's), so the sheet is computable **from the first advance sale onward**, and a promoter and tour manager both want to know at 6pm what the night is tracking toward. The value is not the projection itself — it is that **nobody arrives at 1am surprised**, which is what makes the settlement room calm enough for a derivation to be read rather than argued over. The hazard is exact and known: 17.03.03 D-02 names "the artist banks a provisional number" as the trust failure this class of feature most invites. So a projection is a distinct, unsignable state, labelled on every figure — not a preview of the settlement. |
| DT-11 | The derivation should show the deal's expression | The natural reading of "show the arithmetic" — transparency means showing the formula | ❌ REJECTED, and this is where transparency and comprehension come apart. `meta/personas.md` names "signs a split sheet they don't understand" as the Musician's **worst accidental behaviour**, and 17.03.01's Role Lens already commits to them reading their deal "rendered as outcomes rather than as an expression". A sheet that renders `max(2000, 0.85 × (gross − min(expenses, 6000)))` is transparent to precisely the party who already understood the deal — which is the party with the information advantage. **That is the asymmetry reproduced in typography**, dressed as its cure. The derivation is worked arithmetic in the deal's own words, line by line, each line naming the clause that authorised it. |
| DT-12 | A settled show is proof the gig happened | This file's own Cross-Cut Notes, and the sub-domain index's claim that settlement makes a performance credit "verifiable rather than self-asserted" | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the hole is structural rather than incidental.** The proof's strength comes entirely from **counterparty attestation** — two adverse parties signed the same number, so neither invented it. But `meta/personas.md`'s defining structural fact is the multi-hyphenate, and `meta/personas.md` says the Operator "often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves". A **self-promoted show** — routine at this platform's exact tier — has one principal on both sides. The sheet computes; both signatures are real; and the platform has manufactured a "verified" draw record (17.11.01), a payment-reliability fact (17.12) and an evidenced performance credit (02) out of one person agreeing with themselves. This is the thesis's strongest claim being forgeable by the persona model's defining characteristic, and nothing in the domain addresses it. The settlement must record **whether the two sides are independent principals**, and every consumer of a settlement-as-evidence must read that flag. Detecting shared control is a real problem (an entity chain, a bank account, a household) and is not solvable here — but recording the ones the platform *can* see, and refusing to launder the ones it cannot, is the difference between an honest claim and a marketable one. |

Consumes **`sold`** — the paid count, tier by tier — and never an admissions figure (17.09.02 D-08, name-bound by its D-18: `sold` drives the money, `admissions_paid` the draw, `admissions_total` occupancy and merch per-head); a sheet computed on admissions pays for people who came, not people who paid.
- Touches **17.09.03 Show Expense & Receipt Capture** ([./17.09.03-show-expense-receipt-capture.md](./17.09.03-show-expense-receipt-capture.md)) — **trigger dependency**, bounded by the deal's cap (CX-02). Over-cap expenses render as captured-but-not-deductible; credits render as their own line, never as a silent negative.
- Touches **17.05.02 Deposits & Balances** ([../17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation/](../17.05-deposits-balances-cancellation/)) — **shared entity, newly identified (DT-08).** The deposit is an advance against this settlement and must appear as a derivation line, or the sheet states a number nobody will pay.
- Touches **17.03.03 Multi-Show Deals** ([../17.03-deal-structures-economics/17.03.03-multishow-cross-collateralization.md](../17.03-deal-structures-economics/17.03.03-multishow-cross-collateralization.md)) — **state conflict. Contradicts per-show finality** (index Q-01, this file Q-04). Also supplies the pattern this feature reuses: provisional markers on every view, recomputation as an event.
- Touches **17.04 Performance Contracts** ([../17.04-performance-contracts-deal-memos.md](../17.04-performance-contracts-deal-memos.md)) — **shared entity.** The executed contract as amended is the reference terms; every derived line names the contract version that authorised it.
- Touches **17.09.05 Settlement Signoff & Variance** ([./17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md](./17.09.05-settlement-signoff-variance.md)) — **state race (CX-03).** A computed number is a proposal until signed. This feature owns computation versioning; 17.09.05 owns what a signature binds to. The Incomplete state refuses signature; the Stale state permits one that validates on reconnect.
- Touches **17.09.06 Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes** ([./17.09.06-settlement-audit-trail-disputes.md](./17.09.06-settlement-audit-trail-disputes.md)) — **trigger dependency.** Every computation and recomputation writes the trail; determinism is what lets the trail answer "why did this number change" with an input diff.
- Touches **17.09.04 Merch Settlement** ([./17.09.04-merch-settlement-venue-cut.md](./17.09.04-merch-settlement-venue-cut.md)) — **CX-05, this feature's half now resolved (D-09).** The sheet always declares merch's disposition — on-sheet line or settled-separately — and is never silently missing it, whichever way 17.09.04 Q-01 lands.
- Touches **17.08.02 Commission Accrual & Deduction** ([../17.08-agency-representation-commission/17.08.02-commission-accrual-deduction.md](../17.08-agency-representation-commission/17.08.02-commission-accrual-deduction.md)) — **permission boundary (D-11).** The agent reads the artist's sheet to compute commission; the sheet carries no commission line, because a shared sheet showing it would leak the act's agency terms to the promoter negotiating against them (domain index Role Matrix note 3).
- Touches **17.10.02 Disbursement Execution** ([../17.10-live-income-payout-tax/17.10.02-disbursement-execution.md](../17.10-live-income-payout-tax/17.10.02-disbursement-execution.md)) — **trigger dependency + reconciliation.** Final signoff fires disbursement. The rounding residual (DT-05) must be assigned here or the sum of disbursements will not equal the settled total.
- Touches **17.09.07 Settlement Statement & Export** ([./17.09.07-settlement-statement-export.md](./17.09.07-settlement-statement-export.md)) — **CX-06.** The signed computation becomes the statement; the artist's accountant is its real consumer, and is the person a rounding residual or an unstated FX position lands on.
- Touches **17.11.01 Verified Draw Record** ([../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/17.11.01-verified-draw-record.md](../17.11-draw-history-market-intelligence/17.11.01-verified-draw-record.md)) — **notification/derivation dependency, newly qualified (DT-12).** Consumes **`admissions_paid`** — the paid-only projection of the derived admissions total (17.09.02 D-17, D-18) — never this sheet's `sold` count and never the gate-observed `scanned` component; but it does consume this sheet's *signature* as the thing that makes the record "verified". Must therefore read the independent-principals flag (D-12), or a self-promoted show manufactures a verified draw.
- Touches **17.12 Counterparty Relationship & Payment Reliability** ([../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md](../17.12-counterparty-relationship-payment-reliability.md)) — same dependency, same qualification: a reliability fact derived from one person paying themselves is not a reliability fact (DT-12).
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([../../02-credits-attribution/](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **a settled show is proof the gig happened**, making a performance credit verifiable rather than self-asserted. The domain's clearest link to the D-18 thesis — **and DT-12 is the hole in it.** 02 must read the independent-principals flag before treating a settlement as evidence.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — a held line makes this sheet Incomplete; 24 adjudicates, this feature recomputes (17.09.06 D-02).
- Touches **23 Career, Finance & Business Management** ([../../23-career-finance-business/](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — the artist's own tour costs are not show expenses and never appear here (17.09.03's boundary); this sheet's output is an income event there.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Structured Economic Terms → Computed Statement engine** — serves 10 (royalty statements), 11 (licence fees), 05 (service invoices), 17. The un-evaluated-term pattern (D-05), the never-default-to-zero rule (D-07) and the rounding-residual rule (DT-05) are properties of the **engine**, not of live settlement — every consumer of this mechanism inherits the same three failure modes.
- Cross-cut mechanism: **Multi-Currency & FX** (DT-06, 17.03.01 DT-03) — serves 05, 13, 14, 17, 19, 23. The rate is the grammar's; the **position in the derivation order** is the statement engine's.
- Not-product: offline computation and sync substrate — architecture, routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. Driven by the product question Q-02.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The deal expression is evaluated, never re-entered | Re-entry is a second source of truth that will disagree with the first — the failure the domain exists to prevent. |
| D-02 | The output is the derivation, not the number | A total is the status quo with better typography (DT-02). |
| D-03 | A computed number is a proposal until signoff | Settlement is a negotiation over a computation, not a report (DT-01, CX-03). |
| D-04 | The asymmetry claim is qualified, not accepted | The platform closes it over the formula, not over the inputs it does not own (DT-03). Escalated because it bears on 19's priority. |
| D-05 | **Terms the grammar cannot compile become un-evaluated terms, never silence** | Retained verbatim; rendered as a line with no derivation; resolved only by an amount plus **counter-attestation from the other party**; the sheet is Incomplete and unsignable until then. Silent incompleteness would make the derivation — the entire product — a confident lie. Resolves the file's sharpest unresolved risk and its prior Q-03. |
| D-06 | The un-evaluated-term count is the grammar's honest, per-settlement ceiling measurement | Reframes 17.03.01 Q-01 (closed vocabulary vs open language) from a philosophical question into a measurable one, and removes the catastrophic downside from the "closed" answer: a closed grammar that names what it cannot see is honest; one that silently drops is dangerous. |
| D-07 | **A missing input is never zero** | `0` propagates and yields a defensible-looking wrong number whose bias depends on which side of the expression the input sits (DT-07). The line is un-evaluated; the sheet is Incomplete. This is the most dangerous default available to an implementer and it is closed by decision, not by convention. |
| D-08 | The deposit already paid is a derivation line; the sheet's headline is the **balance due** | The room cares about what moves tonight. A sheet stating a fee nobody will pay gets "corrected" with a calculator, reinstating the artefact this feature abolishes (DT-08). |
| D-09 | The sheet always declares merch's disposition explicitly | On-sheet line or `settled separately — see 17.09.04`. Never silently absent, whichever way 17.09.04 Q-01 lands. Resolves this feature's half of CX-05 without pre-empting theirs. |
| D-10 | **One show → one gross, one expense pool, N sheets** — one per deal on the bill | Domain index D-10 already established that one-artist-one-promoter is wrong on most shows; the correction never reached this file (DT-04). A single N-section sheet would need N+1 signatures on one artefact and would leak every act's terms. **Shared-line visibility follows expression reference**: a party sees exactly those shared lines their own deal's expression names — gross if their deal references gross, the expense pool if it references net, nothing if they are on a flat fee. |
| D-11 | The sheet carries no commission, withholding or platform-fee line | Those are operations on the artist's side of the money. A commission line on a sheet the promoter reads leaks the act's agency terms to the party negotiating against them (domain index Role Matrix note 3). |
| D-12 | **The settlement records whether its two sides are independent principals** | A self-promoted show is one person agreeing with themselves, and the platform's strongest provenance claim borrows all its credibility from counterparty attestation (DT-12). Downstream consumers of settlement-as-evidence (17.11.01, 17.12, 02) must read the flag. The platform cannot detect every shared-control case — but it must not launder the ones it cannot see into "verified". |
| D-13 | Rounding convention is stated on the sheet; the residual is assigned to a named party as its own line | An absorbed residual makes the sum of disbursements differ from the settled total, which surfaces as a reconciliation failure on the accountant's desk — 17.09.07's actual consumer (DT-05). |
| D-14 | FX conversion is an explicit derivation line at a stated position in the deduction order | Convert-then-deduct and deduct-then-convert differ by real money (DT-06). If the deal did not state the basis, the FX term is an un-evaluated term (D-05) — the agent does not guess. |
| D-15 | A negative settlement is rendered, never clamped | A guarantee is a floor and clamps to itself; a deal with no floor genuinely can leave the artist owing. Clamping silently transfers the loss to whichever party the clamp favours (edge cases). Whether the platform *collects* it is Q-03. |
| D-16 | The derivation is worked arithmetic in the deal's own words — never the expression | Rendering `max(2000, 0.85 × (gross − min(exp, 6000)))` is transparency legible only to the party who already had the advantage. That is the asymmetry reproduced in typography (DT-11), and `meta/personas.md` names not-understanding-what-you-sign as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour. |
| D-17 | Offline degradation is specified independently of whether an offline surface is built | The sheet degrades to last-computed read-only with a staleness marker; a signature binds to the version its signer saw and validates on reconnect, invalidating per 17.09.05 if superseded. This needs no new machinery — it is the mid-room recomputation case, which already exists. Whether a surface can do it at all remains Q-02. |
| D-18 | Recomputation is an event with a visible input diff; determinism is what makes that possible | Same expression version + same inputs = same sheet, always. This is what lets a signature bind to a version (17.09.05 D-01) and lets the trail answer "why did my number change" with a diff rather than an apology (17.03.03 D-03). |
