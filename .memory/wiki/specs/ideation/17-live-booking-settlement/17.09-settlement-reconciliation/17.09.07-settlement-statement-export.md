# Feature: Settlement Statement & Export

> **Number**: 17.09.07
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The artefact that leaves the building. The signed computation rendered as a statement the artist's
accountant, the agency, and eventually a tax authority can actually use — because a settlement that
lives only inside the platform has not replaced the piece of paper, it has added a screen next to it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Exports their own statement. **Their accountant is the real consumer** — the artist forwards it and never looks at it again. This is also the feature that answers `meta/problem-statement.md`'s open question about earned vs hostile lock-in (Q-02 there): a platform that exports cleanly is one you stay with by choice. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Issues the statement; their own bookkeeping consumes it from the other side of the same show. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

The statement is a **rendering of the final signed computation** (CX-06) — a clean, point-in-time
artefact. It reads the signed sheet, **not** the audit trail (sub-domain R-02): change history has no
place in an accounting document, and regenerating statements from the trail would let a filed
statement silently change after the fact.

Its consumers are not the people in the room:

- The **artist's accountant**, who wants it in their software's shape, not a PDF.
- The **agency**, reconciling commission (17.08.02).
- The **tax authority**, eventually, via domain 23 — including the withholding evidence a foreign
  artist needs to reclaim (17.10.03).

A settlement that cannot leave has not solved the problem; it has moved it.

### Happy Path

1. Settlement signs off (17.09.05); the computation is final.
2. Statement renders from the signed sheet with every line and its derivation.
3. Artist exports — to their accountant, in a format that software can read.
4. Agency reconciles its commission against the same statement.
5. The statement flows into the artist's finances (domain 23) as live income for the year.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Producer or Fan reaches for the export | Neither has any role in live settlement (Role Lens; sub-domain Role Matrix) | The export surface is absent from their UI; the API rejects with 403. Export is scoped to the show's two principals — the artist and the operator — and nobody else |
| Settlement not yet signed off | There is no final computation to render; a proposal is not a statement | No statement — "Available once both parties sign off" (States → Unavailable). The export action is disabled with that reason inline |
| Settlement amended after a prior export | A filed statement is now stale; the accountant has already used the number | A **new version** is issued; the prior export is retained immutably and marked **Superseded** (D-03, States → Superseded). The exporter is told a newer version exists and can re-send. The file already in the accountant's hands never silently mutates |
| Show inside an open cross-collateralized run | The number is provisional until the run closes (17.03.03; sub-domain Q-01) | An explicit **Provisional** marker is carried into the export *itself*, not merely shown on screen — otherwise the exported artefact lies to an accountant who never saw the screen |
| Statement exported before disbursement | The number is agreed; the money has not moved | "Agreed" and "Paid" are distinct, labelled facts on the statement (D-04) — agreed ≠ paid |
| Foreign artist needs withholding evidence to reclaim tax | The statement is the reclaim evidence (17.10.03) | Withholding appears as a distinct, evidenced line, not netted silently into a single total |
| A stale (superseded) export is re-sent as if current | Someone forwards last week's version to mislead, or by mistake | Every export is stamped with its version number and issue timestamp; the platform can always report which version is current, so a stale file is *detectable* rather than authoritative |
| Signed under protest (17.09.05) | The number is final for payment; the objection is real and material to an accountant | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` — whether the protest annotation appears on the statement is a values/product call (Q-02). Level-1 default: the payable number is authoritative on the statement regardless of the protest's presentation |
| Accountant wants a format nobody anticipated | The accounting-software long tail is real (Q-01) | A structured machine-readable export (CSV plus the signed line data) and a PDF fallback are **always** available, so export never hard-fails on format. The specific named integrations (e.g. ledger-software imports) are `[PENDING — Q-01]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unavailable | Settlement unsigned | No statement — a proposal is not a statement |
| Available | Both parties signed | Statement renderable with full derivation |
| Provisional | Show inside an open run | Statement carries an explicit provisional marker |
| Exported | User exported | Version recorded; prior exports immutable |
| Superseded | Settlement amended after export | New version; prior export retained and marked superseded |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The statement is a PDF of the settlement | The obvious deliverable, and what the industry produces today | ❌ REJECTED: **the consumers are machines and accountants, not the people in the room.** The people in the room already saw the sheet — they signed it. What has to leave is something an accountant's software ingests and a tax authority accepts. A PDF is a picture of the data and reproduces the re-keying that generates the errors this domain exists to eliminate: an accountant retyping a settlement into their ledger is `meta/problem-statement.md`'s reconstruction failure, one step downstream. Export format is the feature; the PDF is a fallback. |
| DT-02 | Export is a convenience feature, low priority | Its placement as the last child of the sub-domain | ❌ REJECTED, on thesis grounds. `meta/problem-statement.md` leaves Q-02 explicitly open: **"Is the lock-in earned or hostile? If a user leaves, what do they take?"** — flagged as a values decision for the owner. This feature is where that question gets answered concretely for the highest-value data the domain holds. A platform whose settlement history cannot leave is practising hostile lock-in whatever it says in its marketing; one that exports cleanly is betting that being where the work happens is enough. **That is a decision the owner should make deliberately here**, not discover as a consequence of deprioritising an export button. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The statement reads the signed sheet, never the audit trail | Change history has no place in an accounting artefact, and regenerating from the trail would let a filed statement silently mutate (sub-domain R-02). |
| D-02 | Machine-readable export is the feature; the PDF is a fallback | An accountant retyping a settlement is the reconstruction failure one step downstream (DT-01). |
| D-03 | Prior exports are immutable and versioned | An accountant has already used the number; silently changing it under them is the worst possible behaviour. |
| D-04 | "Agreed" and "paid" are distinguishable on the statement | A statement exported before disbursement states a number that has not moved. |
