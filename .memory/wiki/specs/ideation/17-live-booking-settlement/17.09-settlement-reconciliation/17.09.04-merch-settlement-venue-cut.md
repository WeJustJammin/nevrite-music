# Feature: Merch Settlement & Venue Merch Cut

> **Number**: 17.09.04
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Counting the merch and computing the house's cut. Merch has its own grammar — soft goods versus hard
goods at different rates, with-seller versus without-seller, exempt items, and a count that is done by
hand from a box at 1am. On a bad night merch **is** the artist's income, which is why the venue cut is
the term artists resent most and argue about hardest.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Merch is the artist's money and frequently their real income — a band can lose on the door and go home solvent on t-shirts. They count in and count out, and they see the house cut computed against the deal's merch terms. This is the line where a working musician most feels the difference between a fair room and a bad one. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Computes and takes the house cut, at the rates their deal specifies. Provides (or doesn't) a seller — which changes the rate materially. |
| Fan | None | Never visible — but the Fan bought the shirt. Merch is the one place a fan's money reaches the artist almost directly, which is exactly what `meta/personas.md` says the Fan wants ("money reaches the artist rather than a reseller"). |

## Behavior

Merch settlement is **count in, count out, sell-through is the difference**. There is no till and no
manifest — a road manager counts 60 shirts into a box before doors and 41 out after, and 19 were
sold. The count is physical, manual, and done by a tired person at 1am.

The grammar has its own vocabulary, distinct from the show deal:

- **Soft goods** (t-shirts, hoodies) — typically the higher house rate, often 15-25%.
- **Hard goods** (vinyl, CDs) — typically lower or zero; many territories treat recorded music
  differently by convention.
- **With seller / without seller** — if the venue provides the person behind the table, the rate goes
  up. If the band sells its own, it goes down.
- **Exempt items** — signed items, bundles, tour-exclusive pressings are commonly carved out.
- **Basis** — gross or net of sales tax, which is a real and routinely-fudged distinction.

Whether merch lands as a **line on the settlement sheet** or as a **parallel cash settlement** varies
by room (CX-05). Both are real.

### Happy Path

1. Road manager counts stock in before doors and records it against the show.
2. Show plays; merch sells.
3. Road manager counts out; sell-through computes per item type.
4. System applies the deal's merch grammar — soft rate, hard rate, seller adjustment, exemptions.
5. House cut computes; it lands on the settlement sheet (17.09.01) or is settled in cash, per the
   room.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Merch settled in cash at the table before the show settlement starts | Common; the platform sees an assertion, not a payment (D-01) | Recordable as an asserted cash settlement, like the on-the-night balance — the cut lands as a parallel settlement, not a sheet line |
| Show settles with no merch counted in | The `Not counted` state was never left; there is no stock baseline to compute sell-through from | Merch terms stay visible from the deal, but the cut shows `No merch counted — nothing to settle`; the settlement is not blocked on it |
| Operator tries to edit the artist's count | The count is an assertion **by the artist's side** (DT-02); the Operator is the one taking the number on trust | Operator has read-only visibility of the count and may raise a discrepancy, but cannot overwrite the artist's counted-in/out figures |
| Band under-reports counted-out to shrink the cut | The only place in domain 17 where the abuse runs *toward* the Operator (DT-02); a low sell-through means a low cut | Both sides see the derived sell-through and the cut; a discrepancy against cash-taken (where visible) is surfaced, and the number is retained on 17.12 as reliability signal |
| Two people (road manager + merch seller) record a count-out concurrently | Last-write-wins would silently drop one count; both are asserting the same box | Counts are attributed per recorder; a conflicting count-out on the same item type is flagged for the artist's side to reconcile, not merged |

| Venue cut on hard goods where local convention says zero | Territory-specific; the deal's grammar must carry it | Zero-rate hard goods derive a £0 cut with the convention cited (ties 17.03.01 Q-02) |
| Bundle: shirt + vinyl at one price | Soft and hard rates apply to one transaction | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` — a real grammar gap (Q-02) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Merch settlement is a line on the show settlement | The candidate's placement, and the tidy model | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — it is frequently a parallel settlement.** Merch is often counted and settled in cash at the merch table before the show settlement even starts, by different people (road manager and merch seller, not tour manager and promoter). Modelling it as strictly a sheet line would misrepresent how it actually happens and would force a cash-settled cut into a sheet that settles by transfer. It must be able to be either (CX-05, Q-01). |
| DT-02 | Merch sell-through is derived from sales data | E-commerce instinct — count the transactions | ❌ REJECTED: **there is no till.** Sell-through is `counted in − counted out`, done by hand from a box by a tired person at 1am, and the money is a mix of cash, the band's own card reader, and the venue's. There is no transaction log to derive from. This means the merch count is an **assertion by the artist's side** — which is the settlement asymmetry running in the *opposite* direction from everywhere else in this sub-domain, and the only place in domain 17 where the Operator is the one who has to take a number on trust. Nothing in the sweep noticed this inversion. |
| DT-03 | The venue merch cut is a deal term like any other | Its grammatical form — a percentage in the offer | ❌ REJECTED as *like any other*: it is the term with the **worst ratio of money to resentment** in live music. A band that loses on the door and goes home solvent on t-shirts experiences a 20% soft-goods cut as the venue taking a fifth of the only money that worked. Modelling it neutrally is correct; presenting it neutrally may not be. This has a concrete design consequence — the cut should be rendered in the offer's outcome view (17.02.01 D-02) at *realistic* merch numbers, so it is agreed with open eyes rather than discovered at the table. It is also a strong candidate for what artists will use 17.12's relationship record to compare rooms on. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Merch can settle as a sheet line **or** as a parallel cash settlement | Both are real; forcing one misrepresents how it happens (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Sell-through is `counted in − counted out`, not derived from transactions | There is no till (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Merch sold at the show is a different pool from D2C merch (domain 20) | An online sale has no venue cut; merging the pools would attach one. |
| D-04 | Cash/sell-through discrepancies are surfaced, never silently reconciled | The discrepancy is information — about shrinkage, comps, or the count being wrong. |
