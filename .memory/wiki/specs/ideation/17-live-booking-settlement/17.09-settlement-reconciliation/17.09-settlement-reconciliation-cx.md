## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Box Office Count Reconciliation ↔ Settlement Sheet Computation

**Relationship**: The deal expression compiled at offer time (17.03.01) is **inert until a count
arrives**. The count is the variable, and it is supplied by the Operator from their own box office —
which is the information asymmetry the domain index identifies as running one direction.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: supplies the count from a system they control.
- **Musician**: has historically had to accept it. **Their right to check is the entire point of this
  sub-domain.**
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected — though the Fan is the count.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The count is owned by the Operator's box office but consumed as fact by
   both. When the platform *is* the box office (domain 19), the asymmetry collapses — when it is not,
   the count is an assertion.
2. **Trigger chain**: Count → evaluate → sheet. A corrected count recomputes everything downstream,
   including commission that may already be disbursed (17.08.02 DT-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator supplies; the artist checks. Neither can unilaterally
   set the number that decides both their money.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A count change moves the money — both principals, plus the agent whose
   commission moves with it.
5. **State transition conflict**: Counts arriving while the sheet is being signed. Real at 1am.

### CX-02: Show Expense & Receipt Capture ↔ Settlement Sheet Computation

**Relationship**: "85% of net after $6,000 expenses" splits into two owners — the **cap** is a deal
term (17.03.01), and **what fills it** is captured here. Expenses are the only settlement input the
Operator both supplies and benefits from inflating, which is precisely why the cap exists and why
this edge is adversarial by construction.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: captures expenses at the venue, on a phone, at a loading dock.
- **Musician**: sees deductible expenses and can contest them — a line-item dispute (17.09.06).
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Expenses are Operator-supplied and artist-contestable. There is no merge
   — there is a dispute.
2. **Trigger chain**: Capture → categorise → cap check → deduct. An expense over the cap is not an
   error; it is simply not deductible, which is a different thing and must be shown as such.
3. **Permission intersection**: The artist cannot delete an expense; they can contest it. The
   distinction is the whole design.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Expenses appearing after signoff is a trust event, not a data event.
5. **State transition conflict**: Expenses landing during signoff — the classic 1am "one more
   receipt".

### CX-03: Settlement Sheet Computation ↔ Settlement Signoff & Variance

**Relationship**: **A computed number is a proposal, not a fact.** This is the sub-domain's most
easily-missed truth: settlement is a negotiation over a computation, not a report. The system computes
a defensible number; the parties agree it or argue about it; signoff converts it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: signoff is meaningless without theirs — an Operator-only signoff is just the status
  quo with better typography.
- **Operator**: signs, and explains variance where the number differs from what was modelled.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The sheet is shared; signoff is per-party against a specific
   computation — the same version-binding insight as 17.02.03's approval chain.
2. **Trigger chain**: Compute → sign → sign → final → disburse (17.10.02). A refusal returns to
   computation or escalates to dispute.
3. **Permission intersection**: Signing is not the same as agreeing every line — a party may sign
   under protest, which the industry does constantly.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Final signoff triggers disbursement, commission accrual, draw record,
   and reliability update. The widest fan-out in the domain.
5. **State transition conflict**: A recomputation between the two signoffs invalidates the first —
   exactly the v2/v4 failure from 17.02.03.

### CX-04: Settlement Signoff & Variance ↔ Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes

**Relationship**: The trail records every signoff, every refusal, and every post-signoff amendment.
It exists to answer "what did we agree, and who changed it after" — which `meta/problem-statement.md`
identifies as the exact question that gets asked years later, from memory, once it is contested and
valuable.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician** / **Operator**: both Read-only. **Nobody writes the trail** — it is written by the
  system as a consequence of their acts elsewhere. An editable audit trail is not an audit trail.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None by construction — the trail is append-only and unowned.
2. **Trigger chain**: Any settlement act → trail entry. A contested line re-opens the computation and
   the re-opening is itself recorded.
3. **Permission intersection**: Read-only for everyone; the enforcement is what makes it evidence.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A post-signoff amendment must notify everyone who signed and everyone
   whose money moved — including the agent (17.08.02).
5. **State transition conflict**: The amendment-after-disbursement problem — money has moved and the
   trail records an obligation the platform may not be able to execute.

### CX-05: Merch Settlement & Venue Merch Cut ↔ Settlement Sheet Computation

**Relationship**: Merch has **its own grammar** (soft vs hard goods, with/without seller, exempt
items) and lands as a line on the sheet — sometimes. It is frequently settled in cash, separately,
before the show settlement even starts.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: merch is often the artist's real income on a bad night; the venue cut is the term
  they most resent.
- **Operator**: computes and takes the house cut.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected — though the Fan bought the shirt.

**Synthesis questions answered**: Medium confidence. The open question is whether merch is a line on
the settlement or a parallel settlement that happens to occur in the same room — `[PENDING —

### CX-06: Settlement Sheet Computation ↔ Settlement Statement & Export

**Relationship**: The signed computation becomes the artefact that leaves the building — to the
artist's accountant, the agency, and eventually the tax authority. It is the settlement's only
externally-legible form.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: exports their own statement; their accountant is the real consumer.
- **Operator**: issues it.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

### CX-07: Box Office Count Reconciliation ↔ Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes

**Relationship**: A contested count is the **most common settlement dispute** — "how many comps?" is
the classic 1am argument. The trail is what makes the argument resolvable rather than a matter of who
is more insistent.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: raises the line dispute; the trail is their evidence.
- **Operator**: responds; the trail is equally their evidence.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: High confidence. Detail deferred — the interaction with domain 24's
dispute machinery is the open boundary (index D-03).

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Show Expense & Receipt Capture | Merch Settlement & Venue Merch Cut | Rejected. Both are settlement inputs and both are captured at the venue, which makes them look adjacent — but they share no state and neither triggers the other. Merch cost of goods is the artist's business and never a show expense; show expenses never reduce the merch cut. The only thing they have in common is the loading dock. Modelling an edge would imply merch could be expensed against the show, which is a category error that would materially misstate both. |
| R-02 | Settlement Statement & Export | Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes | Rejected as an interaction. The statement is a **rendering of the final computation**; the trail is the **history of how it got there**. A statement does not read the trail — it reads the signed sheet. Coupling them would put change history into an accounting document that must be a clean point-in-time artefact, and would tempt an implementer to regenerate statements from the trail, which would let a superseded statement silently change after it was filed. |
| R-03 | Box Office Count Reconciliation | Show Expense & Receipt Capture | Rejected. Both feed the computation (CX-01, CX-02) but they do not touch each other — the count does not bound expenses and expenses do not affect the count. The tempting false edge is per-head expenses (security scaled to attendance), but that is the *deal grammar's* concern (a term whose value references the count), not an interaction between these two features. |
