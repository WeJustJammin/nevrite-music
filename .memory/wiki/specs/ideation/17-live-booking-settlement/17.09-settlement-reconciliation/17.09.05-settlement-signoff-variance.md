# Feature: Settlement Signoff & Variance Explanation

> **Number**: 17.09.05
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The moment a computed number becomes a real one. Both parties sign the settlement, and where it
differs from what was expected, the variance is explained rather than absorbed. **A settlement is a
negotiation over a computation, not a report** — and signoff is the event that models that truth.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Signoff is meaningless without the artist's.** An Operator-only signoff is the status quo with better typography — a number handed over to be accepted. Signs, refuses, or signs under protest, which is what the industry actually does at 1am when everyone wants to leave. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Signs, and explains variance where the number differs from the deal's modelled expectation — why the security cost double, why 90 comps went out. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Signoff binds to a **specific computation**, not to "the settlement" — the same version-binding
insight as the offer approval chain (17.02.03 DT-02). A recomputation between the two signoffs
invalidates the first (CX-03), or the platform reproduces the v2/v4 failure in the settlement room.

Three outcomes, not two:

- **Sign** — agreed.
- **Refuse** — returns to computation or escalates to a dispute (17.09.06).
- **Sign under protest** — agreed to the payment, not to the number. **This is the one the industry
  actually uses**, and a system offering only sign/refuse cannot represent the most common way a
  contested settlement ends.

Variance explanation is the feature's second half: the deal was modelled (17.03.02), the show
happened, and the delta between them is where the learning is — both for this relationship and for
the draw record (17.11.01).

### Happy Path

1. Sheet computes (17.09.01) with counts and expenses reconciled.
2. Variance against the modelled expectation renders — what differed and by how much.
3. Operator signs and explains material variances.
4. Artist reviews the derivation and the variance, and signs.
5. Number is final. Disbursement (17.10.02), commission (17.08.02), draw record (17.11.01) and
   reliability (17.12) all fire.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Party signs under protest | The most common contested outcome — the money moves, the objection is recorded | Must be a first-class state, not a workaround (D-02) |
| Recomputation between the two signoffs | Invalidates the first signature (CX-03) — the v2/v4 failure in the settlement room | First signer re-notified; signature rebinds to the new computation |
| Artist refuses to sign and the promoter has cash on the table | Real: the band needs to be paid to get to the next city | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — does refusal block payment? (Q-01) |
| Everyone wants to leave; signing is the fastest exit | The structural pressure that makes settlement asymmetric in the first place | Sign-under-protest is the honest release valve (DT-02) |
| Amendment after both signed | Trail records it; commission may already be disbursed (17.08.02 DT-02) | Amendment event; all signers re-notified |
| Show inside an open cross-collateralized run | The number cannot be final (17.03.03) | Signoff signs a **provisional** — a different act (index Q-01) |
| Nobody signs; the show is just over | Common — settlement is verbal and the money moved | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — an unsigned settlement is a real state |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unsigned | Computation ready | Derivation, variance, sign/refuse/protest actions |
| Partially signed | One party signed | Whose signature is outstanding; the computation is locked |
| Signed | Both signed | Final; downstream fires |
| Signed under protest | A party signed with a recorded objection | Final for payment; objection preserved and visible in the trail |
| Refused | A party refused | Returns to computation or escalates to 24 |
| Invalidated | Recomputation between signatures | Prior signature void; signers re-notified |
| Amended | Post-signoff change | New computation; trail records; clawbacks may arise |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Signoff is a confirmation step | The obvious UX reading — a button at the end of a computation | ❌ REJECTED: it is the event that **converts a proposal into a fact** (CX-03). The whole sub-domain's arithmetic produces a *number*; only signoff produces an *agreement*. Modelling it as a confirmation would make the computation authoritative on its own, which reverses the actual power relationship — the parties settle, the system computes. A system whose number is final without both signatures has not automated settlement; it has replaced one party's calculator with its own. |
| DT-02 | The outcomes are sign or refuse | Binary instinct | ❌ REJECTED: **"sign under protest" is what the industry actually does.** At 1am, with a van to load and a drive to Glasgow, a tour manager who disputes an expense line signs anyway to get the cash and argues next week — and the objection is real and is often honoured later. A system offering only sign/refuse forces that person to either lie (sign, agreeing) or blow up the night (refuse, no payment). It would drive the most common contested outcome off-platform, which is precisely where the platform loses the record it exists to keep. |
| DT-03 | Variance explanation is a reporting nicety | Its placement as a secondary half of the feature | ❌ REJECTED: variance is the **only feedback loop the domain has.** The deal was modelled at offer time (17.03.02); the show happened; the delta is the learning — and it feeds three things: this relationship's trust (17.12), the draw record's accuracy (17.11.01), and the next offer's realism. Without it, every show is settled in isolation and the platform accumulates numbers without accumulating knowledge. `meta/problem-statement.md` says the value is in what compounds; variance is what makes settlement compound rather than merely repeat. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Signoff binds to a specific computation, not to "the settlement" | A recomputation between signatures otherwise reproduces the v2/v4 failure in the settlement room (CX-03). |
| D-02 | "Sign under protest" is a first-class outcome | It is what the industry does; offering only sign/refuse drives the most common contested outcome off-platform (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Both signatures are required for finality | An Operator-only signoff is the status quo with better typography (DT-01). |
| D-04 | Variance is a feedback loop, not a report | It is what makes settlement compound into draw intelligence and reliability rather than repeat (DT-03). |
