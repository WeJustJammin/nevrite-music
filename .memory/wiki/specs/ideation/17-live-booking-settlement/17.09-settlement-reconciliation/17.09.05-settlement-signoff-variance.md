# Feature: Settlement Signoff & Variance Explanation

> **Number**: 17.09.05
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The moment a computed number becomes a real one. Both parties sign the settlement, and where it
differs from what was projected at offer time, the variance is explained rather than absorbed.
**A settlement is a negotiation over a computation, not a report** — and signoff is the event that
models that truth.

The feature exists because 17.09.01 produces a *derivation* and 17.09.06 produces a *record*, and
neither of those is an *agreement*. Without signoff the platform has replaced the promoter's
calculator with its own and changed nothing about who decides. Signoff is where the artist's
consent becomes a structural requirement rather than a courtesy — and where the settled fact that
domains 02, 17.11 and 17.12 all depend on is actually minted.

Variance explanation is the second half. The deal was projected at offer time; the show happened;
the delta between them is the only feedback loop the domain has (DT-03), and forcing it to be
explained *before the Operator can sign* is what stops the delta being silently absorbed into a
number the artist is handed at 1am.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Signoff is meaningless without the artist's.** Reads the derivation (17.09.01), the shared variance panel, and the Operator's explanations for every material adverse line. Chooses **Sign**, **Sign under protest**, or **Refuse** — and on refusal still receives the undisputed floor (D-09). Additionally sees a **private** variance against their own cost model (crew, transport, commission) that the Operator never sees. Signs *as an entity*, under the band's configured settlement-authority rule (D-06) — at 1am this is almost always a tour manager holding delegated authority, not the four members. Sees, on any re-presentation, **what changed since the version they signed** — never the whole sheet again (D-11). |
| Producer | None | No role. Live settlement has no Producer lens: the persona owns the studio room (domain 16), not the stage. A Producer standing in the venue at 1am is acting as **Musician** (they played) or **Operator** (they run the room), and gets that lens' access — not a third one. |
| Operator | Full | Signs, and **cannot sign until every material adverse variance line they own carries an explanation** (D-05) — why security cost double, why 90 comps went out against an allowance of 20. Supplies the inputs the variance is measured on (the count, CX-01; the expenses, CX-02), which is exactly why the explanation obligation attaches to their signature and not the artist's. Sees the artist's protest reason and holds a permanent **right of reply** to it (D-13). Their own margin variance is private and never rendered to the artist. |
| Fan | None | Never visible, and there is no fan-facing surface anywhere in this feature. Noted explicitly because the Fan is the *source* of the gross the whole sheet derives from — their ticket purchase (domain 19) is the count. They fund the arithmetic and never see it, which is correct: a settlement is a commercial document between two businesses. |

## Behavior

### 1. Signature binds to a computation, not to "the settlement"

A signature is taken against a **specific computation version** stamped by 17.09.01 — the same
version-binding insight as the offer approval chain (17.02.03 DT-02). Any event that changes the
computation between the two signatures invalidates every signature already taken against the
superseded version (CX-03). Modelling signoff as "they agreed the settlement" instead of "they
agreed *this exact computation*" reproduces the v2/v4 failure — band approves v2, agent accepts v4 —
inside the settlement room, where the money is already on the table.

Three events invalidate an existing signature:

1. A **recomputation** — a corrected count (17.09.02), a late expense (17.09.03), a merch line
   landing (17.09.04), or an amended contract term (17.04).
2. A **line dispute raised before finality** (17.09.06) — the disputed line is held out, the
   computation is therefore incomplete, and a signature against an incomplete computation is not an
   agreement to anything. (A dispute raised *after* finality does not invalidate; it opens the
   amendment path — protest-then-dispute is the expected sequence, 17.09.06 edge cases.)
3. A **scope change** — a merch line moving onto or off the sheet (CX-05).

Signature ordering is established by **when the platform received the signature**, never by the
signer's device clock. A phone in a venue basement at 1am, possibly across a tour's worth of
timezones, is not a trustworthy clock, and the ordering decides which signature survives an
invalidation.

### 2. Three outcomes, not two

| Outcome | What it means | Money | Finality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sign** | Agreed — the number and the derivation | Full computed payable moves | Yes, on the second signature |
| **Sign under protest** | Agreed to the **payment**, not to the **number** | Full computed payable moves | Yes — final for payment, permanently flagged |
| **Refuse** | Not agreed | **Undisputed floor** moves; the contested delta is held (D-09) | No — settlement stays open |

**Sign under protest is the one the industry actually uses.** At 1am, with a van to load and a drive
to Glasgow, a tour manager who disputes an expense line signs anyway to get the cash and argues next
week — and the objection is real and is often honoured later. A system offering only sign/refuse
forces that person to either lie (sign, agreeing) or blow up the night (refuse, no payment). It
would drive the most common contested outcome off-platform, which is precisely where the platform
loses the record it exists to keep.

A protest carries a mandatory **reason** (plain text, ≤ 2,000 characters, always displayed as text,
never as markup) and optionally names the specific lines objected to. A protest reason is **immutable** once
submitted — it is trail content (CX-04) — but the counterparty holds a permanent right of reply that
travels with it wherever it is displayed or exported (D-13).

### 3. Refusal pays the undisputed floor

Refusal blocks **finality**, not **payment**. Because disputes are line-scoped by design (17.09.06
D-02), the computation can be evaluated a second way: with every disputed line resolved **in the
counterparty's favour**. That evaluation is the **undisputed floor** — the amount neither party
contests — and it is payable immediately. The contested delta is held pending resolution.

This dissolves what looked like a binary product trade-off (Q-01, now D-09). "Refusal blocks
payment" would make the platform the enforcement mechanism of the very pressure it exists to
relieve — the artist who checks the number gets stranded. "Refusal doesn't block payment" would make
refusal a gesture with no consequence. Paying the floor is what commercial parties actually do
everywhere else: pay what isn't in dispute, argue about the rest.

Refusal is **symmetric**. An Operator refuses too — over a merch count, or an artist-side expense
claim — and the floor logic runs in the same direction: the Operator pays the floor of what they do
not dispute.

### 4. Variance is measured against the offer-time projection — not against anyone's model

The baseline is **the projection rendered into the offer** (17.02.01 / 17.03.02 happy path step 5) —
the only expectation both parties saw, at the moment they agreed the deal. It is **not** either
party's what-if model: modelling is private and speculative, nothing is stored on the deal
(17.03.02), and each side's model contains the *other side's* commercially confidential cost
assumptions. Measuring shared variance against a private model would leak the counterparty's costs
out of a feature designed to keep them private (DT-04).

Two variance surfaces, therefore:

| Surface | Baseline | Visible to |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared variance** | The offer-time rendered projection — deal lines only | Both parties, identically |
| **Private variance** | The party's own what-if model, with their own cost assumptions | That party only |

**A deal transacted off-platform has no projection and therefore no shared variance.** A paper
contract uploaded to 17.04 settles fine and explains nothing — which is a real consolidation
incentive, and exactly the thesis (D-18): being where the deal was made is the precondition for
knowing what it was supposed to pay.

### 5. Materiality gates the Operator's signature

A shared-variance line is **material** when it moves the artist's payable by **≥ 5% of the projected
artist payable** for that settlement. Two qualifications:

- **Direction matters.** Only variances that *reduce* the artist's payable are explanation-gated. A
  promoter does not have to justify why the artist did better than projected. The obligation exists
  to counter an asymmetry that runs one direction (domain index) — making it bidirectional would
  double the 1am friction and buy nothing.
- **Comps above the deal's allowance are always material**, at any value. "How many comps?" is the
  classic 1am argument (CX-07) and a 5% floor would let 40 unexplained comps through on a big night.

The Operator's **Sign** action is disabled until every material adverse line they own carries an
explanation. This is a direct application of the design principle `meta/personas.md` states for the
Producer's worst-accidental behaviour — **make the lazy path the correct path**. The Operator wants
their signature, because the signature is what moves the money and closes the show; gating it on the
explanation is the only lever that survives 1am. The platform cannot police whether an explanation is
*sincere* — but an explanation recorded permanently, shown to the counterparty, and exported into an
accountant's statement (17.09.07) is a materially different act from no explanation at all.

The 5% default is an **agent-chosen** starting value (Q-01). A deal may state its own materiality
threshold as a term (17.03.01), which overrides the default.

### 6. Signing is an entity act with its own authority rule

A settlement is signed by an **entity**, not a person — the band, not the drummer. The band's
governance rule lives in domain 01 and is consumed, not redefined here (the same boundary 17.02.03
D-03 draws). But **"who may approve an offer" and "who may sign a settlement" are different
questions with different clocks**, and collapsing them breaks in both directions:

- An offer's approval chain runs over 48 hours (17.02.03 CX-03). A settlement is signed in a car
  park in four minutes. A unanimous band rule applied to settlement deadlocks every time.
- Delegating settlement authority to whoever is present reintroduces the DT-01 failure at speed: the
  TM binds the band to a number nobody else saw.

Both are resolved by the same mechanism the industry already uses, and it is the one the sweep
would never have found: **sign-under-protest is what makes delegated settlement authority
survivable**. A TM with delegated authority can take the cash without signing away the band's
objection. Delegation without a protest outcome is a trap; delegation with one is just how touring
works.

So: domain 01 carries a **settlement-signing authority** slot alongside the offer-binding slot; one
authority model, two rules (D-06).

### 7. Finality fans out — the widest fan-out in the domain

Second signature → **Signed** → the following fire, and nothing fires before it:

| Consumer | What it gets | Under protest? |
|---|---|---|
| 17.10.02 Disbursement | The payable | Yes — the money moves |
| 17.08.02 Commission | The accrual basis | **Marked provisional** (D-08) |
| 17.11.01 Verified Draw Record | `admissions_paid` as a *verified* fact — the paid admissions quantity, never `sold` and never the all-bodies `admissions_total` (17.09.02 D-08, D-18) | Yes, unless self-dealt (D-07) |
| 17.12 Payment Reliability | Directed facts, not a score | Yes — see D-12 |
| 02 Credits & Attribution | Proof the gig happened | Yes, unless self-dealt (D-07) |

### Happy Path

1. Show plays. Counts arrive (17.09.02), expenses are captured (17.09.03), merch settles on its own
   grammar (17.09.04). 17.09.01 completes the computation and stamps it with a version.
2. System renders the derivation beside a **shared variance panel** against the offer-time
   projection — line by line: projected gross vs actual gross, projected deductions vs actual,
   projected artist payable vs computed. Each party separately sees their **private** variance
   against their own model; neither sees the other's.
3. System flags every adverse shared-variance line ≥ 5% of projected artist payable — plus any comps
   above the deal's allowance — as **requiring explanation**, attributed to the party who owns the
   input (counts and expenses are the Operator's, CX-01/CX-02).
4. Operator writes an explanation against each flagged line. Their **Sign** action unlocks only when
   none are outstanding.
5. Operator signs. Signature binds to the computation version, writes the trail (CX-04), and the
   artist is notified with the sheet, the variance panel and the explanations.
6. Artist reviews the derivation and the explanations, and chooses Sign / Sign under protest /
   Refuse. The action completes only once the platform confirms the signature is recorded — the
   signer is never shown a signature as taken before it is confirmed (see the connectivity edge
   case).
7. Artist signs. Second signature binds the same computation version. Settlement → **Signed**.
8. Finality fans out: disbursement (17.10.02), commission (17.08.02), verified draw record
   (17.11.01), reliability facts (17.12), and performance-credit evidence (domain 02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Party signs under protest** | The most common contested outcome. Money moves at the full computed payable; the objection is recorded permanently; settlement is final-for-payment and flagged | First-class outcome with a mandatory reason field, not a workaround (D-02) |
| **Both parties sign under protest** | Real, and not absurd: a number neither party agrees with that both accept to end the night. Final; doubly flagged; both objections preserved | Two protests displayed side by side; the strongest available signal that this deal's terms failed (17.12) |
| **Recomputation between the two signoffs** | Invalidates every signature bound to the superseded version (CX-03) — the v2/v4 failure in the settlement room | Prior signer re-notified with **a diff of what changed since the version they signed**, never the whole sheet again (D-11) |
| **Operator recomputes repeatedly after the artist signs** | The invalidation rule, weaponised: re-present a slightly worse number to a tired signer until they stop reading. **≥ 3 post-signature recomputations in one settlement** raises a flag on the settlement and emits a directed fact to 17.12 | Artist sees the count of times they have been asked to re-sign, and the cumulative diff from their **first** signature — not just the last one |
| **Artist refuses and the promoter has cash on the table** | Refusal blocks finality, not payment. The undisputed floor — the computation re-evaluated with every disputed line conceded to the Operator — is payable now; the delta is held (D-09) | "£X is not in dispute and is payable now. £Y is contested." Resolves Q-01 |
| **Operator refuses** (merch count, artist-side expense) | Symmetric — the floor logic runs the same way. The Operator pays the floor of what they do not dispute | Same surface, mirrored. The design is not artist-protection wearing a costume |
| **Everyone wants to leave; signing is the fastest exit** | The structural pressure that makes settlement asymmetric in the first place | Sign-under-protest is the honest release valve (DT-02); the platform does not pretend the pressure isn't there |
| **Nobody signs; the show is just over; cash moved** | **A supported state, not a failure.** Recorded as `Unsigned — payment asserted`, the same assertion pattern already ratified for cash balances (17.05.02 D-03) and disbursement (17.10.02 D-04). It produces **no** verified draw record and **no** payment-reliability fact | "This settlement was never signed. £X was recorded as paid, by assertion." Both parties can still sign later, from anywhere. Resolves Q-02 (D-10) |
| **Artist signs; a recompute invalidates it; the artist has left the building and never returns** | Stale partial signature. **Never silently expires into finality.** Reminder at 72h; at 30 days flagged `Stale — unsigned` and surfaced to both parties as an unresolved settlement | The settlement sits in both parties' outstanding lists indefinitely, with the outstanding signature named |
| **Amendment after both signed** | An amendment is a **new signoff against a new computation**, requiring the same signature set — never an edit (D-14). If either party refuses the amendment, the original stands and the disagreement escalates to 24 | Amendment presented as a fresh signoff with a diff against the signed version; all original signers re-notified |
| **Amendment after disbursement** | Clawback across parties who have spent it (17.10.02 Q-02); commission may already be gone (17.08.02 DT-02). The two-signature amendment rule bounds this: nothing can be clawed back that both parties did not re-sign | Obligation recorded in the trail; enforceability is genuinely uncertain and is presented as such, not as a resolved balance |
| **Show inside an open cross-collateralized run** | The payable cannot be final (17.03.03 DT-02). Signoff signs a **provisional** — a different act with different semantics: it binds the date's *inputs and derivation*, not an *amount* (D-03) | "You are agreeing this date's counts, expenses and arithmetic. The amount is provisional until the run closes." Resolves Q-03 |
| **Run closes; the aggregate payable is now final** | A **second, distinct signature**: sign-the-run. It binds the payable across every date. It does not re-open any date's inputs — those were bound date by date | Run-level signoff listing every date's already-bound derivation and the aggregate that falls out |
| **Artist signed a date provisionally, then the run's aggregate moves it downward** | Exactly the trust failure 17.03.03 D-02 names. The date signature stands (it bound inputs, not money); the run signature is where the artist agrees the moved number | Recomputation is an event, not a background job (17.03.03 D-03) — the artist is told the number moved, by how much, and which date caused it |
| **The artist and the Operator are the same human** (self-promoted show; musician who runs the room) | Two signatures from one human is **not** two signatures. Permitted — the entities are genuinely distinct and the record is legitimate — but marked **self-dealt**: it produces no verified draw record at full weight (17.11.01) and **no** payment-reliability fact (17.12) at all (D-07) | "Both sides of this settlement are the same party. This settlement is a record, not evidence." |
| **The counterparties are related but not identical** (artist's own label promotes the show; two entities, one controlling owner) | The same self-dealing hazard through one more hop. The platform detects what it can see (shared controlling entity in domain 01) and flags it; it cannot detect what it cannot see, and does not pretend to | Flagged the same way when detectable; the honest limit is stated rather than papered over |
| **Bill has a headliner and two supports** (17.14 D-01) | **Three settlements, three signature pairs, not one.** The supports' settled fees are an *expense input* to the headliner's computation — so a support's settlement must be signed before the headliner's can be final, or the headliner signs a number containing an unsettled deduction | Headliner sees the support-cost line's **amount** and that it is a settled artist fee — never the support's deal terms (DT-08) |
| **Headliner's derivation is therefore incomplete at the support-cost line** | A genuine tension with 17.09.01 D-02 ("the output is the derivation"). Commercial confidentiality wins: the headliner has no right to another act's deal | The line reads "support fees (2 acts, settled) — £X", explicitly marked as a summarised line, not silently rendered as if derived |
| **£50 local opener added on Tuesday for Friday** | The full signoff ceremony does not survive the scale (17.14 DT-03). Their settlement is the lighter path's settlement, and the headliner's expense line does not wait on it if it was never on-platform | Support-cost line shows what was captured; if the opener never entered the platform, the line is an Operator-asserted expense like any other (17.09.03) |
| **Merch settled in cash separately before the show settlement started** (CX-05) | The signature's **scope** must name what it covers. A show signature that silently swallows an uncovered merch line is how a promoter later waves a signature at a dispute it never touched | Signature scope is rendered explicitly: "This signature covers the show settlement. Merch was settled separately in cash and is not covered." |
| **No connectivity in the back room at 1am** | The moment the feature exists for is the moment it may not work. **The sign action reports success only once the platform confirms the signature is recorded — a signature is never shown as taken before then.** A signature the signer believes exists and the record does not have is worse than no signature, because both parties then act on it | Action fails visibly; state stays Unsigned; retry offered. **Never** a checkmark on an unconfirmed signature. Whether the act can be completed offline at all is index Q-02 (the mobile-surface question) |
| **Deal contains free-text terms outside the grammar** (17.09.01 Q-03) | The computation is silently incomplete, and signing an incomplete computation is signing a number that omits a term both parties agreed. Signoff must not present a complete-looking sheet over an incomplete computation | The sheet is marked `Incomplete` and signature is available only with an explicit acknowledgement that unmodelled terms exist and are not in the arithmetic |
| **Protest reason is abusive, defamatory, or names a third party** | Permanent, non-editable, counterparty-visible, and **exportable to an accountant, an agency and a tax authority** (17.09.07). It cannot be redacted — it is trail content — but it can be **answered** | Counterparty's right of reply travels with the protest everywhere it renders or exports (D-13). Abuse routes to domain 24, which owns the machinery |
| **Protest reason contains markup or a script payload** | Plain text only, always displayed as text on every render, ≤ 2,000 characters. Never interpreted as markup — `security-first` | Text renders as text. Over-length is rejected at entry with the count shown, not truncated silently |
| **Both parties sign at the same instant against the same computation** | Both valid. Order recorded by when the platform received each; finality is set at the later one | No conflict; both see Signed |
| **Both sign at the same instant against *different* computations** (a recompute landed between) | The signature bound to the superseded version is invalidated; the other stands. The invalidated signer is re-notified with a diff | "Your signature was taken against a version that has since changed" — never a silent drop |
| **Signature lands the same instant a line dispute is raised** | The dispute holds the line out; the computation is no longer complete; signatures against it invalidate. (After finality the same act is an amendment instead) | Signer told their signature was invalidated by a dispute, and which line |
| **The signing human leaves the band / the venue changes hands before an amendment** | Signatures bind **entities**. A successor authorised by the entity's rule (domain 01) may sign the amendment. The original signature's human is recorded permanently in the trail regardless | Amendment shows who signed originally and who is signing now — both facts are load-bearing |
| **Currency of the deal differs from the currency of a variance line** | FX on live money is unmodelled (17.10.02, 17.03.01 DT-03). Materiality compares within a single currency only; a cross-currency line cannot be assessed for materiality without a rate | Cross-currency lines are shown and **not** materiality-flagged, marked "not assessed — no rate". Silently applying a rate would fabricate a threshold result |
| **Artist protests every settlement, including clean ones** | The naive reliability derivation marks them difficult — the exact failure 17.12 Q-01 names. **A protest is a directed fact about the settlement, attributed to the party whose line was contested**, and becomes a negative about the Operator only if it is later upheld. An unresolved protest is a fact about neither party | The artist who checks every number is the behaviour the platform exists to enable. It is never a demerit (D-12) |
| **Settlement decided verbally in the room; the trail has a hole exactly where the decision happened** | The same capture hole as the offer thread (17.02.02 DT-03). The platform records what it saw. A verbal agreement followed by both signatures is captured *as the signature*; a verbal agreement followed by no signature is `Unsigned — payment asserted` | The honest limit is stated: the trail is evidence of what the system witnessed, not of what the room said |
| **Artist signs, then discovers a material variance was never flagged because it fell at 4.9%** | The threshold is a line, and lines have edges. An unflagged variance is still *rendered* — it is only the explanation gate it misses | Every variance line is visible regardless of materiality; materiality decides what must be *explained*, never what is *shown* |
| **Settlement has zero variance** (the show did exactly what was projected) | Nothing to explain; the Operator's sign action is unlocked immediately | Variance panel renders "as projected" against each line — the absence is itself worth seeing |

### States — Settlement Lifecycle

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unsigned | Computation complete and stamped | Derivation, variance panel, explanations; Sign / Sign under protest / Refuse |
| Explanation outstanding | Material adverse variance lines unexplained | Operator's Sign disabled, with each outstanding line named |
| Partially signed | One party signed | Whose signature is outstanding; the computation is held against changes |
| Signed | Both signed against the same computation version | Final. Fan-out fires (disbursement, commission, draw, reliability, credit) |
| Signed under protest | A party signed with a recorded objection | Final for payment; objection and right of reply preserved and visible in the trail and the statement |
| Refused | A party refused | Undisputed floor payable; contested delta held; settlement stays open |
| Invalidated | Recompute / dispute / scope change between signatures | Prior signature void; signers re-notified **with a diff**, not the sheet |
| Provisional (date-signed) | Show inside an open run; date's inputs bound | Inputs and derivation agreed; amount explicitly provisional until the run closes |
| Run-signed | Final date settled; aggregate bound | Per-date amounts final; disbursement unblocks |
| Unsigned — payment asserted | Money moved, nobody signed | Recorded, not verified. No draw record, no reliability fact. Still signable later |
| Stale — unsigned | Partially signed, 30 days elapsed | Surfaced to both parties as unresolved; never auto-finalised |
| Self-dealt | Both sides are the same or a related controlling entity | Signed and recorded; excluded from reliability, discounted for draw |
| Amended | New signoff against a new computation, signed by the same set | Diff against the previously signed version; clawback obligations recorded if money already moved |

### States — Surface

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Signoff opened; computation and projection being fetched | Skeleton of the derivation and the variance panel; **no signature control rendered until the computation version is confirmed** — a sign button over an unknown version is a signature over an unknown number |
| Empty | Show played, counts not yet in (17.09.01 `Awaiting inputs`) | "Nothing to sign yet" with the outstanding inputs named and who owes them |
| Populated | Computation stamped, projection present | Full derivation, shared variance panel, private variance, explanations, three outcome actions |
| Error | Sign action failed (network, conflict, invalidated version) | The specific cause, the state left unchanged (still Unsigned), and retry. **Never a checkmark.** A version conflict shows the diff rather than a generic failure |
| Partial | Deal was off-platform (no projection), or a line is cross-currency, or unmodellable free-text terms exist | The sheet renders with the gap named — "no offer projection: variance unavailable", "not assessed — no rate", "unmodelled terms present". Signature remains available with explicit acknowledgement; the gap is never hidden to make the page look finished |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Signoff is a confirmation step | The obvious UX reading — a button at the end of a computation | ❌ REJECTED: it is the event that **converts a proposal into a fact** (CX-03). The whole sub-domain's arithmetic produces a *number*; only signoff produces an *agreement*. Modelling it as a confirmation would make the computation authoritative on its own, which reverses the actual power relationship — the parties settle, the system computes. A system whose number is final without both signatures has not automated settlement; it has replaced one party's calculator with its own. |
| DT-02 | The outcomes are sign or refuse | Binary instinct | ❌ REJECTED: **"sign under protest" is what the industry actually does.** At 1am, with a van to load and a drive to Glasgow, a tour manager who disputes an expense line signs anyway to get the cash and argues next week — and the objection is real and is often honoured later. A system offering only sign/refuse forces that person to either lie (sign, agreeing) or blow up the night (refuse, no payment). It would drive the most common contested outcome off-platform, which is precisely where the platform loses the record it exists to keep. |
| DT-03 | Variance explanation is a reporting nicety | Its placement as a secondary half of the feature | ❌ REJECTED: variance is the **only feedback loop the domain has.** The deal was projected at offer time; the show happened; the delta is the learning — and it feeds three things: this relationship's trust (17.12), the draw record's accuracy (17.11.01), and the next offer's realism. Without it, every show is settled in isolation and the platform accumulates numbers without accumulating knowledge. `meta/problem-statement.md` says the value is in what compounds; variance is what makes settlement compound rather than merely repeat. |
| DT-04 | Variance is measured against "the modelled expectation" (17.03.02) | This file's own [SURFACE] draft said exactly that, and it is wrong | ❌ REJECTED — **the modeller is private and stores nothing.** 17.03.02 is explicit: "Modelling is private and speculative. Nothing is notified, nothing is stored on the deal", and each side models with **their own cost assumptions, which the other never sees**. Measuring a *shared* variance against a *private* model would either (a) have no baseline to read, or (b) leak the counterparty's confidential cost structure out of the one feature built to protect it. The only expectation both parties saw and agreed is **the projection rendered into the offer** (17.03.02 happy path step 5 / 17.02.01 D-02). That is the baseline. Private variance against a private model still exists — privately, per party. **This rejection also produces a thesis consequence**: an off-platform deal has no projection, so it settles without variance. The feedback loop is a consolidation reward, not a universal feature. |
| DT-05 | Whoever is standing there may sign for the band | How it works today, and the path of least resistance | ❌ REJECTED as modelled, ✅ CONFIRMED as a **new requirement the sub-domain does not have**. Domain 17 built an entire feature around "who may bind the band to a Tuesday in Leeds" (17.02.03) and then let anyone bind the band to a *number*. That is the same failure at the moment money is on the table. But the fix cannot be the offer's fix: an approval chain with a 48h clock does not run in a car park at 1am, and a unanimous rule deadlocks every settlement the band ever plays. The resolution is that **settlement signing is a second authority slot in the same domain-01 model** — one authority model (17.02.03 D-03), two rules — and that **sign-under-protest is precisely what makes delegated settlement authority survivable**: the TM can take the cash without signing away the band's objection. Delegation without a protest outcome is a trap; delegation with one is just how touring works. The sweep could not have found this, because it never modelled authority at all (17.02.03 is itself a Deep Think addition). |
| DT-06 | Both parties are always different parties | The premise embedded in every settlement feature in the sub-domain | ❌ REJECTED — **`meta/personas.md`'s central structural fact is the multi-hyphenate**, and the live case is ordinary: the musician who runs the room, the artist who self-promotes, the band whose own label books the tour. Then one human holds both signatures, and **two signatures from one human is not two signatures**. This is not merely awkward; it is an *attack*. The Musician's documented worst-intentional behaviour is "games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation" — and the live equivalent is settling shows with yourself to manufacture a verified draw record (17.11.01) and a payment-reliability history (17.12) out of nothing. A settlement is only evidence because two adverse parties agreed it. **Self-dealt settlements must be permitted (the record is legitimate) and excluded from the artefacts that derive their value from adversity.** Nobody designs for this because it looks like a rounding error; it is the cheapest available way to forge the thing the platform is staking its differentiation on. |
| DT-07 | The invalidation rule protects the artist | Its framing throughout this sub-domain — it exists to stop the v2/v4 failure | ❌ REJECTED as one-sided. **It is also a fatigue attack.** An Operator who recomputes after the artist signs forces a re-signature; do it four times, at 1am, against a tired tour manager, and the fourth number gets signed unread. The countermeasure is not to remove invalidation but to change what re-presentation *is*: the signer sees **the diff since the version they signed**, and on repeat, the **cumulative diff since their first signature** — never the whole sheet again. This is exactly 17.02.03's happy-path step 3 ("what changed since any version they previously approved"), which confirms the two features are the same mechanism and must not be solved twice. And the count itself is information: **≥ 3 post-signature recomputations in one settlement is a directed fact**, not a UI annoyance. |
| DT-08 | A settlement has two signers | Implicit everywhere in 17.09 | ❌ REJECTED at the show level: **a bill is 2–3 deals** (17.14 D-01), so a show is 2–3 settlements with 2–3 signature pairs. The consequence nobody has drawn: **the supports' settled fees are an expense input to the headliner's computation**, so a support's signature is a *precondition* of the headliner's finality — a cross-settlement dependency the sub-domain does not model. It also creates a real, unresolvable-by-preference tension with 17.09.01 D-02 ("the output is the derivation"): the headliner may see the support-cost *amount* and must never see the support's *deal terms*. Commercial confidentiality wins, and the honest consequence is that the headliner's derivation is **incomplete by design at exactly one line** — which must be marked as a summarised line rather than silently rendered as if it were derived. A derivation with an undisclosed hole that looks solid is worse than one that names its hole. |
| DT-09 | Whether refusal blocks payment is a genuine binary product trade-off | This file's own [SURFACE] draft raised it as Q-01 and deferred to the owner | ❌ REJECTED — **the binary is false and the sub-domain already contains its resolution.** Disputes are line-scoped by construction (17.09.06 D-02) specifically so contesting one comp count does not blow up the sheet. That property makes a second evaluation computable: resolve every disputed line **in the counterparty's favour** and you have the **undisputed floor** — the amount nobody contests. So refusal blocks *finality*, not *payment*: the floor moves now, the delta is held. Both horns of the original dilemma were bad — "blocks payment" makes the platform the enforcement arm of the 1am pressure it exists to relieve (refuse and get stranded), while "doesn't block" makes refusal theatre. Paying the undisputed portion is what commercial parties do everywhere else. This is derivation from a ratified decision, not a product choice — but it is the owner's to override, since it decides who holds the leverage at 1am. |
| DT-10 | An unsigned settlement is a failure state to be prevented | The instinct behind "both signatures are required" (D-03) | ❌ REJECTED: **it is the status quo, and it is the majority case at launch.** The show ends, the cash moves, nobody signs anything — 17.10.02 DT-03 already establishes that "the overwhelmingly normal live payment is cash, in a back room, at 1am", and both 17.05.02 D-03 and 17.10.02 D-04 already ratify **assertion** as the pattern for exactly this. Treating unsigned-but-paid as an error would mean the platform's record has a hole precisely where the money was, which *is* the reconstruction failure `meta/problem-statement.md` exists to end. Support it as `Unsigned — payment asserted`. The design lever is not prohibition but **what signing buys**: a signed settlement mints a verified draw record, a reliability fact and credit evidence; an asserted one mints none of them. Signing is how you get the compounding artefacts, which is the thesis (D-18) expressed as an incentive rather than a rule. |
| DT-11 | Signing a provisional and signing a final are the same signature | The convenient reading, and what a schema would default to | ❌ REJECTED: **they bind different things.** Inside an open cross-collateralized run the payable is a function of dates that have not happened (17.03.03 DT-02), so signing a date cannot possibly mean "I agree this amount". It means "I agree this date's count, expenses, merch and arithmetic" — it binds **inputs**. The run signature binds the **payable**. Two acts, two semantics. The valuable observation is which one is the provenance artefact: **the date signature is**, because the date's inputs are the perishable facts that exist only at 1am in that room, while the run's aggregate is just arithmetic over facts already captured. The run signature is a payment authorisation; the date signature is the thing no competitor can retroactively manufacture. This resolution also **survives either answer** to the domain's open grain question (17 Q-02 / index Q-01) — provisional-until-close and per-show-final-with-true-up both need exactly these two signature semantics — which is why an agent may settle it without preempting the owner. |
| DT-12 | An amendment is an edit to a signed settlement | The word "amendment", and how the [SURFACE] draft's edge case read | ❌ REJECTED: an amendment is **a new signoff against a new computation, requiring the same signature set**. Anything less is a hole in the two-signature rule (D-03) wide enough to drive the entire asymmetry back through: if one party can amend a signed number unilaterally, then "both signatures required" was decoration. This also bounds the sub-domain's hardest downstream problem — nothing can be clawed back from a third party (17.08.02 DT-02) or from five recipients who have spent it (17.10.02 Q-02) **that both principals did not re-sign**. It does not make clawback enforceable; it makes it *agreed*, which is the only thing this feature can honestly contribute to it. If either party refuses the amendment, the original stands and the disagreement is 24's. |
| DT-13 | A protest reason is a free-text note | Its obvious shape — a textarea next to a button | ❌ REJECTED: it is **permanent, non-editable, counterparty-visible, and exportable** into the artefact that reaches an accountant, an agency and a tax authority (17.09.07, CX-06). That is a defamation and abuse surface with a longer reach than any UGC field in the domain, and it is created by a design decision (D-02) that is otherwise entirely good. It cannot be redacted — it is trail content, and an editable trail is not a trail (17.09.06 D-01). So the countermeasure has to be additive rather than subtractive: **a permanent right of reply that travels with the protest wherever it renders or exports.** Abuse routes to 24, which owns the machinery. The general lesson the sub-domain should absorb: every immutable field visible to an adversary needs a reply channel, or immutability becomes a weapon. |

| D-01 | Signoff binds to a specific **computation version**, not to "the settlement" | A recomputation between signatures otherwise reproduces the v2/v4 failure in the settlement room (CX-03, 17.02.03 DT-02). Ordering is by when the platform received the signature, never the signer's device clock — a phone in a venue basement is not a trustworthy clock. |

| D-04 | Variance is a feedback loop, not a report | It is what makes settlement compound into draw intelligence and reliability rather than repeat (DT-03). |
| D-05 | The Operator's signature is **gated** on explaining every material adverse variance line they own | The only lever that survives 1am is the one attached to the thing the Operator wants (their signature → the money → the close). Direct application of `meta/personas.md`'s "make the lazy path the correct path". Default materiality: **≥ 5% of projected artist payable**, adverse direction only; **comps above the deal's allowance are always material** (CX-07). Threshold calibration is Q-01. |
| D-06 | The shared variance baseline is the **offer-time rendered projection**, never a private what-if model | The modeller stores nothing and holds the counterparty's confidential costs (17.03.02, DT-04). | D-07 | Settlement signing is a **second authority slot in domain 01's existing model** — one authority model, two rules | 17.02.03 D-03 established the boundary; a 48h approval chain cannot run in a car park (DT-05). Delegated settlement authority is only survivable **because** sign-under-protest exists — the TM takes the cash without signing away the band's objection. |
| D-08 | **Self-dealt settlements are permitted and flagged**, and excluded from the artefacts that derive value from adversity | Two signatures from one human is not two signatures (DT-06). Permitted because the record is legitimate; excluded from reliability entirely and discounted for draw, because a settlement is evidence only when two adverse parties agreed it. This closes the cheapest available forgery of the platform's central claim. |
| D-09 | **Refusal blocks finality, not payment** — the undisputed floor is payable immediately | Derivable from line-scoped disputes (17.09.06 D-02): re-evaluate with disputed lines conceded to the counterparty and the floor falls out (DT-09). | D-10 | **`Unsigned — payment asserted` is a supported state**, not an error | Cash at 1am is the norm (17.10.02 DT-03) and assertion is already the ratified pattern (17.05.02 D-03, 17.10.02 D-04). | D-12 | Reliability facts emitted here are **directed and conditional**, never a "contests a lot" tally | 17.12 Q-01 warns the naive derivation punishes the artist who checks — the exact behaviour the platform exists to enable. | D-13 | A protest reason is **immutable but answerable** — a permanent right of reply travels with it | It is permanent, counterparty-visible and exportable to an accountant, an agency and a tax authority (DT-13). It cannot be redacted (17.09.06 D-01) so the countermeasure must be additive. Plain text, ≤ 2,000 chars, always displayed as text, never as markup (`security-first`). |
| D-14 | An **amendment is a new signoff**, requiring the same signature set — never a unilateral edit | Otherwise "both signatures required" is decoration (DT-12). It bounds clawback: nothing can be clawed back that both principals did not re-sign. Refusal of an amendment leaves the original standing and escalates to 24. |
| D-15 | The signature's **scope is rendered explicitly**, naming what it does and does not cover | Merch settling as separate cash (CX-05, 17.09.04 D-01) means a show signature that silently swallows it becomes a weapon in a later dispute. |
| D-16 | The sign action **reports success only once the platform confirms the signature is recorded** — a completed signature is never shown before then | A signature the signer believes exists and the record does not have is worse than none, because both parties then act on it. At 1am in a basement this is the likely failure, not the unlikely one. |
| D-17 | On a bill, the **headliner sees the support-cost amount, never the support's deal terms** | Commercial confidentiality wins over derivation completeness (DT-08). The honest cost is that the headliner's derivation has one designed hole — which must be marked as a summarised line, because a derivation with an undisclosed hole is worse than one that names it. |
