# Feature: Box Office Count Reconciliation

> **Number**: 17.09.02
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Establishing how many people actually paid — reconciling the manifest, the sold count, the comps, the
holds, the guest list and the walk-ups into the single number the deal formula multiplies by. "How
many comps?" is the classic 1am argument, and this feature exists to make it answerable rather than
a contest of insistence.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The artist's right to check the count is the whole point of this feature.** Historically they were handed a number and had to take it. Here they see the count broken into its parts — manifest, sold, comps against the deal's comp allowance, holds released or not, walk-ups — and can contest any part (17.09.06). |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Supplies the count from their box office. When the platform *is* the box office (domain 19), they supply nothing — the count is already a platform fact, and the asymmetry collapses. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this — but the Fan **is** the count. Every line in this feature is an aggregate of individual fans buying, being comped, or not turning up. |

## Behavior

The count is not one number. It is a reconciliation of several, and the argument is always about the
gap between them:

- **Manifest** — how many tickets existed to sell.
- **Sold** — how many were paid for, at which scale/price tier.
- **Comps** — free tickets, against the deal's comp allowance. A comp beyond the allowance is a
  ticket the artist arguably paid for.
- **Holds** — kill dates, production holds, unreleased inventory. An unreleased hold is capacity that
  never had a chance to sell.
- **Walk-ups** — cash at the door, the least auditable money in live music.
- **Drop count / scan count** — who actually came through the door, which is *not* the paid count and
  matters for the draw record (17.11.01), not the money.

The deal's grammar (17.03.01) must reference these precisely — a bonus threshold that says "sellout"
means nothing until it names which of these numbers it counts.

### Happy Path

1. Show plays; the box office closes.
2. Count arrives — from domain 19 as a platform fact, or from the Operator as an assertion.
3. System renders the reconciliation: manifest → sold → comps (against allowance) → holds → walk-ups.
4. Artist checks it; the comp count is checked against what the deal allowed.
5. Reconciled count feeds the formula (17.09.01) and, separately, the draw record (17.11.01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Comps exceed the deal's allowance | The excess is capacity the artist arguably paid for — the classic argument | Excess shown explicitly against the allowance, not buried in a total |
| Walk-up cash at the door | The least auditable money in live music; the platform sees an assertion | Recordable as an assertion; flagged as unverified |
| Platform is not the box office | The count is asserted by the party who benefits from it (17.09.01 DT-03) | Provenance of the count is shown — platform fact or Operator assertion |
| Scan count differs materially from paid count | Normal (people don't turn up) and it matters — for draw, not for money | Both surfaced, clearly distinguished by purpose |
| Count corrected after settlement signed | Recomputes everything, including disbursed commission (17.08.02 DT-02) | Amendment event, never silent |
| Unreleased holds reduced sellable capacity | Capacity that never had a chance to sell — invisible in a sold/manifest ratio | Holds shown as a distinct line, not netted into manifest |
| Multi-tier scaling (£15 advance, £18 door, £40 VIP) | Gross is not count × price; it is a sum over tiers | Grammar must reference tiers (17.03.01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Awaiting | Show played, box office not closed | Skeleton with the deal's comp allowance shown |
| Asserted | Operator supplied the count | Reconciliation with an explicit "supplied by Operator" provenance |
| Verified | Count is a platform fact (domain 19) | Reconciliation with platform provenance; no assertion |
| Reconciled | Artist checked, no contest | Feeds the formula |
| Contested | A line disputed | Routed via 17.09.06; the formula waits |
| Corrected | Count amended | Recomputation event; downstream notified |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The count is a number the box office supplies | The obvious model, and the one the sweep's candidate implies | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **reconciliation of at least six numbers** — manifest, sold, comps, holds, walk-ups, scans — and the argument is always about the gap between them, never about the headline. A model with one `attendance` field cannot represent "you comped 90 people against a 20-comp allowance", which is the single most common settlement dispute in live music. |
| DT-02 | Paid count and attendance are the same thing | Casual conflation everyone makes, including in the domain index's phrase "settlement computed from deal + counts" | ❌ REJECTED: **paid count drives the money; scan count drives the draw record** (17.11.01). They routinely differ by 10-20% and they answer different questions. Conflating them would either corrupt the settlement (paying on people who didn't come) or corrupt the draw intelligence (recording a sellout as the number who showed up in the rain). Two numbers, two purposes — and the sweep's language suggests one. |
| DT-03 | The comp allowance protects the artist | The plain reading — the deal caps how many freebies the promoter can give away | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the inversion is the interesting part.** Comps cut both ways: the *promoter* comps to paper a weak house (which costs the artist on a door deal), and the *artist* comps their own guest list (which costs the promoter). The allowance is a mutual constraint, and a domain expert would model **whose comps** as well as how many. A single comp counter with one allowance would let the artist's 30 guests eat the promoter's 20-comp budget — or vice versa — and produce a number both sides reject. Nothing in the sweep suggests comp attribution was considered. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The count is a reconciliation of several numbers, never one field | A single `attendance` field cannot express the argument that actually happens (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Paid count and scan count are distinct, with distinct purposes | Money vs draw; conflating them corrupts one or the other (DT-02). |
| D-03 | Comps are attributed to whoever issued them | A single comp counter lets one side eat the other's allowance and produces a number both reject (DT-03). |
| D-04 | The count's provenance is always visible — platform fact or Operator assertion | The artist has a right to know whether the number was verified or asserted. |
