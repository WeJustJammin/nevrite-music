# Feature: Box Office Count Reconciliation

> **Number**: 17.09.02
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Establishing how many people actually paid — reconciling the manifest, the sold count, the comps, the
holds, the guest list and the walk-ups into the numbers the deal formula multiplies by. "How many
comps?" is the classic 1am argument, and this feature exists to make it **answerable rather than a
contest of insistence**.

The problem it solves is not arithmetic. It is that the count is supplied by the party the count pays
out, and the artist has historically been handed a total and had to take it. This feature's product is
the **decomposition and its provenance**: every constituent number shown separately, each labelled with
how the platform came to know it, and every arithmetic identity between them checked. An artist who can
see that sold + comps + unsold + killed does not equal the manifest does not need to be more insistent
than the promoter — they need only point at the line that does not balance.

Per `meta/problem-statement.md`, this is provenance applied to live money: the count is a fact that is
true for exactly one night, and is otherwise reconstructed months later from a spreadsheet held by one
of the two interested parties.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The artist's right to check the count is the whole point of this feature.** They see all six numbers separately (never a total), each with its provenance tier (D-06); their own guest list submissions (19.03.01) reconciled against the comps attributed to them; the promoter's comps against the promoter's allowance; every failed invariant named; and the money-material delta of each. They may contest any line (17.09.06) and may **not** edit any number they do not own — including their own guest list, which is a 19 fact by the time it lands here. They cannot see fan-level rows (D-11). |
| Producer | None | No role. Live settlement has no Producer surface. A Producer who books the room is acting as an **Operator**; one who plays the show is acting as a **Musician** (`meta/personas.md` — personas are lenses on behaviour). |
| Operator | Full | Supplies the count from their box office — decomposed, never as a total (D-09), and never as a figure the platform will accept without running the invariants against it. Attaches evidence where the platform is not the box office (D-06, D-07). Responds to contested lines. When the platform **is** the box office (domain 19), they supply nothing: the count is already a platform fact, the close is certified in the room (19.05.05), and the asymmetry collapses. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this — but the Fan **is** the count. Every line here is an aggregate of individual fans buying, being comped, or not turning up. Their transaction-level data is deliberately never exposed to either principal (D-11) — it is theirs, it is consented under 19.10, and neither party to the deal has a basis for it. |

## Behavior

### The count is six numbers, not one

The argument is always about the gap between them, never about the headline:

| # | Number | What it is | Who consumes it |
|---|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Manifest** | How many units existed at all — the room's ticketed capacity for this show | The denominator of every ratio; capacity claims |
| 2 | **Sold (paid)** | Units paid for, **decomposed by price tier and fee component** (D-09) | The deal formula (17.09.01) — this and only this drives money |
| 3 | **Comps** | Units issued free, **attributed to the issuer** (D-03), against **that issuer's allowance** (D-05) | The allowance check; excess comps are chargeable to whoever issued them |
| 4 | **Holds / kills** | Production holds, unreleased inventory, killed units — each with the timestamp it was released or killed (D-12) | Sellable capacity; every "sellout" threshold's denominator |
| 5 | **Admissions — paid** (`admissions_paid`) | Paying customers who were admitted — the paid-only projection of the admissions total (D-17) | The **draw record** (17.11.01) — see DT-07 |
| 6 | **Admissions — total** (`admissions_total`) | Every body admitted, paid or comped — **derived**: gate-observed `scanned` net of reversals, plus window `admitted` net of un-admissions (D-17) | Merch per-head (17.09.04); occupancy and safety |

Walk-ups are not a seventh number — they are **sold units acquired at the window on the night**
(19.05.04), and they matter here only because they are the sold units the platform is least likely to
have observed. They are decomposed out of `sold` for that reason alone.

**`scanned` is not one of the six, and it is not row 6.** It names the **gate-observed** admissions
only — what the platform's own scanners saw — because a walk-up buyer is admitted at the window by the
selling device and never scans (19.05.04 D-01). Rows 5 and 6 are the canonical admissions quantities
and both are derived from the gate counter `scanned` and the window counter `admitted`, which domain 19
stores separately and totals derived (19.05.01 D-09, D-17 here). This feature consumes the derived
quantities and reaches for a component only where an invariant is **explicitly gate-scoped** — INV-06,
and only INV-06 (D-18).

### The invariants — this is the feature

Every count, at every provenance tier, is checked against arithmetic identities that hold regardless of
who supplied it. A failed invariant is not a rejection; it is a **named, attributed, unresolvable-by-
insistence fact** that both parties can see:

| # | Invariant | What a failure means |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| INV-01 | `sold + comps + unsold + killed + held = manifest` | The ledger does not balance. Units are unaccounted for; a number is wrong or the manifest moved. |
| INV-02 | `admissions_total ≤ sold + comps` | More people came in than tickets existed. Backdoor admissions, double-scans, pass-out re-entry, or an understated sold count. |
| INV-03 | `admissions_paid ≤ sold` | Structurally impossible otherwise — **at close**. After close it can fail honestly: a post-scan online refund reduces `sold` and, being exit-scoped, leaves the admission standing (D-19). |
| INV-04 | `admissions_total − admissions_paid ≤ comps` — the comp-only complement of the total | As INV-03, for comps. Stated as the complement rather than as a new name: the admissions vocabulary is authored once, in 19.05.01 D-09, and it names the paid projection only. |
| INV-05 | `comps_issued_by(party) ≤ comp_allowance(party)` — per issuer, per D-05 | Excess comps. **Not an error** — a chargeable quantity against the party that issued them. |
| INV-06 | `walk_ups_asserted ≥ gate_admissions_without_platform_ticket` — **gate-scoped**, against `scanned` alone and never against `admissions_total` (D-18) | The Operator asserts fewer walk-ups than the platform's own scanners admitted people it cannot match to a ticket. The assertion is contradicted by platform-observed fact (DT-06). Run against the derived total instead, the check would compare the assertion with a quantity that already contains the platform's own window admissions — a number against a component of itself (19.07.03 D-07). |
| INV-07 | `sold ≤ manifest − comps − held` at the moment of sale | Oversold. Real, and it becomes a refund liability, not a settlement number. |
| INV-08 | `occupancy_count ≥ admissions_total` where a statutory occupancy count exists | The door clicker and the scanner disagree. The clicker is the legally-operative number for capacity; the derived admissions total is the commercially-operative one — and it is the right side of this comparison precisely because it counts bodies admitted at the window as well as at the gate. |

### Materiality — computed, never a threshold

A discrepancy is **money-material** if re-evaluating the deal expression (17.09.01) with the corrected
number yields a different figure. It is **integrity-material** if it fails an invariant. Both are
binary and computed — the feature invents no percentage threshold, because the deal already defines
what the money is sensitive to. A 40-unit `admissions_paid`/`sold` gap on a flat guarantee is money-immaterial and
draw-material; the same gap on a door deal moves four figures. The deal decides, not a constant.

### Provenance is a ladder, not a binary — resolves Q-02

Every number carries the tier at which the platform knows it. The tier is per-number, not per-count: a
show can be T4 on comps and T0 on walk-ups, and the reconciliation must show that seam rather than
average it away.

| Tier | Name | What the platform actually knows | What the artist gets |
|------|------|----------------------------------|---------------------|
| **T0** | Unevidenced assertion | An Operator typed a number | The number, labelled as typed by the counterparty, plus every invariant it fails |
| **T1** | Evidenced assertion | Typed, with the box office's own statement attached | The document alongside the number. Evidence of what they claim, not of what happened — but it is what an auditor would ask for and it is signed by someone |
| **T2** | Third-party attested | Pulled from an external ticketing system (19.07) or an agency drop report (19.05.02) | A number a party other than the Operator produced — while noting the Operator configures the integration and the agency is the Operator's vendor (DT-12) |
| **T3** | Platform-observed, partial | The platform issued some units, or scanned the door, but did not own the whole box office | The observed portion as fact; the rest as assertion; the seam named explicitly |
| **T4** | Platform fact | Every unit issued by the platform, the door scanned by the platform, the box office closed and certified on-platform (19.05.05) | No assertion remains. The asymmetry has collapsed |

**The critical asymmetry in that ladder**: T3 is reachable by scanning the door alone, without
replacing the venue's ticketing (DT-06). That is a materially smaller ask of the Operator and it buys
INV-02, INV-04 and INV-06 — the checks that catch the most common count manipulations. This is the
feature's answer to domain Q-04 and 17.09.01 Q-01, and it is escalated because it changes what domain
19 must own for domain 17's claim to hold.

### What the feature never does

- It never produces a number nobody asserted. Where two sources disagree, it shows both and names the
  gap; it does not average, prefer, or adjudicate (D-10, DT-11).
- It never nets. Holds are not folded into the manifest; comps are not folded into sold; the
  promoter's comps are not folded into the artist's. Every netting is an argument someone lost silently.
- It never shows a fan-level row to either principal (D-11).

### Happy Path

1. Show plays. Doors close. The box office closes (19.05.05) — the window stops selling, scanners
   reconcile, counters freeze, and the close is certified in the room by the human who ran it.
2. The count arrives, decomposed: manifest, sold by price tier with fee components, comps attributed
   per issuer, holds and kills with their release timestamps, and admissions — the derived
   `admissions_total`, its paid projection `admissions_paid`, and, beneath both, the gate (`scanned`)
   and window (`admitted`) components it is composed from (19.05.01 D-09). From domain 19 it arrives as
   a platform fact (T4); from an Operator's own box office it arrives as an assertion (T0–T2) with
   whatever evidence they attach.
3. System runs INV-01..INV-08 and stamps a provenance tier onto **each** number.
4. System renders the reconciliation: six numbers, never a total; each with its tier; every failed
   invariant named with its money-material delta computed against the deal expression.
5. Artist reads it. The comp lines are shown against **their** allowance and **the promoter's**
   allowance separately, each with the excess broken out and priced (excess × face value × the
   artist's split percentage — the same arithmetic 19.03.02 already shows them before the deadline).
6. Artist accepts the reconciliation, or contests a specific line (17.09.06 — line-scoped, never
   sheet-scoped).
7. On acceptance the count snapshot is **versioned and frozen**. Settlement binds to that version
   (D-14), exactly as signoff binds to a specific computation (17.09.05 D-01).
8. The frozen snapshot fans out: `sold` by tier → the formula (17.09.01, CX-01); `admissions_paid` →
   the draw record (17.11.01, DT-07); `admissions_total` → merch per-head (17.09.04); the whole
   snapshot → the audit trail (17.09.06, CX-07).
9. If the bill carried more than one act (17.14), step 8 fires once per act's settlement — one count,
   N settlements (DT-08).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Comps exceed an issuer's allowance | INV-05 fails. Not an error — the excess is a chargeable quantity attributed to the party that issued it | Excess shown against **that party's** allowance, priced at face × split, never buried in a comp total |
| Artist's guest list eats the promoter's comp budget | The failure a single comp counter guarantees (DT-03) | Structurally impossible — allowances are per-issuer (D-05) and every comp carries its issuer from 19.03.02 |
| Promoter papers a weak house with 150 comps, 90 turn up | Costs the artist on a door deal, and — worse — inflates `admissions_total` | Comps priced against the promoter's allowance; `admissions_paid` unaffected, so the draw record is not inflated (DT-07) |
| Walk-up cash at the door, no evidence | The least auditable money in live music. T0 | Labelled unevidenced; INV-06 run against the scan differential if the platform scanned the door |
| Walk-up assertion is **below** the scan differential | The Operator's own claim is contradicted by the platform's scanners: more people were admitted without a matchable ticket than they say they sold at the window | INV-06 fails with the exact shortfall and its money-material delta. **This is the check that makes door-scanning worth more than it costs** (DT-06) |
| Platform scanned the door but the venue also ran its own list | The platform's scan data is partial; the differential is a lower bound, not a proof | T3 with the seam named — "the platform observed N of M admissions"; INV-06 runs on the observed portion only |
| Platform is not the box office at all | The count is asserted by the party who benefits from it (17.09.01 DT-03) | T0/T1 on every number, with every invariant that can still be run, run |
| External ticketing integration supplies the count (19.07) | Better than typed; not a platform fact — the Operator configures the integration | T2, explicitly labelled third-party attested (DT-12) |
| `admissions_total` exceeds sold + comps | INV-02 fails. Backdoor admissions, double-scans, pass-out re-entry, or an understated sold count | Named as an integrity failure with the exact overage, and decomposed into its gate and window halves so the parties can see which side of the room it came from; **the platform does not guess which cause** (D-10) |
| `admissions_paid` materially below sold | Normal — people don't turn up, and it rains | Both surfaced, clearly distinguished by purpose. Money-immaterial by construction; draw-material |
| Admissions count differs materially from sold count | Normal and it matters — for draw, not for money | Three numbers with three consumers named (DT-07) |
| Pass-outs / re-entry double-counted | Inflates `admissions_total` through its gate component, which inflates merch per-head and can breach INV-02 | Re-entries identified by 19.04's scan log where scanning is on-platform; where it is not, INV-02's failure is shown without a cause attributed |
| Unreleased holds reduced sellable capacity | Capacity that never had a chance to sell — invisible in a sold/manifest ratio | Holds shown as a distinct line with their release timestamps, **never netted into the manifest** (D-12) |
| Holds released so late they could not sell | Sellable capacity was constructively lower than the manifest; a percentage deal's ceiling was lowered by a decision the artist never saw | Release timestamps shown against the on-sale timeline — the artist can see when capacity became available (D-12) |
| "Sellout" bonus threshold does not name its denominator | Manifest or sellable? The gap between them is the promoter's holds, which the promoter controls (DT-05) | The grammar must name it (17.03.01). An unnamed denominator is flagged as unevaluable, not silently resolved |
| Multi-tier scaling (£15 advance, £18 door, £40 VIP) | Gross is not count × price; it is a sum over tiers | Sold decomposed per tier (D-09); the grammar references tiers (17.03.01) |
| VAT / sales tax / amusement tax inside the face price | UK ticket prices are VAT-inclusive at 20%; US municipal amusement taxes vary by city. Settlement gross is conventionally **net** of them. Settling on the tax-inclusive number misstates the money by the tax rate | Fee and tax components decomposed per tier; the deal's gross definition selects which components it includes (D-09, DT-04) |
| Facility fee or ticketing fee paid to an entity the promoter owns | Real, and the classic related-party extraction: "gross" is reported net of a fee the promoter collects | Every fee component shown with its recipient. The platform does not judge it; it makes it visible (DT-04) |
| Currency of the sale differs from the settlement currency | A €-denominated show settled in £; the rate and its date are terms nobody writes down | Sold decomposed in the currency of sale; conversion is the deal grammar's problem (17.03.01 DT-03), not this feature's |
| Count corrected after settlement was signed | Recomputes everything downstream, including commission that may already be disbursed (17.08.02 DT-02) | Amendment event, never silent. New count version; all signers re-notified; the trail records it (17.09.06) |
| A ticket is refunded after settlement was signed | The sold count was correct at close and is wrong now. 19.06 owns the refund; this feature owns what it does to a frozen number | Amendment against the frozen version, with the money-material delta computed |
| An advance ticket is refunded online **after its holder was scanned in** | Exit-scoped (D-19): `sold` falls, admissions do not — that person was in the room and the night's occupancy is a fact about bodies, not about who kept their money | `sold` amended; `admissions_total` and `admissions_paid` unmoved. INV-03 can fail as a consequence, and that failure is **content, not a fault** (D-15): a body was admitted on a ticket that has since been reversed, which is a true statement about the night |
| A walk-up is refunded at the window minutes after being admitted | Exit-scoped the other way (D-19): the buyer either never entered or has left, so the window un-admission decrements `admitted` and the derived totals fall with it (19.05.04 D-11, 19.05.01 D-11) | `sold` and both admissions figures fall together. Occupancy, merch per-head and the draw all read a room that no longer contains that body — and the walk-up skim signal 19.05.04 depends on stays honest |
| A chargeback lands 90 days after settlement | The money left the platform on a number that has now changed. Card scheme dispute windows run to ~120 days for the common reason codes | **Q-01 — who bears it is unresolved.** The count amends regardless; the liability does not follow automatically (DT-05) |
| Count amended while the sheet is being signed | Real at 1am (CX-01 Q5) | Signoff binds to a count version (D-14); an amendment invalidates in-flight signatures exactly as a recomputation does (17.09.05 D-01) |
| Two Operator staff submit different counts | Concurrency at the window and the office | Last write does not win. Both assertions are retained, the conflict is named, and neither is authoritative until the Operator resolves it — the platform will not pick (D-10) |
| One act on a multi-act bill contests the count; the others want their money | The dispute is cross-sheet, not line-scoped — 17.09.06 D-02 scopes disputes within a sheet and cannot reach this | A contested count freezes only the contesting act's settlement; the others settle on the disputed version and inherit an amendment if it moves (DT-08). ✅ **The escalation this row carried has landed as this file's D-13** — "A count correction fans out to **every** settlement on the bill; a count dispute freezes only the contesting act's sheet" — and D-16 (owner decision DQ-04.04) confirms it: "on conflicting box-office evidence, the count stays **provisional and undisputed portions settle** — the platform never picks a winner and never adjudicates." Nothing is outstanding |
| Self-promoted show — the Musician **is** the Operator | The two-party check collapses: one person checks their own count. Extremely common at club level, and it is the multi-hyphenate acting in two roles at once | Provenance tier records that no counter-party checked. The reconciliation still runs, and it still has value — for the artist's own tax record, their draw record, and the fans' evidence — but it is labelled self-asserted-unchecked (DT-09) |
| Free / RSVP show — no paid count at all (19.11) | `sold = 0` is a valid count, not an empty state. There is a manifest, comps, and admissions | Reconciliation renders with sold explicitly zero. The deal formula, if any, evaluates on the numbers that exist |
| Cash float discrepancy at close | The window's takings do not match the units it issued | Surfaced from 19.05.05 as a named exception against walk-up, not netted into sold |
| No connectivity in the back room at 1am | The moment the feature exists for is the moment it may not work | **Sub-domain Q-02 / domain Q-09** — the strongest driver of the open mobile-surface question in `meta/constraints.md` |
| Operator inflates comps on a door deal | Each comp is a unit that could not be sold; on 85% of net it costs the artist directly | Caught by INV-05 against the promoter's allowance — **only if allowances are per-issuer** (D-05). A single allowance makes this indistinguishable from the artist's own guest list |
| Operator understates walk-ups and pockets the cash | The oldest fraud in live music | Caught by INV-06 **only where the platform scanned the door.** Where it did not, the platform can label the assertion and nothing more — and saying so plainly is more honest than implying a check that does not exist |
| Artist claims phantom walk-ups to raise their percentage | The mirror fraud, and the reason the feature is not built as an artist tool | INV-06 is a floor, not a ceiling; an artist's claim above the differential is an assertion like any other, labelled as such |
| Artist demands the box office's raw transaction ledger | The intuitive transparency ask, and the wrong one | Refused. Aggregates decomposed, never fan-level rows — the data is the Fan's, consented under 19.10 (D-11, DT-10) |
| Oversold — sold exceeds sellable capacity | INV-07 fails. It is a refund liability (19.06), not a settlement number | Named as an integrity failure; the excess does not silently become gross |
| Count arrives before the box office actually closed | The number is a live observation (19.05.01), not a close — and the admissions figures are **derived**, so they are only as fresh as their **stalest** component: a scanner still reconciling holds `admissions_total` behind real time even while the window is live (19.05.01 D-10, D-17) | Rejected as a settlement input. Reconciliation requires a certified close (19.05.05) or an explicit T0 assertion that the Operator states is final. Where a derived figure is displayed before close it carries its stalest input's as-of age, never a fresher one |
| Platform ran the window but not the gate | The inverse of the door-scanning case: window admissions are a platform fact, every advance admission is unobserved. `admissions_total` is derived from one observed component and one absent one | T3 with the seam named the other way round — the platform observed the walk-up admissions and none of the gate. INV-06 is moot rather than failing: the walk-up is a platform fact here, not an assertion needing a floor |
| Show cancelled mid-set; partial performance | A count exists; whether a deal does is 17.05's problem | The reconciliation renders and freezes regardless — the count is a fact about the night even when the money is not yet a fact |
| Comp voided after the snapshot froze | Inventory returned to sale after the count was frozen | Amendment against the frozen version, like any other correction — never a silent mutation of a signed number |

### States

**UI states** (per the template):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Reconciliation opened; numbers being assembled from 19 | Skeleton showing the deal's comp allowances (per issuer) and the manifest — the two facts known before the show |
| Empty | Show played; box office not closed; no count supplied | Not a blank page: the deal's allowances, the manifest, and an explicit statement of which numbers are outstanding and who owes them |
| Populated | All six numbers present; invariants run | Full decomposition, per-number provenance tiers, invariant results, money-material deltas |
| Error | An invariant failed, or two sources conflict | The failure named, attributed, and priced. **Never a generic error** — a failed invariant is content, not a fault |
| Partial | Some numbers T3/T4 and others T0 — e.g. advance on-platform, walk-up at the venue's window | Both, with the seam named explicitly. The reconciliation is usable; it is simply honest about which half is fact |

**Lifecycle states** (the count's own machine):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Awaiting | Show played, box office not closed | Skeleton with per-issuer comp allowances shown |
| Asserted | Operator supplied the count | Reconciliation with per-number provenance T0–T2 and every runnable invariant run |
| Observed | Platform saw part of it — scanned the door, or issued some units | T3, with the observed/asserted seam named |
| Verified | Count is a platform fact end-to-end (19.05.05 certified close) | T4. No assertion remains |
| Reconciled | Artist accepted; snapshot **versioned and frozen** | The frozen version; settlement binds to it (D-14) |
| Contested | A line disputed | Routed via 17.09.06; the formula waits; on a multi-act bill only the contesting act's sheet waits (DT-08) |
| Amended | Count corrected after freeze — correction, refund, chargeback, void | New version. Recomputation event; every signer and every downstream consumer notified; the trail records who changed which number (17.09.06) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The count is a number the box office supplies | The obvious model, and the one the sweep's candidate implies | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **reconciliation of at least six numbers** — manifest, sold, comps, holds, walk-ups, scans — and the argument is always about the gap between them, never about the headline. A model with one `attendance` field cannot represent "you comped 90 people against a 20-comp allowance", which is the single most common settlement dispute in live music. |
| DT-02 | Paid count and attendance are the same thing | Casual conflation everyone makes, including in the domain index's phrase "settlement computed from deal + counts" | ❌ REJECTED: **paid count drives the money; scan count drives the draw record** (17.11.01). They routinely differ by 10-20% and they answer different questions. Conflating them would either corrupt the settlement (paying on people who didn't come) or corrupt the draw intelligence (recording a sellout as the number who showed up in the rain). Two numbers, two purposes — and the sweep's language suggests one. **Refined by DT-07: it is three numbers, not two.** **Name-bound by D-18**: the draw quantity is `admissions_paid`, never the gate-observed `scanned` — "scan count" in this row's prose predates the split and no longer names it. |
| DT-03 | The comp allowance protects the artist | The plain reading — the deal caps how many freebies the promoter can give away | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the inversion is the interesting part.** Comps cut both ways: the *promoter* comps to paper a weak house (which costs the artist on a door deal), and the *artist* comps their own guest list (which costs the promoter). The allowance is a mutual constraint, and a domain expert would model **whose comps** as well as how many. A single comp counter with one allowance would let the artist's 30 guests eat the promoter's 20-comp budget — or vice versa — and produce a number both sides reject. Nothing in the sweep suggests comp attribution was considered. |
| DT-04 | Gross is the count multiplied by the ticket price | The arithmetic everyone does in their head, and the one the phrase "settlement computed from deal + counts" encodes | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most under-appreciated line in live settlement. A ticket's face price is a **stack**: base price, ticketing fee, facility fee, and tax — and the deal's definition of "gross" selects a subset of that stack. UK prices are VAT-inclusive at 20%; US municipal amusement taxes vary by city; settlement gross is conventionally net of both. Settle on the wrong layer and the number is wrong by the tax rate before anyone has argued about a comp. Worse, the facility and ticketing fees are frequently paid to entities the promoter owns — **"gross net of our own fee" is a related-party extraction that is legal, standard, and invisible in a total**. The consequence for this feature: it must deliver `sold` **decomposed by tier and by fee component with each component's recipient**, or the deal's gross definition (17.03.01) is unevaluable and 17.09.01's derivation is a derivation of the wrong number. |
| DT-05 | The count is final when the box office closes | The premise of the entire sub-domain, and of settlement-at-1am as a practice | ❌ REJECTED, and it creates an obligation nobody has priced. The count is final for **cash**, not for **money**. A ticket sold and settled at 1am can be refunded next week (19.06) and charged back up to ~120 days later under the card schemes' common dispute reason codes. The platform disburses (17.10.02) against a number that is still mutable for a quarter of a year. This resolves a question 17.09.06 Q-01 asks without knowing the constraint exists: **the settlement amendment window cannot be shorter than the chargeback window**, or the platform has closed a record on money it may yet lose. It also means "how long may a settlement be amended" is not a UX preference — it has a floor set by a card scheme. Who *bears* the chargeback is a genuine product decision (Q-01) and depends on the payments provider, which `meta/constraints.md` still lists as an open stack decision deferred to `/create-prd`. |
| DT-06 | Closing the count asymmetry requires owning the box office | 17.09.01 DT-03, domain Q-04, and sub-domain Q-10 all assume the box office is the unit of ownership | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and the correction is the most consequential finding in this file.** Owning the box office gets T4 and total closure.
