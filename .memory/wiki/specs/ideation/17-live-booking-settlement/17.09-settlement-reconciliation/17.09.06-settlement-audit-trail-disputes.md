# Feature: Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes

> **Number**: 17.09.06
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The permanent, unwritable record of every number in a settlement and who changed it — and the
mechanism for contesting a single line without blowing up the whole sheet. This is **the provenance
thesis applied to live money**: `meta/problem-statement.md` says the industry's defining failure is
that facts get reconstructed from memory once they become contested and valuable. A settlement is
exactly such a fact, and this feature is the reason it never has to be reconstructed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the trail; raises **line disputes** — contests the comp count without contesting the whole settlement. Cannot write the trail. The trail is what lets them argue with evidence instead of insistence, which is the first time that has been possible. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads the trail; responds to line disputes. Equally cannot write it — and equally protected by it: a promoter who did nothing wrong now has proof rather than a reputation. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

**Nobody has write access.** The trail is written by the system as a consequence of acts performed
elsewhere — a count corrected in 17.09.02, an expense added in 17.09.03, a signature in 17.09.05. An
editable audit trail is not an audit trail, and both principals being Read-only is the design, not an
oversight.

The trail records: every input and its provenance (platform fact or asserted, and by whom), every
computation, every signature, every protest, and every post-signoff amendment.

**Line disputes** are the domain-specific half. A settlement dispute is not "we disagree" — it
contests **a specific line** (the comp count, one expense) and its resolution **changes a number**.
That specificity is what distinguishes it from the generic dispute machinery in domain 24, which
handles raising, mediating and resolving. This feature identifies *what is contested and what it is
worth*; 24 adjudicates it.

### Happy Path

1. Every settlement act writes the trail automatically — nobody chooses to log anything.
2. Artist reviews the sheet and disagrees with one line: the comp count says 90, the deal allowed 20.
3. Artist raises a line dispute against that line, stating the basis; the line is held out.
4. Operator responds with evidence from the trail; the line resolves — corrected, or upheld.
5. The sheet recomputes if the line changed; the dispute and its resolution are permanently recorded.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Amendment after commission was disbursed | **Clawback from a third party already paid** (17.08.02 DT-02) — the platform holds an obligation it may not be able to execute | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the sub-domain's hardest downstream problem |
| Dispute unresolved between the parties | Escalates to domain 24's machinery, with the trail as evidence | Handoff; this feature does not adjudicate |
| Settlement was verbal; the trail has a hole exactly where the decision happened | The same capture hole as the offer thread (17.02.02 DT-03) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the trail records what the system saw, not what the room did |
| Amendment window open indefinitely | Every settled show is a permanent liability | `[PENDING — Step 5]` (index Q-03) |
| Line disputed after both parties signed | Signing under protest (17.09.05) is precisely the setup for this | Protest-then-dispute is the expected path, not an anomaly |
| Trail contradicts a party's memory | That is the feature working | The trail is evidence, not opinion |
| Whole-settlement dispute rather than a line | Rare; usually a relationship failure not an arithmetic one | Routed to 24 without line-level scoping |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Recording | Any settlement act | Trail grows automatically; nobody logs anything |
| Clean | No disputes | Full history, read-only, permanent |
| Line disputed | A line contested | That line held out; sheet incomplete pending resolution |
| Resolved | Line corrected or upheld | Recomputation if changed; resolution recorded |
| Escalated | Parties cannot resolve | Domain 24 owns it; trail is the evidence |
| Amended | Post-signoff change | New computation; clawback obligations may arise |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The audit trail is compliance/reporting infrastructure and should be routed out as not-product | Node Classification Gate, architecture-in-costume test — it looks like logging | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is load-bearing. The **append-only storage substrate is** architecture and *is* routed out (sub-domain D-04). But what a user can see and rely on is the product, and here it is **the product's central claim applied to live money**. `meta/problem-statement.md`: "a split captured on the day costs one signature; the same split reconstructed three years later may cost a lawsuit". A settlement line is identical. Routing this out as logging would delete the reason domain 17 supports the thesis rather than merely consolidating a workflow. |
| DT-02 | Settlement disputes belong entirely to domain 24 | Cross-cut test — raising, mediating and resolving a dispute is generic and serves 05, 13, 14, 17, 19 | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — split the candidate** (sub-domain D-03). The dispute **machinery** is 24's and duplicating it here would be a mechanism wearing a product costume. What is irreducibly domain-specific: a settlement dispute contests **a line item**, its resolution **changes a number**, and that number **recomputes a formula and may claw back money from a third party**. Domain 24 cannot know any of that. The honest split is: this feature owns what-is-contested-and-what-it-is-worth; 24 owns the adjudication process. |
| DT-03 | The trail protects the artist | The framing of the whole sub-domain — the artist is the party with less information | ❌ REJECTED as one-sided, and this reframing matters commercially. **The trail protects the Operator at least as much.** `meta/personas.md` gives the Operator a switching trigger of "getting dragged into a dispute they facilitated but can't evidence" — that is the Producer's, but the Operator's structural position is the same: a promoter who did nothing wrong currently has only their reputation to defend with. A permanent record of a clean settlement is *an asset to the honest promoter* and a liability only to a dishonest one. This is the strongest available argument for why Operators would adopt a tool that appears to reduce their information advantage — and it is not made anywhere in the sweep. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Nobody has write access to the trail | An editable audit trail is not an audit trail. It is written by the system as a consequence of acts performed elsewhere. |
| D-02 | Disputes are **line-scoped**, not settlement-scoped | Contesting one comp count must not blow up the whole sheet; the resolution changes a number, which only this domain can know (DT-02). |
| D-03 | The trail is product, not logging | The storage substrate is architecture; the reliable, visible record is the domain's central claim applied to live money (DT-01). |
| D-04 | The trail is framed as protecting **both** parties | It is an asset to the honest Operator, which is the strongest argument for their adoption (DT-03). |
