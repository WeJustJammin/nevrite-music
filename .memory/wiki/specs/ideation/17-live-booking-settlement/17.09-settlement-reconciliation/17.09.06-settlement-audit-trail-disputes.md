# Feature: Settlement Audit Trail & Line Disputes

> **Number**: 17.09.06
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The permanent, unwritable record of every number in a settlement and who put it there — and the
mechanism for contesting a single line without blowing up the whole sheet. This is **the provenance
thesis applied to live money**: `meta/problem-statement.md` says the industry's defining failure is
that facts get reconstructed from memory once they become contested and valuable. A settlement is
exactly such a fact, and this feature is the reason it never has to be reconstructed.

Two halves, one purpose. The **trail** answers *what is this number made of, and who asserted each
part*. The **line dispute** is the act of saying *that part is wrong, by this much* — and it is
line-scoped precisely so that the answer is a correction rather than a collapse. Domain 24 owns the
process of adjudicating a fight; this feature owns knowing exactly what is being fought over and what
it is worth.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the trail: every settlement line broken to its lineage — this £4,312 is 620 paid × £15 advance tier, less £6,000 capped expenses, of which £800 security was asserted by the Operator at 19:42 from a phone at the venue. Raises **line disputes** — contests the comp count without contesting the whole sheet. Cannot write the trail. The trail is what lets them argue with evidence instead of insistence, which is the first time that has been possible. Where the Musician side is represented (tour manager, agent), the trail records **which natural person acted and under what authority** (DT-08). |
| Producer | None | No role. Live settlement has no Producer (domain index Role Matrix note 1). A Producer who books the room is acting as an Operator; one who plays the show is acting as a Musician. |
| Operator | Read-only | Reads the **shared** trail; responds to line disputes with the lineage as their evidence. Equally cannot write it — and equally protected by it: a promoter who did nothing wrong now has proof rather than a reputation. **Does not see the artist-side deduction trail** — agency commission is invisible to the buy side (domain index Role Matrix note 3, D-07 below). |
| Fan | None | Never visible. The Fan is the *source* of nearly every number in the trail — their ticket is the gross, their chargeback can restate it 90 days later (DT-11) — and they never see the arithmetic it feeds. |

## Behavior

**Nobody has write access.** The trail is written by the system as a consequence of acts performed
elsewhere — a count corrected in 17.09.02, an expense added in 17.09.03, a signature in 17.09.05. An
editable audit trail is not an audit trail, and both principals being Read-only is the design, not an
oversight. It follows that a party who wants to add something to the record does not add to the trail;
they perform an act (assert an expense, raise a dispute, attach evidence to that dispute) and the trail
records the act (D-14).

### What the trail records

Per entry: the **act**, the **line** it touched, the **before and after value**, the **natural person**
who acted, the **role they acted under** (Musician side / Operator side), the **authority basis** for
acting in that role, the **server-assigned time**, the **device-asserted time** where it differs, and
the **provenance grade** of the resulting value.

Provenance grade is a fixed, three-value ladder — the artist's right to know whether the number was
verified or asserted (17.09.02 D-04) applies to every input, not only the count:

| Grade | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform fact** | The platform observed the event itself | Paid count where the platform is the box office (domain 19) |
| **Asserted, evidenced** | A party asserted it and attached an artefact captured at the moment of the event | An £800 security expense with a receipt photographed at the loading dock at 19:42 |
| **Asserted, unevidenced** | A party asserted it and nothing corroborates it | Walk-up cash; a count from an Operator's own box office; a term agreed verbally in the room |

The trail never silently upgrades a grade and never invents an antecedent. Where a decision happened in
the room and the system only saw its outcome, the trail says so — **"asserted, unevidenced — no platform
antecedent"** — rather than presenting the outcome as though it had a history. That hole is real
(17.02.02 DT-03) and rendering it honestly is the only defensible behaviour; concealing it would make
the trail a confident liar exactly where it matters most.

### The primary rendering is the line, not the clock

The question asked years later is not "what happened that night" — it is **"what is this number made
of"** (DT-04). The trail's primary view is therefore per-line lineage, drilled from the settlement
sheet: click £4,312, see its inputs, see each input's asserter and grade, drill again. Chronology is a
secondary view, available and never the default.

### Line disputes

A settlement dispute is not "we disagree". It contests **a specific line** and its resolution
**changes a number**. That specificity is what distinguishes it from the generic dispute machinery in
domain 24, which handles raising, mediating and resolving. This feature identifies *what is contested
and what it is worth*; 24 adjudicates it.

A dispute is only a dispute if it names three things (D-09):

1. **The line** — a specific, *asserted* line. Disputing a derived line is redirected to its lineage
   inputs (D-10, DT-07): you cannot contest a gross of £12,400, because nobody asserted it — the
   formula produced it. You contest the count, the tier, or the expense that made it.
2. **The basis** — free text, required, non-empty. "The deal allowed 20 comps and the sheet shows 90."
3. **The quantified exposure** — the amount the raiser claims the line is wrong by, in the settlement's
   currency. A dispute without an exposure is an objection, and an objection's home is
   sign-under-protest (17.09.05 D-02), not this feature.

**One open dispute per line per party.** Re-raising a resolved line is not a second dispute; it is an
amendment request, which is a different act with a different window (D-12).

**A disputed line does not stop the money.** The settlement has a **payable floor** — the sheet
recomputed with every disputed line held at the value the *disputing* party concedes (D-08). Dispute an
£800 security line and the other £4,000 remains payable. This is the feature's load-bearing behaviour:
if raising a dispute cost the artist their whole night's cash, no artist on a van to Glasgow would ever
raise one, and every dispute would go off-platform — which is precisely where the platform loses the
record it exists to keep (DT-06).

### Escalation

Escalation is always an **act**, never a timeout (D-13). Silence does not manufacture a case. Where a
dispute has had no response for **72 hours**, the raiser is *offered* escalation; they take it or they
don't. 72 hours is chosen because both principals are people who work nights and weekends — an artist
mid-run, an Operator running three more shows — and a shorter clock would convert normal working rhythm
into a formal case. The value is a product parameter the owner may change; the mechanism (offer, never
auto-fire) is a decision.

Escalating creates a claim in 24 (24.04.01), whose evidence bundle (24.09) sources from this trail. A
line dispute **does not** create a 24 case on its own (D-05, resolves the former Q-02): a back-room
argument that resolves in ninety seconds must not manufacture a case with a state machine and a deadline.

### Amendment and restatement — two different things

A finalised settlement changes for two structurally different reasons, and conflating them is what makes
the amendment-window question unanswerable (DT-11):

| Class | Cause | Windowed? | Trail act |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Party-initiated amendment** | A party wants to reopen a settled number | **Yes** — closes at the amendment window; afterwards it is a claim to 24, which can produce agreement but cannot force recomputation (D-12) | `amendment` |
| **Fact-initiated restatement** | The underlying fact changed by external force — a ticket chargeback (19) voids paid tickets 90 days out; a card settlement reverses | **No** — nobody amended anything; the fact moved, and the sheet was always a function of the fact | `restatement` |

Either way, a change to a final settlement emits a **restatement delta**: a per-party statement of what
each party's money was, what it is now, and who consequently owes whom. That is what this feature owns.
It does **not** own executing the clawback — an amendment after commission was disbursed is a claim
against a third party who has already been paid and possibly already spent it (17.08.02 DT-02), and the
mechanism for recovering it is 17.08.02 Q-01's to decide. The trail's job is that the obligation is
named, attributed, timestamped and impossible to lose. A platform that cannot execute a clawback but can
prove precisely who owes what has still done the thing no incumbent does.

### Trail scoping

The trail is **not one undivided record** (DT-05, D-07):

- **The shared settlement trail** — both principals, plus the artist's agency, read it. Everything up to
  the promoter's obligation being discharged.
- **The artist-side deduction trail** — Musician side and their agency only. Commission (17.08.02),
  its basis, its derivation. The domain index is explicit that what an act pays its agent is invisible
  to the promoter, "a promoter who could see it would negotiate against it". One flat trail would break
  that in the most quietly damaging way available.

### Happy Path

1. Every settlement act writes the trail automatically — nobody chooses to log anything. The Operator's
   count arrives at 23:14 (asserted, unevidenced); the £800 security expense was captured at the loading
   dock at 19:42 with a receipt (asserted, evidenced); the sheet computes at 23:20.
2. The artist opens the sheet and drills the comp line: 90 comps, asserted by the Operator, against a
   deal allowance of 20. The 70 excess comps are shown against the allowance, not buried in a total
   (17.09.02 D-01).
3. Artist raises a line dispute against the comp line: basis = "deal allows 20"; exposure = the gross
   attributable to 70 seats at the advance tier.
4. The line is held at the artist's conceded value. The sheet re-renders showing two numbers: the
   **payable floor** (the settlement with the disputed line at its conceded value) and the **contested
   ceiling** (the settlement as the Operator asserted it). The delta is exactly the exposure.
5. The Operator is notified with the disputed line, the basis, the exposure, and the lineage of their
   own assertion. They respond: 40 of the 90 were the artist's own guest list (17.09.02 D-03 — comps are
   attributed to whoever issued them), which the trail confirms because 18's guest list landed here
   attributed.
6. The line resolves — partially upheld, partially corrected — with the resolution and its reasoning
   written to the trail. Neither party's original assertion is deleted; both are history.
7. The sheet recomputes. The floor and the ceiling converge on the resolved number.
8. Both parties sign (17.09.05). The number is final; disbursement (17.10.02), commission (17.08.02),
   draw record (17.11.01) and reliability (17.12) all fire.
9. The dispute, its exposure, both parties' positions, and its resolution are permanently readable from
   the comp line's lineage — not from a log somebody has to scroll.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Amendment after commission was disbursed | Trail emits a **restatement delta**; a named obligation is created — debtor, creditor, amount, cause — and handed to 17.08.02 / 17.10.02. This feature records the obligation; it does not execute it (17.08.02 Q-01 owns the recovery mechanism) | "Restatement — 3 parties affected." Per-party before/after and, where a party owes, a named obligation with its cause. Never a silent recomputation |
| Amendment after the window closed, party-initiated | Rejected as an amendment; offered as a claim to 24. 24 can produce an agreed correction; it cannot force a recomputation (D-12) | "This settlement closed for amendment on {date}. You can raise a claim — the other party must agree to any change" |
| Ticket chargebacks void 40 paid tickets 90 days after the show | **Not an amendment** — a fact-initiated restatement (DT-11). No window applies; the count was always a function of the fact | Restatement delta, same as any other. The cause names the chargeback, not a party |
| Dispute raised against a derived line (gross, net, the versus result) | Redirected to lineage — the system cannot resolve a contest against a number nobody asserted (D-10) | "Gross of £12,400 is derived from 620 paid × £15 advance + 80 × £18 door. Which input do you contest?" with each input selectable |
| Dispute raised with no quantified exposure | Not a dispute (D-09). Offered the correct instrument instead | "This reads as an objection, not a correction. Sign under protest to record it, or tell us what the line should be" |
| Both parties dispute the same line simultaneously | **One contest object with two positions**, not two disputes. The floor is the *lower* of the two conceded values | Both positions rendered side by side against the same line |
| Operator edits the line while a dispute against it is open | The dispute binds to a **line version**. An edit does not auto-resolve it — the dispute is re-presented against the new value with the raiser asked to confirm or withdraw | "This line changed while your dispute was open: £800 → £640. Does that resolve it?" |
| The edit fully concedes the exposure | Still not auto-resolved — only the raiser closes their own dispute. Silent auto-resolution would let a party close a contest by moving the number and hoping nobody looked | Same prompt; the raiser's confirmation is the resolving act and is recorded as such |
| Line disputed after both parties signed | Signing under protest (17.09.05 D-02) is precisely the setup for this. Protest-then-dispute is the expected path, not an anomaly | The protest is already on the line; the dispute attaches to it. The trail shows the sequence |
| Every line disputed | Still line-scoped — N contests, one per line. The payable floor may be £0, which is a legible outcome rather than a broken one | Floor £0, ceiling as asserted, N open contests. Not escalated wholesale |
| Whole-settlement dispute rather than a line | Rare; usually a relationship failure not an arithmetic one. Routed to 24 without line-level scoping | Handoff to 24.04.01; the trail goes with it as evidence |
| Dispute against a line that is subsequently withdrawn (Operator drops the expense) | Dispute resolves as **moot — line withdrawn**. Nothing is deleted; the assertion, the dispute and the withdrawal are all history | "Resolved: the line was withdrawn." The withdrawn expense remains readable in the trail |
| Dispute unresolved between the parties | Escalates to domain 24's machinery (24.04.01) with the trail as evidence (24.09). This feature does not adjudicate | Handoff; the line stays held at the floor until 24 returns a resolution |
| No response to a dispute for 72 hours | Escalation is **offered**, never fired. Silence is not a verdict and must not manufacture a case (D-13) | "No response in 72 hours. Escalate to a formal claim?" — the raiser's choice |
| Settlement was verbal; the trail has a hole exactly where the decision happened | The same capture hole as the offer thread (17.02.02 DT-03). The trail records what the system saw, never what the room did | The line renders **"asserted, unevidenced — no platform antecedent"**. Honest and useless-looking is correct; a confident-looking fabrication is not |
| The back room at 1am has no connectivity | Acts are captured offline and sync later. The trail records **two times** — device-asserted and server-assigned — and never collapses them | An entry synced at 09:15 showing "asserted 23:40, recorded 09:15". The gap is visible, not smoothed |
| A party's device clock is wrong or set deliberately | Server time is the trail's time. Device time is recorded as a *claim by that party*, at its own provenance grade | Both times shown where they differ by more than a minute; neither is silently preferred |
| One human is both principals — DIY show at their own venue | Valid and common at small capacity. Both acts recorded with the **role** they were performed under, plus a self-dealing marker (DT-08) | "Signed as Musician side and as Operator side by the same identity." Flagged, not blocked — it is a real way shows happen |
| Tour manager settles on the artist's behalf | The trail records the natural person, the role, and the **authority basis** they acted under — the same question 17.02.03 answers for binding the band | "Operator side signed by {person}, as {role}, under {authority}." The years-later question is never "did the artist sign" but "who signed and were they allowed to" |
| A band member with no settlement authority raises a dispute | Raising is not signing. The dispute is recorded with the raiser's role and authority; whether it binds the Musician side is 17.02.03's authority model, not this feature's to invent | The dispute stands; the trail names who raised it and under what authority |
| Operator can see the artist's agency commission | **Must not.** Trail scoping (D-07) — the shared trail ends at the promoter's obligation being discharged | The Operator's trail simply does not contain those entries. Not redacted-looking; absent |
| A party floods disputes to stall disbursement | Structurally capped: one open dispute per line per party, each requiring a basis and a quantified exposure. A settlement with 12 lines admits at most 12 contests per party, and each one is a specific, answerable claim | No rate-limit error — the shape of the instrument is the limit |
| A resolved dispute changes a count a third party already relied on | The **verified** draw record (17.11.01) and the reliability record (17.12) are derived facts and must restate too. A corrected count that leaves a "verified" draw record standing has manufactured a verified lie | Restatement delta names every derived fact that moved, not only the money |
| Amendment inside an open cross-collateralized run | The number was never final (17.03.03) — it is provisional. Amending a provisional is not a restatement, and no clawback obligation arises because nothing was discharged | "Provisional — updated." No obligation, no delta. Distinct from a restatement and labelled as such (index Q-01 gates this) |
| Two amendments in flight concurrently | Serialised. The second rebases on the first and is re-presented to its author against the new values; if the first changed a line the second depends on, the second is returned rather than silently applied | "The sheet changed while your amendment was open" with the conflicting line named |
| A party requests erasure of their personal data (GDPR) | **Anonymise-and-retain, never delete** — the Privacy cross-cut's rule as adopted by 24.09. The actor is tombstoned; the role, the authority, the act and the number survive | Entries show a tombstoned actor with the role intact. The arithmetic never develops a hole because someone left |
| Dispute evidence contains a third party's data or PII | Evidence attaches to the **dispute**, not to the trail (D-14), and is scoped to the dispute's participants — inheriting 24.09's chain-of-custody rather than becoming a permanent settlement record | The attachment is visible inside the contest, not in the line's lineage |
| The sheet recomputes for an unrelated reason while a dispute is open | Recomputation must not resolve, void, or silently re-scope an open contest. The disputed line's held value survives the recompute | The contest persists; the floor and ceiling both move by the unrelated delta |
| Trail contradicts a party's memory | That is the feature working | The trail is evidence, not opinion |
| The trail is unavailable (read failure) | The sheet must not render numbers whose lineage cannot be shown — a number without its derivation is exactly the status quo the domain exists to replace (17.09.01 DT-02) | "Can't show how these numbers were derived right now." Totals suppressed, retry offered. Fail closed |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Lineage requested for a line or a sheet | Skeleton of the line's structure with the line's own value shown; derivation rows resolve in. Never a total without its lineage |
| Empty | Settlement object exists; no act has occurred (show hasn't played, or played minutes ago) | The deal's terms and the shape of what is awaited — "waiting on: count, expenses". Not a blank page; the expected lineage is itself information |
| Recording | Any settlement act | Trail grows automatically; nobody logs anything. New entries surface on the lines they touch |
| Clean (Populated) | Inputs present, no disputes | Full per-line lineage, each input graded, read-only, permanent. Chronology available as a secondary view |
| Partial | Some inputs unevidenced or unsynced; some lineage has no platform antecedent | The sheet computes and says what it does not know: "3 of 9 inputs asserted, unevidenced" with those lines marked in place. Never a silent gap |
| Line disputed | A line contested with basis + exposure | That line held at its conceded value. **Two numbers**: payable floor and contested ceiling, with the exposure as the visible delta |
| Awaiting response | Dispute raised, counterparty not yet responded | Elapsed time against the 72-hour escalation offer; the offer appears at 72h and is never auto-taken |
| Resolved | Line corrected, upheld, or moot | Recomputation if the number changed; floor and ceiling converge; resolution and reasoning written to the line's lineage |
| Escalated | The raiser chose escalation | Domain 24 owns the case (24.04.01); the trail is its evidence (24.09). The line stays at the floor pending 24's resolution |
| Signed | Both parties signed (17.09.05) | Final. The amendment window opens and its closing date is stated |
| Amended | Party-initiated change inside the window | Restatement delta; new computation; obligations named and attributed |
| Restated | Fact-initiated change — chargeback, reversal — at any time | Restatement delta whose cause names the fact, not a party. No window applies |
| Window closed | Amendment window elapsed | "Closed for amendment on {date}." Changes now require a 24 claim and the other party's agreement |
| Provisional | Show inside an open cross-collateralized run | Every number marked provisional; amendments produce no obligations because nothing was discharged (index Q-01) |
| Error | Trail read failure | "Can't show how these numbers were derived right now." Totals suppressed — fail closed, because an underived total is the status quo |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The audit trail is compliance/reporting infrastructure and should be routed out as not-product | Node Classification Gate, architecture-in-costume test — it looks like logging | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is load-bearing. The **append-only storage substrate is** architecture and *is* routed out (sub-domain D-04). But what a user can see and rely on is the product, and here it is **the product's central claim applied to live money**. `meta/problem-statement.md`: "a split captured on the day costs one signature; the same split reconstructed three years later may cost a lawsuit". A settlement line is identical. Routing this out as logging would delete the reason domain 17 supports the thesis rather than merely consolidating a workflow. |
| DT-02 | Settlement disputes belong entirely to domain 24 | Cross-cut test — raising, mediating and resolving a dispute is generic and serves 05, 13, 14, 17, 19 | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — split the candidate** (sub-domain D-03). The dispute **machinery** is 24's and duplicating it here would be a mechanism wearing a product costume. What is irreducibly domain-specific: a settlement dispute contests **a line item**, its resolution **changes a number**, and that number **recomputes a formula and may claw back money from a third party**. Domain 24 cannot know any of that. The honest split is: this feature owns what-is-contested-and-what-it-is-worth; 24 owns the adjudication process. |
| DT-03 | The trail protects the artist | The framing of the whole sub-domain — the artist is the party with less information | ❌ REJECTED as one-sided, and this reframing matters commercially. **The trail protects the Operator at least as much.** `meta/personas.md` gives the Producer a switching trigger of "getting dragged into a dispute they facilitated but can't evidence"; the Operator's structural position is the same — a promoter who did nothing wrong currently has only their reputation to defend with. A permanent record of a clean settlement is *an asset to the honest promoter* and a liability only to a dishonest one. This is the strongest available argument for why Operators would adopt a tool that appears to reduce their information advantage — and it is not made anywhere in the sweep. |
| DT-04 | The trail is a chronological record of what happened | The universal shape of an audit log, and the shape the word "trail" implies | ❌ REJECTED: **the question is never "what happened that night" — it is "what is this number made of".** A reverse-chronological feed of 200 events is technically complete and practically useless: nobody reconstructs one line by scrolling. A tour accountant reads a settlement **line-first** and drills. So the primary rendering is per-line lineage and chronology is a secondary view. This is not a UI preference — it decides whether the trail is evidence a human actually uses or an artefact that exists to be pointed at. Every audit log that has ever gone unread was chronological by default. |
| DT-05 | Everyone who touched the settlement can read the whole trail | The plain reading of "both principals are Read-only" — one record, two readers | ❌ REJECTED, and this is a **cross-domain break the sub-domain does not see.** Commission accrual (17.08.02) writes to the settlement's history, and the domain index is explicit that 17.08 is `None` for Operator by design: "a promoter who could see it would negotiate against it." One flat trail hands the buy side the artist's agency rate — the single most negotiation-sensitive number the artist has — as a side effect of an audit feature nobody thought of as a permission surface. The trail must be **scoped by money-side**: a shared settlement trail ending at the promoter's obligation being discharged, and an artist-side deduction trail that the Operator does not have. Nothing in the sub-domain or its CX file surfaces this. |
| DT-06 | Disputing a line holds the settlement until it resolves | The obvious model, and what "the line is held out" implied in the surface pass | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the **feature-killer if got wrong.** At 1am with a van to load, if raising a dispute over an £800 expense costs the artist the other £4,000 they need to reach Glasgow, they will not raise it — they will take the money and complain later, off-platform. The platform would have built a dispute mechanism whose price is exactly the thing the disputing party cannot afford, and then wondered why disputes never appear in the data. **A disputed line must not stop the undisputed money.** The sheet needs a **payable floor** (every disputed line at its conceded value) and a **contested ceiling**, with the exposure as the visible delta. This is the same insight as sign-under-protest (17.09.05 DT-02) — the industry's contested outcomes all resolve by *paying the agreed part now and arguing about the rest* — applied one level down, to the line. A system that cannot represent that drives its most valuable records off-platform. |
| DT-07 | Any line on the sheet can be disputed | Symmetry instinct — it is a sheet of lines and a line is a line | ❌ REJECTED: **derived lines are not contestable, because nobody asserted them.** Gross is not a claim anyone made; it is 620 × £15 + 80 × £18. A dispute against gross has no respondent and no resolution — the Operator cannot defend a number the formula produced from terms both parties agreed months ago. Allowing it produces a contest that cannot terminate. The correct behaviour is redirection: "gross derives from these three inputs — which do you contest?" This is only possible **because** the lineage exists (DT-04), which is why these two are one design rather than two. It also quietly does the artist a favour: it converts a vague sense that the number is wrong into the specific assertion that is wrong, which is the only form a dispute can be won in. |
| DT-08 | The trail records who acted | The natural reading — an actor per entry | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — identity is not enough; role and authority are the load-bearing parts.** Two facts break identity-only recording. First, `meta/personas.md`'s structural claim: **one human routinely occupies several roles**, and at small capacity the DIY show — artist promoting themselves at a room they run — is not an edge case, it is the majority. A trail reading "Rob changed the count; Rob signed; Rob signed" is worthless as evidence; "signed as Musician side and as Operator side by the same identity" is a fact, and a self-dealing flag. Second, **the person at the settlement is usually not the principal** — a tour manager settles for the artist, and the years-later question is never "did the artist sign" but "who signed and were they allowed to". That is the same authority question 17.02.03 answers for binding the band, and if this feature answers it differently the platform has two contradictory models of who may commit a party. Nothing in the sweep or the CX file raises authority at settlement. |
| DT-09 | The trail should be built to be admissible — notarised, externally timestamped, court-grade | The instinct that follows from "provenance for contested money", and the direction an ambitious reading of the thesis pulls | ❌ REJECTED as a **design target**, though not as a possible future property. The overwhelming majority of settlement arguments never reach anyone's court — they are settled between two parties who intend to work together again next year, and they end when one party looks at the record and concedes. **The design target is concession, not admissibility.** Optimising for the rare adversarial-legal case would pull effort into notarisation and external anchoring — which are architecture (D-04 already routes the substrate out) — and away from the thing that actually ends arguments: a lineage a promoter can read in ten seconds and recognise as true. Chasing admissibility would also invite a legal-sufficiency claim the platform is not positioned to make. Build for the argument in the room; the substrate can be hardened later without redesigning the product. |
| DT-10 | A party should be able to add evidence or a note to the trail | The natural objection to "nobody has write access" — surely the artist can attach the text message where the promoter agreed to 20 comps? | ❌ REJECTED, and the objection has a better answer than a compromise. **Evidence attaches to the dispute, not to the trail.** The trail's entire value is that it is a record of what the system observed, not a record of what people submitted; the moment a party can append to it, it becomes two people's filings with a timestamp — which is what every reconstruction-era system already is and what `meta/problem-statement.md` says does not work. The text message is real and it matters, so it goes where filings belong: into the contest, inheriting 24.09's chain of custody, visible to the dispute's participants, and never becoming part of the line's lineage. The distinction is the same one 17.09.03 D-01 draws for expenses — **contest, never delete** — read from the other end: **submit, never inscribe.** |
| DT-11 | An amendment window makes a settled show safe to close | The framing of index Q-03 and this feature's own former Q-01 — pick a duration and the liability ends | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — a window defends against parties, and cannot defend against facts.** A window stops someone *reopening* a number. It does nothing about the number *changing*: a fan charges back 40 tickets 90 days after the show (domain 19), a card settlement reverses, and the paid count is now different. Nobody amended anything — the sheet was always a function of a fact, and the fact moved. Treating that as a late amendment forces an absurd choice between rejecting a true correction and having no window at all, which is exactly why index Q-03 has felt unanswerable. Two classes: **party-initiated amendment**, windowed, becoming a 24 claim afterwards; **fact-initiated restatement**, never windowed, cause naming the fact rather than a party. This makes the window's *duration* a much smaller decision, because it no longer has to be long enough to catch chargebacks. Nothing in the sub-domain distinguishes these. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Nobody has write access to the trail | An editable audit trail is not an audit trail. It is written by the system as a consequence of acts performed elsewhere. |
| D-02 | Disputes are **line-scoped**, not settlement-scoped | Contesting one comp count must not blow up the whole sheet; the resolution changes a number, which only this domain can know (DT-02). |
| D-03 | The trail is product, not logging | The storage substrate is architecture; the reliable, visible record is the domain's central claim applied to live money (DT-01). |
| D-04 | The trail is framed as protecting **both** parties | It is an asset to the honest Operator, which is the strongest argument for their adoption (DT-03). |
| D-05 | A line dispute does **not** create a domain-24 case; only escalation does | Resolves the former Q-02. A back-room argument that resolves in ninety seconds must not manufacture a case with a state machine and a deadline — that cost would drive the flow off-platform, which is where the record is lost. Escalation creates a 24.04.01 claim and hands it this trail as evidence via 24.09. |
| D-06 | The trail's primary rendering is **per-line lineage**; chronology is a secondary view | The question years later is "what is this number made of", not "what happened that night". A chronological feed is complete and unusable (DT-04). |
| D-07 | The trail is **scoped by money-side** — a shared settlement trail and an artist-side deduction trail | The domain index makes agency commission invisible to the Operator by design; one flat trail breaks that silently and hands the buy side the artist's most negotiation-sensitive number (DT-05). |
| D-08 | A disputed line does **not** block the undisputed remainder — the sheet has a **payable floor** and a **contested ceiling** | If raising a dispute costs the artist their night's cash, no artist raises one and every dispute goes off-platform (DT-06). Same insight as sign-under-protest, one level down. |
| D-09 | A dispute must name a **line**, a **basis**, and a **quantified exposure** | Without an exposure it is an objection, and an objection's instrument is sign-under-protest (17.09.05 D-02), not a dispute. The requirement is also what makes the flood attack structurally impossible. |
| D-10 | A dispute against a **derived** line is redirected to its lineage inputs | Nobody asserted a derived number; a contest against it has no respondent and cannot terminate (DT-07). |
| D-11 | Every entry records the **natural person**, the **role acted under**, and the **authority basis** — not identity alone | One human routinely holds several roles (`meta/personas.md`); the DIY show has one identity on both sides; the tour manager is not the artist. Identity-only recording produces a trail that is worthless exactly where it is needed (DT-08). |
| D-12 | **Party-initiated amendment is windowed; fact-initiated restatement is not** | A window defends against a party reopening a number; it cannot defend against the fact changing. Conflating them is why index Q-03 has felt unanswerable (DT-11). After the window, a party may raise a 24 claim, which can produce agreement but cannot force recomputation. |
| D-13 | Escalation is always an **act**, never a timeout | Silence is not a verdict and must not manufacture a case. The offer surfaces after 72 hours (a parameter the owner may change); taking it is always the raiser's choice. |
| D-14 | Parties never write the trail; **evidence attaches to the dispute** | The trail records what the system observed. The moment a party can append to it, it becomes two people's filings with a timestamp — which is the reconstruction-era failure the product exists to replace (DT-10). |
| D-15 | Every settlement input carries a **provenance grade** — platform fact / asserted, evidenced / asserted, unevidenced | 17.09.02 D-04 grants this for the count; the same right applies to every input. The trail never upgrades a grade or invents an antecedent for a decision made in the room. |
| D-16 | The trail records **two times** — device-asserted and server-assigned — and never collapses them | Settlement happens at 1am in a room with no signal (index Q-02). Server time is the trail's time; the party's claimed time is recorded at its own grade. |
| D-17 | A restatement fans out to **derived facts**, not only to money | A corrected count that leaves a *verified* draw record (17.11.01) standing has manufactured a verified lie — worse than an unverified truth. |
