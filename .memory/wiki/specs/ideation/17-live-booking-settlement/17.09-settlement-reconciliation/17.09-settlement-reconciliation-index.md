## Overview

The flagship. Computing what the show actually owes — evaluating the deal's formula against real
ticket counts, reconciling those counts against the box office, folding in expenses and merch,
getting both sides to sign, and keeping a permanent record of every number and who changed it.
This is the work that currently happens **in a back office at 1am with a calculator**, with
information asymmetry running one direction, and automating it is the single most flagship-grade
opportunity in the domain.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: seven capabilities in dense mutual interaction — the
computation reads the counts, the counts are contested against the box office, expenses and merch are
inputs with their own grammars, signoff is what makes a number real, the audit trail is what makes it
defensible, and the statement is what leaves the building. No two of these can be understood
independently.

**Why it is provenance, not just arithmetic**: `meta/problem-statement.md` says the defining industry
failure is that facts are reconstructed from memory once they become contested and valuable. A
settlement is exactly such a fact. A settled show is also **proof the gig happened** — which makes a
performance credit (domain 02) verifiable rather than self-asserted, and makes payment reliability
(17.12) a derived fact rather than a review.
