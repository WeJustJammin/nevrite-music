# Feature: Show Expense & Receipt Capture

> **Number**: 17.09.03
> **Parent**: [Settlement & Reconciliation](./17.09-settlement-reconciliation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Capturing what the show cost — security, sound, backline hire, catering, marketing spend — with
receipts, categorised, and checked against the deal's expense cap. On a versus or net deal these
numbers come directly out of the artist's money, which makes this the one settlement input the
Operator both supplies and benefits from inflating.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees every deductible expense with its receipt and can **contest** any line (17.09.06) — but cannot delete one. The distinction is the entire design: an artist who could delete expenses would be settling their own show. On a net deal, every line here is their money. |
| Producer | None | No role. |
| Operator | Full | Captures expenses at the venue, on a phone, at a loading dock — `meta/personas.md` explicitly notes much of their work is "on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk". Categorises against the deal's expense schedule and cap. |
| Fan | None | Never visible. |

## Behavior

Expenses split into two owners: the **cap** is a deal term (17.03.01), and **what fills it** is
captured here (CX-02). "85% of net after $6,000 expenses" means the promoter may deduct up to $6,000
of qualifying costs — and which costs qualify is as contested as the total.

An expense over the cap is **not an error**. It is simply not deductible — the promoter spent it and
eats it. That is a materially different thing from a rejection and must be shown as such.

The capture context is the design constraint. This happens in a loading dock with a phone and a pile
of paper receipts, not at a desk with a spreadsheet. A flow requiring a laptop will not be used, and
the expenses will arrive as a photograph of a sheet of A4 at 1am — which is the status quo.

### Happy Path

1. Operator incurs a cost — pays the security firm, hires backline.
2. Operator photographs the receipt on their phone at the venue and categorises it against the deal's
   expense schedule.
3. System runs the total against the deal's cap and shows headroom remaining.
4. At settlement, expenses appear as itemised deductions with receipts attached, in the deal's stated
   deduction order.
5. Artist reviews; contests anything they dispute (17.09.06); the rest deducts.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Expense exceeds the cap | Not an error — not deductible. The promoter eats it | Shown as captured-but-not-deductible, with headroom exhausted |
| Expense category not in the deal's schedule | Captured but flagged out-of-schedule; held out of the deductible total until the schedule question is settled — never silently deducted, never silently dropped | Line shown with an "off-schedule" marker and excluded from headroom; whether it can ever deduct is the schedule-structure decision (Q-01) |
| Expenses appear after signoff | A trust event, not a data event (CX-02) — a signed-off sheet is a frozen fact | Capture is blocked against a settled sheet; the only route is a full amendment that re-opens signoff (17.09.06) |
| No receipt — cash paid to a local crew member | Routine and unavoidable in live | Recordable as an unreceipted assertion, visibly distinguished from a receipted line; whether it deducts is Q-03 |
| Expense inflated to eat the artist's net | The adversarial case this feature is built around | The cap is the structural defence (bounds the total); contest (17.09.06) is the procedural one (challenges the line) |
| No connectivity at the loading dock | The capture moment has no signal — a photo + category taken offline | The line is captured and held locally, marked pending-sync, and appears the moment signal returns; nothing is lost. The sync *mechanism* is architecture (index Q-02), but the product guarantee — capture never blocks on signal — is fixed here |
| Same receipt photographed twice | Fat-fingered double capture at 1am is the likely error mode, not a rare one | Near-duplicate flagged for confirm-or-dismiss before it counts toward headroom; a false double-count directly overstates the deduction |
| Expense edited after the artist has viewed it | The artist reviewed a number that then changed underneath them | The edit is versioned and re-surfaced to the artist; a silently-mutated line is indistinguishable from the inflation this feature exists to prevent |
| Per-head expenses (security scaled to attendance) | The expense's value references the count | Belongs to the deal grammar, not to capture (sub-domain R-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Receipt uploading / expense list fetching | Line shown as saving with the photo thumbnail; the running total does not update until the line is committed |
| Empty | No expenses captured | Cap and schedule shown; headroom full; a capture CTA |
| Captured (Populated) | Expense photographed and categorised | Line with receipt, running total, headroom remaining |
| Pending-sync | Captured offline, no signal | Line present and marked pending-sync; counts locally, reconciled when connectivity returns |
| Unreceipted | Expense recorded without a receipt | Visibly distinguished as an assertion |
| Over cap | Total exceeds the deal's cap | Excess shown as non-deductible, not as an error |
| Deducted | Settlement computed | Itemised in the deal's deduction order |
| Contested | Artist disputed a line | Routed via 17.09.06; the line is held out pending resolution |
| Error | Capture failed — upload rejected, or write against a settled sheet | Line not committed; total unchanged; a distinct message per cause (upload retry vs "sheet is settled — amend instead"), never a silent drop |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is expense tracking — a bookkeeping feature | The candidate's framing ("Show Expense & Receipt Capture") | ❌ REJECTED: it is an **adversarial input to someone else's money.** On a net or versus deal, every expense line reduces the artist's take pound for pound, and it is supplied by the party who benefits from it being larger. That is not bookkeeping — it is the mechanism `meta/personas.md` implicitly points at when it describes settlement asymmetry. The design consequence is concrete: the artist gets **contest**, never **delete**, and the cap is a structural defence that exists precisely because the incentive is real. |
| DT-02 | The cap is the artist's protection | The obvious reading of "after $6,000 expenses" | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the cap bounds the total, not the composition.** A promoter can fill a $6,000 cap with $6,000 of genuinely-incurred costs that the artist would never have agreed to individually, and the cap is satisfied. The real protection is the **expense schedule** — which categories qualify — and the schedule is exactly the term that gets written vaguely or not at all. Capping the total while leaving qualification undefined protects the artist from the amount and not from the argument. A domain expert would push the schedule to be as structured as the cap; nothing in the sweep's candidate suggests it. |
| DT-03 | Receipts are supporting evidence | Their obvious purpose | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient. **A receipt proves money was spent; it does not prove the expense qualifies, was for this show, or was at market rate.** A real receipt for a real £800 of security at a venue running three shows that week is genuine evidence of nothing in particular. The industry's actual defence is the schedule plus the cap plus the relationship — and this platform adds one thing none of those have: the expense is captured **at the loading dock, at the moment it was incurred**, rather than assembled at 1am from a shoebox. That is the provenance thesis (`meta/problem-statement.md`) applied to expenses, and it is a better argument for this feature than the receipt is. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The artist gets contest, never delete | An artist who could delete expenses would be settling their own show; the input is adversarial by construction (DT-01). |
| D-02 | Over-cap expenses are shown as non-deductible, not rejected | The promoter spent the money and eats it — a materially different fact from an error. |
| D-03 | Capture happens on a phone, at the venue, at the moment of spend | It is a loading-dock flow (`meta/personas.md`); a desk flow will not be used and the expenses will arrive as a photo of A4 at 1am. |
| D-04 | Capture-at-source is the feature's real argument, not the receipt | A receipt proves spend, not qualification (DT-03). Capture-at-the-moment is the provenance thesis applied to expenses. |
