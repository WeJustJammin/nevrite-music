# Feature: Release Date & DSP Lead Time

> **Number**: 12.04.01
> **Parent**: [Release Scheduling & Windows](./12.04-release-scheduling-windows-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Setting the date the release comes out, and enforcing the delivery lead time each store requires
to honour it. Lead time is the single most misunderstood mechanic in distribution: an artist picks
a date two weeks out, delivers, and the release appears — but with no editorial consideration, no
pre-save window and no playlist chance, because the windows that mattered closed before they
started.

The feature's job is arithmetic the artist cannot do, because it needs facts they do not have:
each partner's lead time, what that partner counts as a "day", when they stop ingesting, how much
headroom a rejection would eat, and which of the release's windows the chosen date has already
forfeited. Every one of those is knowledge WeJammin holds and the artist does not. **The output is
not a validity check — it is a price tag on a date**, produced at the moment the date is chosen.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Picks the date and sees immediately what that date **costs** — which windows are still open, which have closed, and what each closure forfeits. Sees the four meaningfully different "earliest" dates (Behavior §3) rather than one number. Sees the per-store delivery schedule the date implies, including which store is the binding constraint and what dropping it would buy. Moves the date, and is told what the move costs before it lands: which deadlines shift, which contributors get a new date, whether the announce has already happened (DT-08), and which stores will refuse the change (12.05.02). Never has a date moved for them (D-06). |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the date because it is their deadline too — the master is due before **delivery**, not before **release**, and the gap between those is the lead time. A Producer working to the release date is already late by however long the lead time is, which is a real and common failure. They see their own derived deadline through 12.04.04 (D-04 there), and they see *this* feature's date as read-only context for it. They cannot move the release date; they can report slippage, which correctly forces a conversation rather than letting the plan absorb it (12.04 CX-01 Q3). Where the multi-hyphenate is both — Musician and Producer on their own release — the two lenses collapse and the "your deadline is not the release date" warning is the only thing that still has work to do. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An Operator holds no fact about a partner's ingestion schedule and has no stake in a release object (domain Role Matrix). The one adjacency — a release show pinned to this date — reaches them as a **booking** through 16/17, and appears on the rollout plan (12.04.04) under that feature's Medium-confidence read-only lens gated by parent Q-03. It is never the date-setting act, and an Operator never learns an unannounced release date from this feature. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and the invisibility is load-bearing. The Fan learns the date when the artist announces it (20/21) or via a pre-save link (12.04.03), and the date they were told is the artist's to break — never ours (12.04 CX-05 Q4). The only Fan-visible consequence of this feature is a record that appears, or does not appear, on the day they were promised. |

## Behavior

### 1. Five dates, not one

Products routinely collapse these into a single field. They are distinct facts with distinct
owners, and every conflation below has a named failure attached.

| Date | Owned by | Meaning | What breaks when it is collapsed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivery date** | Us (computed) | When the message goes to the partner | A Producer works to the release date and is late by the lead time |
| **Release date** | Artist | When the release should be live | — (the anchor) |
| **Live date** | Partner | When it actually appears | `Accepted` gets read as `Live`; the artist finds out from a fan (12.03.02) |
| **First-live instant** | Physics | The earliest moment the record exists anywhere on earth | The announce fires ~17 hours after the record went live in Europe (DT-04) |
| **Original release date** | The recording's history | When this recording *first* came out, ever | A migrated 2014 catalog appears as 2026 new releases (DT-09) |

The first three were in the `[SURFACE]` pass. The last two are Deep Think additions and both are
real, both are silent when wrong, and neither is modelled by any competitor's date field.

### 2. Lead time is profile data — and so are its semantics

Lead time is **per-partner profile data** (12.02.02), not a global constant (DT-02). Store lead
times commonly run days to weeks; editorial pitch windows demand considerably more. Missing a lead
time does not fail loudly — the store just publishes late, or publishes on time with none of the
benefits the artist was counting on.

The `[SURFACE]` pass stopped at "lead time is a number per partner". It is not. **Every input to
the arithmetic is partner-specific knowledge, and each one is separately unknowable from the
standard** — the same structural fact that makes 12.02.02 the domain's real moat and its largest
cold-start deficit:

| Profile input | The question it answers | If we do not have it |
|---|---|---|
| Lead time value | "How far ahead does this partner need it?" | No computed delivery date for that partner (D-08) |
| Day semantics | Calendar days or business days? "10 days" differs by ~4 depending on the answer | The headline number is wrong by up to a working week |
| Blackout / freeze periods | Does this partner ingest between 20 Dec and 5 Jan? Around major territory holidays? | Every Q1 release is computed weeks wrong (DT-05) |
| Rejection turnaround | How long does one reject → fix → redeliver cycle actually take here? | Headroom cannot be sized (DT-06) |
| Release-time rule | Local midnight per territory, or a chosen instant? | The first-live instant is unknown (DT-04) |
| Non-Friday acceptance | Does this partner reject non-Friday dates outright? | We cannot tell a convention from a rule (D-09) |
| Max scheduling horizon | How far out will this partner accept a date? | A date is accepted here and rejected there |
| Post-accept date change | Will this partner take a new date after accepting? | "Move it" is offered in ignorance of whether it works |

**Where a partner's profile does not carry an input, the platform does not present a confident
number for that partner.** This is the domain's ratified "never claim more than you know" pattern
applied to this feature's headline output. A fabricated "deliver by 2 Aug" is worse than no date:
the artist plans against it, and the failure surfaces as a missed release with no visible cause —
the exact silent failure the domain exists to eliminate. See D-08 and the `Partial` state.

### 3. Four earliest dates, not one

Every competitor shows one "earliest possible date". There are four, they can be weeks apart, and
the difference between them is where editorial is lost (DT-01):

| # | Earliest date | Bounded by | What picking it forfeits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Earliest **deliverable** | Longest selected store's lead time | Everything below. This is the number every competitor shows. |
| **E2** | Earliest **safe** | E1 + one full rejection cycle | Nothing, if nothing is rejected. Everything, if something is (DT-06). |
| **E3** | Earliest with **pre-save viable** | Delivery accepted + enough runway for a campaign to exist | The pre-save window (12.04.03) — the only pre-release signal a DSP algorithm reads |
| **E4** | Earliest with **all windows open** | The editorial pitch window (12.04.02) — usually the furthest out by a wide margin | Nothing. This is the honest recommendation. |

E4 is presented as the recommendation, labelled with what it protects. E1 is presented as what it
is: possible, and expensive. **Neither is pre-selected** (D-10) — a picker that arrives with a date
already in it has made the artist's decision for them, and per `decision-classification.md` the
release date is a product decision that belongs to the artist absolutely.

### 4. The arithmetic that produces "deliver by"

Per store, backwards from the release date:

```
delivery_date(store) = release_date
                     − lead_time(store)                    ← 12.02.02 profile
                     − rejection_headroom(store)           ← DT-06, sized from 12.03.03 history
                     adjusted for day_semantics(store)     ← calendar vs business days
                     adjusted around blackouts(store)      ← DT-05
```

**Delivery is scheduled at the latest date that still preserves one full rejection cycle** — not
the earliest possible, not the last possible (D-05). Delivering earlier buys safety and spends
optionality: a store that has already accepted may refuse a date change (12.05.02). Delivering
later saves optionality the artist will probably never use and spends the headroom they will
(DT-07). The two are in direct tension and the `[SURFACE]` pass named only one.

Different stores therefore get the message on different days. This is transparent by default and
surfaced only when it matters — when a store cannot make the date, or when a store is the binding
constraint on it.

### 5. A release date is not an instant

A release date is a **local date at each store, in each territory**. "Friday" is not one moment: the
first territory at UTC+14 reaches midnight roughly **25 hours** before the last inhabited territory
at UTC−11. A record with one release date exists for a full day in some places while not existing
at all in others (DT-04).

Consequences the artist is never shown and always trips over:

- **The announce is anchored to the wrong instant.** An artist in Los Angeles posting "out now" at
  9am PT is announcing a record that has been live in Europe for ~17 hours and in New Zealand for
  ~21. The rollout plan's announce milestone (12.04.04) derives from the release date; unless it
  derives from the **first-live instant**, it derives from a fiction.
- **"Release day" spans two calendar days** in every metric that counts one (22).
- The artist is shown two moments, not one: **their own** first-live moment and the **global**
  first-live moment. They differ, and today the artist plans against the wrong one.

Whether a partner releases at local midnight per territory or at a chosen instant is **profile
data** (12.02.02), not a platform choice. Whether WeJammin lets an artist *choose* an hour where a
partner supports it is a scope question — see Q-03.

### 6. Global Release Day

Friday has been the global release day since IFPI standardised it on 10 July 2015, and it is
simultaneously a convention artists may ignore and a **hard rule at some partners**. The `[SURFACE]`
pass deferred this to parent Q-02. It is resolved here by applying the domain's own ratified rule —
12.01.04 D-01: **block what a partner rejects; advise what a person might regret** (D-09).

| Case | Behavior |
|---|---|
| A selected partner's profile rejects non-Friday dates | Not a platform rule — a **per-store consequence**, identical in shape to a lead-time miss: "Beatport only takes Friday dates. Pick 22 Aug, or drop Beatport." |
| No selected partner rejects it | **Advice, once, inline.** "21 Aug is a Thursday. Nothing will reject it — you'll just miss New Music Friday's cycle." No modal, no nag, no second confirmation. |

The artist releasing on a Tuesday for an artistic reason is making a legitimate choice a platform
has no standing to override. The artist releasing on a Tuesday because nobody told them Friday
existed is the case this sentence is for.

### 7. Reachability, not a countdown

**This is the most consequential mechanic in the feature and it did not exist in the `[SURFACE]`
pass.** The gate's chase actions are rate-limited by ratified upstream caps — 02.04.01 D-09
(1 request + nudges at ≥7d and ≥21d, then closed forever) and 09.02.04 D-10 (3 resends per rolling
7 days). Composed with a delivery deadline, those caps produce a state nobody designed:

> A date can become **arithmetically unreachable by chasing** days before its deadline, while a
> countdown is still ticking and implying that action remains.

Concretely: if the delivery date is 5 days away, a Family A consent row is outstanding, and its
last nudge went out 2 days ago, then the next permitted nudge (≥7d) falls **after** the deadline.
No action exists that reaches the date. A countdown showing "5 days left" is a lie by implication
— the same species of lie as 12.04.02 DT-03's `pending` status, in the time dimension instead of
the response dimension.

So the at-risk signal states **reachability**, not time remaining (D-14):

| Signal | Condition | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Reachable | An action exists whose earliest permitted completion precedes the delivery date | "3 items outstanding. Priya's nudge unlocks in 5 days — that still lands before Spotify's 2 Aug deadline." |
| Unreachable by chasing | Every remaining action's earliest permitted completion falls after the delivery date | "Nothing you send now can arrive in time for 14 Aug. Your moves: cut track 5, or move to 28 Aug." |
| Unreachable at all | The item has no action (12.01.03 `Exhausted` / `Unresolvable`) | "Nothing left to try on track 5. Cut it, or move the date." |

This is the sibling of 12.01.03 DT-05 (`Exhausted`): that finding located the deadlock in the
**action** space, this one locates it in the **time** space. Both are produced by correct decisions
composed across domains, and neither is visible from inside the domain that made them.

### 8. When the delivery window arrives and the gate is not green

The pressure case. The `[SURFACE]` pass deferred it entirely, pointing at 12.01.03 Q-01. Q-01 is a
genuine owner decision — but it decides **exactly one bit**, and everything else is specified here.

**Specified under both branches:**

1. **The scheduler holds the dispatch.** It does not deliver a release the gate has not cleared;
   that is 12.01.03's call, not this feature's.
2. **The scheduler never moves the release date.** Not silently, not with a notification, not
   "helpfully" to the next feasible Friday. The date is the artist's product decision; a platform
   that reschedules an announced release on the artist's behalf has broken the artist's promise
   for them (D-06). This generalises 12.01.03 D-16 — the platform does not act on its own behalf
   inside someone else's relationship — from nudges to dates.
3. **The hold is announced at the moment it becomes inevitable, not on the day.** The reachability
   test (§7) fires as soon as no action can land in time — typically days before the deadline —
   because the artist's real options (cut the track, move the date) both need lead time of their own.
4. **The escalation names the person, not the state** (12.04.04 D-03): "Jamie's confirmation is
   what's holding 14 Aug", never "readiness gate: blocked".
5. **The release date is not deleted.** A held release keeps its date and gains a `Held` state
   carrying the reason. The artist's announced date and the platform's reality diverge visibly,
   which is the honest rendering — 12.03.02's `Unknown` principle applied to our own machinery.

**What Q-01 decides — one bit: whether a third option appears at the hold.**

| Q-01 resolves | Artist's options at the hold |
|---|---|
| **Hard** | Cut the blocking track (12.01.01), or move the date. That is all. Note 12.01.03 DT-05: with a silent contributor and spent nudges, both remaining options cost the artist material or their date — there is no third door. |
| **Soft-with-teeth** | The above, plus: deliver with a published, permanent, ledger-recorded override; the release carries `unverified splits` until resolved (12.01.03 Q-01's recommendation). |

Family C (third-party rights — samples, AI/likeness) has no override under either branch
(12.01.03 D-09), so a Family C block at the delivery window has exactly two options under both.

### 9. Moving a date that has already been announced

The moment the artist tells the world, the date stops being data and becomes a **promise**. Every
competitor offers "move the date" as a cheap edit, in total ignorance of whether the artist
announced it to 40,000 people three weeks ago — because their distributor and their marketing tool
are different companies.

**WeJammin can know.** The announce is a milestone on the rollout plan (12.04.04) and an act in 21.
Once it is complete, a date change is reclassified from an edit into a broken promise, and the
platform says so — and then lets them do it anyway (DT-08, D-13). It is their promise. The platform
is not the enforcer of it; it is the only thing in the artist's toolchain that can see it exists.

This is the consolidation thesis producing a fact no point solution can produce, in one sentence of
UI copy.

### 10. Design principle

**Surface the cost of the date at the moment of the choice.** An artist picking a date three weeks
out is not making an error; they are making a trade they cannot see. Showing "this date works, but
you'll miss Spotify editorial — the earliest date that keeps that open is 14 Aug" converts an
invisible forfeit into an informed decision.

The forfeit is stated **inline, before confirmation, on the confirm affordance itself** — never in
a modal, never as a second confirmation step, never after (D-15). A separate "are you sure?" dialog
is paternalism, and per the domain's ratified pattern the price belongs *at* the decision, not
wrapped around it. The confirm button says what it costs: "Set 21 Aug — forfeits Spotify editorial".

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens scheduling on a release whose stores are selected (12.03.01). No date is set.
2. Platform computes and presents the four earliest dates (§3) against the current profile data for
   exactly the stores selected: E1 possible, E4 recommended and labelled with what it protects.
   Nothing is pre-selected.
3. Musician picks 21 Aug — three weeks out, between E1 and E4.
4. Platform states the price inline, before confirmation: "21 Aug works on all 7 stores. It
   forfeits Spotify editorial — that window closed 4 days ago. 11 Sep keeps it open."
5. Musician moves to 11 Sep. The forfeit statement clears; the recommendation and the choice now
   agree.
6. Platform shows the per-store delivery schedule the date implies — 7 stores, 4 distinct delivery
   dates, each with one rejection cycle of headroom preserved — and names the binding constraint:
   "Beatport's 10-day lead time is what sets your earliest date. Dropping it would buy you 6 days."
7. Musician confirms. The date is written; the rollout plan (12.04.04) materialises with derived
   deadlines; the Producer's master deadline is 2 Aug, not 11 Sep, and they are told so directly.
8. Deliveries are queued per store. The reachability signal (§7) runs against the gate (12.01.03)
   continuously, not on a schedule.
9. Each store's window arrives; the gate is green; dispatch proceeds (12.02.03). Rows move to
   `Sent` on 12.03.02's board on four different days.
10. Stores accept. The pitch window (12.04.02) and the pre-save link (12.04.03) open together —
    one notification, not two (12.04 CX-03 Q4).
11. 11 Sep: the record goes live at local midnight per territory. The artist is shown their own
    first-live moment (00:00 PT) and the global one (~21 hours earlier, UTC+14), and the announce
    milestone is anchored to the one they chose knowingly.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Date is inside a store's lead time | That store cannot honour it; it publishes when it publishes | "Beatport needs 10 days. Pick 24 Aug, or accept Beatport being late." |
| Date is achievable but misses the editorial window | Not an error — a forfeit, and an invisible one | "This works, but Spotify editorial closed. Move to 14 Aug to keep it open." |
| Different stores have different lead times | Staggered delivery, one release date | Transparent by default; surfaced only if a store cannot make it, or is the binding constraint |
| Partner's **day semantics** unknown (never certified) | No computed delivery date for that partner. The arithmetic is not attempted (D-08) | "We don't know whether Beatport counts weekends. We won't guess a date we'd be planning your release against — treat Beatport as unscheduled." |
| Computed delivery date lands in a partner **blackout** (20 Dec–5 Jan) | Delivery is scheduled before the blackout opens; the effective lead time balloons | "For a 16 Jan release, Apple needs it by 18 Dec — they stop ingesting over the holidays. That's 4 weeks, not 12 days." |
| Computed delivery date lands on a weekend/holiday but the partner has no blackout entry | Delivered on the computed date; day semantics govern whether it counts | Nothing surfaced. A partner with calendar-day semantics genuinely does not care. |
| **Lead time changes** after the date is confirmed (profile update, 12.02.02) | Re-evaluated against the **current** profile — never pinned (D-07). A scheduled date can become infeasible without the artist touching anything | "Apple changed their lead time to 21 days. Your 14 Aug delivery is now 3 days late for them. Options: deliver early, or move to 21 Aug." |
| A lead-time increase lands across the whole fleet | Every scheduled release to that partner is simultaneously late. Blast radius is fleet-wide, not per-release (12.02.02 DT-08) | Per artist: their own release, their own options. The fleet event is ours to absorb, never framed as their problem (12.02.02 Role Lens) |
| Store **rejects** the delivery; headroom absorbs it | Fix → redeliver inside the reserved cycle. The date holds. **This is what D-05 bought** | "Apple rejected it — artwork's 1400px, they want 3000. Fixed and resent; you're still fine for 14 Aug." |
| Store rejects and headroom is already spent | The date is gone on that store | "Apple rejected it and there's no room left before 14 Aug. They'll be late. Everyone else is on time." |
| Store rejects the day before the release date | No cycle exists. Not recoverable for that store | "Apple rejected this yesterday. 14 Aug isn't happening there — the rest of your stores are fine." |
| Gate is not green and the delivery window arrives | §8. Dispatch held; **the date is never auto-moved**; the person is named | "Held. Jamie hasn't confirmed track 5's split, and Spotify needed it yesterday. Cut track 5, or move to 28 Aug." (+ override, if Q-01 resolves soft) |
| Gate is not green and no nudge can legally land before the deadline (§7) | The date is unreachable by chasing, **days before** the deadline. Say so instead of counting down | "Nothing you send now arrives in time for 14 Aug — Priya's next nudge unlocks in 5 days and Spotify needs it in 3. Cut track 5, or move the date." |
| Non-Friday date, no partner objects | Advice, once, inline (D-09) | "21 Aug is a Thursday. Nothing rejects it — you'll miss New Music Friday's cycle." |
| Non-Friday date, a selected partner rejects it | Per-store consequence, identical in shape to a lead-time miss | "Beatport only takes Friday dates. Pick 22 Aug, or drop Beatport." |
| Date moved **later** after delivery | Some stores accept a date change; some do not (profile data) | "Spotify will take the new date. Apple won't — it's already locked there. You'd be out 14 Aug on Apple and 28 Aug everywhere else." |
| Date moved **earlier** after delivery | Much harder than moving later; usually impossible. Can retroactively violate a lead time already passed | "You can't pull this forward on stores that already have it." |
| Date moved earlier while pre-saves exist | **The worst case in the sub-domain** (12.04 CX-05 Q5). The record can go live before a store re-points the pre-save; the pre-save fires against nothing | "Pulling this forward risks every pre-save on Spotify firing against a record that isn't there yet. We can't undo that for your fans." |
| Date moved after the **announce** milestone is complete | Reclassified from an edit to a broken promise (DT-08, D-13). Still permitted | "You announced 14 Aug on 3 Jul. Moving it is your call — but you told people." |
| Date moved; derived deadlines shift, artist-added milestones do not | The plan splits into two populations (12.04 CX-01 Q1) | "Moving to 28 Aug shifts 6 deadlines. Your video shoot stays put — want to move it?" |
| Date moved; a Producer's master deadline moves into the past | A date pulled forward can impose already-expired work on a contributor who never agreed | "Moving to 7 Aug puts Sam's master deadline 2 days ago. Talk to Sam before you do this." |
| Date changed while a dispatch is **in flight** | The window fired mid-edit. The dispatch wins; the change lands as a post-delivery date change (12.05.02), not a schedule edit | "Spotify already has it — that went out 40 minutes ago. Changing the date now is a redelivery, not a reschedule." |
| Two co-owners change the date concurrently | Last write does **not** silently win. The second writer is shown the first's change and the resulting price before their own is applied | "Dara moved this to 28 Aug 6 minutes ago. You're about to move it to 21 Aug — that reopens Spotify editorial. Still want to?" |
| A co-owner moves the date to sabotage a campaign | Real, and out of this feature's reach. Delivery permissions belong to 09/12.01.03 Q-04 (consent-to-release), which is where the standing question lives | The change is attributed and visible to every co-owner. Attribution is the only mechanism this feature owns. |
| Artist **adds a store** after the others' delivery dates have passed | The new store's lead time may make the date impossible for it alone | "Adding Beatport now means they'd need it 6 days ago. They'll be late — everyone else is unaffected." |
| Artist **removes** the store that was the binding constraint | An earlier date is now possible. The artist is told, because it is a price change and prices are surfaced (D-01) | "Dropping Beatport means 8 Aug is now possible. Want it?" |
| Date is in the past, or is today | Not deliverable anywhere. Rejected at input, with the reason | "8 Aug has gone. Your earliest is 5 Sep with everything open, 24 Aug at a push." |
| Date is beyond a partner's max scheduling horizon | Per-store consequence, not a global block | "Beatport won't take a date more than 12 months out. Everyone else will." |
| Date is beyond **every** partner's horizon | No store can take it | "Nobody will schedule 3 years out. Set a date you can hit and move it later — moving later is easy; moving earlier isn't." |
| Delivery date computes to **before today** at the moment the date is set | The plan is impossible from the start | "14 Aug can't work — Spotify needed it 2 days ago. Earliest realistic: 5 Sep." |
| Artist has **two releases** on the same date | They compete with themselves — most DSPs consider one editorial pitch per artist per cycle (12.04.02) | "You've also got *{single}* out 14 Aug. Spotify will only look at one pitch." |
| Artist is a **featured contributor** on someone else's release the same week | Detectable only because WeJammin holds the credit graph (02) — no competitor's distributor can see a release it does not distribute (DT-11). **Shown only where the artist already has visibility of that release**; where they do not, nothing is shown, because a warning about a release you cannot see *is* the leak | "You're featured on *{release}*, out the same Friday." — or silence. Never a hint. |
| Territory-limited exclusive forces a different date in one territory | The date is territory-scoped by construction (D-11); one global date is the default value, not the model | "Your Japan licence starts 1 Oct. Japan can't be 14 Aug." (UI exposure of per-territory dating: Q-02) |
| Release date falls on 29 February | A valid date every 4 years and an invalid one otherwise. A date moved off a leap day by "+1 year" arithmetic silently lands on 1 March | Accepted when valid; a year-shift never silently relocates it |
| Daylight-saving transition on the release date | The local midnight the partner releases at is still local midnight. The **first-live instant** shifts by an hour relative to the artist's clock | Both moments are shown; the artist plans the announce against a real instant, not a computed one (DT-04) |
| **Original release date** set on a catalog migration (12.08) | Preserved as the recording's own fact, distinct from this delivery's release date (DT-09). Without it, a 2014 record ships as a 2026 new release | "This came out 3 Mar 2014. We'll deliver it as a 2014 record — otherwise your catalog arrives as new releases and gets treated like one." |
| Original release date **asserted** on an imported record we never witnessed | An artist claim, not a platform fact. Marked as such — the same rule as 12.01.03 D-14 (*imported, unattested*) | "You told us 2014. We weren't there, so that's how the record will read." (Confidence tier: domain Q-09 / Q-04) |
| Original release date contradicted by WeJammin's own record | Only WeJammin can catch this — the session record (07) says the master was rendered in 2019 | "You've put 2014, but this master was made here in 2019. Which is right?" Asked, never adjudicated (24 D-10) |
| Profile service unreachable at date-selection time | No arithmetic. The date can still be **set**; it cannot be **priced** | "Can't reach our store data. You can set a date — we can't tell you what it costs yet." Never a confident number from stale data. |
| Some store profiles complete, others unknown | Mixed. Priced stores show real dates; unpriced ones say so (D-08) | "5 of 7 stores scheduled. Beatport and Traxsource: we can't compute a date yet — that's on us." |
| No stores selected | Nothing to compute against | "Pick your stores first — the date's earliest possible depends entirely on which ones." |
| Store reports `Unknown` status as the release date arrives (12.03.02) | The date passes with no confirmation from that store. Honesty, not optimism | "It's release day and we still can't tell whether Beatport has it. We're not going to say it's out when we don't know." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unset | No date | The four earliest dates, E4 recommended and labelled, **nothing pre-selected** (D-10) |
| Pricing | Date entered; arithmetic running against profiles | Skeleton price row under the picker. Non-blocking — the picker stays live. |
| Unpriceable | Profile data missing or unreachable (D-08) | "You can set a date. We can't price it yet — that's our gap, not your release." Setting is not blocked. |
| Set | Date chosen and confirmed | Date + per-store delivery schedule + the binding constraint named + what each closed window cost |
| Scheduled | Deliveries queued | Per-store countdown to each delivery date, and what is still fixable before each one |
| Reachable | Gate not green; an action still lands in time (§7) | "3 items outstanding. Priya's nudge unlocks in 5 days — that lands before 2 Aug." |
| Unreachable | Gate not green; no permitted action lands before the deadline (§7, D-14) | "Nothing you send now arrives in time for 14 Aug. Cut track 5, or move the date." Not a countdown. |
| Delivering | Window reached, gate green | Routes to 12.02.03. Dispatch in flight; the date is no longer freely editable (see `Locked`) |
| Held | Window reached, gate not green (§8) | Dispatch held, date retained, person named, options enumerated. Never a silent slip, never an auto-move. |
| Locked | Delivered and accepted somewhere | Date changes route to 12.05.02 as a redelivery, not a reschedule — with the per-store split stated |
| Split | A date change accepted by some stores, refused by others | Two real dates, rendered as two real dates: "14 Aug on Apple, 28 Aug everywhere else." The sub-domain has no clean way to render this and must not pretend otherwise (12.04 CX-01 Q5) |
| Partial | Some stores priced, some not | Mixed schedule with explicit unpriced rows. Same shape as 12.03.02's exception-first board. |
| Live | Release date passed; ≥1 store confirmed live | Both first-live moments shown (own + global). Routes to 12.03.02 for per-store truth. |
| Slipped | Release date passed; a store is late or `Unknown` | Honest: what actually happened, per store, and what it cost. No green board over a red reality. |
| Error | Scheduling itself failed — not the facts, the machinery | Non-destructive banner + auto-retry. The date is not lost; editing is unaffected. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | "Earliest possible date" is one number and the right thing to show | The obvious affordance; every competitor shows exactly this | ❌ REJECTED — there are at least **three** meaningfully different earliest dates: the earliest that is technically deliverable, the earliest that keeps pre-save viable, and the earliest that keeps editorial open. They can be weeks apart. Collapsing them to one number is how artists forfeit editorial without ever knowing there was a decision — a silent loss, invisibly taken, which is a small structural echo of the reconstruction failure in problem-statement.md: a fact that mattered was never surfaced when it was true. **Sharpened by DT-06 to four**: "technically deliverable" is itself optimistic, because it reserves no room for a rejection. |
| DT-02 | Lead time is a global constant | Simplification | ❌ REJECTED — it is per-partner and it changes, so it is profile data (12.02.02) like everything else partner-specific. A hardcoded 14 days would be wrong for every partner in different directions and would silently rot as partners change their terms. |
| DT-03 | Missing a lead time is a loud failure the artist will notice | Naive assumption from ordinary deadline systems | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the mechanic that makes the feature necessary. Missing a lead time is **silent**: the release still comes out, usually on time, just with none of the benefits. The artist concludes the release "flopped" and never learns that a date they picked casually cost them their editorial shot. This is exactly the domain's founding property — distribution fails silently — applied to time instead of delivery. The counter is not enforcement; it is making the cost visible at the moment of the choice. |
| DT-04 | **A release date is an instant** | The universal mental model, and what a date field encodes | ❌ REJECTED — a release date is a *local date per territory*, so a record with one release date exists for roughly **25 hours** before it exists everywhere (first local midnight at UTC+14, last inhabited at UTC−11). Three consequences nobody designs for: (1) the announce is anchored to the release date and therefore to a fiction — an artist in LA posts "out now" when the record has been live in Europe for ~17 hours; (2) "release day" spans two calendar days in every metric that counts one (22); (3) whether a partner releases at local midnight or a chosen instant is *their* rule, so even the first-live instant is profile data (12.02.02), not arithmetic we own. The fix is cheap and nobody does it: show the artist **two** moments — theirs and the world's — and anchor the announce to the one they picked knowingly. |
| DT-05 | **Lead time is a number; the arithmetic is trivial once you have it** | The `[SURFACE]` pass's framing, and every competitor's | ❌ REJECTED — **the number is the smallest part of it.** "10 days" is unusable without knowing whether the partner counts calendar or business days (a ~4-day swing), whether they ingest at all over the December freeze (a multi-week swing that makes *every* Q1 release's arithmetic wrong), and how long one rejection cycle costs there. Each of those is separately unknowable from the DDEX standard and obtainable only through certification or a rejection an artist already paid for — the exact structural fact 12.02.02 DT-02 identifies as the distributor's real moat. **The consequence is the domain's own "never claim more than you know" pattern turned on this feature's headline output**: where we lack the semantics, we must not present a computed delivery date, because a fabricated one is worse than none — the artist plans against it and the failure arrives with no visible cause. A feature whose entire value is a number it computes must be willing to not show the number. |
| DT-06 | **"Deliver by X" is wrong; the real deadline is X minus one rejection cycle** | Domain expertise: professionals deliver 4–6 weeks out and cannot articulate why beyond "you need room". 12.03.03 exists because rejection is normal, not exceptional | ✅ CONFIRMED — **the failure mode nobody designs for.** Every distributor computes the lead-time boundary and calls it the deadline. A release delivered *on* that boundary and rejected on day 2 has no time to fix, and the artist experiences a store missing their date for a reason they will never trace back to a scheduling decision made six weeks earlier. Lead time is the partner's *processing* window, not a buffer — it assumes acceptance on arrival. So the honest earliest date is E2 (E1 + one full cycle), and the honest delivery date reserves that cycle. **WeJammin is unusually placed to size it**: 12.03.03's rejection history and 12.04.04 Q-03's retrospective make "one cycle at Apple is typically 4 days" a measured fact rather than folklore — the same "only WeJammin can do this correctly" shape as 12.01.05 DT-01. Nobody would ask for this; it is the difference between a date that holds and a date that holds *unless anything goes wrong*. |
| DT-07 | Deliver to every store as early as possible — more headroom is strictly better | The obvious corollary of DT-06 | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — early delivery buys rejection headroom and **spends date-change optionality**: a store that has accepted may refuse a date change (this file's own edge cases; 12.05.02). The two are in direct tension and the `[SURFACE]` pass named only one of them. Resolution (D-05): deliver at the **latest** date that still preserves one full rejection cycle — not the earliest possible (which forfeits the ability to move a date the artist has not yet announced), not the last possible (which forfeits the cycle). This is the only point on the line that pays for both. |
| DT-08 | **Once announced, the date is a promise, not a field — and only a consolidated platform can know it was announced** | Adversarial: the platform cheerfully offers "move to 28 Aug" while knowing nothing about the artist's Instagram post. 12.04.04's announce milestone; D-18 | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the cleanest instance of the thesis in the sub-domain. Every competitor offers a date change in ignorance of whether the date is public, **because their distributor and their marketing tool are different companies** — the exact fragmentation problem-statement.md describes, producing a concrete harm. WeJammin holds the announce (21) and the date (12) at once, so it can reclassify a date change from an edit into a broken promise and say so. Crucially it must then **let them do it** (D-13): it is the artist's promise, and the platform is not its enforcer — it is the only thing in their toolchain that can see it exists. One sentence of copy that no point solution can write. |
| DT-09 | **The three modelled dates are complete** | The `[SURFACE]` pass's own D-04 | ❌ REJECTED — **a fourth and fifth exist, and both are silent when wrong.** (1) The **original release date** is a fact about the *recording*, not a plan: a catalog migration (12.08) that ships a 2014 record without it delivers 2026 new releases, resetting catalog age, mis-entering new-release shelves, and inviting duplicate-detection takedowns. This is a classic and expensive migration disaster, it is invisible until it has happened to the whole catalog at once, and 12.08 exists precisely because we want artists to be able to bring catalog in and take it out. (2) The **first-live instant** (DT-04) is what the announce should anchor to and the release date is not it. Both are additions to D-04, not corrections — see D-12. There is also a provenance angle: an original release date is a claim about when a record first existed, and on an on-platform record WeJammin *witnessed* it (07), while on an imported one it is the artist's assertion. Those are different facts and 12.01.03 D-14 already settled how to render the difference — *imported, unattested*. |
| DT-10 | **A single global release date is the model; territories are a delivery detail** | The `[SURFACE]` pass's implicit assumption — never stated, therefore never examined | ❌ REJECTED — it would make the release date **the one non-territorial object in a thoroughly territorial domain**. 12.03.01 scopes stores by territory; 12.01.03 D-1 blocks per territory; 12.03.02's status is per (release × store × territory); 09.03.02 makes rights territory-scoped, so a territory-limited exclusive genuinely produces a different date in that territory. A globally-singular date would have to be retrofitted the first time a licensing deal touched it, and retrofitting a date field is how the delivery/release/live conflation happened in the first place. Resolution (D-11): the date is **territory-scoped by construction; one global date is the default value, not the model.** Whether the v1 UI *exposes* per-territory dating is a separate and legitimate scope decision — Q-02. |
| DT-11 | **The artist competes with themselves, and only WeJammin can see it** | Domain expertise: most DSPs consider one editorial pitch per artist per cycle (12.04.02). personas.md's structural fact — the multi-hyphenate is on other people's records constantly | ✅ CONFIRMED — a Musician with a single out on 14 Aug who is also featured on a bandmate's release that Friday has split their own editorial shot, and nothing in their toolchain can tell them, because a distributor sees only the releases it distributes. WeJammin holds the **credit graph** (02), so it can see the collision from the other side of the record. **But the permission is the whole problem, and it resolves cleanly against precedent**: the warning is shown only where the artist *already* has visibility of that release under 02/09's existing rules — the gate's rule (12.01.03 Role Lens: "the gate never widens 09's visibility") generalises exactly. Where they lack visibility, **nothing is shown at all**, not a hint, not a count — a warning about a release you cannot see *is* the leak, and it would expose a third party's unannounced date, which parent Q-03 already identifies as commercially sensitive. The residual — whether a credited contributor has standing to see the release's date — is 12.01.03 Q-04, not this feature's to decide. |
| DT-12 | **The at-risk warning is a countdown** | The `[SURFACE]` pass's "escalating warning"; what every deadline system does | ❌ REJECTED — **the most consequential finding in this file.** Composed with the ratified chase caps (02.04.01 D-09: nudges at ≥7d and ≥21d; 09.02.04 D-10: 3 resends/7d), a date becomes **arithmetically unreachable by chasing days before its deadline**: if the delivery date is 5 days out and the last nudge went 2 days ago, the next permitted nudge falls after the deadline. A countdown showing "5 days left" implies an action that does not exist — the identical lie-by-implication as 12.04.02 DT-03's `pending`, moved from the response dimension into the time dimension. This is the sibling of 12.01.03 DT-05 (`Exhausted`), which found the same deadlock in the *action* space. Both are produced by correct decisions composed across domains, and **neither is visible from inside the domain that made them** — which is the argument for Step 6 synthesis existing at all. The signal must state **reachability**, not time (D-14). |
| DT-13 | The scheduler should move the date to the next feasible one when the plan becomes impossible | The obvious helpful feature; every calendar tool does it; it is what a stressed artist would ask for at 2am | ❌ REJECTED — the release date is a **product decision belonging to the artist** (`decision-classification.md`), and a platform that reschedules an announced release on their behalf has broken their promise for them (DT-08). This generalises 12.01.03 D-16 — "the platform never nudges on its own behalf" — from nudges to dates: both are the platform spending something of the artist's (relationship capital there, public credibility here) without being asked, and both are **most tempting exactly at the deadline, which is when the damage is greatest**. The scheduler holds, names the person, enumerates the options, and waits. It does not decide (D-06). |
| DT-14 | The recommended date should be pre-filled in the picker | It is the recommendation; pre-filling is a kindness; conversion improves | ❌ REJECTED — **a default is a decision.** A picker that arrives with 11 Sep in it has chosen the artist's release date, and per `decision-classification.md` that is theirs absolutely. It also has a market effect nobody would notice: at scale, a pre-filled default clusters releases, and a platform that quietly schedules its users' records has become a force in a market it is meant to serve. Suggest, label with what it protects, do not pre-select (D-10). The cost is a click. |
| DT-15 | Non-Friday dates should be blocked, because Friday is the industry standard | Global Release Day (IFPI, 10 July 2015) is real, universal, and some partners genuinely enforce it | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection is **derived, not chosen**: 12.01.04 D-01 already settled the general form ("block what a partner rejects; advise what a person might regret"), so this needs no new policy. A partner that rejects non-Friday dates makes it a **partner rule**, surfaced as a per-store consequence identical in shape to a lead-time miss. Everywhere else it is a convention, surfaced once, inline, without a nag. The artist releasing on a Tuesday for an artistic reason is making a legitimate choice the platform has no standing to override; the artist releasing on a Tuesday because nobody told them Friday existed is who the sentence is for. This resolves parent Q-02 by application of an existing rule rather than a new decision. |
| DT-16 | Lead times are longer for albums than for singles | Plausible-sounding folklore; the kind of rule a scheduling feature would love to have | ❌ REJECTED — **no partner evidence, and modelling it would put superstition in the spec.** The same discipline as 12.04 CX R-01 (which rejected the widely-believed pre-save→editorial edge for exactly this reason). If a partner's lead time varies by release shape, that is profile data (12.02.02) discovered through certification, and it will arrive as profile data. Inventing the rule here would produce a number the artist plans against that we made up — which is the specific harm D-08 exists to prevent. |
| DT-17 | Pinning the lead time at schedule time makes the plan stable, like the gate pins its evidence | Symmetry with 12.01.03 D-10 (evidence pinning at gate-green, re-checked at dispatch) | ❌ REJECTED — **and the asymmetry is the interesting part.** The gate pins facts **we hold** (split sheets, asset versions, rule packs) because their versions are ours to reason about. A lead time is a **third party's current rule**, and it is whatever the partner says it is *now*, not what it said in June. Pinning it would produce a schedule that is internally consistent and externally wrong — the platform confidently delivering to a deadline the partner has abandoned. So: pin evidence you own; **never pin a rule you do not** (D-07). Re-evaluate continuously against the current profile and alert on a newly-infeasible date. Second-order finding: because a profile change is fleet-wide (12.02.02 DT-08), a single lead-time increase makes **every scheduled release to that partner late at once** — an event with no per-release cause, which must be absorbed as ours and never framed to the artist as their problem (12.02.02 Role Lens). |
