# Feature: Release Date & DSP Lead Time

> **Number**: 12.04.01
> **Parent**: [Release Scheduling & Windows](./12.04-release-scheduling-windows-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Setting the date the release comes out, and enforcing the delivery lead time each store requires
to honour it. Lead time is the single most misunderstood mechanic in distribution: an artist picks
a date two weeks out, delivers, and the release appears — but with no editorial consideration, no
pre-save window and no playlist chance, because the windows that mattered closed before they
started.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Picks the date and sees immediately what that date **costs** — which windows are still open, which have closed, and what each closure forfeits. Sees the earliest date that keeps everything open, and the earliest date that is merely possible. Those are different dates and no competitor distinguishes them. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the date because it is their deadline too — the master is due before delivery, not before release, and the gap between those is the lead time. A Producer working to the release date is already late by however long the lead time is, which is a real and common failure. |
| Operator | None | Not visible here. (An Operator may see a release-linked booking on the rollout plan — 12.04.04 — but never the date-setting act.) |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The Fan learns the date when the artist announces it (20/21) or via a pre-save link (12.04.03). |

## Behavior

Three distinct dates that products routinely conflate into one:

| Date | Owned by | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery date** | Us | When the message goes to the partner |
| **Release date** | Artist | When the release should be live |
| **Live date** | Partner | When it actually appears |

Lead time is the gap between the first two, and it is **per-partner profile data** (12.02.02), not
a global constant. Store lead times commonly run days to weeks; editorial pitch windows demand
considerably more. Missing a lead time does not fail loudly — the store just publishes late, or
publishes on time with none of the benefits the artist was counting on.

The design principle: **surface the cost of the date at the moment of the choice.** An artist
picking a date three weeks out is not making an error; they are making a trade they cannot see.
Showing "this date works, but you'll miss Spotify editorial — the earliest date that keeps that
open is 14 Aug" converts an invisible forfeit into an informed decision.

Timezone and global-release-day handling (`[PENDING — Step 5]`): a release date is a *local* date
at each store, in each territory, which means "Friday" is not one moment.

### Happy Path

1. Musician picks a target release date.
2. Platform computes, per selected store, the required delivery date from that store's lead time.
3. It shows what is still achievable and what is not, naming what each closed window forfeits.
4. Musician confirms, or moves the date to reopen a window they care about.
5. Delivery is scheduled per store — different stores get the message on different days.
6. The rollout plan (12.04.04) derives its deadlines from the confirmed date.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Date is inside a store's lead time | That store cannot honour it; it publishes when it publishes | "Beatport needs 10 days. Pick 24 Aug or accept Beatport being late." |
| Date is achievable but misses the editorial window | Not an error — a forfeit, and an invisible one | "This works, but Spotify editorial closed. Move to 14 Aug to keep it open." |
| Different stores have different lead times | Staggered delivery, one release date | Transparent by default; surfaced only if a store cannot make it |
| Date moved after delivery | Some stores accept a date change; some do not | "Spotify will take the new date. Apple won't — it's already locked there." |
| Date moved forward (earlier) after delivery | Much harder than moving later; usually impossible | "You can't pull this forward on stores that already have it." |
| Readiness gate not green as the delivery date arrives | **The pressure case.** Depends on 12.01.03 Q-01 | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Non-Friday date | Convention, and a hard rule at some partners | Parent Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unset | No date | Date picker with the earliest all-windows-open date suggested |
| Set | Date chosen | Date + per-store delivery schedule + what each window costs |
| Scheduled | Delivery queued | Countdown, and what is still fixable before the window closes |
| Delivering | Window reached | Routes to 12.03.02 |
| Locked | Delivered | Date changes route to 12.05.02 |
| At risk | Gate not green with the delivery date approaching | Escalating warning naming the person being waited on |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | "Earliest possible date" is one number and the right thing to show | The obvious affordance; every competitor shows exactly this | ❌ REJECTED — there are at least **three** meaningfully different earliest dates: the earliest that is technically deliverable, the earliest that keeps pre-save viable, and the earliest that keeps editorial open. They can be weeks apart. Collapsing them to one number is how artists forfeit editorial without ever knowing there was a decision — a silent loss, invisibly taken, which is a small structural echo of the reconstruction failure in problem-statement.md: a fact that mattered was never surfaced when it was true. |
| DT-02 | Lead time is a global constant | Simplification | ❌ REJECTED — it is per-partner and it changes, so it is profile data (12.02.02) like everything else partner-specific. A hardcoded 14 days would be wrong for every partner in different directions and would silently rot as partners change their terms. |
| DT-03 | Missing a lead time is a loud failure the artist will notice | Naive assumption from ordinary deadline systems | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the mechanic that makes the feature necessary. Missing a lead time is **silent**: the release still comes out, usually on time, just with none of the benefits. The artist concludes the release "flopped" and never learns that a date they picked casually cost them their editorial shot. This is exactly the domain's founding property — distribution fails silently — applied to time instead of delivery. The counter is not enforcement; it is making the cost visible at the moment of the choice. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Show the **cost** of a date at the moment of choice, never just its validity | Missing a window is a silent forfeit, not an error (DT-03) |
| D-02 | Distinguish "earliest deliverable" from "earliest with all windows open" | They are weeks apart and the difference is where editorial is lost (DT-01) |
| D-03 | Lead time is per-partner profile data | It varies and it changes (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Delivery date, release date and live date are three distinct modelled dates | Conflating them is how "delivered" comes to mean nothing |
