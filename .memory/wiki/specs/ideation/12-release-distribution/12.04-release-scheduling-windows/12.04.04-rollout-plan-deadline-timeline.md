## Overview

The timeline counted backwards from the release date: masters due, artwork due, delivered, pitch
submitted, pre-save live, announce, single one, single two, release day. Most independent artists
run this in their head or a notes app, discover the ordering constraints by violating them, and
learn the lead times only by missing them.

> **Merged from sweep candidate 08** ("Release Rollout Deadlines"). It is a child of scheduling,
> not a peer — every deadline here is derived from the release date and the windows above it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the whole rollout as one timeline with real dates derived from real partner lead times, not from a blog post's template. Adds their own milestones (video shoot, press, release show), sees what is late and what it threatens downstream. |
| Producer | Config | Sees and owns their own deadlines — masters due, alternate mixes, any re-render a rejection demands. Their deadline is the **delivery** date minus asset processing, not the release date, and the gap between those is exactly the misunderstanding that makes masters late (12.04.01 Role Lens). Can move their own dates; cannot move the release. |
| Operator | Read-only | **The only Operator access in domain 12**, and narrow: where the rollout contains a booking they are already party to via 16/17 — a release show, a studio block for the video — it appears on their side as a dated commitment linked to a release. They see the booking, not the release plan. Confidence Medium; gated by parent Q-03, which asks whether exposing an unannounced release date to a third party is acceptable. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The Fan sees the rollout's *outputs* — the announce, the single, the pre-save link — through 20/21, never the plan. |

## Behavior

The rollout plan's value is that **its dates are derived, not guessed**. Every competitor's rollout
advice is a generic template ("announce 4 weeks out"); WeJammin can compute the real thing from
the actual lead times of the actual stores the artist actually selected (12.02.02 profile data),
the actual pitch windows (12.04.02), and the actual state of the readiness gate (12.01.03).

Deadlines are of three kinds, and conflating them is the mistake:

| Kind | Example | If missed |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard, third-party owned** | Store lead time, pitch window close | The window is gone. Nobody can fix it |
| **Hard, ours** | Splits attested before delivery | Delivery blocked |
| **Soft, artist's own** | Announce, video, press | The plan degrades; nothing breaks |

A plan that renders these identically teaches the artist that all deadlines are negotiable, at
which point the hard ones start being missed. The pitch window is not a suggestion; the video
shoot is.

The **critical path runs backwards through the gate**: masters → assets analysed → validation
clean → gate green → delivered → accepted → pitch window → pre-save → release. A contributor who
has not confirmed a split is not a footnote on this timeline; they are on the critical path, and
the plan should say so by name.

### Happy Path

1. Release date is set (12.04.01); the plan materialises with derived deadlines.
2. Hard third-party deadlines are computed from real lead times and pitch windows.
3. Our deadlines (assets, gate) are computed backwards from the delivery date.
4. Musician adds their own milestones; these are visibly soft.
5. Contributors with a deadline see it — the Producer sees "masters due 30 Jul", not "album out 14 Aug".
6. Slippage on a soft milestone degrades the plan; slippage on a hard one raises a real alarm.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Release date moves | Every derived deadline moves; artist-added milestones may not want to | "Moving to 28 Aug shifts 6 deadlines. Your video shoot stays put — want to move it?" |
| Hard deadline missed | Irrecoverable for this release | Honest: "Pitch window's gone. Nothing to do — here's what to do differently next time." |
| Gate is not green and the delivery deadline is close | The contributor is on the critical path | "Jamie's confirmation is what's holding the release date." Naming the person is the point |
| Plan is impossible from the start | The date cannot be met from where they are | "14 Aug can't work — masters aren't done and Spotify needs 12 days. Earliest realistic: 5 Sep." |
| Artist ignores the plan entirely | Fine. It is advice, not enforcement | No nagging; hard deadlines still alarm |
| Producer's deadline is set against the release date | The classic error — they are already late by the lead time | The plan shows *their* date, never the release date, as their deadline |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No release date | "Set a date and we'll build the timeline" |
| Derived | Date set | Timeline with hard/soft deadlines visibly distinguished |
| On track | Nothing overdue | Quiet; next milestone surfaced |
| At risk | Hard deadline approaching, precondition unmet | Escalation naming the specific person or artifact |
| Slipped | Hard deadline missed | Honest consequence + what it forfeits |
| Complete | Release day passed | Retrospective — what actually happened vs the plan |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The rollout plan is a generic task list and should reuse a project/task system (07) rather than be release-native | It is a list of dated tasks; a second task system in one product is a real smell | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — the **tasks** are generic; the **deadlines are not**. "Announce 4 weeks out" is a template anyone can write and every blog already has. "Deliver to Beatport by 2 Aug because their lead time is 10 days and your date is 14 Aug" requires the partner profile, the store selection and the gate state — three things only domain 12 holds. The derivation is the entire value; the task-list part is commodity. Proposed split: **12 computes the deadlines, 07 owns the tasks** (parent Q-01). This is a clean instance of the consolidation thesis — the plan is only useful because one platform holds the delivery reality and the calendar at once, which is exactly what a fragmented toolchain cannot do. |
| DT-02 | All deadlines on the plan are equivalent and can render identically | Simplicity; a timeline is a timeline | ❌ REJECTED — three kinds with wildly different consequences: third-party-hard (gone forever, nobody can fix it), ours-hard (blocks delivery), artist-soft (nothing breaks). Rendering them the same teaches the artist that every deadline is negotiable, and the ones that actually are not then get missed. The pitch window is not a suggestion; the video shoot is. This is the same failure as 12.01 CX-02 (validation vs gate presented as one list) in a different surface — a recurring hazard in this domain worth naming as a pattern. |
| DT-03 | The critical path is a scheduling concern about assets and dates | The natural reading of a timeline | ❌ REJECTED — the critical path runs **through a person**. The most common thing standing between a release and its date is not a master or a deadline; it is a contributor who has not confirmed a split (12.01.03). A timeline that shows "readiness gate: blocked" instead of "Jamie hasn't confirmed, and Jamie is why 14 Aug is at risk" hides the actual dependency behind a system state. Naming the person is what converts an abstract blocker into a phone call — and per personas.md the Producer's characteristic failure ("we'll do it later") is precisely a person-shaped deadline nobody made visible. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Deadlines are **derived** from real lead times, windows and gate state — never templated | The derivation is the whole value; the task list is commodity (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Hard-third-party, hard-ours and soft deadlines are visibly distinct | Rendering them alike teaches that none of them matter (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The critical path names **people**, not system states | The blocker is usually a person, and a name produces a phone call (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Contributors see their own deadline, not the release date | A Producer working to the release date is late by the lead time |
