# Feature: Pre-Save & Pre-Add Links

> **Number**: 12.04.03
> **Parent**: [Release Scheduling & Windows](./12.04-release-scheduling-windows-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Generating the links that let a fan commit to a release before it exists — pre-save on Spotify,
pre-add on Apple Music — so that on release day the record lands in their library automatically.
Pre-saves are the only pre-release signal a DSP's algorithm reads, which makes them the one
promotional act that plausibly affects the release itself rather than just its audience.

**The only feature in domain 12 with a Fan lens.** Per D-11 fans are first-class users, and this
is where a professional distribution tool produces an artifact a fan actually touches.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Generates the links once the stores have accepted delivery, sees which stores support pre-save and which do not, and sees pre-save counts as they accumulate. Hands the link to the campaign side (20/21) — this feature makes the artifact; it does not run the campaign. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. Pre-save is audience work, and the Producer's relationship to the record ended at the master. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | **Full** | Clicks the link, authorises their DSP, and the release lands in their library on the day. **This is the whole Fan experience of domain 12** — one link, one button, no vocabulary from the professional surface above it. Per personas.md the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"; if any part of the builder leaks into this page, the feature is broken. |

## Behavior

A pre-save link is a **distribution artifact, not a marketing one**: it depends on the store's
pre-release URI, which only exists once that store has accepted the delivery. That dependency is
why the link lives here and the campaign lives in 20/21 (parent D-03) — it is not a boundary of
taste, it is a data dependency.

Mechanics differ per store and are genuinely awkward: pre-save typically requires the fan to grant
the platform an OAuth permission on their DSP account, and that permission is broad — the scope
that lets us add a record to their library also lets us do considerably more. Many pre-save
services have historically abused it (auto-following the artist, adding to playlists, retaining
the token indefinitely). Per D-11's premise that fans are users rather than records to be
harvested, the scope, the duration and the exact consequence must be stated plainly on the page
and the permission dropped once used.

Pre-save counts are the artist's only pre-release signal and the only pre-release input the DSP
algorithms read — which is exactly why the temptation to inflate them exists.

platform-hosted landing page or a link-out is correct.

### Happy Path

1. Stores accept delivery (12.03.02 `Accepted`); pre-release URIs become available.
2. Platform generates a pre-save link per supporting store, plus one combined smart link.
3. Musician hands the link to their campaign (20/21) or shares it directly.
4. Fan clicks, picks their DSP, authorises with a plain statement of exactly what is being granted.
5. On release day the record lands in the Fan's library; the platform's permission is dropped.
6. Pre-save counts accumulate and feed the artist's release-day picture.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Store has not accepted delivery yet | No URI exists; no link can be made | "Pre-save opens once Spotify has your release." The "usually {N} days" clause is appended **only** where we hold ≥10 observations for that `(store × new release)` ack and is their observed **p50** (12.02.03 D-05; 12.03.02 D-11) — below that floor the artist is told we don't know yet rather than given a guess |
| Store does not support pre-save | Excluded from the smart link | "Beatport doesn't do pre-save." |
| Fan revokes DSP permission before release day | Pre-save silently fails for that fan | Fan sees nothing. **Nobody finds out.** See DT-03 |
| Release date moves | Pre-saves must survive it | Must hold — a date move that dropped every pre-save would be catastrophic and is not the fan's fault |
| Release is cancelled or taken down | Pre-saves are orphaned; fans authorised something that will not happen | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the permission must still be dropped |
| Fan pre-saves on two DSPs | Both honoured | Two links, two grants |
| Fan is a minor | OAuth on a DSP account; age assurance is D-13(c)'s problem arriving here | Routes to the Safeguarding & Minor Protection cross-cut |
| Pre-save counts inflated by bots | The number is a signal the algorithm reads, so there is a real incentive | Routes to 24 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unavailable | Delivery not accepted | Musician: "Opens once stores accept" |
| Available | URIs exist | Musician: links + per-store support; Fan: the page |
| Active | Fans pre-saving | Musician: counts; Fan: "You're all set for 14 Aug" |
| Fulfilled | Release day passed | Musician: conversion; Fan: the record is in their library |
| Expired | Release day passed, link still circulating | Fan: link resolves to the live release, never to a dead page |
| Orphaned | Release cancelled | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Pre-save belongs to Promotion (21) or Fanbase (20) — it is a marketing tool | It is plainly promotional, fan-facing, and every competitor files it under marketing | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — the **artifact** is distribution's (the link cannot exist before a store accepts delivery — a hard data dependency on 12.03.02), while the **campaign** is 20/21's (who receives it, when, through what channel). The split is at the artifact boundary, not at the persona boundary. Filing the whole thing under 21 would put link generation in a domain that cannot see delivery state and would recreate the exact disconnect artists suffer today, where the marketing calendar and the distribution reality are different systems. Recorded as a cross-domain touchpoint. |
| DT-02 | The OAuth scope required for pre-save is an implementation detail | It is plumbing; the fan clicks a button and it works | ❌ REJECTED — the scope that lets us add a record to a fan's library also permits considerably more, and the pre-save industry has a long history of quietly exercising it (auto-follows, playlist injections, tokens retained for years). D-11 makes fans **first-class users, not CRM records**; harvesting a fan's DSP account under cover of a pre-save is precisely the CRM-record treatment D-11 rejects. State the scope plainly, use it once, drop it. This costs conversion, and that is the correct trade for a product whose thesis is that its record is honest. |
| DT-03 | A pre-save is a reliable promise to the fan | The implied contract of the button, and what every pre-save page says | ❌ REJECTED — pre-saves fail silently and routinely: the fan revokes permission, changes account, or the token expires. Nobody finds out. The artist's count over-reports and the fan believes they are covered. This is the **domain's founding property — silent, partial failure — reaching the Fan persona**, and it is the one place the Fan is exposed to it. Per personas.md the Fan's whole success criterion is "never misses a release/show by an artist they follow"; a pre-save that silently did not fire is that criterion failing invisibly. Whether we can even detect it is unknown and matters more than it appears. |
