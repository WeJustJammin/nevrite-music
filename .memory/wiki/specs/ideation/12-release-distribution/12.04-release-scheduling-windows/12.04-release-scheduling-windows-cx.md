## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Release Date ↔ Rollout Plan

**Relationship**: The date is the origin of the plan's coordinate system. Every derived deadline — deliver by, masters by, gate green by — is the date minus a real interval taken from partner profiles (12.02.02), pitch windows (12.04.02) and our own asset processing. Moving the date is therefore not editing a field; it is recomputing a dozen commitments, several of which have already been communicated to other people.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: moves the date, absorbs the consequence. Must see what moves and what does not before confirming — a date change that silently reshuffles a Producer's deadline is how trust in the plan dies.
- **Producer**: their deadline moves with the date, and they must be *told*, not left to re-read a timeline. Per personas.md they are in a DAW, not the browser.
- **Operator**: a release show pinned to a date does not move because the plan moved — the venue is booked. This is the one place an Operator-held commitment can constrain a Musician's date rather than the reverse.
- **Fan**: not affected directly; feels it through pre-save (CX-05).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **release date** is owned by 12.04.01; the plan reads it. But artist-added milestones are plan-owned and do not derive, so a date move splits the plan into two populations — derived items that follow and pinned items that do not. The plan must make that split visible, not resolve it silently.
2. **Trigger chain**: Date set/changed → recompute derived deadlines → re-evaluate feasibility → notify anyone whose deadline moved. If the recompute makes the plan impossible (12.04.01's "at risk"), the plan must say so at the moment of the change, not on the day the deadline lands.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. The Musician can move the release date; the Producer can move only their own milestones. So a Producer can *report* slippage but cannot move the release to accommodate it — which correctly forces the conversation rather than letting the plan absorb it silently.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A date move fans out to every contributor with a derived deadline, plus any Operator holding a linked booking (gated by parent Q-03). This is one of the largest fan-outs in domain 12 and one of the few that is unambiguously worth it.
5. **State transition conflict**: A date move while a delivery is in flight. Some partners accept a date change post-delivery, some do not (12.04.01 edge cases), so the plan can end up with a release that is 14 Aug on Spotify and 21 Aug on Apple — a genuinely split reality the plan has no clean way to render. `[PENDING — Step 5]`

### CX-02: Release Date ↔ Editorial Pitch Window

**Relationship**: The most consequential pair in the sub-domain, and the one artists never see. The pitch window closes far earlier than any store's lead time, so it — not delivery — is usually the real constraint on how soon a record can come out *well*. An artist choosing a date three weeks out has not made an error; they have silently forfeited editorial without ever being shown that a choice existed.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the only actor. The whole design problem is making an invisible forfeit visible at the moment of the choice, without turning it into a nag.
- **Producer**: affected indirectly — an editorial-preserving date pushes their master deadline out, which is usually welcome.
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — the window is derived from the date and the partner profile. Neither writes the other.
2. **Trigger chain**: Date proposed → windows evaluated → forfeits surfaced *before* confirmation. Ordering is the entire feature: surfaced after confirmation, it is a regret notice, not a decision aid.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A closing window is one of the few genuinely urgent and genuinely actionable notifications in domain 12 (12.04.02 D-01 territory). Most notifications here are informational; this one has a deadline and a fix.
5. **State transition conflict**: The window depends on delivery acceptance (CX-03), which depends on the gate, which depends on a contributor. So a silent chain — Jamie has not confirmed → gate blocked → delivery late → window closed → editorial forfeited — can run to completion with no single step ever looking like a failure. **This is the domain's founding property (asynchronous, partial, silent failure) reaching all the way from a person to a lost opportunity, and no individual feature can see the whole chain.** It is the strongest argument for the rollout plan naming people on the critical path (12.04.04 DT-03).

### CX-03: Editorial Pitch ↔ Pre-Save (shared precondition)

**Relationship**: Not an interaction so much as a shared gate: both are inert until a store has *accepted* the delivery, because both need something only the store can give — a pitch slot and a pre-release URI. Accepted (not delivered, not live) is the trigger for both, which makes 12.03.02's insistence on an honest `Accepted` state load-bearing for this entire sub-domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: experiences it as "the release-week tools switch on together" — an emergent moment worth designing for rather than discovering.
- **Fan**: the pre-save half; sees nothing until the link exists.
- **Producer / Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None. Both read per-store status (12.03.02); neither writes it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Delivery → accepted → both open. If acceptance is late, both windows compress in lockstep, and the pitch window may not compress at all — it may simply be gone.
3. **Permission intersection**: Divergent, and notably so. The pitch is Musician-Full/Producer-Config; pre-save is Musician-Full/Fan-Full. One precondition, two entirely different audiences — the seam where domain 12 stops being a professional tool.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Acceptance is worth one notification that opens both ("Spotify's got it — you can pitch and your pre-save link is live"), not two. This is the argument against per-feature notification design in this domain.
5. **State transition conflict**: A store accepting and then rejecting on re-review would retract both. Rare, real, and unhandled. `[PENDING — Step 5]`

### CX-05: Release Date ↔ Pre-Save

**Relationship**: Pre-saves are commitments fans made to a *record*, not to a *date*. A date move must carry them, and the fan should not need to do anything. This is the one place in domain 12 where a professional decision (moving a date) silently propagates to thousands of non-professional users who never consented to the change.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: moves the date and should be told plainly that existing pre-saves carry — otherwise the fear of losing them distorts a legitimate scheduling decision.
- **Fan**: ideally notices nothing. Per personas.md their success criterion is "never misses a release by an artist they follow"; a date move that quietly dropped their pre-save is that criterion failing invisibly — the same failure shape as 12.04.03 DT-03.
- **Producer / Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The pre-save grant lives at the DSP, not with us — we cannot re-point what we do not hold. Whether a store's pre-save survives a date change is **the store's behaviour, not our decision**, which means D-04 in 12.04.03 ("pre-saves survive a date change") may be an intention we cannot actually honour. That needs confirming per store before it is stated to artists. `[PENDING — Step 5]`
2. **Trigger chain**: Date change → per-store pre-save re-point or re-issue → fans carried or silently lost. The silent-loss branch is the dangerous one, and it is invisible on both sides.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Fan granted a scope for a specific release on a specific date. A large date move (weeks) is arguably outside what they consented to, and per D-11 (fans are users, not records) that is worth taking seriously rather than assuming.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Should a date move notify pre-saving fans? It is the only fan-facing notification domain 12 could produce. It is also the artist's news to break, not ours — telling their fans before they do would be a real breach of the artist relationship. Leaning: 12 surfaces the fact to the artist; 20/21 own any fan-facing message.
5. **State transition conflict**: A date moved *earlier* than a pending pre-save's assumed date is the worst case — the record could go live before the store has re-pointed, and the pre-save fires against nothing.

| R-01 | 12.04.02 Editorial Pitch | 12.04.03 Pre-Save (direct) | Considered because pre-save counts are widely believed to strengthen an editorial pitch, which would make this a real edge. They share a precondition (CX-03) and nothing more. Revisit only if evidence appears. |
