# Feature: Editorial Pitch Windows

> **Number**: 12.04.02
> **Parent**: [Release Scheduling & Windows](./12.04-release-scheduling-windows-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Pitching a release to DSP editorial teams inside the window each one keeps open before release
day. Editorial playlisting is the closest thing streaming has to radio, the pitch is the only
supported way in, and the window is unforgiving — miss it and the release is fine, it simply never
gets considered. Most independent artists find out the window existed after it closed.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Writes the pitch — the story, the context, what this record is and who it is for. Sees each store's window with a live countdown, submits, and sees an honest account of what happens next (usually: nothing, and no reply). |
| Producer | Config | Contributes the production facts that make a pitch credible and specific — how it was tracked, who played, what room, what the arrangement is doing. These are the details that separate a pitch from a press release, and they are byproducts of the session (07/02), not things the Producer should be asked to type. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. Editorial pitching is a recorded-release act; it has no venue/studio/room dimension, so nothing about it maps to the Operator's inventory of time and space. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A pitch is a private argument to a DSP editor about an unreleased record; exposing it to fans would leak the release and misrepresent a lottery submission as a promise. |

## Behavior

Each DSP with an editorial programme has its own window, its own form and its own field set —
`[PENDING — Step 5]` for the real matrix. Spotify's pitch is the best-known and its window is the
one most often missed, because it closes weeks before release and its opening depends on delivery
having already happened.

**The pitch and MEAD (12.02.04) are one set of claims about the record, told to two audiences.**
MEAD is machine-read; the pitch is human-read. Sourcing them separately means the artist tells the
algorithm and the editor different stories — worse for both, and twice the work. One source, two
projections (12.02.04 DT-03).

Pre-fill from what the platform already knows: the session record (07), credits (02), the artist's
own history. Ask only for the part the artist genuinely has to author — the story.

**Honesty about outcomes is the hard part.** Editorial pitching is a lottery with no feedback:
most pitches are never acknowledged, let alone answered. A product that presents the pitch as a
submission with a pending status is lying by implication. The honest presentation is "submitted —
you almost certainly won't hear back, and no reply is not a no."

### Happy Path

1. Release is scheduled (12.04.01); the platform shows each store's pitch window and its deadline.
2. Pitch draft is pre-filled from the session record, credits and MEAD claims.
3. Musician writes the story; the Producer's technical detail is already there.
4. Musician selects the focus track — a required choice at most DSPs and one that materially matters.
5. Submitted inside the window; the platform records what was sent and when.
6. No response arrives, which is expected and was stated up front.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Window closes before delivery | The commonest failure. Pitching requires delivery first, so a late delivery forfeits the pitch entirely | "Spotify's window closed 3 days ago. Nothing to do for this release — for the next one, deliver by {date}." |
| Store has no editorial programme | Nothing to pitch | Not surfaced for that store |
| Artist pitches every track | Most DSPs allow one focus track | "Pick one. Spotify only considers one per release." |
| Pitch submitted, no response ever | **The normal outcome** | Stated honestly at submission, not left as a hopeful "pending" |
| Release date moves after pitching | Window may reopen, close, or the pitch may be invalidated | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — per-store behaviour unknown |
| Artist has no pitch-worthy story | Genuinely common and not a failure | Pre-fill gives them the factual bones; the story is optional |
| Pitch contradicts the MEAD descriptors | One record, two stories | Should be structurally impossible (12.02.04 DT-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Assembling the pre-filled draft from session (07), credits (02) and MEAD (12.02.04) | Skeleton draft with the story field showing "pulling in your session details…" |
| Not yet open | Delivery not accepted | "Opens once Spotify has your release" |
| Open | Window active | Countdown + pre-filled draft |
| Closing | Near deadline | Escalating, honest urgency — the window really is hard |
| Submitted | Pitch sent | "Sent {date}. You probably won't hear back — that's normal, and it isn't a no." |
| Closed | Window passed unused | "Missed for this release. Here's the date that keeps it open next time." |
| Error | Submission to the DSP failed (API/link-out fault) while the window is still open | "Couldn't send to Spotify — {reason}. Window still open until {deadline}; retry." Never presented as sent until the store confirms receipt. |
| Not applicable | Store has no programme | Not shown |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The pitch is a marketing feature and belongs in domain 21 | It is promotional writing aimed at a human, which reads like Promotion's job | ❌ REJECTED — the pitch is **gated by delivery and bounded by a distribution window**. It cannot open until the store has accepted the release, and it closes on the store's schedule. Its content is promotional but its mechanics are entirely distribution's. Putting it in 21 would put the window logic in a domain with no access to delivery state, which is how artists miss it today: the marketing calendar and the distribution calendar are different systems that do not talk. The campaign *around* the pitch is 21's; the window is 12's. |
| DT-02 | The pitch and MEAD are separate authoring surfaces | They target different consumers and live in different sub-domains | ❌ REJECTED — same conclusion as 12.02.04 DT-03, reached independently from this side, which strengthens it. They are one set of claims. Separate sources guarantee the artist tells Spotify's editor and Spotify's algorithm different things about the same record — measurably worse for both, and twice the typing. |
| DT-03 | Pitch submission should show a `pending` status while awaiting a response | Standard submission UX; it is what a form does | ❌ REJECTED — `pending` implies a response is coming, and it usually is not. Editorial is a lottery with no feedback channel: the overwhelming majority of pitches are never acknowledged. A status that quietly promises an answer is a lie by implication, and it produces the specific misery of an artist refreshing a dashboard for weeks. Per D-18, this product's claim is that its record is true; the honest state is "sent — no reply is normal and is not a rejection." **The pattern generalises: everywhere in domain 12 where the platform does not control the outcome, honesty about the unknown beats a comforting status.** Same rule as 12.03.02 D-04 (`Unknown`) and 12.02.03 D-01 (`Overdue`). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The pitch window lives in 12; the campaign around it lives in 21 | The window is gated by delivery state, which 21 cannot see (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Pitch and MEAD share one source of claims | One record, one story (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Submission state is honest about the absence of a reply | `pending` promises a response that is not coming (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Pre-fill the facts; ask only for the story | The Producer will not type what the session already knows |
