# Feature: Per-Partner Profile Conformance

> **Number**: 12.02.02
> **Parent**: [DDEX Delivery Messaging](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The per-partner knowledge that makes a standard message actually work: which ERN version a partner
consumes, which profile subset they implement, which optional fields they secretly require, which
extensions they invented, which technically-valid constructs they reject, what their acknowledgements
mean, and how long they normally take. DDEX is a standard in the way HTML is a standard — everyone
implements a different dialect, and the gap between the spec and the partner is where distribution
actually lives.

This feature exists because **the dialect is not obtainable from the standard**. DDEX publishes the
message; it does not publish what Apple does with it. That knowledge has exactly two sources —
certification with the partner, or a rejection an artist already paid for — which is why it is
simultaneously the distributor's real moat (DT-02) and WeJammin's largest cold-start deficit.

It is also the highest-blast-radius object in domain 12. One wrong rule breaks one release. One wrong
profile entry breaks **every** delivery to a partner, for every artist, silently (DT-08).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible and must never be. If an artist ever learns that Apple wants ERN 4.3 with a proprietary extension while Spotify takes 3.8.2, the platform has failed at its core job (12.02 D-03). The *consequence* reaches them as a per-store finding in 12.01.02 ("Apple rejects ALL-CAPS titles") or a per-store status in 12.03.02 — naming the **partner**, never the **profile** (12.01.02 D-09). Critically: when a profile gap blocks a delivery, the artist is told **it is our problem, not theirs** ("We can't deliver to Beatport yet — that's on us, not your release"). A profile gap presented as an artist gap is the single most damaging thing this feature can do, because it makes the artist search for a fault that does not exist in their work. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. The profile silently governs their work in one place — PC-07 decides which renditions are derived from the master they delivered (bit depth, sample rate, container). They never encode for a partner and never learn a partner's spec; the platform derives from one source (12.01.04 D-02). If a partner's rendition requirement cannot be met from the delivered source, that surfaces to the Producer as an asset finding in 12.01.04, phrased as a property of the source ("this master is 16/44.1; some stores want 24-bit"), never as a partner profile. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An Operator holds no fact about a partner's message dialect and has no stake in a release object (domain Role Matrix). There is no degraded view and no reason for one. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and this feature is the reason a Fan's most common question is answerable at all. "Why is this album on Spotify and not Apple?" is answered in 12.03.02's public-facing status vocabulary, never here — **confirming the assumption in parent Q-03 from this side: message and profile state have no Fan-visible or public projection.** A Fan who could see profile state could infer which partners WeJammin is failing to deliver to, which is commercially sensitive and useless to them. |

> **Every persona is `None`, and that is the correct answer, not a gap.** This feature's actor is a
> platform admin/integration engineer — a role ideation-index.md Q-02 flags as not yet a persona.
> It has a substantial internal surface (profile authoring, partner onboarding, certification runs,

## Behavior

A partner profile is **versioned data describing everything WeJammin knows about delivering to one
partner**. It is authored by platform admins, corrected by rejections (CX-04), pinned per delivery,
and retained forever.

### The one rule that governs everything below

> **A profile may decide the message's *shape*. It may never decide the record's *content*.**

This is not a preference — it is already forced by 12.02.01 D-01 (generation is a pure projection;
nothing is defaulted or authored). A profile entry that rewrites an artist's title at generation
time would make the generator an author, which is forbidden. The consequence was never stated, and
there is a live cross-feature defect hiding in it (DT-05): if the profile title-cases a title at
generation, it silently nullifies the artist's 12.01.02 D-06 override, and the platform sends
something the artist explicitly refused. Content-shaped partner knowledge reaches the message
**only** as a rendering the artist approved in 12.01.02 (D-04 there). See D-04 below.

### Profile content classes — what a profile must be able to express

The **class list is exhaustive and Q-01-invariant** (DT-04). Direct vs aggregator changes *how many*
profiles exist and *who populates them* — it does not change what a profile must express. Rule
*instances* are versioned data seeded by certification and corrected by the learning loop; the
classes below are the taxonomy the store must support. This mirrors 12.01.02's RC-01..RC-12 by
construction, because they are two projections of one store (D-01).

| # | Class | What it holds | Representative real divergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| PC-01 | **Party identity & routing** | Sender/recipient party identifiers (DPIDs), the partner-issued vendor/label account identity, and whether the partner requires the *label's* identity as sender rather than the distributor's | Some partners route on a distributor DPID; others require a per-label vendor ID they issue at onboarding. Ties to 12.01.05 (distributor of record) |
| PC-02 | **Message version & profile subset** | ERN version (3.8.2 vs 4.x), the release-profile subset (audio album / audio single / video / classical), the choreography standard version | ERN 4.x is current; large partners still consume 3.8.2. Direct delivery means supporting both concurrently (parent Q-02) |
| PC-03 | **Required-beyond-spec** | Elements optional in DDEX that this partner mandates in the *message* | The message-layer twin of 12.01.02 RC-06. One is "Beatport rejects a release with no mix name"; this is "Beatport's ERN must carry the element even when empty" |
| PC-04 | **Prohibited constructs** | Technically-valid ERN this partner rejects or silently drops | Multiple deals in one message; territory expressed as an inclusion list where the partner wants worldwide-plus-exclusions. **The class with no falsification channel — see DT-06** |
| PC-05 | **Partner extensions** | Proprietary namespaced elements the partner invented and requires or tolerates | Every major partner has some. None are in the standard; all are learned at certification |
| PC-06 | **Deal & usage vocabulary** | Which commercial models and use types the partner accepts, and which combinations are legal for them | A download-only partner rejects an on-demand-stream deal outright; some partners reject a message carrying both |
| PC-07 | **Asset rendition requirements** | Codec, bit depth, sample rate, container, artwork dimensions and colour space — the input to 12.01.04's derivation (D-02 there) | 24/96 FLAC vs 16/44.1 WAV; 3000×3000 RGB artwork. The Producer delivers one source; this class decides what is derived from it |
| PC-08 | **Asset reference & naming** | File naming conventions, how the message references assets, checksum/hash requirements | Naming is significant and partner-specific; a mis-named file is a delivery that lands and is ignored |
| PC-09 | **Transport binding & windows** | Transport (SFTP/S3/API), ordering, batching, completion markers, ingest windows and rate limits — consumed by 12.02.03 (CX-02) | Some partners ingest continuously; some run a weekly batch, which **is** the lead time 12.04.01 quotes |
| PC-10 | **Acknowledgement semantics & norms** | Which ack types exist, what each maps to in the normalised vocabulary (received/parsed/accepted/rejected/live), and the expected-response window resolved **per (partner × message type)** — *not* per ack stage (D-13) | The same word means different things per partner (12.02.03). The window is what makes `overdue` real — see the evidence rules below and DT-14. A takedown and a new release get separate windows for the same partner; a takedown is frequently expedited for legal reasons while a new release queues |
| PC-11 | **Territory, date & lead-time rules** | Date semantics (global instant vs local midnight per territory), embargo rules, minimum lead time, pre-order support, and which territories the partner actually operates in | A partner that does not operate in Japan cannot serve a Japan-only release, however cleanly it is delivered |
| PC-12 | **Update & takedown semantics** | How this partner wants a supersede (full re-send vs delta), the takedown form, message-ID rules | Determines whether 12.05's messages are executable at all — and must survive suspension (DT-09) |
| PC-13 | **Capability flags** | MEAD support (CX-05), Content ID/UGC, pre-order, price tiers, **and whether store-side read-back is available** | Read-back availability (12.01.02 Q-03) is profile data: it records which partners' knowledge can *only* ever be inferred, because they will never tell us they normalised our title |
| PC-14 | **Scar tissue** | The accumulated rejection history and certification results that are the evidence base for every inferred entry above | Not a rule — the audit trail that makes an entry's evidence tier checkable rather than asserted |

### An entry's authority is bounded by its evidence

The same discipline as 12.01.02 D-06, because it is the same store — but the falsification channels
are **different**, and one class has none at all (DT-06).

| Entry provenance | Authority | How it gets falsified |
|---|---|---|
| DDEX published standard | Binding | Self-falsifying — a non-conforming message fails our own schema check before it leaves (12.02.01 happy path step 4) |
| Partner's published spec / certification result | Binding | Contested by 2+ independent rejections on 2+ distinct releases (see below) |
| Partner's written instruction (email, onboarding) | Binding | As above |
| Inferred from rejections, human-reviewed (12.03.03 D-03) | Binding on the message shape | The next rejection, or the certification harness |
| **Inferred avoidance of a valid construct** (PC-04) | Binding | **Nothing. Ever.** We never send the construct, so we never learn we were wrong. Falsifiable only by the certification harness (DT-06, D-06) |
| Content-shaped knowledge (casing, taxonomy, field placement) | **Not binding here at all** — projects into 12.01.02 as a rule, reaches the message only as an artist-approved rendering | 12.01.02 D-06's artist override |

**Contested entries.** When **2 independent rejections on 2 distinct releases** contradict a
*documented* entry, the entry is marked `contested`: the observed behaviour takes effect after human
review, the documented value is retained alongside it, and the discrepancy is reported to the
partner — a spec/behaviour mismatch is usually a partner bug they want to know about. One rejection
never contests a documented entry; a single rejection is as likely to be a partner glitch as a fact
(12.03.03 D-03). *(Threshold deliberately identical to 12.01.02's promotion threshold — one store,
one evidence discipline. Initial value; see Q-05.)*

**Expected-response windows carry evidence too, and absence is not a default** (DT-14). The window
is resolved **per (partner × message type)** (D-13) and its precedence is **observed over
contractual** (D-12):

| Tier | Source | Condition | Marked | Artist-facing consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Observed** | ≥30 completed `signal-complete → first terminal ack` observations for that (partner × message type) within the last 180 days. Window = observed **p95**, rounded up to the next whole partner-business-day, floor 1 business day | `measured` | 12.02.03 may raise `Overdue` — we have measured what usual is |
| 2 | **Contractual** | The partner states a processing SLA in the delivery agreement, and tier 1's sample floor is not met | `stated` | As above, attributed to the partner |
| 3 | **Seeded** | Neither exists | `unmeasured` | 12.02.03 must say "Apple has had this 6 days. We don't yet know what's normal for them." — never "longer than usual", which would claim a norm we do not have. No artist-visible `Overdue` qualifier |
| — | **Unconditional ceiling** | Independent of tier | — | Internal escalation fires at a platform-wide **10 partner-business-days** regardless (D-12). Nothing artist-facing — the ceiling exists to wake an operator, never to make a claim |

The **artist-facing expectation clause is a different statistic from the alarm**: the "usually N days"
copy is the observed **p50 (median) at N ≥ 10**, and below N = 10 no expectation clause is shown at
all (12.02.03 D-05). Two statistics coexist by design — p95@30 fires the ladder, p50@10 backs the
copy — because quoting a p95 after the word *usually* misdescribes the partner.

The split is the point: **internal escalation needs a number; artist-facing claims need evidence.**
Dressing a default as a norm on the most-viewed screen in the domain is the domain's ratified "never
claim more than you know" pattern failing at exactly the moment it matters.

**Why observed outranks contractual, and what does *not* argue for it.** The inversion is supported
from inside this file: DT-07 (a profile certified in March is wrong in July and *nothing tells us*)
and DT-15 (partner documentation is stale, thin, and changed without announcement) are precisely the
argument against treating a stated SLA as authoritative. **Explicit non-support**: the contested-entry
rule above is an *analogy*, not an argument, and must not be cited as one — it fires on 2 independent
rejections on 2 distinct releases, which is a falsification **event**, whereas a timing divergence
produces no rejection at all. A partner inside our observed window but outside their own written SLA
is **not late by the window**; that divergence is a commercial signal routed to partner management
(12.02.03 D-05), never surfaced to the artist — and it currently has no owner, no surface and no
threshold anywhere (Q-07).

### Resolution is keyed by more than the partner

"Apple's profile" is imprecise and the imprecision is a bug (DT-11). A profile resolves on
**(partner × release type × ERN version × deal shape)**, optionally narrowed by territory where
PC-11 differs. DDEX profile subsets are per release type by construction — a partner-keyed lookup
silently emits an album profile for a single. A release-type change in 12.01.01 therefore
**re-resolves the profile and regenerates**, exactly as a date change does (12.02.01 edge cases).

### Certification is a standing harness, not an onboarding gate

Partners change requirements by email, with no changelog, often after the change is live. A profile
certified in March is wrong in July and **nothing tells us** — except an artist's rejection, which
is a bill we sent to a user for our own staleness (DT-07).

The certification suite therefore runs: at onboarding (the gate — D-03), **weekly** thereafter, on
every profile version change, and on any partner announcement. Weekly bounds an artist's exposure to
a stale profile at ≤7 days. It runs against the partner's test/certification endpoint where one
exists — which is also the only falsification channel PC-04 avoidance entries have (DT-06), and the
only one that costs no artist anything. Where a partner offers no persistent sandbox, its avoidance
entries are permanently unfalsifiable and must be recorded as such (Q-04).

### Profile edits are the most dangerous write in the domain

12.03.03 D-03 requires human review of rules **learned from rejections**. Nothing guards a
**hand-authored** profile edit, and a hand edit has the same blast radius (DT-08). Therefore:

- Every profile change is a **new version**; versions are immutable and never deleted. A rollback is
  a new version, not an erasure — a pinned version referenced by a retained message must resolve
  forever, or 12.02.01's retention stops explaining anything.
- Profile edits are **never last-write-wins**. Two admins editing one version conflict; the second
  sees a diff and re-bases. (Contrast 12.01.02's per-field last-write-wins for release metadata —
  correct there, because the blast radius is one release; here it is every artist.)
- Structural entries (PC-01..PC-06, PC-09, PC-12) require a **second reviewer**. Content-shaped and
  advisory entries do not.
- Every change is attributed and durable (Audit Log & Provenance Ledger cross-cut). An edit by an
  admin that unblocks a release they hold an interest in is **flagged for a second party** (DT-13).

### The profile holds no personal data — and that is load-bearing

A profile describes a *partner's* dialect: versions, elements, transports, norms. It holds no
contributor names, no party identities of users, no PII (DT-12). Unlike the retained ERN — which
carries contributor names and faces a real GDPR/provenance conflict (12.02.01 Q-01) — **a profile
version can be retained forever with no statutory tension.** That is what makes the pinned reference
durable, and it partially de-risks 12.02.01 Q-01: even if a retained message must one day be
minimised, the *profile it was generated against* survives, so "which rules did we send that under?"
stays answerable even where "what exactly did we say about Jamie?" cannot.

### Under the aggregator path, profiles split — they do not disappear

Parent Q-01 dominates this feature's *size*, not its *model* (DT-04). Via an aggregator:

- **Delivery profiles** (N≈1–3): the aggregator is itself a DDEX partner with its own dialect, its
  own required-beyond-spec fields, its own transport and its own ack semantics. Every class above
  still applies. The work collapses; it does not vanish.
- **Knowledge profiles** (N≈150): downstream store rejections still arrive — via the aggregator —
  attributed to stores WeJammin has no transport to. Those stores still need PC-03..PC-08, PC-11 and
  PC-13 so that 12.01.02 can warn the artist *before* delivery, and so the learning loop still
  compounds. Their PC-09 (transport) is simply empty.

This is why the model is specifiable now, and why the provisional deferral it replaced — the SURFACE draft's block on parent Q-01, which framed the whole class taxonomy as un-writable until direct-vs-aggregator resolved — was mis-scoped: the content classes (PC-01..PC-14) are Q-01-invariant, so Q-01 governs cardinality and cost, never shape (DT-04, D-05).

### Happy Path

The path that runs ~40 times per release, with no human in it:

1. Delivery is scheduled; 12.01.02 validation is clean across the selected store set and the
   readiness gate (12.01.03) is green.
2. At hand-off, the generator (12.02.01) requests the profile for
   **(partner × release type × ERN version × deal shape)**.
3. The partner-knowledge store resolves it at its current version and **pins that version to this
   delivery** — pinning the validation projection at the same instant, to the same version number,
   because there is one store (12.01.02 D-07).
4. The profile answers the generator's questions about **shape only**: version, subset, required
   elements, extensions, constructs to avoid, deal vocabulary (PC-02..PC-06). It answers no question
   about content — the artist's canonical values and their approved renderings (12.01.02 D-04) are
   the only content inputs.
5. The generator emits a message conforming to that profile and self-validates it (12.02.01 step 4).
6. Conformance checks derived from **the same pinned profile** run before transport — including the
   partner's known avoidance constructs (PC-04), which the DDEX schema will not catch.
7. PC-07 renditions are derived from the stored source (12.01.04 D-02); PC-08 naming and checksums
   are applied.
8. Choreography (12.02.03) reads PC-09 for transport and ordering, and PC-10 for what to expect back
   and when.
9. The partner's ack is normalised through PC-10 into the internal vocabulary and written to
   per-store status (12.03.02) — never claiming more than the ack supports (12.02.03 D-02).
10. The pinned profile version is recorded alongside the retained message, so "why did we send that?"
    is answerable years later (DT-03).

#### Second path: partner onboarding and certification (the admin path)

1. A commercial relationship exists (or the aggregator relationship covers the partner). Admin
   creates a profile in `Draft`.
2. Admin authors PC-01..PC-13 from the partner's spec, style guide and onboarding correspondence.
   Every entry is tagged with its evidence source at authoring time — an untagged entry cannot be
   promoted out of Draft.
3. **Model-gap check**: any entry requiring a fact the release model has no concept of blocks
   certification here. The partner stays un-offerable and no artist ever meets the gap (D-08).
4. **Truth check**: any entry that would require the platform to assert something it knows to be
   false — a distributor-formatted (P) line over the artist's, a fabricated identity — is not
   implementable. The construct is not certifiable and the conflict escalates commercially, never
   technically (DT-10, D-09).
5. Profile moves to `Certifying`. The suite runs against the partner's test endpoint: representative
   releases per release type, per deal shape, including known-hard cases (compilation, classical,
   featured artists, explicit, territory-excluded, takedown, update).
6. Partner confirms conformance. Structural entries pass second review. Profile → `Active`.
7. The store becomes offerable in 12.03.01 (D-03) — and only now.
8. The profile enters a **probation window**: the first **25 deliveries or 14 days, whichever is
   later**. Rejections in probation are treated as **profile defects until proven otherwise** and
   auto-escalate to review at priority — because a new partner's rejections are far more likely to be
   our ignorance than the artist's error, and the artist must not absorb our onboarding cost.
9. The certification suite thereafter runs weekly and on every version change, indefinitely.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Day one: no profiles exist at all** | The store picker is empty; no partner is offerable (D-03); the product cannot distribute. This is DT-02's cold start rendered as a real state, and it is the same question as 12.01.02 Q-02 | Nothing shippable. This is a launch-blocking fact, not a UI state — see Q-01/Q-02 |
| Partner changes requirements without notice | Rejections arrive (12.03.03) → human review → profile version bump → affected releases redelivered (12.05.02), scoped to the rejecting partner only (12.03.03 D-02) | "Apple changed a rule. We've fixed it and resent — nothing for you to do." |
| Partner changes requirements **and announces it** (version sunset, spec bulletin) | Proactive migration window; certification suite re-run against the new spec before the cutover; no artist ever hits it | Nothing, if handled before the deadline |
| Profile updated mid-flight | The version pinned at hand-off governs to completion; the update applies to deliveries not yet started | Nothing |
| Profile updated between validation and generation | **Impossible by construction.** Both pin at hand-off to one version (12.01.02 D-07) | Nothing |
| Profile and validation rule pack disagree | **The trust-destroying case.** Structurally impossible, not monitored: one store, one version number, two read paths (D-01) | — (must never surface) |
| **Profile has no entry for something the generator needs** | Generation is blocked; **nothing is defaulted** (12.02.01 D-01). This is a certification defect that escaped step 3 of the onboarding path | "We can't deliver to Beatport yet — **that's on us, not your release.**" Attribution is mandatory: a profile gap presented as an artist gap sends them hunting a fault that does not exist |
| Partner requires a field the release model has no concept of | Caught at certification (onboarding step 3); the partner is never offered. Reaching delivery means certification was incomplete — treat as a certification bug, not a delivery bug. *(Resolves the `[PENDING]` on this row in 12.02.01's edge-case table from this side.)* | Nothing — the store was never offerable |
| **Profile entry would require asserting a falsehood** (partner mandates a distributor (P) line over the artist's) | Not implementable at any price. The construct is not certifiable; escalate commercially. A profile cannot be permitted to make the platform lie about ownership on behalf of every artist at once (DT-10) | Nothing, unless it costs the partner — in which case the store is simply absent, and that is the correct outcome |
| **Profile entry would rewrite artist-authored content at generation** | Refused by construction. Shape yes, content never (D-04). Content knowledge lives in 12.01.02 and reaches the message only as an approved rendering | Nothing. The alternative is the platform silently overriding an artist's explicit 12.01.02 D-06 decision — see next row |
| **Artist overrode an inferred blocking rule (12.01.02 D-06); the profile still "knows" the partner's preference** | The override wins. The generator projects the approved canonical value; the profile's content preference is inert at generation. The rejection-or-acceptance that follows is the falsification signal 12.01.02 DT-06 requires | "We think Beatport will reject this. Sending it as you wrote it." A profile that quietly complied would destroy the only falsification channel the rule pack has — and lie to the artist |
| New partner onboarding | Profile authored, model-gap and truth checks passed, conformance-certified with the partner, structural entries second-reviewed, then `Active` + probation | Store appears in the picker only once certified (D-03) |
| **Rejection during the probation window** | Treated as a profile defect by default; auto-escalated to review at priority; the artist is not asked to change anything until the profile is cleared | "Deezer just came online for us and rejected this. That's almost certainly our setup, not your release — we're on it." |
| Partner deprecates an ERN version | Profile → `Deprecated`; migration window; every profile on that version moves; certification re-run on the new version before cutover | Nothing, if handled ahead of the deadline |
| Deprecation deadline passes with no migration | Profile → `Suspended`. Store unavailable in 12.03.01 with a reason; queued releases **held, not failed** | "Beatport is unavailable right now — their end of our setup, not your release." |
| Partner rejects something the profile says is valid | Profile is wrong. Rejection feeds back via 12.03.03; 1 rejection ⇒ review, not an automatic change | Handled by 12.03.03 |
| **2+ independent rejections contradict a *documented* entry** | Entry marked `contested`; observed behaviour takes effect after review; documented value retained alongside; discrepancy reported to the partner | Nothing — but the partner hears about it, which is how a spec gets fixed for everyone |
| **Partner silently normalises instead of rejecting** (12.01.02 DT-10) | The profile learns **nothing** — the delivery succeeded. Detectable only via store-side read-back, which PC-13 records as available-or-not per partner | Nothing, which is the problem. For read-back-less partners, the profile's content-shaped entries are falsifiable only through artist overrides (12.01.02 Q-03) |
| **Avoidance entry (PC-04) is wrong — the partner would have accepted the construct** | Nothing happens, forever. We never send it, so we never learn. The certification harness is the only channel; where a partner has no sandbox, the entry is permanently unfalsifiable (DT-06) | Nothing. Silent over-restriction is this feature's version of 12.01.02 DT-06's unfalsifiable rule — and here the artist cannot override it, because they are not in the loop |
| Transport credentials expire / contract lapses | Profile → `Suspended`. **One partner event, not forty release failures** — fan-out is per-partner, and every queued release is held at once (CX-02) | Store shows unavailable with a reason; releases held, not failed |
| **Legally-compelled takedown while the profile is `Suspended`** (12.05.03) | **Suspension scopes to new deliveries only.** PC-12 takedown/update messages remain permitted against the pinned version. Suspending withdrawal alongside delivery would make a compelled removal unexecutable — a legal exposure, not an inconvenience (DT-09) | Nothing artist-facing. The removal happens |
| Two admins edit one profile version concurrently | **Never last-write-wins.** Second author sees a diff and re-bases. A silently merged profile corrupts partner knowledge for every artist | — (admin surface) |
| **Admin edits a profile that unblocks their own release** | Permitted but flagged to a second party; the edit is attributed and durable. Personas.md's multi-hyphenate fact applies to platform staff; the industry precedent (a distributor favouring its in-house label) is real (DT-13) | — (admin surface, audited) |
| Compromised or malicious admin edit | Highest-blast-radius write in domain 12. Structural entries require a second reviewer; every version is immutable and attributed; rollback is a new version. Detection is the audit trail, not prevention alone (DT-08) | — (see Q-06 for the residual) |
| Profile version deleted or purged while a retained message pins it | **Must be impossible.** A pinned version is immutable and retained forever. The profile holds no PII (DT-12), so no statutory duty ever compels its erasure — this is why "why did we send that?" survives even if the message itself must be minimised (12.02.01 Q-01) | Nothing |
| Partner-knowledge store unreachable at resolution | Generation blocks; nothing defaults; deliveries queue. Editing and validation are unaffected (they degrade per 12.01.02's own rules) | "We're holding delivery to your stores for a moment — nothing's wrong with your release." Never a partial delivery to the partners that did resolve |
| Only *some* profiles resolve for a multi-store delivery | **Partial: deliver to the stores that resolved; hold the rest.** Never presented as a complete delivery — 12.03.02's per-store status is the honest projection and this is exactly why it exists | "Sent to 11 of 12 stores — Beatport's on hold at our end." |
| Release type changes after generation, before send | Profile **re-resolves** on the new key; the queued message is discarded and regenerated, not amended (12.02.01 CX-03). An album profile emitting a single is a partner rejection at best and a mis-shelved release at worst (DT-11) | Transparent to the user |
| Deal shape changes (artist drops download rights) | Same: re-resolve, regenerate. PC-06 vocabulary differs per deal shape | Transparent |

| Partner requires the **label's** identity as sender, not ours (PC-01) | Resolvable only if the label's partner-issued identity exists. ### States

Two axes. Neither is persona-visible; the third column gives the admin view and the artist's
*consequence*, which is the only thing that reaches a persona.

**Profile lifecycle** (per partner × release type × ERN version × deal shape):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Empty** | No profile exists for this partner at all — the day-one state of the entire feature | Admin: empty partner list. Artist: the store simply does not exist in 12.03.01. Nothing claims to be ready, because nothing is |
| **Draft** | Being authored; entries may be untagged and unreviewed | Admin: editable, cannot promote. Artist: store not offered |
| **Certifying** | Model-gap and truth checks passed; conformance suite running with the partner | Admin: suite results per release type/deal shape. Artist: store not offered |
| **Active** | Certified, structural entries second-reviewed | Admin: read/edit with review. Artist: store selectable in 12.03.01 (D-03) |
| **Active (probation)** | First 25 deliveries or 14 days from first delivery, whichever is later | Admin: rejections auto-escalated as suspected profile defects. Artist: store selectable; rejections attributed to us, not them |
| **Contested** | 2+ independent rejections contradict a documented entry | Admin: both values shown, review required, partner notified. Artist: nothing — the store keeps working on the observed behaviour |
| **Deprecated** | Partner is sunsetting this version | Admin: migration deadline + affected release count. Artist: nothing, if migrated in time |
| **Suspended** | Credentials expired, contract lapsed, partner outage, or a missed deprecation deadline | Admin: cause + held-release count. Artist: store unavailable in 12.03.01 **with a reason**; releases held, not failed. **Takedown/update still permitted** (DT-09) |
| **Retired** | Partner relationship ended | Admin: read-only. Artist: store gone from the picker; live releases unaffected. Version remains resolvable forever — retained messages still pin it |

**Resolution** (per delivery, at hand-off — the runtime axis):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Resolving** | Generator requests a profile at hand-off | Nothing. Invisible in the happy path — no spinner reaches an artist for an internal lookup |
| **Pinned** | Version resolved and frozen to this delivery, with validation, at one version number (12.01.02 D-07) | Nothing during flight. Later, on the release: "sent against rule pack {v}, {date}" — the record that makes the retained message explainable |
| **Partial** | Some partners' profiles resolved, others did not | "Sent to 11 of 12 stores — Beatport's on hold at our end." Never a complete-delivery claim |
| **Unresolvable** | No profile for this key, or a gap in the resolved one | Generation blocked; nothing defaulted. "We can't deliver to Beatport yet — that's on us, not your release" |
| **Error** | Partner-knowledge store unreachable | Deliveries queue; editing and validation untouched. "We're holding delivery for a moment — nothing's wrong with your release" |
