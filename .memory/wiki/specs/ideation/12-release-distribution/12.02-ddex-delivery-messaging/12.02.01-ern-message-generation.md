# Feature: ERN Message Generation

> **Number**: 12.02.01
> **Parent**: [DDEX Delivery Messaging](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Turning the release object into a DDEX ERN — the standard XML message that tells a DSP a release
exists, what is in it, who owns it, where it may be sold and from when. Every delivery, update and
takedown in the domain is an ERN of some kind, so this is the narrow waist the whole distribution
path passes through.

It exists because a DSP will not read our database. The only way a fact WeJammin holds becomes a
fact Spotify acts on is by being asserted in a message. That makes this feature the point where
the provenance record stops being an internal artifact and becomes a **public claim made under the
artist's name** — which is why almost every decision below is about refusing to say things we do
not know.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Never sees an ERN in the normal path and never should. What they can open, per (release × store), is a **delivery receipt**: a plain-language rendering of what was asserted and when — "on 14 Aug we told Apple: 12 tracks, release date 14 Aug, sold everywhere except Japan, (P) 2026 Hollow Coast, Jamie Okafor credited on bass" — plus who confirmed each fact and when. This is the direct answer to "I don't know what my distributor told them", which is the defining frustration of the incumbent experience. Raw message download is Q-02. They cannot edit, re-run, or cancel a generation: the inputs are editable (12.01), the projection is not. |
| Producer | None | No permission, and the **most-represented party in the message** — their roles, their attestations and their (P)-line evidence are all projected into it. This is the domain's recurring shape (see the domain index's Role Matrix reading: high-power, low-authority) at its most extreme: the message is substantially a statement about the Producer's work, made without asking them. Their contribution is authored upstream in 02, 07 and 12.01, never here. A Producer who is *also* the release owner sees the receipt through the **Musician** lens — it is a release-ownership permission, not a Producer one. |
| Operator | None | Not visible, and structurally so: no Operator holds a fact that reaches an ERN. A studio owed a points position on the master appears in the message as a **rights holder** projected from 09 — under the Musician lens, never as an Operator permission. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The Fan's real question — "why is my favourite band's album on Spotify but not Apple?" — is answered by per-store status (12.03.02), never by message internals. This resolves the parent index's Q-03 from this feature's side: message state has no Fan-visible consequence, because the state a Fan cares about is the store's, not the message's. |

> **The platform-admin surface.** Message inspection, thread history, replay against a candidate
> profile, and the conformance test harness are real internal surfaces whose actor is a platform
> admin/integration engineer — a role `ideation-index.md` Q-02 flags as not yet a persona. The
> `None` rows above mean "no *persona* UI", not "no UI".

## Behavior

An ERN is generated per **(release × partner)**, not once per release — the same release yields
materially different messages for different partners (12.02.02). Generation is a **pure projection**
of already-locked facts: release object (12.01.01), identifiers (12.07), rights and territory
scoping (09 / 12.03.01), label copy (12.01.05), credits (02), dates (12.04.01), assets (12.01.04).
It authors nothing. Anything the generator would have to invent is a gap upstream, and must be
caught by validation (12.01.02) or the readiness gate (12.01.03) rather than defaulted here — a
defaulted ERN field is a fabricated fact delivered to a partner under the artist's name.

Message types in scope: the initial delivery, the update/supersede form (12.05.02), and the
takedown form (12.05.01). These are variations on one generator, not three (DT-03).

Every generated message is **retained verbatim** with the version of every input that produced it
and the version of the profile that shaped it. Retention has two jobs, and the second is usually
forgotten: it is the **evidence** when a partner and an artist disagree, and it is the **diff
baseline** that makes a safe update possible (DT-07). An unretained message is a fact the platform
failed to record, which is precisely what the product exists not to do.

### What gets projected — the fact-class mapping

> **This resolves the breadth pass's deferred field-level mapping.** The element-level map
> (which ERN element carries which fact, in which version's namespace) is **profile-derived by
> construction** — CX-01 says the profile is the generator's grammar, and the grammar differs per
> partner and per ERN version. Pinning element names here would contradict the reason 12.02.02
> exists, and would be fabrication while parent Q-01 (direct vs aggregator) and Q-02 (ERN version)
> are open. The element map belongs to `/write-architecture-spec`, downstream of those answers.
>
> What is **not** version-dependent, what is fixed now, and what an implementer actually needs is
> the table below: **which classes of fact must be projected, who solely owns each, and what
> happens when one is absent.** Every ERN version in commercial use carries these five classes;
> they differ in spelling, not in substance. See DT-09 for why the fork does not block this.

| Fact class | What it asserts to the partner | Sole source | If absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Envelope** | Who is sending, to whom, when, whether this is a **live or test** message, which thread this belongs to and which message it is | Platform + pinned profile (12.02.02). Live/test is derived from the profile's certification state, never chosen (D-05) | Cannot be absent — profile-derived |
| **Parties** | Every human or entity the message references: display artists, contributors, the label, the sender | 01 Identity (party records + party identifiers), 02 Credits (who, in which role) | **Block.** An unidentified party is the wrong-artist-page failure (12.03.04) — the most common real distribution disaster |
| **Resources** | Each recording: ISRC, title, duration, display artist, contributors and their roles, (P) line, language, parental warning, preview start point, and the asset renditions that carry it. Plus artwork | 12.07 (ISRC) · 12.01.01 (membership) · 02 (credits/roles) · 12.01.05 ((P) line) · 12.01.04 (assets) | **Block** (DT-01) |
| **Releases** | The product: UPC, title, display artist, label name, release type, release date, original release date, genre, (C) line, and the track listing with sequence and volume | 12.01.01 · 12.07 (UPC) · 12.01.05 · 12.04.01 (dates) | **Block** |
| **Deals** | The commercial terms per territory: where it may be sold, from when, under which use types and commercial models. **A takedown is a deal too** — it is expressed as terms ending, not as a delete | 09 Rights + 12.03.01 (territory) · 12.04.01 (dates) · 11 (licence terms where a licensed inclusion applies, 12.01.01 D-05) | **Block.** Never default to worldwide (12.03.01 D-04) |

The Deal class is the one that is routinely mistaken for a settings panel. It is not: every Deal
element is the assertion *"we hold the right to sell this, here, on these terms, from this date"*,
made to a counterparty who will act on it and to a market where someone else may hold an exclusive.
See DT-04.

### Happy Path

1. The delivery window arrives (12.04.01) for a release that is green at validation (12.01.02) and
   at the readiness gate (12.01.03), with stores and territories scoped (12.03.01) and identifiers
   assigned (12.07).
2. For each selected partner **independently**, the generator resolves that partner's profile
   (12.02.02) at its then-current version and **pins** it for the life of this delivery attempt.
   Pinning happens at generation start, not at scheduling (D-11).
3. It takes a **snapshot** of every input in the fact-class table and records the version of each.
   Edits made after the snapshot do not ride along; they produce a new generation or nothing.
4. It opens the message thread for this (release × partner), or resumes the existing one if this
   release has been delivered to this partner before, and mints a message ID within it (D-06).
5. It projects the snapshot into the five fact classes the pinned profile's grammar requires, with
   live/test derived from the profile's certification state (D-05).
6. It **self-checks the emitted message** against the pinned profile's schema and conformance rules.
   A message that fails our own check never reaches the partner. It does not re-check the release —
   that was validation's job and duplicating it creates a second rule source that will drift (D-08).
7. If this message supersedes a prior one in the thread, it is **diffed against the retained prior
   message**; deltas the artist did not ask for are surfaced for acknowledgement before hand-off
   (D-07), on 12.05.02's surface.
8. Message + asset manifest are handed to transport (12.02.03). Generation's involvement ends here.
9. The message is retained verbatim, keyed to release, partner, thread, message ID, every input
   version, and the pinned profile version — so "why did we send that?" is answerable years later.
10. The receipt (Role Lens) becomes available to the Musician for this (release × store).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A required fact has no source value | **Generation fails for that partner; nothing is defaulted.** The gap is a gate/validation escape — a bug in ours, not a problem with their release | Musician: "We can't send this to Apple yet — {plain-language gap}." Internally: recorded as a gate escape and routed to the rule pack, so the next artist is warned before delivery, not blocked at it |
| Generated message fails our own schema/conformance check while the gate and validation are green | Blocked before send. **This is definitionally our defect**, never the artist's, and must never be reported to them as a metadata problem | "Delivery to {partner} is held. This one's on us — we're fixing it." Platform-operator alert; never a raw schema error |
| Partner's profile requires a fact the release model has no concept of (e.g. a partner demanding an original-release-date distinction the model never captured) | **Resolves the breadth pass's deferred model-gap question.** That partner alone is blocked; every other partner proceeds on schedule. It needs a model change, not a workaround, and never a default. An internal model-gap request is raised against 12.01 | "Beatport needs something we don't have on file yet. Your release still goes to the other 12 stores on 14 Aug — you can wait for Beatport or ship without it." (12.03.01 D-03: dropping a store is a supported, costed choice) |
| Same release delivered to 40 partners | 40 independent generations, threads and retained messages. One failure blocks one partner (D-09) | Status per store (12.03.02), never one "delivered" flag |
| Rights change between gate-green and the delivery window (co-owner revokes; a territory shrinks) | Generation projects the **current** rights, finds a mismatch with the pinned scope, and blocks **all** partners rather than silently narrowing the Deal. 12.01.01 D-07 already re-validates membership at hand-off; this is the same principle applied to terms | "The rights on track 4 changed since you scheduled this. We've held the whole release rather than quietly selling it in fewer places." |
| Rule-pack/profile update between scheduling and the window invalidates a green release | Release falls back to blocked **before** the window with an explanation; it never fails silently at it (domain CX-04) | "Apple changed a rule. We caught it before your date — here's the one thing to fix." |
| Regeneration after a profile or rule-pack update | New message in the same thread; supersedes via 12.05.02 semantics, never silently overwrites | "We're resending to Apple with a fix — nothing for you to do." |
| Release date changed after generation, before hand-off | The queued message is **discarded and regenerated**, never amended (domain CX-03). An amended in-flight message is how duplicates get made | Transparent |
| Release edited *during* generation | Generation is a snapshot taken at step 3; the edit does not ride along. If the edit is material, the generated message is discarded before hand-off and regenerated from a fresh snapshot | Transparent |
| **Two deliveries to the same partner for the same release in flight** (scheduled delivery + a manual redelivery) | The second generation finds the open thread and **supersedes within it**; it never opens a parallel thread. A parallel thread is a duplicate release on the store — worse than a failure, because it looks like success (12.02.03 DT-03) | Nothing. The artist should never be able to cause this and never has to know it was possible |
| **Takedown requested for a (release × partner) with no accepted thread** | You cannot take down what was never delivered. Generation **refuses to emit a takedown**; the queued delivery is cancelled instead. Same button, different mechanism | "Apple never took this in the first place — we've cancelled the delivery instead of removing it." |
| **Takedown requested while a delivery to that partner is in flight** | The race with the worst outcome: a takedown referencing a thread the partner has not processed can be discarded, after which the release goes live and stays live. The takedown is **queued behind the acknowledgement** — we wait for the partner to confirm they have it, then supersede | "Removing from Apple — waiting for them to confirm they've received it first, so the removal can't get lost. Usually {N} hours." |
| Takedown/update requested for a release delivered by a **prior distributor** (imported catalog) | No thread exists, because we never sent it. Generation cannot address a message into someone else's thread. Routes to 12.08 | "We didn't deliver this one — DistroKid did. Only they can take it down. Here's what to ask them for." |
| Update to a live release regenerates the whole message and an **unrelated fact has changed since** (someone corrected rights last month) | Caught by the diff (D-07). ERN updates are whole-truth reassertions, not deltas — the unrelated change would otherwise silently overwrite the partner's copy | "Heads up: this also changes track 7's (P) line, because the rights record was corrected on 3 Jun. Send both, or hold?" |
| One human appears on a recording under several roles (drummer + mixer + producer) — **the multi-hyphenate, which is the common case, not an edge one** | Every role is projected. The generator never collapses a person to a "primary" role, because dropping a role is dropping a credit (D-10). If the pinned profile cannot represent multi-role parties, that is a profile constraint surfaced as a per-store finding at validation — never a silent collapse here | Nothing at generation. If a partner genuinely cannot carry it: "Beatport will only list Jamie once. We'll list them as {role}; our record keeps all three." |
| Display artist name has an ambiguous party (two acts with the same name) | The message carries a party reference, not a string. No resolved party ⇒ block; a guessed party is the wrong-artist-page disaster (12.03.04) delivered flawlessly | "Which 'Nirvana' is this? We won't guess — it'd end up on someone else's page." |
| Contributor/title contains characters the partner cannot carry (non-Latin script, diacritics) | The per-store **derived rendering** is projected; the canonical value is never altered (12.01.02 D-04). Both are retained, so the receipt shows what was actually sent, not what we wished we had sent | Surfaced earlier at validation; nothing new here |
| **Malicious or structure-breaking input** — a title containing markup or control characters | Content is encoded at projection; a value that cannot be safely represented is a validation finding upstream, never a silently mangled field. **No input value may ever alter the message's structure** | Handled at 12.01.02 with a concrete compliant example |
| Same recording on two releases delivered to the same partner at once (single + album) | Legitimate and common. One ISRC in two messages is **not** a collision and must never be treated as one (12.07 D-01: one recording, one ISRC, forever) | Nothing |
| Release date sits near a timezone boundary | The date is projected through the partner's **declared date semantics** (profile data), never as a naive date. A day's error drops the record early in one hemisphere or late in another, and can move it into the wrong chart week (DT-13) | Nothing, when correct. The date the artist chose is the date they get |
| An asset rendition the partner requires cannot be derived from the source master | The manifest cannot be completed, so that partner is blocked at hand-off. Assets are derived at transfer (12.02.03), but the manifest is generation's assertion that they will exist | "Beatport wants a higher-quality master than the one on file. The other stores are fine." |
| Delivering party changes between scheduling and the window (a label deal is signed) | Label copy and distributor of record (12.01.05) are now different facts. Generation blocks rather than projecting stale ones — the message names who controls the master | "Your label deal changed who this release comes from. Confirm the new label copy and we'll send." |
| Release has zero deliverable recordings at the window | Cannot occur — 12.01.01 gates it. If it does, generation refuses and records it as a gate escape | "Something's wrong on our side — we've stopped rather than sent something empty." |
| Storage of a retained message fails after the message was handed to transport | **The message was sent and we cannot prove what we sent.** Treated as a Sev-1 provenance incident, not a storage error: hand-off must not be reported as complete until retention is durable | Receipt shows the Error state (below) with an honest admission |

### States

Generation state is per **(release × partner)** — one release is legitimately in several of these
at once, which is why per-store status (12.03.02) is a first-class object rather than a flag.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Delivery scheduled; window not reached | "Scheduled for {date}" — no message internals |
| Generating | Window reached; profile pinned, snapshot taken | Nothing (sub-second in the happy path; not a user-visible phase) |
| Blocked | A required fact is absent, a model gap, a rights change, or our own conformance check failed | Plain-language reason on the release + the choice to wait or drop that store; platform-side alert |
| Awaiting acknowledgement | Diff (D-07) found deltas the artist did not ask for | "This also changes {N} other things. Review before we send." |
| Generated | Message built and self-validated, not yet handed off | Nothing (invisible in the happy path) |
| Handed off | Passed to transport with its asset manifest | Status moves to 12.02.03's vocabulary |
| Superseded | A newer message exists in the thread | Prior message retained and marked superseded; the receipt shows the thread |
| Discarded | Generated but never sent — an input changed before hand-off | Nothing. Retained anyway, marked never-sent, so the record has no gaps |
| Refused | Takedown requested against a thread with nothing accepted in it | "Apple never took this in — cancelling the delivery instead." |

**The Musician's read-only receipt view** carries the template's UI states:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Receipt opened; retrieving the retained message and its input versions | Skeleton of the receipt rows |
| Empty | No message has ever been sent to this store for this release | "We haven't sent this to Apple yet." + what it's waiting on — never a blank panel |
| Populated | Retained message resolves fully | Plain-language rendering of every fact asserted, when it was sent, who confirmed each fact and when, and the thread of any prior messages |
| Partial | The message is retained but an input version no longer resolves — e.g. a contributor's party record was erased on request (Q-01's collision, arriving in the UI) | "Sent 14 Aug. One contributor's details have since been removed at their request, so we can't show that part." The message itself is never silently re-rendered to hide the gap |
| Error | The retained message is missing or unreadable | "We can't show you what we sent to Apple. That's our failure, not yours, and we've recorded it as one." No retry theatre — a missing retained message does not come back |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The generator may default missing fields to keep deliveries flowing | Standard distributor pragmatism — a "sensible default" ships releases | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's sharpest collision with the thesis. A defaulted ERN field is a **fabricated fact delivered under the artist's name to a partner**, which is a machine-generated version of the exact reconstruction failure problem-statement.md identifies as the root cause. Defaults are how "℗ 2024 {artist name}" ends up on a master owned by a band. Generation must fail loudly and route the gap back to the gate. Accepted cost: more blocked deliveries than a competitor, which is the trade D-10 already made. |
| DT-02 | Every generated message must be retained verbatim with its input versions | Provenance thesis (D-18); Audit Log & Provenance Ledger cross-cut; the "my distributor won't tell me what they sent" complaint | ✅ CONFIRMED — cheap to do, and it is the only evidence when a partner and an artist disagree. It also makes the rejection→rule feedback loop (12.01.02 DT-02) analysable after the fact rather than only in the moment. An unretained message is a fact the platform failed to record, which is precisely what the product exists not to do. DT-07 later found a **second** justification nobody had priced: retention is also the diff baseline that makes updates safe. |
| DT-03 | Takedown and update are separate machinery from initial delivery | Sweep listed "Takedowns & Redelivery" as its own candidate, implying separate mechanics | ❌ REJECTED at the *message* layer — they are ERN variants sharing one generator, one profile resolution and one transport. But ✅ CONFIRMED at the *product* layer: their triggers, permissions and human consequences are genuinely different (a takedown can be involuntary and legally driven). Hence 12.05 exists as a sub-domain while its messages are generated here. This split — shared mechanism, distinct product surface — is the single most important structural call in the domain. |
| DT-04 | The Deal section is distribution configuration — a territory picker rendered as XML | The way every distributor UI presents it, and the way it reads in the spec | ❌ REJECTED — **each Deal is a rights assertion made to a counterparty**: "we hold the right to sell this, here, on these terms, from this date." Territory is derived from the rights record precisely because it is a contractual fact with someone on the other end of it (12.03.01 D-01/D-04). This is where WeJammin's rights record leaves the building and becomes actionable by a third party, so the Deal list is the highest-stakes region of the message — and the region a naive implementation treats as checkboxes. Delivering into a territory covered by someone else's exclusive is a breach, not a bug, and it is committed *here*, in this fact class, by whoever thought a default was harmless. |
| DT-05 | Live vs test is a per-delivery choice an operator makes | It is one flag; every partner requires test deliveries during certification, so someone must set it | ❌ REJECTED as a human choice — it must be **derived from the profile's certification state** (12.02.02: `Certifying` ⇒ test, `Active` ⇒ live). The two failure directions are both unrecoverable and neither is loud: a test flag on a live delivery makes the release vanish into a partner's sandbox while our status board says sent; a live flag on a certification run **creates a real release from test data** on a real store, under a real artist's name. It is the highest consequence-to-keystroke ratio in the domain, which is exactly the kind of decision a human must not be offered. A domain expert expects this question; nothing in the breadth pass asked it. |
| DT-06 | The message thread is transport's concern — it is a delivery detail | Threading looks like a protocol nicety, and transport is what talks to the partner | ❌ REJECTED — the thread is keyed to **(release × partner)**, which is *generation's* unit, not transport's. Transport only knows what it was handed (domain R-03 rejected retention there for the same reason). The thread is the durable spine linking new → update → takedown for one release at one store, and it is what makes supersede-not-resend (12.02.03 D-03) mean anything: a message that supersedes must name what it supersedes. Owning it here also makes "what have we ever told Apple about this release?" one query against retention — which is the receipt in the Role Lens, and the only honest answer to the incumbent black box. |
| DT-07 | An update message carries only what changed | Every other system in software works this way, and the artist's mental model is "I fixed a typo" | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the nastiest thing in the feature. **An ERN update is a whole-truth reassertion**: the entire release is re-stated, and every field in the resend overwrites the partner's copy. So fixing a misspelled title on track 3 also re-asserts track 7's ℗ line, the deal terms, and every credit — at their *current* values, not the values that were live when the release shipped. An unrelated, correct upstream edit from three months ago silently rides along, and the artist experiences a mysterious change they never made to a record they thought they were leaving alone. Mitigation, and the reason it is tractable: **we retained the prior message** (DT-02), so regeneration can diff against exactly what the partner was last told and surface unintended deltas before send. This is what turns retention from a compliance artifact into a live safety mechanism, and it is the failure mode nobody designs for because it only bites months after launch. |
| DT-08 | A contributor has a role; the generator projects it | The obvious data shape, and the shape every credits form implies | ❌ REJECTED — **the multi-hyphenate is the structural fact of this product** (personas.md), so one human routinely appears on one recording as drummer, mixer and producer. Collapsing them to a "primary" role to fit a tidy party list is dropping credits — the exact evaporation-of-proof problem-statement.md names as the root cause, committed by our own bookkeeping rather than by anyone's memory. Every role is projected, always. Where a partner genuinely cannot carry multi-role parties, that is a **profile** constraint surfaced as a per-store finding at validation (12.01.02 D-01, D-09) — never a silent collapse at generation, because a silent collapse is indistinguishable from the credit never existing. |
| DT-09 | This feature cannot be specified until parent Q-01 (direct vs aggregator) and Q-02 (ERN version) resolve | The breadth pass's own stated reason for deferring the field mapping | ❌ REJECTED, which is why the field-mapping deferral above is now resolved. The fork changes the **target grammar**, not the **generation contract**. By CX-01 the grammar is profile data *by construction* — the generator has no partner knowledge of its own, so "which ERN version" and "whose party ID" are 12.02.02's answers arriving through a pinned profile, not branches in this feature. Everything genuinely owned here — projection-not-authorship (D-01), the five fact classes, retention (D-02), threading (D-06), the diff (D-07), test/live derivation (D-05), per-partner independence (D-09) — is invariant across both paths. An aggregator absorbs the partner relationships; it does not absorb our obligation to refuse to fabricate. The version-specific element map is genuinely blocked, and it is genuinely not this layer's job (`/write-architecture-spec`). |
| DT-10 | A release's 40 partner messages should be generated as one atomic batch — all or nothing | Transactional instinct; it feels safer to ship consistently or not at all | ❌ REJECTED — it inverts the domain's founding premise. "Delivery is asynchronous, partial and fails silently" is *why* per-store status is a first-class object rather than one flag. Atomic generation converts one partner's model gap into a total delivery failure, so the artist's album misses its date at 39 stores because Beatport wants a field. Partial delivery is not a degraded outcome here; it is the **correct** one, and the product's job is to name it honestly per store, not to prevent it by refusing to ship. |
| DT-11 | The raw ERN download is a transparency feature for the Musician | Radical transparency as a value; the file's own Q-02 leaning | ❌ REJECTED **as stated**, and the reframing changes the answer to Q-02. The Musician cannot read an ERN and would learn nothing from one — parent D-03 says they should never even meet the word. What answers "what did my distributor tell them?" is the **receipt**: the plain-language rendering, which is decided (D-13) and always available. The raw message's real consumers are three people the artist *hands it to*: their next distributor at exit (12.08), a label's ops person during an acquisition, and a lawyer in a dispute. That is a much stronger argument for the download existing than transparency-as-aesthetics — it makes the raw message part of what leaves with them, which is the direct answer to problem-statement.md Q-02 (earned vs hostile lock-in). It also means the artifact should be **exportable**, not merely viewable, which is a different feature than a download link. Still the owner's call (Q-02), but on better grounds. |
| DT-12 | Generation is deterministic — the same inputs and profile always produce the same message | Assumed silently by DT-07's diff and by DT-02's retention | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **required property**, not an observation, modulo the two fields that cannot be (message ID and creation timestamp). Non-determinism would quietly destroy both: the diff would report phantom deltas until it was ignored, and a retained message could no longer be shown to be a projection of the record rather than an artifact of the day it ran. Determinism is what lets us say "this message *is* what the record said on 14 Aug" instead of "this message is what our code emitted on 14 Aug". Worth stating explicitly because it is the kind of property that is free if asserted up front and unrecoverable if noticed in year two. |
| DT-13 | A release date is a date | Every UI in the industry presents it as one, and the artist thinks of it as one | ❌ REJECTED — it is a date **plus per-partner semantics**, and the naive projection is wrong in both directions at once: the record drops a day early in Auckland or a day late in Los Angeles. Two real consequences a domain expert expects and no node in this domain has mentioned: Global Release Day is Friday and chart weeks run Friday–Thursday, so a day's slip can push first-week numbers into the wrong chart week; and a premature drop in any territory forfeits new-release editorial eligibility that cannot be re-earned. The generator therefore projects the date through the partner's **declared date semantics** (profile data, CX-01) and never as a naive value. The canonical policy — what "14 Aug" means globally — belongs to 12.04.01; the point here is that this is where the mistake would be *made*, silently, by a correct-looking projection. |

Generation owns the thread and message ID (DT-06); transport's idempotency (12.02.03 D-03) is enforced *against* them. Regeneration discards rather than amends an in-flight message (CX-03). Takedown-during-flight must queue behind the acknowledgement — a race that spans both features and is owned by neither today.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Generation is a pure projection; nothing is defaulted or authored | A defaulted field is a fabricated fact delivered under the artist's name (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Every message retained verbatim with input versions and the pinned profile version | Evidence in a partner dispute, baseline for the update diff, and the substrate of the receipt (DT-02, DT-07) |
| D-03 | One generator for new/update/takedown; distinct product surfaces above it | Shared mechanism, different human consequences (DT-03) |
| D-04 | The **fact-class mapping is fixed here; the element-level mapping is profile-derived** and belongs to `/write-architecture-spec` | Element names vary by partner and ERN version — that variance is exactly what 12.02.02 owns (CX-01). Fixing them here would fabricate what parent Q-01/Q-02 have not decided, while the fact classes are invariant across both forks (DT-09) |
| D-05 | Live vs test is **derived from the profile's certification state**, never a human choice | Both failure directions are silent and unrecoverable; the highest consequence-to-keystroke ratio in the domain (DT-05) |
| D-06 | Generation owns the **message thread** per (release × partner); every message names its thread and what it supersedes | The thread is keyed to generation's unit, not transport's; it is what makes supersede-not-resend mean anything (DT-06, 12.02.03 D-03) |
| D-07 | A regenerated message is **diffed against the retained prior message**; deltas the artist did not request are surfaced for acknowledgement before hand-off | An ERN update is a whole-truth reassertion, so unrelated upstream edits ride along silently (DT-07). This is the mechanism behind 12.05.02 D-01 |
| D-08 | Generation **never re-validates the release**; it self-checks only its own output against the pinned profile | A second rule source drifts from the first, and drift tells the artist two contradictory things (12.02.02 DT-01). A self-check failure on a green release is our defect and is never reported as a metadata problem |
| D-09 | Generation is **per-partner independent**; no atomic batch across partners | Partial delivery is the correct outcome, not a degraded one — atomicity makes one partner's model gap miss the artist's date at 39 stores (DT-10) |
| D-10 | Every contributor role is projected; a person is never collapsed to a primary role | The multi-hyphenate is the structural fact of the product; a silent collapse is indistinguishable from the credit never existing (DT-08) |
| D-11 | The profile is **pinned at generation start** (the delivery window), not at scheduling | Pinning at scheduling would knowingly deliver against a stale profile; pinning live would make the retained message unexplainable. Resolves the ambiguity between 12.02.02 D-02 and domain CX-04 |
| D-12 | Dates are projected through the partner's **declared date semantics**, never as a naive date | A day's error costs a chart week or new-release editorial eligibility (DT-13); the canonical policy stays in 12.04.01 |
| D-13 | The Musician-facing artifact is a **plain-language receipt**, always available; the raw message is a separate question | The artist cannot read an ERN and parent D-03 says they must never meet the word. The receipt is what answers the black-box complaint (DT-11) |
| D-14 | Generation is **deterministic** given the same snapshot and pinned profile, modulo message ID and creation timestamp | Otherwise the diff reports phantoms and the retained message stops being provable as a projection (DT-12) |
| D-15 | A takedown is **refused** where no message in the thread was ever accepted; the queued delivery is cancelled instead | You cannot remove what was never delivered — same button, different mechanism, and the distinction is invisible to the artist unless we draw it |

| Q-01 | **Message retention period.** Provenance says forever; ERNs contain personal data (contributor names, party identifiers), so GDPR minimisation says otherwise. A real conflict between the product thesis and a statutory duty — constraints.md leaves GDPR unresolved, and domain Q-08 (multi-party export) is the same contradiction from another angle: a retained message is not one user's data. The Partial receipt state above is what this conflict looks like in the UI once it is real. **Recommendation**: retain the message verbatim and tombstone the *party record* it references, so what we said stays provable while the person stops being findable — but whether that satisfies a regulator is a legal question, not a product one. | User | `/create-prd-security` |
| Q-02 | **Can a Musician export the raw message?** Reframed by DT-11: not a transparency feature for the artist, who cannot read it, but an **export artifact for whoever they hand it to** — their next distributor at exit (12.08), a label's ops team, a lawyer in a dispute. That makes it the cheapest concrete proof that the lock-in is earned rather than hostile (problem-statement.md Q-02, domain Q-05). **Recommendation**: yes, as part of the 12.08 export rather than as a download link on the receipt — and note it is not free, because Q-01's answer bounds what can still be exported years later. Values call, not an implementation one. | User | `/ideate-validate` |

If `/create-prd` treats them as blocking ideation, that is a misreading of what this layer owns. | User | `/create-prd` (parent Q-01/Q-02) |
