# Feature: Delivery Choreography & Acknowledgement

> **Number**: 12.02.03
> **Parent**: [DDEX Delivery Messaging](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Physically handing the message and its assets to each partner in the order and by the transport
they require, then interpreting what comes back. Partners disagree about everything here —
transport (SFTP, S3, API), whether assets go before or after the message, batching, naming
conventions, and whether an acknowledgement means "received", "parsed" or "accepted". This is
where distribution's defining property lives: **delivery is asynchronous, partial, and fails
silently.**

The feature exists because the artist's question is never "what transport did you use?" — it is
"where is my record, and is anyone doing anything about it?" Every incumbent answers that question
with a spinner. This feature's whole job is to convert an asynchronous, partially-observable,
frequently-silent machine into a set of statements that are **true** and that name what is
happening — including when nothing is.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees delivery progress as human states per store — "sending to Apple, 60% of files, ~40 min left", "Apple has it", "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days" — with honest elapsed time and, where we have measured it, an expectation. Never sees a transport, a path, or a message ID. Can act only where action exists: chase, or answer the forced choice when a store is silent into the release date (see *Overdue past the release date*). The read-only access matters most in the failure case: silence for six days is the incumbent experience, and naming the silence is a real product feature. |
| Producer | None | **`None` is a lens on the role, not a gate on the human** (DT-11). Transport is not a Producer concern — their work ended at the master, and giving them controls over another party's commercial distribution would be wrong. But the *outcome* of their work reaches them through 12.03.02's read-only board, and a Producer who is also the release owner (the self-releasing artist-producer — the common case per personas.md) sees this feature through the **Musician** lens on their own release. Implementers must not read this row as "user has producer role ⇒ hide". |
| Operator | None | Not visible, and there is no path by which it becomes visible. The Operator's inventory is time and space (personas.md); a release delivery has no venue, studio or booking surface at any point in its lifecycle. This is a genuine structural `None`, not an unexplored one. |
| Fan | None | No transport surface, and never any. **But the Fan-visible consequence is real and is not zero** (DT-04): a pre-save link (12.04.03) cannot exist until a store returns an `accepted` ack, and a store that accepts and then goes silent produces pre-saves that never fire on release day. The Fan experiences this feature's failures without ever seeing the feature. This contradicts the parent index Q-03's stated assumption ("assumed no" Fan-visible consequence) — logged here, resolution owned by the parent. |

## Behavior

Choreography is per-partner and per-profile, and it **has no transport logic of its own** (CX-02):
it executes what the pinned profile (12.02.02) describes — which transport, in what order, with
what naming, what an ack means, and how long a response should take. Everything partner-specific is
profile data; what lives here is the **step vocabulary those profiles compose from**, the
**ordering invariants** that hold regardless of transport, and the **honesty rules** on what comes
back.

### The step vocabulary

The breadth pass left a deferral marker on this section asking for "the real matrix" of per-partner choreographies, and
made it conditional on parent Q-01 (direct vs aggregator). **That framing was wrong, and resolving
it is a correction, not a deferral.** The per-partner matrix is not a spec gap here — it is
12.02.02's data, authored per partner and never enumerable in this file. What Q-01 changes is *how
many* profiles exist and *who authors them*; it does not change what a choreography step is. The
specifiable thing — and the thing an implementer actually needs — is the contract every profile
composes against, and it is invariant across both arms of Q-01:

| # | Step | What it does | Failure meaning |
|---|------|-------------|-----------------|
| S1 | `resolve` | Pin the profile version for this delivery (12.02.02 D-02) | No certified profile → delivery never starts; store was not offerable (12.02.02 D-03) |
| S2 | `derive` | Produce the partner's asset renditions from the stored source (12.01.04) | Source asset cannot satisfy the profile → blocked upstream, not here |
| S3 | `stage` | Place each asset at the partner's required location under the partner's naming convention | Transport fault — retryable, resumable |
| S4 | `verify-staged` | Confirm every asset is **complete at rest**, by whatever the profile's transport can prove | An asset is short/corrupt → S5 must not run |
| S5 | `deliver-message` | Transfer the ERN handed over by 12.02.01 | Transport fault — retryable; the message is never amended in place (CX-03) |
| S6 | `signal-complete` | Emit the partner's completion marker — batch completion file, API commit, bucket object convention | Until this succeeds the partner has not been told anything |
| S7 | `await-ack` | The response window opens | Window exceeded → `Overdue` (D-01) |
| S8 | `normalise` | Map the partner's ack vocabulary into the internal vocabulary | Unmappable ack → quarantine, never discard (DT-06) |
| S9 | `record` | Write to per-store status (12.03.02) and feed the profile learning loop (CX-04) | — |

The three common shapes in the wild are compositions of these, not separate machinery:

- **Batch/SFTP**: `stage` assets → `verify-staged` → `deliver-message` (manifest) → `signal-complete`
  (completion marker file). Order is significant; a message referencing assets not yet fully
  uploaded is rejected or, worse, **silently ignored**.
- **API**: `deliver-message` posted, assets by URL or upload, `signal-complete` is the commit call.
  Synchronous receipt, asynchronous acceptance — the sync 200 is S6 succeeding, *not* an ack.
- **Cloud bucket**: `stage` assets to a partner-owned bucket; `signal-complete` is an object
  convention. The most silent of the three — nothing responds at all until S7.

### Ordering invariants

These hold for every partner, every transport, every message type. They are the reason the step
vocabulary is worth naming.

| # | Invariant | Why |
|---|-----------|-----|
| I1 | **No message may reference an asset that has not passed `verify-staged`.** | The single most common batch-transport failure, and its worst form is silent: some partners neither reject nor process a manifest pointing at a partial file. It simply never happens, and we would report `Sent` forever. |
| I2 | **`signal-complete` is always last, and is the only step that makes the delivery visible to the partner.** | Before S6 the delivery is retractable at zero cost — no partner state exists to conflict with. After S6 every correction is a supersede (12.05.02), not an edit. This is the cheapest safety property in the domain. |
| I3 | **Every step is idempotent by (message ID, step).** A repeated step is a no-op, not a second effect. | D-03 / DT-03 — the duplicate-release hazard. |
| I4 | **Retries resume at the failed step; they never restart the sequence.** | Restarting re-runs `stage` (hours of transfer, DT-12) and, worse, risks a second `signal-complete` — which is exactly how a duplicate is born. |
| I5 | **The response window starts at `signal-complete`, not at first byte.** | A 6-hour asset transfer is not partner silence. Starting the clock at S1 would fire `Overdue` on our own slowness and blame the partner for it — a violation of D-06. |

### Acknowledgement normalisation

The same word means different things per partner, so partner acks are normalised into one internal
vocabulary. The rule is absolute: **never claim more than the ack supports** (D-02).

| Internal | Means | Surfaced to the artist as (12.03.02) |
|---|---|---|
| `received` | The partner has the bytes | `Received` |
| `parsed` | The message is well-formed; no decision yet | `Received` — **collapsed** |
| `accepted` | The partner accepted the release | `Accepted` |
| `partially-accepted` | Release accepted, ≥1 track rejected (DT-07) | Board has no equivalent — see Cross-Cut Notes |
| `rejected` | The partner refused | `Rejected` → 12.03.03 |
| `unmappable` | The ack does not map to anything we know | Quarantined; no state change |

**`parsed` collapses to `Received` for the artist, and that is deliberate.** The distinction is not
actionable by them — there is nothing different to do — and 12.03.02's vocabulary has no `parsed`.
Collapsing *downward* is safe because it under-claims; collapsing upward would be the sin D-02
exists to prevent. Choreography keeps the distinction internally because it is a real signal about
the partner (a partner that parses in 20 minutes and decides in 9 days has a different problem from
one that never parses at all), and because it feeds the profile learning loop (CX-04).

**`live` is not in this vocabulary and must never be inferred from an ack.** No partner ack means
live. `Live` is 12.03.02's, established from store-side truth (12.03.02 D-02).

### The expected-response window (resolves prior Q-01)

`Overdue` is only worth having if the window is real rather than decorative. The window is
per-partner profile data (CX-02), per **(partner × message type)** — a takedown is frequently
expedited for legal reasons while a new release sits in a queue, so one window per partner would be
wrong in both directions.

**(partner × message type) is the whole key.** Two axes were considered and declined:

- **Per ack stage** is **dropped, not merged** (12.02.02 PC-10's per-stage phrasing is withdrawn;
  see 12.02.02 D-13). The axes are orthogonal but the **statistic is not axis-neutral**: tier 1's
  interval is end-to-end (`signal-complete → first terminal ack`), and per-stage intervals are
  defined nowhere, so keying per stage would require inventing an observation model this file does
  not have. The third-source support usually cited for it — 12.03.02 DT-15 — is about **ack-wait
  versus live-wait**, which is a different distinction entirely.
- **Live-wait** (`accepted` but not yet live) is **not folded into this window**. It stays 12.03.02's
  concern and is evaluated there against store-local midnight plus the partner's publish window
  (12.03.02 D-07), not against any statistic here.

Window value resolves by precedence:

| Tier | Source | Condition | Marked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Observed** | ≥30 completed `signal-complete → first terminal ack` observations for that (partner × message type) within the last 180 days. Window = observed **p95**, rounded up to the next whole partner-business-day, floor 1 business day. | `measured` |
| 2 | **Contractual** | The partner states a processing SLA in the delivery agreement, and tier 1's sample floor is not met | `stated` |
| 3 | **Seeded** | Neither exists | `unmeasured` |

**Observed outranks contractual, and that inversion is intentional.** The window's job is to detect
*abnormality*, and normal is what the partner actually does, not what they said they would do. The
supporting evidence is inside the file that carries the contractual value: **12.02.02 DT-07** (a
profile certified in March is wrong in July and nothing tells us) and **12.02.02 DT-15** (partner
documentation is stale, thin, and changed without announcement) are precisely the argument against
treating a stated SLA as authoritative.

> **Explicit non-support, recorded so it is not cited again.** 12.02.02's **contested-entry rule**
> (2 independent rejections on 2 distinct releases contest a documented entry) is an **analogy for
> this inversion, not an argument for it**. That rule fires on a falsification **event**; a timing
> divergence produces **no rejection at all**, so the rule never fires on it and cannot decide the
> matter. An earlier draft cited it as though it did.

Where a partner is contractually late but behaviourally normal, that is a **commercial** signal —
recorded, routed to partner management, and never surfaced to the artist, whose board should say
"this is unusual for Apple", not "Apple is in breach of a contract you are not party to". A partner
inside our observed window but **outside** their own written SLA is **not late by the window**. That
comparison is a second path with **no owner, no surface and no threshold** in any file — a follow-on
gap created by this inversion, flagged at **12.02.02 Q-07**.

Two further consequences of the ratified precedence, recorded rather than discovered later:

- For a partner with a **published SLA but few deliveries**, we ignore a real observed number we
  already hold until the tier-1 sample floor is met. Accepted.
- A **low-volume partner, or an uncommon message type**, may never reach 30 observations in a rolling
  180 days and therefore sits **permanently** on the tier-3 seeded window with no `measured` value.
  Accepted — the price of a statistic resistant to a single slow week.

Thresholds and their justification:

| Number | Value | Justification | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| p50 minimum sample | N ≥ 10 | Median is stable at small N. Below this, no expectation clause is shown at all. Deliberately **lower** than the alarm floor — a reassurance clause should reach evidence sooner than an alarm does. | Reasoned |
| p95 minimum sample | N ≥ 30 | The 95th-percentile order statistic does not meaningfully exist below N=20; ~30 makes it non-absurd, and 30 over 20 buys a statistic resistant to a single slow week. The price is longer silence for low-volume partners, accepted. | Reasoned |
| Rounding and floor | Round **up** to the next whole partner-business-day; floor **1** business day | Part of the ratified rule, **not** an implementation detail: an unrounded window is not expressible on the counting clock D-07 locks. | Reasoned |
| Observation half-life | 180 days | Partners change behaviour without notice (12.02.02); older observations describe a partner that no longer exists. | **Tunable guess** |
| Seeded default | 5 partner-business-days | Long enough not to cry wolf on a normal queue; short enough to catch true silence inside one working week. | **Guess — one of two numbers here with no evidence behind it.** Flagged, not disguised. |
| Unconditional escalation ceiling | 10 partner-business-days | An always-on internal backstop that fires **regardless** of tier — it wakes an operator even when the tiered window is silent, seeded or wrong. Distinct from the tier-3 seed, which applies only when neither a stated nor an observed window exists. Internal only; never an artist-facing claim. Owned as profile-store policy at 12.02.02 D-12. | **Guess — tunable, not evidenced** (12.02.02 Q-05) |

**The window is counted in partner business days in the partner's timezone, not elapsed hours**
(DT-13). Elapsed time shown to the artist is wall-clock, because wall-clock is what they feel. When
the two diverge enough to explain the calm, say so:

> "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days — 4 Apple business days, so this includes a weekend. Usually 2.
> We've chased."

**When the window is `unmeasured`, the expectation clause is omitted entirely.** Saying "usually 2
days" with no measurement is a fabricated fact delivered to the artist as truth — the same defect
12.02.01 DT-01 rejects at the message layer, committed at the UI layer instead. At launch this is
the normal case, not the edge case (12.02.02 DT-02: WeJammin starts with zero accumulated partner
knowledge):

> "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days. We don't know yet what's normal for Apple — this is one of our
> first deliveries there. We've chased."

### Escalation ladder

| Trigger | What fires | Artist told? |
|---|---|---|
| Window exceeded (1×) | Automated chase via the partner's stated support channel, quoting the message reference. `Overdue` is surfaced only where the window is observed or contractual; a seeded cold-start window remains internal. | Only if release date ≤ 72h away |
| 2× window | Escalation to a named human partner contact | Yes |
| **10 partner-business-days since `signal-complete`, regardless of tier** | **Unconditional internal escalation ceiling** (12.02.02 D-12). Fires even where the tiered window is `unmeasured`, seeded, or measured-and-wrong — it is an always-on backstop, not a rung of the tiered ladder. Counted on D-07's clock, not wall-clock. Its value is a tunable guess (12.02.02 Q-05) | **No — internal only.** It never raises an artist-facing `Overdue` qualifier and never speaks in copy |
| Window exceeded **and** release date ≤ 72h | Severity jump; forced choice opens (below) | Yes, regardless of any other threshold |
| Release date passed, store not live | Maximum severity, immediate | Yes |

**`Overdue` never triggers an automatic redelivery** (DT-10). It triggers a chase.

### Overdue past the release date (resolves prior Q-02)

The release date is a **promise the artist made publicly**, not a system deadline. It cannot be
silently moved, and it cannot be silently missed.

When a store is `Overdue` (or `accepted`-but-not-live) with the release date ≤ 72h away, the artist
is presented with a **forced, deadlined choice**. Both options are theirs — this is a commercial
strategy decision about their own record, which per `decision-classification.md` the platform
presents and never makes:

| Option | What happens | The cost, named at the moment of choice |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Go live where we can** | The release goes live on the stores that are ready; the silent store lands whenever it lands | Pre-saves on the silent store do not fire on the day (12.04.03); the announce campaign (21) points at a store that 404s |
| **B — Hold the whole release** | Attempt a coordinated date change across all stores | 12.04.01: some stores will not accept a date change once locked, and a date cannot be pulled forward. **A hold can itself produce the split release it was meant to prevent** — and now on a date nobody announced |

**If the artist does not answer by release date − 24h, option A is the default and the announced
date stands.** Rationale: inaction must not move a date the artist has publicly announced. Moving
it is the more destructive and less reversible action (12.04.01 DT-01/edge cases), and a
platform-initiated silent date move would be the platform taking a product decision it does not
own. Defaulting to "the promise you made holds" is the only default that is not a decision made on
the artist's behalf.

The artist is **never** told the release "failed". It went live in 37 of 40 places. The board is
exception-first (12.03.02 D-01) and the copy reflects proportion, not catastrophe.

### Happy Path

1. Generator (12.02.01) hands over the message + asset manifest for one (release × partner).
2. `resolve` — the partner's profile is pinned at its current version for this delivery (12.02.02 D-02); transport, ordering, naming, ack semantics and response window are all read from it.
3. `derive` — assets are rendered into the partner's required renditions from the stored source (12.01.04).
4. `stage` — renditions are transferred to the partner's required location under the partner's naming convention. Board shows `Sent` with live progress and a real ETA.
5. `verify-staged` — every asset is confirmed complete at rest. Nothing references an asset until this passes (I1).
6. `deliver-message` — the ERN is transferred in the partner's required order.
7. `signal-complete` — the partner's completion marker is emitted. The delivery is now visible to the partner and is no longer retractable (I2). The response window starts here (I5).
8. `await-ack` — the partner acknowledges receipt. `normalise` maps it to `received`.
9. `record` — per-store status (12.03.02) moves to `Received`, carrying elapsed time and, if `measured`, the expectation.
10. The partner later acknowledges acceptance → `accepted` → board `Accepted` with the store's expected live date. Pre-release URIs become available to 12.04.03.
11. Observed `signal-complete → ack` duration is written to the partner's observation history, tightening the window for every future delivery (CX-04).
12. The release goes live on that store's own schedule, confirmed by 12.03.02 against store-side truth — **never** inferred from the ack.

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Partner never responds** — the dominant case | Window exceeded → `Overdue`, not `pending`. Chase fires; escalation ladder runs | "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days. That's longer than usual — we've chased." |
| Partner never responds, and we have no history for them | At the seeded 5-business-day window, automated chase and internal escalation fire; the artist-visible status remains elapsed-time-only, with **no** `Overdue` qualifier until an observed or contractual expectation exists (12.03.02 D-11). | "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days. We don't know yet what's normal for Apple. We've chased." |
| **Low-volume partner, or an uncommon message type, never reaches 30 observations in a rolling 180 days** | That (partner × message type) sits **permanently** on tier 3 (`unmeasured`) — the sample floor is never met and observations keep ageing out. Accepted cost of the ratified statistic (D-05). The seeded window governs internal chase; the unconditional 10-partner-business-day ceiling is the only always-on backstop | Elapsed time only, forever, for that message type. No expectation clause, no `Overdue` qualifier. Honest, and permanently thinner than for a high-volume partner |
| **Partner publishes an SLA, but we have fewer than 30 deliveries of that message type** | Tier 2 governs: the `stated` value is used and the real observations we already hold are ignored until the floor is met. Accepted cost of choosing the higher floor | "Apple's stated window is 5 business days" — attributed to them, not claimed as our measurement |
| **Partner is inside our observed window but outside their own written SLA** | **Not late by the window.** Recorded and routed to partner management as a commercial signal; the artist is never told (D-05). The routing has no owner, no surface and no threshold — 12.02.02 Q-07 | Nothing. The board says "this is unusual for Apple" or nothing at all — never "Apple is in breach of a contract you are not party to" |
| **The tiered window is silent or wrong, and nothing else fires** | The unconditional ceiling fires at 10 partner-business-days regardless of tier (12.02.02 D-12). This is the case it exists for: an `unmeasured` partner with no seeded chase escalation left, or a `measured` window that a behaviour change has made too generous | Nothing — internal escalation only |
| Assets transfer, message fails | Partial delivery; retry resumes at S5 only — assets are not re-staged (I4) | "Retrying — nothing for you to do." |
| Message references an asset that is short/corrupt at rest | `verify-staged` fails → S5 never runs. The silent-ignore case is structurally prevented (I1) | Nothing, unless it recurs past the lead time |
| Ack means "received" and the artist reads "delivered" | Structurally prevented — the internal vocabulary never over-claims (D-02) | "Apple has your release. They haven't accepted it yet." |
| `parsed` ack arrives | Recorded internally; board stays `Received` (collapsed) | No change — the distinction is not actionable |
| **Partner accepts the release but rejects one track** (DT-07) | `partially-accepted`. The release is neither `Accepted` nor `Rejected` — both would be lies | "Apple took 11 of your 12 tracks. Track 7 was refused: {plain reason}." Routes to 12.03.03 for that track |
| Partner accepts, then rejects/takes down days later | No state is terminal (DT-05). The inbound channel stays open for the life of the release; a post-acceptance rejection is accepted and applied | "Apple has pulled this. Here's why." → 12.05.03 |
| **Ack arrives for a superseded message** | Recorded against that message; does **not** move release-level status backward. Ordering is by our send sequence, not receipt time (DT-06) | Nothing |
| **Ack arrives for a message ID we never sent** | Quarantined and alerted — never discarded. This is the only passive detector we have that a duplicate exists (DT-06) | Nothing, unless it confirms a duplicate |
| Duplicate delivery after a retry | Partner may create a duplicate release — **worse than a failed delivery**. Idempotency by message ID (I3) + supersede-not-resend | Nothing in the normal case; if a duplicate is confirmed, it is disclosed, not buried |
| `Overdue` tempts an automatic resend | Refused (DT-10). Silence means we do not know the partner's state; resending blind is the highest-risk act available | "We've chased Apple" — never "we've resent" |
| Transport credentials expire | All deliveries to that partner fail identically at once → partner `Suspended` (12.02.02); affected releases `Held`, **not** `Failed` | "Our connection to Apple lapsed — that's our fault, not theirs. Fixing it now; your release is held, not lost." (D-06) |
| Partner endpoint is down | Deliveries `Held`; retry per architecture policy | "Apple's delivery system is down. We'll send as soon as it's back." (D-06 — attributed honestly to them) |
| Partner rate-limits during a heavy batch | Backoff; deliveries queue | Nothing, unless the lead time (12.04.01) is threatened |
| **Batch of 200 releases fails as a unit** (DT-08) | All 200 marked `Failed (batch)` with a distinct cause. 199 correct releases must not be reported as individually defective. Retry recomputes batch membership — never a blind resend (I3) | "A delivery batch to Beatport failed. Your release wasn't the problem — resending." |
| One artist's malformed release poisons a shared batch | Offender is ejected from the batch; the remaining 199 redeliver without it | The 199: transparent. The offender: routed to 12.03.03 |
| Delivery succeeds; release never appears on the store | The silent failure. `accepted` ≠ live. Requires store-side reconciliation (12.03.02) | "Apple accepted this 9 days ago but it's not live. We're chasing." |
| Store silent into the release date | Forced deadlined choice (A/B); default A at T−24h | "Apple still hasn't responded and you're out on Friday. Two options, and you have until Thursday." |
| Store silent **past** the release date, fans pre-saved | The highest-emotion case. Release is live elsewhere; pre-saves on the silent store do not fire | "You're live on 37 of 38 stores. Apple is still silent — 340 people pre-saved there and it hasn't landed for them. Here's what we're doing." |
| Release date moved after `signal-complete` | Not amendable — supersede only (I2, CX-03) | Routes to 12.05.02 |
| Generator regenerates while a delivery is in flight | In-flight message is discarded before S6, or superseded after S6. **Never amended** — an amended in-flight message is how duplicates are created (CX-03) | Transparent |
| Transfer takes 6 hours and the artist thinks it hung | Real progress and a real ETA, because the transfer genuinely is slow (DT-12) | "Sending to Apple — 60% of files, ~40 min left." Never a bare spinner |
| Weekend/holiday at the partner | Window counts partner business days; no false `Overdue` (DT-13) | "…4 Apple business days — this includes a weekend." |
| Two deliveries to the same partner for the same release, concurrently | Structurally prevented — one in-flight delivery per (release × partner). The second queues behind the first or supersedes it; it never races it | Nothing |
| Ack storm — 40 partners ack simultaneously at a release window | Acks are applied per (message ID, partner); no cross-delivery contention exists by construction | Nothing |
| Partner sends an ack that is well-formed but semantically absurd (accepts a release we withdrew) | Quarantined as `unmappable`; alerted. We do not act on it | Nothing |
| Malicious/spoofed inbound ack | An ack is only actioned if it authenticates as the partner **and** references a message ID we sent to that partner. Both conditions, not either | Nothing |
| Partner ack claims `live` | Ignored for the `Live` determination (12.03.02 D-02). Recorded as a hint to reconcile against | Nothing until store-side truth confirms |
| The multi-hyphenate: Producer is also the release owner | Sees the delivery through the **Musician** lens on their own release; `None` through the Producer lens on releases they merely played on (DT-11) | Correct view per release, not per account flag |

### States

> **Transport state machine.** Per (release × store). One release is legitimately in several of
> these at once — which is why per-store status (12.03.02) is a first-class object rather than a
> flag on the release. **No state is terminal** (DT-05): the inbound channel stays open for the life
> of the release, so `Accepted` can be followed by `Rejected` months later.

| State | Trigger | Board state (12.03.02) | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---|---------------|
| Queued | Scheduled, awaiting the delivery window | `Queued` | "Scheduled for {date}" |
| Transferring | S3–S5 in flight | `Sent` + progress | "Sending to Apple — 60% of files, ~40 min left" |
| Awaiting ack | S6 succeeded; window open | `Sent` | "Sent to Apple {N}h ago" |
| Received | Ack: `received` | `Received` | "Apple has it" |
| Parsed | Ack: `parsed` | `Received` (collapsed) | No visible change — not actionable |
| Accepted | Ack: `accepted` | `Accepted` | "Apple accepted it — live on {date}" |
| Partially accepted | Ack: `partially-accepted` (DT-07) | **no equivalent — gap** | "Apple took 11 of 12 tracks. Track 7 was refused." |
| Overdue | Window exceeded, in partner business days | `Overdue` | "Apple hasn't responded in {N} days" + what we're doing |
| Rejected | Ack: `rejected` | `Rejected` | Routes to 12.03.03 |
| Failed | Transport failed after the architecture's retry policy | **no equivalent — gap** | "We couldn't reach Apple. Retrying." |
| Held | Partner suspended, endpoint down, or our credential lapsed | **no equivalent — gap** | "Held, not lost" + honest attribution (D-06) |

> **Observation states.** The artist's view of the above, distinct from the state machine itself —
> following the pattern 12.03.02 established for the same reason.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fetching delivery state | Skeleton rows — never a bare spinner on a multi-hour transfer |
| Empty | No delivery scheduled for this release yet | "Nothing sent yet" + link to scheduling (12.04.01) |
| Populated | Delivery state known for every selected store | Exception-first: what is late or wrong; healthy stores collapsed to a count |
| Partial | Some stores' state is stale or unknown | Mixed view with explicit "last checked {time}" — never a confident stale claim |
| Error | Delivery-state service unreachable | "Can't refresh — showing last known from {time}." The underlying deliveries are unaffected and this must be said, or the artist reads our outage as their release failing |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Silence must be a first-class state with a per-partner expected-response window** | The domain's own top-level rationale ("delivery is asynchronous, partial and fails silently"); the Operator persona's analogue — a hold nobody released | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the highest-leverage cheap win in the domain. Modelling only success/failure produces an eternal "in progress" — the black box artists already have and hate. Naming the silence converts the single worst distribution experience into the product's most visible act of honesty, for the cost of a timeout and a copy string. **Scope note added on ratification (D-05)**: the hypothesis says *per-partner*; the ratified key is **(partner × message type)** (12.02.02 D-13), so a takedown and a new release carry separate windows for the same partner. The per-ack-stage axis was declined outright and the live wait stays with 12.03.02 — neither is folded in. |
| DT-02 | The internal status vocabulary can flatten partner acks into "delivered" | Simplicity; universal incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — received ≠ parsed ≠ accepted ≠ live, and the gaps between them are days. Over-claiming is how artists come to believe distributors lie to them. For a product whose entire thesis (D-18) is that the record it keeps is *true*, over-claiming a delivery state would be the thesis failing on the most visible screen in the domain. Never claim more than the ack supports. |
| DT-03 | Retry is safe because delivery is idempotent | Optimistic assumption from ordinary API work | ❌ REJECTED — **it is not**, and this is the sharpest technical hazard in domain 12. A retried NewReleaseMessage can create a **duplicate release** on the store: two Spotify pages, split streams, split royalties, and a cleanup that requires a takedown of the artist's own record. A duplicate is materially worse than a failure, because a failure is visible and a duplicate looks like success. Idempotency by message ID, plus supersede-not-resend semantics, is mandatory — and the retry policy itself is an architecture concern routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. |
| DT-04 | Message state has no Fan-visible consequence | Parent index Q-03's stated assumption; the sub-domain is "invisible by design" | ❌ REJECTED — the assumption is wrong, and the parent's own sibling proves it. A pre-save link (12.04.03) **cannot exist until a store returns `accepted`** — its happy path step 1 is literally "stores accept delivery; pre-release URIs become available". So an overdue partner means a missing pre-save link on the only Fan-facing artifact in domain 12, and an accept-then-silence means pre-saves that never fire for fans who committed. The Fan never sees this feature and is nonetheless the party most concretely harmed by its dominant failure mode. Per D-11 fans are users, not records — a user who was promised a thing and did not get it is owed something. Routing "why is it on Spotify and not Apple?" to 12.03.02 (parent Q-03's proposed answer) does not cover this, because 12.03.02 has no Fan lens at all. |
| DT-05 | `Accepted` is a terminal state — once a partner accepts, choreography's job is done | The natural request/response mental model; the ack looks like a reply | ❌ REJECTED, and this one is a genuine domain-expert trap. Partner→distributor traffic is **not a request/response pair, it is an open inbound stream for the life of the release.** Partners send unsolicited state changes days or months after acceptance: content-policy pulls, metadata violations (a "feat." in the wrong field), artist-name disputes, rights conflicts raised by a claimant. A state machine that treats `Accepted` as terminal will receive these and drop them on the floor — the release vanishes from the store and our board still says green, which is 12.03.02 DT-03's "a green board can be a lie" arriving through a door nobody left open. **No delivery state is terminal.** The inbound channel is bound to the release's lifetime, not the delivery's, and post-acceptance rejections route to 12.05.03. |
| DT-06 | Acks arrive in order, once, and only for messages we sent | Every ordinary API integration's assumption | ❌ REJECTED on all three counts, and the third is the valuable one. **Out of order**: a partner can ack a superseded message after its replacement is already sent — so acks are ordered by *our send sequence*, not receipt time, and a late ack for a superseded message must never move release-level status backward. **More than once**: duplicate acks are common and must be no-ops. **For messages we never sent**: this is the interesting case. An ack referencing an unknown message ID is the **only passive detector we have that a duplicate release exists** (DT-03's catastrophe) — it may be a partner's response to a message a retry created without our knowledge. Silently dropping unrecognised acks discards the one signal that would surface the worst outcome in the domain. Quarantine and alert; never discard. |
| DT-07 | A partner accepts or rejects a release as a unit | The binary the whole state vocabulary implies | ❌ REJECTED — **partial acceptance is real and routine**: a partner takes the release and refuses a single track (explicit-content metadata mismatch, a sample flag, a title-field violation on one track). Both available answers are lies — `Accepted` conceals that the album is now 11 tracks, `Rejected` conceals that 11 tracks are going live on Friday. The artist must know **before release day** that their album is about to come out incomplete, because it is fixable then and a catastrophe after. This also exposes a modelling gap in a sibling: 12.03.02's status object is per (release × store × territory) and has **no track axis**, so it cannot represent this outcome at all. Flagged to Step 6 rather than fixed here. |
| DT-08 | Batch delivery is a transport detail with no product consequence | It is, after all, just how the bytes go | ❌ REJECTED — batching **couples unrelated releases across tenants**, which is a fairness property, not a plumbing one. Partners require or prefer batched delivery; aggregators batch nightly. So 200 releases from 200 unrelated artists ride one batch, and a malformed member fails all 200 — including 199 that were perfect. Two consequences nobody designs for: (a) per-release status must never report a batch failure as an individual defect ("your release was rejected" when it was not), and (b) **one stranger's bad metadata can miss another artist's release date.** The offender is ejected and the batch redelivers without it; membership is recomputed on retry, never blindly resent (I3). The reason this matters beyond correctness: "your release didn't go out because of someone else's mistake" is a sentence the platform will have to say, and it can only say it honestly if the batch axis is modelled. |
| DT-09 | Failure copy should attribute the failure to the partner | Incumbent behaviour, and it is the comfortable option | ❌ REJECTED — **and the honest version is a differentiator, not a liability.** "We couldn't reach Apple" is true when Apple is down and a lie when our credential lapsed. The two cases are indistinguishable to the artist and opposite in meaning: one is nobody's fault and needs no action, the other is our failure and the artist deserves to know their release is sitting still because of us. Every incumbent blames the partner because the artist cannot check. D-18's thesis is that the record this platform keeps is *true* — a product that fabricates fault attribution has already failed that thesis in the one moment the artist is paying closest attention. **Say when it is our fault.** The cost is embarrassment; the return is the only currency this product actually trades in. |
| DT-10 | `Overdue` should trigger an automatic redelivery — the artist wants action, and doing nothing looks like negligence | The obvious engineering instinct, and the one a competent implementer will reach for | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most important rejection in the file because it sits exactly where D-01 and D-03 collide. `Overdue` means **we do not know the partner's state.** They may have the message and be slow; they may have processed it and lost the ack; they may never have received it. Resending while blind is the single highest-risk action available — it is precisely the path to DT-03's duplicate release, and it is *triggered automatically, at scale, on the domain's most common failure mode.* A system that auto-retries into silence will manufacture duplicates for every slow partner it has. `Overdue` escalates to a **human chase**, never a resend. The artist gets action; the action is asking, not shouting. |
| DT-11 | Producer `None` means the UI hides delivery from users with the Producer role | The obvious reading of the Role Lens table | ❌ REJECTED — this is personas.md's central structural fact applied here: "a persona model of 'user has a role' is wrong for this product". `None` is a lens on the **role**, not a gate on the **human**. The self-releasing artist-producer is the common case, not the exotic one; they are Producer on Tuesday's session and Musician on their own record, simultaneously. Implemented as an account flag, this row would hide a producer's own release from them — a failure personas.md predicts explicitly ("any UX forcing 'are you an artist OR a producer?' fails them immediately"). The lens resolves per (human × release × their relationship to it), never per account. |
| DT-12 | Transfer is fast enough to treat as instantaneous in scheduling and in the UI | The web-request mental model | ❌ REJECTED — a 12-track album at 24/96 is multi-GB **per partner**, and 40 partners is the same bytes 40 times. Delivery is measured in hours, not seconds, and a catalog migration (12.08) is measured in days. Two consequences: (a) the artist watching a bare spinner for six hours concludes it has hung, so real progress and a real ETA are required, not decorative; (b) **12.04.01's lead time must be computed as partner lead time + transfer duration**, not partner lead time alone — a store needing 10 days' notice actually needs 10 days plus however long our upload takes, and getting that wrong misses a release date for a reason the artist can never see. Flagged to 12.04.01 as a cross-cut rather than decided here. |
| DT-13 | The response window can be counted in elapsed hours | The obvious implementation of a timeout | ❌ REJECTED, and this is what makes `Overdue` real rather than decorative — which was exactly what prior Q-01 asked for. **DSP staff process deliveries on business days, in their own timezone.** A release delivered Friday evening sits untouched until Monday. A 48-hour elapsed window fires `Overdue` on Sunday for a partner behaving completely normally — the system cries wolf on its flagship honesty feature — while a genuinely stuck Tuesday delivery looks calm. The music-specific sting: **Friday is global release day**, so the delivery pipeline is at peak load exactly when partner staff are least available. Windows count **partner business days in the partner's timezone**, with the partner's non-working days as profile data; elapsed time shown to the artist stays wall-clock, because wall-clock is what they feel. |

## Not-Product Note

The delivery **queue, retry policy, backoff, dead-lettering, transfer resumability, and credential
storage** for partner endpoints are architecture/infrastructure, not product. Routed to
`/create-prd-architecture` (queue, retry and resumability semantics — note I4's requirement that
retries resume at the failed step, which is a behavioural constraint *on* that policy, not the
policy itself) and `/create-prd-security` (partner credential storage; inbound ack authentication).

**state vocabulary the artist sees**, and the **honesty rules** governing it (D-01, D-02, D-05,
D-06, D-09, D-10, D-12).
