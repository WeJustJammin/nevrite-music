# Feature: Delivery Choreography & Acknowledgement

> **Number**: 12.02.03
> **Parent**: [DDEX Delivery Messaging](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Physically handing the message and its assets to each partner in the order and by the transport
they require, then interpreting what comes back. Partners disagree about everything here —
transport (SFTP, S3, API), whether assets go before or after the message, batching, naming
conventions, and whether an acknowledgement means "received", "parsed" or "accepted". This is
where distribution's defining property lives: **delivery is asynchronous, partial, and fails
silently.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees delivery progress as human states per store — "sent to Apple", "Apple confirmed receipt", "waiting on Apple" — with an honest elapsed time. Never sees a transport. The read-only access matters most in the failure case: silence for six days is the incumbent experience, and naming the silence ("Apple hasn't responded in 6 days; that's longer than usual; we've escalated") is a real product feature. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. Delivery is the release owner's concern; the Producer's work ended at the master. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Choreography is per-partner and per-profile. Common shapes — `[PENDING — Step 5]` for the real
matrix, which depends on parent Q-01:

- **Batch/SFTP**: assets uploaded to a partner-specified path, then a manifest/message, then a
  completion marker. Order is significant; a message referencing assets not yet fully uploaded is
  rejected or, worse, silently ignored.
- **API**: message posted, assets by URL or upload, synchronous receipt but asynchronous acceptance.
- **Cloud bucket**: assets to a partner-owned bucket; delivery signalled by an object convention.

Acknowledgement semantics are the hard part, because the same word means different things per
partner. WeJammin must normalise partner-specific acks into one internal vocabulary — received /
parsed / accepted / rejected / live — and it must **never claim more than the ack supports**.
Saying "delivered to Apple" when Apple has only confirmed receipt is a lie the artist finds out
about later, and it is the standard behaviour of the incumbents.

**Silence is the dominant failure mode and must be a first-class state.** The single most common
real-world outcome is not a rejection — it is nothing at all. A system that only models
success and failure will report "in progress" forever, which is exactly the black box artists
already have. Every partner needs an expected-response window, and exceeding it must produce a
state ("overdue"), not a spinner.

### Happy Path

1. Generator (12.02.01) hands over the message + asset manifest.
2. Choreography resolves the partner's transport from the pinned profile (12.02.02).
3. Assets are derived (12.01.04) into the partner's renditions and transferred.
4. The message is transferred in the partner's required order.
5. Partner acknowledges receipt; the ack is normalised and written to per-store status (12.03.02).
6. Partner later acknowledges acceptance (or rejects → 12.03.03).
7. The release goes live on that store's own schedule (12.03.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Partner never responds** — the dominant case | Expected-response window exceeded → `overdue`, not `pending`. Escalation path fires | "Apple hasn't responded in 6 days. That's longer than usual — we've chased it." |
| Assets transfer, message fails | Partial delivery; retry the message only, not the assets | "Retrying — nothing for you to do." |
| Ack means "received" and the artist reads "delivered" | **Must be structurally prevented** — the internal vocabulary never over-claims | "Apple has your release. They haven't accepted it yet." |
| Transport credentials expire | All deliveries to that partner fail identically at once | Store marked unavailable (12.02.02 Suspended); affected releases held, not failed |
| Delivery succeeds; release never appears on the store | The silent failure. Accepted ≠ live. Requires reconciliation from the store side (12.03.02) | "Apple accepted this 9 days ago but it's not live. We're chasing." |
| Partner rate-limits during a heavy batch | Backoff; deliveries queue | Nothing, unless the lead time (12.04.01) is threatened |
| Duplicate delivery after a retry | Partner may create a duplicate release — **worse than a failed delivery** | Idempotency by message ID is mandatory; see DT-03 |

### States

> Per (release × store). One release is legitimately in several of these at once — which is why
> per-store status (12.03.02) is a first-class object rather than a flag on the release.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Queued | Scheduled, awaiting the delivery window | "Scheduled for {date}" |
| Transferring | Assets/message in flight | "Sending to Apple" |
| Received | Partner acked receipt | "Apple has it" |
| Accepted | Partner acked acceptance | "Apple accepted it — live on {date}" |
| Overdue | Response window exceeded | "Apple hasn't responded in {N} days" + what we're doing |
| Rejected | Partner nacked | Routes to 12.03.03 |
| Failed | Transport failed after retries | "We couldn't reach Apple. Retrying." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Silence must be a first-class state with a per-partner expected-response window** | The domain's own top-level rationale ("delivery is asynchronous, partial and fails silently"); the Operator persona's analogue — a hold nobody released | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the highest-leverage cheap win in the domain. Modelling only success/failure produces an eternal "in progress" — the black box artists already have and hate. Naming the silence converts the single worst distribution experience into the product's most visible act of honesty, for the cost of a timeout and a copy string. |
| DT-02 | The internal status vocabulary can flatten partner acks into "delivered" | Simplicity; universal incumbent behaviour | ❌ REJECTED — received ≠ parsed ≠ accepted ≠ live, and the gaps between them are days. Over-claiming is how artists come to believe distributors lie to them. For a product whose entire thesis (D-18) is that the record it keeps is *true*, over-claiming a delivery state would be the thesis failing on the most visible screen in the domain. Never claim more than the ack supports. |
| DT-03 | Retry is safe because delivery is idempotent | Optimistic assumption from ordinary API work | ❌ REJECTED — **it is not**, and this is the sharpest technical hazard in domain 12. A retried NewReleaseMessage can create a **duplicate release** on the store: two Spotify pages, split streams, split royalties, and a cleanup that requires a takedown of the artist's own record. A duplicate is materially worse than a failure, because a failure is visible and a duplicate looks like success. Idempotency by message ID, plus supersede-not-resend semantics, is mandatory — and the retry policy itself is an architecture concern routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. |

## Not-Product Note

The delivery **queue, retry policy, backoff, dead-lettering and credential storage** for partner
endpoints are architecture/infrastructure, not product. Routed to `/create-prd-architecture` (queue
and retry semantics) and `/create-prd-security` (partner credential storage). What remains product
here is the **state vocabulary the artist sees** and the **honesty rules** governing it (D-01, D-02).
