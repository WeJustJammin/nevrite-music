# Feature: MEAD Enrichment Delivery

> **Number**: 12.02.04
> **Parent**: [DDEX Delivery Messaging](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Delivering DDEX MEAD (Media Enrichment and Description) — the descriptive layer that tells DSPs
what a recording *is like* rather than what it is: mood, tempo, key, instrumentation, vocal
register, themes, focus-track intent, and the artist's own positioning. MEAD is what algorithmic
and editorial systems consume, so it is the closest thing to a supported, structured channel into
DSP recommendation — as opposed to the folklore and guesswork the pitch process usually runs on.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Describes the release the way they would describe it to a person: mood, themes, what it is for, which track is the focus and why. Sees where the descriptors came from — their own words, the project record, or an inference — and can correct any of them. This is the one part of the DDEX layer where the artist genuinely has the authoritative answer. |
| Producer | Config | Contributes the technical descriptors they actually know and the artist often does not remember: tempo, key, instrumentation, whether it is live or programmed, which room it was tracked in. These are byproducts of the session and, per the Producer's constraint in personas.md, must never require a form — if they are not already known from the project record (07), asking here is a losing proposition. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible in the delivery path. Fans experience MEAD's downstream effect (better recommendations, correct mood placement) without ever seeing it. |

## Behavior

MEAD is delivered alongside or after the ERN, per partner profile (12.02.02). Not all partners
consume it, so it is opportunistic: valuable where supported, harmless where not.

The strategic point is that **most MEAD fields are byproducts of work WeJammin already witnessed**.
Tempo, key, instrumentation, personnel and live-vs-programmed are facts a project (07) and its
credits (02) already hold. A conventional distributor must ask the artist to fill in a form months
later — reconstruction, with all its problems. WeJammin can pre-fill from the session record.
This is the same consolidation→provenance mechanic as the split sheet (D-18), applied to
descriptive metadata: being in the room means you already know.

Where a value is inferred rather than witnessed (audio analysis for tempo/key), it must be marked
as inferred and be correctable. Passing an inference to a partner as an artist-asserted fact is a
small instance of the fabrication problem in 12.02.01 DT-01.

### Happy Path

1. Release reaches enrichment; the builder pre-fills what it already knows from 07 and 02.
2. Audio-derived values (tempo, key) are added, marked as inferred.
3. Musician reviews, corrects, and adds what only they know — mood, themes, focus-track intent.
4. Producer is prompted only for gaps they are uniquely authoritative for; most are already filled.
5. MEAD is delivered to partners whose profile supports it; skipped silently for those who do not.
6. Descriptors feed the editorial pitch (12.04.02), which is the same information told to a human.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Partner does not consume MEAD | Skipped silently; no failure | Nothing |
| Inferred tempo/key is wrong | Correctable; correction should flow back to the recording (02/07), not just this release | "We heard 128 BPM in G minor. Wrong? Fix it." |
| Musician skips enrichment entirely | Delivery proceeds — **MEAD is never blocking** | Advisory: "Adding this improves your odds of algorithmic placement" |
| MEAD delivered after the ERN is accepted | Normal; enrichment can follow | Nothing |
| Descriptors contradict the editorial pitch | Two channels telling one partner different stories | Should be structurally impossible — one source (DT-03) |
| Off-platform master with no project record | Nothing to pre-fill; the reconstruction problem returns | Full manual entry — the incumbent experience, correctly scoped to the case that earns it |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pre-filled | Project record supplied values | Fields populated with provenance markers (witnessed / inferred / yours) |
| Empty | No project record | Manual entry with an honest note about what it buys |
| Partial | Some fields set | Progress affordance, never a blocking one |
| Delivered | Sent to supporting partners | "Sent to {N} of {M} stores that use it" |
| Skipped | User declined | No penalty, no nag |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | MEAD is low-value metadata busywork — a `could`, near the bottom of the domain | Initial read; it is optional, and most distributors ignore it | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — it is low-value *for a conventional distributor*, who must ask the artist to remember. It is structurally cheap for WeJammin because the facts are byproducts of the session record (07/02). Same asymmetry as the split sheet: **being present makes the expensive thing free.** It is a genuine, if minor, demonstration of the thesis — the first place a user notices the platform already knows something a competitor would have made them type. Still `could` on MoSCoW (it does not block delivery, and its value depends on partner adoption), but the reasoning for its cheapness is worth carrying forward. |
| DT-02 | Inferred values can be delivered as artist-asserted facts | Convenience; nobody would notice | ❌ REJECTED — same defect as 12.02.01 DT-01 at a smaller scale. A tempo the platform guessed is not a fact the artist asserted. For a product selling the truth of its record, laundering inference into assertion anywhere is corrosive — and the marker costs nothing. |
| DT-03 | MEAD and the editorial pitch (12.04.02) are separate — one is machine-read, one is human-read | They target different consumers, sit in different sub-domains, and have different formats | ❌ REJECTED as separate **sources** — they are one set of claims about the record, delivered to two audiences in two formats. Two independent sources mean the artist tells Spotify's algorithm and Spotify's editor different stories, which is both a worse pitch and more work. One source, two projections. This crosses a sub-domain boundary (12.02 ↔ 12.04) and is recorded in the domain CX file. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Pre-fill from the session record; ask only for what is genuinely unknown | Producer will not fill a form; the facts are already witnessed (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Inferred values are marked inferred and are correctable | Inference is not assertion (DT-02) |
| D-03 | MEAD and the editorial pitch share one source of claims | Otherwise the artist tells one partner two stories (DT-03) |
| D-04 | MEAD is never blocking | It is enrichment; blocking delivery on it would be indefensible |
