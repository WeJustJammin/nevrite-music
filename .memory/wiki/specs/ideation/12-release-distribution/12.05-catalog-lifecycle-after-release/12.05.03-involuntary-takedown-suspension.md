# Feature: Involuntary Takedown & Delivery Suspension

> **Number**: 12.05.03
> **Parent**: [Catalog Lifecycle After Release](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

A release coming down, or being blocked from going out, because someone other than the artist said
so: a copyright claim, an ownership dispute, a sample nobody cleared, a DSP's own decision, or a
statutory demand. The artist is a subject here, not an actor — which makes this the only feature
in domain 12 where the platform acts against a user, and the one where getting the balance wrong
does the most damage in either direction.

> **Deep Think addition** — split out of the sweep's "Takedowns & Redelivery" candidate. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Read-only** | Sees that their release has been suspended or removed, **who initiated it**, on what basis, what it blocks, and exactly how to contest it. Cannot lift it — that is what involuntary means. This is the single most distressing screen in the domain and the one where a vague message ("your release has been removed for a policy violation") does the most harm. |
| Producer | Read-only | Told a record they made was pulled, and why. Frequently the person who **knows the answer** — whether the sample was cleared, who actually played the part, what was agreed in the room. Per personas.md they are the trust broker; a dispute resolution process that does not reach them is discarding its best evidence. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The record vanishes from their library with no explanation from anyone. |

## Behavior

Trigger classes, which differ enough that treating them alike is the core mistake:

| Trigger | Initiator | Our posture |
|---|---|---|
| Copyright claim (DMCA or equivalent) | A third party | Statutory process; we have a defined role |
| Ownership dispute between co-owners | Another WeJammin user | **We hold the ownership record** — see DT-02 |
| Uncleared sample discovered post-release | Anyone, including us | The gate (12.01.03) missed it |
| DSP's own removal | The store | We are informed, not consulted; we cannot lift it |
| Statutory/legal demand | A court or authority | Compelled |
| Platform trust & safety action | Us | Routes to 24 |

**Suspension is broader than takedown.** A live release comes down; a pending release is blocked
from delivering; a queued update is frozen. Suspension is a state on the *release*, not an event,
and it gates 12.05.01 and 12.05.02 while it lasts.

The design tension is real and unresolvable by cleverness: act too readily and the platform
becomes a weapon any claimant can point at any artist; act too slowly and it becomes a haven that
DSPs and rights holders will not tolerate. Both failures are severe and they pull in opposite
directions. What the platform *can* do — uniquely — is be evidence-based where it holds evidence.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the process, timelines and thresholds. These are
legal-posture decisions (parent Q-02), not product decisions, and inventing them here would be
irresponsible.

### Happy Path

> There is no happy path. The least-bad path:

1. A claim or dispute arrives, from a third party, a co-owner, a DSP or a statutory source.
2. Platform classifies it and determines whether it holds evidence bearing on it (09).
3. Where the ownership record answers the question, that is stated to both parties immediately.
4. Where it does not, suspension follows the posture set for that trigger class.
5. The artist is told precisely: who, what, on what basis, what it blocks, how to contest.
6. Contest routes to 24; resolution lifts the suspension or makes the removal permanent.
7. Every step is recorded — this is provenance too, and it is provenance about a fight.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Claim is **false** or malicious | The platform was used as a weapon | Contest path; **and a record of the claimant's history** — a repeat false claimant is a trust signal (24) |
| Ownership dispute where **we hold the record** | We can answer it from evidence, not adjudicate it from claims | "Rights shows a 50/50 split attested by all three of you on 3 Mar 2024." See DT-02 |
| Splits disputed post-release | Should the release come down? **Recommend: no — freeze the money** | Parent Q-01 |
| Uncleared sample found post-release | The gate missed it; downstream collaborators are exposed | The Musician anti-persona's accidental failure (personas.md), now real |
| DSP removes unilaterally | We are informed, not consulted, and cannot lift it | "Spotify removed this. They haven't told us why. We've asked." Honest about our powerlessness |
| Artist contests and wins | Redelivery is a **new release** — the damage is done regardless | 12.05.01 DT-01 asymmetry, at its cruellest |
| Suspension while an update is in flight | Update frozen | "This release is suspended. The fix is on hold." |
| Claimant is also a WeJammin user | Both parties are ours; both see a version of this screen | Routes to 24 |

### States

This feature has two state dimensions: the **UI render states** of the Musician's read-only
status screen, and the **release-level suspension states** that drive what that screen shows.

**UI render states** (the read-only screen the Musician lands on):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Screen opened; suspension detail being fetched | Skeleton of the notice card — never a blank screen for a distressing message |
| Empty | Release is Clear (no claim, no suspension) | The screen is not surfaced at all; the release shows normal in-catalog status (12.05.01/12.05.02 remain available) |
| Populated | An active claim, suspension, or takedown exists | The notice: who initiated, what basis, what it blocks, how to contest, and the current suspension state (below) |
| Error | Suspension detail fails to load | "We can't show the details of this suspension right now. It is still in effect. Retry / contact support." — the release state is never silently presented as Clear on a fetch failure |

**Release-level suspension states** (the domain state machine that gates 12.05.01/12.05.02):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Clear | Normal | Not surfaced |
| Claimed | Claim received, not yet actioned | "A claim was filed. Here's what and by whom." — never a silent hold |
| Suspended | Delivery/updates blocked | What is blocked, why, by whom, how to contest |
| Taken down | Removed from stores | Same, plus what was lost |
| Contested | Artist has responded | Status + honest timeline; routes to 24 |
| Resolved — cleared | Claim failed | Suspension lifted; redelivery is a new release |
| Resolved — upheld | Claim succeeded | Permanent; the record of it persists |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Takedown is one feature; who initiated it is a parameter | The sweep's candidate list ("Takedowns & Redelivery") implies exactly this, and the machinery genuinely is shared | ❌ REJECTED — a takedown the artist **requested** and one **inflicted on them** are different products sharing a message format. Different initiator, different permissions (the artist has none), legal weight, and a suspension state that gates the other two features. Merging them yields the specific absurdity of a **lift button on a legally-compelled removal** — the artist's Full access on voluntary takedown silently becoming Full access on a court order. Splitting them is what surfaced this feature at all; the candidate list contained no node for the case with actual legal exposure. |
| DT-02 | An ownership dispute is a dispute like any other and should route to a generic resolution process | How every platform handles it — they hold no evidence, so they can only referee claims | ❌ REJECTED, and this is **domain 12's strongest argument for D-10 that nobody has made yet.** Every other platform adjudicates ownership disputes between two strangers' assertions, because that is all they have. WeJammin **holds the ownership record, attested at creation by the parties themselves** (12.01.03). For any dispute over a WeJammin-originated release, we are not refereeing — we are reading. "The split is 50/50, all three of you attested it on 3 Mar 2024, here is the record" ends most disputes before they start. That is the *entire* value proposition of provenance arriving in the one moment it was always meant for: **the moment it becomes contested and valuable** — which problem-statement.md names as the exact moment where reconstruction fails. This is the wedge cashing out. It is also strictly limited to releases the platform witnessed; for imported catalog we are as blind as anyone, which is a real and worth-stating boundary. |
| DT-03 | The platform should suspend quickly on any credible claim to stay safe | Standard defensive platform posture; it is what the incumbents do | ⚠️ CONTESTED — fast suspension makes the platform a **weapon**: any claimant can silence any artist by filing, and the artist's release dies during a process they did not start. Slow suspension makes it a haven DSPs will not do business with. There is no clever resolution — it is a values decision with legal consequences, escalated to parent Q-02. But DT-02 changes the shape of the problem in a way worth stating: for releases we witnessed, the question is often *answerable from evidence* rather than a judgement call, which means the fast/slow dilemma applies mainly to imported catalog and third-party claims. That does not eliminate the tension; it materially shrinks it, and it is the only structural advantage available here. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Split from voluntary takedown as a distinct feature | Different initiator, permissions and legal weight; merging yields a lift button on a court order (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Where we hold the ownership record, we **answer** disputes from evidence rather than refereeing claims | This is the wedge cashing out at the moment it was built for (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The artist is always told who, what, on what basis, and how to contest | A vague suspension notice is the most damaging message the product can send |
| D-04 | Every involuntary takedown is a **gate post-mortem** | If a sample surfaced here, 12.01.03 missed it — and the gate's success metric is how little reaches this feature |
