# Feature: Metadata Update & Redelivery

> **Number**: 12.05.02
> **Parent**: [Catalog Lifecycle After Release](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Fixing a live release without destroying it: correcting a misspelled title, adding a credit that
was missed, replacing artwork, swapping a master. Most updates are cheap and invisible; a few
require the release to come down and go back up, losing everything it had accumulated. Knowing
which is which — before the artist clicks — is the whole feature.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Edits a live release and is told, per field, whether this is a quiet fix or a rebuild. Sees which stores will accept the change in place and which will not — because they disagree, and the artist's cost depends entirely on which stores they are on. |
| Producer | Config | Owns asset-driven updates: a corrected master, a fixed mix. **This is also where a Producer adds a credit that was missed at release** — the reconstruction case the platform exists to prevent, arriving late but still worth catching. Per personas.md this is their characteristic failure ("we'll do it later"), and this feature is the last place "later" can still happen. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Fans experience metadata updates as things quietly becoming correct, which is the intended experience. |

## Behavior

Update cost is a spectrum most products hide behind one "edit" button:

| Change | Typical cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Add/fix a credit | Cheap — metadata-only | Usually accepted in place. **The most important one for the thesis** |
| Fix a typo in a title | Cheap-to-moderate | Some stores accept in place; some want a redelivery |
| Replace artwork | Moderate | Usually accepted; propagation is slow and uneven |
| Change release date | Expensive-to-impossible | Locked at most stores once live |
| Replace a master | Expensive | New asset, sometimes a new ISRC — a new recording, arguably |
| Change the primary artist | Effectively a new release | Identity change; wrong-page risk (12.03.04) |
| Change (P) line / label | Expensive | Ownership assertion change |

The design rule follows 12.05.01 DT-01: **state the cost before the click**, per field, per store.
An artist fixing a typo should not discover afterwards that they reset their playlist positions.

**Credit updates deserve special treatment.** A late credit is a provenance win, not an
inconvenience — it is exactly the fact the platform exists to hold, arriving late but arriving.
The friction on adding one should be as close to zero as the stores permit, and where a store will
not take it, the credit must still land in WeJammin's record (02). The DSP's refusal to carry a
credit does not make the credit less true, and that gap is precisely where D-10 pays: the platform
is the authority, the store is a rendering of it.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens a live release and edits a field.
2. Platform states the cost immediately: "quiet fix on all your stores" or "Apple will need this
   taken down and redelivered — you'd lose the playlist positions".
3. Musician confirms with the cost visible.
4. Update ERNs are generated (12.02.01) and sent per store, in the form each store wants.
5. Cheap updates propagate quietly; expensive ones show their real consequence.
6. WeJammin's own record updates regardless of what any store accepts.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Store accepts in place; another needs a rebuild | Split cost — the same edit is free on one store and expensive on another | "Spotify: quiet fix. Apple: takedown and redeliver. Your call." |
| Master replaced — same recording or a new one? | A genuinely hard line. A new mix is arguably a new recording needing a new ISRC | "Is this a fix, or a different version? It changes whether this keeps its streams." |
| Credit added years later | Cheap and valuable. **Land it regardless of store acceptance** | "Added to your credits. Spotify will show it in a few days; Beatport won't show credits at all." |
| Update rejected by a store | Live release keeps the old (wrong) metadata | "Apple rejected the fix. It's still showing the typo — here's why." |
| Update in flight when a takedown is requested | Race; takedown wins | Cancel the update |
| Involuntary takedown active (12.05.03) | Updates blocked while unresolved | "This release is suspended. Fix the dispute first." |
| Store silently ignores the update | The delivery silence problem again | "Sent 6 days ago; Beatport still shows the old title." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Live | Normal | Editable, with per-field cost surfaced on focus |
| Costing | Field edited | Immediate per-store cost statement |
| Updating | Update sent | Per-store progress |
| Updated | Stores accepted | Quiet confirmation |
| Partial | Some stores took it | Per-store breakdown — the honest state |
| Rejected | Store refused | Old data still live, with the reason (12.03.03) |
| Blocked | Involuntary takedown active | Suspended, with the route to contest |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Updating a live release is one operation with one cost | The single "Edit" button every distributor ships | ❌ REJECTED — the cost varies from **free to catastrophic** depending on the field *and* the store, and the artist has no way to know which they are about to trigger. Fixing a typo can cost the same as taking the release down, or nothing at all, and the difference is invisible. Per `specificity-standards.md` the cost must be concrete and stated per field per store, before the click. This is the same principle as 12.04.01 D-01 (show the cost of the date at the moment of choice) — a recurring rule in this domain: **wherever the platform knows a price the artist cannot see, show it at the decision.** |
| DT-02 | A credit added post-release is an edge case | It is late, rare, and looks like tidying up | ❌ REJECTED — it is **the thesis arriving late**, and one of the most valuable events in the product. Per problem-statement.md the failure is that credits get reconstructed years later; a musician adding a credit to a two-year-old record is that reconstruction happening *inside* the system that can hold it, rather than in a Notes app. It should be the cheapest, most encouraged action in this feature. And where a store will not carry it, the credit still lands in WeJammin's record — **the DSP's refusal does not make the fact untrue.** That gap between our record and the store's rendering is exactly what D-10 buys and what no competitor has. |
| DT-03 | Replacing a master is an asset update | It is a file swap; the release is the same | ⚠️ CONTESTED — a materially different mix is arguably a **different recording**, which means a different ISRC, which means it is not an update at all but a new recording on the same release. Nobody in the industry draws this line consistently and the standards do not settle it. It matters here more than elsewhere because WeJammin's ISRC is attached to a provenance chain: silently swapping the audio under an ISRC means the credits now describe a performance nobody can hear. The honest move is to ask the artist which it is, since only they know — but that is a question most artists have never been asked and will not expect. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Update cost is stated per field, per store, before the click | The cost ranges from free to catastrophic and is invisible (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Credit updates are the cheapest, most encouraged action, and land in our record regardless of store acceptance | A late credit is the thesis arriving late (DT-02) |
| D-03 | A replaced master prompts the artist: fix or new version? | Only they know, and the ISRC's provenance chain depends on the answer (DT-03) |
