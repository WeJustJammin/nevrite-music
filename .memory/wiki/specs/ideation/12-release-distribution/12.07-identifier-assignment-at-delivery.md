# Feature: Identifier Assignment at Delivery

> **Number**: 12.07
> **Parent**: [Release & Distribution](./release-distribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Making sure every recording has an ISRC and every release a UPC before it goes anywhere — assigned
from WeJammin's prefix pool, or accepted from the artist if they already have their own. Identifiers
are the primary keys of the entire music industry: royalties, credits, Content ID claims and chart
reporting all resolve through them, and an identifier assigned carelessly at delivery is a data
error that propagates for decades and cannot be recalled.

The feature is small, automatic, and almost invisible — and it is the single point in domain 12
where a two-second bookkeeping act decides whether a Producer's credit still points at the right
recording in 2040. It is the thesis wearing a very boring costume (DT-03).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Supplies an identifier they already hold (their own registrant prefix, their label's, or one issued by a prior distributor) before delivery, choosing from a required **source** list — this is not a free-text field (D-08). After assignment, sees every identifier on the release, per track, with its prefix provenance stated in three values (**ours / yours / a third party's** — D-13), and can copy them out immediately, because they need them for their own SoundExchange and CMO registrations (DT-09) and those registrations happen before the record is out. Cannot choose a specific designation code, cannot change an identifier after delivery (it has propagated — see the Permanent state), and cannot delete one. Told plainly which prefix an identifier came from and what that means when they leave (12.08), because an ISRC drawn from our prefix is ours in a sense that matters later. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the ISRC of a recording they made, and can copy it — it is the key their credits (02) resolve through, so it is the handle by which their career's proof is found. **Holds the deciding fact and none of the permission**: only the person who ran the session knows whether the file about to be delivered is the same recording as the one that already has an ISRC or a materially different one that needs its own (12.05.02 DT-03). They are surfaced that question and given a **request path — never an edit path** (D-12), mirroring 12.05.02 Q-02's shape. If a recording is silently re-issued a second ISRC, their credit is attached to the wrong one and their work quietly detaches from the record. |
| Operator | None | Not visible, and correctly so. An Operator has no relationship to a recording's identity. A live album tracked in their room reaches this feature as a **recording contributed by a Musician**, never as an Operator action, and a studio holding points under a spec deal appears in 09's ledger, not here. Downstream spec writers must not invent an Operator surface for this feature. |
| Fan | None | No lens — and the only persona who ever sees the **consequence**. Every fan-facing surface in the industry resolves through these codes: a duplicate ISRC is why a Fan lands on two track pages for one song with the play count split across them, and a wrong UPC is why the album they pre-saved is not the album that appeared. They experience identifier errors as the product being broken, with no vocabulary to name what happened. |

## Behavior

### The two identifiers, and why they are not one thing

| | **ISRC** | **UPC/EAN (GTIN)** |
|---|---|---|
| Identifies | A **recording** (a performance) | A **release** (a product) |
| Question it answers | Which performance is this? | Which product is this? |
| Format | 12 characters: `CC-XXX-YY-NNNNN` — country (2), registrant (3), year of reference (2), designation (5) | 12 digits (UPC-A) or 13 (EAN-13) |
| Issued by | A national ISRC agency or IFPI, to a **registrant** | GS1, as a licensed **company prefix** |
| Error detection | **None. There is no check digit.** | Mod-10 check digit — typos are catchable |
| Tenure of the prefix | Permanent, one-time allocation | **Annual licence.** Not owned; leased |
| Capacity | 100,000 designation codes per registrant code per year | Depends on the licensed prefix length |
| Travels with | The recording, across every release it appears on | Nothing — one per product, forever new |

**They are not the same kind of thing and must not share one policy** (DT-05). Every asymmetry in
that table has a product consequence, and three of them are load-bearing: the missing check digit
makes a supplied ISRC unverifiable by arithmetic (DT-06), the annual GS1 licence makes the UPC's
permanence contingent on an invoice (Q-03), and the ISRC's permanence is what makes D-01 possible.

### The core rule: one recording, one ISRC, forever

The same recording on a single, an album and a compilation keeps its ISRC. That is what makes the
recordings-not-files model (12.01.01 D-01) coherent, and what lets credits, royalties and claims
all resolve to one thing. A new UPC per release; the ISRCs come along unchanged.

The artist-visible half of this rule is the part nobody explains, and per the domain's
**show the price at the decision** pattern it must be surfaced at the moment the release is built,
not discovered afterwards: DSPs deduplicate on ISRC. One ISRC across the single and the album is
what consolidates a track's streams and popularity onto one thing; two ISRCs for one recording
fragments them across two track pages, halves the chart signal, and splits the royalty line. The
exact per-DSP dedupe behaviour is partner knowledge and belongs in the partner-knowledge store
(CX-02), not restated here — but the direction of the consequence is stable enough to state to
the artist.

### When assignment happens

Assignment happens at **delivery** — specifically at ERN generation (12.02.01), which occurs at the
partner's lead time *before* the public release date, not on it. Assignment is meaningless earlier
(the recording is not yet a commercial object) and impossible later (generation blocks without it —
12.02.01 treats identifiers as required Resource and Release fields).

The schedule triggers the moment but does not inform the assignment, and the assignment does not
constrain the schedule — the relationship is pure sequence (CX R-04). Nothing about the date
reaches this feature.

The consequence the artist feels: their identifiers exist and are copyable from delivery, which is
days-to-weeks before release day, which is exactly when they need them for their own registrations
(DT-09). Before that, the pre-delivery view states *when* they will be assigned rather than showing
a blank (see the Unassigned state).

### Allocation rules

Resolves the breadth pass's open item on prefix pool management and allocation strategy. Whether
WeJammin becomes an ISRC registrant in its own right remains **Q-01** — it is a genuine product and
commercial decision that depends on 12.02 Q-01 (direct vs aggregator) and belongs to the owner.
The rules below hold under either answer.

1. **Never reuse a designation code.** Not after a cancelled release, not after a failed delivery,
   not after a takedown. A retired identifier stays retired. Identifiers are cheap (100,000 per
   registrant code per year); a reused one is a permanent lie.
2. **Assignment is idempotent per recording.** A recording that already carries an ISRC — from any
   prefix — is never assigned a second one. This is the guarantee, not an optimisation (D-05).
3. **Assignment survives delivery failure.** It is never rolled back, and a retry never allocates a
   new identifier (DT-04). This is the failure mode the "assign at delivery" framing invites and it
   is the most dangerous thing in the feature.
4. **Newly assigned identifiers on one release are allocated atomically** from a single registrant
   code and a single year of reference (D-10). A tracklist whose tracks straddle a pool rollover
   looks forged to a label and to a DSP, and it is the only product-visible face of pool exhaustion.
5. **Reused identifiers on one release need not share a prefix**, and normally will not. A
   compilation legitimately carries ISRCs from a dozen registrants. Prefix provenance is therefore
   recorded and displayed **per recording, never per release** (D-13).
6. **Year of reference is the year the ISRC was assigned**, not the year the recording was made.
   Migrated catalog assigned identifiers today carries this year (DT-13).

### Accepting artist-supplied identifiers

Not optional. A musician with an existing catalog, a label prefix, or an ISRC from a prior
distributor must be able to bring it — refusing would force a duplicate ISRC onto a recording that
already has one, which is a real and permanent data error (DT-02).

But accepting them is also an attack surface (DT-08), and the honest model is narrow:

- Supplied identifiers are **normalised** on entry (hyphens stripped, uppercased) and **validated
  on blur** against format: ISRC = 2 alpha (a valid ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code or an IFPI-allocated
  non-geographic code) + 3 alphanumeric + 2 digits + 5 digits; UPC/EAN = 12 or 13 digits with a
  correct mod-10 check digit. Inline error, no submit until it passes.
- **Format validation is not verification.** An ISRC has no check digit, so a transposed digit
  yields a structurally perfect ISRC pointing at a different recording or at nothing. The UI must
  never render a supplied ISRC as "valid" or "verified" — only as *well-formed* (D-08). Per the
  domain's **never claim more than you know** pattern, over-claiming here is the same class of
  failure as claiming `Live` on an `Accepted` ack.
- A **source is required** before save, from a closed list: *my registrant prefix* / *my label's
  prefix* / *issued by a prior distributor* / *from an existing release of this recording*. The
  answer is recorded as the provenance of the claim and is what a later collision conversation
  starts from.
- A supplied identifier is stored as an **assertion with a source**, never as a validated fact. If
  the domain adopts a visible confidence tier (domain Q-09), this is one of its clearest cases: a
  supplied ISRC is materially weaker evidence than one we allocated, and saying so is true.

### Collision detection, and its honest limit

There is no authoritative, complete, real-time global ISRC registry. IFPI's search covers a subset
and lags. **We can check format, and we can check our own records. That is the whole of it**
(DT-07). The product says exactly that and no more — a green tick reading "no collisions found"
would be a claim about the world we cannot support, on a product whose thesis is that its record
is true.

A supplied ISRC that matches a recording we already hold under a **different rights holder** is
**blocked**, not accepted with a warning (D-09). This is the ISRC-hijack case (DT-08) and a warning
would be an invitation.

### Prefix provenance and the exit

Every identifier carries a three-valued provenance — **ours**, **the artist's**, or **a third
party's** (D-13). The third value is not a technicality: a producer who runs a label supplies an
ISRC from their own registrant prefix for a recording that later appears on someone else's release,
and the ISRC travels with the recording (D-01). That release now carries an identifier from a prefix
belonging to neither the platform nor the artist — which breaks the binary the exit table in 12.08
assumes, and nobody has designed for it (DT-12).

Provenance is stated in exit terms at the moment it is set, because per D-18 the lock-in must be
earned and identifier hostage-taking is the textbook hostile kind. What actually leaves is 12.08's
decision (Q-01 there, domain Q-05), and this feature's job is to make sure the answer is
*computable per recording* rather than guessed at on the day.

## Happy Path

1. A release reaches its delivery window; ERN generation (12.02.01) requests identifiers for the
   release and for every recording on it.
2. Each recording is checked for an existing ISRC — from a prior release here, or supplied by the
   artist during building.
3. Recordings that already carry one **keep it**. No new allocation, no second identifier, whatever
   prefix it came from.
4. Recordings without one are allocated an ISRC — atomically, from one registrant code and one year
   of reference (D-10), designation codes never reused.
5. The release is allocated a UPC unless the artist supplied one. A UPC is always new: this release
   has never existed before.
6. Every identifier — supplied and allocated — is checked for format and against WeJammin's own
   records. A match against a different rights holder's recording blocks (D-09). A match against
   the same recording is a **reuse, not a collision**, and is silent (D-01).
7. Assignment commits and is now independent of the delivery's outcome (D-05). ERN generation
   proceeds with the identifiers as required message fields.
8. The Musician sees the full identifier set, per track, with per-recording prefix provenance, and
   can copy it out — for SoundExchange, their CMO, and their own records (DT-09).
9. Delivery proceeds. Partners accept, reject or go quiet independently (12.03.02). **The
   identifiers do not change on any of those outcomes.**
10. On the release date the identifiers are permanent, in our record and in everyone else's.

## Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Artist supplies an ISRC already held here for a **different** recording under a different rights holder | **Blocked.** The ISRC-hijack case (DT-08). Not a warning — a stop, routed to 24 | "That ISRC belongs to a different recording. Where did you get it?" — with the source they already declared shown back to them |
| Artist supplies an ISRC we hold for the **same** recording | Reuse, not collision. Silent no-op | "This recording already has this ISRC — that's correct." |
| Artist supplies an ISRC belonging to a recording we do **not** hold | **Accepted as an assertion with a source. We cannot know.** The honest limit (DT-07) | Well-formed, source recorded. Never "verified" |
| Artist pastes an ISRC copied off a DSP page for a famous track | Structurally perfect, semantically fraud. If we hold the recording it is blocked (D-09); if we do not, it reaches the DSP and surfaces as a store rejection or a Content ID conflict (12.06.03) | Blocked here, or a partner rejection later routed through the learning loop (CX-04) |
| Artist typos one digit of their own ISRC | **Undetectable.** No check digit (DT-06). The identifier is well-formed and wrong | Read-back confirmation of the supplied value is the only defence. The UI shows what will be delivered, formatted, before commit |
| Artist typos a UPC | Caught by the mod-10 check digit | Inline error on blur: "That barcode's check digit doesn't match — one of the digits is wrong." |
| Recording reused from a prior release | ISRC reused, not reissued | "Same recording, same ISRC — that's correct." |
| Same recording delivered to one partner on a single and an album at once | **Not a collision** and must never be treated as one (12.02.01, D-01) | Nothing |
| Alternate mix treated as the same recording | It needs its **own** ISRC; sharing one means credits describe a performance nobody can hear | 12.05.02 DT-03 / Q-01 — the line is genuinely contested and the industry has no consistent answer. The Producer is asked; they cannot decide (D-12) |
| Master replaced post-delivery | Fix or new version? Only the room knows. Answer decides whether a new ISRC is needed | 12.05.02 D-03 owns the prompt; this feature owns the consequence |
| **Delivery fails after assignment** | Identifier is **kept**. Retry reuses it. Never rolled back, never reallocated (D-05, DT-04) | Nothing — the artist never learns this almost went wrong |
| **Allocation from an external pool times out ambiguously** | Did we get it or not? **No blind retry.** Resolved by lookup keyed on the recording before any second attempt — a blind retry is how one recording gets two ISRCs (DT-04) | Delivery holds; identifiers are not "pending" twice |
| Two co-owners deliver two releases containing the same un-ISRC'd recording simultaneously | Race. Idempotency is per **recording**, not per release — exactly one ISRC is allocated and both releases carry it (D-05) | Nothing. The correct outcome is the invisible one |
| Release cancelled after assignment, before delivery | Identifiers burned; the recording **keeps** its ISRC. If it is released later, same ISRC | "This recording already has its ISRC from the release you cancelled. It keeps it." |
| Release taken down (12.05.01) | ISRCs survive — provenance survives takedown (12.05.01 D-02). UPC is spent | "Your credits and identifiers are still here. The record is off the stores." |
| Redelivery after a takedown | It is a **new release** (12.05.01 D-03): **new UPC, every ISRC unchanged** | "New barcode, same recordings — the tracks keep their history." |
| Store rejects an ISRC as already registered elsewhere | The recording is live via another distributor | Routes to 12.08 (migration). "Take it down there first" — the loss is real and stated (12.08 D-03) |
| Migrated catalog arrives with no ISRCs | Assigning now stamps **this year** as the year of reference on a 2019 recording (DT-13) | Q-04 — the artist should be told their 2019 record will carry a 2026 identifier before we stamp it |
| Migrated catalog arrives with ISRCs from the prior distributor's prefix | Kept, provenance = *a third party's*. They are not ours and not the artist's | "These came from DistroKid's prefix. They're on your recordings and they stay on them." |
| Producer's label prefix on another artist's release | Provenance = *a third party's*. Breaks 12.08's ours/theirs binary (DT-12) | Stated per track, not per release (D-13) |
| Compilation of 14 recordings from 9 registrants | Correct and normal. Nine prefixes on one tracklist | Per-track provenance; no release-level claim |
| **Partial supply** — artist supplies ISRCs for 3 of 11 tracks | Mixed provenance on one release. Legitimate | 3 rows *yours*, 8 rows *ours*. No release-level summary that flattens the difference |
| Prefix pool exhausted (100,000 designation codes in a year) | Operational — an additional registrant code. Product-visible **only** via D-10: a release must not straddle two registrant codes | Routed to `/create-prd-architecture`. The atomicity rule is the product-side requirement it must satisfy |
| Artist expects their ISRC to say `GB` because they are British; it says `QZ` | The country code is the **registrant's**, and IFPI's non-geographic codes are not a country at all (DT-10) | Explained at the point they see it, or it becomes a support ticket and a suspicion that we got it wrong |
| Artist wants an identifier changed post-delivery | Effectively impossible; it has propagated | "It's out there now — royalty systems, Content ID, charts. It can't be recalled." |
| Recording turns out to be someone else's; involuntary takedown (12.05.03) | We allocated an ISRC from our prefix to a recording we had no right to identify. **ISRCs are never reassigned**, so it persists, pointing at a contested recording | Routed to 12.05.03 / 24. No product affordance retracts an ISRC, because none exists in the standard |
| Artist leaves mid-assignment (12.08) | Assignment is not a state anyone can be "mid". It commits or it has not started | 12.08 owns what leaves; this feature guarantees provenance is per-recording and therefore answerable |
| GS1 company prefix licence lapses | Every UPC issued from it becomes questionable — the prefix can be re-licensed to another company. **The permanence of a "permanent" identifier is contingent on an annual invoice** | Q-03. No user-facing surface; a real operational risk with no owner today |

## States

### Identifier lifecycle (per identifier)

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Unassigned** | Pre-delivery, no identifier supplied | Not blank — "ISRC assigned when this release is delivered (14 Aug)". Artists ask where their ISRC is; silence reads as an error |
| **Supplied** | Artist provided one, source declared | Well-formed + source + prefix provenance. **Never** "verified" (D-08) |
| **Allocated** | Drawn from a pool at ERN generation | Shown with prefix provenance = *ours*, and what that means on exit |
| **Reused** | The recording already had one | "Same recording, same ISRC" — no allocation occurred |
| **Blocked** | Supplied ISRC matches another rights holder's recording (D-09) | Blocking, with the specific question and their declared source shown back |
| **Permanent** | Delivered | Read-only, forever. Copyable, never editable |

### View states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Identifier panel opened on a release mid-delivery | Skeleton rows per track. Never a spinner over an empty list — the tracklist is already known, only the codes are not |
| **Empty** | Pre-delivery release, nothing supplied, nothing allocated | Per-track rows showing *when* identifiers arrive, plus the affordance to supply one now. Not an empty state — a scheduled one |
| **Populated** | Assignment committed | Per-track ISRC + release UPC, each with prefix provenance in three values, all copyable individually and as a set |
| **Error** | Supplied identifier fails format, or is blocked | Inline, per field, on blur. The specific fault named — "check digit doesn't match", not "invalid barcode". A blocked ISRC is a different affordance from a malformed one: **different consequences need different affordances** |
| **Partial** | Some tracks supplied, some allocated, some still unassigned | Explicit per-row provenance. **No release-level roll-up** — a summary that says "identifiers: assigned" over a mixed set is the flattening that D-13 exists to prevent |

| DT-01 | Identifier assignment is a feature of domain 12 | The sweep's framing, and the assignment does happen at delivery | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a split. The **assignment act** is 12's — it happens at delivery, gated by delivery, and is meaningless before it. But the **identifier space** is a cross-cut serving 01 (IPI/ISNI party identifiers), 02 (credits resolve by ISRC), 09 (works by ISWC), 10 (royalty statements key on ISRC), 11 (licences reference both) and 12 (ISRC/UPC/GRid). | DT-04 | Assignment happens at delivery, so a failed delivery can roll the assignment back and a retry can allocate again | The obvious transactional reading of "assign at delivery", and the shape any implementer will reach for first | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the most dangerous idea in the feature.** Delivery in this domain is asynchronous, partial and silent by design (domain D-01: live on Spotify, rejected by Apple, pending on Beatport). A rollback-and-retry that fires after one partner has already accepted produces two ISRCs for one live recording — the platform committing, by its own bookkeeping, the exact error it blocks artists from committing. The corollary is subtle and matters more: an **ambiguously timed-out allocation** from an external pool must never be blindly retried, because the first call may have succeeded. Assignment must be idempotent per recording (D-05) and must **outlive** any single partner's outcome. Nobody designs for this because assignment looks like a step inside the delivery transaction, and it cannot be one. |
| DT-05 | ISRC and UPC are two flavours of the same thing and can share one assignment policy, one provenance model and one exit answer | They are both "identifiers assigned at delivery", they are both in the ERN, and 12.08's exit table lists them on one row | ❌ REJECTED — they differ on every axis that matters, and the differences are not cosmetic. **Tenure**: an ISRC registrant code is a permanent one-time allocation; a **GS1 company prefix is an annual licence**, and GS1's model names the licensee — us — as the brand owner. **Error detection**: UPC carries a mod-10 check digit, ISRC carries nothing. **Scope**: the ISRC travels with the recording across every release forever; the UPC is per-product and never travels. The consequence lands squarely on CX-10 and 12.08 DT-02, which recommend "identifiers leave with the recording" as one answer to one question: **that answer is clean for ISRC and genuinely unclear for UPC.** An artist can take an ISRC from our prefix and it costs us only the retention mechanism. An artist taking a UPC from our GS1 prefix is using a barcode whose licensed brand owner is a company they no longer deal with, on a lease we might stop paying. 12.08's single-row treatment hides a real distinction, and it is raised here as Q-03 rather than resolved unilaterally, because it is the owner's call. |
| DT-06 | Format validation makes a supplied identifier safe to accept | It is what every distributor's ISRC field does, and it feels like diligence | ❌ REJECTED — **an ISRC has no check digit.** A transposed digit produces a structurally perfect ISRC that points at a different recording, or at none, and no amount of format validation will ever catch it. UPC is the opposite case: mod-10 catches single-digit typos. So D-02's promise (bring your own identifiers) opens a data-integrity hazard on ISRC with **no arithmetic defence at all**, on the one identifier where the error is permanent. The only real defences are behavioural: a required source (D-08), a read-back before commit, and a UI that says *well-formed* and never *verified*. This is why D-08 exists and why the confidence-tier question (domain Q-09) has a clearer case here than almost anywhere else in the product. |
| DT-07 | We can detect ISRC collisions | It is in the breadth pass's own happy path — "checked against our own records for collision" — and it sounds like table stakes | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the qualifier is the whole point. **There is no authoritative, complete, real-time global ISRC registry.** IFPI's search covers a subset and lags; there is no lookup that answers "is this ISRC already in use anywhere". We can check format, and we can check our own records. That is the entire extent of it. The breadth pass's phrasing is correct and the UI built naively from it would not be: a green "no collisions found" is a claim about the world we cannot support. Per the domain's **never claim more than you know** pattern (12.02.03 D-02, 12.03.02 D-04), this is the same failure class as rendering `Accepted` as `Live` — and it is worse here, because the artist has no way to discover it was a guess until a royalty goes missing years later. The product states the limit in plain words. |
| DT-08 | Accepting artist-supplied identifiers (D-02) is a purely pro-artist decision with an upside and no cost | It is the anti-lock-in move, it is what D-18 requires, and DT-02 already confirmed it | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **it is also an attack surface, and DT-02 opened it without naming it.** A supplied ISRC is an unverifiable assertion (DT-06, DT-07). Supplying an ISRC that belongs to someone else's live recording is a real and cheap attack: DSPs dedupe on ISRC, so the payload attaches your delivery to their track — hijacking a stream count, a chart position, a royalty line, or a Content ID asset (12.06.03). The anti-persona sections of personas.md already describe exactly this appetite: the Musician who "games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation". The mitigations are the narrow model above — required source, assertion-not-fact, and a **block rather than a warning** where a supplied ISRC hits another rights holder's recording (D-09). This does not reverse DT-02; it prices it. A pro-artist decision that ships without its adversarial half is a gift to the anti-persona. |
| DT-09 | Identifiers are needed only at delivery, so there is no reason to show them to the artist before then | Assignment is automatic and the codes are opaque — the breadth pass's own States table says the pre-delivery state is "not surfaced" | ❌ REJECTED — the artist needs their ISRCs **before the record is out**, and not for us. SoundExchange registration, CMO/PRO registration and neighbouring-rights claims all key on the ISRC, and an artist who misses those registrations around release loses collectable money that domain 10 exists to recover. The registrations are their obligation, on their timeline, in systems we do not control. Since assignment lands at ERN generation — days to weeks before release day (see When assignment happens) — the identifiers exist exactly when they are needed, and hiding them converts a solved problem into a support ticket. The pre-delivery state is therefore not "hidden" but "scheduled": it states *when*, because an artist who cannot find their ISRC concludes we lost it. |
| DT-10 | An ISRC's country code tells the artist something about them — their country, or their label's | It is literally called the country code, it sits in the first two characters, and every artist reads it that way | ❌ REJECTED — it is the **registrant's** country, not the recording's, not the artist's, and IFPI's non-geographic codes (`QM`, `QZ` and similar, allocated when a national agency's space overflowed) are not a country at all. A British artist on our prefix sees a code that says nothing about Britain. Small, and worth logging for two reasons: it generates real support load at scale, and — sharper — an artist who thinks their identifier is wrong has been given a reason to distrust the record on the one screen whose only job is to be trustworthy. The fix is one sentence of explanation at the point of display; the cost of omitting it is a suspicion we cannot afford. |
| DT-11 | A release's identifiers should all come from one prefix — it is one release, it should look coherent | Tidiness, and the assumption baked into "the release draws from a prefix pool" | ⚠️ SPLIT — true for **newly allocated** identifiers, false for **reused** ones, and conflating them produces a wrong rule either way. Newly allocated identifiers on one release must be atomic from one registrant code and one year of reference (D-10): a tracklist straddling a pool rollover looks forged, and this is the only product-visible consequence of pool exhaustion. But a compilation legitimately carries ISRCs from a dozen registrants, because the ISRC travels with the recording (D-01) and always has. Any rule enforcing single-prefix coherence across a release would break compilations, which is a large and normal category. The resolution is that **provenance is per-recording, never per-release** (D-13) — which also kills the tempting release-level "identifiers: yours/ours" summary, since a mixed set has no honest release-level value. |
| DT-12 | An identifier's prefix is either ours or the artist's — it is a binary, and the exit turns on it | 12.08's exit table, CX-10, and DT-02 all frame it exactly this way | ❌ REJECTED — **there is a third value, and it is common.** A producer who runs a label supplies an ISRC from their own registrant prefix; the recording later appears on a different artist's release, and the ISRC travels with it (D-01). That release carries an identifier from a prefix belonging to neither the platform nor the artist. Migrated catalog is the same shape at scale: ISRCs from a prior distributor's prefix are a third party's, permanently. This is a live break in 12.08's model — the exit table has rows for "their prefix" and "our prefix" and no row for "someone else's", which is the row a Producer-run label produces every time. Per personas.md the multi-hyphenate is the structural fact of this industry; a binary that assumes the artist and the label are different people fails on the exact user this product is built for. Provenance is three-valued (D-13), and 12.08 needs the third row. |
| DT-13 | The year in an ISRC is the year the recording was made | It is the only reading the four characters suggest, and it is how artists and A&R read it | ❌ REJECTED — the Year of Reference is the year the **ISRC was assigned**, which is a different fact and usually a different number. The consequence is concentrated in exactly one place and it is 12.08's: an artist migrating a 2019 catalog that never had ISRCs gets identifiers stamped with **this** year. Their back catalog now carries identifiers that read as newly-created recordings — which, to anyone reading the code as a date, makes a genuine 2019 record look like a re-recording. Nothing in the standard is broken; the artist is simply handed a fact that misrepresents them, by us, at the moment they are trusting us with their history. It is disclosable in one sentence before we stamp it, and it is not disclosable at all afterwards. Raised as Q-04 because whether to assign at all — versus leaving migrated catalog un-ISRC'd — is a product decision with real costs both ways. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- **Proposed cross-cut: Music Identifier Registry (ISRC/ISWC/UPC/GRid/IPI/ISNI)** — allocation, storage, resolution, collision detection and prefix provenance, serving 01, 02, 09, 10, 11, 12 (DT-01). Recorded in the domain CX file and returned for the global CX. **Step 6 note**: the registry must carry the idempotency guarantee (D-05) and the three-valued provenance (D-13), because both are properties of the *space*, not of this moment — a registry that allocates without them re-opens DT-04 and DT-12 for every domain that consumes it.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../02-credits-attribution/)) — **shared entity**: credits resolve by ISRC (DT-03). A second ISRC on one recording silently detaches every credit attached to the first. This is the feature's highest-consequence cross-cut and the one with no visible failure signal.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../09-rights-ownership/)) — works carry ISWCs, recordings carry ISRCs; the two are linked and issued by different parties at different moments. **Permission**: D-09's block turns on "a different rights holder", which is a fact only 09 holds — this feature cannot evaluate its own blocking rule without it.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../10-royalties-collections/)) — **two distinct dependencies.** (a) DSR statements key on ISRC; an identifier error here is an unmatched-royalty error there, which is the black-box problem. (b) **Trigger dependency, and the one the breadth pass missed**: the artist's own SoundExchange/CMO registrations key on the ISRC and happen *around release*, so identifiers must be copyable from assignment, not from release day (DT-09). If 10 owns registration assistance, this feature's assignment moment is its upstream trigger.
- Touches **12.02.01 ERN Generation** ([path](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging/12.02.01-ern-message-generation.md)) — **trigger dependency, both directions**: generation is what requests assignment (it is the delivery moment in practice), and identifiers are required Resource/Release fields it blocks without. Generation is a pure projection (CX-01) — it must never default or synthesise an identifier.
- Touches **12.08 Catalog Migration & Exit** ([path](./12.08-catalog-migration-exit.md)) — prefix ownership determines what leaves with the artist (DT-02, CX-10). **Two corrections this feature owes 12.08**: its exit table needs a third row for third-party prefixes (DT-12), and its single "ISRC/UPC from our prefix" row conflates two identifiers whose exit answers differ (DT-05, Q-03). Migration *in* also inherits DT-13 (Q-04).
- Touches **12.05.01 Voluntary Takedown** ([path](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.01-voluntary-takedown.md)) — **state dependency**: ISRCs survive takedown (12.05.01 D-02 — provenance survives), and redelivery is a new release (12.05.01 D-03), which means **new UPC, same ISRCs**. The asymmetry is the clearest possible demonstration of DT-05 in the artist's own hands.
- Touches **12.05.02 Update & Redelivery** ([path](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.02-metadata-update-redelivery.md)) — a replaced master may or may not need a new ISRC; the line is contested (12.05.02 DT-03, Q-01). 12.05.02 D-03 asks the artist; **this feature adds that the Producer is the one who knows** and gives them a request path, not an edit path (D-12).
- Touches **12.05.03 Involuntary Takedown** ([path](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.03-involuntary-takedown-suspension.md)) — an ISRC allocated from our prefix to a recording that turns out not to be the claimant's cannot be retracted; the standard has no mechanism. A permanent artefact of a fraudulent delivery, with no owner.
- Touches **12.06.03 Claim Disputes & Ownership Conflicts** ([path](./12.06-content-id-ugc-claiming/12.06.03-claim-disputes-ownership-conflicts.md)) — a hijacked ISRC (DT-08) surfaces there as an ownership conflict, and imported catalog's third-party identifiers arrive without attestations (CX-12), so the evidence advantage does not apply to exactly the identifiers we are least able to verify.
- Touches **12.01.05 Label Copy & Distributor of Record** ([path](./12.01-release-builder/12.01.05-label-copy-distributor-of-record.md)) — **CX-07**: distributor of record determines whose prefix is used, and 12.01.05 already commits to stating that in plain language at the moment it is set. That statement and this feature's exit-terms provenance are one promise made twice; they must not drift.
- Touches **24 Trust & Safety & Disputes** ([path](../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **permission + escalation**: D-09's block needs a resolution path and an evidence bar this feature does not own (Q-05). The ISRC-hijack case (DT-08) is a fraud report, not a validation error.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — the third-party-prefix case (DT-12) is a *label entity* from 01 holding a registrant code, distinct from the human who supplied it. The registry cross-cut carries IPI/ISNI for the same reason.
- Consumes cross-cut **Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution** — identifiers are the industry's entity resolution mechanism, and DT-07 is the reason it only half-works.
- Consumes cross-cut **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** — the source declared for a supplied identifier (D-08) is only worth recording if it is unforgeable later; the collision conversation starts from it.
- **Explicitly not a cross-cut**: 12.04 Scheduling. The schedule triggers the delivery that triggers assignment, but does not inform it, and assignment does not constrain the schedule. Pure sequence (CX R-04). Recorded because "assignment happens at delivery, and delivery is scheduled" reads like a dependency and is not one.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | One recording, one ISRC, forever — reused across every release it appears on | Makes the recordings-not-files model coherent (12.01.01 D-01) and keeps credits resolvable |
| D-02 | Artist-supplied identifiers are accepted, with prefix provenance stated | Refusing forces duplicate ISRCs and is hostile lock-in (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The assignment act stays in 12; the identifier registry is a cross-cut | The space is shared; the moment is ours (DT-01) |
| D-04 | Prefix provenance is visible and explained in exit terms | Earned lock-in must be legible (D-18) |
| D-05 | **Assignment is idempotent per recording, and survives delivery failure.** Never rolled back, never reallocated on retry; an ambiguous allocation timeout is resolved by lookup, never by a blind second attempt | Delivery is async and partial, so there is no single outcome to roll back to. Rollback-and-retry after one partner accepted is the platform committing the exact double-ISRC error it blocks artists from committing (DT-04) |
| D-06 | Assignment happens at **ERN generation**, not on the release date; identifiers are visible and copyable to the Musician from that moment | It is when the identifier is first required (12.02.01), and it is before the artist's own SoundExchange/CMO registrations are due (DT-09) |
| D-07 | **ISRC and UPC have separate policies** — for allocation, provenance and exit | They differ on tenure (permanent registrant code vs annual GS1 lease), error detection (none vs mod-10) and scope (travels with the recording vs never travels). One policy gets at least one of them wrong (DT-05) |
| D-08 | A supplied identifier is stored as an **assertion with a required source**, never as a validated fact. The UI says *well-formed*; it never says *verified* | We cannot verify it — no check digit on ISRC (DT-06), no global registry (DT-07). Claiming otherwise is the `Accepted`-rendered-as-`Live` failure on the one screen whose only job is to be trustworthy |
| D-09 | A supplied ISRC matching a recording we hold under a **different rights holder** is **blocked** and routed to 24 — not accepted with a warning | It is the hijack case (DT-08). A warning on an attack is an invitation |
| D-10 | Newly assigned identifiers on one release are allocated **atomically** from one registrant code and one year of reference | A tracklist straddling a pool rollover looks forged. This is the only product-visible face of pool exhaustion, and the requirement `/create-prd-architecture` must satisfy (DT-11) |
| D-11 | Redelivery after takedown gets a **new UPC** and keeps **every ISRC** | It is a new release (12.05.01 D-03) containing the same recordings. The asymmetry is D-07 made concrete in the artist's hands |
| D-12 | The Producer is **asked** the same-recording-or-new question and given a **request path, never an edit path** | They hold the deciding fact (12.05.02 DT-03) and no authority — the domain's recurring Producer shape. Mirrors 12.05.02 Q-02: a request flow is not a permission grant |
| D-13 | Prefix provenance is **three-valued** (ours / the artist's / a third party's) and recorded **per recording, never per release** | The producer-run-label case and all migrated catalog produce the third value (DT-12); compilations make any release-level roll-up dishonest (DT-11) |
| D-14 | Collision checking is stated as **format + our own records**, in plain words, with no claim about the world | There is no global registry (DT-07). "Never claim more than you know" is domain policy, and this is its least visible and most permanent application |
