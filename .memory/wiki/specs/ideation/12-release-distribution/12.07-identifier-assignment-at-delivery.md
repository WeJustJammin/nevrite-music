# Feature: Identifier Assignment at Delivery

> **Number**: 12.07
> **Parent**: [Release & Distribution](./release-distribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Making sure every recording has an ISRC and every release a UPC before it goes anywhere — assigned
from WeJammin's prefix pool, or accepted from the artist if they already have their own. Identifiers
are the primary keys of the entire music industry: royalties, credits, Content ID claims and chart
reporting all resolve through them, and an identifier assigned carelessly at delivery is a data
error that propagates for decades and cannot be recalled.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the identifiers assigned to their recordings and release, or supplies their own if they hold a registrant prefix or their label issued them. Told plainly which prefix an identifier came from and what that means when they leave (12.08) — because an ISRC drawn from our prefix is ours in a sense that matters later. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the ISRC of a recording they made. Consequential: the ISRC is the key their credits (02) resolve through, so it is the handle by which their career's proof is found. If a recording is silently re-issued a second ISRC (12.05.02 DT-03), their credit is attached to the wrong one and their work quietly detaches from the record. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, though every fan-facing surface in the industry resolves through these codes. |

## Behavior

Two identifiers matter at delivery, and they answer different questions:

| Identifier | Scope | Assigned by | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISRC** | Recording | Registrant (us, a label, or the artist) | Which performance is this? |
| **UPC/EAN** | Release | Us or the artist's label | Which product is this? |

The rule: **one recording, one ISRC, forever.** The same recording on a single, an album and a
compilation keeps its ISRC — that is what makes the recordings-not-files model (12.01.01 D-01)
coherent, and what lets credits, royalties and claims all resolve to one thing. A new UPC per
release; the ISRCs come along unchanged.

Assignment happens at delivery because that is when the recording becomes a commercial object. But
the identifier is used by Credits (02), Rights (09), Royalties (10) and Licensing (11) — so
**assignment is domain 12's act, while the identifier space is a cross-cut** (see below and the
domain CX file).

Accepting artist-supplied identifiers is not optional. A musician with an existing catalog, a
label prefix, or an ISRC from a prior distributor must be able to bring it — refusing would
force a duplicate ISRC on an existing recording, which is a real and permanent data error.

### Happy Path

1. Release reaches delivery; every recording is checked for an ISRC.
2. Recordings already carrying one (reused from a prior release, or supplied by the artist) keep it.
3. Recordings without one are assigned from the appropriate prefix pool.
4. The release is assigned a UPC unless the artist supplied one.
5. Identifiers are validated for format and checked against our own records for collision.
6. Delivery proceeds; identifiers are now permanent and appear in every message (12.02.01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Artist supplies an ISRC already used by a different recording | A collision, and a real data error | "That ISRC belongs to another recording. Where did it come from?" |
| Recording reused from a prior release | ISRC reused, not reissued | "Same recording, same ISRC — that's correct." |
| Alternate mix treated as the same recording | It needs its **own** ISRC; sharing one means credits describe a performance nobody can hear | 12.05.02 DT-03; the line is genuinely contested |
| Artist has a label prefix | Their prefix, not ours — and it changes what leaves with them | "These are yours. They come with you if you go." |
| Store rejects an ISRC as already registered elsewhere | The recording is live via another distributor | Routes to 12.08 (migration) |
| Prefix pool exhausted | Operational, not product | Routed to `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Artist wants an identifier changed post-delivery | Effectively impossible; the identifier has propagated | "It's out there now — royalty systems, Content ID, charts. It can't be recalled." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unassigned | Pre-delivery | Not surfaced — assignment is automatic |
| Supplied | Artist provided one | Validated + prefix ownership stated |
| Assigned | Allocated from our pool | Shown with prefix provenance |
| Reused | Recording already had one | "Same recording, same ISRC" |
| Collision | Conflicts with a known recording | Blocking, with the specific question |
| Permanent | Delivered | Read-only, forever |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Identifier assignment is a feature of domain 12 | The sweep's framing, and the assignment does happen at delivery | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a split. The **assignment act** is 12's — it happens at delivery, gated by delivery, and is meaningless before it. But the **identifier space** is a cross-cut serving 01 (IPI/ISNI party identifiers), 02 (credits resolve by ISRC), 09 (works by ISWC), 10 (royalty statements key on ISRC), 11 (licences reference both) and 12 (ISRC/UPC/GRid). Modelling the whole registry inside 12 would make Release the de-facto owner of identifiers that other domains assign at other moments — an ISWC is issued by a CMO on work registration, nowhere near a release. Recorded as the proposed **Music Identifier Registry** cross-cut; this feature keeps only the assignment-at-delivery moment. |
| DT-02 | WeJammin should always assign identifiers from its own prefix — it is simpler and it is what distributors do | Operational simplicity; DistroKid and TuneCore both do exactly this | ❌ REJECTED — an artist with their own registrant prefix or an existing catalog **must** be able to supply theirs, and refusing would force a duplicate ISRC onto a recording that already has one: a permanent, propagating data error committed by a platform whose entire thesis is that its record is true. There is also a lock-in dimension. An ISRC from our prefix is, in a soft but real sense, ours — and the incumbents' quiet reliance on that is part of what makes leaving them feel like a loss. Per D-18 the lock-in must be **earned**; identifier hostage-taking is the textbook hostile kind, and problem-statement.md Q-02 asks this question directly. |
| DT-03 | Identifiers are administrative plumbing with no product surface | They are opaque codes nobody looks at, and no artist has ever asked to see one | ❌ REJECTED — the ISRC is **the key the artist's proof of work resolves through**. Per problem-statement.md the whole product exists so that "the fact of the work accretes to their identity"; that accretion is implemented by an identifier. A silently reissued ISRC (12.05.02 DT-03) detaches a Producer's credit from the recording it describes — the exact evaporation-of-proof the product was built to stop, caused by the platform's own bookkeeping. Identifiers are not plumbing here; they are the spine of the thesis wearing a very boring costume. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- **Proposed cross-cut: Music Identifier Registry (ISRC/ISWC/UPC/GRid/IPI/ISNI)** — allocation, storage, resolution, collision detection and prefix provenance, serving 01, 02, 09, 10, 11, 12 (DT-01). Recorded in the domain CX file and returned for the global CX.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([path](../02-credits-attribution/)) — credits resolve by ISRC (DT-03).
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../09-rights-ownership/)) — works carry ISWCs; recordings carry ISRCs; the two are linked and issued by different parties at different moments.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../10-royalties-collections/)) — DSR statements key on ISRC. An identifier error here is an unmatched-royalty error there, which is the black-box problem.
- Touches **12.02.01 ERN Generation** ([path](./12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging/12.02.01-ern-message-generation.md)) — identifiers are required message fields; generation cannot proceed without them.
- Touches **12.08 Catalog Migration & Exit** ([path](./12.08-catalog-migration-exit.md)) — prefix ownership determines what leaves with the artist (DT-02).
- Touches **12.05.02 Update & Redelivery** ([path](./12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.02-metadata-update-redelivery.md)) — a replaced master may or may not need a new ISRC; the line is contested.
- Consumes cross-cut **Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution** — identifiers are the industry's entity resolution mechanism, and the reason it half-works.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | One recording, one ISRC, forever — reused across every release it appears on | Makes the recordings-not-files model coherent (12.01.01 D-01) and keeps credits resolvable |
| D-02 | Artist-supplied identifiers are accepted, with prefix provenance stated | Refusing forces duplicate ISRCs and is hostile lock-in (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The assignment act stays in 12; the identifier registry is a cross-cut | The space is shared; the moment is ours (DT-01) |
| D-04 | Prefix provenance is visible and explained in exit terms | Earned lock-in must be legible (D-18) |
