# Feature: Catalog Migration & Exit

> **Number**: 12.08
> **Parent**: [Release & Distribution](./release-distribution-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Bringing an existing catalog in from another distributor, and taking it out again. Both directions
of the same door. Migration in is an adoption problem — a musician with 40 releases on DistroKid
cannot use WeJammin as a distributor until those releases live here. Migration out is a **values**
problem, and it is the concrete answer to the question problem-statement.md asks about this entire
product: is the lock-in earned or hostile?

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. See DT-01.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Imports their catalog from a prior distributor, and — if they leave — takes it out: their recordings, masters, identifiers, credits, splits, attestations and delivery history, in a form another platform can actually read. Sees plainly, before they ever commit, what is theirs and what is not. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees whether a record they made is migrating, because it determines whether their credit survives. **The most exposed persona here**: a Producer's credits attach to recordings owned by dozens of different artists, so their career's proof is spread across other people's exit decisions. If an artist leaves and takes the recording, does the Producer's attested credit go with it, stay, or exist in both? Nobody has asked this. See DT-03. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

**Migration in.** An existing release lives on stores via another distributor. WeJammin cannot
simply deliver it again — that creates a duplicate (12.02.03 DT-03). The real sequence is: the
artist takes the release down at the old distributor, we deliver it here, and it comes back as a
new release with a new date and zero streams. That is a genuinely terrible trade and it is why
catalog migration is the moat every distributor actually relies on. Some of it can be softened
(retaining ISRCs, preserving the original release date in metadata where stores permit) —
`[PENDING — Step 5]` for what is really possible.

Note the honest limit: imported releases carry **no attestations**. We were not in the room. They
are catalog we hold, not provenance we witnessed (12.06.03 DT-02).

**Migration out.** What leaves with the artist:

| Asset | Leaves? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Master audio | ✅ Theirs | Always was |
| ISRC/UPC from **their** prefix | ✅ Theirs | 12.07 D-02 |
| ISRC/UPC from **our** prefix | ⚠️ The whole question | See DT-02 |
| Credits, splits, attestations | ✅ Must | This is the point |
| Delivery history, messages, per-store status | ✅ Should | Cheap; it is their record |
| Store artist page links | ✅ Theirs | They are their pages (12.03.04) |
| Rejection knowledge learned from their releases | ❌ Ours | Aggregate, not theirs |

**The exit is the proof of the thesis.** D-18 says provenance is "the value they cannot take with
them and no competitor can retroactively manufacture" — which is uncomfortably close to describing
a hostage. The resolution: they take **everything**, and the reason they stay is that no other
platform can *use* it, not that we refuse to hand it over. A competitor receiving a WeJammin export
gets a pile of true facts and no way to have witnessed them. The lock-in is that the attestations
happened *here*, not that the file is locked in a drawer.

That distinction is the entire difference between D-18 being a strategy and being a trap.

### Happy Path — In

1. Musician has an existing catalog on a prior distributor and chooses to bring it to WeJammin.
2. They import the metadata — titles, artists, ISRCs/UPCs, release dates, credits, splits — by
   file (a distributor export, a spreadsheet) or by supplying identifiers the platform resolves.
3. Platform states the honest cost **before commitment**: to distribute here, the release must
   come down at the old distributor and be re-delivered, arriving as a new release with a new date
   and zero accumulated streams (D-03). ISRCs are retained where the prefix allows; original
   release date is preserved in metadata where stores permit.
4. Each imported release is marked **Imported / not witnessed** — it is catalog the platform holds,
   not provenance it witnessed (D-02).
5. Musician confirms per-release; migration runs and reports per-release progress.
6. The catalog now lives here, visibly distinguished from natively-released, witnessed work.

### Happy Path — Out

1. Musician decides to leave and requests their catalog.
2. Platform states exactly what leaves and what does not, before anything is committed.
3. Export is produced: masters, identifiers with prefix provenance, credits, splits, attestations,
   delivery history — in an open, documented format.
4. Distributor-of-record change and takedown/redelivery consequences are stated honestly.
5. They leave. The record they built goes with them.
6. Their account and identity persist (they are still a Musician, a Producer, a Fan) unless deleted.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| ISRCs came from our prefix | The crux. Do they keep them? | DT-02. Recommend yes — see below |
| Release is co-owned; one owner leaves | Their share leaves; the release does not | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — an org-governance question (01/09) |
| Producer's credit on a departed artist's recording | Whose fact is it? | DT-03. Genuinely unresolved |
| Migration in duplicates a live release | Duplicate DSP pages: split streams, split royalties | Blocked. "Take it down at DistroKid first. You'll lose the streams — that's not us, that's how this works." |
| Artist leaves mid-dispute (12.05.03) | Cannot export contested facts as settled | Export marks what is contested |
| Imported catalog has no attestations | We were not there | Stated: it is catalog, not provenance (12.06.03 DT-02) |
| Artist deletes their account entirely | GDPR erasure vs a split sheet other people rely on | **A real conflict.** Their co-writers' provenance depends on facts about them. Routed to `/create-prd-security` |

### States

Two axes: the per-release **lifecycle** state, and the standard interaction states of the
import/export flow itself.

**Lifecycle (per release):**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Native | Released through WeJammin | Full provenance; witnessed |
| Imported | Migrated in | Catalog with an explicit "not witnessed" marker |
| Importing | Migration in progress | Per-release progress + honest loss statement |
| Exportable | Normal | "Take your catalog" is always available, never buried |
| Exporting | Export running | Progress + manifest of what is included |
| Departed | Artist left | Their record went with them; identity persists |

**Interaction (import/export flow):**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Manifest of what-leaves / what-imports is being computed | Skeleton; "working out what's yours…" — never an empty screen that implies nothing leaves |
| Empty | Artist has no catalog yet (nothing to export, nothing imported) | Neutral empty state; export CTA remains present and honest, not hidden |
| Populated | Manifest resolved | The full what-leaves / what-imports table, with prefix provenance and contested-fact markers |
| Error | Import parse fails, export job fails, or upstream distributor lookup times out | Named failure + retry; **partial exports are never presented as complete** — a failed export is failed, not a truncated success |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The candidate list is missing catalog migration entirely | The sweep's 8 candidates describe how a release is *made and delivered*; none describes how a catalog *arrives* or *leaves*. But problem-statement.md Q-02 asks explicitly whether the lock-in is earned or hostile, and D-18 stakes the product on the answer. That question has no node anywhere in the domain map | ✅ CONFIRMED — added as a feature. The omission is structural, not accidental: **a sweep enumerating what a distributor does will never surface the exit, because no distributor wants it to exist.** DistroKid, TuneCore and CD Baby all rely on migration friction as their retention mechanism. A domain map derived from their behaviour inherits their silence. This is the clearest case in domain 12 for Deep Think as a distinct step from drilling candidates. |
| DT-02 | ISRCs assigned from WeJammin's prefix stay with WeJammin when an artist leaves | Standard industry position, and defensible — it is our registrant prefix and our obligation | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the single most consequential decision in the feature. An ISRC is **the key an artist's proof of work resolves through** (12.07 DT-03): royalties, credits, Content ID and charts all key on it. Keeping it means their career's spine stays here while they leave, and every future royalty statement for that recording points at a platform they no longer use. That is not earned lock-in; it is the textbook hostile kind D-18 and problem-statement.md Q-02 warn against. **Recommend: identifiers leave with the recording, with prefix provenance recorded.** It costs us the retention mechanism every competitor secretly depends on, and it is the price of the thesis being true rather than merely marketed. Owner's call — see Q-01. |
| DT-03 | An artist's export contains their catalog | Obvious framing: their releases, their record, their export | ❌ REJECTED — **the record is not one person's.** A split sheet is a fact about three people; a credit is a fact about the Producer as much as the artist. If a Musician exports and deletes, their co-writers' provenance loses the counterparty that made it verifiable, and the Producer's proof of a session — their most valuable career asset per personas.md — is now a fact about a departed stranger. Provenance is **inherently multi-party**, which means "data portability" here is not one user's right but several users' entangled rights, and GDPR erasure sits directly on top of the contradiction. Nobody has designed for this. The naive per-user export model quietly breaks the product's core artifact, and no competitor hits it because none of them holds multi-party facts worth exporting. Routed to `/create-prd-security` and 09. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Export is always available and never buried | An exit that is hard to find is a hostage arrangement with good manners |
| D-02 | Imported catalog is explicitly marked **not witnessed** | We were not in the room; the boundary protects the record's value (12.06.03 DT-02) |
| D-03 | The migration-in loss is stated honestly before commitment | The artist loses streams and playlists. That is real, it is not our doing, and hiding it would be a lie they discover afterwards |
| D-04 | Lock-in is earned by attestations having happened here, not by withholding the export | The distinction between D-18 being a strategy and being a trap |
