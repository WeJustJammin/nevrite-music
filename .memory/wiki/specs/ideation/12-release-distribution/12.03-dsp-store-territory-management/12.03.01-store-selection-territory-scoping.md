# Feature: Store Selection & Territory Scoping

> **Number**: 12.03.01
> **Parent**: [DSP Store & Territory Management](./12.03-dsp-store-territory-management-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Choosing which stores a release goes to and in which territories — and, crucially, checking that
the artist actually holds the rights to send it there. Delivering a release to a territory covered
by someone else's exclusive licence is not a validation error; it is a breach of contract with a
counterparty who will find out.

This feature is where D-10 pays for itself or does not. Every competitor renders territory as a
checkbox the artist ticks, then pushes the liability into the ToS. WeJammin holds the rights record
(09), so it is the only platform that can **derive** the answer instead of asking for it. If it
asks, D-10's entire cost — the most regulated, most integration-heavy scope in the industry — has
bought a checkbox.

It is also the feature that creates the sub-domain's other three. Selection is the only act that
brings status rows into existence (CX-01); a store the artist never picked can never be rejected,
never be linked to an artist page, never be `Unknown`.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Picks stores (defaulting to every certified store this release can go to) and territories (defaulting to the **derived** scope, which is usually but not always worldwide). Sees every unavailable territory greyed **with its reason class and its counterparty named** — "Japan is exclusive to {label} until 12 Mar 2027", never "unavailable". Sees the four reason classes rendered differently because they cost different things: someone else's exclusive (cannot fix), a licence not held (can fix, routes to 11), a store that does not operate there (nobody can fix), unknown rights (blocks, routes to 12.01.03). Can deselect any store and is shown what that costs **from their own history**, or told plainly that we cannot say. Can declare a single-store selection an exclusive window, which writes a grant into 09 (DT-14). |
| Producer | None | Not visible. Store and territory selection is a commercial decision belonging to the rights holder. A Producer with a points position cares about the outcome but has no say — that asymmetry is real and should not be softened by giving them a view they cannot act on. **The multi-hyphenate exception**: the same human seen as a rights holder in 09 (points position, co-owned master, spec-deal studio share) reaches this feature through the **Musician** lens as a co-owner, not through the Producer lens. Their notification of the delivered footprint (DT-09) is a rights-holder notification issued by 09, not a Producer permission granted here. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. No Operator holds a rights position in a recording *as an Operator*; a studio owed points appears via 09 under the Musician lens, exactly as in 12.01.03. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The Fan meets territory scoping as the "not available in your country" wall on the DSP — a consequence, never a control. The one place this leaks into their surface is pre-save (12.04.03): a fan in an excluded territory can currently pre-save a release that will never reach them, which is this feature's obligation to prevent (DT-08), not theirs to discover. |

## Behavior

Two coupled choices with different characters.

**Store selection** is preference. Default to every certified store (12.02.02) that can accept this
release; deselect what you do not want. Deselecting is legitimate — some artists withhold from
specific DSPs on principle, or drop a store rather than comply with its metadata rules.

**Territory scoping** is *rights*, wearing preference's clothing. The artist may want worldwide;
whether they may *have* worldwide is a fact in 09. The artist does not type it. They read it.

### The constraint model

Availability is **not** a property of a territory. It is a property of the tuple:

> **(recording × territory × commercial model × partner)**

All four axes are load-bearing, and collapsing any of them produces a specific real-world failure:

| Axis | Why it cannot be collapsed | Failure if collapsed |
|---|---|---|
| **recording** | Sample and cover clearances are per-recording (DT-02) | One track's uncleared sample silently restricts, or silently fails to restrict, the whole release |
| **territory** | Exclusives, clearances and mechanicals are territorial | The breach this feature exists to prevent |
| **commercial model** | A sample clearance is scoped by **use**, not only by place — a clearance for streaming distribution does not automatically cover permanent downloads (DT-11) | A release legitimately live on Spotify is a breach on Beatport, in the same territory, on the same day |
| **partner** | Partners address different territory sets, express different granularity, and have their own catalogue policies and operating status (DT-04, DT-06, DT-13) | "Worldwide" means a different set of countries at Apple than at Spotify, and the artist is told a number that is true nowhere |

### The four reason classes

Every unavailable cell has exactly one reason class. They look identical in every competitor's UI
and they are not remotely the same thing — this is the domain's **"different consequences need
different affordances"** pattern (domain index), applied to a picker.

| Class | Meaning | Source | Can the artist fix it? | What they are told |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Encumbered** | Someone else holds an exclusive here | 09 / 11 | **No** — it is a contract with a third party | Counterparty named + expiry date + a scheduled expansion (DT-07) |
| **Uncleared** | A licence this release needs here is not on file | 11 (sample 11.05, cover mechanical 11.09, master use) | **Yes** — routes to 11 with the specific licence named | "Track 5's sample isn't cleared for Japan. Clear it, or exclude Japan." |
| **Unreachable** | The store does not operate there — withdrawn market, sanctions regime, or a territory the partner has never addressed | 12.02.02 partner profile | **No, and it is not about them** | "Apple Music doesn't operate in Russia. Nothing to do with your rights." |
| **Unknown** | 09 holds no rights record for this recording, or ownership is unresolved | 09 | **Yes, but not here** — blocks and routes to the gate (12.01.03) | "We don't know what territories you control. Let's fix that before delivery." |

Conflating **Encumbered** with **Unreachable** is the sharpest error available here: one tells the
artist they made a deal, the other tells them geopolitics happened. An artist who reads "Russia
unavailable" and concludes they signed something they don't remember has been actively misinformed
by a product whose entire claim is that its record is true.

### Silence is not absence — the refinement of D-04

D-04 says missing territory data blocks and never defaults to worldwide. Applied naively this
blocks essentially every release, because **almost no split sheet mentions territory**. A split
sheet says "50/50". It does not say "50/50 worldwide". If silence blocks, the gate fires on every
release, and per 12.01.03 a gate that only says "invalid" teaches the artist to fabricate.

Two different facts wear the same silence, and only one of them blocks:

| What 09 holds | Derived scope | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A complete, attested rights record for the recording with **no territorial encumbrance recorded** | **Worldwide** | "No exclusive exists on file" is a *positive assertion the registry can make*. It is not a guess. |
| **No rights record**, or ownership unresolved, or competing claims (09.04) | **Nothing — blocks** | There is no basis for any assertion. This is D-04's actual target. |

The distinction matters because it is precisely what separates WeJammin from DistroKid. DistroKid
asks the artist to assert worldwide. WeJammin asserts worldwide **from the registry**, and asks the
artist only to confirm the registry is not missing an encumbrance — a far narrower question, one
they can actually answer, asked once per encumbrance rather than once per release. And when the
dispute arrives, there is a dated record of what was on file and what was asserted on top of it.
That is a smaller liability shift than a ToS clause and a much better record, which is the trade
D-18 makes everywhere.

**The honest limit**: the artist may have signed a paper exclusive that was never entered into 09.
WeJammin cannot know this and must not pretend to. What it can do is show the derivation's basis
("worldwide, based on 3 rights records with no territorial encumbrance on file") rather than a bare
"Worldwide ✓", so the artist is looking at a claim they can check rather than a checkbox they can
tick without reading.

### Territory is not a list of countries

The picker's canonical taxonomy is DDEX territory coding (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 plus CISAC TIS codes
for groupings such as "Worldwide"). Partners do not share it. Apple addresses **storefronts**;
Spotify addresses **markets**; neither set equals ISO 3166-1, and they do not equal each other. So
"worldwide" is not one footprint — it is one intent resolved differently per partner.

Three consequences the artist must be shown, not shielded from:

1. **Excluding a territory a partner cannot address separately is a no-op.** It must say so, not
   silently succeed. A greyed cell the artist believes is protecting them and is not is worse than
   no cell at all.
2. **Excluding a territory a partner only sells inside a bundle costs the bundle.** Show the price
   at the decision (domain pattern).
3. **WeJammin never draws the map.** Territories are rendered by the partner's own storefront name
   in a partner view, and by the canonical DDEX code and name in the canonical view. The platform
   does not invent a territory name, does not render a world map with borders, and does not
   editorialise Taiwan, Kosovo, Crimea, Palestine, Western Sahara, or Hong Kong/Macau. Each DSP has
   already taken a position; WeJammin displays theirs and takes none (DT-06).

### The track-level problem — resolving Q-02

A constraint can be per-recording ("Track 5's sample is cleared for North America only") while the
picker feels per-release. The breadth pass left the presentation open. The resolution:

**It is not a choice. It is a partner capability.** Whether track-level territory exclusion is
available is a fact in the partner profile (12.02.02), not a preference for the artist to express.
The picker therefore does not offer three bad options globally — it resolves per partner and only
asks the artist where the partner genuinely forces a decision:

| Partner supports track-level territory scope? | What happens | Artist's decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | Track 5 is excluded in Japan; the other 9 tracks deliver | **None.** It is handled. The artist is told, not asked. |
| **No** | The partner can only take the whole release or nothing in Japan | **Now they choose**, and only between two honest options: exclude Japan on this partner entirely, or drop this partner. |

The consequence is that **one release can have a different territory footprint on every store**,
which is exactly what the sub-domain's D-02 already asserts (status is per release × store ×
territory). This feature is where that shape is born rather than discovered.

**The detail nobody mentions**: excluding one track in one territory changes the tracklist there.
The album is 10 tracks in the US and 9 in Japan. Track numbers either renumber — breaking the
sequence the artist designed, every external reference to "track 7", and the artwork if it lists
titles — or leave a gap. Which one happens is the partner's behaviour, not ours, and it must be
stated before the artist agrees, because "Track 5 excluded in Japan" and "your album is a different
album in Japan" are the same fact and only one of them is legible.

**The third option — split the release** (a separate Japan edition without the track) — is
deliberately **not offered here**. It is a new release object with its own identifiers (12.07), its
own artwork and its own catalogue existence. Offering it as a checkbox inside a picker would let a
control designed to scope one release quietly manufacture a second one.

### Selection is not always a preference (DT-14)

An artist selecting one store for a two-week window is not expressing a taste. They are performing
a **deal** — and it is a rights grant that WeJammin's own picker just created and its own rights
record does not know about. The platform that derives territory from 09 would have manufactured the
exact untracked encumbrance it exists to prevent.

But **inference is forbidden**: deselecting Spotify does not mean granting Apple an exclusive, and
guessing would be a fabrication with a contractual counterparty. So the picker **offers, never
infers**. When the selection is a single store plus a date range, the picker asks once: "Is this an
exclusive window?" If yes, an exclusive grant is written into 09 with the counterparty and the end
date, and it will grey the other stores itself — the feature's own output becomes its own input on
the next release. If no, nothing is recorded and nothing is asked again. This is capture-at-source
(D-18) applied to a control that would otherwise leak the very fact the product is built to hold.

### The selection lock, and what happens after it

Selection is **frozen at delivery lock** (12.04.01), not at delivery. Before the lock, changes are
free and leave no trace. After it, the same gesture means something else entirely: deselecting a
store is a **takedown** (12.05.01), and reducing a territory on a live release is a **withdrawal**
with a live-release footprint. Same control, radically different consequence on either side of one
moment — the hazard CX-03 flags. The picker is therefore read-only after the lock and routes every
change to 12.05, rather than accepting an edit and quietly meaning a takedown.

### Rights drift — resolving CX-01's open state hazard

Rights move under live releases. An exclusive is signed after delivery. A clearance lapses. A chain
of title transfers (09.03). The delivered footprint and the rights record then disagree, and the
disagreement is an **accruing breach** — every day the release stays live in an encumbered
territory is a day of damages.

The behaviour:

1. Derivation is **re-evaluated on every 09/11 rights-record change event** affecting any recording
   on a delivered release. Not a nightly sweep — an exclusive can be effective immediately or
   retroactively, and a sweep would sleep through the window that matters.
2. If the re-derivation shrinks a **live** footprint, the artist is notified **immediately**, at the
   same interruption tier as `Rejected` and `Overdue` (12.03.02 Q-02), naming the counterparty, the
   territory, and the date from which the release has been out of scope.
3. **The platform does not withdraw it.** Silently taking down a live release to clear its own
   inconsistency would be R-01's rejected automation with far higher stakes — the platform altering
   an artist's commercial footprint without asking, invisibly. It surfaces, proposes the withdrawal
   as a one-action route into 12.05.01, and waits.
4. **Unless it is compelled.** A legally-mandated withdrawal is not this feature's decision and not
   the artist's — it is 12.05.03 (involuntary takedown), where the artist has no permissions by
   design.
5. If the re-derivation **grows** the footprint (an exclusive expired), it is a scheduled expansion,
   not an alarm (DT-07).

### Re-derivation at the lock

The derivation the artist saw at selection may be hours or weeks old at delivery. At the lock
moment, it is re-run. If the footprint changed, **delivery halts and the artist is shown a diff** —
it is not silently applied. Silently applying it would deliver a footprint the artist never chose;
silently ignoring it would deliver a footprint 09 says is a breach. Halting is the only option that
does not lie, and it is the same reasoning as 12.03.02 D-02: never claim more than you know, on the
screen where the claim is expensive.

### Happy Path

1. Musician reaches store selection from the release builder, having cleared blocking validation
   (12.01.02) and the readiness gate (12.01.03).
2. Platform resolves rights from 09 for **every recording** on the release — ownership, territorial
   encumbrances, and licence scopes (sample, cover mechanical, master use) from 11. The picker is
   **not interactive** until this resolves; it never renders a selectable control over an unresolved
   derivation, because a control that defaults to worldwide while it thinks is a checkbox with extra
   steps.
3. Derivation returns a scope with its basis stated: "Worldwide — from 3 rights records, no
   territorial encumbrance on file."
4. Every certified store that can accept this release is pre-selected. Each store row carries what
   we know about it: typical lead time, its stated catalogue policy where it has one, and whether it
   ever confirms `Live` (DT-12).
5. Restricted territories are shown greyed, grouped by **reason class**, each naming its
   counterparty and expiry where one exists.
6. Track-level constraints resolve per partner: handled silently where the partner supports it, and
   surfaced as a two-option choice only at partners that do not.
7. Musician deselects a store. The cost is shown from **their own history** — "Beatport was 11% of
   your last release's revenue" — or, on a first release, "You have no history on Beatport. We can't
   tell you what dropping it costs." Any 12.01.02 finding that store contributed clears live.
8. Musician confirms. The footprint is written per (store × territory), including the per-partner
   divergences — this is not one footprint, it is one intent resolved N ways.
9. Selection passes to scheduling (12.04.01). At the delivery lock, derivation re-runs; unchanged,
   it locks.
10. Delivery proceeds (12.02.03); each (store × territory) becomes a status row on the board
    (12.03.02, CX-01).
11. Selection is now read-only. Any change routes to 12.05.
12. On a future rights change, drift re-evaluation fires (above) — and on an exclusive's expiry, the
    scheduled expansion delivers the territory without the artist remembering it existed.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One track's sample is cleared for fewer territories than the release | Resolved **per partner**, not offered as a global choice. Handled silently where the partner supports track-level territory scope; a two-option decision only where it does not | "Track 5's sample isn't cleared for Japan. Apple can drop just that track. Beatport can't — either Japan loses the whole release there, or you drop Beatport." |
| Track excluded in one territory; album is now 9 tracks there | Partner-dependent: renumber or gap. Stated **before** confirmation, because "your album is a different album in Japan" is the same fact as "track 5 excluded" and only one is legible | "In Japan this album is 9 tracks. Apple renumbers, so your track 7 is track 6 there." |
| Exclusive licence expires mid-rollout | Scheduled **expansion**, delivered at `expiry_date − partner_lead_time` so the territory opens *on* the expiry date, not weeks after it | "Japan opens 12 Mar 2027. We'll deliver 20 Feb so it's live that morning." |
| Two exclusives on the same territory with different expiries | The **later** expiry governs. Both counterparties named — an artist who double-granted needs to see both, not the max | "Japan: exclusive to {A} until Mar 2027 and {B} until Sep 2027. Opens Sep 2027. You may want to look at that." |
| An exclusive's expiry passes but the counterparty asserts renewal | Rights fact, not a distribution fact. The expansion is **held**, not delivered on a contested date; routes to 09.04 | "We were going to open Japan on 12 Mar. {label} says the deal renewed. That's a rights question — we've paused the expansion." |
| Rights record has no territory data at all | **Cannot derive.** Blocks, routes to the gate (12.01.03). Never defaults to worldwide (D-04) | "We don't know what territories you control. Let's fix that before delivery." |
| Rights record is complete and records **no** encumbrance | **Derives worldwide.** Silence in a complete record is a positive assertion, not a gap (D-05) | "Worldwide — from 3 rights records, no territorial encumbrance on file." |
| Ownership on one recording is contested (09.04) | Not an `Unknown` territory — an unresolved *ownership*. Blocks at the gate. Delivering contested ownership manufactures the dispute the platform exists to prevent (12.01.03) | "Track 3's ownership is contested. That has to settle before this goes anywhere." |
| Artist excludes a territory their partner cannot address separately | **No-op, stated.** A greyed cell the artist believes is protecting them and isn't is worse than no cell | "Spotify has no separate market for {territory} — excluding it here does nothing there." |
| Artist excludes a country the partner only sells inside a regional bundle | Excluding it costs the bundle. Shown at the decision, not after | "Apple sells {country} inside {region}. Excluding it excludes {region} — 14 countries." |
| Artist selects a store that is suspended (12.02.02) | Shown unavailable with a reason, not silently dropped | "Beatport is unavailable right now — not your release, their end." |
| Store's stated catalogue policy excludes this release's genre | Shown, **never auto-deselected** (CX R-01). Its *stated policy* is a fact; "will reject" is a prediction, and this domain does not claim more than it knows | "Beatport's stated catalogue policy is electronic. You've tagged this Metal." |
| Store has no reconciliation path — will never confirm `Live` | Cost shown at selection, not discovered at status. Selecting it buys a permanent `Unknown` row (12.03.02 D-04) | "{Store} never tells us whether it went live. You'll see 'delivered, not confirmed' forever." |
| Territory unavailable because the store withdrew from the market | **Unreachable**, not Encumbered. Rendered differently and never named as a rights fact | "Apple Music doesn't operate in Russia. Nothing to do with your rights." |
| Territory unavailable under a sanctions regime | **Unreachable**. Reason stated without editorialising | "No store we deliver to operates in {territory}." |
| Artist deselects every store | Nothing to deliver | "Pick at least one store." |
| Artist excludes every territory | Selection is empty in a way a store count hides — 40 stores selected, zero territories, delivery is a no-op | "You've excluded everywhere. There's nothing to deliver." |
| Artist selects one store plus a date range | Picker **asks once** whether it is an exclusive window. Never infers | "Just Apple, for two weeks. Is this an exclusive deal? If so we'll record it — it affects your next release." |
| Artist says yes to the exclusive-window question | Grant written to 09 with counterparty and end date; it will grey the other stores itself next time | "Recorded: Apple exclusive to 26 Mar. Your rights record knows now." |
| Artist says no | Nothing recorded. Not asked again for this release | — |
| Release already live somewhere via a prior distributor | Delivering creates a duplicate — worse than not delivering. Routes to 12.08 (migration), not handled here | "This is already live in the US through {distributor}. Delivering again makes a duplicate. Let's migrate it instead." |
| Prior distributor holds the release live in *some* territories only | Partial overlap. Delivery is scoped to the non-overlapping territories; overlapping ones route to 12.08 | "You're live in the US and UK through {distributor}. We'll take the rest now and migrate those two." |
| Rights change **after** selection, before the lock | Re-derivation at the lock **halts delivery** and shows a diff. Never silently applied, never silently ignored | "Since you picked this, Japan closed — {label} signed an exclusive on 4 Feb. Here's what changed." |
| Rights change **after** the release is live, shrinking the footprint | Immediate notification at the `Rejected`/`Overdue` tier. Platform proposes withdrawal; **does not** act | "You're live in Japan and {label}'s exclusive started 4 Feb. Every day this stays up is a breach. Withdraw Japan?" |
| Rights change after live, **growing** the footprint | Scheduled expansion, not an alarm | "Japan opened. Want it out there?" |
| Legally compelled territory withdrawal | Not this feature's decision and not the artist's | Routes to 12.05.03 (involuntary) |
| Cover song, no compulsory mechanical route in a territory | The US compulsory mechanical is not a global construct. Elsewhere a cover is licensed via a society blanket or by asking the publisher — and a publisher can say no. Per-territory, per-composition | "Your cover is fine in the US. In {territory} there's no compulsory route — {publisher} has to agree, and they haven't." |
| Sample cleared for streaming, not for downloads | **Commercial-model** constraint, not territorial. Same territory, same day, different answer per store | "The sample clearance covers streaming. Beatport is a download store — that's not cleared." |
| One partner bundles several commercial models under one name | Selection is per **destination**, not per brand. YouTube Music, Art Track and Content ID are separate lines because they carry different rights consequences | "YouTube isn't one thing. Music delivery is here; Content ID is a claiming policy — that's over in 12.06." |
| Artist deselects YouTube expecting to also opt out of Content ID | Content ID is **12.06's** decision, surfaced here, written there. This picker must not silently change a claiming policy | "Dropping YouTube Music doesn't touch Content ID. That's a separate call — here's where." |
| Two co-owners of one master disagree on the footprint | Not resolvable by "the release owner decides". Routes to 09.04 (rights conflicts). This picker does not arbitrate ownership | "{Co-owner} has a position on this master and hasn't agreed the footprint. That's a rights conflict, not a picker setting." |
| A co-owner is not on WeJammin | Cannot be notified in-app and their consent cannot be evidenced. An honest limit, not a silent pass | "We can tell you {co-owner} has a position. We can't reach them here." |
| Selection is edited after the lock | Read-only. Routes to 12.05 — the pre/post-lock asymmetry is never absorbed silently | "This is live. Changing where it's sold is a takedown, not an edit. Here's what that means." |
| A store is dropped while its delivery is in flight | In-flight delivery is **cancelled**, not left to land. Otherwise the release goes live somewhere it was just withdrawn from (CX-03) | "Stopping the Beatport delivery. If it already landed, this becomes a takedown." |
| Derivation service unavailable | The picker does **not** render. It does not degrade to worldwide — a degraded default here is a fabricated rights claim, which is the failure mode, not the fallback | "Can't reach your rights record. Not going to guess what you own." |
| Release has no recordings yet | Nothing to derive against | "Add tracks first — territory depends on what's on the record." |
| No certified store can accept this release at all | Empty for a real reason, not a bug | "None of our stores take this release type. That's on us, not you." |
| Artist deselects a store on a first release, wants to know the cost | We have no history. **We say so** rather than inventing a reach figure | "You have no history on Beatport. We can't tell you what dropping it costs." |
| Concurrent edit — two co-owners open the picker at once | Selection is single-writer; the second sees the first's state, not a silent overwrite | "{Co-owner} changed this 2 minutes ago. Here's what's different." |
| Artist confirms the registry has no missing encumbrance, but a paper exclusive exists | **The honest limit of the system.** WeJammin cannot know it. The record shows what was on file and what was asserted on top of it, dated | The derivation basis is always visible: "Worldwide — from 3 rights records, no territorial encumbrance on file." Never a bare "Worldwide ✓". |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Resolving rights from 09 and licence scopes from 11 | Skeleton picker, **non-interactive**. No control is selectable over an unresolved derivation |
| Empty | Release has no recordings, or no certified store can accept this release type | "Add tracks first — territory depends on what's on the record." / "None of our stores take this release type." |
| Populated | Derivation resolved, no restrictions | All stores selected, worldwide **with its basis stated**, never a bare tick |
| Restricted | Derivation resolved, some cells unavailable | Greyed cells grouped by reason class — Encumbered (counterparty + expiry), Uncleared (route to 11), Unreachable (not about you) |
| Partial | Derivation resolved for some recordings, not all | Picker stays non-interactive. A partial rights answer is not a partial footprint — one unresolved track can restrict the release. "Still resolving track 5." |
| Blocked | 09 has no record, or ownership unresolved/contested | Blocking; routes to the gate (12.01.03) rather than guessing |
| Error | Derivation service unreachable | "Can't reach your rights record. Not going to guess what you own." **Never degrades to worldwide.** |
| Locked | Delivery lock passed (12.04.01) | Read-only; all changes route to 12.05 |
| Diff | Re-derivation at the lock changed the footprint | Delivery **halted**; diff of what changed and why, with the counterparty named |
| Drifted | Rights changed under a live release, shrinking the footprint | Immediate alert at the `Rejected`/`Overdue` tier; proposed withdrawal route into 12.05.01. Platform has **not** acted |
| Expanding | An exclusive expired; a territory is opening | "Japan opens 12 Mar 2027. We'll deliver 20 Feb so it's live that morning." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Territory scoping is a preference the artist declares — "worldwide" is a default, not a claim | Every competitor's model; it is a checkbox on DistroKid | ❌ REJECTED, and this is a **gap in the sweep's candidate list with real legal weight**. Territory is a *rights fact* (09), not a preference. A self-declared "worldwide" over a territory covered by someone else's exclusive is a breach with a named counterparty who monitors DSPs for exactly this. Competitors get away with it by pushing liability onto the artist in the ToS — which is precisely the "asking people to remember" pattern problem-statement.md names as the root cause, applied to contracts instead of credits. WeJammin holds the rights record (D-10), so it is the only platform that **can** derive this. If it does not, D-10's cost is being paid for nothing. |
| DT-02 | Territory constraints are release-level | Simplicity; it is how the picker naturally reads | ❌ REJECTED — sample and cover clearances are **per-recording**, so a single track can restrict a territory for the whole release on partners that lack track-level territory support. This is genuinely messy and cannot be modelled away: it is a real property of the rights layer meeting a real limitation of partner profiles. Both levels must exist, and the UI has to make a per-track fact legible in a per-release picker. |
| DT-03 | Deselecting a store to dodge a metadata rule should be discouraged | Instinct to protect the artist's reach | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **legitimate and informed trade**, and framing it as a mistake is paternalistic. An artist who would rather skip Beatport than ALL-CAPS-strip their title is making a real choice about their record. Show the cost (reach, revenue) and let them choose. Per `decision-classification.md` this is a product decision belonging to the user, and the same logic as 12.01.04 DT-01 (advise, never block artistic choices). |
| DT-04 | "Worldwide" is one footprint — a territory list the artist picks from once, resolved identically at every partner | The picker reads as a single map; every distributor presents it that way | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the largest unflagged defect in the feature.** Apple addresses *storefronts*; Spotify addresses *markets*; DDEX codes territories in ISO 3166-1 plus CISAC TIS groupings. None of these sets are equal. "Worldwide" is therefore **one intent resolved N ways**, and a single canonical footprint is a number that is true nowhere. Three consequences all reach the artist: excluding a territory a partner cannot address separately is a silent no-op (a greyed cell that protects nothing — worse than no cell); excluding a country a partner sells only inside a regional bundle costs the bundle; and the count of territories the artist thinks they excluded is not the count that was excluded. This is the domain's "show the price at the decision" pattern, and it is invisible until you look at two partners' territory tables side by side. |
| DT-05 | Silence in the rights record means the territory is unavailable — no recorded grant, no delivery | The literal reading of D-04 ("never defaults to worldwide") | ❌ REJECTED, **with a refinement that saves D-04 from itself.** Almost no split sheet mentions territory — a split sheet says "50/50", not "50/50 worldwide". If silence blocks, the gate fires on every release, and per 12.01.03 a gate that only says "invalid" teaches the artist to fabricate, which is worse than no gate. Two facts wear the same silence: a **complete, attested record with no encumbrance on file** is a *positive assertion* the registry can make and derives worldwide; **no record at all**, or unresolved ownership, has no basis for any assertion and blocks. D-04's real target is the second. Getting this wrong in either direction is fatal — block on silence and the gate is theatre; default on absence and you have DistroKid's checkbox with a database attached. |
| DT-06 | A territory picker is a neutral list of places | It is a form control; forms are not political | ❌ REJECTED — **a territory picker is a political object and nobody designs for it.** Taiwan, Kosovo, Crimea, Palestine, Western Sahara, Hong Kong and Macau each exist, don't exist, or exist as something else depending on whose list you read. Every DSP has already taken a position in its storefront list. If WeJammin renders its own map with its own borders and its own names, it adopts a foreign policy it never decided to have, and it will be asked to defend it. Resolution: display the **partner's** storefront names in a partner view and the **canonical DDEX code and name** in the canonical view; never draw a map with borders; never invent a territory name. The platform displays other people's positions and holds none. Adjacent and distinct: sanctions and market withdrawals (Russia post-2022) make territories unavailable for reasons that have nothing to do with the artist's rights — which is DT-10's problem, not this one's. |
| DT-07 | An expiring exclusive is the artist's job to remember and redeliver | It is how every distributor handles it: the artist comes back and does it again | ❌ REJECTED — this is **"asking people to remember"** (problem-statement.md root cause) with a two-year fuse, and it is the single clearest thing only a platform holding the rights record could ever do. WeJammin knows the expiry date, because the expiry is *in the record it already holds*. Not scheduling the expansion is choosing to hold the fact and not use it. Concrete: schedule at `expiry_date − partner_lead_time` so the territory opens **on** the expiry date rather than weeks after it, since the artist waited two years for that morning. The failure modes are real and specifiable, not reasons to defer: two exclusives on one territory → the **later** expiry governs and both counterparties are named (an artist who double-granted needs to see it); a contested renewal → **hold** the expansion, route to 09.04, never deliver on a disputed date; partner lead time changed since scheduling → re-derive at the lock like any other delivery. |
| DT-08 | Territory scoping never reaches the Fan except as the DSP's "not available in your country" wall | The Role Lens says Fan: None, and this is a professional surface | ❌ REJECTED — **pre-save leaks it, and leaks it at the worst possible moment.** 12.04.03 is Fan-`Full` and the one place domain 12 becomes fan-facing. A pre-save link is territory-blind: a fan in Japan pre-saves a release excluded from Japan, waits, and gets nothing on release day — having taken the one action the platform asked of them. Per personas.md the Fan's success criterion is the alert→attendance/purchase line, and this breaks it *silently* on both sides: the fan thinks they're covered, the artist sees a pre-save count that includes people who will never receive it. The fix is this feature's obligation, not 12.04.03's: the delivered footprint must be readable by the pre-save page so it resolves the fan's storefront and states availability **before** capture, not after. Note the symmetry with CX-04 — a wrong artist page and a pre-save into an excluded territory are the same class of failure: a green metric measuring nothing. |
| DT-09 | Store and territory selection is one rights holder's decision, so one rights holder decides | Role Lens: Musician Full. The release has an owner and the owner picks | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — jurisdictionally contingent, and the contingency is real.** Joint ownership of a master does not work the same way everywhere: under US law a co-owner may grant a **non-exclusive** licence unilaterally, subject to a duty to account — and a DSP distribution is non-exclusive, so one co-owner can arguably do this alone. Under UK law, joint owners' consent is required for any licence. Same two people, same master, opposite answer depending on where you ask. This is above what ideation can settle (Q-03). What **is** settleable, and is specified: this picker does **not** arbitrate ownership. Co-owners are **notified of the delivered footprint at delivery** — a notification issued by 09 under the rights-holder lens, not a Producer permission granted here — and any disagreement routes to **09.04**, which is the domain that exists for it. This is also where the multi-hyphenate bites: the Producer with a points position is `None` *as a Producer* and a full participant *as a rights holder in 09*. Two lenses, one human, one release. Resolving that by giving Producers a picker view would be the wrong fix to the right observation. |
| DT-10 | An unavailable territory is an unavailable territory — one greyed cell, one meaning | It is one visual state, so it reads as one fact | ❌ REJECTED — **four different things are wearing one colour**, and this is the domain's "different consequences need different affordances" pattern (domain index) arriving in a picker. *Encumbered* (someone else's exclusive — cannot fix, has a counterparty and an expiry), *Uncleared* (a licence not on file — **can** fix, routes to 11), *Unreachable* (the store doesn't operate there — nobody's fault, nothing to do), *Unknown* (no rights record — blocks, routes to 12.01.03). The sharpest available error is conflating Encumbered with Unreachable: an artist who reads "Russia unavailable" and concludes they signed something they don't remember has been actively misinformed by a product whose entire claim is that its record is true. Each class needs a different affordance because each implies a different next action — and one of the four has no next action at all, which is itself information. |
| DT-11 | Rights constraints are (recording × territory) — place and track are the two axes | The constraint table reads that way; territory is the feature's name | ❌ REJECTED — **there is a third axis and it is invisible: commercial model.** A sample clearance is scoped by *use*, not only by place — a clearance covering streaming distribution does not automatically cover permanent downloads, and the scope is a term of the licence (11.05), not a property of the sample. So a release legitimately live on Spotify can be a breach on Beatport, in the **same territory, on the same day**, for a reason the territory picker structurally cannot show. Adding the partner axis (DT-04, DT-06) makes it four: availability is a property of **(recording × territory × commercial model × partner)**, and every collapse of that tuple has a named real-world failure. This is why the feature is genuinely harder than it looks and why competitors' single-axis checkbox is not a simplification but a wrong answer. |
| DT-12 | Every certified store is equally worth selecting, so pre-select them all and let the artist subtract | Maximum reach is the artist's default interest; it is also how the default is built | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED with a correction**: pre-selecting all is right — deselection is the artist's move to make (DT-03) — but **stores are not interchangeable and the picker currently hides it.** Some never confirm `Live` at all (12.03.02 D-04), so selecting them buys a permanent `Unknown` row on the board the artist will judge the product by. Some have a stated catalogue policy that has nothing to do with this release (Beatport takes electronic; a metal release is not a fit). The domain's own **"show the price at the decision"** pattern says both belong at selection, not at status — and the "never claim more than you know" pattern says we state Beatport's **stated policy** (a fact) and never "Beatport will reject you" (a prediction we would be making about someone else's business). Per CX R-01, nothing is auto-deselected: the platform silently dropping a store to tidy its own board would be a betrayal of commercial intent, performed invisibly. |
| DT-13 | A store is one destination, so one checkbox per store | The picker is a list of logos | ❌ REJECTED — **several "stores" are bundles of commercial models with different rights consequences.** YouTube is YouTube Music *and* Art Tracks *and* Content ID; Amazon spans streaming tiers *and* a download store. One "YouTube" checkbox therefore conflates a delivery with a **claiming policy that 12.06 owns** — and an artist deselecting YouTube expecting to opt out of Content ID would be silently wrong about something that determines whether their fans' videos get claimed. Resolution: selection is per **destination**, and where a partner offers multiple commercial models each is a separate line, because DT-11 means they can have different answers. Where a line is another feature's decision (Content ID), it is **shown here and written there** — a picker that silently changed a claiming policy would be exactly the wrong seam. |
| DT-14 | A store selection is always a preference, so the picker only reads from 09 and never writes to it | The feature is a consumer of rights, not a producer of them | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the closed loop the feature was missing.** An artist selecting one store for a two-week window is not expressing a taste; they are performing a **deal**, and that deal is a rights grant. A platform that derives territory from 09 but lets its own picker create untracked encumbrances has manufactured the exact defect it exists to prevent — and would then grey nothing on the next release, because its record never learned. But **inference is forbidden**: deselecting Spotify is not granting Apple an exclusive, and guessing at a grant with a contractual counterparty is a fabrication. So: **offer, never infer.** When the selection is a single store plus a date range, ask once — "Is this an exclusive window?" — and on yes, write the grant into 09 with counterparty and end date, where it will grey the other stores itself next time and schedule its own expiry expansion (DT-07). This is D-18's capture-at-source logic applied to a control everyone else treats as a preference, and it is the same move as capturing a split in the session rather than reconstructing it: the fact is cheap here and expensive later. |
| DT-15 | If the derivation is slow or unavailable, render the picker with worldwide selected and correct it when rights resolve | Standard optimistic-UI instinct; it keeps the flow moving | ❌ REJECTED — **the optimistic default is the failure mode wearing a fallback's clothes.** A picker that shows "worldwide" while it thinks *is* DistroKid's checkbox, with the additional insult of a database behind it that disagrees. An artist who confirms during the optimistic window has self-declared worldwide, which is exactly DT-01. Therefore: the picker is **non-interactive until derivation resolves**, a *partial* derivation is also non-interactive (one unresolved track can restrict the release, so a partial rights answer is not a partial footprint), and on service failure it says "Can't reach your rights record. Not going to guess what you own." Refusing to render is the correct behaviour, not the degraded one — this is the one screen in the product where a helpful default is a legal claim. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **shared entity, bidirectional.**
  *Read*: territory availability is derived from the rights registry (09.01) and chain of title (09.03);
  without 09 this feature degenerates into a competitor's checkbox (DT-01). *Write*: an artist-confirmed
  exclusive window writes a grant back into 09 (DT-14), and the **delivered footprint must be published
  as a readable fact** so 09 can warn at *grant* time — an artist can otherwise sign a Japan exclusive
  for a release already live in Japan, and only this feature holds the fact that makes it checkable.
- Touches **09.04 Rights Conflicts & Disputes** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/09.04-rights-conflicts-disputes/)) —
  **permission.** Co-owner disagreement over the footprint routes here; this picker never arbitrates
  ownership (DT-09). Also the destination for a contested exclusive renewal that holds an expansion.
- Touches **11 Music Licensing** ([path](../../11-music-licensing/)) — **trigger dependency.** Licence
  scopes are `(territory × use)`, not territory alone: sample clearance (11.05), cover mechanical
  (11.09), master use. The `Uncleared` reason class routes here with the specific licence named, and it
  is the only one of the four classes the artist can actually fix (DT-10).
- Touches **12.02.02 Per-Partner Profile Conformance** ([path](../12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging/12.02.02-per-partner-profile-conformance.md)) —
  **trigger dependency, and this feature's largest hidden dependency.** Four separate profile facts are
  required here and none are in the profile's current description: the partner's **territory taxonomy**
  (storefronts vs markets vs ISO — DT-04), whether it supports **track-level territory scope** (the whole
  of Q-02's resolution), its **stated catalogue policy** (DT-12), and its **operating status per
  territory** (the `Unreachable` class — DT-10). Parent Q-03 (direct vs aggregator) determines whether
  this data exists at all.
- Touches **12.01.02 Metadata Validation** ([path](../12.01-release-builder/12.01.02-metadata-validation-conformance.md)) —
  **trigger dependency.** Deselecting a store clears its findings live (DT-03, CX-03).
- Touches **12.01.03 Delivery Readiness Gate** ([path](../12.01-release-builder/12.01.03-delivery-readiness-gate.md)) —
  **trigger dependency.** The `Unknown` reason class blocks and routes here (D-04). D-05's refinement is
  load-bearing for the gate's usability: if silence blocked, this gate would fire on every release.
- Touches **12.03.02 Per-Store Delivery Status** ([path](./12.03.02-per-store-delivery-status.md)) —
  **shared entity (CX-01).** Selection creates every status row. Per-partner footprint divergence (DT-04)
  means the board's rows are not a product of one footprint but of N — the 8,000-cell problem the board
  absorbs is *authored here*.
- Touches **12.04.01 Release Date & DSP Lead Time** ([path](../12.04-release-scheduling-windows/12.04.01-release-date-lead-time.md)) —
  **trigger dependency + state race.** The delivery **lock** is defined there and is this feature's freeze
  point; re-derivation fires at it. Territory-scoped release dates are a real construct (a staggered Japan
  date, and every scheduled expansion in DT-07 is one), so the artist making one decision — "Japan, later"
  — is touching two features. The DDEX Deal that fuses territory and date is 12.02's object, not either's.
- Touches **12.04.03 Pre-Save & Pre-Add Links** ([path](../12.04-release-scheduling-windows/12.04.03-pre-save-pre-add-links.md)) —
  **notification / fan-out.** The one place territory scoping reaches a Fan-`Full` surface. The delivered
  footprint must be readable by the pre-save page so a fan in an excluded territory is told before they
  act, not after (DT-08). Currently 12.04.03 has no way to know.
- Touches **12.05.01 Voluntary Takedown** ([path](../12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.01-voluntary-takedown.md)) —
  **state race.** Post-lock footprint reduction is a takedown, not an edit (CX-03, CX R-04). Also the
  proposed route out of rights drift — proposed, never taken automatically.
- Touches **12.05.03 Involuntary Takedown & Suspension** ([path](../12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.03-involuntary-takedown-suspension.md)) —
  **permission.** A legally-compelled territory withdrawal is neither this feature's decision nor the
  artist's; drift that is *compelled* rather than *noticed* routes here.
- Touches **12.06 Content ID & UGC Claiming** ([path](../12.06-content-id-ugc-claiming/)) — **shared
  entity.** The YouTube line in the picker is a claiming policy 12.06 owns: shown here, written there
  (DT-13). A picker that silently changed a claiming policy would be the wrong seam.
- Touches **12.07 Identifier Assignment at Delivery** ([path](../12.07-identifier-assignment-at-delivery.md)) —
  **shared entity.** The rejected "split the release" option for track-level restrictions would mint a
  second release with its own identifiers; recorded so the boundary is explicit.
- Touches **12.08 Catalog Migration & Exit** ([path](../12.08-catalog-migration-exit.md)) — **state race.**
  A release live via a prior distributor cannot be delivered over. Partial overlap is the common case:
  live in 2 territories, deliverable in 198.
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) and **22 Analytics &
  Market Intelligence** ([path](../../22-analytics-market-intelligence/)) — **other.** The cost-of-dropping-a-store
  figure comes from the artist's own history. On a first release there is none, and we say so rather than
  invent a reach number (DT-12, "never claim more than you know").
- Touches **20 Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan** ([path](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)) — **notification.**
  Excluding a territory invalidates fan reach there. Same signal 12.03.02 CX-04 says domain 12 owes
  domain 20 for a wrong artist page, for the same reason: a metric that measures nothing.
- Consumes cross-cut **Geo, Location & Map Discovery** — territory taxonomy (ISO 3166-1, CISAC TIS
  groupings, per-partner storefront/market maps, disputed territories). DT-06 constrains it: this feature
  needs the codes, and needs the cross-cut **not** to hand it a world map with borders.
- Consumes cross-cut **Notifications & Alerts** — drift on a live release is one of the few events worth
  interrupting an artist for, at the same tier as `Rejected`/`Overdue` (12.03.02 Q-02), because it accrues
  damages daily.
