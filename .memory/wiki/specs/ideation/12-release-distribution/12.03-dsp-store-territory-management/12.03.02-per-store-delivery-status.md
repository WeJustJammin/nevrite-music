# Feature: Per-Store Delivery Status

> **Number**: 12.03.02
> **Parent**: [DSP Store & Territory Management](./12.03-dsp-store-territory-management-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The board that tells an artist the truth about where their release actually is: live on Spotify,
accepted-but-not-live on Apple, rejected by Beatport, overdue at Amazon, blocked in Japan. This is
the destination screen of domain 12 — the one an artist opens repeatedly in release week — and the
one whose honesty determines whether they believe anything else the platform tells them.

It exists because the incumbent experience is a single `released` boolean sitting next to a record
that is not, in fact, released everywhere — and because the gap between what a partner *says* and
what a store *does* is where artists lose days, campaigns and trust. Per D-18 the platform's whole
claim is that the record it keeps is true. This is the screen where that claim is tested in public,
every Friday.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees one row per store, each with a real state, how long it has been in that state, what is expected, and what is being done. Acts where action exists: open the live link, chase an `Overdue` store, fix a `Rejected` one (12.03.03), reschedule a `Queued` one (12.04.01), expand a healthy group. Sees `Unknown` where the platform genuinely does not know — a state, not an omission. Receives the four interrupting alerts (D-09) only if they are the release's **named delivery contact** (D-15); otherwise the daily digest. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees where a record they made ended up, per store, with the same honest states — but no chase, no retry, no reschedule, no link-out to remediation except the asset rejections routed to them by 12.03.03 (which is that feature's Config access, not this one's). Receives **no per-store status notifications**: one release-day summary if they hold a credit, nothing else (D-09). Deliberate: giving them controls over another party's commercial distribution would be wrong; hiding the outcome of their own work would be worse, and it is what every existing tool does. Per personas.md the Producer has mentally closed the project — a board that pages them 200 times is a board they mute. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. An Operator sells time and space (personas.md); a release's DSP state has no bearing on a booking, a rider or a settlement. There is no view, no notification, and no read-only entry point. If an Operator is also in the band, they see this board as a **Musician on that release** (D-12) — the persona is not what grants access; the relationship to the release is. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. "Is it out yet?" is answered by 20/21 and by the DSP itself — never by this board, which is a professional operations view of a commercial process. The Fan is nevertheless the party most damaged by this board being wrong: a green board over a wrong-page landing (CX-04) severs their follow→discovery line silently, and no surface in the product shows it. |

> **Access is resolved from the viewer's relationship to *this release*, not from their persona
> (D-12).** A drummer who produced the record and is a member of the owning band is a Musician here
> with Full access; a producer-for-hire on the same record is Read-only. The multi-hyphenate holds
> both shapes simultaneously across different releases, and the board must resolve each one
> independently — see DT-13.

## Behavior

Status is a per (release × store × territory) object, not a flag — parent D-02. Its value is
normalised from partner acks (12.02.03) and from store-side reconciliation, and it must never
over-claim (12.02.03 D-02; the domain index's **"Never claim more than you know"** pattern).

### The state vocabulary

| State | Meaning | Source of truth | Can the artist act? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Queued` | Scheduled, not yet sent | Us | Yes — change the schedule (12.04.01) |
| `Sent` | Handed to the partner | Us | No — wait |
| `Received` | Partner confirmed receipt | Partner ack | No — wait |
| `Accepted` | Partner accepted; not yet published | Partner ack | No — wait for the store's own schedule |
| `Live (pre-order)` | Purchasable / pre-addable, **not streamable** | Store-side | Open the link — but it is not the release going live (DT-09) |
| `Live` | Confirmed streamable on the store | **Store-side, never an ack** (D-02) | Open the link |
| `Rejected` | Partner refused | Partner nack | Fix it (12.03.03) |
| `Withdrawn` | **We** asked for removal | Us | See 12.05.01 |
| `Removed by store` | The store pulled it; **we did not ask** | Store-side | Route to 12.05.03 (DT-07) |
| `Unknown` | We genuinely cannot tell | — | Told plainly, with the reason |

**`Accepted` ≠ `Live`, and the gap between them is where the worst experience in the domain
lives.** A partner can accept a delivery and then simply not publish it — for days, with no signal.
Detecting `Live` requires store-side truth (Q-01), not the partner's ack, and that is the hard part
of this feature.

### Mixed track outcomes (D-18)

A partner may accept some recordings in a release and reject others. The outer status identity stays
`(release × store × territory)`: **do not create a track-status row**. Instead attach one rejected-item
detail for every affected recording. Each detail includes stable affected-item ID, partner plus
original delivery/message correlation, evidence timestamp and normalized reason, triage/remediation
state, and successor-delivery correlation after redelivery.

The parent projects `Partial acceptance` while evidence remains mixed. It displays accepted, rejected,
and pending counts plus the actionable rejected items; it never renders the row as wholly `Accepted` or
wholly `Rejected`. Triage may clear only the item detail confirmed resolved by new partner evidence.
The `Partial acceptance` projection ends only when evidence supports a healthy result for the full
release. Redelivery stays limited to the rejecting partner.

**`Withdrawn` and `Removed by store` are separate states, mirroring the domain's ratified split of
voluntary from involuntary takedown (domain 12 D-05).** Merging them would put a "lift" affordance
next to a removal the artist did not cause and cannot reverse.

### `Overdue` is a qualifier, not a state (DT-15)

`Overdue` is **not** a member of the vocabulary above. It is a time-in-state flag the platform
raises on `Sent`, `Received` or `Accepted` — because "overdue waiting for an ack" and "overdue
waiting to go live" are different problems, with different messages, different escalations and
different people to chase. Collapsing them into one `Overdue` state destroys the only information
that makes the row actionable.

- `Sent · overdue` → "Amazon hasn't confirmed they received this in 4 days." → transport/partner problem.
- `Accepted · overdue` → "Apple accepted this 9 days ago and it still isn't live." → the domain's worst case.

**The two qualifiers are evaluated against different clocks, and that split is ratified (D-19).**
The **ack wait** (`Sent · overdue`, `Received · overdue`) fires on 12.02.03 D-05's
(partner × message type) window. The **live wait** (`Accepted · overdue`) does **not**: it is this
feature's own concern, evaluated against store-local midnight plus the partner's publish window
(D-07), and is never folded into the ack window. Recorded because DT-15's ack-wait/live-wait
distinction is frequently mis-cited as an argument for keying the ack window *per ack stage* — it is
not; per-stage keying was declined outright (12.02.02 D-13), because the observed interval is
end-to-end and per-stage intervals are defined nowhere.

> **Reconciliation note for Step 6**: 12.02.03 D-01 lists `overdue` as a first-class *state*. This
> feature owns the status object (CX-02), so the refinement is made here — silence stays
> first-class, but it attaches to the state it interrupts rather than replacing it. 12.02.03's
> vocabulary and this one must be reconciled as one vocabulary during synthesis, not two.

### Legal transitions

| From | To | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `Queued` | `Sent`, `Withdrawn` | Deselection before delivery removes the row entirely (CX-03) |
| `Sent` | `Received`, `Rejected`, `Unknown` | Transport failure is not a state here — it is 12.02.03's `Failed` and stays `Sent` on the board until it resolves or ages into `Sent · overdue` |
| `Received` | `Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Unknown` | |
| `Accepted` | `Live (pre-order)`, `Live`, `Rejected`, `Unknown` | **`Accepted` → `Rejected` is legal** — stores reject at QC after accepting a delivery |
| `Live (pre-order)` | `Live`, `Rejected`, `Removed by store`, `Withdrawn` | |
| `Live` | `Removed by store`, `Withdrawn`, `Unknown` | `Live` is **not terminal** (DT-07) |
| `Withdrawn` | `Sent` | Only via an explicit new delivery (12.05.02) |
| `Rejected` | `Sent` | Only via redelivery to the rejecting partner (12.03.03 D-02, CX-06) |
| any | `Unknown` | Reconciliation lost; the prior state and its timestamp are retained and shown |

**Backwards transitions are illegal and are never applied.** A late `Received` ack arriving after
`Live` is recorded in the row's history but does not move the row (D-14).

### Elapsed time and expectation

Every state carries **elapsed time and an expectation**. "Pending" is worthless; "Received by Apple
6 days ago — usually 2" is the entire product. This is the same honesty rule as 12.02.03 D-01,
surfaced.

But an expectation is a **claim**, and the platform must hold itself to the same standard it holds
partners to (D-11):

The expectation clause and the `· overdue` alarm are **two different statistics over the same
observation history**, keyed **per (partner × message type)** — never per ack stage (12.02.02 D-13):

| Evidence available | What the row says |
|---|---|
| **≥10** of our own completed observations for this (partner × message type) | "Received by Apple 6 days ago — usually 2." The **p50 (median)** of our own observations (12.02.03 D-05). |
| Fewer than 10 observations, but a window published or contracted by the partner, carried in their profile (12.02.02) | "Received by Apple 6 days ago — Apple's stated window is 5 business days." Attributed to them, not to us. |
| Neither — **cold start** | "Received by Apple 6 days ago. We don't have enough history with Apple to say whether that's normal yet." Elapsed time only. **No `overdue` flag is raised**, because there is nothing to be over. |

> **Observed outranks contractual (12.02.03 D-05).** A partner's stated SLA is used only where
> observation is insufficient, and a partner inside our observed window but outside their own written
> SLA is **not late** by this board's reckoning — that divergence is a commercial signal routed to
> partner management, never shown to the artist (12.02.02 Q-07 owns the un-owned routing).
>
> **The expectation copy and the alarm do not share a number, by design.** The "usually N days"
> clause is the **p50 at N ≥ 10**; the `· overdue` qualifier fires on the **p95 at N ≥ 30 over the
> last 180 days**, rounded up to the next whole partner-business-day (12.02.03 D-05). Using one
> number for both is the single option that produces an actually wrong sentence — quoting a p95 (or
> a P90) after the word *usually* misdescribes the partner. The lower copy floor is the right
> asymmetry: a reassurance clause should reach evidence sooner than an alarm does.
>
> The threshold in this row **was** a stated default of 20 and is now **ratified at 10** — this file
> always described 20 as a default rather than a law whose exact number it defends, so nothing here
> is overridden. What is not tunable is the rule that an expectation requires evidence: below the
> floor the board shows **no expectation clause at all** rather than a guess, because a wrong
> expectation is worse than no expectation — it manufactures alarm.
>
> **This is a cold-start cost the incumbents do not have**, and it is the same cold-start shape
> 12.03.03 DT-01 identifies for rejection rules. On day one the board's honest answer to "is 6 days
> normal?" is "we don't know yet" — which is a worse product than DistroKid's fifteen years of scar
> tissue and a better product than DistroKid's confident lie. It resolves itself with volume — except
> for low-volume partners and uncommon message types, which may **never** reach the p95 floor and sit
> permanently unmeasured for the alarm (12.02.03 edge cases). 12.02.03 D-05 is where the
> partner-contract half comes from.

### The board is exception-first (D-01)

Forty stores across two hundred territories is eight thousand cells. Being *correct* about status
and being *useful* about status are different problems.

- Rows **not** in `{Accepted · on-schedule, Live}` are shown individually, most-urgent first:
  `Rejected` → `Removed by store` → any `· overdue` → `Unknown` → everything else.
- Rows in `{Accepted · on-schedule, Live}` collapse into **one summary row** — "34 stores live" —
  regardless of count, expandable in one action. Healthy rows are always collapsed; there is no
  count threshold at which the rule changes.
- Territory detail is nested under a store row and rendered only when a territory's status
  **differs** from the store's. A store live everywhere it was sent shows no territory detail at all.

### Territory rows are *requested*, not *confirmed* (D-05, DT-06)

A store does not acknowledge per-territory. It accepts one delivery carrying one territory list and
applies availability internally. Whether the record actually shows in Japan is invisible from the
partner side — it can only be observed *from Japan*.

Therefore a territory row carries one of exactly three values, and the board never blurs them:

| Territory value | Meaning | Display rule |
|---|---|---|
| `Requested` | We asked for this territory and the store accepted the delivery containing it | The default for every territory of an `Accepted`/`Live` store. Labelled as **asked for, not confirmed** — never rendered in the same visual language as a confirmed `Live`. |
| `Confirmed live` | Independently observed available in this territory | Requires a per-territory check that may not exist (Q-01) |
| `Excluded` | We did not ask — a rights constraint (12.03.01) or an artist choice | Carries the reason and the counterparty from 12.03.01 |

Rendering 200 `Requested` territories as 200 green ticks would be **fake precision**: eight thousand
confident cells derived from forty acks. That is a worse lie than the boolean this feature exists to
replace, because it is a lie at higher resolution.

### Monitoring never stops (D-06, DT-07)

Reconciliation does **not** end at `Live`. A store can pull a release on its own — a rights
conflict, a Content ID dispute (12.06), a QC sweep, a takedown against a different party that
catches this one — and tell nobody. Every distributor stops watching at `Live`; the artist finds out
from a fan. The board watches for the life of the catalog, and `Live` → `Removed by store` is a
first-class detection, not an anomaly.

### Release day is not a day (D-07, DT-08)

Global release day is Friday, and DSPs publish at **local midnight in each territory**. Between the
first territory (UTC+14) and the last (UTC−11) there are **25 hours** in which a release is
legitimately live in some territories and legitimately not live in others. This is by design, not a
failure.

- A store showing `Accepted` at 09:00 UTC on release Friday is **normal**, not overdue.
- No `· overdue` flag may be raised against a release-day go-live until **local midnight has passed
  in the territory being evaluated**, plus that partner's publish window.
- The board states liveness in **store-local terms**: "Live in 41 territories; Japan goes live in
  3 hours" — not "partially live", which reads as breakage.

Firing "Apple is late" at 00:00 UTC on release Friday would make the board cry wolf on every single
release, on the one day the artist is watching it. It would be the fastest possible way to teach
artists to ignore this screen.

### Notification rules (D-09 — resolves Q-02)

Forty stores × ten states ≈ two hundred transitions per release. Notifying on state change is a
firehose; notifying only on `Live` misses the two states the artist actually needs.

**Interrupt** (immediate, out-of-band) — exactly four triggers, and only these:

| Trigger | Recipient | Why it earns an interrupt |
|---|---|---|
| `Rejected` | The person who can fix it (12.03.03 D-04 routes asset rejections to the Producer), **not** the release owner by default | Actionable, time-critical, blocks the release on that store |
| any `· overdue` | Named delivery contact (D-15) | A promise the platform made about time has been broken |
| Wrong-page mismatch (CX-04) or live-but-zero-plays (D-10) | Named delivery contact | The green board is lying and only we can see it |
| Early go-live (D-13) | Named delivery contact | An embargo is already broken; every minute costs |

**Summary** (one per release, on release day, store-local rollup complete):
"Your release is live on 38 of 40 stores. Beatport rejected it; Amazon hasn't responded." Sent to
the owning entity's members **and** to credited Producers. This is the Producer's only status
notification, ever.

**Digest** (daily, in-app, no interrupt): everything else, while any row is unhealthy. Suppressed
entirely when the board is all-green — a healthy board sends nothing.

**Caps and dedupe**:
- Dedupe by `(release × store × state)` — a state never notifies twice, even if it flaps.
- Maximum **one interrupt per release per 24 hours per trigger class**; further events of the same
  class join the digest.
- A store flapping `Live` → `Unknown` → `Live` (a reconciliation wobble, not a real change) must
  settle for **two consecutive checks** before it notifies at all.

## Happy Path

1. Delivery is scheduled (12.04.01). Every selected (store × territory) from 12.03.01 becomes a row
   — CX-01. The board shows one summary row: "40 stores queued for Fri 12 Sep."
2. At the delivery window, rows move to `Sent` as 12.02.03 hands each partner its message.
3. Acks land and normalise; rows move to `Received`. The board still shows a single collapsed
   healthy group — nothing here needs the artist's attention, and it says so by showing nothing.
4. Rows move to `Accepted`, each carrying the store's expected live date.
5. The artist opens the board in release week. It shows: "40 stores accepted. Nothing needs you."
   Elapsed time and expectation are one tap away on any row.
6. Release Friday, 00:00 in the earliest territory (UTC+14): reconciliation confirms store-side
   truth and the first rows move to `Live`, per territory, with links.
7. The board tracks the wave westward: "Live in 63 territories. Next: Japan, in 3 hours." No row is
   flagged late, because no local midnight has passed for the rows that are not live yet.
8. Within 25 hours the last territory's local midnight passes and reconciliation confirms it.
9. One summary notification fires: "Live on all 40 stores." It is the only interrupt the release
   produced.
10. The wrong-page check (CX-04) and the live-but-zero-plays check (D-10) run against the linked
    artist IDs from 12.03.04 and clear.
11. The board goes all-green and collapses to a single row. The artist stops checking. **That is
    success** — the destination screen of domain 12 is one the artist should be able to leave.
12. Reconciliation keeps running for the life of the catalog (D-06). It will notice if the record
    ever disappears, months later, and it will be the only thing that does.

## Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Accepted, release date passed, not live** | The worst case. Partner ack says fine; reality says no. `Accepted · overdue` — the qualifier names *which* wait broke (DT-15) | "Apple accepted this 9 days ago and it still isn't live. We're chasing — here's the reference." |
| **Partner reports live, store-side check disagrees** | Trust the store, not the ack (D-02). Row stays `Accepted`, never advances on an ack alone | "Spotify says it's live but we can't find it. Looking into it." |
| **Store-side check says live, no ack ever arrived** | Store truth wins in both directions. Row moves to `Live`; the missing ack is a partner-integration defect, not the artist's problem | "Live on Spotify." (The absent ack is invisible to the artist and logged for us.) |
| Live in some territories, not others, **on release day** | Normal — local-midnight stagger (D-07). Not a failure, not flagged | "Live in 63 territories. Japan goes live in 3 hours." |
| Live in some territories, not others, **72h after the last local midnight** | Now a real partial-delivery failure | "Live everywhere except Japan and Korea. That's not the schedule — we're asking Apple why." |
| **Release live on the wrong artist page** | Technically live — every status is green and the outcome is a disaster | Routes to 12.03.04. **A green board can be a lie**; see DT-03 |
| **Live for 72h, zero plays, artist has a baseline** | The only available detector of the wrong-page failure (D-10). Alert | "This is live on Spotify but has no plays after 3 days, and your last release had 400 by now. That usually means it's on the wrong artist page." |
| **Live, zero plays, artist is a debut with no baseline** | **Cannot detect.** No prior release to compare against, no alert fires | Nothing. This is the honest limit: the detector is weakest exactly where the risk is highest, because a debut release is the one that creates the permanent page (12.03.04 D-02). Recorded in DT-05, not papered over. |
| **Store goes live early**, before the release date | Embargo broken. Pre-saves (12.04.03) fire early or not at all; the editorial pitch window (12.04.02) is compromised; 21's campaign is misaligned | "Deezer put this live 2 days early. Your pre-save campaign and your announce are now out of sync — here's what's affected." Alert, not celebration (D-13) |
| **Live → gone**, no takedown requested | `Removed by store`. Detected only because monitoring never stops (D-06) | "Spotify has removed this. We didn't ask them to and they haven't told us why. We're finding out." Routes to 12.05.03 |
| **Accepted then rejected** (QC after acceptance) | Legal transition. The artist has already been told it was accepted — the board must own the reversal, not hide it | "Apple accepted this on Tuesday and has now rejected it. That's on them to explain and us to chase — here's what they said." |
| **Reconciliation source unavailable** | Status is stale; must say so. Row keeps its last state and its last-checked timestamp | "Last checked 3 hours ago" — never a confident stale claim |
| **Reconciliation stale > 24h** | Row is marked `Stale` and its state is stated in the past tense | "As of yesterday, live on Spotify. We haven't been able to check since." |
| **Store has no reconciliation path at all** | `Unknown` is the honest terminal state (D-04) | "{Store} doesn't tell us whether it went live. We'll show it as delivered, not confirmed." Said once, not on every row forever |
| **Store retires or merges** (e.g. Google Play Music → YouTube Music) | The row refers to a store that no longer exists. History is not rewritten | "Google Play Music shut down in 2020. Your release was live there until then." The row is archived, not deleted — deleting it would falsify the record (D-18) |
| **40 stores × 200 territories** | 8,000 status cells; a table is unusable | Summarise by exception — show what is wrong, collapse what is fine (D-01) |
| **200 territory rows rendered as confirmed** | Would be fake precision — 8,000 confident cells from 40 acks | `Requested` is visually distinct from `Confirmed live` and labelled as asked-for (D-05, DT-06) |
| **Out-of-order acks** — `Received` arrives after `Live` | Ordered by the **partner's** timestamp, not arrival time. Stale ack is recorded in history and does not transition the row (D-14) | Nothing. A board that regressed from `Live` to `Received` because of an SFTP batch ordering quirk would destroy trust in one frame |
| **Duplicate acks for the same message** | Idempotent by message ID (12.02.03 D-03). No state change, no notification | Nothing |
| **Ack for a delivery we never sent** | Matched against our own message IDs. An unmatched ack **never creates a row** | Nothing — and it is logged as a partner-integration anomaly. A partner (or an attacker) must not be able to conjure a status row |
| **Rejection reason contains markup or a script payload** | Raw partner text is untrusted input. Escaped on render; the translated form (12.03.03 D-01) is what the artist normally sees | Plain text, always. The raw string stays available and buried (12.03.03 DT-02) |
| **Two band members both hit "chase" on the same overdue store** | Debounced per `(release × store)`. The second action joins the first, it does not open a second escalation | "Already chasing — raised 20 minutes ago by Sam." Naming who did it is what stops the third person doing it again |
| **Band-owned release, five members, nobody is the contact** | The bystander problem (DT-14). Without a named contact, five people each assume another handled the Apple rejection | Blocked at scheduling: a release cannot be delivered without a named delivery contact (D-15) |
| **Named delivery contact leaves the band or deletes their account mid-rollout** | Alerts would dead-end into nobody | Ownership falls back to the owning entity's release-management role holders (01), and the board **blocks on naming a new contact** at the next interrupt-worthy event |
| **Multi-hyphenate: producer of this record is also a member of the owning band** | Access resolves from the relationship to *this release*, not the persona (D-12) | Full access — as a Musician on their own release. They do not get a degraded read-only view because they also produced it |
| **Multi-hyphenate: same human, two artist identities, both with releases out** | Each identity's releases are separate boards with separate contacts (12.03.04 DT-02) | Two boards. Merging them by human would leak one project's commercial state into another's, which for a producer-for-hire alias is a real confidentiality problem |
| **Producer wants to chase an overdue store on a record they made** | No affordance exists. Read-only is read-only (Role Lens) | The state, the elapsed time, the expectation — and no button. The asymmetry is real and is not softened |
| **Rights lapse under a live release** — an exclusive begins, a clearance expires | The board says `Live` in a territory the artist no longer has the right to be live in. **The board is factually correct and legally wrong** | `Live · rights conflict` — the board keeps reporting the truth it can see and surfaces the divergence rather than resolving it. Routes to 09 and 12.05. See CX note on 09 |
| **Artist opens the board with 0 releases** | Empty, not broken | "Nothing delivered yet" + link to scheduling |
| **Network fails while the board is open** | Last-known state is retained and timestamped; no spinner that never resolves | "Can't refresh — showing what we had at 14:02." |
| **Reconciliation flaps** `Live` → `Unknown` → `Live` | Must settle for two consecutive checks before notifying (D-09) | Nothing. A wobble in our own polling must never page an artist about their release disappearing |
| **Store-for-artists connection revoked by the artist mid-rollout** | The reconciliation path this feature depends on may vanish by user action (DT-14) | "We can't confirm Spotify any more — you disconnected Spotify for Artists. Rows will show as delivered, not confirmed." Honest about the consequence of their own choice |
| **A store's status is commercially sensitive to a label but not to the band** | Who sees the board is an org permission question (Q-03) | Unresolved — see Q-03. The default is: everyone with a stake in the release sees the same board |

## States

> The feature's own UI states, distinct from the per-store status vocabulary above.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No delivery scheduled or sent for this release | "Nothing delivered yet" + link to scheduling (12.04.01). No skeleton, no zeroes — an empty board is not a broken board |
| Loading | First fetch of the board | Skeleton rows sized to the known store count from 12.03.01 — the count is known before the statuses are, so the board never reflows |
| Populated | Statuses known and fresh | Exception-first board (D-01): unhealthy rows individually, healthy rows collapsed to one summary row, territory detail nested and shown only where it differs |
| Partial | Some stores reconciled, some not | Mixed board with explicit `Unknown` rows carrying their reason. The unreconciled stores are **not** rendered in the same language as the confirmed ones |
| Stale | Board loaded, but reconciliation is behind (> 24h since last check) | Every affected row stated in the past tense with its timestamp: "As of yesterday, live on Spotify." The board still works; it just stops claiming the present tense |
| Error | Status service unreachable | "Can't refresh — showing last known from {time}." Last-known state always beats an error page: an artist in release week needs the stale truth more than they need a retry button |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A 40-store × 200-territory status table is the right presentation because per-store status is a first-class object | Following D-02 in the parent index to its literal conclusion | ❌ REJECTED — 8,000 cells is data, not information. Being *correct* about status and *useful* about status are different problems, and the domain's founding insight (status is per-store) solves only the first. The board must be **exception-first**: surface what is wrong or late, collapse what is fine to a count. The rejection matters because the correct data model quietly invites the wrong UI, and this is the screen the product is judged on. |
| DT-02 | The partner's acceptance ack is sufficient to report a release as live | The information the partner gives us; what every distributor uses | ❌ REJECTED — accepted means accepted, not published, and the gap is routinely days. Reporting `Live` off an ack is exactly the over-claiming rejected in 12.02.03 DT-02, and it produces the domain's most infuriating experience: a green dashboard next to an absent release. Confirming `Live` needs store-side truth, which needs an answer to Q-01 — and the only method that reliably catches the silent case (checking the public store) is the least respectable one. That tension is real and unresolved. |
| DT-03 | A green board means the release succeeded | The implicit promise of the whole screen | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most important annotation in the sub-domain. A release can be `Live` on every store and still be a **disaster**: on the wrong artist page (12.03.04), with the wrong (P) line, with splits nobody attested. Green means *delivered*, not *right*. The board must not imply an all-clear it cannot support — and this is precisely why 12.03.04 exists as a Deep Think addition, because the sweep's candidate list had a domain that could report total success while the artist's release was attached to a stranger's discography. |
| DT-04 | The board should carry per-store streams and sales, because artists conflate "where is it" with "how is it doing" and will expect both here | Parent Q-02; the observed behaviour is real — artists open the status board looking for numbers | ❌ REJECTED as a feature of this board. Performance is domain 22 and belongs there. The rejection is not about boundary tidiness — it is that the two screens have **opposite success conditions**. This board succeeds when the artist *stops looking at it* (Happy Path 11); an analytics board succeeds when they come back daily. Fusing them converts the product's honesty instrument into its dopamine instrument, and the first casualty is the exception-first rule: nobody collapses the row with the numbers on it. Worse, it makes the board's silence ambiguous — is nothing wrong, or is nothing happening? Those must never be the same screen. **Resolves parent Q-02: 22 owns performance; this board links to it and shows none of it — with the single, narrow exception in DT-05.** |
| DT-05 | The board nevertheless needs exactly **one** signal from 22 — *live but zero plays* — because it is the only available detector of the wrong-page failure | DT-03 + CX-04: a wrong-page landing produces no rejection, no ack anomaly, and no status divergence. It is invisible to every input this feature has | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the sharpest thing in this file. The wrong-page disaster is undetectable from delivery data by construction — the delivery *worked*. The only observable is an absence: live, and nobody is listening. So the board consumes one derived boolean from 22 (`live ≥72h ∧ plays = 0 ∧ artist's prior releases on this store had plays by 72h`) as a **health check**, not a performance display — it renders as an alert, never as a number. This is a principled exception to DT-04, not a crack in it: it is 22 telling 12 that 12's own status is wrong. **And it fails precisely where it matters most**: a debut artist has no baseline, so no alert fires — and the debut release is the one that creates the permanent artist page (12.03.04 D-02). We can detect the wrong-page failure for artists who least need it and not for the artist who most does. That limit is stated in the edge case table rather than hidden behind a threshold. |
| DT-06 | Per-territory status is confirmable, so 200 territory rows per store can each carry a real state | Parent D-02 makes per-territory status a first-class object, which reads as a promise that it can be *known* | ❌ REJECTED — and this is a genuine defect in the naive reading of D-02. **A store does not acknowledge per territory.** It accepts one delivery carrying one territory list and applies availability internally; whether the record actually appears in Japan is observable only *from Japan*. So 7,960 of those 8,000 cells would be derived from 40 acks and rendered as though independently known. That is **fake precision** — a higher-resolution version of the `released` boolean this feature exists to destroy, and worse than the boolean because it is more convincing. Territory rows therefore carry `Requested` (asked for, store accepted the delivery, not confirmed), `Confirmed live` (independently observed) or `Excluded` (a rights fact from 12.03.01), and the first is never rendered in the visual language of the second. D-02 is preserved — per-territory status stays a first-class object — but its honest default value is "we asked", not "it's there". |
| DT-07 | Monitoring can stop once a store reports `Live` — the delivery is done | Every distributor's implicit model; the delivery pipeline's natural terminus | ❌ REJECTED — `Live` is not terminal. Stores pull releases **on their own**: rights conflicts, Content ID disputes (12.06), QC sweeps, collateral damage from a takedown aimed at someone else. Nobody is told. The artist finds out from a fan, or from a royalty statement that stops. This is the same structural failure as DT-02 wearing different clothes: the platform stops looking at exactly the moment it decided everything was fine. A distribution product that watches for the life of the catalog and says "Spotify removed this, we didn't ask, we're finding out" is doing something no incumbent does, for the cost of not switching the loop off. `Live` → `Removed by store` is a first-class detection, and it is separate from `Withdrawn` because the domain already ratified that split (12 D-05). |
| DT-08 | Release day is a day, so a store not live on release day is late | The word "date" on the release object; the mental model every artist and every agent brings | ❌ REJECTED — and this would have been a shipped bug that fired on **every release, on the one day the artist is watching**. Global release day is Friday and DSPs publish at **local midnight per territory**: 25 hours separate the first territory (UTC+14) from the last (UTC−11). A store showing `Accepted` at 09:00 UTC Friday is behaving perfectly. An `overdue` flag evaluated in UTC would cry wolf on every release until artists learned to ignore the board — the exact outcome this feature exists to prevent, achieved by being *too* vigilant rather than too little. Liveness and lateness are both store-local facts, and the board's release-day language must be a wave ("live in 63 territories, Japan in 3 hours"), not a fraction ("partially live"), because a fraction reads as breakage. |
| DT-09 | `Live` is one state | It is one word, and the artist asks one question | ❌ REJECTED — **`Live (pre-order)` and `Live (streaming)` are different facts with different consequences.** Apple takes pre-orders and pre-adds; an album can be purchasable and un-streamable for weeks, and an instant-gratification track from that album can be streamable while the album is not. Collapsing these means the board says "live on Apple" while a fan following the announce finds nothing to play — and the artist, looking at a green row, has no way to know why. It also breaks 12.04.03: a pre-save campaign's whole point is the gap between pre-order-live and streaming-live, and a board that cannot express the gap cannot tell the artist their campaign is aimed at the wrong date. |
| DT-10 | Every state change is worth notifying, because the artist wants to know | The instinct behind every "we've updated your order status" email; also the literal reading of "status board" | ❌ REJECTED — 40 stores × ~10 transitions ≈ 200 events per release. The failure mode is not annoyance, it is **calibration**: an artist who has been paged 200 times will miss the one that says "Apple rejected this". Over-notifying and under-notifying produce the same outcome (the artist doesn't act) by opposite routes. The inverse — notify only on `Live` — is what incumbents do, and it misses both states that are actually actionable (`Rejected`, `Overdue`). **Resolves Q-02**: interrupt only where the recipient can act or has been misled — `Rejected`, `· overdue`, mismatch/zero-plays, early go-live — routed to the person who can act rather than to the owner by default (12.03.03 D-04); one release-day summary; everything else in a daily digest that is suppressed entirely when the board is green. A healthy release should produce **exactly one** notification in its life (Happy Path 9). |
| DT-11 | A store going live early is a happy accident — the artist gets a head start | The intuition that earlier is better | ❌ REJECTED — an early go-live is a **broken embargo** and it is expensive. The pre-save campaign (12.04.03) converts on release day and now fires against a record already available; the editorial pitch (12.04.02) assumed a date; 21's announce is scheduled for Friday and the record is out on Wednesday to nobody. The artist has lost the coordinated moment that the entire rollout was built around, and every status on the board is green while it happens. It is DT-03's principle in a second instance: **a good-looking state can be the failure**. The board alerts, immediately, and names what is now out of sync — because the artist has hours, not days, to re-aim a campaign. |
| DT-12 | The expectation ("usually 2 days") is available whenever we need it | D-03 asserts every state carries an expectation, which quietly assumes the data exists | ❌ REJECTED — on day one WeJammin has **zero** deliveries and cannot say what is usual for Apple. D-03 as written is a promise the platform cannot keep at launch, and the tempting fix — a plausible default — would be the platform doing the exact thing it condemns partners for: stating a confident number it has not earned. The honest board says "Received by Apple 6 days ago. We don't have enough history with Apple to say whether that's normal yet", raises **no** `overdue` flag (there is nothing to be over), and gets better with volume. Where a partner's contract or published documentation carries a window — and **only** where our own observation is insufficient, since observed outranks contractual (12.02.03 D-05) — it is stated **and attributed to them**. This is the same cold-start shape as 12.03.03 DT-01, and the same answer: the loop pays off with volume and we do not fake the interest. **Ratified refinement**: the evidence floor for the expectation *clause* is the p50 at N ≥ 10 for this (partner × message type), deliberately lower than the p95 at N ≥ 30 that fires the alarm — and for a low-volume partner or an uncommon message type, the alarm floor may never be reached at all, so the row is permanently elapsed-time-plus-median with no `· overdue`. That is the honest outcome, not a defect. |
| DT-13 | A viewer's access to the board follows their persona | The Role Matrix is a persona table, so persona looks like the key | ❌ REJECTED — access follows the viewer's **relationship to this release**, not their persona. personas.md's founding structural fact is the multi-hyphenate: the drummer who produced the record and is in the band is not "a Producer" here, they are an owner, and handing them a read-only view of their own band's release because they also engineered it would be exactly the "are you an artist OR a producer?" failure personas.md says breaks this user immediately. Conversely a band member who is a producer-for-hire on a *different* release is read-only *there*, at the same moment, in the same session. The Role Lens is a lens on the relationship, which is what the personas file said it was all along — the board is just the place where getting it wrong is most visible. |
| DT-14 | The release owner is the right notification recipient | It is the default in every tool, and it is obviously true when the owner is one person | ❌ REJECTED for the case the product is actually built for. Per D-19 and personas.md the **band is an entity**, so "the owner" can be five people — and five people receiving "Apple rejected your release" produces the **bystander effect**: everyone assumes someone else is on it, and nobody is. This is not a hypothetical; it is the default outcome of group notification, and it is worst on the highest-stakes alert. Each release therefore names a single **delivery contact** who receives the four interrupts; the entity's other members get the digest and the release-day summary. Naming who is chasing also solves the concurrency case (two members both hitting "chase"): the second sees "already chasing — raised 20 minutes ago by Sam", and the third does not try. |
| DT-15 | `Overdue` is a member of the state vocabulary, as 12.02.03 D-01 models it | The sibling's own state table lists it as a peer of `Received` and `Accepted` | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **qualifier on time-in-state**, not a state. `Overdue` is the only value in the vocabulary that is not a fact about the release: it is the platform's judgment about the clock. Modelling it as a peer state destroys the information that makes the row actionable — "overdue waiting for an ack" (a transport or partner-integration problem, chase the pipe) and "overdue waiting to go live" (the domain's worst case, chase the store) are different problems with different escalations, and as a peer state they are the same word. **Scope note added on ratification (D-19)**: this is a distinction about *which wait broke*, and it is **not** an argument for keying the expected-response window per ack stage — that axis was declined outright (12.02.02 D-13). The ack wait runs on 12.02.03 D-05's (partner × message type) window; the live wait runs on D-07's store-local clock; neither is folded into the other. Silence stays first-class (12.02.03 D-01 is preserved); it attaches to the state it interrupts rather than replacing it. This feature owns the status object (CX-02) so the refinement lands here — but the two vocabularies must be reconciled into one during Step 6, or the domain ships two disagreeing state machines. |
| DT-16 | Reconciliation method is purely an architecture decision (Q-01) | It reads like one — webhook vs polling vs scraping is a mechanism question | ❌ REJECTED — **one of the options has a user-facing consent surface, so the choice is not purely architecture's to make.** Store-for-artists accounts (Spotify for Artists, Apple Music for Artists) expose an artist's own catalog to that artist, which is a legitimate, non-scraping reconciliation path — but it exists only if the artist connects it, which is a product act with an ask, a consent, a revocation, and a degraded board when they decline. That reframes Q-01: it is not "how do we check?" but "what do we do for artists who don't connect, and is a two-tier board honest or coercive?" This feature's answer: unconnected stores are not broken, they are **less confirmable**, and the board says so plainly rather than nagging. The mechanism stays with `/create-prd-architecture`; the ask, the refusal, and the revocation are behaviour and are specified here (edge cases). It also connects to 12.03.04 Q-01 — if the platform helps artists *claim* their store profiles, it acquires the reconciliation path as a byproduct of a feature artists already want, which is the consolidation thesis (D-18) paying for the provenance machinery. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **12.02.03 Delivery Choreography & Acknowledgement** ([path](../12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging/12.02.03-delivery-choreography-acknowledgement.md)) — **trigger dependency + state race**. Normalised acks are this feature's primary input; the tightest coupling in the domain. Three specific items for Step 6: (a) the two state vocabularies must be reconciled into one (DT-15 makes `overdue` a qualifier where 12.02.03 D-01 makes it a state); (b) acks arrive out of order and must be ordered by the **partner's** timestamp, not arrival (D-14); (c) the **ack wait** is governed by 12.02.03 D-05's (partner × message type) window while the **live wait** stays here on D-07's store-local clock (D-19) — one window, two clocks, never merged.
- Touches **12.03.01 Store Selection & Territory Scoping** ([path](./12.03.01-store-selection-territory-scoping.md)) — **shared entity**. The selection set is owned there and read here; it is the only thing that creates rows (CX-01). It is frozen at delivery — post-delivery changes are 12.05, never an edit here (CX R-04).
- Touches **12.03.03 Rejection Triage & Remediation** ([path](./12.03.03-rejection-triage-remediation.md)) — **trigger dependency + permission**. `Rejected` is the only state that is a door; triage owns everything past it and drives the row back to `Sent` via redelivery (CX-06) rather than writing status directly. The rejection notification routes to whoever can fix it (12.03.03 D-04), which is why this feature's notification rule cannot assume the release owner is the recipient.
- Touches **12.03.04 Artist Profile Linking & Disambiguation** ([path](./12.03.04-artist-profile-linking-disambiguation.md)) — **shared entity + other**. A green board can hide a wrong-page disaster (DT-03, CX-04). This feature consumes the linked store artist IDs (owned by 01) to run the post-delivery verification, and it is the surface that raises the mismatch alert. 12.03.04 Q-01 (claiming store profiles) is also this feature's most promising reconciliation path — see DT-16.
- Touches **22 Analytics & Market Intelligence** ([path](../../22-analytics-market-intelligence/)) — **shared entity, deliberately narrow**. Resolves parent Q-02: 22 owns performance and this board shows **none** of it (DT-04), with exactly one exception — 22 supplies one derived boolean (`live ≥72h ∧ plays = 0 ∧ prior-release baseline > 0`) as a wrong-page health check (DT-05, D-10). It renders as an alert, never as a number. **Step 6 must confirm 22 will emit it** — this is 12 asking 22 for a signal about 12's own correctness, which is an unusual direction of dependency and easy to lose.
- Touches **12.04.01 Release Date & Lead Time** ([path](../12.04-release-scheduling-windows/12.04.01-release-date-lead-time.md)) — **trigger dependency**. The release date plus store-local midnight is what makes "not live yet" normal or late (D-07, DT-08). A board that evaluates lateness in UTC cries wolf on every release.
- Touches **12.04.02 Editorial Pitch Windows** ([path](../12.04-release-scheduling-windows/12.04.02-editorial-pitch-windows.md)) — **trigger dependency**. An early go-live (D-13) invalidates a pitch that assumed a date.
- Touches **12.04.03 Pre-Save / Pre-Add Links** ([path](../12.04-release-scheduling-windows/12.04.03-pre-save-pre-add-links.md)) — **trigger dependency**. `Live (pre-order)` vs `Live` is exactly the gap a pre-save campaign lives in (DT-09); an early go-live breaks the conversion the campaign was built for (DT-11).
- Touches **12.05.01 Voluntary Takedown** ([path](../12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.01-voluntary-takedown.md)) and **12.05.03 Involuntary Takedown & Suspension** ([path](../12.05-catalog-lifecycle-after-release/12.05.03-involuntary-takedown-suspension.md)) — **trigger dependency**. `Withdrawn` and `Removed by store` are separate states mirroring domain 12 D-05; a store pulling a release on its own (DT-07) routes to 12.05.03 and is detected *only* because monitoring never stops (D-06).
- Touches **12.02.02 Per-Partner Profile Conformance** ([path](../12.02-ddex-delivery-messaging/12.02.02-per-partner-profile-conformance.md)) — **shared entity**. The partner's published response window, whether they support track-level territory, their publish behaviour, and their retirement/merge status are all profile data this board reads to say anything honest (D-11, and the store-retirement edge case). The window is resolved **per (partner × message type)**, not per ack stage (12.02.02 D-13), and the published SLA is used **only** where our own observation is insufficient — observed outranks contractual (12.02.02 D-12 / 12.02.03 D-05). A partner inside our observed window but outside their own SLA is a commercial divergence routed to partner management and never rendered on this board; that routing has no owner (12.02.02 Q-07).
- Touches **12.06 Content ID & UGC Claiming** ([path](../12.06-content-id-ugc-claiming/)) — **trigger dependency**. A Content ID or rights dispute is one of the reasons a `Live` release becomes `Removed by store` with no warning (DT-07).
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **state race**, and the one with legal weight. A right can lapse *under a live release* — an exclusive begins, a clearance expires — so the board's truth ("live in Japan") and the rights record ("not licensed for Japan") silently diverge. The board reports what it observes and surfaces the divergence (`Live · rights conflict`); it does not resolve it, and resolution has no obvious owner. This is CX-01's synthesis question 5, answered as far as this feature can answer it: **detection here, remedy in 09/12.05.**
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **permission**. Three things live there, not here: the store artist IDs the verification check reads (12.03.04 D-01); the owning entity for a band-owned release and its release-management roles (D-15, DT-14); and the multi-hyphenate identity model that makes D-12's relationship-based access resolvable at all (personas.md Q-04).
- Touches **21 Promotion & Marketing** ([path](../../21-promotion-marketing/)) — **trigger dependency**. An announce campaign firing against a store that is not live — or a store that went live early (DT-11) — is a real, expensive coordination failure. 21 needs this board's per-store state as an input, not just a release-level date.
- Touches **20 Fanbase & Direct-to-Fan** ([path](../../20-fanbase-direct-to-fan/)) — **notification**, indirect. The Fan has no access here, but a wrong-page landing severs their follow→discovery line invisibly (12.03.04 DT-03), and this board's zero-plays check (D-10) is the platform's only detector of it.
- Consumes cross-cut **Notifications & Alerts** — state changes are the most notification-worthy events in the domain and the most easily over-sent. The full rule is D-09: four interrupt triggers, one release-day summary, a suppressible daily digest, dedupe by `(release × store × state)`, one interrupt per release per 24h per trigger class, and a two-check settle before any flap notifies.
- Consumes cross-cut **Analytics Instrumentation & Per-Domain Reporting** — the observation history per **(partner × message type)** is what makes "usually 2 days" sayable (D-03), and its absence at launch is what makes D-11's cold-start clause necessary. It must yield **two** statistics off one history: the **p50 at N ≥ 10** behind the copy and the **p95 at N ≥ 30 over 180 days** behind the alarm (12.02.03 D-05). A single number would satisfy neither.
- Consumes cross-cut **Integrations, Public API & Webhooks** — partner acks, store-side reconciliation, and the store-for-artists connection whose consent surface is specified here (DT-16).
- Consumes cross-cut **Canonical Data, Taxonomy & Entity Resolution** — territory taxonomy (ISO codes, DSP territory groupings) and the store registry, including retired and merged stores whose historical rows must not be rewritten.
