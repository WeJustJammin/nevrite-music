## Overview

Where a release goes, whether it is allowed to go there, what state it is in at each destination,
and what to do when a destination says no. This is the sub-domain that makes the domain's defining
fact tractable: **a release is not "released" — it is live on Spotify, rejected by Apple, pending
on Beatport and blocked in Japan, all at once.** Per-store, per-territory status is a first-class
tracked object here, never a flag on the release.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: store selection, territory/exclusivity scoping,
status tracking, rejection triage and artist-profile linking are five capabilities in tight
mutual interaction. Selection determines what gets tracked; territory scoping is a rights
constraint that can veto a selection; a rejection re-opens the builder; artist-profile linking
is a per-store fact that only exists once a store has accepted delivery.

**This is also the artist's destination.** The per-store status board is the screen an artist
actually opens during a release, and the one they judge the whole product by. Everything else in
domain 12 is machinery; this is the window.

> **Producer read-only on status (12.03.02) is a deliberate call, not a default.** The Producer
> made the record and has a legitimate interest in where it went — often a points position — but
> no authority over its distribution. This is the same high-power/low-authority shape identified
> in 12.01: their *facts* are load-bearing, their *permissions* are not. Their Config access on
> triage (12.03.03) exists because an asset-caused rejection is theirs to fix and nobody else's.
>
> **Fan is `None` throughout.** The Fan-facing "is it out yet?" question is answered by the DSP
> and by domains 20/21, never by this board.
