# Feature: Rejection Triage & Remediation

> **Number**: 12.03.03
> **Parent**: [DSP Store & Territory Management](./12.03-dsp-store-territory-management-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

What happens when a store says no: translating the partner's rejection into something a musician
can act on, routing them to the exact thing that needs fixing, redelivering, and — the part that
compounds — turning every rejection into a rule so the next artist is warned before delivery
instead of rejected after it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the rejection in plain language with the specific field or asset named, one action that takes them to it, and an honest statement of what it costs (usually: the release date slips on this store). Never sees `DELIVERY_ERR_4021`. |
| Producer | Config | Gets the rejections **they** can fix — asset failures, master problems, technical spec mismatches — routed to them directly, not relayed by an artist who cannot action them. Per personas.md the Producer works in a DAW, not a browser: an asset rejection must be actionable from a notification, and a re-render must be re-attachable without a builder session. |
| Operator | None | Not visible — release-to-store delivery is a Musician/Producer concern. The Operator's inventory is time and space (personas.md), not recordings; a DSP rejection has no bearing on their surface. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — the Fan experiences only the published result. A store rejection is upstream delivery plumbing they must never be exposed to; they see the release when (and only when) it is live, per the transparency-about-jargon boundary (DT-02). |

## Behavior

A rejection is a **partner's opinion about a specific thing**, and triage's job is to convert an
opaque partner verdict into a located, actionable, plain-language item. Three routes:

| Rejection class | Routes to | Who fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Metadata (title case, banned characters, genre) | 12.01.02 field | Musician |
| Asset (spec, format, artwork dimensions/content) | 12.01.04 file | Producer |
| Rights/identifier (ISRC collision, ownership conflict, duplicate release) | 09 / 12.07 | Musician + possibly a counterparty |

**Rejections are per-store.** A release rejected by Apple and live on Spotify is normal, and the
fix must not disturb the stores that are already fine. Redelivery is scoped to the rejecting
partner (12.05.02 semantics) — never a global resend, which would risk the duplicate hazard in
12.02.03 DT-03.

The compounding part: every rejection is evidence that validation (12.01.02) or the partner
profile (12.02.02) is wrong. Triage proposes the rule; a human confirms; the rule protects
everyone thereafter. This is the mechanism by which WeJammin accumulates the partner knowledge
that 12.02.02 DT-02 identifies as the real distributor moat — and it is the only path to closing
a cold-start gap that provenance differentiation does nothing to address.

### Happy Path

1. Partner nacks; ack normalises to `Rejected` on that store's row (12.03.02).
2. Triage classifies the rejection and locates it — this field, this file, this identifier.
3. Musician (or Producer, for assets) is notified with plain language and one action.
4. They open the located item, fix it, and confirm redelivery.
5. Redelivery goes **only** to the rejecting partner; unaffected stores are untouched.
6. In parallel, triage proposes a validation rule / profile correction; a human reviews it;
   accepted, it warns every subsequent artist pre-delivery.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Rejection reason is unintelligible even to us | Cannot auto-locate; needs a human | "Apple rejected this and their reason isn't clear. We're on it — you don't need to do anything yet." Never dump the raw text and call it transparency |
| Rejection is wrong (partner error) | Not fixable by the artist; needs a partner conversation | "We think Apple is wrong here. We've raised it." |
| Same rejection across many stores | One root cause, many rows | Grouped: "This one thing is blocking 6 stores." |
| Fix requires a person who has left the project | Asset rejection, Producer gone | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — real and unhandled; see 12.01 CX-03 |
| Rejection arrives after release date | The release is late on that store and the artist is already promoting | "Apple rejected this. Fixing now — Apple will be ~3 days behind the others." Honest, not soothing |
| Rejection is an ISRC collision | Identifier problem, not metadata; may mean the recording is already released elsewhere | Routes to 12.07 / 12.08 |
| Triage auto-derives a rule from a one-off partner glitch | Would block valid deliveries for everyone | Human review before any rule goes live (D-03) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No rejections | Not surfaced — the healthy case shows nothing |
| Triaging | Rejection received, classification running | "Apple rejected this. Working out why." — brief and honest |
| Located | Classified and pinned to an item | Plain-language item + one action |
| Unlocatable | Cannot classify | Escalated to platform; artist told it is not their problem yet |
| Fixing | User is editing | Item open in the builder |
| Redelivering | Fix confirmed, resent | Store row returns to `Sent` |
| Resolved | Partner accepted | Store row rejoins the healthy set |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Every rejection should become a validation rule** | 12.01.02 DT-02 (rule packs are learnable data); 12.02.02 DT-02 (partner knowledge is the moat) | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the domain's only compounding asset, and the only answer to WeJammin's genuine cold-start disadvantage against fifteen years of DistroKid's accumulated scar tissue. Each artist's rejection buys protection for every artist after them. It is also a network effect that works at small scale, unlike most: 100 artists produce enough rejections to be materially useful, and the loop starts paying immediately. |
| DT-02 | Showing the artist the partner's raw rejection text is radical transparency and therefore good | The transparency instinct, which is otherwise correct throughout this domain (12.02.01 DT-02, 12.03.02 D-04) | ❌ REJECTED — transparency about **state** is honesty; transparency about **jargon** is abdication. "DELIVERY_ERR_4021: invalid PLine" tells a musician nothing and makes them feel stupid, which is exactly the incumbent distributor experience. The honest move is to translate; where we genuinely cannot, say *that* plainly ("their reason isn't clear, we're on it") rather than paste the string and call it openness. Raw text stays available, buried, for the artist who wants it. |
| DT-03 | Rule derivation from rejections can be automatic | It is the obvious way to make DT-01's loop scale | ❌ REJECTED — a partner glitch or a one-off outage would auto-derive a rule that blocks valid deliveries **for every user**, and a false blocking rule is far more expensive than a missing one: it stops releases that would have succeeded, and it does so invisibly. Human review before a rule goes live. This caps the loop's throughput, which is the correct trade — the moat compounds over years, not hours. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every rejection is translated and located to a specific item with one action | Raw partner jargon is abdication, not transparency (DT-02) |
| D-02 | Redelivery is scoped to the rejecting partner only | Global resend risks duplicates (12.02.03 DT-03) and disturbs healthy stores |
| D-03 | Rejections propose rules; humans confirm them before they go live | A false blocking rule costs more than a missing one (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Asset rejections route to the Producer directly | The artist cannot re-render a master (personas.md) |
