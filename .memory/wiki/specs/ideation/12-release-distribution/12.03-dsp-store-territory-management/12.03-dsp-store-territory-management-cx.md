## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Store Selection ↔ Per-Store Status

**Relationship**: Selection is what brings the status board into existence. Each selected (store × territory) becomes a tracked object with its own lifecycle. This is where the domain's founding observation becomes concrete: forty stores selected means forty independent asynchronous conversations, each of which can succeed, fail or go silent on its own. It is also where the presentation problem is born — the selection that produces 8,000 status cells is a single "worldwide, all stores" click (12.03.02 DT-01).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: makes the selection, reads the board. The cost of "select all" is invisible at selection time and overwhelming at status time — the board must absorb that, not the picker.
- **Producer**: reads the board only; had no say in selection. Sees the outcome of a decision they were not part of.
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **selection set** is owned by 12.03.01 and read by 12.03.02. Once delivered it is frozen — changing where a live release is sold is a catalog-lifecycle act (12.05), not an edit. Neither feature may mutate the other's state.
2. **Trigger chain**: Selection → scheduling (12.04.01) → delivery (12.02.03) → status rows appear. A store deselected *before* delivery leaves no trace; deselected *after*, it is a takedown on that store (12.05.01). Same gesture, radically different meanings on either side of the delivery boundary — a real hazard for downstream spec writers.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the sub-domain's sharpest asymmetry. Selection is Musician-only (Full); status is Musician-Full and Producer-read-only. The Producer can see forty rows about a record they made and act on none of them — except in triage (CX-02), where asset rejections are theirs alone to fix. High-power, low-authority, again.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Status changes notify the release owner. Whether they notify the Producer is unresolved — they legitimately care, and notifying every contributor of every state change across forty stores is a firehose. `[PENDING — Step 5]`; likely exceptions-only for the Producer.
5. **State transition conflict**: A territory constraint can *change* after selection — an exclusive expires, a clearance lapses — while the release is live. Rights (09) moving under a delivered release means the board's truth and the rights record can silently diverge. This is a genuine consistency hazard with legal weight and no obvious owner. `[PENDING — Step 5]`

### CX-02: Per-Store Status ↔ Rejection Triage

**Relationship**: `Rejected` is the only state on the board that is a door rather than a fact. Every other state is something to wait through; a rejection is something to act on. Triage owns everything past that door: classification, location, translation, the fix route, and the redelivery.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees the rejection on the board, acts through triage. Needs to know immediately whether this is theirs to fix or ours.
- **Producer**: **the routing target for asset rejections**, and the reason their access on triage is Config rather than read-only. An asset rejection relayed through an artist who cannot re-render a master is a dead end, and per personas.md the Producer will not tolerate a flow that drags them out of the session to receive it.
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **status object** is owned by 12.03.02; triage reads it and drives transitions through redelivery rather than by writing status directly. One writer, which matters because a status the artist cannot trust is worse than no status.
2. **Trigger chain**: `Rejected` → classify → locate → notify the right person → fix → redeliver → back to `Sent` (CX-06). Multi-day, multi-actor, and it can fail at the classify step, which is why "unlocatable" is an explicit state rather than an error.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — the fix route determines the actor. Metadata → Musician; asset → Producer; rights/identifier → Musician plus possibly an external counterparty. Triage is effectively a router over three different permission worlds, which is unusual and worth flagging to spec writers.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Rejection is one of only two states worth interrupting an artist for (the other is `Overdue`). Critically, the notification must reach **the person who can fix it**, not the release owner by default. Getting this wrong turns the Producer's read-only status view into the artist's second job.
5. **State transition conflict**: A rejection can arrive *after* the artist has already fixed the same problem for another store — the fix is in flight when the second rejection lands. Triage must recognise an in-flight remediation and not ask twice. Related: a rejection can arrive after the release date, when the artist is already promoting a record that is not on Apple.

### CX-03: Rejection Triage ↔ Store Selection (the escape hatch)

**Relationship**: There are two ways out of a rejection — comply, or leave. Dropping the store is a legitimate resolution (12.03.01 DT-03), and treating it as a failure would be paternalistic. But it is not symmetric: dropping a store *before* delivery is free, while dropping it *after* is a takedown (12.05.01) with a live-release footprint.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: makes the trade. Must see what it costs — reach, revenue — stated honestly rather than framed as a mistake.
- **Producer / Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Triage does not write the selection set; it routes the artist back to 12.03.01 to change it themselves. Deliberate — a system that silently dropped stores to clear its own error state would be the worst possible behaviour here.
2. **Trigger chain**: Rejection → artist chooses to drop → selection changes → status row for that store is removed (pre-delivery) or becomes a takedown (post-delivery). The pre/post-delivery asymmetry is the whole risk in this pair.
3. **Permission intersection**: Selection is Musician-only, so a Producer facing an asset rejection **cannot** take the escape hatch. Correct — dropping a store is a commercial decision — but it means the Producer's only route is to fix it, which should shape how that ask is framed to them.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Dropping a store post-delivery should notify contributors, since it changes where a record they are credited on is sold. Pre-delivery, it is nobody's business but the owner's.
5. **State transition conflict**: Dropping a store while its redelivery is in flight. The in-flight delivery must be cancelled, not left to land on a store the artist has already left — otherwise the release goes live somewhere it was just withdrawn from.

### CX-04: Artist Profile Linking ↔ Per-Store Status

**Relationship**: The most important pair in the sub-domain, and the reason 12.03.04 exists at all. Status answers "did it arrive?"; linking answers "did it arrive *at the right person*?" Status cannot see the difference — a release on a stranger's page is `Live` by every measure the board has. The board is therefore capable of reporting complete success while the artist's work sits in someone else's discography and their fans hear nothing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees green, sees no streams, and has no way to connect the two without this feature. Blames the algorithm, the promo, or themselves.
- **Producer**: often the first to notice, because they see the pattern across many releases rather than one.
- **Fan**: **the most affected and the least visible**. Their follow is attached to the DSP's artist entity; a wrong page means the alert→discovery line personas.md defines as their success criterion breaks silently. No party can see that it broke.
- **Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **store artist ID** is owned by identity (01), not by either feature here. Both read it. That placement (12.03.04 DT-02) is what makes linking survive across releases and across a multi-hyphenate's several identities.
2. **Trigger chain**: Delivery → store matches → post-delivery verification compares the landed page against the linked ID → mismatch raises an alert that status alone would never produce. Without that verification step the failure is undetectable by the platform, and detectable by the artist only through absent streams.
3. **Permission intersection**: Linking is Musician-Full because it is an identity assertion; status is broader. A featured artist cannot link themselves on someone else's release — their ID must come from their own identity, which is why the feature only works if 01 owns the links.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A mismatch must reach the artist immediately and honestly, including the ugly part: recovery is a per-store support process taking weeks, and we cannot promise it. This is also the one place domain 12 owes domain 20 a signal — a wrong page invalidates fan reach, and 20 currently has no way to know.
5. **State transition conflict**: A store can merge or split artist pages **on its own**, without telling anyone, invalidating a stored ID for a release that is already live and correct. Linking is therefore not a one-time act but a fact requiring periodic re-verification. `[PENDING — Step 5]`

## Rejected Pairs

| R-01 | 12.03.01 Store Selection | 12.03.03 Rejection Triage (rule learning) | Considered: should a rejection auto-deselect the offending store? Rejected outright — the platform silently dropping a store to clear its own error state would be a betrayal of the artist's commercial intent, and would do it invisibly. | R-02 | 12.03.04 Artist Profile Linking | 12.03.01 Store Selection (territory) | No relationship. An artist's page identity and a release's territory rights are orthogonal — the same page serves every territory, and no territory constraint depends on which page a release lands on. |
| R-03 | 12.03.04 Artist Profile Linking | 12.03.03 Rejection Triage | Considered because a wrong-page landing feels like something triage should catch. Rejected: a wrong page produces **no rejection** — the store accepts happily and reports success. That is exactly what makes it dangerous (12.03.04 DT-03), and routing it through triage would model it as a partner refusal, which it is not. It needs its own detection path (post-delivery verification, CX-04), not triage's. The one genuine exception — a store rejecting a delivery for an *invalid* artist ID — is an ordinary metadata rejection and needs no special edge. |
| R-04 | 12.03.02 Per-Store Status | 12.03.01 Store Selection (post-delivery change) | Considered as a direct edge for "change where this live release is sold". Rejected: that is not a selection edit, it is a takedown or a new delivery, and it belongs to 12.05 (catalog lifecycle). Modelling it as an edge here would let a live release's footprint be changed by a picker, which is precisely the pre/post-delivery confusion CX-03 warns about. |
