# Feature: Metadata Validation & Store Conformance

> **Number**: 12.01.02
> **Parent**: [Release Builder & Delivery Readiness](./12.01-release-builder-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The rule engine that checks a release's metadata against both the DDEX schema and each target
store's additional, undocumented and mutually contradictory house rules — before delivery rather
than after rejection. Metadata rejections are the most common distribution failure and the most
demoralising, because they arrive days later, from a partner the artist has no relationship with,
in language nobody outside a distributor understands.

Two things make this more than a linter. First, **it is where the platform's accumulated partner
knowledge becomes visible to a human** — the rule packs it reads are the same artifact the DDEX
generator reads (12.02.02 D-01), and the thing every rejection teaches (12.03.03 DT-01). Second,
**it is a provenance capture point disguised as a compliance check**: a musician typing
`Song (feat. Jamie)` into a title field is telling the platform about an uncredited human, and
this is the only feature positioned to notice (DT-05).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees every finding inline on the field that caused it, in plain language ("Apple rejects ALL-CAPS titles" — not `ERR_TITLE_CASE_4021`), with a concrete compliant value they can accept in one click. Fixes and re-validates without leaving the builder. Sees which specific stores each finding affects, so they can drop a store rather than comply (12.03.01 D-03). Can **override any finding whose rule WeJammin inferred rather than read** (D-06), at a stated cost. Cannot override a finding sourced from the DDEX schema or a partner's published spec — there is nothing to be right about. |
| Producer | Config | On a release they do not own: sees the full finding list read-only (they already read the composition per 12.01.01), and can **act on findings attached to recordings from sessions they ran** — version/subtitle ("Radio Edit", "Live at X"), remixer credit, instrumentation and contributor roles, explicit declaration, declared vs actual duration. Cannot act on release-level fields (title, primary artist, release date, label copy). **Config is additive authority, not a lock** — the Musician can always fix these fields too (D-05). Rationale: the Producer knows the recording's truth; the Musician owns the product and must never be unable to ship. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. A venue/studio operator holds no metadata fact about a recording. A live album tracked in their room reaches this feature as a recording contributed by a Musician — the room's name appears in a version field ("Live at X") authored by the Musician or Producer, never by the Operator. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and there is no degraded view. The Fan encounters only the *consequence* of bad metadata — the wrong artist page, the missing credit, the release that isn't searchable — on the DSP, months later, with no idea a validator existed. |

## Behavior

### The three severities

The distinction matters more than the rules themselves, because it is the difference between
"you have work to do" and "you might regret this".

| Severity | Meaning | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Blocking** | Will be rejected by a selected store, or violates the DDEX schema | Delivery to the affected store(s) is impossible until fixed or overridden per D-06 |
| **Warning** | Accepted, but degrades outcome — no editorial eligibility, poor algorithmic placement, likely wrong-artist-page | Delivery allowed; the warning and the decision to ignore it are recorded |
| **Advisory** | Style/consistency; the release will be fine | Informational; never gates anything |

**Findings are per-store scoped; there is no global `valid` flag** (D-01). A finding always names
the stores it affects and the store set is the currently-selected one (12.03.01). Deselect
Beatport and every Beatport-only finding withdraws.

### The rule taxonomy — what the engine must be able to express

Rule *instances* are versioned data, not code (D-02) — they change without notice and are seeded
and corrected by the learning loop (12.03.03 DT-01). The **rule classes** below are exhaustive for
this feature and are what the engine must express. Each class is per-store except where noted.

| # | Class | What it constrains | Representative real divergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| RC-01 | **Schema conformance** | DDEX ERN required fields, types, cardinality | Not per-store — universal, and the only class that is machine-readable from a published spec |
| RC-02 | **Character set & script** | Permitted characters; emoji; control characters; mixed-script and homoglyph detection | Emoji in titles broadly rejected; script rules differ per partner |
| RC-03 | **Casing & typography** | ALL-CAPS, title case, spacing, punctuation | Apple's style guide requires title case; Beatport is permissive |
| RC-04 | **Structural placement** | *Which field a fact belongs in* | The featured-artist split: `(feat. X)` in the title vs `X` in the artist list. Partners disagree, and both renderings derive from one structured fact (DT-05) |
| RC-05 | **Vocabulary & taxonomy** | Genre lists, contributor-role vocabularies, language and script codes | Beatport's electronic taxonomy and Apple's genre list do not map cleanly onto each other; some values have no target at all |
| RC-06 | **Required-beyond-spec** | Fields optional in DDEX that a partner mandates | Beatport requires a mix/version name where Apple wants the field empty |
| RC-07 | **Prohibited content** | Promo text, URLs, prices, "out now", chart claims, keyword stuffing | Universally banned, differently worded, differently enforced |
| RC-08 | **Declaration flags** | Explicit/clean, live, instrumental, remaster year, compilation | Explicit declaration is required by major partners; a mis-declaration is a removal, not a rejection |
| RC-09 | **Length & cardinality** | Field length caps, max artist count, track count and duration per release type | Release-type boundaries differ per partner (12.01.01 DT-03) |
| RC-10 | **Cross-field consistency** | Declared duration vs asset-derived duration; release year vs (P) year; version string vs version field | The one class that reads an asset-derived value — it is a metadata rule, not a coupling to 12.01.04 (12.01 CX R-01) |
| RC-11 | **Referential** | ISRC/UPC/ISNI *format*; presence of a linked artist profile | Format lives here; **collision** is 12.07's, **profile linking** is 12.03.04's |
| RC-12 | **Territory & date** | Date formats, per-partner embargo and lead-time rules | Lead-time *policy* is owned by 12.04.01; this class only checks conformance of what 12.04.01 decided |

### A rule's severity is bounded by its evidence

This is the load-bearing rule of the feature and it follows directly from the domain's own ratified
policy — **never claim more than you know**. A rule WeJammin *inferred* from a rejection is a
hypothesis. Hard-blocking a release on a hypothesis is the platform asserting knowledge it does
not have, on the most operational screen in the domain, in a product whose entire thesis is that
its record is true (D-18).

| Rule provenance | Severity ceiling | Overridable by the artist? |
|---|---|---|
| DDEX ERN schema (published, machine-readable) | Blocking | No — the message would not parse |
| Partner's published spec / style guide | Blocking | No |
| Partner's written instruction to WeJammin (email, certification) | Blocking | No |
| Inferred, confirmed by **2+ independent rejections on 2+ distinct releases**, human-reviewed | Blocking (on promotion) | **Yes** — D-06 |
| Inferred from **1 rejection**, human-reviewed | Warning (never blocking) | n/a — warnings never block |
| Inferred, **not yet human-reviewed** | Not live at all — per 12.03.03 D-03, humans confirm before a rule goes live | n/a |
| WeJammin house style / cross-track consistency | Advisory only | n/a |

**Promotion and decay.** A rule promotes from Warning to Blocking on its 2nd independent
confirming rejection, subject to the human review 12.03.03 D-03 already requires. It decays the
other way on counter-evidence: **3 successful deliveries that the rule would have flagged, and
that the partner accepted**, demotes a Blocking inferred rule to Warning and retires a Warning
entirely. *(Initial values — 2 to promote, 3 to demote. Tunable from real rejection data; see
Q-04. They are stated rather than deferred so nothing downstream is blocked on picking them.)*

**Why this matters, and why it is not decoration**: a rejection teaches you about a *missing*
rule. **Nothing teaches you about a wrong one** — an over-strict blocking rule stops valid releases
silently and forever, and generates no signal, because the release that would have disproved it
was never sent. D-06's override is the only falsification channel this system has. Without it the
rule pack is unfalsifiable, which is the same defect as an unattested credit: a claim with no
mechanism to be wrong (DT-06).

### Per-store divergence: derived renderings, not artist labour

When a selected store's rule and the artist's canonical value conflict, the platform proposes the
compliant value rather than demanding the artist retype it. Three mechanisms, in order of
preference:

1. **Derived rendering** (D-04) — the transformation is deterministic and the platform knows the
   compliant value exactly: title-casing an ALL-CAPS title, moving `(feat. Jamie)` out of the title
   into the artist list for a partner that wants it there, mapping a genre onto a partner's
   taxonomy. The artist approves it once; it applies per-store at delivery. **The canonical value
   they typed is never altered** — the rendering is an accommodation, recorded as a divergence,
   the same pattern 12.01.05 uses for label copy.
2. **Drop the store** (12.03.01 D-03) — a legitimate, costed choice. An artist who would rather
   skip Beatport than strip their title's capitals is making a real decision about their record.
   Show the reach cost; do not moralise.
3. **Artist-authored variant** — a genuinely different value per store. Not resolvable here; see
   Q-01.

A derived rendering is **proposed, never silently applied** (DT-07). Silently rewriting an artist's
title is precisely what the DSPs already do to them, and doing it ourselves while claiming to hold
the true record would be self-refuting.

### When validation runs, and what it is pinned to

| Moment | Rule pack version used | Effect of a rule-pack update |
|---|---|---|
| While authoring (pre-gate) | Current, live; **pinned per validation run** so one run's findings are internally consistent | Findings re-compute; new blocking findings can appear on untouched work |
| Gate-green, awaiting scheduled delivery | Current, live — validation is **not** pinned yet | The release falls back to Blocked **on the affected store only**; the artist is notified (see edge cases) |
| At hand-off to the DDEX layer (12.02.01 requests the profile) | **Pinned** — to the same partner-knowledge-store version the profile pins to (12.02.02 D-02) | None. In-flight deliveries complete against the pinned version |
| After hand-off / in flight | Pinned | None. If the new rule was right, we learn it from the real rejection (12.03.03) |
| After the release is live | n/a | A new rule affecting a live release is a 12.05.02 update-and-redelivery decision, not a validation state |

**This resolves the pinning asymmetry** flagged as unresolved in the domain CX file (CX-02,
synthesis Q5): profiles pin per delivery, validation appeared to pin to nothing. The asymmetry was
an artifact of treating them as two things. They are two projections of one partner-knowledge
store (12.02.02 D-01) — **pinning one pins both, at hand-off, to one version** (D-07). Before
hand-off both are live; after it both are frozen; they cannot disagree because there is only one
version number.

### Validation as a provenance capture point

Structural-placement rules (RC-04) require the builder to hold featured artists as **structured
data**, not as a string inside a title — because `(feat. Jamie)` and a Spotify-shaped artist list
are two renderings of one fact and neither can be derived from free text. The consequence is the
most valuable thing this feature does and no competitor does it: **when a musician types `feat.
Jamie` into a title field, the platform has just been told about a contributor who may hold no
credit record.** The finding that extracts it into structure is also a route into 02 (credits) and,
if Jamie holds a share, into the readiness gate (12.01.03). See DT-05.

### Happy Path

1. Musician composes the release (12.01.01); stores default to all certified (12.03.01).
   Validation runs continuously in the background from the first field.
2. Fields validate **on blur, never on keystroke** — a validator that argues mid-word is a
   validator people disable. Findings for a changed field surface within **500ms P95** of blur and
   never steal focus. *(Feeds `/create-prd-compile`'s performance budget.)*
3. Findings appear inline on the offending field, grouped by severity — Blocking first — each
   tagged with the stores it affects and each carrying a one-line plain-language rule statement.
4. Musician clicks a finding; the builder focuses the field, explains the rule in plain language,
   and shows a **concrete compliant value**.
5. Where the transformation is deterministic, one click accepts it as a per-store derived rendering
   (D-04); the canonical value is left alone and the divergence is recorded.
6. The finding clears live. Fixing a release-level field re-validates the whole release; fixing a
   track-level field re-validates that track only.
7. Zero Blocking findings across the selected store set → the release advances to the Delivery
   Readiness Gate (12.01.03). Validation must be clean **before** the gate is presented — chasing a
   co-writer for a confirmation on a release that will bounce for a casing rule spends the one
   favour the artist gets to ask (12.01 CX-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two selected stores have contradictory rules for one field | Resolved by derived rendering (D-04) where deterministic; otherwise the artist drops a store or hits Q-01 | "Apple wants this title-cased; Beatport doesn't care. We'll send each what it wants — your title stays as you wrote it." |
| Contradiction is **not** deterministically resolvable | No rendering exists; the artist must choose | "Apple and Beatport want different things here and we can't split the difference. Change it, or drop Beatport (−12% of your reach)." |
| Rule pack is stale — store rejects something that validated clean | Rejection returns via 12.03.03, proposes a rule, human-reviews it; the rule enters as a **Warning** (1 rejection ⇒ warning ceiling) | "Apple rejected this for a rule we didn't know about. We've updated our checks — here's the fix." |
| **Rule pack update invalidates a gate-green release awaiting scheduled delivery** | Release falls back to **Blocked on the affected store only**; unaffected stores stay on schedule. Artist is notified immediately — this is interrupt-worthy, being the pre-emptive form of a rejection. If unresolved by that store's lead-time cutoff (12.04.01), the store is dropped from the wave and delivered late rather than delaying the release | "Beatport changed a rule and your release no longer passes it. Fix it by Thu and Beatport ships with everyone else; after that Beatport goes ~3 days late. Nothing else is affected." |
| Rule pack updates **mid-validation-run** | Run is pinned to the version it started with; a superseded run is discarded and re-run rather than merged — mixed-version findings would contradict each other within one panel | Brief `Checking…` re-enters; no flicker of contradictory findings |
| Rule pack updates **after hand-off**, delivery in flight | No effect — validation and profile are pinned to one version (D-07) | Nothing |
| Rule pack update affects an **already-live** release | Not a validation state; routes to 12.05.02 as an update-and-redelivery decision with its cost shown | "A Beatport rule changed. Your live release still works. Updating it means a redelivery — here's what that costs." |
| Musician removes a store to dodge a finding | Allowed and recorded; the store's findings withdraw | Finding greys out: "no longer applies — Beatport removed" |
| Musician **overrides an inferred Blocking rule** (D-06) | Delivery proceeds to that store. Outcome is the falsification signal: accepted ⇒ counter-evidence toward decay; rejected ⇒ confirming evidence toward promotion | "We think Beatport will reject this — we've seen it twice, but we've never seen their rulebook. If we're wrong you lose nothing. If we're right, Beatport runs ~3 days behind. Send it anyway?" |
| Musician tries to override a **documented** Blocking rule | Refused. There is nothing to be right about — the message would not parse, or the partner published the rule | "This one isn't a guess — it's in Apple's spec. It has to change." |
| `feat. Jamie` typed inside the title string | Extracted to structured featured-artist data (RC-04); routed to 02 as a possible uncredited contributor (DT-05) | "Featured artists go in their own field — some stores need them there. Is this Jamie Okafor? They're not credited on this track yet." |
| Featured artist named in the title is **not resolvable to any person** | Extraction still happens (the field placement rule is independent); no credit route is offered | "We've moved 'feat. Jamie' into the featured-artist field. Add Jamie to the credits if they played on it." |
| Emoji or control characters in title/artist | Blocking on stores with a documented character rule; Warning elsewhere (inferred) | "Most stores strip emoji from titles — Apple rejects them outright." |
| **Homoglyph / mixed-script artist name** (Cyrillic `а` inside a Latin name) | Warning on validation grounds + a Trust & Safety signal to 24 — this is the impersonation vector, and validation is the only place it is mechanically detectable | "This name mixes Latin and Cyrillic characters. That usually breaks store search — and it can look like impersonation." |
| Promo text, URL, price, or chart claim in a field | Blocking where documented, Warning where inferred | "'OUT NOW' can't go in a title — every store rejects promotional text." |
| Keyword stuffing in the version field | Warning; not mechanically separable from a legitimate long version string | "'(Remix Deep House 2026 Club Mix Extended)' reads as keyword stuffing. Editorial teams see it too." |
| Title is **legitimately** ALL-CAPS (stylised artist/band name) | The false-positive case, and the reason D-06 exists. Inferred casing rules are overridable; documented ones require the artist to use the partner's own stylisation exception path | "Apple title-cases everything unless the stylisation is your registered name. Send as-is, or let us title-case it just for Apple?" |
| Genre has **no target** in a store's taxonomy (RC-05) | Warning, never blocking — a genre-less release is deliverable but editorially invisible | "Beatport has no 'Shoegaze'. We'll send 'Rock' unless you'd rather pick — with no genre you won't be considered for their editorial." |
| Explicit flag not declared | Blocking (documented, RC-08) | "You have to tell the stores whether this is explicit. Getting it wrong gets releases pulled, so we can't guess." |
| Explicit flag declared, accuracy unknown | The platform **cannot verify** it and must not imply it did — it holds no lyrics fact | Declaration is shown as the artist's, not as verified: "You've declared this clean." |
| A "clean" version added as the same recording | Blocked at composition (12.01.01 — a clean edit is a **distinct recording** needing its own ISRC); validation restates the consequence at the field | "The clean version is a different recording. It needs its own ISRC — we'll assign one at delivery." |
| Cover song with no writer credit / no mechanical licence | Warning here; the licence itself is 11's and the clearance block is the gate's (12.01.03) | "Covers need the original writers credited. The licence side is in Licensing — that'll block delivery separately." |
| Declared duration ≠ asset-derived duration (RC-10) | Blocking on partners that check; the value is asset-derived but the rule is metadata's | "You've declared 3:42; the file is 3:58. Apple checks this." |
| Producer has left the project; a finding sits on a Producer-authoritative field | **The Musician fixes it themselves.** Config is additive, not exclusive (D-05) — otherwise a departed collaborator makes a release permanently unshippable, and CX-02's ratified premise ("a validation failure is fixable by the author alone") breaks | Nothing unusual. The field is editable; the Producer's prior value, if any, is shown as theirs |
| Musician **is** the Producer (multi-hyphenate, own release) | No conflict. Validation is a machine check — counter-attestation has no meaning here, which is exactly why it is separate from the gate (12.01 CX-02) | One lens, no role switch, no prompt |
| Producer and Musician edit the same track concurrently | Last write wins **per field**, not per record; the loser is told what changed and by whom rather than losing keystrokes | "Sam changed the version field to 'Radio Edit' while you were editing." |
| Producer views a release they contributed one track to | Finding list is scoped: they act only on recordings from their sessions. Release-level findings are visible read-only (they already read the composition per 12.01.01) but carry no action | Their track's findings are actionable; the rest are greyed with "the release owner handles this" |
| A finding references a track removed concurrently | Finding **withdraws immediately**. A stale finding on a track that no longer exists is the same trust leak as a stale nudge (12.01 CX-01) | Finding disappears; no tombstone, no explanation needed |
| Store is suspended (12.02.02) while its findings are live | Findings withdraw with the store, but are **retained not deleted** — the store returns and so do they | "Beatport is unavailable right now — not your release, their end. Its 2 findings are parked." |
| A **new store** is certified after the release validated clean | New findings can appear on untouched work if the artist's store set defaults to all-certified | "Deezer just came online and wants one thing changed. Add it, or leave it out." |
| Compilation exceeds a partner's distinct-primary-artist cap | Blocking (RC-09); the partner requires a "Various Artists" primary | "Apple wants 4+ different lead artists filed under 'Various Artists'. Switch, or drop to 3 leads." |
| Release has zero tracks | Nothing to validate; not an error | Empty state — validation is simply absent, not "passing" |
| Validation service unreachable | Delivery blocked, **editing never blocked**. Last-known findings shown with an explicit staleness timestamp. Auto-retry 3× with exponential backoff (2s, 8s, 32s), then a 5-minute poll | "We can't check against store rules right now — these results are from 14:02. Your work is saved; we'll re-check automatically." |
| Client is offline mid-edit | Edits queue locally; validation is server-side by construction (rule packs are data, D-02) and cannot run offline | Findings freeze with a stale marker; no false green |
| Only *some* rule packs resolve | Partial — the release is deliverable to the stores that checked out, blocked on the rest. Never presented as clean | "Checked 11 of 12 stores — Beatport's rules are unavailable. We won't send there until we can check." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Release has no tracks and no metadata | Validation absent entirely — no panel, no green tick. Nothing has passed, so nothing claims to |
| Checking | A validation run is in flight | Inline `checking…` on affected fields only. **Never a blocking spinner over the form** — the artist keeps typing |
| Clean | Zero findings across the selected store set | "Passes all checks for {N} stores" — scoped to the selection, never a global `valid` |
| Populated | Findings exist | Grouped panel: Blocking → Warning → Advisory, each scoped to the stores it affects, each with a compliant example and (where deterministic) a one-click rendering |
| Partial | Some store rule packs unresolved | "Checked {N} of {M} stores — {store}'s rules are unavailable" + which stores are consequently un-deliverable |
| Stale | Last run succeeded but the service is now unreachable | Findings shown with an explicit "as of {time}" marker and an auto-retry indicator. Green never implies fresh |
| Pinned | Handed off to the DDEX layer (12.02.01) | Findings frozen at the handed-off version; the panel reads "sent against rule pack {v}, {date}" — this is the record that makes "why did we send that?" answerable |
| Invalidated | A rule-pack update broke a previously-clean, gate-green release | Amber, store-scoped: "{store} changed a rule — this no longer passes for them. Everyone else is unaffected" + the lead-time cutoff |
| Locked | Release is live on 1+ stores | Read-only findings; edits route to 12.05.02 (update & redelivery) with the cost shown |
| Error | Validation service down and no prior run exists | Non-destructive banner + auto-retry. Delivery blocked; editing untouched |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Validation can be a single global ruleset with a `valid` boolean | Simplest model | ❌ REJECTED — partner rules genuinely contradict each other. A global boolean forces the union of all rules (over-strict, blocks valid deliveries) or the intersection (under-strict, guarantees rejections). Findings must be per-store scoped, and the delivery decision must be per-store too — which is exactly why 12.03 tracks status per store rather than one `released` flag. |
| DT-02 | Rule packs must be data, versioned and hot-updatable — not code | Partner rules change silently and without changelogs; the rejection feedback loop from 12.03.03 must be able to *write a new rule* | ✅ CONFIRMED — this makes the distributor's hardest-won asset (accumulated rejection knowledge) a first-class product artifact rather than tribal knowledge. It is also a compounding moat: every rejection any WeJammin user hits improves validation for all of them. |
| DT-03 | Error messages can pass through the partner's own text | Cheapest implementation | ❌ REJECTED — DSP rejection text is written for distributors, not artists (`DELIVERY_ERR: invalid PLine format`). Per `specificity-standards.md` and the Musician persona (not a metadata professional), every finding must be translated to plain language with a compliant example. Untranslated partner errors are the reason artists believe distribution is a black box. |
| DT-04 | A rule's severity is a property of the rule | The natural model — rules carry a severity field | ❌ REJECTED — severity is a function of **(rule, evidence, store selection)**. Store selection is obvious (a Beatport rule is severity-zero if Beatport isn't selected). Evidence is not, and is the important half: a rule WeJammin *inferred* from one rejection and a rule WeJammin *read in Apple's published spec* are not the same kind of object, and treating them identically means hard-blocking releases on guesses. This is the domain's own ratified "never claim more than you know" pattern, applied to the platform's knowledge of its partners rather than to its knowledge of delivery state. |
| DT-05 | Validation is only a compliance check | Its obvious job, and how every competitor scopes it | ❌ REJECTED — **it is a provenance capture point, and a free one.** Structural-placement rules (RC-04) force featured artists into structured data, which means the moment a musician types `(feat. Jamie)` into a title the platform has learned about a human who may hold no credit record. Per problem-statement.md the root cause is *absence at the point of truth* — and this is a point of truth nobody identified as one, in a feature everybody builds. It is small, cheap, and only WeJammin can act on it, because only WeJammin has a credits graph to route it into (D-10). Every distributor strips `feat.` from titles; none of them notices it names a person. |
| DT-06 | The rule-learning loop (12.03.03 DT-01) is sufficient — rules get better over time | The loop is real and 12.03.03 confirmed it | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **the loop learns but cannot unlearn.** A rejection is evidence of a *missing* rule; nothing whatsoever is evidence of a *wrong* one. An over-strict inferred blocking rule stops valid releases silently and permanently, and produces no signal, because the delivery that would have disproved it was never sent. 12.03.03 DT-03 correctly priced a false blocking rule as more expensive than a missing one, then specified no mechanism by which one is ever detected or removed. The gap is structural: an unfalsifiable rule pack has the same defect as an unattested credit — a claim with no way to be wrong. D-06's artist override is the only falsification channel available, which is what makes it a correctness requirement rather than a courtesy. |
| DT-07 | Where the compliant value is deterministic, just apply it and deliver | It is what the artist wants and what a good distributor does | ❌ REJECTED — silently rewriting the artist's title is precisely what the DSPs do *to* them, and the whole complaint. Doing it ourselves while claiming to hold the true record is self-refuting. Propose it, show the exact before/after, apply on one click, and **never alter the canonical value** — the rendering is a per-store accommodation with a recorded divergence, the same shape 12.01.05 uses for label copy. |
| DT-08 | Producer "Config" means the Producer is the *only* party who can fix recording-level fields | The natural reading of an authority model, and how the SURFACE draft read | ❌ REJECTED — it breaks a ratified premise. 12.01 CX-02 distinguishes validation from the gate on exactly this: "a validation failure is fixable by the author alone; a gate failure requires another human." An exclusive Producer lock would make every validation finding a social dependency, collapsing the distinction the sub-domain is built on, and would make a release permanently unshippable the moment its Producer moved on. Config is **additive authority**: the Producer's word carries weight and their edits are attributed, but the Musician can always act. The counter-attestation asymmetry belongs to the gate, where facts are contested — not here, where a machine is reading a style guide. |
| DT-09 | Validation and profiles pin differently, and the asymmetry needs resolving | Domain CX-02 synthesis Q5 flagged it unresolved | ✅ CONFIRMED and **dissolved rather than resolved** — the asymmetry was an artifact of modelling them as two things. 12.02.02 D-01 already made them two projections of one partner-knowledge store; a store has one version number, so pinning one pins both. They pin together at hand-off (D-07). This is a small structural payoff from a decision made for a different reason, and it is worth noting *why* it worked: the drift CX-02 feared and the pinning asymmetry it flagged were the same problem, and the single-store decision solved both at once. |
| DT-10 | DSPs reject bad metadata | The premise of the entire feature | ⚠️ CONFIRMED BUT INCOMPLETE — **they also silently fix it.** A partner that title-cases your title without telling anyone has not rejected the delivery; it has changed the record. WeJammin reports `Delivered` while the store displays something the artist did not write, and nobody finds out. Every competitor is structurally indifferent to this — their claim is "we sent it", which remains true. WeJammin's claim is that its record is true (D-18), which this quietly falsifies. Two consequences: (a) a silent normalisation is a **learning signal exactly as valuable as a rejection**, and cheaper to obtain, since it tells us the real rule *and* that the partner won't tell us; (b) it is the only channel that can falsify a rule the artist never overrode. Detection requires reading back what the store published, which is 12.03.02's surface to own and not uniformly possible per partner — see Q-03. Flagged as a genuine feasibility unknown rather than assumed. |
| DT-11 | The artist should be protected from knowing which store wants what | Kindness; and 12.02.02's Role Lens is emphatic that partner *profiles* must never reach the artist | ❌ REJECTED for findings, and the distinction is sharp. Hiding the **mechanism** (ERN versions, profile subsets, message dialects) is correct — 12.02.02 is right. Hiding the **attribution** of a finding is not: "your title is invalid" is the black box artists already hate, while "Apple rejects ALL-CAPS titles, Beatport doesn't care" is the fact that lets them make the trade 12.03.01 D-03 explicitly guarantees them. An artist cannot make an informed choice to drop a store without being told which store is asking. Name the partner in the finding; never name the profile. |
| DT-12 | Advisory findings are harmless — worst case they are ignored | They block nothing by definition | ❌ REJECTED — advisories are the tier that destroys the other two. A panel that cries about capitalisation consistency next to "Apple will reject this" trains the artist to dismiss the whole panel, and the dismissal is indiscriminate. Advisories must be collapsed by default and must never share visual weight with a blocking finding. This is the domain's own "different consequences need different affordances" pattern (12.01 CX-02, 12.04.04 DT-02, 12.05 CX-03) surfacing a fourth time — which is now enough occurrences to treat it as domain policy rather than a per-feature preference. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Findings are per-store scoped; no global `valid` flag | Partner rules contradict (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Rule packs are versioned data, not code | Partner rules change without notice; rejections must write rules (DT-02) |
| D-03 | All partner errors are translated to plain language with a concrete compliant example | Musician is not a metadata professional (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Deterministic conflicts resolve as **per-store derived renderings**; the canonical value is never altered | The platform knows the compliant value — demanding the artist retype it is theatre. Recording the divergence rather than overwriting the truth is the same pattern as 12.01.05's label copy (DT-07) |
| D-05 | Producer `Config` is **additive authority, not an exclusive lock** | An exclusive lock breaks 12.01 CX-02's ratified premise that validation is fixable by the author alone, and makes a release unshippable once its Producer leaves (DT-08) |
| D-06 | **A rule's severity is bounded by its evidence.** Inferred blocking rules are overridable by the artist at a stated cost; documented ones are not | The domain's "never claim more than you know" pattern applied to partner knowledge. The override is also the system's only falsification channel — without it the rule pack cannot be wrong, which is the same defect as an unattested credit (DT-04, DT-06) |
| D-07 | Validation and the partner profile **pin together at hand-off**, to one partner-knowledge-store version | Resolves the pinning asymmetry left open in domain CX-02 Q5. One store ⇒ one version ⇒ the two projections cannot disagree (DT-09) |
| D-08 | Featured artists are **structured data**; `feat.` inside a title is a finding that extracts it and routes the person to 02 | Both partner renderings derive from one structured fact, and the extraction is a free provenance capture at a point of truth nobody identified (DT-05) |
| D-09 | Findings name the **partner**, never the **profile** | The artist cannot make 12.03.01 D-03's guaranteed trade without knowing which store is asking; the mechanism stays hidden, the attribution does not (DT-11) |
| D-10 | Advisories are collapsed by default and never share visual weight with blocking findings | A noisy panel is dismissed indiscriminately, taking the blocking findings with it — the domain's "different consequences need different affordances" pattern, fourth occurrence (DT-12) |
