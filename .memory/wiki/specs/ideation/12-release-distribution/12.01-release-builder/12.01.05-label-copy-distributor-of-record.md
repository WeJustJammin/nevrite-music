# Feature: Label Copy & Distributor of Record

> **Number**: 12.01.05
> **Parent**: [Release Builder & Delivery Readiness](./12.01-release-builder-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The metadata that names who owns and administers this release: the (P) line (sound recording
copyright), the (C) line (composition copyright), the label name that appears on the DSP, the
catalog number, and the distributor of record. These are small text fields with disproportionate
consequences — they are what a future acquirer, a CMO, or a court reads to determine who controls
the master, and they are what determines whether the artist can leave WeJammin with their catalog
intact.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets the label name (their own name, a band entity from domain 01, or a real label), the (P)/(C) lines and year, the catalog number if they run a numbered catalog. Sees plainly stated who is named as distributor of record and what that means for leaving. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees the label copy because it affects them — the (P) line names who owns the master they made, and a production agreement may entitle them to points on it. Cannot edit: this is an ownership assertion belonging to the rights holder, not to the person who made the recording. If the Producer disagrees with the (P) line, that is a **rights dispute** (09/24), not a metadata correction. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible in the builder. The Fan sees the (P) line and label name on the DSP after delivery — often the only place a Fan ever encounters this data. |

## Behavior

Label copy is where the release object touches the ownership record (09) and must not contradict
it. The (P) line names the sound recording owner; if 09 says the master is co-owned by a band
entity and the (P) line names one member personally, the release is asserting something the
platform's own records refute. This is exactly the kind of quiet contradiction that becomes
expensive years later — so label copy should be **derived from 09 by default** and require an
explicit, recorded justification to diverge.

The **distributor of record** is the party the DSP holds responsible for the delivery. Naming
WeJammin has real consequences the artist must understand before, not after: it affects who the
DSP talks to, who can issue a takedown, whose ISRC prefix is used, and what happens at exit
(12.08). The industry norm is to bury this; per D-18's requirement that lock-in be *earned*, it
should be stated in plain language at the moment it is set.

Detailed field-level rules: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`.

### Happy Path

1. Builder pre-fills (P)/(C) lines from the recording's ownership record in 09 and the release year.
2. Musician confirms or overrides the label name (defaults to their artist name or a band entity
   from 01).
3. If they override a derived (P) line, they must say why; the divergence is recorded and visible
   to co-owners.
4. Distributor of record is shown with a plain-language statement of what it means, including what
   happens if they leave (links to 12.08).
5. Catalog number auto-increments if a catalog exists, or is left blank.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| (P) line contradicts the ownership record in 09 | Warning + required justification; co-owners notified | "Rights says this master is owned by {band}. You've named yourself. {Co-owners} will see this." |
| Artist signs to a real label mid-rollout | Distributor of record and label copy change → requires redelivery (12.05.02), not an edit | "Changing the label on a live release means redelivering it. Here's what that affects." |
| Musician names a label they do not own | Not mechanically detectable | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — likely a trust/moderation concern (24) rather than a validation rule |
| (C) line names a publisher not in the rights record | Warning; publishing splits are the CMO's business, not the DSP's | "We don't have {publisher} on file for this work." |
| Band entity dissolves after release | Label copy on delivered releases is frozen; future releases need a new decision | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Derived | Pre-filled from 09 | Fields populated with a "from your rights record" affordance |
| Overridden | User diverged | Amber marker + recorded justification, visible to co-owners |
| Locked | Delivered | Read-only; edits route to 12.05.02 |
| Empty | No ownership record in 09 | Blocking — routes to the readiness gate (12.01.03) rather than asking for free text here |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Label copy is free-text metadata like any other field | How every distributor treats it | ❌ REJECTED — the (P) line is an **ownership assertion**, and WeJammin uniquely holds the ownership record that can confirm or refute it (D-10: the platform owns the chain of title end-to-end). Letting it be free text next to an authoritative rights record is a self-inflicted contradiction — the platform would be publishing a claim its own database disputes. Derive by default; diverge only with a recorded reason. This is a small feature that only WeJammin *can* do correctly, precisely because of the thesis. |
| DT-02 | Naming WeJammin as distributor of record is a routine implementation detail | Industry norm — everyone buries it | ❌ REJECTED — it determines who controls the delivery relationship and what leaves with the artist. problem-statement.md Q-02 asks whether lock-in is earned or hostile; burying distributor-of-record is how it becomes hostile *by default and without anyone deciding to*. Stating it plainly at the moment it is set costs one paragraph and is the cheapest possible proof that D-18's lock-in is earned. |
| DT-03 | Label copy could be a domain-level feature rather than living in the builder | Structural alternative considered during classification | ❌ REJECTED — it is metadata authored in the same mental act as the rest of the release. Splitting it out produces a second screen for one intent. It stays in the builder; the *consequences* of the distributor-of-record choice live in 12.08. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([path](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — (P)/(C) derive from the ownership record; divergence is a flag, not a preference (DT-01).
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([path](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — the label/band entity is a first-class org entity, not a string. Per personas.md, "bands, labels, studios are first-class entities that own and get paid independently of their members."
- Touches **12.08 Catalog Migration & Exit** ([path](../12.08-catalog-migration-exit.md)) — distributor of record is the hinge the whole exit question turns on (DT-02).
- Touches **10 Royalties & Collections** ([path](../../10-royalties-collections/)) — the (C) line and publisher data are what CMO registration reads.
- Consumes cross-cut **Contracts & E-Signature** — a distribution agreement backs the distributor-of-record designation.
