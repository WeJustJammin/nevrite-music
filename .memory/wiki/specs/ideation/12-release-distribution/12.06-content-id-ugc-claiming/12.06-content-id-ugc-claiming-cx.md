## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Registration ↔ Claim & Whitelist

**Relationship**: The ordering constraint is the whole of it. Registration is a one-way door that starts an automated claiming machine; the whitelist is the only thing that stops it pointing at people the artist knows. Configure the whitelist after registration and the claims have already landed — on the artist's own official video, on their bandmate's session clip, on their label's channel. Technically undoable in a day; socially undoable never.

The strong version, from 12.06.02 DT-02: most of the whitelist is **derivable** from what the platform already holds — the artist's channels and band (01), the recording's credited contributors (02), the label (12.01.05). Competitors make the artist reconstruct this from memory after the damage. This is the being-in-the-room asymmetry applied to a mundane list, and it is the cheapest demonstration of the consolidation thesis in the sub-domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: reviews a derived whitelist rather than authoring one from scratch. The review is real work and must not be skippable — a derived list is a good draft, not a decision.
- **Producer**: no access to the whitelist, yet their own session footage is a prime accidental-claim target. They are protected only because they appear in the credits (02) that derive the list — protected by the provenance record rather than by a permission. Worth noting as an unusual and rather elegant shape.
- **Fan**: the claimed party, with no lens into either feature (12.06.02 DT-01, parent Q-02).
- **Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **whitelist** is owned by 12.06.02 and read by the registration flow as a precondition. Its *sources* (01, 02, 12.01.05) are owned elsewhere and only read — meaning the whitelist is a projection that can go stale when a band's line-up changes or a contributor is added late (12.05.02 DT-02). Whether it re-derives is `[PENDING — Step 5]` and matters: a credit added post-release should plausibly whitelist that person retroactively.
2. **Trigger chain**: Derive whitelist → artist reviews → register → claims begin, filtered. If registration proceeds without a reviewed whitelist, the failure is immediate and social. Registration must hard-require it, not merely suggest it.
3. **Permission intersection**: Musician-only on both. The Producer's exclusion from the whitelist while being read-only on registration means they can *see* the door about to open and cannot position themselves behind it — an odd asymmetry that Step 5 should look at.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Registration should notify contributors — a record they played on is about to start claiming videos, possibly theirs. Nobody does this, and it is nearly free given the credits already exist.
5. **State transition conflict**: A contributor added to the credits *after* registration (the late-credit path, 12.05.02 DT-02) is not on the derived whitelist and may already have been claimed. The two features' timelines diverge and nothing currently reconciles them.

### CX-02: Claim & Whitelist ↔ Disputes & Conflicts

**Relationship**: Disputes are claims that should not have been made, arriving from the far side. The artist's whitelist decisions and the dispute queue are two views of one question — who should not be claimed — separated only by whether we got there first. Every dispute is a whitelist entry the platform failed to derive, which makes the dispute queue a feedback signal on the whitelist's quality, exactly as rejections are a feedback signal on validation (12.03.03 DT-01).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: acts on both. Releasing a claim should always offer to whitelist the channel, or the same person gets claimed again next month.
- **Producer**: `Config` on disputes because ownership conflicts turn on facts they hold; no access to whitelisting. So they can help settle a fight they could not have prevented.
- **Fan**: a disputing fan is a WeJammin user contesting a WeJammin artist through a third party's process. Nobody has designed for this.
- **Operator**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Claims are the shared entity, owned by the platform partner rather than by us — we hold a projection of their state. Neither feature is authoritative, which means both can be stale and neither can promise an outcome.
2. **Trigger chain**: Claim → disputed → evidence assembled (12.06.03) → response → resolution → whitelist updated so it does not recur. That last step is the one everyone skips, and skipping it guarantees repetition.
3. **Permission intersection**: Releasing a claim (12.06.02) and conceding a dispute (12.06.03) are different acts with the same outcome and different records. Worth keeping distinct — one is a decision, the other is a defeat, and conflating them loses information about which.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Disputes are worth an interruption; claims are not. Claim volume is a firehose, dispute volume is not.
5. **State transition conflict**: A claim released while its dispute is in flight — the artist conceded before the process finished. Harmless in outcome, but the dispute record should show it was withdrawn rather than lost.

### CX-03 / CX-04: Registration ↔ Disputes & Conflicts

**Relationship**: An ownership conflict can precede any claim: two parties register the same audio, and the platform holds both assertions. This is the case where 12.06.03 DT-01 has the most force — for a witnessed recording, the conflict is not a dispute but a question our record answers, and it is answerable *before* anyone loses money rather than after months of frozen revenue.

The back-edge (CX-04) is the unresolved part: an ownership assertion that turns out to be wrong on YouTube is wrong **everywhere**, because it is an assertion about the recording, not about the platform. An unresolved conflict should therefore hold registration on Meta and TikTok too. Whether that is enforceable per-platform is unknown.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees the conflict and our evidence together, rather than a blank dispute form.
- **Producer**: `Config` — their attestation is often what settles it, and per personas.md it is worth more than a self-claim.
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The **ownership assertion** is the shared entity and it lives in 09, not here. Both features project it outward — registration asserts it, conflicts test it. If 09 is incomplete, registration is dangerous and conflicts are unanswerable, which is why 12.06.01's gate is hard (12.06.01 DT-01).
2. **Trigger chain**: Register → platform detects prior registration → conflict → evidence → resolution → registration proceeds or is abandoned. Multi-week, partner-paced, and the artist can do nothing but wait.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Producer's `Config` on disputes and `Read-only` on registration is the right shape — they cannot start the machine, but they can help settle what it collides with.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A conflict must reach the Producer, who frequently holds the deciding fact. Notifying only the release owner discards the platform's best evidence — the same mistake 12.05.03 warns about.
5. **State transition conflict**: Registering on platform B while a conflict is open on platform A (CX-04). The assertion is identical; only the audience differs. Proceeding is arguably making a claim we already know is contested — which is a different and worse thing than making one we believed was sound.
