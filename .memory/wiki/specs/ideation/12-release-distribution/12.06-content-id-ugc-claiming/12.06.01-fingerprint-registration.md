# Feature: Fingerprint Registration

> **Number**: 12.06.01
> **Parent**: [Content ID & UGC Claiming](./12.06-content-id-ugc-claiming-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Registering a recording's audio fingerprint with UGC platforms so their matching systems recognise
it inside other people's videos. Registration is a one-way door: once an asset is in Content ID,
it claims automatically, at scale, against everyone, forever, with no human deciding any
individual case.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Chooses whether to register, per recording, and is told plainly what registration *does* — not what it earns. Sees which platforms the recording is registered with and can withdraw it, though withdrawal does not undo claims already made. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees that a recording they made is registered. Consequential in a way that is easy to miss: if the recording contains a sample they know is not cleared, registration is about to create claims that expose the sample owner's claim against *this* record. The person most likely to know that is the Producer, and they cannot stop it. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — and the party registration acts against. A fan's video is claimed by a machine with no notice to them from this platform. |

## Behavior

Registration submits the master audio and an ownership assertion to each UGC platform's rights
system. The ownership assertion is the dangerous part: **the artist is telling YouTube they own
100% of this recording in these territories.** Getting that wrong does not produce a validation
error — it produces claims against people who actually own it, and eventually a conflict (12.06.03)
or a lost partner relationship.

Per-recording, not per-release: a compilation may contain recordings the artist does not control.

Preconditions that are not optional:

| Precondition | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership is known and complete | The registration asserts it | 09 |
| Samples are cleared | Registering a track with an uncleared sample creates claims *and* exposes the sample owner's claim | 09 / 11 |
| Territory scope is accurate | Claims are territorial | 09 / 12.03.01 |
| The recording is not already registered by someone else | Otherwise: an ownership conflict | 12.06.03 |
| No unresolved recording-ownership conflict exists | Repeating a contested ownership assertion elsewhere expands the harm | 12.06.03 |

**Registration should be gated at least as tightly as delivery** (12.01.03), and arguably more —
a wrong delivery embarrasses you, a wrong registration accuses strangers.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — per-platform registration mechanics. YouTube
Content ID, Meta Rights Manager and TikTok differ substantially, and Content ID access is itself
gated by YouTube on a partner basis, which may make this moot (Q-01).

### Happy Path

1. Release is delivered and live.
2. Musician is offered registration per recording, with a plain statement of what it does.
3. Platform verifies ownership completeness, sample clearance and territory scope from 09.
4. Musician confirms, having been told this claims automatically against everyone.
5. Assets are registered per platform; registration state is tracked per (recording × platform).
6. Claims begin. The whitelist (12.06.02) must be configured *before* this, not after.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recording contains an uncleared sample | Registration would claim revenue on audio partly owned by someone else, and advertise the infringement | **Blocked.** "Track 5's sample isn't cleared. Registering this would claim money that isn't yours and tell them you did." |
| Recording is co-owned; only one owner registers | The asserted 100% is false | Blocked or scoped to their share, per platform capability. `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Someone else already registered the recording | Ownership conflict blocks new registration of that recording on all supported UGC platforms; existing registrations retain their platform-specific state | Routes to 12.06.03; no automatic cross-platform withdrawal |
| Artist registers, then withdraws | Claims already made are **not** undone | "Withdrawing stops new claims. The ones already made stay." |
| Registration claims the artist's own official video | Predictable and avoidable | Whitelist must be set before registration (12.06.02), not after |
| Cover version registered as an original | Claims the composition owner's rights | Blocked; routes to 11 |
| Live recording of a covered song | Same problem, harder to detect | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not registered | Default | Offered, with a plain description of what it does |
| Blocked | Ownership/clearance incomplete | The specific reason and route to fix (09/11) |
| Registering | Submitted | Per-platform progress |
| Registered | Active | Per-platform state + claim activity (12.06.02) |
| Conflicted | Someone else claims the same audio | New registration on every supported UGC platform is blocked; existing platform state is retained and routes to 12.06.03 |
| Withdrawn | Artist withdrew | Honest: new claims stop, old ones stand |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Registration should be gated by the same conditions as delivery | Symmetry with 12.01.03 — both are acts of putting a record into the world | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, **and it needs to be gated harder.** Delivery asserts "here is my record"; registration asserts "I own this, now go take money from anyone who uses it, automatically, forever." A wrong delivery embarrasses the artist; a wrong registration **accuses strangers at machine scale**. The delivery gate's soft-override option (12.01.03 Q-01) should not exist here — there is no equivalent of "the release date is tomorrow" pressure justifying an unverified ownership assertion against third parties, because registration has no deadline. This is the one gate in domain 12 that should be unambiguously hard. |
| DT-02 | Registering a track with an uncleared sample is a rights problem for later | It is the same uncleared-sample issue the gate already flags, so it looks like a known, handled risk | ❌ REJECTED — it is **actively self-incriminating**, which the delivery case is not. Registering fingerprints a recording containing someone else's audio into the very system that scans for it, and then claims revenue on it. The platform would be filing the evidence and the offence together. Per personas.md this is the Musician's *accidental* anti-persona failure ("uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream") reaching its most expensive possible expression — and unlike delivery, the sample owner is likely to be notified automatically. Hard block, no override. |
| DT-03 | Content ID is a revenue feature and should be presented as one | It is how every distributor sells it — "monetise your music on YouTube" | ❌ REJECTED — it is a **claiming** feature that produces revenue as a side effect, and the difference determines whether the artist understands what they just switched on. Framing it as revenue means artists enable it without grasping that they have deputised a machine to accuse people on their behalf — including, per D-11, fans of theirs who are users of this platform (parent Q-02). Present what it *does*, then what it earns. This is a specific instance of the domain's recurring rule (12.05.02 DT-01, 12.04.01 D-01): **wherever the platform knows a cost the artist cannot see, show it at the decision.** Here the cost is not money — it is who they are about to become. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Registration is gated **harder than delivery**, with no override | It accuses strangers automatically and has no deadline pressure to justify one (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Uncleared samples are a hard block | Registration is self-incriminating in a way delivery is not (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Presented as a claiming feature that earns, not a revenue feature that claims | The artist must understand what they are switching on (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Withdrawal is honest: new claims stop, existing ones stand | It is a one-way door and pretending otherwise sets up a worse surprise |
| D-05 | An unresolved recording-ownership conflict blocks new registration on all supported UGC platforms | Existing registrations and claims retain their recorded platform-specific state; cross-platform withdrawal occurs only through a successful, recorded supported remediation |
