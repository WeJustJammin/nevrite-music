# Feature: Claim Disputes & Ownership Conflicts

> **Number**: 12.06.03
> **Parent**: [Content ID & UGC Claiming](./12.06-content-id-ugc-claiming-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens when a claim is contested, or when two parties assert ownership of the same audio.
Content ID's dispute machinery is famously hostile — the claimant adjudicates their own dispute —
and ownership conflicts between rights holders can leave a recording unclaimable by anyone,
generating revenue that goes nowhere while both parties argue.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees disputes against their claims and conflicts over their recordings, and — the part that matters — sees **what evidence WeJammin holds** that bears on it. Responds, escalates, or concedes with the record in front of them rather than from memory. |
| Producer | Config | Contributes the facts that decide most conflicts: who played what, whether a sample was cleared, which master this actually is. Per personas.md their attestation is worth more than a self-claim, and here that asymmetry has money attached. A conflict process that does not reach the Producer is discarding the best evidence available. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — **and a disputing fan is on the other side of this**. A fan who disputes a claim on their video is a WeJammin user in a fight with a WeJammin artist, mediated by YouTube, with our platform on one side. Nobody has designed for this. |

## Behavior

Two distinct situations that Content ID conflates:

**Dispute** — someone claimed says the claim is wrong (fair use, licensed, their own audio). The
claimant reviews their own dispute, which is the design flaw the whole system is known for. Our
role is to give the artist the facts, not to automate a rejection.

**Ownership conflict** — two parties tell the platform they own the same recording. Usually a real
data problem: a prior distributor never released the asset, a label registered a track the artist
also self-registered, a sample owner's fingerprint overlaps. Both parties' claims can be suspended
and the revenue held while it is resolved.

**This is where D-10 pays, again and more concretely than anywhere else.** For a WeJammin-witnessed
recording, an ownership conflict is not two assertions to be refereed — it is a question our
record answers, with attestations from the parties themselves, timestamped at creation. Same
argument as 12.05.03 DT-02, but here it converts directly into money that would otherwise sit
frozen for months.

Boundary worth stating: this only holds for recordings the platform witnessed. Imported catalog
is as opaque to us as to anyone.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — per-platform dispute mechanics and timelines.
These are the partner's processes, not ours, and inventing them would be fabrication.

### Happy Path

> There is no happy path. The least-bad path:

1. A dispute or conflict arrives from the platform.
2. WeJammin assembles what it holds: the ownership record, the attestations, the session record,
   the clearances, the delivery history.
3. Artist sees the dispute alongside the evidence — not a blank form.
4. Where the record answers it, they respond with the record and it ends.
5. Where it does not (imported catalog, third-party claimant), they respond on their own account
   with an honest statement of our limits.
6. Resolution restores claiming, or the claim is dropped and the conflict recorded.
7. While an ownership conflict remains unresolved, new registration of that recording is blocked on
   every supported UGC platform. Existing registrations and claims keep their recorded
   platform-specific state; no automatic cross-platform withdrawal occurs unless that platform's
   supported remediation succeeds and is recorded.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two WeJammin users conflict over the same recording | We hold both sides' attestations. **Most such conflicts should never have happened** | "You both attested this on 3 Mar 2024: 60/40. Here it is." Routes to 24 if they still disagree |
| Conflict on one UGC platform | The ownership assertion may be false everywhere | New registration is blocked across supported UGC platforms; existing registrations retain per-platform state pending recorded remediation |
| Conflict on **imported** catalog | We hold nothing. As blind as any competitor | Honest: "We weren't there for this one. You'll need your own records." The boundary of the thesis, stated |
| Fan disputes a claim on their video | A WeJammin fan against a WeJammin artist, refereed by YouTube | Nobody has designed for this. Parent Q-02 territory |
| Claimant reviews their own dispute and rejects it | The system's known flaw; not ours to fix | State plainly what the artist's real options are |
| Sample owner's fingerprint overlaps ours | Both claims are correct — the audio genuinely contains both | Requires a split at the platform, if it supports one. `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Conflict freezes revenue for months | Common; the money sits with nobody | "This revenue is held until it's resolved. That usually takes months." |
| Artist concedes a conflict they should win | Fatigue. It happens constantly | Show them what we hold before they give up |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Ownership conflicts are a fact of Content ID life and the platform can only relay them | Every distributor's posture, because they hold no evidence and can only pass messages | ❌ REJECTED — for **witnessed** recordings we hold attestations from the parties themselves, timestamped at creation (12.01.03). A conflict between two WeJammin users over a WeJammin-originated recording is not a dispute to be refereed but a question our record already answers. This is problem-statement.md's thesis at its sharpest: the fact was captured *when it was true and free*, and it is being read back at exactly "the moment it becomes contested and valuable". Content ID conflicts freeze real money for months — this is the wedge converting into cash rather than into an argument. |
| DT-02 | This capability applies to WeJammin's whole catalog | Enthusiasm for DT-01 | ❌ REJECTED — it applies **only to what we witnessed**. Imported catalog (12.08) and off-platform masters (12.01.01 Q-02) carry no attestations; for those we are exactly as blind as DistroKid. Saying so plainly is important for two reasons: it stops the product over-promising, and it is a strong argument for the two-tier confidence model 12.01.01 Q-02 raises — a platform that treats witnessed and imported facts identically has quietly diluted the only thing that makes its record worth more than anyone else's. **The boundary is the feature.** |
| DT-03 | We should auto-respond to disputes using our ownership record | The obvious automation once DT-01 is accepted, and it would scale | ❌ REJECTED — auto-responding means the platform asserts ownership on the artist's behalf against a third party, at machine scale, with no human deciding. That is the same one-way-door hazard as registration (12.06.01 DT-01) with a legal edge: an auto-response is a formal assertion in someone else's statutory process. And DT-02 caps it — the record only answers *some* conflicts, so an automation confident enough to be useful would be wrong exactly where we are blind. Surface the evidence to the artist; let them respond. The value is in them not having to remember, not in them not having to decide. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | For witnessed recordings, surface **our record as evidence** rather than refereeing assertions | The wedge cashing out at the contested moment (DT-01) |
| D-02 | State the boundary plainly: imported catalog carries no attestations and we are blind there | The boundary is the feature; over-promising dilutes the record's whole value (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Never auto-respond to a dispute on the artist's behalf | It is a formal assertion in a third party's process, at machine scale, exactly where we may be blind (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Show the artist what we hold **before** they concede | Fatigue-driven concession is the commonest bad outcome, and it is preventable |
| D-05 | An unresolved recording-ownership conflict blocks new registration across supported UGC platforms | Existing registration and claim state remains platform-specific; no automatic cross-platform withdrawal occurs without successful, recorded supported remediation |
