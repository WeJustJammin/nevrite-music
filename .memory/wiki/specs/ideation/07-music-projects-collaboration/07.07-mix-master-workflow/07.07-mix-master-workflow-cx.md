## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Mix Brief ↔ Mastering Workflow

**Relationship**: A reference board is a mastering brief nobody realised they were writing. When an artist says
"make it sound like this record", they have specified — implicitly and quite precisely — a loudness, a tonal
balance and a dynamic range. That information is far more useful to the mastering engineer than to the mix
engineer, and it currently evaporates between the two.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full on the brief; Read-only on mastering. They supply the references and never see them travel.
- **Producer**: both ends. Frequently the person who has to re-explain the artist's references to the mastering engineer from memory.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — the brief is upstream and read-only to mastering.
2. **Trigger chain**: brief references → measured loudness/tonal targets → surfaced to mastering. Passive; nothing breaks if absent.
3. **Permission intersection**: a mastering engineer needs brief visibility, which means a roster role that grants it (`07.03.01`) — currently the mastering profile is the most restricted (`07.03.03`). Real tension: least privilege says give them less, and this says give them the brief.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none.
5. **State transition conflict**: brief changes after mastering started. Rare; advisory.

### CX-02: Alternate Version Matrix ↔ Mastering Workflow

**Relationship**: **The multiplier, and the sub-domain's most expensive interaction.** Every alternate needs a
master, and every alternate's master must be *treated identically to the primary* — same chain, same settings —
or the instrumental audibly does not match the vocal version. The standard industry failure is exactly this: the
instrumental is requested six months later for a sync, mastered separately by whoever is available, and comes back
sounding like a different record. The fix is to master the alternates **at the same time, in the same chain**,
which requires knowing the matrix before mastering starts.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — carries the whole workload.
- **Musician**: Config on the matrix (which alternates exist is partly a commercial decision), Read-only on mastering.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the matrix owns which variants exist; mastering owns each variant's master. Clean.
2. **Trigger chain**: matrix defined → mastering scope known → N masters produced in one pass. A variant **added after** mastering completes triggers a recall — expensive and the exact thing CX-05 tries to prevent.
3. **Permission intersection**: no.
4. **Notification fan-out**: adding a variant post-master notifies the mastering engineer that a recall is needed. This is a costly notification and it should feel like one.
5. **State transition conflict**: the primary master is re-done; every alternate is now stale and must be re-derived. **Lineage (`07.04.01`) is what makes this detectable** rather than discovered by a sync agent in nine months.

### CX-03: Mastering Workflow ↔ Format-Specific Masters

**Relationship**: Format masters derive from the **approved** stereo master. A vinyl pre-master is that master
with vinyl-specific treatment (bass mono'd below a crossover, sibilance controlled, sides length-checked); a DDP
is a container with track marks and gaps; an Apple Digital Master is the same master meeting a delivery spec.
None of them is a new master.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — in practice this is the mastering engineer's work.
- **Musician**: None (Role Matrix) — an artist has no reason to touch a DDP.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — derivatives with lineage.
2. **Trigger chain**: master approved → format masters derived. Master re-done → **all format masters stale** (same shape as CX-02, same lineage-dependency).
3. **Permission intersection**: no.
4. **Notification fan-out**: staleness notifies whoever holds the format deliverable — often a plant or a distributor, i.e. outside the platform.
5. **State transition conflict**: a format master shipped to a plant while the stereo master changed. **This is a real-money failure** — a pressed record cannot be recalled. The pin/resolve rule (`07.04.02` D-03) applies with maximum force: what went to the plant is pinned, forever.

### CX-04: Alternate Version Matrix ↔ Immersive / Atmos

**Relationship**: The load-bearing negative. Atmos is **not** an alternate version of the stereo mix — it is a
different mix, made in a different session, often by a different engineer, with its own creative decisions and its
own credits. Modelling it as a row in the alternate matrix would be a category error (sub-domain D-03), and it
would produce a data model that cannot express the truth: an Atmos mix needs its **own** alternate matrix (an
Atmos instrumental is a real deliverable).

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — usually a specialist Atmos engineer, who is a distinct professional the persona set does not name (sub-domain Q-03).
- **Musician**: None (Role Matrix).
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — separate lineages entirely. That separation *is* the point.
2. **Trigger chain**: none automatic. The stereo mix changing does **not** automatically invalidate the Atmos mix — they are independent works. This surprises people and is correct.
3. **Permission intersection**: an Atmos engineer needs stems (the sensitive class, `07.03.03`), so the least-privilege default does not fit them.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none.
5. **State transition conflict**: stereo and Atmos diverging creatively is **normal and accepted**, not a defect to be reconciled.

### CX-05: Mix Brief ↔ Alternate Version Matrix

**Relationship**: Declaring the required alternates in the brief is dramatically cheaper than requesting them
later — a mixer who knows an instrumental is needed keeps the session structured for it, whereas producing one
nine months later means recalling the session (`07.06.04`), and the recall may not be possible at all
(`07.06.03` DT-01). Medium confidence: real practice, but whether the brief is where this belongs — versus the
service order in domain 05 — is unvalidated.

**Synthesis questions**: deferred to Step 5 per the Medium/Low rule.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Format-Specific Masters | Immersive / Atmos — **as a merge** | Considered one "deliverable formats" feature. Rejected (sub-domain D-03): a DDP is a *container* around an approved master; Atmos is *a different mix*. Merging them models Atmos as a format conversion — the single most common misunderstanding about immersive audio — and would produce a schema in which an Atmos mix cannot have its own credits, its own session, or its own instrumental. The similarity is superficial and the cost of believing it is a wrong data model. |
| R-02 | Mix Brief | Production Stage Board (`07.01.03`) | Considered a `brief approved` stage gate. Rejected for the third time in this domain (see `07.02` R-03, `07.06` R-03): the stage vocabulary stays audio-craft-specific (`07.01` D-02), and briefs are revised continuously throughout a mix rather than approved once. A gate would model a waterfall that does not exist. |
| R-03 | Mastering Workflow | Take & Comp (`07.04.03`) | No relationship. Mastering operates on a finished stereo mix and has no visibility of, or interest in, individual takes — a mastering engineer who has seen the takes is in the wrong job. Independent lifecycles, no shared state, no trigger dependency. |
