# Feature: Immersive / Dolby Atmos Deliverables

> **Number**: 07.07.05
> **Parent**: [Mix & Master Workflow](./07.07-mix-master-workflow-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Spatial audio deliverables — principally Dolby Atmos, delivered as ADM BWF with a binaural render — treated as
what they actually are: **a separate mix, not a format conversion** (sub-domain D-03). An Atmos mix is made in a
different session, frequently by a different engineer, with its own creative decisions, its own credits, and its
own need for alternates.

> ⚠️ Like `07.07.04`, a strong `Won't` candidate on population and spec surface. Unlike `07.07.04`, it has a
> genuine provenance claim — the Atmos engineer is a real, uncredited contributor — which is the only reason the
> node survived the gate at all.

## Behavior

An Atmos deliverable is **its own version lineage**, parallel to the stereo one and deliberately not derived from
it (parent CX-04). It has:

- Its own session (`07.06.01`) and therefore its own attendance and credits
- Its own alternate matrix — an Atmos instrumental is a real deliverable
- A binaural render that is what most listeners actually hear, and that must be checked
- No automatic relationship to stereo master changes: **the stereo mix changing does not invalidate the Atmos mix**, because they are independent works

That last point surprises people and is the crux of sub-domain D-03. Modelling Atmos as a row in the alternate
matrix would make it impossible to express its own credits, its own session, or its own instrumental — a wrong
data model produced by a plausible-sounding simplification.

### Happy Path

1. Artist commissions an Atmos mix; a separate session is created with the Atmos engineer on the roster
2. Engineer works from the stems (`07.04.04`) — **not from the stereo mix** — in their own Atmos-capable room
3. Delivers ADM BWF + binaural render; both ingest as a parallel lineage
4. Session close (`07.06.02`) captures the Atmos engineer's credit — **a credit that today is routinely lost**, which is this feature's only real claim on the domain
5. Delivered via domain 12 alongside the stereo master, independently

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Stereo master re-done | Atmos mix is **not** invalidated — independent works (parent CX-04) | No staleness flag. This is correct and will surprise users, so it must be explained rather than silent |
| Binaural render sounds materially different from the Atmos mix | Real and common; binaural is what most people actually hear. The platform can flag a large divergence but cannot judge it | Advisory only |
| Atmos and stereo diverge creatively (different arrangement decisions) | **Normal and accepted**, not a defect to be reconciled | No flag |
| Atmos delivered as a stereo file mislabelled | Detectable — an ADM BWF has a specific structure | "This doesn't look like an ADM BWF" |
| Artist has no Atmos-capable monitoring and cannot review it | Very common. Binaural render on headphones is the only realistic review path, and it is an imperfect proxy | Binaural render is the reviewable artifact (`07.05.01`) |
| Atmos engineer is not on the platform | The likely default — a small specialist community. Unclaimed identity (`07.03.01` D-02) | Credit as an unclaimed claim awaiting counter-attestation |
| Dolby's spec changes | Third-party spec drift — the same maintenance trap as `07.07.03` DT-03 | Spec as maintained data |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Atmos is a **deliverable format** of the stereo master and belongs in the alternate matrix | The overwhelmingly common assumption, and the one the sweep's candidate list implies by placing it next to DDP and vinyl | ❌ **REJECTED — this is the single most important correction in this sub-domain, and it is a data-model error rather than a scoping one.** An Atmos mix is made **from the stems, in a different session, in a different room, usually by a different engineer**, with different creative decisions (where things sit in space is a choice with no stereo equivalent). It is a *sibling* of the stereo mix, not a child. Modelling it as a variant row (`07.07.02`) would make it impossible to express: its own session, its own credits, its own instrumental, and its independence from stereo revisions. Sub-domain D-03 exists because this misunderstanding is so common that a plausible simplification produces a schema that cannot represent the truth. |
| DT-02 | The Atmos engineer's credit is this feature's real justification | Testing the node against the domain thesis, as `07.07.04` DT-01 did — and getting a different answer | ✅ CONFIRMED — **and it is the only thing keeping this node alive.** Spatial mix engineering is new, specialised, and creatively substantial, and the credit for it is *routinely lost* — the release metadata says who mixed the record, meaning who mixed the *stereo*. That is precisely the class of fact `meta/problem-statement.md` says evaporates: real work, done by a real person, with no system present to record it. So this feature has a genuine provenance claim that `07.07.04` (DDP/vinyl) conspicuously lacks. **But** — and this is the honest part — that credit does not need *this feature*. It needs a **session with a roster** (`07.06.01`, `07.03.01`), which already exists. An Atmos mix could simply be a version in a parallel lineage from an Atmos session, with no dedicated feature at all. The credit is real; the node may not be. |
| DT-03 | Immersive audio is a growing requirement and the platform should be ready for it | The forward-looking argument, and the strongest case for shipping | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — but the timing argument cuts the other way.** Atmos is growing and is a real DSP requirement for a *minority* of releases, concentrated at the label end of the market — which is the end of the market least likely to adopt a new platform first. `meta/problem-statement.md`'s beachhead question (Q-03) matters here: this feature serves almost nobody in the platform's first phase, while its spec surface (ADM BWF, bed/object structure, binaural rendering, Dolby's evolving requirements) is among the largest in the domain. **Being early to a format the target user does not need yet is not readiness; it is misallocation.** The right posture is to make sure the *data model* can express a parallel lineage (DT-01) — which costs nothing now and is expensive to retrofit — and build nothing else until the users ask. Note the asymmetry: the schema decision is cheap-now/expensive-later, the feature is expensive-now/cheap-later. Do the cheap one. |
