## Overview

What the artist actually wants, in the only language that works: other records. A mix brief is a set of reference
tracks with notes ("the drums on this", "the vocal sits like this"), plus whatever can be said in words. It exists
because "make it warmer" means nothing and "like the snare at 0:48 of this" means everything — and because the
references currently live in a WhatsApp thread that the mix engineer scrolls back through for twenty minutes.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Assembles references; annotates specific moments; says what they want. This is their artistic intent, and it is the one document in this sub-domain that is genuinely theirs. For an artist who cannot articulate mix language — most of them — a reference board is the only way they can be precise. |
| Producer | Full | Reads it, works from it, adds to it, translates artist references into craft decisions. Also the recipient: a mix engineer receiving a brief with annotated references starts hours ahead of one receiving "make it punchy". |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A brief is references + annotations + notes. The references are commercial records, and the annotations are
timestamped moments in them ("the snare at 0:48") — the same interaction shape as `07.05.01`'s timestamped review,
applied to someone else's record instead of your own.

That similarity is a real design opportunity and a legal problem at the same time (DT-02): pointing at a moment in
a commercial track requires *playing* that track, and the platform has no licence to stream it.

The brief is **living, not fixed**. It is revised throughout the mix as the artist hears things and refines what
they meant — which is why a `brief approved` stage gate was rejected (parent R-02). Its value is as a shared
reference point, not as a contract.

### Happy Path

1. Artist creates a brief on the song; adds three reference tracks
2. Annotates: "the drums here — that room sound", pointing at 0:48 of a specific record
3. Adds notes: "vocal up front, but not shouty"
4. Mix engineer opens the brief before starting, hears exactly what the artist means at exactly the right moment, and does not have to scroll a WhatsApp thread
5. The brief stays live; the artist adds a reference in week two when they hear something better

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| References are commercial records the platform cannot legally stream | **The central problem** — see DT-02 | Links out to a streaming service, timestamped — never in-platform playback |
| References contradict each other (two records with opposite aesthetics) | Surfaced, never resolved — same principle as `07.05.03` D-01: the system does not adjudicate taste | "These two references are very different" |
| Artist cannot articulate anything at all | The **normal case**, and the reason the feature exists. References carry the whole load. | Reference-only brief is complete and valid — no empty "describe your vision" field shaming them |
| Brief changes mid-mix | Expected and normal. Not versioned as a contract. | Living document with a change history |
| Reference is an unreleased track by the same artist (their own demo) | Works natively — it is already in the platform | In-platform playback, no licence issue |
| Mastering engineer wants the brief (parent CX-01) | Their roster role is the most restricted (`07.03.03`); the brief is genuinely useful to them | Real tension between least privilege and this need |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No brief | "No brief yet" + CTA. Never a blank "describe your vision" prompt |
| Populated | References/notes exist | Reference list with annotations and notes |

| Contradictory | References conflict | Advisory; no resolution |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **References are the brief; words are optional** | `meta/personas.md` describes working musicians, not critics. The actual vocabulary of mix feedback — "warmer", "punchier", "it needs to breathe" — is unactionable to anyone who was not in the room, which `07.05.01` DT-02 already established. But an artist pointing at a record is *completely precise*: everything about that snare is specified, exactly, forever. The whole industry runs on this and no tool treats it as the primary input. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a reference-only brief must be a complete, valid brief, and the UI must not present a "describe your vision" text field as the primary affordance. That field is where artists feel stupid and abandon the feature. Inverts the obvious design. |
| DT-02 | The platform can host reference playback so annotations point at audible moments | The natural design if DT-01 is right — and the feature is dramatically weaker without it | ❌ **REJECTED — on licensing, and it hurts.** Streaming a commercial record inside WeJammin requires a licence the platform does not have and cannot cheaply get; `meta/constraints.md` already flags DMCA/copyright as an unresolved compliance area. Two escape routes, both partial: (a) **link out with a timestamp** to a streaming service — legal, free, and it breaks the experience, since the engineer leaves the platform to hear the thing (and `07.05.02` DT-01 says leaving the platform is how the platform loses the room); (b) match the reference to a service the user already subscribes to and deep-link the timestamp — better, still an exit. **This is the feature's defining constraint and it caps its quality below what the idea deserves.** Worth stating plainly rather than designing around: the best version of this feature is illegal. Escalated as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | The brief serves the mix engineer | Its name and apparent purpose | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — it also serves mastering, and that is nearly free.** Parent CX-01: when an artist points at a record, they have implicitly specified a **loudness, tonal balance and dynamic range** — information that is arguably *more* useful to the mastering engineer than to the mixer, and that currently evaporates in the handoff between them. The platform can measure a reference's loudness and tonal profile (from the user's own copy, or from published data) and carry that target forward automatically. This costs almost nothing given the brief exists, and it closes one of the most common quality gaps in independent releases: a master that does not sound like the records the artist was aiming at, because nobody ever told the mastering engineer what those records were. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **References are the brief; words are optional.** A reference-only brief is complete | Per DT-01 — inverts the obvious design; the text field is where artists feel stupid and quit. |
