# Feature: Song Record & Work Entity

> **Number**: 07.01.01
> **Parent**: [Song, Release & Production Board](./07.01-song-release-production-board-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The Song is the spine of the production workspace. It is the record that begins as a voice memo and persists
through demo, tracking, mix, master and delivery — the one durable subject that every version, session, roster
entry, comment and capture prompt in domain 07 points at. Without a durable subject the provenance thesis has
nothing to attach to: facts captured at the moment of creation need something to be captured *about*.

**The Song is not a rights object.** [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)
(`[DEEP]`) owns the composition/master duality and holds the two records that carry money — the **work** (ISWC,
publishing) and the **recording** (ISRC, master). That feature's D-09 is explicit: a recording exists in the
registry only by **one explicit assertion**, never by promotion of a session artifact. This feature is the other
side of that boundary. The Song is the **production container**: the thing you are working on, which may yield
zero, one, or several rights objects, and which is perfectly real before any of them exist and after all of them
are done.

The distinction is what makes both features tractable. A Song has a stage, a roster, forty versions and a
completeness debt; a work has none of those and never should. A work can exist with no Song (a song written in
1998 and imported as a credit). A Song can exist with no work (an instrumental nobody has claimed a composition
on yet; a cover of someone else's work). Conflating them puts session detritus in the rights registry — the
failure [09.01.01 DT-12](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)
names — and puts a legal opinion on a scratch idea.

## Role Lens

> **Persona access is a lens, not an account type** (`meta/personas.md`: the multi-hyphenate is the structural
> fact). Nothing here keys off an account role. A venue Operator who engineers a session is acting as a Producer
> at that moment and gets the Producer surface on that Song, through the roster
> ([07.03.01](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)) —
> not through anything about their account.

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates a Song with a working title and nothing else. Owns it, or the band entity they belong to owns it. Renames it freely — the working title carries no rights consequence and never propagates to a released work. Sees every version, session, roster entry and capture prompt attached to it, and its completeness debt. Sees **"songs in production" across every project, band and client engagement at once** — the cross-context view no existing tool gives them (DT-03), and the reason a Song is not filed inside one project. Shelves a Song when it dies, which is the modal outcome and must be silent. Cannot see a Song they were never rostered on and whose owning party they are not a member of. |
| Producer | Full | Creates Songs on behalf of a client or a band, choosing the owning party at creation from the parties they hold a mandate for ([01.03](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.03-membership-representation-mandate/)) — so the client's band owns the container from second one and there is no ownership transfer to do later. The primary stage-mover ([07.01.03](./07.01.03-production-stage-board-milestones.md)) and therefore the primary target of the capture prompts a Song's transitions fire. Sees the Song's whole session history — the view no other role gets. **Asserts a recording** from a Song at session close, which is where this feature hands off to [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) and where the wedge closes. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator books rooms, not songs. They may see that a **session** occupied their room ([07.06.01](../07.06-sessions-documentation-recall/), domain 16) — the fact, the time, the party — but never the Song that session produced, never its title, never its stage. The work is the client's confidential property, and a studio with visibility into its clients' unreleased catalogue is unbookable (domain index, Role Matrix finding 2). There is **no Operator surface in this feature — do not build one.** |
| Fan | None | Unreleased work is invisible. A Fan encounters music only after domain 12 publishes it and domain 20 surfaces it — as a released recording, which is a [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) object rendered through [09.06.04](../../09-rights-ownership/09.06-rights-evidence-public-record/09.06.04-public-rights-lookup.md) and domain 20, never as this entity. The Song never becomes Fan-visible; it is the workshop, not the shop. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

### What the Song owns, and what it only points at

The Song deliberately owns almost nothing. Field accretion is the failure mode this feature is designed against
(DT-02): every field added to the Song is a field someone has to fill in, and `meta/personas.md` states the
Producer will not leave the session to fill anything in.

| The Song **owns** | The Song **points at** — and must never copy or duplicate |
|---|---|
| A stable internal identifier, permanent from creation | The **work(s)** and **recording(s)** it yielded — [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) |
| A **working title** — mutable, non-identifying, no rights meaning | Its **versions**, and through the canonical resolver its key/tempo/canonical audio — [07.04](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/) |
| Its **owning party** (a person, alias or band entity — domain 01) | Its **roster** — [07.03.01](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md) |
| Its **lifecycle**: `active` · `shelved` · `archived` · `unadministered` | Its **credits** — domain 02 |
| Its **`current_stage`** — single writer of record for the board, typed to the approved immutable shared enum version once P-01 validation passes (CX-01) | Its **splits and ownership** — domain 09 |
| Its **working-title history** — append-only | Its **sessions** — [07.06.01](../07.06-sessions-documentation-recall/) |
| Its **project and release memberships** — many-to-many, held on the edge | Its **completeness debt** — [07.08.03](../07.08-delivery-readiness-qc/07.08.03-metadata-completeness-readiness-score.md) |

It owns **no musical attributes**. Key, tempo and sample rate are properties of a *fixation*, not of an idea — a
version pitched up a semitone has a different key from its parent, and a Song has no key at all until something
has been played. They belong to versions ([07.04](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/)) and arrive as a
byproduct of the DAW bridge ([07.09](../07.09-daw-bridge-capture-at-source/)). The Song *displays* its canonical
version's. Where `07.09` supplies nothing, those fields are simply **blank** — the platform does not
backfill by re-introducing the twelve-field creation form DT-02 exists to prevent (DT-12).

> **Settled 2026-07-22 (domain D-08, resolving domain Q-01).** They are blank. No non-web client on the
> producer's machine is authorised (`07.09` D-05), so for the whole v1 window no key, tempo or sample rate
> arrives as a byproduct — a version carries only what the manual upload path can determine, and everything
> else stays empty. DT-12's rule is therefore load-bearing in v1 rather than hypothetical: blank is the truth,
> not a bug for a form to fix. `07.09` keeps `Should`, unphased (domain D-10) — parked on evidence, not
> rejected.

### The three ownerships — which are routinely conflated, and must not be

This is the feature's central rule. Three different things are called "owning a song", they move independently,
and every real dispute in this area comes from someone assuming one implies another.

| # | What | Who holds it | What changes it | What does **not** change it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Container ownership** — who administers the production workspace | The owning party: a person, alias, or band entity (domain 01) | An explicit transfer, accepted by the receiving party | A member leaving the band; an account closing; a project ending |
| 2 | **Rights ownership** — publishing and master shares | Whoever domain 09's ledger says, per party | A rights event in [09.03.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md): assignment, reversion, inheritance | **Anything at all in domain 07.** A production surface never writes a ledger |
| 3 | **Workspace access** — who can open it | The roster ([07.03.01](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)), plus current members of the owning party | Roster changes; joining or leaving the owning band | Rights ownership. A departed member who owns 25% of the master has money, not keys |

The consequence that matters, stated so a UI cannot get it wrong: **leaving a band takes away your keys. It does
not take away your name on the record, and it does not take away your money.** This is
[07.03.01 D-05](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)
("attribution persists after removal from the roster; access does not") and
[01.04.04](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.04-dissolution-succession.md)
("dissolution disposes of the party's assets and authority; it never touches the record of what was done"),
enforced at the Song. Any flow that removes a person from a Song must state, in the confirmation, exactly what is
**not** being removed (DT-09).

### Titles — three of them, and they must be allowed to diverge

A Song called `idea 4` can yield a recording titled *Ghost Town* of a work titled *Ghost Town*. Three titles, three
owners, three lifetimes.

- The Song's title is a **working title**. Free to change, by any roster member holding a producer-or-owner role,
  at any time, forever. It identifies nothing — the stable ID does that.
- On first recording assertion, the Song's current title is **offered as the default** for the recording and work
  titles. It is never pushed again after that moment.
- Divergence is normal and permanent. Bands call a record by its working title internally for a decade.
- **Renaming a Song never renames a work or a recording.** A production surface writing a rights record is the
  failure nobody designs for: a user tidying up their song list in 2029 silently renames a work registered with a
  PRO in 2027 (DT-05).
- The working-title history is retained append-only, and is **offered to domain 09 as candidate alternate titles**
  at registration — PRO and DDEX registrations hold alternate titles as first-class, and today they are retyped
  from memory. This is a provenance byproduct nobody asked for and it costs one list (DT-06).

### Lifecycle — abandonment is normal and must be silent

Lifecycle is **orthogonal to stage**. The stage model is fixed and craft-specific
([07.01 D-02](./07.01-song-release-production-board-index.md)): one shared platform-owned vocabulary is required,
but its candidate labels remain provisional until P-01 validation passes and the product owner approves an
immutable version. `abandoned` is not a craft stage and must never be added to that version. Most songs die, at
every stage, and that is the normal condition of songwriting, not a failure to nag about (DT-08).

| Lifecycle | Meaning | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `active` | Being worked on, or not — no inference is made | Full read/write per role |
| `shelved` | **User-declared** dormant. Never inferred from inactivity | Hidden from the board by default, fully intact, one click back to `active`, **zero notifications, ever** |
| `archived` | Terminal for a Song carrying provenance (D-02) | Read-only. Every version, credit, split and session visible and citable; nothing new attaches |
| `unadministered` | The owning party exists but no live account can act for it (DT-10) | Read-only to everyone who had access. Rights and royalties continue in domain 09, untouched. A successor or estate claims administration via [01.10](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.10-estates-legacy-accounts.md) / [09.03.04](../../09-rights-ownership/09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.04-estate-succession-catalog-legacy.md) |

A Song is **never** stale-nagged, never auto-shelved, never surfaced as "needs attention" for age alone. What is
offered instead is an opt-in view — "songs you haven't opened in a year" — which the user asks for. Measure and
show; never judge ([domain D-05](../music-projects-collaboration-index.md)).

### Deletion vs archival

| Song condition | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Zero versions, zero sessions, zero roster entries beyond the creator, zero credits, zero asserted recordings — a mistyped `New Song` | **Hard delete**, by the creator, with an undo toast. This is the only case |
| Anything else | **Archive only.** Hard delete refused, permanently, with no admin override |

The threshold is *content*, not a clock. It differs deliberately from
[09.01.01 D-11](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md)'s
15-minute window because the objects differ: a rights record's value is legal evidence the instant it is
consented, so its window is short; a Song's value is other people's uploads, comments and time, which accrete
visibly and are gone if deleted. A time window on a Song would let someone delete a collaborator's stems at
minute 14.

### Creation

Title is the only required field (D-01). The owning-party picker renders **only** if the creator can act for more
than one party, defaulting to their personal party — so the common case is still one field. The title commits on
confirmation, not on a save button: capture data is unrepeatable, and a client-side draft pending an explicit save
is a data-loss surface at exactly the moment the platform exists to protect
([09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md), same rule).

Creation is reachable in **≤ 2 interactions** from the board, a project, a band, and the idea inbox
([07.02.01](../07.02-songwriting-composition-workspace/)). Typed title → committed record, **P95 < 500 ms**.

### Happy Path

1. Musician or Producer hits "New Song" from a project, a band, or the idea inbox and types a working title. If they hold a mandate for more than one party, an owner picker appears, defaulted to their personal party.
2. System commits the Song on confirmation — permanent internal ID, `current_stage` set to the approved enum version's initial semantic stage, `lifecycle: active`, owning party set, creator added to the roster as the first contributor ([07.03.01](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)). Until P-01 validation approves a version, the initial label remains provisional rather than a hardened contract. P95 < 500 ms. No other field is requested.
3. Work happens against the Song, not against a form: versions upload ([07.04](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/)), sessions log ([07.06](../07.06-sessions-documentation-recall/)), contributors get invited ([07.03.02](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.02-contributor-invitation-scoped-onboarding.md)), the stage moves ([07.01.03](./07.01.03-production-stage-board-milestones.md)) and each approved semantic transition fires its non-blocking capture prompt.
4. The Song's detail view assembles itself from pointers: canonical version and its key/tempo ([07.04.02](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/07.04.02-canonical-version-resolver.md)), roster, session history, current stage, completeness debt ([07.08.03](../07.08-delivery-readiness-qc/07.08.03-metadata-completeness-readiness-score.md)). Composition and recording panels render whatever domain 09 holds — empty at this point, and that is not an error.
5. Musician renames it from `idea 4` to *Ghost Town*. Every attached fact follows the ID, not the name. The old title is retained in the title history.
6. Producer closes the session and asserts one comp as a **recording** ([09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) Happy Path steps 1–2). This is one explicit act, not a sync — the Song's other 60 takes stay session artifacts and never reach the registry.
7. System offers the Song's current title as the default recording title, and offers the title history as candidate alternate titles. The Song now points at one recording; the recording points at its work per 09.01.01. **Nothing about the Song's own record changed** — it gained a pointer, not a percentage.
8. Splits and credits capture through domain 09/02, prompted by the stage transition, never by this feature.
9. Months later the band records a live version of the same song. It is a **new recording** on the **same work** ([09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) `live` link). Whether it is a new Song is the user's call — a fresh production usually is, and it points at the same work.
10. The record ships. The Song stays exactly where it is: `delivered`, `active`, citable, and still the only place the session history lives.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two Songs with identical titles under one party | Allowed. Duplicate titles are normal in music ("Intro", "Untitled") and a title is not an identity — same rule as [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md). Disambiguated by created-date and canonical version, never by blocking. | Both listed, with created-date and last-activity as the distinguisher |
| Song deleted after any content exists | Hard delete refused. A Song carrying versions, credits or provenance is archived, not destroyed. No admin override. | "This song has 4 confirmed credits and a signed split. It can be archived, but not deleted." with an archive CTA |
| Song deleted 20 seconds after a mistyped `New Song`, nothing attached | Hard-deleted. The only deletable case. | Gone, with an undo toast |
| Two people create the same Song within minutes of each other | Duplicate detection scoped to **(owning party, fuzzy title, ≤ 10 min)** offers a merge. **Never auto-merged** (DT-11). | "Sam created a song called 'Ghost Town' 2 minutes ago. Use theirs?" — with both shown, and neither touched until someone picks |
| Duplicate detection across **different** parties | **Does not run.** Songs are private production containers; telling a stranger their title matches yours leaks unreleased work with no rights value to justify it (DT-14). Contrast [09.01.01 DT-15](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md), where the leak at least buys ledger integrity. | Nothing. The other party is never told and never learns the Song exists |
| A demo becomes a completely different song | User forks the Song, creating a new production with a lineage pointer to the parent. The fork inherits **nothing but the link** and an optional roster copy — no versions, no stage, no credits, no work link. | "Fork as new song" — with the inheritance stated explicitly before confirming |
| Fork while a version upload to the parent is in flight | No race. The fork copies nothing, so the upload lands on the parent as normal and never appears on the child. | Upload completes on the parent; the fork is empty |
| **Member leaves the band that owns the Song** | Container ownership: unchanged — the band still owns it. Rights: unchanged — their consented shares are domain 09's and survive absolutely ([01.04.04](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.04-dissolution-succession.md)). Credits: unchanged, permanently ([07.03.01 D-05](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)). **Access**: reviewed — the membership edge gets an end date and their band-derived access to the band's Songs ends on that date. A roster entry naming them personally survives and keeps its own access. | Departing member: "You keep every credit and every share you hold on 14 songs. You lose access to 9 songs owned by the band." — itemised, both halves named |
| **Band that owns the Song dissolves** | The band entity persists in `dissolved` ([01.04.04](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.04-dissolution-succession.md)) — so the Song still has an owning party and never becomes ownerless. If no successor administrator is recorded, the Song → `unadministered`. Catalogue rights disposition is domain 09's flow, triggered by 01.04.04, and it does not touch this container. | "The band that owns this song has dissolved and no successor is recorded. The song is read-only. Your credits and shares are unaffected." |
| **Every member of a defunct band closes their account** | The Song is not ownerless (DT-10). The owning band's party record survives; the member party records survive as closed/memorialised. Song → `unadministered`, read-only. Royalties continue accruing in domain 09 to whoever the ledger names. | Anyone with prior access: read-only view with the reason named and a route to [01.10](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.10-estates-legacy-accounts.md) if they have standing |
| Container ownership transfer (producer created it, client's band should own it) | Requires **acceptance by the receiving party**. On acceptance, container ownership and band-derived access move. **Nothing in domain 09 moves** — a rights transfer is [09.03.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.03-chain-of-title-lifecycle/09.03.01-chain-of-title-ledger.md)'s event and has to be done there, deliberately, by the parties. | Transfer confirmation names what moves (the workspace) and what does not (every split and credit), with a link to 09 if a rights transfer was what they actually meant |
| Ownership transfer offered to a party the receiver cannot act for | Refused at proposal time. | "You can't accept on behalf of [band] — you'd need a mandate ([01.03](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.03-membership-representation-mandate/))" |
| Two users rename the same Song within seconds | **Last-write-wins**, with a toast naming who and when. Both titles are in the title history, so nothing is lost. Deliberately unlike [09.01.01 D-12](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) (link writes rejected, never merged): a title moves no money, and a rename that demands a conflict resolution is friction over a field with nothing at stake | "Sam renamed this to 'Ghost Town' while you were typing" — with a one-click revert to your title |
| Two users move the same Song's stage concurrently | Last-write-wins on `current_stage`; the Song is the single writer of record (CX-01). No lock — stage is advisory craft state and locking the board makes people avoid the board (domain D-04). **But** the capture prompts are deduplicated per `(song, transition, 24h)` so two people dragging to `mix` does not ask the roster the same question twice. | "Sam moved this to Mix while you were looking" toast; the board reflects current state |
| Two people assert the same comp as a recording from the same session | Not this feature's race — [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) holds per-session creation idempotency; the second asserter joins the first's in-progress record | The second user lands in the first user's record, with their presence visible |
| Song archived while a version upload is in flight | Archive takes effect immediately (non-blocking is absolute, domain D-04). The in-flight write is rejected with the actor and time named. Unarchive is offered to anyone holding the role. | "This song was archived by Sam 8 seconds ago. Your upload wasn't saved." — with **Unarchive and retry** and the file still selected |
| Song reaches `master approved` with **zero asserted recordings** | Allowed — nothing blocks (domain D-04). But it is the thesis failing in the one place it is supposed to work, so it is the **loudest** signal [07.08.03](../07.08-delivery-readiness-qc/07.08.03-metadata-completeness-readiness-score.md) carries: a finished record that exists as a rights object nowhere. | At the delivery gate, not before: "This song is mastered but isn't registered as a recording. Nothing you've captured can be paid." |
| Network failure mid-create | The title is one field and commits on confirmation. Nothing typed is discarded; the write retries. | Inline "Saving…" → "Couldn't save that — retrying", typed text stays on screen, manual retry available |
| Network failure mid-rename | Same. The prior title stands until the new one commits. | Field keeps the typed text; retry offered; no silent revert |
| Song's canonical version audio is unavailable | Song loads; only the player degrades. | "Audio unavailable — file integrity check failed" ([07.04.05](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/07.04.05-file-integrity-missing-media.md)) rather than an empty player |
| Song has versions but no canonical version resolved | Normal, not an error — most versions are canonical for nothing ([07.04.02](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/07.04.02-canonical-version-resolver.md)). | Version list renders; the header player shows "No canonical version set for this stage" with a picker |
| **Multi-hyphenate is both the Musician and the Producer on their own Song** | Prompts deduplicate **per human, not per role** — otherwise the person who is their own producer gets asked who played on it twice, and learns to dismiss prompts (DT-13). | One prompt, once |
| A person has access via **two paths** — band member and separately rostered session player | Access is the **union** of the paths (the most permissive), never the intersection. The Song records **which edge granted access**, so "why could this person see this?" is answerable later — which is the only thing that makes a leak investigable ([07.03.03](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.03-rights-aware-asset-vault-nda-gating.md)). | Their access is simply present; the audit view shows both edges |
| New member joins a band that owns 40 unreleased Songs | Joining grants read access to the band's Songs from the join date. This is correct band behaviour and also a real confidentiality event: the platform records the join so the "who could have seen this" question is answerable, and does **not** claim to have prevented anything (domain D-06 — honest claims). Band governance ([01.04.01](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/01.04-band-ensemble-governance/01.04.01-partnership-terms-decision-rules.md)) may scope it tighter | New member: the catalogue, visible. Existing members: "Alex joined and can now see 40 songs" — stated, not buried |
| Song referenced by a project, and the project is deleted | The Song survives untouched. A project *references* Songs; it does not contain them (DT-03). Membership is an edge. | "This song is in 2 other projects and stays in your library" |
| Song removed from a project it was the only Song in | Same — the Song is unaffected. | Nothing about the Song changes |
| A session player rostered on one Song opens the Release it sits on | Refused. Roster is per-song ([07.03.01 D-01](../07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality/07.03.01-contributor-roster-role-assignment.md)); a session player on track 4 is not on the album. | The song is visible; the release it sits on is not ([07.01.02](./07.01.02-release-container-sequencing-assembly.md)) |
| Malicious: Song created with a famous song's title to poison search | Inert. The Song is a private production container, is not public, is not in any cross-party index, names nobody, and grants its creator nothing. Titles are not identity. | Nothing — nobody outside the owning party ever sees it |
| Malicious: bulk Song creation to exhaust storage | Rate-limited per party: **120 Song creations per party per hour**, with a distinct authenticated **bulk-import path** for the legitimate burst (a musician onboarding a 200-song back catalogue is real and must not be mistaken for abuse). | "You've created a lot of songs quickly — importing a back catalogue? Use import." with the import route, not a hard stop |
| A roster member renames the Song to something abusive | Allowed to happen, attributable and reversible: title history is append-only, so the change names its author and reverts in one click. Escalation is domain 24's. | Any owner: "Sam renamed this 4 minutes ago" with revert |
| Session player without a producer-or-owner role tries to rename | Refused. Rename is owner-party members and roster members holding a producer-or-owner role only (CX-01 synthesis q3). | Title renders read-only, with the reason on hover — not a silent no-op |
| A Song is started **from an existing recording** (a remix production) | Supported. The source recording is an **input**, not something this Song owns; any new master it yields is a new recording asserted per 09.01.01 with a `remix-of` lineage link. The source master's owner permission is domain 09/11's, not this feature's | "New song from *Ghost Town (master)*" — with the source shown as an input and the clearance requirement named by 09 |
| A user imports 200 released back-catalogue songs | They become **works and recordings in domain 09**, not Songs here. There was no production on WeJammin, so there is no production container to create — and creating 200 empty Songs at stage `delivered` would fill the board with work that is finished (DT-07). | The catalogue appears in domain 09 / domain 02's discography. The board stays empty until they start something |
