# Feature: Song Record & Work Entity

> **Number**: 07.01.01
> **Parent**: [Song, Release & Production Board](./07.01-song-release-production-board-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The Song is the spine of the platform. It is the record that begins as a voice memo and persists through
demo, tracking, mix, master, release, sync placement and royalty statement — the one identifier that every
credit, split, version, session and payment in WeJammin ultimately points at. Without a durable work entity
the provenance thesis has nothing to attach to: facts captured at the moment of creation need a subject.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates a Song; names it; sets key/BPM/genre; sees every version, session, credit and split attached to it; sees "my songs" across every project and band they belong to — including songs owned by a band entity they are a member of. Cannot see a Song they were never rostered on. |
| Producer | Full | Creates Songs on behalf of a project or client; is usually the one who fills the technical fields (key, tempo, sample rate) because they are the one in the DAW. Sees the Song's whole session history — the view no other role gets. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator books rooms, not songs. They may see that a **session** occupied their room (see `07.06.01` and domain 16) but never the Song that session produced — the work is the client's confidential property. |
| Fan | None | Unreleased works are invisible. A Fan encounters a Song only after domain 12 publishes it, and then as a released recording in domain 20 — not as this entity. |

## Behavior

A Song is created cheaply — a name is enough — and accretes structure as work happens. It is deliberately
*not* a form to fill in. Every field beyond the title is either optional or captured as a byproduct of doing
something else (the DAW bridge supplies tempo; the session supplies contributors; the stage board supplies
progress). The Song carries a stable internal identifier from creation, so that a rename, a re-record or a
title change three years later never orphans the credits already attached.

The critical modelling question is the **work vs recording** distinction (Q-01): in industry terms a *work*
(composition — one ISWC, publishing splits) can have many *recordings* (masters — each with its own ISRC and
master splits). A demo, the album cut, and a live version are three recordings of one work, with identical
publishing splits and completely different master splits.

### Happy Path

1. Musician or Producer hits "New Song" from a project, a band, or the idea inbox; types a working title
2. System creates the Song, assigns a permanent internal ID, sets stage to `writing`, and adds the creator to the roster as the first contributor
3. Work happens — versions get uploaded (`07.04`), sessions get logged (`07.06`), contributors get invited (`07.03`)
4. The Song's detail view assembles itself: current stage, canonical version, roster, session history, completeness score (`07.08.03`)
5. Musician renames it from "idea 4" to its real title; every attached fact follows the ID, not the name

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two Songs with identical titles in one project | Allowed — duplicate titles are normal in music ("Intro", "Untitled"). System disambiguates by creation date and canonical version, never by blocking. | Both listed, with created-date and last-activity as the distinguisher |
| Song is deleted after credits/splits exist | Hard delete refused. A Song carrying confirmed provenance is archived, not destroyed — the platform's whole promise is that the record survives. | "This song has 4 confirmed credits and a signed split. It can be archived, but not deleted." with an archive CTA |
| A demo becomes a completely different song | User forks the Song, creating a new work with a lineage pointer to the parent | "Fork as new song" — the new song inherits nothing but a link and an optional roster copy |
| Song ownership transfers (member leaves the band) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — depends on the band-entity model in domain 01 and the ownership record in domain 09 | — |
| Two people create the same Song from the same session | Duplicate detection on (project, title, near-identical creation window) offers a merge | "Producer already created a song called 'Ghost Town' 2 minutes ago. Use theirs?" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Song detail opened | Skeleton of the header (title, stage, canonical version) with the version list streaming in |
| Empty | Song created, nothing attached | Title, stage `writing`, and three CTAs: upload a version, invite a contributor, log a session |
| Populated | Versions and roster exist | Full song view — canonical version player, version timeline, roster, session history, completeness score |
| Error | Fetch failed | "Couldn't load this song" with retry; the canonical version stays playable from cache if already loaded |
| Partial | Song exists but its canonical version's audio is unavailable | Song loads; the player shows "Audio unavailable — file integrity check failed" (see `07.04.05`) rather than an empty player |
| Archived | Archived after provenance existed | Read-only. Credits, splits and versions all visible and citable; no new work can be attached |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The Song must separate **work** (composition) from **recording** (master), because publishing splits and master splits are different sets of people with different percentages, and conflating them is the single most common amateur error in split sheets | Industry structure — ISWC vs ISRC, PRO vs label. A drummer who played on the record but wrote nothing has a master credit and zero publishing. A conflated model gives them publishing. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the distinction is real and load-bearing. **But** where it is modelled is Q-01: this feature may be the *work*, with recordings living in `07.04` as versions, or both may need first-class entities. Flagged, not resolved. |
| DT-02 | A Song should be creatable in under 3 seconds with only a title, and every other field captured as a byproduct | `meta/personas.md` Producer constraint: "any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and go fill in a web form will not be used." A Song-creation form with 12 required fields is exactly that form. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives the "title is the only required field" behavior above, and pushes tempo/key/sample-rate capture into `07.09` (DAW bridge). |
| DT-03 | Songs are owned by projects, so a Song's home is its project folder | Obvious structural assumption inherited from software-project mental models | ❌ REJECTED — the Musician persona is a multi-hyphenate in 2+ bands. Their songs exist across projects, bands, and client work simultaneously, and "my songs" must be a first-class cross-project view. A song nested inside exactly one project folder recreates the fragmentation the platform exists to end. The Song is owned by an **identity or band entity** (domain 01); a project *references* it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Title is the **only** required field to create a Song | Per DT-02 — a heavy creation form is the exact failure mode the Producer persona describes. Everything else is captured as a byproduct or left blank. |
| D-02 | A Song with confirmed provenance can be **archived but never hard-deleted** | The platform's promise (D-18, thesis) is that the record survives. A delete button that destroys captured splits contradicts the product's reason to exist. Note: this collides with GDPR erasure — routed as an open question. |
| D-03 | Songs belong to identities/bands, not to projects | Per DT-03 — the multi-hyphenate persona breaks any single-project home. |
