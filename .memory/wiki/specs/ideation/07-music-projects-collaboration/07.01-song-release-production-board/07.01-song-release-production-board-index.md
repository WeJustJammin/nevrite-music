## Overview

The container layer of the domain: the entities that everything else in Music Projects hangs off.
A **Song** is the work-level record that persists from first riff to released master and beyond;
a **Release** is the creative assembly (tracklist, sequence, album-level assets) that groups songs
for a market moment; the **Production Board** is the craft-specific stage machine that tracks where
each Song is through one platform-owned shared enum version. P-01 Option B keeps candidate labels
provisional until practitioner validation passes and the product owner approves a version.

**Why sub-domain, not feature**: three distinct capabilities that interact continuously — a Release's
readiness is a function of the approved-master terminal semantic of every Song it contains, sequencing
uses approved semantic milestones, and a Song's stage transition is what moves the board. Change one and
the other two must react.

| Q-01 | ~~Is a Song the same entity across a demo, a re-record, and a live version — or does each get its own Song with a lineage link? This is the **work vs recording** distinction (ISWC vs ISRC). It is decided here but owned by domains 02/09.~~ **Resolved — each fixation is its own recording of one work; the Song models neither.** [09.01.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.01-work-recording-duality.md) is `[DEEP]` and owns both records: its D-04 gives "**Two link classes**, not one overloaded type: Recording→Work (typed, weighted) and Recording→Recording lineage (kinded)" — `cover`/`live` describe a relationship to a work, `remix`/`re-recording` to a prior master — and its D-09 holds that "a recording exists here only by **explicit assertion**, never by promotion of a session artifact". So a demo, a re-record and a live version are separate **recordings** on the same **work**, never one entity. The **Song** is the production container and "owns pointers, never percentages" ([07.01.01](./07.01.01-song-record-work-entity.md) D-04); whether a re-record gets its own Song is the user's call at the time (07.01.01 Happy Path step 9), with `fork` supplying the production-side lineage pointer. The edge's character is carried by [07.04.01](../07.04-audio-version-control-lineage/07.04.01-audio-version-control-lineage-timeline.md) D-14 (`same-recording` vs `new-recording`). | Q-02 | ~~Can a Song belong to more than one Release (compilation, deluxe reissue, soundtrack placement)? Assumed yes — but that makes Release↔Song many-to-many and breaks "delete the release, delete the songs".~~ **Resolved — yes, many-to-many on the membership edge, and nothing cascades.** [07.01.02](./07.01.02-release-container-sequencing-assembly.md) D-01: "Membership edges hold sequence, selected master and variant — **the Release does not copy songs** … one song on three releases with three different masters is routine. Copying breaks provenance by fragmenting the work." Deleting a Release therefore removes edges, never Songs — the same rule 07.01.01 already states for projects ("A project *references* Songs; it does not contain them"). Confirmed downstream at delivery: [12.01.01](../../12-release-distribution/12.01-release-builder/12.01.01-release-composition-sequencing.md) allows a recording already released on an earlier WeJammin release with "ISRC preserved and not reissued".
