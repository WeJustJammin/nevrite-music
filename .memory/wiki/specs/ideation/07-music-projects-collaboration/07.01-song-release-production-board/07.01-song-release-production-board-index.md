## Overview

The container layer of the domain: the entities that everything else in Music Projects hangs off.
A **Song** is the work-level record that persists from first riff to released master and beyond;
a **Release** is the creative assembly (tracklist, sequence, album-level assets) that groups songs
for a market moment; the **Production Board** is the craft-specific stage machine that tracks where
each song actually is (writing → demo → tracking → overdubs → comp → mix → master → delivered).

**Why sub-domain, not feature**: three distinct capabilities that interact continuously — a Release's
readiness is a function of the stage of every Song it contains, sequencing cannot begin until enough
Songs reach `mix approved`, and a Song's stage transition is what moves the board. Change one and the
other two must react.

## Decision Log

| # | Decision | Context | Source |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| D-01 | The **Release container lives here, not in domain 12** | Domain 12 (Release & Distribution) owns *delivery to DSPs* — the outbound pipeline. What lives here is the *creative* assembly: which songs, in what order, with what gaps and album art intent. Splitting them keeps 12 free to be an integration domain and keeps sequencing next to the songs it sequences. Handoff between them is CX. | Node Classification Gate, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
| D-02 | Stage model is **craft-specific and fixed**, not a user-defined kanban | The domain rationale (D-01, domain index) states stage gates are craft-specific. A generic "add your own columns" board yields nothing downstream; a fixed music stage vocabulary (`tracking`, `comp`, `mix`, `master`) lets the platform infer *what kind of asset* a version is and *which capture prompt* to fire. The stage vocabulary is what makes the provenance byproduct extractable. | Deep Think, `/ideate-discover` Step 3 |
