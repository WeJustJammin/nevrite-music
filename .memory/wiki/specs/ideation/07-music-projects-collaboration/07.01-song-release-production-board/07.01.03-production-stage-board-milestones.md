# Feature: Production Stage Board, Milestones & Creative Deadlines

> **Number**: 07.01.03
> **Parent**: [Song, Release & Production Board](./07.01-song-release-production-board-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-21

## Overview

A board showing where every song actually is, using one **fixed, craft-specific stage vocabulary**. It is
not a generic kanban with user-defined columns. The fixed vocabulary is the point: it lets the platform
know what kind of asset arrives next, which provenance prompt applies, and when a craft phase is closed.
**The board is the trigger surface for provenance capture** — that is what earns it a place in a domain
whose thesis is capture-at-source. **Restated for the v1 window (domain D-11, 2026-07-22)**: capture-at-source
is the *direction*, not the current claim. No `07.09` feature ships in v1, so for the whole v1 window the
product captures at the **first sharing moment** (`07.05.02`) plus the close prompt — and this board's stage
transitions are one of the two prompt-worthy moments that still fire (`07.09.03` D-07). That makes the board
*more* load-bearing in v1, not less, because it is a capture trigger the platform can actually observe.

**P-01 Option B:** no stage labels are yet approved as a lifecycle contract. The existing draft
`writing → demo → tracking → overdubs → comp → mix → mix approved → master → master approved → delivered`
is candidate evidence only. Before an enum becomes enforceable, the product owner must approve one
immutable version after the practitioner-validation gate in
[p01-production-stage-vocabulary-validation.md](../../../audits/p01-production-stage-vocabulary-validation.md)
passes. That gate tests the same candidate vocabulary against representative beatmaker and session-player
workflows, retains every mismatch/disposition, and rejects user-configurable columns, production-model
splits, second stage machines, and unnormalised exceptions.

Until approval, `Song.current_stage`, the board, capture prompts, and release readiness retain their
existing **semantic** roles but no provisional label set is a hardened downstream enum. Validation may
refine the selected version's labels, definitions, ordering, initial state, approved-master terminal
semantic, and prompt mappings; it may not replace one shared platform-owned vocabulary, Song ownership of
stage, roster authorization, non-blocking capture, or advisory milestones.

An approved version maps the existing capture events to its semantic stages: roster seed, take selection,
mix-credit confirmation, and split-finality confirmation. Each mapping remains non-blocking. P-02's
large-catalogue presentation is resolved separately in D-05; it was not evaluated or resolved by this gate.
P-03 (approval behavior after a backward move) remains a separate unresolved decision.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the board for their project or band; moves stages on songs they own; receives capture prompts on transitions where they are a rostered contributor; sees creative deadlines and what is late. As a multi-hyphenate, also gets a cross-project "everything waiting on me" view — the one view that no existing tool gives them. |
| Producer | Full | The primary operator of the board. Moves most transitions because they are the one who knows the work is done. Is the main target of transition capture prompts — per `meta/personas.md` they are the trust broker whose attestation carries weight. Sees blocked/waiting-on-client states across every project they run. |
| Operator | None | A venue or studio does not track a client's song progress. They may see a *booking* on their calendar (domain 16) that happens to correspond to a session, but the craft stage is invisible. |
| Fan | None | No visibility of unreleased work in progress. |

## Behavior

Each Song holds one `current_stage`. The board and, at large scale, the catalogue table are projections of
those values — scoped and filterable by project, band, or "assigned to me". The selected scope counts unique,
visible, authorized Songs **before** local search, sort, or table-only filters: the board renders at `0–59` Songs;
the dense catalogue table renders at `60+` Songs. This is an automatic presentation rule, not a user setting or
an alternate lifecycle. A Song that belongs to several Releases counts once; its sequence, selected master, and
release-specific edit remain membership data. Moving a board card or using a table row action writes the same
Song field and **fires the same stage-appropriate capture prompt**:

| Transition | Prompt fired |
|---|---|
| Roster-seed semantic | "Who's playing on this?" — seeds the roster (`07.03.01`) |
| Take-selection semantic | "Which takes made it?" — links takes to contributors (`07.04.03`) |
| Mix-credit-confirmation semantic | "Confirm the mix credit" — writes to domain 02 |
| Split-finality semantic | "Is the split final?" — the highest-stakes prompt; writes to domain 09 |

The approved enum version maps these semantics to its labels. Candidate mappings are recorded in the
P-01 validation packet and never silently become an enforceable prompt contract.

The prompt **never blocks the transition**. Per the Producer's accidental anti-persona behavior ("closes a
project without capturing splits"), a blocking prompt teaches users to avoid the board — and a board nobody
touches captures nothing. Instead, a dismissed prompt accrues visible completeness debt on the song
(`07.08.03`) and gets re-offered at the next natural moment.

Creative deadlines are attached to milestones (e.g. "all songs at mix by 14 Nov") and are advisory. Music
deadlines are missed as a matter of routine; a tool that punishes lateness gets abandoned. What the board owes
the user is an *honest* picture, not a disciplinary one.

### Catalogue-Table Parity at Large Scale

The table is a dense view, not a second catalogue, a Release tracker, or a task board. Each row renders the
Song title/identity, current approved semantic and display label, canonical version, roster/contributor context,
completeness indicator, and advisory milestone context. It does not flatten Release-membership fields into Song
fields, add configurable columns, per-card assignees, or a table-owned stage.

A row-stage action uses the exact same authorized `Song.current_stage` transition as card drag-and-drop:
optimistic move and rollback, non-blocking capture prompt, dismissal debt, roster notification, and the
last-write-wins attributed notice all apply per Song. The same write recomputes derived readiness for every
Release containing that Song, without granting a Song-only contributor visibility of any Release. A backwards
table move preserves the current interim rule: later sign-offs become superseded, while P-03 alone decides
whether a later re-advance re-collects them.

V1 has **no bulk stage transition**. Dense presentation does not make a mass write safe: every Song still has
its own authorization, prompt/debt, concurrency, and Release-readiness effects. Server-mediated bounded table
results are required; cursor shape, page size, ordering, responsive behavior, and cache semantics are deferred
to `/write-be-spec` and `/write-fe-spec`.

### Happy Path

1. Producer finishes tracking drums; opens the board
2. Drags "Ghost Town" from `tracking` to `overdubs`
3. System commits the transition instantly and shows a non-blocking prompt: "Who played on the tracking session? Sam (drums) was in the room — confirm?" — pre-filled from the session record (`07.06.01`)
4. Producer taps confirm; a credit is written to domain 02, attested by the Producer rather than self-claimed
5. Board updates; the Release's blocked-by list (`07.01.02`) recomputes; Sam is notified that a credit was attributed to them and can counter-attest

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Non-rostered user tries to move a card | Blocked. Only a rostered contributor on the song (typically the Producer or an owning Musician) may transition stages; a viewer or unrelated member cannot. | Drag handle disabled with tooltip "Only contributors on this song can move it" |
| Backwards transition out of a later semantic stage (e.g. an approved master rejected) | Allowed — this is normal, not an error. Sign-offs collected at the later stage are marked superseded, not deleted (see Q-03 in parent index). On re-advance, `07.05.04` D-07 governs: they reinstate iff no new version landed in between, otherwise the gate re-collects. | "Moving back to Mix. 2 approvals for the master will be marked superseded." |
| Capture prompt dismissed | Transition still commits. Completeness debt accrues on the song. | Song shows a completeness indicator; prompt re-offered at the next transition |
| Two people transition the same song simultaneously | Last write wins, with a visible notice — a lock would be worse than the race here | "Alex moved this to Mix while you were looking" |
| Song is stuck in one stage for months | No punishment. An optional digest surfaces it. | "3 songs haven't moved in 60 days" — a nudge in a weekly digest, not a red card |
| Deadline passes | Advisory only. Never blocks, never auto-escalates. | Milestone shown as overdue, with the songs that caused it |
| Project has 60+ visible, authorized Songs in the selected scope | Automatic catalogue table replaces the board after counting unique Songs before local filters; same Song projection and row-stage action apply. A local filter never switches the view back to board. | Dense rows with search/filter/sort affordances and a Song detail drill-in; no bulk stage action |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Board opened | Column skeletons |
| Empty | No songs in project | "No songs yet" with a create CTA |
| Populated | 1–59 visible, authorized Songs in selected scope | Cards grouped by stage, each with title, canonical version, contributor avatars, completeness indicator |
| Catalogue | 60+ visible, authorized unique Songs in selected scope | Dense table over the same Song projection: stage, canonical version, roster context, completeness indicator, milestone context, and Song detail drill-in |
| Filtered | Local filter applied after presentation mode selection | Subset with an active-filter chip and clear affordance; a filter cannot switch catalogue mode back to board |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |
| Optimistic | Card dragged | Card moves immediately; reverts with a toast if the write fails |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The stage vocabulary must be **fixed and music-specific**, not user-configurable | Domain rationale (D-01, domain index): "stage gates are craft-specific" and "music-native primitives yield credits and splits as a byproduct, where generic tasks yield nothing." A configurable board cannot know that entering `mix` means the tracking roster is now final — so it cannot fire the right prompt. The configurability that feels like a feature is precisely what destroys the byproduct. | ✅ CONFIRMED — sub-domain D-02. The cost is real (some workflows will not fit) and accepted. |
| DT-02 | The capture prompt must be **non-blocking**, even though blocking would capture more | `meta/personas.md`, Producer, worst accidental: "closes a project without capturing splits — the design must make the lazy path the correct path." A blocking prompt does not make the lazy path correct; it makes the board the enemy and pushes the work back to WhatsApp. Capture rate is maximised by being present at the right moment with a pre-filled answer, not by holding the door shut. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives the non-blocking + completeness-debt behavior. This is the single most important interaction design constraint in the domain. |
| DT-03 | Songs need per-stage **assignees** and due dates, like a task tracker | Software project-management instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a song at `mix` is implicitly assigned to whoever holds the mix role on the roster (`07.03.01`); an explicit assignee field duplicates the roster and immediately drifts from it. Music work is not assigned, it is *rostered* — the mix engineer is the mix engineer for the song, not the assignee of a mix ticket. Deadlines stay at the **milestone** level (a date the record needs to hit), not per card. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Fixed music stage vocabulary; not user-configurable | Per DT-01 and sub-domain D-02. |
| D-02 | Capture prompts are **non-blocking**; dismissal accrues completeness debt | Per DT-02 — the defining interaction constraint of the domain. |
| D-03 | No per-card assignees; roles come from the roster, deadlines live at milestone level | Per DT-03. |
| D-04 | Deadlines are advisory — never blocking, never auto-escalating | Music deadlines slip as a matter of routine. A tool that punishes gets abandoned, and an abandoned board captures nothing. |
| D-05 | **P-02:** Present `0–59` visible, authorized unique Songs as the board and `60+` as an automatic dense catalogue table | Both presentations project the same `Song.current_stage`; local filters do not change mode, table rows invoke the same per-Song transition, and V1 has no bulk stage action. |
