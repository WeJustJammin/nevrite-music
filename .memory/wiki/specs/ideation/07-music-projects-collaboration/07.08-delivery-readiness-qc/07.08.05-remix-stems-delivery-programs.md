# Feature: Remix Stems Delivery & Remix Programs

> **Number**: 07.08.05
> **Parent**: [Delivery, Readiness & QC](./07.08-delivery-readiness-qc-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Deliberately publishing stems outward — to a commissioned remixer, or to the public as a remix competition or an
open remix pack. It is **the inverse of everything else in domain 07**, which exists to keep unreleased audio in.
Here the artist chooses to release the most sensitive asset class in the platform (`07.03.03` DT-01) to people
they will never meet, permanently.

> This is the **only feature in all of domain 07 where the Fan has any access** — which is precisely why it
> deserved scrutiny rather than a quick classification, and why sub-domain Q-03 asks whether it belongs here at all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Decides to run a remix program, picks which stems go out, sets the terms (what remixers may do with the result), publishes. Owns the consequence — including the one they cannot undo. |
| Producer | Config | Prepares the stem pack; advises on what should and should not go out (the a cappella is the highest-value and highest-risk stem). Does not normally decide to publish — that is an artist/commercial call. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | Read-only | **The only Fan access in domain 07.** Downloads the published stems, makes a remix, submits it. Also — per parent CX-05 — **the party who gets harmed** if the stems contain an undeclared sample: they inherit a liability they cannot see and did not create. |

## Behavior

A remix program publishes a curated stem set with terms. Two distinct shapes hide under one name:

**Commissioned remix** — stems to one named professional, under a contract. This is *not this feature*: it is a
handoff (`07.08.01`) with a `remix` spec, to a person on a roster, watermarked and revocable. It should not be
built here.

**Public remix program** — stems to everyone, anonymously, irrevocably. This is the actual feature and it has
almost nothing in common with the first (parent R-02).

Three properties define the public case:

**It is irrevocable.** Once a fan downloads the a cappella, it exists forever. Watermarking (the cross-cut) is
meaningless when the recipient is everyone. Revocation is impossible. There is no undo, and the UI must say so
before the button, not after.

**Every contributor has a stake.** Publishing stems releases *their* performance as raw material. A session
drummer who played on the record did not agree to their isolated drum take being downloadable by 40,000 people —
and might well object (DT-02).

**Source declarations must be verified first** (parent CX-05) — the strongest candidate for a genuine hard block
in the whole domain, because the harm lands on third parties who never consented to the risk.

### Happy Path

1. Artist decides to run a remix competition on a released single
2. Selects the stems to publish — deliberately excluding the a cappella (the highest-risk stem)
3. System checks source declarations (`07.08.04`); all clear (parent CX-05)
4. **Every contributor on the roster is notified and can object** before publication (DT-02)
5. Program publishes with terms; fans download; remixes are submitted; the record of who remixed what is itself provenance

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Stems contain an undeclared third-party sample | **The worst case in the sub-domain** (parent CX-05). Every downloader inherits a liability they cannot see. Strongest hard-block candidate in the domain. | Publication blocked pending declaration review |
| A contributor objects to their stem being published | Real and legitimate. A drummer's isolated take being downloadable forever is a professional exposure they never agreed to | See DT-02 — objection must be possible **before** publication |
| Artist publishes, then regrets it | **No undo exists.** The files are gone. The platform can stop *serving* them and cannot recall them | Warned unambiguously, before the act |
| A cappella published | The highest-value leak in the industry, published on purpose. Legitimate — and it deserves a specific, non-generic warning | "The a cappella can be used in any track, forever" |
| Remix submitted that samples something else | A new source declaration problem, one level down | Submission carries its own declaration requirement |
| Program on an **unreleased** song | Contradicts the whole domain. Almost certainly an error. | Strongly discouraged; heavy confirmation |
| Terms breached by a remixer | Enforcement is outside the platform's power; the terms are evidence, not a fence | Consistent with `07.03.03` D-05 — no overclaiming |
| Fan downloads and re-releases the stems as their own | Not preventable. The published stems are public material | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | No program | Absent |
| Draft | Program being configured | Stem picker, terms, contributor notification list |
| Blocked | Undeclared sources | The specific blocker (parent CX-05) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A remix program is a handoff package with "the public" as the recipient | The elegant unification — it would reuse `07.08.01`'s spec and QC machinery for free, and it is how I first classified it | ❌ **REJECTED** (parent R-02) — and the elegance is exactly the trap. A handoff is **private, identity-bound, watermarked, revocable, to a known professional under known terms**. A public program is **anonymous, irrevocable, un-watermarkable, to everyone**. Modelling the public as a "recipient" inherits every wrong default: per-recipient watermarking is meaningless when the recipient is everyone; revocation is a button that cannot work; the access log records nothing useful. They share a verb ("send stems") and not one property. **The unification would have produced a feature that lies about what it can do** — and given how many times this domain has rejected overclaiming (`07.03.03` D-05, `07.05.02` D-04, `07.06.03` D-01, `07.08.04` D-05), building a revoke button that cannot revoke would be the worst instance of it. |
| DT-02 | The artist can publish stems unilaterally, since it is their song | The default assumption — they own the record and it is their commercial decision | ❌ **REJECTED — and nobody in the sweep noticed this.** Publishing stems releases **each contributor's isolated performance as public raw material, forever**. A session drummer agreed to play on a record; they did not agree to their solo drum take being downloadable by 40,000 strangers, loopable, meme-able, and re-releasable. That is a distinct and material professional exposure, and their master-side split does not obviously grant it. There is a clean parallel with `07.04.03` DT-03 (takes are personally sensitive) and it lands harder here because the audience is unbounded. **The roster must be notified with an objection window before publication.** Whether an objection can *veto* is a rights question above this feature's pay grade (Q-01) — but proceeding without even telling them is indefensible, and it is what every current tool does. |
| DT-03 | This feature belongs in domain 07 | Its position in the candidate list, inherited unexamined | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — it is a tenant, not a native.** A remix program is a **fan-engagement and marketing** act: it exists to generate participation, content and reach, which is domain 20 (Fanbase) and 21 (Promotion) work. It sits in 07 only because the *artifact* is a stem pack and the *gate* is a source declaration — i.e. it borrows this domain's assets and checks and then does something entirely foreign with them. Notice what it does to this domain's Role Matrix: it is the sole Fan cell in 24 features, and that anomaly is the tell. **Proposed boundary**: 07 owns the *stem set and its clearance gate*; 20/21 own the *program* — its terms, its submissions, its judging, its promotion. Under that split there is barely a feature here, which is the honest reading. Escalated as sub-domain Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A **commissioned remix is a handoff** (`07.08.01` with a `remix` spec) and is not built here | Only the public program is this feature; they share a verb and nothing else. |
| D-02 | A public program is **irrevocable**, and the UI says so **before** the act | Per DT-01 — a revoke button that cannot revoke would be the domain's worst overclaim. |
| D-03 | **Contributors are notified with an objection window before publication** | Per DT-02 — publishing someone's isolated performance to an unbounded audience is a material exposure they never agreed to. |
| D-04 | Source declarations are **verified before publication** — the strongest hard-block candidate in the domain | Parent CX-05 — the harm lands on third parties who never consented to the risk. |
| D-05 | No watermarking; no false protection claims | Per DT-01 — meaningless when the recipient is everyone. |
| D-06 | **Proposed boundary**: 07 owns the stem set and the clearance gate; 20/21 own the program | Per DT-03 — under this split there is barely a feature left here, which is the honest reading. |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | [OWNER] Per DT-02 — can a contributor **veto** publication of their stem, or only object? A veto gives a session player a hold over the artist's commercial decision; no veto means their performance goes public over their stated objection. A real rights question, not a UX one. Domain 09 does not answer it: `09.01.03` D-07's owner-consent rule governs **owners** by share and expressly "does not replace … performer approval", and `09.01.05` (Performer & Neighbouring Rights) records no consent or veto power at all. A non-owning session player therefore has no vote anywhere in the tree, which is precisely the gap this question names. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-02 | [OWNER] Per DT-03 / sub-domain Q-03 — **does this feature belong in domain 07 at all?** Recommendation: the program moves to 20/21; the stem set and clearance gate stay (D-06's proposed boundary). It is the only Fan cell in 24 features, and that anomaly is the tell. Ratified together with parent Q-03 and `07.08.01` Q-04. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-03 | [OWNER] Does the platform host remix **submissions**, and do submitted remixes get their own provenance (they are derivative works with their own credits, and they are also excellent provenance data)? That is a genuinely attractive idea and it is squarely domain 20's — where nothing on remixes exists yet, so there is no owner to answer it. Downstream of Q-02: whether to build it at all is scope only the owner can set. | User | `/create-prd` |
