## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Handoff Package Builder ↔ Automated Audio QC

**Relationship**: There is no universal QC ruleset (`07.04.04` DT-03) — the recipient spec supplies it. A
mastering handoff checks headroom, sample rate consistency and that the mix is the canonical one. A sync handoff
checks that the instrumental exists and contains no vocal bleed. A DSP delivery checks true peak and metadata
completeness. Same machinery, different rules, selected by who is receiving.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full on both — they build packages and read the QC results.
- **Musician**: Read-only. They see that a package went out and whether it passed, not the check detail.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the spec is reference data; the package and its QC results are per-build artifacts. Nothing contested.
2. **Trigger chain**: build → resolve spec → run QC → report. **QC warns; it does not block the build** (`07.04.04` D-03) — except for integrity failures (`07.04.05`, parent CX-04 there), where shipping a corrupt master is the one harm large enough to justify a hard stop.
3. **Permission intersection**: building a package requires access to everything in it, so the package is bounded by the builder's vault grants (`07.03.03`).
4. **Notification fan-out**: QC failures notify the builder only. The recipient never sees a failed build.
5. **State transition conflict**: a version supersedes mid-build. The package pins what it built from (`07.04.02` D-03).

### CX-02: Source Declaration ↔ Readiness Score

**Relationship**: An undeclared sample is categorically different from a missing BPM. Both are incompleteness;
only one **exposes other people**. `meta/personas.md` names the harm precisely — a stem with an uncleared sample
exposes every collaborator downstream, none of whom did anything wrong and none of whom can discover the problem.
So the score cannot treat all gaps as equal weight.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full on both. The person who introduces an undeclared sample is usually the person the score is talking to.
- **Producer**: Full — often the one who actually knows what is in the track.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none — but a Fan is downstream of the harm via `07.08.05` (CX-05).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — the declaration is an input to the score.
2. **Trigger chain**: declaration missing → readiness reduced → surfaced at handoff/release. Whether it ever *blocks* is sub-domain Q-01, and this is the strongest candidate for a legitimate block in the entire domain.
3. **Permission intersection**: anyone on the roster can declare a source; nobody can declare on another's behalf without attribution.
4. **Notification fan-out**: a declaration added late notifies the roster — because it may change everyone's position.
5. **State transition conflict**: a sample declared **after** release. Real, and the platform's response matters: routes to 09/11 for retroactive clearance, and every downstream artifact is affected.

### CX-03: Automated Audio QC ↔ Readiness Score

**Relationship**: Technical failures feed the score alongside metadata gaps. A song whose master clips is not
ready, however complete its paperwork. This is what stops the score being a paperwork score.

**Role scoping**: Producer Full; Musician Read-only on QC and Full on the score (it is their song's readiness).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — QC results are derived.
2. **Trigger chain**: version ingested → QC → score recomputed. QC is async; the score must render honestly while checks are pending rather than showing a falsely high number.
3. **Permission intersection**: no.
4. **Notification fan-out**: only material changes notify — a score that notifies on every recompute becomes noise and gets muted, taking the important notifications with it.
5. **State transition conflict**: a canonical change (`07.04.02`) recomputes readiness against the new canonical.

### CX-04: Readiness Score ↔ Handoff Package Builder

**Relationship**: **The score is recipient-relative, and this is the design decision that makes it useful.** A
song can be entirely ready for a mix handoff (the stems are good, they sum, they are named) and nowhere near
ready for a DSP release (no ISRC, no splits, no clearances). A single global "80% complete" answers a question
nobody asked. The right question is always "ready for *what*".

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full — "can I release this?" is their question, and it is the one the score exists to answer.
- **Producer**: Full — "can I hand this off?" is theirs.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — the score is derived per target.
2. **Trigger chain**: pick a target → score against its spec → the gaps become an actionable list rather than a number.
3. **Permission intersection**: readiness may depend on facts the viewer cannot see (a split they are not party to). The score must then say "incomplete" without disclosing what is missing — an awkward and necessary state.
4. **Notification fan-out**: "this song is now ready to release" is one of the genuinely good notifications in the product.
5. **State transition conflict**: none.

### CX-05: Source Declaration ↔ Remix Programs

**Relationship**: **The highest-consequence edge in the sub-domain.** A remix program publishes stems to the
public. If those stems contain an undeclared third-party sample, the artist has handed that liability to every
fan who downloads them — people with no relationship to the rights holder, no knowledge of the problem, and no way
to discover it. `meta/personas.md`'s named harm ("exposes every collaborator downstream") scales from a handful of
collaborators to an entire fanbase.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full — they publish the program and own the consequence.
- **Producer**: Config.
- **Fan**: Read-only, and **the party harmed** — the only place in domain 07 where a Fan can be hurt by a provenance failure.
- **Operator**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none.
2. **Trigger chain**: publish program → **must** verify source declarations first. This is the sub-domain's strongest candidate for a genuine hard block (sub-domain Q-01), because the harm lands on third parties who never consented to the risk.
3. **Permission intersection**: publishing stems requires rights to publish them — which the platform can partially know (`07.03.03` is "rights-aware") and cannot fully verify.
4. **Notification fan-out**: publishing notifies the roster — every contributor has a stake in their work being released as raw material.
5. **State transition conflict**: a sample declared after the program is live. The stems are already out and cannot be recalled. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Readiness Score | Production Stage Board (`07.01.03`) — **as a gate** | Considered blocking stage transitions on readiness. Rejected for the fourth time in this domain (see `07.02` R-03, `07.06` R-03, `07.07` R-02): the board must never become an enemy (`07.01.03` D-02), because a board people avoid captures nothing. The score *informs* the board — a card can show completeness debt — but never gates it. The gate, if one exists anywhere, belongs at **release** (domain 12) and at **remix publication** (CX-05), where the consequences are external and irreversible rather than internal and creative. |
| R-02 | Remix Programs | Handoff Package Builder | Considered making a remix program a handoff package with "the public" as the recipient — elegant, and it reuses the spec/QC machinery for free. Rejected: a handoff is a **private, identity-bound, watermarked** transfer to a known professional under known terms; a remix program is a **public, anonymous, irrevocable** release. They share a verb and nothing else. Modelling the public as a "recipient" would inherit exactly the wrong defaults — watermarking per recipient is meaningless when the recipient is everyone, and revocation is impossible once stems are downloaded. |
| R-03 | Automated Audio QC | Take & Comp (`07.04.03`) | Considered QC-ing individual takes. Rejected: takes are raw performance material where "flawed" is not a defect — a clipped take can be the best take, and a system flagging a singer's cracked note as a technical failure would be both wrong and cruel (`07.04.03` DT-03 established how sensitive takes are). QC belongs at the **deliverable** boundary, where a technical fault has a downstream consequence, not at the performance boundary, where it is just what happened in the room. |
