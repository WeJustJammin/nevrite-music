## Overview

Declaring what in this song did not originate with the people making it: a sample of a recording, an
interpolation of a composition, a library loop, a preset, an AI-generated stem. It is the provenance thesis
pointed **backwards** — the domain's other features capture who *made* this; this one captures where the parts
*came from*. Both are facts that are trivially knowable at the moment of creation and effectively unknowable
three years later, which is exactly when they become expensive.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares sources on their own contributions. As a **writer**, declares interpolations (lyrics or melodies quoting another work). Sees the song's full source picture — including, crucially, what *other people* brought in, which is the thing that can hurt them and that they currently have no way to learn. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the one who **actually knows** — they pulled the loop from Splice, they ran the AI stem separator, they know the drums came from a library. Their declaration is the highest-quality one available. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible here — but a Fan is the **downstream victim** of a failure via `07.08.05` (parent CX-05). |

## Behavior

A declaration attaches to an **asset or a section**, not to the song as a whole — "the drums in the second verse
are from a library" is the actual fact, and a song-level flag cannot express it or clear it.

Types differ legally and cannot be collapsed:

| Type | What it is | Clears through |
|---|---|---|
| **Sample** | A piece of a *recording* | Both master and publishing — two rights holders, two negotiations |
| **Interpolation** | A re-performed *composition* (melody or lyric quote) | Publishing only |
| **Library / preset** | Licensed material | Its own licence terms — usually pre-cleared, sometimes not |
| **AI-generated** | Machine-produced audio | Not a clearance question at all — a *copyrightability* and *disclosure* question |

The platform's job is **capture and propagation**, not clearance. Clearance is domains 09 and 11 (sub-domain
Q-02). What 07 owns is being present at the moment the fact is knowable and recording it — and then making sure
it travels to everyone it can harm.

That propagation is the point: `meta/personas.md`'s harm is that the sample exposes **every collaborator
downstream**, none of whom did anything wrong and none of whom can discover the problem. A declaration is how they
find out while it is still cheap.

### Happy Path

1. Producer drops a library loop into the second verse
2. The DAW bridge (`07.09.02`) sees an audio file whose origin is a known sample library and asks — **at that
   moment**, not at delivery — "this came from Splice. Declare it?"
3. One tap; declaration attached to the region, with the library and licence terms recorded
4. It flows into readiness (parent CX-02), the handoff package (`07.08.01`), and the roster's view — everyone on
   the song can now see what is in it
5. At sync pitch time (domain 11), the clearance status is already known rather than reconstructed

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Undeclared sample discovered after release | The harm the feature exists to prevent. Routes to 09/11 for retroactive clearance; every downstream artifact is affected | Prominent; the roster is notified — they are exposed and did not know |
| Contributor declares a sample **the others did not know about** | **The whole value.** They find out now, cheaply, instead of via a claim | Roster notified: "Alex declared a sample in the second verse" |
| AI-generated stem | Not a clearance question. A copyrightability question (in the US, purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable), a DSP-policy question, and a credit question | Declared as AI with the tool recorded — see DT-03 |
| Sample is from the artist's **own** earlier work | Still a declaration; still needs the split from that earlier work applied. Self-sampling is not free | Links to the source Song if it is on the platform |
| Producer does not know what the previous engineer used | Genuinely unknowable. Declarable as "unknown origin" — an honest state that is far better than silence | "Origin unknown" — flagged as a risk, not a failure |
| Nobody declares anything | The realistic default without a prompt at the right moment (DT-02). This is why the DAW bridge matters here | Readiness gap at release |
| A "preset" that is a full melodic loop | The line between preset and sample is genuinely blurry and the user will not know it either | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| None | No declarations | "No sources declared" — ambiguous between clean and unexamined (see DT-04) |
| Declared | Sources recorded | List by asset/section, with type and status |
| Unknown origin | Declared as unknown | Honest risk flag |
| Cleared | Clearance recorded (via 09/11) | Cleared badge |
| Uncleared | Declared, not cleared | Readiness gap, weighted heavily (parent CX-02) |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the **provenance thesis pointed backwards**, and its absence from 35 candidates is a real gap | `meta/personas.md` names it as the Musician's worst accidental behavior with a named blast radius: "exposes **every collaborator downstream**." `meta/problem-statement.md`'s logic applies exactly — the fact (this loop came from Splice on 14 Nov) is free to record now and effectively unrecoverable in three years, when a claim arrives and nobody remembers where the drums came from. The sweep found 35 candidates about capturing who *made* the song and none about where the *parts came from*. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the same argument that justifies the entire domain justifies this feature. It is arguably the **second-most-thesis-aligned feature in domain 07** after `07.06.02`, and it was invisible to a 14-lens sweep. |
| DT-02 | Declaration must be prompted **at the moment of use**, not at delivery | Direct application of `07.06.02` DT-01's logic. At delivery, "did you use any samples?" is a blank question about work done weeks ago by possibly someone else — it will be answered "no" by default, honestly and wrongly. At the moment the loop lands in the session, the bridge can see a file that came from a known library path and ask a *confirmable* question. **Same principle, same dependency, same failure without it.** | ✅ CONFIRMED — this feature is **structurally dependent on `07.09` (the DAW bridge)**, exactly like `07.04.01` and `07.06.02`. Without it, this is a delivery-time form that gets a false "no". Three features in this domain now share that dependency, which is itself the strongest available argument for the bridge's MoSCoW placement. |
| DT-03 | AI declaration is the same mechanism as sample declaration | The merge that created this feature (samples + AI in one node) | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — the mechanism is shared, the semantics are not, and conflating them would be wrong in a way that matters.** A sample is a **clearance** question: someone owns it, you negotiate, you pay. AI-generated audio is a **copyrightability and disclosure** question: in the US, purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable, so an AI stem may mean part of the record *nobody owns* — which is not a clearance problem, it is a rights-model problem, and it propagates into domain 09's splits in a way no sample does. Plus DSP policies on AI content are evolving and inconsistent, and the disclosure obligation is regulatory rather than contractual. **Shared capture surface, divergent downstream handling.** They stay in one feature because the *capture moment* is identical (declare where this came from) and because forcing users to find two different forms guarantees neither gets used — but the model must not treat AI as a sample with a different label. |
| DT-04 | Declaring nothing means the song is clean | The natural reading of an empty declaration list, and the one a sync agent or a label will make | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the feature's sharpest trap.** An empty list is ambiguous between "we checked and there is nothing" and "nobody ever thought about it", and those are opposite facts with opposite risk. Worse: a sync agent seeing "no samples declared" will read it as a **clearance assurance the platform never made**, which turns a UI ambiguity into a commercial representation. The fix is to make the distinction explicit — a song is either **`sources declared: none`** (someone actively confirmed) or **`sources not reviewed`** (nobody looked), and those must never render alike. This mirrors `07.04.05` D-05 and `07.06.03` D-01: **honest degradation over false assurance**, now the fourth appearance of that pattern in this domain and unambiguously a global principle rather than a local choice. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Declarations attach to an **asset or section**, not to the song | "The drums in verse 2" is the actual fact; a song-level flag can neither express nor clear it. |
| D-02 | Prompted **at the moment of use** via the DAW bridge, not at delivery | Per DT-02 — a delivery-time question gets an honest, wrong "no". |
| D-03 | 07 owns **capture and propagation**; 09/11 own **clearance** | Sub-domain Q-02 — the natural boundary of the thesis: capture at source, clear elsewhere. |
| D-04 | AI content shares the capture surface but **not** the downstream model | Per DT-03 — a clearance question and a copyrightability question are not the same thing. |
| D-05 | **`sources declared: none`** and **`sources not reviewed`** are distinct states that never render alike | Per DT-04 — an empty list read as clearance assurance is a representation the platform never made. |
| D-06 | "Unknown origin" is a **first-class, honest** state | Genuinely unknowable is common (a previous engineer's work) and far better recorded than silently absent. |
