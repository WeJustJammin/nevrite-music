## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Idea Capture Inbox ↔ Lyric / Chart Workspace

**Relationship**: An idea is pre-Song: a voice memo, a phrase, a photo of a napkin. When it graduates, the
system creates a Song (`07.01.01`) and attaches the original artifact **immutably** as the work's origin
record. The capture timestamp and the capturing identity become the first attribution event. This matters far
beyond convenience: in an authorship dispute, "I have a timestamped voice memo from before we met" is the
evidence that settles it, and today that evidence lives in a phone that gets replaced.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: captures ideas; graduates them; is the default first attributee of anything they captured.
- **Producer**: captures ideas in-session (a loop, a chord move); graduation may attribute *them* — which is the point of the counter-attestation model, since a producer's captured idea is real authorship the industry routinely erases.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the idea is immutable once captured — nothing to merge. Graduation is a one-way copy-forward; the original artifact is never edited, only referenced.
2. **Trigger chain**: graduate → create Song → attach origin artifact → write first attribution event to domain 02. If the Song creation fails, no attribution is written and the idea stays in the inbox — the write must be atomic, or a dispute could hinge on a half-written record.
3. **Permission intersection**: an idea captured in a shared band inbox is visible to the band; graduating it into a Song owned by one member is a **quiet appropriation risk** — the exact anti-persona behavior in `meta/personas.md`. Graduation from a shared inbox should notify the inbox's members. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: graduation from a shared inbox notifies its members (per the above).
5. **State transition conflict**: two people graduating the same idea concurrently must yield one Song, not two. Duplicate detection at `07.01.01` covers the near-miss.

### CX-02: Chart Workspace ↔ Lyric Workspace

**Relationship**: The section map is shared structure, owned by the chart. The lyric document's blocks are
anchored to sections rather than to absolute line numbers, so that reordering the arrangement (moving the
bridge earlier — a routine edit) moves the lyric with it and, crucially, **carries its attribution along**.
Anchoring attribution to absolute line positions would silently corrupt authorship on every arrangement edit.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: edits both; experiences them as one document with two views.
- **Producer**: edits the chart (arrangement is squarely producer territory); read-only on lyrics per sub-domain D-02. A producer reordering sections therefore *moves* lyric content they cannot edit — which is correct, and is exactly why anchoring must be section-relative.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the chart owns the section map. The lyric holds section-anchored blocks. Deleting a section with lyrics in it must not destroy attributed content — it orphans the block into an "unplaced" bin. Never a silent delete.
2. **Trigger chain**: section rename → lyric block re-labels (reference, not copy). Section delete → lyric block orphaned, user notified.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes, and it is asymmetric by design — Producer can restructure sections but not edit words. See the Role scoping note above.
4. **Notification fan-out**: structural edits notify writers with attribution in affected sections — their attributed work just moved.
5. **State transition conflict**: concurrent section reorder + lyric edit is the live race. Real-time co-editing is a cross-cut mechanism (see the domain CX file), not solved here.

### CX-03: Lyric ↔ Chart — composition attribution

**Relationship**: Medium confidence, blocked on Q-01. Both surfaces emit composition-side attribution into the
same publishing split, and words-vs-music can be attributed to different people for the same section. Whether
the product models this granularity or simply records "these four people are the writers" is unresolved and is
the highest-value question in this sub-domain.

**Synthesis questions**: deferred to Step 5 per the template's Medium/Low confidence rule.

## Rejected Pairs

| R-01 | Idea Capture Inbox | Audio Version Control (`07.04`) | Considered treating a voice-memo idea as version 0 of the song, putting the inbox inside version control. Rejected: an idea has no Song to be a version *of* — that is what graduation creates. Forcing ideas into version control would require a Song to exist before the idea, inverting the workflow. The origin artifact is attached to the Song at graduation (CX-01) and is deliberately **outside** the version lineage: it is evidence, not a candidate for release. |
| R-02 | Chart Workspace | Take & Comp Management (`07.04.03`) | Considered linking chart bars to takes, so a comp could be assembled against the chart. Genuinely attractive, and it is how a tracking session actually works ("let's punch in from bar 33"). Rejected **for this level**: it is a cross-sub-domain edge (composition ↔ recording), so it belongs in the domain CX file, not here. Recorded there instead. |
| R-03 | Lyric Workspace | Production Stage Board (`07.01.03`) | Considered a `lyrics done` stage gate. Rejected: lyrics are rewritten in the vocal booth, after tracking, routinely — a stage gate implying lyrics complete before recording models a workflow that does not exist. The stage vocabulary stays audio-craft-specific per `07.01` D-02. |
