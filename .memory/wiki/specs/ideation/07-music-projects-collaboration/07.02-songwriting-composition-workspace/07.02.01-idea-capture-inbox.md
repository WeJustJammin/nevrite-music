# Feature: Idea Capture Inbox

> **Number**: 07.02.01
> **Parent**: [Songwriting & Composition Workspace](./07.02-songwriting-composition-workspace-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A low-friction bin for musical ideas that do not yet deserve a Song — a hummed melody, a riff on a phone, a
lyric fragment, a photo of handwriting, a two-bar loop bounced out of a session. It exists because the gap
between "I had an idea" and "there is a record of it" is where songs die, and because the timestamp on that
first capture is the earliest and often the only evidence of authorship that exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Captures ideas in seconds — record, upload, or type. Sees a personal inbox plus any shared band inboxes they belong to. Graduates an idea into a Song when it earns it. Tags and searches ideas ("that thing in D minor from last spring"). |
| Producer | Full | Captures in-session fragments — a loop, a chord move, a bounced sketch — often into a shared or client-visible inbox. Graduation may attribute *them*, which is real authorship the industry routinely erases (see parent CX-01). |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Deliberately: an idea inbox is the most private surface in the product, and a Fan-visible "unreleased ideas" surface would be a serious trust breach. |

## Behavior

Capture must cost nothing. The idea inbox is judged entirely by whether a musician reaches for it instead of
their phone's voice memo app at 1am — and the honest baseline is that the voice memo app is very hard to beat,
because it is one button on a lock screen. Anything requiring login, project selection, or naming has already
lost.

Ideas are **immutable** once captured. They are not edited or versioned — a second attempt at the same riff is
a second idea. This is what makes the timestamp evidentiary: an editable artifact proves nothing.

Graduation (idea → Song) attaches the original artifact to the Song permanently as its origin record and writes
the first attribution event. The idea is never consumed or deleted by graduation.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens the inbox (or a capture shortcut) and hits record
2. Hums 20 seconds of a melody; stops
3. System stores it immediately with a timestamp, auto-titles it from date/context ("Idea — 14 Nov, 01:12"), and returns to idle — **no naming step, no save dialog, no project picker**
4. Weeks later they search the inbox, find it, and hit "Make this a song"
5. System creates a Song (`07.01.01`), attaches the original recording as the origin artifact, and records the capture timestamp as the work's first attribution event

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Offline capture (rehearsal room, no signal — the single most common capture context) | Recorded locally, queued, synced when connectivity returns. The local timestamp is preserved as the capture time. | Idea shows a "will sync" indicator; no data loss, no blocking |
| Inbox becomes a graveyard of hundreds of untitled ideas | Accepted — this is the normal end state, not a defect. Search, tags and auto-derived musical attributes (key, tempo) do the retrieval work. | Searchable list; optional "ideas you never came back to" resurfacing |
| Idea graduated from a **shared band inbox** by one member into a personally-owned Song | Permitted but notified — this is a quiet-appropriation risk (parent CX-01, synthesis Q3) | Band members notified: "Alex made 'riff idea 14 Nov' into a song" |
| Two people graduate the same shared idea | Duplicate detection at `07.01.01` catches the near-miss and offers a merge | "Alex made this a song 2 minutes ago. Use theirs?" |
| Idea deleted after graduation | Refused — it is now the Song's origin evidence | "This is the origin record for 'Ghost Town' and can't be deleted" |
| Capture interrupted (call comes in mid-record) | Partial audio saved, never discarded — a truncated idea beats no idea | Idea saved with a "truncated" note |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | Inbox open | Big capture affordance; recent ideas below |
| Recording | Capture started | Live waveform, elapsed time, stop |
| Saving / Queued | Capture stopped | Optimistic entry, sync indicator if offline |
| Empty | No ideas | "Nothing here yet" + capture CTA |
| Populated | Ideas exist | Reverse-chronological list with inline playback, tags, search |
| Error | Sync failed | Idea retained locally with a retry affordance — **never dropped** |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The capture timestamp is the feature's real value, not the storage — it is the earliest evidence of authorship, and evidentiary weight requires **immutability** | Authorship disputes are settled by "who had it first". `meta/problem-statement.md`: "the facts of the work are never recorded... then reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." An idea inbox that lets you edit the artifact proves nothing. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives immutability and the origin-artifact behavior. Also drives what would otherwise look like over-engineering: an idea you can't delete after graduation. |
| DT-02 | This feature's real competitor is the **phone's built-in voice memo app**, and WeJammin loses on friction unless capture is genuinely one action | Honest competitive read. The lock-screen voice memo is free, offline, instant, and already there. Any flow with a login or a project picker is dead on arrival — and the domain's thesis is capture as close to the source as the surface allows, so losing the *first* capture moment undermines everything downstream. | ✅ CONFIRMED — but with a hard consequence flagged: **on responsive web (`meta/constraints.md`: web is the only declared surface) this feature cannot win.** No lock-screen entry point, no reliable background mic, no offline-first guarantee. This is concrete product evidence for the open mobile-surface question in `meta/constraints.md` — see Q-01. **Two 2026-07-22 corrections to the framing, neither of which rescues the feature**: (a) capture-at-source is the *direction*, not the v1 claim (domain D-11) — for the v1 window the platform captures at the first sharing moment plus the close prompt, so this feature is not carrying the thesis alone; (b) `ideation-index.md` D-28 (User, 2026-07-18) already locked **PWA installability and web push** into v1, which supplies a home-screen icon but still **not** a lock-screen mic or a background recorder. The gap DT-02 names is narrower than it was and is still real. |
| DT-03 | Ideas should be versioned, so a musician can iterate a riff in place | Instinct carried over from `07.04` version control | ❌ REJECTED — contradicts DT-01. Versioning implies mutation, and a mutable artifact has no evidentiary value. A second pass at a riff is a *second idea*, cheaply captured; they can be linked, but never merged into one editable object. Iteration is what a Song is for; the inbox is for the moment before there is a song. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Ideas are **immutable** — never edited, never versioned | Per DT-01/DT-03. Immutability is what makes the timestamp evidence. |
| D-02 | No required naming, project selection or save step at capture | Per DT-02 — the competitor is a one-button voice memo. Every field added loses capture events. |
| D-03 | Graduation attaches the origin artifact permanently; the idea survives graduation | The origin artifact is the work's earliest provenance and outlives the inbox. |
| D-04 | Offline capture with local timestamp preservation is a **requirement**, not an enhancement | The most common capture context (rehearsal room, van, backstage) has no signal. An online-only inbox captures nothing at the moment that matters. |
