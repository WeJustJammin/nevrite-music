# Feature: Chord, Arrangement & Chart Workspace

> **Number**: 07.02.03
> **Parent**: [Songwriting & Composition Workspace](./07.02-songwriting-composition-workspace-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The song's structure in the form working musicians actually use: a chart. Section map (`intro / verse / chorus /
bridge`), bar counts, chord symbols, key, tempo, time signature, and the practical annotations that make a
session run ("half-time feel from bar 33", "no drums till the chorus"). It is explicitly **not notation** — a
chart is what gets handed to a session player twenty minutes before downbeat, and it is the most widely used
document in professional music while being the least well served by software.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Writes and reads charts. As a session player this is the document they are handed and play from — often on a phone or tablet on a music stand, in a dark room, one-handed. As a writer, they define the arrangement here. Owns the section map that the lyric workspace anchors to. |
| Producer | Full | Arrangement is squarely producer territory — restructuring sections, changing bar counts, marking the feel changes. Note the asymmetry: a Producer can reorder sections (which moves lyric blocks) while being read-only on the words themselves (sub-domain D-02, parent CX-02). |
| Operator | None | Not their business. A backline tech might want a chart, but that is a show-production concern (domain 18), not a studio operator one. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The chart owns the **section map**, which is the spine of the whole composition workspace: the lyric document
anchors its blocks to sections (parent CX-02), and the take/comp workflow refers to sections and bars ("take 4
is good from the second chorus"). Reordering the arrangement therefore moves lyrics with their attribution
intact — the reason section-relative anchoring is non-negotiable.

Chords are entered as **symbols** (`Bbmaj7#11`), not as notes on a staff. Optional Nashville Number System
display is a genuine professional need rather than a nicety: session players in Nashville and far beyond read
numbers so a chart transposes for free when the singer wants it a third down — a decision routinely made in the
room, minutes before recording.

The **reading** context deserves as much design weight as the writing context, and gets less attention than it
deserves in every existing tool: a chart is read on a stand, at a distance, in poor light, while both hands are
occupied. Legibility, transposition and page-turn behavior are the product.

### Happy Path

1. Musician creates a chart on a Song; sets key and tempo — **typed by hand for the whole v1 window**, since no non-web client is authorised to read them from a session (`07.09` D-05); they arrive pre-filled from the DAW bridge (`07.09`) only if that gate reopens
2. Lays out sections: intro 4 bars, verse 16, chorus 8
3. Enters chords per bar; adds a feel annotation on the bridge
4. Session day: hits transpose to Eb because the singer wants it lower — the whole chart moves, and the Nashville view did not need to
5. Players open it on their phones from the session record (`07.06.01`); the chart is the document the session runs from

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Transpose to a key with awkward enharmonics (`D#` vs `Eb`) | System picks the conventional spelling for the target key and allows manual override — this is a real musical judgement, not a lookup | Transposed chart, with a note that spellings can be overridden |
| Section deleted while lyrics are anchored to it | Lyric block orphaned to "unplaced", never deleted (parent CX-02) | Warning naming the affected lyric content before confirm |
| Free-time / rubato section with no bar count | Supported — not everything is in 4/4 with countable bars, and a tool that requires bar counts fails at the intro of a huge share of real songs | Section marked free-time; no bar grid |
| Chord symbol the parser does not recognise | Accepted verbatim as free text. **Never rejected** — musicians write chords in idiosyncratic ways and a chart tool that argues with your notation gets closed. | Symbol shown as typed; transposition unavailable for that symbol with a quiet indicator |
| Time signature changes mid-song | Supported per section | Per-section meter |
| Reading on a phone on a stand, mid-take | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the reading surface (font scaling, contrast, auto-scroll, page turns, hands-free advance) is under-specified and is arguably the whole feature | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Chart opened | Section skeletons |
| Empty | New chart | Key/tempo prompt and an "add first section" CTA |
| Populated | Sections and chords exist | Full chart — sections, bars, chords, annotations |
| Reading mode | Opened on a small screen / explicitly entered | High-contrast, large-type, minimal-chrome view built for a music stand |
| Transposed | Transpose applied | Chart in the new key with an indicator of the original |
| Error | Save failed | Local buffer retained, retry offered |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The chart is a **reading** document more than a writing document, and the reading context (dark room, music stand, phone, hands full) should drive its design | Nobody in the sweep's candidate list distinguished authoring from reading. Yet a chart is written once and read by five people under bad conditions — the read is where the value is delivered, and where every existing tool is weakest. This also connects directly to the mobile-surface question in `meta/constraints.md`: a chart on a stand is a phone/tablet artifact, not a desktop browser artifact. | ✅ CONFIRMED — "reading mode" is a first-class state, not a responsive afterthought. Adds concrete weight to the open mobile-surface question. |
| DT-02 | Nashville Number System support is a niche nicety that can be cut | Initial scoping instinct — it looks like a regional curiosity | ❌ **REJECTED** — it is the opposite of niche. NNS exists precisely because the key changes at the last minute, which is the single most common late change in a tracking session. A chart that cannot transpose instantly is the chart that gets rewritten by hand on the day. It is also cheap: if chords are stored as symbols with a known key, numbers are a derived view, not a separate document. Rejecting the cut. |
| DT-03 | The chart should link bars to takes, so comping can be assembled against structure ("take 4 from bar 33") | Real session workflow — punch-ins and comps are discussed in bars and sections, always | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **cross-sub-domain edge**, not built here. This is composition (`07.02`) touching recording (`07.04.03`) and it is recorded in the domain CX file, per the parent's rejected-pair R-02. Flagged because it is the interaction that would make comping feel native to musicians rather than to software engineers — and because if `07.04.03` is built without it, retrofitting is expensive. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Charts, **not notation** | Per parent Q-02 — engraving is an enormous specialism with entrenched incumbents (Dorico, Sibelius, MuseScore) and near-zero provenance value. A chart is what professionals actually pass around. |
| D-02 | Chords stored as **symbols with a key**, making transposition and Nashville numbers derived views | Per DT-02 — makes the highest-value session behavior (last-minute key change) nearly free. |
| D-03 | Unrecognised chord symbols are accepted verbatim, never rejected | A tool that argues with a musician's notation gets closed. Graceful degradation: the symbol just does not transpose. |
| D-04 | The chart owns the **section map**; the lyric workspace anchors to it | Per parent CX-02 — section-relative anchoring is what preserves lyric attribution across arrangement edits. |
| D-05 | Reading mode is a first-class state | Per DT-01. |
