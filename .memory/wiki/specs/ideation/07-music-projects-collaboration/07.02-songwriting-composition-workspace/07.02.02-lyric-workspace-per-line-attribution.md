# Feature: Lyric Workspace with Per-Line Attribution

> **Number**: 07.02.02
> **Parent**: [Songwriting & Composition Workspace](./07.02-songwriting-composition-workspace-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A collaborative lyric document organised by song section, where every line carries a record of who wrote it and
when. It is the composition-side expression of the domain's thesis: `07.06` captures who played on the master,
this captures who wrote the work. The attribution is a **byproduct of writing**, not a form filled in afterwards
— which is the only reason it will ever be accurate.

> ⚠️ **This feature carries the sub-domain's largest open risk (Q-01).** Writers rarely split by line count;
> they agree "equal thirds" in the room. Per-line attribution may be excellent *evidence in a dispute* and a
> poor *split calculator*. The Deep Think below takes that objection seriously rather than designing around it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Writes and edits lyrics in a shared document organised by section. Sees per-line authorship as an ambient, low-key indicator — not a scoreboard. Can view the authorship history of any line. Grants writer access to co-writers. When acting as a **lyricist** this is their primary surface in the whole product. |
| Producer | Read-only | Can read lyrics (they need them to run a vocal session — punch points, comping against words) but **cannot edit by default**, per sub-domain D-02. A producer who genuinely co-writes is granted writer access explicitly by the Musician — and that grant is itself an attributable, logged act, which is the point. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator has no access to a client's unreleased lyrics. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Published lyrics are a domain 20/21 concern and are a *render* of the final text, never this workspace. |

## Behavior

The document is section-anchored (`intro`, `verse 1`, `chorus`), with the section map owned by the chart
(`07.02.03`, parent CX-02). Attribution is captured continuously as people type: each line records its author
and a timestamp, and edits to an existing line record the editor without erasing the original author — the line
carries a history, not a single owner.

The critical design constraint is **restraint in presentation**. A lyric document that renders authorship as
coloured territory turns co-writing into a visible land-grab, and songwriting rooms are socially fragile in a
way that a document with a scoreboard in the margin will not survive. The attribution is recorded faithfully
and displayed quietly — it is there for the day it is needed, not to be argued with while writing.

Attribution here is **evidence**, not arithmetic. It does not compute the publishing split. The split is agreed
by humans in domain 09; this record is what they consult, and what settles the argument when memory conflicts.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens the lyric workspace on a Song; sections already exist from the chart
2. Types the first verse; each line silently records them as author with a timestamp
3. Co-writer joins and rewrites two lines in the chorus; those lines now carry both names in their history
4. Weeks later, when the split conversation happens (fired by the `master approved` prompt in `07.01.03`), they open the document and see who actually wrote what
5. They agree a split — quite possibly not proportional to line count — and record it in domain 09, **citing** this document as the evidence

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Someone pastes in an entire finished lyric written offline | The whole block attributes to the paster, which may be badly wrong (they may have transcribed a co-writer's page) | Paste of a large block prompts once: "Did you write all of this?" with a way to attribute the block to someone else |
| A line is rewritten so heavily it is new | Line history shows both; the system takes **no view** on whether the rewrite constitutes authorship — that is a human judgement it must not pretend to make | History view; no automatic reassignment, no percentages |
| Two people edit the same line simultaneously | Character-level merge via the Real-Time Collaborative Editing cross-cut — no keystrokes lost, no last-writer-wins clobber. The line's history absorbs **both** editors with their timestamps, so authorship reflects the merged contribution rather than a winner | Live cursors and each other's edits appear in real time; the line's authorship history lists both names |
| A Producer (or a writer whose access was revoked) tries to edit without a granted writer role | Edit is refused at the document boundary; read access is unaffected. No silent no-op that looks like a save | Editing affordances are absent with a reason: "Read-only — ask the owner for writer access" |
| The Song has no chart sections yet (chart not started) | The workspace opens with a single default "unsectioned" area so writing is never blocked on the chart; lines written there re-home into sections once the chart creates them (parent CX-02) | "No arrangement yet — write here and lines will slot into sections when the chart exists" |
| Section deleted from the chart while it has attributed lyrics | Lyric block orphaned into an "unplaced" bin, never destroyed (parent CX-02) | "Verse 3 was removed from the arrangement. Its lyrics are in Unplaced." |
| A writer leaves the project / is removed from the roster | Their attribution **persists** — you cannot un-write a line. Access is revoked; authorship is not. | Lines still show their name; they lose edit access |
| Lyric contains a sample/interpolation of another writer's work | Not detected here; declared at `07.08.04`. A lyric quoting another song is an interpolation with real publishing consequences. | Cross-link to the source declaration |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Workspace opened | Section skeletons |
| Empty | No lyrics yet | Section headings from the chart, empty, with a prompt to start writing |
| Populated | Lyrics exist | Section-organised document, quiet authorship indicators, presence of other editors |
| Read-only | Producer, or a writer whose access was revoked | Document renders; editing affordances absent with a reason shown |
| Conflict | Concurrent edit on the same line | Live cursors and merged text render in real time; no blocking modal — the merge is silent and the authorship history absorbs both contributors |
| Error | Save failed | Local buffer retained, retry offered — **never lose typed words** |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Per-line attribution should **compute** the publishing split — the platform knows who wrote 60% of the lines, so it can propose 60% | The obvious feature, and the one the candidate list's name implies | ❌ **REJECTED** — and this is the most important rejection in the sub-domain. Publishing splits are not proportional to line count and never have been. Writers routinely agree equal splits regardless of contribution, partly as a deliberate social technology for keeping bands together. A system that proposes "you wrote 60% of the lines, take 60%" would inject a fight into every writing room and be *wrong* in the majority of cases. The feature's value is **evidentiary, not arithmetical**: it answers "who wrote this?" when memory conflicts. It must never propose a number. |
| DT-02 | Authorship display must be **quiet**, because visible attribution changes writing-room behavior | Second-order reasoning about the social dynamics of co-writing, which no candidate in the sweep addressed. A document rendering each writer's lines in their own colour makes contribution a live scoreboard. People write differently — worse, and more defensively — when scored in real time. | ✅ CONFIRMED — attribution is recorded faithfully, displayed unobtrusively, and surfaced fully only on demand or at the split conversation. This is a real product constraint, not a styling preference. |
| DT-03 | The workspace must handle **lyrics rewritten in the vocal booth**, after tracking — the take that ships often differs from the document | Standard vocal-session reality: lines get changed at the mic and the document is never updated, so the "official" lyric on the DSP is wrong. This is a genuine industry defect nobody in the candidate list noticed. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the lyric document must be editable *after* the song leaves `tracking` (so it cannot be gated by the stage board — confirming parent CX rejected-pair R-03), and the `master approved` prompt should ask "do the lyrics match the take?" A mismatch between the shipped vocal and the registered lyric has real consequences for sync licensing (11) and lyric distribution. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Attribution is **evidence, never arithmetic** — the system does not propose or compute a split from line counts | Per DT-01. This is a values decision as much as a product one: the platform records what happened and lets humans decide what it is worth. |
| D-02 | Authorship is displayed quietly; full attribution is surfaced on demand, not ambiently as a scoreboard | Per DT-02. |
| D-03 | Attribution **persists** after a writer loses access or leaves the project | You cannot un-write a line. Access and authorship are orthogonal — conflating them would let a project owner erase a co-writer by removing them, which is precisely the abuse the platform exists to prevent. |
| D-04 | The lyric document is **not** gated by the production stage board | Per DT-03 — lyrics change at the mic, after tracking. A gate would model a workflow that does not exist. |
