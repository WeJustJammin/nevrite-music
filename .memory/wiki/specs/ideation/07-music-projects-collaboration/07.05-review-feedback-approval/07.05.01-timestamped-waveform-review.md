# Feature: Timestamped Waveform Review

> **Number**: 07.05.01
> **Parent**: [Review, Feedback & Approval](./07.05-review-feedback-approval-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Comments anchored to a moment in a specific version of a song. Click the waveform at 1:47, say "the vocal
disappears here", and the note lives at 1:47 of v6 forever. Per the domain rationale this is "the highest-value
interaction in music collaboration and table stakes" — it is also the feature most likely to get a producer to
open the platform on day one, before they believe anything about provenance.

It solves a specific, universal failure: today this conversation happens as a WhatsApp voice note saying "about
two minutes in, the vocal thing" against a file called `Mix_FINAL_v3_ACTUAL.wav`, and the producer spends ten
minutes finding the moment and guessing which bounce was meant. Every part of that is recoverable if the note
knows its own position and its own version.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Leaves timestamped notes on mixes of songs their vault grant covers; replies; retracts their own; marks their own comments resolved; reopens a comment resolved by someone else if the problem is still audible in the new version ("still there" — D-12). As a band member, sees the other members' internal notes — which is how a band discovers it disagrees about the chorus before the mix engineer does. Sends links (`07.05.02`) and therefore sees the "via link" identity distinction on the notes that come back. Cannot see another link recipient's stream (D-14). |
| Producer | Full | Receives the feedback and works from it; replies; resolves-with-reason (D-05); links a resolution to the version that addresses it (D-09). Chooses the audience of every note they write, and is the persona most exposed by getting that wrong (DT-11) — the internal aside about a client is written on the same surface the client can be given access to. Their real problem is not reading comments but reconciling contradictory ones, which is why `07.05.03` exists as a separate feature. When they are also the song's Musician (the self-produced artist — the multi-hyphenate), they author and resolve the same comment; permitted, and the reason is still on the record (D-05). |
| Operator | None | Not their business. A studio operator who could read their client's mix notes would have visibility into their client's creative business, and per `07.06` R-01 that studio is unbookable. The line held by `07.06.01` D-05 — "the Operator sees the event, never the music" — holds here without exception. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and unreachable by any path. A fan never sees an unreleased mix or the argument about it. **Exception**: a non-roster *link recipient* (parent CX-01) can comment without an account — but that is a link, not a Fan account, and a Fan-account holder gains nothing from holding one (D-15: identity on this surface comes from the vault grant or the link, never from the account tier). |

## Behavior

A comment is `(version, anchor, author, audience, body, created_at)` — five things, all of which are load-bearing,
and the last four of which most tools get wrong.

### The anchor

The anchor is a **point or a range** — "the whole second chorus is too dry" is not a moment, it is a region, and
forcing it to a point makes it wrong (D-02). A point anchor is a single position; a range anchor is `[start, end]`
with `end > start` and a minimum span of 250 ms (below that it is a point and the UI treats it as one).

Where the platform knows the song's tempo and section map — which it does when the version came through the DAW
bridge (`07.09`) — the anchor is stored **musically as well as temporally**: `bar 49 beat 1` / `section: chorus 2`
alongside `00:01:47.320`. The musical anchor is the one that survives an arrangement change (DT-04), and it is the
single largest determinant of whether carry-forward works. Where no tempo map exists, the anchor is timecode only,
and the feature degrades honestly rather than guessing a bar number. **For the whole v1 window that is the only
branch that runs** (D-18): no non-web client is authorised (`07.09` D-05), so no bridge-supplied tempo map exists
and every anchor is timecode-only unless a map arrives by some other route.

Anchor precision must remain **≤ 250 ms at maximum zoom regardless of version length** — a 45-minute live set
zoomed out puts ~2 seconds behind every pixel, and a note anchored ±2 s is a note pointing at the wrong bar.

### The audience — one stream, two scopes

Every comment carries an **audience** fixed at the moment it is posted, and it is one of exactly two values:

| Scope | Who reads it | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Anyone whose vault grant on the song covers the version (`07.03.01` D-05 → parent CX-01). Never a link recipient. | Default for roster-authored comments |
| **Shared with `<link>`** | The roster (as above) **plus** the one link recipient the thread belongs to | Default for a reply to a link comment; explicit for a roster member's own note |

Three rules make this survivable rather than a leak generator (D-13, D-14, DT-11):

1. **The audience is always displayed on the composer**, as words, before a keystroke is typed — "Visible to: roster (5)" or "Visible to: roster (5) + Sam (via link)". Not an icon. Not a colour alone.
2. **A posted comment's scope is immutable.** Internal can never be promoted to shared (the author wrote it believing it was private), and shared can never be demoted to internal (the recipient already read it; hiding it is theatre). To say an internal thing publicly, quote it into a new shared comment — a deliberate act, attributed.
3. **A link recipient sees only their own thread**, never the roster's internal stream and never another link recipient's comments (D-14). The manager must not learn what the label said, and vice versa. Comment visibility is derived from the vault grant and the link — there is **no separate comment ACL** (D-15). One source of truth for access, or the two will drift and one of them will leak.

### Entry

Entry must be fast and possible while listening. The realistic input context is a producer with headphones on who
does not want to stop playback to type — and, increasingly, a phone at a loading dock.

- **Playback never stops to compose** (D-04). The composer is an overlay; the audio keeps running behind it.
- **The anchor is captured at compose-press, not at post.** The user presses the comment control at 1:47 and types for twenty seconds; the note anchors at 1:47, not 2:07. This is the single most consequential detail in the entry flow and the one most tools get wrong.
- **The anchor is draggable before posting**, with audio scrubbing to it as it moves, because human reaction lag is real and the platform must not fabricate a correction for it (DT-06).
- **On viewports < 768 px the waveform tap seeks, it does not anchor.** A finger on a 375 px waveform is ±3 s of error. The mobile anchor comes from the playhead at compose-press. Precision comes from the drag-and-scrub, not from the tap.

### Version anchoring and carry-forward

Comments anchor to a **version, never to the song** (sub-domain D-02). This is the design decision that makes the
loop honest: a note about v6's vocal balance applied to v9 — where the vocal was already fixed and something else
changed — causes a genuinely wrong fix. Every tool that anchors comments to "the track" has this bug.

The consequence is the feature's hardest problem, and DT-01 named it: when v7 lands, what happens to v6's open
comments? The resolution (D-07 … D-12):

**1. Open comments carry forward along the lineage.** When v7 is created deriving from v6 (`07.04.01` D-03), every
comment on v6 with status `open` is carried to v7. Carry is **transitive while open** — a v6 note still open at v9
has carried three times, and its provenance line reads "written on v6 · open through v7, v8".

**2. Carry is a second view of one comment, never a copy.** Replies land on the one comment. Resolving it resolves
it everywhere. Duplicated comments are how a review surface becomes a graveyard.

**3. Only the direct lineage ancestor carries.** Siblings do not cross-pollinate. If v6a and v6b are parallel mixes
(`07.04` D-02 — no branching, no merging, siblings resolved by choosing), a note on v6a does not appear on a version
derived from v6b. It was written about audio that version never contained.

**4. Position is mapped by confidence tier, and the tier is disclosed:**

| Tier | Condition | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact** | Durations match within ±50 ms and the version's change record (`07.04.01` D-04) shows no timeline edit | Marker at the mapped position, no caveat |
| **Musical** | A tempo/section map exists on both versions (`07.09`) and the anchor has a bar/beat | Marker at the mapped bar, caveat "re-placed by bar — v7's timeline moved" |
| **Uncertain** | Durations differ, no musical anchor, no reported edit | **No marker.** The comment goes to the carried-unplaced list |

**5. An uncertain comment is never given a fabricated position** (D-03). It appears in a **carried-unplaced list**
beside the waveform, quoting its original anchor verbatim — "Sam, written at 1:47 of v6" — with two actions:
**Hear it on v6** (plays the *original* version at the original position, which is the only truthful way to convey
what was meant), and **Place it** (a human drags it onto v7 with scrub feedback). A placement made this way is
recorded as an act by that human, timestamped and attributed — it is never presented as something the system knew.

**6. Carried comments live in their own lane.** A new version renders two comment lanes: **"On this version"** and
**"Carried (n)"**. They are never merged into one marker row — v7's fresh notes are what the producer is working on
and must not be drowned by v6's residue. The carried lane is collapsed by default when `n > 10`.

**7. A new version never resolves anything** (D-08 / DT-07). A bounce is not a fix. Auto-resolving on version
creation would let a producer clear an inconvenient note by rendering a file, and would silently discard exactly the
fact this product exists to preserve.

**8. Resolution requires a reason, a resolving version, or both — never neither** (D-05, D-09). "Fixed in v7" is a
reason *with a link*, and it is the common case: at bounce time the producer ticks which open comments this version
addresses. The author is notified with what they can act on.

**9. The author can reopen** — "still there" (D-12). They listen to v7 at the mapped position, still hear the
problem, and the comment reopens against v7 with a new anchor and an incremented reopen count. **The reopen count is
the feature's most valuable derived signal**: a note on its third return is not a comment, it is a disagreement, and
it belongs in triage (`07.05.03`) and in the round conversation (`07.05.05`).

**10. Staleness is surfaced, never actioned.** A comment that has carried unresolved through **3 successive
versions** is flagged `stale`, sorted to the top of the carried lane, and fires **one** notification to the version
author — not one per version. Nothing is auto-closed. Ever.

### Optional axes

- **Stem/element tag** (DT-10): where the version is a stem set (`07.04.04`) or the stem list is known, a comment may optionally name what it is about — "the snare", "the vocal bus". Optional, never required: most comments come from clients who do not think in stems, and a required field would kill the two-second entry D-04 exists to protect.
- **Playback context** (DT-05): the comment optionally records what the commenter was listening on — the first question every mix engineer asks about every note. Auto-captured only where the platform can genuinely know it (device class; output device label where the browser grants it), offered as a one-tap choice otherwise (`phone speaker / earbuds / laptop / headphones / monitors / car`), and rendered as **"unknown"** where neither applies. Never inferred, never guessed.

### Notification

Consumes the `Notification & Messaging Fan-Out` cross-cut, with this feature's own batching rules:

| Event | Who is notified | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| First comment on a version by an author | The version's author + roster members with review notifications on | Immediate |
| Subsequent comments by the same author on the same version | Same | Batched into a 15-minute digest — a client leaving 12 notes in 6 minutes must not fire 12 notifications |
| A link recipient comments | The link's creator | **Immediate, always, never batched** — this is the thing they are waiting for |
| Reply | Thread participants (within the thread's audience) | Immediate |
| Resolution / rejection | The comment's author, with the reason (D-05, parent CX-02) | Immediate |
| Comment goes `stale` (3 carries) | The version's author | Once, on crossing the threshold |

### Voice and reference comments — behavior specified, shipping deferred

Per DT-02 these are the two additions worth more than better text. The **scope decision is the owner's** (Q-02 →
MoSCoW); the **behavior is specified now** so the answer is implementation-ready either way:

- **Voice comment**: hold-to-record on the same anchor, 60 s maximum. Playing a voice note seeks the mix to the anchor and plays the mix **underneath the note at −12 dB** — the note plays over the moment it is about, which is exactly how this conversation happens in a room. Auto-transcribed for search and for clustering (`07.05.03`); the transcript is an assistant and the **audio is the record** — a wrong transcript never overrides what was said. Transcription unavailable → the note still posts, the transcript reads "not transcribed", nothing blocks.
- **Reference-linked comment**: links to a position in another version of this song, another song in the project, or an item on the mix reference board (`07.07.01`). Renders as an inline playable chip; playing it jumps to the reference and returns to where you were. References are **by board item**, never by arbitrary URL — WeJammin does not host third-party audio, and curating the board is `07.07.01`'s flow, not this composer's. A board item that is an external link opens externally.

### Record integrity

- **Comments are never deleted** (D-05). An author may **retract** (D-10): the body is hidden, "retracted by Sam" is shown, the record is retained, and any triage state the producer already set survives with the source marked retracted. This covers the real case — the thing said in anger about a bandmate's take — without letting the record be rewritten.
- **Edits are a typo window, not a revision system** (D-11): editable within **5 minutes**, only while the comment has **zero replies** and **has not been triaged**. Outside that: no edit. Retract and repost, or add a follow-up. A comment the producer already acted on cannot change underneath them.
- Comments appear in **the order the platform received them**; a commenter's device clock never reorders the thread — a phone with the wrong time cannot make its note jump ahead of one written before it.

## Happy Path

1. Musician opens v6 from the version timeline (`07.04.01`) on the review player; the waveform renders and v6's markers stream in
2. They press play; at 1:47 the vocal drops behind the guitars
3. They press the comment control at 1:49 — reaction lag — and the composer opens **with playback still running**, anchored to the playhead at compose-press
4. They drag the anchor back ~2 s; the audio scrubs as it moves; they land it on 1:47.3 and hear the exact moment confirm itself
5. They type "vox disappears under the gtrs here". The composer reads **"Visible to: roster (5)"** the whole time
6. They press Enter; the marker lands at 1:47.3 of v6, attributed, in the "On this version" lane
7. The Producer is notified immediately (first comment from this author on v6)
8. Producer opens it, clicks the marker, lands at 1:47.3 within the seek budget, and hears exactly the moment — no hunting, no "about two minutes in"
9. They fix it and bounce v7 through the DAW bridge *(post-gate only — in v1 v7 arrives by manual upload, D-18)*; at bounce time they tick this comment as **addressed by v7**
10. The comment resolves with `resolved in v7` — a reason with a link. The Musician is notified with something they can act on
11. Musician opens v7, clicks through from the resolution, hears 1:47 in the new mix, and it is fixed. The comment stays visible, dimmed, permanently attached to v6 where it was true
12. Three other v6 notes were not addressed; they carry into v7's **Carried (3)** lane, exact-tier (v7 is the same length), and the loop continues

## Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| New version lands with open comments | Open comments carry along the lineage into the **Carried** lane, unresolved-against-the-version-they-were-written-on (D-07) | "Carried (4)" lane, each showing "written on v6" |
| v7 is the same length as v6 | **Exact tier** — 1:1 mapping, no caveat | Marker at the mapped position, normal treatment |
| v7 is 8 s longer; no tempo map | **Uncertain tier** — no marker is fabricated (D-03) | Carried-unplaced list: "Sam, written at 1:47 of v6 — v7's timeline moved. [Hear it on v6] [Place it]" |
| v7 cut 4 bars from the intro; tempo/section map present (`07.09`) | **Musical tier** — the anchor re-places by bar, which is still correct | Marker at the new time, caveat "re-placed by bar 49" |
| A **range** comment spans an edit boundary | Treated as uncertain even if one end maps — half a correct range is a wrong range | Unplaced, original range quoted verbatim |
| Human drags an uncertain comment onto v7 | Recorded as **their** act — attributed and timestamped, never presented as system knowledge | "Placed on v7 by Producer" on the comment |
| Comment carried unresolved through 3 versions | Flagged `stale`, sorted top of the carried lane, **one** notification (D-08) | "Open since v6 — 3 versions ago" |
| Producer bounces v7 while someone is mid-compose on v6 | The comment lands on **v6** — its target was explicit — then carries to v7 by the normal rules | "v7 landed while you were writing. Your note is on v6 and carried to v7." |
| Two people comment on the same moment | Both land; append-only; clustered by `07.05.03` | Stacked markers |
| Two comments posted at the same instant (race) | Both persist. They appear in the order the platform received them; a genuine tie resolves to one fixed order so every viewer sees the same sequence | Both rendered, stable order across reloads |
| Two producers resolve the same comment simultaneously | **First resolution wins.** The second's typed reason is offered as a reply rather than discarded — never lose typed text | "Already resolved by Ana 2 s ago. Post your reason as a reply?" |
| Reply arrives while the comment is being resolved | Reply lands; the resolution stands; the resolver is notified. A reply does **not** reopen — reopening is an explicit act by the author (D-12) | Resolved comment with a new reply beneath it |
| Author disagrees with someone else's resolution | Reopens with "still there"; comment reopens against the current version with a new anchor; reopen count increments (D-12) | "Reopened by Sam — 2nd time" |
| Reopen/resolve ping-pong | Nothing is blocked. At **3 reopens** the comment is flagged to triage (`07.05.03`) — it is a disagreement, not a comment | "Reopened 3× — this is a decision, not a note" |
| Someone resolves their own comment (self-produced artist — the multi-hyphenate in two roles) | Permitted. Author and resolver are the same person, shown as such, and the reason is still required — the record is what closes the loophole, not a permission check | "Resolved by Sam (author)" |
| One human is both a roster member and a link recipient | **Roster identity wins.** If the viewer has an authenticated session with a vault grant on the song, the link is ignored for identity and they comment as themselves (D-15) | Their normal identity; no "via link" mark |
| Link recipient comments | Lands with **weaker identity**, visibly distinguished. Their typed name is never matched to a platform account, never linked to a profile, never given a verification-implying avatar (D-06) | "Sam (via link)" — never rendered identically to an authenticated member |
| Link recipient types "Rick Rubin" as their name | It renders as typed text, unlinked and unverified. The invited email is visible to the link's sender. The platform makes **no identity claim it cannot support** | "Rick Rubin (via link — sent to s.okafor@…)" on the sender's view |
| Link recipient tries to read the band's internal comments | Not possible — they are outside the audience (D-13/D-14). Their existence is not disclosed either; an "n hidden comments" counter is itself a leak | Their own thread only |
| One link recipient tries to see another's comments | Not possible. Per-link isolation (D-14) — the manager does not learn what the label said | Their own thread only |
| Producer writes an internal aside on a link recipient's thread | Supported and valuable ("ignore this, he always says this"). Renders to the roster with a **persistent distinct background**, not an icon — invisible to the recipient (DT-11) | Inline internal reply, unmistakably marked |
| Someone tries to promote an internal note to shared | **Refused.** Scope is immutable (D-13). Quoting it into a new shared comment is the supported path — a deliberate, attributed act | "Internal notes can't be shared. Quote it in a new comment?" |
| Link revoked while the recipient is mid-compose | A post submitted within **60 s of revocation** is accepted (parent CX-01 — losing feedback someone took the trouble to give is worse than a slightly late close). After that the post is refused and **the text stays on screen to copy** | "This link is no longer active — your comment wasn't posted. Copy your text before leaving." |
| Link expires mid-listen | Grace to the end of the current play (`07.05.02`); commenting is available for the same grace | Playback and commenting complete; refresh is dead |
| Network drops mid-compose | The draft is saved locally as they type — at most a couple of seconds of typing is ever at risk. On reconnect the post is **retried automatically up to 3 times over roughly 20 seconds** with widening gaps, then held as a draft with a manual Retry. **A typed comment is never discarded** | "Not posted — saved as a draft. [Retry]" |
| Post fails permanently | Draft retained across reload and across sessions | Draft badge on the version; "1 unsent note" |
| Comment on a version that is not the canonical mix | **Never blocked** — reviewing an old mix is legitimate (an A&R with a two-week-old link). Warned, with a one-click switch that preserves the position | "You're commenting on v6. The current mix is v9. [Switch to v9 at 1:47]" |
| Version's audio has been tiered to cold storage (`07.04.01` Q-01) | Comments remain fully readable; playback requires a restore. Comments never depend on hot bytes | "Audio archived — restore to listen." Restore ETA comes from the storage tier; the platform states it rather than estimating it |
| Waveform rendering fails; audio streams fine | **Feedback is never blocked on a visualisation.** Falls back to a time scrubber; anchoring, seeking and commenting all still work | Scrubber with markers as a timeline strip; "waveform unavailable" |
| Audio still transcoding | Comments readable; playback disabled | "Preparing audio…" with the existing comment list live |
| 45-minute live set / DJ mix | Zoomable waveform; anchor precision holds at ≤ 250 ms at max zoom | Zoom control; marker density thins at low zoom with a count badge |
| 60 comments on one version | Normal for a label record. This feature renders them; making them survivable is `07.05.03`'s job | Marker clustering by proximity + full list; "60 comments — [Open triage]" |
| Comment body contains HTML or script | Escaped, always. Rendered as text, never as markup | The literal text they typed |
| Comment body exceeds the cap | Capped at **5,000 characters**; counter appears at 4,750; input stops at the cap rather than truncating on post | "4,812 / 5,000" |
| Link recipient floods comments | Rate limited: **20 comments/hour/link** for link recipients, **60/hour/song** for authenticated roster members — unauthenticated is stricter by design. Over the limit is refused, not queued | "You're posting too fast — try again in 12 minutes." The roster gets an anomaly signal (`07.05.02`) |
| Comment author leaves the roster | Comment persists; access to the song is revoked (`07.03.01` D-05 — a member who leaves keeps the credit for the take they played; the same rule keeps the note they left) | Comment remains attributed |
| Author's account is erased (GDPR) | **Identity is redacted; the record is not destroyed** (D-16). Body redaction, where the body itself carries personal data, is a domain 24 action and never self-serve | "Removed user" with the body intact |
| Author retracts a comment | Body hidden, "retracted by Sam" shown, record retained, triage state preserved and marked retracted-source (D-10) | "Sam retracted this comment" |
| Author edits 6 minutes later / after a reply / after triage | Refused. Retract, or add a follow-up | "Comments can't be edited after 5 minutes or once someone's replied." |
| New roster member added at v9 | Comment visibility follows the **vault grant** (`07.03.01` D-05 → parent CX-01). There is no separate comment ACL (D-15). If their grant covers the song's history they see v6's comments; if not, they are not shown and their existence is not counted | Whatever their grant covers — nothing more, no teaser counter |
| Reply thread becomes a full discussion | Supported, but the argument about the chorus is not what this feature is for. Long threads are a signal for `07.05.03` | Threaded replies with collapse; "8 replies — [Open triage]" |
| Producer disagrees and does not do it | **Resolve-with-reason, not silent close** (parent CX-02, D-05) | The author is told, and told why |
| Another user posts while you are listening | Comment appears live without a reload (`Real-Time Collaborative Editing & Presence` cross-cut) | Marker fades in; no scroll jump, no playback interruption |

## States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Version opened | Waveform skeleton; transport usable as soon as audio is ready; markers stream in |
| Empty | No comments on this version and none carried | Waveform with a hint: "Play it and click where something needs attention" |
| Populated | Comments exist | Two lanes — "On this version" and "Carried (n)"; list alongside; open/resolved filter; author filter |
| Composing | Compose-press | Inline composer with a live audience label; anchor draggable with scrub feedback; **playback keeps running** (D-04) |
| Carried forward | Comments inherited from a lineage ancestor | Distinct lane, "written on v6" provenance line, tier caveat where applicable |
| Carried-unplaced | Uncertain-tier carry (D-03) | List beside the waveform, original anchor quoted, [Hear it on v6] / [Place it] — **no marker on the waveform** |
| Stale | Carried unresolved through 3 versions | Flag; sorted to the top of the carried lane; one notification |
| Resolved | Marked resolved with a reason and/or a resolving version | Dimmed; still visible; reason and version link shown; never deleted; [Still there] available to the author |
| Retracted | Author retracted | "Sam retracted this comment"; body hidden; the record and any triage state persist |
| Partial | Older comments paginated, or a member's grant covers only part of the history | Loaded set with "Load earlier"; grant-limited views show nothing they cannot access, with no teaser count |
| Offline | Connection lost | Existing comments readable from cache; composer stays open; drafts queued; "Offline — your note is saved and will post when you reconnect" |
| Error | Post failed after retries | Local draft retained, manual retry offered — **never lose a typed comment** |
| Audio unavailable | Version tiered to cold storage, or transcode incomplete | Comments fully readable; playback control replaced by "Restore to listen" / "Preparing audio…" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The unsolved problem is comment carry-forward across versions**, and nobody in the sweep noticed it | Version-anchoring (sub-domain D-02) is obviously right — but it creates the hard case immediately. A mix has 12 open comments on v6; the producer fixes 8 and bounces v7. Do the 4 remaining comments appear on v7? If **no**, they are lost and the artist re-types them, and the feature has failed at its job. If **yes**, they must be re-anchored to a version whose timeline may have shifted — and a comment silently moved to the wrong bar is worse than one that was lost, because it produces a confident wrong fix. Every existing tool in this space either drops the comments or lies about their position. | ✅ CONFIRMED as **the** design problem of this feature — **and resolved at Step 5**: D-07 (carry along lineage, one comment not a copy, siblings excluded), D-03/D-08 (three confidence tiers; uncertain never gets a fabricated marker; carried-unplaced list with "Hear it on v6" and human "Place it"), D-08 (no auto-resolve; staleness surfaced at 3 carries, never actioned), D-12 (author reopen; reopen count as the derived signal). The refusal to fabricate a position is the trustworthy choice and it is now a specified behavior, not a sentiment. |
| DT-02 | Text comments are sufficient | Implicit in the candidate | ❌ **REJECTED** — musicians communicate about music badly in text. Voice notes also connect back to the domain's own thesis — capture in the medium the work happens in. **Behavior for both is specified at Step 5**; only the ship decision remains (Q-02). |
| DT-03 | This feature is a differentiator | The domain rationale calls it "the highest-value interaction in music collaboration" | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED AS TABLE STAKES, NOT DIFFERENTIATOR** — the rationale itself says "and table stakes", and it is right. Frame.io does this for video; Disco, Filepass, Bandlab and Splice do versions of it for audio; every producer already has a tool. So this feature **wins adoption, not defensibility** — it is the daily hook of D-18's consolidation half, not the moat. Its actual differentiation is structural rather than featural: the comment attaches to a *version in a lineage, with a roster, that produces a credit* — so the review that a competitor treats as the whole product is here just the surface where provenance gets manufactured. That framing matters for MoSCoW: this must be **excellent** because it is the door, and it must not be mistaken for the reason to stay. |
| DT-04 | **Timecode is the wrong anchor — musicians think in bars and sections, not seconds** | Domain-expert reasoning; nobody in the sweep or the breadth pass raised it. A producer says "the second chorus" or "bar 49"; "1:47" is a proxy they translate to reluctantly. The consequence is structural, not cosmetic: **a timecode anchor breaks under any arrangement edit and a musical anchor does not.** If v7 cuts four bars from the intro, a note at 1:47 now points at the wrong bar — but a note on "bar 49 beat 1" is still exactly right. This is the missing half of DT-01: the carry-forward problem is only unsolvable for versions whose musical position is unknown. | ✅ **CONFIRMED** — the anchor is stored musically **and** temporally wherever a tempo/section map exists, and the musical anchor is authoritative for carry-forward (D-04 tier table). **Honest caveat, not hidden**: the map only exists where the version came through the DAW bridge (`07.09`), so most versions will be timecode-only at first and degrade to the uncertain tier. This is a real coupling — the review feature gets materially better when the bridge is present, which strengthens `07.04.01` Q-03's point that `07.09`'s MoSCoW placement drags others with it. Escalated as Q-05. |
| DT-05 | **A mix note is meaningless without knowing what it was heard on** | Domain-expert reasoning. Every mix engineer's first response to "the vocal's too quiet" is "what were you listening on?" — because on a phone speaker it is *true and should not be fixed*. Fixing a phone-speaker note on monitors damages the mix for everyone else. This is universal professional practice and no reviewed tool in the space captures it. | ✅ **CONFIRMED** — optional playback-context capture: auto-recorded only where genuinely knowable (device class; output label where the browser grants it), one-tap otherwise, **"unknown" where neither** — never inferred. Non-blocking, because a required field kills the two-second entry D-04 protects. The payoff is downstream: "4 of 6 'too quiet' notes came from phone speakers" is precisely the insight that stops a producer making a bad fix — which makes this a real input to `07.05.03`'s clustering, and arguably the strongest non-obvious feature in the sub-domain. |
| DT-06 | The system should auto-correct for reaction lag by anchoring at `playhead − 1.5 s` | Agent proposal — the lag is real: recognising a problem, deciding to flag it, and finding the button takes 1–2 s, so every note lands late | ❌ **REJECTED as an automatic offset**, ✅ **CONFIRMED as a problem** — the lag is real and unhandled, but a silent 1.5 s shift is a **fabricated position**, which is the exact sin D-03 forbids two paragraphs earlier. It would be wrong for anyone who paused first, wrong for range comments, and unfalsifiable to the user. The honest fix is two behaviors, not a constant: **anchor at compose-press, not at post** (the 20 s of typing must not drag the note to 2:07 — this alone is the single highest-value entry detail and most tools get it wrong), and **make the anchor draggable with audio scrub feedback before posting** so the human corrects their own lag in the medium they can actually judge it in. The correction belongs to the ear, not to a constant. |
| DT-07 | A new version should auto-resolve the comments it addresses | Agent proposal — it keeps the list clean and matches the intuition that a bounce means "I did the notes" | ❌ **REJECTED** — a bounce is not a fix. Auto-resolve would let a producer clear inconvenient notes by rendering a file, and would silently destroy the contemporaneous record of what was asked for — the exact class of fact this platform exists to preserve (`meta/problem-statement.md`). It also inverts the incentive on the persona the design most needs to keep honest: `personas.md` records the Producer's worst accidental behavior as closing a project without capturing what happened, and warns that **the lazy path must be the correct path**. Auto-resolve makes the lazy path a cover-up. The supported flow — ticking addressed comments at bounce time — takes the same three seconds and leaves a true record with a version link (D-09). |

Two reasons. (1) Timestamped review exists *because people are not listening together* — it is asynchronous by construction, and presence solves a problem this feature does not have. (2) It is surveillance-adjacent, and this sub-domain already carries that discomfort in `07.05.02` DT-03; "the label can see you scrubbing back to the chorus for the fourth time" is a materially worse version of it, aimed at the persona with the least power in the room. If a shared-listen workflow is wanted, it is a different feature with a different consent model, not a toggle on this one. |

(b) **Erasure.** A link recipient's typed name is personal data, GDPR/CCPA is live in `meta/constraints.md`, and "we never delete" is not a lawful answer. The resolution is that **erasure redacts identity, not the record** (D-16) — but the boundary between "the identity" and "personal data inside the body" is a compliance judgement I cannot make here, so it escalates as Q-04 rather than being invented. |

But the persona who most often comments is a client or a label who does not think in stems and would either abandon the note or pick a wrong tag to get past the field — and a wrongly-tagged note is worse than an untagged one, because it routes a producer's attention to the wrong element with false confidence. Required fields are how a two-second interaction becomes a form, and forms are what D-04 and `personas.md` both say will not be used. Leaked audio is traceable, bounded and survivable (`07.05.02`); a producer's internal "ignore this, he always says this" rendered to the client is a lost account, and no watermark helps. Every messaging tool in history has produced this failure — the reply that went to the wrong audience — and this feature deliberately puts two audiences on one surface (parent CX-01), which is precisely the configuration that generates it. ✅ The confirmed consequence is that scope must be **loud, immutable, and pre-commitment**: the audience is stated in words on the composer before a keystroke (D-13), internal replies carry a persistent distinct background rather than an icon (an icon is a thing you stop seeing by the second week), and a posted scope can never be promoted (D-13). This feature emits the raw stream **plus three signals triage depends on and could not derive itself**: the reopen count (a 3rd return is a disagreement, not a note), the playback context (DT-05 — "4 of 6 'too quiet' notes came from phone speakers"), and the optional stem tag. Triage owns clustering and accept/reject/defer; this feature owns authoring, anchoring and resolve-with-reason. Rejection notifications originate there and land on comments here.
- Touches **07.05.04 Approval Gates** ([./07.05.04-approval-gates-signoff-trail.md](./07.05.04-approval-gates-signoff-trail.md)) — **state pin**: parent CX-05. Approval snapshots the comment state at that instant, including open comments ("approved, but fix the click at 2:14"). Carried and unplaced comments must be pinned in the state they were actually in — a pin that quietly drops the unplaced list misrepresents what was agreed.
- Touches **07.05.05 Revision Rounds** ([./07.05.05-revision-round-counting-scope.md](./07.05.05-revision-round-counting-scope.md)) — **indirect trigger**: rounds start from triage acceptance, never from a comment (parent R-02). The reopen count is the strongest evidence available for the "is this a new round or the same note again?" argument that feature exists to settle.
- Touches **07.07.01 Mix Brief & Reference Board** ([../07.07-mix-master-workflow/](../07.07-mix-master-workflow/)) — **entity reference**: per DT-02, a reference-linked comment points at a **board item**, never an arbitrary URL. The board owns curation and rights posture; this feature owns playing it inline at a position.
- Touches **Domain 24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** — **evidence + moderation + erasure**: the review thread is contemporaneous evidence of who asked for what, which is the provenance thesis applied to craft rather than to splits. Body redaction (as distinct from identity redaction, D-16) is a 24 action, never self-serve. Harassment inside a comment thread is 24's, not this feature's.
- Touches **Domain 02 Credits** — **weak, and deliberately so**: leaving good notes is not a credit. Noted only to close the loop — the review surface must not become a back door into the credit graph.
- **Consumes the `Audio Playback, Waveform Rendering & Streaming Delivery` cross-cut** — with two requirements this feature adds and the cross-cut must carry: anchor precision ≤ 250 ms at max zoom on any duration, and a **scrubber fallback when waveform rendering fails** (feedback is never blocked on a visualisation).
- **Consumes the `Real-Time Collaborative Editing & Presence` cross-cut** — **narrowly**: live comment arrival only. Presence cursors are rejected (DT-08).
- **Consumes the `Notification & Messaging Fan-Out` cross-cut** — with this feature's own batching rules (15-minute digest for same-author runs; link-recipient comments never batched; one stale notification per comment, not per version).
- **Performance inputs for `/create-prd-compile`** (stated as product requirements, not budgets): clicking a marker must land at the anchor fast enough that the reviewer does not lose the moment; live comment arrival must not interrupt playback; the composer must open without a playback stall (D-04). The numeric budget is that workflow's to set.

| D-01 | Comments anchor to a **version**, never to the song | Sub-domain D-02 — a comment applied to the wrong version causes a real wrong fix. |
| D-02 | Comments support **ranges**, not only points; minimum span 250 ms | "The whole second chorus is too dry" is a region; forcing it to a point misrepresents it. |
| D-03 | Carry-forward is **explicit and honestly caveated** — never a fabricated position | Per DT-01 — a silently re-anchored comment is worse than a lost one. Formalised as the three-tier model in D-04/D-07. |
| D-04 | The anchor is stored **musically and temporally** where a tempo/section map exists; the musical anchor is authoritative for carry-forward. Three confidence tiers: **exact** (±50 ms, no timeline edit), **musical** (bar/beat remap, caveated), **uncertain** (no marker) | Per DT-04 — musicians think in bars, and only a musical anchor survives an arrangement edit. Timecode-only is the honest fallback, not a failure. |
| D-05 | Playback **never stops** to compose a comment | The input context is headphones-on, mid-listen. Stopping playback to type loses the thing you were commenting on. |
| D-06 | Resolution is **with reason**, always visible to the author; comments are never deleted | Parent CX-02 — silence makes the author repeat the note next round. |
| D-07 | Link-recipient comments are **visibly distinguished**; typed names are never matched to accounts, linked to profiles, or given verification-implying chrome | Parent CX-01 — an anonymous note and a roster member's note must not look equally authoritative, and the platform must make no identity claim it cannot support. |
| D-08 | **Carry-forward model**: open comments carry along the direct lineage, transitively while open; carry is a second **view** of one comment, never a copy; siblings never cross-pollinate; carried comments render in their own lane, collapsed above 10 | Resolves Q-01 with DT-01. Copies create duplicate threads and a graveyard; siblings are versions of audio the other never contained (`07.04` D-02). |
| D-09 | An **uncertain** carried comment gets **no marker** — it goes to a carried-unplaced list quoting its original anchor, with **Hear it on v6** (plays the original version at the original position) and **Place it** (a human drags it, and the placement is attributed to them) | The truthful way to convey what was meant is to play what was meant. Re-anchoring is a human judgement, so a human makes it and signs it. |
| D-10 | **A new version resolves nothing.** No auto-resolve, ever. Staleness (3 carries) is surfaced with one notification and never auto-actioned | Per DT-07 — a bounce is not a fix, and auto-resolve makes the lazy path a cover-up for the exact persona `personas.md` warns about. |
| D-11 | Resolution requires **a reason, a resolving version link, or both — never neither**. Ticking addressed comments at bounce time is the primary path | "Fixed in v7" is a reason with a link, and it gives the author something they can act on rather than a closed ticket. |
| D-12 | The comment's author (or a roster admin) can **reopen** — "still there". The reopen count is the feature's most valuable derived signal — a note on its third return is a disagreement, and disagreements belong in `07.05.03`, not in a comment thread. |
| D-13 | Comment **audience is fixed at post and immutable** — internal is never promotable to shared, shared is never demotable. Promotion betrays an author who wrote in confidence; demotion is theatre. Quoting into a new comment is the only crossing, and it is deliberate and attributed. Icons stop being seen by week two. |
| D-14 | A link recipient sees **only their own thread** — never the roster's internal stream, never another link recipient's comments. Hidden comments are not counted or teased | Resolves Q-03. The manager must not learn what the label said; a "3 hidden comments" counter is itself a disclosure. |
| D-15 | Comment visibility derives from the **song's vault grant** (`07.03.01` D-05 → parent CX-01) plus the link. **There is no separate comment ACL.** Roster identity always wins over a link identity for the same human | One source of truth for access, or the two drift and one leaks. A grant that lets you hear the version lets you read its internal notes — anything else is a second permission system to keep in sync. |
| D-16 | Authors may **retract** (body hidden, act attributed, record and triage state retained); edits are a **5-minute typo window** requiring zero replies and no triage; **erasure redacts identity, not the record** | Per DT-09 — "never deleted" alone drives people to stop leaving honest notes and is not a lawful answer to a DSAR. Retraction handles the human case; identity redaction handles the legal one; the record survives both. Body-level redaction is domain 24's, not self-serve. |
| D-17 | The review player and the compare player (`07.04.06`) **share the playback/waveform cross-cut engine but are not one component** | Answers sub-domain Q-02. The engine is already a cross-cut (`07.04` D-04). The workflows are different jobs: review is anchor-and-annotate on one version; compare is loudness-matched, position-locked switching between two. Merging the surfaces would compromise both — the comparison needs the switch to be imperceptible, the review needs the composer to be instant. |

Nothing is demoted: `07.09` keeps `Should`, unphased | **Resolves Q-05.** Owner ratification DQ-08.4 (with DQ-08.2), 2026-07-22. The re-scope trigger is conditional on the bridge being a `Won't` and it is not — it is deferred behind an evidence gate. The honesty this question protected is discharged at the thesis instead (`vision.md`, D-70(d)): v1 claims capture at the **first sharing moment**, and this feature is that moment (`07.05.02`) |
