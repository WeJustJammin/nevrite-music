# Feature: Private Share Links & Listen Analytics

> **Number**: 07.05.02
> **Parent**: [Review, Feedback & Approval](./07.05-review-feedback-approval-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Split from sweep candidate 19** per sub-domain D-01. The sweep bundled this with timestamped review; they
> have different shapes and radically different risk. Review is *authenticated collaboration* with people on the
> roster. This is *distribution* to people who are not — and it is the domain's second-largest leak surface,
> which bundling would have hidden inside a comments feature.

## Overview

A link that puts an unreleased mix in front of someone who is not on the roster and has no account: a label A&R,
a manager, a sync agent, a mastering engineer being auditioned, the artist's mum. Plus the record of what they
did with it — did they open it, did they listen, did they finish, how many times. It is the single most-used
professional workflow in music that no roster-based permission model can express, and it is how unreleased audio
actually leaves a project.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Creates links for their manager, label, or a sync pitch. Sees who opened and listened. Revokes. The analytics are the emotionally loaded part — see DT-03. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume user: sends every client mix this way. This is *the* workflow the platform must win to be in the room at all — if a producer sends mixes by Dropbox, WeJammin never sees the session, and every downstream capture is lost. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Cannot receive a project link as a Fan-account action. A fan may *physically* be sent a link — the link does not care who you are — which is precisely the risk this feature manages rather than prevents. |

## Behavior

A link points at a specific version (or a set — a whole record for an A&R), carries an expiry, an optional
passcode, an optional download permission, and per-recipient watermarking (sub-domain D-03). Recipients need no
account, can leave timestamped comments (parent CX-01), and their identity is weak by construction.

**The link is a leak surface and that is not fixable, only manageable.** A link sent to an A&R can be forwarded.
The realistic posture, per `07.03.03` DT-03, is: make leaks *traceable* (watermarking), *bounded* (expiry,
streaming-only by default), and *evidenced* (access log). Never claim prevention.

Listen analytics exist because the information is genuinely valuable — "the label opened it and stopped at 40
seconds" tells an artist something real — and because they are the least comfortable thing in the domain. See
DT-03.

### Happy Path

1. Producer finishes v7; creates a link for the client, expiring in 14 days, streaming-only, watermarked
2. Sends it; client opens on their phone with no account and no signup wall
3. Client plays it twice, leaves two timestamped comments (parent CX-01)
4. Producer sees: opened, played twice, finished both times, two comments
5. Comments enter the same stream as the roster's — visibly marked "via link" — and go to triage (`07.05.03`)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Link forwarded to someone unintended | Cannot be prevented. Watermark makes the *leaked audio* traceable; the access log shows an unexpected device/geo | Owner can see access anomalies and revoke |
| Recipient downloads and the file leaks publicly | Watermark identifies which link the copy came from — the feature's actual purpose | Leak investigation via domain 24 |
| Link recipient shares the link publicly (a hundred opens) | Anomaly detection; auto-revoke is defensible and also risks killing a legitimate label-wide share | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Passcode-protected link, recipient loses the passcode | Standard recovery via the sender, not via the platform — the platform does not know who the recipient is | Sender re-sends |
| Link expires mid-listen | Grace to the end of the current play. Cutting off someone's listen at 2:58 of 3:00 is gratuitous. | Playback completes; refresh is dead |
| Recipient never opens it | The most common real outcome, and useful information | "Not opened in 9 days" — the nudge that gets records unstuck |
| Version superseded while the link is live | Link points at the **pinned version** it was created for; does not silently follow the canonical (`07.04.02` D-03) | Optional "update this link to v8?" prompt |
| Analytics on a link sent to a friend/family | See DT-03 — the surveillance discomfort is real | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Creating | Version picker, expiry, passcode, download toggle, watermark indicator |
| Active | Link live | Link plus live analytics |
| Opened | First access | "Opened by [recipient] 2 h ago" |
| Listened | Playback recorded | Play count, completion, drop-off point |
| Expired | Expiry reached | Link dead; analytics retained |
| Revoked | Owner revoked | Immediate; in-flight playback fails closed |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **This is the workflow the platform must win to be in the room at all** — and it is far more important than its position in the candidate list suggests | `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis (D-18): consolidation puts WeJammin in the room, and being in the room is the precondition for provenance. Sending a mix for review is the *most frequent* thing a producer does with a finished bounce — several times a week, for years. If that one action happens over Dropbox or WeTransfer, the platform is not in the room for the moment that matters, and every downstream capture (`07.06.02`) never fires. Conversely, if the review link is the best in the industry, the platform is present at every review, which is where versions, comments, approvals and credits all get manufactured. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is a **wedge feature disguised as a utility**, and probably the strongest single candidate for the beachhead (`meta/problem-statement.md` Q-03, sub-domain Q-01). It must be genuinely better than WeTransfer at the thing WeTransfer does, or nothing else in the domain gets a chance. |
| DT-02 | Streaming-only links meaningfully protect the audio | The instinct behind "no download" toggles everywhere | ❌ **REJECTED** — anyone can record the output of their own machine, or hold a phone to a speaker. A streaming-only link stops casual file-forwarding and nothing else. Its real value is narrower and worth stating honestly: it prevents **accidental** redistribution (the A&R who forwards the whole email with the attachment) rather than **determined** leaking. Consistent with `07.03.03` DT-03 — the product must not overclaim. Watermarking is the only measure with teeth, and its teeth are *deterrence and attribution after the fact*, not prevention. |
| DT-03 | Listen analytics are an unambiguous benefit | Implicit in the candidate name | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED** — analytics on a private link is **surveillance of a named individual**, and nobody in the sweep said so. "Your manager opened it and stopped at 40 seconds" is genuinely useful and also genuinely uncomfortable: the recipient usually does not know they are being measured, and the reading is often wrong (they stopped because a call came in, not because the chorus is weak). It also creates a nasty social dynamic — an artist watching whether the label has listened yet, and drawing conclusions. Three consequences: (a) recipients must be **told** they are measured, which is likely a GDPR requirement anyway, not merely a courtesy; (b) drop-off must be presented as **a fact, not a verdict** — "stopped at 0:40", never "lost interest at the chorus"; (c) there must be a way to send a link **without** analytics, because sending your mum a mix and then measuring her is grim. Escalated as Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Links require **no account** from the recipient and have no signup wall | Per DT-01 — the A&R will not sign up; a wall means the producer uses WeTransfer and the platform leaves the room. |
| D-02 | Links **pin a version**; they do not follow the canonical | `07.04.02` D-03 — a link silently changing what it plays is the `FINAL_v3` disease in the highest-stakes context. |
| D-03 | Per-recipient **watermarking** by default on any link that permits download | The only measure with teeth (DT-02). |
| D-04 | The product **must not claim** streaming-only prevents leaking | Per DT-02 and `07.03.03` D-05 — overclaiming to professionals is a trust breach. |
| D-05 | Recipients are **told** they are measured; drop-off is presented as fact, not verdict; analytics-free links are possible | Per DT-03 — the consent half is likely a legal requirement, and the framing half is an honesty requirement. |
| D-06 | Expiry grants a grace period to the end of the current play | Cutting someone off at 2:58 of 3:00 is gratuitous. |
