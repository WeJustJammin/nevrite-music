# Feature: Private Share Links & Listen Analytics

> **Number**: 07.05.02
> **Parent**: [Review, Feedback & Approval](./07.05-review-feedback-approval-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Split from sweep candidate 19** per sub-domain D-01. The sweep bundled this with timestamped review; they
> have different shapes and radically different risk. Review is *authenticated collaboration* with people on the
> roster. This is *distribution* to people who are not — and it is the domain's second-largest leak surface,
> which bundling would have hidden inside a comments feature.

## Overview

A link that puts an unreleased mix in front of someone who is not on the roster and has no account: a label A&R,
a manager, a sync agent, a mastering engineer being auditioned, the artist's mum. Plus the record of what they
did with it — did they open it, did they listen, did they finish, how many times. It is the single most-used
professional workflow in music that no roster-based permission model can express, and it is how unreleased audio
actually leaves a project.

Three things it must be at once, and the tension between them is the whole design: **frictionless** (the A&R has
200 links this week and will not sign up — DT-05), **traceable** (a leaked pre-release master is a permanent
loss of control — DT-07), and **honest** (it cannot prevent leaking and must never say it can — D-04). Every
decision below is a trade between those three.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As song owner**: creates links for their manager, label, or a sync pitch; sees every link that exists against their song *including ones the Producer created* (D-12), and can revoke any of them — it is their song and therefore their leak surface. Sees who opened and listened. **As roster member but not owner**: sees only links they created. Clicking someone else's link while signed in resolves to the project view, not the link view (D-11). The analytics are the emotionally loaded part — see DT-03. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume user: sends every client mix this way. This is *the* workflow the platform must win to be in the room at all — if a producer sends mixes by Dropbox, WeJammin never sees the session, and every downstream capture is lost. Creates per-recipient links from a saved recipient list (the same six people, every project). Their links are visible to the song owner (D-12) — a deliberate asymmetry they are told about at creation, not discovered later. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. A studio operator never creates or sees a link to client audio, even for a session that physically happened in their room. Consistent with the domain Role Matrix — the Operator knows the session occurred, never what was made in it. |
| Fan | None | Cannot receive a project link as a Fan-account action, cannot see one in any Fan surface, and no link ever appears in fan-facing discovery. A fan may *physically* be sent a link — the link does not care who you are — which is precisely the risk this feature manages rather than prevents. A signed-in Fan-only account opening a link gets the ordinary anonymous recipient view (D-11 does not fire for them — they have no roster access to upgrade to). |

## Behavior

A **share** is not a URL. A share is a set of **per-recipient links** — one per named human — each pointing at a
specific pinned version (or a set of versions: a whole record for an A&R), each carrying its own expiry,
optional passcode, optional download permission, and its own watermark (DT-04, D-07). Recipients need no
account, can leave timestamped comments (parent CX-01), and their identity is weak by construction: the *sender*
asserts who they are, the platform never demands it (D-14).

A **public link** — one URL, unnamed, for the WhatsApp band thread — also exists, because refusing to build it
means the producer pastes a Dropbox link instead. It is a lesser object with named forfeits: aggregate analytics
only, a watermark that identifies the link rather than a person, and no anomaly detection worth the name. The
product says so at creation, in one line, without a lecture (D-07).

**The link is a leak surface and that is not fixable, only manageable.** A link sent to an A&R can be forwarded.
The realistic posture, per `07.03.03` DT-03, is: make leaks *traceable* (watermarking — of the stream as well as
the download, DT-07), *bounded* (expiry, streaming-only by default), and *evidenced* (access log). Never claim
prevention.

Listen analytics exist because the information is genuinely valuable — "the label opened it and stopped at 40
seconds" tells an artist something real — and because they are the least comfortable thing in the domain. The
recipient is told they are measured; drop-off is reported as a fact, never a verdict; and an analytics-free link
exists for when the recipient is your mum (DT-03, D-05).

Two behaviors that look like plumbing and are actually the feature:

- **The expiry clock starts at first access, not at creation** (DT-06, D-08). The most common real outcome is
  that the link is never opened; a 14-day creation-relative expiry dies before the A&R ever reaches it, and the
  producer's follow-up is a dead link — the worst possible moment for friction.
- **The access log outlives the link.** A private link is the first time a recording leaves the project, dated
  and versioned. That is a **first-disclosure record**, and it is evidence in exactly the disputes this platform
  exists to prevent (DT-12, D-16). The behavioral analytics expire; the fact of disclosure does not.

### Happy Path

1. Producer finishes v7 and opens Share. The recipient list is pre-populated from prior sends — the same six
   people every project (the label A&R, the manager, the client, two bandmates, the sync agent)
2. They pick the client, accept the defaults (expires 14 days after first open, streaming-only, watermarked,
   analytics on), and hit send. One screen, no wizard — the whole act must beat pasting a Dropbox link
3. Platform mints a per-recipient link, pins it to v7 (never the canonical pointer — `07.04.02` D-03), and
   watermarks the stream for that recipient (D-09)
4. Client opens it on their phone, on cellular, with no account and no signup wall. **Audio is audible in under
   2 seconds (P95)** — no download, no app, no "preparing your file". A single line tells them the sender can
   see whether they listened (D-05)
5. Client plays it twice, finishing both times, and leaves two timestamped comments (parent CX-01), typing a
   name once at first comment — never before playback (D-14)
6. Producer sees: opened 14:06, played 2×, completed 2×, furthest position 3:41 of 3:41, two comments. Presented
   as those facts and no interpretation (D-05)
7. Comments enter the same stream as the roster's — visibly marked "via link" (`07.05.01` D-06) — and go to
   triage (`07.05.03`). They do **not** start a revision round; only triage acceptance does (parent R-02)
8. The client cannot see the band's internal comments, and cannot see that the same v7 also went to two other
   labels (D-10)
9. Fourteen days after the client's first open the link expires; playback dies, the analytics remain, and the
   disclosure record — "v7 disclosed to this client on this date" — persists for the life of the song (D-16)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Link forwarded to someone unintended | Cannot be prevented. The watermark makes the *leaked audio* traceable to the recipient the link was minted for; the access log shows an unexpected device/geo | Anomaly alert to the song owner within 5 min; owner can revoke. The platform never says "prevented" |
| Recipient downloads and the file leaks publicly | Watermark identifies which per-recipient link the copy came from — the feature's actual purpose | Leak investigation via domain 24, using the watermark + access trail as evidence |
| Recipient screen-records or line-out captures a **streaming-only** link | Also watermarked (D-09) — this is exactly why the stream carries a mark and not only the download. A streaming-only link is not an untraceable link | Same trace path as a download leak |
| Link recipient shares the link publicly (a hundred opens) | **Alert, never auto-revoke** (DT-11, D-13). At 100 opens the audio is already gone — revoking protects nothing not already taken, and the same signature is produced by a legitimate label-wide share at the moment of maximum commercial consequence | "This link has been opened from 37 devices in 6 countries — expected?" with a one-tap Revoke and a one-tap "This is fine, stop asking" |
| A&R legitimately forwards to their whole label team | Indistinguishable from a leak by signature alone, which is why D-13 refuses to guess. Owner marks it expected; the alert stops for that link | The alert, then silence once dismissed. See Q-04 — a first-class forwardable link may be the honest answer |
| Passcode-protected link, recipient loses the passcode | Recovery is via the sender, never the platform — the platform does not know who the recipient is and must not pretend to | "Ask whoever sent you this link" — no self-service reset, no email-based recovery |
| Passcode brute-forced | 10 attempts per link per hour, then the link locks for 1 h and the owner is alerted | Recipient: "Too many attempts — try again in an hour." Owner: an alert |
| Link expires mid-listen | Grace to the end of the current play, capped at remaining duration + 60 s. Cutting off someone's listen at 2:58 of 3:00 is gratuitous (D-06) | Playback completes; refresh is dead; "This link expired on [date] — ask [sender] for a new one" |
| Link **revoked** mid-listen | Fails closed — the current media segment finishes, the next is refused; audible stop within 10 s. Revocation is a deliberate leak response and does **not** inherit expiry's grace (`07.03.03` D-04) | Playback halts: "Access to this link has been withdrawn" |
| Link revoked while the recipient is mid-comment | Audio dies immediately; a comment whose composition began before revocation is accepted for **120 s** after. Losing feedback someone took the trouble to give is worse than a slightly late close (parent CX-01 Q5) | Comment posts with a note to the roster: "left after the link was revoked" |
| Recipient never opens it | The most common real outcome, and useful information | Sender nudged at 72 h and 7 d; link card reads "Not opened in 9 days" — the nudge that gets records unstuck |
| Version superseded while the link is live | Link points at the **pinned version** it was created for; does not silently follow the canonical (`07.04.02` D-03) | Sender sees "This link plays v7; canonical is now v8 — update it?" Updating mints a **new** link and expires the old one; it never mutates what a live link plays |
| Sender updates the link to v8 while the recipient is listening to v7 | The old link finishes its current play under expiry grace, then dies. The audio a person is hearing never changes underneath them | "This mix has been updated — [new link]" on the dead page |
| Roster member opens a link while signed in | Resolves to the in-project view for that pinned version. Identity is **not** downgraded to "via link", and the listen is **not** measured (DT-09, D-11) | Normal project view; sender's link card shows "opened by a project member — not counted" |
| Roster member opens a link signed out (their own phone, no session) | Anonymous recipient view. Unavoidable — the platform cannot know who they are | Standard recipient view, with a sign-in affordance that upgrades in place if taken |
| Musician who is *also* the client on a Producer's link (the multi-hyphenate) | D-11 fires: they get the project view, and the Producer's analytics show "not counted — project member" rather than a false "never listened" | Producer is told why the count is empty. A silent zero here would be read as "the client is ignoring me" and is simply wrong |
| Two link recipients are shopping rivals (three labels, same v7) | Recipients are **mutually invisible** (DT-08, D-10). No recipient can see that another exists, or what they said | Each sees only their own comments and replies to them |
| Link recipient tries to see the band's internal comments | Not served. The A&R does not see the band arguing about the chorus | The recipient view has one comment scope — their own. There is no visible "hidden" affordance to probe |
| Producer replies to a link comment | Producer must be told **which scope they are replying in** — a reply to a link comment is visible to that recipient. Replying "yeah the label hates it" in the label's scope is a career event | Reply box is labelled with the audience: "Visible to Sam (via link)" |
| Analytics on a link sent to a friend/family | Analytics-free links exist and are one toggle (D-05). See DT-03 — the surveillance discomfort is real | Toggle at creation; recipient's notice says "the sender cannot see whether you listened" |
| Recipient is in a jurisdiction requiring consent for measurement | Notice is shown to every recipient regardless of jurisdiction — a per-geo notice is a bug farm and a bad-faith signal | One line, before playback, non-blocking. Exact model is Q-02 |
| Recipient later creates a WeJammin account with the same email | No account-linking behavior is specified for prior weak-identity listens. Q-02 must decide whether, and under what consent model, any association may occur | No defined behavior; decision routed to Q-02 |
| Link created against a master the song owner does not own (label deal) | Boundary case, not resolved here — `07.03.03` Q-02 owns "who sets policy when the artist holds the song and the label holds the master". This feature inherits that answer | Blocked pending that decision; recorded in Cross-Cut Notes |
| Song deleted / project archived while links are live | All links fail closed. The disclosure record survives the audio (D-16) — it is evidence, and evidence that a deletion erases is not evidence | "This link is no longer available" |
| Audio asset fails to load (storage error, not permissions) | Distinguished from expiry and revocation in the UI. A recipient told "expired" when the truth is "our storage hiccuped" will not come back | "Something went wrong loading this — retry" with retry. Sender notified: the link is fine, delivery failed |
| Recipient on a dead connection mid-listen | Buffered audio plays out; the play is counted only if ≥5 s elapsed. A network drop is not a drop-off, and must never be reported as one | Recipient: buffering state. Sender: no phantom "stopped at 0:40" |
| Recipient scrubs to 2:30 and plays 6 s | Not a completion; furthest position 2:36; play counted. Scrub-then-stop is a *skim*, and reporting it as "listened" would be a lie the artist acts on | "Skimmed — played 6 s from 2:30" |
| Recipient leaves the tab open and audio loops/idles | Idle time is not listen time; playback events only. A tab left open overnight must never produce "played 240 times" | Honest counts |
| Same person opens on phone then desktop | Two devices, one recipient. Counted as one recipient with 2 devices — not 2 opens by 2 people | "Opened on 2 devices" |
| 60 links live across 9 songs, owner wants the picture | Owner-level link inventory: every live link against everything they own, sortable by age and by anomaly. Without it the leak surface is unauditable, which defeats D-12 | An inventory view with bulk revoke |
| Link set contains a whole record (12 songs) for an A&R | Supported — the unit of an A&R pitch is a record, not a track. Analytics are per-track within the set: **which** track they stopped on is the entire point | Per-track breakdown; "played 1, 2, skipped 3, stopped 40 s into 4" |
| Malicious recipient replays to inflate the count | Counts are facts, not scores, and nothing downstream is gated on them (parent R-01). Inflating them buys nothing | No mitigation needed — a metric nobody spends is a metric nobody games |
| Link URL leaks into a search index or a pasted screenshot | Links are unguessable and non-enumerable, and pages carry no-index directives. Beyond that it is the same leak as any forward | Anomaly alert per D-13 |
| Sender revokes the wrong link | Revocation is not undoable — a revoked link stays dead — but re-issuing to the same recipient is one tap and preserves the pinned version and the analytics history | "Revoked. Re-send to Sam?" |
| Sender's own account is suspended (domain 24) | Live links fail closed. Audio distribution by a suspended account is the platform distributing on their behalf | Recipients: "no longer available" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft (sender) | Creating | Version picker (pinned, defaulting to canonical but never following it), recipient picker with prior-recipient list, expiry, passcode, download toggle, analytics toggle, watermark indicator, and the per-recipient/public choice with its forfeits stated in one line |
| Loading (sender) | Link panel opened | Skeleton link cards; analytics stream in after |
| Empty (sender) | No links on this version yet | "No one outside the project has heard v7 yet" + Create link |
| Active (sender) | Link live, unopened | Link, copy affordance, expiry, and an unopened age — "Not opened in 9 days" |
| Opened (sender) | First access | "Opened by Sam 2 h ago" and the expiry clock starts here, not at creation (D-08) |
| Listened (sender) | ≥5 s contiguous audio elapsed | Play count, completions, furthest position at 1 s resolution, per-track for a set. Facts only — no verdict, no "lost interest" |
| Partial (sender) | Analytics unavailable or off | Never a zero. "Analytics off for this link" or "Listen data unavailable — the comment landed" (a failed listen record must never block or fake anything — parent CX-01 Q2) |
| Anomalous (sender) | Anomaly signals tripped | Non-modal alert with the evidence, Revoke, and Dismiss. Never auto-revoked (D-13) |
| Expired (sender/recipient) | Expiry reached | Sender: link dead, analytics retained, one-tap re-issue. Recipient: "This link expired on [date] — ask [sender] for a new one" |
| Revoked (sender/recipient) | Owner or creator revoked | Immediate; in-flight playback fails closed within 10 s; in-flight comments accepted 120 s |
| Loading (recipient) | Link opened | Player chrome and the measurement notice render immediately; **audible sample in under 2 s (P95) on 4G**. Never a spinner-then-download — that is the WeTransfer experience this feature exists to beat (DT-05) |
| Gated (recipient) | Passcode set | Passcode field only. No name demanded, no account offered as a precondition (D-14) |
| Playing (recipient) | Playback | Waveform, timestamped comment affordance, sender identity, measurement notice. Their own comments only (D-10) |
| Upgraded (recipient) | Signed-in roster member opened it | Resolves to the project view; not measured; not marked "via link" (D-11) |
| Error (recipient) | Asset fetch failed | "Something went wrong loading this — retry", explicitly distinguished from expired and revoked |
| Error (sender) | Link mint or analytics fetch failed | Error with retry; a link that may or may not have been minted is never rendered as sent |

| DT-01 | **This is the workflow the platform must win to be in the room at all** — and it is far more important than its position in the candidate list suggests | `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis (D-18): consolidation puts WeJammin in the room, and being in the room is the precondition for provenance. Sending a mix for review is the *most frequent* thing a producer does with a finished bounce — several times a week, for years. If that one action happens over Dropbox or WeTransfer, the platform is not in the room for the moment that matters, and every downstream capture (`07.06.02`) never fires. | DT-02 | Streaming-only links meaningfully protect the audio | The instinct behind "no download" toggles everywhere | ❌ **REJECTED** — anyone can record the output of their own machine, or hold a phone to a speaker. A streaming-only link stops casual file-forwarding and nothing else. Its real value is narrower and worth stating honestly: it prevents **accidental** redistribution (the A&R who forwards the whole email with the attachment) rather than **determined** leaking. Consistent with `07.03.03` DT-03 — the product must not overclaim. Watermarking is the only measure with teeth, and its teeth are *deterrence and attribution after the fact*, not prevention. |
| DT-03 | Listen analytics are an unambiguous benefit | Implicit in the candidate name | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED** — analytics on a private link is **surveillance of a named individual**, and nobody in the sweep said so. "Your manager opened it and stopped at 40 seconds" is genuinely useful and also genuinely uncomfortable: the recipient usually does not know they are being measured, and the reading is often wrong (they stopped because a call came in, not because the chorus is weak). It also creates a nasty social dynamic — an artist watching whether the label has listened yet, and drawing conclusions. Three consequences: (a) recipients must be **told** they are measured, which is likely a GDPR requirement anyway, not merely a courtesy; (b) drop-off must be presented as **a fact, not a verdict** — "stopped at 0:40", never "lost interest at the chorus"; (c) there must be a way to send a link **without** analytics, because sending your mum a mix and then measuring her is grim. Escalated as Q-02. |
| DT-04 | **A link is a person, not a URL** — and the entire feature collapses if it is a URL | Both of this feature's teeth depend on knowing *who*. A watermark that identifies "the link" identifies nobody if the link went to a mailing list. Analytics reading "opened 5 times" is worthless; "Sarah at Atlantic opened it" is the product. Anomaly detection has no baseline without an expected recipient. Yet the sweep, the candidate name, and the ordinary meaning of "share link" all say *one URL*. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a share is a **set of per-recipient links** (D-07). But the inverse is not rejectable: producers genuinely paste one link into a WhatsApp thread with the band, and a product that refuses loses to Dropbox on the spot (DT-01). So the **public link ships too**, as an explicitly lesser object with its forfeits stated at creation: aggregate analytics, link-level watermark, no meaningful anomaly detection. Honest degradation beats a refusal the user routes around. |
| DT-05 | **The competitor is friction, not security** — and the recipient's phone is the battlefield | Nobody chooses WeTransfer for its security posture; they choose it because it is zero-effort. Meanwhile the *recipient* experience of the incumbents is genuinely bad: a Dropbox link makes an A&R download a 60 MB WAV before hearing a note, on a phone, in a car, on cellular. That is the actual competitive gap, and it is a *recipient-side* gap that no sender-side feature list addresses. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it produces a **useful inversion of the domain's mobile question** (domain Q-02). Four features argue for a phone *for the user*. This one argues for the phone *for a person who will never install anything* — an A&R will not download an app to hear your demo, ever. So this feature is an argument **against** a native surface and **for** excellence in responsive web: the recipient path must be the best-executed mobile-web experience in the product. Concretely: audible in under 2 s (P95) on 4G, zero interaction before audio (D-14), no download, no app, no login. Five phone arguments, and this one points the other way — which makes it evidence, not noise. |
| DT-06 | **Expiry measured from creation is the wrong clock** and it fails at the exact moment the feature matters | The file's own edge case already concedes that "recipient never opens it" is *the most common real outcome*. Compose those two facts and the default is self-defeating: a 14-day creation-relative link sent on the 1st is dead on the 15th, and the A&R who finally gets to their inbox on the 20th hits a dead page. The producer's follow-up — the single highest-intent moment in the whole workflow — lands on an error. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the expiry clock **starts at first access** (D-08), with an absolute creation-relative cap so an unopened link does not live forever. This is not a tweak: creation-relative expiry converts the most common outcome into the worst outcome. Nobody in the sweep noticed because nobody modelled the unopened link as the base case, and it is. |
| DT-07 | **The stream must be watermarked, not only the download** — D-03 leaves the larger hole open | D-03 watermarks "any link that permits download". But DT-02 already established that streaming-only stops nothing determined — so under D-03 as written, the *default* link (streaming-only) is the *untraceable* one, and the feature's only measure with teeth is absent from the configuration the product recommends. The likely leak from a streaming-only link is a screen recording or a line-out capture, and both would carry a per-recipient mark if the stream carried one. | ✅ CONFIRMED — closes a real gap in D-03; becomes D-09. Note the honest cost: a per-recipient stream watermark means audio cannot be served from one shared cached object, which has real delivery and cost consequences. That is `/create-prd`'s problem, not this file's — but it is not free, and pretending otherwise would be the same overclaiming this feature is built to avoid. It also **couples to DT-04**: per-recipient watermarking is meaningless without per-recipient links. |
| DT-08 | **Link recipients must be mutually invisible** — and the reason is commercial, not privacy hygiene | Standard, unremarkable industry practice: a track gets shopped to three labels at once, and a sync agent gets it while an A&R has it. Every one of those recipients is a rival. If recipient A can see that recipient B exists, or read B's comments, the platform has disclosed the artist's negotiating position to the counterparty — for free, by default, as a side effect of a comments feature. `07.05.01` Q-03 asks whether an A&R can see the *band's* comments; this is the sharper question nobody asked, because it is recipient-to-recipient rather than recipient-to-roster. | ✅ CONFIRMED — becomes D-10. Each recipient sees only their own comments and replies to them. The corollary bites the Producer: they now reply into **scoped audiences**, and the reply box must name the audience, because "yeah the label hates it" delivered into the label's scope is a career event. This is the one place in the sub-domain where the comment stream is genuinely not one stream. |
| DT-09 | **The multi-hyphenate breaks the link's identity model** — a roster member opening a link is downgraded by the platform's own design | `meta/personas.md`'s structural fact: one human holds several roles simultaneously. Compose with reality: the producer sends the link to the whole client-side group, and two of those people are on the roster. They click the link — because it is the thing in the message — and the platform, which knows exactly who they are, hands them an anonymous weak-identity session, marks their comment "via link" (`07.05.01` D-06), and measures them. Three separate harms from one click: a verified member's comment is rendered *less* authoritative than it is, a colleague is surveilled, and the vault's access model is bypassed by the vault's own users. | ✅ CONFIRMED — becomes D-11. A link opened by a signed-in user with roster access to that song resolves to the **project view** for the pinned version: real identity, no "via link" marker, no measurement, and the sender's card explains the empty count rather than showing a false zero. That last part matters more than it looks: an uncorrected zero reads as "the client is ignoring me", which is a wrong fact about a relationship — the same class of error as a comment re-anchored to the wrong bar (`07.05.01` D-03). |
| DT-10 | **Nobody asked who can *see* the analytics**, and the obvious answer is wrong | DT-03 established that measurement is uncomfortable for the recipient. The unasked question is who does the watching. "Everyone on the roster" is intolerable — a band where every member can see that the drummer never opened the mix is a band having an argument the platform started. "Only the link creator" is also wrong, and more subtly: the Producer creates most links, so under creator-only visibility the **song owner cannot audit the leak surface of their own unreleased record**. Both defaults fail. | ✅ CONFIRMED — becomes D-12. Analytics are visible to the **link creator and the song owner**, never the roster at large. The song owner sees *every* link against their song, including the Producer's, and can revoke any of them — it is their audio and their exposure (consistent with `07.03.03`'s "sees who has actually accessed what"). The Producer is told about this asymmetry **at creation**, not on discovery: a professional finding out later that their client relationships were visible all along is a trust breach of exactly the kind D-06 forbids. Where the song owner is not the master owner, this inherits `07.03.03` Q-02 rather than re-deciding it. |
| DT-11 | Anomalous access should **auto-revoke** (the file's own Q-04) | The instinct is sound: 100 opens looks like a leak, and a leak is what this feature exists to bound | ❌ **REJECTED** — on three independent grounds, any one of which is sufficient. (a) **It is too late to help.** At 100 opens the audio is already distributed; revoking removes nothing that has not been taken. It buys the *feeling* of a control, which is the definition of overclaiming (D-06). (b) **The false positive is catastrophic and common.** An A&R forwarding to their whole team — marketing, the head of the label, legal — is not an edge case, it is *how a label decides to sign someone*. Auto-revoke kills the record's biggest moment, from the artist's own tool, with no human in the loop. (c) **It contradicts two ratified domain decisions**: D-05 ("measure and show; never judge") and D-04 ("non-blocking is absolute"). A platform that unilaterally cuts off an A&R mid-listen has become an adversary of the person it needs most — the same failure the sub-domain already rejected in R-01. Resolution: **alert the owner within 5 min, never auto-revoke** (D-13). The human revokes, or marks it expected and the alert stops. This closes Q-04. |
| DT-12 | **The link log is provenance evidence, not just analytics** — and this is what makes the feature serve the wedge and not only the platform half | Worth pressure-testing whether this feature earns its keep against D-18's *provenance* half at all, or only its *consolidation* half. It does, and more directly than DT-01's presence argument: a private link is **the first time a recording leaves the project**, and it is timestamped, version-pinned, and attributed to a named recipient. That is a first-disclosure record. "I sent you this demo in March; you released it in October" is a real and recurring dispute in this industry, and it currently turns on whoever kept better emails. The record costs nothing extra — it is a byproduct of the access log that already exists for leak tracing. | ✅ CONFIRMED — becomes D-16, and it forces a **split in retention** that resolves half of DT-03's discomfort as a bonus. The *behavioral analytics* (play counts, drop-off) are the intrusive part → short retention, default 24 months, and they die with the link. The *disclosure fact* (this version, to this person, on this date, opened or not) is evidence → retained for the life of the song, surviving expiry, revocation and project archival. Minimal retention of the surveillance; permanent retention of the fact. That is both the defensible privacy posture and the more valuable half. |
| DT-13 | The link should require a name before playback — it would fix weak identity and make analytics attributable | Tempting, and it is what a data-hungry product does | ❌ **REJECTED** — it is a signup wall wearing a hat, and D-01 exists precisely to forbid that. Per DT-05 the recipient is an A&R with 200 links and no patience; a form between them and the audio loses the play, and a lost play loses the record. It is also **unnecessary**, which is what settles it: under DT-04 the sender already named the recipient, so the platform knows who the link went to without asking the human who received it. Identity is **asserted by the sender, never demanded from the recipient** (D-14). Name capture at *first comment* is fine and already required — a comment needs an author — but that is after the audio has done its job, and by then the person has a reason to type. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Links require **no account** from the recipient and have no signup wall | Per DT-01 — the A&R will not sign up; a wall means the producer uses WeTransfer and the platform leaves the room. |
| D-02 | Links **pin a version**; they do not follow the canonical | `07.04.02` D-03 — a link silently changing what it plays is the `FINAL_v3` disease in the highest-stakes context. Updating a link mints a new one; it never mutates what a live link plays. |
| D-03 | Per-recipient **watermarking** by default on any link that permits download | The only measure with teeth (DT-02). Extended by D-09. |
| D-04 | The product **must not claim** streaming-only prevents leaking | Per DT-02 and `07.03.03` D-05 — overclaiming to professionals is a trust breach. |
| D-05 | Recipients are **told** they are measured; drop-off is presented as fact, not verdict; analytics-free links are possible | Per DT-03 — the consent half is likely a legal requirement, and the framing half is an honesty requirement. |
| D-06 | Expiry grants a grace period to the end of the current play, capped at remaining duration + 60 s | Cutting someone off at 2:58 of 3:00 is gratuitous. **Revocation does not inherit this grace** — it fails closed within 10 s, because revoking is a leak response, not a clock. |
| D-07 | A share is a set of **per-recipient links**. A **public link** also exists, as an explicitly lesser object with its forfeits stated at creation: aggregate analytics only, link-level watermark, no meaningful anomaly detection | Per DT-04 — watermarking, analytics and anomaly detection are all worthless without a named recipient. But refusing the public link loses to the WhatsApp band thread, and a feature the user routes around captures nothing (DT-01). Honest degradation beats refusal. |
| D-08 | The expiry clock **starts at first access** — default 14 days — with an absolute cap of 90 days from creation (sender-adjustable, hard maximum 365 days) | Per DT-06 — the unopened link is the base case, and a creation-relative clock kills the link exactly at the producer's highest-intent follow-up. The absolute cap exists so an unopened link is not immortal. |
| D-09 | **Streams are watermarked, not only downloads** | Per DT-07 — under D-03 alone the default configuration (streaming-only) is the untraceable one. The likely leak from a stream is a screen or line-out capture, which a marked stream traces. Delivery cost is real and belongs to `/create-prd`. |
| D-10 | Link recipients are **mutually invisible**. A recipient sees only their own comments and replies to them — never the roster's, never another recipient's. Replies to a link comment are **labelled with their audience** | Per DT-08 — a track shopped to three labels at once is standard practice, and leaking that fact to a counterparty is commercial damage the platform caused. |
| D-11 | A link opened by a **signed-in user with roster access** to that song resolves to the project view for the pinned version: real identity, no "via link" marker, **no measurement**. The sender's card explains the empty count rather than showing a false zero | Per DT-09 — the multi-hyphenate is the norm (`meta/personas.md`), and the platform must not downgrade an identity it already knows, surveil a colleague, or report "never listened" about someone who listened as themselves. |
| D-12 | Analytics are visible to the **link creator and the song owner** only, never the roster at large. The song owner sees **every** link against their song — including the Producer's — and may revoke any of them. The Producer is told this **at creation** | Per DT-10 — roster-wide visibility starts band arguments; creator-only leaves the owner unable to audit the leak surface of their own unreleased record. Disclosing the asymmetry up front is required by D-06; discovering it later would be the trust breach. |
| D-13 | Anomalous access **alerts the owner within 5 min; it never auto-revokes**. Owner can revoke or mark expected. Default v1 signals: ≥4 distinct devices, or ≥3 distinct countries, within 24 h on a per-recipient link — **explicitly a starting default to be calibrated against real data, not a claim** | Per DT-11 — resolves Q-04. Auto-revoke is too late to help, catastrophic on its common false positive (the legitimate label-wide forward), and contradicts domain D-04 (non-blocking is absolute) and D-05 (measure and show; never judge). |
| D-14 | **Zero interaction before audio.** No name, no account, no prompt — only a passcode field if the sender set one. Identity is asserted by the sender, never demanded of the recipient. Name capture happens at first comment | Per DT-13 and DT-05 — a form between an A&R and the audio loses the play. Under D-07 the sender already named them, so asking is both hostile and pointless. |
| D-15 | Revocation **fails closed for audio** (next segment refused; audible stop ≤10 s) but **accepts an in-flight comment for 120 s** | Per parent CX-01 Q5 — losing feedback someone took the trouble to give is worse than a slightly late close, and a comment is not a leak. |
| D-16 | **Retention splits by kind.** The *disclosure record* (version, recipient, date, opened y/n) is retained for the life of the song, surviving expiry, revocation and project archival — it is evidence. The *behavioral analytics* (play counts, drop-off) default to 24 months and die with the link | Per DT-12 — the link is a first-disclosure record, which is the feature's contribution to the provenance half of D-18. The split also minimises retention of the intrusive data while keeping the valuable half, which partly answers DT-03's discomfort. Exact periods ride with Q-02. |
| D-17 | A **link inventory** exists at owner level: every live link across everything they own, with age, anomaly state, and bulk revoke | Without it D-12's "the owner can audit their leak surface" is a claim the product does not deliver — and per D-06 the platform does not make claims it does not deliver. |
| D-18 | Delivery failure is **never** rendered as expiry or revocation | A recipient told "expired" when the truth is a storage fault does not come back, and the sender is not told their delivery failed. Three different causes, three different messages. |
