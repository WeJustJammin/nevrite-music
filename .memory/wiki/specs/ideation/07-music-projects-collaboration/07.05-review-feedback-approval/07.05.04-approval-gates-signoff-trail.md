# Feature: Formal Approval Gates & Sign-Off Trail

> **Number**: 07.05.04
> **Parent**: [Review, Feedback & Approval](./07.05-review-feedback-approval-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The record of who approved what, when, and which exact version they approved. It ends the industry's most common
low-grade dispute — "you approved this" / "I never approved that" — by making the approval an object that pins an
immutable version (parent CX-05) rather than a thumbs-up in a thread that referenced a file that has since been
replaced twice.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Approves** — it is their record, and sign-off is theirs to give. Sees the full sign-off trail. Sees what they are being asked to approve and what happens when they do (round counting stops, `07.05.05`), stated plainly *before* they click. |
| Producer | Config | **Configures** who must approve before a stage advances; requests approvals; chases missing ones. Does not normally approve on the artist's behalf — that would defeat the purpose — though they may be an approver in their own right (a mix engineer signing off that the master is faithful to their mix). |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

An approval is `(version, approver, timestamp, scope, comment-state-at-approval)`. It **pins the version**
(parent CX-05): since versions are immutable (`07.04.01` D-01), what was approved is unambiguous forever. This is
a direct payoff of the version model and the reason this feature can offer something a thread cannot.

The approver set is configured per song and per gate, and **may include people who are not on the roster** — a
label A&R, a manager, a sync client. The people whose approval matters commercially are frequently not the people
doing the work, which no roster-derived permission model can express (this is `07.05.02`'s problem again, in a
different costume).

Approving with open comments is legitimate — "approved, but fix the click at 2:14 in the master" is a normal and
useful act — and must not be blocked. But the open comments are pinned into the approval record, or the approval
misrepresents what was agreed.

**Scope is the honest limitation.** Whether an in-product approval has contractual force is genuinely unresolved
(sub-domain Q-03, and `07.03.03` Q-01 for NDAs). What it certainly is: a timestamped, attributed, version-pinned
record that is enormously better evidence than a WhatsApp thumbs-up. That may be all it needs to be.

### Happy Path

1. Producer configures the mix gate: the artist and the label A&R must both approve
2. v7 is ready; producer requests approval; both are notified with a link (the A&R via `07.05.02`, since they have no account)
3. Artist listens, approves — the approval pins v7 and the 2 open comments at that moment
4. A&R approves two days later, after a "waiting on you" nudge
5. Gate satisfied → round counting stops (parent CX-04) → the stage board advances to `mix approved` (`07.01.03`), which fires the credit capture prompt

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Approval given, then the version is superseded | The approval stays pinned to v7. It does **not** transfer to v8 — an approval that follows the file is worthless | "v7 is approved. v8 is not." |
| Backwards stage transition after approvals collected (`07.01` Q-03) | Approvals marked **superseded**, not deleted | "2 approvals for the master were superseded when it went back to mix" |
| An approver never responds | The most common real failure. Nudges; the gate stays open; the record stalls honestly rather than pretending. | "Waiting on the label since 12 Nov" — the notification that actually unblocks records |
| Approver approves without listening | Not blocked (parent rejected-pair R-01) — gating on listening is patronising and trivially defeated | Producer may *see* it via analytics; deferred |
| Approval requested from a non-user | Via a private link (`07.05.02`); identity is weak, which weakens the evidence | Approval recorded as "via link" — never rendered as equivalent to an authenticated approval |
| Approver changes their mind after approving | Cannot un-approve. They can approve a *later* version or raise a new request — the trail is append-only, because a retractable approval is not evidence | "You approved v7. Request changes on v8?" |
| Producer approves on the artist's behalf ("they said yes on the phone") | Permitted only as an explicitly attributed proxy approval, never as the artist | "Approved by Producer on behalf of Sam (verbal)" — visibly weaker |
| Gate configured with an approver who leaves the project | Gate stalls | Owner prompted to reconfigure |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unconfigured | No gate set | "No approval required" — a legitimate default |
| Pending | Approval requested | Who is required, who has responded |
| Partial | Some approved | Progress with the outstanding names |
| Approved | All approvals collected | Gate satisfied; version badged; stage advances |
| Superseded | Backwards transition | Approvals shown as superseded with the reason |
| Stalled | No response for a long time | Honest stalled state with nudge affordances |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The approval must **pin an immutable version**, and this is why a comment-thread thumbs-up can never do the job | The dispute is never "did you say yes?" — it is "yes to *what*?" A thumbs-up in a thread references a file that has since been replaced twice, in a folder where three files claim to be final. `meta/problem-statement.md`: facts are "reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable." An approval is exactly such a fact. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a **free** payoff of `07.04.01` D-01's immutability. This is the clearest demonstration in the domain of why the version model earns its cost: because versions cannot change, an approval that points at one is self-evidently unambiguous. The feature is almost trivial to build *given* the version model, and impossible without it. |
| DT-02 | An in-product approval is legally meaningful | Implicit in "Formal Approval Gates" | ⚠️ **UNRESOLVED — and must not be claimed.** Whether a click constitutes contractual acceptance is jurisdiction-dependent and unanswered (sub-domain Q-03; same question as `07.03.03` Q-01 for NDAs). But the framing "is it legally binding?" is probably the wrong question, because **the value is overwhelmingly in dispute *prevention*, not dispute *resolution*.** Most of these arguments never reach anyone's lawyer; they are resolved by someone producing a screenshot, badly. A timestamped, attributed, version-pinned record that both parties can see *at the time* stops the argument from starting. That is worth building even if it would not survive a courtroom — and the UI must not imply that it would. |
| DT-03 | The approver set should be **derived from the roster** | Consistent with `07.03.03` DT-02's principle that access derives from roles — it would be elegant | ❌ **REJECTED** — the people whose approval matters commercially are routinely *not* on the roster. The label A&R who signs off the mix never touched the session. The sync client approving a bespoke edit is not a contributor. The manager is on nobody's roster. So the approver set is **independently configured** and frequently includes non-users reached via `07.05.02`. This is the second time in this sub-domain (after `07.05.02` itself) that the roster model fails to describe the people who actually matter — which is worth noting as a pattern: **the roster describes who *makes* the record; it does not describe who *decides* it.** Those are different sets, and conflating them would make the platform unusable for anyone with a label, a manager, or a client. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An approval **pins a specific immutable version** and never transfers to a later one | Per DT-01 — an approval that follows the file is worthless. |
| D-02 | The **approver set is configured independently** of the roster and may include non-users | Per DT-03 — the roster describes who makes the record, not who decides it. |
| D-03 | Approving with open comments is **allowed**; the comment state is pinned into the record | "Approved, but fix the click at 2:14" is a normal, useful act; the record must reflect what was actually agreed. |
| D-04 | Approvals are **append-only** — no un-approving, only approving a later version | A retractable approval is not evidence. |
| D-05 | Proxy approvals are permitted but **visibly attributed and visibly weaker** | "The artist said yes on the phone" is real; passing it off as the artist's own approval is not. |
| D-06 | The product **does not claim legal force** | Per DT-02 — the value is dispute prevention; overclaiming is a trust breach. |
