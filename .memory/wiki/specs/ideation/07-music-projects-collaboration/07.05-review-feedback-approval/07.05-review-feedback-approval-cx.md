## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Private Share Links ↔ Timestamped Review

**Relationship**: Link recipients comment without accounts. Their comments carry a **weaker identity** —
a name they typed, bound to a link that was sent to an email — but they land in the same stream as authenticated
roster comments. This is necessary (the A&R who decides the record will not sign up to say "vocal up") and it is
a trust problem: an anonymous comment and a roster member's comment must not look equally authoritative.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sends links to their manager/label; sees their feedback alongside the band's, with the identity difference visible.
- **Producer**: sends links to clients; this is the highest-volume use of the whole sub-domain.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: comments are append-only against a version. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: link opened → listen recorded (`07.05.02`) → comment optionally left → roster notified. Failure to record the listen must never block the comment.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes, and asymmetrically — a link recipient can comment while seeing neither the roster, the other versions, nor the rest of the project. **Comment audience is fixed at post and immutable**: a link recipient sees only their own thread, never the roster's internal stream or another link recipient's comments; hidden comments are neither counted nor teased (`07.05.01` D-13–D-14; `07.05.02` D-10).
4. **Notification fan-out**: link comments notify the roster. The link recipient is generally not notified of replies unless the link is stateful.
5. **State transition conflict**: link revoked while the recipient is mid-comment. Comment should land if it was in flight — losing feedback someone took the trouble to give is worse than a slightly late close.

### CX-02: Timestamped Review ↔ Consolidation & Triage

**Relationship**: Triage reads the raw comment stream and clusters it — by playback position, by topic, by
stakeholder. The producer's actual problem is not reading comments, it is that five people commented on the same
12 seconds and two of them want opposite things. Consolidation makes the contradiction *visible* so a human can
adjudicate it; it must never resolve it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — the triage view is built for them; it is the sub-domain's power surface.
- **Musician**: Read-only — they see the consolidated picture, which is often the first time they realise their manager and their bandmate disagree.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: triage state (`accepted`, `rejected`, `deferred`) is owned by the producer; comments are owned by their authors. Clean separation — you cannot edit someone's feedback, only your response to it.
2. **Trigger chain**: comment → cluster → triage decision → optionally into a revision round (CX-03). A rejected comment must be visibly rejected *to its author*, not silently dropped.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — per CX-01, whose comments a given viewer sees varies.
4. **Notification fan-out**: triage decisions notify comment authors. Being told "we're not doing that" is more respectful than silence, and it prevents the comment being repeated next round.
5. **State transition conflict**: a comment arrives *after* triage closed the round. It goes to the next round, and the author is told which round it landed in.

### CX-03: Consolidation & Triage ↔ Revision Rounds

**Relationship**: A batch of accepted feedback **is** a revision round. This is the join between craft and
commerce: "3 rounds of revisions included" is a term in nearly every mix agreement, and today rounds are counted
by memory and argument. Making the round a real object with real contents makes the term enforceable — and,
more importantly, makes the argument unnecessary.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — rounds are their commercial protection against infinite tweaks.
- **Musician**: Read-only — sees where they are against their agreed rounds. A client seeing "round 3 of 3" *before* asking for one more thing changes their behavior, which is worth more than the enforcement.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the round is owned by the producer; its contents come from triage.
2. **Trigger chain**: round closed → work → new version → new round opens. Whether that is automatic is `07.05.05`'s core question.
3. **Permission intersection**: the round counter is producer-writable and musician-readable. **An editable-by-both counter protects nobody** — the whole point is that it is not negotiable after the fact.
4. **Notification fan-out**: round close/open notifies both sides. Approaching the round limit is the most valuable notification here, because it lands *before* the awkward conversation.
5. **State transition conflict**: feedback arriving during an open round joins it; after close, it starts the next one.

### CX-04: Revision Rounds ↔ Approval Gates

**Relationship**: Approval closes the loop and stops the counter. An approved version cannot accrue further free
revisions — reopening after approval is a new commercial conversation, not a continuation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: approves; is the one bound by it.
- **Producer**: configures who must approve; is protected by it.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: approval is append-only against (version, approver).
2. **Trigger chain**: all required approvals collected → version marked approved → round counting stops → stage board advances (`07.01.03`). Post-approval change requests must route to a *new* commercial conversation (domain 05), not silently to round 4.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — the approver set is configured per song and may include people who are not on the roster (a label). See `07.05.04`.
4. **Notification fan-out**: approval notifies everyone; a *missing* approval notifies the person it is waiting on. The "waiting on you" notification is what actually unblocks records.
5. **State transition conflict**: the backwards stage transition (`07.01` Q-03) — a master demoted to mix leaves collected approvals orphaned. Superseded, not deleted.

### CX-05: Approval Gates ↔ Timestamped Review

**Relationship**: An approval pins the exact version approved plus the comment state at that instant. "You
approved it" is only defensible if *what* was approved is unambiguous and immutable — and since versions are
immutable (`07.04.01` D-01), the pin is trivially reliable. This is a direct payoff of the version model.

**Role scoping**: as CX-04.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — both immutable.
2. **Trigger chain**: approval → snapshot of (version, open comments, approver, timestamp).
3. **Permission intersection**: no.
4. **Notification fan-out**: covered by CX-04.
5. **State transition conflict**: approving a version while comments are still open is legitimate ("approved, but fix the click at 2:14 in the master") and must not be blocked — but the open comments must be part of the pinned record, or the approval misrepresents what was agreed.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Listen Analytics (`07.05.02`) | Approval Gates (`07.05.04`) | Considered requiring an approver to have actually **listened** before approving — technically detectable, and it targets a real failure (the manager who approves a mix they never played). Rejected here as a *gate*: it is patronising to professionals, trivially defeated by leaving a tab playing, and would make the platform an adversary of the person it needs most. The related idea for canonical nomination is logged as `07.04.02` Q-03; as a **hard gate** it is rejected in both places. Surfacing "approved without listening" as *information to the producer* is a different and more defensible idea — deferred to Step 5. |
| R-02 | Private Share Links (`07.05.02`) | Revision Round Counting (`07.05.05`) | Considered auto-starting a revision round when a link recipient comments. Rejected: a link recipient is frequently *not* the client — a manager's passing remark is not a commercial instruction, and auto-counting would let a third party spend the artist's rounds. Rounds are started by triage acceptance (CX-03), which is a deliberate producer act. |
| R-03 | Timestamped Review (`07.05.01`) | Formal Approval Gates (`07.05.04`) — **as a merge** | Considered collapsing "approve" into a comment type ("👍 approved"), which is how most tools do it. Rejected: an approval has legal and commercial weight a comment does not. It pins a version (CX-05), stops a counter (CX-04), and may need to be produced as evidence months later. A thumbs-up in a comment thread cannot carry that, and conflating them is exactly why "you approved it" is currently unprovable across the industry. |
