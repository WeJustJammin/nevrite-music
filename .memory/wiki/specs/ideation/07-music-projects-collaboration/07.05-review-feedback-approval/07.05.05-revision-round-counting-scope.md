# Feature: Revision Round Counting & Scope Enforcement

> **Number**: 07.05.05
> **Parent**: [Review, Feedback & Approval](./07.05-review-feedback-approval-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

"Includes 3 rounds of revisions" is a term in nearly every mix and master agreement in the industry. Today the
rounds are counted from memory, by both parties, differently — and the disagreement surfaces at exactly the wrong
moment, when the producer finally says "that's round five" to a client who thought it was round two. This feature
makes the round a real object with real contents, so the count is a fact rather than an argument.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees where they are against their agreed rounds — "round 2 of 3" — **before** asking for one more thing. Cannot edit the count. This visibility is the feature's real value to them: it lets them spend their rounds deliberately instead of discovering the limit by being billed. |
| Producer | Full | Sets the agreed round count; opens and closes rounds; sees what each contained. Rounds are their commercial protection against infinite tweaks — the failure mode that quietly destroys the economics of freelance mixing. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A round is a batch of accepted feedback (parent CX-03) worked into a new version. It has contents, an open and a
close, and a sequence number. Approval stops the counting (parent CX-04).

Two properties define it:

**The count is producer-writable and musician-readable.** An editable-by-both counter protects nobody — the whole
point is that it is not renegotiable after the fact. This is deliberately asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the
feature.

**It informs; it does not enforce.** "Scope enforcement" in the candidate's title is the wrong mechanism (DT-01).
The platform must not refuse a producer's work or auto-bill a client. What it does is make the count visible to
both parties *continuously*, which is what actually prevents the argument.

The scope question — "is this a revision or a new request?" — is where the money is, and the system cannot answer
it (DT-02). "Make the vocal louder" is a revision. "Actually can we try it without the second chorus" is a new
arrangement wearing a revision's clothes. Only a human can tell the difference.

### Happy Path

1. Producer sets the mix agreement: 3 rounds included (mirrored from the domain 05 service order where one exists)
2. Round 1 opens when the producer accepts the first triaged batch (`07.05.03`)
3. Work happens; v7 lands; round 1 closes
4. Client sees "round 1 of 3 complete" — and, critically, sees it *before* they send their next note
5. After round 3, further requests are visibly out of scope; the producer has a fact to point at instead of a conversation to have

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Client asks for a change that is a **new request**, not a revision | System cannot tell (DT-02). Producer classifies it. | Producer can mark an item "out of scope" with a reason; the client sees the classification and can dispute it |
| Rounds exhausted; client asks for more | **Not blocked.** Visibility only. | "This is beyond the 3 included rounds" — shown to both, with a route to domain 05 for a change order |
| Producer does a small fix without opening a round | Correct and normal — not every tweak is a round. Rounds open by deliberate act. | No round opened |
| Client sends notes in dribs and drabs over two weeks | **The actual failure mode**, and the one that destroys mix economics. Ten separate one-line notes are ten context-switches and feel like ten rounds while counting as one — or none. | See DT-03 — batching windows |
| Producer inflates the count | Client sees each round's **contents**, so an empty round is visible. The transparency is the check on the asymmetry. | Round contents always visible to both |
| No commercial agreement at all (a band mixing their own record) | Feature is irrelevant; must not intrude. Rounds are off by default. | Not shown unless enabled |
| Round count disagreement despite the record | Routes to domain 24 with the round contents as evidence | Dispute with the trail attached |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Disabled | No revision agreement | Feature absent — the default for non-commercial work |
| Configured | Round count agreed | "3 rounds included" |
| Open | Round in progress | Current round, its contents, what is left |
| Closed | New version delivered | Round summary; count incremented |
| Exhausted | Included rounds used | Visible to both; **never blocking** |
| Out of scope | Producer classified an item | Client sees the classification and the reason |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The system should **enforce** scope — block work or auto-bill once rounds are exhausted | The candidate's own title says "Scope Enforcement" | ❌ **REJECTED** — enforcement is the wrong mechanism and misreads where the value is. Blocking a producer from doing work they have decided to do is absurd (it is their business, not the platform's), and auto-billing a client for round 4 without a human conversation would generate rage and chargebacks. The value is **visibility, not enforcement**: an argument about round counts happens because two people have different numbers in their heads. A shared, continuously-visible count means the disagreement never forms. The candidate's name is wrong about the mechanism while being right about the need — the same error as `07.04.04` DT-02 ("naming enforcement"), and the same correction: **make the fact visible, let humans act.** |
| DT-02 | The system can distinguish a **revision** from a **new request** | Implicit in the idea of scope enforcement | ❌ **REJECTED** — and this is where all the money is. "Make the vocal louder" is a revision. "Can we try it without the second chorus" is a new arrangement wearing a revision's clothes, and it is half a day's work. The difference is a judgement about *intent and effort* that requires knowing the craft and the deal. A system that misclassifies here either bills a client for a trivial ask or lets a producer be robbed of half a day. So: the **producer classifies**, the client sees the classification and can dispute it (domain 24), and the platform's job is to make the classification explicit and visible at the moment it is made — rather than implicit and contested six weeks later. |
| DT-03 | Rounds are the right unit for counting revision work | The industry's own convention, unexamined | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED** — rounds are the convention, but the convention is what producers are being exploited *through*, and nobody in the sweep noticed. The real damage is not a client using four rounds instead of three; it is a client sending ten separate one-line notes over two weeks. Each is trivial. Together they are ten context-switches — reopening the session, recalling the mix, re-listening, re-bouncing — which costs vastly more than one round of ten notes. This is *the* economic failure mode of freelance mixing and the round model is blind to it, because a round is a unit of *feedback batch* and the cost is a unit of *context switch*. Consequence: rounds need a **batching window** — feedback within a period accumulates into one round, and the platform should actively encourage clients to batch ("you have 3 open notes — send them together?"). That single nudge may be worth more to a working producer than the entire counting apparatus. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The feature **informs; it never enforces**. No blocking, no auto-billing | Per DT-01 — the value is a shared count, not a gate. |
| D-02 | The **producer classifies** revision vs new request; the client sees it and can dispute | Per DT-02 — the judgement needs craft knowledge and deal context; the system's job is to make it explicit at the time. |
| D-03 | The count is **producer-writable, musician-readable** — deliberately asymmetric | An editable-by-both counter protects nobody. The check on the asymmetry is that round contents are always visible. |
| D-04 | Rounds have a **batching window**, and the platform nudges clients to batch | Per DT-03 — the drip-feed context-switch is the real economic harm, and the nudge may be worth more than the counter. |
| D-05 | Off by default; only present where a revision agreement exists | A band mixing their own record must never see this. |
