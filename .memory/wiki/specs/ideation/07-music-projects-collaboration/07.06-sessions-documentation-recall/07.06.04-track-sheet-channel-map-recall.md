## Overview

The human-readable record of how a session was actually set up: what is on each track, what the channel map was,
which mic was on the kick and where it sat in the room, what the outboard was doing. Half of it is derivable from
the session snapshot (`07.06.03`) and half of it has never existed in any file — which is why recall sheets are
still handwritten on paper in 2026, and why they are lost within a month.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Rarely looks. When they do, it is because they are trying to recreate something from three years ago and it matters enormously — "what did we use on the vocal on the first record?" is a question with real artistic consequences and no current answer. |
| Producer | Full | Writes the analogue half at teardown; reads it at recall. The only role that creates here. This is a document they already make, badly, on paper — which is the feature's opportunity and its risk (DT-02). |
| Operator | Read-only | **Room-scoped only** — the studio sees their own gear and patching, never the creative content or the songs. Parent R-01. There is a real reciprocal value here: a studio that knows which of its mics were on which sessions has a maintenance and inventory record it currently lacks entirely. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The sheet has two halves with completely different economics:

**The digital half is derived, free, and nobody needs to type it.** Track names, channel counts, sample rate,
plugin chains — the snapshot's manifest already has all of it (parent CX-03).

**The analogue half has never been in a file and cannot be derived.** Which mic, which preamp, where the mic was,
what the compressor was set to, how the room was arranged. It exists only in the producer's head, and it exists
there for about a day.

That asymmetry is the feature. There is no point building a beautiful form for the half that is free; the entire
product question is whether the producer will ever fill in the half that is not — at teardown, tired, with a taxi
waiting (parent CX-04, and DT-02 below).

### Happy Path

1. Session closes; the snapshot's manifest pre-populates the digital half — 24 tracks, names, plugin chains, 48 kHz
2. Producer is prompted **separately from the credit prompt** (`07.06.02` D-06 — nothing rides along with the four-second capture)
3. They add the analogue half: "kick — D112, 2 inches off the beater; vox — U87 through a 1073, 4:1 at −6"
4. Sheet is stored against the session, immutable-by-default with attributed edits
5. Fourteen months later they are asked for a recall to add a vocal; the sheet tells them exactly what to rebuild — and the Operator's copy tells the studio which mics to patch before the band arrives

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Producer skips the analogue half | The **realistic majority case** (DT-02). The digital half still exists and still has value. | Sheet with a derived half and a visible gap; no nagging |
| Session is entirely in-the-box (no analogue at all) | The sheet is 100% derived and the feature is essentially free — and, notably, complete | Full sheet, nothing to add |
| Remembered the next day | Editable after close, with attribution — memory genuinely improves overnight, and refusing the edit would lose real information | "Added by Producer, 2 days after the session" |
| Studio's own gear changed since the session | The recall points at gear that no longer exists in that room | "The studio no longer lists a 1073" — Operator's inventory drift, visible via 16 |
| Operator sees creative track names ("Sam's terrible take 4") | **Real leak** — the digital half contains creative content, and track names are candid | Room-scoped view must filter to gear/patching only, never track names |
| Handwritten sheet photographed and uploaded | Should be accepted as-is. A photo of the real sheet beats an empty perfect form. | Image attached to the session; OCR is optional and not required |
| Same setup reused across many sessions | Templating from a prior session is the only realistic way this gets filled in twice | "Same setup as 12 Nov?" — one tap |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Derived | Snapshot processed | Digital half populated; analogue half empty |
| Partial | Some analogue detail added | Mixed; gaps visible but not nagged |
| Complete | Producer filled it in | Full sheet |
| Templated | Copied from a prior session | Pre-filled, editable |
| Image-only | Photo of a paper sheet | Image attached; fields unstructured |
| Room-scoped | Operator viewing | Gear and patching only — no track names, no songs |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The analogue half is the **only** part with real value, because the digital half is derivable | Reasoning about substitutability (the same lens as `07.06.03` DT-02). Anyone can read track names out of a session file. Nobody but the person in the room knows the mic was two inches off the beater. | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — and the correction matters.** The analogue half is the *scarce* part but not the only valuable part, because value is about **who needs it**, not about how hard it was to obtain. The derived digital half is worthless to the producer (they have the session file) and genuinely valuable to *everyone who does not*: the mix engineer receiving a handoff, the Operator prepping the room, the artist looking at their own record for the first time. **Scarcity and value are different axes**, and conflating them would have cut the half that serves three of the four roles. Ship both; invest the *design* effort in the analogue half, since it is the one that needs a human to act. |
| DT-02 | The producer will fill in the analogue half if the form is good enough | The implicit premise of the whole feature | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the feature's core risk.** Recall sheets are handwritten on paper in 2026 not because software has failed to offer a form, but because *filling them in has almost no payoff for the person doing it*. The producer knows their own setup; the sheet serves their future self at low probability and other people at high probability — a classic externality. At teardown, tired, with a taxi waiting, a form loses every time. Three honest implications: (a) this feature must **never** ride along with `07.06.02`'s four-second credit prompt (`07.06.02` D-06) — it would destroy the interaction that actually matters; (b) **templating** ("same as last time?") is likely the only mechanism that ever gets it filled twice; (c) accept a **photo of the paper sheet** as a first-class input, because the real sheet already exists and the platform's job is to stop it being lost, not to replace it. That reframes the feature from "digitise recall" to "**don't lose the sheet the producer already made**" — a much smaller, much more achievable, and much more likely-to-be-used product. |
| DT-03 | The Operator's room-scoped view is a nice-to-have | Its position in the Role Matrix suggests a minor read-only cell | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS MORE THAN THAT — it is a reciprocal-value trade the domain has nowhere else.** A studio that can see which of its mics and preamps were used on which sessions has an inventory, maintenance and utilisation record it currently does not have at all (which mic is on every session? which preamp has never been patched?). That is real value to the Operator persona from a domain they otherwise have almost no stake in — and it flows *from* a document the Producer had no incentive to fill in. So it may partially solve DT-02: the studio has a reason to want the sheet completed, and studios have staff. This is the only place in domain 07 where the Operator's presence changes the design rather than decorating it. It also sharpens parent R-01's boundary — the room-scoped filter must be exact, because track names are candid and leak creative content. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Track sheet, channel map and recall sheet are **one document** | Sub-domain D-04 — splitting them models a distinction no studio makes. |
| D-02 | Ship both halves; invest design effort in the **analogue** half | Per DT-01 — the derived half is worthless to its author and valuable to everyone else. |
| D-03 | The framing is "**don't lose the sheet the producer already made**", not "digitise recall" | Per DT-02 — a photo of the paper sheet is a first-class input; the real sheet already exists. |
| D-04 | **Templating** from a prior session is the primary completion mechanism | Per DT-02 — nothing else plausibly gets it filled in twice. |
| D-05 | This **never** rides along with `07.06.02`'s capture prompt | Per DT-02 and `07.06.02` D-06 — it would destroy the four-second interaction the domain depends on. |
| D-06 | The Operator's view is **room-scoped and exactly filtered** — gear and patching only, never track names | Parent R-01 and DT-03 — track names are candid creative content. |
