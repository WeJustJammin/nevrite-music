## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Session Record ↔ Session Close & Capture Prompt

**Relationship**: This is the edge the platform's thesis rests on. The session record knows who was in the room —
from attendance, from the DAW bridge's track-to-contributor mapping (`07.09.02`), from the roster (`07.03.01`),
from the studio booking (domain 16). The close prompt uses all of it to ask a **confirmable question instead of an
open one**.

The distinction is everything. "Who played on this?" is a form, and forms at 1am after a ten-hour session get
dismissed. "Sam played drums, Alex engineered, split still open — confirm?" is a single tap on facts the system
already worked out, and it takes four seconds. `meta/personas.md` states the requirement precisely: capture must
be "a byproduct of running the session — zero extra admin."

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the primary target. They are the trust broker; their attestation is worth more than a self-claim, and they are the one who knows.
- **Musician**: receives the counter-attestation request ("Producer says you played drums — confirm?"). This is their half of the pair, and it is the half that turns a claim into a credit.
- **Operator**: none — the studio does not participate in the capture of creative facts, even though the session happened in their room. See R-01.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the session owns attendance; the prompt writes credits into domain 02 and splits into domain 09. The prompt is a *producer* of facts elsewhere, not an owner of them.
2. **Trigger chain**: session close → prompt → confirm → credit written (02) + split written (09) → counter-attestation requests fan out to attendees. **If the prompt is dismissed the close still commits** — non-blocking is absolute here (`07.01.03` D-02). A blocked close teaches producers not to close sessions, and an unclosed session captures nothing at all, which is strictly worse than a dismissed prompt.
3. **Permission intersection**: the producer can attest about others; others counter-attest about themselves. Neither can unilaterally create a verified credit — that requires the pair.
4. **Notification fan-out**: the largest in the domain. One close notifies every attendee with a claim about them, and each is an invitation to counter-attest (and, for non-users, an invitation to the platform — `07.03.02` DT-01).
5. **State transition conflict**: the split is agreed at close but a contributor later disputes it. The record shows what was attested, by whom, when, and who confirmed — routes to domain 24. Nothing is rewritten; the dispute is appended.

### CX-02: Session Record ↔ Session Snapshot

**Relationship**: The snapshot is the machine state at close — session file, plugin manifest, sample rate,
channel count, referenced media. It belongs to the session, not to the song, because it is a record of *a moment
of work*, not of the work's identity.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — they own the machine.
- **Musician**: Read-only — they can see that the session is archived and reopenable, which is genuinely reassuring and is the thing they will care about in five years.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — the snapshot is immutable, taken once.
2. **Trigger chain**: close → snapshot → integrity/missing-media check (`07.04.05`). A snapshot that fails its reference check is a **broken archive**, and it must say so loudly — an archive that silently cannot be reopened is worse than no archive, because it was trusted.
3. **Permission intersection**: the snapshot inherits the song's sensitivity class; it contains everything, so it is the most sensitive artifact in the domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: failures notify the producer.
5. **State transition conflict**: none.

### CX-03: Session Snapshot ↔ Recall Sheet

**Relationship**: The snapshot supplies the derivable half of a recall sheet — the digital state, which a machine
can read. The recall sheet adds the half no machine has ever known: which mic, which preamp, where the mic was in
the room, what the outboard was set to. That analogue half is why recall sheets are still handwritten in 2026, and
it is why this feature cannot be automated away.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — writes the analogue half.
- **Musician**: Read-only.
- **Operator**: Read-only, and **only for the parts about their own room** — the studio's own gear and patching. See R-01.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the snapshot is immutable; the recall sheet is editable (memory improves; someone remembers the mic the next day). Separate stores, no merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: snapshot → recall sheet pre-populated → producer fills the analogue half at close (CX-04).
3. **Permission intersection**: yes, and it is the domain's sharpest — the Operator sees their own room's gear, never the creative content. Same document, two audiences, one boundary.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether recall sheet edits after close need attribution.

### CX-04: Session Close ↔ Recall Sheet

**Relationship**: Close is the **last moment the analogue detail exists**. Ask "what mic was on the kick?" the
next morning and it is genuinely gone — not forgotten, but never encoded. Medium confidence because there is a
real tension: the close prompt's power comes from being *short*, and adding "and what mic was on the kick?" to
the credit prompt risks destroying the four-second interaction that makes `07.06.02` work at all.

**Synthesis questions**: deferred to Step 5 per the template's Medium/Low rule. The sequencing question — credits
first, gear second, or a separate teardown prompt — is `07.06.04` Q-01 and matters more than it looks.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Session Record | Session Close & Capture Prompt — **for the Operator** | Considered giving the Operator a role in capture: they know the session happened in their room, they often know who came in, and they could confirm attendance — which looks like free, high-quality provenance from a neutral witness. **Rejected**, and the reasoning is the domain's sharpest privacy boundary: an Operator confirming *who played on a song* would give a studio visibility into their client's creative and commercial facts — who the writers are, what the split is — which is categorically not their business and would make professional clients refuse to book. The Operator's legitimate knowledge is bounded to **the event and the room**: a session occurred, these people entered, this gear was patched. The creative facts are the client's. The Operator therefore appears in `07.06.01` and `07.06.04` (room-scoped only) and **never** in `07.06.02`. |
| R-02 | Session Snapshot | Take & Comp Management (`07.04.03`) | Considered folding takes into the session snapshot, since takes are produced in a session and the snapshot captures the session. Rejected: takes have their own lifecycle, their own (stricter) sensitivity class per `07.04.03` DT-03, and their own lineage into comps and versions. The snapshot is a *moment*; takes are *material*. They are linked by session, which is enough. |
| R-03 | Recall Sheet | Production Stage Board (`07.01.03`) | Considered a "documentation complete" stage gate. Rejected for the same reason as `07.02`'s R-03: the stage vocabulary is audio-craft-specific (`07.01` D-02), and gating a song's progress on paperwork completeness would make the board an enemy. Documentation debt surfaces via `07.08.03`'s completeness score, which informs rather than blocks. |
