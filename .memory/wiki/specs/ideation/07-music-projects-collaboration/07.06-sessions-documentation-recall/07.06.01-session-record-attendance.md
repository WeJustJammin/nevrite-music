## Overview

A session is the dated event where humans were in a room and work happened: who was there, from when to when,
where, on which songs, and what came out of it. It is the **atomic unit of provenance**. A song is the output; a
session is the event — and a credit without a session has no timestamp, no witness list, and no context, which
is precisely why credits reconstructed years later are contestable.

The feature exists for two people. For the **Producer** it is the working unit they already think in, and it must
cost them nothing — it is the substrate that makes `07.06.02`'s prompt pre-fillable rather than blank (parent
CX-01). For the **Musician** it is the first honest record of their own working life: `meta/personas.md` names
"an unpaid session" as the *first* thing in their switching trigger, and a session record is the evidence that
the session happened, that they were in it, and that nobody has paid for it yet (DT-05).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only (own attendance: Attest) | Sees every session they attended across every project and band, filterable by date, project, location and co-attendee — which, for the multi-hyphenate, is the first honest record of their own working life that has ever existed. "I played on 34 sessions last year, here they are, 31 paid" is a career artifact they currently cannot produce at any price (DT-05). Cannot edit the session, cannot see attendees on a confidential project they were not part of, cannot see the songs on a session they did not attend. **Can** confirm or dispute the session's attendance list as a witness (DT-08) — this is the only write they hold, and it is what makes the witness list itself witnessed. Their own session history is **private by default** (DT-07). |
| Producer | Full (per session they own) | Creates sessions — ideally without noticing (DT-02) — records attendance with one tap per person, links songs, closes them (`07.06.02`). Exactly one Producer owns each session (DT-09), and the lens is **per-session, not per-account**: the same human is the Producer of the sessions they run and a read-only Musician on the sessions they merely attended. The session is their working unit — they think in sessions, not in songs. Cannot see attendance for sessions they do not own. Cannot make their own attendance a verified fact unilaterally (DT-08). |
| Operator | Read-only, **disclosure-scoped** | **The only place an Operator appears in domain 07.** A session physically happened in their room, so they know it occurred and when it ran. They see the *event*, never the music: no songs, no versions, no credits, no splits. They see **headcount, not the creative attendance list**, and identities only for people their own booking already disclosed to them (DT-07, D-11) — the platform never *introduces* a studio to an attendee they were not told about. See parent R-01: this boundary is load-bearing, and it gets sharper in `07.06.02`, where the Operator has no role at all. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — no view, no aggregate, no count. A session is a working record about named professionals at a place and time; there is no version of it that is safe to make public, and the confidentiality analysis in DT-07 applies with full force. Fans encounter its *downstream* output — a credit on a release (domain 02) — never the session. |

## Behavior

A session is `(owner, start, end, location, attendees, songs worked on, outputs, capture grade)`. Attendance is
the critical field and the reason the feature exists: it is what makes `07.06.02`'s prompt pre-fillable (parent
CX-01), and it is the witness list that gives a credit its weight.

### The four properties that define it

**1. It costs the Producer nothing.** If creating a session is a form, sessions will not exist, and everything in
this sub-domain fails with them (sub-domain Q-03). Sessions are **inferred**: from a studio booking (16.06), from
a service order (05.03), from DAW activity (`07.09`), from people being present in a real-time room (the
transport cross-cut, D-15). Manual creation exists as a fallback, never as the path (D-02).

**2. Attendance is coarse by default.** "Present at this session" — one tap per person, no dialog, no time
fields. Fine-grained timing of named humans is surveillance (DT-03) and only ever exists opt-in, where the person
tracked is the one who benefits (D-13). Evidentiary precision comes from **output timestamps** — facts about the
work, not about the human.

**3. Attendance is not contribution and not engagement.** Being in the room implies no credit (D-03) and no fee.
A manager, a partner, a friend are all "in the room" and mean different things. The session evidences that you
were *there*; what you *did* is `07.06.02`'s question, and what you are *owed* is domain 05's.

**4. It is honest about how it knows.** Every session carries a **capture grade** (D-08) that never lies about
its own provenance:

| Grade | Meaning | How it is earned |
|---|---|---|
| `captured` | The platform was present. Session created by an inference source *while it was running*; attendance recorded inside the session window. | Only at the time. **Never** awardable later, by anything. |
| `confirmed` | Someone who was there recorded it at or near the close, while it was still true. | Created or completed within the close window (≤ 24h of session end). |
| `reconstructed` | Made from memory, after the fact. | Any session created > 24h after its stated end (DT-11). |

The grade is displayed on every session and **inherited by every credit that comes out of it**. A `reconstructed`
session can gain witnesses (attendee attestation, DT-08) and become *witnessed memory* — it can never become
`captured`, because that fact is gone. This is the domain's honest-claims principle (domain D-06) applied to the
platform's own evidence.

### Session, booking and service order are three different facts

Resolving sub-domain Q-01 / Q-02 at the behavioral level (DT-04). They are **distinct**, linked `0..1 : 0..N`,
and never reconciled:

| | Booking (16.06) | Service order (05.03) | Session (here) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | A commercial reservation of a room for a time | A commercial engagement for scoped work | A creative event that occurred |
| **Parties** | Payer ↔ Operator | Client ↔ provider | Attendees |
| **Lifecycle** | held → confirmed → completed → settled | quoted → engaged → delivered → accepted | scheduled → active → closed |
| **Can be cancelled** | Yes, with a refund policy | Yes | **No** — it either happened or it did not |
| **Exists without the others** | Constantly (rehearsals, no-shows) | Constantly (a mix delivered with no session) | Constantly (bedrooms — the majority case) |

Cardinality is genuinely many-to-many-ish and must not be flattened: one booking can hold two sessions (afternoon
tracking, evening mixing); one session can span two bookings (it overran and a second block was bought); most
sessions have neither. Storage representation is `/create-prd-architecture`'s call — the **behavioral** rule is
that the platform never forces one of these facts to agree with another (D-06, D-07).

### What a session must let you find

Recall is the point, and in five years the query is fragmentary. A session is findable by **date range,
attendee, project/song, location, capture grade, and output** — and by combinations of them, because the real
question is "the one at that studio with Sam where we cut the Rhodes." Every filter is scoped to what the asker
is allowed to see (DT-07): a Musician's session search returns only sessions they attended.

### What this feature is not

- **Not a calendar** (DT-10). The platform does not schedule sessions; it records them. A `Scheduled` session
  exists only as a mirror of a booking (16.06) that someone else already made. Building scheduling here would
  violate domain D-07 (be the record, not the tool) and lose to the calendar the Producer already uses.
- **Not a container** (DT-16). Takes, versions and comps belong to `07.04`; the snapshot belongs to `07.06.03`.
  The session **links** material; it never owns it.

### Happy Path

> **v1 scope on this path** (owner decision 2026-07-22, `ideation-index` D-70, queue entry DQ-08.2/DQ-08.3;
> the assignment itself is `07.06.02` D-17/D-18). **No non-web client on the producer's machine is
> authorised**, so every `07.09` bridge input below — the take landing at 14:05 (step 2), the
> track-to-contributor suggestions (step 3), and the DAW-close wrap signal (step 5) — **has no source in
> v1**. For the whole v1 window: the session is activated by the **booking mirror** and by the Producer's
> own acts, attendance candidates come from the **roster (`07.03.01`) and the booking's named party
> (16.06) only**, and the wrap signal is a **producer tap, the booked end, or the 72 h backstop**
> (`02.02.03`'s ladder, rungs 1/4/5). The steps are retained, not deleted: they reactivate only if the
> Desktop row reopens on its enumerated evidence (`meta/constraints.md` § Desktop Surface — Reopen
> Evidence) and `07.09` D-04's parser gate is separately satisfied.

1. Producer books a studio day for 14 Nov, 14:00–23:00 (16.06). WeJammin mirrors it as a **`Scheduled`** session
   — start, end, location, and the paying client already known from the booking. The Producer did nothing.
2. At 14:05 the first take lands via the DAW bridge (`07.09.01`). The session flips to **`Active`** and its
   capture grade is provisionally `captured` — the platform is in the room.
3. People arrive. Attendance is confirmed with **one tap per person** from a pre-filled candidate list drawn from
   the roster (`07.03.01`), the booking's named party (16.06), and the bridge's track-to-contributor mapping
   (`07.09.02`). Nobody is auto-marked present — the bridge *suggests*, the Producer *asserts* (D-05).
4. Work happens. Takes and versions attach to the session as they are created, each with its own timestamp. The
   Producer never opens WeJammin.
5. At 23:40 the Producer ends the session (or the bridge detects the DAW closed). The session goes **`Closed`**;
   capture grade locks at `captured`.
6. `07.06.02`'s prompt fires and is **pre-filled from attendance** — "Sam played drums, you engineered" — because
   this feature knew who was there. Four seconds, one tap. *This is the whole point of the feature.*
7. Each attendee gets one notification: "You were at Ghost Town — 14 Nov, Chalice. Confirm?" One tap. Attendance
   becomes **witnessed** (DT-08), and the session's evidentiary weight is now independent of the Producer's word.
8. The session persists forever as the timestamped, witnessed context for every credit that came out of it — and
   it appears in Sam's own session history that evening, without Sam ever having filled in anything.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Session with **no studio** (bedroom, laptop on a train) | Must work — most modern sessions have no Operator at all (sub-domain Q-02, resolved: **yes**, and it is the majority case). Location optional; no booking; no Operator row. | Session with no location; no Operator visibility |
| Distributed session (three people, three cities, over a real-time room) | Attendance is presence in the room, not physical location (D-15) | Attendees with their locations, if disclosed |
| Someone was in the room but **did not play** | Real and common (a manager, a partner, a friend). **Attendance ≠ contribution** — conflating them would manufacture false credits at scale, which is exactly the DT-02 risk in `07.03.01`. | Attendee listed; **no credit implied**; absent from `07.06.02`'s prompt |
| Someone **played but was never marked present** | The prompt cannot pre-fill them; the Producer adds them at close (`07.06.02`), or they self-claim later and are counter-attested. Their take's timestamp is still evidence they were there. | Manual add at close; attendance backfilled, session grade unaffected (the session was still `captured`) |
| Session runs **past midnight** (normal) | A session is a continuous block, not a calendar day | Single session spanning dates |
| **Two sessions in one day** (afternoon tracking, evening mixing) | Two sessions — different work, different attendees, different outputs — even under **one booking** (D-07) | Two records, one booking link |
| **One session, two bookings** (overran; bought another block) | One session. The session's start/end are the work's, not the booking's. | One session, two booking links |
| Session **moves rooms** mid-session | One session, ordered location list. Splitting on location would model a distinction nobody makes (they were the same people doing the same work). | Session showing both rooms |
| Session created but **nothing happened** | Fine. Empty sessions are normal and are **not a defect** — some days nothing works. Never chased, never flagged as debt. | Session with no outputs; no prompt fires (`07.06.02` has nothing to ask) |
| **Booking exists; nobody turns up** (no-show) | The `Scheduled` session never activates. At 24h past its booked end it is **discarded**, not closed-empty — a session that never happened must not enter the record. The *booking* still happened and is the Operator's business (16.06). | Scheduled session disappears; the Operator's no-show/deposit flow is untouched |
| **Booking cancelled** after a session was mirrored from it | `Scheduled` session is discarded. If the session had already gone `Active`, the booking cancellation is ignored — **the work happened regardless of what the invoice says** (D-07). | Cancelled booking; session retained if it activated |
| **Rehearsal booking** (16.04) | Does **not** auto-create a session. Rehearsal rooms are booked hundreds of times a year and auto-creation would drown the record in noise. Promotable to a session in one tap by anyone in the booking — because arrangements genuinely get written in rehearsals. | Booking with a "was this a session?" affordance, offered once, never nagged |
| **Two inference sources fire for the same event** (booking mirror + DAW bridge) | **Deduplicated, not duplicated.** Candidate sessions that overlap in time and share a project *or* a location merge into one: earliest start, latest end, union of attendees, highest capture grade. Merging is silent; unmerging is a manual split. | One session, multiple sources listed |
| Dedupe merges **two sessions that were genuinely different** | The Producer splits it. The split is a manual act with an attendee re-assignment step — the system will not guess who was in which half. | "Split this session" — attendees re-assigned by hand |
| **DAW bridge infers a session for a project not on WeJammin** | Session held as **unlinked**, attributed to the bridge's owner only, with no attendees and no songs. It is a private note-to-self until a project exists. Never fabricates a project. | "Unlinked session — attach to a project?" |
| Producer **never closes**; session runs forever | Auto-closes after **12h with no activity** (no takes, no attendance changes, no bridge signal). A real session runs 10–14h *of activity*; 12h of silence is over. Marked `closed automatically`; capture grade unaffected; `07.06.02`'s prompt goes to **`Deferred`**, never auto-confirmed — the system will not attest on a human's behalf. | Session closed automatically; prompt waiting at their next appearance |
| Session **resumes after a break** (dinner, a night's sleep) | Activity resuming within **6h** of the last activity **re-opens the same session**. Beyond 6h it is a new session. Rationale: a dinner break is 1–2h; an overnight gap is a new day's work with different energy, often different people. | Session reopens, or a second session appears |
| Auto-closed session resumes at hour 13 | Reopens (within the 6h window) and un-closes. A `Deferred` prompt is withdrawn rather than fired twice. | Session live again; no duplicate prompt |
| Attendee **arrives late / leaves early** | Not recorded. Coarse attendance is deliberate (D-04) — "you were 40 minutes late" is a fact the platform refuses to hold about a worker. | Present, full stop |
| **Overlapping attendance** — the multi-hyphenate present at two sessions at once | **Permitted and never auto-resolved.** Genuinely possible: physically tracking at Studio A while logged into Band B's remote writing room. The platform does not adjudicate where a human "really" was. **But**: if both sessions feed a billing claim (the time-tracking cross-cut), the conflict is surfaced **to the person themselves**, never to either counterparty. | Nothing, unless it would double-bill — then a private "these overlap" flag to the attendee only |
| Producer is **also a player** in their own session | They mark themselves present. That is a **self-claim with no witness** (DT-08) and is graded as one until another attendee confirms the session's attendance. Their Producer lens does not launder their Musician claim. | Own attendance shown as unwitnessed until an attendee confirms |
| **Only one person** was there (writing alone at 2am) | A valid session. Attendance of one, self-claimed, unwitnessed forever unless output evidence corroborates. Solo work is most of songwriting; refusing to record it would make the career artifact a lie. | Session of one; `self-recorded` on the witness line |
| Producer marks **40 people present** to farm the credit graph | `meta/personas.md`'s named worst-intentional behavior ("games the verified-credit graph"). **Structurally neutralised by D-03**: attendance confers no credit, so farming attendance farms nothing. No cap is imposed — an orchestral date genuinely has 70 people. Each falsely-listed person is notified and can dispute in one tap (DT-08). | Every named person notified; disputes visible on the session |
| Attendance exceeds the room's stated capacity (16.03) | **A signal, not a block.** Surfaced to nobody as an accusation; available to domain 24 if the session is ever disputed. Rooms are over-filled all the time. | Nothing |
| Attendee is **not a WeJammin user** | Unclaimed identity created (`07.03.01` D-02); the attestation request is the invitation (`07.03.02` DT-01). Attendance stays a claim until they arrive to confirm it. | "Awaiting Sam" — a pending invitation, not a failure (`07.06.02` D-05) |
| Two attendees with the **same name** | Never auto-merged. The Producer picks from roster/contact candidates or creates a distinct unclaimed identity. Guessing here silently attributes a stranger's career to the wrong human. | Disambiguation list with context (last session together, project) |
| Someone **adds an attendee who was not there** (malicious or mistaken) | Only the session owner writes attendance. The named person is notified and disputes in one tap; the dispute is appended, never overwritten. Nothing is deleted. | Named person sees "X says you were at Y — confirm or dispute" |
| **Attendee disputes** being listed | Attendance flagged disputed on the session; routes to domain 24. Neither party overwrites the other; both positions persist. Downstream credits from that session show the dispute. | Both positions visible; dispute flag on the session and on derived credits |
| Attendee wants to be **removed** from a closed session (not a dispute — a preference) | Attendance is **not silently editable after close**. A closed session is multi-party evidence; letting one party quietly rewrite it destroys its value for everyone else. It goes through dispute (24), or the owner amends with the amendment recorded. | "Closed sessions are amended, not edited — the change is recorded" |
| Session **record edited after close** (owner fixes a genuine error) | Permitted, attributed, timestamped, and visible. The original value remains readable. | Edit history on the session; "amended by X on DD" |
| **Confidential project** — the session's *existence* is the secret | Attendance and session existence inherit the project's sensitivity class (`07.03.03`). A Musician's own history view **excludes** confidential sessions from any shared or exported version by default (DT-07). Nobody's career page ever leaks "was at Chalice with Artist X on Tuesday". | Confidential sessions visible privately, marked; excluded from anything shareable |
| Studio booking made under a **pseudonym** (standard practice for high-profile artists) | The Operator's disclosure-scoped view (D-11) shows **headcount and the booking's named contact only**. The platform never surfaces the real attendance list to the studio. What the Operator saw with their own eyes is theirs; what the platform knows is the client's. | Operator sees "9 people, 14:00–23:00, booked by [contact]" — no names the booking did not give them |
| **Attendee exercises erasure** (GDPR) | The **event** persists — it is other people's evidence, and their credits' witness list. The **person** degrades to a tombstone: "a contributor (record erased)". Derived credits keep their witness *count* and lose the name. One person's erasure never erases another person's evidence. Q-05 covers the residual legal question. | "1 attendee, record erased" — the witness list shrinks in identity, not in count |
| **Producer deletes their account** | The session survives its creator (DT-06). Ownership transfers to the project owner (`07.03.01`); if none, the session becomes ownerless-but-readable to its attendees. A session that vanishes with its creator destroys the provenance of everyone who was in it. | Session intact; "owner account closed" |
| **Network fails** mid-session; attendance taps lost | Taps queue locally and reconcile on reconnect. The session window is defined by the *work's* timestamps, not the app's connectivity — a studio basement with no signal is the normal case, not the edge case. | Optimistic ticks; "syncing" indicator; no data loss, no re-tapping |
| **Concurrent edits** — Producer and assistant tap attendance simultaneously | Attendance is a **set of presence assertions, not a list** — concurrent adds of different people both land; concurrent adds of the same person are idempotent. There is no last-write-wins because there is nothing to overwrite. | Both taps land; no conflict UI |
| Concurrent **conflicting** acts — owner removes an attendee while that attendee confirms | The confirmation wins as a *record* (it is now an attested disagreement) and the session shows a dispute. The system does not silently pick a winner on a question of fact. | Disputed attendance |
| **Retroactive session** — Producer backfills 400 sessions from a 20-year career | **Permitted and valuable** — it is the platform's only day-one answer for an existing career. Every one is graded `reconstructed` (D-08) and carries no more weight than the memory it came from. Attendees can witness them, which makes them *witnessed memory*, never `captured`. | Bulk import; every session badged `reconstructed`; the badge is permanent |
| Retroactive session dated **in the future** | Rejected at entry. A session is a thing that happened. | "Sessions are recorded, not scheduled — book the room instead" (links to 16.06) |
| Retroactive session dated before the attendee was born / obviously false | Not adjudicated by the platform (domain D-05: measure and show, never judge). It is `reconstructed`, unwitnessed, and worth exactly that. If someone disputes it, 24 handles it. | `reconstructed`, `unwitnessed` — the weakest grade the record has |
| **Booking made by someone else** in the band; the Producer runs the session | The booking's payer and the session's owner are different people and that is normal (D-07). Ownership goes to whoever the work identifies — the bridge's operator, or the booking payer if there is no other signal, with one-tap reassignment (DT-09). | "Is this your session?" once, to the payer; reassignable |
| Session has **no Producer at all** — two musicians in a bedroom | Personas are lenses, not account types. Whoever's context created the session holds the Producer lens **for that session** (DT-09). Every session has exactly one owner, always, because `07.06.02` needs someone to prompt. | Whoever started it owns it; transferable in one tap |
| Fetch fails / session list unavailable | Error with retry; cached sessions remain readable. | Error with retry |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | History or session view opened | Skeleton rows; cached sessions render immediately, freshness reconciles behind them |
| Empty (no sessions) | New user, or a project with no sessions yet | "No sessions yet — they appear when you book a room, take an order, or start working." **Never a "create session" primary CTA** (D-02) — offering the form as the answer is the failure this feature exists to avoid. Manual creation is a secondary affordance. |
| Scheduled | Mirrored from a confirmed booking (16.06), in the future | Upcoming session with expected attendees from the booking. **Not created by us** (DT-10). |
| Active | First activity signal (take, tap, bridge, room presence) | Live session; attendance addable in one tap; outputs attach as they land |
| Closed | Producer closed it, or 12h inactivity auto-close (`07.06.02`) | Record with attendance, songs, outputs; amendable with attribution, not silently editable |
| Empty (closed, no outputs) | Closed with nothing produced | Recorded honestly — not every session produces something. No prompt, no debt, no nag. |
| Partial | Attendance recorded but unwitnessed; or songs linked but no outputs; or a `reconstructed` session with no attestations | The record with an explicit gap statement — "3 attendees, 0 confirmed" / "reconstructed, unwitnessed". Never presented as complete. |
| Awaiting witnesses | Attendance claimed, attestations dispatched | "2 of 5 confirmed" — a **pending invitation, not a deficiency** (`07.06.02` D-05). This is the majority state for the platform's entire first phase. |
| Witnessed | ≥1 attendee other than the owner confirmed | The witness line the credit's weight actually rests on |
| Disputed | An attendee contested their attendance | Flagged on the session and on every credit derived from it; both positions visible; routes to 24 |
| Discarded | Scheduled session never activated, 24h past its booked end; or its booking was cancelled pre-activation | Gone from the session record. The booking is unaffected — that is the Operator's business. |
| Tombstoned | An attendee exercised erasure | "1 attendee, record erased" — the count survives, the identity does not |
| Error | Fetch/write failed | Error with retry; queued attendance taps preserved, never silently dropped |

Escalated as Q-03. **Amended by DT-14** — the union case shows the opt-in is not a nicety but the thing that pays some musicians. |
| DT-04 | A session, a **studio booking** (16.06) and a **service order** (05.03) are the same underlying thing wearing three hats | Sub-domain Q-01 named this "the sharpest cross-domain question in the domain", and the intuition is seductive: a booked studio day *is* all three at once — the Operator sells it, the Producer runs it, the platform credits from it. One entity, three lenses, no sync problem. | ❌ **REJECTED — they are three distinct facts that share a moment.** The seduction is real but the counter-evidence is decisive and it is everywhere: (a) **most sessions have neither** — the bedroom case is the majority, so a session cannot be a projection of a booking; (b) **most bookings are not sessions** — rehearsal rooms, no-shows, photo shoots; (c) **cardinality does not hold** — one booking holds two sessions (afternoon tracking / evening mixing), one session spans two bookings (it overran); (d) **the lifecycles contradict** — a booking can be *cancelled and refunded*, a session cannot be un-happened; a booking is `settled`, a session is `closed`; (e) **the parties differ** — payer↔Operator vs. attendees, which D-06 already refuses to reconcile; (f) **the visibility differs irreconcilably** — the Operator sees their booking in full and the session only as an event (R-01), which is impossible if they are one record. Forcing them together produces the nullable-column disaster D-14 rejected at the marketplace level, and it would leak the creative facts into the commercial record, breaking R-01 structurally rather than by policy. **Decided: distinct, linked `0..1 : 0..N`, never reconciled** (D-07). Resolves sub-domain Q-01 and Q-02 behaviorally; the storage representation remains `/create-prd-architecture`'s. |
| DT-05 | The session is the evidence of **an unpaid session** — provenance is not only about credits, it is about money already earned | `meta/personas.md`, Musician switching trigger, read literally: *"Losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove — **an unpaid session**, a credit that went to someone else, a split they agreed verbally and can't evidence."* An unpaid session is the **first** item in the trigger, and every prior reading of this domain treated the session as credit-infrastructure only. But an unpaid session fee is not a split dispute and not a credit dispute — it is a working musician not being paid for a day's labour, which is the most common financial injury in this profession and the one furthest from any rights database. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it materially raises the value of the Musician's read-only lens, which looked like a vanity view. "34 sessions last year" is a career artifact; "34 sessions, 31 paid, 3 outstanding, here are the dates and who else was there" is **leverage**. It also constrains the design: the session must be *linkable* to a service order (05.03) without *being* one (DT-04), because the fee is owed under an engagement and the session is the evidence the engagement was performed. And it means the session record has value **even with zero credits attached** — the rehearsal-room drummer with no writing split still gets paid, or doesn't. This is the strongest argument in the domain that this feature is not merely `07.06.02`'s input. |
| DT-06 | The session must **survive its creator** | Producers quit, studios close, accounts get deleted, people die. If a session record is the Producer's property, then every credit witnessed by that session is contingent on the Producer's continued existence and goodwill — which is exactly the fragility `meta/problem-statement.md` says the industry already suffers ("the proof of it evaporates"). A provenance record that a single party can unilaterally delete is not a provenance record. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the session is **multi-party evidence**, not the owner's document. Account deletion transfers ownership (project owner, else ownerless-but-readable to attendees); it never deletes the event. **The collision this exposes is real**: an attendee has a statutory erasure right, and attendance is personal data about a named human at a place and time. One party's erasure must not destroy another party's evidence. Direction: the *event* persists, the *identity* tombstones ("a contributor, record erased"), and the witness count survives even when the name does not — so a credit does not silently lose its witnesses. `meta/constraints.md` leaves GDPR unresolved (deferred to `/ideate-validate`); the residual legal question is Q-05. **This is the immutable-ledger vs right-to-be-forgotten collision, and it lands here first**, before it reaches domain 02 or 09, because attendance is the first personal fact the provenance chain records. |
| DT-07 | **Attendance is itself confidential** — a session's *existence* can be the commercial secret | Nothing in the file treated attendance as sensitive; it was framed as a neutral fact. It is not. Studio secrecy is an active, enforced industry norm: sessions are NDA'd, artists book under pseudonyms, and "who is in the room with whom" is market-moving information (an exclusive-contract breach, a leak of an unannounced collaboration, a label's secret). The domain already has a whole feature for this (`07.03.03` — NDA gating), and this feature was quietly bypassing it. And the harm is asymmetric: the Musician's own "34 sessions last year" view — the career artifact this feature is proudest of — is the **leak vector**, because it aggregates exactly what the norm exists to keep apart. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the sharpest thing the deepening pass found. Consequences: (a) session existence and attendance **inherit the project's sensitivity class** (`07.03.03`); (b) the Musician's session history is **private by default**, and any shared or exported version excludes confidential sessions unless individually released; (c) **the Operator's row must tighten** — a studio's booking under a pseudonym is a norm the industry actively maintains, and the platform surfacing the real attendance list to that studio would break it. There is a genuine distinction between "the Operator saw them with their own eyes" (theirs) and "the platform told them and stored it" (not theirs). Refines parent R-01 rather than contradicting it: R-01 grants the Operator "these people entered" as legitimate room knowledge, and the refinement is that the platform never *introduces* the Operator to an attendee the booking did not disclose. Escalated honestly as Q-04 rather than overridden here. |
| DT-08 | Attendance should itself be **attestable** — the witness list needs witnesses | Follows directly from DT-01. If a credit's weight comes from having witnesses, then an *unwitnessed witness list* contributes nothing: "the Producer says these five people were there" is a single human's uncorroborated assertion, which is the same epistemic object as a self-claimed credit — the thing `07.03.01` DT-03's attestation model exists to reject. The domain built a rigorous claim/counter-attestation model for credits and left the foundation they rest on as one person's word. **The gap is structural, and it is worst exactly where it matters most**: a Producer who also played marks their own attendance, then attests their own credit off the back of it — a self-claim laundered through the Producer lens. | ✅ CONFIRMED — attendance mirrors the credit model, which is cheap because the mechanism already exists: one tap, one notification, the same pattern as `07.06.02`'s counter-attestation. Each attendee's confirmation strengthens the record; "3 of 5 confirmed" is the witness line the credit's weight actually rests on. **This also neutralises the credit-farming abuse for free**: a Producer marking 40 people present notifies 40 people, each of whom disputes in one tap — the attack is loud by construction. And it closes the self-attestation hole: the Producer's own presence is unwitnessed until someone else confirms the session, and their Producer lens does not launder their Musician claim. Solo sessions stay `self-recorded` forever and are honest about it. |
| DT-09 | Every session needs **exactly one owner**, and the Producer lens is **per-session, not per-account** | Two things forced this. First, `07.06.02` must prompt *somebody* — a session with no owner captures nothing, which is the domain's whole failure mode. Second, `meta/personas.md`'s structural fact: personas are "lenses on behaviour, not account types; one human routinely occupies three of them in one day." The original Role Lens read as though Producer were an account type — but the drummer who engineers at a studio and sits in two bands is the Producer of the sessions they run and a read-only Musician on the sessions they merely attended, **on the same Tuesday**. A per-account reading of this table would fail the platform's central persona. | ✅ CONFIRMED — ownership is derived from the context that created the session (bridge operator > booking payer > room creator > first attendee), always resolves to exactly one person, and is reassignable in one tap. It also resolves the bedroom case, where nobody is "a Producer" in any professional sense: whoever's context created the session holds the lens for it. This is the Role Lens correction the multi-hyphenate demands, and it is the kind of error that is cheap here and expensive in `/write-be-spec`. |
| DT-10 | The platform should let Producers **schedule** sessions | The `Scheduled` state was already in the file, and scheduling is the obvious next step — it looks like table stakes, and every session tool has a calendar. | ❌ **REJECTED — and the `Scheduled` state is narrowed to a mirror because of it.** Domain D-07 (be the record, not the tool) applies exactly: Producers have a calendar, it is on their phone, it has their dentist in it, and it is not going to be replaced by a music platform's calendar. Building scheduling would (a) compete with Google Calendar and lose, (b) reintroduce the form D-02 exists to kill — a scheduled session is *created by hand* by definition, (c) put us in the notification/reminder business, and (d) generate a permanent population of scheduled-but-never-happened sessions polluting the provenance record. A `Scheduled` session may therefore exist **only** as a mirror of a booking (16.06) somebody already made elsewhere — the platform records the intention it was told about; it does not solicit it. This narrows the feature usefully and is the second time in this file that the honest answer is "the Producer already has that". |
| DT-11 | **Retroactive sessions must be allowed** — and must never be graded as captured | A domain expert asks this immediately: a producer with a 20-year back catalogue wants their 400 past sessions in here, and a musician's "34 sessions last year" is worthless on day one if the platform only knows what it saw. Refusing backfill makes the platform useless to anyone with a career, which is everyone it is for. **But a retroactively-created session is precisely the reconstruction-from-memory that `meta/problem-statement.md` identifies as the root failure** — "they are asking people to remember, and memory is contested, lossy, and self-interested." Building the industry's failure mode into the feature that exists to fix it is a genuine trap, and it is invisible if backfill is treated as a mere import feature. | ✅ CONFIRMED — resolved by refusing the false choice. Backfill is **permitted** (the cold-start answer) and **graded** (`reconstructed`, D-08), permanently, with the grade inherited by every credit derived from it. A reconstructed session can gain witnesses via DT-08 and become *witnessed memory*; it can **never** become `captured`, because that fact is gone and no amount of later agreement recreates it. This is domain D-06 (honest claims) applied to the platform's own evidence, and it mirrors `07.06.03` D-05 exactly (unknown formats degrade honestly, never a false verified claim). It also gives the platform something no competitor has: not "we have more credits", but **"we can tell you which of ours were witnessed at the time"** — which is the only claim in this market that a reconstruction engine structurally cannot make. |
| DT-12 | Sessions that **never end** are a silent, total failure of the domain | Reasoning about DT-02's consequence: if sessions are inferred rather than created, they are also not *closed* by anyone in particular. A bridge-inferred session whose Producer simply walked away sits `Active` forever. And a session that never closes **never fires `07.06.02`** — so the single most important interaction in the platform silently never happens, for a session that in every other respect worked perfectly. This is worse than a dismissed prompt, because a dismissal is at least a recorded decision; this is nothing at all, and nobody notices. | ✅ CONFIRMED — auto-close after **12h of inactivity** (a real session runs 10–14h *of activity*; 12h of silence is over), marked `closed automatically`, capture grade unaffected. **The prompt goes to `Deferred`, never auto-confirmed** — `07.06.02` D-04 means the system does not attest on a human's behalf, and an auto-confirmed credit would be a fabricated attestation, which is the one thing this platform can never do. Paired with a **6h resume-merge window**: activity resuming within 6h re-opens the same session (a dinner break is 1–2h), beyond it a new session (an overnight gap is a new day's work, often different people). Without both numbers, one long day becomes four sessions or four days become one. |
| DT-13 | A session's **location is PII** when it is not a commercial venue | Nobody flags this because "location" reads as a studio name. But the majority case is the bedroom (sub-domain Q-02), and a bedroom session's location is **a musician's home address**. A platform that stores and displays "session at 14 Elm Road" has built a home-address disclosure into a career artifact designed to be shown to strangers — and DT-07 has just established that the career view is the aggregation vector. Add device-derived location and it is worse: a geocoded home, timestamped, cross-referenced with named co-attendees. | ✅ CONFIRMED — location is either a **linked Place record** (16.01, a commercial venue that chose to be listed) or a **free-text label the owner types** ("my place", "Alex's basement"). Never geocoded, never auto-derived from device location, never a postal address for a non-commercial space. The convenience lost is negligible; the failure avoided is the kind that ends a platform's reputation in one news cycle. |
| DT-14 | **The session record already exists as a legal artifact** — and it is a timesheet | The domain-expert question nobody asked: what does the industry *already* do with session records? For union sessions it files them. Union session reports (AFM in the US, MU agreements in the UK) list the musicians, the instruments, the date, the studio and **the hours**, and they are the mechanism by which session players receive scale, new-use payments and pension contributions. This is a fifty-year-old artifact with the exact shape of this feature — and it means the hours are not an intrusion, they are **how some musicians get paid**. | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and it reframes DT-03/Q-03 rather than reversing it.** The surveillance analysis stands: coarse presence remains the default, because the *platform* holding a timesheet on a freelancer for a *studio's* benefit is the harm, and that harm is real. But DT-03's caveat — "opt-in, tied to time tracking, where the person tracked is the one who benefits" — turns out to have a concrete, load-bearing instance: the union session is exactly the case where the musician **wants** the hours recorded, because the hours are the pension contribution. So the opt-in is not a hedge, it is a named workflow with an existing paper form. **What is confirmed**: coarse-by-default, opt-in fine-grained, the tracked person controls it, and the Operator never receives it (their hours come from their own booking). **What is not confirmed and must not be fabricated**: the precise conformance shape — which forms, which territories, which fields, whether WeJammin ever *files* anything or merely holds the facts a filing needs. That is real regulatory surface with real liability and it is Q-06, not a guess made here. `meta/constraints.md` leaves its whole compliance section unresolved, deferred to `/ideate-validate`. |
| DT-15 | Attendance should be **automatic** — geofence the studio, or detect the phone on the room's network | The most tempting idea in the file, because it appears to satisfy DT-02 perfectly: zero taps, zero admin, attendance simply *known*. If sessions must cost the Producer nothing, why should attendance cost them even one tap per person? | ❌ **REJECTED, three ways.** (1) **It is surveillance, and worse than the DT-03 kind** — DT-03's timing at least described work; a geofence tracks a human's body continuously, in the background, to a place, and would have to run all day to catch the arrival. D-04 refuses far less than this. (2) **It is wrong** — studios are basements and concrete boxes with poor GPS and shared Wi-Fi; it would mark the courier, the next band loading in, the person in the café downstairs, and the assistant who never entered the live room. Attendance is a *provenance* fact; a provenance record that includes a courier is not a rounding error, it is a corrupted witness list, and D-03 means the false entries are not even caught by the credit prompt. (3) **It cannot be attested honestly** — DT-08's model requires a human assertion for another human to confirm or dispute; "your phone was here" is not an assertion anyone made, so there is nothing to counter-attest and no one accountable for the error. The one tap stays. It is not the cost DT-02 was worried about — the cost DT-02 refuses is *a form*, and one tap on a pre-filled name is not a form. |
| DT-16 | The session should be the **container** for everything produced in it | Intuitive and tidy: "everything from Tuesday lives under Tuesday", which is how a producer's Dropbox folder is actually organised. | ❌ **REJECTED — the session links material, it never owns it.** `07.04` owns takes, versions, comps and their lineage; `07.06.03` owns the snapshot; parent R-02 already rejected the mirror-image version of this (folding takes into the snapshot) with reasoning that applies unchanged: **the session is a *moment*; takes are *material***. Material outlives its moment and moves between them — a take cut Tuesday is comped Thursday and lands in a version next month, and its lineage is `07.04`'s spine, not a folder tree. Ownership here would make the session the object every other feature must go through, which is how a "unit of provenance" quietly becomes a filesystem. The session's claim on the work is a **timestamped link**, and that is exactly enough to be the witness — witnessing does not require custody. |
