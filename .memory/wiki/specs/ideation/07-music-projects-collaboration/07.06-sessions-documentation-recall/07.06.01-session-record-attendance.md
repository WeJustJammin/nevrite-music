# Feature: Session Record & Attendance

> **Number**: 07.06.01
> **Parent**: [Sessions, Documentation & Recall](./07.06-sessions-documentation-recall-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list (sub-domain D-01). The sweep had session
> *artifacts* (snapshot, documentation) but no **session entity**.

## Overview

A session is the dated event where humans were in a room and work happened: who was there, from when to when,
where, on which songs, and what came out of it. It is the **atomic unit of provenance**. A song is the output; a
session is the event — and a credit without a session has no timestamp, no witness list, and no context, which
is precisely why credits reconstructed years later are contestable.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees sessions they attended across every project and band — which, for the multi-hyphenate persona, is the first honest record of their own working life that has ever existed. "I played on 34 sessions last year, here they are" is a career artifact they currently cannot produce at any price. Cannot edit the session; can dispute their attendance. |
| Producer | Full | Creates sessions (ideally without noticing — see DT-02), records attendance, links songs, closes them (`07.06.02`). The session is their working unit: they think in sessions, not in songs. |
| Operator | Read-only | **The only place an Operator appears in domain 07.** A session physically happened in their room, so they know it occurred, who entered, and when it ran. They see the *event*, never the music: no songs, no versions, no credits, no splits. See parent R-01 — this boundary is load-bearing and gets sharper in `07.06.02`, where the Operator has no role at all. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A session is `(start, end, location, attendees, songs worked on, outputs)`. Attendance is the critical field and
the reason the feature exists: it is what makes `07.06.02`'s prompt pre-fillable rather than blank (parent CX-01),
and it is the witness list that gives a credit its weight.

The design problem is that **a session must cost the Producer nothing to create**. If creating a session is a
form, sessions will not exist, and everything in this sub-domain fails with them (sub-domain Q-03). So sessions
should be inferred wherever possible: from a studio booking (domain 16), from DAW activity (`07.09`), from a
service order (domain 05), from people simply being present in a real-time room (the transport cross-cut, D-15).

Attendance is not binary. A drummer who played for two hours, an engineer who was there for ten, and a friend who
came by are all "in the room" and mean different things. Whether the product models that is DT-03.

### Happy Path

1. Producer books a studio day (domain 16); a session is created automatically from the booking — start, end, location, and the client already known
2. On the day, people arrive; attendance is confirmed with a tap, or inferred from the DAW bridge's track-to-contributor mapping (`07.09.02`)
3. Work happens; takes and versions attach to the session as they are created
4. Producer closes the session (`07.06.02`); the prompt is pre-filled from attendance — **because this feature knew who was there**
5. The session persists forever as the timestamped, witnessed context for every credit that came out of it

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Session with **no studio** (bedroom, laptop on a train) | Must work — most modern sessions have no Operator at all (sub-domain Q-02). Location optional. | Session with no location; no Operator visibility |
| Distributed session (three people, three cities, over a real-time room) | Attendance is presence in the room, not physical location | Attendees with their locations, if disclosed |
| Someone was in the room but did not play | Real and common (a manager, a partner, a friend). **Attendance ≠ contribution** — conflating them would manufacture false credits at scale, which is exactly the DT-02 risk in `07.03.01`. | Attendee listed; **no credit implied** |
| Someone played but was never marked present | The prompt cannot pre-fill them; they can be added at close, or self-claim later and be counter-attested | Manual add at close |
| Session runs past midnight (normal) | A session is a continuous block, not a calendar day | Single session spanning dates |
| Two sessions in one day (afternoon tracking, evening mixing) | Two sessions — different work, different attendees, different outputs | Two records |
| Session created but nothing happened | Fine. Empty sessions are normal and are not a defect — some days nothing works. | Session with no outputs |
| Attendee disputes being listed | Removable; the dispute is recorded | Routes to domain 24 if contested |
| Operator's booking says 6 people; the session records 4 | Not reconciled. The studio's capacity/billing count and the creative attendance list are **different facts** and must not be forced to agree. | No conflict surfaced |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Scheduled | Created from a booking, in the future | Upcoming session with expected attendees |
| Active | In progress | Live session; attendance can be added; outputs attach as they land |
| Closed | Producer closed it (`07.06.02`) | Immutable record with attendance, songs, outputs |
| Empty | Closed with no outputs | Recorded honestly — not every session produces something |
| Disputed | Attendance contested | Flagged; routes to 24 |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **session is the atomic unit of provenance**, and the platform is incoherent without it | `meta/personas.md`, Producer: "The moment that matters: prompts/confirms who did what and what the split is, **while everyone is present**." Presence is a session. `meta/problem-statement.md`: "no system is present at the moment of creation." The moment of creation *is a session*. The sweep produced 35 candidates for this domain and not one of them was the event at the centre of the thesis — it captured the artifacts a session leaves behind and missed the session itself. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the strongest structural addition in this domain. It also answers the "what is a credit *about*?" question: a credit is a claim about **what someone did in a session**, which gives it a timestamp, a witness list, and a context. That is what makes it defensible where a self-asserted profile claim is not. |
| DT-02 | Sessions must be **inferred**, never created by a form | The Producer's constraint (`meta/personas.md`) applies with full force: they will not leave the DAW to create a calendar entry. And this feature is the base of the whole sub-domain — if sessions do not exist, `07.06.02` has nothing to fire on, `07.06.03` has nothing to snapshot, and the domain's thesis has no mechanism. **The dependency chain makes a form here fatal, not merely annoying.** | ✅ CONFIRMED — sessions are inferred from bookings (16), service orders (05), DAW activity (`07.09`), or real-time room presence. Manual creation exists as a fallback, never as the path. This is a hard dependency on domains 16/05 or on `07.09`, and it means **this feature cannot ship truly standalone** — it needs at least one inference source or it becomes the form it must not be. Escalated as Q-01. |
| DT-03 | Attendance should record **duration and role**, not just presence | Reasoning about what a credit needs to be defensible. "Sam was at the session" is weak; "Sam was there 14:00–16:30 and 6 drum takes appeared in that window" is evidence. The DAW bridge makes the second one nearly free — the takes have timestamps. | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — with a real caution.** The evidentiary value is genuine and the correlation is nearly free. But per-minute attendance tracking of named humans is **surveillance**, and it lands on musicians in a workplace context: "you were 40 minutes late" is a fact the platform would now hold, and studios could use it against freelancers. This is `07.05.02` DT-03's problem in a more dangerous form, because it is about a person's labour rather than their listening. Direction: record **coarse presence** (attended this session) by default, derive evidence from *output timestamps* (which are facts about the work, not about the human), and treat fine-grained attendance timing as opt-in — most likely tied to time tracking (the billing cross-cut), where the person being tracked is the one who benefits. Escalated as Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The **Session is a first-class entity** — the atomic unit of provenance | Per DT-01, sub-domain D-01. Added to the candidate list by Deep Think. |
| D-02 | Sessions are **inferred** from bookings, service orders, DAW activity or room presence; manual creation is a fallback | Per DT-02 — a form here is fatal to the whole sub-domain. |
| D-03 | **Attendance ≠ contribution.** Being in the room implies no credit | Conflating them would manufacture false credits at scale — the abuse surface named in `07.03.01` DT-02. |
| D-04 | Default attendance is **coarse** (present at this session); evidence comes from output timestamps, not from tracking humans | Per DT-03 — fine-grained presence tracking of workers is surveillance the platform should not default into. |
| D-05 | The Operator sees the **event, never the music** | Parent R-01 — the domain's sharpest privacy boundary. |
| D-06 | The studio's headcount and the creative attendance list are **different facts** and are never reconciled | Forcing them to agree would corrupt one to satisfy the other. |
