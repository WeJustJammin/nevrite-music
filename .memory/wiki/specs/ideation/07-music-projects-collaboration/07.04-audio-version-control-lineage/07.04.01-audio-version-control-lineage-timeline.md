# Feature: Audio Version Control, Lineage & Immutable Timeline

> **Number**: 07.04.01
> **Parent**: [Audio Version Control & Lineage](./07.04-audio-version-control-lineage-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Merged from sweep candidates 09 ("Audio Version Control & Lineage") and 11 ("Immutable Version Timeline &
> Change Summary")** per sub-domain D-01 — version control *is* the immutable timeline; the change summary is a
> property of a version, not a separate system.

## Overview

An append-only timeline of every bounce a song accumulates, each version knowing what it came from, who made it,
when, and what changed. It is the direct answer to `Mix_FINAL_v3_ACTUAL.wav` — an industry-wide failure that is a
joke until the wrong master ships. It is deliberately **not Git for audio**: there is no branching, no merging,
no conflict resolution, because audio cannot be merged (sub-domain D-03). What it provides is lineage, identity
and an immutable record.

It exists because of `meta/problem-statement.md`'s root cause — *no system is present at the moment of creation,
so the record is always a reconstruction*. The timeline is the record. Every other feature in this sub-domain is
a read over it (the resolver points into it, the A/B player listens across it, integrity gates entry to it), and
domains 02 and 24 consume it as evidence. If the timeline is wrong or incomplete, everything built on it inherits
the defect silently.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the whole timeline of any song they are on the roster of (`07.03.01`): every version in ingest order, its platform number **and the producer's own label** (DT-06), who bounced it, when, what changed, what it came from, and which versions carry a canonical badge (`07.04.02`). Can open any two in the A/B player (`07.04.06`). **Cannot** create versions (they come from the DAW), cannot delete them, cannot correct lineage, cannot edit a change summary they did not write. This is often the first time an artist has ever seen the actual history of their own record rather than a folder of ambiguously-named files. |
| Producer | Full | Creates versions, usually via the DAW bridge (`07.09`) rather than by uploading. Writes or confirms the change summary within the edit window (D-11). Corrects lineage when the system inferred it wrongly — and the correction is itself recorded and attributed (D-06). Declares a lineage edge as recording-crossing when the system asks (DT-04). The heaviest user of this feature by a wide margin: on a working record they touch it several times a day, and every other persona touches it weekly at most. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator has no access to a client's version history. They know a session is booked in their room (domain 16) and may know its duration and headcount for capacity reasons; **what was bounced in it is the client's confidential business.** The distinction matters: the operator owns the room, never the work made in it. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, at any status, ever. The history of a record is professional information — and often unflattering. A Fan meets the canonical master *after* it becomes a released recording via domain 12; they never meet the pointer, the timeline, or the 34 versions that lost. |

> **The multi-hyphenate rule (DT-08).** The rows above are lenses on a **roster role held on this specific song**
> (`07.03.01`), not on an account type. One human is routinely Musician on their own record and Producer on
> someone else's, in the same hour. Access is the **union of the roles that person holds on *this* song**: an
> artist who produces their own record holds both roles here and is therefore Full — read-only would lock them
> out of their own history, which is absurd. Conversely, a producer who also played drums on a song they do not
> produce is Full through the producer role and gains nothing from the player role. Access resolves per song and
> per person, never per account. `meta/personas.md` is explicit that a UX forcing "are you an artist OR a
> producer?" fails the platform's central persona immediately.

## Behavior

A version is an audio asset plus its **record**: platform sequence number, the producer's own label, lineage
(`derived from v6`), the character of that lineage edge (same-recording or new-recording, per DT-04), author,
ingest timestamp, claimed authored-at, type (`rough / mix / master / stem set / comp`), checksum, derived
metadata (duration, sample rate, channel count, integrated loudness, true peak), machine-measured deltas against
the parent, and a human change summary.

Versions are **immutable and append-only** (D-01): no edit, no delete, no rewrite, no reordering. That is what
makes the timeline a *record* rather than a document, and what makes it usable as evidence in a dispute
(domain 24). Everything the feature can do about a mistake, it does by **appending** — a correction is a new
recorded fact, never an erasure of the old one (D-06).

Release-time tempo/key corrections from domain 12 obey the same rule but target the exact audio version,
never the Song. A version owner or Producer carrying explicit descriptor-edit authority may append the
version correction under the release correction's shared ID. Release access alone grants no project write:
without that exact-version authority, domain 12 creates a pending proposal for an authorized version owner
or Producer. Acceptance appends and attributes the version fact; rejection changes no project fact. The
initiating release may keep its own local correction either way (D-14).

**The record and the bytes are two different things** (D-07). This is the resolution of the immutability/cost
collision named in DT-03 and sub-domain Q-02. The *record* is kilobytes, is immutable, and is never tiered,
never deleted, never lost. The *bytes* have a residency state — `hot`, `cold`, or `tombstoned` — and may move.
A tombstoned version is still a full row in the timeline with its lineage, author, checksum, deltas and change
summary intact; only the audio is gone. **A tombstone is still evidence**: it proves the version existed, who
made it, what it came from and what it measured. The lineage chain from v1 to v20 is never broken by tiering,
because the links live in the record, not in the audio. What the tiering *policy* is — the horizons, and who
pays for hot storage of a twelve-year-old stem pack — is a pricing decision the owner must make (Q-01).

**Order is arrival, not claim** (D-08, per DT-05). The timeline's one true order is ingest sequence. A version's
claimed authored-at is metadata supplied by a machine whose clock may be wrong, whose session was bounced from a
2019 archive, or whose owner has an interest in the answer. It is displayed; it never reorders the record. An
append-only log that resequences itself when a laptop's clock is wrong is not append-only.

**Identity is dual** (D-09, per DT-06). Every version carries the platform's sequence number (`v7` — immutable,
authoritative, per song) *and* the producer's own label lifted verbatim from the filename (`Ghost Town mix 9 rev
B`). Both are searchable. The platform never renames the producer's file and never asks them to adopt its
numbering. A version-control system that renames your files is the version-control system nobody uses — and if
the platform says `v7` while the WhatsApp thread says `mix 9`, it has rebuilt `FINAL_v3_ACTUAL` out of its own
numbers.

**Lineage is inferred, not typed** (D-03). A producer who must fill in "this came from v6" will not, and the
lineage will be wrong within a week. The frequency is what makes manual entry fatal: this prompt would fire on
every bounce, several times a day, and `meta/personas.md` is unambiguous that the Producer will not leave the
session for a web form. The DAW bridge (`07.09`) knows the parent because it knows the session that produced the
bounce; where the bridge is absent, the system infers from timing, filename and audio similarity, and asks only
when genuinely uncertain.

**The change summary is machine facts plus a human note** (D-04). The system detects *measurable* deltas
("2.1 LU louder, 4 s longer, a stem appeared") but not *intent* ("brought the vocal up, tamed the 3k"). The
deltas are a cheap sanity check on the human's note, not a replacement for it. Critically, **an uncomputed delta
is never rendered as a zero delta** (D-12) — "couldn't measure" and "no change" are different facts, and
conflating them turns the sanity check into a lie.

**Nothing here blocks the work.** Consistent with the domain-wide rule (`07.01.03` D-02, `07.04.04` D-03): a
failed parse still yields a version; an unmatched bounce still lands; an undecodable file still becomes a row;
a dismissed summary prompt still closes. The single exception in this sub-domain is integrity gating
canonicality (parent CX-04), and that block lives in `07.04.02`, not here.

### Happy Path

> **This is the post-gate happy path — steps 1–3 are not v1 (D-19).** No non-web client on the producer's
> machine is authorised (`07.09` D-05), so there is no watched folder, no agent and no session identifier for
> the whole v1 window. The v1 shape of the same path is: the producer **uploads the bounce manually**, and the
> parent is inferred from timing, filename and audio similarity — the fallback the Behavior section above
> already specifies — with the system asking rather than guessing where inference is weak. **Steps 4–8 are
> unchanged and run in v1**: the record is written immediately, the deltas are computed from the audio, the
> roster is notified, the canonical pointer does not move, and the change summary is prompted non-blockingly.

1. Producer bounces a new mix in their DAW into the watched folder (`07.09.01`) — exactly as they have for fifteen years, changing nothing
2. The agent waits for the file to settle, uploads it, and hands it to ingest with the session identifier that produced it
3. System matches it to the song, infers the parent from the session (v6, same session, same song), and assigns the next platform sequence number — **v7** — preserving the producer's own label `Ghost Town mix 9 rev B` verbatim alongside it
4. System writes the record immediately: v7 exists, is attributed, is timestamped by arrival, and is playable, before any derived work has run
5. Behind it, the system computes checksum, duration, sample rate, channel count, loudness and true peak, and diffs them against v6's stored metadata → `+2.1 LU, +4 s, same channel count`
6. Roster is notified that a new mix exists; **the canonical pointer does not move** (`07.04.02` D-02) — the newest bounce is very often an experiment
7. Producer is prompted — non-blockingly, and pre-filled with the machine deltas — "what changed?"; types "vocal up 1dB, de-essed". The note is attributed to them and becomes immutable when the edit window closes (D-11)
8. Anyone on the roster can now A/B v6 against v7 (`07.04.06`); the timeline permanently records who made what, when, and what it came from — and will still record it after v7's bytes have long since tiered to cold

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Identical file uploaded twice** (same checksum, same song) | Detected by checksum; no duplicate version created. Dedupe is scoped **to the song** — the same bytes arriving against a different song are a legitimate new version of that song, not a duplicate. | "This is identical to v6 — nothing was added" |
| **Same session re-bounced, bytes differ, audio identical** (dither is stochastic; DAWs embed bounce timestamps) | The checksum dedupe silently misses this — it is one of the most common real duplicates. Caught by comparing derived metadata and audio fingerprint: **all deltas zero** → confirm *before* creating, because D-01 means a version created in error can never be deleted. | "This sounds identical to v7. Add it as v8 anyway?" |
| **File renamed and re-dropped** | Same checksum → dedupe (no new version). The **first** label is retained; the rename is not a version event. | "Already ingested as v6 (`Ghost Town mix 8`)" |
| **Bounce with no discernible parent** (first version, or a mix from another studio) | Version created as a **lineage root**. Roots are legitimate, not errors — work arrives from outside the platform constantly. | Version shows "no parent" without alarm |
| **Version arrives before its parent** (agent was offline; v8 uploads before v7) | The system holds an **unresolved parent reference** (it has the session id, not the version) for **7 days**. When the parent lands, it links and records the link as **system-inferred**, not as a human correction — the distinction matters because corrections are evidence (D-06). After 7 days it settles as a root. | "Waiting on the version this came from" → resolves silently, or settles as a root |
| **Lineage inferred wrongly** | Correctable by the Producer. The correction is itself an appended, attributed record — you cannot quietly rewrite history. | "Parent changed from v6 to v5 by Producer, 14 Aug" in the timeline |
| **Lineage correction made after the version was nominated canonical, or after a dispute is open** (DT-07) | Permitted, but flagged at **higher prominence** and notified to the song owner and every dispute participant. This is the timeline's forgery surface: quietly re-parenting a version after the stakes are known is exactly how you make an evidence log lie. | "Lineage was changed after this version became canonical" — persistent marker on the row, not a toast |
| **Lineage correction by someone who is not the version's author** | Permitted (a producer correcting a colleague's mis-inferred parent is normal) but attributed to the corrector, not the author, and notified to the author. | "Parent changed by Alex (v7 was bounced by Sam)" |
| **Lineage correction would create a cycle** (v7's parent set to v9, which descends from v7) | Rejected at write. A cycle is not a wrong fact, it is a nonsensical one — history cannot descend from its own descendant. | "v9 already comes from v7 — this would make the history circular" |
| **Lineage correction points at a version in a different song** | Allowed — cross-song lineage is real (a remix's parent is another artist's recording). Requires the corrector to have vault access to the parent song (`07.03.03`), and **crosses a recording boundary by definition** (DT-04), so it is flagged to both songs' owners: it asserts a derivation across a rights boundary and is very often a licensing act, not a filing act. | "This says v3 comes from a version of *Other Song*. Both owners will be told." |
| **Version author leaves the roster** | Version and authorship persist (`07.03.01` D-05 — access and authorship are orthogonal). Removing someone revokes their access; it cannot un-bounce their mix. | Author still shown |
| **Bounce cannot be attributed to a person** (shared studio machine — `07.09.01` Q-03) | The author field is **never null**. The version lands attributed to the agent's registered account with a visible **unconfirmed attribution** marker. A silently-wrong attribution is a provenance error introduced by the provenance mechanism — the worst failure available to this product — so it is flagged, not guessed. Whose account the agent acts as is `07.09.01` Q-03's problem; the timeline's requirement is that it must be *someone*, and that an uncertain answer must look uncertain. | "Bounced from Studio A's machine — attribution unconfirmed" with a one-tap fix |
| **200 versions on one song** | Normal for a heavily-worked record and not a defect. Timeline needs filtering by type, author, canonical status and date, not a cap. | Filtered views; canonical versions pinned prominently |
| **Two producers bounce simultaneously from different sessions** | Both land as siblings of the same parent. **This is the correct outcome, not a conflict** — parallel mixes are real. Resolution is by choosing (`07.04.02`), never merging (D-02). | Two versions shown as siblings |
| **Two people correct the same version's lineage concurrently** | Last write wins; **both are recorded** in the append-only correction log and both are attributed. Consistent with `07.04.02`'s concurrent-nomination handling — silence here would recreate the confusion the sub-domain exists to end. | "Alex changed this parent 30 seconds after you did" |
| **Release-time tempo/key correction arrives from domain 12** | Resolve the exact audio version and current descriptor-edit authority. Authorized version owner/Producer appends under the shared correction ID; otherwise create a pending proposal. Acceptance or rejection is terminal and attributed. Never write a Song attribute or overwrite prior metadata. | Authorized reviewers see the proposal and its release origin; the release submitter sees only appended, pending, accepted or rejected status. |
| **Claimed timestamp is impossible** (clock skew; a bounce dated 1970 or three days in the future; a child claiming to predate its parent) | Ingest order stands (D-08). The claimed time is shown with a **disputed-time marker**; it never reorders the timeline. | "This file says it was made before the version it came from — showing arrival order" |
| **Session was duplicated as a template for a new song** | The session id matches song A, but the audio shares no fingerprint with any of song A's versions. **Do not guess** — a mis-filed bounce is a provenance error. Routes to the unmatched bin (`07.09.01` D-04). | "This doesn't sound like anything on *Ghost Town*. Which song is it?" |
| **Non-audio file dropped in the watch folder** (a PDF, a `.DS_Store`) | Not ingested as a version. **Never silently dropped** (`07.09.01` D-04) — surfaced in the unmatched bin with a reason. | "Not an audio file — ignored" |
| **Audio the platform cannot decode** (exotic codec, DSD, unusual bit depth) | Version is created anyway — never block the record. Marked unplayable in-browser; deltas uncomputable. Honest degradation, matching `07.04.05` D-05: never claim a check that was not performed. | "v9 is here but we can't play or measure this format" — row present, playback disabled |
| **Delta computation job fails** (DSP crash, timeout on a 4 GB stem pack) | Version stays present and playable. Deltas render as **"couldn't measure"**, never as zero (D-12). Absent is not the same fact as unchanged, and rendering it as unchanged would silently break the sanity check D-04 exists to provide. | "Changes not measured" with retry — never "no changes" |
| **Truncated bounce that is a valid file** (the bounce stopped early) | Checksums cannot catch this — the bytes are intact and the audio is wrong. Caught by duration delta against the parent (`07.04.05` DT-02). Non-blocking. | "This mix is 12 s shorter than v6 — intentional?" |
| **Version fails an integrity check later** (bit rot, storage fault) | Flagged; ineligible for canonical (parent CX-04). If it *is* canonical, `07.04.02` D-05 alarms rather than silently falling back. The record survives regardless — a corrupt version is still a true statement that a version existed. | Version flagged; prominent alert to owner and producer |
| **Non-canonical version untouched past the cold horizon** | Bytes move to cold storage. Record untouched. Playback still works, with a wait. | "Retrieving — about 2 minutes" then plays normally |
| **Version's bytes tombstoned; someone tries to A/B it** (`07.04.06`) | Comparison is impossible and the system says so plainly rather than failing obscurely. | "v3's audio is no longer stored — you can't compare it" |
| **Tombstoned version is the parent needed for a plausibility check** | Works anyway. Duration, sample rate, channels and loudness live in the **record**, not the bytes (D-07) — so "12 s shorter than v6" stays computable forever, even after v6's audio is gone. This is the specific reason derived metadata is part of the record. | No degradation; the check still fires |
| **Someone nominates a version as canonical while it is being tombstoned** | Nomination wins the race — canonical pins hot permanently (D-10). If the bytes are already gone, the nomination is refused: the resolver may only point at audio that exists. | "v3's audio is no longer stored and can't be made canonical" |
| **Owner requests deletion of a version** | Refused. D-01 has no exception for the owner — an owner-deletable history is not a record, and the owner is precisely the party a contributor most needs the record against. | "Versions can't be deleted. You can mark it superseded." |
| **Contributor demands erasure of a version they authored** (GDPR/CCPA) | The **person** is redacted; the **version** persists (DT-09). The audio is a collaboration's work product, not one contributor's personal data, and deleting it would destroy every other contributor's provenance. Legal force of this position is a live compliance question (Q-04). | Author renders as "removed contributor"; lineage, deltas and summary intact |
| **Change summary contains a typo** | Editable by its author within **60 minutes**, and only while the version is unreferenced. After that, an appended correction — the original stays visible (D-11). | "Edit closes in 43 min" → afterwards, "Add a correction" |
| **Change summary needs correcting after the version was approved, made canonical, or disputed** | Edit window closes **immediately** on any of those events regardless of the clock — the moment a note becomes evidence, it stops being editable. Corrections append. | "This version has been approved — you can add a note, not change this one" |
| **Change summary is a deliberate lie** ("minor tweak" on a mix that is 3 LU louder and 8 s shorter) | Not preventable at write. Detectable at read: the machine deltas sit beside the note and contradict it (D-04). The note is attributed and immutable, which makes the lie durable evidence rather than a deniable memory. Disputes route to domain 24. | Note shown next to "+3.0 LU, −8 s" — the contradiction is the product |
| **Version uploaded to a song by a roster member acting maliciously** (planting a version to claim credit) | Lands, attributed and immutable. Not blockable — write access is roster-derived and the platform cannot know intent. The record is the defence: it says exactly who added it and when. Routes to domain 24. | Version present with author; disputable |
| **Bulk ingest flood** (thousands of files into the watch folder) | Unusual ingest volume on one song is flagged to the song owner. Rate and quota mechanics are architecture, routed. | "412 versions were added to this song today" |
| **Roster access is revoked mid-playback of a version** | Playback stops at the current buffer; the timeline row disappears from that person's view on next load. Their authored versions remain in everyone else's view, attributed to them. | "You no longer have access to this song" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Timeline opened | Version row skeletons |
| Empty | No versions on this song | "No audio yet" + bridge setup CTA, with manual upload as the honest fallback (`07.09.01` DT-03) |
| Populated | Versions exist | Timeline in ingest order with platform number, producer's label, lineage, author, change summary, machine deltas, canonical badges, and filters (type / author / canonical / date) |
| Ingesting | Upload or bridge transfer in progress | Progress with an optimistic entry; large stem packs take real time and a failed 4 GB upload at 90% is product-abandoning (routed: resumable chunked upload) |
| Processing | Ingested; checksum, metadata and deltas being computed | **Version exists, is attributed, and is playable**; derived metadata fills in behind it. The record never waits on the DSP. |
| Partial | Deltas uncomputable (job failed, or format undecodable) | Row complete; changes read "couldn't measure" — **never** "no changes" (D-12) |
| Awaiting parent | Parent reference unresolved, inside the 7-day window | "Waiting on the version this came from" — the version is fully usable meanwhile |
| Attribution unconfirmed | Bridge could not attribute the bounce to a person | Author shown with an unconfirmed marker and a one-tap fix — never silently guessed |
| Cold | Bytes tiered; record untouched | Row identical; play button says "Retrieving — about 2 minutes" |
| Tombstoned | Bytes dropped per the retention policy; record permanent | Full row — lineage, author, checksum, deltas, summary — with "audio no longer stored". **Still a link in the chain, still evidence.** |
| Integrity failed | Checksum or media check failed (`07.04.05`) | Version flagged, ineligible for canonical (parent CX-04) |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Lineage must be **inferred from the DAW bridge**, not entered by hand — hand-entered lineage is wrong within a week | `meta/personas.md` Producer constraint: "any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and go fill in a web form will not be used." Lineage is exactly such a form, and it is the highest-frequency one: it recurs on every bounce, several times a day. Frequency is what makes manual entry fatal here. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this feature is **structurally dependent on `07.09` (DAW Bridge)**. Without the bridge, versions arrive as manual uploads with guessed lineage, and the timeline degrades into a slightly better Dropbox. This is a hard dependency that MoSCoW must respect: shipping this without the bridge delivers much less than it appears to. |
| DT-02 | The system can auto-generate a useful **change summary** by analysing the audio diff | The candidate name ("Change Summary") implies it, and it is technically feasible to compute loudness/spectral/length deltas | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED** — the system can detect *measurable* deltas ("2.1 LU louder, 4 s longer, a stem appeared") but not *intent* ("brought the vocal up, tamed the 3k"). Measurable deltas are genuinely useful as a **cheap sanity check on the human's summary** — if the producer says "tiny vocal tweak" and the file is 3 LU louder and 8 seconds shorter, something is wrong and that is worth catching. But it cannot replace the human note, and presenting spectral deltas *as* a changelog would be technically impressive and practically useless. Ship both: machine facts as a check, human note as the summary. |
| DT-03 | Immutability (D-01) is compatible with the platform's storage economics | Assumed when sub-domain D-02 was made | ❌ **REJECTED as stated — and now resolved structurally.** Every version of every stem pack of every song, forever, is a very large bill for a solo-built platform (`meta/problem-statement.md`: "buildable without a platform team"). But immutability is not decorative — a mutable history is not a record. **Resolution: separate the record from the bytes** (D-07). The record (existence, sequence, lineage, author, checksum, derived metadata, summary) is kilobytes, immutable, permanent. The bytes have a residency state and may tier to cold or tombstone. The provenance claim survives intact because the claim was never made of the audio — it was made of the record. What remains genuinely open is the *policy*: the horizons and who pays. That is pricing, not architecture, and it is Q-01. |
| DT-04 | Every version in a lineage chain is a version of **one recording** — so "which version" is a filing question, not a rights question | The natural reading of a linear timeline, and the assumption baked into sub-domain Q-01 ("is a version a first-class recording entity?") | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the finding that resolves sub-domain Q-01.** In music, **some lineage edges cross a recording boundary and most do not**, and the difference decides whether master splits carry down the chain or reset. Within-recording edges: mix revision, re-comp from the same session's takes (CX-02 — the performances already existed, so a re-comp is a new *assembly*, not a new recording), master-within-the-release-cycle, alternate versions derived from the same mix (`07.07.02` — instrumental, clean, radio edit). Boundary-crossing edges: a **remix** (new elements, a third party, a different derivative recording — and a licensing act if the remixer is outside the rights chain); a **re-record** (new performances of the same work — new ISRC, same ISWC); a **remaster reissued as a distinct commercial product** years later. **The line is: the boundary is crossed when the set of performances changes, or when the recording is re-issued as a distinct product.** Consequences: (a) an ISRC attaches to a *recording that ships*, not to every bounce — so most versions never get one and asking for one per version would be nonsense; (b) master splits attach at the recording, so they carry down within-recording edges and **reset** at boundary-crossing ones; (c) the timeline must be able to *express* the character of each edge, which is inferable in the common case (a bounce from the same session is same-recording) and must be **asked** when a version arrives claiming an outside parent. The *entity model* — whether a recording gets its own row — is architecture and joins `07.01.01` Q-01, the domain's highest-leverage schema decision. What is settled here is the behavior: **the edge carries the character; the version does not carry an ISRC.** |
| DT-05 | The timeline should be ordered by when each version was **made** | Obviously what a user wants — they think in "the Tuesday mix", not "the ninth thing you uploaded" | ❌ **REJECTED as the ordering, retained as a display.** The claimed authored-at comes from a machine whose clock may be wrong, whose session may have been bounced out of a 2019 archive, and whose owner has a direct interest in the answer once a dispute exists. An append-only log that resequences itself on a claimed field is not append-only — and this one is *evidence* (domain 24), where "the order changed after the fact" is fatal. **Ingest order is the record's order** (D-08); authored-at is shown, and flagged when impossible (before its own parent, or in the future). The subtlety worth naming: this is the first place where "what the user would naively want" and "what makes the artifact trustworthy" diverge, and the record has to win. |
| DT-06 | The platform should assign version numbers, and that is the identity | The obvious design — a sequence per song, clean and authoritative | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH A CAVEAT THAT IS ACTUALLY THE POINT.** The platform's sequence must exist (it is the only stable, forgery-resistant identity). But if it is the *only* identity, the platform has built a second numbering system alongside the producer's own — and the producer's does not go away. The platform says `v7`; the file says `Ghost Town mix 9 rev B`; the WhatsApp thread says "use mix 9". **That is `FINAL_v3_ACTUAL` reborn inside the product, in the product's own numbers, caused by the feature that exists to kill it.** Resolution: **dual identity** (D-09). The sequence is authoritative and immutable; the producer's own label is preserved **verbatim**, never normalised, never corrected, never used to rename their file; both are searchable and both are displayed. The platform's numbering must never become a translation layer the humans have to do in their heads — and a version-control system that renames your files is the version-control system nobody uses. |
| DT-07 | Immutability (D-01) plus recorded corrections (D-06) make the timeline forgery-proof | The natural conclusion from an append-only design, and the basis of its value as evidence in domain 24 | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — the correction affordance is the attack surface, and it is the one D-03 forces us to have.** Lineage must be correctable (inference is wrong sometimes) and corrections are recorded (D-06). But "recorded" only helps if the surface that matters actually *shows* the correction history rather than the current state — and the natural attack is precisely to correct lineage **after** the stakes are known: re-parent a version once it is canonical so that your mix appears to be the ancestor of what shipped, or once a dispute opens. Nobody re-parents v4 in week two for gain; they do it in month nine. Resolution: corrections are permitted always (blocking them would make honest corrections impossible and drive the real lineage back into filenames), but a correction made **after the version was nominated canonical, or while a dispute is open on the song**, is flagged persistently on the row and notified to the owner and every dispute participant — and it is a persistent marker, not a toast, because a toast is a thing you dismiss. **The lesson generalises: append-only is necessary and not sufficient. What makes a log evidence is that the reading surface shows the appends, not just the result.** |
| DT-08 | The Role Lens can be read per persona, since a person is a Musician or a Producer | The default reading of any Role Lens table, and it is wrong here specifically | ❌ **REJECTED — and it bites hardest on exactly this feature.** `meta/personas.md`'s first structural fact is that these are lenses on behaviour, not account types, and this table asserts Musician-Read-only and Producer-Full — a hard split. The artist producing their own record is both, and a naive reading locks them out of the write side of their own history. Resolution: **access is the union of the roster roles that person holds on *this song*** (`07.03.01`), resolved per song and per person, never per account. Two consequences downstream: (a) the same human legitimately has different access to two songs *simultaneously*, so any cached "your role" is a bug waiting to happen; (b) `07.03.01`'s roster is not merely the access list for this feature, it is the *definition* of it — which raises this feature's dependency on the roster from "reads it" to "is meaningless without it". |
| DT-09 | A version can be deleted when law requires it, so GDPR erasure and D-01 do not actually collide | The comfortable assumption — nobody wants to design this | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — and the collision is real, but it is the wrong collision.** The instinct is that erasure means deleting the version. It does not, because **a version's audio is a collaboration's work product, not one contributor's personal data**: deleting the mix a departing engineer bounced would destroy the drummer's, the writer's and the artist's provenance — erasing four people's record to satisfy one. What is personal data here is small and separable: the author identity and the free-text summary. So the product's position is **redact the person, never the version** — the author renders as "removed contributor" and lineage, deltas, checksum and sequence stay intact, which is exactly the "redacted but structurally intact" shape `07.01.01` Q-02 is already reaching for. Two things I will not assert: that this survives contact with a regulator (it is an argument, not a ruling), and that a *vocal* recording is comfortably "not personal data" — an identifiable voice is a harder case than a drum bounce and I will not pretend otherwise. The position is stated so it can be tested; the legal question is Q-04, and it is genuinely a compliance decision, not an agent's call. |
| DT-10 | The timeline should render as a **tree**, since lineage is a graph and branching is real | Lineage plus siblings is literally a DAG, and the Git analogy makes a tree feel inevitable | ❌ **REJECTED — the last echo of parent R-01, and it fails for a different reason.** Real lineage on a real record is overwhelmingly linear: 200 versions, ~195 of them a straight chain, with a handful of sibling pairs where two people bounced at once. Rendered as a tree that is a 195-node trunk with three twigs — a worse list, with the useful information (which is canonical, what changed, who did it) squeezed out by whitespace drawn to depict branching that mostly is not there. Worse, it *teaches the Git model the sub-domain spent D-02 rejecting*: a user shown a tree looks for the merge, and there is none. Resolution: **linear list in ingest order by default**, lineage shown inline as a "from v6" reference; a graph view only surfaces where siblings actually exist, and only locally around them. Show the fork where there is a fork; do not draw a tree to prove you have one. |
| DT-11 | Version type (`rough / mix / master`) can be **auto-detected from the audio** | It looks measurable — a master is louder, more limited, and lower in crest factor than a rough. This is real DSP and it would remove a field | ❌ **REJECTED from audio — ✅ CONFIRMED from the filename.** Loudness is not a discriminator: a loud rough with a limiter on the 2-bus is routine (every producer does it), and a quiet, dynamic master is what half of modern mastering is for. So audio-analysis typing would be wrong often. The damage is not the wrongness but the *shape* of it: the type is **pre-filled**, and people accept pre-filled defaults, so a confident wrong guess propagates into `07.04.02`'s canonical nomination — where "someone nominates a rough as the canonical master" is already a known edge case that the type is supposed to warn about. A wrong type makes the warning fire on the honest case and stay silent on the dangerous one. But the producer **already encodes the type, reliably, in the filename** — `Ghost Town MASTER 2.wav`, `GT_rough_v3.wav`, `stems/` — because they have to find these files themselves. That is free, high-signal intent, already being expressed. Resolution: infer type from the label as a **suggestion the producer can reject**, never from audio, and never applied silently. **The general principle: read the intent the user already expresses; do not compute a worse version of it from physics.** |

| D-01 | Versions are **immutable and append-only** — no edit, no delete, not even by the owner | Sub-domain D-02. A mutable history is not a record. No owner exception: the owner is precisely the party a contributor most needs the record against. |
| D-02 | **No branching, no merging** — parallel versions are siblings, resolved by choosing | Sub-domain D-03 and parent rejected-pair R-01. The Git analogy is seductive and wrong. |

| D-08 | **Ingest order is the record's order.** Claimed authored-at is displayed, flagged when impossible, and never reorders anything | Per DT-05 — a log that resequences on a claimed field is not append-only, and this one is evidence. |
