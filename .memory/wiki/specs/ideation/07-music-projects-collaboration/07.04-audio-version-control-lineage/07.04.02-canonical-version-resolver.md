# Feature: Canonical Version Resolver

> **Number**: 07.04.02
> **Parent**: [Audio Version Control & Lineage](./07.04-audio-version-control-lineage-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The single authoritative answer to "which one is the real one?" — held **per slot**, not as one "latest" flag.
The canonical rough is v3, the canonical mix is v7, the canonical master is v9; most versions are canonical for
nothing. This is the smallest feature in the sub-domain and arguably the highest-value one in the entire domain:
`Mix_FINAL_v3_ACTUAL.wav` is not a naming problem, it is a **missing pointer** over a set of files that already
exists. Everyone has the files. Nobody has the answer.

Two things make it more than a convenience. First, the **act of nomination is a provenance fact** — it is the
recorded moment a named human said "this is the record", captured at source, which is exactly the thesis
(`meta/problem-statement.md`, D-18). Second, the resolver is the only surface that knows which versions matter,
which makes it the input to questions far beyond its own panel — what to retain, what to credit, what to ship.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Decides** which version fills each slot. This is the one place in the sub-domain where the artist outranks the producer, and correctly so — "is this the mix?" is an artistic judgement, not a technical one. As song owner: reserves slots (D-10), reverts proxy nominations, clears a slot back to unset. As a non-owner contributor: sees the panel, sees which slots are filled, cannot nominate into a reserved slot. Sees at a glance which version everyone should be listening to. |
| Producer | Full | Nominates (the highest-volume nominator — they are the one who just bounced it) and is **bound** by the result: the handoff package (`07.08.01` D-03) builds from the canonical, not from whatever is newest in the folder. Where the owner has reserved a slot and is unavailable, may **proxy-nominate** — the pointer moves, visibly attributed and visibly weaker, revertible in one tap (D-10). Cannot reserve slots; cannot revert an owner's nomination. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. A studio operator knows a session is booked in their room (domain 16); which mix the client blessed is the client's confidential business — the same boundary `07.03.01` draws for the roster. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The canonical master eventually becomes the released recording via domain 12 — the Fan meets the *output*, never this pointer, and never the fact that the record was v6 for three weeks before it was v9. |

> **The multi-hyphenate collapse (DT-09)**: on the median indie record, the Musician and the Producer above are
> **one human**. The Musician/Producer split in this table is a *lens on two judgements* — "is this good?" and
> "did I bounce this?" — not a two-person workflow. Where one identity holds both, nomination is a single act
> with no second confirmation and no notification (there is no one to notify). Any design that makes the solo
> artist perform a handoff to themselves has misread `meta/personas.md`'s central structural fact.

## Behavior

The resolver holds a set of named pointers into the timeline (`07.04.01`), one per **slot**. Nominating a version
writes a pointer. Pointers are mutable — that is the difference between the resolver and the timeline, and the
reason they are separate features. The *log* of pointer movements is not (D-07).

### What a slot is — and the vocabulary the resolver does not own

A slot is the triple `(stage, variant, format)`:

| Dimension | Members | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| `stage` | `rough`, `mix`, `master` — exactly 3, fixed | **This feature.** The only vocabulary the resolver owns. |
| `variant` | `primary` (default) plus whatever the song declared in the Alternate Version Matrix — `instrumental`, `clean`, `radio edit`, `TV mix`, `a cappella`, `extended` | `07.07.02` D-02 — fixed vocabulary with a defined extension path |
| `format` | `primary` (default) plus whatever formats the song declared | `07.07.04`; immersive/Atmos slots come from `07.07.05` on the same rule (`07.07` CX-04 — Atmos is not a variant) |

**Slots are derived, never authored here** (D-08). A song that declared nothing has exactly **3** slots: canonical
rough, canonical mix, canonical master. A song declaring an instrumental and a clean version, with both a
streaming and a vinyl master, has **10**: `rough/primary`; `mix × {primary, instrumental, clean}`;
`master × {primary, instrumental, clean} × {streaming, vinyl}`. Variants create `mix` and `master` slots only —
per `07.07.02`'s happy path, variants are bounced at mix-down and mastered in the same pass; nobody needs a
canonical rough instrumental.

This is what answers the vocabulary question without the resolver ever holding a list that can drift out of sync
with `07.07.02`'s. When the matrix grows a row, slots appear. The resolver is a consumer of that decision, not a
second place to make it.

### The three properties that do the work

**"Latest" is deliberately not offered.** Auto-canonical-on-upload would be a one-line feature that recreates the
entire problem: the newest bounce is very often an experiment, an alternate, or a mistake. The pointer moves only
when a human says so. **The friction is the feature** (DT-02).

**Nomination is loud, and loudness scales with the stage.** When the canonical mix changes, the roster is told.
Today this information travels by WhatsApp — "use the new one" — and gets lost, and someone masters the wrong mix.
But a uniform alarm on every slot is how the highest-value notification in the product gets muted (DT-12), so
severity is a property of the slot: `rough` is quiet, `mix` is normal, `master` is loud, and a movement that puts
the pointer into disagreement with an existing commitment (a release, an approval, a delivered ISRC) is the
loudest event this feature produces.

**Resolution happens once, at the act, and pins there.** This is the corrected form of the pin/resolve rule —
see DT-04, which rejects the "transient vs committed" axis the earlier pass used. Live resolution exists in
exactly one place: a surface literally asking *"what is canonical right now?"*. Everything that leaves the
resolver pins at the moment of the act:

| Act with a consequence | What pins | Ratified in |
|---|---|---|
| Sending a share link | the version it plays | `07.05.02` D-02 |
| Approving | the version approved | `07.05.04` D-01 |
| Building a handoff package | the whole contents, resolved at build start | `07.08.01` D-03 |
| Adding a song to a release | the selected master and variant | `07.01.02` D-01, D-03 |
| Registering an ISRC / delivering to a DSP | the recording identity | domain 12; see DT-06 |

### Nomination rights

Both Full roles may nominate. The owner may **reserve** a slot — reserved slots are theirs alone, and reservation
is **opt-in with nothing reserved by default**, because a default reservation blocks the common case and the
domain's D-04 is that the platform never blocks upstream. Where a slot is reserved and the owner is unavailable,
the Producer may proxy-nominate: the pointer moves (the work does not stop), the entry is marked `proxy`, the
owner is notified at `master`-tier loudness, and one tap reverts it. This is the same shape as `07.05.04` D-05 —
proxy acts are real, permitted, visibly attributed, and visibly weaker than the thing they stand in for.

### Coherence — the failure nobody designs for

Each pointer can be individually valid while the set is nonsense. The canonical master was derived from v7; the
canonical mix has since moved to v8. The master is a fine master. The mix is a fine mix. **The master is not a
master *of* the mix**, and the record about to ship is not the record that was approved. Nothing in a per-slot
model detects this, because nothing in a per-slot model ever compares two slots.

The resolver checks it (DT-05): the canonical master's lineage either reaches the canonical mix or it does not.
It is a graph reachability question over metadata that `07.04.01` already holds — no audio, no cost. It **warns
and never blocks**, because divergence is sometimes deliberate (`07.08.01` D-04 — integrity is the domain's only
justified hard stop).

### Refusing to guess is not blocking

With no canonical set, the resolver returns no answer — guessing is the failure mode this feature exists to
eliminate (D-06). But it does not block: it **asks**, inline, wherever the question was posed. A handoff builder
that needs "the canonical mix" and has none presents the picker in place. Choosing there pins the package (per
`07.08.01` D-03) and offers "also make this the canonical mix" — **checked by default when the slot is empty**,
because the decision has just been made in substance, and **unchecked when a canonical already exists**, because
deliberately deviating from the canonical for one recipient is not a re-nomination.

### What the resolver publishes outward

- **Retention signal** (D-13): most versions are canonical for nothing. That set is precisely the tiering
  candidate list `07.04.01` DT-03 needs, and a canonical version's bytes must never be tiered to unavailability.
  The resolver publishes the signal; the policy is not its call (`07.04.01` Q-01).
- **Credit evidence** (D-14): "v7 was nominated canonical mix by Jo on 3 March; v7 was bounced by Sam" is a fact
  worth far more than "Sam bounced 40 versions". The resolver exposes the fact and **never computes a credit** —
  domain D-05 and `07.02.02` D-01 (attribution is evidence, not arithmetic).

### Happy Path

> **Step 1's arrival path is post-gate — not v1 (D-17).** No non-web client on the producer's machine is
> authorised (`07.09` D-05), so there is no watched folder and no bridge ingest for the whole v1 window. The v1
> shape of step 1 is: the producer **uploads the bounce manually**, it lands on the timeline with lineage
> inferred or asked for (`07.04.01` D-19), the roster is notified, and **the canonical still does not move**.
> **Steps 2–8 are unchanged and run in v1** — this feature's substance is what happens *after* a version
> exists, and nothing in nomination, logging, notification, coherence or pinning depends on how it arrived.

1. Producer bounces v7 into the watched folder (`07.09.01`); it lands on the timeline (`07.04.01`); the roster is notified a new mix exists; **the canonical does not move**
2. Musician A/Bs v6 against v7 (`07.04.06`, loudness-matched per its D-01) and decides v7 is the one
3. Musician opens the resolver panel — 3 slots for this song, `mix` currently holding v6
4. Musician nominates v7 into the `mix` slot; the panel notes, to them alone, that they have played it (D-11)
5. Pointer moves; an append-only log entry records `mix: v6 → v7, by Jo, 3 March 14:22` (D-07)
6. Roster is notified at `mix` tier: "the mix is now v7"
7. Coherence check runs: the `master` slot is empty, so nothing to disagree with — no warning
8. Producer builds the mastering handoff (`07.08.01`); it resolves `mix → v7` at build start and **pins** it. The mastering engineer cannot master the wrong mix by accident, and the package remains an exact record of what was sent even after the pointer moves again

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Canonical nominated, then that version fails an integrity check (`07.04.05`) | **Alarm, not silent fallback.** A canonical that has gone bad is a five-alarm event; silently reverting to v6 would hide it. Pointer does not move — only a human moves it. | "The canonical master failed its integrity check" — prominent, to the owner and the producer, naming the specific fault (`07.04.05` D-04) |
| Two people nominate different versions into the same slot concurrently | Last write wins with a loud, attributed notice. **Neither nomination is erased** — both are in the log (D-07), which is what makes this recoverable rather than merely announced. | "Alex made v8 canonical 30 seconds after you made v7" — with a one-tap revert to v7 |
| No canonical nominated; a downstream consumer asks for "the mix" | Refuses to guess (D-06), **asks rather than blocks** (domain D-04). Picker rendered in place. | "No canonical mix has been chosen" + inline picker + "also make this the canonical mix" pre-checked |
| Canonical master re-nominated after a release was assembled | Release does **not** move (pin, `07.01.02` D-03); the disagreement is surfaced at the loudest tier | "This release ships v7. The canonical master is now v9. Update?" |
| **Canonical master does not descend from the canonical mix** (coherence break, DT-05) | Detected by lineage reachability over metadata. **Warns, never blocks** — divergence is sometimes deliberate. | "The canonical master was mastered from v7. The canonical mix is now v8." + both lineages shown |
| **Canonical master re-nominated after its ISRC was registered / the recording was delivered to a DSP** | The ISRC pinned at the act of registration (DT-06) and does not follow. The slot moves; the recording in the world does not. Highest-severity notice in the feature. | "v7 is registered as ISRC XX-XXX-YY-NNNNN and is live on 4 stores. Making v9 canonical does not change what is released." |
| **Canonical mix moves while a handoff package is building** | Package pins what it resolved at build start (`07.08.01` D-03). **Never auto-rebuild** — a silent re-resolve is the disease. Drift surfaced on completion. | "The canonical mix changed to v8 while this package was building. It contains v7. Rebuild?" |
| **Nominator is also the author of the version** (solo artist; multi-hyphenate, DT-09) | One act. No second confirmation, no self-handoff. Notification suppressed where the roster is 1. Log records author and nominator as one identity **without comment** — the platform does not judge (domain D-05). | Nominate button on the version itself; no ceremony |
| **Nominator has never played the version they are blessing** | Shown as a fact **to the nominator only**, at the moment of nomination. Never gates (that would be judging craft, domain D-05). Never broadcast (that would be judging the person, `07.05.02` D-05). | "You haven't played v9." — inline, dismissible, not a barrier |
| **Slot's target is in the sensitive class and viewer lacks vault access** (`07.03.03`) | **Occupancy is visible; identity is not.** "A canonical stem set exists" is a readiness fact `07.08.03` needs and is not sensitive; *which* version it is, and playback, follow the vault. | "Canonical stem set: set" — no version, no player, no error framing |
| **Owner reserved the `master` slot and is unavailable at the deadline** | Producer proxy-nominates. Pointer **moves** — the work does not stop (domain D-04). Marked `proxy`, attributed, revertible. | Producer: "Jo reserved this slot. Nominate as proxy?" · Owner: "Sam made v9 the canonical master as proxy while you were away. Keep / Revert" |
| Proxy nomination reverted by the owner | Both the proxy nomination and the revert are log entries. The proxy's choice is not erased — it is a record of what a professional thought at the time. | Timeline of the slot shows both, attributed |
| **Clearing a canonical back to unset** (D-12) | Allowed. The alternative is forcing someone to nominate a version they know is wrong in order to express "none of these" — a lie the system extracted. Logged; notified at the slot's tier. Pinned consumers unaffected (they pinned). | "The canonical master has been cleared. 2 pinned artifacts still reference v9." |
| **A variant is declared in `07.07.02` after canonicals were already set** | New empty slots appear. Existing slots untouched. No migration, no prompt storm. | Panel grows two rows: "Canonical mix (instrumental) — not set" |
| **A variant is removed from the matrix while its slot holds a canonical** | Slot is **archived, not deleted**; its log survives. Same principle as `07.04.04` D-05 (stale stems marked, never deleted) — the record of what was once the canonical instrumental is still true. | Slot moves to an "archived" section with its history intact |
| **Producer nominates their own mix as canonical master right before delivery, without the artist** (anti-persona: "pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention") | **Not preventable, and the product must not claim otherwise** (domain D-06). The defence is entirely detection: `master`-tier notification, immutable attributed log, one-tap revert, and — if the artist cares — slot reservation. | Owner gets the loudest notification the feature sends, with revert |
| **Canonical rough moves 5 times in one working day** (notification fatigue, DT-12) | Severity is per-stage, so this is quiet by construction and does not train the roster to mute the channel that matters. | Rough movements appear in the activity feed, not as an alert |
| **Nomination targets a version the nominator cannot access** (`07.03.03`) | Not offered in the picker. If attempted from a stale surface, refused **with the access reason**, never a generic error. | "You don't have access to the stem set for v9" — not "Nomination failed" |
| **Two slots point at the same version** (v9 is both canonical mix and canonical master) | Legitimate. A mixed-and-mastered in-the-box bounce is one file that is honestly both. No warning — warning here would flag the normal case for a large population of the core persona. | Two badges on v9 |
| **Nominating a version belonging to a different song** | Refused. Slots are per-song, as the roster is per-song (`07.03.01` D-01). | "v4 belongs to another song" |
| **Network failure mid-nomination** | The pointer moved or it did not — never a partial. Retry is **idempotent**: re-nominating the same version into the same slot is a no-op, not a second log entry. | Last confirmed state + retry; no duplicate log rows on retry |
| **Song ownership transfers** (an artist signs to a label; the owner becomes an entity, domain 01) | Reservations attach to the **owner role**, not the person, so they survive the transfer. The transfer itself is a log entry. Whether a label entity can hold the owner role at all is an open question (Q-02). | "Ownership transferred. 1 reserved slot now held by [label]." |
| Version canonical for a slot is superseded by a newer version | Nothing automatic. The newer version is just a version until someone blesses it (D-02). | New version listed; canonical badge unmoved; "3 newer versions" on the slot |
| Someone nominates a `rough`-typed version into the `master` slot | Allowed with a confirm — type is advisory (`07.04.01` D-04 treats the type as a property of the version, not a gate), and unusual things happen in real records. | "v3 is typed as a rough. Make it the canonical master anyway?" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Panel opened | Slot-row skeletons — one per derived slot, so the count is known before the pointers are |
| Unset | A slot has no canonical | "No canonical mix yet" — an honest empty state, never a guess (D-06) |
| Partial | Some slots filled, others empty | The normal mid-project state: `rough` set, `mix` set, `master` empty. Rendered as progress, **not as a deficiency** — a song without a master is not failing, it is unfinished |
| Set | Slot holds a canonical | Badge on the version; panel shows every slot at a glance |
| Stale | Newer versions exist beyond the canonical | Canonical badge plus "3 newer versions" — informational, never automatic |
| Incoherent | Canonical master's lineage does not reach the canonical mix (DT-05) | Advisory on the panel naming both versions and both lineages; never blocks |
| Diverged | A pinned commitment (release, approval, ISRC) disagrees with the current pointer | Named, per-artifact: "This release ships v7 · canonical is v9 · Update?" — the pin is never moved for you |
| Proxy-held | Reserved slot filled by a proxy nomination (D-10) | Pointer live and usable, badged `proxy` with the nominator named, one-tap revert for the owner |
| Conflicted | Concurrent nomination into one slot | Last write shown with an attributed notice and a revert; the losing nomination visible in the slot's log |
| Compromised | Canonical failed integrity (`07.04.05`) | Alarm state; blocked from handoff (parent CX-04, `07.08.01` D-04 — the domain's only justified hard stop) |
| Restricted | Viewer lacks vault access to the slot's target (`07.03.03`) | Occupancy only: "set" — no version identity, no player, no error framing |
| Archived | Slot's variant was removed from the matrix (`07.07.02`) | Collapsed archived section; history preserved |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The canonical pointer must be **per purpose**, not a single "latest" or "final" flag | A song simultaneously has a canonical rough (for the label), a canonical mix (for the mastering engineer), and a canonical master (for the release) — and they are three different files, live at the same time, needed by three different people. A single flag forces the exact hack it is meant to prevent: users encode purpose in the *filename*, and `FINAL_v3_ACTUAL` is reborn inside the platform. | ✅ CONFIRMED — per-purpose pointers are the whole design. The failure this feature addresses is not "too many files", it is "no answer to which file". |
| DT-02 | Canonical should update automatically on upload, since the newest bounce is usually the best one | The obvious convenience feature, and it is one line of code | ❌ **REJECTED — and this rejection is the feature.** The newest bounce is very often an experiment, an alternate for comparison, or a mistake. Auto-canonical means the mastering engineer picks up the producer's 3am "what if the drums were louder" test. The pointer must move only by a deliberate human act, and the *friction is the point*: an act with consequences should require an act. This is precisely the discipline the industry lacks and why the disease persists. |
| DT-03 | Because the canonical resolves for everyone, downstream consumers should always resolve live rather than pin | Consistency instinct — one answer, everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — for **releases**, live resolution is catastrophic. A release that silently changes its master because someone re-nominated is the `FINAL_v3` disease with commercial consequences. The rejection stands, but the *replacement rule* this pass proposed ("transient resolves live, committed pins") was itself wrong — see DT-04. |
| DT-04 | The pin/resolve split is **transient vs committed**: review links, handoff drafts and "what should I be listening to" resolve live; releases, approvals and delivered packages pin | DT-03's replacement rule, written in the breadth pass | ❌ **REJECTED — the axis is wrong, and the sibling features had already found it.** `07.05.02` D-02 pins share links *citing this file's D-03*; `07.05.04` D-01 pins approvals; `07.08.01` D-03 resolves-then-pins packages. Three siblings independently landed on "pin", including the one this rule explicitly classified as transient. They are right. A review link sent to an A&R is not transient the moment it is sent — it has been *acted on*: the A&R listens on Monday and comments "the vocal's too loud"; the pointer moves to v8 on Tuesday which fixed exactly that; the comment now anchors to nothing, and `07.05.01`'s version-anchored review is destroyed. **The correct axis is time, not durability: resolution happens once, at the moment of an act with a consequence, and pins there.** Live resolution survives in exactly one place — a surface literally asking "what is canonical *right now*", which is the panel itself. Anything that leaves the resolver has been acted on and pins. |
| DT-05 | Per-slot pointers are independently sufficient — if each pointer is valid, the set is valid | Implicit in DT-01's per-purpose model, and never questioned by it | ❌ **REJECTED — this is the failure mode nobody designs for.** Pointers can each be valid while the set is nonsense: the canonical master was mastered from v7, the canonical mix has since moved to v8, and now **the master is not a master *of* the mix**. Every pointer passes its own check. The record about to ship is not the record that was approved, and no per-slot model can see it because no per-slot model ever compares two slots. A mastering engineer would catch this in conversation; a platform that replaces the conversation must catch it in software. It is *cheaply* detectable — the canonical master's lineage (`07.04.01`) either reaches the canonical mix or it does not; metadata reachability, no audio, no cost. It **warns and never blocks**: divergence is sometimes deliberate, and `07.08.01` D-04 reserves the domain's only hard stop for integrity. **Canonicality is a claim about a record, not a set of independent facts, and the claim can be internally incoherent.** |
| DT-06 | A canonical pointer moving is always a reversible, internal act | Assumed by "pointers are mutable — that is the whole point" | ❌ **REJECTED at the domain-12 boundary, and this is the sharpest edge in the feature.** Once a canonical master is registered with an ISRC and delivered to DSPs, a fact has escaped into the world that this platform cannot retract. The ISRC pins at the act of registration, like every other act (DT-04) — but unlike a release membership or an approval, **the pin is enforced by an external system that has never heard of us**. Re-nominating the canonical master afterwards is legal, sometimes correct (you fixed a fault and will deliver an update), and *always* means the platform's answer to "which one is the real one" now disagrees with the world's. That disagreement must be stated in exactly those terms — "v7 is live on 4 stores; making v9 canonical does not change what is released" — never implied by a moved badge. This is `07.07.02` Q-03's ISRC question arriving here as a behaviour, not a schema: the moment a slot is registered externally, it stops being purely ours. |
| DT-07 | The pointer is mutable, so its history is disposable | Implied by the timeline/resolver split — the timeline is the immutable half, so the resolver is the mutable half | ❌ **REJECTED — the split is about the *pointer*, not about the *record of moving it*, and conflating them throws away the feature's best asset.** "What was the canonical master when we delivered on 3 March?" is a question a dispute (domain 24) will ask, and a mutable pointer with no history cannot answer it — which would make the resolver the one hole in a sub-domain whose entire value rests on the record surviving (sub-domain D-02). Worse, it discards a *pure provenance fact*: the nomination event is the recorded moment a named human said "this is the record", captured at source with a timestamp and an author. That is the thesis (D-18) in miniature, and it is nearly free — the log is metadata, not audio, so none of `07.04.01` DT-03's storage tension applies. **The pointer is mutable; the log of its movements is append-only and immutable.** This also upgrades the concurrent-nomination case from "announced" to "recoverable": the losing nomination is not erased, it is in the log. |
| DT-08 | The resolver should own its own purpose vocabulary — `rough / mix / master` and whatever else it needs | The breadth pass's open question Q-02 assumed the vocabulary was this feature's to define and grow | ❌ **REJECTED — it should own exactly one dimension and derive the rest.** `07.07.02` D-02 already owns the variant vocabulary and its extension path; `07.07.04` owns formats; `07.07.05` owns immersive. If the resolver held a second list of the same things, the two would drift, and a variant declared in the matrix but absent from the resolver's list is a canonical slot that cannot exist for a deliverable the song has promised. So: **the resolver owns `stage` (3 members, fixed) and derives slots as `stage × declared variants × declared formats`.** Slots come into existence when the matrix declares them and archive when it retracts them. This resolves the vocabulary question without the resolver ever holding a list that can go stale — and it means the answer to "how many canonicals does this song have?" is a property of the song's declared ambitions, which is exactly right: a song with no variants has 3 slots, and a song promising instrumentals and a vinyl cut has 10. |
| DT-09 | The Musician/Producer split on this feature describes two people | The Role Lens table's shape, and the sub-domain's note that "the artist outranks the producer here" | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — it describes two *judgements*, which are very often held by one human, and that human is the core persona.** `meta/personas.md` opens on this: "one human routinely occupies three of them in one day"; a UX forcing "artist OR producer" "fails them immediately". On the median indie record the person who bounced v7 is the person who decides v7 is the one. If nomination is designed as a handoff — producer proposes, artist blesses — the solo artist performs a ceremony with themselves, and the feature reads as bureaucracy invented by people who have never made a record alone. **So the split is a lens, not a workflow**: where one identity holds both, nomination is a single act on the version itself, no confirmation, no notification (the roster is 1). Where two humans hold them, the same single act is simply performed by the one with the right, and the *log* — which records author and nominator separately (DT-07) — makes the distinction visible without the workflow enforcing it. A self-nomination is genuinely weaker evidence than an artist accepting a producer's mix (one-party vs two-party fact), but per domain D-05 the platform **shows** that and never **scores** it. |
| DT-10 | Nomination rights are a single question — either the owner decides or anyone with access does | The breadth pass's Q-01, which posed it as a binary and escalated it to the user | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — posed as a binary it has no good answer, because both answers are right at different moments.** "The artist decides" is correct artistically and fails at 2am when the delivery is at 9. "Any producer decides" is operationally sane and hands the anti-persona ("assigns themselves a larger split... while contributors aren't paying attention") the one act that decides what ships. **`07.05.04` D-05 already solved this exact shape for approvals**: proxy acts are permitted, visibly attributed, visibly weaker. Applied here: nomination is open to both Full roles by default; the owner may **reserve** a slot; a reserved slot under an absent owner accepts a **proxy nomination** that moves the pointer (domain D-04 — never block), badges itself, notifies loudly, and reverts in one tap. Nothing is reserved by default, because a default reservation blocks the common case to defend against the rare one. This is derivation from a ratified sibling decision, not a new product call — what remains genuinely open is only *who the owner is* when a label holds the song (Q-02). |
| DT-11 | Requiring the nominator to have actually listened to the version would prevent a real failure | The breadth pass's Q-03 — blessing a mix you never played is a genuine and embarrassing failure, and playback is detectable | ❌ **REJECTED as a gate; ✅ CONFIRMED as a fact — and domain D-05 makes the call, not taste.** "Measure and show; never judge" is ratified five times over (`07.02.02` D-01, `07.05.03` D-01, `07.07.03` D-01, `07.08.02` D-01, `07.01.03` D-04). Gating nomination on listening is the platform adjudicating whether a human is entitled to their own artistic judgement — and it is trivially defeated by pressing play and walking away, so it buys nothing but insult. But *showing* the nominator "you haven't played v9" is measuring and showing, costs nothing, and catches the real error (blessing the wrong row in a list). Two constraints on it: shown **only to the nominator** — broadcasting "Alex blessed a mix he never played" is judging the person, which `07.05.02` D-05 forbids in almost these words — and shown as a **fact, not a verdict**, dismissible, never a barrier. Telling someone about their own behaviour is not surveillance; telling the roster about it is. |
| DT-12 | "Loud" is a property of the feature — canonical changes are important, so they all alert | D-04 as originally written, which specified loudness once for the whole feature | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — uniform loudness is how the highest-value notification in the product gets muted.** The claim that this notification matters is true and is the reason the feature exists (it currently travels by WhatsApp and gets lost, and someone masters the wrong mix). But on a heavily-worked record the canonical rough moves several times a day, and a channel that alarms on a rough is a channel the roster mutes within a week — at which point the canonical *master* alarm, the one that actually prevents the disaster, is muted too. **Severity must scale with consequence**: `rough` quiet, `mix` normal, `master` loud, and loudest of all a movement that puts the pointer into disagreement with an existing commitment (release, approval, delivered ISRC), because that is the only case where the platform knows something has already been acted on. Rarity is what preserves the alarm's meaning, and rarity is achievable here because master nominations *are* rare — but only if the feature does not spend its credibility on roughs. |
| DT-13 | Canonicality is only an answer to "which one is real" | The feature's framing, and the scope of its own panel | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — it is also the only place in the domain that knows which versions *matter*, and two starving neighbours need exactly that.** (a) **Retention**: `07.04.01` DT-03 names the sub-domain's most serious unresolved tension — every version of every stem pack forever is an unaffordable bill for a solo-built platform — and proposes tiering, but has no signal for *what* to tier. The resolver has it, precisely and for free: **most versions are canonical for nothing**, and that set is the tiering candidate list, while a canonical version's bytes must never be tiered to unavailability. The feature that answers "which one is real" answers "which one must stay hot" as the same sentence. (b) **Credit**: bouncing the mix that shipped is a materially different fact from bouncing 40 versions nobody used, and domain 02 currently cannot tell them apart. **Hard boundary on both**: the resolver *publishes signals*, it does not *set policy* — retention economics are `07.04.01` Q-01 and a pricing decision (Q-03 below); credit derivation is domain 02's, and `07.02.02` D-01 ("attribution is evidence, not arithmetic") plus domain D-05 forbid this feature computing anything. Publish the fact; never do the arithmetic. |
| DT-14 | The resolver should hold per-recipient canonicals — the mastering engineer's canonical mix, the label's canonical mix | Symmetry with the per-purpose insight (DT-01): if "latest" is the wrong question because purposes differ, perhaps recipients differ too | ❌ **REJECTED — that is a package, and it already exists.** A recipient-specific answer to "which files" is exactly `07.08.01`'s handoff package, which resolves from canonicals and pins (its D-03). Growing per-recipient pointers here would rebuild a delivery system inside a pointer, duplicate `07.08`, and break DT-01's own logic: a *purpose* is a property of the song (a mix exists whether or not anyone asked for one), a *recipient* is a property of a transaction. The one honest cousin of this idea is already handled — deviating from the canonical for a single package is supported at the picker, is not a re-nomination, and pins to that package alone. |
