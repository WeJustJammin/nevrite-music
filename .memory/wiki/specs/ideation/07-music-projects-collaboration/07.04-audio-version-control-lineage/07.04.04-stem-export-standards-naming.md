# Feature: Stem Export Standards & Naming Enforcement

> **Number**: 07.04.04
> **Parent**: [Audio Version Control & Lineage](./07.04-audio-version-control-lineage-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Stem packs that are consistent, correctly named, correctly aligned, and permanently linked to the mix they came
from. Stems are the most-passed-around asset in professional music — to the mixer, the mastering engineer, the
remixer, the sync agent, the live playback rig — and they are passed around badly: inconsistent naming, silent
offsets, missing parts, and no way to know whether they still match the mix. Every one of those failures is
discovered downstream by someone else, at cost.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Deliberately absent. Stems are the sensitive class (`07.03.03` DT-01), and the *export standards* are engineering configuration an artist has no reason to touch. An artist who needs stems (for a live rig, say) receives a **package** (`07.08.01`), not access to this. |
| Producer | Full | Defines the project's stem convention; exports; sees validation results. The only role that operates this feature. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A fan encounters stems only via a published Remix Program (`07.08.05`) — a deliberately-released set, categorically different from these working stems. |

## Behavior

A stem export is a **derived version** — a child of the mix it came from (parent CX-03), never an independent
upload. That lineage is what lets the platform detect the single most expensive silent failure in delivery:
stems that have drifted from their mix because the mix moved on, and nobody noticed until the mastering engineer
called.

The project declares a stem convention (which stems, what naming pattern, what format). Exports are validated
against it. Two properties matter more than the naming, though naming is what the candidate list noticed:

**Alignment is the failure that actually costs money.** A stem pack where one file starts 40 ms late is
catastrophic and *invisible* until someone imports all of them and hears phasing. Naming errors are annoying;
alignment errors destroy the work. All stems must be full-length from an identical start point — the industry
convention exists precisely because the alternative is unusable.

**Validation warns; it does not block.** Consistent with the domain-wide rule (`07.01.03` D-02) — a producer at
2am exporting stems for a deadline will not be stopped by a naming lint, they will bypass the platform and
email a zip. Then the platform is out of the room and every downstream capture is lost.

### Happy Path

> **Steps 2–3 are post-gate — not v1 (D-08).** No non-web client on the producer's machine is authorised
> (`07.09` D-05), so there is no watched folder and no bridge ingest for the whole v1 window: the "export with
> no bridge" edge case below is the v1 path, and stem-set lineage is flagged uncertain by default. **Steps 4–5
> are unchanged and run in v1** — the offset, null-sum, naming and sample-rate checks all operate on an
> uploaded zip, and they are this feature's actual value.

1. Producer sets the project's stem convention (or accepts the default recipient spec — mastering, sync, live)
2. Exports stems from the DAW into the watched folder (`07.09.01`)
3. Bridge ingests them as a stem set, child of the canonical mix v7
4. System validates: 11 stems present, naming matches, all full-length from bar 1, sample rate consistent
5. Stem set enters the timeline as a derived version, available to the mix engineer per their vault grant

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One stem is 40 ms offset from the others | **Detected and surfaced prominently** — this is the expensive one | "Stem 'gtr_2' starts 40 ms after the others" |
| Stems do not sum to the mix they came from | Detected by null-test against the parent mix. **The single best check in this feature** — it catches a muted stem, a missing part, a bus that was not printed, an effect that only lives on the master bus | "These stems don't sum to v7 — something is missing or the master bus has processing on it" |
| Naming does not match the convention | Warned, never blocked. Offers a rename. | "3 stems don't match the naming convention" + fix affordance |
| Parent mix supersedes; stems go stale | Marked **stale**, not deleted (parent CX-03) — the stems remain a true record of the mix they came from | "These stems are from v6. The canonical mix is v7." |
| Stem count differs between exports of the same song | Not an error — arrangements change. Diffed against the previous set. | "This set has 11 stems; the last had 12 (no 'perc_2')" |
| Export with no bridge (manual zip upload) | Accepted; lineage guessed or asked for; validation still runs. **This is the v1 path for every export** (D-08), not a fallback | Version created with lineage flagged uncertain |
| Multi-GB stem pack over a studio's poor upstream | Resumable chunked upload required — a failed 4 GB upload at 90% is a product-abandoning experience | Progress with resume; routed to `/create-prd-architecture` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **null test** (do the stems sum back to the mix?) is the highest-value check here, and nobody in the sweep proposed it | Reasoning from the actual failure modes rather than from the candidate's title. The candidate says "naming enforcement" — but a perfectly-named stem pack that is missing the parallel-compression bus is *worse* than a badly-named complete one, because the naming looks right and the error is silent until a remixer wonders why it sounds thin. Summing the stems and null-testing against the parent mix catches: missing parts, unprinted busses, master-bus processing that will not survive the handoff, and phase errors. It is the one check that verifies the pack is *complete and truthful* rather than merely tidy. | ✅ CONFIRMED — the defining check of this feature. It is only possible because of the lineage in parent CX-03: without knowing which mix the stems came from, there is nothing to null against. This is a concrete payoff of treating stems as derived versions rather than uploads. |
| DT-02 | Naming enforcement should **block** non-compliant exports, since that is what "enforcement" means | The candidate's own title ("Naming Enforcement") | ❌ **REJECTED** — a producer at 2am exporting stems for a deadline will not be stopped by a naming lint; they will email a zip and the platform loses the session. The candidate's name is wrong about the mechanism, though right about the need. The correct model is **validate, warn, and offer to fix** — and rename on the platform's side rather than demanding a re-export, since the platform holds the files and can rename them itself. Consistent with `07.01.03` D-02: the lazy path must be the correct path. |
| DT-03 | A universal stem naming convention can be defined and applied across all projects | Implied by "Standards" in the candidate name | ❌ REJECTED — there is no single convention. Requirements differ irreconcilably by *recipient*: a mastering engineer wants the mix; a sync agent wants a specific instrumental/vocal/FX split; a live playback rig wants click and cues; a remixer wants everything separated. So the convention is not a project setting at all — it is a **recipient spec** (`07.08.01`), and this feature validates against whichever spec applies. This reframes the feature from "our standard" to "does this pack satisfy the spec of whoever is receiving it", which is the question that actually has consequences. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Stems are **derived versions**, children of their parent mix — never independent uploads | Parent CX-03. This lineage is what makes the null test (DT-01) possible at all. |
| D-02 | The **null test against the parent mix** is the defining validation | Per DT-01 — it verifies completeness and truthfulness, not tidiness. |
| D-03 | Validation **warns and offers to fix**; it never blocks the export | Per DT-02 — a blocked producer emails a zip and the platform leaves the room. |
| D-04 | Naming/format conventions are **recipient specs**, not project settings | Per DT-03 — there is no universal convention; the question is always "does this satisfy the receiver". |
| D-05 | Stale stems are **marked, never deleted** | They remain a true record of the mix they came from. |
| D-06 | Alignment errors outrank naming errors in every surface | Naming is annoying; alignment destroys the work. Severity ordering must reflect that. |
| D-07 | A **stale** stem set **warns loudly but never blocks** its use in a handoff package (`07.08.01`) | Resolves Q-02 at Step 5. Blocking contradicts D-03 (the platform never stands between a producer and delivery). But shipping stems that no longer sum to the master is the exact expensive silent failure this feature exists to catch — so the warning at pack-build time is prominent and names the version drift ("these stems are from v6; canonical mix is v7"), matching the D-06 severity ordering. Warn, don't block. |
| D-08 | **The bridge ingest path is post-gate; the manual zip is the v1 path.** No non-web client is authorised (`07.09` D-05), so stem sets arrive as uploads with lineage flagged uncertain for the whole v1 window | Owner ratification DQ-08.2 / DQ-08.4, 2026-07-22. Nothing else in this feature changes: D-02's null test, the alignment check, naming validation and D-03's warn-never-block all run on an uploaded zip, which is why this feature loses least of the seven bridge-dependent ones. What must be stated rather than implied (domain D-06) is that "child of the canonical mix v7" is **asked or inferred** in v1, never read from a session. Not re-scoped; `07.09` keeps `Should`, unphased (domain D-10). |
