# Feature: Take & Comp Management

> **Number**: 07.04.03
> **Parent**: [Audio Version Control & Lineage](./07.04-audio-version-control-lineage-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Individual performances (takes) and the composite performance assembled from them (the comp). This is where the
performance that actually ships gets decided, and today that decision is invisible the moment the session ends —
it lives in a DAW playlist lane nobody outside the room will ever open. Capturing it is what makes "who played
the part that shipped" answerable, which is a question the entire industry currently answers from memory.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | As the player: sees their own takes and which of them made the comp. Genuinely new information — no player has ever been told this — and occasionally uncomfortable. It is also the evidence behind their performance credit. Whether they see *other* players' takes is deliberately unresolved (Q-02). |
| Producer | Full | Comping is producer/engineer work, done in the DAW against the chart. Marks keepers, assembles the comp, bounces it as a version. The only role that creates here. |
| Operator | None | Not their business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — and should never be. Outtakes are the most privacy-sensitive audio a musician produces (see DT-03). |

## Behavior

A take is an immutable capture of one part, by one player, at one time, against a song section (`07.02.03`'s
section map — see DT-01). Takes are **candidates, not versions** (sub-domain Q-03): a take is not a version of
the song, it is raw material for one.

A comp is an ordered assembly of take segments. When bounced it **becomes a version** (parent CX-02) carrying
lineage back to every take it used — and therefore to every player whose performance is in the thing that
shipped.

The realistic constraint on all of this is that comping happens in the DAW, at speed, with a producer flipping
between playlist lanes and hearing the differences. **WeJammin cannot be where comping happens** — Pro Tools and
Logic are extremely good at this and the platform has no path to competing. What it can do is *receive the
decision*: the comp map exported from the session, so the record of who played what survives the session.

### Happy Path

1. Drummer plays 6 takes of the chorus in a tracking session; the DAW bridge (`07.09`) ingests them as takes against the song's chorus section
2. Producer comps in the DAW — chorus 1 from take 3, chorus 2 from take 5
3. Bounces the comp; the bridge parses the comp map and ingests it
4. System creates a version (parent CX-02) with lineage to takes 3 and 5, and therefore to the drummer
5. At session close (`07.06.02`), the drummer's performance credit is pre-filled and **evidenced** — not "Sam says he played on it", but "takes 3 and 5 are in the shipped comp"

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Comp uses takes from two different players (two drummers, one part) | Both attributed. This is common, delicate, and exactly the fact that gets lost today. | Lineage shows both; both credited |
| Comp assembled without the bridge (no comp map available) | Version created with **incomplete lineage** — never blocked. Producer can attribute manually. | Version flagged "take lineage unavailable" |
| Take captured from a player who is not on the roster | Roster entry created against an unclaimed identity (`07.03.01` D-02) | "Unknown performer" prompt at session close |
| Player asks for their unused takes to be deleted | See DT-03 — a real and legitimate request the system must have an answer to | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Hundreds of takes on one song | Normal for a vocal comp. Needs section/player filtering, not a cap. | Filtered by part, player, section, keeper status |
| Comp re-done after the version shipped | New comp → new version. The old one is untouched (`07.04.01` D-01). | Both in the timeline as siblings |
| Take storage volume (every take of every session, forever) | Collides directly with `07.04.01` DT-03 — and takes are the largest volume in the domain by far | `[PENDING]` — see `07.04.01` Q-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Take list opened | Skeletons per part |
| Empty | No takes | "No takes captured" — expected when the bridge is absent |
| Populated | Takes exist | Grouped by part and section, with keeper marks and player attribution |
| Comping | Comp map present | Comp view showing which take fills which section |
| Bounced | Comp became a version | Link to the version in the timeline |
| Partial | Takes present, comp map absent | Takes listed; comp lineage marked unavailable |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Takes must be anchored to **chart sections and bars** (`07.02.03`), because that is how comping is actually discussed — "take 4 from the second chorus", "punch in at bar 33" | `07.02.03` DT-03 confirmed this as a cross-sub-domain edge. Nobody comps by timecode; they comp by song structure. A take list anchored to seconds is a list built for software engineers, not musicians. | ✅ CONFIRMED — with a hard consequence: **this must be designed in from the start, not retrofitted.** A take model keyed on timecode cannot later be re-anchored to sections without re-deriving every take's position. Flagged in `07.02.03` Q-03 and here as Q-01. |
| DT-02 | WeJammin should provide comping tools — a lane view where you assemble the comp in the browser | It is the natural product instinct: the takes are here, so let people comp here | ❌ **REJECTED** — decisively. Comping is a high-speed, high-precision, latency-critical craft activity that Pro Tools and Logic have refined over three decades. A browser comp editor would be worse in every dimension and would be used by nobody, while consuming an enormous share of the domain's build. The winning position is *not to compete*: **let the DAW comp, and receive the decision**. This is the general shape of the domain's correct relationship with the DAW — be the record, not the tool. |
| DT-03 | Takes are just data and can be retained like any other asset | Implicit assumption in treating takes as immutable capture | ❌ **REJECTED** — takes are the most personally sensitive audio in the entire platform, and nobody in the sweep noticed. A vocal comp session contains a singer's failures: cracked notes, bad takes, swearing, crying, the producer saying something unkind between takes. Musicians have real, legitimate fear about this material existing on someone else's server forever. Two consequences: (a) takes need their **own sensitivity class** in `07.03.03`, more restrictive than stems — arguably visible only to the player and the producer; (b) a player must have some **right of erasure over their own unused takes**, which collides head-on with `07.04.01` D-01 (immutability). The resolution is probably that *used* takes (in a shipped comp) are provenance and persist, while *unused* takes are personal and are the player's to delete. Escalated — this is a values decision, not an agent's call. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Takes are **candidates**, not versions; only a bounced comp becomes a version | Sub-domain Q-03 — conflating them makes the timeline unreadable, since a vocal session produces hundreds of takes and one version. |
| D-02 | **WeJammin does not build comping tools.** The DAW comps; the platform receives the decision | Per DT-02 — the domain's correct relationship with the DAW throughout: be the record, not the tool. |
| D-03 | Takes anchor to **chart sections and bars**, not timecode | Per DT-01 — and this must be designed in from the start; it cannot be retrofitted. |
| D-04 | Missing comp lineage **never blocks** the version | Consistent with the domain-wide non-blocking rule (`07.01.03` D-02). |
| D-05 | Takes get a **stricter sensitivity class than stems** | Per DT-03 — outtakes are the most personally exposing audio in the product. |
