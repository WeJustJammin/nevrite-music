## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Version Timeline ↔ Canonical Resolver

**Relationship**: The timeline is append-only and holds everything. The resolver holds *pointers*: "the canonical
rough is v3, the canonical mix is v7, the canonical master is v9." One version may be canonical for several
purposes; most versions are canonical for none. Crucially the pointer is **per purpose**, not a single "latest"
flag — the resolver's whole value is that "latest" is the wrong question, and asking it is what produces
`FINAL_v3_ACTUAL` in the first place.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full on the resolver, Read-only on the timeline. They decide which mix is *the* mix; they do not create versions. This asymmetry is deliberate and correct.
- **Producer**: Full on both. Creates versions, and usually nominates the canonical.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the timeline owns versions (immutable, no conflict possible). The resolver owns pointers (mutable, single writer per purpose). Nothing to merge — this is exactly why the split exists.
2. **Trigger chain**: nominating a canonical fires a notification to the roster and updates every downstream reference *that is not pinned*. Release memberships (`07.01.02` D-03) **are** pinned and deliberately do not move — a release silently changing because someone re-nominated a master would be catastrophic.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — nominating the canonical master is an owner-level act, while creating versions is a producer-level act.
4. **Notification fan-out**: canonical changes notify the roster ("the mix everyone should be listening to is now v8"). This is one of the highest-value notifications in the product: today this information travels by WhatsApp, badly.
5. **State transition conflict**: two people nominating different canonicals for the same purpose concurrently — last write wins with a loud, attributed notice ("Alex made v8 canonical 30 seconds after you made v7"). Silence here would recreate the exact confusion the feature exists to end.

### CX-02: Take & Comp ↔ Version Timeline

**Relationship**: Takes are candidates, not versions (sub-domain Q-03). A comp is assembled from takes and, when
bounced, **becomes** a version with lineage back to every take it used. This is what makes "who played the part
that actually shipped" answerable — and it is unanswerable in every existing tool, because the comp decision
lives in a DAW playlist that nobody outside the session ever sees.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — comping is producer work, done in the DAW.
- **Musician**: Read-only. A player seeing which of their takes made the comp is genuinely new information, and occasionally uncomfortable — it is also the evidence for their performance credit.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the comp is owned by its creator; takes are immutable once captured. No merge.
2. **Trigger chain**: comp bounced → new version created with take lineage → performance credits become attributable to the players whose takes were used. If lineage capture fails, the version still lands (never block the work) but flags incomplete lineage.
3. **Permission intersection**: a session player sees their own takes; whether they see *other players'* takes is a real question — hearing five other drummers' failed takes on your song is socially loaded. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: comp completion could notify players whose takes were used. Attractive; also potentially cruel to those whose takes were not. Deferred.
5. **State transition conflict**: takes captured after the comp was assembled do not retroactively enter it. Explicit re-comp only.

### CX-03: Stem Export ↔ Version Timeline

**Relationship**: A stem pack is not an independent upload — it is a **derived version**, child of the mix it was
exported from. This lineage is what allows the platform to detect the most expensive silent failure in delivery:
stems that no longer match their mix because the mix moved on. Today this is discovered by a mastering engineer
or a remixer, weeks later, and costs a re-export at best.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: Full — they export the stems from the DAW.
- **Musician**: no direct access (roster matrix) — stems are the sensitive class (`07.03.03` DT-01).
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none, except via the Remix Program (`07.08.05`), which is a *published*, deliberately-released stem set — a different thing entirely from these working stems.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — both immutable.
2. **Trigger chain**: mix version supersedes → its stem children are marked **stale** relative to the new canonical mix. Stale, not deleted: the stems are still a true record of the mix they came from.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — stems inherit the sensitivity class, so lineage does not imply equal access to parent and child.
4. **Notification fan-out**: anyone holding a stem grant is notified when their stems go stale. This is a genuinely valuable notification that no current tool sends.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether "stale" should block use in a handoff package (`07.08.01`) or merely warn.

### CX-04: File Integrity ↔ Canonical Resolver

**Relationship**: Integrity is a **precondition of canonicality**. A version whose checksum fails, or whose
session references missing media, cannot be nominated as canonical. This is the one place in the sub-domain
where the system genuinely blocks rather than warns — shipping a corrupt master has the largest blast radius
of any failure in the domain, and unlike most failures it is cheaply detectable.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees integrity status; fixes by re-uploading.
- **Musician**: sees why the version they wanted to bless cannot be blessed — in plain language, not a checksum error.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — integrity status is a derived property.
2. **Trigger chain**: integrity failure → canonical nomination blocked → owner notified. If the *already-canonical* version later fails a check, the resolver must raise an alarm rather than silently fall back.
3. **Permission intersection**: no.
4. **Notification fan-out**: failure notifies the uploader and the song owner.
5. **State transition conflict**: a version passing at upload and failing later (bit rot, storage fault) is the alarming case — see `07.04.05`.

### CX-05: A/B Compare ↔ Version Timeline

**Relationship**: The player reads any two versions from the timeline and switches between them at the same
playback position with matched loudness. The change summary on a version is metadata; **the audio is the truth**,
and no mix is ever approved by reading a changelog.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: Full — this is their primary evaluation tool and, for many, the most-used feature in the domain.
- **Producer**: Full — uses it to demonstrate the change they just made.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — read-only over immutable data.
2. **Trigger chain**: none — pure read.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — you can only compare versions you can access (`07.03.03`), so a session player's A/B set is smaller than a mix engineer's.
4. **Notification fan-out**: none.
5. **State transition conflict**: none.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Version Timeline | Version Timeline — **branching and merging** | Considered modelling alternate mixes as branches with a merge path, by analogy with Git. Rejected (sub-domain D-03): audio does not merge. Two people's mix edits cannot be reconciled by any automated process, and a merge affordance would promise something the medium cannot deliver. Alternate mixes are handled as what they actually are — parallel versions with shared lineage, resolved by *choosing* (`07.04.02`), not merging. The Git analogy is seductive and wrong, and this is the single most important rejection in the sub-domain. |
| R-02 | Stem Export | Take & Comp | Considered exporting stems per-take for archival completeness. Rejected: a take is a performance of one part, and a stem is a mixed group of parts — exporting "stems per take" is a category error that would produce enormous data volume with no consumer. The legitimate need (which take made the comp) is CX-02 and needs no audio duplication. |
| R-03 | File Integrity | A/B Compare | Considered gating playback on integrity verification. Rejected: verifying a multi-GB asset before every play is prohibitive, and a musician trying to hear their song should never be blocked by a background hygiene check. Integrity gates **canonicality** (CX-04), which is where the decision has consequences — not playback, where it only has friction. |
