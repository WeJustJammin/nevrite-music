# Feature: Contributor Invitation & Scoped Onboarding

> **Number**: 07.03.02
> **Parent**: [Contributors, Access & Confidentiality](./07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The flow that brings a person onto a song's roster with a scope matching their actual involvement — and, for
people who are not yet WeJammin users, the first thing they ever see of the product. That second job matters
more than the first: per `meta/personas.md`, one of the two switching triggers for the Musician persona is
"a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin." **This feature is the platform's
primary organic growth loop**, disguised as an access-control flow.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Invites co-writers and players onto their songs. Receives invitations into other people's projects constantly — the multi-hyphenate is on both sides of this flow weekly. Accepts, declines, or ignores. As an invitee with no account, this is their first contact with the product. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume inviter: they run the room, so they bring the people. Often inviting someone mid-session, on a phone, with 30 seconds of attention available. |
| Operator | None | Cannot invite or be invited to a project roster. A studio's relationship with a session is a booking (domain 16), not a roster membership. |
| Fan | None | Hard boundary — a Fan cannot be invited onto a project roster. If a fan account is invited, the invitation is refused rather than silently upgrading them into a professional context. |

## Behavior

An invitation names the song, the intended role, and the person — by WeJammin identity, or by email/name where
no account exists (`07.03.01` D-02). Acceptance instantiates the roster entry and its derived vault grants
(parent CX-01, CX-02). Where confidentiality terms apply, the NDA gate fires inside this flow at the moment of
first access (parent CX-03), never as a separate email.

Two hard constraints shape the design:

**The invite must survive the invitee having no account and no interest.** The landing experience for a
non-user must show *what they are being invited to and by whom* before demanding a signup. An invitation that
opens on a login wall converts nothing and, worse, wastes the strongest acquisition signal the platform has:
a trusted peer vouching for it.

**The work happens before the acceptance.** This is the state-transition conflict in parent CX-02, and it is
the most important behavior in this feature. Someone plays the session on Tuesday; the invitation is sent
Tuesday; they accept in three weeks or never. If capture requires acceptance, the platform records nothing on
the day — which is the exact failure it exists to prevent. So a `pending` roster entry is fully creditable
(`07.03.01`), and the credit simply waits to be counter-attested.

### Happy Path

1. Producer adds Sam to the roster (`07.03.01`) as `session player · drums`; Sam has no WeJammin account
2. System sends an invitation naming the song, the role, and the inviter
3. Sam opens it and sees — **without signing up** — "Producer credited you as session drummer on 'Ghost Town'", who else is on it, and a play button for the rough mix if the role's access profile allows
4. Sam signs up (the credit is the reason — he wants it on his profile), and the identity is claimed
5. The roster entry converts from unclaimed to claimed; Sam counter-attests the credit; his vault access activates

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Invitee never accepts | Roster entry stays `pending` and remains creditable indefinitely. **Not expired** — the drum take does not expire. The *access* grant may expire; the attribution does not. | Inviter sees "pending" with a nudge affordance |
| Invitation link forwarded to someone else | Real leak vector — pre-release audio is behind that link. Link must be identity-bound, not bearer-token, wherever it grants asset access | Wrong recipient hits an identity check, not the audio |
| Invitee declines | Roster entry survives as a **disputed or declined** attribution, not a silent delete. A decline of a *credit* is meaningful information (they may be saying "I didn't play on this"), quite different from declining *access*. | Inviter notified with the distinction surfaced |
| Invitee is a Fan account | Refused. No silent upgrade of a fan into a professional context. | "This account can't join project rosters" |
| Email typo — invitation to a stranger | Stranger receives an invite naming a confidential song and its personnel. **This is a confidentiality leak caused by the product.** Mitigation: unverified-recipient invites reveal minimal detail until identity is established | Stranger sees "You've been invited to a project by [inviter]" and nothing more |
| Inviter's own access is revoked before acceptance | Invitation is void | "This invitation is no longer valid" |
| Same person invited to 12 songs at once (album session) | Bulk invite as one message, not twelve | One invitation covering the set |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Composing | Role and scope picker with the derived access profile shown in plain language |
| Sent / Pending | Invitation dispatched | Inviter sees pending; invitee sees the invitation |
| Accepted | Invitee accepted | Roster entry active; grants live |
| Declined | Invitee declined | Distinguished from expired; inviter notified with the reason if given |
| Expired | Access grant window elapsed | Attribution persists; access does not |
| Revoked | Inviter or owner cancelled | Link dead |
| Error | Send failed | Retry; the roster entry is unaffected — invitation failure must never lose the attribution |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The invitation is the platform's **primary organic growth loop**, and the invitee-side landing is therefore a top-tier surface, not a transactional email | `meta/personas.md`, Musician switching trigger: "a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin." Combined with `07.03.01` DT-01 (rosters address non-users), every session on the platform generates invitations to people who are not on it. The acquisition mechanic is: someone you trust says you played on something, and you want that credit. **You are not being asked to try a product — you are being handed something that is already yours.** | ✅ CONFIRMED — the invitee landing must render the credit and the social proof *before* any signup wall. Elevates this feature well above its apparent importance in the candidate list. |
| DT-02 | Invitation acceptance should be a **precondition** for crediting someone — you cannot credit a person who has not agreed to be on the project | Reasonable-sounding consent instinct | ❌ **REJECTED** — this would destroy the thesis. The Producer's "moment that matters" (`meta/personas.md`) is capture *while everyone is present*; acceptance happens later, or never. Gating capture on acceptance means the platform records nothing on the day and reconstructs later from memory — the precise failure in `meta/problem-statement.md`. Capture is decoupled from consent: the roster entry is a **claim** the moment it is made; consent arrives later as **counter-attestation** and is what elevates claim → verified credit. Consent gates *verification and publication*, never *capture*. |
| DT-03 | Scoped onboarding should teach the invitee the product | Standard onboarding instinct | ❌ REJECTED — the invitee is a working musician who was pulled in to do one thing, often on a phone, mid-session. A product tour is friction at the worst possible moment. The invitation should deliver them to exactly the thing they were invited for (hear the rough mix / confirm the credit / read the chart) and let the product be discovered later. The *credit* is the onboarding. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The invitee landing shows the credit and context **before** any signup wall | Per DT-01 — this is the growth loop; a login wall wastes the platform's strongest acquisition signal. |
| D-02 | **Capture is decoupled from consent.** A pending invitation is fully creditable; consent gates verification and publication, not capture | Per DT-02 — the single most important decision in this feature. Reversing it would break the thesis. |
| D-03 | Invitation links that grant asset access are **identity-bound**, not bearer tokens | Forwarded links are a pre-release leak vector, and pre-release leaks are a career-scale harm for the artist. |
| D-04 | Attribution never expires; access grants may | The drum take does not expire. Conflating the two lifetimes would silently erase provenance. |
| D-05 | Fan accounts cannot join project rosters | Hard boundary between the consumer and professional contexts (D-11/D-13). |
