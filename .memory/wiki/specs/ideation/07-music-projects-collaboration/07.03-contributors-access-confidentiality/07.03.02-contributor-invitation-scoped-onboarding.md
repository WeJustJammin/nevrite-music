# Feature: Contributor Invitation & Scoped Onboarding

> **Number**: 07.03.02
> **Parent**: [Contributors, Access & Confidentiality](./07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The flow that brings a person onto a song's roster with a scope matching their actual involvement — and, for
people who are not yet WeJammin users, the first thing they ever see of the product. That second job matters
more than the first: per `meta/personas.md`, one of the two switching triggers for the Musician persona is
"a trusted collaborator invites them into a project already on WeJammin." **This feature is the platform's
primary organic growth loop**, disguised as an access-control flow.

It is also the platform's **notice mechanism**. Because a roster entry is already a soft credit claim
(`07.03.01` DT-03), the invitation is how a person learns that a claim has been made *about them* — which is
the precondition for disputing it. An invitation that fails to arrive is not merely a lost signup; it is an
unanswerable assertion sitting in the provenance graph.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Inviter side**: adds a co-writer or player to a song's roster (`07.03.01`), which emits the invitation — there is no separate "send invite" step (DT-04). Sees per-recipient delivery state (`sent` / `delivered` / `bounced` / `opted-out` / `blocked`), nudges at most 1×/7d up to 3× total, retracts a not-yet-attested claim, and copies a disclosure-safe shareable link to paste into WhatsApp (DT-05). **Invitee side**: receives invitations weekly — the multi-hyphenate is on both sides of this flow constantly. Proves their address in one tap, sees the credit and who else is on it, accepts or declines *per roster entry* (they may accept `drums` and decline `mix engineer` on the same song), counter-attests after claiming, or declines with a typed reason including "wrong person — it was X". |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume inviter: they run the room, so they bring the people. Invites mid-session, on a phone, with 30 seconds of attention — so the invitation is a byproduct of adding the name, never a form. Holds `may_invite` by role default and may delegate a **single-song, single-use** `may_invite` to a contractor/fixer who knows the section's names when they do not (DT-09). Cannot see whether the recipient *read* the message — only whether it was delivered. |
| Operator | None | The Operator lens exposes no invitation surface. A studio's relationship to a session is a booking (domain 16); the roster is the client's confidential business (`07.03.01` Role Lens). Note the multi-hyphenate: the same human who operates the room is routinely invited to engineer the date — but they are invited **under their Producer lens**, not as the venue. The invitation never addresses a venue/studio *entity*. |
| Fan | None | No invitation surface in the fan-facing product; a roster invitation can never be addressed to a follower relationship, and the fan surfaces render no roster or vault UI. **This is not an account-type refusal** — per D-19 the Fan is a lens, not an account type, so there is no "fan account" to reject (DT-06). A person whose account has only ever used consumer surfaces *can* be invited to play on a song — accepting is precisely the transition into the Musician lens, and refusing it would sever the growth loop DT-01 identifies. The one hard refusal is an account below the platform's age-assurance threshold (D-13): they may hold a roster entry (they played), but cannot accept confidentiality terms or receive vault access (Q-04). |

## Behavior

An invitation names the song, the intended role(s), and the person — by WeJammin identity, or by email/phone
where no account exists (`07.03.01` D-02). It is not composed; it is **emitted** by the roster addition
(DT-04). Acceptance instantiates the roster entry's derived vault grants (parent CX-01, CX-02). Where
confidentiality terms apply, the NDA gate fires inside this flow at the moment of first asset access
(parent CX-03), never as a separate email.

Three hard constraints shape the design.

**The invite must survive the invitee having no account and no interest.** The landing experience for a
non-user must show *what they are being invited to and by whom* before demanding a signup. An invitation that
opens on a login wall converts nothing and, worse, wastes the strongest acquisition signal the platform has:
a trusted peer vouching for it.

**The work happens before the acceptance.** This is the state-transition conflict in parent CX-02, and it is
the most important behavior in this feature. Someone plays the session on Tuesday; the invitation is sent
Tuesday; they accept in three weeks or never. If capture requires acceptance, the platform records nothing on
the day — which is the exact failure it exists to prevent. So a `pending` roster entry is fully creditable
(`07.03.01`), and the credit simply waits to be counter-attested.

**The link will be forwarded, and the platform must be indifferent to that.** The Producer will paste it into
the session WhatsApp group. Designing against this produces a screenshot instead — strictly worse. So the link
carries nothing worth leaking until the recipient proves control of the address (DT-05).

### The disclosure ladder — resolves Q-01

The line is **address proof, not the signup wall.** The growth-loop hook (who named you, and as what) sits
above the line; everything about the song sits below it. Proving the address costs one tap of a code that was
already sent to it, which closes the typo-leak vector completely at negligible friction.

| Tier | Reached by | Discloses | Withholds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0 — unproven** | Opening the link (or receiving the message). A forwarded link lands here. | Inviter's **handle** + verification state, "named you as a contributor", the role in plain language ("session drummer"), and the single CTA "Confirm it's you". | Song title, project name, artist, any other roster member, any audio, any note text, the inviter's email. |
| **T1 — address proven** | Entering a 6-digit code sent to the invited address. **Not a signup.** | The credit in full: song title, the inviter, the other roster members' display names, the free-text note, and — **if the song is not marked confidential** — streaming (never download) of assets the role's default profile allows. | Downloads. Stems regardless of role. Anything on a confidential song. The NDA terms text. Any other song on the project. |
| **T2 — identity claimed** | Signup or sign-in binding the address to a WeJammin identity. | Everything the roster role's derived grant allows (parent CX-01), **after** the NDA gate fires in-flow if the song requires terms (parent CX-03). Counter-attestation becomes available. | Nothing beyond the role's grant. |

Concrete parameters: code is 6 digits, TTL 10 minutes, 5 attempts then a 15-minute lockout, resend capped at
3/hour/address. A successful proof binds a device token valid 30 days; opening from a second device re-runs the
code. The link itself is single-use for *binding* but idempotent for *reading* — a second open by the bound
device is not an error.

**The subject line is T0 content, always.** Lock screens are public. A confidential song's title never appears
in a subject, push preview, or SMS body.

### Anatomy of the invitation object

- **One invitation per (inviter, recipient, 24h window)** — a 12-song album session is one message covering a
  set, not twelve (DT-16). Additional roster adds inside the window join the open set; adds after it dispatch
  a new message no sooner than 24h after the previous one.
- **An invitation is a container for N roster entries**, each independently acceptable and declinable. Sam
  invited as `drums` + `mix engineer` on one song gets one message and two decisions; his access is the
  **union** of the grants for the entries he accepts.
- **Note field**: optional, max 280 characters, plain text, URLs stripped (an attacker's link carried by the
  platform's sending reputation is a phishing gift — DT-10), moderated as messaging content (domain 24),
  delivered at T1+ only. The platform's `From`/`reply-to` is never the inviter's address.
- **Inviter display**: handle + verification state, never the raw display name alone. Display names are
  user-controlled; "WeJammin Support" as a display name is a phishing primitive (DT-10, domain 01/24).
- **No credential prompt ever appears in the invitation flow.** The only credential event is the T2 claim, on
  the canonical domain `wejamm.in` (`meta/constraints.md`), reached from a code sent out-of-band to the same
  address. The invitation email is a perfect phishing template and will be cloned; the defence is that the
  genuine article never asks for a password.

### Who may invite

`may_invite` is a capability carried by the roster role, not a global permission (parent CX-02, synthesis Q3;
aligns with `07.03.01` Q-02):

| Role | `may_invite` | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Song owner | Yes, unscoped | It is their song. |
| `producer` | Yes, unscoped on songs they are on | This is what makes in-session capture work — the constraint from `meta/personas.md`. |
| `mix engineer`, `mastering engineer`, `engineer` | No by default | They were brought in; they do not bring people. |
| `session player`, `writer`, `featured artist` | No | A session player must not be able to invite the world into a confidential project. |
| **Delegated (the fixer)** | Single-song, single-use, expires 7 days, capped at 12 entries | The contractor knows the horn section's names; the producer does not (DT-09). Every entry created under a delegation is stamped with both the delegate and the delegator, and the delegator is notified on each. |

### Decline is not one thing

A single `declined` state destroys the most valuable signal in the flow (DT-11). Declines are typed:

| Decline reason | Meaning | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `not-me` — wrong person | Misidentification, usually a name collision (DT-15) | Entry marked **misattributed**, removed from the roster, retained in the audit log. Inviter notified with high salience: this was a false claim, not a refusal. |
| `wrong-person-was-X` | "I didn't play on this — Ben did" | As above, **plus** a suggested entry addressed to X, stamped `suggested-by: [decliner]`, requiring the inviter's confirmation before it is created. Never auto-created — a decliner must not be able to write to someone else's roster. |
| `didnt-play` — I was not on this | A **disputed attribution**, not a refusal of access. Materially different and materially more serious. | Entry flagged `disputed`; routes to domain 24; carries zero weight anywhere (`07.03.01` D-03) and is never publicly rendered. |
| `role-wrong` | "I played bass, not guitar" | Entry stays; role contested; inviter prompted to correct. One tap for the inviter; no dispute needed. |
| `no-thanks` — declining *access* | They played, they accept the credit, they don't want the project in their life | Attribution intact; access declined; roster shows them as credited, no active grant. |
| `dont-contact-me` | Global opt-out (DT-14) | See below. |

### Retraction, revocation, and opt-out — three different things

| Action | Who | Effect on the claim | Effect on access |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retract** ("wrong person, my mistake") | Inviter | Entry **deleted** if not yet counter-attested; the retraction itself is logged. A typo must not become a permanent public accusation. | Link dies immediately. |
| **Retract** after counter-attestation | Inviter | **Refused.** The inviter cannot unilaterally erase an attested fact about someone else's work. Routes to domain 24 as a dispute. | Unaffected pending the dispute. |
| **Revoke** ("they're off the job") | Owner or inviter | Claim **survives** — they played (D-04). | Grants revoked, fail-closed, per parent CX-01. |
| **Opt out** ("never invite me to WeJammin") | Recipient, from any T0 landing without an account | Existing attributions **survive**; they can never be counter-attested, so they remain permanently unverified and weightless. | No delivery, ever. |

The opt-out consequence is shown honestly to the inviter — "this person has opted out of WeJammin contact; this
credit can't be verified" — because the alternative is an inviter who thinks the platform is broken and a
credit that silently never becomes real (DT-14).

### Abuse limits — product rules (Q-03)

Enforcement mechanism routes to `/create-prd-security`; the **product behavior** is specified here:

- **50 invitations per rolling 24h to addresses the inviter has never previously invited or collaborated
  with.** Unlimited to existing collaborators — the album session is the legitimate high-volume case and must
  never hit a wall.
- **3 spam reports from distinct recipients within 30 days** → invite privileges suspended pending review
  (domain 24). The inviter's existing roster entries are untouched.
- **A blocked inviter cannot create new roster entries naming the blocker at all** (DT-12) — a hard stop, not
  a channel suppression. Entries made before the block stand.
- Unverified inviters (`07.03.01` — no verified credits, account < 30 days) are capped at **5 new-address
  invitations per 24h** and cannot invite to songs marked confidential.
- Nudges: 1 per 7 days, max 3, then the affordance is disabled permanently for that invitation. The platform
  **never auto-nudges** — consistent with the domain's no-nagging posture (`07.08.03` D-03).

### Bands as invitees

An invitation may address a **band entity** (domain 01; parent Q-03, resolved for this feature only). Delivery
fans out to the members holding the band's invite-handling permission; acceptance by any one of them creates a
**single band roster entry** and nothing else — **band membership never fans out to member entries or member
credits** (`07.03.01` D-10, parent Q-03). The horn section is one negotiation and **one section credit**, frozen
to the contribution-date lineup; fanning it out would fabricate a credit for the member who missed the session.
The members' vault access is a separate axis and is computed live from the band's current membership
(`07.03.01` D-11) — access is present tense, attribution is past tense. A member who *separately* played gets a
roster entry in their own right, written as an independent act by whoever holds `roster:write` (`07.03.01`
D-08); both lines then stand as two independent facts, and neither is ever derived from the other. If no member
has claimed the band entity, the invitation degrades to an unclaimed identity exactly as an individual's would.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is running a tracking session; adds "Sam Okafor" to the roster of "Ghost Town" as
   `session player · drums` (`07.03.01`). Sam has no WeJammin account, so the Producer types his email. **No
   separate invite step exists** — the roster add emits the invitation (DT-04).
2. System dispatches one message. Subject and preview carry T0 content only: "Producer credited you on a
   record." The roster entry is `pending` and **already creditable** — the session can close and capture at
   `07.06.02` without Sam ever having clicked (D-02).
3. Sam opens the link on his phone in the car park. He sees T0: "**@producer_handle** (verified) named you as
   a **session drummer**." One button: "Confirm it's you." No song title, no personnel, no audio.
4. Sam taps it; a 6-digit code arrives at the invited address; he enters it. **He has not signed up.**
5. T1 renders the credit: "session drummer on **Ghost Town**", the other four names on the roster, the
   Producer's note ("that fill in the second chorus — thank you"), and a play button for the rough mix,
   because `session player` → rough-mix streaming and the song is not marked confidential.
6. Sam taps **"Yes, that's me — I played drums on this."** This is recorded as a **provisional attestation**,
   stamped `evidence: address-proven, identity: none`. The credit does **not** become verified and does **not**
   appear anywhere public (DT-07).
7. Sam signs up — the credit is the reason; he wants it on his profile. The address binds to a new identity;
   the roster entry converts from unclaimed to claimed.
8. His provisional attestation is presented for one-tap ratification: "You confirmed this before you had an
   account. Still true?" He confirms. **Now** it is a counter-attested credit: verified, weighted, and
   publishable on his profile (DT-08 resolves Q-02).
9. His vault grant activates. Ghost Town appears in his projects; the Producer is notified once ("Sam Okafor
   joined Ghost Town"), and the roster's other members see him in the next daily digest, not immediately.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Invitee never accepts | Roster entry stays `pending` and remains creditable indefinitely. **Not expired** — the drum take does not expire. The *access* grant expires; the attribution does not (D-04). | Inviter sees `pending` with a nudge affordance (1/7d, max 3) |
| Invitation link forwarded to someone else | Nothing leaks. The forwarded link lands at **T0**, which carries no song, no personnel, no audio — it carries a name and a role. The forwardee cannot pass the code gate. | Forwardee sees "@producer_handle named someone as a session drummer" and a code prompt they cannot satisfy |
| Producer pastes the link into the session WhatsApp group | **Supported, not fought** (DT-05). The link is disclosure-safe by construction; the first person to open it binds nothing. | Everyone in the group sees T0; only the invited address can proceed |
| Email typo — invitation to a stranger | Stranger reaches T0 only: inviter handle + role, **no song title, no personnel, no note, no audio**. The subject line likewise carries no title. Confidentiality is preserved by design rather than by the inviter's typing accuracy. | "@producer_handle named you as a contributor. Not you? Tell us." — with a one-tap `not-me` and a `dont-contact-me` |
| Typo'd address is a *real person who confirms it* | Worst case in the flow: a stranger proves an address the inviter chose and reaches T1. Bounded by the T1/T2 split — they can stream a non-confidential rough mix, never download, never reach a confidential song. A `no-account` provisional attestation carries zero weight (DT-07), so no false verified credit can be minted. | Stranger sees a song they don't recognise; the `not-me` decline is the most prominent action at T1 |
| Invitee declines — `didnt-play` | Roster entry survives as a **disputed** attribution, not a silent delete. A decline of a *credit* is meaningful information, categorically different from declining *access*. Routes to domain 24. | Inviter notified with the distinction surfaced: "Sam says he didn't play on this" |
| Invitee declines — `not-me` / `wrong-person-was-X` | Entry marked misattributed and removed; suggested replacement requires the inviter's confirmation, never auto-created | Inviter: "Sam says it wasn't him — he thinks it was Ben. Add Ben?" |
| Invitee is a Fan-lens account | **Not refused.** There is no fan account type to refuse (D-19, DT-06). The invitation renders; accepting is the transition into the Musician lens and is the growth loop working exactly as intended. | Normal invitation; on accept, the professional surfaces open |
| Invitee is below the age-assurance threshold (D-13) | Roster entry and attribution stand — they played. Confidentiality terms cannot be accepted and vault access does not activate. | "We can't give this account project access yet" + inviter told the credit stands but access is held (Q-04) |
| Inviter's own access is revoked before acceptance | Invitation is void; **entries the inviter already created stand** — revoking someone's access does not retract their statements (symmetry with D-04). | Invitee: "This invitation is no longer valid." Inviter: nothing — they can no longer see the song |
| Same person invited to 12 songs at once (album session) | Bulk invite as one message covering the set (DT-16). Each song's entry is independently acceptable. | One invitation, 12 rows, per-row accept/decline |
| Album invite: 9 accepted, 2 declined, 1 untouched | The **Partial** state — the normal outcome, not an error | Per-row status; inviter sees a 9/12 roll-up, not 12 notifications |
| Same person invited as `drums` **and** `mix engineer` on one song | One invitation, two entries. Access is the **union** of accepted entries' grants. He may accept `drums` and decline `mix engineer` — declining the mix credit while keeping the drum credit is coherent and common. | Two rows, two decisions |
| Multi-hyphenate is inviter on song A and invitee on song B from the same producer, same day | Digests do not merge them. An "invitation to you" and a "your invite was accepted" are different messages with different CTAs. | Two distinct notifications |
| Two inviters (owner + producer) add the same person to the same song simultaneously | Deduped on (song, identity-or-address). One invitation object. Different roles → both entries created, one message. Concurrent identical role → idempotent, one entry, one message. | Invitee sees one invitation with both roles |
| Invitee accepts at the same instant the inviter revokes | **Fail closed to revoked.** If the counter-attestation landed inside the same transaction it **stands** — it is a fact about the work, not a permission. Access is denied. | "This invitation was withdrawn." The credit remains on their profile |
| Invitee taps accept twice / retries on a flaky connection | Accept is **idempotent per (invitation-entry, identity)**. The binding token is single-use; the accept is not. | Success, not "invitation already used" |
| Delivery fails (hard bounce) | 3 send retries with exponential backoff (1 min, 5 min, 25 min), then dead-letter. Roster entry **unaffected** — invitation failure must never lose the attribution. | Inviter sees `bounced` with "try another address" — never a silent failure |
| Delivery lands in spam | Undetectable to the platform; the growth loop dies quietly here more than anywhere. Mitigation is the shareable link (DT-05) as an explicit alternate channel. | Inviter sees `delivered` but no activity; "Send it to them yourself" is offered after 72h |
| Recipient has opted out globally (DT-14) | No delivery, ever. Roster entry stands, permanently unverifiable. | Inviter told plainly at add-time: "This person has opted out of WeJammin contact. The credit will stay unverified." |
| Recipient has blocked the inviter (domain 24) | **The inviter cannot create the entry at all** (DT-12) — a hard stop at write time, not a suppressed email. Suppressing only the channel would let a blocked person make claims the blocker can never see or dispute. Entries predating the block stand. | Inviter: "You can't add this person." No detail — confirming a block to a blocked user is itself a harm |
| Name collision — three "Sam Okafor" on the platform | Search-by-name never binds alone (DT-15). The picker requires a disambiguator: handle, avatar, mutual-collaborator count, most recent verified credits. If none matches, "invite by email instead" is the prominent path. | Disambiguation list with mutual-collaborator counts, never a bare name list |
| Inviter picks the wrong Sam from the list | Both a false credit and a leak of the song's existence to a stranger. Bounded by: the wrong Sam is at T2 already (an account), so he sees the song title — a real disclosure. Mitigated by `not-me` being the most prominent T1/T2 action, and by the entry carrying zero weight until counter-attested. Not fully preventable; the disambiguator is the defence. | Wrong Sam: "@producer_handle says you played drums on Ghost Town. Not you?" |
| Abusive or harassing note text | Note is 280 chars, plain text, URLs stripped, moderated as messaging (domain 24). Platform never sends from the inviter's address. A reported note suspends the inviter's invite privileges at 3 distinct reports/30d. | Recipient can report from the T0 landing without an account |
| Inviter's display name is "WeJammin Support" | Invitations render the **handle + verification state**, never the display name alone (DT-10). Display-name moderation is domain 01/24. | "@some_handle (unverified)" — the mismatch is the tell |
| Cloned/phishing invitation email | Undefeatable at source; the defence is the genuine flow's shape: **no invitation ever contains a credential prompt**, the code is out-of-band to the same address, and the claim happens only on `wejamm.in`. | Published, consistent invite anatomy users can recognise |
| Song is deleted while an invitation is pending | A song with ≥1 non-owner roster entry **cannot be hard-deleted** — it archives. Pending invitations are voided; attributions persist. | "This project is no longer available." Credit remains on the invitee's record |
| Confidential song, invitee reaches T1 | Metadata renders (title, roster, note) because the invited address was proven. **No streaming** — a confidential song's audio requires T2 + the NDA gate (parent CX-03). | "Ghost Town — audio unlocks when you sign in and accept the terms." Never a bare locked icon |
| NDA terms text itself is confidential (names the label/artist) | Terms are presented at **T2 only**. Never at T0/T1, where the reader's identity is unproven. | The gate appears after sign-in, in-flow, at first play |
| Contributor is deceased | Address bounces or a family member replies. Entry stays `pending`, uncontactable, permanently unverified. Posthumous credits are common and contested in music; the estate path is domain 01's identity-claim question, not this feature's. | Inviter sees `bounced`; the entry is honest about being unverified (Q-05) |
| Delegated `may_invite` (fixer) is abused | Capped: one song, 7-day expiry, 12 entries. Every entry stamped with delegate **and** delegator; the delegator is notified per entry and can revoke the delegation instantly. The delegator remains accountable for the claims. | Delegator sees a live feed of what the fixer added |
| Invitee already on the roster with the same role | Idempotent. No second entry, no second message. | Nothing — silence is correct here |
| Invitee's proven address is later removed from their account | The binding survives (it was true at proof time); the attestation and credit are unaffected. Future delivery to that address stops. | Nothing |
| 500 invitations from one new account in an hour | Rate limit (5/24h for unverified inviters) trips at #6. Soft block with an appeal path — a real album session from a real producer must never be mistaken for spam, so the appeal is fast and human. | "We've paused invitations from this account. Tell us what you're working on." |

### States

**Surface states** (what the invitee's landing renders):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Link opened; invitation resolving | Skeleton of the credit card — inviter handle line first, since that is the payload (DT-01) |
| Empty | No invitation matches the link (never sent, wrong link) | "We can't find that invitation" + a path to search for credits naming their address. Never a 404 |
| Populated | Tier resolved (T0, T1, or T2) | The disclosure ladder's contents for that tier, with the next-tier CTA as the single primary action |
| Partial | Multi-entry set with mixed decisions, or T1 on a confidential song | Per-row status; explained absences ("audio unlocks at sign-in"), never a silent filter — consistent with `07.03.03` D-06 |
| Error | Code service down, delivery failure, network loss mid-accept | Retry with the failure named. **The roster entry is never at risk** — invitation failure must not lose the attribution |

**Invitation lifecycle states**:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Roster add in progress (`07.03.01`) | Role and scope picker, with the derived access profile in plain language ("hears the rough mix; not the stems") |
| Sent | Dispatched | Inviter: `sent`. Invitee: the message |
| Delivered | Accepted by the receiving system | Inviter: `delivered`. **Never `read`** — read receipts on a professional invite are surveillance, and the Producer does not need them |
| Bounced | Hard bounce after 3 retries | Inviter: "we couldn't reach that address" + change-address action. Entry intact |
| Pending | Delivered, no decision | Inviter: `pending` + nudge (1/7d, max 3). Invitee: the invitation, indefinitely — the credit waits forever (D-04) |
| Address-proven (T1) | Code entered | Inviter: `viewed`. Invitee: the credit in full |
| Accepted | Entry accepted at T2 | Roster entry active; grants live (after the NDA gate if applicable) |
| Provisionally attested | T1 confirmation without an account | Recorded, stamped `evidence: address-proven`. **Not** rendered publicly; ratification pending (DT-07) |
| Declined | Any typed decline | Distinguished by reason — misattribution, dispute, role-wrong, and access-refusal are four different outcomes (DT-11) |
| Access-expired | 30 days of T1 inactivity, or grant window elapsed | **Attribution persists; access does not** (D-04). The identity-claim function of the link never expires |
| Retracted | Inviter retracted a pre-attestation claim | Entry deleted; retraction logged. Invitee (if they saw it): "@handle withdrew this" |
| Revoked | Owner or inviter cancelled access | Link dead; claim survives |
| Opted-out | Recipient global opt-out | No delivery. Entry permanently unverifiable, and the inviter is told so |
| Suspended | Inviter tripped abuse limits | Invitee sees nothing; inviter sees the pause and the appeal path |
| Error | Send failed | Retry; the roster entry is unaffected — invitation failure must never lose the attribution |

## Cross-Cut Notes

> **T1 vault reconciliation (`07.03.03` D-09):** after address proof, a T1
> invitee may stream only a non-confidential rough mix as invitation disclosure.
> This is never a persistent role-derived vault grant: stems, downloads, terms
> acceptance, roster identity, and ongoing access still wait for a claim.

- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **shared entity + trigger dependency**. The unclaimed-identity claim flow is the T2 transition and is owned there, not here; this feature is its highest-volume producer. Also: band entities as invitees (parent Q-03) need a "who may accept on the band's behalf" permission; display-name moderation is a DT-10 dependency; age-assurance state (D-13) gates T2 acceptance of terms.
- Touches **02 Credits & Attribution** ([../../02-credits-attribution/](../../02-credits-attribution/)) — **shared entity**. Per DT-02/DT-07/DT-08, this flow is where consent enters the credit graph. Domain 02 must model three attestation tiers, not two: `claimed` (identity bound), `provisionally attested` (address-proven, weightless, unpublished), `counter-attested` (verified, weighted, publishable). Publication is gated on the third — this is the resolution of Q-02 and it is domain 02's rule to enforce.
- Touches **03 Community & Networking** ([../../03-community-networking/](../../03-community-networking/)) — **trigger dependency**. Per DT-01 this is the growth surface; the collaborator graph is built from accepted invitations. Also supplies the mutual-collaborator count that DT-15's disambiguator needs — meaning a *safety* control here depends on a *social* feature there.
- Touches **07.03.01 Contributor Roster** — **the same action** (DT-04). The roster add emits this invitation; `07.03.01` owns the entry, this file owns delivery, disclosure, and the claim. `may_invite` is a roster-role capability and must be specified consistently with `07.03.01` Q-02 (who may edit the roster).
- Touches **07.03.03 Asset Vault** ([./07.03.03-rights-aware-asset-vault-nda-gating.md](./07.03.03-rights-aware-asset-vault-nda-gating.md)) — **permission**. The disclosure ladder is a *pre-roster* access tier the vault does not currently model: T1 streaming to an address-proven non-user is a grant to someone who is not a member and has no identity. `07.03.03`'s "you cannot grant stems to an email address" holds — but "you may stream a non-confidential rough mix to a proven address" is new and must be reconciled there.
- Touches **07.06.02 Session Close & Capture Prompt** ([../07.06-sessions-documentation-recall/](../07.06-sessions-documentation-recall/)) — **trigger dependency**. The domain CX file records that each capture prompt's attestation request "is also an invitation (`07.03.02` DT-01)". That fan-out is this feature's largest volume source and must respect DT-16's digest window, or a session close sends one person eight messages.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — **permission + notification**. Owns: typed `didnt-play` disputes (DT-11), the block semantics inverted in DT-12 (blocking as name-revocation is a **stronger** primitive than 24 probably models today), invite-spam suspension at 3 reports/30d, note-field moderation, and the retraction-after-attestation dispute path.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([../../09-rights-ownership/](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **trigger dependency**. A pending, never-accepted roster entry that is fully creditable (D-02) is also a person with a potential claim on a split. The split flow must handle a party who has never consented to anything and may be uncontactable (DT-14).
- **Consumes the `Notification & Messaging Fan-Out` cross-cut** (domain CX) — with three requirements the cross-cut may not cover: delivery-state reporting to the sender (`delivered`, never `read`), a **non-user-addressable channel** with its own opt-out and its own abuse limits, and DT-16's per-(sender, recipient, 24h) digest window.
- **Routes to `/create-prd-security`** — enforcement of the abuse limits in this file's product rules (Q-03), the code/lockout parameters, link-binding mechanics, and email sending reputation. The *rules* are specified here; the *mechanism* is not this layer's job.
- See parent CX-02 (invitation → roster) and CX-03 (NDA gating inside the flow).
