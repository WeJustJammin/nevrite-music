# Feature: Rights-Aware Asset Vault & NDA Gating

> **Number**: 07.03.03
> **Parent**: [Contributors, Access & Confidentiality](./07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The policy layer deciding which contributor can hear, stream, or download which asset of a song, and under what
confidentiality terms. A session player hears the rough mix of the song they played on; the mix engineer gets
the stems; the mastering engineer gets the mix but never the stems and never the tracklist. Access is **derived
from the roster role** (parent CX-01) rather than granted asset-by-asset, because a producer who must tick
fifteen checkboxes per person will simply email a Dropbox link instead — and then the platform is out of the room.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | As song owner: sets whether the song requires confidentiality terms, sees who has access to what, sees who has actually accessed what, revokes. As a contributor: sees only their own access, and — importantly — sees *what they cannot reach and why*, rather than a mysteriously empty folder. |
| Producer | Config | Configures the access policy for projects they run (client work for a label routinely requires terms). Cannot override the song owner's confidentiality setting. In practice the Producer is the one who *causes* grants by assigning roles (`07.03.01`), which is the point — access is a byproduct of describing reality. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator never has access to client assets through this vault. Note the real-world gap this creates: audio physically exists on the studio's drives regardless of what this vault says. The vault governs the platform's copy, not the world's — a limit worth stating honestly (see DT-03). |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Pre-release audio reaching a fan is the failure this feature exists to prevent. |

## Behavior

Every asset carries a sensitivity class derived from what it is (`rough mix`, `mix reference`, `stems`,
`session file`, `master`, `pre-release master`, `tracklist/metadata`) and what stage its song is at. Every
roster role carries a default access profile. The grant is the intersection, overridable per-song by the
owner but rarely needing to be.

**Default access profiles** (proposed defaults per DT-01's least-privilege principle — validation against
how real sessions share files is Q-03, User-owned). Access is the intersection of the row's grant and the
asset's sensitivity class; higher classes are never implied by lower ones:

| Roster role (`07.03.01`) | Rough mix / mix ref | Stems | Session file | Master | Tracklist / metadata | Other songs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Song owner (Musician) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | per-song |
| Producer / project runner | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | roster-scoped |
| Songwriter / co-writer (non-audio) | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Session player / performer | ✅ (song they played on) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Mix engineer | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Mastering engineer | ✅ (the mix only) | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ (their output) | ❌ | ❌ |

The mastering-engineer row is the counter-intuitive one and the point of the whole model: professionally the
most trusted contributor, yet they need the *least* — the mix in, the master out, never the stems, never the
tracklist. Least privilege tracks need, not seniority.

**Sensitivity is not uniform across a song's assets, and this is the whole reason the feature exists.** The
stems are far more sensitive than the rough mix: stems are re-usable material, and a leaked stem pack is a
permanent loss of control. Meanwhile the *tracklist* (`07.01.02`) is commercially confidential in a way the
audio is not — an unannounced album's shape is a marketing asset. A single project-wide access flag cannot
express any of this.

Where confidentiality terms apply, the NDA gate fires **at the moment of first access**, inside the flow
(parent CX-03) — not as an emailed PDF, which is never signed. The acceptance record (identity, timestamp,
terms version) is the evidence trail; whether it carries legal force is genuinely open (parent Q-02).

Revocation must be **immediate and fail-closed**: role change or removal invalidates live streaming URLs and
outstanding download tokens. A removed contributor who can still pull stems is a leak the product caused.

### Happy Path

1. Musician marks "Ghost Town" as confidential (label deal; unannounced)
2. Producer adds a mix engineer to the roster as `mix engineer`
3. The mix engineer's derived grant: stems + rough mix + mix references. Not the tracklist. Not the other songs.
4. Mix engineer opens the song for the first time; the NDA gate appears in-flow; they accept; the acceptance is recorded with terms version and timestamp
5. Stems become downloadable, watermarked per recipient via the watermarking cross-cut (sub-domain D-02)
6. Job ends; the owner revokes; live URLs die immediately; the mix credit and the mixed version persist forever

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Contributor removed mid-download | Fail closed — transfer aborts | "Your access to this song has been removed" |
| NDA declined | Roster entry survives; vault stays closed. A legitimate, under-modelled state: **on the roster, cannot hear the song** | Contributor sees why; owner is notified — a decline needs a human response, not an error page |
| Terms re-versioned while people hold access | Default: existing holders are **not** re-gated and keep access; their acceptance record stays pinned to the version they accepted (immutable, per D-03). New accessors get the new version. The owner may flag a re-version as a **material change**, which re-gates existing holders at their *next* access — currently-active streams/downloads are not interrupted (D-07). | Nothing on a minor re-version. On a material change: NDA gate re-appears in-flow at next open, showing what changed |
| Owner tries to loosen NDA terms retroactively to expose more assets | Terms changes govern *acceptance*, not *grants* — loosening terms never widens what a role can reach. Widening access is a role change (`07.03.01`) or a per-song override, each independently gated | Owner sees the access matrix, not a terms toggle, as the lever for who-sees-what |
| Asset shared outside the platform anyway (AirDrop, Dropbox, a phone recording of a playback) | **The vault cannot prevent this.** Watermarking (cross-cut) makes leaks *traceable*, not impossible | Honest framing in the UI — never imply the audio is protected once downloaded |
| Song owner is not the rights owner (label-owned master) | Real conflict: the artist may hold the WeJammin song while the label holds the master. The vault must resolve *who sets policy* against the domain-09 rights record, not against WeJammin song-authorship alone. Default direction: master-owner policy wins where a master exists; artist controls pre-master assets. Exact precedence is a rights-record question. | `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` — domain 09 boundary; policy owner is Q-02 (User) |
| Contributor sees an empty asset list due to policy | Never a bare empty state — the absence must be explained, or it reads as a bug and generates a support ticket | "Stems aren't shared with session players on this song" |
| Access granted to an unclaimed identity | Impossible — you cannot grant stems to an email address. Access waits for the claim (`07.03.01` D-02) | Entry shows "access pending claim" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Vault opened | Asset list skeleton |
| Empty | No assets yet | "Nothing uploaded yet" |
| Gated | Terms not accepted | The NDA gate, in-flow, with the asset visible-but-locked behind it |
| Populated | Access granted | Assets, each with its access level and watermark status |
| Partial | Some assets above the role's grant | Visible assets plus an explained absence — never a silent filter |
| Revoked | Access removed | Explanatory state, not a 404 — a 404 to a person who legitimately played on the song is hostile |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Stems are categorically more sensitive than mixes**, and a single per-song access flag is therefore useless | A leaked rough mix is embarrassing and ephemeral. Leaked stems are permanent loss of control: they are re-usable raw material — anyone can remix, retrain on, or re-release them, and the a cappella is the highest-value leak in the industry. Nobody in the candidate list distinguished asset sensitivity classes. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives per-asset-class sensitivity and role-derived grants. Also drives the counter-intuitive mastering-engineer profile: *more* trusted professionally, but they need less, so they get less. Least privilege, not seniority. |
| DT-02 | Access must be **derived from roster roles**, not granted per-asset per-person | Direct consequence of `meta/personas.md`'s Producer constraint. A producer mid-session will not administer an ACL. If granting access is work, they will bypass the vault entirely and email a link — and the platform is out of the room, which forfeits every downstream capture. **The access model's real job is to be invisible.** | ✅ CONFIRMED — parent CX-01. Per-song overrides exist for the exceptions but must never be the default path. |
| DT-04 | Re-versioned confidentiality terms should re-gate everyone who already holds access | Symmetry intuition: if the terms changed, everyone should re-accept. | ❌ **REJECTED as a default** — blanket re-gating interrupts live work (a mix engineer mid-render suddenly locked out is the D-04 revocation failure in reverse) and trains users to click through gates without reading. The acceptance record is already immutable and version-pinned (D-03), so a superseded record is not a defect — it faithfully records what *was* agreed. The correct model: minor re-version → no re-gate, new accessors get the new version; owner-flagged **material** change → re-gate at *next* access only, never mid-transfer. Preserves both the evidence trail and the invisibility DT-02 demands. |
| DT-03 | An in-product NDA plus watermarking meaningfully protects unreleased audio | The feature's implied premise | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED** — the vault governs *the platform's copy*, not *the world's*. Audio exists on the studio's drives, in the mix engineer's DAW, on someone's phone as a room recording. An NDA's legal force is unresolved (parent Q-02) and probably jurisdiction-dependent. What this feature genuinely delivers is: (a) **least-privilege by default**, so fewer people hold the dangerous assets at all — the real risk reduction; (b) an **evidence trail** of who accepted what terms and who accessed what when; (c) **traceability** of leaks via watermarking. It does not deliver prevention, and the UI must not imply it does. Overclaiming here would be a trust breach with the exact professional users whose confidence the platform most needs. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Assets carry a **sensitivity class**; access is the intersection of class and roster role | Per DT-01 — a single per-song flag cannot express "hears the mix, never the stems". |
| D-02 | Grants are **derived from roles**, never hand-administered per asset | Per DT-02 — if access control is work, the producer bypasses the platform and every downstream capture is lost. |
| D-03 | NDA gate fires **in-flow at first access**, and records identity + timestamp + terms version | Per parent CX-03 — an emailed NDA is never signed. |
| D-04 | Revocation is **immediate and fails closed**, including live URLs and download tokens | A removed contributor who can still pull stems is a leak the product caused. |
| D-05 | The product **must not claim to prevent leaks** — it claims least privilege, evidence and traceability | Per DT-03 — overclaiming protection to professional users is a trust breach, and these are exactly the users the platform cannot afford to lose. |
| D-06 | Denied access is always **explained**, never rendered as an empty list or a 404 | A 404 to someone who legitimately played on the song is hostile and generates support load. |
| D-07 | Terms re-versions **do not re-gate existing holders by default**; acceptance records stay version-pinned. Only an owner-flagged **material change** re-gates, and only at next access — never mid-transfer | Per DT-04 — blanket re-gating breaks live work and dulls the gate; the immutable record already captures what was agreed (D-03). **Ratified at owner level as P-07a, 2026-07-21.** Materiality here is **owner-declared**, not platform-detected: the owner authored the terms change, so the correct party is judging. This is why it does not repeat the semantic-materiality reading P-03 rejected, where the only available judge was the wrong party. Re-gating happens **at the gate, at next access** — the clause that keeps it on the right side of DT-04's lockout objection — and denial remains explained per D-06. What the flag *records* is Q-05. |
| D-08 | Default role × sensitivity-class profiles are **validation-gated**. Practitioner validation and an approved profile version are required before any candidate becomes an enforceable v1 default. | Owner ratification for A-04 / `r-53[0]`, 2026-07-20. Validation may refine profile grants but cannot replace role-derived, per-song access with manual per-asset ACLs, project-wide grants, or an owner-configured-only regime. |
