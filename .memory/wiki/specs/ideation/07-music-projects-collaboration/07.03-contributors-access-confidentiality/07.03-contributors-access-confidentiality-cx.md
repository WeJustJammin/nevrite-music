## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Contributor Roster ↔ Asset Vault

**Relationship**: Access is **derived from the roster role**, not granted per-asset by hand. This is the design
decision that makes the vault usable: a producer adding a session drummer should not then have to tick fifteen
asset checkboxes. The role carries a default access profile (`session player` → rough mixes of their song;
`mix engineer` → stems + references; `mastering engineer` → the mix, not the stems, not the tracklist), which
is overridable per-song but rarely needs to be.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees their own access level; sees the roster of songs they are on. As a song owner, sets roles and can override defaults.
- **Producer**: the usual operator — adds people, assigns roles, and thereby grants access without thinking about access. This is the point: access is a byproduct of describing reality.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: the roster owns the role; the vault owns per-asset overrides. Single writer each — no merge. Role change recomputes derived grants but **never silently discards an explicit override**; an override that contradicts a new role is surfaced, not dropped.
2. **Trigger chain**: role change → recompute grants → **revoke is immediate**, including invalidating live streaming URLs and any outstanding download tokens. If revocation fails partially the access must fail closed. A removed contributor who can still pull stems is a leak the product caused. **This chain is the access half only.** The credit half of the same edit does not recompute: per `07.03.01`'s edge case and D-19, `02.04.01` D-10 is the single materiality boundary, run as a **flat identity test** over (party, role, work) — **any** change to the role facet invalidates a counter-attestation and re-asks, and no taxonomy operation ever does (owner ratification DQ-05.1 / DQ-05.2, 2026-07-22). The two halves move independently and must not be modelled as one recompute.
3. **Permission intersection**: this *is* the intersection — the roster is the permission source for the whole domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: a role change notifies the affected person ("you now have stem access"). **Resolved by P-07b — see `07.03.01` D-09 and D-17:** a downgrade notifies the affected person *and* the roster, exactly as any other roster write does. Withholding it is how a quiet demotion stays quiet, and the roster is the team's only coordination record — co-contributors otherwise keep routing stems to someone whose access is already dead. The roster audience is scoped by D-16: only members who can already see that person's entry are told.
5. **State transition conflict**: removal from a roster while a download is in flight. Fail closed mid-transfer.

### CX-02: Invitation ↔ Contributor Roster

**Relationship**: The invitation names the person (possibly by email, before they have an account), the song,
and the intended role. Acceptance creates the roster entry. The window between send and accept is a real state:
the person is *expected* but not yet *present*, and the session may happen before they ever click.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: invites co-writers and players; accepts invitations into other people's projects — the multi-hyphenate is on both sides of this flow constantly.
- **Producer**: the highest-volume inviter; runs the room, so brings the people.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none — a fan cannot be invited to a project roster. This is a hard boundary and the correct one.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — the invitation is a distinct object with its own lifecycle (`pending`, `accepted`, `declined`, `expired`, `revoked`).
2. **Trigger chain**: accept → roster entry → derived vault grants (CX-01) → optional NDA gate (CX-03). Failure anywhere leaves the invitation `pending` rather than half-accepted.
3. **Permission intersection**: who may invite is itself a roster permission — a session player must not be able to invite the world into a confidential project.
4. **Notification fan-out**: acceptance notifies the inviter and the roster; the new arrival is visible to everyone who can already see the song.
5. **State transition conflict**: **the real one** — the work happens before acceptance. Someone plays on the session, the credit prompt fires at `07.06.02`, and they are not a roster member yet. The capture must be able to attribute to an *unaccepted, possibly account-less* person, or the platform loses the exact fact it exists to capture. This is the most important consequence in this CX file.

### CX-03: Invitation ↔ Asset Vault (NDA gating)

**Relationship**: Where confidentiality terms apply to a song, the NDA is presented **inside** the access flow —
you agree at the moment you first press play on the unreleased audio, not in an email you never opened. The
acceptance is recorded with a timestamp, the identity, and the specific terms version, which is what makes it
an evidence trail rather than a checkbox.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: accepts terms when joining a confidential project; as an owner, decides whether their song requires terms at all.
- **Producer**: accepts terms for client work routinely; configures terms on projects they run for labels.
- **Operator**: none.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: none — acceptance is an append-only record against (person, song, terms version).
2. **Trigger chain**: first access attempt → terms presented → accept → grant activates. Decline leaves the roster entry intact but the vault closed, which is a legitimate and under-modelled state: **on the roster, cannot hear the song**.
3. **Permission intersection**: yes — the NDA is a precondition layered on top of the role's derived grant.
4. **Notification fan-out**: the song owner is notified of acceptance/decline. A decline is a meaningful signal that needs a human response.
5. **State transition conflict**: terms are re-versioned while people hold access. **Resolved by P-07a — see `07.03.03` D-07:** existing holders are *not* re-gated by default and their acceptance records stay version-pinned; only an **owner-flagged material change** re-gates them, at their next access and never mid-transfer. Materiality is declared by the owner who authored the change, never detected by the platform.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | Contributor Roster | Contributor Invitation — **as a merge** | Considered collapsing these into one "membership" feature. Rejected: they have genuinely different lifecycles and different failure surfaces. The roster is durable state that outlives the project (attribution persists after removal); the invitation is a transient, expiring, revocable object that can address a person who has no account and may never accept. Merging them would force the roster to model email-addressed non-users as first-class members. |
| R-02 | Asset Vault | Idea Capture Inbox (`07.02.01`) | Considered gating the idea inbox behind vault policy. Rejected: an idea inbox is personal (or band) space that predates any roster or confidentiality regime — there is no song yet, so there is no per-song role to derive access from. Ideas are private by ownership, not by policy. |
