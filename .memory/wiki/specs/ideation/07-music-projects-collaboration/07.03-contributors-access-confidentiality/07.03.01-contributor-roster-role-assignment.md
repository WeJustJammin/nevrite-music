# Feature: Contributor Roster & Per-Song Role Assignment

> **Number**: 07.03.01
> **Parent**: [Contributors, Access & Confidentiality](./07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The list of humans and entities attached to a song, each with a role: who played what, who wrote, who produced,
who mixed, who mastered. It is the domain's most load-bearing small feature because it is three things at once
— the **access policy** (parent CX-01), the **pre-fill source** for every credit prompt (`07.06.02` D-01), and
the **addressable subject list** that domain 02's credit graph is built from. **A blank roster makes every
downstream capture a blank form, and blank forms get dismissed** (`07.06.02` D-02).

It is where `meta/problem-statement.md`'s thesis becomes a data structure. Provenance is capturable only
because the platform is in the room; the roster is what the platform knows *about* the room. Every fact
downstream — the credit, the attestation, the split negotiation, the vault grant — is a statement about a party
the roster made addressable. If the roster is wrong, everything built on it is wrong in the same direction.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **As song owner**: adds people and entities, assigns and changes roles, ends involvement, retracts mistaken claims, decides whether personnel are visible to contributors (D-16), and grants `roster:write` to others (D-08). **As a contributor**: sees the full roster of any song they are on (default), sees their own role and their own derived access, confirms or disputes the role someone assigned them, and sets their own credit Private (D-14). Seeing the other names is not a courtesy — it is how they discover they are in good company, or that someone they never worked with is claiming a role beside them. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume operator: they run the room, so they know who is in it, and they add people mid-session on a phone with ~30 seconds of attention (`07.03.02` Role Lens). Holds `roster:write` by default on songs they are rostered on as `producer` (D-08) — the only non-owner role that does. Their assignments carry more downstream weight because a producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim (`meta/personas.md`), which is precisely what makes this a trust-critical write surface rather than an admin one (DT-04). Cannot grant an access profile wider than their own (D-08), cannot remove the song owner, and cannot suppress anyone's public credit (D-14). |
| Operator | None | A studio operator knows a session is booked in their room (domain 16) and knows a headcount for capacity and catering. They never see the roster. `07.06.01` D-06 is explicit: the studio's headcount and the creative attendance list are **different facts and are never reconciled** — and `07.06.01` D-05 holds the line generally ("the Operator sees the event, never the music"). A studio with visibility into who its clients will credit is a studio with visibility into its clients' business, and per `07.06` R-01 that studio is unbookable. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and not reachable by any path. A Fan account cannot be added to a roster at all (`07.03.02` D-05) — the add is refused rather than silently upgrading a consumer account into a professional context. A fan sees credits after release (domain 02 `02.01.02`, domain 20) as a published render of the *credit graph*, never this working roster. |

> **No Operator or Fan cell will ever open.** Both absences are load-bearing, not omissions: the Operator's is
> the domain's sharpest privacy boundary (`07.06.01` D-05), and the Fan's is the professional/consumer boundary
> (`ideation-index.md` D-11, D-13). Downstream spec writers should treat any proposal to open either as a
> contradiction of a ratified decision, not as a scope question.

## Behavior

A roster entry asserts: **this party held this role on this song**, asserted by this asserter, at this time.
Roles come from domain 02's controlled vocabulary (`02.06`) — the roster does not own, extend or duplicate it
(D-06). Each canonical role carries a **default access profile** that the vault derives grants from (parent
CX-01): `session player` → rough mixes of this song; `mix engineer` → stems + references; `mastering engineer`
→ the mix, not the stems, not the tracklist (`07.03.03` D-01).

Five properties make this feature harder than its size suggests.

### 1. The roster must address people who are not users

The session drummer who played on Tuesday may have no WeJammin account and may never get one. If the roster can
only hold accounts, `07.06.02`'s capture prompt cannot name them, and the platform fails to record the exact
fact it exists to record. So an entry may reference an **unclaimed identity** — a display name, optionally a
contact channel — which becomes claimable later via domain 01 (D-02).

This is a cold-start requirement, not a nicety: it lets the platform capture value before it has a network, and
it turns the resulting invitation into the growth loop (`07.03.02` DT-01). It also opens the platform's single
worst abuse surface, closed by D-03 and DT-02.

### 2. Attribution outlives membership

Removing someone from the roster revokes their access. It does not and cannot un-play their drum take. Access
and authorship are **orthogonal**, and conflating them would let a project owner erase a contributor by
removing them — precisely the abuse this platform exists to prevent. `02.01.01` D-08 states the same rule from
the credit side and names it a constraint on this domain: works are withdrawn, never deleted, and a delete
would let one Producer destroy four other people's evidence.

This is why removal is **two actions and never one** (D-13, DT-09).

### 3. Assigning a role is a credit claim

Per D-06/D-07, roster role and credit role are one vocabulary, so adding someone to the roster **is already a
soft credit claim** — an assertion by an identified asserter about a party's role on a work, which is exactly
what `02.01.01` defines a credit record to be. That is either the product's best idea or a false-credit
factory, and the difference is the attestation model:

| Act | What it produces | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Roster entry created | A **claim** — asserted, unattested | Zero in ranking, search, and reputation (D-03) |
| Named party confirms | **Counter-attestation** (`02.04.01`) | Raises the rung (`02.04.02`) |
| Named party disputes | A **contest** (`02.01.01` D-11, domain 24) | Marks; never suppresses |

Only the confirmed pair is a credit anyone should act on. `07.06.02` D-04 already depends on this: "Credits are
written as claims; only counter-attestation makes them verified."

### 4. The roster is a log, not a list

"The roster" is a **projection of a log of membership events at a point in time**, not a mutable set of rows
(D-12, DT-06). The current roster is the projection at *now*. This is not tidiness — the sibling vault's entire
honest claim rests on it: `07.03.03` D-05 promises "least privilege, evidence and traceability", and tracing a
leak means answering *who had stem access in March 2026*, which a mutable row set cannot answer at any price.
The surface pass's "last write wins per entry" was wrong in a way that would have quietly destroyed the
evidence trail the sibling feature sells.

### 5. Bands are members; band membership does not fan out

Where a role admits the party type (`02.06` D-21), a band or organization may hold a roster entry directly —
the horn section booked as a unit is one negotiation and one section credit (D-10). But `02.01.01` D-07 is
ratified and exact: **band credits and member credits are independent records; neither is derived from the
other.** Fan-out fabricates a credit for the member who missed the session; roll-up fabricates a band credit
for a two-member session.

The access half resolves by applying D-05 to entities (D-11):

| Fact | Tense | Source | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Band credit** | Past | Frozen to the contribution-date lineup (`02.01.01` D-07) | A member who leaves keeps the credit for the take they played |
| **Band access** | Present | Computed live from current membership (`01.03.01`) | A member who leaves loses the vault tomorrow; a member who joins after the session can hear it |

A new member hearing a track they did not play on is correct — bands share their own material. A departed
member keeping a credit they earned is correct — that is D-05. Both fall out of one rule.

### Roster write

`roster:write` is a **per-song, role-carried permission** (D-08). The song owner always holds it. The
`producer` role holds it by default because `07.03.03` DT-02 is confirmed: if adding a person is
administrative work, the producer emails a Dropbox link instead and the platform is out of the room, forfeiting
every downstream capture. No other role holds it by default; the owner may grant it per-song.

Three guards bound the power, none of which block:

1. **No privilege escalation** — a writer cannot create an entry whose derived access profile is wider than
   their own. A `mix engineer` granted `roster:write` cannot mint a collaborator with tracklist access.
2. **No orphaning** — the song owner's entry cannot be removed by anyone, and the last remaining
   `roster:write` holder cannot drop their own write permission.
3. **Every write is announced** (D-09) — to the named party, and to the existing roster. This is the answer to
   `meta/personas.md`'s named Producer anti-persona behaviour ("assigns themselves a larger split than agreed
   and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention"). The platform **surfaces, never judges**
   (domain D-05): it does not block a producer from claiming `writer`, because that claim is routinely true. It
   makes the claim impossible to push through unnoticed.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is running a tracking session for "Ghost Town"; opens the song's roster from the session record
   (`07.06.01`) — one tap, no navigation away from the session
2. Types "Sam Okafor"; system resolves against existing users first, then the producer's own collaborator
   history, then unclaimed identities already named on this project — Sam is an existing WeJammin user and
   resolves on the first tier
3. Producer types the role as they say it — "drums" — and `02.06`'s three-tier resolver returns
   `Performer · Drums` **with the match shown** (`02.06` D-18), so a wrong resolution is caught by the person
   who typed it. No DDEX code is ever displayed
4. System appends a membership event and shows the derived access in plain language *before* it commits:
   "Sam will hear the rough mixes of Ghost Town. Not the stems. Not the tracklist." Producer confirms
5. The entry lands. Sam's vault grant activates (parent CX-01). A claim is emitted into domain 02's graph,
   asserted-by Producer, contribution-date = this session, assertion-date = now — a gap of ~0, which
   `02.01.01` D-04 reads as the signature of capture rather than reconstruction
6. Sam is notified: "Producer added you to Ghost Town as Performer · Drums." Two affordances: **Confirm** or
   **This isn't right**. The existing roster is notified of the addition in the same fan-out, deduplicated to
   one notice per party
7. Sam confirms. The counter-attestation lands (`02.04.01`); the claim's rung rises (`02.04.02`); the credit
   is now a fact two independent humans agree on
8. At session close (`07.06.02`), Sam is **pre-filled** in the capture prompt — because the roster already knew
   he was there. One screen, one tap, four seconds (`07.06.02` D-06). Nobody filled in a form
9. Three months later a dispute asks who could reach the stems on the day of the leak. The membership log
   answers it exactly (D-12) — including the fact that Sam never could

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Contributor has no account** | Entry created against an unclaimed identity (display name, optional contact channel). Claimable later via domain 01. The claim is emitted and is fully creditable; **access grants are unavailable until claimed** — you cannot grant stems to an email address (`07.03.03` edge case) | "Sam Okafor (not on WeJammin) — invite them". The entry shows `access pending claim`, not an error |
| **Same person added twice under different roles** | Correct and expected — one person is routinely drummer *and* co-writer on one song. Two entries, two claims, two independently-derived tiers (`02.01.01` D-01). The multi-hyphenate is the platform's central persona; a one-role-per-person roster fails them on day one | Two lines against one identity, added in one act without re-navigating |
| **Same person added twice under the *same* role** | Deduplicated to one entry — uniqueness is (party, role, song), matching `02.01.01` D-03. The second add accretes evidence (a second session) to the existing fact rather than creating a rival one | "Sam is already on this as Performer · Drums" — and the second session is now attached to that entry |
| **Person is added to a role they did not perform** | Not preventable at write time, and should not be — the platform cannot know who played. Detectable at read: the named party is notified (D-09) and can dispute (domain 24, `02.05`). Until they confirm, the claim carries zero weight (D-03) | Notification with Confirm / This isn't right |
| **Producer adds four famous names to a fabricated session** | The reputation-manufacturing attack named in `meta/personas.md`. Three independent controls, none of which block the write: (a) unclaimed and unattested claims carry zero weight anywhere (D-03); (b) `02.04.02` D-08 — an attestation only counts if the attester is neither asserter nor credited party, so the fabricator cannot corroborate themselves; (c) the emitted claim is marked as arising from a session with **no independent attendee** (DT-04), which is the signal `02.01.01` D-10's publication gate needs | The names appear on the fabricator's own project. They do **not** publish to the named strangers' pages |
| **Contributor removed after playing — job ended** | "End involvement": access revoked immediately and fail-closed, including live streaming URLs and outstanding download tokens (parent CX-01, `07.03.03` D-04). **Attribution persists at full weight.** No dispute is raised — nothing was contested | Removed from the *active* roster; still present in credits, in session history, and in the membership log |
| **Contributor removed after playing — the add was a mistake** | "Retract claim": a different action entirely (D-13). If the claim is unattested, it retracts and the named party is notified. If the named party has **counter-attested**, retraction is not unilateral — it routes through `02.01.06` amendment / `02.04.03` retraction, because a producer must not be able to delete a fact two people agreed on | Retraction of an attested claim is refused as a roster action and offered as an amendment proposal |
| **Owner tries to remove a contributor to erase them** | Structurally impossible. "End involvement" leaves attribution intact by construction (D-05); "Retract claim" on an attested credit is not a roster power (D-13). Deleting the whole song does not help either — `02.01.01` D-08: works are withdrawn, never deleted, and credits survive a withdrawn work at full tier | Neither path presents itself as an erasure. There is no button that does this |
| **Ghost producer / uncredited session player — contractually invisible** | A real and common arrangement (DT-05). The entry exists (they need vault access, and they did the work); the **public rendering** of their credit is set Private by **them**, via `02.01.05` — never by the owner (D-14). The roster is not the venue for hiding people | The ghost sees a Private toggle on their own credit. The owner sees no control over anyone else's visibility |
| **Owner asks to hide a contributor from the public credits** | Refused. Suppression is the credited party's call only (D-14). Allowing owner-side suppression re-opens removal-as-erasure through a door D-05 does not watch | "Only Sam can make Sam's credit private" |
| **Band added as one entry (horn section)** | Permitted where the role admits an organization party type (`02.06` D-21). **One** section credit, frozen to the contribution-date lineup (D-10, `02.01.01` D-07). It does **not** create four member credits and it does **not** grant four vault logins. Members' access is computed live from the band's current membership (D-11) | "The Ghost Town Horns — Performer · Horns". Below it: "4 current members have access" |
| **A player leaves the band after the session** | Credit unchanged — frozen lineup, past tense. Vault access ends — live membership, present tense (D-11). This is D-05 applied to entities and it is the correct asymmetry | Ex-member keeps the credit on their profile; the vault closes |
| **A player joins the band after the session** | Gains vault access (current member). Gains **no credit** (not in the frozen lineup). Correct: bands share their own material; bands do not retroactively credit people who were not there | New member can hear it; no credit appears on their profile |
| **The band dissolves mid-project** (`01.04.04`) | Band-derived access follows the band entity's lifecycle. The band's credit is untouched forever — the record does not dissolve with the partnership | Access recomputes; credits do not move |
| **Individual is on the roster *and* their band is on the roster** | Both stand. Two independent facts: the band played the section; the individual played the solo. `02.01.01` D-07 forbids deriving either from the other, so there is nothing to deduplicate | Two lines. Access is the union — the individual's own grant plus their band-derived grant |
| **Role does not exist in the vocabulary** ("topliner", "beat doctor") | Captured as a requester-scoped pending alias (`02.06` D-03, D-12); the roster entry commits at full tier with the literal retained. **Blocking the person to protect a lookup table is exactly backwards** (D-17) | "We'll add that" — never a dead end. The entry shows the producer's literal |
| **Vocabulary is unreachable when the producer taps add** | The entry commits with its literal; role resolution is deferred, never abandoned (`02.06` D-19, D-17 here). The derived access grant cannot be computed from an unresolved role, so it **fails closed**: attribution lands, access waits | "Role saved as typed — we'll match it. Access starts once we do" |
| **Role changed after grants exist** (`session player` → `mix engineer`) | Grants recompute immediately (parent CX-01). An explicit per-song override that contradicts the new role is **surfaced, never silently discarded** (parent CX-01 synthesis Q1). A *downgrade* notifies the affected person: it is honest, and it is also how someone learns they were quietly demoted — which is a reason to send it, not to withhold it | Affected person: "Your access to Ghost Town changed: stems are no longer shared with you" |
| **Role changed after the credit was counter-attested** | The access half recomputes freely. The credit half does not: `02.04.02` D-13 — an amendment preserves attestations only if the new claim is **entailed** by the attested one. "Performer · Drums" → "Performer · Drums, Percussion" preserves (instruments accrete, `02.06` D-16). "Performer · Drums" → "Producer" does not — it invalidates and re-asks. This closes the tambourine → "produced by" laundering path at the roster, not only at the credit | "Sam confirmed 'drums'. Changing this to 'Producer' asks Sam again" |
| **Two people edit the roster during a live session** | Entries are independent facts, so concurrent *adds* do not conflict and both land. Concurrent writes to the **same** (party, role, song) serialize; the second writer sees the first's result rather than a stale view (matching `02.06`'s concurrent-promotion rule). The membership log records both events with both asserters — nothing is lost to a last-write-wins overwrite (D-12) | Both additions land. A collision shows "Someone just changed this" rather than silently discarding one |
| **Producer assigns themselves `producer` **and** a large writing role** | Permitted — often simply true, and blocking it would make the platform adjudicate authorship, which domain D-05 forbids. The write is announced to every other writer on the song (D-09); the writing claim carries zero weight until a co-writer counter-attests; and `02.01.01` D-05 records that asserter == party, so `02.04.02` can tell a self-claim from a trust-broker attestation even though both arrived through a session | Other writers: "Producer added themselves as a writer on Ghost Town" — with Confirm / This isn't right |
| **Session player tries to add someone** | Refused — no `roster:write` on that role (D-08). Parent CX-02 names the risk exactly: a session player must not be able to invite the world into a confidential project | The add control is **absent**, not an error. "Ask the song owner or producer to add someone" |
| **Producer with `roster:write` tries to grant tracklist access** | Refused — no privilege escalation beyond the writer's own profile (D-08). Not a policy check bolted on; the roles a writer can assign are bounded by the roles they hold | Wider roles are not offered in the picker |
| **Producer removed from the song, then tries to write** | `roster:write` is derived from a live roster entry, so it dies with the entry. Any in-flight write fails closed | "Your access to this song has been removed" |
| **Sole `roster:write` holder tries to remove themselves** | Refused for the owner (never removable); for a granted holder, permitted only while the owner's entry exists — the owner is always a writer, so the roster can never be orphaned | "You'd be the last person who can manage this roster" — only if the owner's entry were somehow absent |
| **Personnel are confidential** (unannounced label project) | Personnel visibility follows the song's confidentiality setting (D-16). Restricted rosters show a contributor **their own entry plus the owner**, and the remainder **counted and explained** — never silently filtered, matching `07.03.03` D-06 | "3 further contributors aren't shown on this song" — the absence is stated, not implied by a short list |
| **Restricted personnel hide a false claim from the person best placed to catch it** | The real cost of D-16, stated rather than papered over. Someone falsely claiming a role beside you is discoverable only if you can see them. Restriction is therefore **off by default** and is a deliberate owner choice on confidential songs, not a platform default | Owners enabling it see the trade-off named in the control, not buried in a help page |
| **Unclaimed identity demands erasure** (GDPR/CCPA) | A name and email are being processed about a data subject who never signed up. The **contact channel** is erasable independently of the attribution (DT-07). Whether the *attribution itself* must go is a live legal question the product cannot settle by design — and it collides with `02.01.01` D-08's joint-fact rule, since the credit is also four other people's evidence | Q-01. The contact channel deletes on request today; the attribution question escalates |
| **Email typo puts an unclaimed identity's contact on a stranger** | The entry is a claim about a *name*; the contact channel is a routing hint, not the identity. A wrong channel misroutes an invitation (`07.03.02`'s typo edge case owns that leak surface) but does not corrupt the roster fact. Correcting the channel is not an amendment to the claim | Owner edits the contact channel without touching the credit or its attestations |
| **Abusive or PII-bearing literal in a role or display name** | The retained literal is UGC rendered on fan-facing pages (`02.06` DT-10). Bounded at entry (≤64 characters, one line, no URLs, no markup) and screened before public render by domain 24's screening cross-cut. Screening failure removes **the string, never the entry** — the fact was never the problem | Roster members see the literal immediately; the public sees it post-screening |
| **70-piece orchestral date; 40-voice choir** | The mechanisms compose: the section is an entity (D-10), and the contractor's list is an import (`02.03.01`), which never fuzzy-resolves roles (`02.06` D-18). What is genuinely absent is a bulk ingest path *here* — and per DT-10 that is correctly absent, because designing the roster around a 70-line date would cost the 4-person session that is the median | Sections added as entities; a 70-line list is an import, not 70 taps |
| **Song has no composition bound, and a `writer` role is assigned** | The canonical role determines the work-type binding (`02.06` D-09, `02.01.01` D-06 — which a credit binds to is never optional). A composition-binding role on a song with no composition cannot emit a valid claim. The **roster entry and its access grant still land**; only the credit emission waits | "Writer credits need the composition — this song only has a recording so far". Ties to domain Q-04 |
| **Song is withdrawn / project abandoned** | Roster and its claims survive at full tier (`02.01.01` D-08). The abandoned project is the **high-value** case, not the disposable one — the session happened, and deleting a project cannot un-happen it | Roster remains readable to its members; access grants follow the vault's own rules |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Roster opened | Row skeletons. The **add field is typeable immediately** — the literal is captured regardless of whether the vocabulary has loaded (D-17), so a slow lookup never blocks a keystroke mid-session |
| **Empty** | New song, no contributors yet | Just the creator, with a single "add contributors" CTA. Never a bare list — an empty roster is the state that costs the platform every downstream capture, so it is the one state that asks for something |
| **Populated** | Entries exist | Party, role (plain label, plus the asserter's literal where it differs), instruments on one line, derived access indicator, claimed/unclaimed status, and confirmation state per entry |
| **Partial** | Personnel restricted by D-16, **or** some entries' roles are still unresolved pending aliases | Visible entries plus a stated count of what is hidden and why — never a silent filter (`07.03.03` D-06). Unresolved roles render as the literal with "we'll match it", not as a gap |
| **Pending** | Invitation sent, not accepted (parent CX-02) | Entry shown as pending — **visible and creditable**, without active access. Deliberately **dignified, not deficient** (`07.06.02` D-05): early on, nearly every entry is unattested, and it must not look broken |
| **Awaiting confirmation** | Entry created; the named party has not yet confirmed or disputed | The claim's evidence stated as a sentence, not a badge (`02.04.02` D-02, D-04): "Added by Producer at the session, 14 March. Not yet confirmed by Sam" |
| **Confirmed** | Named party counter-attested (`02.04.01`) | The rung rises; the sentence changes to describe the evidence, never to conclude ("Confirmed by Sam, same day") |
| **Disputed** | Named party pressed "This isn't right" | Entry **marked, never suppressed** (`02.01.01` D-11). Stays on the roster at zero derived weight; routes to domain 24. Suppression-on-filing would make a dispute a censorship tool |
| **Access pending claim** | Entry references an unclaimed identity | Creditable now; access waits for the claim (D-02). Explained, not shown as an error |
| **Ended** | Involvement ended (D-13) | Off the active roster; present in credits, session history, and the membership log at full weight |
| **Error** | Roster fetch failed | Error with retry. Distinguished from Empty — a failed fetch that renders as "no contributors yet" would invite a producer to re-add people who are already there |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The roster must support **non-users and unclaimed identities**, or the provenance thesis fails at its most common case | The session drummer with no account is not an edge case — it is the median case for the next several years, since the platform starts with near-zero network. If capture requires everyone present to be a user, then early sessions capture nothing, and the product's core loop never starts. The record must be creatable *about* someone before they arrive. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives unclaimed identities (D-02) and the `pending` state being creditable. A cold-start requirement, not a nicety: it lets the platform capture value before it has the network, and gives it a growth loop (the claim invitation is a *credit* someone wants — `07.03.02` DT-01). |
| DT-02 | Unclaimed identities are safe, since the person can claim and correct later | Optimistic reading of DT-01 | ❌ **REJECTED** — a serious abuse surface that DT-01 itself creates. A user can populate a roster with famous names to manufacture reputation, which `meta/personas.md` names explicitly as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour. An unclaimed credit must be **visibly unverified everywhere it appears** and carry zero weight in any ranking, search or reputation surface until claimed and counter-attested (D-03). The claim flow is the trust boundary; the platform must never render an unclaimed credit as though it were confirmed. |
| DT-03 | Roster roles and credit roles (domain 02) should be **one vocabulary** | Both describe "what this person did on this song". Two vocabularies means a mapping table, and mapping tables drift — the roster says `session player · drums` and the credit says `drums`, and eventually they disagree. | ✅ **CONFIRMED — and resolved this pass (D-06, D-07), closing Q-01.** `02.06` is now `[DEEP]` and settles ownership: D-05 there gives domain 02 the vocabulary; D-14 there says consumers **map onto it and never extend it**; D-15 there keeps *rights outcomes* out of the taxonomy and in domain 10's own mapping. The exact parallel resolves this file: **the roster consumes `02.06`'s canonical vocabulary and domain 07 holds the role → access-profile mapping.** The consequence the sweep feared is real — roster addition *is* a soft credit claim — but it is not a false-credit factory, because a claim is not a credit: the attestation model (`02.04`) is what separates them, and `07.06.02` D-04 already depends on that separation. |
| DT-04 | Fabricating credits still requires getting past domain 02's publication gate, so the roster's abuse surface is contained | `02.01.01` D-10 — a self-asserted credit naming another party does not publish to that party's page; it queues for acknowledgement | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP — found at the 07/02 intersection, visible from neither file alone.** D-10's gate is "deliberately narrow — **session capture and external import both pass**", and that exemption is sound *only because a real session has real witnesses*. Nothing stops one person creating a session with zero other real humans, rostering four famous names, and having every fabricated credit ride the session-capture exemption straight onto strangers' public pages. The gate is checking the **shape** of the assertion when it means to check the **independence** of it. Domain 02 owns the gate, but **07 owns the input**: a claim emitted from this roster must carry whether the session had any attendee independent of the asserter (`07.06.01` D-04's coarse attendance is exactly enough), so the gate can tell a witnessed session from a solo assertion wearing a session's clothes. Escalated as Q-02 — 02's rule is theirs to change. |
| DT-05 | Everyone on the roster wants to be credited | The unexamined premise of the whole feature, and of the capture prompt downstream | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the domain expert's first question.** Music runs on uncredited work: ghost producers, ghostwriters, session players brought in for a day, mix doctors. The arrangement is often *contractually* invisible — being absent from the credit is the deal. Those people still played, and they still need the stems. A roster that equates "on the roster" with "will be publicly credited" forces every ghost arrangement off-platform — the producer emails the file, and the platform loses the whole session, including the credits of everyone who *did* want them. So membership and public rendering must be separable. **But the separation is dangerous in one direction only**: if the *owner* may suppress a contributor's credit, removal-as-erasure returns through a door D-05 never watched. The resolution needs no new mechanism — `02.01.05` already lets a party set their own credit Private without touching the work's ledger (`02.01.01` D-11). So: **the entry always exists; the public rendering is the credited party's own call, never the owner's** (D-14). A ghost achieves the contractual outcome by their own hand, and the platform never becomes a tool for hiding someone who did not choose it. |
| DT-06 | The roster is current state — a list of who is on the song now | The surface pass's own model, expressed in its "last write wins per entry" | ❌ **REJECTED — and the rejection is load-bearing for a sibling feature's central promise.** `07.03.03` D-05 commits the platform to "least privilege, **evidence and traceability**" rather than prevention — an honest claim, and the only one it can make. But traceability means answering *who had stem access on the day of the leak*, in 2029, about March 2026. A mutable row set cannot answer that at any price: the roster **is** the access policy (CX-01), so a roster with no history makes the vault's audit trail a record of grants whose justification has been overwritten. Last-write-wins would also silently destroy the concurrent-edit case the surface pass thought it was solving. So the roster is a **log of membership events**, and "the roster" is its projection at a time (D-12). The tell that this was missed: the file that promises the evidence trail and the file that owns the evidence are different files. |
| DT-07 | Attribution permanence (D-05) is a product decision the platform can simply make | The surface pass's framing, and the framing of every file that cites D-05 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED, with a limit the product cannot design around.** An unclaimed identity is a name and an email **processed about a data subject who never signed up, never consented, and may not want to exist here.** `constraints.md` still carries GDPR/CCPA as an unresolved compliance constraint and DSAR/deletion as a live obligation. D-05 says attribution persists; an erasure request says it does not. Two things make this genuinely hard rather than merely unresolved: (a) the credit is not only *their* data — it is four other contributors' evidence, which is `02.01.01` D-08's joint-fact problem arriving through a statutory door; (b) the platform *created* the record about them, which is a materially worse position than hosting one they wrote. What **is** decidable and is decided: the **contact channel is erasable independently of the attribution** — it is a routing hint, not the fact. The rest is a legal question, escalated as Q-01, and it is the one place in this file where a confident product answer would be a fabrication. |
| DT-08 | The roster should show each contributor's split — it is the natural place for it, and the people are already listed | The strongest false step available here: the roster has the parties, the roles, and the producer's attention, all in one screen at the exact moment splits get agreed | ❌ **REJECTED, and the reason is the domain's founding distinction.** `02.01.01` D-02 is absolute: a credit asserts contribution only, never ownership, never percentage — "enforced at the schema, not by convention: the record has no field a split could hide in." A split field on the roster would put an ownership assertion on the one surface where a **producer has unilateral write access** (D-08 here), and `meta/personas.md` names that producer's worst intentional behaviour as "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention." That is not a hypothetical abuse of the feature — it is a description of the feature, if built. Splits live in domain 09, require signature, and are a different act with a different consent model. The roster **seeds** the split's party list; it must never state one. |
| DT-09 | Removing someone from the roster is one action | Every roster UI ever built, and the surface pass's own single "removed after playing" row | ❌ **REJECTED — the single-button UI is a trap, and it fails in whichever direction it is built.** "Remove Sam" means one of two unrelated things: **(a)** Sam's job ended — revoke access, keep the credit, nothing is contested; the routine weekly case. **(b)** Sam was never on this — a typo, a wrong Sam, a mistaken add; the claim itself is wrong and should not persist. One button forces a choice with no good side: implement it as (a) and mistaken adds become permanent false claims about real people that the asserter cannot withdraw; implement it as (b) and the routine end-of-job becomes a silent erasure tool, defeating D-05 through a control labelled as an access action. So: **two actions** (D-13) — *End involvement* (access dies, attribution persists, no dispute) and *Retract claim* (the assertion was wrong). And retraction has a hard edge: once the named party has **counter-attested**, retraction is not the asserter's to make — a producer must not be able to delete a fact two independent people agreed on, so it routes to `02.01.06` amendment / `02.04.03` retraction. The failure nobody designs for is that the destructive action and the routine one share a verb. |
| DT-10 | The roster is small — four to six people | Unstated everywhere, which is why it is worth stating: every example in every file in this sub-domain has ≤5 names | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as an assumption, and mostly resolved by composition rather than by new work.** A choir is 40 voices; an orchestral date is 70; an all-star track carries 25 names — and these are precisely the sessions with the most credits at stake and the deepest metadata gap, so losing them is not a rounding error. But the mechanisms already exist: the section is an **entity** (D-10), and a contractor's 70-line list is an **import** (`02.03.01`), which never fuzzy-resolves (`02.06` D-18). What is genuinely absent is a bulk-add path in this feature, and it should stay absent: designing the roster around a 70-piece date would cost the 4-person session that is the median, and `meta/personas.md`'s Producer constraint is measured in seconds of attention. Recorded as a known ceiling with a named route (entity + import), not as a gap. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Roster is **per-song**, not per-project | Sub-domain D-01 — a session player on track 4 is not on the album, and must not see the tracklist. |
| D-02 | Roster entries may reference **unclaimed, non-user identities** (display name + optional contact channel) | Per DT-01 — the cold-start requirement. The median contributor has no account. Access waits for the claim; attribution does not. |
| D-03 | Unclaimed and unattested claims are **visibly unverified** and carry zero weight in ranking, search, or reputation until claimed and counter-attested | Per DT-02 — DT-01's abuse surface, closed. Non-negotiable: rendering an unattested claim as confirmed would corrupt the graph that is the platform's entire moat. |
| D-04 | One party may hold **multiple roles** on one song — two entries, two claims, two independent tiers | The multi-hyphenate is the central persona (`meta/personas.md`). Matches `02.01.01` D-01. |
| D-05 | **Attribution persists after removal from the roster; access does not** | Access and authorship are orthogonal. Otherwise removal becomes an erasure tool — the exact abuse the platform exists to prevent. `02.01.01` D-08 states the same rule from the credit side and names it a constraint on this domain. |
| D-06 | The roster **consumes `02.06`'s canonical role vocabulary**; it does not own, extend, or mirror it. **Domain 07 owns the role → access-profile mapping** | **Resolves Q-01 (half 1)**, and resolves parent Q-01. Exactly parallel to `02.06` D-15: the taxonomy carries no rights outcome because domain 10 owns that mapping; it carries no access outcome because domain 07 owns this one. Two vocabularies would drift (DT-03); an extended vocabulary would sprawl (`02.06` D-14). |
| D-07 | **A roster role assignment is a credit claim**, emitted into domain 02's graph — not a parallel record kept in step by a mapping table | **Resolves Q-01 (half 2)**, per DT-03. The feared consequence is real and is the point: capture happens by describing reality, exactly as the thesis demands. It is not a false-credit factory because a claim is not a credit — `02.04`'s attestation model is the separation, and `07.06.02` D-04 already relies on it. |
| D-08 | **`roster:write` is a per-song, role-carried permission.** Owner always; `producer` by default; every other role only by the owner's explicit per-song grant. Bounded by: no privilege escalation beyond the writer's own access profile, no removal of the owner, no orphaning | **Resolves Q-02.** Owner-only fails `07.03.03` DT-02 — a producer who cannot add mid-session emails a Dropbox link and the platform leaves the room, forfeiting every downstream capture. Any-member fails parent CX-02 — a session player must not be able to invite the world into a confidential project. |
| D-09 | **Every roster write is announced** — to the named party, and to the existing roster. Surface and show; never judge. |
| D-10 | **Bands and organizations may hold roster entries** where the role admits the party type; **band membership never fans out to member entries or member credits** | **Resolves Q-03 / parent Q-03**, per `02.01.01` D-07 (ratified) and `02.06` D-21. The horn section is one negotiation and one section credit. Fan-out fabricates a credit for the member who missed the session; roll-up fabricates a band credit for a two-member session. |
| D-11 | **Band-derived access is computed live from current membership; the band's credit is frozen to the contribution-date lineup** | Q-03's access half. D-05 applied to entities: access is present tense, attribution is past tense. A departing member loses the vault and keeps the credit; a joining member gains the vault and earns no credit. Both are correct, and both fall out of one existing rule. |
| D-12 | **The roster is a log of membership events; "the roster" is its projection at a point in time** | Per DT-06. Not tidiness — `07.03.03` D-05 sells traceability rather than prevention, and tracing a leak means knowing who had access *then*. A mutable row set cannot answer that. Also removes the last-write-wins data loss the surface pass had accepted. |
| D-13 | **Removal is two actions, never one**: *End involvement* (access dies, attribution persists, nothing contested) and *Retract claim* (the assertion was wrong). Retracting a **counter-attested** claim is not a roster power — it routes to `02.01.06` / `02.04.03` | Per DT-09. One button fails in whichever direction it is built: it either makes mistaken adds permanent, or it makes the routine end-of-job a silent erasure tool wearing an access label. |
| D-14 | **Public credit suppression is the credited party's own call** (`02.01.05` Private), **never the owner's** | Per DT-05. Ghost arrangements are real and must not be forced off-platform, but owner-side suppression re-opens removal-as-erasure through a door D-05 does not watch. The party achieves the contractual outcome by their own hand; the platform never becomes a tool for hiding someone who did not choose it. |
| D-15 | The roster carries **no split percentage and no ownership field** | Per DT-08 and `02.01.01` D-02 ("the record has no field a split could hide in"). A split on a producer-writable surface is a purpose-built tool for `meta/personas.md`'s named Producer abuse. The roster seeds domain 09's party list; it never states a split. |
| D-16 | **Personnel visibility follows the song's confidentiality setting**, default **visible to all roster members**. Restricted rosters **count and explain** what is hidden — never a silent filter | Matches `07.03.03` D-06 (denied access is always explained; a 404 to someone who played on the song is hostile). Default-visible because seeing who is beside you is how a false claim gets caught — restricting it has a real cost, and the control must name it. |
| D-17 | The roster **never blocks on the vocabulary.** An unknown role becomes a pending alias and the entry commits; an unreachable vocabulary commits the literal and defers resolution — but the **derived access grant fails closed** until the role resolves | Generalises `02.06` D-03/D-19. Blocking the person to protect a lookup table inverts their importance. The asymmetry is deliberate: attribution failing *open* loses nothing; access failing *open* is a leak the product caused (`07.03.03` D-04). |
