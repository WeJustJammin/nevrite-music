# Feature: Contributor Roster & Per-Song Role Assignment

> **Number**: 07.03.01
> **Parent**: [Contributors, Access & Confidentiality](./07.03-contributors-access-confidentiality-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The list of humans (and bands) attached to a song, each with a role: who played what, who wrote, who produced,
who mixed, who mastered. It is the domain's most load-bearing small feature — it is simultaneously the access
policy (parent CX-01), the pre-fill source for every credit prompt (`07.06.02`), and the addressable subject
list that domain 02's credit graph is built from. **A blank roster makes every downstream capture a blank form,
and blank forms get dismissed.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees the roster of any song they are on, including their own role. As song owner, adds people, assigns and changes roles, removes people. As a contributor, sees who else is on it — which is how they discover they are in good company, or that someone they never worked with is claiming a role. |
| Producer | Full | The highest-volume operator of the roster: they run the room, so they know who is in it. Adds people mid-session. Their assignments carry extra weight downstream because a producer's attestation is worth more than a self-claim (`meta/personas.md`) — which is exactly why their write access here is a trust-critical surface. |
| Operator | None | A studio operator knows a session is booked in their room (domain 16) and may know how many humans are coming for capacity reasons, but the *roster* — who they are, what they played, what they will be credited for — is the client's confidential business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A fan sees credits after release (domain 02/20) as a published render, never this working roster. |

## Behavior

A roster entry is `(song, person-or-band, role, added-by, added-at)`. Roles come from a music vocabulary
(`writer`, `producer`, `session player + instrument`, `engineer`, `mix engineer`, `mastering engineer`,
`featured artist`) and each carries a default access profile that the vault derives grants from (parent CX-01).

Two properties make this feature harder than it looks:

**1. The roster must address people who are not users.** The session drummer who played on Tuesday may have no
WeJammin account and may never get one. If the roster can only hold accounts, the capture prompt at
`07.06.02` cannot name them, and the platform fails to record the exact fact it exists to record. So an entry
may reference an unclaimed identity (name + email, or just a name), which becomes claimable later — a
real-world need that carries a real abuse surface (see DT-02).

**2. Attribution outlives membership.** Removing someone from the roster revokes their access. It does not and
cannot un-play their drum take. Access and authorship are orthogonal, and conflating them would let a project
owner erase a contributor by removing them — precisely the abuse this platform exists to prevent.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is running a tracking session; opens the song's roster
2. Adds "Sam Okafor" — already a WeJammin user, found by search — with role `session player · drums`
3. System creates the entry, derives Sam's vault access (rough mixes of this song; not the stems, not the tracklist), and notifies Sam
4. Sam sees "Producer added you to Ghost Town as session drummer" and can confirm or dispute the role
5. At `07.06.02` (session close), Sam is pre-filled in the credit prompt — because the roster already knew he was there

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Contributor has no account | Entry created against an unclaimed identity (name, optionally email). Claimable later via domain 01. Access grants are unavailable until claimed — you cannot grant stems to an email address. | "Sam Okafor (not on WeJammin) — invite them" |
| Same person added twice under different roles | Allowed and correct — one person is routinely drummer *and* co-writer on one song. The multi-hyphenate is the platform's central persona; a one-role-per-person roster fails them immediately. | Two entries, both shown against one identity |
| Someone is added to a role they did not perform | Not preventable at write time. Detectable at read: the person is notified and can dispute (domain 24). | Notification with confirm/dispute |
| Contributor removed after playing | Access revoked immediately (parent CX-01). Attribution persists. | Removed from the active roster; still present in credits and session history |
| A band added as one entry (horn section) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — parent Q-03. A section is one negotiation and four credits. | — |
| Roster edited during a live session by two people | Last write wins per entry; entries are independent so concurrent adds do not conflict | Both additions land |
| Producer assigns themselves `producer` **and** a large writing role | Permitted — often true. Flagged to the other writers rather than blocked. | Writers notified: "Producer added themselves as a writer on this song" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Roster opened | Row skeletons |
| Empty | New song | Just the creator, with an "add contributors" CTA |
| Populated | Entries exist | People, roles, instruments, access indicator, claimed/unclaimed status |
| Pending | Invitation sent, not accepted (parent CX-02) | Entry shown as pending — **visible and creditable**, but without active access |
| Disputed | Contributor disputed their role | Entry flagged; routes to domain 24 |
| Error | Fetch failed | Error with retry |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The roster must support **non-users and unclaimed identities**, or the provenance thesis fails at its most common case | The session drummer with no account is not an edge case — it is the median case for the next several years, since the platform starts with near-zero network. If capture requires everyone present to be a user, then early sessions capture nothing, and the product's core loop never starts. The record must be creatable *about* someone before they arrive. | ✅ CONFIRMED — drives unclaimed identities and the `pending` state being creditable. This is a cold-start requirement, not a nicety: it lets the platform capture value before it has the network, and gives it a growth loop (the claim invitation is a *credit* someone wants). |
| DT-02 | Unclaimed identities are safe, since the person can claim and correct later | Optimistic reading of DT-01 | ❌ **REJECTED** — this is a serious abuse surface and DT-01 creates it. A user can populate a roster with famous names to manufacture reputation, which `meta/personas.md` names explicitly as the Musician's worst intentional behavior ("games the verified-credit graph to manufacture reputation"). An unclaimed credit must therefore be **visibly unverified everywhere it appears** and must carry zero weight in any ranking, search or reputation surface until claimed and counter-attested. The claim flow is the trust boundary, and the platform must never render an unclaimed credit as though it were confirmed. |
| DT-03 | Roster roles and credit roles (domain 02) should be **one vocabulary** | Both describe "what this person did on this song". Two vocabularies means a mapping table, and mapping tables drift — the roster says `session player · drums` and the credit says `drums`, and eventually they disagree. | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH A CAVEAT** — one vocabulary is right, but it has a consequence the sweep did not see: if they are the same vocabulary, then **adding someone to the roster is already a soft credit claim**. That is either the product's best idea (capture happens by describing reality, exactly as the thesis demands) or a false-credit factory (per DT-02). The resolution is the attestation model: roster entry = *claim*; contributor confirmation = *counter-attestation*; only the confirmed pair is a credit. Escalated as parent Q-01 — it is a domain 02 boundary decision. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Roster is **per-song**, not per-project | Sub-domain D-01 — a session player on track 4 is not on the album, and must not see the tracklist. |
| D-02 | Roster entries may reference **unclaimed, non-user identities** | Per DT-01 — the cold-start requirement. The median contributor has no account. |
| D-03 | Unclaimed credits are **visibly unverified** and carry zero reputational weight until claimed and counter-attested | Per DT-02 — DT-01's abuse surface, closed. Non-negotiable: rendering an unclaimed credit as confirmed would corrupt the graph that is the platform's entire moat. |
| D-04 | One person may hold **multiple roles** on one song | The multi-hyphenate is the central persona. |
| D-05 | **Attribution persists after removal from the roster**; access does not | Access and authorship are orthogonal. Otherwise removal becomes an erasure tool. |
