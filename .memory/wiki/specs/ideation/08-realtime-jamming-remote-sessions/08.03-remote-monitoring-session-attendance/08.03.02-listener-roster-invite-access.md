# Feature: Listener Roster, Invite Links & Access Windows

> **Number**: 08.03.02
> **Parent**: [Remote Monitoring & Session Attendance](./08.03-remote-monitoring-session-attendance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Controls who hears the room. The asset on the wire is unreleased music, and the realistic threat is not
a stranger guessing a link — it is an insider whose access should have ended: the label rep after the
pitch, the engineer after the job, the manager after the falling-out. This feature owns the roster, the
invite links, and the windows that make access expire by default instead of by memory.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Producer | Full | Owns the roster. Invites listeners by identity or link, sets access windows, revokes mid-stream, and sees who is listening right now and who listened historically. This is their client relationship and their liability, so it is their control surface. |
| Musician | Read-only | Sees who is on the roster and who is currently listening to them play. Cannot add or remove. Per `meta/personas.md`, this persona's structural fear is losing control of their own work — knowing the audience for their performance is not a courtesy, it is the minimum. Whether they can *object* is Q-02. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees the roster for sessions running in their room, for duty-of-care reasons — they are responsible for what happens on their premises. Cannot alter it; it is not their session. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Rejected deliberately (parent D-04): fan listening is a Fanbase (20) product. Admitting fans would put an unreleased master in front of a moderation population (per D-13) inside a professional room. |

## Behavior

The design principle: **access expires by default.** Every alternative relies on someone remembering to
revoke, and the personas file records the Producer's worst accidental behaviour as exactly this class
of omission — closing a project without doing the admin. A roster that requires diligence will fail in
the same way split sheets fail, and for the same reason.

Access grant types:

| Type | Use | Expiry |
|------|-----|--------|
| Identity | A named platform user | Window or session-bound |
| Link | Someone not on the platform (a label A&R who will not sign up for a 40-minute listen) | **Always** windowed; never open-ended |

**Revocation must cut an in-flight stream** (parent CX-01). Revocation that applies only at reconnect is
not revocation, because the listener you want gone is already connected.

**Presence is not optional information.** If someone is hearing a performance, the performer sees it.
There is no legitimate reason to listen to a musician play without their knowledge, and the one case
that argues otherwise — a label listening anonymously to avoid pressure — is a request to deceive the
performer about their audience, which is the Producer's to negotiate openly rather than the platform's
to enable silently.

### Happy Path

1. Producer invites a listener — by identity, or generates a link.
2. Producer sets a window (session-bound by default; explicit expiry for links).
3. Listener joins; roster shows them as present; Musicians in the room see the audience change.
4. Stream is gated on the roster continuously, not on a grant cached at connect time.
5. Window closes or session ends; access ends automatically. No cleanup step for anyone to forget.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Invite link forwarded to someone else | The link's real failure mode — links are bearer tokens and people forward them. Mitigations (single-use, device binding, identity challenge) are `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]`. | `[PENDING]` |
| Producer revokes mid-stream | Stream cuts for that listener immediately. The listener is told their access ended — not left with an ambiguous fault. | "Your access to this session has ended." |
| Access window expires mid-take | Same as revocation. Must not silently extend to a take boundary; a window that stretches is not a window. | "Your access has expired." |
| Musician objects to a listener | Not currently modelled. Genuinely open — the Producer owns the roster, but the Musician's performance is the asset. See Q-02. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Roster service unavailable mid-stream | **Fails closed** — the stream stops. Cannot verify authorisation, cannot send unreleased audio. Deliberately the opposite of 08.02.03's fail-open posture. | "Verification failed — stream stopped." |
| Listener is on the roster of a session that moved rooms | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Historical listener list requested during a dispute | The roster is a record of who heard an unreleased work and when — directly useful to domain 24. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Nobody invited | "Nobody is listening" + invite CTA (Producer only). |
| Invited | Listeners invited, not connected | Roster with pending listeners and their windows. |
| Active | Listeners connected | Live presence, per-listener join time and window remaining. |
| Expiring | Window closing soon | Warning to Producer and listener before the cut, not at it. |
| Revoked | Access ended | Listener sees the ending explicitly; roster shows them as removed. |
| Error | Roster unavailable | Fails closed. Stream stops with an explicit reason. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Access must expire by default**, because a roster requiring manual revocation will fail exactly the way split sheets fail — through the Producer's documented worst accidental behaviour (closing things without doing the admin). The design must make the lazy path the correct path. | `meta/personas.md`, Producer anti-persona: "closes a project without capturing splits ('we'll do it later') — the design must make the lazy path the correct path." The same human, the same failure, a different artifact. | ✅ CONFIRMED — windows are mandatory, not optional. This is the persona file's central design instruction applied to access control, and it is why "expires by default" beats "revoke when done" even though the latter is more flexible. |
| DT-02 | Presence must be **visible to the performer**. Anonymous listening is a request to deceive someone about who is hearing them play. | Reasoning: the one argued case (a label listening without applying pressure) is a social negotiation the Producer can have openly. Building silent listening into the platform makes the platform complicit in it by default, and the Musician persona's core fear is precisely losing control of their work. | ✅ CONFIRMED — no anonymous listening. Accepted cost: some A&R will dislike it, and the owner may overrule this on commercial grounds. Flagged as a decision, not a fact. |
| DT-03 | Invite links are unsafe and only identity-based access should exist. | Adversarial: links are bearer tokens; bearer tokens leak. | ❌ REJECTED — as a *product* call, though the security concern is real. Requiring a label A&R to create an account for a 40-minute listen kills the feature's most valuable use case, and they will simply refuse and ask for a WeTransfer instead, which is strictly worse for everyone including the rights holder. The mitigation is hard windows + single-use + audit trail, not abolition. Routed to `/create-prd-security` (Q-01). This is a case where the secure answer and the correct answer differ, and pretending otherwise just moves the leak off-platform. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Access expires by default; windows are mandatory | The lazy path must be the correct path. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Revocation cuts in-flight streams, not just future connections | The listener you want gone is already connected. |
| D-03 | No anonymous listening — performers always see their audience | See DT-02. Owner may overrule on commercial grounds; recorded as a decision so the override is deliberate. |
| D-04 | Invite links survive despite being bearer tokens | Killing them moves the leak off-platform to WeTransfer, which is worse. Mitigate, don't abolish. See DT-03. |
| D-05 | Fails closed — cannot verify means cannot stream | Opposite posture to 08.02.03 (fail-open), and correctly so: here a false allow leaks a master. |
