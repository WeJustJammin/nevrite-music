# Feature: Monitoring Trust & Playback Context

> **Number**: 08.03.04

> **Status**: [SURFACE]

> owner's directives sources it. Nobody in the category ships it. That is either whitespace or evidence

## Overview

Makes the listener's playback chain visible, so a Producer knows what a client's opinion is actually
worth. "The bass is too loud" from someone on laptop speakers — which reproduce almost none of the bass
— is information about the speakers, not the bass. The whole monitoring product guarantees fidelity up
to the listener's device and then goes blind for the last three feet, which is where most of the damage
to a mix decision happens.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Declares their own playback chain when they are the listener — interface, monitors or headphones, room. Sees when their own setup is limiting what they can honestly judge. This is the persona most likely to *want* this, because they know what a monitoring chain is and have opinions about it. |
| Producer | Read-only | Sees each listener's declared or detected playback context alongside their notes. Uses it to weight feedback — not to dismiss it, to interpret it. Cannot edit what a listener declared; that would be marking your client's homework. |
| Operator | Config | Declares a room's playback chain when their room is the *listening* end — a studio's mastering suite is a trustworthy listening context and that is a saleable fact. Per `meta/personas.md`, their asset is the room's real specs, and this is one of them. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into the monitor stream (parent D-04). |

## Behavior

`[PARTIAL]` — this feature's shape depends on an unresolved product judgment (parent Q-01): is telling a
client their monitoring is inadequate a service or an insult?

What is confidently known:

**Declaration is cheap and honest; detection is unreliable and creepy.** A listener saying "I'm on
Yamaha HS8s in a treated room" versus "I'm on my laptop in a hotel" is one control and is information
the Producer does not otherwise have. Trying to *detect* the playback chain via device fingerprinting
or audio probing is both technically unreliable (the OS knows there is a Bluetooth sink; it does not
know it is a £30 earbud in a noisy café) and a surveillance surface nobody asked for.

**The output is weighting, not gating.** The client's right to listen badly is not ours to remove
(parent CX rejected pair R-01). The Producer's need to *know* is what this serves.

**The honest version is modest**: a self-declared context, a lightweight warning when a note's category
and the declared chain are in obvious tension ("low-end note from a listener on laptop speakers"), and
nothing more clever than that.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: whether any detection is acceptable; how the tension
warning is worded so it informs the Producer without insulting the client; whether the listener sees
their own context assessment.

### Happy Path

1. Listener joins a monitor stream and declares their playback context (or skips — it is optional).
2. Producer sees each listener's declared context in the roster view.
3. Listener leaves a note (08.03.03).
4. If the note's category and the declared context are in obvious tension, the Producer sees a quiet flag next to the note — not a verdict, a caveat.
5. Producer interprets accordingly.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Listener declines to declare | Fine. Context is unknown, not assumed. An assumed context is worse than none because it is confidently wrong. | Note shows "playback context not declared". |
| Listener declares inaccurately (aspirationally or by mistake) | Unverifiable and it is a real limit on the feature's value. A client who says "monitors" while on a phone in a car has broken it and we cannot tell. | Nothing — this is an accepted limit. |
| Listener changes their playback mid-session (unplugs headphones) | The declaration is now stale and nothing knows. Realistic and common. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Tension warning insults the client | The whole social risk. If the Producer's screen says "this client can't hear bass" and the client sees it, real damage. | Warning is Producer-only. Listener never sees an assessment of their own credibility. |
| Producer uses this to dismiss valid feedback | A client on laptop speakers noticing a vocal is buried is still right. The feature must weight, never dismiss. | Warning is worded as context, not as a verdict. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Undeclared | Listener skipped | "Not declared" — never an inferred default. |
| Declared | Listener stated their chain | Context shown in the roster. |
| Stale | `[PENDING — Step 5]` | `[PENDING]` |
| Tension flagged | Note category conflicts with declared chain | Quiet caveat beside the note, Producer-only. |

| DT-01 | A domain expert expects the **last three feet to be the weakest link**, and the monitoring product to be blind to it. The category guarantees bit depth to the listener's device and then says nothing about whether the device can reproduce it — a 24-bit lossless stream to Bluetooth earbuds is a guarantee that dies on arrival. Nobody in the sweep raised this. | Domain reasoning: engineers spend fortunes on monitoring precisely because the reproduction chain determines what you can judge. Every mix engineer knows the client's playback is the variable they cannot control. | DT-02 | The output must be **weighting, not gating**, and the assessment must be Producer-only. | Reasoning: the client's right to listen badly is theirs. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Self-declaration only; no device fingerprinting or audio probing | Detection is unreliable and is a surveillance surface. The gain does not justify either cost. |
| D-02 | Producer-only visibility; the listener never sees an assessment of their own credibility | A client seeing "this person can't hear bass" is relationship-ending. |
| D-03 | Weight, never gate or dismiss | A client on bad speakers noticing a buried vocal is still right. |
| D-04 | Undeclared means unknown, never an assumed default | An inferred context is confidently wrong, which is worse than an admitted gap. |
