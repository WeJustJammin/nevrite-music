## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Listener Roster ↔ Monitor Stream

**Relationship**: The roster is the access control plane over the stream. Every listener is on the
roster or is not receiving audio; there is no third state. The hard requirement is that revocation is
immediate on an active stream — the realistic threat is not an outsider guessing a link, it is an
insider whose access should have ended (a label rep after the pitch, an engineer after the job) still
hearing an unreleased mix. Revocation that only applies at reconnect is not revocation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: owns the roster. Adds, removes, sets windows. This is their room and their client's asset.
- **Musician**: sees who is listening — a right, not a courtesy. Playing to an unknown audience is a legitimate objection, and the personas file records this persona's fear of losing control of their work.
- **Operator**: sees the roster for a session in their room for duty-of-care reasons; cannot alter it.
- **Fan**: not affected — no fan path (index D-04).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The roster owns access truth. The stream is a consumer that must re-check, not cache a grant for the session's duration. Cached grants are how revocation silently fails.
2. **Trigger chain**: Revocation → immediate stream termination for that listener. Synchronous, and it must fail closed: if the system cannot confirm a listener is still authorised, it stops sending audio. This is the exact inverse of 08.02.03's fail-open posture, and correctly so — here the cost of a false allow is a leaked master.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. Roster membership is the *only* grant. Being in the room, being on the project, or having been invited previously grants nothing by itself.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A listener joining notifies the Producer and is visible to Musicians. Whether the Musician is notified actively or passively is `[PENDING — Step 5]`, but the information is not optional.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — a listener can be mid-stream when revoked. The stream must cut, and the cut must be legible to the listener ("your access ended") rather than presenting as a network fault, or support absorbs the confusion.

### CX-02: Monitor Stream ↔ Live Feedback

**Relationship**: A note is `(content, position, author)`. The position is only meaningful against a
timeline both ends agree on. The stream is the only thing that can supply that reference. The subtlety
is that the listener is hearing the stream *late* — so a note stamped at the listener's "now" refers to
a moment already past for the room. Naively stamping at receipt time puts every note late by the
stream delay, which in a monitoring context is seconds, not milliseconds. That is enough to attach a
comment about the vocal to the guitar part that followed it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: reads the notes; needs them to land on the right moment or they are noise.
- **Musician**: may leave notes; may see notes about their own performance, which is a sensitive surface.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The stream owns the timeline. Notes reference it and never define it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Note created → anchored to a stream position → handed to Projects (07) for its permanent home. If the handoff to 07 fails, the note must survive locally, not vanish. See index D-03.
3. **Permission intersection**: Roster membership grants listening; it does not necessarily grant commenting. Two different grants — `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A note during a live take must not interrupt the performer. Anything that pops up in front of someone who is playing is a defect wearing a feature's clothes.
5. **State transition conflict**: The listener's delay against the room's now is the core issue, and it must be compensated at stamp time rather than corrected afterward.

### CX-03: Playback Context ↔ Live Feedback

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — the premise is that a note's weight depends on what the listener heard
it on. "The bass is too loud" from a listener on laptop speakers, which reproduce almost none of the
bass, is information about the listener's monitoring rather than about the bass. Whether the product
should say this out loud is genuinely unresolved (index Q-01) — it involves telling a paying client
their opinion is unreliable, which is socially expensive even when true.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the consumer of the weighting. Their whole problem is deciding which client note to act on.
- **Musician**: `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Medium confidence, deferred per the CX template's rule for non-High entries.

### CX-04: Playback Context ↔ Monitor Stream

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — the quality contract promises fidelity to the listener's device; the
playback chain determines whether that promise means anything. Streaming 24-bit/96k to Bluetooth
earbuds is a guarantee that dies in the last three feet, and the honest product move might be to say
so rather than to bill for bit depth the listener cannot hear.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: needs to know the guarantee is being wasted.
- **Musician**: `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
- **Operator**: relevant when the listening end is their room.
- **Fan**: not affected.
