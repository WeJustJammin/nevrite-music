# Feature: Interruption, Reconnect & Session Continuity

> **Number**: 08.08
> **Parent**: [Real-Time Jamming & Remote Sessions](./realtime-jamming-remote-sessions-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

> **Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep's candidate list. Added because a network session that
> dies has money attached to it, and nobody owns the question of what happens next.

## Overview

Owns what the room does when the network breaks — which it will. The reconnect *mechanics* belong to the
transport cross-cut, but three questions are product and are currently unowned: does the take survive
(yes, per local-first capture), does the clock keep billing, and what gets recorded about the failure so
the resulting argument has a fact in it. A paid remote session dropping at minute 40 is a dispute, and
disputes need evidence, not memories.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees, at the moment of panic, that their local recording is still running — the single most reassuring thing the product can say. Sees their reconnect state and whether the session is waiting for them. Their cue mix and profile survive the round trip rather than needing to be rebuilt. |
| Producer | Full | Decides whether the session holds for a missing participant or continues without them. Sees who dropped and for how long. Owns the commercial consequence — a session paused for 20 minutes waiting for someone's router is billable time that somebody is going to argue about. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees interruptions attributable to their room's endpoint. Relevant to them because a room with a flaky uplink is a room with a reputation problem, and per `meta/personas.md` their reputation accrues from sessions that went smoothly. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into this domain. |

## Behavior

**The reframe that makes this survivable**: because capture is local-first (08.05.01 D-02), a network
interruption damages the *experience*, not the *asset*. The takes keep recording on every endpoint
regardless of what the network does. This turns a catastrophic failure into an unpleasant one, and it is
the single most valuable consequence of the local-first decision.

So the honest priority order when a link dies:

1. **Reassure** — tell the player their recording is still running, immediately. This is the moment they will otherwise stop playing.
2. **Preserve** — the take, the cue mix, the profile, the session state.
3. **Reconnect** — transport's job.
4. **Record** — what happened, when, to whom, for how long. This is the evidence.
5. **Decide** — hold or continue. The Producer's call.

**The billing question is the unowned one.** Domain 05 owns the booking, domain 24 owns the dispute, but
neither owns *what the room did when the network died* — and that is the fact both of them need. A
session that dropped for 20 minutes: does the clock run? Whose fault was it? Was pre-flight (08.06) run,
and did it pass? This feature does not decide the commercial policy — that is domain 05's product
decision — but it is the only thing positioned to record the facts the policy will be applied to.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: hold semantics (how long does a room wait?), whether a
take is split at the interruption or continues as one, and whether a reconnecting participant rejoins
into the same take.

### Happy Path

1. A participant's link drops mid-session.
2. Their local recording continues, uninterrupted, and they are told so immediately.
3. Other participants see who dropped, not just that "something happened."
4. Producer chooses: hold the session, or continue without them.
5. Participant reconnects; their cue mix and profile are restored rather than rebuilt.
6. Session end: the interruption is in the record — who, when, how long — available to 05 and 24.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Drop mid-take | Local recording continues. The take survives. This is the payoff of local-first. | "Connection lost — **you are still recording.**" |
| The **conductor** drops (08.01.03) | The room loses its timing reference. Cannot silently promote someone mid-take — that shifts everyone's time under their hands. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — cross-referenced from 08.01.03 Q-02. |
| Producer drops | The session owner is gone. Does the room continue? Does capture keep running with nobody in charge? | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Everyone drops | Each endpoint has a local take of a session that stopped existing. The takes are still real and still valuable. | Takes preserved; session ends; uploads still prompted. |
| Repeated flapping | Worse than a clean drop — the session neither continues nor fails. Needs a threshold that declares someone out. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Paid session, 20-minute hold | Billable time nobody agreed to. **The dispute.** | Recorded; policy is domain 05's. |
| Pre-flight was skipped, then a drop | Directly relevant to fault. | Recorded together — 08.06's result is part of the interruption record. |
| Reconnects but their take fragmented | Alignment problem — 08.05.02 Q-03. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Connected | Normal | Nothing. |
| Dropped (local) | Your link died | **"Still recording"** first, everything else second. |
| Dropped (remote) | Someone else's link died | Who, by name. Not "a participant". |
| Holding | Producer chose to wait | Hold state + elapsed. Elapsed is visible because it is billable. |
| Continuing | Producer chose to proceed | Session continues; absence recorded. |
| Reconnecting | Link returning | Progress; cue mix restoring. |
| Ended (abandoned) | Session could not continue | Takes preserved; uploads prompted; interruption recorded. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Local-first capture turns an interruption from an asset failure into an experience failure** — and therefore the first thing the product must say to a dropped player is "you are still recording", not "reconnecting". | Derived from 08.05.01 DT-02. Reasoning: the player's instinct on seeing a disconnect is to stop playing. If they are still recording and do not know it, the product has thrown away a take it successfully captured — a failure caused entirely by bad messaging rather than bad engineering. | ✅ CONFIRMED — reassurance is priority one, ahead of reconnection. This is the cheapest high-value detail in the feature, and an implementer focused on transport will put "reconnecting…" there instead. |
| DT-02 | The **billing consequence of an interruption is unowned** — 05 owns the booking, 24 owns the dispute, and neither owns what the room did when the network died. That fact is the input both of them need. | Reasoning: trace the failure. A paid session drops for 20 minutes; the client disputes the invoice. Domain 05 has a booking, domain 24 has a dispute process, and the actual question ("what happened, whose link, was pre-flight run?") is answerable only by the thing that was in the room. Same structural argument as 08.05.04: presence at the moment of truth is what makes the record possible. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this feature records the facts; domain 05 owns the policy applied to them. The split matters: an agent could easily invent a billing policy here, which is a product decision belonging to the owner and to 05. |
| DT-03 | Reconnect is transport's problem and this whole feature is not-product. | Adversarial: reconnection is plainly infrastructure. | ❌ REJECTED — **partially, and the split is the point.** The reconnect mechanics *are* the transport cross-cut and are routed there. What is unowned and product: the reassurance messaging (DT-01), the hold-or-continue decision, the cue mix/profile restoration, the conductor-loss problem (08.01.03), and the interruption record as dispute evidence (DT-02). Absorb this into transport and you get a reconnect that works perfectly and a product that loses takes because it told people the wrong thing, and bills that nobody can adjudicate. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | "You are still recording" is the first message a dropped player sees | They will otherwise stop playing and throw away a take we captured. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | This feature records interruption facts; domain 05 owns the billing policy | An agent inventing a billing policy would be making a product decision that is not its to make. |
| D-03 | Participants are named on drop, never anonymised to "a participant" | The room needs to know who is missing to decide anything. |
| D-04 | Hold elapsed time is visible because it is billable | Hiding the meter on a paid session is how disputes start. |
| D-05 | Cue mixes and profiles survive a reconnect | Rebuilding mid-session is a real cost paid at the worst moment. |
