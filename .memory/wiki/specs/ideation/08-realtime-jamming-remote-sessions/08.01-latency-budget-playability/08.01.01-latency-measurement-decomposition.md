# Feature: Latency Measurement, Decomposition & Remediation

> **Number**: 08.01.01
> **Parent**: [Latency Budget & Playability](./08.01-latency-budget-playability-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The millisecond ledger. Measures end-to-end mouth-to-ear latency between two endpoints, then breaks it
into the parts a human can actually act on — network transit, jitter buffer, audio interface I/O,
OS/driver, application — and tells them which one to attack first. It exists because "it feels laggy"
is not a diagnosis, and because every competitor in this space (JamKazam, JackTrip, Source-Connect)
burns its support budget on users who cannot tell a bad route from a bad buffer setting.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Runs a measurement against a named collaborator or a room. Sees their total mouth-to-ear figure and the stacked breakdown of where it went. Sees the one remediation that would buy them the most milliseconds ("you are on Wi-Fi — a cable saves you ~9 ms"). Can re-measure on demand. Cannot see another player's internal breakdown unless that player shares it. |
| Producer | Full | Everything the Musician sees, plus the aggregate view for the whole room: a per-player ledger table showing who the weak endpoint is. This is the persona who has to fix someone else's rig while a client's clock is running, so they get the diagnosis for players who cannot read their own. Can request a re-measure from any player. |
| Operator | Config | Configures and publishes their room's *endpoint* figures as a saleable spec — a studio advertising "remote-session ready, measured 18 ms to the London exchange". Sees their own room's ledger. Does not see individual visiting musicians' home-rig breakdowns; that is not their business and it is other people's diagnostic data. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. This is a production instrument inside a professional room the Fan persona has no path into. Fans encounter nothing from domain 08; live fan-facing audio lives in domains 20 and 21. |

## Behavior

A measurement is a probe between two endpoints that returns a decomposed ledger rather than a single
number. The decomposition is the product — the single number is available everywhere and helps nobody.

The five buckets, and why each is separated:

| Bucket | Why it is its own bucket |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Network transit | The only bucket bounded by physics. Not fixable by settings — only by choosing a different collaborator or a different route. |
| Jitter buffer | The largest *discretionary* cost and the one users most often set wrong. Trades stability against milliseconds. |
| Audio interface I/O | Fixable by buying different hardware. Worth knowing before blaming the network. |
| OS / driver | Fixable by configuration (driver model, sample rate, power profile). |
| Application | Ours. If this bucket is large, it is our defect, and exposing it publicly is a deliberate discipline. |

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens the latency panel and selects a collaborator (or a room they are about to join).
2. System runs a probe and shows a live-updating estimate while it gathers samples.
3. System renders the ledger — total figure plus the five-bucket stacked breakdown, with the network transit portion marked as the floor that cannot be optimised away.
4. System surfaces the single highest-yield remediation, with the milliseconds it would recover and the trade-off it costs ("smaller buffer saves ~11 ms; risk of dropouts rises").
5. Musician applies the remediation (or does not) and re-measures; the ledger updates and the delta is shown against the previous run.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Probe cannot reach the peer at all (NAT/firewall) | Measurement fails. Must NOT render as "0 ms" or as a green state. Hands off to Pre-Flight (08.06), which owns the reachability diagnosis. | "We could not reach {peer}. This is a connection problem, not a latency problem." → link to Pre-Flight. |
| Measurement is unstable — jitter so high the number is meaningless | System reports a range and a confidence, not a point value. A wide range is itself the finding. | "Between 34 and 91 ms — your connection is too unstable to give one number. Instability is your problem, not distance." |
| Peer's rig cannot self-report its internal buckets | Ledger renders with the peer's internal segments as `Unknown` rather than omitting them. A partial ledger that hides its gaps is worse than none. | Stacked bar with a hatched "unknown" segment and a note on what could not be read. |
| Route changes mid-measurement | Result is discarded, not averaged across two different network paths. | "Your route changed while we measured. Re-measuring." |
| User re-measures obsessively, hunting a better number | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether to rate-limit, and whether to show variance across runs rather than encourage cherry-picking the best one. | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No measurement ever run for this pair | Empty state: "We don't know yet what you two can do." + Measure CTA. |
| Loading | Probe running | Live-updating provisional figure with a settling indicator — explicitly marked provisional so nobody acts on a half-gathered number. |
| Populated | Probe complete, both endpoints reported | Full five-bucket ledger + highest-yield remediation + timestamp. |
| Partial | Probe complete, peer internals unavailable | Ledger with hatched unknown segments + explanation. |
| Error | Probe could not reach peer | Reachability error, routed to Pre-Flight (08.06). Never a numeric result. |
| Stale | Measurement older than its validity window | Ledger dimmed with an age label and a re-measure prompt. A stale number must never be presented as current. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert expects the ledger to be **decomposed**, not a single RTT number — because the discretionary buckets (buffer, interface, driver) are usually larger than the network bucket at typical collaboration distances, which inverts the naive user's assumption that distance is their problem. | Domain reasoning: at a few hundred miles, network transit is often ~10–20 ms while a badly-set buffer alone can exceed that. Every tool in the category (JackTrip, Source-Connect) exposes buffer settings precisely because they dominate. | ✅ CONFIRMED — decomposition is the feature; a single number is a commodity ping and is why users blame the wrong thing. |
| DT-02 | The application bucket should be exposed publicly even though it is our own overhead. | Reasoning: hiding it makes the ledger untrustworthy, and a ledger nobody trusts is not used. Also, a competitor will measure it anyway. | ✅ CONFIRMED — self-incriminating measurement is what makes the rest of the ledger credible. Accepted cost: our own defects are visible to users. |
| DT-03 | Latency measurement is really a cross-cut — Education's lesson room and Services' remote session need it too, so it should be extracted like the transport pipe was. | Adversarial: the same objection D-15 sustained against the transport pipe. | ❌ REJECTED — the *telemetry* is the cross-cut and already lives there per D-15; the *interpretation into a human judgment* is what other domains do not have and would each reinvent badly. Other domains consume the derived `playable-with?` predicate (see domain CX), not this panel. This is the same line the domain index draws with Stripe/Payments vs Gear Marketplace. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Measurement, decomposition and remediation ship as one feature | The diagnosis and the prescription are the same panel. See parent index D-01. |
| D-02 | Fail-closed: an unmeasured or unreachable link renders `Unknown`, never a green or a zero | A false green in this domain costs a paid session. The failure mode of optimism here is someone's money. |
| D-03 | The application's own latency contribution is shown to users | Credibility of the whole ledger depends on it not flattering us. |
