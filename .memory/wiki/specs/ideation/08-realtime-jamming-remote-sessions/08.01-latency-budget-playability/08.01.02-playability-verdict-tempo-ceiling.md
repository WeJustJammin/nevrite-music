# Feature: Playability Verdict & Tempo Ceiling

> **Number**: 08.01.02
> **Parent**: [Latency Budget & Playability](./08.01-latency-budget-playability-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Converts a millisecond ledger into the only answer a musician wants: *can we play together, and at
what tempo?* It renders a go/no-go verdict scoped to a declared musical context, a tempo ceiling above
which the ensemble will fall apart, and — critically — a route into Overdub Mode (08.07) when the
answer is no. The verdict is the feature that makes latency legible to people who do not think in
milliseconds and never should have to.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees a plain verdict for a specific pairing and musical context — "you two can play at up to ~104 BPM; above that it will drift." Declares the context (instruments, genre, target tempo) that scopes the verdict. When the verdict is red, is offered Overdub Mode rather than a dead end. Cannot override a red verdict into a green one — but can proceed anyway, informed. |
| Producer | Full | Sees the verdict for every pairing in the room, not just their own — they need to know *which* link is the weak one before a client's session starts. Selects or overrides the timing model the verdict recommends (symmetric vs conductor). Can proceed against a red verdict and owns that call. |
| Operator | Read-only | Sees the verdict for their own room's endpoint against a given counterpart, as evidence for what they can honestly advertise. Per `meta/personas.md`, this persona's anti-pattern is misrepresenting the room's specs — a verdict they cannot edit is a deliberate guard against exactly that. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path exists into a jam room; this domain's fan-facing sibling concepts were split out to Community (03) and Fanbase (20) at domain-map time. |

## Behavior

The verdict is a function of `(ledger, musical context)` — never of the ledger alone. The same 38 ms
is comfortable for a vocalist overdubbing to a bed and fatal for a drummer and bassist trying to lock.
A verdict that ignores context is a number pretending to be advice.

The tempo ceiling exists because latency's musical cost scales with tempo: a fixed delay is a larger
fraction of a beat at 160 BPM than at 70 BPM. This is why "can we play?" has no context-free answer
and why the product should express the ceiling in BPM — the unit musicians actually think in — rather
than in milliseconds.

Verdict values:

| Verdict | Meaning | Route offered |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Playable | Within the ensemble threshold for the declared context | Enter the room |
| Playable with a ceiling | Works below a stated BPM | Enter the room, ceiling shown |
| Conductor only | Only workable if one player's time is authoritative | Enter with a timing model prompt (08.01.03) |
| Not playable live | Beyond what any timing model rescues | **Overdub Mode (08.07)** — the escape hatch, not a dead end |
| Unknown | No valid measurement | Measure first. Never a green. |

### Happy Path

1. Musician selects a collaborator and declares context — instruments, roughly the genre, a target tempo if they have one.
2. System reads the current ledger (08.01.01), recomputing if stale.
3. System renders the verdict in musical terms — a BPM ceiling and a plain-language sentence, with the milliseconds available but secondary.
4. If green: Musician enters the room.
5. If red: system offers Overdub Mode (08.07) in the same breath as the refusal — the point is to reroute the work, not to reject it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Verdict is red but the Musician wants to try anyway | System does not block. It records that the verdict was red and the user proceeded — this matters when a paid session later goes into dispute (domain 24). | "This is likely to fall apart above ~70 BPM. Try anyway / Switch to Overdub." |
| Ledger is stale | Verdict expires with it. A stale green must never render as green. | Verdict dimmed with an age label + re-measure prompt. |
| Context not declared | Verdict renders against a conservative default and says so — it must not silently assume the most forgiving case. | "Assuming a general ensemble. Tell us your instruments for a real answer." |
| Three or more players, each pair different | The room's verdict is bounded by its **worst pair**, not its average. Averaging here would produce a green room containing an unplayable link. | Room verdict + "the limiting link is {A}↔{B}". |
| Verdict flips red mid-session | Producer is notified (they are billing). Whether players are interrupted is `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a modal mid-take is its own kind of damage. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No measurement for this pair | "Measure first" + CTA. Never a speculative verdict from geography alone. |
| Loading | Ledger being gathered | Skeleton verdict; no provisional green — a provisional green would be acted on. |
| Populated | Verdict computed | Verdict + BPM ceiling + limiting factor + route. |
| Partial | Some pairs in a multi-player room unmeasured | Verdict for known pairs, unmeasured links named explicitly as unknown. |
| Error | Measurement failed | "We can't tell you yet" + route to Pre-Flight (08.06). |
| Stale | Ledger aged out | Dimmed, age-labelled, re-measure prompt. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The verdict must be expressed as a **tempo ceiling in BPM**, not milliseconds, because latency's musical cost is a fraction of a beat and therefore tempo-dependent. Nobody in the category does this — JamKazam and Jamulus surface raw ms and leave the translation to the user. | Domain reasoning: a fixed 30 ms is ~2% of a beat at 40 BPM and ~8% at 160 BPM. The same link is fine for a ballad and fatal for a punk song. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the single most differentiating idea in the sub-domain and it costs nothing to compute. It is also the one thing here that makes latency legible to the persona who is not an engineer. |
| DT-02 | The ~25–30 ms ensemble ceiling from D-15 is a single constant that can be applied uniformly. | D-15's own framing. | ❌ REJECTED — instrument- and role-dependent. A drummer/bassist lock demands far tighter sync than a vocalist over a finished bed; a keys pad is more forgiving still. A single constant would be simultaneously too strict for half the use cases and too loose for the other half. Logged as Q-01 on the parent index. **This does not weaken D-15** — it means the playable radius is a *set* of radii by instrument pair, which makes the matching feature richer, not weaker. |
| DT-03 | A red verdict is a rejection and the flow ends there. | Naive framing. | ❌ REJECTED — and rejecting this is the most important product move in domain 08. A red verdict must route into Overdub Mode (08.07), where latency is irrelevant because the remote player tracks against local playback and the take is aligned afterward. Most real "remote session" work never needed sub-30 ms at all. The verdict's job is to *sort* work into the right mode, not to gatekeep. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The verdict is expressed in BPM, not milliseconds | Musicians think in tempo. Milliseconds are available but secondary. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | A red verdict never blocks — it informs, records, and offers Overdub Mode | Blocking users from trying is paternalistic and they will do it anyway on another tool. Recording the informed override is what protects everyone later. |
| D-03 | A multi-player room's verdict is bounded by its worst pair, never averaged | An averaged green room can contain an unplayable link. This is the exact arithmetic error that would make the feature untrustworthy. |
