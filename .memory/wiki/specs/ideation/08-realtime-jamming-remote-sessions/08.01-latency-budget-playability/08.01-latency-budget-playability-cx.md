## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Measurement ↔ Playability Verdict

**Relationship**: The measurement produces a decomposed millisecond ledger (network transit, jitter
buffer, audio interface I/O, OS/driver, application). The verdict consumes that ledger plus a declared
musical context (instrument pair, genre, target tempo) and emits a go/no-go plus a tempo ceiling. Data
flows one way. There is no verdict without a fresh measurement, and a measurement older than the
current network conditions must expire the verdict rather than show a stale green light.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees both — the ledger as "why", the verdict as "what now". Most will only look at the verdict.
- **Producer**: sees both and cares about the ledger, because they are the one who has to fix it for a paying client.
- **Operator**: sees the verdict for their own room's endpoint as a saleable spec; the ledger only when diagnosing.
- **Fan**: not affected — no visibility into either.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ledger is owned by the measurement child. The verdict is derived, never stored as an independent truth. No merge conflict is possible because nothing writes back.
2. **Trigger chain**: Measurement completes → verdict recomputes synchronously. If measurement fails, the verdict must render `Unknown`, never `Playable`. Fail-closed: an unmeasured link is not a green light.
3. **Permission intersection**: None. Anyone who can see the ledger can see the verdict.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A verdict transition from green to red mid-session must notify the Producer (they are the one billing). Whether it notifies players is `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes, they can race — network conditions change faster than a user reads. The verdict must carry the timestamp and conditions of the measurement it was derived from, so a stale green is legible as stale rather than as true.

### CX-02: Playability Verdict ↔ Shared Clock

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — the verdict selects a timing model, and the clock implements it. If the
verdict is "playable, symmetric", every player hears every other player late and the group tolerates
it. If the verdict is "playable only with a conductor", one endpoint's time becomes authoritative and
the others accept a deliberate local delay so that what the conductor hears is coherent. The exact
selection rule is `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` and depends on Q-03 in the index,
which is an owner decision.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: chooses or overrides the timing model; this is their call because they own the room.
- **Musician**: experiences the consequence (they may be asked to accept delayed self-monitoring) but does not select it.
- **Operator**: not affected.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The timing model is one value per room, owned by the room, set by the Producer. Last-write-wins with an audible warning is the likely rule — `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
2. **Trigger chain**: Verdict change → proposed clock model change. This must NOT auto-apply mid-take. Changing the timing reference while people are playing is destructive; it must be a prompt, not an action.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — only the Producer role can change the timing model, even though every Musician can see the verdict that suggests it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A timing model change must notify every player in the room before it applies, because their monitoring is about to change.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — Step 5 deepening]`.

### CX-03: Measurement ↔ Shared Clock

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — the clock needs per-direction one-way delay per endpoint to place the
click correctly for each listener. The measurement child is the only producer of that number. The open
risk is that one-way delay is genuinely hard to measure without a synchronized reference, which is
precisely the thing the clock is trying to establish — a circularity that `/create-prd` must resolve
rather than ideation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: sees the consequence (click alignment quality) but not the mechanism.
- **Musician**: sees only whether the click feels right.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — this pair is
Medium confidence and the synthesis questions are deferred per the CX template's rule for non-High entries.
