## Overview

A remote player tracks against locally-buffered playback, records locally at full quality, and their take
is aligned server-side afterward. **Network latency is irrelevant** — the player is not playing *with*
anyone in real time; they are playing *to* something already downloaded. A guitarist overdubbing onto a
finished bed, a vocalist doing a comp, a session drummer replacing a programmed part: none of these need
sub-30 ms, and all of them are what people mean when they say "remote session."

The problem it solves is not latency. It is that **the physics of the network had been allowed to decide
which work is possible**, when in fact the physics only constrains simultaneous ensemble playing — a
narrow band of the work. Overdub restores the wide band, and it does so on the locked stack
(`meta/constraints.md`) while still capturing the thesis fact (D-18): who played what, recorded as the
work happens rather than reconstructed afterward.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |

Records as many passes as they want, keeps or discards each one themselves, may flag a preferred pass, and uploads the keepers. Sees per-pass quality checks (silent, clipped, bed-bleed, sample-rate mismatch) **while still at the instrument**, and the measured residual timing error of each pass. Works on any connection, from anywhere — the playable radius (08.02) does not apply. Per `meta/personas.md`, this persona lives on a portfolio of small income streams; this is the mode that makes remote session work actually accessible to them. |

## Behavior

**The reframe**: domain 08 is built around a physics problem (the ~25–30 ms ensemble ceiling, D-15) that
caps the playable radius at a few hundred miles. Overdub Mode sidesteps the network physics entirely,
because a player tracking to local playback experiences **zero network latency** in their monitoring.
Their own input is local; the bed is already on their disk.

What is required, and it is all cheap:

| Requirement | Where it lives |
|-------------|---------------|
| Deliver the bed package to the player | File transfer. Storage + CDN. |
| Player monitors bed + own input | Local. Their machine, their interface, no round trip. Balance is a **local** control, not a networked cue mix ([CX R-02](./realtime-jamming-remote-sessions-cx.md#rejected-pairs)). |
| Record locally at full quality | 08.05.01 — already the design |
| Measure the player's **local** round-trip | 08.01.01's local half — see DT-04. This is the offset alignment will use. |
| Align the take to the project timeline | 08.05.02 — the bed *is* the reference |
| Bind the take to the human | 08.05.04 — at a stated grade, see DT-08 |

### The bed is a package, not a bounce

A stereo mixdown is not enough to play to (DT-11). The bed package is:

| Component | Why it is required |
|-----------|-------------------|
| The bounce | What the player plays to. Fidelity is the Producer's choice (below). |
| Tempo / bar map | So "play from bar 33" means something, and so the take lands on a grid the project already has. |
| Count-in | **2 bars**, standard practice. A bed starting at bar 1 with no count-in makes bar 1 unplayable — the player is still finding the tempo. |
| Section markers | The brief says "the chorus"; the player must be able to find the chorus. |
| The brief | What is wanted, in words, from the person who wants it. |
| Bed version stamp | See below. The single fact that makes a stale-bed take diagnosable rather than mysterious. |

**Bed fidelity is a Producer decision, and it is a disclosure decision** (DT-07). Options are a
full-quality bounce, a reference mix, or a watermarked reference. The product does not pretend any of
these is DRM: the file is on the player's disk and **cannot be recalled**. What the product does is
*record the disclosure* — who received which bed version, at which fidelity, when — which is the same
provenance move the thesis makes for credit, applied to what left the building.

### Beds are versioned, and versions are product-visible

Mixes move. A player tracking against a bed that changed under them is the most common way overdub work
is silently invalidated, and it is invisible to everyone until the comp.

- Every bed package carries a **version**. Every take is stamped with the bed version it was played to.
- Changing the bed creates a **new version**; it never mutates the one in flight.
- A player mid-request is **notified** that a newer bed exists, told what changed at section granularity,
  and given the choice — keep going against v1, or re-download v2. **The choice is theirs**, because only
  they know whether the change touches what they are playing. A forced re-download discards work.
- A take against a superseded bed is **delivered, aligned, and labelled** — never rejected. Bar positions
  are stable across versions in the common case (a mix change, not an arrangement change); the label tells
  the Producer to check rather than the system to discard.
- If the **bar map changed**, the label is escalated: the take's grid is not the project's grid, and
  alignment says so explicitly rather than placing it confidently on the wrong bars.

### The local round-trip is real, and it is the thing everybody gets wrong

The breadth pass wrote "there is nothing to be late." That is true of the **experience** and false of the
**recording** (DT-04). The player hears the bed after their output buffer and converter; their playing
arrives at the recorder after their input converter and buffer. Round-trip on real consumer rigs lands in
the order of **5–25 ms** depending on buffer size and interface. The player plays perfectly in time with
what they hear, and the file lands systematically **late by their rig's round-trip**.

This is exactly the shape of failure 08.05.02 DT-02 warns about: it is silent, it passes a casual check,
and it is fatal for a rhythm part. So:

- The round-trip is **measured per rig, per session, during overdub pre-flight** — never assumed, never
  taken from a device database.
- The measurement carries its own **uncertainty**, and both numbers travel with the take.
- Alignment (08.05.02) uses the measured round-trip as the offset. The bed is the reference; there are no
  peer clocks; the hard half of 08.05.02 genuinely does evaporate — but the offset does not.
- The **residual timing error** after compensation is reported per pass. What residual is acceptable is
  instrument-dependent and inherits 08.01.02 Q-01 (the thresholds have no source yet) — see Q-02.

**And there is a compensating gift**: when the bed plays out and the input records in on the *same
interface*, both share one crystal, so **there is no drift** (DT-06) — the problem that dominates
08.05.02 in a live session. Overdub has offset without drift. Unless the player splits devices, at which
point drift returns and brings acoustic bleed with it.

### Passes, not takes

"A vocalist doing a comp" is not one take (DT-10). It is fourteen passes over the chorus, of which the
Producer will keep four syllables. So:

- The unit the player records is a **pass**. The unit the Producer receives is **every pass the player kept**.
- The player **discards their own bad passes** — that is their right and their judgment, and nobody wants
  the false start uploaded.
- The player **may flag a preferred pass**. The flag is **advisory only**. Choosing among passes is the
  Producer's job (`meta/personas.md`: they comp); a player who chooses for them has deleted the Producer's
  work, and Producers regularly keep the pass the player hated.
- A pass may be **multi-channel** — a guitarist delivering DI + mic so the part can be reamped is ordinary
  session practice. 08.05.01 already carries this; overdub does not narrow it.

### Every kept pass is checked locally, before upload, while the player is still at the instrument

This is the timing that matters. A defect found after the player has packed up is a defect found too late,
and it is the same argument 08.06 DT-02 makes about pre-flight. The checks:

| Check | The failure it catches | Why it is silent otherwise |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| **Signal present** | The take is silent — wrong input selected, interface muted, phantom power off. | The single most common amateur session failure. The player was wearing headphones and heard the bed the whole time. Nothing told them their own signal never arrived. |
| **Not clipped** | Digital clipping. | Unfixable, and inaudible on small monitoring. |
| **Bed not bleeding into the take** | The player is monitoring on **speakers**, so the bed is in their microphone — phase-shifted, un-removable, ruinous (DT-05). | Detectable by correlating the delivered take against the delivered bed. The player cannot hear it; the room did. |
| **Sample rate as declared** | Resample-on-import, done naively, reintroduces drift. | Passes every check until the end of a long pass. |
| **Bed version stamp present** | An orphan take that cannot be placed. | — |

Failures name the **specific fix**, per 08.06 D-04: not "correlation anomaly detected" but *"the backing
track is bleeding into your mic — you're monitoring on speakers. Use closed-back headphones and do the
pass again."* A check that ships an error code has done nothing.

### Provenance: what is certain, and what is not

**This is where the breadth pass overclaimed.** In a live session the platform *is* the transport, so it
observed the playing. In an overdub, the platform observed an **upload**.

| Certain in an overdub | Why |
|---|---|
| Which identity accepted the request | They authenticated |
| Which bed version, at which fidelity, was delivered to them, and when | The platform sent it |
| Which passes arrived, when, from which endpoint | The platform received them |
| That the passes were played against **that** bed | The bed is in the timing, and the version is stamped |
| That the Producer accepted (or did not) | They acted |

| **Not** certain, and never to be inferred | Why |
|---|---|
| **That the human whose identity it is actually played it** | A file can be played by a ghost, generated, or bought. In a live room this is hard; here it is a drag-and-drop. **This is overdub's provenance hole and it must be labelled, not papered over.** |
| That they contributed to the work | Inherited from 08.05.04 D-02. A discarded pass added nothing. |
| What instrument they played | Declared, never detected (08.05.04 D-03). |
| What the split should be | A negotiation. Never an inference (08.05.04). |

So the record states **its own basis**:

| Grade | Basis | How it is reached |
|-------|-------|-------------------|
| **Delivered-by** | An authenticated identity delivered these passes against this bed by this deadline. | The default for every overdub. |
| **Observed-playing** | A second party heard it happen. | The Producer (or a listener on the roster, 08.03.02) monitored the pass live via 08.03. **This is what "optional attendance" actually buys** — a witness (DT-09). |
| **Counter-attested** | The Producer accepted the pass into the project. | Acceptance is signed and attributable (08.05.04 D-01). It is not proof the player played it; it is the party with the most to lose saying they believe it. |

A single-party overdub where the **Producer and the player are the same human** (the multi-hyphenate, and
utterly routine — someone overdubbing on their own band's track) reaches *delivered-by* and stops. Their
"attestation" would be a self-claim wearing a Producer hat, and 08.05.04 D-06 exists to prevent exactly
that. The record says so rather than quietly grading itself up.

**08 supplies the grade; Credits (02) decides what a grade is worth.** Same line as 08.05.04 D-06 — the
instrument's precision is not its authority. See Q-03.

### Commercial boundary

**Domain 05 owns the commercial wrapper; domain 08 owns the session mechanics and the facts.** This is the
same split the domain already applies at CX-05 (05 owns billing policy, 24 owns disputes, neither can
observe what the room did) and it resolves the breadth pass's Q-02:

| 05 (Services) owns | 08 owns |
|---|---|
| Whether this overdub is a purchase, its price, escrow, payout | The request, the bed package, the passes, the alignment |
| How many revision rounds are included, and what a late delivery costs | That rounds exist, what happened in each, and when |
| The acceptance/rejection policy and its money consequences | What was asked (the brief, immutable), what was delivered, and whether the rig was sound |
| The dispute's commercial outcome (with 24) | The dated evidence the dispute is adjudicated against |

**Overdub is not inherently commercial.** A band member overdubbing on their own project is wrapped by
07 and touches 05 not at all. The mechanics are identical; only the wrapper differs. A request created
from a project (07) is collaboration; a request created from a booking (05) is work.

**Revision rounds accrete inside the request, not beside it.** A round is a brief delta plus its passes.
One request holds the whole conversation, because the earlier rounds are still evidence of the work — and
in a paid dispute, "how many times did you ask me to redo it" is precisely the contested fact.

### The stack

**This is the one feature in domain 08 that runs on the locked stack.** Per `meta/constraints.md`
(Astro/Cloudflare/Supabase) and the domain index's own admission that real-time jamming "will not run on
Cloudflare Workers + Supabase and needs dedicated media infrastructure" — overdub needs file storage,
background processing, and no media infrastructure at all. The optional monitor stream (08.03) does need
it; overdub without a witness does not. That is a MoSCoW-relevant seam: the mode ships on the locked
stack, and the *provenance upgrade* is what costs infrastructure.

### Happy Path

1. Producer creates an overdub request against a project (07): selects the bed, chooses its fidelity (full bounce / reference mix / watermarked reference), writes the brief, sets the deadline, marks the target section(s).
2. System assembles the bed package — bounce + tempo/bar map + 2-bar count-in + section markers + brief — and stamps it **version 1**.
3. Musician is invited: directly, via casting (04), via a red playability verdict (08.01.02 DT-03), or via an unfixable pre-flight failure (08.06). They see the brief, the deadline, and the bed's duration, size and fidelity **before** accepting.
4. Musician accepts. **Delivery is recorded** — this identity received bed v1 at this fidelity at this timestamp.
5. Bed downloads, resumably. Overdub pre-flight (08.06) runs: disk headroom, sample rate, monitoring path (headphones), and round-trip calibration.
6. Round-trip calibration measures the player's own output→input delay and its uncertainty. Both numbers are attached to the session. **Measured, not assumed** (DT-04).
7. Musician plays to the bed locally. Bed/input balance is a local control (CX R-02). Network state is irrelevant, and the UI says so in as many words rather than showing a connection indicator that implies otherwise.
8. They record passes. Each is a local file at full quality. They keep or discard each pass themselves.
9. On keeping a pass, the local checks run immediately — signal present, not clipped, no bed bleed, sample rate as declared, bed version stamped. Any failure is surfaced **now**, with the specific fix, while the player is still at the instrument.
10. Kept passes upload in the background, resumably, surviving app close (08.05.01 D-03). The player may flag a preferred pass; the flag is advisory.
11. Alignment (08.05.02) places each pass: bed is the reference, offset is the measured round-trip, no peer clocks, no drift (DT-06). Residual timing error is reported per pass.
12. Attendance record (08.05.04) binds the passes to the identity **and records its own basis** — *delivered-by* by default, *observed-playing* if the Producer monitored live (DT-09).
13. Producer receives the aligned passes in the project (07) with the brief, the bed version, the timing residual and the provenance grade attached — and either **accepts** (counter-attesting the record) or opens a revision round with a brief delta.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Bed changes after the player started** | New bed **version** created; the in-flight package is never mutated. Player is notified with a section-granularity diff and chooses: continue against v1 or re-download v2. Forcing v2 would discard work only the player can value. | "The mix changed (v2 — chorus vocal, bars 33–48). You're on v1. Keep going / Get v2." |
| **Bed's bar map changed, not just its mix** | Escalated. The take's grid is no longer the project's grid; alignment must not place it confidently on the wrong bars. | "The arrangement changed, not just the mix — your take's bars won't match. Get v2." Take, if delivered, arrives labelled `grid-mismatch`. |
| **Take delivered against a bed the project has since superseded** | Delivered, aligned, labelled with its bed version. **Never discarded** — bar positions are usually stable, and the Producer decides. | Producer sees "played against v1; project is on v3." |
| **Player monitors on speakers → bed bleeds into the take** | Detected at the local check by correlating the take against the delivered bed. Ruinous and un-removable; the player cannot hear it (DT-05). | "The backing track is bleeding into your mic — you're on speakers. Use closed-back headphones and redo the pass." |
| **Player's local round-trip uncompensated or miscalibrated** | Every pass lands systematically late by 5–25 ms. Silent, passes a casual check, fatal for a rhythm part (DT-04). | Calibration is a pre-flight readiness item, not a gate (08.06 D-05); residual + uncertainty shown per pass. A high residual is flagged, never hidden. |
| **Player plays the bed on one device and records on another** (phone playing bed aloud, laptop recording) | Two crystals → **drift returns** (DT-06), plus guaranteed acoustic bleed. The one configuration that reintroduces 08.05.02's hard half. | Pre-flight names it: "Your playback and recording devices are different. Use one device and headphones." Take is labelled `split-device`. |
| **Player's sample rate differs from the bed** | Resample is required, and a naive resample reintroduces drift. Declared rate is checked at pre-flight and re-verified at upload against the file. | Pre-flight: "Your interface is at 44.1 kHz; the bed is 48 kHz. Match it." Never a silent resample. |
| **Player's monitoring latency (their own interface) is bad** | Their rig, not the network — but it ruins their timing and they will blame the platform. Round-trip calibration makes it a **number** rather than a feeling. | Pre-flight (08.06) names the buffer setting. Overdub is the mode where this is the *only* latency, so it is also the mode where it is most fixable. |
| **Take is silent** (wrong input, muted, no phantom power) | Caught by the local signal check before upload. The player was in headphones hearing the bed and had no way to know. | "This pass has no signal on your input. Check your interface and redo it." |
| **Take is clipped** | Caught locally. Unfixable, so it must be caught while a redo is still possible. | "This pass is clipping. Turn your gain down and redo it." |
| **Player records 14 comping passes** | Normal, not an error (DT-10). Player discards what they don't want; every kept pass uploads; Producer receives all of them. | Per-pass upload state. Producer sees all kept passes, preference flag advisory. |
| **Bed download never completes** — the player's connection is poor | The irony overdub must handle: this is the mode people are routed to *because* their connection failed. Download is resumable and survives restart. Bed **fidelity** is the lever — a reference mix is a fraction of a full bounce, and a player who cannot receive the bed cannot do the work at any quality. | Resumable progress. On repeated failure: "This bed is too big for your connection — ask for a reference mix." |
| **Upload interrupted** | Resumable, chunked, survives app close (08.05.01 D-03). Resumption is not time-boxed short — a take abandoned at 90% is how work is lost. Persists until the request expires. | Resumable per-pass progress; "you have unuploaded passes" on return. |
| **Player uploads nothing** | The 08.05.01 Q-02 leverage dynamic, minus the room — and worse here, because there is no session ending to prompt them. The passes are on their disk and the platform cannot compel them. Persistent prompt on return; deadline governs the commercial consequence (05, per the boundary above). The platform **does not** hold their local files hostage or claim ownership of them. | "You have 3 unuploaded passes for {project}. Deadline: Friday." Producer sees `accepted, nothing delivered` — never a blank that reads as fine (08.06 D-03). |
| **Player accepts then goes silent** | Deadline passes → request state `expired`. The observation stands: they accepted, received bed v1, delivered nothing. That is a fact and it is evidence. | Producer: "Accepted Tuesday, bed delivered Tuesday, nothing delivered, expired Friday." |
| **Player delivers after the deadline** | Accepted and aligned. **08 does not enforce commercial deadlines** — it records the timestamps and 05 decides what a late delivery costs. A platform that silently bins a late take has destroyed work to enforce someone else's policy. | Delivered, labelled `late`. |
| **Take arrives out of time despite correct alignment** | **Two different problems and the product must never conflate them.** Alignment reports its residual — if the residual is low, alignment worked and the performance is the variable. The system reports *what it measured about the file* and **never characterises the performance.** Telling a musician the machine says they rushed is a fight, and it is also frequently wrong — an intentionally laid-back backbeat is not an error. | Musician sees: "Aligned — residual 1.2 ms." Nothing about their playing. Producer sees the same number and forms their own judgment, which is their job. |
| **System proposes to time-correct the take to the grid** | Never automatically (DT-12). Quantising is a creative decision the Producer owns, done in their tools, on material they chose. | Not offered here. |
| **Player is on a phone/tablet** | Plausible in overdub and only in overdub — per `meta/constraints.md`'s open mobile question, this is the one mode where it is realistic, because there is no real-time transport to sustain. But the *hard* constraints bite hardest here: round-trip on a phone's built-in path is large and variable, and a phone monitored on its own speaker guarantees bed bleed (DT-05). | Overdub pre-flight applies unchanged and will usually fail the monitoring-path check on a bare phone. Honest capability statement + "you need headphones and preferably an interface." Not blocked — 08.01.02 D-02 applies: inform, record, let them proceed. |
| **Player's disk fills mid-pass** | 08.05.01's worst failure, inherited whole. Predicted at pre-flight (CX-07), guarded live at capture, fails loudly, never truncates silently. | Pre-flight headroom warning; hard warning at the threshold. |
| **Producer edits the brief while the player is recording** | The brief is **immutable within a round**. An edit opens a new round with a delta. The player is told; work already done against the old brief is not invalidated, it is round 1. | "The brief changed (round 2): {delta}." Round 1's passes remain delivered and attributed. |
| **Two players are sent the same brief** (Producer cast two guitarists) | Both are legitimate. Both accept, both receive the bed, both deliver, one gets kept. **Both attendance records are true and neither is a credit** — 08.05.04 D-02 exactly: presence (here: delivery) is not contribution. The kept pass is what Credits (02) may act on. | Each player sees only their own request. Producer sees both. Whether players are told they are one of several is a **05** commercial-etiquette decision, not 08's. |
| **Producer and player are the same human** (multi-hyphenate on their own band's track) | Routine, not an edge. The record reaches **delivered-by** and stops — no witness, and self-acceptance is not counter-attestation (08.05.04 D-06). The record says so rather than grading itself up. | Grade shown honestly: "delivered-by (single party)." |
| **Player delivers a take they did not play** (ghost player, generated, stock loop) | **The provenance attack overdub is exposed to and a live room is not** (DT-08). The system cannot detect it and must not pretend to. It records what it knows: an identity delivered this. The Producer's acceptance is the only counter-signal, and a live monitor (08.03) is the only upgrade. | Nothing accusatory. The grade is the disclosure: `delivered-by` is not `observed-playing`, and 02 decides what that is worth. |
| **Player delivers a take containing an uncleared sample or someone else's playing** | Named in `meta/personas.md` as the Musician's worst *accidental* behaviour — "uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream." Overdub is a prime vector: an unsupervised player pulling something in from their own library. 08 cannot detect it; 08's contribution is that the binding makes it **attributable** — the pass is bound to a named human who accepted a brief. Routes to 09 (rights) and 24. | The declaration at delivery is the moment to ask. Exposure is 09's problem; attribution is 08's contribution to it. |
| **Player is a band/shared entity account** | Breaks the person↔track binding the record depends on, exactly as 08.05.04 Q-03 describes — and **worse here**, because there is no live session in which a human was observably present. A band account delivering an overdub is delivery-certainty about an *entity*, which is nearly content-free as credit evidence. | Inherits 08.05.04 Q-03. This feature is where that question is most acute; see Q-04. |
| **Player is removed from the project (07) mid-request** | Platform access to the bed is revoked. **The file on their disk is not** — and the product must say so plainly rather than implying a recall it cannot perform (DT-07). Passes already delivered remain; the request closes. | "This request was withdrawn." Producer sees what was delivered before withdrawal. The disclosure record already names what they hold. |
| **Producer cancels after the player has recorded but before delivery** | The work exists and the platform knows it was requested. Passes may still be delivered against the closed request and are preserved as evidence. Whether it is paid is **05's**; whether it happened is **08's**, and 08's answer is yes, with timestamps. | "This request was cancelled. Your passes are still yours — deliver them for the record?" |
| **Producer never listens and never accepts** | The take is in limbo: the player's payment and their provenance grade both hang on someone else's inbox. Delivery is recorded regardless — **acceptance is not required for the observation to be true** (08.05.04: unconfirmed ≠ invalid). An acceptance deadline is 05's to set. | Player: "Delivered Tuesday. Awaiting {Producer}." Record stands at `delivered-by`, unaccepted. |
| **Two of the player's devices upload the same pass** | Deduplicated on the pass's identity, not on file bytes (a re-render differs byte-wise). Never two credits, never two passes in the comp. | Single pass. |
| **Player delivers a non-audio or malformed file** | Rejected at the local check with a plain reason, before it consumes their upload. | "That isn't an audio file we can read." |
| **Player delivers an implausibly large file** | Accepted if it is genuinely a long multichannel pass — this is the mode where a 40-minute multitrack is legitimate. Bounded by the request's declared scope, not by a guess. | Progress, and a size warning against their own remaining upload, not a refusal. |
| **Player's take has no bed version stamp** (imported from outside the flow) | Cannot be attributed to a bed, so alignment has no reference. **Never silently placed at zero** (08.05.02 D-05). | "We can't tell which mix this was played to — place it manually." |
| **Request has no live monitor and the Producer later wants the higher grade** | Not retrofittable. A witness is only available while the pass happens — the same "present at the moment of truth" logic the thesis rests on, and the reason DT-09 matters at request time rather than after. | The grade is fixed at delivery. Offered *before* the pass, not after. |

### States

**Request lifecycle** — the state the two humans actually track:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Producer sent it | Player: brief + bed size/fidelity + deadline, with accept/decline. Producer: "sent, awaiting response." |
| Declined | Player declined | Producer: declined, with the player's reason if given. Recast via 04. |
| Delivering bed | Player accepted | Resumable download progress. **Disclosure recorded at this moment**, not at first play. |
| Pre-flight | Bed local | Overdub checklist: disk, sample rate, monitoring path, round-trip calibration. Not a gate (08.06 D-05) — but a skipped calibration is stated, never blank. |
| Ready | Pre-flight run | Play. **No network dependency from here**, stated in words. |
| Recording | Pass underway | Local recording indicator + elapsed + disk remaining. Network state irrelevant and shown as such. |
| Checking | Pass kept | Local checks running: signal, clipping, bed bleed, sample rate, version stamp. Seconds, at the instrument. |
| Pass rejected locally | A check failed | The specific fix. The pass is not deleted — the player decides. |
| Uploading | Passes queued | Resumable per-pass progress. Safe to close the app. |
| Delivered | Upload complete | "Delivered {timestamp}." Player is done; the record does not need the Producer to be true. |
| Aligned | Processing done | Passes placed against the bed + residual per pass + bed version. |
| Under review | Producer has it | Player: "awaiting {Producer}." Producer: passes, brief, grade, residual. |
| Revision round N | Producer opened a round | Brief delta + prior rounds intact and attributed. |
| Accepted | Producer accepted a pass | Counter-attested. Handed to 07 as a project asset, to 08.05.04 as a graded record, to 02 as evidence. |
| Expired | Deadline passed, nothing delivered | Producer: accepted / bed delivered / nothing delivered / expired — a timeline, not a blank. |
| Withdrawn | Producer cancelled or access revoked | Closed. Delivered passes preserved. Disclosure record names what the player still holds. |

**Canonical view states** — per the template:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Request opening; bed manifest fetching | Skeleton with the brief text first — it is the smallest and the most useful thing. Never a spinner over an empty page. |
| Empty | Producer has created no overdub requests on this project | Empty state with the CTA and the one fact that makes it make sense: "Overdub works on any connection, from anywhere. No latency limit." |
| Populated | Request active | Brief, bed version, deadline, pass list with per-pass state, grade, residual. |
| Error | Bed cannot be delivered / upload cannot resume / alignment failed | Named cause + the specific action. **Audio is never lost to an error** (08.05.02 D-03): passes remain local and downloadable, unaligned if necessary. |
| Partial | Some passes delivered, some not; or bed superseded mid-request; or alignment succeeded for some passes | Explicit gaps by name — "2 of 5 passes delivered", "played against v1, project is on v3", "1 pass unaligned." Never a summary that averages a gap away (08.01.02 D-03 arithmetic). |
| Stale | Pre-flight result or round-trip calibration aged since the run, or the rig changed | Age-labelled + re-run prompt (08.06 D-02). A stale calibration must never silently become the alignment offset. |

The player is notified of a new version with a section-granularity diff and **chooses** whether to switch — only they know if the change touches their part, and a forced re-download discards their work. A take against a superseded bed is delivered, aligned and **labelled, never discarded**. A changed *bar map* escalates the label, because the grid — not the mix — is what alignment depends on. |
| D-07 | **05 owns the commercial wrapper; 08 owns the mechanics and supplies the facts** | Resolves breadth-pass Q-02. Same split as CX-05 applies to technical blame. 08 never enforces a commercial deadline — it records timestamps and 05 decides what late costs. A platform that bins a late take has destroyed work to enforce someone else's policy. |
| D-08 | **Overdub is not inherently commercial** | A band member overdubbing on their own project is wrapped by 07 and touches 05 not at all. Identical mechanics, different wrapper. Follows from D-07 and prevents 05 from becoming a false dependency of the domain's shippable feature. |
| D-09 | **The bed is a package — bounce + tempo/bar map + 2-bar count-in + section markers + brief + version stamp** | A bounce alone is unplayable and unalignable (DT-11), and every one of its failures reads as the player's fault. |
| D-10 | **Producer attendance is optional — and is the provenance-grade selector, not merely a convenience** | Removes the calendar-intersection requirement, which is a real constraint on remote session work. **Amended at Step 5**: per DT-09, attending is what converts `delivered-by` into `observed-playing`. It is offered before the pass, never retrofittable after — the same "present at the moment of truth" logic as the thesis itself. |
| D-11 | **Every kept pass is checked locally before upload, while the player is still at the instrument** | Silent take, clipping, bed bleed, sample-rate mismatch and a missing version stamp are all silent to the player and all ruinous. In a live session an engineer catches them in ten seconds; in an overdub **nobody is listening** (DT-14). Checks name the specific fix (08.06 D-04), never an error code. |
| D-12 | **The pass is the unit of work; the player discards their own passes; the Producer chooses among the keepers; a player preference flag is advisory** | Comping is fourteen passes, not one take (DT-10). The Producer comps (`meta/personas.md`) — a player who chooses for them has deleted their job, and Producers routinely keep the pass the player hated. |
| D-13 | **Revision rounds accrete inside the request; the brief is immutable within a round** | The round history is the contested fact in a paid dispute, and earlier rounds remain evidence of work done. An edit mid-round would rewrite what someone was asked to do after they did it. |
| D-14 | **The system reports what it measured about the file and never characterises the performance** | Compensating a measured rig round-trip is arithmetic; quantising a performance is a creative decision belonging to the Producer (DT-12). A laid-back backbeat is not an error, and telling a musician the machine says they rushed is a fight the platform cannot win. |
| D-15 | **Bed fidelity is the Producer's choice; disclosure is recorded; recall is not promised** | The file is on the player's disk and cannot be recalled (DT-07). The honest product records who received which version at which fidelity and when — the thesis move applied to what left the building — and never implies a control it does not have. |
| D-16 | **Overdub needs its own pre-flight checklist, and needs it more than a live session does** | Different checks (disk, sample rate, monitoring path, round-trip calibration; no reachability or uplink), higher stakes (no witness) — DT-14. Consistent with 08.06 D-05: it informs and records; it does not block. |
