# Feature: Playable-Radius Map & Partner Discovery

> **Number**: 08.02.01
> **Parent**: [Playable Radius & Peer Matching](./08.02-playable-radius-peer-matching-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A map of the musicians you can physically play with in real time. Speed of light plus buffering caps
the workable radius at a few hundred miles, so a musician's real-time collaborator pool is a bounded,
knowable set — and nobody has ever shown it to them. This is the discovery surface D-15 cited as the
reason domain 08 survives: latency-aware matching is a product decision, not infrastructure.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sees their own playable boundary drawn on a map, and the musicians inside it filtered by instrument, genre and availability. Sees *why* the boundary sits where it does ("your radius is short because you're on Wi-Fi behind a slow first hop" vs "you're just far from everyone"). Controls their own location precision and whether they appear on other people's maps at all. |
| Producer | Full | Uses the map to staff a live remote session — "who can actually make this tracking date, given it has to be simultaneous." Sees the boundary from the *room's* endpoint rather than their personal one, because the session may run out of a studio. Can view candidate radii by instrument pair, since a bassist needs a tighter link than a vocalist. |
| Operator | Config | Configures their room's presence on the map — a studio advertising itself as a remote-session endpoint with a known-good rig and a published reach. Cannot edit the measured radius, only whether the room is listed and how it is described. Per `meta/personas.md` this persona's worst intentional behaviour is misrepresenting the room's specs, so the number they most want to inflate is exactly the one they cannot touch. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A fan-facing "musicians near you" map was considered and rejected — fan discovery belongs to Community (03) and Fanbase (20), and reusing this surface would re-import the boundary D-15 deliberately removed. |

## Behavior

The map's core claim is that your real-time collaborator pool is finite and locatable. Everything else
follows from getting the boundary honest.

Three things make it more than a distance circle:

1. **The boundary is not a circle.** Network topology is not geography. It follows peering and backbone
   routes, so the real shape is lobed — long reach along a good backbone, short reach across a
   badly-peered border a hundred miles away.
2. **The boundary is a set, not one line.** Per parent D-03, the radius differs by instrument pair. A
   drummer/bassist boundary is materially tighter than a vocalist/keys boundary.
3. **The boundary is a claim we must be able to defend.** Until probe history exists it is a prediction
   (08.02.02 corrects it), and the map must say which it is showing.

### Happy Path

1. Musician opens the map. System draws their playable boundary from the best source available, labelled as predicted or measured.
2. Musician filters by what they need — instrument, genre, availability.
3. Map shows candidates inside the boundary, each with a confidence on the pairing rather than a bare pin.
4. Musician opens a candidate; sees the pairing's expected verdict and tempo ceiling (08.01.02) before committing to anything.
5. Musician invites them to measure for real, or to a room, or — if the verdict is red — to an overdub-mode collaboration (08.07).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody inside the boundary | Genuine and common in rural areas. Must not be dressed up. The honest move is to route to Overdub Mode, where distance is irrelevant. | "Nobody within real-time reach of you right now. Overdub collaboration has no distance limit." + CTA. |
| Boundary is predicted, not measured | Map labels it as an estimate and does not draw a hard line — a crisp boundary implies a precision that priors do not have. | Soft/gradient boundary + "estimated — we haven't measured your connection yet." |
| Musician has hidden their location | They do not appear on others' maps but can still use their own. Asymmetric by design. | Their own map works; a note explains they are not discoverable. |
| Candidate's radius and viewer's radius disagree | Playability is a property of a *pair*, not of a person. The pairwise value governs; neither party's personal radius is authoritative. | Pairwise verdict shown on the candidate, not two competing circles. |
| Map used to infer where someone lives | Real risk — see parent Q-01. Location precision and probing are both disclosure surfaces. | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty (no radius) | Connection never measured | "We don't know your reach yet" + measure CTA. No speculative circle. |
| Empty (no candidates) | Radius known, nobody inside | Honest empty state + Overdub route. |
| Loading | Map/candidates loading | Skeleton map; boundary drawn last — a boundary that moves as data arrives looks like a bug. |
| Populated | Boundary + candidates | Lobed boundary, source label, filtered candidates with pairwise confidence. |
| Partial | Boundary measured, some candidates unmeasured | Measured candidates marked; unmeasured shown as estimates, visually distinct. |
| Error | Map/candidate load failed | Error + retry. Must not fall back to a stale boundary silently. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert expects the boundary to be **lobed, not circular** — network reach follows peering topology, not distance. Two people 50 miles apart across a bad peering boundary can be worse-connected than two people 300 miles apart on the same backbone. | Domain reasoning: internet routing is topological; a circle drawn on a map is a geographic metaphor for a non-geographic fact. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a circle would be wrong in both directions and users would catch it fast ("my mate across town is red but Manchester is green?"). The lobed shape is also the visible proof the product measured something rather than doing arithmetic on coordinates. |
| DT-02 | The empty map (nobody in reach) is the **most important state**, not an edge case — for rural users it is the default, and it is where the domain either routes them to Overdub Mode or loses them. | Reasoning: the map's honest failure rate is high in low-density areas, and a map whose common state is "nobody here" is a churn surface unless the empty state does work. | ✅ CONFIRMED — treated as a first-class state. This also reinforces 08.01.02 DT-03: the whole domain's job is sorting work into the right mode, not gatekeeping. |
| DT-03 | This map is really a Community (03) feature — "find musicians near you" is social discovery, and domain 03 already owns discovery. | Adversarial: the same invocation objection D-15 sustained against transport. | ❌ REJECTED — Community's discovery is about *affinity* (genre, scene, taste, people you'd want to know). This map is about *physics* (who your network can reach in under N ms), which is orthogonal — the answer sets barely overlap, and the pairwise-latency computation is not something 03 has or wants. Community consumes the `playable-with?` predicate as a filter; it does not own the map. Consistent with parent D-01. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The boundary is drawn lobed and topology-shaped, never as a distance circle | A circle is a confident lie. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Predicted boundaries render soft; measured boundaries render hard | The rendering must carry the epistemics. A crisp line implies measurement. |
| D-03 | Playability is a property of a pair, not a person | Avoids the incoherent "whose radius wins" question entirely. |
| D-04 | The empty state routes to Overdub Mode rather than apologising | For a large share of users this is the normal state; it must do work. See DT-02. |
