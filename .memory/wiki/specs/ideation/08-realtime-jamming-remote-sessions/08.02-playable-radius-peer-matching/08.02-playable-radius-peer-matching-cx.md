## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Route-Aware Correction ↔ Radius Map

**Relationship**: The map renders a boundary. That boundary comes from a geographic/topology prior
until probe history exists, then from measurement. The correction child owns the history and the
arbitration rule; the map child owns the rendering and must display *which* source it is showing. A map
that silently switches from "we guessed" to "we measured" without saying so is misleading in the
direction that costs users a wasted session.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees their boundary move over time and must be told why — an unexplained shrinking radius reads as the product getting worse.
- **Producer**: same, plus cares about the boundary for people they might hire for live sessions.
- **Operator**: sees their room's boundary corrected; this directly affects what they can honestly advertise, which is why it is read-only to them (they cannot inflate it).
- **Fan**: not affected — no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The radius value is owned by the correction child. The map is a pure reader. No write-back, so no merge strategy needed. The map may not cache a boundary past its stated freshness.
2. **Trigger chain**: New probe result → correction recomputes radius → map re-renders. Async. If correction fails, the map falls back to the prior and must label it as a prediction, not a measurement. Fail-visible, not fail-silent.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is deliberate — Operators can configure their room's *presence* on the map but cannot edit its measured radius. Per `meta/personas.md` this persona's worst intentional behaviour is misrepresenting the room's specs; a read-only measured radius is the guard.
4. **Notification fan-out**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether a materially shrunk radius notifies the user. It is bad news about their own connection and there is a real argument both ways.
5. **State transition conflict**: Sparse history is the live risk. A single bad probe on a transient network event must not permanently shrink someone's radius. The correction needs a confidence model, not a last-value-wins.

### CX-02: Route-Aware Correction ↔ Match Filter

**Relationship**: The predicate is the exported form of the same radius the map renders. Because four
other domains consume the predicate, a correction here has effects outside domain 08 — a Services
search result set changes because someone's ISP peering changed. This is the highest-blast-radius
relationship in the sub-domain and the reason the predicate must carry its own confidence rather than
being a bare boolean.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: may become invisible in other people's searches because of a network fact they never see. This is the sharpest edge in the sub-domain.
- **Producer**: gets a shortlist that silently excludes people. They need to know exclusion happened, not just see what survived.
- **Operator**: their room's bookability for remote sessions moves with it.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Single source of truth in the correction child. The predicate is derived.
2. **Trigger chain**: Correction → predicate flips → downstream search results change. Async, eventually consistent. A cached predicate in a consuming domain going stale is the real risk — the predicate must ship with a freshness stamp, not just a value.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — the predicate leaks a fact about person A's network into person B's search experience. That is a privacy surface and it is why Q-01 on the parent index is a security question.
4. **Notification fan-out**: No. A predicate flip must not notify the far side; person B should not learn that person A's connection degraded.
5. **State transition conflict**: A pairing can be `playable` in a booking flow and `not playable` by the time the session runs. Domain 05 (Services) owns the commercial consequence of that; this sub-domain owns telling the truth at booking time. See Q-03 on 08.01.02.

### CX-03: Radius Map ↔ Match Filter

**Relationship**: Two views on one judgment. The map is browse; the filter is search-time application
inside other domains. They must resolve from the same radius value at the same freshness, or the
product contradicts itself. This is a consistency contract, not a data flow.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: encounters both surfaces and will notice a disagreement immediately.
- **Producer**: same.
- **Operator**: encounters the filter (in bookings) more than the map.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Both read the same value. Neither writes. The contract is that neither may apply its own thresholds locally.
2. **Trigger chain**: None — they are siblings, not a chain.
3. **Permission intersection**: The map respects location-disclosure settings (Q-01); the filter must respect the *same* settings, or the filter becomes a location-disclosure side channel — someone could binary-search a person's location by varying their own. Flagged for `/create-prd-security`.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: Divergent caching between the two surfaces is the entire risk. One freshness policy, not two.
