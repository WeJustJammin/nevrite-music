# Feature: Per-Performer Cue Mixes

> **Number**: 08.04.02
> **Parent**: [Talkback & Cue Mixes](./08.04-talkback-cue-mixes-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Gives every performer their own headphone balance, because nobody plays well to someone else's mix. "More
me" is the single most-requested thing in any tracking session, which is why every modern cue system is
performer-controlled rather than engineer-controlled. In a network room this becomes both more necessary
(nobody can just point at their headphones) and harder (the sources arrive at different times).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Owns their own mix. Adjusts the level of every source they can hear — other performers, playback, click, their own input — and the balance is theirs alone; changing it affects nobody else. Per parent D-02, an engineer adjusting someone's headphones across a network with latency is worse than the problem it solves. |
| Producer | Full | Can build a starting mix for a performer and can see what each performer is hearing — which is genuinely useful ("the drummer can't hear the click, that's why they're rushing"). Can help but not override: a performer's adjustment always wins over the Producer's suggestion. |
| Operator | Config | Declares the room's cue infrastructure when their room is an endpoint — how many independent cue sends it has, whether it can honour per-performer mixes or only one shared foldback. A room with one headphone amp cannot deliver four mixes, and that is a room spec that belongs in 16. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into a jam room. |

## Behavior

`[PARTIAL]` — this feature has a real conceptual problem, logged as parent Q-01 and not glossed over
here.

**What is confidently known:**

The performer owns the mix. Sources are individually adjustable. The mix is room-scoped and
session-lifetime, populated at join time from the performer's portable profile (08.04.03). Talkback ducks
it transiently without modifying it (CX-01). The Producer can see it, seed it, and not override it.

**What is genuinely problematic:**

A cue mix in a *physical* room is a mix of sources that are all present *now*. A cue mix in a *network*
room is a mix of sources that arrived at different times. "More me" is trivially solvable — your own
input is local and has no network delay. "More drummer" is bounded by the same physics as everything
else in this domain: the drummer's audio is as late as the drummer's link.

So the cue mix does not have one coherent latency; it is a blend of a zero-latency local source and
several late remote ones. This is not a bug to fix — it is the domain's central constraint appearing
inside a feature that pretends to be a simple mixer. Whether a performer can build a *useful* mix out of
sources with heterogeneous delay is an honest open question, and it is Q-01.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: whether the mix exposes per-source delay; whether local
and remote sources are visually distinguished; whether deliberately *delaying* the local source to match
remote ones (aligning the performer's mix at the cost of self-monitoring latency) is offered — which is
the conductor model (08.01.03) reappearing at the individual level.

### Happy Path

1. Performer joins the room; their cue mix is populated from their profile (08.04.03) mapped onto the room's sources.
2. Performer adjusts — more themselves, less guitar, click up.
3. Mix applies only to them; nobody else's monitoring moves.
4. Performer plays.
5. At session end, they can save changes back to their profile — explicitly, never automatically.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A source arrives much later than others | The mix contains audio at different delays. Unavoidable. The question is whether the performer is told. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — likely: per-source delay indicator. |
| A performer joins mid-session | New source appears in everyone's mix at a sane default, not at zero. A performer who cannot hear the late-arriving bassist and does not know why is debugging instead of playing. | New source appears, flagged as new. |
| Room's cue infrastructure cannot deliver per-performer mixes | An Operator's single-foldback room physically cannot. The product must degrade honestly, not pretend. | "This room provides one shared mix." |
| Performer solos themselves entirely | Permitted — it is their mix and some players track that way. Not an error. | No warning. |
| Profile recall maps badly (stored sources absent) | Unmapped preferences are visibly unset rather than silently approximated. See CX-02. | "Some of your saved settings don't apply to this room." |
| Producer adjusts while performer adjusts | Performer wins. Per D-02, the last word on someone's headphones is theirs. | Producer sees their change was superseded. |
| Cue mix state lost on reconnect | Must survive a reconnect — rebuilding a mix mid-session is a real cost. Interacts with 08.08. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Default | No profile, fresh room | Neutral balance, all sources audible. Never a previous performer's mix. |
| Recalled | Profile applied | Mix from profile + flags on anything unmapped. |
| Adjusted | Performer changed something | Their mix; unsaved-change indicator against their profile. |
| Degraded | Room cannot deliver independent mixes | Honest statement of the room's limit. |
| Partial | Some sources not yet connected | Present sources adjustable; absent ones listed as not-yet-connected. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert expects a cue mix to be **performer-owned**, not engineer-owned — this is the settled convention in every modern cue system (Aviom, Hear Back, DAW-based cue sends) and reversing it is the mistake a software team makes because engineer-controlled is easier to model. | Domain reasoning: "more me" is the most-requested thing in tracking, and round-tripping it through an engineer over a network — where the request itself is delayed — makes a fast physical gesture into a slow remote negotiation. | ✅ CONFIRMED — performer-owned, Producer-assisted. Also drives D-02: performer adjustment always wins. |
| DT-02 | A network cue mix has **heterogeneous latency** — the local source has none and remote sources are late — which means it is not a mixer, it is a blend of things from different moments. Nobody raised this and it may be the feature's fatal flaw. | Domain reasoning: physical cue mixes blend co-present sources. This one cannot. The performer's own input is instant; the drummer's is 30 ms old. A "balance" between them is not the same object a musician thinks they are adjusting. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real property — **not** resolved as a design. It escalates to parent Q-01 (does a networked cue mix make sense at all?) and it is the honest reason this feature is `[PARTIAL]` rather than confidently specified. Refusing to paper over this is the point. |
| DT-03 | The cue mix should be built centrally and streamed to each performer, which is simpler to reason about. | Naive engineering framing. | ❌ REJECTED — central mixing adds a round trip to the performer's *own* voice: their input travels to the mixer and back before they hear themselves. Self-monitoring latency is the one delay musicians cannot tolerate at all (it is why hardware direct monitoring exists on every audio interface ever made). The local source must be mixed locally. This forces local mixing at each endpoint, which is parent Q-02 — recorded here because it looks like an architecture choice and is actually forced by a musical constraint. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The performer owns their mix; the Producer seeds and observes but never overrides | Settled convention, and remote round-tripping makes engineer-control worse than in a physical room. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | The local source is mixed locally, always | Self-monitoring latency is the one delay musicians cannot tolerate. Forced by physics, not chosen. See DT-03. |
| D-03 | Profile changes save explicitly, never automatically | One weird room should not permanently corrupt someone's default. |
| D-04 | Rooms that cannot deliver independent mixes say so rather than approximating | An Operator's single-foldback room is a real constraint; pretending otherwise fails at the worst moment. |
