# Feature: Talkback Channel & Ducking

> **Number**: 08.04.01
> **Parent**: [Talkback & Cue Mixes](./08.04-talkback-cue-mixes-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Lets the person running the session speak to the people playing it, without that speech ending up in
the recording. Every studio has solved this with a hardware button for sixty years, and every
general-purpose conferencing tool gets it wrong by treating all audio as equal. Talkback is not a chat
channel — it is a privileged, transient, targeted intervention into other people's headphones that must
be structurally incapable of reaching the take.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Producer | Full | Keys talkback — momentary (hold) or latching (toggle) — chooses who hears it (all performers, or just the drummer), sets ducking depth, and sees which performers are currently receiving. This is the primary instrument for running a room and it belongs to the person who runs it. |
| Musician | Read-only | Hears talkback and sees who is speaking and to whom. Sets how much talkback intrudes into their own mix (their monitoring is theirs, per parent D-02) but cannot mute the engineer entirely — an unreachable performer is a broken session. Cannot key talkback themselves; a performer with a talkback button is just a participant with a microphone, which is what the room already is. |
| Operator | Config | Declares whether their room, as an endpoint, has a talkback-capable rig — a physical control room with its own talkback that must interoperate rather than fight with the platform's. Does not key talkback for other people's sessions. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into a jam room; this is a professional production instrument. |

## Behavior

Three rules, all of them the kind a software team breaks by default:

**1. Talkback never enters the take** (parent D-01). This must be *structural*, not configured. "Record
everything, filter later" is a sensible software instinct and a catastrophic audio one — the engineer
saying "that was rubbish, go again" baked into a keeper take is unrecoverable damage, and the take you
lose to it is by definition the good one. The capture path must not be wired to receive talkback at all.

**2. Talkback ducks the cue mix.** The engineer's voice must be intelligible in headphones currently
full of loud music. Without ducking, talkback is technically present and practically useless.

**3. Talkback is targeted.** Speaking to one performer without the others hearing is routine — a private
word with a singer having a bad day is not something the whole room needs.

Momentary vs latching matters more than it looks: latching talkback left on is how an engineer's private
conversation reaches the room. Every hardware desk defaults to momentary for this reason.

### Happy Path

1. Producer holds the talkback key.
2. System ducks the targeted performers' cue mixes and opens the Producer's mic to them.
3. Performers hear the engineer clearly over ducked music; they see who is talking.
4. Producer releases; the mic closes and cue mixes recover to their prior state — unmodified.
5. Nothing about the exchange exists in the recording.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Talkback keyed during a take | Permitted and normal — this is what talkback is for. It must not enter the recording (D-01) and must not stop the take. | Performers hear it; the take continues. |
| Ducking fails or is slow | Talkback is still sent. An inaudible engineer beats a silent one. A slow duck loses the first word — which is usually "stop". | Talkback present, possibly quiet. |
| Latching talkback left on | The classic failure. Needs an unmissable indicator, and possibly a timeout. | Prominent persistent "TALKBACK LIVE" state — not a subtle icon. |
| Producer's mic is also a performance mic | Real when the Producer is playing. Talkback and the take then share a source, which is exactly where D-01 gets violated. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — likely: separate device required, or explicit warning. |
| Performer mutes talkback entirely | Not permitted. Their intrusion level is theirs; their reachability is not. | Level control bottoms out above silence. |
| Physical room with hardware talkback | The room's desk and the platform both duck, resulting in double-ducking or a fight. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — Operator declares the room's rig (see Role Lens). |
| Latency makes talkback arrive late | It does. "Stop" arrives after the bar it referred to. Bounded by the same physics as everything here. | Accepted limitation. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | Not keyed | Talkback key available (Producer); nothing for performers. |
| Live (momentary) | Key held | "Talking to {targets}" for Producer; "{Producer} is talking" for targets. |
| Live (latching) | Toggled on | Unmissable persistent live indicator — deliberately loud UI, because the failure it prevents is embarrassing and expensive. |
| Targeted | Subset selected | Producer sees targets; targeted performers see the exchange is not room-wide. |
| Unavailable | No mic / permission denied | Explicit error. A Producer who cannot reach the room must know immediately, not discover it by talking to nobody. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Talkback exclusion from the take must be **structural, not configured** — the capture path must be incapable of receiving talkback rather than set not to. | Domain reasoning: "record everything, sort it out later" is the correct default in almost all software and the wrong one here. A config flag is a thing that gets flipped, defaulted wrong on a new code path, or lost in a refactor; the cost of one failure is a ruined keeper take, and by definition you only notice on the take worth keeping. | ✅ CONFIRMED — architecturally separate paths, flagged to `/create-prd`. This is the single most important sentence in this feature file, and it is the one an implementer with no studio experience will not think of. |
| DT-02 | A domain expert expects **momentary as the default**, not latching — and expects a latched-open talkback to be screamingly obvious in the UI. | Domain reasoning: every hardware desk defaults to momentary because the failure mode (private conversation broadcast to the room) is embarrassing and common. This is a solved problem the software should inherit rather than rediscover. | ✅ CONFIRMED — momentary default, latching available, deliberately loud live indicator. Cheap to build, prevents a genuinely bad day. |
| DT-03 | Talkback is just voice chat with a mute button, so the transport cross-cut's existing voice channel covers it. | Adversarial: the D-15 invocation test — the pipe already carries voice. | ❌ REJECTED — voice chat is symmetric, persistent, and recorded by default. Talkback is privileged (overrides performers' mix control), transient (momentary by default), targeted (per-performer routing), ducking (modifies other channels), and structurally excluded from capture. Those are five properties voice chat does not have, four of which are *interactions with other features*. The transport carries the voice; the semantics are the feature. Same line D-15 draws for the domain as a whole. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Talkback exclusion from capture is structural, not configurable | A config flag is a thing that gets flipped. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Momentary is the default; latching is available with an unmissable live indicator | Inherited from sixty years of hardware convention for a good reason. See DT-02. |
| D-03 | Performers control talkback's intrusion level but cannot mute it | Their monitoring is theirs; their reachability is the session's. |
| D-04 | Ducking failure does not suppress talkback | An inaudible engineer beats a silent one. |
| D-05 | Talkback does not modify the underlying cue mix — it ducks over it transiently | Persisting the ducked state would erode performers' mixes across a session. |
