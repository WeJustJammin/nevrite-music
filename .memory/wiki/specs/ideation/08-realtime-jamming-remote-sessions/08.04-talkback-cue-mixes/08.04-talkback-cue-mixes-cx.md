## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Talkback ↔ Cue Mixes

**Relationship**: Talkback is not a channel alongside the cue mix — it is an intervention inside it. When
the Producer keys talkback, every targeted performer's cue mix ducks so the voice is intelligible, then
recovers. The routing is per-performer (talk to the drummer without the singer hearing), and the ducking
depth is per-performer too, since it interacts with each person's own balance. This coupling is the
reason these are one sub-domain rather than two unrelated features.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: keys talkback, chooses targets, sets ducking depth. This is their instrument for running the room.
- **Musician**: hears their mix duck and the engineer appear. Can set how much talkback intrudes into their own mix (per index D-02, their monitoring is theirs) but cannot silence the engineer entirely — an unreachable performer is a broken session.
- **Operator**: configures the room's talkback capability when their room is an endpoint. Does not key it.
- **Fan**: not affected. No fan path.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The cue mix is owned by the performer. Talkback does not modify it — it applies a transient duck over it. Nothing is written back, so a performer's balance survives every talkback intact. Getting this wrong (persisting the ducked state) would slowly destroy people's mixes over a session.
2. **Trigger chain**: Talkback keyed → targeted cue mixes duck → voice → release → recover. Synchronous and must be fast; a slow duck means the first word is lost, which is precisely the word that says "stop". If ducking fails, talkback must still be *sent* — an inaudible engineer is better than a silent one.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes. The Producer's talkback right overrides the performer's mix control for the duration of the key. This is a deliberate hierarchy: the person running the session must be able to reach the people in it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Performers see who is talking and to whom. Talkback to a subset must be visible to that subset — someone hearing the engineer while unsure whether the others heard it is a real confusion in physical rooms and does not need reproducing.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes — talkback during a take. Talkback must never enter the recording (index D-01), so it races the capture path. The capture must be structurally incapable of receiving it, not merely configured not to. Configuration fails; structure does not.

### CX-02: Recall ↔ Cue Mixes

**Relationship**: The profile stores what a performer wants; the room provides what exists. Recall maps
one onto the other and the mapping is lossy — a stored preference of "less guitar, more click" is
partially meaningless in a room with two guitars and no click. The honest behaviour is a best-effort
map with the unmapped parts visibly unset, rather than a silent approximation the performer then has to
discover by ear mid-take.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: owns the profile; benefits from recall; must be told what could not be mapped.
- **Producer**: sees that a performer recalled a profile (useful — it explains a mix they did not build) but does not manage it.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The profile is person-owned and lives at identity level. The cue mix is room-scoped and session-lifetime. Recall copies profile → mix; changes in the room do not write back unless the performer explicitly saves. Auto-saving every in-session tweak back to the profile would let one weird room permanently corrupt someone's default.
2. **Trigger chain**: Performer joins → profile fetched → mapped onto available sources → unmapped sources flagged. If the profile fetch fails, the mix starts at a neutral default and says so. It must never start at a *previous performer's* mix — an obvious bug with an unpleasant failure.
3. **Permission intersection**: The profile is private to the performer. The Producer sees the resulting mix, not the stored preferences behind it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None outward. The performer sees the mapping result.
5. **State transition conflict**: Mid-session source changes (a player joins late) leave the recalled mix incomplete. New sources should arrive at a sane default rather than silently at zero — a performer who cannot hear the late-arriving bassist and does not know why is debugging instead of playing.

### CX-03: Talkback ↔ Recall

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — talkback loudness and ducking depth are personal monitoring preferences
and belong in the portable profile alongside everything else. The open part is whether a performer may
carry a *minimal ducking* preference into a room where the Producer needs to be heard, which is a small
instance of the same authority question CX-01 answers.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: carries their talkback preference between sessions.
- **Producer**: may need to override it in the moment.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

| R-01 | 08.04.02 Cue Mixes | 08.03.01 Monitor Stream | Considered: let a remote listener pick a cue mix to hear. Rejected — different purposes with no shared state. A cue mix is a performer's working tool optimised for playing; a monitor stream is a judgment surface optimised for evaluating. A client listening to the drummer's "more me" mix would be judging a deliberately unbalanced mix. Also a cross-sub-domain pair, so it belongs in the domain CX if anywhere. |
