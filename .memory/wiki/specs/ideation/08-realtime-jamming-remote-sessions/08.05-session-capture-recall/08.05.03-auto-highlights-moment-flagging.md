# Feature: Auto-Highlights & In-the-Moment Flagging

> **Number**: 08.05.03
> **Parent**: [Session Capture & Recall](./08.05-session-capture-recall-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Finds the moment worth keeping. Nobody presses record on the magic take — it happens during the warm-up,
between the takes, while someone is still tuning, and by the time anyone realises it was good it is gone.
This feature makes flagging a moment cost nothing (one gesture, no interruption) and proposes candidate
moments afterward from what the session's own signals already reveal.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Flags a moment while playing with a single gesture that does not break the take. Reviews flagged and proposed moments afterward. Sees their own flags and — pending Q-01 — probably everyone's, since "that was the one" is a shared judgment in every real room. |
| Producer | Full | Same flagging, plus ownership of the review: which candidate moments become takes, which are discarded, which go to the project. This is the persona deciding what the session produced. |
| Operator | None | Not visible. What was good in someone else's session is not the room provider's business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into this domain. |

## Behavior

Two halves, and the cheap one is the valuable one:

**1. In-the-moment flagging (manual).** One gesture, mid-play, non-interrupting. This is the reliable
half: a human who just played something good knows it, immediately, better than any signal analysis will.
The design constraint is that it must cost nothing — a flag that requires stopping, or a dialog, or
attention, will not be used at the exact moment it matters. Per 08.03.03 D-02, it must never interrupt
anyone playing.

**2. Auto-proposal (inferred).** Afterward, the system proposes candidate moments from what it already
observed. Honest signals available without pretending to judge music:

| Signal | What it actually indicates |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Everyone was playing simultaneously | The band was locked in rather than one person noodling |
| A passage was played repeatedly then stopped | They got it and moved on — the last repetition is usually the keeper |
| Talkback was silent for a long stretch | The engineer had nothing to fix |
| Reactions/chatter immediately after a passage | Something happened |
| A flag from any participant | The strongest signal, by far |

**None of these judge whether the music was good.** They locate moments *worth reviewing*, which is a
much weaker and far more honest claim than "we found your best take". A system that claims aesthetic
judgment will be wrong loudly and stop being trusted; one that says "these six minutes look worth
hearing again" is useful and modest.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: whether any audio analysis is in scope at all, or
whether the metadata signals above are sufficient. The metadata signals require no DSP and no model,
which given `meta/constraints.md` matters.

### Happy Path

1. Session runs; capture is active (08.05.01).
2. Something good happens; a Musician flags it with one gesture, without breaking their playing.
3. Session ends. Alignment establishes the canonical timeline (08.05.02) and flags are re-anchored to it.
4. System presents flagged moments plus proposed candidates, with the reason for each proposal stated.
5. Producer reviews, keeps what matters, discards the rest; kept regions go to Projects (07).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Flag placed late (they realised after it happened) | The normal case, not an edge case. A flag means "the thing that just happened", so it must anchor to a window *before* the gesture, not at it. | Flag creates a region ending at the gesture, not starting there. |
| Alignment never completes | Flags degrade to approximate regions rather than vanishing. See parent CX-03. | "Approximate — this session wasn't aligned." |
| Auto-proposals are all wrong | Likely at first. Proposals must be dismissible and must not crowd out manual flags, which are the reliable signal. | Manual flags primary; proposals clearly secondary. |
| Nothing was flagged and nothing proposed | Common — most sessions have no magic take. Must not manufacture one to appear useful. | "Nothing stood out. The full session is here." |
| Flag on a take later discarded | The flag dies with the take. | Silent. |
| Everyone flags the same moment | The strongest possible signal. Should be visibly stronger, not deduplicated into a single anonymous mark. | Region shown with everyone who flagged it. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle (in session) | Capture running | An always-available flag gesture and nothing else. No UI competing for a player's attention. |
| Flagged | Someone flagged | Quiet acknowledgement; no interruption to anyone. |
| Processing | Session ended | "Looking through the session." |
| Reviewed | Candidates ready | Manual flags first; proposals second, each with a stated reason. |
| Empty | Nothing flagged or proposed | Honest empty state. Full session available. |
| Approximate | Alignment incomplete | Regions marked approximate. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A flag must anchor to a window **ending at the gesture**, not starting at it — because a human flags a moment *after* realising it was good, which is by definition after it happened. Anchoring at the gesture captures the silence following the thing you wanted. | Domain reasoning: this is exactly why retrospective record exists on hardware loopers and why "capture the last 30 seconds" is the standard idiom for this problem. The naive implementation places a marker at the button press and misses the take entirely. | ✅ CONFIRMED — a retrospective window is the feature. Getting this backwards makes it useless in a way that is not obvious until someone tries to use it. |
| DT-02 | The system must **not claim aesthetic judgment**. It locates moments worth reviewing using metadata signals (simultaneity, repetition-then-stop, talkback silence, flags) and says so — it does not find "your best take". | Reasoning: aesthetic claims fail loudly and publicly, and one confidently-wrong "here's your best take" destroys trust in every subsequent proposal. The weaker claim is defensible, is achievable from metadata the session already produces, requires no DSP or model, and is genuinely useful. | ✅ CONFIRMED — proposals state their reason, always. "Everyone played continuously for 4 minutes and nobody spoke afterward" is a fact the user can evaluate; "this is your best take" is a claim they can only resent. |
| DT-03 | Auto-highlights is the valuable half and manual flagging is a fallback for when the detection misses. | The framing implied by the candidate's name ("Rehearsal & Jam Capture with **Auto**-Highlights") — automation as the headline. | ❌ REJECTED — inverted. Manual flagging is the reliable signal: a human who just played something good knows it immediately and with certainty no inference will match. Auto-proposal is the fallback for when nobody remembered to flag. Building the automation first would produce a clever feature resting on a missing cheap one. The one-gesture flag is also nearly free to build, which makes the inversion embarrassing as well as wrong. Recorded because the candidate list's own name pushes an implementer toward the mistake. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A flag anchors to a retrospective window ending at the gesture | Humans flag after realising. See DT-01. |
| D-02 | Manual flagging is primary; auto-proposal is the fallback | Inverts the candidate's own framing. See DT-03. |
| D-03 | The system never claims aesthetic judgment; every proposal states its reason | "Worth reviewing" is defensible and achievable. "Your best take" is neither. |
| D-04 | Nothing is manufactured to appear useful — an empty session says so | Most sessions have no magic take. |
| D-05 | Talkback metadata is a signal; talkback audio is never touched | Preserves 08.04.01 D-01 while still using what the room knows. |
