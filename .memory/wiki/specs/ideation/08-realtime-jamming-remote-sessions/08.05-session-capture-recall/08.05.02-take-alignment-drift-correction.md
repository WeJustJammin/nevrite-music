# Feature: Take Alignment & Clock Drift Correction

> **Number**: 08.05.02
> **Parent**: [Session Capture & Recall](./08.05-session-capture-recall-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Makes four independently-recorded tracks into one multitrack. Local-first capture (08.05.01) produces
pristine individual takes and a collective mess: each endpoint started at a different instant and runs on
its own crystal, so the tracks are misaligned at the start and drift apart as the session runs. This
feature establishes the canonical timeline, places every track on it, and corrects the drift — and
without it, local-first capture yields a pile of files rather than a session.

The feature's governing fact, established at depth below: **distributed endpoints share no acoustic
content**, so none of the standard multitrack-alignment techniques transfer. Alignment is only possible
against a reference that was *recorded at capture time*. That makes alignment a decision made **before**
the session, not a process run after it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Producer | Full | Receives the aligned multitrack and owns the result. Nudges a track manually when the automatic result is wrong; forces a re-run when a late track arrives; explicitly accepts a low-confidence alignment (acceptance is an act, never a timeout). Seals the aligned set, which is what releases the timing witness and hands the multitrack to 07. This is their deliverable and they are the persona who discovers months later if it was never right. |
| Musician | Read-only | Sees whether **their own** track aligned, by which method, and at what confidence — and, when it failed, what to do about it (re-upload, or hand it to the Producer to place). ## Behavior

Two distinct problems that a naive implementation conflates:

**1. Offset** — the tracks have different zeros. Everyone hit record at a different moment. This is a
constant per track and is the easy half.

**2. Drift** — the tracks run at *different rates*. Two consumer interfaces nominally at 48 kHz are not
actually at the same 48 kHz; each free-runs on its own crystal. Consumer crystal tolerance is commonly
±20 to ±100 ppm, so two endpoints can differ by up to ~100–200 ppm relative. The arithmetic is not
subtle:

| Relative rate error | 5-minute take | 1-hour session |
|---|---|---|
| 20 ppm | 6 ms | 72 ms |
| 50 ppm | 15 ms | 180 ms |
| 100 ppm | 30 ms | 360 ms |

A 5-minute take that ends 30 ms out is audibly loose on any transient material. An hour-long session at
100 ppm ends over a third of a second out. Drift is not fixable by sliding the track — the track has to
be resampled to a common rate.

**Correcting only the offset is the trap.** It produces a multitrack that is perfectly aligned at the
downbeat and progressively wrong thereafter — which sounds fine on a check of the first bar and falls
apart in the chorus. The failure is delayed, which is what makes it dangerous.

**Alignment is non-destructive** (parent CX-01): the output is an offset and drift model stored beside
the audio, never baked in. Alignment decisions get revised, and baking makes revision impossible.

**A failed alignment must never lose audio.** Unaligned tracks are recoverable by hand; lost tracks are
not.

### The Reference Ladder

Alignment places a track by comparing it to a reference **that the endpoint's own clock recorded**. This
is the mechanism: when the endpoint records the reference, the reference's *apparent duration in that
file* directly reveals the endpoint's rate error. One recording yields both offset and drift. This is why
the reference question (Q-01) dominates the feature.

| Tier | Reference | Requires | Yields | Target residual | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **Embedded timing reference** — the session's shared timing signal recorded into each endpoint's local file | 08.01.03 Q-03 answered yes; capture wired to record it | Offset **and** drift | ≤ 1 ms | High |
| **R2** | **Timing witness correlation** — each endpoint's local, pre-talkback recording of the inbound ensemble mix, correlated against the other endpoints' tracks | Witness enabled at capture time (see below) | Offset **and** drift | 1–5 ms | Medium |
| **R3** | **Timestamps + measured directional delay** (08.01.01) | Nothing extra | Offset **only** | ±tens of ms | **Not an alignment** |
| **R4** | Nothing usable | — | — | — | Failed → manual |

**R3 is not a tier of alignment — it is the trap wearing a tier's clothing.** It cannot measure drift, so
by D-01 it cannot produce an aligned result. An R3 outcome is published as **unaligned with a placement
hint**, never as an aligned track. This is the ladder's most important property: it has only two rungs
that produce alignment, and the bottom of the ladder is honesty rather than a worse answer.

### The Ladder Is a Pre-Session Commitment, Not a Runtime Fallback

**You cannot fall back to R2 if nobody recorded a witness.** Every rung requires something to have been
recorded *while the session was running*; nothing on this ladder can be retrofitted afterward. Therefore:

- The session's alignment method is **selected before capture starts** and verified in 08.06 Pre-Flight,
  alongside disk headroom. A session that starts with no reference and no witness is a session whose
  takes are already unalignable — and that fact is knowable at minute zero and undiscoverable at minute
  forty.
- If the primary reference (R1) is unavailable for the session, the timing witness (R2) is **enabled at
  capture time**, as a deliberate act, not summoned later.
- Pre-flight reports the session's alignment tier in plain language before anyone plays: *"Takes from
  this session will align to about ±1 ms"* / *"…to about ±5 ms"* / *"**Takes from this session cannot be
  aligned automatically.**"*

### The Timing Witness Must Be Tapped Pre-Talkback

The witness is a local recording of the inbound ensemble mix, used only to measure time. Two independent
constraints force it to be tapped **before** the talkback injection point:

1. **Structural talkback exclusion** (domain D-07, parent D-04, 08.04.01 D-01). Talkback is targeted into
   performers' cue mixes by design. A witness tapped from what the performer *heard* therefore records
   the engineer's voice — which is exactly the constraint the domain declares must be structurally
   impossible, violated by a feature nobody would think to check.
2. **Correlation would break anyway.** Talkback *ducks* the cue mix (08.04.01). A post-talkback witness
   carries gain modulation synchronised with the engineer talking — amplitude steps that corrupt the
   correlation the witness exists to serve.

If a pre-talkback tap is not available at an endpoint, that endpoint has **no witness**, not a
compromised one. R2 is unavailable there and pre-flight says so.

The witness is a measurement artifact, not an asset: it is never mixed, never offered for download, never
handed to 07, and it is discarded when the aligned set is sealed. Its quality requirement is *timing
only* — recording the network-degraded inbound mix as a **timing witness does not violate D-02**, which
forbids the network stream as the **master**. A witness is not a master; it is never heard. (Consistent
with parent R-01, which rejected the *outbound* stream and explicitly allowed a rough reference mix.)

### Confidence Tiers

Confidence is the measured residual after correction, end-to-end across the take — not a guess:

| Tier | Residual | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| High | ≤ 1 ms (≈48 samples @ 48 kHz) | Trustworthy. What R1 actually delivers. |
| Medium | 1–5 ms | Usable; flagged for a check. Below where transient material flams. |
| Low | 5–20 ms | Prominently flagged. Audible on drums. |
| Failed | > 20 ms, or no fit | Not published as aligned. Approaches the domain's own ~25–30 ms live desync ceiling (D-15) — an alignment error the size of the entire live playability budget is unambiguously broken. |

**The reference track is labelled `reference`, never `High`.** The endpoint that defines the timeline has
zero offset and zero drift *by definition* — that is a tautology, not a measurement. Displaying it as
100%-confident invites the Producer to trust the one number on the screen that means nothing, and it is
most misleading in the common case where the Producer is also playing and the reference is their own
track.

### Relative vs Absolute Timeline

Alignment makes tracks consistent **with each other**, against the reference endpoint's clock. It does
not make them correct against absolute time. If the reference's own crystal runs 50 ppm fast, the whole
multitrack is 50 ppm fast — inaudible in isolation (0.005%, far below any pitch or tempo perception
threshold) and irrelevant until the multitrack meets a grid:

- **07 Projects**: a DAW tempo grid drifts against the audio — ~12 ms over a 4-minute song at 50 ppm.
  Small, real, and maddening to diagnose.
- **08.07 Overdub**: a later overdub cut to a true-rate click drifts against the bed.

The system must therefore **declare which guarantee it delivered** — `relative` (internally consistent) or
`absolute` (disciplined to network time) — because 07 and 08.07 need to know which one they received. A
consumer that assumes absolute and receives relative has an undiagnosable bug. Whether absolute is
achievable is Q-04.

### Fragments

A track arrives as multiple fragments whenever a recorder paused or restarted — a suspended browser tab,
a reconnect (08.08), a manual stop. This is common, not exceptional, and it is handled by three rules:

1. **Each fragment is aligned independently.** It gets its own offset. Never concatenate fragments and
   align the result as one file — concatenation destroys the gap and makes everything after it wrong.
2. **The gap is preserved as real silence.** Closing the gap is the destructive error. The silence is
   what actually happened.
3. **Drift rate is inherited across fragments of the same track.** Drift is a property of the **clock**,
   not the take — every fragment of one track came from the same crystal. A fragment too short to measure
   drift against (under ~30 s, where a 50 ppm rate error produces ~1.5 ms — below the noise floor of the
   measurement) inherits the rate measured from its siblings. This is what makes a 4-second fragment
   alignable at all.

If a track's *only* fragment is too short to measure and it has no siblings, drift is unmeasurable → the
track is published unaligned with a placement hint (an R3 outcome), never as aligned.

### Nominal Rate ≠ Drift

A participant who recorded at 44.1 kHz in a 48 kHz session has a **nominal rate mismatch**, not drift.
The distinction is not academic: 44.1 vs 48 is 88,400 ppm; drift is ~50 ppm. They are more than three
orders of magnitude apart, and feeding a rate mismatch to a drift estimator produces either a failure or
an absurd, confidently-wrong rate correction. **Nominal rate reconciliation always precedes drift
measurement**, and the two are reported separately — they have different causes and different fixes.

### Stability Under Re-Run

Alignment re-runs when a late track arrives (08.05.01: someone's laptop was closed for a week). Re-running
**must not move already-placed tracks.** New information places the new track; it does not relitigate the
old ones. Re-placing accepted tracks would invalidate the Producer's spot-check and silently re-anchor
every highlight (CX-03) — the Producer would have no way to know their verification had expired.

A manual nudge is an offset adjustment layered **on top of** the model, stored separately and preserved
across every re-run. An automatic pass that stomps a Producer's manual correction destroys their work
silently, which is the worst available failure shape.

The single exception: if the **late arrival is the reference track itself**, the timeline changes and
every track is re-placed. This is a re-alignment of the session, and it is announced as one — the
Producer's prior acceptances are explicitly invalidated rather than quietly overwritten.

### Happy Path

1. Pre-flight (08.06) establishes the session's alignment tier before anyone plays and states it in plain
   language: *"Takes from this session will align to about ±1 ms (recorded reference)."*
2. Session runs. Each endpoint records its own track and the session's timing reference through its own
   clock (R1), or its pre-talkback timing witness (R2).
3. All local tracks upload (08.05.01).
4. System reconciles nominal sample rates — any 44.1/48/96 kHz mismatch is resolved and reported before
   drift is touched.
5. System establishes the canonical timeline from the session's reference and labels that track
   `reference`.
6. For each track: offset resolved against the reference, then drift measured at multiple anchor points
   across the take's full length — never from the endpoints alone.
7. System fits a rate model per track (linear, or piecewise where the residual demands it), applies it
   non-destructively, and measures the **residual** end-to-end.
8. System publishes the aligned set: per-track method, offset, drift in ppm, residual in ms, and
   confidence tier — plus an explicit `relative` or `absolute` timeline declaration.
9. Producer is notified; spot-checks; nudges anything the automatic pass got wrong.
10. Producer **seals** the set. Highlights re-anchor to the canonical timeline (CX-03), the timing witness
    is discarded, and the multitrack hands off to 07.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A track has no usable reference | Cannot be aligned automatically. Presented as unaligned with a manual tool — never silently placed at zero, which looks aligned and is not. | "We couldn't align {track} — place it manually." |
| **Drift is non-linear** (thermal, power-state change, CPU throttle) | A single linear rate correction is wrong. Real, and worse on laptops that throttle mid-session. Handled by measuring at multiple anchors across the take: if the residual after a linear fit exceeds 1 ms, the model escalates to piecewise segments anchored at the inflections. Reported honestly — a 6-segment model is a signal the rig was misbehaving. | "This track's clock wandered — corrected in 6 segments." Residual shown either way. |
| **A participant paused/restarted their recorder mid-session** | Track is now N fragments. Each aligned independently; gaps preserved as real silence; drift rate inherited across fragments of the same clock. Never concatenated-then-aligned. | "4 fragments placed, 2 gaps preserved" — with the gaps visible on the timeline, not closed. |
| Alignment succeeds but is wrong | The dangerous case: it looks done. Residual is measured and exposed so a low-confidence alignment is checked rather than trusted. | Per-track confidence and residual, prominently. |
| Not all tracks uploaded | Align what exists; show the gap explicitly. Waiting silently for a track that will never arrive is not a strategy. | Partial set with named missing tracks. |
| Offset corrected, drift ignored | The trap. Aligned at the top, wrong by the chorus. | Must not happen — R3 outcomes publish as *unaligned*, never as aligned (D-01). |
| **Session had no reference and no witness** | Not recoverable after the fact — nothing was recorded to align against. This must be caught at pre-flight, where it is a decision; discovering it here means the session is already lost. | At pre-flight: "Takes from this session cannot be aligned automatically." Here: tracks preserved, unaligned, downloadable. |
| **Nominal sample-rate mismatch** (44.1 in a 48 session) | Detected and reconciled *before* drift measurement. Never fed to the drift estimator — 88,400 ppm vs 50 ppm. | "Recorded at 44.1 kHz — resampled to the session rate." Reported separately from drift. |
| **Dropped buffers during capture** | Produces a step discontinuity that mimics drift: a track 30 ms short could be 50 ppm over 10 minutes, or one dropped buffer. Multi-anchor measurement distinguishes a smooth ramp from a step. Steps are corrected piecewise, anchored either side. | "Discontinuity at 4:12 — corrected." Flagged as a rig fault, not drift. |
| **A track is silent** (wrong input selected, mic muted, phantom power off) | No content to correlate; nothing to align. Distinct failure from "no reference" — this is a capture rig fault that 08.06 should have caught, and alignment is a horrible place to discover it. | "{track} contains no audio — this looks like a capture problem, not an alignment one." Routed back to pre-flight guidance. |
| **The reference endpoint's own track is unusable** | The timeline's definition is intact (the reference signal was still distributed and recorded by everyone else) even if the reference player's audio is broken. Alignment proceeds; the reference *player* has a capture failure, which is 08.05.01's problem, not the timeline's. | Session aligns; the reference player is told their take failed. |
| **The reference track arrives late**, after others were accepted | The only case where re-running re-places everything. The Producer's prior acceptances are explicitly invalidated and re-requested, never quietly overwritten. | "The reference take arrived — the session has been re-aligned. Please re-check." |
| Producer nudges a track, then a late track triggers a re-run | Nudge is preserved. It is stored as a layer on top of the model, not as a replacement for it. | Nudge survives visibly; re-run does not touch it. |
| Producer nudges a track while a re-run is in flight | Per-track lock. The re-run may not write a track the Producer is holding; the nudge wins and the re-run's result for that track is discarded rather than merged. | Nudge applies. No silent overwrite. |
| **Two tracks from the same endpoint** (a player recording two inputs) | Share one clock, therefore one drift rate. Measuring them independently and getting two different rates is a signal the measurement is wrong, not that the clocks differ. Rates are fitted jointly. | Single rate for the endpoint. A disagreement is reported as a measurement fault. |
| **Producer is also the reference and also playing** (multi-hyphenate) | Their track is the timeline. It shows `reference`, never a confidence score — a tautology displayed as a measurement is a lie told to the one person judging the result. | Labelled `reference` with an explicit "this track defines the timeline" note. |
| **A track cannot be aligned to the session at all** (no timing relationship whatsoever) | Weak evidence the audio did not come from this session (see DT-10). Recorded as a provenance signal for 08.05.04. **Never surfaced as an accusation** — a broken reference produces an identical symptom, and false fraud accusations are socially unrecoverable. | "We couldn't align {track}" — the neutral message. Identical to the broken-reference case, deliberately. |
| Solo session — one endpoint, one track | Nothing to align. The machinery does not run and no confidence theatre is shown. A confidence score on a set of one is noise. | "Single track — no alignment needed." |
| **Extremely long session** (3+ hours) | Drift is large (~360 ms at 100 ppm) but the *rate* is no harder to measure; a long take gives a better rate estimate, not a worse one. Thermal non-linearity is the real risk over hours, handled piecewise. | Normal. Segment count may be higher. |
| Every track is low confidence | Points at the *reference*, not at the tracks — a common-mode failure has one common cause. Must be reported as a session-level fault rather than N independent track faults. | "This session's timing reference looks unreliable" — one message, not four. |
| Alignment runs before uploads finish | Blocked. Alignment does not begin on a set known to be incomplete unless the Producer explicitly requests a partial pass. | "Waiting on {names}" — with an explicit "align what we have" action. |
| Overdub session (08.07) | The bed is an embedded reference recorded through the player's own clock — R1 by construction, and the peer-drift problem does not arise (08.07 D-02). A residual playback-vs-capture drift exists only when the bed plays out of a different clock than the take records into, which is a single-endpoint check, not a peer problem. | High confidence, R1. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Empty** | No tracks uploaded yet | "No takes yet." Session's alignment tier from pre-flight, so expectations are set before anything exists. |
| **Waiting** | Uploads incomplete | Who is missing, by name. Explicit "align what we have" action — never a silent wait. |
| **Loading / Aligning** | Processing started | Per-track progress and the current stage (rate reconciliation → offset → drift → residual). Stages are named because a stall in "drift" means something different from a stall in "upload". |
| **Populated — Aligned (High)** | R1, residual ≤ 1 ms, all tracks | Aligned multitrack. Per-track method, offset, drift (ppm), residual (ms). Reference track labelled `reference`. Timeline declared `relative`/`absolute`. |
| **Populated — Aligned (Medium/Low)** | R2, or residual > 1 ms | Same, plus an explicit "check this" flag on the affected tracks. Never silent. Acceptance is an act, never a timeout. |
| **Partial** | Some tracks unalignable, or some never uploaded | Aligned set + the unaligned tracks with a manual tool + named missing uploads. The three are visually distinct — "not uploaded", "uploaded but unalignable", and "aligned" are different problems with different fixes. |
| **Error — session-level** | Common-mode failure: reference unreliable | One session-level message, not N track messages. Points at the cause. |
| **Error — no reference** | No R1, no witness | Tracks preserved, unaligned, downloadable, with a placement hint if R3 data exists. Audio is never lost to an alignment failure. Explains that this was determined at pre-flight. |
| **Sealed** | Producer accepted | Read-only. Highlights re-anchored, witness discarded, handed to 07. Re-opening is an explicit act that re-invalidates downstream anchors. |

The marginal fit improvement is sub-millisecond; the cost is that verification means nothing. New information places the new track; it does not relitigate the old ones. The one exception — the *reference* arriving late — genuinely changes the timeline and is therefore announced as a re-alignment with acceptances explicitly invalidated. |
| DT-13 | **Nominal sample-rate mismatch will be mistaken for drift.** | Failure analysis of the estimator's input domain. 44.1 vs 48 kHz is 88,400 ppm; drift is ~50 ppm — more than three orders of magnitude apart. | ✅ CONFIRMED — an estimator fed a rate mismatch either fails or returns an absurd correction *confidently*. Reconciliation precedes measurement, and the two are reported separately because they have different causes (wrong setting vs physics) and different fixes (re-export vs resample). |
| DT-14 | **Dropped buffers mimic drift** and a single-rate model will absorb them wrongly. | Failure analysis: a track 30 ms short can be 50 ppm over 10 minutes (a ramp) or one dropped buffer (a step). Both are "short". A linear fit over the endpoints cannot tell them apart — and will smear a step across the whole take, making every moment slightly wrong instead of one moment obviously wrong. | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is why drift must be measured at **multiple anchors across the take**, not from its endpoints. It also unifies the machinery: steps, fragments, and thermal non-linearity all resolve to the same piecewise model. One mechanism, three problems. |
| DT-15 | **The reference track's confidence is a tautology, not a measurement** — and it is displayed to the person least able to question it. | Reasoning about the multi-hyphenate: the Producer is frequently also playing, and frequently the timing reference. Their track has zero offset and zero drift *by definition*. | ✅ CONFIRMED — showing `High` (or 100%) for the reference is a lie told by the UI to the one person judging the whole result, about the one track they most want to be fine. Labelled `reference`, never scored. Small detail, and exactly the class of thing that decides whether a Producer trusts the confidence numbers at all — one number they can tell is meaningless discredits the rest. |
| DT-16 | **Alignment makes tracks consistent with each other, not correct against absolute time** — and the difference only surfaces in another domain. | Intersection analysis with 07 and 08.07. If the reference crystal runs 50 ppm fast, the whole multitrack is 50 ppm fast: inaudible (0.005%) and irrelevant — until it meets a tempo grid in 07 (~12 ms slip over a 4-minute song) or an overdub click at true rate (08.07). | ✅ CONFIRMED — the timeline's guarantee must be **declared** (`relative` vs `absolute`), because a consumer that assumes absolute and receives relative has a bug that is undiagnosable from inside 07: the audio is fine, the tracks agree with each other, and the grid is mysteriously wrong. Declaring the guarantee costs one field; not declaring it costs someone a week. Whether `absolute` is achievable is Q-04. |
| DT-17 | Alignment could run continuously **during** the session rather than after it, so the result is ready at the downbeat's end. | Reasoning: latency-to-result is a real product quality, and the data arrives progressively. | ❌ REJECTED — the tracks do not exist during the session. Local-first capture (D-01/08.05.01) means the audio is on four laptops and does not upload until afterward, by design; that is the whole point of local-first. There is nothing to align in real time. Rejected as *incoherent with the architecture* rather than merely undesirable — worth recording because "why isn't this live?" is the obvious question and the answer is structural, not a scoping choice. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Drift correction is mandatory, never optional. A method that cannot measure drift cannot produce an "aligned" result. | Offset-only alignment fails late and quietly. See DT-01, DT-02. Makes R3 a non-tier. |
| D-02 | Alignment is non-destructive — an offset/drift model beside the audio, never baked in | Alignment decisions get revised; baking makes revision impossible. |
| D-03 | A failed alignment preserves the audio, unaligned and downloadable | Unaligned tracks are recoverable by hand; lost tracks are not. |
| D-04 | Per-track confidence is always exposed, as a **measured residual** rather than a score | The dangerous failure is an alignment that looks finished and is wrong. A residual in ms is checkable; a percentage is not. |
| D-05 | Unalignable tracks are shown as unaligned, never silently placed at zero | Zero looks like a decision. It is not. |
| D-06 | **The alignment method is committed to before the session and verified at pre-flight (08.06)** | Every rung of the ladder requires something recorded at capture time. Nothing here is retrofittable. See DT-07. |
| D-07 | **The timing witness is tapped pre-talkback, or there is no witness** | Two independent reasons: the domain's structural talkback exclusion, and gain modulation that corrupts correlation. See DT-06. |
| D-08 | The timing witness is a measurement artifact — never mixed, delivered, or downloadable — and is discarded at seal | It is a recording of a room, made for arithmetic. Retaining it as an asset creates a consent surface for no benefit. See Q-05. |
| D-09 | **Drift is measured at multiple anchors across the take, never from its endpoints** | Endpoints cannot distinguish a ramp (drift) from a step (dropped buffer), and a linear fit smears a step across the whole take. See DT-14. |
| D-10 | **Nominal rate reconciliation precedes drift measurement**, and the two are reported separately | 88,400 ppm vs 50 ppm. Different causes, different fixes. See DT-13. |
| D-11 | **Fragments align independently; gaps are preserved as silence; drift rate is inherited within a track** | Concatenation destroys the gap and corrupts everything after it. One clock, one rate. Resolves Q-03. See DT-08. |
| D-12 | **A re-run never moves an already-placed track** — except when the reference itself arrives late, which is announced as a re-alignment | Stability beats optimality: silent re-placement invalidates the Producer's verification and re-anchors highlights with no visible event. See DT-12. |
| D-13 | **A manual nudge layers on top of the model and survives every re-run** | An automatic pass that silently stomps a Producer's correction is the worst available failure shape. |
| D-14 | **The reference track is labelled `reference`, never given a confidence score** | Its zero offset and zero drift are definitional, not measured. See DT-15. |
| D-15 | **The timeline declares whether it is `relative` or `absolute`** | 07's tempo grid and 08.07's click are consumers that need to know which guarantee they got. See DT-16. |
| D-16 | **Alignability is recorded to 08.05.04 as evidence, never surfaced as an accusation** | Broken reference, silent input, and fabricated audio are indistinguishable here. See DT-10. |
| D-17 | **Drift rates are never carried across sessions as a per-rig prior** | Rigs change silently; a stale prior is a confidently-wrong answer in exactly the case it was built to help. See DT-09. |
| D-18 | **A common-mode failure is reported once, at session level** | Every track low-confidence means the reference is bad. Four identical warnings hide the one cause. |
