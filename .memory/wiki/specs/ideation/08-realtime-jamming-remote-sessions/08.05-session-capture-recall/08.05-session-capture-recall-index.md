## Overview

Keeps what happened. Records every participant's audio locally at full quality rather than capturing the
network-degraded stream, aligns those tracks afterward despite independent clocks, surfaces the moment
worth keeping, and — most importantly for the thesis — records *who was actually there playing what*.

**This is where domain 08 meets D-18.** A network session is the most instrumentable room that has ever
existed: the platform *is* the transport, so it knows with certainty who was connected, for how long, and
which track came from which human. That is provenance captured at source, with a confidence no physical
room can offer, as a byproduct of the session running. Per `meta/problem-statement.md`, the root cause of
music's most litigated failure is "absence at the point of truth" — and here, absence is impossible.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: four capabilities that feed each other. Capture produces
per-participant tracks; alignment makes them usable; highlights mark which parts matter; the attendance
record turns "these are four audio files" into "these four humans played these four parts." Remove
alignment and capture yields unusable stems. Remove the attendance record and the whole thing is a
recorder, not a provenance instrument.

**Merged from candidates**: the sweep listed "Rehearsal & Jam Capture with Auto-Highlights" (05) and
"Multitrack Capture of Network Sessions" (06) as separate children. They are one thing — capturing what
happened in a network session — with highlights as a feature inside it, not a parallel product. Merged
per the Node Classification Gate.
