# Feature: Local-First Multitrack Capture & Upload

> **Number**: 08.05.01
> **Parent**: [Session Capture & Recall](./08.05-session-capture-recall-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Records each participant's audio where it is made — at full quality, before it touches the network —
then uploads it afterward. The audio that crosses a network arrives with jitter concealment, packet loss
and codec artifacts baked in permanently; capturing *that* produces a flawless recording of the network
having a bad day. What is worth keeping never crosses the wire at all.

The feature exists for the Producer, who per `meta/personas.md` is the persona who *knows* who played
what and has never had a tool that records it at the moment it is true. Capture is that tool's audio half:
it produces the per-human tracks that 08.05.04 binds into the provenance record, and it does so as a
byproduct of the session running rather than as an act of admin. Its second job — less obvious and more
important than it looks — is to make the *state* of every take an explicit fact, so that a track which
never arrives is a named, attributable condition rather than an unanswered message in a group chat.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their local recording of their own playing, at their interface's real quality. Sees an unambiguous capture indicator, elapsed time, and headroom **in minutes of recording remaining** (D-17). Owns their own track absolutely: may decline capture of their own endpoint before or during the session (D-16), and after the session chooses exactly one of Upload / Withhold / Discard (D-09) — but may never start or stop capture for anyone else, and may never capture other participants. Per `meta/personas.md` this persona has been on both ends of stems held as leverage; D-10 makes the lever visible rather than removing it, because it cannot be removed. |
| Producer | Full | Starts and stops session capture and sets take boundaries (D-06). Sees every endpoint's arm/record/finalise/upload state **by name**, never as "a participant" (08.08 D-03). Receives the assembled multitrack and hands it to alignment (08.05.02). May nudge a Pending take; may not compel it. When they are also playing — the multi-hyphenate case `meta/personas.md` calls structural — they hold the capture control and an instrument simultaneously, which is why start/stop must not require a focused window and the indicator must be readable without looking (DT-08). This is the persona the personas file names as the capture point; capture is the instrument that makes the record exist without extra admin. |
| Operator | Config | Declares their room's capture capability when the room is an endpoint: local disk, interface channel count, sample rate/bit depth, and whether the room's own multitrack rig is the capture source rather than a browser (D-13). Sees capture and upload state for sessions in their room; never sees who played what — that is not the room provider's business. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and not reachable. No fan path into a jam room exists anywhere in domain 08 (domain Role Matrix; CX R-04) — per `meta/personas.md` the Fan has no professional stake, will not tolerate professional-tool complexity, and per D-13 brings a moderation population with no business in a room containing unreleased masters. A Fan encounters this feature's output only years downstream, as a released record via domain 12, by which point nothing of this feature is visible in it. |

## Behavior

**Local-first is the whole design** (parent D-02). Each endpoint records its own input at full quality to
local storage. The network stream is monitoring, never the master.

This inverts the naive design (record centrally, it is simpler) and buys three things:

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---------|---------------|
| No network artifacts | The take is what the microphone heard, not what survived the wire |
| No dropout gaps | A network failure ruins the *monitoring*, not the recording |
| Full quality regardless of bandwidth | Someone on a poor connection still delivers a usable take |

And it costs three things, honestly:

| Cost | Why it is real |
|------|---------------|
| Alignment is now mandatory | Independent clocks, independent zeros — see 08.05.02 |
| Upload is a separate, failable step | The take exists on a laptop that may be closed, wiped, or never opened again |
| Local disk is a dependency | A browser recording multitrack audio to a full disk fails in the middle of the good take |

**A network failure must not stop local recording.** This is the property that makes local-first
genuinely valuable rather than merely purist: when the session falls apart, the takes survive. It is also
the connection to 08.08 — the interruption ruins the experience, not the asset.

**Talkback never enters capture** (parent D-04, 08.04.01 D-01) — structurally, not by configuration.

### What capture owns, and what it does not

| Capture owns | Owned elsewhere |
|---|---|
| The take: the audio file, its format, its boundaries, its integrity | The canonical timeline and drift model (08.05.02) |
| The facts observed while recording — signal presence, clip events, gaps, segment timestamps | Whether presence was contribution (08.05.04, and finally domain 02) |
| The endpoint→track binding, and its **strength** (observed vs attested) | The identity behind the endpoint (domain 01) |
| The take's lifecycle state, including refusal to upload | The billing/contract consequence of a withheld take (domain 05, per 08.08 D-02) |
| The behavioural requirements a capture host must satisfy (Q-01 R1–R4) | Which host satisfies them (`/create-prd-stack`) |

### The take model

A session is **N takes, not one recording** (D-06). Capture produces exactly one file per
`(session, take, endpoint)`. A three-hour tracking session is a dozen takes; a ninety-minute free jam is
one. The Producer sets the boundaries; the boundary is a real object because a highlight (08.05.03), an
alignment run (08.05.02) and an attendance fact (08.05.04) all need to know *which take*.

Where an endpoint's recording is interrupted and resumes inside one take, capture emits **segments with
session-relative timestamps and an explicit gap marker** — never a concatenation. A concatenated file is
a lie about continuity, and it is the lie that makes 08.05.02 Q-03's fragmented-track problem
undiagnosable. Capture emits segments and gaps; how alignment consumes them is 08.05.02's decision, not
this feature's.

### Format and headroom — the arithmetic

The reference case is 24-bit / 48 kHz, which is `3 bytes × 48,000 = 144 kB/s` per channel, i.e.
**8.64 MB per channel-minute** or **518 MB per channel-hour**. This is arithmetic, not an estimate:

| Case | Local disk consumed |
|---|---|
| One musician, one channel, 3-hour session | ~1.5 GB on their machine |
| Four solo endpoints, 3-hour session | ~1.5 GB each, ~6.2 GB across the session |
| Operator room endpoint, 8 channels, 3-hour session | ~4.1 GB on that one machine |

**Pre-flight requires free space ≥ 2 × `channels × 8.64 MB/min × planned minutes`** (D-04). The 2× is not
padding: sessions overrun, and an operating system on a genuinely full disk fails in ways that have
nothing to do with audio and everything to do with losing the take.

During capture, headroom is displayed as **minutes of recording remaining**, computed as
`free_bytes ÷ (channels × 144,000)`. Never gigabytes (D-17) — minutes is the unit the work is measured
in, and "4.2 GB free" requires a musician to do division while playing.

**Full quality is not negotiable** (D-07). A lossy capture is a *better-degraded* copy than the network's,
which is a difference of degree rather than of kind, and it forfeits the exact thing the category charges
for. Where a rig genuinely cannot sustain full quality (a phone endpoint), a reduced-quality capture is an
**explicit, per-endpoint, marked** fallback recorded on the take — never a silent default.

### The two capture paths

**Observed capture** — the platform records the endpoint's input itself. The endpoint→track binding is
observed, because the platform made the file.

**Delivered capture** — the endpoint's own rig recorded it (a Producer's DAW, an Operator's console) and
delivers the file bound to the session participant (D-13). Per `meta/personas.md`, the Producer's work
happens inside a DAW and any flow requiring them to leave it will not be used; a studio with a console
will not record through a web page. Insisting on being the recorder loses precisely the endpoints that
produce the best audio and run the most sessions.

The binding for a delivered file is **attested, not observed**, and the take records which (DT-05). This
is not a formality: 08.05.04's entire value is that its record is an observation rather than a claim, so a
path that produces a claim must be labelled as one. Provenance strength is a recorded property of each
take, not a constant of the feature.

### Attribution when the endpoint is a room

The endpoint→human binding is **1:1 only for solo endpoints** (D-12). A room endpoint — the Operator's
case, or simply two players sharing one interface — produces N channels from M humans. Auto-binding all
channels to the connecting account would put one human's identity on another human's track: a manufactured
credit, which is the exact fraud `meta/personas.md` names as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour
and the exact failure 08.05.04 D-02 exists to prevent.

So: capture records channel count as a fact. Where channel count > 1, or the endpoint is declared a room,
**per-channel attribution is required and is marked as declared, never observed.**

### Capture state is visible to everyone, in both directions

Every participant sees continuously whether capture is rolling (D-19). Both directions matter and one rule
serves two problems:

- **Rolling → everyone knows.** A room that records people who do not know it is recording is a
  surveillance product (Q-03).
- **Not rolling → everyone knows.** The session where four people each assumed someone else was
  recording is a classic, and it destroys the one artifact the session existed to produce.

### Uploads

**Uploads never run while the endpoint is in a live room** (D-08). Uploading take 1 while take 2 is being
played consumes the upstream bandwidth carrying the monitor stream — the platform would be sabotaging its
own remote-monitoring product (08.03) with its own capture product, and the damage lands on the take
currently being performed. Upload begins the moment the endpoint leaves the live room, automatically, with
no user action. Under 08.07 Overdub there is no live room, so uploads run immediately.

Uploads are **resumable and never abandoned** (D-03): exponential backoff from 2 s, capped at 5 minutes,
retrying indefinitely while the app is open and resuming on next open. There is no dead-letter, because a
dead-lettered take is a lost take, and restarting a 40-minute multitrack upload from zero is how takes get
abandoned by humans rather than by systems.

### The six take states — non-upload is never ambiguous

This is D-09, and it is the resolution of the feature's hardest problem. "Not uploaded" is useless as a
state because it conflates conditions with opposite meanings: is their laptop shut, or are they holding
out? That ambiguity is what poisons the relationship, and it is precisely the kind of fact this product
exists to make explicit at the moment it is true.

| State | Meaning | Who set it |
|---|---|---|
| **Uploading** | In flight, resumable | System |
| **Pending** | The take exists locally and has not been sent. No decision has been made. | System |
| **Uploaded** | The platform has it, integrity verified | System |
| **Withheld** | The owner has explicitly chosen not to send it. A first-class, deliberate, attributable act. | Track owner |
| **Discarded** | The owner deleted their local take | Track owner |
| **Lost** | The local file is gone — storage evicted, machine wiped, browsing data cleared. Not a choice. | System |

**Withheld and Lost must be distinguishable**, because one is a decision and the other is an accident, and
treating them alike accuses the unlucky and shelters the strategic.

**The participant owns the local take and the platform never compels it** (D-10). It is on their machine;
no amount of product design changes that. What the platform *can* do is refuse to let the withholding be
silent. Per `meta/personas.md`, withholding stems as leverage is the Producer's worst intentional
behaviour, and local-first hands the identical lever to every participant. The honest response is not to
pretend the lever does not exist but to make its use a named, visible, attributable state.

**Crucially, withholding withholds the asset, not the fact.** 08.05.04's attendance record is an
observation, sealed independently of the audio: it already says this human was connected, bound to this
track, audible for this long. A withheld take costs the Producer a mix; it does not erase the provenance.
That decoupling is what defuses the leverage problem down to a commercial dispute (domain 05, per
08.08 D-02) instead of a provenance failure.

### How hard the platform pushes for a Pending take

Specified, because "persistent prompt" is not a specification:

1. Upload is automatic on leaving the room — the lazy path is the correct path, per the personas file's
   explicit instruction.
2. If the app is closed with a Pending take, the next open shows a prompt that **blocks that session's
   surface only** — not a dismissible toast, not the whole product. It names the take, its size, its
   session, and **who is waiting for it, by name**.
3. The prompt **does not expire.** It persists until the take reaches Uploaded, Withheld, or Discarded.
4. The Producer sees the Pending state by name and may send **one** nudge per take. The platform itself
   never nags on a schedule. Naming the collaborator who is waiting is the honest lever; a notification
   cadence is a dark pattern, and it would train people to ignore the one prompt that matters.
5. Discard and Withhold both require an explicit confirmation naming the consequence — this take will not
   exist in the multitrack — because both are irreversible in practice.

### Behavioural requirements a capture host must satisfy

Not architecture; the requirements against which `/create-prd-stack` must test any option (Q-01):

| # | Requirement | Why it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **Durable local storage** the host OS/runtime will not reclaim without asking | A take evicted under storage pressure is indistinguishable from a withheld one (DT-09) |
| **R2** | **Multichannel input at declared quality**, with exclusive or reliable device access | The Operator's room case and any 8-channel endpoint fail without it |
| **R3** | **Execution while unfocused, backgrounded, or the machine is asleep-adjacent** | The Producer is playing; the browser tab is not front-most. A throttled recorder silently produces a fragmented take |
| **R4** | **Survival of the application closing**, with the take intact and resumable | The take must outlive the session UI, or the upload problem becomes a loss problem |

### Happy Path

1. **Before the session**, pre-flight (08.06) confirms per endpoint: input device present and acquirable, channel count, sample rate and bit depth, local storage durability granted (R1), and free disk ≥ 2 × the estimate for the planned duration. Producer sees a per-participant result; per 08.06 D-05 it informs and does not block.
2. Producer starts capture for the session.
3. Each endpoint **arms**: acquires its device, confirms format, reserves headroom, reports Armed. Capture is **not gated on the slowest endpoint** — each begins recording as it arms, and alignment absorbs the offsets that creates. The Producer sees who is armed and who is not, by name.
4. Each endpoint records locally at full quality. Every participant sees the capture indicator, elapsed time, and headroom in minutes (D-17). Every participant can see capture is rolling (D-19).
5. Session runs. Network conditions affect monitoring; they do not affect the recordings. Signal presence and clip events are recorded as **latched facts on the take** (D-14), with timestamps.
6. Producer stops capture. Each endpoint **finalises**: the file is closed and made playable before anything else happens. Finalising is uninterruptible and always yields a playable file (D-18).
7. The take is sealed as `(session, take, endpoint)` carrying format, duration, channel count, segment timestamps and gap markers, the signal/clip fact log, the alignment reference, and its binding strength (observed or attested). The binding is handed to 08.05.04.
8. **Uploads do not start.** The endpoint is still in the live room (D-08).
9. Each participant leaves the room → their upload begins automatically, resumable, no action required. Progress is shown as **time remaining**, not percent.
10. Producer sees every take's state by name: Uploading / Pending / Uploaded / Withheld / Discarded / Lost.
11. When the set is complete — or the Producer proceeds with explicitly named gaps — it is handed to alignment (08.05.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Network drops mid-take | Local recording continues uninterrupted. The take survives; only the experience was damaged. This is the payoff. | "Connection lost — you are still recording." First message, before anything else (08.08 D-01). |
| Endpoint loses contact and never regains it | Capture **continues**. No auto-stop (D-15) — over-recording is recoverable by alignment; under-recording is not. At the session's scheduled end + 30 min, a **prompt**, never a stop. Disk is the only legitimate bound. | "Still recording. The session was scheduled to end at {time} — stop?" |
| Local disk crosses 15 min remaining | First warning, dismissible. 15 min is enough to end a take deliberately. | "About 15 minutes of recording left." |
| Local disk crosses 5 min remaining | Second warning, unmissable, persistent. | "5 minutes of recording left — free space or stop the take." |
| Local disk exhausted mid-take | Capture stops. The file is **finalised valid up to that point** (D-18). Never a silent truncation; never a corrupt file. | Hard, loud, persistent error naming the take and its recovered duration. |
| Browser/OS reclaims the local recording | The vicious one (DT-09). The take becomes **Lost**, not Withheld — the distinction is the whole point of D-09. Prevented up-front by R1: capture that cannot obtain durability declares so before the session rather than discovering it after. | "Your take for {session} is gone — your browser reclaimed the storage." Labelled Lost, not blamed on them. |
| Capture cannot obtain storage durability | Capture **does not silently start** (D-11). It either declares it cannot guarantee the take, or does not arm. A recorder that may lose the take without saying so is worse than no recorder, because people rely on it. | "This browser won't guarantee your recording survives. Use {alternative} or record in your own rig." |
| Browser tab backgrounded / throttled | R3. The recorder is throttled and the take fragments. Capture **must detect the discontinuity and emit a gap marker** with timestamps, never concatenate. A fragmented take that looks continuous is undiagnosable (08.05.02 Q-03). | "Your recording was interrupted at {time} — keep this tab in front." Gap visible on the take. |
| Laptop sleeps / lid closed mid-take | Same class. On resume, capture opens a **new segment** with a fresh session-relative timestamp. The gap is a recorded fact. | Gap marker + honest duration; the take reports recorded time, not wall time. |
| Audio interface unplugged mid-take | Capture for that endpoint stops immediately and finalises what exists. It **never writes silence to keep the timeline pretty** — silence is indistinguishable from playing nothing, and the take would lie. Other endpoints are unaffected; the session continues. | "Your interface disconnected at {time}. Take saved up to that point." Named, loud, immediate. |
| Another app seizes the interface (DAW opens, exclusive mode) | Same as unplugged, and more common — the Producer opening their DAW mid-session is a normal act. Pre-flight names this specifically (08.06 D-04). | "{App} took your interface. Take saved up to {time}." Names the app and the fix, per 08.06 D-04. |
| Sample rate changes mid-session | The remainder plays at the wrong speed — a failure that sounds like a tape machine, not like an error. Capture detects the change, closes the segment at the old rate, opens a new one at the new rate, and marks it. Never one file at two rates. | "Your interface changed to {rate} at {time}." Marked on the take; alignment is told. |
| Channel silent for the whole take (phantom power off, wrong input) | The career-ruining classic. If a capturing channel stays below −60 dBFS for 30 continuous seconds while other endpoints show signal, warn **that participant locally** — never the room, which would interrupt a performance. Silence is legitimate (a player resting), so it is a warning, never an error. The fact is recorded and is what 08.05.04 consumes as "whether they were audible". | Peripheral, persistent, local: "No signal on your input for {duration}." |
| Take clips | Unrecoverable — the take *is* what the microphone heard, so a clipped take is a ruined one. Digital clip = 3 consecutive full-scale samples. The indicator **latches** (D-14), per sixty years of console convention (the same precedent 08.04.01 D-02 invokes): a transient clip light nobody was looking at is a clip nobody knows about. | Latched clip indicator with timestamp; survives until acknowledged. Clip events logged on the take. |
| Browser cannot record multichannel at quality | Real constraint (R2). A browser is not a DAW, and the Operator's room case exists precisely because of this. | Honest capability statement; route to a room, a local agent, or delivered capture (D-13). |
| Room endpoint: N channels, M humans | Per-channel attribution required, marked **declared, not observed** (D-12). Auto-binding all channels to the connecting account manufactures credits. Accountability for a wrong attribution is Q-04. | Operator/Producer assigns each channel to a human before the set is sealed. Unattributed channels ship as unattributed — never guessed. |
| Producer records in their DAW instead | Delivered capture (D-13) — a first-class path, not a workaround. Binding is attested; the take says so. | "Delivered from your rig — binding attested, not observed." Visible, not hidden. |
| Delivered file does not match the observed session envelope | Duration, channel count and signal-presence pattern are all known from the session. A file materially outside that envelope is suspect. | "This file doesn't match what we saw in the session — check you sent the right one." Flagged, not silently accepted; not auto-rejected either, because honest mismatches exist. |
| Delivered file is a different performance entirely | Substitution — a better take recorded elsewhere, or someone else's playing. The platform carried a degraded copy of the real performance and can correlate against it (DT-06). Detection, not prevention: the take is flagged and its binding strength downgraded. Routes to 24. | "This doesn't sound like what we heard in the session." Attributable flag on the take. |
| Upload interrupted | Resumable from the byte, not the beginning (D-03). Backoff 2 s → 5 min cap, indefinite, resumes on next open. Never dead-lettered. | Resumable progress; time remaining, not percent. |
| Upload on a metered/cellular connection | A 6 GB upload over a phone hotspot is a bill, not a transfer. Never starts silently on a connection the OS reports as metered. | "This take is {size}. You're on a metered connection — upload now, or wait for Wi-Fi?" Explicit choice, remembered per connection. |
| Upload attempted while still in the live room | Suppressed (D-08). It would consume the upstream carrying the monitor stream and damage the take currently being performed. Queued, not cancelled. | "Uploads start when you leave the room." |
| Participant closes app before upload | The take is Pending. On next open, a non-expiring prompt blocking only that session's surface, naming the take, its size, and **who is waiting for it by name**. The Producer may nudge once. The platform never nags on a schedule. | "Your take from {session} hasn't been sent. {Producer} is waiting for it." Persistent until Uploaded / Withheld / Discarded. |
| Participant explicitly withholds | **Withheld** — a named, deliberate, attributable state (D-09, D-10). Their track, their call, and the platform cannot compel it. But the withholding is visible to the session rather than being an unanswered message. **The attendance record (08.05.04) is unaffected** — they withheld the asset, not the fact. Consequence is a domain 05 contract matter, per 08.08 D-02. | Owner: explicit confirmation naming the consequence. Producer: "{Name} withheld their take." Not "missing", not "pending". |
| Local take is gone on return (cleared data, wiped machine) | **Lost**, not Withheld. Distinguishing them is the point — one is an accident, the other a decision, and conflating them accuses the unlucky. | "Your take from {session} is no longer on this machine." Labelled Lost to the room. |
| Upload arrives after alignment already ran | Late uploads **re-open alignment**. The published aligned set is not silently mutated — a new revision is produced (08.05.02 D-02, non-destructive). | Producer: "{Name}'s take arrived — realigning. Revision {n}." |
| Duplicate upload of the same take (two tabs, two devices) | Idempotent on `(session, take, endpoint)`. A second upload of the same take is a no-op unless the stored copy failed integrity, in which case it replaces it. | Nothing. Silent and correct — this is a case where quiet is right, because nothing was lost. |
| Uploaded file fails integrity check | Detected **on arrival**, never at mix time. The take stays Pending and the local copy is not released for deletion. | "That upload arrived damaged — retrying." Automatic; local copy retained. |
| Malicious oversized upload | The expected size is *known* from the observed session (duration × channels × format). A file materially beyond the envelope is rejected before it consumes storage. | "This file is far larger than the session could have produced." Rejected with the arithmetic shown. |
| Participant joins mid-session | Their local recording starts late; alignment handles the offset. Normal, not an error. | Normal. Producer sees their arm time. |
| Participant leaves and rejoins mid-take | Two **segments** inside one take, with session-relative timestamps and an explicit gap — never one file that pretends to be continuous. Supplies 08.05.02 Q-03 from the capture side; how alignment consumes segments is theirs to decide. | Gap visible on the take, with its real duration. |
| Producer stops capture while a participant is disconnected | The stop never reaches them; they keep recording (D-02, D-15). Their file runs long. Alignment truncates against the canonical timeline. Over-recording is recoverable; the alternative is not. | Producer: "{Name} was disconnected at stop — their take may run long." Participant: still recording, prompted at scheduled end + 30 min. |
| Producer is also a player (multi-hyphenate) | The structural case per `meta/personas.md`. They hold capture control while their hands are on an instrument. Start/stop must not require a focused window; the indicator must be readable without looking (DT-08). They are also both the person waiting for takes and the owner of one — the D-09 states apply to them identically, with no self-exemption. | Same indicator, same states. No privileged path over their own take. |
| One human, two endpoints (laptop + phone) | An identity may hold multiple endpoints. Each endpoint's track binds to `(identity, endpoint)` and they are **never auto-merged** — two endpoints is two tracks, and merging them would assert a mix decision the system has no basis for. | Two tracks, both theirs, both named by endpoint. |
| Participant declines capture | Legitimate (D-16). Their endpoint does not record. 08.05.04 records attendance without a track binding — "honestly weaker", per its own edge table. | Room sees capture is not rolling for that participant (D-19). No track, no ambiguity. |
| A participant tries to capture the session unilaterally | Refused (D-16). Session capture is the Producer's; a participant may decline their own but never capture others. The platform cannot stop someone running a separate recorder — but it will not *be* the instrument of a non-consensual recording. | "Only {Producer} can start capture for this session." |
| Nobody started capture; everyone assumed someone had | The classic session-destroying failure, and the reason D-19 runs in both directions. Capture-not-rolling is continuously visible to every participant, not just the Producer. | Persistent, room-wide: "Not recording." |
| Local clock is wrong or deliberately changed | Local wall-clock timestamps are untrustworthy and are never the reference. Capture stamps segments with **session-relative time from the platform's clock**. | Nothing — the participant never had to be right about this. |
| Talkback bleeds into a capture | Must be structurally impossible. See 08.04.01 DT-01, D-01. | N/A — prevented by architecture. |
| Participant deletes their uploaded take | The asset goes; **the attendance record does not** (08.05.04 D-01 — an observation, not a claim). Whether deletion is even available post-upload is Q-02. | Q-02 gates the action. The record's survival is not in question. |
| Session ends with zero takes | Empty is a real state, not an error: capture never started, or every participant declined. The attendance record exists; the audio does not. | "No audio was captured for this session." Stated plainly, at the end — and D-19 meant it was never a surprise. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | Not capturing | Ready + headroom in minutes + **"Not recording", visible to the whole room** (D-19). |
| Arming (Loading) | Capture started, device being acquired | "Getting your input…" — per endpoint, not gated on the room. |
| Arm failed (Error) | Device unavailable, no durability (R1), no exclusivity (R2) | Names the specific fix, not the error (08.06 D-04). Capture does not silently proceed (D-11). |
| Recording | Armed and rolling | Unambiguous indicator + elapsed + minutes remaining. Readable without looking (DT-08). |
| Recording (disconnected) | Network lost, capture continues | "Still recording" — reassurance at the exact moment of panic, and the first thing shown (08.08 D-01). |
| Recording (degraded) | Signal absent > 30 s, or a clip has latched | Local, peripheral, persistent. Never interrupts the room. |
| Finalising | Capture stopped | Writing the local file; uninterruptible; always yields something playable (D-18). |
| Queued | Finalised, endpoint still in the live room | "Upload starts when you leave the room" (D-08). |
| Uploading | Endpoint left the room | Resumable progress per track, as **time remaining**. |
| Uploaded | Integrity verified | Confirmed; safe to close. The only state that says that. |
| Pending | App closed / upload not started, no decision made | Non-expiring prompt naming the take, its size, and who is waiting. |
| Withheld | Owner's explicit choice | Named and attributable to the room. Not "missing". |
| Discarded | Owner deleted the local take | Named. Distinct from Withheld — they did not keep a copy either. |
| Lost | Local file gone without a decision | Named as an accident, never as a refusal. |
| Incomplete (Partial) | Some takes not Uploaded | Producer sees exactly who is missing, by name, **and in which state** — the states are the information. |
| Empty | Session ended, no takes | "No audio was captured for this session." |
| Error (disk) | Disk exhausted | Hard, loud failure + the recovered duration. Never a silent truncation. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert expects capture to be **local-first**, because the network stream carries irreversible damage — concealment, loss, codec artifacts — and recording it produces a permanent record of the transport's failures rather than of the performance. | Domain reasoning + category precedent: JackTrip and Source-Connect both record locally; Source-Connect's entire premium proposition is fetching the lossless local original for anything the network mangled. The whole category converged here, which is strong evidence. | ✅ CONFIRMED — local-first, network stream is never the master. The candidate list said "multitrack capture of network sessions", which reads as capturing the network stream — precisely the wrong architecture, and worth naming as a near-miss. |
| DT-02 | A network failure must **not** stop local recording — and this is the property that makes local-first valuable rather than merely correct. | Reasoning: the session's worst moment is a dropout. If the takes die with the connection, the domain's biggest failure mode destroys its most valuable output. If they survive, an interruption is an inconvenience instead of a loss. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it reframes 08.08 (Interruption & Reconnect) entirely: the interruption becomes an experience problem, not an asset problem, which is a far more survivable class of failure. |
| DT-03 | Since the platform is already carrying the audio, central capture is simpler and avoids the whole upload problem. | Naive engineering framing, and genuinely tempting — it eliminates alignment, upload failure, and disk dependency in one move. | ❌ REJECTED — it would capture exactly the degraded stream (DT-01), making the recording hostage to the worst network moment of the session. Worse, it fails silently: the file plays, so nobody knows the artifacts are permanent until the mix. The costs of local-first (alignment, upload, disk) are all *visible* failures with recoverable outcomes; central capture's cost is an *invisible* failure with an unrecoverable one. Choosing loud recoverable failures over quiet unrecoverable ones is the right trade every time. |
| DT-04 | The endpoint→human binding is **1:1 only for solo endpoints**. A room endpoint — the Operator's console, or simply two players sharing one interface — produces N channels from M humans, and auto-binding them all to the connecting account is the default any implementer would write. | Domain reasoning: a band jamming in one rehearsal room and joining a remote session is one connection and four humans. The Operator's `Config` role in the domain Role Matrix exists *because* rooms are endpoints, so the multi-human endpoint is not an exotic case — it is the Operator's entire case. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the sharpest hazard in this feature. Auto-binding would put one human's identity on another human's track, manufacturing a credit: exactly the fraud `meta/personas.md` names as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour, committed accidentally, by the system, at scale, and stamped "verified". Capture records channel count as a fact; where count > 1, per-channel attribution is **required and marked as declared, never observed** (D-12). This is new information for 08.05.04 Q-03 — the binding breaks on shared *endpoints* as well as on shared *identities*, and the two failures are independent. |
| DT-05 | **Capture and binding are separable, and the platform's value is the binding, not the recording.** The provenance mechanism is track↔human↔session; that the platform also made the file is incidental to it. | `meta/personas.md`, Producer unique constraint: "their work happens inside a DAW, not a browser. Any capture flow requiring them to leave the session and go fill in a web form will not be used." And the Operator's room "is not going to record through a web page" — already conceded in this file's own Role Lens, without its consequence being drawn. | ✅ CONFIRMED — delivered capture (D-13) is a first-class path. If the platform insists on being the recorder, it loses precisely the endpoints with the best audio and the most sessions, and thereby loses the provenance too — over an implementation preference. **The caveat is load-bearing**: a delivered file's binding is *attested*, not *observed*, and 08.05.04's entire value rests on the observation/claim distinction. So provenance strength becomes a recorded property of each take rather than a constant of the feature. That is more honest than either pretending delivered files are observed, or refusing them and capturing nothing. |
| DT-06 | **The network stream is a bad master and an excellent witness.** The platform already carried a degraded copy of every participant's audio; correlating an uploaded local take against what the network heard proves the take is the take. | Reasoning from the feature's own integrity gap: local-first means the platform never sees the master until someone chooses to send it, so it has no independent basis to know the file it receives is the performance it observed. But it does — it was listening the whole time. Verification and mixing have wildly different quality requirements: a recording far too degraded to mix is trivially good enough to answer "is this the same performance?" | ✅ CONFIRMED as a requirement — and it **does not reopen CX R-01**. R-01 rejected the monitor stream as an *audio asset*, on the correct grounds that D-01/D-02 forbid the network stream being the master. A witness is not a master; it is never mixed, never delivered, never heard. Defends substitution, mis-binding, and the honest wrong-file mistake — the last being far more common than fraud. **Flagged, not papered over**: a witness recording is still a recording, so it inherits Q-03's consent problem entirely and may not survive it (Q-05). It is also a candidate answer to 08.05.02 Q-01 (the alignment reference), which is 08.05.02's decision to make, not this feature's. |
| DT-07 | **Upload must never compete with a live session for upstream bandwidth.** Uploading take 1 while take 2 is being played is the obvious optimisation — it spreads the transfer and gets takes home sooner. | Reasoning about the intersection with 08.03: the upstream link carrying a participant's monitor stream is the scarcest resource in the session, and it is what everyone is judging the performance through. A background upload of a 1.5 GB take would saturate it. | ✅ CONFIRMED — uploads are suspended while the endpoint is in a live room and begin when it leaves (D-08). The failure mode is what makes this sharp: the platform would be **sabotaging its own remote-monitoring product with its own capture product**, and the damage would land on the take currently being performed — destroying a new take to deliver an old one. Note the corollary for MoSCoW: 08.07 Overdub has no live room, so uploads run immediately — one more way that mode is structurally easier than the domain's name implies. |
| DT-08 | The capture indicator must be perceivable by someone whose hands and eyes are on an instrument — and the natural fix is an audible cue, the way a studio uses a talkback beep or a record tone. | `meta/personas.md`: the multi-hyphenate is the structural fact, so the Producer who runs capture is routinely also playing. A visual indicator in an unfocused browser tab is invisible to the exact person it exists to protect. | ✅ CONFIRMED for the requirement, ❌ **REJECTED for the audible solution — it defeats itself.** An audible recording cue would be picked up by the microphone it is warning about and printed onto the take, which is the same class of error as talkback bleed (08.04.01 D-01, parent D-04) committed by the safety feature. So: the indicator must be perceivable **peripherally and non-acoustically**, and start/stop must not require a focused window. Worth logging precisely because the rejected answer is the one a studio would reach for first. |
| DT-09 | **Local storage is not durable.** The storage a browser gives a web page is reclaimable under pressure, without asking, and a take can simply cease to exist. | Domain reasoning about the feature's actual substrate: local-first means the only copy of the master lives in the least durable place in the system, defended by a runtime that treats it as cache. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a hard behavioural requirement (R1, D-11). The consequence is worse than data loss: **an evicted take is indistinguishable from a withheld one**, so a storage failure becomes an accusation. That is why Lost and Withheld are separate states (D-09). Capture that cannot obtain durability must say so *before* the session, not discover it after — a recorder that may silently lose the take is worse than no recorder, because people rely on it. This is also the single strongest piece of evidence in Q-01, and it is the one no stack comparison would surface on its own. |
| DT-10 | Capture to a compressed format. Two of the three shrink by an order of magnitude for a quality difference most listeners cannot identify in a blind test. | ❌ REJECTED — it dissolves the feature's premise. A lossy local capture is a *better-degraded* copy than the network's, which is a difference of degree, not of kind: DT-01's argument is that the take should be what the microphone heard, and a lossy take is not. If quality is negotiable, local-first is not worth paying alignment, upload and disk for, and DT-03 wins after all. It also forfeits precisely what Source-Connect charges money for. **Retained as an explicit, marked, per-endpoint fallback** where a rig genuinely cannot sustain full quality (a phone endpoint) — never as a silent default, because the whole failure class this feature exists to prevent is quality quietly lost and discovered at the mix (D-07). |
| DT-11 | Auto-stop capture when an endpoint loses contact with the session — it bounds disk consumption and prevents a six-hour orphan file from a laptop left running. | Adversarial, and a reasonable engineering instinct: an unbounded recorder with no supervisor is a resource leak. | ❌ REJECTED — it contradicts D-02, and it fires at the exact moment D-02 exists for. The dropout *is* the scenario; an auto-stop makes the feature fail precisely when it is supposed to pay off. The asymmetry decides it: over-recording is recoverable (alignment truncates against the canonical timeline), under-recording is not. Disk is the only legitimate bound (D-15). A prompt at the scheduled end + 30 min is acceptable because it leaves the human in charge; an auto-stop is a system deciding a take is over while someone is still playing. |
| DT-12 | Signal presence and clipping must be recorded as **latched facts on the take**, not rendered as transient UI. | Domain convention: console clip indicators have latched for sixty years — the same precedent 08.04.01 D-02 invokes for talkback — because a clip light that blinks while nobody is watching is a clip nobody knows about. The inverse failure is the classic: a channel recorded for forty minutes with phantom power off. | ✅ CONFIRMED — both are recorded on the take with timestamps (D-14). The two failures are mirror images: silence is nothing where there should be something, clipping is too much of something, and **both are invisible in real time to the person who could still fix them**. Silence is warned locally, never room-wide, because a warning that interrupts a performance to report a rest is worse than the problem. This is also the fact 08.05.04 consumes as "whether they were audible" — that column in its table is produced *here*, and it exists only because capture bothered to observe it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Local-first; the network stream is never the master | See DT-01. The whole category converged here. |
| D-02 | Network failure never stops local recording | See DT-02. Turns the domain's worst failure into an inconvenience. |
| D-03 | Uploads are resumable and never abandoned — backoff 2 s → 5 min cap, indefinite, resumes on next open, no dead-letter | Restarting a 40-minute multitrack upload is how takes get abandoned. A dead-lettered take is a lost take. |
| D-04 | Disk headroom is checked before the session: free ≥ 2 × `channels × 8.64 MB/min × planned minutes` | Predictable failures must be predicted. The 2× covers overrun and the fact that a full disk fails the OS, not just the audio. |
| D-05 | Loud recoverable failures over quiet unrecoverable ones | The principle behind rejecting DT-03, and applicable well beyond this feature. |
| D-06 | One file per `(session, take, endpoint)`. A session is N takes, not one recording | Highlights, alignment and the attendance record all need to know *which take*. |
| D-07 | Full quality is not negotiable; lossy capture is an explicit, marked, per-endpoint fallback, never a silent default | See DT-10. If quality is negotiable, local-first is not worth its three costs. |
| D-08 | Uploads never run while the endpoint is in a live room | See DT-07. The platform must not sabotage the take being performed to deliver the one before it. |
| D-09 | Non-upload is never ambiguous: Uploading / Pending / Uploaded / **Withheld** / **Discarded** / **Lost** are distinct, visible, named states | "Not uploaded" conflates a shut laptop with a hold-out. That ambiguity is what poisons the relationship. Withheld is a decision; Lost is an accident; conflating them accuses the unlucky. |
| D-10 | The participant owns their local take and the platform never compels upload — but withholding is explicit, visible and attributable, never silent | The lever cannot be removed; it can be made honest. And withholding withholds the *asset*, not the *fact* — 08.05.04's record is sealed independently, which reduces this from a provenance failure to a domain 05 contract dispute. |
| D-11 | Capture does not start unless local storage durability is obtained; otherwise it declares it cannot guarantee the take | See DT-09. A recorder that may silently lose the take is worse than no recorder, because people rely on it. |
| D-12 | The endpoint→human binding is 1:1 **only** for solo endpoints. Multi-channel and room endpoints require per-channel attribution, marked as declared, never observed | See DT-04. Auto-binding manufactures credits — the fraud the thesis exists to prevent, committed by the system. |
| D-13 | Delivered capture (DAW, console) is a first-class path; its binding is attested, not observed, and the take records which | See DT-05. Refusing it loses the best endpoints and the most sessions — and their provenance with them. |
| D-14 | Signal presence and clip are latched facts recorded on the take, not transient UI | See DT-12. Sixty years of console convention, and the fact 08.05.04 consumes as "whether they were audible". |
| D-15 | No auto-stop on an unresolved disconnect. Disk is the only bound; a prompt at scheduled end + 30 min, never a stop | See DT-11. Over-recording is recoverable; under-recording is not. |
| D-16 | The Producer starts and stops session capture. A participant may decline capture of their own endpoint; no participant may ever capture others | The platform cannot stop someone running their own recorder, but it will not *be* the instrument of a non-consensual recording. |
| D-17 | Headroom is displayed in minutes of recording remaining, never gigabytes | Minutes is the unit the work is measured in. "4.2 GB free" asks a musician to do division while playing. |
| D-18 | Finalising is uninterruptible and always yields a playable file | A truncated take is recoverable; a corrupt one is not. See D-05. |
| D-19 | Capture state is visible to every participant continuously, in **both** directions — rolling and not rolling | One rule, two problems: rolling-and-unannounced is surveillance (Q-03); not-rolling-and-unnoticed is the session where everyone assumed someone else was recording. |
