# Feature: Local-First Multitrack Capture & Upload

> **Number**: 08.05.01
> **Parent**: [Session Capture & Recall](./08.05-session-capture-recall-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Records each participant's audio where it is made — at full quality, before it touches the network —
then uploads it afterward. The audio that crosses a network arrives with jitter concealment, packet loss
and codec artifacts baked in permanently; capturing *that* produces a flawless recording of the network
having a bad day. What is worth keeping never crosses the wire at all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Their local recording of their own playing, at their interface's real quality. Sees that recording is happening, how much disk it needs, and its upload state. Owns their own track — including, contentiously, whether to release it (Q-02). Per `meta/personas.md`, this persona has been on both ends of stems held as leverage. |
| Producer | Full | Starts and stops capture for the session, sees every participant's capture and upload state, and receives the assembled multitrack. This is the persona the personas file names as the capture point; capture is the instrument that makes the record exist without extra admin. |
| Operator | Config | Declares their room's capture capability when the room is an endpoint — local disk, interface channel count, whether the room's own multitrack rig is the capture source rather than a browser. A studio with a real console is not going to record through a web page, and pretending otherwise loses the session. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No fan path into a jam room. |

## Behavior

**Local-first is the whole design** (parent D-02). Each endpoint records its own input at full quality to
local storage. The network stream is monitoring, never the master.

This inverts the naive design (record centrally, it is simpler) and buys three things:

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---------|---------------|
| No network artifacts | The take is what the microphone heard, not what survived the wire |
| No dropout gaps | A network failure ruins the *monitoring*, not the recording |
| Full quality regardless of bandwidth | Someone on a poor connection still delivers a usable take |

And it costs three things, honestly:

| Cost | Why it is real |
|------|---------------|
| Alignment is now mandatory | Independent clocks, independent zeros — see 08.05.02 |
| Upload is a separate, failable step | The take exists on a laptop that may be closed, wiped, or never opened again |
| Local disk is a dependency | A browser recording multitrack audio to a full disk fails in the middle of the good take |

**A network failure must not stop local recording.** This is the property that makes local-first
genuinely valuable rather than merely purist: when the session falls apart, the takes survive. It is also
the connection to 08.08 — the interruption ruins the experience, not the asset.

**Talkback never enters capture** (parent D-04, 08.04.01 D-01) — structurally, not by configuration.

### Happy Path

1. Producer starts capture for the session.
2. Each endpoint begins recording locally at full quality; every participant can see they are recording.
3. Session runs. Network conditions affect monitoring; they do not affect the recordings.
4. Producer stops capture. Each endpoint finalises its local file.
5. Uploads begin in the background; participants see progress and can close the app once complete.
6. Assembled set is handed to alignment (08.05.02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Network drops mid-take | Local recording continues uninterrupted. The take survives; only the experience was damaged. This is the payoff. | "Connection lost — you are still recording." |
| Local disk fills mid-take | The worst realistic failure. Must be predicted before the session (08.06 Pre-Flight), not discovered at minute 40 of a good take. | Pre-flight warning beforehand; hard warning at the threshold. |
| Participant closes their browser/app before upload | The take exists on their machine and the platform has nothing. Genuinely bad and probably common. | Persistent "you have an unuploaded take" prompt on return. `[PENDING — Step 5]` on how hard to push. |
| Participant refuses to upload | Their track, their call — the same leverage dynamic the personas file names in both directions. See Q-02. | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Browser cannot record multichannel at quality | Real constraint. A browser is not a DAW, and the Operator's room case exists precisely because of this. | Honest capability statement; route to a room or a local agent. |
| Upload interrupted | Resumable. Restarting a 40-minute multitrack upload from zero is how takes get abandoned. | Resumable progress. |
| Participant joins mid-session | Their local recording starts late; alignment handles the offset. Normal, not an error. | Normal. |
| Talkback bleeds into a capture | Must be structurally impossible. See 08.04.01 DT-01. | N/A — prevented by architecture. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Idle | Not capturing | Ready + disk headroom estimate. |
| Recording | Capture started | Unambiguous recording indicator + elapsed + disk remaining. |
| Recording (disconnected) | Network lost, capture continues | "Still recording" — reassurance at the exact moment of panic. |
| Finalising | Capture stopped | Writing local file; must not be interrupted. |
| Uploading | Upload in progress | Resumable progress per track. |
| Uploaded | Complete | Confirmed; safe to close. |
| Incomplete | Some participants not uploaded | Producer sees exactly who is missing, by name. |
| Error (disk) | Disk full | Hard, loud failure. Never a silent truncation. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A domain expert expects capture to be **local-first**, because the network stream carries irreversible damage — concealment, loss, codec artifacts — and recording it produces a permanent record of the transport's failures rather than of the performance. | Domain reasoning + category precedent: JackTrip and Source-Connect both record locally; Source-Connect's entire premium proposition is fetching the lossless local original for anything the network mangled. The whole category converged here, which is strong evidence. | ✅ CONFIRMED — local-first, network stream is never the master. The candidate list said "multitrack capture of network sessions", which reads as capturing the network stream — precisely the wrong architecture, and worth naming as a near-miss. |
| DT-02 | A network failure must **not** stop local recording — and this is the property that makes local-first valuable rather than merely correct. | Reasoning: the session's worst moment is a dropout. If the takes die with the connection, the domain's biggest failure mode destroys its most valuable output. If they survive, an interruption is an inconvenience instead of a loss. | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it reframes 08.08 (Interruption & Reconnect) entirely: the interruption becomes an experience problem, not an asset problem, which is a far more survivable class of failure. |
| DT-03 | Since the platform is already carrying the audio, central capture is simpler and avoids the whole upload problem. | Naive engineering framing, and genuinely tempting — it eliminates alignment, upload failure, and disk dependency in one move. | ❌ REJECTED — it would capture exactly the degraded stream (DT-01), making the recording hostage to the worst network moment of the session. Worse, it fails silently: the file plays, so nobody knows the artifacts are permanent until the mix. The costs of local-first (alignment, upload, disk) are all *visible* failures with recoverable outcomes; central capture's cost is an *invisible* failure with an unrecoverable one. Choosing loud recoverable failures over quiet unrecoverable ones is the right trade every time. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Local-first; the network stream is never the master | See DT-01. The whole category converged here. |
| D-02 | Network failure never stops local recording | See DT-02. Turns the domain's worst failure into an inconvenience. |
| D-03 | Uploads are resumable | Restarting a 40-minute multitrack upload is how takes get abandoned. |
| D-04 | Disk headroom is checked before the session, not discovered during it | Predictable failures must be predicted. |
| D-05 | Loud recoverable failures over quiet unrecoverable ones | The principle behind rejecting DT-03, and applicable well beyond this feature. |
