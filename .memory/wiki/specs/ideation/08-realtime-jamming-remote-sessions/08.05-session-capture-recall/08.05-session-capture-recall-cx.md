## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Local-First Capture ↔ Take Alignment

**Relationship**: This is the sub-domain's defining tension. Local-first capture is chosen because the
network stream is permanently damaged (index D-02) — but recording at each endpoint means each track is
made by a different clock, started at a different instant, and drifting at a different rate. Capture
hands alignment a set of tracks that are pristine individually and useless collectively. Alignment is
not a nicety on top of capture; it is what makes capture's output a thing rather than a pile.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: owns the aligned result and is the one who discovers, months later, that it was never aligned properly.
- **Musician**: contributes a track and needs it to land in the right place; largely does not want to think about this.
- **Operator**: configures the room's local capture when their room is an endpoint.
- **Fan**: not affected — no fan path in this domain.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Each endpoint owns its own local track exclusively — nothing else may write it. Alignment produces an *offset and drift model per track*, stored alongside, never destructively applied. Baking alignment into the audio makes the decision irreversible, and alignment decisions get revised.
2. **Trigger chain**: Capture completes → upload → alignment runs → aligned set published to Projects (07). Async. If alignment fails, the tracks must still exist and be downloadable unaligned — a failed alignment must never lose audio. Unaligned tracks are recoverable by hand; lost tracks are not.
3. **Permission intersection**: A participant owns their own local track. Whether the Producer can obtain it before the participant releases it is a real question — per `meta/personas.md`, the Producer's worst intentional behaviour is withholding stems as leverage, and the inverse (a musician withholding their take) is equally available. Escalated to 08.05.01 Q-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Alignment completion notifies the Producer. A *failed* alignment must notify loudly — a silently unaligned multitrack discovered later is worse than an obvious failure now, because by then everyone has moved on.
5. **State transition conflict**: Uploads arrive at different times (someone's laptop is closed). Alignment cannot run until the set is complete, and the set may never be complete. Partial alignment with a visible gap is the honest behaviour; waiting silently forever is not.

### CX-02: Local-First Capture ↔ Attendance Record

**Relationship**: The thesis mechanism. Every captured track has exactly one human origin, and the
platform knows it with certainty rather than by assertion — because the platform is the transport that
carried the connection. In a physical room, "who played the bass part" is reconstructed from memory
(the failure `meta/problem-statement.md` identifies as the root cause). Here it is a fact the system
observed while doing its ordinary job. The binding is created at capture time and cannot be
retrofitted — which is precisely why capture and the attendance record belong in one sub-domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their playing becomes provably theirs, without them doing anything. Per `meta/personas.md`, their success criterion is "credits are cited rather than claimed" — this is the mechanism.
- **Producer**: the capture point per the personas file. This is the tool that makes the credit a byproduct rather than admin, which is the stated success criterion for the persona.
- **Operator**: sees that a session in their room produced a record; not a party to the creative facts.
- **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The track↔person binding is owned here and is immutable — it records an observation, not a claim. Credits (domain 02) may *disagree* with it (a player was there but contributed nothing), and that is legitimate: this record is evidence, not a conclusion. Index Q-01.
2. **Trigger chain**: Capture starts → participant bound to track → session ends → record sealed → offered to Credits (02) and Rights (09). If the downstream handoff fails, the record survives here. It is the asset.
3. **Permission intersection**: Being on the roster (08.03.02) is hearing; being bound to a track is playing. Different grants, different meanings, and conflating them would put listeners in the credits.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Session end → contributors prompted to confirm. Per `meta/personas.md`, "the moment that matters" is credit captured while everyone is present and friendly — so the prompt fires at session end, not months later, and that timing is the whole point.
5. **State transition conflict**: A participant who joins, leaves, and rejoins produces a fragmented presence record. Whether that is one attendance or several matters for a credit — escalated to 08.05.04.

### CX-03: Auto-Highlights ↔ Take Alignment

**Relationship**: A highlight is `(region, timeline)`. Before alignment there are N timelines and no
canonical one, so a highlight flagged during the session must be recorded against a session-level
reference and *re-anchored* once alignment establishes the true timeline. A highlight anchored to a
local recorder's clock is anchored to a lie.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: flags a moment; expects it to land on the right moment.
- **Producer**: reviews highlights against the aligned multitrack.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Alignment owns the canonical timeline. Highlights reference it and never define it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Highlight flagged (session-relative) → alignment establishes canonical timeline → highlights re-anchored. If alignment never completes, highlights degrade to approximate regions rather than vanishing.
3. **Permission intersection**: Anyone in the session can flag. Whether everyone sees everyone's flags is `[PENDING — Step 5]`.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None during the session — a flag must not interrupt anyone playing (same rule as 08.03.03 D-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: Flags land during the session; alignment happens after. The re-anchoring is a batch correction, and it must be visible if it moves a flag materially.

### CX-04: Auto-Highlights ↔ Attendance Record

**Relationship**: `[PARTIAL]` — presence is not contribution. A player connected for three hours whose
every take was discarded contributed nothing to the work, and a credit derived from connection time
alone would be wrong in a way that helps the wrong people. Highlights are the signal that separates
"was in the room" from "is on the record". The exact relationship is genuinely unresolved and is the
substance of index Q-01.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: their credit may depend on whether their playing survived, which is a consequential and contestable inference.
- **Producer**: the person who actually knows, and the one whose attestation `meta/personas.md` says is worth more than a self-claim.
- **Operator** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — Medium confidence, deferred per the CX template's rule for non-High entries. Flagged as consequential: this is where an over-eager inference could manufacture or erase a credit.
