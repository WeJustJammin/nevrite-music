## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Parsing ↔ Line Matching

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's most important separation, and the one a first implementation would collapse. Both failures present to the user as the same sentence — *"my money isn't showing up"* — and they have nothing else in common.

A **parse failure** is the platform's fault, is fixed by an engineer shipping an adapter, and is **locally detectable with certainty**: the lines either sum to the number the source printed on its own statement, or they do not (`10.02.02` D-02). A **match failure** is a catalogue or identifier problem, is fixed by a user or by domain 09, and is **never certain** — no amount of evidence proves a line belongs to a work.

`10.02.02` DT-01 spells out the cost of merging them: the stated-total oracle stops working. If parsing and matching are one step, a wrong sum no longer tells you *which* went wrong, and the only hard check in domain 10 — a domain otherwise built entirely on inference (`10.01.04` D-03, `10.02.03` D-01, `10.01.07` D-03) — becomes unusable.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: read-only on parsing, config on matching. They cannot fix a parser and must never be asked to (`10.02.02` DT-02); they are the only ones who can fix a match.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent throughout.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: `10.02.02` owns lines-as-parsed (immutable, versioned by adapter). `10.02.03` owns line→catalogue assertions. The line is never rewritten by matching.
2. **Trigger chain**: Parse → **reconcile to stated total** → *(gate)* → match → confirm → calculate. The gate is hard: a failed reconciliation stops the pipeline rather than warning (`10.02.02` D-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: Asymmetric by design — the platform owns parsing entirely, the user owns matching confirmation entirely.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Parse failures notify the platform (an adapter is broken). Match candidates notify the user (a decision is waiting). Same pipeline, different audiences.
5. **State transition conflict**: An adapter correction re-parses statements whose lines are already matched — and possibly already paid (`10.02.02` Q-03). The re-parse must produce a new interpretation alongside the old, never in place, or money that has moved silently disagrees with its own basis.

### CX-03: Line Matching ↔ Exception Queue

**Relationship**: The pair that makes `10.02.03` D-01 ("money never moves on a probabilistic match") survivable rather than merely principled. Refusing to auto-accept is only defensible if the money you decline to attribute stays **visible and denominated** — otherwise the strict matcher is just a machine that loses money more politely than a loose one.

`10.02.05` DT-01 states the stake: an unattributed line held quietly is, precisely, a black box — money held by an institution that cannot name its owner. That is the thing `10.05.01` sells against. A platform whose activation event is *"we found you £800 the industry lost"* cannot accumulate its own pile of £340s behind a `NULL`.

The two features therefore encode one position: **be strict about attribution, and be loud about what strictness costs.**

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees their own unattributed money as money. The honest sibling of `10.05.01`'s pitch, pointed inward.
- **Producer**: the party most able to resolve a line — they ran the session and recognise the mangled title.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The line is one object with an attribution state. Matching writes it; the queue is the view over `unattributed` — not a copy. A copy would eventually disagree with its source, which is the whole failure being avoided.
2. **Trigger chain**: Match attempt → below-threshold or ambiguous → queue with amount, source, period and reason → group → resolve → **re-enter calculation with money intact**.
3. **Permission intersection**: You only ever see your own unattributed money. Label statements naming third parties are the sharp edge (parent `Q-03`), because the document is one user's and the money is several people's.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Ageing alarms, sized in money and escalating (`10.02.05` D-05). Row counts notify nobody, which is the point.
5. **State transition conflict**: CX-07's reverse edge — a catalogue change re-sweeping the queue while a user is manually resolving the same group. The sweep must not overwrite a human decision; a confirmation outranks a re-match.

### CX-04: Source Registry ↔ Exception Queue

**Relationship**: Two failures that look unrelated and are the same failure. `10.02.01` DT-02: a statement that never arrives produces **no event** — nothing is red, nothing is late, because there was never a promise of a specific amount on a specific date. `10.02.05` DT-03: money sitting unattributed produces **no event** — it is a row in a table nobody opens.

Both are absences. Both are invisible by default. Both are how royalty money is lost in the real world, and both are fixed the same way: **declare an expectation, then let a clock manufacture the event the world will never send.** The source registry does it with a cadence; the queue does it with an ageing threshold.

This is the third independent occurrence of the pattern in this domain — `10.01.03` D-03 (expected-by on submissions) and `10.01.04` D-02 (silence is synthesised) reached it first, from society registration. Three unrelated features converging on one principle is what promotes it to the parent's domain-level position.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: both alarms are personal financial facts, not system errors, and must read that way.
- **Producer**: same at volume; the alarms aggregate across administered entities.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — one is about a source, the other about a line. They share a principle, not an entity, which is exactly why the shared principle belongs at the parent level rather than in either feature.
2. **Trigger chain**: Declare cadence → *(silence)* → overdue → leakage finding (`10.05.03`). And: attribute → *(fail)* → queue → *(silence)* → ageing → alarm → possibly stale past the source's claim window, which is permanent loss.
3. **Permission intersection**: None.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both must be denominated in money. "PRS didn't distribute" and "£340 unattributed" are financial statements; "source overdue" and "412 rows" are ops noise, and ops noise gets muted.
5. **State transition conflict**: A late statement arriving after an overdue alarm resolves it — the same shape as `10.01` CX-02's late acknowledgement. Neither may leave two contradictory beliefs standing.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 10.02.01 Source Registry | 10.02.03 Line Matching | Rejected as a direct pair. Knowing a line came from PRS narrows the *right type* and the *territory* — but that arrives through the parse (`10.02.02`), which is the only thing that ever reads the file. A direct edge would imply matching reaches back to the source registry for context, putting source knowledge in two places and inviting them to disagree. The real path is CX-05 → CX-01 → CX-06. |
| R-02 | 10.02.04 FX & Period | 10.02.05 Exception Queue | Rejected as a *currency* relationship, which is the tempting reading. The queue is denominated in money and therefore obviously touches FX — but only through the same view mechanism every other surface uses (`10.02.04` D-01: conversion is a view). The genuine edge is narrow and already covered by `10.02.04` D-05: a line with **no usable period** is unattributable and must land in the queue rather than defaulting to the arrival date. That is a period edge, not an FX one, and conflating them would suggest the queue holds converted amounts — which would destroy the source-amount-is-the-fact principle at exactly the point where it matters most. |
| R-03 | 10.02.02 Parsing | 10.02.05 Exception Queue | Rejected, and the boundary is load-bearing. Unparseable lines are **not** exceptions: they are a parse failure, and a parse failure blocks the whole statement at the reconciliation gate (`10.02.02` D-02). The queue holds lines that parsed correctly and could not be *attributed* — money the platform has read and cannot place. Routing parse failures here would let a broken adapter's output leak past the gate as "exceptions", silently converting a platform bug into a user problem, and quietly defeating the domain's only hard oracle. |
| R-04 | 10.02.01 Source Registry | 10.02.04 FX & Period | Rejected: a source's usual currency is a property of the source, but a *statement's* currency is a property of the file — and they disagree often enough to matter (aggregated foreign income, multi-currency statements, a source changing its remittance currency). Trusting the registry over the file would substitute a stale expectation for an observed fact, which is the same class of error as `10.02.04` D-04 (rates pinned at use, never re-fetched). The file wins; the registry has no vote. |
