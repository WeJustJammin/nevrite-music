# Feature: Line Matching & Catalogue Resolution

> **Number**: 10.02.03
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Deciding **which of your works or recordings a royalty line is actually about**. A statement line says `"MIDNIGHT (LIVE) - SMITH/J"` and pays £0.83; the platform must decide whether that is your song, a different song with the same title, or a stranger's. Sometimes there is an ISRC or an ISWC and the answer is easy. Frequently there is a mangled title, a misspelled writer surname, and nothing else.

This is entity resolution against an adversarially inconsistent external vocabulary, at scale, where **being wrong means paying the wrong person**. It is the hardest feature in domain 10 and the one whose quality determines whether everything downstream is worth anything.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Confirms or corrects matches against their own catalogue, and — the part that pays — reviews low-confidence candidates the platform found but would not assert. Their leverage is knowledge nobody else has: they know `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` is the same song as `Midnight`, because they were there. |
| Producer | Config | Same, at volume and across entities they administer. Also the party most able to resolve a line about a recording whose ISRC nobody remembers issuing. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

Matching runs a cascade, strongest evidence first:

1. **Identifier match** — ISRC or ISWC present and known. Near-certain, and the reason `10.01.02`/domain 09's identifier discipline pays off here rather than where it is entered.
2. **Identifier match against an unknown identifier** — the line names an ISRC the platform has never seen. Not a failure: it may be *your* recording under an identifier a distributor issued without telling you. A finding, not a miss.
3. **Fuzzy match** — title + party surname + duration + release proximity. Probabilistic. Produces candidates and confidences, never conclusions.
4. **No match** — → `10.02.05`, visibly, with the money attached.

The governing rule, inherited from `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 (*the platform does not adjudicate truth*) and applied to money: **a probabilistic match is a suggestion until a human confirms it, and money never moves on a suggestion.** The temptation to auto-accept a 0.94 match is enormous — it clears the queue, it makes the product feel magical, and it is wrong 6% of the time in a domain where wrongness means a stranger receives someone's money and nobody notices for years.

WeJammin's genuine advantage here is real and worth stating precisely: it holds the **credits graph** (domain 02). A generic matcher compares a statement line to a title list. This platform can ask "does the writer surname on this line correspond to someone we know was in the room?" — which is a discriminator no competitor has, because none of them were present at the session. That is the thesis (`D-18`) doing concrete work on a matching problem.

### Happy Path

1. Parsed lines arrive from `10.02.02`, reconciled to the stated total
2. Identifier-bearing lines match against the catalogue with near-certainty
3. Remaining lines produce ranked candidates using title, parties, and the credits graph
4. High-confidence candidates are surfaced for confirmation; the user confirms in bulk
5. Confirmed matches feed `10.03.01`; the rest go visibly to `10.02.05` with their money intact

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Line names an ISRC the platform doesn't know | Possibly your recording, identified by someone else's issuance | **A finding** — the identifier gap flows to domain 09, not a silent miss |
| Two works, same title, same writer surname | Genuinely ambiguous. Common for prolific writers | Ambiguous, both candidates shown; the platform must not pick |
| Live/remix/edit variants | `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` and `Midnight` are different recordings of one work — the work/recording duality (domain 09) decides which level the line is about | Right type disambiguates; performance ≠ mechanical ≠ neighbouring |
| Cover by someone else | It IS your work, and the money is yours. The line names a performer you have never heard of | The single highest-value fuzzy match available, and the one most likely to be discarded as noise |
| Mangled/transliterated title | Routine on foreign statements | Fuzzy candidates; low confidence is honest |
| A confirmed match was wrong | Money paid to the wrong party. Discovered late, if ever | → `10.03.04` overpayment. A confirmation is reversible and must be |
| Matched to a work whose splits later changed | The match stands; the *attribution* changes as-of | Parent D-02 (as-of resolution); the match is not the split |
| Statement line about a work you don't own at all | A label statement naming other artists | Not yours; must not enter your catalogue's totals. Parent `Q-03` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Matched (identifier) | ISRC/ISWC hit | Matched, with the identifier as the evidence |
| Candidate | Fuzzy hit above threshold | Suggested — **money has not moved** |
| Confirmed | Human accepted | Feeds calculation |
| Ambiguous | Multiple viable candidates | Both shown; no auto-pick |
| Unmatched | Nothing viable | → `10.02.05`, money visible |
| Rejected | Human said no | Fed back; the rejection is training data and evidence |
| Reversed | A confirmed match was wrong | → `10.03.04` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | High-confidence fuzzy matches should auto-accept — a 0.95 threshold clears the queue and the product feels magical | Every matching system ever built, and the only way the queue is tractable at volume | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the load-bearing decision of the feature.** At 0.95, one line in twenty pays the wrong person. The failure is **silent, monetary, and effectively permanent**: the wrongly-paid party does not report it, the rightful party never knew the money existed, and the only detector is `10.05.03` noticing an expectation that never arrived — years later, if at all. It is the mechanism by which the industry's black box was built in the first place, and a platform whose entire pitch is "we found you £800 someone else's bad matching lost" cannot ship an auto-matcher that creates the same money. **Money never moves on a suggestion.** The queue-volume problem is real and is answered by better evidence (DT-02), not by a lower bar. |
| DT-02 | Matching is a generic entity-resolution problem, so it should ride the platform's `CX-M17` resolution cross-cut and hold nothing of its own | Node Classification Gate — it looks exactly like a mechanism, and `02.01.04` already does duplicate/identifier merging | ❌ **REJECTED, narrowly and importantly.** The *machinery* — fuzzy comparison, candidate ranking, confirmation UX, identifier authority — is genuinely `CX-M17`'s and is routed there. What is irreducibly domain-owned is **the evidence WeJammin uniquely has**: the credits graph. A generic matcher compares a statement line to a title list. This platform can ask *"was this writer surname in the room for this session?"* — a discriminator that exists only because domain 02 captured the session. That is the thesis (`D-18`) cashing out on a specific technical problem, and routing it away would discard the platform's only real matching advantage. |
| DT-03 | An unmatched line means we don't own it | The intuitive read — it didn't match your catalogue, so it isn't yours | ❌ **REJECTED, in both directions, and the asymmetry is the point.** An unmatched line is far more often **your money described badly** than someone else's money described well — mangled titles, missing ISRCs, a cover you never knew existed, a distributor's identifier you were never told about. Treating unmatched as "not mine" throws away exactly the money `10.05.01` exists to recover, having already been handed it. The inverse error is worse: treating unmatched-but-plausible as "probably mine" is DT-01 with extra steps. The only honest handling is a **visible queue with the money attached** (`10.02.05`), which is why that feature exists rather than a `NULL` and a shrug. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Money never moves on a probabilistic match.** No auto-accept at any threshold | DT-01. A silent misattribution is how the industry's black box was built; the platform cannot sell recovery and manufacture the same loss. |
| D-02 | Matching is domain-owned **because of the credits graph**; the generic machinery rides `CX-M17` | DT-02. The graph is the discriminator no competitor has. |
| D-03 | Unmatched lines go to a **visible queue with their money attached** — never dropped, never assumed foreign | DT-03. Unmatched is usually your money described badly. |
| D-04 | An unknown ISRC on a line is a **finding**, routed to domain 09 | It frequently means a distributor issued an identifier for your recording and never told you. |
| D-05 | Confirmations are **reversible** and their rejections are retained | A wrong confirmation is an overpayment; a rejection is evidence and training data. |
