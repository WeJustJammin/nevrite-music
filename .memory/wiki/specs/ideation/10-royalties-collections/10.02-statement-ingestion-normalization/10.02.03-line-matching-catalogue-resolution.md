# Feature: Line Matching & Catalogue Resolution

> **Number**: 10.02.03
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Deciding **which of your works or recordings a royalty line is actually about**. A statement line says `"MIDNIGHT (LIVE) - SMITH/J"` and pays £0.83; the platform must decide whether that is your song, a different song with the same title, or a stranger's. Sometimes there is an ISRC or an ISWC and the answer is easy. Frequently there is a mangled title, a misspelled writer surname, and nothing else.

This is entity resolution against an adversarially inconsistent external vocabulary, at scale, where **being wrong means paying the wrong person**. It is the hardest feature in domain 10 and the one whose quality determines whether everything downstream is worth anything.

The deepening pass changed the feature's shape in one decisive way. **The unit of matching is not the line — it is the distinct identity the source uses to name a thing** (`D-06`). A DistroKid statement is 40,000 lines describing 200 recordings across 60 territories, 12 stores and 3 months; a PRS statement is 3,000 lines describing 90 works. Matching 40,000 lines is impossible under `D-01`'s no-auto-accept rule. Matching 200 identities is a Tuesday afternoon — and the decision is made **once, and never again**, because every future statement from that source reuses the identity the human already confirmed. That is what makes "money never moves on a suggestion" survivable rather than merely principled (`DT-04`).

## Behavior

### The unit is the identity, not the line

Every parsed line (`10.02.02`) carries the fields the source used to name what it paid for: some subset of title, party names, ISRC, ISWC, a **source-scoped proprietary code** (a PRS tunecode, an ASCAP work ID, a distributor catalogue number, a UPC), duration and release. The tuple of identifying fields — *not the money, not the period, not the territory* — is the line's **source identity**.

Lines are collapsed onto their distinct source identities before anything is matched. The collapse factor is `territories × stores/outlets × months` and is routinely 100×–1,000×. **Matching, confirmation, reversal and re-sweep all operate on identities. The line is never the unit of a human decision** (`D-06`).

### The cascade

Matching runs a cascade against the user's own catalogue, strongest evidence first:

1. **Confirmed identity mapping (deterministic replay)** — this source identity has been confirmed by a human before. Attribute it. This is **not** a probabilistic match and does not violate `D-01`: the human already decided, and replaying a recorded decision is arithmetic (`D-07`).
2. **Global identifier match** — ISRC or ISWC present and known. Near-certain, and the reason `10.01.02`/domain 09's identifier discipline pays off *here* rather than where it is entered.
3. **Source-scoped identifier match** — the source's own code for the thing, once it has been bound to a work by a prior confirmation. A PRS tunecode is not an ISWC and is not portable, but **within PRS it is deterministic**, and society statements carry it far more reliably than they carry an ISWC (`D-09`, `DT-07`).
4. **Identifier match against an unknown identifier** — the line names an ISRC the platform has never seen. Not a failure: it may be *your* recording under an identifier a distributor issued without telling you. **A finding, not a miss** (`D-04`).
5. **Fuzzy match** — title + party name + right type + duration + release proximity + **the credits graph**. Probabilistic. Produces ranked candidates and confidences, **never conclusions**. A candidate is only surfaced as confirmable when **≥2 independent signal classes agree** (`D-15`); a title-only hit is not a candidate.
6. **No viable candidate** — → `10.02.05`, visibly, with the money attached (`D-03`).

### What the platform will not do

The governing rule, inherited from `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 (*the platform does not adjudicate truth*) and applied to money: **a probabilistic match is a suggestion until a human confirms it, and money never moves on a suggestion** (`D-01`). The temptation to auto-accept a 0.94 match is enormous — it clears the queue, it makes the product feel magical, and it is wrong 6% of the time in a domain where wrongness means a stranger receives someone's money and nobody notices for years.

Two further boundaries are load-bearing:

- **A match is not an ownership assertion** (`D-11`). Matching answers *"which work is this line about?"* Domain 09's ledger answers *"whose money is it, as of the usage period?"* (parent domain `D-02`). Confirming that a line is about `Midnight` does not assert that you own `Midnight`, and cannot be used to acquire it. The two questions are adjacent, they are asked in the same breath, and collapsing them is how a matching surface becomes a theft surface.
- **A match is not a split.** The match binds a line to a work; the *attribution* of that work's money resolves against 09 **as of the usage period**, not as it stands today (parent `D-02`, `Q-05`). A mapping confirmed in 2026 correctly attributes a Q1 2024 line to the people who owned the work in Q1 2024.

### The discriminator, and its honest limits

WeJammin's genuine advantage here is real and worth stating precisely: it holds the **credits graph** (domain 02). A generic matcher compares a statement line to a title list. This platform can ask *"does the writer surname on this line correspond to someone we know was in the room?"* — a discriminator no competitor has, because none of them were present at the session. That is the thesis (`D-18`) doing concrete work on a matching problem.

Two limits, both established by this pass and both material:

- **At launch the discriminator is imported, not captured** — and imported credits are the same public MusicBrainz/Discogs data any competitor can also import. The advantage is real but **arrives late**, and it arrives on the *forward* catalogue, not the back catalogue where the recovery pitch points (`DT-08`, `D-16`).
- **The graph holds performing names; statements print registered names.** A drummer credited as *Jonny Blaze* is registered at PRS as `SMITH, JOHN ANDREW`. The party-name discriminator therefore misses **exactly the population most likely to use a stage name**, which is most of them (`DT-06`, `D-13`).

### Happy Path

1. Parsed lines arrive from `10.02.02`, **already reconciled to the stated total** — the reconciliation gate is hard and nothing reaches matching without passing it (`10.02.02` D-02, CX-01)
2. Lines are collapsed onto distinct source identities; the statement's 41,880 lines resolve to 214 identities (`D-06`)
3. Identities already carrying a confirmed mapping attribute deterministically, with no human involvement and no new decision — on a returning source this is the overwhelming majority (`D-07`)
4. Remaining identities run the cascade: global identifier → source-scoped code → fuzzy with the credits graph
5. Identifier-bearing identities match with near-certainty and are presented for a **one-click confirmation**, not an investigation
6. Fuzzy identities produce ranked candidates; each is shown with **the money it carries**, the evidence behind it, and the candidate it would attribute to
7. The user confirms in bulk — highest-money-first, evidence visible, one decision per identity covering every line that bears it (`D-04`, `10.02.05` D-04)
8. Each confirmation writes a **sticky, catalogue-scoped identity mapping** with its provenance: who confirmed, when, on what evidence (`D-07`, `D-08`)
9. Confirmed identities feed `10.03.01` with their money intact; attribution resolves against 09 **as of the usage period**, not today (parent `D-02`)
10. Everything the platform could not honestly assert goes visibly to `10.02.05` with its money attached and its ageing clock started (`D-03`, `10.02.05` D-05)
11. **The next statement from that source is nearly free** — only genuinely new identities queue for a human. The first statement is the expensive one; every subsequent one is a rounding error (`DT-04`)

| **Cover by someone else, recording-side statement** (DSP/PPL) | The coverer's master. **Not your money at all** | Correctly *not* matched. The duality is what prevents this from being an expensive false positive |
| Mangled/transliterated title | Routine on foreign statements | Fuzzy candidates; low confidence is honest, not a defect |

| **A confirmed mapping was wrong** | Not one bad line — the mapping mis-pays **forever, across every future statement, at growing volume**. `D-01` protected the line; the mapping amplifies a single human error into a standing liability | Reversal is first-class and **cascades to every line the mapping ever attributed** → `10.03.04` overpayment (`D-05`, `D-10`). The user is told the blast radius *before* confirming the reversal, in money and in lines |
| **A mapping's money jumps an order of magnitude** | Either a sync placement (real) or a mis-map now catching a stranger's catalogue (theft in progress). Indistinguishable without a human | The mapping **re-enters review**; attribution continues on the historical baseline and the delta queues (`D-10`). Threshold is a starting value, not a validated one — `Q-05` |
| **The source re-identifies a work** — re-registration, new work code, old one retired | The mapping keeps working for old lines and **silently fails for new ones**. Parent `D-04`'s clock, applied to identity rather than arrival |
| Matched to a work whose splits later changed | The match stands; the *attribution* changes as-of | Parent `D-02` (as-of resolution). **The match is not the split** |

Domain 09's ledger decides whose money it is, and it says "not yours" (`D-11`). The match surface is not an acquisition surface |
| **Two users map the same source identity to different works** | Looks like a conflict. It is a **co-write** — both are correct, and both are owed | Not an error, not a race, no lock. Mappings are catalogue-scoped, never global (`D-08`, `DT-13`) |

| **Re-sweep collides with a human mid-decision** | CX-07's reverse edge: a growing catalogue re-sweeps the queue while the user is resolving the same identity | **A confirmation outranks a re-match, always.** The sweep's result for that identity is discarded, not merged (CX-03 §5) |
| **Adapter is corrected; lines re-parse under a new version** | Money may already have moved on the old parse (`10.02.02` Q-03) | Mappings are keyed on identity, not line — so a re-parse **re-attributes automatically through existing mappings** and costs the user nothing. A designed property of `D-06`, not luck. If the re-parse changes identity *extraction*, the identity is new and queues honestly |
| **Line has no usable period** | Cannot resolve against 09 as-of; cannot be attributed | → `10.02.05` rather than defaulting to the arrival date (`10.02.04` D-05, CX R-02) |

| **Title-collision squatting** | A malicious actor registers a work titled identically to a famous one, hoping to catch fuzzy candidates | Blast radius bounded to candidate noise: no auto-accept (`D-01`), matching is not ownership (`D-11`), and mappings are catalogue-scoped (`D-08`). Three independent controls, none of which is the matcher's confidence score |
| **Fabricated statement uploaded** | A user uploads an invented statement claiming large earnings | Matches nothing they don't own; pays nothing (`D-11`). Consequence depends entirely on parent `Q-07` (does WeJammin move money?). Compute-only, they have lied to themselves |
| Duration/release-proximity signals absent | Society statements rarely print duration; statements carry **usage periods, not release dates** | Both are weak signals and are weighted as such. A cascade leaning on them would perform well in a demo and badly on a PRS file |

### States

Two state models, deliberately separate. The first is the domain fact; the second is the surface.

#### Attribution state — per source identity

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Unresolved** | Identity extracted from parsed lines, cascade not yet run | Counted, money denominated, no assertion |
| **Matched (global identifier)** | ISRC/ISWC hit | Matched, with the identifier as the evidence. One-click confirm, not an investigation |
| **Matched (source-scoped code)** | Prior binding on this source's own code hit (`D-09`) | Matched, with the source's code as the evidence |
| **Candidate** | Fuzzy hit, ≥2 independent signal classes agree (`D-15`) | Suggested, ranked, with evidence and the money it carries — **money has not moved** |
| **Ambiguous** | Multiple viable candidates, none dominant | Both shown; **no auto-pick**; the platform states that it cannot tell |
| **Confirmed** | Human accepted; sticky mapping written with provenance | Feeds `10.03.01`. Every future line bearing this identity replays deterministically (`D-07`) |
| **Rejected** | Human said no | Never re-proposed. The rejection is retained as evidence and as training data (`D-05`) |
| **Not mine** | Human disclaimed (a label statement's other artists) | First-class outcome, not a failure to match (`10.02.05` D-06) |
| **Unmatched** | Nothing viable, or single-signal only | → `10.02.05`, money visible, ageing clock started |
| **Under review (drift)** | Mapping's attributed money moved an order of magnitude | Attribution continues on the historical baseline; the delta queues (`D-10`) |
| **Re-identified** | Identity retired at source; successor detected (`D-14`) | One decision: "PRS appears to have re-coded this work" |
| **Reversed** | A confirmed mapping was wrong | Cascades to every line it ever attributed → `10.03.04` (`D-10`) |

#### Surface state

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Cascade running over a freshly parsed statement | Progress denominated in **identities resolved / identities found** — never lines, never a spinner over a number the user was never shown |
| **Empty (no catalogue)** | User has no works or recordings at all — the launch condition | Not a zero state: "we read this statement and have nothing to match it against." Routes to `02.03.01` import, and offers the statement's identities as claim proposals (`D-12`) |
| **Empty (nothing to decide)** | Every identity replayed from confirmed mappings | The good state, and the destination: "attributed automatically. Nothing needs you." |
| **Populated** | Identities awaiting decision | Ranked **by money**, highest first — never by count, never alphabetically, never by confidence (`10.02.05` D-04) |
| **Error** | Cascade could not run — catalogue unreadable, 09 unreachable for as-of resolution | Explicit and blocking. **Nothing is attributed on a degraded read**; a partial cascade must not present as a complete one (`10.02.02` D-05's principle, one step downstream) |
| **Partial** | Some identities resolved, some cannot be (09 as-of unavailable for a period) | Named and quantified in money. The resolved set proceeds; the unresolved set is visible and is **not** silently held — that is `10.02.05`'s job and it must be reached, not approximated |

This is `D-16`'s tension ("recover the back catalogue once; prevent from today, forever") reappearing as a technical fact rather than a strategic one: matching's advantage is on the *prevention* side. The recovery pitch must not be sold on a matching advantage the platform will not have for years. |

Collapse them and the matching surface becomes an **acquisition surface**: confirm a plausible fuzzy candidate against a work you don't own, and money follows a UI gesture rather than a rights record. Kept separate, the attack is inert — a malicious confirmation resolves which work a line concerns and pays nothing, because 09 says the work isn't theirs. The corollary constrains the whole feature: **matching must never be the control that decides ownership**, no matter how convenient, and no confidence score is ever an ownership signal. |
| DT-10 | Matching needs a catalogue, so a user with an empty catalogue simply cannot ingest statements yet | The dependency read, and it is correct as far as it goes — it just stops one step too early | ✅ **CONFIRMED, with a reverse edge that solves the cold start.** The dependency is real and under-acknowledged: `10.02` is sold as the **ungated beachhead** (`D-05`, parent `D-03`) — "upload your statement, see what you earned" — and it is **inert for a user with no catalogue**, which is every new user before `02.03.01` runs. Statement ingestion is *downstream* of catalogue import, not parallel to it, and any sequencing that ships them independently ships a dead first-run. But the reverse edge is the interesting half: **a statement addressed to you, paying you, describes your catalogue.** Its distinct identities *are* your recordings, asserted by a counterparty with no incentive to overstate what it owes you — evidentially stronger than a Discogs scrape. So the unmatched set on an empty catalogue is not a queue dump, it is a **seeding opportunity**. The constraint from DT-09 is what keeps it honest: it must flow as **claim proposals into `02.03.02`**, never as a direct import, because "this statement paid me for it" is strong evidence of ownership and is not proof of it — label statements name other artists (parent `Q-03`). Candidate → human claim → confirmed, which is the shape `02.03` already has. |
| DT-11 | A source is a stable identifier of its own works — a tunecode issued for a work is that work's code | The reasonable assumption, and the one that makes DT-04/DT-07's stickiness look safe | ❌ **REJECTED, and the failure is invisible by construction.** Societies re-register and re-code works: a duplicate registration is merged, a work is re-registered under a corrected writer split, a catalogue transfers between publishers and gets a new code. The sticky mapping then keeps working perfectly for **historical** lines and silently stops matching **new** ones. Nothing errors. Nothing is red. The user's income from that work appears to *drop*, and a brand-new unmatched identity appears somewhere else in the queue — and there is nothing connecting the two events. This is the parent domain's `D-04` pattern ("an absence produces no event, so a clock must manufacture one") applied to **identity rather than arrival**, which is a surface nobody has instrumented: `10.02.01` watches for statements that don't arrive, but nothing watches for an identity that stops appearing *inside* a statement that arrived perfectly normally. Detection is cheap and specific: an identity with an established history that stops appearing, plus a new identity in the same source with matching title/writer, is a probable re-identification and is one decision, not two mysteries. |
| DT-12 | One work, one match, one income fact — collapsing a user's statements onto their catalogue gives them "what *Midnight* earned" | The obvious consolidation win, and precisely the sentence the platform wants to be able to say | ✅ **CONFIRMED as the goal, REJECTED as the model — and the multi-hyphenate is what breaks it.** `personas.md`'s central structural fact is that one human holds several roles at once. Apply it here: a musician who wrote *Midnight*, played on it, and owns the master receives **three statements about one song in one quarter** — PRS (writer, performance), PPL (performer, neighbouring) and DistroKid (master owner, mechanical/streaming). All three are correct. All three are the same song. **None of them is the same money.** Collapse them to "your song *Midnight* earned £X" and the user, who knows they were paid three times, reads it as triple-counting and stops trusting the number — and a user who does *not* notice has been handed a wrong figure by the platform whose entire differentiator is `D-08` explainability. The match must therefore carry the **right type** as a first-class part of the attribution (CX-06): one work, three distinct income facts, never merged across right types. The consolidated view the product wants is a *sum over typed facts*, which is a legitimate view — but it is a view, and the typed facts are the record. Same shape as `10.02.04` D-01 (source amount is the fact, conversion is a view): consolidation is a presentation, never a storage decision. |
| DT-13 | Two users mapping the same source identity to different works is a conflict needing adjudication | The instinct from `02.01.04`'s merge world, where two claims on one node genuinely do contend | ❌ **REJECTED — it is a co-write, and treating it as a conflict would break the normal case.** The writer and the co-writer both receive PRS statements naming the same tunecode; both map it into their own catalogue; both are entirely correct and both are owed. Mappings are **catalogue-scoped, never global** (`D-08`), so there is no shared object to contend over, no lock, no race, and no adjudicator. This is the boundary with `02.01.04` doing real work: that feature merges nodes in a **shared** graph, where two humans claiming one identity genuinely conflict and where a wrong person-merge is irreversible. This feature writes assertions in a **private** catalogue, where two humans mapping one identity is the most ordinary thing in music. Same machinery (`CX-M17`), opposite concurrency semantics — which is a real argument in `Q-02`'s ledger: the engines share comparison logic and share nothing about contention. |

| D-01 | **Money never moves on a probabilistic match.** No auto-accept at any threshold | DT-01. A silent misattribution is how the industry's black box was built; the platform cannot sell recovery and manufacture the same loss. |
| D-02 | Matching is domain-owned **because of the credits graph**; the generic machinery rides `CX-M17` | DT-02. The graph is the discriminator no competitor has — qualified by DT-08 on when it arrives. |
