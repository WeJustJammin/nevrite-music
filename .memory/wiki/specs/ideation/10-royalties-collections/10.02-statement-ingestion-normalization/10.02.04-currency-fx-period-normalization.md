# Feature: Currency, FX & Period Normalization

> **Number**: 10.02.04
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate

## Overview

Making money from **different currencies and different calendars comparable** without lying about it. A GEMA performance is collected in euros for Q1 usage, remitted to PRS, distributed by PRS in October, received here in November, and the user thinks in pounds. Four dates and at least two currencies are in play, and choosing which ones to use changes the number the user is **paid** — not merely the number they are shown.

The sweep did not name this. It is included because FX-at-what-rate-on-what-date is a genuinely litigated question in royalty administration, and because getting it wrong produces a wrong payment that **reconciles perfectly against itself**. Every downstream consumer — the calculation engine (`10.03.01`), the payee statement (`10.04.02`), the tax reclaim (domain 23) — inherits this feature's honesty or its lies, and none of them can detect the difference.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Picks the **display reporting currency** they think in and read totals in. Sees, on every line: the source-reported amount in the source currency, the source's own words for the period, and — when a converted figure is shown — the rate, the rate-date and the rate provider. **Cannot** edit a pinned rate (that is editing history), **cannot** supply their own rate for a line the platform could not convert (D-11), and **cannot** hide the source-native amount (D-02). The critical capability is **seeing the original**: a statement that only shows converted amounts cannot be checked against the paper the society sent, which destroys `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation from the user's side and destroys their withholding-tax reclaim from HMRC's side (DT-06). |
| Producer | Config, per entity | Same, plus reporting currency **per administered entity** — and the currency follows the **entity context being viewed, not the human viewing it** (DT-07). A Producer whose personal display is GBP, administering a band entity whose books are EUR, sees EUR on the band's pages and GBP on their own; any screen showing both must label both. The failure this prevents is a Producer reading their personal GBP total on the band's page and paying out that number. |

No royalty statement is ever addressed to them, so no source currency of theirs exists to normalize. The one place they touch domain 10 (`10.06`, setlist reporting) produces usage data, not money. |
| Fan | None | No surface. Fan is `None` across all ten children of domain 10 — the only domain drilled with no Fan surface whatsoever (domain Role Matrix finding). Not simplified, not read-only: **absent**. A Fan never receives a royalty line, so there is nothing here to denominate. |

## Behavior

Every royalty line carries **four dates**, and conflating any two of them is a real error with money attached:

| Date | What it is | What it governs |
|---|---|---|
| **Usage period** | When the music was actually played | **Which split applies** (parent D-02). Nothing else. |
| **Distribution date** | When the society or source paid out | The FX rate the *source* used (usually undisclosed), the withholding event, and what `10.09`'s calendar predicts |
| **Received date** | When it arrived here | Nothing financial. It is an ingest fact and must never govern attribution (D-05) |
| **Payout date** | When the payee actually gets it (`10.04.01`) | The **realised** rate, and Q-01's ownership question |

And at least two currencies: the **source-reported currency** (what the counterparty's statement prints) and the **reporting currency** (what the user reads). Conversion between them requires a rate, and a rate requires a date — which is the whole question.

**The design position: the source-reported amount is the fact; the converted amount is a derived view.** The reported currency and amount are stored, immutable, forever, at the source's stated precision. Conversion is a presentation applied over the top, with the rate, rate-date and provider recorded alongside so the number can be reproduced years later. When money is actually paid out in a different currency from the source, the *realised* rate at payout is a separate, distinct fact from the display rate — and the difference between them is real gain or loss belonging to someone (Q-01).

Three things sharpen that position beyond what it looks like at first reading:

- **The source-reported amount is not the original amount.** For foreign-collected income it is already somebody else's conversion, made at a rate and on a date they did not disclose (DT-04). It is still the only checkable number, which is why D-01 stands — but the honest label is *"as reported by PRS"*, never *"earned"*.
- **The period a statement prints may not be a usage period at all.** On pass-through chains it is frequently the intermediary's receipt period (DT-05). The basis is a property of the source's format that only the adapter knows, and it must be declared rather than inferred.
- **Conversion is not one thing.** A line is converted for display (a preference), for an entity's books (an accounting fact), and for tax (a statute) — three simultaneous conversions of one fact, at three rates, on three dates, only one of which the user picks (DT-07). A display preference leaking into a tax figure is a failure with a regulator attached.

### Happy Path

1. A parsed line arrives from `10.02.02` carrying: the source-reported amount, the source-reported currency, the source's period label **in its own words**, and — declared by the adapter, not read off the file — the source's **period basis** (`usage` / `receipt` / `unknown`) and **period calendar** (D-06).
2. The period label is normalized to **explicit bounds** using the adapter's declared calendar for that source — never the label's ordinary-language meaning. "Q1" from a broadcast-calendar source does not start on 1 January. The source's own words are retained verbatim beside the bounds, forever.
3. Basis `usage` → the bounds **are** the usage period. Basis `receipt` → the bounds are a receipt period, and the usage window is that period shifted back by the source's declared lag from `10.09`'s hand-maintained calendar (D-07). Basis `unknown` → there is no usable usage period; the line is an exception (D-05).
4. The amount is retained **unchanged**: source currency, source's stated precision, that currency's own minor-unit convention (D-16). Not rounded, not converted, not written over. A DSP line of `$0.0003241` stays `$0.0003241`.
5. The usage period is handed to `10.03.01`, which reads domain 09's split **as of that period** (parent D-02). Where the usage period intersects a split change, the line is **allocated pro-rata by calendar-day overlap, in source currency**, at the finest sub-period granularity the source itself supplies (D-08, D-09). The source line is never rewritten — the allocation is a derivation over it.
6. A view in a reporting currency is requested. Each amount is converted **individually** at the pinned provider's rate for its rate-date, and the rate, rate-date and provider are recorded with the derived figure (D-04). Where no rate exists, nothing is invented (D-11).
7. Totals in the reporting currency are the **sum of the converted parts** (D-10), so the displayed arithmetic always closes. Reconciliation to the source's stated total happens in **source currency only** — that is the only place `10.02.02` D-02's oracle is arithmetically meaningful.
8. The user reads a total in the currency they think in; clicks any line; and sees the source-reported amount, the source's own period words, the rate, the rate-date and the provider — enough to hold their paper statement beside the screen and check it, line by line.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Statement states a period vaguely ("2025 H1", "Distribution 2025.2") | Common. Half-years, "accounting periods", society-specific distribution names | Normalized to explicit bounds via the adapter's declared calendar (D-06); **the source's own words retained verbatim** beside them |
| Source uses a broadcast or 4-4-5 calendar | Its "Q1" does not start 1 January and its "March" is whole weeks | Bounds come from the adapter's declared calendar, never from the label's ordinary meaning. A label read literally is a silent multi-day error at exactly the split boundaries that matter |
| Period bounds at a timezone edge | A DSP's "March" ends at midnight in which zone? A stream at 23:30 UTC on 31 March is April in Sydney | Bounds carry the source's declared zone; where undeclared, **UTC, and the assumption is labelled**. Only material where a split change lands on the boundary — which is precisely when it decides who is paid |
| **Statement's period is the intermediary's receipt period, not usage** (DT-05) | A sub-publisher's "H1 2025" line may carry GEMA's Q4 2024 distribution covering Q2 2024 usage. The file does not say which, and the difference is 6–18 months | Basis declared by the adapter (D-06). Usage window derived from `10.09`'s lag. **If a split change falls anywhere in that window, the line is an exception** (D-07) — the platform will not guess which owner a 12-month uncertainty band pays |
| Statement arrives with no period at all | Rare and toxic — the line cannot be attributed to a split as-of anything | Must not default to "today" or to the received date. Held as an exception (`10.02.05`), with its money denominated (D-05) |
| **Usage period overlaps two split versions** | Splits changed mid-period (domain 09). One line covers both owners | **Allocated pro-rata by calendar-day overlap, in source currency** (D-08). Where the source supplies finer detail (monthly lines inside a quarterly roll-up), the finer detail is used and the allocation is exact. Usage distribution is **never invented**. The source line stays one line; the allocation is disclosed as derived, so the user can still find the original £100 on their paper statement |
| Allocation doesn't sum back to the source line | Rounding dust across a 3-way split of an odd amount | The allocations sum **exactly** to the source line, in source currency, with the residual explicit → `10.03.01` D-04. Never in the reporting currency — see D-09 |
| **Source's own statement already contains a hidden conversion** (DT-04) | A UK writer's PRS statement prints GBP for German performances. The euro figure GEMA remitted, and the rate PRS used, are not on the statement and are unrecoverable | The reported GBP is the fact and the only reconcilable number. Labelled **"as reported by PRS"**, never "earned in GBP". The platform states plainly that the underlying euro amount and rate are not disclosed by the source — `10.01.07` D-03's at-source-netting wall, arriving through a currency door |
| **Multi-currency statement with a converted-only stated total** | Real for aggregated foreign income. Checking the total requires the *source's* undisclosed rate | Reconciled **per-currency subtotal** where the statement gives them. Where only a converted grand total exists, this feature declares the reconciliation **advisory**, not gating — a mismatch could be the source's rate, not the platform's parse. Whether `10.02.02` D-02's hard gate honours that is Q-05 |
| Statement shows original + converted + a rate that doesn't multiply out | The source's arithmetic is wrong, or uses an undisclosed intermediate step | The reported net is the fact — it is what was paid. The source's stated rate is retained as an **assertion**, the discrepancy is a **finding** routed to `10.08`, and the platform does not silently "correct" the source |
| **User uploads a doctored statement** — inflated rate, shifted period | Malicious input. The obvious attacks are minting FX gain and shifting usage into a window where they held a bigger split | A statement is **evidence, never truth** (`10.02` D-03). A file-asserted rate is never the platform's rate (D-15) — conversion always uses the pinned provider. Period shifting cannot mint money the source did not send, because the stated-total oracle still binds in source currency; a period outside the source's declared distribution calendar (`10.09`) is a finding |
| Source pays in a currency with **no rate from the pinned provider** | Thin-market currencies; pairs the provider does not publish | **No rate is invented.** The line is source-native only; its reporting-currency view is unavailable with a named reason; and it appears in any converted total as a **named residual**, never omitted (D-11) |
| Currency has **multiple simultaneous rates** | Official vs. parallel regimes (Argentina and Nigeria have both had them). The provider's rate can be legally correct and economically fictional | The pinned provider's rate is used and **labelled with the provider and the rate type**. Never presented as "what you would get". The pair is flagged as multi-rate so the number is read as a convention, not a promise. → Q-03 |
| Money is collected but **not remittable** (capital controls) | The society has it; it cannot leave the territory | An amount that exists, in a currency, that no rate honestly describes and no payout can realise. It is source-native, disclosed as trapped, and **excluded from any figure implying it is payable** — conflating "earned" with "collectible" is how `10.03.01` D-02's earning-vs-payability line gets crossed from a new direction |
| **Rate-date falls on a weekend, holiday, or provider outage** | ECB publishes around 16:00 CET on TARGET business days only. There is no Saturday rate, and Easter closes it for several consecutive days | Last published rate **on or before** the rate-date, within **7 calendar days**, labelled with its **actual publication date** — never the requested date. Beyond 7 days: unconvertible (D-11). 7 covers the longest routine closure with headroom without concealing a genuinely dead pair |
| **Historical rate revised by the provider** | The platform's past numbers would change retroactively | Rates are **pinned at use** (D-04) and never re-fetched. A number already shown, already stated, already paid does not move because a vendor restated a 2024 rate in 2026 |
| Provider changes methodology or delists a pair mid-history | Some lines pinned under the old method, some under the new | Pins are per-line, so history is stable. But a like-for-like comparison across the boundary is not — the **provider and method are pinned alongside the rate**, and comparisons spanning a provider change are labelled |
| **Negative line (return/adjustment) in a foreign currency** | A `-€100` reversal of a `+€100` line from 2024, converted at 2026's rate, does not net to zero in GBP — phantom money appears from nowhere | A reversal **inherits the pinned rate of the line it reverses**, so a full reversal nets to exactly zero in every currency (D-12). An unlinkable reversal pins at its own rate-date and the residual is disclosed, not absorbed |
| **Source restates a prior period** | `10.03.04`. PRS restated your Q1 2024 "down by £12" — when it actually restated it **up by €5** and the pound moved | Deltas are computed and reported in **source currency** (D-13). Any converted delta **decomposes into royalty movement and FX movement**, because a single blended figure reports the opposite of what happened |
| **Retroactive split correction** after lines are attributed | Someone corrects a 2024 split in 2026. Reads of 09 are as-of the usage period, so a *future-dated* split change never races — a **backdated** one does | The platform's own basis changed. Re-derivation produces a new interpretation **alongside** the old, never in place → `10.03.04`. Same shape as `10.02.02` Q-03's adapter correction: money may already have moved on the old basis |
| **User changes their reporting currency** | Every historical total re-derives | Re-derived using rates pinned **as of each line's rate-date** — history is not re-priced at today's rate. The previous currency's pins are **retained**, because a statement already issued in GBP must still reproduce exactly (D-17) |
| **Issued statement says £X; the same line viewed later says £Y** | The statement pinned at issue; an ad-hoc view pins at view-time | An issued payee statement (`10.04.02`) is **immutable and pins its rates at issue** (D-17). Ad-hoc views may differ and must name which pin they used. Otherwise the platform contradicts a document it sent to a payee |
| **Zero-decimal and three-decimal currencies** | JASRAC pays JPY (0 minor digits); KWD/BHD/TND have 3. A 2-decimal assumption mis-stores both | Held at the **currency's own minor-unit convention**, and sub-minor-unit per-stream amounts retained at the source's stated precision (D-16). **No rounding at ingest** — that is `10.03.01` D-04's decision to make later, with the dust visible |
| Sub-cent amounts across millions of lines | Rounding accumulates into real money | Not rounded here at all (D-16). Accumulation and disclosure → `10.03.01` D-04's territory |
| **Same money reported twice, in two currencies** | A society and its sub-publisher both report the same GEMA collection; the amounts differ (different conversions, different deductions) and the periods differ (usage vs receipt) | Duplicate detection is `10.02.01`/`10.02.02`'s job (CX-05) — but this feature is the reason **amount-equality cannot detect it**. Neither currency, amount, nor period matches. Flagged as a detection constraint the duplicate check must be told about, not a check this feature performs |
| **Converted amounts shown, original hidden** | User cannot reconcile against their own statement; and cannot reclaim withholding tax (DT-06) | **Forbidden** — D-02. Not a display preference: a regulatory input |
| **Multi-hyphenate reading two currencies at once** | A Producer whose personal display is GBP administers a band whose books are EUR, on a screen showing both | Currency follows the **entity context**, not the human (DT-07). Both are labelled explicitly. The failure prevented: reading a personal GBP total on the band's page and paying that number out |
| Partial conversion across a total | Some lines convertible, some not (D-11) | A converted subtotal **plus a named residual per unconvertible currency** — never a single number that quietly excludes what it could not convert |

### States

**Line normalization states** — what a line *is*:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Source-native | Line ingested and parsed | The original reported currency and amount, at source precision — **always available, in every state below** |
| Period-normalized | Bounds explicit; basis `usage` | Attributable to a split as-of the usage period |
| Window-bounded | Bounds explicit; basis `receipt` | Usage window derived from `10.09`'s lag. Attributable if no split change intersects it; exception if one does (D-07) |
| Allocated | Usage period spans a split change | Pro-rata by calendar-day overlap, in source currency, disclosed as derived (D-08). The source line still shows as one line |
| Converted (view) | Reporting currency requested and a rate is pinnable | The converted figure **with rate, rate-date and provider** |
| Unconvertible | No provider rate within 7 days of the rate-date; or a trapped/controlled currency | Source-native only, with the reason named. A **residual** in any total (D-11) |
| Unattributable | No usable period; or basis `unknown` | Exception (`10.02.05`), money denominated, **never defaulted to now** (D-05) |
| Realised | Paid out in another currency (`10.04.01`) | The rate actually achieved — a distinct fact from the display rate. Subject to Q-01 and to the rail reporting it at all |
| Superseded | Restated, re-parsed, or split retroactively corrected | The new interpretation **alongside** the old, with the delta decomposed into royalty and FX movement (D-13) |

**View states** — what a screen does:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | A reporting-currency view is being derived | Source-native amounts render **immediately** — they need no rate and no derivation. Only the converted column resolves late. The user is never blocked from their own money by a rate lookup |
| Empty | No statements ingested yet | No currency question exists. The reporting-currency picker is a **display preference and must not block onboarding** — nothing depends on it (D-01), and a user with no lines has nothing to denominate |
| Populated | Lines normalized, rates pinned | Totals in the reporting currency; every line clickable through to source-native, rate, rate-date and provider |
| Partial | Some lines unconvertible (D-11), or some periods still exceptions (D-05, D-07) | Converted subtotal **plus named residuals** plus a count and value of lines held as exceptions. Three honest numbers, never one dishonest one |
| Error | Rate provider unreachable at derivation time | Source-native renders; the converted column names the failure and offers retry. **A stale rate is never presented as a current one.** A conversion that cannot be pinned does not render |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Currency conversion is a display concern — store a normalized amount and show it | The obvious approach, and what a reporting-focused product would do | ❌ **REJECTED — storing only the converted amount destroys the domain's only hard oracle.** `10.02.02` D-02 makes reconciliation-to-the-stated-total the trust foundation of the whole domain; that check is arithmetically impossible against converted figures, because the society's stated total is in *their* currency at *their* period. Store the conversion and you can never again prove your parse was right, and neither can the user holding the paper statement. **The source amount is the fact; the conversion is a view.** This is the same principle as `10.01.07` D-03 (at-source netting is disclosed, never reverse-engineered), reached independently from a different direction — which is what raises it to a domain-level position. |
| DT-02 | The usage period and the payment date are close enough to treat as one | They are both "when", and the gap feels like an implementation detail | ❌ **REJECTED — they are routinely 6–18 months apart, and the gap is where the domain's money actually lives.** The usage period decides **which split applies** (parent D-02): a Q1 2024 usage paid in 2026 belongs to the people who owned it in 2024, even if the split changed in 2025 and the payee list is now different. Collapse the dates and the platform pays today's owners for yesterday's usage — silently, plausibly, and wrongly. The same gap is what `10.09` exists to make legible, and what makes `10.05.03`'s missing-distribution detection possible at all. **Four distinct dates, no conflation.** |
| DT-03 | FX movement between usage and payout is noise | It is a few percent, and it averages out | ❌ **REJECTED — it is somebody's money, and refusing to say whose is a decision made by default.** A euro royalty earned in Q1, distributed in October and paid to a UK writer in November has moved through a rate that shifted; the difference is real gain or loss. Somebody bears it: the platform (absorbing it, and taking a currency position it never agreed to), or the payee (bearing it, and needing to be told). Not deciding means the platform silently absorbs it at small scale and discovers a treasury exposure at large scale. **This is a product and a finance decision, not a rounding detail**, and it belongs in front of the owner rather than inside an implementation. |
| DT-04 | The source-reported amount is the **original** amount — the euro fact, before anyone touched it | D-01's own framing invites this reading: "the source amount is the fact" sounds like "the source amount is the truth" | ❌ **REJECTED — for foreign-collected income the source amount is already somebody else's undisclosed conversion, and the original is destroyed upstream.** A UK writer's PRS statement prints **GBP** for German performances: GEMA collected euros, remitted them under a reciprocal agreement, and PRS converted at PRS's rate on PRS's date. Neither the euro figure nor the rate appears on the statement, and no amount of platform diligence recovers them. This does **not** weaken D-01 — the reported GBP is still the only locally checkable number and the only thing the stated-total oracle works against. What it forbids is the **claim**: the platform must label the figure *"as reported by PRS"* and never *"earned"*. Two consequences bite. First, a foreign line's number is **not fully explainable** — its deduction and its FX both happened at an intermediary — which is `10.01.07` D-03's at-source-netting wall arriving through a currency door instead of a deduction door. Two features hitting the same wall from opposite sides is the evidence that the wall is real and not a gap in either. Second, D-08 (`explainability is the deliverable`) has a **hard ceiling on cross-border income**, and the honest product is to draw the ceiling rather than to imply it isn't there. |
| DT-05 | The period a statement prints is a usage period | It is labelled like one, it is in the period column, and every adapter will be written assuming it | ❌ **REJECTED — on pass-through chains it is routinely the intermediary's receipt period, and the file does not say which.** A sub-publisher's "H1 2025" line may contain GEMA's Q4 2024 distribution covering Q2 2024 usage. The real chain has more than four dates — usage → society distribution → sub-publisher receipt → sub-publisher distribution → arrival here — and most of them are destroyed in transit. So **D-03's four dates are a minimum, not a model**, and D-02 (splits as-of usage) is **not always satisfiable from the file**. The temptation is to infer the basis from the values; that is unsafe, because a receipt period and a usage period look identical. The basis is a **format semantic**, which is exactly the class of thing `10.02.02` D-03 says the platform owns per source, once — so the adapter declares it (D-06). Where basis is `receipt`, the usage window is the receipt period shifted back by the source's lag from `10.09` — and where a split change falls inside that window, the platform **declines to guess** (D-07). This does not conflict with CX R-04 ("the registry has no vote"): R-04 forbids overriding an *observed value* with a stale expectation. Declaring what a column *means* is not overriding what it *says*. |
| DT-06 | The original currency matters because the user wants to check their statement | The reconciliation argument, which is D-02's stated basis and is correct as far as it goes | ✅ **CONFIRMED — and it is a far stronger requirement than reconciliation, because the original currency is a tax document input.** Cross-border royalties carry withholding tax: US-source royalties to a non-resident are subject to **30% statutory withholding**, reduced by treaty (to **0%** on copyright royalties for a UK resident with a valid W-8BEN). To reclaim tax already withheld, the payee needs the **gross and the WHT in source currency** — a converted figure is worthless to a tax authority, which applies its own statutory rate on its own specified date. So converting-and-discarding the original does not merely break the user's check; it **destroys their ability to reclaim money they already paid**, which is domain 23's cross-border reclaim feature failing silently because of a decision made in ingestion. This promotes D-02 from good practice to **required**, and it adds a fifth date the four-date model doesn't hold: WHT applies at the **distribution** event, and the treaty rate depends on the payee's residence **at the time of payment**, not at the time of usage. A person who moved countries between usage and distribution has a different withholding answer than either date alone implies. |
| DT-07 | The reporting currency is a user preference — one person, one currency | It is how every consumer finance product works, and the Role Lens's "picks the currency they think in" implies it | ❌ **REJECTED as stated — a line has at least three simultaneous conversions and only one of them is a preference.** A UK writer in a German band paid by a US label: (1) **display** — GBP, because that is what they think in, and it is genuinely a preference; (2) **the entity's books** — the band entity's accounting currency, which is a property of the entity and not of any member's taste; (3) **tax** — HMRC's rate on HMRC's specified date, which is a statute and not negotiable by anyone. Three conversions, three rates, three dates, one underlying fact. The consequence is that the reporting currency is a **view parameter**, not a stored property of the money — which D-01 already implies but the Role Lens as written did not capture — and, sharply: **a display preference must never leak into a tax figure.** That is a failure mode with a regulator attached, and it is one line of code away in any design that stores "the user's currency" on the user. It also means the currency of a view follows the **entity context**, not the human: a Producer administering an EUR-booked band must not read GBP on the band's page. |
| DT-08 | Convert first, then split — one conversion per line is cheaper than one per payee | The natural engineering shortcut, and it looks equivalent | ❌ **REJECTED — it is not equivalent, and the difference lands on the only check the domain has.** Split £100 three ways in GBP and it closes: 33.33 + 33.33 + 33.34 = 100.00. Convert those three to EUR and they will not sum to the EUR conversion of £100. Convert first and then split and you get a **different set of numbers**, both defensible, neither reconcilable to the other. The rule follows from where the oracle lives: **allocate in source currency, convert the allocations** (D-09) — because source currency is the only place `10.02.02` D-02's sum must close, and `10.03.01` D-04's "the parts sum to the whole" is only a true statement **in one currency**. The corollary is the non-obvious half: a reporting-currency total must be the **sum of its converted parts** (D-10), never a conversion of the source total, so that the arithmetic on the user's screen always closes. That converted total is then a *derived* figure — labelled as such, never used for reconciliation and never used to pay anyone. |
| DT-09 | A restatement is a new line and prices at today's rate | Consistent-looking with D-04 ("rates are pinned at use") — a restatement is a use, so pin it | ❌ **REJECTED — it makes the restatement report the opposite of what happened.** A restatement is a **new use of an old period**, and pinning it fresh gives one usage period two rates. The delta between original and restated then contains an FX component the user reads as a royalty change: *"PRS restated your Q1 2024 down by £12"* when PRS actually restated it **up by €5** and the pound moved. The platform would be reporting a society error that does not exist, against a society that got it right, using a number the platform itself manufactured. So: **restatement deltas are computed and reported in source currency** (D-13), and any converted delta **decomposes** into royalty movement and FX movement. This is DT-02's no-conflation principle arriving in a third place — and the same reasoning forces D-12: a reversal must inherit the pinned rate of the line it reverses, or a `-€100` cancelling a `+€100` leaves phantom pounds behind. |
| DT-10 | The platform should **lock a rate at ingest** so the payee is protected from FX movement | The kind thing to do, and an obvious product answer to Q-01 — it makes the number stable and the promise clean | ❌ **REJECTED — guaranteeing a rate is running an FX book.** A platform that quotes a payee a rate at ingest and pays at a different one months later has taken the movement onto itself: that is a currency position, unhedged, unpriced, uncapitalised, and — at scale — a treasury function nobody staffed. Hedging it properly means buying forwards, which is a licensed activity in most territories and adjacent to Q-04's client-money problem, which the domain has already flagged as heavier than it looks. The answer to FX movement is **disclosure, not absorption** (DT-03, Q-01), and D-15's discipline says so directly: state what you know. Two wrinkles the rejection surfaces and hands to Q-01: (a) the payout currency may be a **fourth** currency — source EUR, display GBP, the payee's account USD, converted by the rail at the rail's own spread — and (b) **the platform may never learn the realised rate**, because some rails report only the debit amount. If the rail does not report its rate, disclosure is not merely unchosen, it is **impossible** — which makes "the rail must report its realised rate" a requirement on `CX-M01`, not a preference. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **10.02.02 Parsing & Format Adapters** ([10.02.02-statement-parsing-format-adapters.md](./10.02.02-statement-parsing-format-adapters.md)) — **trigger dependency + a consequence for a ratified decision.** Supplies source currency, the period label, and (per D-06) the adapter-declared period **basis** and **calendar**, which are format semantics only the adapter knows. Per DT-01, reconciliation only works in source terms. **New consequence**: `10.02.02` D-02's gate is stated unconditionally, but a multi-currency statement whose stated total is a converted grand total cannot be reconciled to equality without the source's own undisclosed rate — the gate would fire on FX noise and block a correct parse. This feature reconciles per-currency subtotals and declares the converted-total case **advisory**; whether the sibling's gate honours that is Q-05, and it is the sibling's decision to make.
- Touches **10.02.01 Source Registry** ([10.02.01-statement-source-ingestion.md](./10.02.01-statement-source-ingestion.md)) — **boundary, deliberately narrow.** CX R-04 stands: a statement's currency is a property of the file, and the registry has no vote. D-06 does not reopen it — declaring what a period column *means* is a format semantic, not an override of an observed value. **New detection constraint**: on pass-through chains the same money arrives twice, in two currencies, with different amounts and different periods, so the duplicate/restatement check (CX-05) **cannot use amount-equality**. That is this feature's fault and the registry's problem; it must be told.
- Touches **10.02.05 Exception Queue** ([10.02.05-unmatched-line-exception-queue.md](./10.02.05-unmatched-line-exception-queue.md)) — **trigger dependency.** Two routes in, both period-shaped, both consistent with CX R-02's reasoning that the edge is a period edge and never an FX one: a line with no usable period (D-05), and — new — a `receipt`-basis line whose usage window straddles a split change (D-07). Both arrive **source-native and denominated**; the queue never holds a converted amount.
- Touches **10.03.01 Calculation Engine** ([../10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.01-royalty-calculation-engine.md](../10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.01-royalty-calculation-engine.md)) — **shared entity + ordering constraint.** The usage period selects the split version (DT-02, its D-03). **New**: D-09 fixes the operation order — allocate in source currency, then convert — because its D-04 ("the parts sum to the whole") is only true in **one** currency, and D-16 means the dust arrives here un-rounded and visible rather than pre-destroyed at ingest.
- Touches **10.03.04 Restatement, Adjustment & Overpayment** ([../10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.04-restatement-adjustment-overpayment.md](../10.03-calculation-recoupment/10.03.04-restatement-adjustment-overpayment.md)) — **shared entity.** D-13: deltas in source currency, converted deltas decomposed into royalty vs FX movement. D-12: a reversal inherits its original's pinned rate. Also the destination for the **backdated split correction** race — the platform's own basis moving under already-attributed lines, the same shape as `10.02.02` Q-03's adapter correction.
- Touches **10.04.01 Payout Run** ([../10.04-disbursement-payee-statements/10.04.01-royalty-payout-run.md](../10.04-disbursement-payee-statements/10.04.01-royalty-payout-run.md)) — **shared entity + Q-01.** The realised rate at payout is a distinct fact from the display rate. Per DT-10, the payout currency may be a **fourth** currency and the rail may never report the rate it achieved.
- Touches **10.04.02 Payee Statements** ([../10.04-disbursement-payee-statements/10.04.02-payee-royalty-statements.md](../10.04-disbursement-payee-statements/10.04.02-payee-royalty-statements.md)) — **state-race.** D-17: an issued statement pins its rates at issue and is immutable, or the platform contradicts a document it sent to a payee. Its DT-01 puts the domain's value here; a statement whose numbers move after issue has no value at all.
- Touches **10.08 Statement Disputes** ([../10.08-statement-disputes-audit-rights.md](../10.08-statement-disputes-audit-rights.md)) — **trigger dependency.** A source whose own rate does not multiply out is a finding, not a thing to silently correct. And a payee disputing their conversion is disputing the platform's **rate provider** (Q-03), which is a dispute the platform can lose on evidence it does not control.
- Touches **10.09 Distribution Calendar & Money-in-Flight** ([../10.09-distribution-calendar-money-in-flight.md](../10.09-distribution-calendar-money-in-flight.md)) — **reverse trigger dependency, new.** The existing edge is that the usage→distribution lag is 10.09's subject matter. The new one runs the other way: **10.09's hand-maintained lag is an input to D-07**, bounding the usage window for a `receipt`-basis line. That makes 10.09 load-bearing for **attribution correctness**, not only for expectation — a materially higher bar than its D-03 ("data, maintained by hand, forever") was accepted under, and a reason its Should ranking may be understated.
- Touches **09 Rights & Ownership** ([../../09-rights-ownership/](../../09-rights-ownership/)) — **shared entity.** As-of split reads (domain Q-05); split **effective dates** are what D-08's pro-rata slices against; and Q-02's residue is a question about 09's split model, not about this feature.
- Touches **23 Career, Finance & Business** ([../../23-career-finance-business/](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — **shared entity, with a regulator attached.** 23's cross-border withholding reclaim needs the **source-currency gross and WHT** (DT-06) — a converted figure is worthless to a tax authority. And per DT-07, 23's statutory conversion is a **different conversion** from this feature's display preference, at a different rate on a different date; the two must never be the same number wearing two labels.
- Rides **Localization, Currency & Timezone** (`CX-M20`) — **permission/machinery boundary.** Rate sourcing, formatting, ISO 4217 minor-unit conventions and timezone handling at period bounds are platform machinery. The **policy** — which rate, which date, which provider, who bears the movement, what happens when there is no rate — is domain-owned and partly unresolved (Q-01, Q-03).
- Rides **Money Rails & Escrow** (`CX-M01`) via `10.04` — **requirement, not preference.** Per DT-10, the rail must **report its realised rate**, or Q-01's disclosure is impossible regardless of which way the owner answers it.
- Proposes cross-cut **Point-in-Time / As-Of Record Resolution** (DT-02) — serves 09, 10, 11, 12, 23. Reinforced by DT-05: the as-of key is sometimes a **window**, not a date, and a resolver that only accepts a date cannot express D-07.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **The source-reported amount is the fact; conversion is a derived view** | DT-01. Storing only conversions makes `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation impossible for the platform and for the user. |
| D-02 | The original currency and amount are **always visible** to the user | They cannot check the platform against their own paper statement otherwise — and per DT-06 they cannot reclaim withholding tax either. Not a preference: a regulatory input. |
| D-03 | **Four distinct dates**: usage period, distribution, received, payout. No conflation | DT-02. Usage period selects the split version; the others do not. Per DT-05 four is the **minimum**, and per DT-06 withholding adds a fifth consideration keyed to the payee's residence at payment. |
| D-04 | Rates are **pinned at use** with rate-date, provider and method recorded, never re-fetched | A provider revising a historical rate would retroactively change numbers already paid. |
| D-05 | A line with no usable period is an **exception**, never defaulted to the arrival date | Defaulting silently attributes old money to today's owners. |
| D-06 | **Period basis (`usage` / `receipt` / `unknown`) and period calendar are adapter-declared** properties of the source, never inferred from the file's values | DT-05. A receipt period and a usage period are indistinguishable by inspection. This is a format semantic, which `10.02.02` D-03 already puts on the platform, per source, once. Does not conflict with CX R-04: declaring what a column means is not overriding what it says. |
| D-07 | A `receipt`-basis line whose usage window (derived from `10.09`'s lag) **intersects a split change** is an exception, not a guess | DT-05 + parent D-02. Cheap in the common case (splits rarely change) and expensive exactly where the money is at risk. The platform does not pick an owner out of a 12-month uncertainty band. |
| D-08 | A usage period spanning a split change is **allocated pro-rata by calendar-day overlap**, at the finest sub-period granularity the source itself supplies. Usage distribution is **never invented** | Resolves Q-02's behaviour. Pro-rata by days is deterministic, reproducible and disclosed; pro-rata by actual usage is *more correct* and requires data the statement does not contain — inventing it is the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` names. Where the source **does** supply finer detail (monthly lines inside a quarterly roll-up), that detail is used and the allocation is exact. |
| D-09 | **Allocate in source currency; convert the allocations.** Never convert then allocate | DT-08. `10.03.01` D-04's "the parts sum to the whole" is only true in one currency, and that currency must be the one where `10.02.02` D-02's oracle lives. |
| D-10 | **Reporting-currency totals are the sum of their converted parts**, never a conversion of a source total. Reconciliation to a stated total happens **only in source currency** | DT-08. Keeps the arithmetic on the user's screen closed, and keeps the one hard check where it is meaningful. A converted total is a derived figure: never reconciled against, never paid from. |
| D-11 | **The platform never invents a rate.** No provider rate on or before the rate-date within **7 calendar days** ⇒ source-native only, reason named, and a **named residual** in any converted total | Resolves the thin-market case. 7 days covers the longest routine provider closure (Easter on the ECB's TARGET calendar) with headroom, without concealing a genuinely dead pair. D-15's discipline: say what you know, say when you don't, never omit. |
| D-12 | A **reversal inherits the pinned rate of the line it reverses**, so a full reversal nets to exactly zero in every currency | DT-09. Otherwise a `-€100` cancelling a `+€100` leaves phantom pounds. An unlinkable reversal pins at its own rate-date and the residual is disclosed. |
| D-13 | **Restatement deltas are computed in source currency**; a converted delta must decompose into **royalty movement** and **FX movement** | DT-09. A blended delta reports a society error that did not happen, using a number the platform manufactured. |
| D-14 | The source figure is labelled **"as reported by {source}"**, never "earned" | DT-04. For foreign-collected income it is already the source's own undisclosed conversion. D-08 (explainability) has a **hard ceiling** on cross-border income; the honest product draws the ceiling. |
| D-15 | A **file-asserted rate is an assertion, never the platform's rate** | Sub-domain D-03: a statement is evidence, not truth. Conversion always uses the pinned provider. The source's rate is retained, and a mismatch is a finding for `10.08`. |
| D-16 | **Nothing is rounded at ingest.** Amounts are retained at the source's stated precision, in the source currency's own minor-unit convention (JPY 0dp, KWD 3dp, per-stream sub-minor-unit amounts intact) | A 2-decimal assumption is wrong for JPY in one direction and KWD in the other, and destroys `$0.0003241` entirely. Rounding is `10.03.01` D-04's decision to make later, with the dust visible. |
| D-17 | An **issued payee statement pins its display rates at issue and is immutable**; ad-hoc views may differ and must name their pin | `10.04.02`. A platform that contradicts a document it sent to a payee has no statement product. Changing reporting currency re-derives from **per-line pins**, never at today's rate, and retains the old currency's pins. |

## Open Questions

| # | Question | Owner | Deferred To |
|---|----------|-------|-------------|
| Q-01 | **Who bears FX movement between the source's distribution and the payee's payout?** (DT-03.) Platform absorbs = an unhedged currency position nobody signed up for. Payee bears = a variable payment that needs explaining. There is no third option — DT-10 kills the tempting one (locking a rate *is* running an FX book, and hedging it properly is a licensed activity adjacent to Q-04) — and not choosing is choosing the first. **Two sharpenings from this pass**: (a) the question is *conditional on domain Q-07* — if WeJammin never moves money it cannot bear the movement, and its job shrinks to naming who does; but even compute-only, telling a band leader "pay Sarah £X" is a number that was wrong by the time they paid it. (b) The payout currency may be a **fourth** currency, and some rails report only the debit amount — so if the rail does not report its realised rate, **disclosure is impossible whichever way you answer**. Requiring the rail to report it is a constraint on `CX-M01`. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-02 | **Does domain 09's split record carry an "earned vs received" basis?** The behaviour is decided (D-08: pro-rata by calendar days, finer where the source supplies it, never invented) — but the *default* is only right if the parties didn't agree otherwise, and in catalogue transactions they always do: "income **earned** after closing" vs "income **received** after closing" is the most-negotiated clause in a catalogue acquisition, and it decides exactly this case. If 09's split has an effective date and nothing else, the platform silently applies its default to deals whose paperwork says the opposite. A split captured in-session won't have the clause; a split arising from a catalogue sale always will. | User | `/create-prd-architecture` (and domain 09) |
| Q-03 | **Which FX rate source, and is it authoritative enough to be contractually defensible?** A payee disputing their conversion is disputing the platform's rate provider, and "we used our vendor's mid-market rate" may not be a sufficient answer — especially when the payee received the **realised** rate, which includes a spread the mid-market rate does not. Sharpened by this pass: the provider's **methodology can change** and pairs can be **delisted** mid-history (pins keep history stable but break like-for-like comparison), and for **multi-rate regimes** (Argentina and Nigeria have both run official and parallel rates simultaneously) the provider's rate can be legally correct and economically fictional. | User | `/create-prd` |
| Q-04 | **Does the platform present a statutory/tax conversion at all, or is that entirely domain 23's?** DT-07 says a display preference must never leak into a tax figure — but somebody must own the statutory rate tables (HMRC's published rates, the IRS yearly average), and they are a different rate on a different date from anything this feature pins. Related: does an **entity** have a *functional currency* in the accounting sense — a property of its primary economic environment, driving its books — or only a display preference? A band entity with real accounting has the former, and 23 will need it. | User | `/create-prd` (and domain 23) |
| Q-05 | **Does `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation gate carve out an FX case?** D-02 is stated unconditionally and its own edge-case table forbids "close enough" — correctly. But a multi-currency statement whose stated total is a **converted grand total** cannot be reconciled to equality without the source's undisclosed rate, so the gate fires on FX noise and blocks a correct parse. This feature's side is decided (D-10: per-currency subtotals where given; advisory otherwise). The sibling's side is not mine to decide, and the honest options are ugly: an FX tolerance (which is the "close enough" D-02 forbids), a gate that skips converted-total statements (a hole in the only hard oracle), or refusing multi-currency statements at ingest (refusing aggregated foreign income, which is where the FX problem lives in the first place). | User | `/create-prd` |
