# Feature: Currency, FX & Period Normalization

> **Number**: 10.02.04
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate

## Overview

Making money from **different currencies and different calendars comparable** without lying about it. A GEMA statement pays euros for Q1 usage, distributed in October, received in November, and the user thinks in pounds. Four dates and two currencies are in play, and choosing which ones to use changes the number the user is paid — not merely the number they are shown.

The sweep did not name this. It is included because FX-at-what-rate-on-what-date is a genuinely litigated question in royalty administration, and because getting it wrong produces a wrong payment that reconciles perfectly against itself.

## Behavior

Every royalty line carries **four dates**, and conflating any two of them is a real error:

- **Usage period** — when the music was actually played. What the money is *about*. The date that governs which split applies (parent D-02).
- **Distribution date** — when the society or source paid out. What `10.09`'s calendar predicts.
- **Received date** — when it arrived here.
- **Payout date** — when the payee actually gets it (`10.04.01`).

And two currencies: the **source currency** (what the counterparty paid) and the **reporting currency** (what the user reads). The conversion between them requires a rate, and the rate requires a date — which is the whole question.

The design position: **the source amount is the fact; the converted amount is a derived view.** The original currency and amount are stored, immutable, forever. Conversion is a presentation applied over the top, with the rate and rate-date recorded alongside so the number can be reproduced. When money is actually paid out in a different currency from the source, the *realised* rate at payout is a separate, distinct fact from the display rate — and the difference between them is real gain or loss belonging to someone (Q-01).

### Happy Path

1. Parsed lines arrive with source currency and the source's period convention (`10.02.02`)
2. Period convention is normalized to an explicit usage period — never inferred from the file's arrival date
3. Amounts are retained in source currency, unchanged
4. Reporting-currency views are derived with the rate and rate-date recorded
5. Totals are comparable across sources; every one of them can be traced back to an original the user can check against paper

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Statement states a period vaguely ("2025 H1") | Common. Half-years, "accounting periods", society-specific calendars | Normalized to explicit bounds, with the source's own words retained |
| FX rate moved 8% between usage and payout | Whose gain or loss? A real question with money attached | Must be **disclosed**, not absorbed. See Q-01 |
| Source pays in a currency with no clean rate | Thin-market currencies, capital controls | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Usage period overlaps two split versions | Splits changed mid-period (domain 09). The line covers both | **The hardest case.** Pro-rata by period, or by usage within it? `[PENDING — Step 5]`; see Q-02 |
| Statement arrives with no period at all | Rare and toxic — the line cannot be attributed to a split as-of anything | Must not default to "today". Held as an exception (`10.02.05`) |
| Converted amounts shown, original hidden | User cannot reconcile against their own statement | Forbidden — D-02 |
| Historical rate revised by the provider | The platform's past numbers change retroactively | Rates are **pinned at use**, never re-fetched |
| Sub-cent amounts across millions of lines | Rounding accumulates into real money | → `10.03.01` D-04's territory |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Source-native | Line ingested | Original currency and amount — always available |
| Normalized | Period bounds explicit | Attributable to a split as-of the usage period |
| Converted (view) | Reporting currency requested | Converted, with rate and rate-date shown |
| Unattributable | No usable period | Exception (`10.02.05`) — never defaulted to now |
| Realised | Paid out in another currency | The actual rate achieved, distinct from the display rate |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Currency conversion is a display concern — store a normalized amount and show it | The obvious approach, and what a reporting-focused product would do | ❌ **REJECTED — storing only the converted amount destroys the domain's only hard oracle.** `10.02.02` D-02 makes reconciliation-to-the-stated-total the trust foundation of the whole domain; that check is arithmetically impossible against converted figures, because the society's stated total is in *their* currency at *their* period. Store the conversion and you can never again prove your parse was right, and neither can the user holding the paper statement. **The source amount is the fact; the conversion is a view.** This is the same principle as `10.01.07` D-03 (at-source netting is disclosed, never reverse-engineered), reached independently from a different direction — which is what raises it to a domain-level position. |
| DT-02 | The usage period and the payment date are close enough to treat as one | They are both "when", and the gap feels like an implementation detail | ❌ **REJECTED — they are routinely 6–18 months apart, and the gap is where the domain's money actually lives.** The usage period decides **which split applies** (parent D-02): a Q1 2024 usage paid in 2026 belongs to the people who owned it in 2024, even if the split changed in 2025 and the payee list is now different. Collapse the dates and the platform pays today's owners for yesterday's usage — silently, plausibly, and wrongly. The same gap is what `10.09` exists to make legible, and what makes `10.05.03`'s missing-distribution detection possible at all. **Four distinct dates, no conflation.** |
| DT-03 | FX movement between usage and payout is noise | It is a few percent, and it averages out | ❌ **REJECTED — it is somebody's money, and refusing to say whose is a decision made by default.** A euro royalty earned in Q1, distributed in October and paid to a UK writer in November has moved through a rate that shifted; the difference is real gain or loss. Somebody bears it: the platform (absorbing it, and taking a currency position it never agreed to), or the payee (bearing it, and needing to be told). Not deciding means the platform silently absorbs it at small scale and discovers a treasury exposure at large scale. **This is a product and a finance decision, not a rounding detail**, and it belongs in front of the owner rather than inside an implementation. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **The source amount is the fact; conversion is a derived view** | DT-01. Storing only conversions makes `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation impossible for the platform and for the user. |
| D-02 | The original currency and amount are **always visible** to the user | They cannot check the platform against their own paper statement otherwise. |
| D-03 | **Four distinct dates**: usage period, distribution, received, payout. No conflation | DT-02. Usage period selects the split version; the others do not. |
| D-04 | Rates are **pinned at use** with their rate-date recorded, never re-fetched | A provider revising a historical rate would retroactively change numbers already paid. |
| D-05 | A line with no usable period is an **exception**, never defaulted to the arrival date | Defaulting silently attributes old money to today's owners. |
