# Feature: Unmatched Line & Exception Queue

> **Number**: 10.02.05
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate

## Overview

Where **money that arrived but could not be honestly attributed** goes, and where it stays **visible** until someone resolves it. Lines that matched nothing (`10.02.03` D-03), lines whose only candidate was a single-signal guess (`10.02.03` D-15), lines with no usable period (`10.02.04` D-05), lines naming an identifier the platform has never seen (`10.02.03` D-04) — all of it is real money that arrived, belonging to somebody, that the platform cannot yet name.

The sweep did not propose this. It exists because the alternative is a `NULL` in a matching table, and a `NULL` is how a platform silently becomes the thing it was built to fight. Domain 10's headline promise is that it can find money the industry lost in exactly this way (`10.05.01`). A queue that hides its own unattributed money would be the industry's black box, rebuilt, by the company selling the cure (parent domain `D-09`).

**The deepening pass changed the feature's shape twice.**

**First: the platform probably does not hold this money.** The original framing — "money the platform received" — assumed a fact that is not decided and probably false (domain `Q-07`: *does WeJammin move money at all?*). PRS pays the writer's bank account directly; DistroKid pays the account holder. The statement is a document *about* a payment that already happened somewhere else. So the modal queued line is not stuck money — it is **money the user already has, that the platform cannot explain** (`DT-06`). That is a different product and a sharper one, because of the second change.

**Second: the primary victim is not the account holder.** If a £340 line cannot be attributed to a work, the split cannot be computed (`10.03.01`), and the drummer's 12% of it stays in the bandleader's account — unpaid, unnoticed, and unknown to the drummer, who has no way to learn the money exists (`DT-07`). **That is `problem-statement.md`'s root cause reproduced inside the platform built to prevent it**: a fact about who is owed what, sitting unrecorded, waiting to be reconstructed from memory once it is contested. The exception queue is where WeJammin either honours its thesis or quietly reproduces the industry's failure with better typography.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Full | Sees **their own unexplained money**, as an amount, never as an error log — *"£340 arrived from PRS that we can't attribute to any of your works"* is the honest sibling of `10.05.01`'s *"we found you £800"*, pointed inward. Resolves identities (never lines — `D-08`) using knowledge no data source holds: they know `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` is *Midnight* **because they were there**. Can disclaim an identity as not theirs (`D-06`), supply a missing period from the paper statement they hold (`D-09`, cause `no-period`), and ask a collaborator who was on the session (`D-12`). Sees the residual on every income total anywhere in the platform (`D-19`) — they are never shown a total that quietly omits what could not be explained. Cannot see, resolve into, or receive a hint from any catalogue but their own and those they administer (`D-11`, `10.02.03` D-08). |
| **Producer** | Full | The same act at volume, across every entity they administer — and the **materially better resolver**, for the same reason they are the provenance capture point (`personas.md`): they ran the session, so they recognise a mangled title as a working title, an ISRC nobody remembers issuing, and a live take mis-titled by a society. They meet the widest identity spread (label and distributor statements) and therefore hit the queue's edges first — multi-party documents (`D-15`, cause `foreign`), unknown identifiers, and identities that exist in one of their catalogues and not another (`D-11`). They are also the persona most often on the **receiving** end of `D-12`'s ask: a Musician's unresolvable identity is frequently a question only their producer can answer. |
| **Operator** | None | No surface, and the absence is structural rather than an oversight. A venue's blanket PRS/PPL licence is a **cost, not an income statement** — Operators receive no royalty statement, hold no catalogue, and therefore have nothing that can fail to attribute. Their single appearance in domain 10 is `10.06` (setlist reporting), where their licence *generated* someone else's money — and the money that reporting produces lands on a Musician's statement, not theirs. |
| **Fan** | None | No surface. Fan is `None` across all ten children of domain 10 — the only domain drilled with no Fan surface whatsoever. Royalty attribution has no consumer-facing half; `D-11` (*fans are first-class users*) has zero consequence here. |

## Behavior

### The queue is a view, not a place

The queue does not hold lines. **The line is one object with an attribution state; matching writes it; the queue is the view over everything not attributed** (`10.02` CX-03 Q1). A copy would eventually disagree with its source, which is precisely the failure being avoided — a second record of the money that drifts from the first is a black box with an index.

The view's scope is deliberately wider than "matching failed". It covers **all money not attributed to a person**, including identities that have a confirmable candidate sitting unconfirmed in `10.02.03`'s surface. Those appear in the queue's total and **deep-link** into `10.02.03` to be decided; the queue never duplicates the decision (`D-07`, `DT-14`). The reason is that the user has exactly one question — *"why isn't this money accounted for?"* — and answering it across two screens that each show part of the number is how a total becomes unbelievable.

### The unit is the source identity, not the line

The queue inherits `10.02.03` D-06's collapse wholesale. Lines are the cross-product of `identity × territory × outlet × month`; a DistroKid statement is 40,000 lines describing 200 recordings, a PRS statement 3,000 lines describing 90 works. The queue's unit is the **source identity** — the tuple of identifying fields the source used to name the thing — and the collapse factor is routinely 100×–1,000×.

**The queue invents no grouping of its own** (`D-08`). The obvious feature — "cluster similar-looking rows so the user makes one decision" — would produce a *second* grouping, computed by different code, that disagrees with matching's. One identity, one decision, one place it was decided. A £1,200 problem presented as 400 £3 rows is not a queue; it is an obstacle.

### Three failures, not one

"Unmatched" is not a reason. Money fails to reach a person at three structurally distinct points, and flattening them into one status is what makes an exception queue unresolvable (`DT-05`):

| Question | Failure | Who can answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Which work is this about?** | Matching found nothing, or nothing it will assert | The user — from memory, or a collaborator (`D-12`) |
| **When was the usage?** | No usable period, so no split version can be selected | The document, the source, or the immateriality proof (`D-10`) |
| **Whose money is it, as of then?** | Work known, period known, and domain 09 holds **no split as-of that period** | Domain 09 — the split must be recorded, or recorded as absent |

The third is the one nobody designs for, because **matching succeeded**. A green tick on the matching surface hides money that still cannot move: a work registered in 2026 whose Q1 2024 usage predates any recorded split has a perfect match, a perfect date, and no owner. It is invisible to every check that watches the matcher (`DT-05`), and it is only detectable at all if domain 09 supports as-of reads (`Q-06`, domain `Q-05`).

### Reason taxonomy — closed set, each with a named route

Every queued identity carries exactly one reason code and a route (`D-09`). The set is closed: a line that fits none of these is a defect, not a new category.

| Code | Meaning | Produced by | Resolution route |
|---|---|---|---|
| `no-candidate` | Nothing in the catalogue resembles the identity | `10.02.03` cascade step 6 | User names it; or the catalogue grows and the re-sweep resolves it (`D-03`); or, on a thin catalogue, it becomes a claim proposal to `02.03.02` (`10.02.03` D-12) |
| `sub-threshold` | Exactly one signal class agreed — title-only | `10.02.03` D-15 | The near-miss **and its evidence** are shown; user confirms or rejects; the rejection is retained (`10.02.03` D-05) |
| `ambiguous` | ≥2 candidates each clear the ≥2-signal floor; nothing discriminates | `10.02.03` cascade step 5 | User picks, or supplies the discriminator (which mix, which session) |
| `unknown-identifier` | The line names an ISRC/ISWC/UPC the platform has never seen | `10.02.03` D-04 | **A finding, not a miss** → domain 09. Money waits here while 09 decides whether the identifier is the user's |
| `no-period` | Work identifiable; no usable usage period | `10.02.04` D-05 | Immateriality proof (`D-10`); else the user supplies it from the document they hold; else the source is asked |
| `no-owner-as-of` | Work known, period known, 09 holds no split as-of that period | This feature (`DT-05`) | → domain 09: record the split as-of, or record explicitly that the work was unowned/unregistered then |
| `foreign` | The document's remittee is not the user or an entity they administer | `10.02.01` source registry (`Q-04`) | **Not a resolution task.** Evidence about someone else's money; never denominated as the user's (`D-15`) |
| `disclaimed` | The user asserts it is not theirs | This feature (`D-06`) | Terminal, reversible. A later re-sweep that contradicts it is a **finding presented once**, never an override (`D-13`) |
| `reversed` | Was attributed; the mapping was reversed and the line returned | `10.02.03` D-10 | Re-enters under its original cause. Money already paid on it is `10.03.04`'s problem, not the queue's |
| `superseded` | The statement version that produced the line was restated | `10.02.01` D-04, `10.03.04` D-01 | Retired from the live total; retained forever (`D-02`). Never deleted, never double-counted |
| `stale` | The source's correction window has closed | This feature (`D-14`, `D-17`) | **No route.** Recorded permanently in the published Closed total (`D-14`) |

### Whose money is it — the remittance relationship decides the sentence

The queue's headline is a financial claim, and the wrong headline is a lie the user will believe. `DT-03` established that denomination in money is what makes the queue get read; `DT-06` established that the platform usually does not hold the money; `10.02` parent `Q-03` establishes that a label statement names every artist on it. Together those make one hardcoded sentence impossible.

The sentence is **derived from the remittance relationship** the source registry records — *who was this money paid to?* (`D-15`, and it requires a registry field that `10.02.01` does not currently state: `Q-04`):

| Remittee | Headline | Why it is the honest one |
|---|---|---|
| The user | *"£340 you were paid that we can't explain — and some of it may be owed to your collaborators."* | They have the money. The urgency is the collaborator's, not theirs (`DT-07`) |
| An entity they administer | *"£3,400 paid to Northgate Records that we can't attribute to any of its works."* | The entity has the money; the administrator is accountable for it |
| A third party | *"This statement describes £3,400 paid to someone else. £900 of it may relate to your works."* | The document is evidence, not income (`10.02` D-03). Claiming it as theirs would be the platform inventing a number — `D-15`'s prohibition, committed by the feature that exists to prevent it |
| Unknown | *"£340 on a statement we can't tell you the recipient of."* | `D-15` (domain): the platform states what it knows and says so when it doesn't |

**This dissolves a dependency, not a question.** `Q-02` (does the platform ever hold this money?) stays open and belongs to the owner — but the design no longer waits on it, because the sentence is derived from a fact the statement already contains rather than from an unmade decision.

### The clock, and what it actually measures

`D-05` established that ageing is the alarm — the domain's fifth independent arrival at *an absence produces no event, so the clock must produce one* (domain `D-04`). The deepening pass established **what the clock is measuring**, and it is not what it looks like (`DT-08`).

The obvious reading is a filing deadline: sources will not correct beyond a window, so beat the window. That is real and it is the **secondary** clock. The primary one is **memory decay**. The only resolver of a `no-candidate` identity is a human's recollection that `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` was the Camden session in March — and **memory is precisely the faculty this platform exists to not rely on** (`problem-statement.md`: *they are asking people to remember, and memory is contested, lossy, and self-interested*). A queue that ages is a reconstruction engine. Every quarter it goes unread, WeJammin becomes more like the institutions it criticises, using the same mechanism.

Two consequences, both concrete:

- **The loudest moment is ingest, not the schedule** (`D-18`). The statement has just arrived; the user is looking at the product; the usage period is as recent as it will ever be. Resolution offered at ingest costs a Tuesday afternoon (`10.02.03` DT-04); the same resolution offered in eighteen months may be impossible at any price. The scheduled ladder is the fallback for what ingest did not clear — not the mechanism.
- **The ask-a-collaborator route exists because the account holder is not always the best resolver** (`D-12`, `DT-09`).

**The ladder** is anchored to the source's declared correction window, from `10.02.01`'s registry (`D-17`), never to a global constant:

| Rung | Trigger | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Ingest | In-product, immediate | The identity, its money, its candidates. The cheapest resolution in the feature's life (`D-18`) |
| 1 | 50% of window elapsed | In-product only | Ranked by value; the residual stated (`D-16`) |
| 2 | 75% elapsed | Joins the periodic digest | Money-denominated, per `D-01` |
| 3 | 90% elapsed | One direct alert | Names the **amount** and the **date it becomes unrecoverable** |
| 4 | Window closed | One final notice, **then never again** | Moves to the Closed total (`D-14`). An alarm that keeps firing after nothing can be done is training to ignore alarms |

Where the source's window is **undeclared**, the clock uses a platform default of 12 months from the received date, **labelled as a default in the UI** — *"we do not know PRS's correction window; this deadline is ours, not theirs"* — and the undeclared window is itself a finding on the source registry. The alternative is choosing between an invented deadline presented as fact (domain `D-15`'s prohibition) and no clock at all (domain `D-04`'s prohibition). The ladder's percentages are a defensible starting rule with no evidence behind them; see `Q-09`.

### The re-sweep — the queue is a standing corpus, not a backlog

`DT-02` established that resolution is not one-shot at ingest: a growing catalogue retroactively resolves old lines. The deepening pass specifies its triggers, its scope, its precedence and its reverse direction.

**Triggers** — every event that could make a previously-unmatchable identity matchable:

| Trigger | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A work or recording is registered | `10.01`, domain 09 | Re-evaluate `no-candidate` + `sub-threshold` identities in **that catalogue only** |
| An ISRC/ISWC arrives | Domain 09 | May resolve `unknown-identifier` findings directly and deterministically |
| A credit is claimed or confirmed | `02.03` | Strengthens the party-name discriminator (`10.02.03` DT-06) — may lift a `sub-threshold` identity to a confirmable candidate |
| **An identity mapping is confirmed** | `10.02.03` D-07 | The highest-yield trigger: confirming one mapping on this quarter's statement resolves **every historical queued line of the same source identity**, deterministically, at once (`10.02.03` D-07 — replay is arithmetic, not a probabilistic match, so `10.02.03` D-01 is untouched) |
| A split is recorded as-of a past period | Domain 09 | Resolves `no-owner-as-of` |
| A mapping is **reversed** | `10.02.03` D-10 | **Reverse flow** — see below |
| An adapter is corrected | `10.02.02` D-04 | May produce different identities from the same file → `Q-03` residual |

**Scope, and why the cost objection deflates.** The sweep is `the user's unmatched identities × the changed thing`, never `all lines × all works`. Two locked facts bound it: the unit is the identity, not the line, which is a 100×–1,000× collapse (`10.02.03` D-06); and mappings are **catalogue-scoped, never global** (`10.02.03` D-08), so registering one work can never trigger a sweep outside its own catalogue. The realistic worst case is a bulk import (`02.03.01` bringing 500 works at once) against one user's few hundred unmatched identities — arithmetic, not a workload. The genuinely unbounded case is a **platform-wide adapter correction** re-parsing every statement from a source for every user, and that belongs to `10.02.02` Q-03, not here. This substantially resolves `Q-03`.

**Precedence: a sweep never overrides a human** (`D-13`, `10.02` CX-03 Q5). If a re-match contradicts a decision a person made — most sharply, an identity disclaimed as *not mine* that the growing catalogue now says **is** theirs — the finding is presented **once**, as a question, with both the human's decision and the new evidence beside each other. It is never silently applied (that is money moving on a machine's opinion, `10.02.03` D-01) and never silently suppressed (that is the platform knowing something about your money and not saying, which is the black box). This is `10.02.03` D-14's shape — a detectable finding presented as one decision — applied to the queue.

**Reverse flow.** The queue is bidirectional and nobody expects it. A mapping reversal (`10.02.03` D-10) cascades to **every line it ever attributed**; those lines lose their attribution and return here under `reversed`. If money already moved on them, that is an overpayment and `10.03.04` owns it. If it did not, the queue's total **goes up**, which is correct and will look like a bug to everyone including the user. It must be explained where it happens, with its cause and its origin mapping.

### Two totals, and the platform publishes its own black box

A single "£340 unattributed" figure fails in both directions. If stale money stays in it, the number never falls, and a number that never falls is a number that stops being read — `DT-03`'s failure arriving through a different door. If stale money is removed, the money disappears from the only record that knew it existed — `D-02`'s failure exactly.

So there are **two permanent totals** (`D-14`):

- **Open** — the source's correction window is still open. Actionable. The alarm ladder runs on this and only this. It falls as identities resolve, which is what makes it worth watching.
- **Closed** — the window has shut. Permanently unexplainable. It never falls, it is never hidden, it is never netted against anything, and **it is published to the user as a standing figure.**

The Closed total is the platform's own black box, stated in the platform's own product. No institution in this industry does that, and per domain `D-09` it is the only position consistent with selling `10.05.01`. It is simultaneously a marketing liability and the single most credible thing on the screen.

**The boundary with `10.04.03` is sequential, not overlapping.** The original edge-case reading — *"unattributed AND unpayable, the black box twice over"* — is wrong on inspection: a line cannot be both, because attribution precedes payment. The queue holds **money with no known owner**; `10.04.03` holds **money whose owner is known and unreachable**. A line leaves here to arrive there. The platform's honest total black box is therefore the **sum** of the two, and that sum is a domain-level figure (parent `D-09`), not a screen in either feature.

### Cross-catalogue hints — the multi-hyphenate's trap

A Producer administers their band's catalogue and holds their own. A PRS statement pays them personally for a work they wrote with the band. The identity `MIDNIGHT (LIVE) / SMITH J / tunecode 4471XZ` is `no-candidate` in their **personal** catalogue and confirmed in the **band's** — one click away, on the same screen, to the same human. And `10.02.03` D-08 (mappings are catalogue-scoped, never global) means matching will never look. **The rule that prevents theft also prevents helpfulness, in the exact case where both catalogues belong to the same person** — which is the modal case for `personas.md`'s structural centre.

The resolution (`D-11`, `DT-10`): the queue may surface a **cross-catalogue hint** across catalogues the **same authenticated principal** administers. It is a candidate, never a match; the human confirms; the mapping is written into whichever catalogue they name. Three guardrails make it safe rather than clever:

- It never crosses **principals**. Two users mapping one tunecode to different works is a co-write, not a conflict (`10.02.03` D-08) — and a hint across users would leak one catalogue into another's queue. Categorically rejected (`DT-10`).
- It does not violate `10.02.03` D-01: a human confirms, so no money moves on a machine's opinion.
- It does not assert ownership, because **a match is not an ownership assertion** (`10.02.03` D-11). Confirming that this line is about the band's work does **not** say the band owns the money — PRS paid the writer their writer share, and domain 09 decides whose it is as of the usage period. The hint is safe *precisely because* that boundary is already locked.

### Ask a collaborator — the graph resolving the queue

The account holder is often not the best resolver; the person who ran the session is (`DT-09`, and `personas.md` says so directly of the Producer). The credits graph knows who was in the room. So an identity the user cannot place may be **asked about** (`D-12`):

- The user chooses who to ask, from their confirmed collaborators (domain 02). The platform may **suggest** collaborators whose credited sessions overlap the identity's usage period.
- The ask carries the **identity as printed** — title, party names, source, right type, period. It carries **no amount, ever.** *"Do you recognise this title from a session we worked on?"* is a memory question; *"do you recognise this £4,000?"* is an invitation.
- The answer is **input to the user's confirmation** — never a mapping, never a decision, never written into anyone's catalogue by the person who answered. `10.02.03` D-01 and D-08 both survive intact.
- Rate-limited against use as a fishing or harassment channel: at most one ask per identity per collaborator, at most 20 identities per statement, and a declined ask is never re-sent. It is a question between two people who already have a confirmed working relationship, or it is not sent (`Q-07`).

This is the thesis (`D-18`) doing concrete work on an operational problem: the platform was in the room, so it knows who to ask.

### The day-1 queue is not a queue

A new user with an empty catalogue uploads their first statement, and **100% of the money is unattributed**. Denominating that as *"£4,200 of your money we can't attribute"* is true, and it makes the product look broken at the precise moment it is trying to prove it works.

It is not broken. On an empty catalogue the platform has **nothing to match against** — this is not an exception, it is onboarding wearing the queue's clothes (`DT-13`). `10.02.03` D-12 already names the correct behaviour: unmatched identities on a thin catalogue are offered as **claim proposals to `02.03.02`**, never imported. So the day-1 screen reads *"your statements say you have 200 recordings — here they are, claim the ones that are yours"*, which is the same data as a third product. Whether that screen is presented as onboarding or as exceptions is a product decision the owner makes (`Q-08`).

The clock's behaviour on day 1 must be honest in a way that is uncomfortable: a user uploading ten years of back-catalogue statements will have identities whose correction windows **closed years ago**. The correct day-1 sentence is not an alarm — it is *"these are already past this source's correction window. We can show you what you earned; nothing here can be corrected."* Nobody says that. It is true, it is useful, and it is the difference between an honest product and an optimistic one.

### What the queue will not do

- **It will not resolve itself, and no internal team can resolve it for the user.** The platform cannot know that `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` was the Camden session. This is `10.02.02` D-03 inverted: there, the user never maps columns *because they cannot answer*; here, the platform never names the work *because it cannot answer*. Both features refuse to route a question to a party who can only guess at it.
- **It will not drop, net, round away or threshold anything** (`D-02`, `D-16`). Rank by value; never gate by value.
- **It will not auto-disclaim.** An identity that fails N sweeps is not "probably not theirs" — it is unresolved, and calling it foreign to clear a number is the black box with an optimisation.
- **It will not gross up a withheld amount.** A foreign society withholding 15% at source prints a net figure; the queue shows what the source printed and says which it is (`10.01.07` D-03 — at-source netting is disclosed, never reverse-engineered). Reclaim is domain 23's.
- **It will not adjudicate.** Two administrators of one catalogue who disagree about an identity are recorded as disagreeing, not refereed (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-10).

### Happy Path

1. A statement is ingested (`10.02.01`), parsed, and **reconciles to its stated total** — the hard gate (`10.02.02` D-02). Nothing reaches the queue until it does; a parse failure is not an exception (`10.02` R-03)
2. Parsed lines are collapsed onto their distinct source identities and run through the matching cascade (`10.02.03`)
3. Identities with no confirmable candidate land here — **at ingest, immediately** (`D-18`), each with its money, source, period, right type, reason code (`D-09`) and the evidence for why matching would not assert it
4. The queue opens on the **derived headline** (`D-15`): *"£340 you were paid that we can't explain — some of it may be owed to your collaborators"*, with Open and Closed totals separated (`D-14`)
5. Identities are value-ranked with the residual stated (`D-16`): *"resolving your top 12 identities accounts for 87% of the Open total; the remaining 2,400 are worth £310 between them"*
6. The user opens the top identity — one decision covering 400 lines, because the group is the identity matching already collapsed onto (`D-08`)
7. They resolve it: **name the work** (→ writes a mapping in `10.02.03`, sticky and replaying deterministically on every future statement from that source — `10.02.03` D-07); or **disclaim** it as not theirs (`D-06`); or **supply the missing period** from the paper they hold; or **ask the producer who ran the session** (`D-12`)
8. The resolved identity's lines re-enter calculation (`10.03.01`) with their money intact and attributed **as of the usage period**, never as of today (parent domain `D-02`)
9. The Open total falls — **and the total, never the row count, is the metric** (`DT-03`)
10. Six weeks later the user registers an old work (`10.01`). The re-sweep fires on that catalogue, resolves 14 historical identities the registration just made matchable, and the Open total falls again **against statements already ingested** (`D-03`) — the same statement is worth more money this year than last

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Thousands of lines worth £3 each | Volume with no value; the queue becomes noise and stops being read | Collapsed onto identities (`D-08`) and value-ranked with the residual stated (`D-16`). **Never** a flat list |
| A single identity worth £4,000 among the £3s | Buried under any row-count-ordered UI | Value ranking is what makes the queue usable at all. Optimising row count rewards clearing the £3s and leaves the £4,000 sitting |
| Queue never gets looked at | The realistic default for every exception queue ever built | **The design risk, not an edge case** — mitigated by denomination (`D-01`), ingest-time presentation (`D-18`), and money-sized escalation (`D-05`). See `DT-03` |
| Line is genuinely not the user's | A label statement's other artists (parent `Q-03`) | Resolvable as `disclaimed` — a **first-class outcome** (`D-06`), never a failure to match |
| The whole document is about someone else's money | The remittee is a third party | Cause `foreign`. Headline never claims it (`D-15`). Displaying it at all is a live privacy question (`Q-10`) |
| Correction window closes | The line can never be corrected at source | Moves to the **Closed** total, published permanently (`D-14`). One final notice, then silence (`D-17` rung 4) |
| Source's correction window is unknown | The clock has no true anchor | 12-month platform default, **labelled as the platform's deadline, not the source's**. The unknown window is a finding on the registry (`D-17`) |
| Catalogue improves later | Old identities become matchable retroactively | Re-sweep, scoped to that catalogue (`D-03`). Resolution is never one-shot at ingest |
| Re-sweep contradicts a `disclaimed` decision | The catalogue now says the disclaimed identity **is** theirs | Finding presented **once**, both sides shown. Never a silent override, never silently suppressed (`D-13`) |
| Re-sweep fires while the user is mid-resolution on the same identity | Race between a machine and a human on one object | The human's decision wins and commits; the sweep's finding queues behind it (`D-13`, CX-03 Q5) |
| Two administrators of one catalogue resolve one identity simultaneously | Both are authorised; both are acting in good faith | First commit wins. The second sees *"Jamie resolved this 12 seconds ago as Midnight"* with the reversal path (`10.02.03` D-05). A disagreement is **recorded, not adjudicated** |
| A mapping is reversed | Every line it ever attributed loses attribution and returns (`10.02.03` D-10) | **The Open total goes up.** Correct, and it looks like a bug — explained in place with its cause and its origin mapping |
| A reversed mapping's money already paid out | Not the queue's problem | → `10.03.04` overpayment. The queue holds the un-moved money only |
| Statement is restated | The superseding version re-parses and re-matches | Old version's queued identities go `superseded` — retired from the live total, **retained forever** (`D-02`). Totals never double-count across versions |
| A duplicate upload is mistaken for a restatement | One keystroke apart, routing oppositely (`10.02.01` D-04) | Distinguished **before** anything reaches the queue. A duplicate treated as a restatement would double the Open total overnight |
| Matched, dated, and domain 09 holds no split as-of that period | `no-owner-as-of`. **Matching shows a green tick** and the money still cannot move | The failure nobody designs for (`DT-05`). Invisible to every check that watches the matcher |
| Domain 09 supports only current-state reads | `no-owner-as-of` is **undetectable**; money silently attributes to today's owners | The exact failure parent `D-02` exists to prevent, and **no test catches it** (domain `Q-05`, `Q-06`) |
| Line has no period at all, and the split never changed in the plausible window | The unknown does not change the answer | Attributed as **`inferred-immaterial`** with the window and the proof recorded (`D-10`). Not a default (`10.02.04` D-05 upheld), not a probability (`10.02.03` D-01 upheld) |
| Line has no period, and the split changed inside the window | The unknown changes the answer | Stays `no-period`. Resolution requires the actual date, from the document or the source. **The platform does not pick** |
| Line names an ISRC the platform has never seen | Frequently a distributor issued an identifier for the user's own recording and never told them | `unknown-identifier` → a **finding** routed to domain 09 (`10.02.03` D-04). The money waits here rather than being disclaimed |
| Only a title matched — one signal class | Plausible noise, which `10.02.03` D-01 exists to refuse | `sub-threshold`, queued **with the near-miss and its evidence visible**. Honest silence beats a ranked guess |
| The answer is one catalogue away, under the same person | Personal catalogue queue vs the band's catalogue they administer | **Cross-catalogue hint**, same principal only (`D-11`). Candidate, never match |
| The answer is in another **user's** catalogue | A co-writer already mapped this tunecode | **Nothing.** Categorically rejected (`DT-10`) — a cross-user hint leaks one catalogue into another and is what `10.02.03` D-08 forbids |
| Two sources describe the same work with no global identifier | GEMA and PRS both name it; the mapping is source-scoped (`10.02.03` D-09) so the user maps it twice | Inherent and correct, but stated: *"you've mapped a work with this title for PRS — this looks like GEMA's identity for the same thing"*. Candidate, never match |
| New user, empty catalogue, first upload | 100% unattributed. An error list at the activation moment | **Not an exception queue** — it is onboarding (`DT-13`). Claim proposals to `02.03.02` (`10.02.03` D-12), never imported |
| Day-1 user uploads ten years of back catalogue | Most correction windows closed years ago | Honest, uncomfortable, and nobody says it: *"past this source's correction window. We can show you what you earned; nothing here can be corrected"* |
| Ageing alarm fires on a day-1 user's entire catalogue | *"£4,200 going stale"* when the true message is *"you haven't finished setting up"* | Never-attemptable (empty catalogue) is distinguished from attempted-and-failed. The alarm runs on the second |
| Foreign society withheld 15% at source | Gross ≠ net, and the queue is denominated in money | Shows **what the source printed**, and says which it is. Never grossed up (`10.01.07` D-03). Reclaim → domain 23 |
| An income total elsewhere omits the residual | 22/23 show *"you earned £12,000"* while £3,400 sits unexplained | Forbidden. The residual travels with **every** income total the platform shows (`D-19`) — a total that omits it is a lie by omission |
| A user reads analytics while a queue is non-empty | The unmatched set is **not random** — it skews to old, live, remixed, collaborative and foreign works | *"My 2013 album earns nothing"* may be a matching artifact, not a fact (`DT-11`). The bias points at the back catalogue, which is where `D-16`'s recovery value is |
| Resolved wrongly | Money moves to the wrong party | Reversible (`10.02.03` D-05, D-10) → `10.03.04`. The user is told what the reversal costs |
| A mapping's attributed money steps by an order of magnitude | Stickiness turned one error into a standing liability | Re-enters review; attribution continues on its historical baseline (`10.02.03` D-10) |
| Ask-a-collaborator used to fish for someone's earnings | An outbound channel with money attached is an attack surface | **No amount is ever sent** (`D-12`). Confirmed collaborators only; one ask per identity per person; 20 per statement; a decline is never re-sent (`Q-07`) |
| A user resolves the Open total to £0 | The best possible outcome, and the queue is now empty | Empty state is a **statement**, not a blank: *"every line on every statement is attributed. Closed total: £120, permanently unexplained."* The Closed number never leaves |
| Adapter is corrected and re-parses statements platform-wide | The identity set itself changes underneath the queue | Unbounded, and **not this feature's** — → `10.02.02` Q-03 (`Q-03` residual) |
| Money is unattributable and the payee is also unknown | **Cannot happen simultaneously.** Attribution precedes payment | Sequential, not compounding. Queue = no known owner; `10.04.03` = owner known, unreachable. The platform's black box is their **sum** (parent `D-09`) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Statement ingested; matching cascade running | Progress against the **identity** count, not the line count (`D-08`) — *"resolving 200 identities from 40,000 lines"*. A line-count progress bar lies about the work |
| **Empty (day 1)** | No catalogue exists to match against | **Not an error.** Onboarding: *"your statements say you have 200 recordings — claim the ones that are yours"* (`DT-13`, `10.02.03` D-12) |
| **Empty (resolved)** | Open total is £0 | A statement, not a blank: *"every line attributed. Closed: £120, permanently unexplained"* — the Closed figure never disappears (`D-14`) |
| **Populated** | Identities queued | Derived headline (`D-15`), Open and Closed totals separated, value-ranked identities, residual stated (`D-16`), reason code and route on each (`D-09`) |
| **Partial** | Some identities have candidates awaiting confirmation in `10.02.03` | Counted in the Open total, **deep-linked** to the decision surface — never a second copy of it (`D-07`) |
| **Error** | Matching could not run (upstream fault) | **Not an exception.** A parse or reconciliation failure blocks the statement upstream (`10.02.02` D-02, `10.02` R-03) — the queue never shows a platform bug as a user problem |
| Queued | Attribution failed | Amount, source, period, right type, reason code, evidence, resolution route |
| Grouped | Lines collapsed onto their source identity | One decision covering many lines. The group is matching's, not the queue's (`D-08`) |
| Ageing | Past a ladder rung (`D-17`) | Escalating alarm, **sized in money**, naming the date it becomes unrecoverable |
| Resolved | Identity named and confirmed | Re-enters calculation with money intact, as of the usage period. The mapping replays on all future statements from that source (`10.02.03` D-07) |
| Disclaimed | User asserts it is not theirs | First-class outcome (`D-06`). Reversible. A contradicting re-sweep is a finding, not an override (`D-13`) |
| Reversed | A mapping was reversed (`10.02.03` D-10) | Returned to the queue under its original cause. **The Open total goes up** — explained in place |
| Superseded | The statement was restated | Retired from the live total, retained forever (`D-02`). Never double-counted |
| Stale (Closed) | Correction window shut | Permanent, published, never decremented, never netted (`D-14`). One final notice, then never again |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Unmatched lines are an implementation detail of matching — a `NULL` and a retry | The obvious engineering treatment, and why the sweep produced no candidate for it | ❌ **REJECTED, and the omission is exactly the failure the domain sells against.** An unattributed line is **money that arrived and belongs to somebody**. A `NULL` in a matching table is, functionally and precisely, a black box: money held by an institution that cannot name its owner. `10.05.01` is built on the premise that the industry does this and that finding the money is worth real activation value — so a WeJammin that quietly accumulates its own unattributed pile is selling a cure for a disease it has. The queue must exist as a **product surface with a currency total**, not as an ops backlog. |
| DT-02 | Resolution happens at ingest — you match what you can and the rest is lost | One-shot pipeline thinking | ❌ **REJECTED.** The catalogue improves continuously: a work gets registered (`10.01`), an ISRC arrives (domain 09), a credit is claimed (`02.03`), a mapping is confirmed (`10.02.03` D-07). Every one makes previously-unmatchable identities matchable **retroactively**. The queue must be swept whenever the catalogue changes, not only when a statement lands. This inverts the pipeline's shape — it is not fire-and-forget; the queue is a standing corpus re-evaluated against a growing graph. It is also, quietly, the mechanism by which the platform's value compounds: **the same statement is worth more money to the user next year than it was this year.** |
| DT-03 | Building the queue solves the problem | The natural conclusion once the queue exists | ❌ **REJECTED — every exception queue ever built goes unread, and there is no reason to think this one is special.** The mitigation is not process, it is **denomination**: a queue headed "412 unresolved rows" is an ops chore and will rot; a queue headed "**£340 of your money we can't attribute**" is a personal financial fact and gets clicked. Same data, different product. The metric follows the same logic — track the **money** and its age, never the row count, because optimising row count rewards clearing the £3 lines and leaves the £4,000 one sitting. |
| DT-04 | The queue should cluster similar-looking rows so the user makes one decision instead of 400 | The obvious usability feature once you see 40,000 lines, and what the surface pass called "grouping" | ❌ **REJECTED as a queue capability — the group already exists and inventing a second one is the bug.** `10.02.03` D-06 established that the unit of matching and of every human decision is the **source identity**, not the line, with a 100×–1,000× collapse. A queue that clusters independently would compute a *second* grouping, in different code, from the same data — and the two would eventually disagree about what one decision covers. That is the same class of error as `10.02` R-01 (source knowledge in two places, invited to disagree). **The queue inherits matching's collapse; it does not perform its own** (`D-08`). |
| DT-05 | The queue holds one failure — "we couldn't match it" | The name of the feature, and how every exception queue is built | ❌ **REJECTED — there are three, and the third is invisible because matching *succeeded*.** Money fails to reach a person at three distinct points: *which work* (matching found nothing), *when* (no usable period, so no split version can be selected — `10.02.04` D-05), and **whose, as of then** (work known, period known, and domain 09 holds no split as-of that period). The third has a green tick on the matching surface, a valid date, and nowhere to go — a work registered in 2026 whose Q1 2024 usage predates any recorded split. Every monitor that watches the matcher is blind to it. Flattening the three into one status is what makes an exception queue unresolvable: the three have different owners, different routes, and only one of them is the user's to fix. It is also only *detectable* if 09 supports as-of reads (`Q-06`). |
| DT-06 | The queue holds money the platform received but could not attribute | The surface pass's own framing, inherited unexamined | ❌ **REJECTED — the platform probably never touches this money, and the correction changes the product.** Domain `Q-07` (*does WeJammin move money at all?*) is open and the likely answer is no: PRS pays the writer's bank directly, DistroKid pays the account holder. The statement is a document *about* a payment that already completed elsewhere. So the modal queued line is not stuck money — it is **money the user already has and the platform cannot explain**, and *"£340 of your money is unattributed"* is then a subtly false sentence: nothing is withheld from them. The honest sentence is derived from the **remittance relationship** — who was this money paid to? — a fact the statement already contains, requiring a registry field `10.02.01` does not currently state (`Q-04`, `D-15`). This dissolves a dependency rather than answering a question: `Q-02` stays the owner's, but the design no longer waits on it. |
| DT-07 | The harm of an unattributed line is to the account holder — they can't see what they earned | The natural reading once DT-06 establishes they already have the money | ❌ **REJECTED — the primary victim is the collaborator, and they cannot even learn they are one.** If the £340 line cannot be attributed to a work, the split cannot be computed (`10.03.01`), so the drummer's 12% stays in the bandleader's account: unpaid, unnoticed, and invisible to the drummer, who has no statement, no line, and no way to know the money exists. **This is `problem-statement.md`'s root cause reproduced inside the platform built to prevent it** — a fact about who is owed what, unrecorded, waiting to be reconstructed from memory at the moment it becomes contested and valuable. It also sharpens the urgency correctly: the account holder feels nothing (their bank balance is right), so **the person with the power to resolve the line is the person with the least incentive to**. Denomination (`DT-03`) is therefore necessary and not sufficient; the sentence must name the collaborator's stake (`D-15`), because that is the stake that is actually at risk. |
| DT-08 | The ageing clock measures a filing deadline — beat the source's correction window | What "stale" obviously means, and the surface pass's reading | ❌ **REJECTED as the primary — it is the secondary clock. The primary is memory decay.** The only resolver of a `no-candidate` identity is a human's recollection that `MIDNIGHT (LIVE)` was the Camden session in March — and **memory is precisely the faculty this platform exists to not rely on** (`problem-statement.md`: *they are asking people to remember, and memory is contested, lossy, and self-interested*). An exception queue that ages **is a reconstruction engine**; every quarter it goes unread, WeJammin becomes more like the institutions it criticises, by the same mechanism. Two concrete consequences: **the loudest moment is ingest** (`D-18`) — resolution costs a Tuesday afternoon now (`10.02.03` DT-04) and may be unobtainable at any price in eighteen months, which is the domain's own argument for capture-at-source turned inward; and the correction window is a real but *external* deadline, so it anchors the ladder (`D-17`) while memory decay justifies its existence. |
| DT-09 | The account holder is the right person to ask | Whose queue it is | ❌ **REJECTED — the producer who ran the session is a better resolver, and the credits graph knows who that is.** `personas.md` says it directly: the Producer is *"the one who is in the room when the facts become true"* and can identify a recording whose ISRC nobody remembers issuing. So an unresolvable identity is routable to a collaborator who was on a session overlapping its usage period — **using domain 02's graph to resolve domain 10's queue**, which is `D-18`'s thesis doing concrete operational work. The design is safe only because of what it withholds: the ask carries the **identity and never the amount** (`D-12`). *"Do you recognise this title from a session we worked on?"* is a memory question; the same question with £4,000 attached is an invitation to a claim. The answer is input to the user's confirmation, never a mapping — `10.02.03` D-01 and D-08 both survive untouched. |
| DT-10 | The platform should never look outside the catalogue being matched | `10.02.03` D-08's rule, applied literally | ✅ **CONFIRMED across users; ❌ REJECTED within one principal — and the distinction is the multi-hyphenate's whole problem.** Across **users**: absolute. A hint from another user's catalogue leaks that catalogue into this one's queue, and two users mapping one tunecode to different works is a **co-write, not a conflict** (`10.02.03` D-08) — there is no shared object to consult and consulting it anyway is the global mapping D-08 forbids. Within one **principal**: the rule stops making sense. A Producer administering their band's catalogue and holding their own has the answer one click away, on the same screen, to the same human — and the rule that prevents theft prevents helpfulness in the modal case for `personas.md`'s structural centre. So: cross-catalogue hints, same principal only, as candidates a human confirms (`D-11`). It is safe **because `10.02.03` D-11 already locked that a match is not an ownership assertion** — confirming the line is about the band's work does not say the band owns the money; PRS paid the writer their writer share, and domain 09 decides whose it is as of the usage period. A boundary locked for another reason turns out to be what makes this feature possible. |
| DT-11 | The unmatched set is noise — a random sample of the statement | The implicit assumption behind showing income analytics while a queue exists | ❌ **REJECTED — it is systematically biased, and biased in the worst available direction.** Lines fail to match because their metadata is bad, and metadata is bad in patterns: **older releases, live recordings, remixes, covers, compilations, collaborative works, and foreign-territory reporting**. So the unattributed pile is disproportionately the back catalogue — which is **exactly where domain `D-16`'s recovery pitch points**. A user reading 22/23's analytics against a non-empty queue may conclude *"my 2013 album earns nothing"* when they are reading a matching artifact, and act on it: stop promoting it, stop registering that era, sell the catalogue. The consequence is not confined to domain 10 — **every income total the platform shows anywhere must carry the unattributed residual** (`D-19`), or it lies by omission (domain `D-15`). The bias also means the queue's *value* is understated by its own totals early on, because the hardest identities are the oldest and largest. |
| DT-12 | Once the correction window closes, the line stops counting — it is noise on a total nobody can act on | Reasonable operationally, and the natural fix for a total that never falls | ❌ **REJECTED — and the two-total split it forces is the most credible thing in the feature.** Both single-total designs fail: keep stale money in and the number never falls, so it stops being read (`DT-03`'s failure through a different door); take it out and the money vanishes from the only record that knew it existed (`D-02`'s failure exactly). So: **Open** (window open, actionable, the alarm runs on it, it falls as work happens) and **Closed** (permanently unexplainable, published, never decremented, never netted, never hidden) — `D-14`. The Closed total is **the platform's own black box, published in the platform's own product**. No institution in this industry does that; per domain `D-09` it is the only position consistent with selling `10.05.01`; and it is simultaneously a marketing liability and the single most credible number on the screen. The same inspection corrects the surface pass's *"unattributed AND unpayable — the black box twice over"*: a line **cannot be both**, because attribution precedes payment. Queue = no known owner; `10.04.03` = owner known, unreachable. Sequential, not compounding — and the platform's honest black box is their **sum**. |
| DT-13 | The day-1 queue is an exception queue with everything in it | What the mechanism literally produces on an empty catalogue | ❌ **REJECTED — on an empty catalogue it is the onboarding flow wearing the queue's clothes.** A new user's first statement is 100% unattributed, and *"£4,200 of your money we can't attribute"* is true and makes the product look broken at the exact activation moment. It is not broken: the platform has **nothing to match against**, which is not an exception — it is a missing catalogue. `10.02.03` D-12 already names the behaviour: unmatched identities on a thin catalogue are **claim proposals to `02.03.02`**, never imported. So the screen reads *"your statements say you have 200 recordings — claim the ones that are yours"*, which is the same data as a third product (`DT-03`'s move, applied again). This inherits `10.02.03` Q-04's dependency — whether `02.03` accepts a statement as a claim source — and `10.02.03` Q-07's sequencing consequence. The clock's day-1 behaviour must be honest and uncomfortable: a user uploading ten years of back catalogue has windows that **closed years ago**, and the correct sentence is not an alarm but *"past this source's correction window — we can show you what you earned; nothing here can be corrected."* Distinguishing **never-attemptable** (empty catalogue) from **attempted-and-failed** is what stops the alarm firing at a user who has simply not finished setting up. |
| DT-14 | The exception queue and `10.02.03`'s confirmation surface are two screens | They have different names, different owners and different reason codes | ❌ **REJECTED as a view; ✅ CONFIRMED as a decision boundary — and conflating those two readings is what produces both errors.** The user has **one** question — *"why isn't this money accounted for?"* — and money not moving has two adjacent causes: no candidate exists (here), or a candidate exists and nobody has confirmed it (`10.02.03`). Answering across two screens that each show part of the number means neither total is believable, and the honest platform-wide figure (`D-19`) cannot be computed from either. So the queue is the **view over all money not attributed**, pending-confirmation identities included and counted, **deep-linked** to `10.02.03` rather than duplicated (`D-07`). The *decision* stays where it is owned — the queue never re-implements confirmation, because two confirmation paths writing one mapping is `DT-04`'s error with money attached. View unified, decision separated. |
| DT-15 | A low-value threshold is reasonable efficiency — 3,000 identities at £3 cost more to resolve than they are worth | The standard operational answer, and superficially just `DT-03`'s value-ranking taken one step further | ❌ **REJECTED — value-ranking directs attention; a threshold destroys evidence, and they are not the same act.** Three reasons, each independently sufficient. **The arithmetic**: 3,000 × £3 is £9,000, which is not a rounding error — it is the long tail that value-ranking exists to make an *informed* choice about, which is why the residual is stated (`D-16`). **The collaborator**: `DT-07` — the drummer's 12% of a £3 line is small; the drummer's 12% of every £3 line across four years is their rent, and it is owed regardless of whether it is worth the platform's attention. **The principle**: a materiality threshold is *"a place to hide"* (`10.03.04` Q-02's exact phrasing), and this feature exists because the industry hides money in precisely such places. `D-02` (nothing is ever dropped) is not a threshold with the value set to zero — it is a refusal to have the dial. **Rank by value; never gate by value** (`D-16`). |

- Touches **22 Analytics** ([../../22-analytics-insights/](../../22-analytics-insights/)) and **23 Career, Finance & Business** ([../../23-career-finance-business/](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — **`D-19` is a platform-wide constraint this feature imposes on two other domains**: every income total anywhere must carry the unattributed residual, or it lies by omission (domain `D-15`). And per `DT-11` the omission is not random — it is systematically biased against the back catalogue, so "what earns" is wrong in a direction that changes user behaviour.
- Rides **Notifications & Alerts** (`CX-M07`) — the ladder's delivery is platform machinery; the **money-denominated framing, the ingest-first rung and the terminal silence after the window closes** are domain-owned (`D-17`, `D-18`).
- Rides **Privacy, Consent & Data Portability** (`CX-M18`) — the queue is the surface **most likely to display third parties' earnings**, because a label statement's unattributed lines are precisely the other artists' (parent `Q-03`, `Q-10`). `D-15` stops the platform *claiming* them; it does not decide whether it may *display and store* them.
- Rides **Point-in-Time / As-Of Record Resolution** _(proposed, `10.02.04` DT-02)_ — `D-10`'s immateriality proof is an as-of query over a **window** rather than a date, which is a shape the proposed cross-cut does not currently name and which serves 09, 10, 11, 12 and 23 alike.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The queue is **denominated in money**, not rows | `DT-03`. The difference between a product surface and an ops backlog nobody reads. |
| D-02 | **Nothing is ever dropped** | `DT-01`. Deleting an unattributed line deletes the only record that the money exists. Superseded and stale lines are retired from the live total and retained forever — never deleted. |
| D-03 | The queue is **re-swept whenever the catalogue changes**, not only at ingest | `DT-02`. A growing graph retroactively resolves old identities — the platform's value compounds over the same statements. Scoped to the changed catalogue; never global (`10.02.03` D-08). |
| D-04 | Identities are **value-ranked**, and the residual is stated | A flat list buries the £4,000 identity under the £3 ones. *Sharpened this pass*: the surface pass said "grouped and value-ranked" — the grouping is not this feature's (see `D-08`); the ranking is. |
| D-05 | Ageing is an **escalating, money-sized alarm** | Source correction windows close. The domain's recurring pattern (`D-04`, five independent arrivals): an absence produces no event, so the clock must. |
| D-06 | "Not mine" is a **first-class resolution** | Some money genuinely belongs to someone else, and forcing every identity to resolve to the user is how misattribution happens. Terminal but reversible. |
| D-07 | The queue is the **view over all money not attributed**, pending-confirmation identities included and deep-linked to `10.02.03`. The **decision** stays where it is owned | `DT-14`. The user has one question; answering it across two screens that each show part of the number makes both totals unbelievable. View unified, decision separated. |
| D-08 | The unit is the **source identity `10.02.03` already collapsed onto**. The queue invents no grouping of its own | `DT-04`, `10.02.03` D-06. A second grouping computed by different code from the same data will eventually disagree with the first about what one decision covers. |
| D-09 | Every queued identity carries a **reason code from a closed taxonomy** and a **named resolution route**. "Unmatched" is not a reason | `DT-05`. Eleven causes with three different owners; flattening them is what makes an exception queue unresolvable. |
| D-10 | A `no-period` line is attributed when the unknown is **provably immaterial** — exactly one split version across the whole plausible window (bounded below by the later of the source's statement-period start and the work's first release/registration; above by the distribution date). Recorded as `inferred-immaterial` with the window and the proof | Upholds `10.02.04` D-05 (it is a **proof**, not a default) and `10.02.03` D-01 (it is deterministic across the entire envelope, not a probability). If ≥2 split versions touch the window, it stays an exception and the platform does not pick. |
| D-11 | **Cross-catalogue hints within one principal; cross-user hints never** | `DT-10`. The rule that prevents theft would otherwise prevent helpfulness for the multi-hyphenate, who is `personas.md`'s structural centre. Safe only because `10.02.03` D-11 already locked that a match is not an ownership assertion. |
| D-12 | The queue may **ask a collaborator** — the identity, **never the amount**. The answer is input to the user's confirmation, never a mapping | `DT-09`. The producer who ran the session is the better resolver and the credits graph knows who they are. Confirmed collaborators only; one ask per identity per person; ≤20 per statement; a decline is never re-sent. |
| D-13 | A re-sweep **never overrides a human decision**. A contradiction is a **finding presented once**, both sides shown | `10.02` CX-03 Q5; `10.02.03` D-14's shape. Silently applying it is money moving on a machine's opinion; silently suppressing it is the platform knowing something about your money and not saying. |
| D-14 | **Two permanent totals — Open and Closed.** The alarm runs on Open only. Closed is published, never decremented, never netted, never hidden | `DT-12`. Both single-total designs fail, in opposite directions. The Closed total is the platform's own black box, published — the only position consistent with selling `10.05.01` (domain `D-09`). |
| D-15 | The headline sentence is **derived from the remittance relationship**, not hardcoded. Four sentences: the user's, an administered entity's, a third party's, unknown | `DT-06`, `DT-07`. The platform probably does not hold this money, and a label statement is about several people's. One hardcoded sentence is a financial claim that is sometimes false. |
| D-16 | **Rank by value; never gate by value.** No materiality threshold drops a line | `DT-15`. £3 × 3,000 is £9,000; the collaborator's share is owed regardless; and a threshold is *"a place to hide"* (`10.03.04` Q-02), which is what this feature exists to refuse. |
| D-17 | The clock is anchored to the **source's declared correction window** (`10.02.01` registry). An undeclared window is a **finding**, and the platform's 12-month fallback is **labelled as the platform's deadline, not the source's** | `DT-08`; domain `D-04` (a clock must exist) and domain `D-15` (never invent a number and present it as fact). Both constraints are satisfied only by an explicit, labelled default. |
| D-18 | **Alarm at ingest.** The loudest rung is arrival, when memory is freshest. The scheduled ladder is the fallback, not the mechanism | `DT-08`. Resolution costs a Tuesday afternoon now and may be unobtainable at any price in eighteen months — the domain's capture-at-source argument turned inward on its own queue. |
| D-19 | The **unattributed residual travels with every income total** the platform shows, anywhere | `DT-11`; domain `D-15`. A total that omits it lies by omission — and the omission is systematically biased against the back catalogue, so it lies in a direction that changes what users do. |
