# Feature: Statement Parsing & Format Adapters

> **Number**: 10.02.02
> **Parent**: [Statement Ingestion & Normalization](./10.02-statement-ingestion-normalization-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turning **each source's file into royalty lines**: DDEX DSR from DSPs, per-society CSVs that share no column names, distributor exports, label statements as spreadsheets, and — routinely — PDFs designed for a human to read and nobody to parse. Every source has its own adapter, and there is no standard that covers even a majority of them.

The feature's real product is not the parse; it is the **honest report of what the platform did and did not understand**. A parser that silently drops the lines it could not read is worse than no parser, because it produces a total that looks complete.

It is also the load-bearing feature of domain 10's ungated half (parent D-05). Nothing downstream — matching, calculation, statements, recovery — is worth anything if the lines under it are wrong. And uniquely in this domain, **wrongness here is detectable**, because the counterparty printed the answer on its own statement.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees a **side-by-side**: the total their statement prints, and the total the platform's parsed lines sum to. When those agree, that is the whole message — one number matching one number, in the source's own currency and the source's own period (CX-02), checkable against the paper in their hand. When they disagree, they are told in money ("PRS Q1 2025: we read £12,480.22; PRS printed £12,491.07 — £10.85 apart, we won't use this until we know why"), never as a stack trace. They are **never asked to map a column** (D-03): "Adj. Net Rec." is not a question a working drummer can answer, and the ask would be indistinguishable from failure. Their only inputs are the file itself and, when a parse is blocked, patience the platform must convert into a clock (D-12). |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, at volume, and they hit the adapters' edges first: a producer ingesting label statements sees the widest format spread in the corpus — spreadsheets with subtotal rows, multi-tab workbooks, footer-only commission deductions, and lines about forty artists who are not them. They are the persona for whom D-13 (parse the whole file) collides head-on with the parent's `Q-03` privacy boundary, because the file they upload is theirs and the money on it is other people's. As a multi-hyphenate they also meet D-19's edge: a label statement whose payee party is their label and whose per-line artists include *themselves*. |
| Operator | None | No surface, and the reason is structural rather than incidental: a venue **pays** a PRS/PPL blanket licence. It is a cost, not an income statement. That licence fee is what *generates* money on somebody else's statement (`10.06`), which is exactly why Operator appears once at `10.06` and nowhere else in domain 10. |
| Fan | None | No surface. A fan's ticket or merch purchase eventually becomes a line on somebody else's statement, but that path runs through 19/13 and arrives here as a distributor's aggregate. Fan is absent across all of domain 10 — the only domain drilled with no Fan surface whatsoever (parent Role Matrix). |

## Behavior

Per source, an adapter defines the mapping from that source's file to normalized lines: (usage period, territory, right type, source identifier for the work/recording, usage quantity, gross, deductions, net, currency). The output is *lines*, not conclusions — matching them to a catalogue is `10.02.03`'s job and must stay separate, because a parse error and a match error feel identical to a user and have completely different fixes (DT-01).

Adapters are **data with versions**, not code paths. Sources change their formats without notice and without a migration; a format change must be a new adapter version applied to statements from that date, with old statements still parseable by the version they arrived under — otherwise re-running an old ingest silently produces a different answer than the one already paid out.

### The two reconciliations

The control that makes the whole sub-domain trustworthy is reconciliation — but it is **two checks, not one**, and collapsing them is how a broken adapter passes (D-06):

1. **Structural reconciliation** — every non-empty row in the file is accounted for. Each row resolves to exactly one classification: `detail` (becomes a line), `header`, `subtotal`, `footer`, `blank`, `continuation`, or `unreadable`. **Zero rows may end up unclassified.** This is what turns D-05 ("nothing is dropped") from a principle into a check: a parser cannot silently skip what it must explicitly classify, and the count of `unreadable` rows is the honest partial-parse number.
2. **Monetary reconciliation** — Σ(parsed line net) equals the stated total the adapter declares those lines sum to (D-07), within the tolerance derived below (D-10).

**Both gate.** A statement passes to `10.02.03` only when both are clean.

### Which total, and how close is close enough

A statement prints **several** totals — gross, a deduction stack, net payable, sometimes a carried balance and an at-source held amount below the society's distribution threshold. Naive "compare against the biggest number on the page" fails on the first society statement, where the lines are gross and the printed remittance is net of a commission that appears only as a footer. So the adapter **declares which stated total its lines sum to**, and captures every other printed total as a fact (D-07) — those other totals are exactly `10.01.07` D-03's at-source deductions and `10.04.03`'s at-source held balances, and they are evidence the moment they are read.

The tolerance is **computed from the file, never a constant** (D-10). Where lines are printed to `p` decimal places, the total to `q`, and there are `n` detail lines:

```
tolerance = 0.5 × 10^-q  +  n × 0.5 × 10^-p
```

- A 12-line PRS statement with `p = q = 2` → tolerance £0.005. Effectively exact; a penny out is a real failure.
- A 400,000-line DSR with `p = 6`, `q = 2` → tolerance ≈ £0.205. A penny out is *expected* and means nothing.

A fixed "£0.01 tolerance" is simultaneously too tight for the second and far too loose for the first — which is the whole reason this is a formula rather than a config value. **Within tolerance** = the source's own rounding, recorded and disclosed, not an error. **Outside tolerance** = blocked. The parse itself never rounds: it preserves the source's printed precision exactly, sub-cent and all, and hands rounding to `10.03.01` D-04 at the only point where it is legitimate — paying an actual person.

### What the oracle proves, and what it does not

D-02's stated-total check is the only hard oracle in domain 10 (DT-03), and it must be described honestly or it becomes a false comfort (DT-04). It proves **completeness of money**: no line was dropped, duplicated, double-counted from a subtotal row, or invented. It proves **nothing** about semantics. A statement whose currency, usage period, territory, right type, or identifiers are uniformly misread sums perfectly to its own stated total and is wrong in every way that matters — wrong currency feeds `10.02.04` a wrong conversion, wrong period selects the wrong split version (parent D-02), wrong right type mis-disambiguates the match (CX-06).

So the semantic fields get **per-adapter assertions** that are not the sum check: declared currency present and a known code; usage periods resolving to explicit bounds within a plausible range for the source's distribution vintage; territory codes in the shared taxonomy (`CX-M17`); right type in the declared set. And the statement **records which checks ran and which did not**, because per D-15 a platform that states what it knows must also state what it merely assumed.

### Determinism

Parsing is deterministic: **the same file plus the same adapter version yields identical lines, forever** (D-11). An adapter reads nothing ambient — not the clock, not the server locale, not a live FX rate. This is not fastidiousness: D-04's version pinning is meaningless if a re-parse under the same version can differ, and `Q-03`'s "do we re-run history?" has no answerable form if the platform cannot say what the old answer even was. It is also why a runtime LLM parser is refused (D-16).

Parent D-03 governs the output: **an ingested statement is evidence, never truth.** The lines record what a source asserted in a file the platform retains — and the parse carries the **custody** of that file with it (D-20), because a statement a user uploaded about themselves and a statement forwarded from a DKIM-signed society email are not the same quality of fact (DT-10).

### Happy Path

1. `10.02.01` hands over a received statement with its declared source identity and the retained original file
2. Source identity + in-file fingerprint select the adapter version in force for the statement's date (CX-05, D-15); the version is pinned for the whole run and does not change if a new version publishes mid-parse
3. Adapter classifies **every** row — detail, header, subtotal, footer, blank, continuation — and structural reconciliation confirms zero rows unclassified
4. Detail rows become lines: usage period, territory, right type, source identifier, usage quantity, gross, deductions, net, currency — at the source's own precision, unrounded
5. Other printed totals (gross, deduction stack, at-source held balance, the source's own FX rate) are captured as facts alongside
6. Σ(line net) is compared to the stated total the adapter declared for those lines, in **source currency and source period** (CX-02), within the tolerance computed from the file
7. Monetary reconciliation matches; the semantic assertions run; the statement records which checks passed and which were unavailable
8. Lines are handed to `10.02.03` for matching, carrying their custody, their aggregation level (D-18), and the payee party the statement is addressed to (D-19)
9. The Musician sees the number that matters: *"your statement says £12,491.07 — we read £12,491.07, across 1,284 lines"*, side by side with the paper

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Parsed lines don't sum to the stated total, structural check failed | **The parse is wrong.** Rows the adapter could not classify prove it | Blocked, with the discrepancy stated in money and the unreadable row count named |
| Parsed lines don't sum, structural check **passed** | Not automatically the platform's fault (DT-06). Either a misclassified subtotal, a misread value, or **the source's own arithmetic doesn't add up** — which is routine and worth real money | Blocked pending triage; if the source's total is confirmed wrong, released with a `source-total-disputed` flag → `10.08`. The platform never asserts source fault automatically (`Q-04`) |
| Within-tolerance discrepancy | The source's own rounding. Recorded and disclosed, not an error (D-10) | "£0.04 of rounding across 400,000 lines" — a footnote, not an alarm |
| Subtotal row parsed as a detail row | Double-counts a whole section. The classic spreadsheet-statement failure | Caught by monetary reconciliation — the sum overshoots. **This is the oracle earning its place** |
| Statement prints **no total at all** | Detail exports exist; two-file statements (summary PDF + detail CSV) exist. The oracle is unavailable (DT-05) | State `Unoracled` — parsed, explicitly unverified, and the fact travels downstream to `10.03.01`'s derivation and `10.04.02`'s statement. **Never silently counted as reconciled** |
| Two-file statement, summary and detail separate | The total is in a file the detail parse never sees | Reconciliation deferred until both are present; the pair is one statement or neither is oracled |
| Lines are gross, printed total is net of a footer commission | Extremely common on society statements. Σ(gross) ≠ net payable **by design** | The adapter declares which total applies (D-07); the commission is captured as an at-source deduction (`10.01.07` D-03) |
| Source changed its format, **subtractively** | A column the adapter needs is gone. Breaks mid-batch, without notice | Fingerprint/structural failure. Fails loudly; a partial parse must not proceed as if complete |
| Source changed its format, **additively** | A new column appears. **The adapter still parses and still reconciles** — and silently discards whatever the column meant, possibly a new deduction type (DT-11) | An unexpected column is a flag even on a clean reconciliation (D-17). The dangerous drift is the one that passes every check |
| Columns reordered, headers present | Adapters bind by header name, not position | No effect |
| Columns reordered, **positional format, no in-file version** | Undetectable and catastrophic — every field shifts and may still reconcile | A positional format without a declared version in the file is unsafe to parse. Blocked rather than guessed |
| PDF statement designed for humans | Common with older labels and some societies. **Two different problems**: a text-layer PDF is an adapter like any other; a scanned image is OCR | Text-layer → adapter, gated identically by both reconciliations. Scanned image → `No adapter`, file retained (`10.02.01` D-03), platform says so. **Reconciliation is what makes PDF extraction acceptable at all**: an extraction that passes both checks is exactly as trustworthy as a CSV that passes both. Scope/investment remains `Q-02` |
| OCR extraction that reconciles | Passes both gates | Trusted to the same standard as any adapter — and no further (DT-04's blind spot applies with more force, since OCR misreads semantics readily) |
| One statement, many usage periods | **Not an edge case — the norm** (DT-07). A single PRS distribution routinely bundles 2025 radio, 2023 live and 2022 international | Period is per-line and always was; a statement-level period is a fiction. Vintages spanning years flow to `10.02.04` and select different split versions (parent D-02) |
| Negative lines (returns, adjustments) | Real and routine. A parser that assumes positive money is wrong on its first restatement | Round-tripped faithfully → `10.03.04` |
| Whole statement nets **negative** | A quarter where returns exceed earnings. The stated total is a negative number | Reconciles against a negative target normally. No absolute values, ever |
| Negatives in accounting notation `(1.23)` | Parsed as −1.23. Misreading as +1.23 flips the sign of a return | Sign errors are caught by monetary reconciliation — the sum overshoots by 2× the amount |
| Locale decimal separator (`1.234,56`) | German/French society statements. A misread is a 1000× error | Caught loudly by reconciliation. Locale is declared **per adapter**, never read from the server environment (D-11) |
| Currency symbol / thousands separator embedded in the amount string | `"€1,234.56"`, `"£ 12.00"` | Adapter-declared parsing of the source's own convention |
| Currency misidentified (EUR read as GBP) | **Reconciles perfectly.** Every number is identical; only the label is wrong | The oracle is blind to this (DT-04). Caught only by the currency assertion, and the honest position is that this class of error is *not* covered by D-02 |
| Statement with zero lines and a stated total of 0.00 | A quarter with no earnings. Reconciles trivially | A genuine, recorded **zero** — not a failure and not silence. *"PRS distributed and paid you nothing"* is a completely different fact from `10.02.01`'s *"PRS didn't distribute"*, and both render as £0 on a careless dashboard |
| Lines present, total 0.00 after deductions | Earnings fully consumed at source | Reconciles; the deduction stack is the story |
| A line with no identifier at all | Just a title string. Very common on older statements | Parsed as a line; matching is `10.02.03`'s problem — do not drop it |
| A line **aggregated at source** ("SACEM international, Q3 2024, £14.22") | The source destroyed the per-work detail before sending. **Unmatchable in principle**, not unmatched-yet (DT-08) | Marked with its aggregation level (D-18) so `10.02.05` does not age-alarm forever about money nobody can ever attribute |
| Deductions itemised vs netted | Some sources show gross and deductions; some show only net. The second makes explainability structurally impossible | Disclosed as net-only, never reverse-engineered (`10.01.07` D-03) |
| Withholding tax deducted at source | US backup withholding / treaty rates on foreign income, shown as a deduction | Captured as a distinct deduction type — losing it at parse loses domain 23's reclaimable money |
| At-source held balance below the society's threshold | Printed as "held: £2.11". Money that exists and did not arrive | Captured as a fact → `10.04.03`, `10.05.03`. It is leakage evidence the instant it is read |
| Multi-currency in one statement | Real for aggregated foreign income | Per-line currency → `10.02.04`. Reconciliation then needs **the source's own FX rate**, which is a parsed fact, never a platform-supplied one (D-14) |
| Source's own FX rate absent on a multi-currency statement | Σ(mixed-currency lines) cannot be compared to a single-currency total | `Unoracled` for the monetary check; structural check still runs and still gates |
| Rounding at the source | Sub-cent per line, material across a million lines | The parse never rounds — source precision preserved exactly. The only rounding question here is D-10's tolerance; distributing rounding dust to people is `10.03.01` D-04 |
| Wrong source declared at intake (PRS file uploaded as DistroKid) | Common, human, and the adapter fails structurally | Fingerprint mismatch names the mismatch and offers the sources the file *does* fingerprint as (D-15). **Never guessed by sniffing** — and never repaired by asking the user to map columns (D-03) |
| Two adapters could claim one file | Adapter selection is declared source + fingerprint, not content sniffing | Ambiguity is refused, not resolved by confidence |
| Statement covers forty artists who are not the uploader | A label statement. Parent `Q-03` | **The parse reads the whole file** (D-13). It has no choice: dropping other people's lines makes Σ(lines) ≠ stated total and destroys the oracle on exactly the statements that need it most. Privacy filtering therefore cannot live at parse — a real constraint on `Q-03`'s answer |
| Uploader is a Producer who appears on their own label's statement | Payee party = the label; a per-line named party = the uploader personally. The same human on both sides of one file | D-19 keeps them distinct: the statement's payee is a parse output separate from the parties named per line |
| Adapter version publishes mid-parse | A race between a fix and a run | Version pinned at run start (D-11). The in-flight run finishes under the version it began with |
| Same statement parsed twice concurrently | Double upload race past `10.02.01`'s dedupe | One interpretation per (statement, adapter version) — idempotent by construction. Determinism (D-11) means the loser of the race is byte-identical to the winner |
| Adapter reads ambient state (today's date, server locale, live FX) | Non-deterministic parse. Re-running the same file yields a different answer | **Forbidden** (D-11). It makes D-04's versioning a lie and `Q-03` unanswerable |
| Adapter corrected; statements already parsed and **already paid** | Re-parse yields different numbers for money that has moved | New interpretation alongside the old, never in place (CX-01). The platform **names its own fault as its own** (`10.03.04` D-04) — an adapter bug reported as "a correction" is the easiest lie in the domain. Whether history re-runs at all is `Q-03` |
| Statement blocked at reconciliation for four quarters | **The platform's own black box** (DT-09). Money that arrived, that the platform read, sitting behind an engineering ticket | Blocked statements age and alarm, denominated in money (D-12) — the fourth independent occurrence of the parent domain's D-04 pattern |
| File is a zip bomb / 2GB DSR / XXE-bearing XML | An uploaded statement is an **untrusted file** | Rejected at structural validation, retained as evidence, never rendered or expanded. Machinery is a security cross-cut; the behaviour is: fail closed, keep the file |
| CSV with a leading `=`/`+`/`-`/`@` in a text cell | Formula injection against anything that re-exports it | Values are data; the parse never evaluates. Export escaping is a downstream concern with the same answer |
| Truncated / partially transferred file | Last row incomplete | Structural check catches it. A truncated statement is `Unreadable`, never `Partial` — partial data with a clean-looking total is the exact failure D-05 exists to prevent |
| User forges a statement to manufacture income | Anti-persona (`personas.md`: inflates credits). Trivial for a self-uploaded file | Parent D-03 holds — the platform records that a *user* asserted a file, not that money exists. Custody (D-20) is what keeps a self-upload from being laundered into an assertion the platform makes on the user's behalf (`Q-05`) |
| Statement in a language/script the adapter's headers don't match | Transliterated or localised column headers from a foreign society | Adapter is per-source and per-format-version, so the source's language is the adapter's problem, not the user's. No adapter → honest gap |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unparsed *(empty)* | Received from `10.02.01`, queued | Waiting. The file is safe and retained |
| Parsing *(loading)* | Adapter version pinned, run started | In progress, with the source and period named — a 400,000-line DSR is not instant and pretending otherwise is a lie |
| Parsed & reconciled *(populated)* | Both checks clean | Line count and total, **matching the statement**, side by side with the paper. The one sentence this feature exists to say |
| Parsed, zero-value | Zero lines, stated total 0.00 | A recorded zero. "PRS distributed £0.00" — a fact, explicitly not silence |
| Unoracled *(partial)* | Parsed; no stated total exists, or a multi-currency statement with no source rate | Parsed and **explicitly unverified**. The flag travels to `10.03.01` and `10.04.02` — never rendered as if reconciled (DT-05) |
| Reconciliation failed — structural *(error)* | Rows unclassified | **Blocked.** The parse is not trusted; nothing proceeds to matching. Unreadable row count named |
| Reconciliation failed — monetary *(error)* | Σ lines ≠ declared total, beyond tolerance; structure clean | **Blocked**, stated in money. Triage decides platform fault vs source fault (`Q-04`) |
| Source-total disputed | Triage confirms the source's own arithmetic is wrong | Released with the flag attached → `10.08`. The platform read it right; the statement is wrong |
| Blocked & ageing | Any blocked state past a threshold | **Escalating, money-sized alarm** (D-12). Bounded by the source's claim window, same as `10.02.05` D-05 |
| Fingerprint mismatch *(error)* | File doesn't match the declared source's known formats | "This doesn't look like a DistroKid statement — it looks like a PRS one." Names the mismatch; does not guess, does not ask for a column map |
| No adapter | Unknown source, unknown format version, or a scanned image PDF | Honest gap: the file is retained (`10.02.01` D-03), the platform cannot read it, and it says so. Queued as platform work (`Q-01`) |
| Partial | Some rows classified `unreadable` while others parsed | Named, quantified, and **not** silently dropped. Cannot pass the gate — a partial parse is a failed parse, it just knows its own size |
| Drift flagged | Clean reconciliation, unexpected column present | Passes, with a flag. Information the platform is discarding is worth a sentence (D-17) |
| Superseded | Adapter version corrected | Re-parse produces a new interpretation; the old one is retained, because money may already have moved on it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Parsing and matching are one step — you read the file and attach the money to the work | The natural framing, and how a first implementation would be written | ❌ **REJECTED, and keeping them apart is what makes the sub-domain debuggable.** They fail identically from the user's side ("my money isn't showing up") and have nothing else in common. A parse failure is the platform's fault, is fixed by an engineer, and is **locally detectable with certainty** via the stated-total check. A match failure is a catalogue/identifier problem, is fixed by the user or by domain 09, and is **never locally certain** — you cannot prove a line belongs to a work. Merging them means a mismatched line and a misparsed line land in the same bucket with the same message, and the stated-total oracle — the only hard check in the domain — becomes unusable, because you can no longer tell whether the sum is wrong or the attribution is. |
| DT-02 | The platform can ask the user to map unknown columns | Standard import-wizard UX; it works fine for CSV contact imports | ❌ **REJECTED.** A royalty statement's columns are not self-describing to their own recipients — "Adj. Net Rec." is not a question a working drummer can answer, and a wrong answer produces a confidently wrong payment rather than a visible error. Worse, per-user mappings mean the same source parses differently for different users, and the platform can never reason about a source's format again. **Adapters are the platform's job, per source, once.** The honest failure mode is "we don't support this source yet" — which is at least true, and is a queue the platform can work through. |
| DT-03 | Reconciliation to the stated total is a validation check | It looks like one — sum the lines, compare, warn | ✅ **CONFIRMED, and promoted to the feature's primary output.** Domain 10 is characterised by facts nobody can verify: registration state is inferred (`10.01.04` D-03), matching is probabilistic (`10.02.03`), society behaviour is opaque, at-source deductions are unknowable (`10.01.07` D-03). The stated total is **the only hard oracle in the entire domain** — a number the counterparty itself published, checkable locally, for free, on every statement, with no relationship required. A domain this full of inference should build its trust story on the one thing it can actually prove. |
| DT-04 | A statement that reconciles to its stated total was parsed correctly | The obvious reading of DT-03, and the one the rest of the pipeline will assume unless told otherwise | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the most dangerous sentence in the feature, because it is 90% true.** The oracle proves **completeness of money**: nothing dropped, duplicated, or double-counted from a subtotal row. It proves **nothing about semantics**. Read a EUR statement as GBP and every number is identical, the sum is perfect, and the money is wrong by the FX rate. Misread the usage period and the sum is perfect while the wrong split version is selected (parent D-02) — paying the right amount to the wrong people, which is the domain's signature failure. Same for territory, right type, and identifiers. **D-02 is a completeness oracle, not a correctness oracle**, and a platform whose discipline is "state what you know and say so when you don't" (D-15) cannot let its one hard check quietly imply more than it delivers. The semantic fields need their own per-adapter assertions, and the statement must record which checks ran (D-08). |
| DT-05 | Every statement prints a total, so the oracle is always available | True of every society and distributor statement you would think of first | ❌ **REJECTED.** Raw detail exports frequently print no total; two-file statements (a summary PDF plus a detail CSV) print it in the *other* file; a multi-currency statement with no stated source rate cannot have its mixed lines summed to a single-currency total at all. The failure mode is not the absence — it is **the absence being indistinguishable from a pass**. A pipeline that only models `reconciled` / `failed` will record an unoracled statement as reconciled by default, and the domain's entire trust story becomes conditional on a fact nobody checked. `Unoracled` must be a distinct state that travels downstream into `10.03.01`'s derivation and onto `10.04.02`'s payee statement, because a number derived from an unverified parse is a different claim from one derived from a verified parse — and D-08 says so out loud. |
| DT-06 | A failed reconciliation means the platform's parse is wrong | The feature's own original framing, and it is right most of the time | ❌ **REJECTED as an automatic verdict — the source's statement not adding up is routine, and the money in that gap is the user's.** Structural reconciliation (every row classified) and monetary reconciliation (lines sum to the declared total) are different checks with different meanings. Structural fail ⇒ the parse is definitely wrong. Structural pass + monetary fail ⇒ *narrowed*, not decided: a misclassified subtotal, a misread value, or a source whose own arithmetic is off. Asserting platform fault there is not merely inaccurate — it **discards a finding worth real money** and hands it back to nobody, when a statement that disagrees with itself is exactly what `10.08` exists to argue about. The gate still holds (D-02): nothing proceeds either way. What changes is the verdict, which requires a human at the platform (`Q-04`) and is never automatic. Structural reconciliation is necessary, not sufficient. |
| DT-07 | A statement covers one period | Every mental model of "the Q1 statement", and the shape of the word "statement" | ❌ **REJECTED — and a domain expert would flag it in the first minute.** A society distribution is a **payment cycle, not a period**: one PRS distribution routinely bundles 2025 UK radio, 2023 live performances that took eighteen months to process, and 2022 international collections arriving via a sub-publisher. Three vintages, one file, one cheque. The consequence is not cosmetic — usage period selects the split version (parent D-02), so a statement-level period would attribute 2022 usage to 2025's owners, silently, plausibly, on the very first real statement the platform ever ingests. Period is per-line and always was; there is no statement-level period, only a distribution cycle that happens to have a name. |
| DT-08 | A line with no identifier is a matching problem | The clean separation of concerns — parse reads, match resolves (DT-01) | ❌ **REJECTED for one specific class, and the exception is load-bearing.** Most identifier-less lines are matchable-in-principle: a mangled title is `10.02.03`'s job and the platform's credits graph is the discriminator. But some lines are **aggregated at source** — "SACEM international, Q3 2024, £14.22" is a bucket the counterparty flattened before sending, and the per-work detail does not exist anywhere the platform can reach. It is not unmatched-yet; it is unmatchable-in-principle, permanently. Only the adapter knows this, because only the adapter knows the source's aggregation level — and if that fact is not carried out of the parse, `10.02.05`'s ageing alarm (D-05 there) will nag a user forever about money that no catalogue improvement, no ISRC, and no credit claim will ever resolve. That teaches the user to ignore the queue, which is `10.02.05` DT-03's failure delivered by a sibling. |
| DT-09 | A blocked statement is a safe failure — the money isn't wrong, it just isn't moving | The whole point of a hard gate, and true on day one | ❌ **REJECTED at four quarters.** Money that arrived, that the platform read, sitting behind an engineering ticket nobody is clocking, is **the platform's own black box** — precisely parent D-09's condition: an institution that knows the amount, knows the source, and cannot get it to the person. `10.05.01` sells the cure for that. A blocked statement is worse than `10.02.05`'s queue in one respect: the user cannot resolve it, because D-03 forbids asking them to, so the clock is the *only* mechanism that exists. This is the fourth independent occurrence of the parent domain's D-04 (`10.01.03`, `10.01.04`, `10.02.01`, `10.02.05` reached it first): **an absence produces no event, so a clock must manufacture one.** Blocked statements age, and the alarm is denominated in money, because "3 statements blocked" gets muted and "£12,491 we've received and can't yet trust" does not. |
| DT-10 | A self-uploaded statement and a source-fetched one are the same fact | Both are files; both parse identically; the parser has no reason to care | ❌ **REJECTED — the parse is identical and the evidentiary weight is not.** `10.02.01` names three channels, and they differ cryptographically, not just in convenience: a **forwarded** society statement email carries DKIM, making the file attributable to the source by signature; a **fetched** file has the platform's own retrieval as its witness; an **uploaded** file has nothing but a user's word — and `personas.md` names statement-inflation as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour. This matters the moment a parsed line becomes something the platform *asserts* rather than merely displays: evidence in a dispute (`10.08`), an income claim in 23's financial identity, an input to `10.10`'s forecast. Parent D-03 ("evidence, never truth") holds the line for display; **custody is what stops a self-upload being laundered into a platform assertion** (`Q-05`). It also gives forwarding a reason to exist beyond low friction, which is a better argument than the one currently made for it. |
| DT-11 | Format changes break loudly, so drift is self-announcing | The feature's own edge case assumed this — "adapter breaks, usually mid-batch, always without notice" | ❌ **REJECTED for the half that matters.** *Subtractive* drift (a column removed, renamed, reordered) does break loudly and is the easy case. **Additive** drift does not break at all: a source adds a "streaming adjustment" or a new deduction column, the adapter ignores what it never knew about, structural reconciliation passes (the row is classified), monetary reconciliation passes (the declared total still holds), and the platform silently discards a fact about the user's money while displaying a green check. The dangerous drift is the drift that reconciles — which is DT-04's blind spot arriving from a second direction. An unexpected column must be a flag on a *clean* parse (D-17), because that is the only moment anyone will ever notice it. |
| DT-12 | The platform should parse only the lines belonging to the uploading user | The obvious privacy instinct on a label statement naming forty artists, and what parent `Q-03` will be tempted by | ✅ **CONFIRMED as impossible here — and that forecloses an answer to `Q-03` before it is asked.** Filtering at parse makes Σ(parsed lines) ≠ stated total by construction, on exactly the statements where the oracle matters most: the multi-party label statements with the widest formats and the least trustworthy arithmetic. The platform would be trading its only hard check for a privacy posture that does not even work, since the file itself is retained regardless (`10.02.01` D-03) and the third-party earnings are already in the building. **The whole file is parsed; privacy is enforced downstream on visibility and attribution, not upstream on comprehension** (D-13). `Q-03` therefore has one fewer possible answer than it looks like it has, and Step 6's synthesis should carry that constraint rather than rediscover it. |
| DT-13 | An LLM can parse arbitrary statements, which solves the long-tail adapter problem outright | The 2026-obvious answer, and it directly attacks `Q-01` (how many adapters is a viable launch?) and `Q-02` (PDFs). It would be irresponsible not to ask | ❌ **REJECTED at runtime; ✅ CONFIRMED as an authoring aid — and the distinction is the useful part.** Three independent objections, any one fatal at runtime: (1) **non-determinism** — the same file must parse identically forever (D-11), or D-04's version pinning is a lie and `Q-03` is unanswerable; a sampled model is not that. (2) **`security-first.md` forbids it** — statements carry PII (names, addresses, payee references, and other people's earnings), and "no PII in AI model requests" is a project rule, not a preference; this is a hard block, not a trade-off. (3) **The errors are plausible rather than structural** — a hand-written adapter fails loudly on a changed column; a model quietly interpolates a column that was never there, and DT-04 says the sum check will not catch it. But the honest half: an LLM drafting a **candidate adapter** from a redacted sample, reviewed by a human and pinned as versioned data, is deterministic at runtime, PII-free, and attacks the real constraint — which is *adapter-authoring throughput*, not parsing capability. `Q-01`'s long tail is a labour problem wearing a technology costume. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Parsing and matching are **separate features** | DT-01. Different fault owners, different fixes, and merging them destroys the stated-total oracle. |
| D-02 | **Reconciliation to the stated total gates everything downstream** | DT-03. The only hard oracle in domain 10. A failed reconciliation stops the pipeline; it does not warn. |
| D-03 | Adapters are the platform's job, per source. **The user never maps columns** | DT-02. Users cannot answer the question, and a wrong answer becomes a wrong payment. |
| D-04 | Adapters are **versioned data**, applied by statement date | Sources change formats silently; re-parsing an old statement under a new adapter would change a number that has already been paid. |
| D-05 | Unparseable lines are **named and counted**, never dropped | A silently short parse produces a total that looks complete. This is the mechanism by which a platform becomes its own black box (parent domain D-09). |
| D-06 | Reconciliation is **two checks**: structural (every row classified) and monetary (lines sum to the declared total). **Both gate** | DT-06. Structural reconciliation is what makes D-05 checkable rather than aspirational — a parser cannot silently skip what it must explicitly classify. |
| D-07 | The adapter **declares which stated total** its lines sum to; every other printed total is captured as a fact | Statements print several totals, and society lines are routinely gross against a net-of-commission remittance. "Compare to the biggest number on the page" fails on the first real statement. The other totals are `10.01.07` D-03's deductions and `10.04.03`'s at-source holds. |
| D-08 | D-02's oracle is **completeness, not correctness** — and the statement records which checks ran | DT-04. Currency, period, territory, right type and identifiers can all be uniformly wrong while the sum is perfect. D-15's discipline applies to the platform's own checks: state what was verified and what was merely assumed. |
| D-09 | A statement with **no stated total is `Unoracled`** — a distinct state that never silently counts as reconciled, and whose flag travels downstream | DT-05. The absence must not be indistinguishable from a pass. A number derived from an unverified parse is a different claim, and `10.03.01`/`10.04.02` must be able to say so. |
| D-10 | Reconciliation tolerance is **computed from the file** — `0.5×10^-q + n × 0.5×10^-p` — never a constant | A fixed penny is too tight for a 12-line PRS statement and far too loose for a 400,000-line DSR. Within bound = the source's own rounding, recorded. Outside = blocked. |
| D-11 | Parsing is **deterministic**: same file + same adapter version = identical lines, forever. Adapters read no ambient state (clock, locale, live FX) | Without it D-04's version pinning is meaningless and `Q-03` has no answerable form — the platform cannot re-run history it cannot reproduce. |
| D-12 | **Blocked statements age and alarm, denominated in money** | DT-09. The fourth independent occurrence of the parent domain's D-04. The user cannot resolve a blocked parse (D-03 forbids asking), so the clock is the only mechanism that exists. |
| D-13 | The parse reads the **whole file**, including lines about third parties | DT-12. Filtering at parse makes Σ(lines) ≠ stated total by construction and destroys the oracle on exactly the multi-party statements that need it most. Privacy is enforced downstream on visibility, not upstream on comprehension. Constrains parent `Q-03`. |
| D-14 | The **source's own FX rate and its at-source deductions are parsed facts**, never platform-supplied | CX-02: reconciliation is only arithmetically possible in source terms. A mixed-currency statement can only be summed with the rate the source itself used. |
| D-15 | Adapter selection is **declared source + in-file fingerprint**, never content sniffing. A mismatch names the mismatch and does not guess | A PRS file uploaded as DistroKid is common and human. Guessing produces a confident wrong parse; asking the user to fix it violates D-03. |
| D-16 | **No runtime LLM parsing.** LLM use is confined to human-reviewed adapter authoring against redacted samples | DT-13. Non-deterministic (violates D-11), and statements carry PII — `security-first.md` forbids PII in model payloads outright. The authoring aid attacks the real constraint (`Q-01`'s adapter throughput) without any of the three objections. |
| D-17 | **Additive format drift is flagged even on a clean reconciliation** | DT-11. An unexpected column passes both gates while the platform silently discards a fact about the user's money. The dangerous drift is the one that reconciles. |
| D-18 | **Aggregation level is a parse output** — lines the source flattened are marked unmatchable-in-principle | DT-08. Only the adapter knows the source's aggregation level. Without the marking, `10.02.05`'s ageing alarm nags forever about money nobody can ever attribute, which teaches users to ignore the queue. |
| D-19 | The **statement's payee party** is a parse output, distinct from the parties named per line | A label statement pays the label and names forty artists. On the multi-hyphenate's own label statement the same human is both — and conflating them attributes their personal lines to the label's context. |
| D-20 | The parse output carries the **custody** of its input file (uploaded / forwarded+DKIM / fetched) | DT-10. The parse is identical; the evidentiary weight is not. Custody is what stops a self-uploaded file being laundered into an assertion the platform makes. |
