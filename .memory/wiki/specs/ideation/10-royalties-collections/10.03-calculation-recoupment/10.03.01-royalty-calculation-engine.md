# Feature: Royalty Calculation Engine

> **Number**: 10.03.01
> **Parent**: [Royalty Calculation & Recoupment](./10.03-calculation-recoupment-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-20

## Overview

The arithmetic that turns **a matched royalty line into per-person amounts**: take £4.11 of PRS performance income for a work, resolve the split that was true during the usage period, apply the deal terms sitting on top of it (`10.03.02`), and produce an amount per party — with the whole derivation preserved.

The number is the easy part. **The derivation is the product.** `problem-statement.md` argues the industry's defining failure is that nobody can explain where the money went; every royalty statement in music is unauditable because the split was reconstructed from memory and the terms were interpreted by the party who owed the money. WeJammin has the split from the room (`D-18`). If the engine cannot show a payee exactly how £4.11 became their £0.62, it has thrown away the only thing it has that Curve and Exactuals do not.

The deepening pass changed the feature's shape in four decisive ways, and each one turns a sentence that read as obvious into a defect:

1. **There is no "the split"** (`DT-04`). The surface pass wrote *"the split as of the usage period"*, singular. A work has a composition split, its recording has a master split, and its performers have a neighbouring-rights split — **three different ledgers, three different party lists, three different answers, for one song**. Resolving "the split" resolves the wrong one, plausibly, and pays a stranger.
2. **As-of is only half of the temporal answer** (`DT-05`). Parent `D-02` pins *valid-time* — which split was true in Q1 2024. It does not pin *transaction-time* — what the ledger **knew** when the calculation ran. Without the second, re-running a calculation returns a different number from the same inputs, and `D-01`'s stored derivation is decorative rather than checkable.
3. **The engine is not arithmetic; it is versioned software with a history** (`DT-06`). The upstream parser already knows this (`10.02.02` D-04, D-11). The engine inherited none of it.
4. **The engine never rounds** (`DT-12`). `DT-03`'s "a million lines × six parties × a sub-cent remainder" is a real problem created entirely by rounding per line. Round once, at the payable boundary, and the residue collapses from millions of orphaned fractions to **half a minor unit per party per payout run**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Read-only | Sees the working: this usage line, this right type, this split as of this period, these deductions, your amount. They cannot alter any of it — every input belongs upstream (parent `D-05`), and a payee who could edit a royalty calculation would be editing it at the moment of maximum financial motivation. The value is that for the first time the answer to *"why is this £0.62?"* is a chain of facts rather than a shrug. `personas.md` describes their money as living in a spreadsheet; this replaces the spreadsheet's guesswork. **What they see is scoped, not total** (`D-11`): their own amount at full depth, the split they themselves agreed at full depth, and any bilateral term they are not party to as a **named, sized deduction whose parameters are not theirs to read** — the derivation is computed whole and disclosed per viewer. |
| **Producer** | Read-only | Same, and identical to a Musician's — the sub-domain's Musician/Producer divergence is **payee vs payer and lives in `10.03.02`/`10.03.03`, not here** (parent index Role Matrix finding). Their points enter as a deal term (`10.03.02`), not as a special case in the engine, and the distinction is load-bearing: a Producer who could adjust the engine could adjust everyone's money, whereas a Producer who adjusts a term adjusts a record that carries their name (`10.03.02` D-03) and triggers a visible restatement (`10.03.04` D-02). **The multi-hyphenate makes this sharper, not softer**: when a Producer is also a co-writer on the work, they are simultaneously the payer of their own points and a payee of the share those points are drawn from — and that is not a permission problem, it is an **arithmetic one with no answer** (`DT-09`, `D-12`). |
| **Operator** | None | No surface, and the absence is a fact rather than an omission. A venue's blanket PRS/PPL licence is a **cost**; Operators receive no royalty statement, hold no catalogue and own no share of a work, so there is nothing here to calculate for them. Their single appearance in domain 10 is `10.06` (setlist reporting), where their licence *generated* someone else's money (parent index Role Matrix finding). |
| **Fan** | None | No surface. Fan is `None` across all ten children of domain 10 — the only domain drilled with no Fan surface whatsoever. Royalty calculation has no consumer-facing half, and `D-11` ("fans are first-class users") has **zero consequence** here (parent index Role Matrix finding). |

## Behavior

### What the engine resolves, in a fixed order

Per matched line (`10.02.03`, already confirmed and reconciled — nothing reaches here on a suggestion, `10.02.03` D-01):

1. **Which right type, which territory, which usage period, which level** — from `10.02.03` D-16 and `10.02.04` D-03. These four **select the ledger**, and selecting the wrong one is the engine's most expensive silent error (`DT-04`).
2. **The applicable split and, for a multi-work recording, its Recording→Work allocation, resolved bitemporally** — read from domain 09 at *valid-time* = the usage period, and at *transaction-time* = the state pinned to this calculation version (`D-03`, `D-08`). A multi-work recording first resolves its valid positive exact-rational link weights totaling 100%, then resolves each linked work's separate ledger; it never merges those ledgers. For temporal overlap the engine consumes only declarant-confirmed Domain 09 weights: it never calculates duration, normalizes, equal-splits, infers contribution or a remainder, or writes an upstream allocation. An unavailable or invalid allocation blocks this input rather than producing a fallback amount. Never read any input as it stands today. This is the single most consequential resolution in the sub-domain and the one most likely to be got wrong silently.
3. **Recursive party resolution** — a split names *parties*, and a party may be an entity (a band, a publisher, an LLC) with its own internal distribution rule. Resolution recurses, as-of at **every** level, until it reaches a party that declares itself terminal (`D-13`, `DT-10`).
4. **Deal terms in effect for that period** — `10.03.02`. Points, admin fees, escalations, net-vs-gross. Applied as recorded; never interpreted (`10.03.02` D-02).
5. **The deduction stack, itemised** — society commission, sub-publisher retention at source (`10.01.07` D-03), platform fee, admin fee. Compounding, and the reason writers cannot state their own effective rate. **Deductions already taken at source arrive as parsed facts** (`10.02.02` D-14) and are disclosed, never recomputed; only deductions the platform itself applies are computed here.
6. **Per-party exact amounts**, each carrying its full derivation. **No rounding occurs** (`D-15`).

Recoupment is deliberately **not** in this list. `10.03.03` decides whether an amount is *payable*; the engine decides what it *is*. Merging them makes an earning indistinguishable from a payment, which is precisely the confusion that lets an unrecouped artist believe they earned nothing (`DT-02`, CX R-01 — the edge is one-way and the one-way-ness is the moral content).

### There is no "the split" — there are three ledgers

One song, one quarter, one human, **three unrelated splits**:

| Right type | Ledger | Party list | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Composition / performance + mechanical** | Work-side (PRS, MLC, HFA) | Writers and their publishers | Writer share + publisher share, **paid separately at source** in most territories |
| **Master / recording** | Recording-side (DSPs via distributor, label) | Master owner, producer points, featured artist | Owner-dominated; points carved out of a named share |
| **Neighbouring rights** | Performer-side (PPL, SoundExchange) | Featured and non-featured performers | Society-defined allocation, **not a negotiated split at all** |

`10.02.03` D-16 already forbids merging attributions across right types. This feature states the consequence the matcher could not: **the right type does not merely tag the money, it selects which ownership record is even relevant.** A drummer who played on a track owns 0% of the composition, 0% of the master, and a real PPL performer share. Resolve "the split" against the composition ledger for a PPL line and you pay the writers for the drummer's performance — an error that reconciles perfectly against itself, names only real people, and is invisible forever.

**The writer/publisher share trap.** Performance societies pay the writer share directly to the writer and the publisher share to the publisher; the two arrive as **separate lines, often from separate statements**. A £4.11 PRS line is frequently already only one side of a 50/50 source split. Applying the full work split to it distributes the writer's half across writers *and* publishers and halves everyone. The engine must therefore know **what base the line's amount represents** — which is a parsed fact (`10.02.02` D-07, D-14), not an engine assumption. Where the source does not state it, the line is unparameterised (`D-16`), not guessed.

### Bitemporality — the half parent D-02 is missing

Parent `D-02` says: read the split **as of the usage period**. That pins *valid-time*. It does not pin *transaction-time*, and both are required:

- **Valid-time** — *when was this split true?* Q1 2024. Fixed by the usage period.
- **Transaction-time** — *when did the ledger learn it?* A dispute resolving in 2026 **retroactively changes what "the Q1 2024 split" is**. The as-of read for Q1 2024 returns one answer in March 2026 and a different one in April 2026, from the same query, with nothing having errored.

Consequence: with valid-time alone, **re-running calculation version 1 does not reproduce version 1**. The stored derivation cannot be checked against its own inputs, `10.03.04`'s delta between two versions is a comparison of two unreproducible numbers, and `D-01`'s claim that the derivation is the product becomes a display decision rather than a verifiable one. **Pinning both is what makes the whole immutable-version model mean anything** (`D-08`) — and it upgrades `Q-01` from *"does 09 support as-of reads?"* to *"is 09's ledger bitemporal?"*, which is a materially larger ask of another domain and must be asked in its larger form.

### Determinism — the engine is versioned software, not arithmetic

`10.02.02` D-11 requires the parser to be deterministic and to read no ambient state, and `10.02.02` D-04 makes adapters versioned data applied by statement date. The engine needs both, for the same reasons, and had neither:

- **The engine reads no ambient state** — no clock, no live FX rate, no current split, no "latest" anything. Every input is pinned into the calculation version (`D-09`).
- **The engine's own logic is versioned**, and the version is part of the derivation. Points-from-artist's-share is an ordering decision; changing that ordering changes every historical number.
- **Therefore: same line + same pinned inputs + same engine version = the identical amount, forever.**

The payoff is that `D-01` becomes **falsifiable**. A stored derivation you cannot re-execute is a receipt; a stored derivation that reproduces its number exactly is a proof. The cost is honest and must be stated: **an engine bug fix is a restatement whose cause is the platform's own defect** (`10.03.04` D-04), identical in shape to `10.02.02` Q-03's adapter correction. The platform must say so rather than shipping it as "a correction".

### The derivation is a function; the line is its argument

`10.02.03` D-06 solved matching's volume problem by changing the noun — 41,880 lines collapse to 214 identities. **That collapse does not transfer here, because the money is per line and every line's money is distinct.** The engine faces the full line count.

But a different collapse is available, and it is what makes `D-01` affordable rather than aspirational. For 40,000 lines resolving to one identity in one usage period under one right type in one territory from one source, the split, the terms, the deduction stack and the engine version are **identical**. What differs is one number.

So the derivation is not stored per line. It is a **resolved parameter set** — split version + term versions + deduction stack + engine version, with their provenance — keyed on `(source identity, usage period, right type, territory, source)`, and every line resolving to that key **references** it and carries only its own arithmetic (`D-10`). The derivation is the function; the line is its argument; the amount is the application. Store the function once and the number is recomputable from it — which is exactly what `D-01` claimed, now with a cost that scales.

### Sub-100%, over-100%, and the reconciliation gate as a security control

`D-04`'s parts-must-sum-to-whole reads as accounting hygiene. It is also **the only detector the engine has for a misrecorded or malicious term set** (`DT-11`), and the two directions get opposite treatments:

- **Σ < whole** (splits sum to 97%): 3% belongs to nobody the platform can name. It is **not redistributed** — redistribution invents a fact, which is `D-15`'s prohibition and `problem-statement.md`'s reconstruction failure committed by the system built to prevent it. It is **not silently retained** — that is parent `D-09`, the platform becoming its own black box. The 97% calculates and flows; the 3% becomes an **explicit residual with its money attached**, routed to `10.02.05` where it is denominated, aged and alarmed (`10.02.05` D-01, D-05), and the gap is raised as a **finding** to domain 09 (an incomplete ledger) and `10.01.04` (a possibly incomplete registration — the society may have distributed only what was registered, in which case the £4.11 was never the whole). Resolves `Q-03`.
- **Σ > whole** (splits sum to 100% and a term adds 3 points "from the top"): the engine would pay out more than came in. This is a **hard block**, not a residual — there is no honest partial answer, and producing one would move real money that does not exist. `10.03.02` records that points-from-the-top vs points-from-artist's-share "must be explicit; there is no safe default"; **this check is what makes that requirement enforceable rather than advisory**, and it is equally the control against a crafted term set attempting to over-draw a line.

### Rounding — once, at the payable boundary, never per line

`DT-03` correctly refused to treat rounding as an implementation detail, but its framing — *a million lines × six parties × a sub-cent remainder* — describes a problem **created by rounding per line**. Round a £0.0004 line across six parties to the minor unit and every party gets £0.00 and the entire amount vanishes. Do it 400,000 times and the platform has silently destroyed real money while reconciling perfectly.

The resolution (`D-15`, resolving `Q-02`):

- **The engine is exact.** It carries decimal arithmetic at a declared fixed precision materially finer than the currency's minor unit — **never binary floating point**, which cannot represent £0.10 and would fail `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation gate spuriously and unfixably.
- **The engine never rounds.** Not per line, not per step, not per party. A line's per-party amount is `£0.000412…`, and that is the fact.
- **Rounding happens exactly once**, at the **payable boundary** — the point where the platform states a figure someone will actually be paid (`10.04.01` when it exists; the stated payable figure when it does not, per parent `Q-03`/`Q-07`). It rounds a **per-party aggregate**, not a line.
- **The residue is allocated by largest-remainder**, which forces Σ(rounded parties) = rounded whole exactly, satisfying `D-04`. Ties break on a stable key derived from the party and the run — **never on list order**, which is precisely `DT-03`'s objection that round-half-up favours whoever is listed first.
- **The residue is therefore bounded at ≤ half a minor unit per party per payout run** — not per line. The orphaned-money problem does not need a policy; it needs the engine to stop rounding.
- **The rounding step is itself a visible line in the derivation.** It is arithmetic performed on the payee's money and it is disclosed like every other step (`D-05`).

Precision floor, with the arithmetic shown: at 6 dp, per-line truncation of up to `0.0000005` across a 400,000-line DSR × 6 parties is ~£1.20 per statement — material. At 9 dp it is ~£0.0012 — immaterial. **≥9 dp is the floor**; the exact digit count is an architecture decision (`Q-04`) and must be **fixed permanently**, because changing it restates history.

### Immutability

The engine is **immutable and versioned** (parent `D-04`). A recalculation produces a new version alongside the old; the old is never overwritten, because a clawback is defined as the difference between two versions and cannot exist if one of them is gone. Combined with `D-08` and `D-09`, every version is **re-executable** — which is what distinguishes a version history from a pile of superseded numbers.

### Happy Path

1. A matched, confirmed line arrives from `10.02.03` with its **right type, territory, usage period, source identity and source-currency amount**, already reconciled to the statement's stated total (`10.02.02` D-02 — a hard gate; nothing unreconciled reaches here)
2. The engine selects the **ledger** from the right type and level — work-side, recording-side or performer-side (`D-07`). A composition line does not touch the master ledger
3. The engine resolves the parameter set for `(identity, usage period, right type, territory, source)`; if one already exists for this key it is **reused, not recomputed** (`D-10`)
4. The applicable split is read from domain 09 **bitemporally** — valid-time = usage period, transaction-time pinned into this version (`D-03`, `D-08`). For a multi-work recording, the engine first pins the valid positive exact-rational Recording→Work allocation from Domain 09 and applies it before each linked work's separate ledger; an invalid/incomplete allocation blocks rather than being calculated, normalized, equal-split, or repaired.
5. Any party that is an **entity** resolves recursively against its own internal rule, as-of at each level, until every branch terminates in a declared payee (`D-13`)
6. Deal terms in effect **for that period** are applied (`10.03.02`); the deduction stack is **itemised, never netted** (`D-05`); at-source deductions are carried through as disclosed parsed facts, not recomputed (`10.02.02` D-14)
7. **Σ(parts) is checked against the whole**: equal → proceed; less → the shortfall becomes a visible residual to `10.02.05` and a finding to 09/`10.01.04`; greater → hard block (`D-14`)
8. Per-party **exact** amounts are produced in **source currency** (`10.02.04` D-01 — conversion is a downstream view and never enters the derivation), each referencing its parameter set and carrying a complete, re-executable derivation chain
9. Amounts flow to `10.03.03` for recoupment — **one-way; the engine never reads the position back** (CX R-01) — then to `10.04` for statement and payout
10. Rounding occurs **once**, at the payable boundary, on per-party aggregates, by largest remainder, and appears in the derivation as its own step (`D-15`)
11. The payee reads a scoped derivation (`D-11`) in `10.04.02` that traces £0.62 back through the deductions, the terms, the split, and the session where the split was agreed (`10.04.02` D-03) — **the sentence no competitor can say honestly**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Multi-work recording has temporal overlap but no valid declared allocation** | No amount is calculated for that multi-work input. Domain 09 must supply positive exact-rational Recording→Work weights totaling exactly 100%; Domain 10 cannot create a duration split, normalize an incomplete set, divide equally, infer a remainder, or edit the links. | "Allocation required before calculation — overlapping works need declared weights totaling 100%." The upstream declaration defect is named; no fallback amount is shown. |
| **Split changed after the usage period** | The engine must use the **old** split. Current-state resolution pays today's owners for yesterday's usage | Parent `D-02`; requires 09's as-of read (`Q-01`) |
| **The ledger's answer for a past period changes** (a 2026 dispute resolves against 2024) | Valid-time is unchanged; **transaction-time moved**. Re-running the old version silently returns a new number | The old version replays from its **pinned** transaction-time and reproduces exactly; the new knowledge is a **new version** → `10.03.04` (`D-08`) |
| **A composition line resolved against the master ledger** | Pays writers for a performer's money, or vice versa. Names only real people, reconciles perfectly, invisible forever | Prevented by construction: the right type **selects the ledger**, it does not tag the output (`D-07`, `10.02.03` D-16) |
| **The multi-hyphenate: one human, three splits, one song** | Writer (PRS), performer (PPL) and master owner (DistroKid) pay the same person for the same song in one quarter | Three derivations, three right types, **never merged** (`10.02.03` DT-12). The statement sums typed facts; the engine does not |
| **The multi-hyphenate: producer is also a co-writer** | Points "from artist's share" where the points-payee **is** one of the artists. The term is **self-referential and has no arithmetic answer** — three defensible readings, differing by real money, and the contract never anticipated it | **Refused**, not defaulted: unparameterised with the reason named (`D-12`, `DT-09`). This is `personas.md`'s central structural fact breaking a deal term, and it is the modal modern production deal — not an edge case |
| **Splits sum to 97% for the period** | The ledger is broken, or the society distributed only what was registered. 3% belongs to nobody the platform can name | 97% calculates and flows; 3% is an **explicit residual with its money attached** → `10.02.05`, aged and alarmed. **Never redistributed** (invention), **never silently retained** (parent `D-09`). Finding raised to 09 and `10.01.04` (`D-14`) |
| **Splits + terms sum to 103%** | The engine would pay out more than arrived. A misrecorded points base (`10.03.02`), or a crafted term set | **Hard block** with the over-subscription named and sized. No partial answer exists; `D-04`'s reconciliation is the detector (`D-14`, `DT-11`) |
| **Line is the writer share only, source doesn't say so** | Applying the full work split halves everyone, plausibly and silently | Base is a **parsed fact** (`10.02.02` D-07, D-14), never an engine assumption. Unstated base ⇒ unparameterised (`D-16`) |
| **A split names a band, not people** | The band is an entity (`personas.md`, domain 01) with its own internal rule and its own membership history | Recursive resolution, as-of at each level. A member who left in 2025 is still paid for Q1 2024 usage (`D-13`) |
| **Entity ownership contains a cycle** | Band A holds 10% of B, B holds 10% of A. Naive recursion never terminates | **Refused and named**, never partially resolved. A cycle is a ledger defect (09/01), not an engine puzzle to solve to N levels (`D-13`) |
| **Entity chain is deep** (writer → publisher → sub-publisher → parent) | The derivation grows a level per hop and stays honest at each | Shown as a chain, aggregated by default and drillable (`10.04.02` D-04) |
| **Entity is a terminal payee, not a pass-through** | A band with its own account is paid as the band; a band that is a bookkeeping fiction distributes to members. **Both exist in reality** | Resolution stops where the entity declares itself terminal — a property of domain 01, not of the engine (`Q-05`) |
| **Deal terms are unrecorded, and a deal is known to exist** | The engine has no parameters. The modal case — most independent deals are a PDF nobody typed in | **No amount is produced.** A raw split shown to a signed artist is wrong by up to a factor of two and looks authoritative; a number with a warning attached is still a number, and users read numbers (`D-16`) |
| **No deal exists at all** | The self-releasing independent writer. The raw split **is** the correct answer | Calculated plainly, stated as raw-split-no-deal — **not** flagged as a gap. Distinguishing this from the row above is the whole of `D-16` |
| **Amount rounds to sub-cent** | £0.0004 per party per line, across millions of lines | **The engine does not round.** Exact to ≥9 dp; rounding happens once, at the payable aggregate, by largest remainder (`D-15`). The residue is ≤ half a minor unit per party per payout run |
| **Negative line rounds asymmetrically** | Round-half-up on a negative rounds toward zero; across millions of returns this systematically favours one side | Rounding is applied to **magnitude with sign preserved**, and only at the aggregate — where the sign is the aggregate's, not the line's (`D-15`) |
| **Binary float used for money** | £0.10 is unrepresentable; `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation fails spuriously and unfixably | Forbidden. Decimal arithmetic at a declared fixed precision (`D-15`, `Q-04`) |
| **At-source netting** (`10.01.07`) | Gross is unknowable; the derivation chain **breaks** at the sub-publisher | Disclosed **at the break** (`10.04.02` D-05). The explainability promise is bounded and must say exactly where — inventing a gross is `D-15`'s prohibition (`10.01.07` D-03) |
| **Negative line** (`10.02.02`) | Returns and adjustments produce negative earnings | Round-trips honestly through the same parameter set → `10.03.04`. Sign is the parse's fact, not the engine's opinion |
| **Negative line exceeds all prior positives for a party** | A crafted or erroneous adjustment drives a party's period earnings below zero | Calculated honestly as negative — the engine computes **earnings**, not payability. Whether a negative earning is recoverable is `10.03.04` Q-01's, not the engine's (`D-02`) |
| **Party is an unclaimed shell** (`02.03`) | Money calculated correctly for a person with no account | Correct, and **unpayable** → `10.04.03`. Calculation is not blocked by payability — collapsing them is `DT-02`'s error one layer out |
| **Party's split is disputed** (domain 09 / `02.05`) | The party is contested; paying either side asserts a verdict the platform does not have | **Calculated and held** → `10.04.04` escrow. The earning is a fact; the payment is not |
| **Domain 09 unreachable for the as-of read** | A partial resolution would present as a complete one | **Blocked, named, money-denominated, aged** (parent `D-04`'s clock). Nothing is calculated on a degraded read — `10.02.03`'s Error-state principle, one step downstream |
| **A confirmed mapping is reversed** (`10.02.03` D-10) | Not one line — **every line the mapping ever attributed**, across every statement, for years | Each affected line gets a **new version** (parent `D-04`); the blast radius is stated in money and lines **before** the reversal is confirmed → `10.03.04` overpayment |
| **An adapter is corrected and lines re-parse** (`10.02.02` Q-03) | Historical amounts change; money may already have moved | Re-parse ⇒ new pinned inputs ⇒ new version. The old version still re-executes to its original number, which is what makes the delta computable at all (`D-09`) |
| **The engine's own logic is fixed** | Every historical number under the old logic is now "wrong" | **The platform names its own fault** (`10.03.04` D-04). Engine version is part of the derivation; a fix is a restatement with the platform as the stated cause — never shipped as "a correction" (`D-09`) |
| **Engine re-run produces a different number from identical inputs** | The derivation is unverifiable and `D-01` is theatre | Impossible by construction — no ambient state, all inputs pinned, logic versioned (`D-09`). If it happens, it is a **defect of the highest order**, not a variance |
| **Restatement cascade re-runs 12 quarters** | One split change restates every calculation, position, statement and payout beneath it | Transitive and bounded by the parameter-set key, not by line count (`D-10`) — but still unbounded work (`10.03.04` Q-03, `Q-07`) |
| **A payee reads a co-payee's bilateral term** | `D-01`'s explainability promise discloses a private commercial relationship | Computed whole, **disclosed scoped** (`D-11`): your amount in full, the split you agreed in full, a third party's bilateral term as a named and sized deduction with unreadable parameters. `10.02.02` D-13's shape — privacy is enforced at disclosure, never by computing less |
| **Derivation is complete and unreadable** | 14 steps behind £0.62 is technically explainable and practically opaque — which is the industry's statement with extra rigour | Structured for progressive disclosure, not emitted as a flat log: **aggregate by default, drill on demand, never omit** (`10.04.02` D-04). A derivation nobody can read is the same as no derivation |
| **Fabricated statement uploaded** | A user invents a statement claiming large earnings | Matches nothing they own (`10.02.03` D-11) so the engine calculates nothing. If it matched, 09's ledger says the work isn't theirs. **The engine is never the ownership control** |
| **Line has no usable period** | Cannot resolve against 09 as-of; the ledger cannot be selected | Never reaches the engine — held at `10.02.05` rather than defaulting to the arrival date (`10.02.04` D-05) |
| **Aggregation the source already flattened** (`10.02.02` D-18) | The source pre-aggregated across territories or periods; the engine cannot resolve a territory-specific chain or a period-specific split | Marked **unresolvable-in-principle at the level the source flattened**, with the lost dimension named. Not a failure to try — a disclosed limit of the input |
| **Two calculation runs race the same line** | Duplicate versions, or a lost update | Versions supersede; they never conflict (CX-03 §1). Identical inputs + identical engine version produce an identical number, so a duplicate run is **idempotent by construction** (`D-09`) — determinism is a concurrency control here, not only an audit property |
| **A term is edited mid-run** | An in-flight calculation mutates underneath itself | Forbidden: inputs are pinned at version creation. New term ⇒ new version ⇒ new calculation; the old is retained (CX-01 §5, parent `D-04`) |

### States

Two state models, deliberately separate — the first is the domain fact, the second is the surface. `10.02.03` established the pattern.

#### Calculation state — per line, per right type

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Pending** | Line matched and confirmed; not yet calculated | Queued, **denominated in money** — never a row count (`10.02.05` D-01's discipline) |
| **Calculated** | Exact per-party amounts derived, Σ reconciles | Per-party amounts with full, re-executable working |
| **Residual** | Σ(parts) < whole — splits don't reach 100% for the period | The attributable share flows; the shortfall is visible money → `10.02.05`, with a finding to 09/`10.01.04` (`D-14`) |
| **Over-subscribed** | Σ(parts) > whole — a term over-draws the line | **Hard block**, over-subscription named and sized. No amount produced (`D-14`) |
| **Unparameterised** | A deal is known to exist and its terms are unrecorded; or a term is self-referential (`D-12`); or the line's base is unstated | **No amount.** What is missing, and who can supply it. Not a flagged number (`D-16`) |
| **Raw split** | No deal exists — the self-releasing writer | The correct answer, stated plainly. Explicitly **not** a gap (`D-16`) |
| **Held** | A party's split is disputed (09 / `02.05`) | Calculated, not payable → `10.04.04` |
| **Blocked** | 09 unreachable, no usable period, invalid/incomplete multi-work Recording→Work allocation, or a cycle in the party graph | Blocked with the **specific** defect named — never a best guess, inferred/normally repaired allocation, or fallback amount. Ages and alarms (parent `D-04`) |
| **Superseded** | Recalculated — new inputs, new engine version, or a reversed mapping | New version; old retained and **still re-executable** (parent `D-04`, `D-09`) → `10.03.04` |

#### Surface state

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | A run is calculating over a freshly matched statement | Progress denominated in **money derived / money matched** — never a spinner, never a line count the user was never shown |
| **Empty (nothing matched)** | No confirmed attributions exist yet | Not a zero state: "nothing has been matched to calculate against" → routes to `10.02.03`, then `02.03.01` if the catalogue is the gap |
| **Empty (nothing to decide)** | Everything calculated and reconciled | The destination state: amounts, working, and nothing needing the user |
| **Populated** | Calculated amounts available | Ranked **by money**; derivation aggregated by default and drillable (`10.04.02` D-04) |
| **Error** | The run could not complete — 09 unreachable, engine version unresolvable | Explicit and blocking. **Nothing is calculated on a degraded read**; a partial run must never present as a complete one |
| **Partial** | Some lines calculated, some blocked or residual | Named and **quantified in money**, both sides. The calculated set proceeds; the blocked set is visible and reaches `10.02.05` — never approximated, never silently held |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The engine's job is to produce correct amounts | The feature's name, and what any royalty system is for | ❌ **REJECTED as the primary framing — the deliverable is the derivation, and the number is a byproduct.** Curve, Exactuals, Reprtoir and every label's internal system already produce correct-ish amounts; none of them can explain one. The reason is structural: their split was reconstructed from memory and their terms were interpreted by the party who owed the money, so the chain ends in a judgement rather than a fact. WeJammin's chain ends in **a split six people confirmed in a room on a date** (`D-18`, `02.02.03`). That is the only differentiator this feature has, and it is destroyed by any optimisation that stores the total and discards the working. **Store the derivation; the number is recomputable from it. Store the number and the derivation is gone forever.** |
| DT-02 | Recoupment belongs in the engine — an unrecouped artist earns nothing, so the amount is zero | Plain reading, and how many label systems genuinely behave | ❌ **REJECTED, and the conflation is a moral failure as much as a modelling one.** An unrecouped artist **earns** their share and **receives** nothing; those are different facts, and collapsing them is exactly how an artist comes to believe their record made no money. The engine computes the earning. `10.03.03` computes payability. Keeping them apart is what lets a payee statement say the true and useful thing — *"you earned £4,200; £4,200 went against your advance; you have £11,800 to go"* — instead of the industry-standard, technically-true, actively-misleading **£0.00**. This is not a schema preference; it is the difference between a statement that informs and one that conceals. |
| DT-03 | Rounding is an implementation detail | Sub-cent amounts are obviously immaterial | ❌ **REJECTED.** A million lines × six parties × a sub-cent remainder is real money with no owner, and every rounding scheme decides who gets it — round-half-up favours whoever is listed first, round-down accumulates a residue the platform silently keeps. The residue is small, it is *someone's*, and a platform that built `10.02.05` on the principle that unattributed money must be visible cannot then quietly pocket rounding dust. It must be **explicit, consistent, disclosed, and reconciled** — the sum of the parts must equal the whole, and where it cannot, the remainder needs a named destination rather than a default. **Sharpened by DT-12**: the premise assumed rounding per line, and that assumption is the entire problem. |
| DT-04 | The engine resolves "the split as of the usage period" — one ledger, one party list, one answer | The surface pass's own Behavior step 2, and how the feature reads to anyone who has not worked a royalty statement | ❌ **REJECTED — there is no "the split", and the singular is the most expensive word in the file.** One song carries **three unrelated ownership records**: the composition split (writers + publishers, work-side, PRS/MLC), the master split (owner + producer points + featured artist, recording-side, DSP/distributor), and the performer allocation (featured and non-featured, performer-side, PPL/SoundExchange — **not a negotiated split at all**, but a society formula). A drummer owns 0% of the composition, 0% of the master, and a real PPL share. `10.02.03` D-16 already forbids *merging* attributions across right types; this is the consequence it could not state from the matcher's side — **the right type does not tag the money, it selects which ledger is even relevant.** Resolve a PPL line against the composition ledger and the platform pays the writers for the drummer's performance: an error that names only real people, reconciles perfectly against itself, and is undetectable forever. And it compounds — the **territory** selects the sub-publishing chain (`10.01.07` D-02) and the **level** selects work vs recording, so the resolution is four-dimensional, not one. A domain expert would have asked "which split?" before reading the second sentence. |
| DT-05 | Reading the split "as of the usage period" (parent `D-02`) is a complete temporal answer | Parent `D-02`, `10.02.04` DT-02 and this file's own `D-03`, all of which say as-of and stop there | ❌ **REJECTED — as-of is valid-time, and valid-time alone cannot reproduce a calculation.** Two clocks are running. *Valid-time* answers "when was this split true?" — Q1 2024, fixed by the usage period, and that is what parent `D-02` pins. *Transaction-time* answers "when did the ledger **learn** it?" — and a dispute resolving in 2026 **retroactively changes what the Q1 2024 split is**. The identical as-of query returns one answer in March and another in April, with nothing having errored and no input having visibly moved. The consequence is fatal to the file's own thesis: **re-running calculation version 1 does not reproduce version 1.** `D-01` says the derivation is the product and the number is recomputable from it — but if the number is not recomputable, the derivation is a receipt rather than a proof, and `10.03.04`'s clawback becomes the difference between two numbers neither of which can be checked. Parent `D-04`'s immutable versioning was built to make deltas computable and this silently unbuilds it. **Both clocks must be pinned** — which upgrades `Q-01` from "does 09 support as-of reads?" to "**is 09's ledger bitemporal?**", a materially heavier ask of another domain that must be made in its heavy form or it will be answered in its light one. |
| DT-06 | The engine is arithmetic, so it needs no versioning and has no history of its own | The name says *engine*; a calculator is not a document, and the surface pass versioned the *calculations* while treating the *calculator* as a constant | ❌ **REJECTED — and the upstream feature already knew this.** `10.02.02` D-04 makes adapters **versioned data applied by statement date**, and D-11 makes parsing **deterministic with no ambient state**, because a source that silently changes format would otherwise retroactively rewrite numbers that have already been paid. The engine has exactly the same exposure and inherited neither control. Whether producer points come off before or after the admin fee is an **ordering decision inside the engine**, not a term; change it to fix a bug and every historical number moves. So: the engine reads **no ambient state** (no clock, no live FX, no "current" split, no "latest" anything), all inputs are **pinned into the version**, and the **engine version is part of the derivation**. Same line + same pinned inputs + same engine version = the identical amount, forever. This is what converts `D-01` from a display promise into a **falsifiable** one: a derivation that re-executes to its own number is a proof; one that cannot is a receipt, and the industry already issues receipts. It also buys idempotency free — a duplicate run cannot produce a different number, so determinism is a concurrency control as well as an audit property. The price is stated honestly: an engine bug fix is a **restatement the platform must own** (`10.03.04` D-04), and it is the easiest lie in the domain to ship as "a correction". |
| DT-07 | Storing a full derivation per party per line is affordable, or alternatively `D-01` must be traded away at DSR volume | `D-01` as written, met with a 400,000-line DSR × 6 parties × a dozen derivation steps ≈ 28.8M facts **per statement** | ✅ **CONFIRMED as the cost, and resolved by a collapse that is not the one that saved matching.** First the bad news, because it must be stated plainly: `10.02.03` D-06 rescued matching by changing the noun — 41,880 lines collapse to 214 identities — and **that collapse does not transfer here.** The identity collapse works because 40,000 lines share one *decision*; the engine faces 40,000 lines that each carry *distinct money*, and money does not collapse. The engine sees the full line count and always will. But a different collapse is available and it is exact rather than approximate: for every line resolving to the same `(source identity, usage period, right type, territory, source)`, the split version, the term versions, the deduction stack and the engine version are **identical** — and what differs is one number. So **the derivation is a function, the line is its argument, and the amount is the application**. Store the resolved parameter set once, with its provenance; every line references it and carries only its own arithmetic. That is precisely what `D-01` claimed — *store the derivation and the number is recomputable* — arriving with a cost that scales, and it only works **because** of DT-06: a parameter set is meaningless unless re-executing it is guaranteed to reproduce the number. The residual risk is real and routed rather than waved away (`Q-07`): the parameter-set count still grows with territories × periods × right types, and `10.03.04`'s restatement cascade re-runs all of it. |
| DT-08 | The derivation can be shown to every party in full — that is what explainability means | `D-01`, taken at face value, and the obvious reading of "show a payee how £4.11 became their £0.62" | ❌ **REJECTED — full disclosure of a full derivation leaks a bilateral commercial relationship, and the promise has to be scoped or abandoned.** The chain that explains a session player's £0.62 necessarily names every other party, their share, and the terms that produced them. Some of that is rightly theirs: **the split is a shared fact they personally confirmed in the room** (`D-18`), and a platform whose thesis is capture-with-everyone-present cannot then treat the agreed split as confidential — that would be the room's whole point, undone. But the deal terms are **bilateral**: the producer's admin-fee arrangement with a label is not the session drummer's business, and disclosing it to establish why a deduction exists would publish a contract to a stranger. The resolution is exactly `10.02.02` D-13's shape, one layer out — that feature parses **the whole file including lines about third parties**, because filtering at parse destroys the reconciliation oracle, and enforces privacy **downstream of the parse**. Here: **compute whole, disclose scoped.** Your amount at full depth; the split you agreed at full depth; a third party's bilateral term as a **named, sized deduction with unreadable parameters** — you see that £0.31 left and why it left, without reading someone else's contract. Computing less would break `D-04`'s reconciliation, which needs every part to sum to the whole. **This is a constraint on the derivation's structure, not a filter applied to its output**, which is why it is the engine's problem and not `10.04.02`'s. |
| DT-09 | Producer points "from artist's share" have a defined base, so the engine applies them like any other term | `10.03.02`'s term list, which enumerates points and demands the base be explicit — and, having demanded it, assumes it can be supplied | ❌ **REJECTED — when the points-payee is inside the share the points are drawn from, the term is self-referential and has no arithmetic answer, and this is the modal modern production deal rather than an exotic case.** Work it: A and B each write 50%; B is also the producer, with 3 points from "artist's share". If artist's share means the combined 100%, points come off the top and B nets 51.5% against A's 48.5%. If it means A's share (because B cannot draw points from themselves), A nets 47% and B nets 53%. If "artist" is a *defined contractual party* — A is the signed artist and B a session writer — the answer is different again. **The three readings differ by real money and the contract does not choose between them, because whoever drafted it never imagined the producer was also a co-writer.** `10.03.02` says points-from-the-top vs points-from-artist's-share "must be explicit; there is no safe default" — this is that requirement discovering it cannot always be satisfied, because the ambiguity is not in the *recording* of the term but in the term's collision with **who the parties turn out to be**. So the engine must **refuse** and say why (`D-12`, `D-16`), not pick the reading that produces a number. `personas.md`'s opening structural fact is that music's defining identity characteristic is the multi-hyphenate and that a model of "user has a role" is wrong for this product; here that fact reaches the arithmetic and breaks it. The engine does not get to resolve it — a human must state the base, with their name on it (`10.03.02` D-03). |
| DT-10 | A split names people, so resolution is a lookup | The mental model behind "read the split from domain 09 and produce an amount per party" | ❌ **REJECTED — a split names *parties*, and `personas.md` states outright that a persona is not always a person: bands, labels, studios, venues and agencies are first-class entities that book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members.** So a 40% share may belong to The Band, which has its own internal distribution rule and its own **membership history** — and the member who left in 2025 is still owed for Q1 2024 usage, which means the recursion needs an as-of read **at every level**, not only the first (parent `D-02` applied recursively, which nobody has said). Real chains go deeper: writer → publisher → sub-publisher → parent company (`10.01.07`), each hop a level of derivation. Two hard consequences fall out. First, **cycles are constructible and not merely theoretical** — band A holds 10% of B while B holds 10% of A, or an entity holds a share of a band one of whose members is that entity; naive recursion never terminates, and resolving it "to N levels" would silently produce a wrong number, so it must be **refused and named** as the ledger defect it is. Second, **whether resolution recurses at all is not the engine's to decide**: a band with its own bank account is a terminal payee and the money stops there; a band that is a bookkeeping fiction passes through to members. Both are real and the difference is a property of **domain 01's entity**, not of this engine (`Q-05`). `10.03.03` Q-02 found the advance half of this ("a band-level advance against member-level earnings — unmodelled"); this is the **split half**, and it was missing entirely. |
| DT-11 | `D-04`'s parts-must-sum-to-whole is an accounting guarantee — reconciliation hygiene | `D-04` as written, framed entirely around rounding residue | ✅ **CONFIRMED and materially re-scoped — it is also the only detector the engine has for a misrecorded or malicious term set, and the two failure directions want opposite treatments.** `10.03.02` states that points-from-the-top vs points-from-artist's-share "must be explicit; there is no safe default" — but a requirement with no enforcement is advice. **Σ(parts) > whole is that enforcement**: splits totalling 100% plus 3 points recorded as "from the top" would pay out 103% of a line, which is either a recording error or a term crafted to over-draw. There is no honest partial answer, because producing one moves money that does not exist — so it is a **hard block**, not a residual. **Σ(parts) < whole is the opposite** and must not be treated as the same defect: the 3% belongs to nobody the platform can name, and the two tempting answers are both forbidden — redistribution invents a fact (`D-15`, and the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` blames the industry for, committed here by the system built to prevent it), silent retention is parent `D-09` (the platform becoming its own black box, which is precisely the condition `10.05.01` sells recovery *from*). The honest treatment is the domain's existing machinery: an **explicit residual, denominated in money, aged and alarmed** at `10.02.05` (D-01, D-05), plus a **finding** to 09 and `10.01.04` — because a 97% ledger against a society that distributes only what is registered may mean the £4.11 was never the whole, and that is a registration story rather than an arithmetic one. Resolves `Q-03`; the residue's ultimate disposition is parent `Q-06` and is not novel to this feature. |
| DT-12 | Rounding needs a policy — largest-remainder to parties, or a named platform account (`Q-02` as posed) | `DT-03`'s conclusion, and `Q-02`, which asked *where does the residue go?* | ✅ **CONFIRMED that the residue is somebody's money — but the question is malformed, and answering it as asked would institutionalise the bug.** `DT-03`'s framing is *a million lines × six parties × a sub-cent remainder*: that quantity **only exists if the engine rounds per line**. Round a £0.0004 line across six parties to the minor unit and every party receives £0.00 — the platform has destroyed the entire amount while reconciling perfectly, 400,000 times per statement. No residue policy repairs that, because the money is gone before the policy is consulted. **The engine must not round at all.** It is exact: decimal arithmetic at a declared fixed precision (never binary float — £0.10 is unrepresentable, and `10.02.02` D-02's reconciliation gate would fail spuriously and unfixably). Rounding happens **exactly once, at the payable boundary**, on a **per-party aggregate** rather than a line, allocated by **largest remainder** so Σ(rounded) = rounded whole exactly — satisfying `D-04` — with ties broken on a stable key derived from party and run, **never list order**, which was `DT-03`'s own stated objection. The residue then collapses from millions of orphaned fractions to **≤ half a minor unit per party per payout run**, and `Q-02`'s "named platform account" option disappears with it — which is a relief, because that option required the platform to hold money, and holding client money is regulated activity (parent `Q-04`). This also quietly resolves a **contradiction between the file's own decisions**: `D-05` itemises deductions and `D-04` requires the parts to sum to the whole, and under per-line rounding those two cannot both hold — rounded itemisations do not reconcile to a rounded total. Exact arithmetic plus one rounding at the aggregate satisfies both, and the rounding step appears in the derivation as its own visible line, because it is arithmetic performed on the payee's money. |
| DT-13 | An unparameterised calculation is still useful — show the raw split and flag the terms unknown (`D-06`) | `D-06`, and `10.03.02` D-05, both of which say *flag, never assume* | ❌ **REJECTED as stated — `D-06` conflates two states that deserve opposite treatments, and the flag is not the protection it appears to be.** *No deal exists* — the self-releasing independent writer — means **the raw split IS the correct answer**, and it should be stated plainly, with confidence, and explicitly not marked as a gap; flagging it teaches the user that the platform's flags mean nothing. *A deal exists and its terms are unrecorded* — the PDF nobody typed in, which `10.03.02` calls the modal case — means **no answer exists at all**, and the raw split is wrong by up to a factor of two while looking authoritative. `D-06` currently buys a flag on a number, and **a number with a warning attached is still a number**: users read numbers and skip warnings, and this platform's entire pitch is that it does not hand people confident figures assembled from missing information. `10.03.03` D-04 reached the identical shape from the advance side — an unrecorded advance makes the position **unknown, not zero**, because an artist who looks recouped and isn't will be clawed back. Same failure, same fix: where a deal is known to exist and its terms are not recorded, **the engine produces no amount** and says what is missing and who can supply it (`D-16`). Distinguishing the two states is the difference between a platform that is honest about its gaps and one that decorates them. |

- Touches **10.08 Statement Disputes & Audit Rights** ([../10.08-statement-disputes-audit-rights.md](../10.08-statement-disputes-audit-rights.md)) — most royalty disputes are term-interpretation disputes wearing an arithmetic costume. A re-executable derivation (`DT-06`) is what makes a dispute about a *number* resolvable without an audit.
- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** ([17-live-booking-settlement/](../../17-live-booking-settlement/)) — a door split after costs against a guarantee is **the same machine** as recoupment against an advance (`10.03.02` DT-03). `DT-12`'s single-rounding-at-the-payable-boundary and `DT-06`'s determinism are the parts most likely to be reinvented differently by a settlement engine written by someone who has never heard of packaging deductions.
- Touches **23 Career, Finance & Business** ([23-career-finance-business/](../../23-career-finance-business/)) — management commission is a rate on receipts; `10.03.02` DT-03's second instance of this engine.
- Rides **Audit Log & Provenance Ledger** (`CX-M19`) — immutability and versioning. `credits-attribution-cx.md` already routed this cross-cut naming domain 10 as a consumer. `DT-06` adds a requirement: the log must pin the **engine version**, not only the inputs.
- Proposes cross-cut **Point-in-Time / As-Of Record Resolution** — parent `D-02`, **upgraded by `DT-05` to bitemporal** (valid-time + transaction-time). Serves 09, 10, 11, 12, 23. The upgrade is not cosmetic: as-of alone does not make a calculation reproducible, and every consumer of this cross-cut inherits that defect.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **The derivation is stored; the number is derived from it** | `DT-01`. Storing the total discards the platform's only advantage in this sub-domain. Made **falsifiable** by `D-09` and **affordable** by `D-10`. |
| D-02 | **Recoupment is not in the engine** | `DT-02`. Earning and payability are different facts; merging them is how artists are told they made nothing. One-way (CX R-01). |
| D-03 | The split is read **as of the usage period** | Parent `D-02`. Current-state resolution silently pays the wrong party. **Necessary and not sufficient — see `D-08`.** |
| D-04 | **Rounding is explicit, disclosed and reconciled** — the parts must sum to the whole | `DT-03`. Rounding dust is somebody's money; a platform built on `10.02.05`'s principle cannot pocket it. **Where it happens is `D-15`; what it detects is `D-14`.** |
| D-05 | Deductions are **itemised, never netted** | A compounded effective rate is why no writer can explain their own royalty. Consistent with `10.01.07` D-04. Reconcilable with `D-04` only because of `D-15` (`DT-12`). |
| D-06 | Unknown deal terms are **flagged**, never assumed to be zero or standard | Assuming a term invents a fact — the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` names. **Split into two states by `D-16` — a flag on a number is not enough.** |
| D-07 | The right type, territory and level **select the ledger** — there is no single "the split" | `DT-04`. Composition, master and performer are three ownership records for one song. Resolving the wrong one pays real people, reconciles perfectly, and is invisible forever. Extends `10.02.03` D-16 from tagging to selection. |
| D-08 | Resolution is **bitemporal**: valid-time (usage period) **and** transaction-time (what the ledger knew) are both pinned into the calculation version | `DT-05`. As-of alone means version 1 does not reproduce version 1, which makes `D-01` a receipt and parent `D-04`'s deltas uncheckable. Upgrades `Q-01`. |
| D-09 | The engine is **deterministic and versioned**: no ambient state, all inputs pinned, engine version part of the derivation. Same line + same inputs + same version = the identical amount, forever | `DT-06`. The direct analogue of `10.02.02` D-11/D-04. Converts `D-01` from a display promise into a proof, and makes duplicate runs idempotent. Price: an engine bug fix is a restatement the platform must **own** (`10.03.04` D-04). |
| D-10 | The derivation is a **resolved parameter set** keyed on `(identity, usage period, right type, territory, source)`; lines reference it and carry only their own arithmetic | `DT-07`. `10.02.03` D-06's identity collapse does not transfer — money is per-line — but the parameters do. The derivation is the function; the line is its argument. Depends on `D-09`. |
| D-11 | The derivation is **computed whole and disclosed scoped** | `DT-08`. Full disclosure publishes a bilateral contract to a stranger; computing less breaks `D-04`. Your amount and the split you agreed at full depth; a third party's term as a named, sized deduction with unreadable parameters. `10.02.02` D-13's shape. |
| D-12 | A **self-referential term has no arithmetic answer** and is refused, never defaulted | `DT-09`. Producer points from a share the producer is inside. `10.03.02` demands an explicit base; the multi-hyphenate is where that demand cannot be met, and it is the modal production deal. |
| D-13 | Party resolution is **recursive through entities**, with an as-of read **at every level**; **cycles are refused and named** | `DT-10`. A split names parties, and `personas.md` makes bands and labels entities. Resolving "to N levels" produces a silently wrong number. Terminal-vs-pass-through is domain 01's property (`Q-05`). |
| D-14 | **Σ(parts) > whole is a hard block**; **Σ(parts) < whole is a visible residual** routed to `10.02.05` with a finding to 09/`10.01.04`. Never redistributed, never silently retained | `DT-11`. Resolves `Q-03`. The two directions are not one defect: over-subscription has no honest partial answer; under-subscription has an honest one that is neither invention (`D-15`) nor a black box (parent `D-09`). |
| D-15 | **The engine never rounds.** Exact decimal at a declared fixed precision (≥9 dp), never binary float. Rounding happens **once**, at the payable boundary, on **per-party aggregates**, by **largest remainder**, tie-broken on a stable key — never list order | `DT-12`. Resolves `Q-02`. Per-line rounding destroys sub-cent lines entirely and no residue policy repairs it. Bounds the residue at ≤ half a minor unit per party per payout run, and is what lets `D-04` and `D-05` both hold. |
| D-16 | **"No deal" and "deal exists, terms unrecorded" are different states.** The first produces the raw split as the correct answer, stated plainly. The second produces **no amount at all** | `DT-13`. Sharpens `D-06`: a number with a warning attached is still a number. Same shape as `10.03.03` D-04 (unknown, not zero) reached from the advance side. |
| D-17 | A multi-work recording consumes its valid **as-of Recording→Work allocation** from Domain 09 before resolving each separate work ledger; the engine never invents or repairs allocation | CQ-07 Option B. Disjoint spans may have an editable Domain 09 duration proposal; temporal-overlap mashups require declarant-confirmed positive exact weights totaling 100%. Missing/invalid allocation blocks calculation rather than allowing duration, normalization, equal shares, inferred remainder, or merged ledgers. |

| Q-01 | **Is domain 09's ledger bitemporal?** (Upgraded from "does 09 support as-of reads?" by `DT-05` — parent `Q-01`/`Q-05`.) `D-03` needs valid-time; `D-08` needs transaction-time as well, or no calculation is reproducible and `D-01` is decorative. **If 09 is current-state, every historical calculation is silently wrong. | Q-02 | ~~Rounding policy: where does the residue go?~~ | User | ✅ **Resolved this pass** — `DT-12`, `D-15`. The question was malformed: `DT-03`'s residue only exists because of per-line rounding. The engine is exact and never rounds; rounding happens once, at the payable boundary, on per-party aggregates, by largest remainder. Residue is ≤ half a minor unit per party per payout run. The "named platform account" option disappears — and it required holding client money (parent `Q-04`) anyway. |
| Q-03 | ~~If splits sum to <100% for a period, who owns the gap?~~ | User | ✅ **Resolved this pass** — `DT-11`, `D-14`. Neither redistribute (invention, `D-15`) nor silently hold (parent `D-09`). The shortfall is an explicit money-denominated residual at `10.02.05`, aged and alarmed, plus a finding to 09 and `10.01.04`. **The residue's ultimate disposition is parent `Q-06`** (money nobody can claim) and is not novel to this feature. |
| Q-04 | **Decimal precision**: `D-15` sets a ≥9 dp floor from the arithmetic (at 6 dp, truncation across a 400,000-line DSR × 6 parties ≈ £1.20/statement — material; at 9 dp ≈ £0.0012 — immaterial). The exact digit count must be **fixed permanently**, because changing it restates every historical calculation. Interacts with the locked Supabase constraint's numeric handling. | Agent | `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Q-05 | **Does a domain 01 entity declare itself a terminal payee or a pass-through?** (`DT-10`, `D-13`.) A band with a bank account is paid as the band; a band that is a bookkeeping fiction distributes to members. Both are real, the engine cannot infer which, and guessing pays the wrong parties. Also needs **entity membership readable as-of**, or a departed member is silently unpaid for usage they owned. Same family as `10.03.03` Q-02 (the advance half). | User | `/create-prd-architecture` |
| Q-06 | ~~**Is the agreed split fully visible to every party in it?** (`DT-08`, `D-11`.) A session player seeing every co-writer's percentage is a disclosure decision with a real downside, and `D-11` currently asserts the permissive answer.~~ | User | ✅ **Resolved — yes, for every party *in* the ledger; `D-11`'s permissive reading holds.** [09.02.01](../../09-rights-ownership/09.02-split-capture-agreements/09.02.01-moment-of-creation-split-capture.md) `D-09`: *"**Ledger parties see the whole ledger; fee parties see its shape, not its percentages**"*, resting on ratified [09.01.02](../../09-rights-ownership/09.01-rights-registry/09.01.02-ownership-ledger-validation.md) `D-01` (*"Consent is to the **whole ledger**, never to a single row"*). The session-player downside is answered by the carve-out rather than by narrowing the split: a bought-out fee party is **not** a party to the split and sees the ledger's shape without percentages — that half is queued for owner ratification at `09.02.01` Q-06. |
