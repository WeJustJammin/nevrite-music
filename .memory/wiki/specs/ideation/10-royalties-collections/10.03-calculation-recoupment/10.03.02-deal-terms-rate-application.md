# Feature: Deal Terms → Royalty Rate Application

> **Number**: 10.03.02
> **Parent**: [Royalty Calculation & Recoupment](./10.03-calculation-recoupment-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate
> **MoSCoW**: **Must** — the ungated half (parent D-03); ratified with all 195 Musts (D-20)

## Overview

The **executable interpretation of a contract**: turning "18% of net receipts escalating to 20% after 100,000 units, less a 25% packaging deduction, with 3 producer points payable from artist's share, recoupable against a £40,000 advance" into rules a calculation engine can run against a royalty line.

The sweep proposed a "Royalty Calculation Engine" with no parameters. But an engine with no terms computes only the raw split, which is correct for a self-released independent writer and wrong for everyone who has ever signed anything. The terms *are* the calculation — the arithmetic is trivial and the interpretation is where every royalty dispute in the history of the industry has lived.

**The problem it solves, for whom**: `personas.md`'s Musician names their accidental worst case as *"signs a split sheet they don't understand and discovers years later they gave away their publishing"*. That failure is not the signing — it is that nothing between the signature and the money ever expressed the deal in a form the signer could check. This feature is that form. It is the first place in a musician's career where the sentence *"your headline rate is 18%; after the deductions recorded on your deal your effective rate on this line is 11.25%"* becomes computable rather than rhetorical.

**Why it is in the Must set** (parent D-03): it needs no accreditation, no society, no counterparty and no permission. A musician who has a statement and a contract can be told the truth about both today.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | **Read-only as payee** (the modal case) · **Config as payer** (see D-12) | *As payee* — sees their own deal **as the platform has recorded it**, expressed in plain terms and, critically, checkable against the paper they signed: each term shows its value, its author, the date it was recorded, and the clause it was extracted from (`23.04.02`). They cannot edit it; a payee editing the terms that determine their own payment is the obvious integrity failure. What they gain is the thing almost no artist has: their own deal, legible, with its consequences computed in front of them — including the **effective rate** (D-11), a number they have never been shown by anyone who owed them money. *As payer* — a Musician who fronted a bandmate's studio bill or paid a session player against points is the payer on that deal and configures its terms there, with identical obligations to the Producer row. `personas.md`'s multi-hyphenate is not a Musician **or** a payer; they are both, on different deals, in the same afternoon. |
| Producer | **Config** (on deals where they are the payer) | Records terms on deals they pay out under — their own points, an advance they fronted, an admin fee, the base those points are calculated on. `personas.md` names *"assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention"* as their worst intentional behaviour; this feature is where that act would be committed, at leisure, in a settings screen, months after the room emptied, against a term the payee has never seen. ## Behavior

A deal term is a **rule with a temporal scope**: *(what it applies to, what it does to the money, in what order, when it is in effect, who it favours, and who said so)*. Six dimensions, and every royalty dispute in history is an argument about one of them.

### The term taxonomy (closed — D-06)

The platform models a **fixed set of term types**. It is not a formula language (DT-04). Where a real contract contains a term outside the set, the deal is marked `partially-modelled` and every calculation beneath it is flagged incomplete — the platform says what it cannot do rather than approximating it.

| Term type | What it carries | Real-world examples (**illustrative — never defaults, D-05**) |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Which figure the rate multiplies | Net receipts (as received) · net receipts at source · gross receipts · PPD · SRLP. The choice changes the answer by up to a factor of two. Required; never inferred (`09.02.02` D-01 reaches the same conclusion for points). |
| **Rate — percentage** | A percentage of the base | 18% of net receipts; 20% admin fee; 15% distribution fee |
| **Rate — fixed per unit** | An amount per copy/stream/unit, not a percentage | US statutory mechanical 9.1¢ per copy under 5:00. **A percentage-only taxonomy cannot express a mechanical royalty at all** — the omission would silently exclude an entire income type |
| **Rate — derived** | A rate expressed *relative to another rate* (D-16) | "75% of the otherwise applicable rate" for foreign sales; "50% for rest of world"; a producer's all-in points as a fraction of the artist's rate |
| **Escalation** | Threshold value · threshold **basis** · **retroactivity** · **counting scope** · reset rule (D-08) | 18% → 20% after 100,000 US royalty-bearing units, prospective, resetting per album |
| **Deduction — percentage** | A percentage removed, with its **position in the order** | Packaging 25% (CD-era, still live in catalogue contracts); free goods 15%; new-media reduction |
| **Deduction — fixed** | A fixed amount removed, with its **position in the order** | £2,000/quarter distribution fee. **Order-sensitive in a way percentages are not** — see DT-05 |
| **Points** | Count · base · **from whose share** · recoupability · reversion | 3 points, all-in (from artist's share) vs 3 points from the top |
| **Reserve** | Percentage withheld · **liquidation schedule** (D-10) | 20% held against returns, liquidated over 4 quarters. **A timing term, not a deduction** |
| **Recoupability** | Which costs recoup against which income, at what rate, cross-collateralised with what | `10.03.03` owns the balance; the *rule* is a term here (CX-04) |
| **Cap** | A ceiling on an aggregate | US controlled composition: 75% of statutory, capped at 10 songs per album (DT-07) |
| **Sunset / decay** | A rate that steps down on a schedule after the term ends | Management: 20% during term → 10% for 2 years post-term → 0 (`23.06.02`) |
| **Scope** | Deal × work/master × income type × territory × period (D-09) | "US only"; "master royalties only"; "tracks 1–12 of *Nightjar*" |
| **`unrepresentable`** | A free-text record of a term the taxonomy cannot express, with its clause citation | The escape hatch. Marks the deal `partially-modelled`; flags every calculation beneath it incomplete. **Required — a taxonomy without one lies by omission** (D-06) |

### The two clocks (D-04, bitemporal)

Every term carries **two independent dates**, and conflating them is a class of silent error:

- **Effective from/to** (*valid time*) — when the term governs money. Selected by the **usage period**, never by today. Parent D-02; `10.01.07` D-02's problem again.
- **Recorded at** (*transaction time*) — when the platform learned it, and who told it.

The consequence is the feature's least obvious and most disruptive fact: **recording a deal is not a setup step, it is a restatement event** (DT-12, D-15). A term typed in today with effect from 2019 is not configuration — it is a correction to six years of arithmetic, cascading through `10.03.04`.

### Order is a term (D-07)

A term set is an **ordered pipeline**, not a bag. The order is itself recorded, authored and visible, because contracts specify it and it changes the money:

- *"18% of net receipts less a £2,000 quarterly distribution fee"* — fee against the base (`(£10,000 − £2,000) × 18% = £1,440`, a payment) or against the royalty (`£10,000 × 18% − £2,000 = −£200`, a debt). Same eleven words.
- *"escalates after 100,000 units"* — shipped units, or **royalty-bearing** units net of 15% free goods? 100,000 shipped is 85,000 royalty-bearing. Two nearly identical clauses; two points of difference on every subsequent unit, forever.

No royalty system the author is aware of models the order as data. All of them hard-code one and inherit whichever contract the first engineer happened to read.

### The platform's position — modest and explicit (D-02)

**It applies terms as recorded; it does not interpret the contract.** A human decides what "net receipts" means in this agreement and records it, with their name on it. The platform is a calculator with a memory, not a lawyer — pretending otherwise is a liability surface of exactly the kind `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 refused elsewhere, and `23.04.02`'s D-01 (*"calibration over accuracy; every term cites its clause and requires confirmation"*) reaches the same refusal from the document side.

Three things this position does **not** forbid, because they are arithmetic on recorded facts rather than judgements about a contract:

1. **The effective rate** (D-11) — *"headline 18%, effective 11.25% on this line"*. A derivation, not an opinion.
2. **A statutory-floor flag** (D-18) — the statutory mechanical rate is a published number; noting that a recorded term sits below it is a comparison, not advice.
3. **The alternative order's number** — where a contract genuinely does not specify order, showing both outcomes is disclosure. Which one *runs* is the recorder's attributed choice (Q-04).

What it forbids absolutely: characterising a term as fair, unfair, standard, above-market or predatory (DT-09). That is advice, the platform has no benchmark data, and it is the exact liability D-02 exists to refuse.

### Worked example — illustrative, not a template

**Recorded deal** (author: `producer@…`, recorded 2024-01-08, source: *Nightjar Recording Agreement* §7.2–7.6 via `23.04.02` extraction, each term confirmed):
base = net receipts as received · packaging 25%, applied **before** the rate · rate 18%, escalating to 20% after 100,000 **royalty-bearing** US units, **prospective** · free goods 15% of shipped · points 3, **all-in** (from artist's share) · advance £40,000, recoupable at 100% from master royalties only.

**Line** (from `10.02.03`): £10,000.00 net receipts · 12,000 units shipped · US · usage period Q1 2024.

| # | Step | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scope + as-of: master income, US, Q1 2024 → select the term-set version effective 2024-03-31 | version 2 of 4 |
| 2 | Base: net receipts | £10,000.00 |
| 3 | Ordered deduction: packaging 25%, before rate | £7,500.00 |
| 4 | Threshold test: royalty-bearing units = 12,000 × 85% = **10,200**; cumulative = 84,102 + 10,200 = **94,302** < 100,000 | rate **18%** (prospective) |
| 5 | Rate: £7,500.00 × 18% | **£1,350.00** all-in |
| 6 | Derived term: points 3 of 18 all-in → 3/18 of the all-in royalty | **£225.00** producer · **£1,125.00** artist share |
| 7 | Split as-of Q1 2024 (domain 09) — never today's ledger (parent D-02) | £1,125.00 across the ledger as it stood 2024-01-01..2024-03-31 |
| 8 | **Effective rate disclosed** (D-11): £1,125.00 ÷ £10,000.00 | **11.25%** against a headline of 18% |
| 9 | Coverage declaration (D-08): unit counter drew on **3 of 5** registered sources (`10.02.01`) | counter published as a **lower bound**, not a fact |
| 10 | → `10.03.03` nets against the £40,000 advance; → `10.04.02` states each step with its clause and author | |

Step 9 is the discipline the domain's D-04 demands: the count that decides the rate is only as complete as the statements ingested, and a bare number would assert a completeness the platform does not have.

### Happy Path

1. **A deal exists.** Its agreement is domain 09's (`09.02.02` for producer points); its signed document is domain 23's (`23.04.01`). Neither models it as an executable rule (DT-01).
2. **Terms are proposed**, either by `23.04.02`'s extraction — each proposed term carrying **the clause it came from** — or typed manually, which is first-class and not a fallback (`23.04.02` D-02 reaches this independently).
3. **A human with payer standing on this deal confirms each term individually** (D-12), choosing the base, the order, the escalation's basis and retroactivity, and from whose share the points come. Nothing is defaulted (D-05). Terms the taxonomy cannot express are recorded as `unrepresentable` with their citation, and the deal becomes `partially-modelled` (D-06).
4. **The term set is versioned** with `effective from/to` and `recorded at`, stamped with its author (D-04). If `effective from` precedes today, the platform states plainly that this is a restatement and shows its scope *before* it is committed: *"this will restate 9 periods and change 3 payments already made"* (D-15, `10.03.04` D-05).
5. **Every affected party is notified in plain language, with the cause attached** (D-03, `10.03.04` D-02): *"Your producer recorded a 25% packaging deduction on Nightjar, effective from 2019-04-01"* — never *"your royalties were adjusted"*. Where an affected party has no account, the term is stamped **unwitnessed** (D-13).
6. **The terms are visible to every party they affect** — value, author, date, clause, and their effect on the last calculated line.
7. **`10.03.01` applies the term set in effect for the usage period** — not for today — in the recorded order.
8. **The payee statement (`10.04.02`) shows the term, its effect, its source clause and the effective rate.** This is where the feature's value is actually delivered; everything above it is plumbing.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **"Net receipts" is undefined in the contract** | Genuinely ambiguous, and the ambiguity is worth a lot of money. **The most litigated phrase in music** | Recorded as an interpretation with an author and a clause citation — never resolved silently by the platform (D-02). The payee sees *whose* reading is running |
| **Term recorded incorrectly** | Every calculation under it is wrong, plausibly, and reconciles perfectly against itself. The worst class of error in the domain: nothing fails | Correction → new version → `10.03.04` restatement + clawback. Never an edit (D-14) |
| **Escalation threshold spans periods and sources** | The counter is cumulative across every period within the term's **counting scope**, and its accuracy is bounded by ingested source coverage (`10.02.01`). A missing source **under-counts**, triggers the escalation **late**, and **underpays** — and the result reconciles perfectly against itself, so nothing detects it | The counter with its coverage declared: *"94,302 royalty-bearing US units counted from 3 of 5 registered sources — threshold 100,000 not reached **on counted data**"*. Never a bare number. D-08; parent D-04, D-15 |
| **Escalation retroactivity unspecified** | Prospective (escalated rate on units above the threshold) vs retroactive (escalated rate on all units from unit one) differ by the entire threshold's worth of units. Both are standard clauses | A required, attributed choice at recording time — no default exists and inventing one invents a fact (D-05, D-08) |
| **Escalation basis: shipped vs royalty-bearing units** | 100,000 shipped is 85,000 royalty-bearing at 15% free goods. One reading escalates, the other does not | The basis is a recorded term, shown with the counter. DT-05 |
| **Threshold denominated in currency, income arriving in 8 currencies** | Counting a "£50,000 of net receipts" threshold against USD/EUR/JPY lines requires FX conversion — and `10.02.04` D-01 holds that the source amount is the fact and the conversion is a **view**. So *whether the escalation has triggered* becomes FX-dependent: a rate move flips it | The trigger is reported **as a view, with its FX basis and date**, never as a settled fact. See Q-08 |
| **Points from artist's share vs from the top** | Changes who pays the producer, and by exactly the producer's points. All-in: artist nets £1,125. From the top: artist nets £1,350 and the payer funds £225. Constantly disputed | Must be explicit; there is no safe default (`09.02.02` D-01 agrees from the agreement side) |
| **Sum of points exceeds the artist's share** | 5 producers × 4 all-in points against a 16-point artist rate = a **negative artist share**. Arithmetically representable, commercially impossible, and it means a term is wrong | **Blocked and named** — never paid as a negative and never silently floored at zero (D-17). *"The recorded points (20) exceed the recorded artist rate (16). One of these is wrong."* |
| **Order of operations unspecified in the contract** | `(£10,000 − £2,000) × 18% = £1,440` vs `£10,000 × 18% − £2,000 = −£200`. A payment or a debt, from the same clause | Both outcomes disclosed; the recorder picks and is attributed. Order is a term (D-07). Whether the platform *should* pick is Q-04 |
| **Two authors record contradictory terms for one scope** | The artist typed their understanding of §7.2; the producer typed theirs. Both are honest; they differ by 7% | **Both retained. Neither wins. Calculation is held** — the platform does not adjudicate a contract (D-02). → `10.08`. **But `10.08` is MoSCoW `Could` and this is `Must`** — at launch there is no adjudication path and the money simply stops. See Q-05 |
| **No contract exists at all** | The handshake deal. Extremely common between a musician and a producer, and the modal case for `personas.md`'s Musician | Raw split, terms flagged **unknown** — never assumed standard (`10.03.01` D-06, D-05) |
| **Contract exists as a PDF nobody typed in** | The genuinely modal case | **No amount is produced.** The known-but-unrecorded deal makes a raw split misleading; the gap names the missing terms and their owner without pricing an answer (`10.03.01` D-16). |
| **A term the taxonomy cannot express** | *"The rate is reduced by 50% for sales through record clubs in Japan in any quarter in which the artist has not delivered"*. Real contracts contain these | Recorded as `unrepresentable` with its clause; deal → `partially-modelled`; **every calculation beneath it flagged incomplete** (D-06). The platform states what it cannot do |
| **Producer records terms favouring themselves** | `personas.md`'s named anti-persona behaviour, executed here, months later, alone. See DT-02 | Every term carries an author, a timestamp and a clause; every change reaching someone's money notifies them with its cause. **This is weaker than counter-signature and must be named as weaker** (Q-02) |
| **The affected payee has no account** | The modal payee — session players and independent producers are overwhelmingly not on the platform (`02.03` unclaimed shells). **D-03's visibility mitigation has an audience of nobody, in the common case.** The feature's only real guard evaporates exactly where it is needed | Term stamped **unwitnessed** permanently. On the day they claim the account, the first thing their statement says is: *"this term was recorded by X on 2024-03-04, while you had no account and could not see it"* (D-13). Not a fix — an honest record |
| **The recorder is also the payee** | A producer holding points who is *also* a co-writer configures a term affecting their own writer share. D-03's visibility guard points at the person who authored the term | Visibility is scoped to **affected parties other than the author**. Where that set is empty, the term is `unwitnessed` and says so (D-13) |
| **Term changes mid-usage-period** | Old usage, old term. A term effective from 2024-02-15 governs February 15 onward; a Q1 line spans both | The line is **apportioned across the term versions covering its usage period**, each portion carrying its own version and derivation. Bitemporal; parent D-02 |
| **Term backdated on entry** | Onboarding an artist's back catalogue means typing in deals with effect from years ago. **`10.03.04`'s "unbounded restatement cascade" (its Q-03) is triggered not by an exceptional event but by the ordinary act of signing up** | Scope stated **before commit**: *"9 periods, 3 payments already made, net −£240.18"*. Never a silent bulk rewrite. DT-12, D-15 |
| **Deal's term expires, income keeps arriving** | Two different clocks that everyone conflates: **the term of the deal** (when new works enter it) and **the term of the royalty obligation** (how long the deal pays on works already in it — usually life of copyright). Conflating them either stops paying someone forever or pays them forever | Recorded as two scopes. Expiry of one does not imply the other (D-09) |
| **Sunset / post-term decay** | Management commission steps 20% → 10% → 0 over 4 years after the term. It is a rate on a schedule, not an on/off switch | Modelled as a scheduled rate; the payee sees the step dates. `23.06.02` |
| **Reserve never liquidated** | 20% withheld against returns, "liquidated over 4 quarters" — and then nothing arrives. Modelled as a deduction, **the money simply vanishes and no event ever fires**. This is precisely the condition `10.05.01` sells recovery *from*, manufactured by the platform (D-09) | A reserve creates a **scheduled expectation** with a clock. Unliquidated at its due date → aged, alarmed, money-denominated, surfaced in `10.09`. D-10 |
| **Controlled composition clause (US)** | A term in a **master** deal (75% of statutory, capped at 10 songs/album) that reduces **publishing** income arriving on a wholly different statement from a wholly different body. It crosses the master/publishing boundary the domain map treats as clean | Term scope is `(deal × work/master × income type × territory × period)` — a master-deal term **can** be scoped to publishing income (D-09). A per-domain scoping model cannot express this at all. DT-07 |
| **Derived rate whose base rate escalated** | "Foreign sales at 75% of the otherwise applicable rate" + an escalation 18% → 20% ⇒ the foreign rate silently moves 13.5% → 15%. Stored as a value, it does not. Stored as a relation, it does | Derived terms stored as relations, never resolved values (D-16). The derivation is shown: *"13.5% = 75% × 18%"* |
| **Fixed per-unit rate on a percentage-only model** | A US statutory mechanical is 9.1¢ per copy — not a percentage of anything | The taxonomy carries fixed-per-unit rates as a first-class type (D-06). A percentage-only model would silently drop an entire income class |
| **Recorded term below a published statutory floor** | A controlled-composition rate recorded at 50% of statutory where the territory's floor is higher; or a term assigning a writer share that is inalienable in that territory | **Flagged as a factual comparison** — *"this rate is below the published statutory rate for this territory"* — and **still applied as recorded**. The platform does not refuse, and does not advise (D-18, D-02). See Q-09 |
| **Rate outside 0–100%** | Typo: `18` entered where `0.18` was meant, or `180`. A malicious or fat-fingered rate | Rejected at recording with the value echoed: *"a rate of 180% of net receipts is not a rate — did you mean 18%?"* Any single rate outside `0 ≤ r ≤ 100` is refused. Note that a *stack* of valid terms can still legitimately compound to a near-total deduction — that is not invalid, it is a real contract, and D-11's effective rate is how the payee finds out |
| **Effective-from precedes the deal's own start date** | A term governing money before the agreement existed | Rejected at recording with both dates shown. A validation, not a judgement |
| **Two deals cover the same work** | A master deal, a separate producer agreement, and a distribution deal all bear on one line. The terms stack — and **the stack order across deals is nowhere in any of the three contracts** | The cross-deal order is itself a recorded, authored decision (D-07), flagged as an interpretation because no clause supplies it. This is the ordering problem one level up and nobody writes it down |
| **Deal counterparty is an entity, not a person** | Terms payable to a Band or a label (`personas.md`: bands are first-class entities, domain 01). Two rule layers — the deal's terms produce the entity's money, then a *second*, unrelated rule distributes it to members | Both layers modelled and both shown. Same family as `10.03.03` Q-02 (band-level advance against member-level earnings) |
| **Confidential terms** | A producer's points are frequently NDA'd from the other producers. But under an **all-in** rate every points-holder is drawing from one pool, so each is *materially affected* by the others' points — which D-03 says makes them visible | A direct collision between D-03 and standard industry confidentiality. Unresolved; see Q-06 |
| **A third party records terms on someone else's deal** | A manager entering their artist's deals is normal practice — and is an arbitrary money-alteration primitive granted to a non-party | Standing derives from a recorded payer or administration relationship on **that specific deal** (D-12), not from a persona or a platform role. See Q-07 |
| **Term edited while a calculation run is in flight** | Race. Mutating the in-flight run produces a calculation belonging to no version | The run **completes on version N**; the new version produces a fresh calculation; a restatement reconciles them (parent D-04, CX-01 synthesis Q5). Never a mid-run mutation |
| **Term edited while a payout run is in flight** | Worse race — paying a figure known to be restating | The payout run **holds**. Paying and clawing back is strictly worse than a delay (CX-05 synthesis Q5) |
| **Two authors editing one term simultaneously** | Last-write-wins would silently destroy an authored term and leave the surviving author's name on a decision they did not make | Optimistic concurrency: the second author is shown the first's version and re-authors on top of it. A term's author must always be the person who actually chose its value |
| **Term save fails midway** | A half-saved term set is a deal in a state no contract describes, and it will compute | A term-set version commits atomically or not at all. There is no partially-saved version; there is only the previous version |
| **Negative line arrives (`10.02.02`)** | A return or a source adjustment produces negative receipts. Applying a 25% packaging deduction to a negative base **increases** it | Terms apply to negative lines with the same order and the same sign convention; the derivation is shown and round-trips honestly → `10.03.04` |
| **Line has no territory (`10.02.04`)** | A territory-scoped term cannot resolve. Guessing pays the wrong rate, plausibly | Held with the gap named — never a best guess (`10.03.01`'s `Broken` state, D-15) |
| **Line's work is outside the deal's scope** | An album deal covering tracks 1–12; a line arrives for a track from a different record | The term set does not apply. Raw split, terms flagged unknown for that line — not the nearest deal's terms |
| **Deal recorded, then the underlying contract is amended by a side letter** | Real deals are chains: original + amendments + side letters (`23.04.01` DT-03). Live terms are a **computed view over the chain**, not the terms of one document | A new term-set version citing the amending instrument. Same shape as `23.04.02`'s chain handling — and the same restatement consequence (D-15) |

### States

**Term-record lifecycle**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No deal recorded | Nothing exists for this work | Raw split applies — **stated plainly, not assumed**. *"No deal recorded for Nightjar; amounts below are the raw split"* (D-05) |
| Partially modelled | Some terms recorded; or an `unrepresentable` term present | **The most dangerous state, because it looks complete.** Named as incomplete on every calculation beneath it, with the missing/unexpressed term shown (D-06) |
| Recorded | Full term set entered and confirmed | Plain-language view: each term with value, author, date, clause, and its effect on the last calculated line |
| Unwitnessed | Recorded, but no affected party other than the author holds an account | Stamped, permanently. Surfaced to the payee the day they claim the account (D-13) |
| In effect | Within `effective from/to` for the line's usage period | Applied by `10.03.01`, in the recorded order |
| Contested | Two authors recorded contradictory terms, or a party disputes the recording | Both versions retained; **calculation held**; affected amounts held → `10.08`. The platform picks neither (Q-05) |
| Blocked | Term set is arithmetically impossible (points > artist share; rate out of range) | **Named, never computed.** *"The recorded points (20) exceed the recorded artist rate (16)."* (D-17) |
| Obligation live, term expired | The deal's intake term ended; the royalty obligation on works already in it has not | Both scopes shown separately. Still paying, and saying why (D-09) |
| Expired | Both scopes past | No longer applied; **historical periods unaffected** |
| Superseded | Terms corrected | New version; old retained permanently; restatement scope shown before commit (D-14, D-15, `10.03.04`) |

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Term set + as-of resolution in progress | The deal name and the period being resolved — never a bare spinner over money |
| Empty | No deal is recorded **and none is known to exist** | Not a blank page: the raw-split amount is the honest result. *"£412.06 across 3 works is calculated on raw splits; no deal is recorded."* |
| Unparameterised | A deal is known to exist but its terms are unrecorded | No amount. *"Nightjar's deal is unrecorded. Record the terms to calculate this entitlement."* |
| Populated | Term set resolved for the period | The ordered pipeline, step by step, each step showing its term, author and clause; **headline rate and effective rate side by side** (D-11) |
| Partial | Some terms resolved; one or more `unrepresentable`, or a scope unresolvable (no territory on the line) | The resolved steps shown, the gap named in place, and the result labelled **incomplete** — not rendered as a final number (D-06, D-15) |
| Error | Term set fails to resolve for the period (ledger gap, contradictory versions, blocked arithmetic) | The failure named in the pipeline at the step it occurred, with the offending term and its author. **No fallback number.** A wrong royalty that looks right is worse than no royalty |

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |

| DT-01 | Deal terms are contract data and belong in domain 23's vault or domain 09's agreements — this is not a domain 10 feature | Node Classification Gate; 23 explicitly has "Deal & Contract Vault with Option/Reversion Alerts" and 09 has "Producer Agreements" | ❌ **REJECTED, with a real boundary rather than a land grab.** The **contract** is 23's (the document, its dates, its alerts, its extracted-and-confirmed key terms — `23.04.02`) and the **agreement** is 09's (who agreed what — `09.02.02`). Neither models the term as an **executable rule applied to a royalty line as of a usage period** — 23 stores a PDF, extracts terms with citations, and reminds you about the option window; 09 records that a producer has 3 points and that the base is a required term (`09.02.02` D-01). Turning "3 points from artist's share of net receipts" into arithmetic that runs against £4.11 of PRS income, in a recorded order, as of Q1 2024, is collections work and exists nowhere else on the map. **But three domains now touch one deal**, which is precisely the multiple-owners failure this platform exists to prevent — hence parent `Q-02`, domain `Q-08`, and Q-01 below. The honest position: the rule lives here, the document does not, and somebody must ratify that. |
| DT-02 | Whoever administers the deal records its terms — that is just how administration works | The obvious operational model, and how every label system does it | ❌ **REJECTED as unsafe by default.** The person recording the terms is the person who **pays** under them, and `personas.md` already names their worst intentional behaviour as "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention". This feature is that act's ideal venue: months after the room emptied, in a settings screen, against a term the payee has never seen and cannot check. The platform's whole pitch is that facts get captured while everyone is present and friendly — and then this feature lets one party author the money rule alone, later, in private. **Mitigation is visibility, not permission**: every term carries an author, is visible in plain language to every party it affects, and a change to a term that reduces someone's money must reach them. That is weaker than counter-signature (Q-02) and it is the minimum — **and DT-11 shows that even this minimum is inoperative in the modal case.** |
| DT-03 | This is royalty-specific machinery | The feature sits in the royalties domain and speaks the royalties dialect | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the most useful structural finding in the sub-domain.** "Compute money from receipts under agreed terms, net against what was fronted" is **not a royalty concept**. A live settlement is a guarantee versus a door percentage after costs, recouping the guarantee — which is an advance and a rate, in different clothes (17.03, 17.09). Management commission is a rate on receipts with a post-term sunset (23.06.02). A publishing admin fee is a rate. Marketplace and service fee schedules are rates (05, 13, 14). Agency commission is a rate (17.08). **They are one engine**: base, ordered deductions, thresholds, derived rates, recoupment against a float, all applied to receipts as of a date. Building it four times guarantees four dialects, four rounding policies and four sets of bugs — and the live-settlement one will be written by someone who has never heard of packaging deductions. Proposed as the **Deal Terms & Settlement Rules** cross-cut (Q-03). |
| DT-04 | The term set should be an open formula language, so any contract can be expressed | The completeness instinct — real contracts contain terms nobody anticipated, so give the user a general tool | ❌ **REJECTED — and its opposite is rejected too; the answer is a closed taxonomy with a mandatory escape hatch.** A formula language turns a royalty platform into a programming environment whose users are not programmers, whose errors are money, whose bugs are unauditable, and whose output the platform would be tacitly warranting — the exact liability D-02 refuses, re-entering through a text box. But a closed taxonomy that silently cannot express a real clause is worse: it produces a confident, complete-looking, **wrong** number, which is this domain's signature failure mode. So: **a fixed set of term types, plus a first-class `unrepresentable` record that marks the deal `partially-modelled` and flags every calculation beneath it incomplete.** The platform's honest sentence — *"your contract contains a term we cannot express; the figure below excludes it and is therefore not your royalty"* — is worth more than a plausible number, and it is D-15 (state what you know, say so when you don't) applied to contract modelling rather than to money. |
| DT-05 | A term set is a set — collect the terms and apply them | The natural reading, and how every royalty system the author is aware of behaves: a hard-coded order inherited from whichever contract the first engineer read | ❌ **REJECTED — a term set is an ordered pipeline, and the order is itself a term with an author.** *"18% of net receipts less a £2,000 quarterly fee"*: fee against the base gives `(£10,000 − £2,000) × 18% = £1,440` — a payment. Fee against the royalty gives `£10,000 × 18% − £2,000 = −£200` — a debt. Same clause, opposite outcomes, and only order distinguishes them. Sharper still: *"escalates after 100,000 units"* against 15% free goods — 100,000 **shipped** escalates, 85,000 **royalty-bearing** does not, and that is two points on every subsequent unit, forever, decided by which of two nearly-identical clauses the recorder read. Percentages compose commutatively, which is exactly why this is missed: the failure only appears when a fixed amount or a threshold test enters the chain — i.e. in the deals large enough for it to matter. **The order must be recorded data, attributed to the human who chose it, and shown to the payee.** |
| DT-06 | Escalation is one concept: a threshold and a new rate | It is how everyone describes it, including the contracts | ❌ **REJECTED — it is four independent decisions wearing one word, and this resolves the breadth pass's deferred marker on escalation.** (1) **Basis** — units or receipts; shipped or royalty-bearing; per territory or worldwide. (2) **Retroactivity** — does the escalated rate apply only above the threshold, or retroactively to unit one? Both are standard; they differ by the entire threshold's worth of units. (3) **Counting scope** — cumulative across all periods and all sources within the deal, and **the count is only as good as the statements ingested**. A user with 3 of 5 sources registered under-counts, escalates late, is underpaid, and the result **reconciles perfectly against itself** — the domain's signature silent failure, and exactly parent D-04's "an absence produces no event". The counter must be published as a **declared lower bound with its coverage**, never a bare number. (4) **Reset** — per album, per period, never. Four terms, four authors' choices, no defaults available for any of them. |
| DT-07 | Terms are scoped to their own domain — a master deal's terms govern master income | The domain map's clean separation of master (09/10) and publishing, which every downstream layer will otherwise assume | ❌ **REJECTED, and the counter-example is a standard American clause.** The US **controlled composition clause** lives in a *record* (master) deal and caps the *mechanical* (publishing) rate at 75% of statutory, with an aggregate cap of ten songs per album. A term recorded against the master deal reduces money arriving on a publishing statement, from a different body, in a different income type, months apart. Any scoping model of the form "terms belong to a domain" cannot express it — and its absence is invisible, because the publishing money simply arrives and is paid at full rate to someone whose contract says otherwise. Term scope must therefore be **(deal × work/master × income type × territory × period)** — five dimensions, orthogonal, where income type is *not* implied by the deal's domain. This is the sort of thing a royalty administrator would name in the first five minutes and a schema written from the domain map would never contain. |
| DT-08 | A reserve against returns is a deduction — it comes off the money | Its position in the contract, its position on every statement, and its name | ❌ **REJECTED — a reserve is a timing term, and modelling it as a deduction makes real money disappear silently.** 20% withheld against returns, "liquidated over the following four quarters", is not money removed; it is money **deferred with a promise attached**. Model it as a deduction and the promise is unrepresentable: if the liquidation never arrives — because the label forgot, because the deal moved, because nobody was counting — **no event ever fires**, because an absence produces no event (parent D-04). The artist's 20% simply is not there, forever, and no one can name the moment it went missing. That is *precisely* the condition `10.05.01` exists to recover money from: an institution holding an amount it knows, for a work it knows, belonging to a person it cannot reach. **The platform would have manufactured its own black box** — D-09's named prohibition. A reserve must therefore create a **scheduled expectation with a clock**: unliquidated at its due date, it ages, alarms, is money-denominated, and surfaces in `10.09`'s money-in-flight. This is D-04's discipline reaching a *sixth* independent site. |
| DT-09 | The platform should tell a musician when a term is unusual — "this 25% packaging deduction is above market" | The obvious user value, and the thing every musician actually wants to know | ❌ **REJECTED as stated — ✅ CONFIRMED in its honest form, and the distinction is the whole ethic of the feature.** Rejected because "above market" is a judgement about a contract, requires benchmark data the platform does not have and could not lawfully assemble from its users' confidential deals, and is advice — the liability D-02 exists to refuse, arriving dressed as helpfulness. But the useful half survives intact and requires no judgement whatsoever: **the effective rate**. *"Your headline rate is 18%. After the deductions recorded on your deal, your effective rate on this line is 11.25%."* That is arithmetic over recorded terms — a derivation, not an opinion — and it is the single most useful number in a musician's career that no one who owed them money has ever volunteered. `10.03.01` D-05 requires deductions itemised and never netted; the effective rate is the honest *summary* of that itemisation, and it is why writers cannot state their own royalty rate. **The platform does not tell you your deal is bad. It tells you what your deal is, and lets you notice.** The same line divides D-18's statutory-floor flag (a comparison against a published number — allowed) from "this deal is predatory" (advice — never). |
| DT-10 | Config on deal terms is a Producer capability — the Role Matrix says Producer: ⚙️ Config, Musician: 👁️ Read-only | The sub-domain and domain Role Matrices, read literally; and the way any spec writer will implement it | ❌ **REJECTED as a permission model, though it is a correct description of the modal case — and the sub-domain index already says so in its own Role Matrix finding ("payee vs payer, not seniority — do not model this as a permission tier"). This sharpens it into a rule.** `personas.md`'s structural fact is the multi-hyphenate: *"one human routinely occupies three [personas] in one day"*. A Musician who fronted a bandmate's studio bill against first earnings is the **payer** on that deal and must configure its terms; a Producer receiving points on someone else's record is the **payee** and must not. **Config derives from the payer/administrator relationship on a specific deal — never from a persona, never from an account type, never from a platform role.** A role-based implementation grants every Producer-flagged account config rights over deals they are merely paid under, which is the anti-persona behaviour of DT-02 handed out as a default permission to every user who ever engineered a session. The acute case is the same human on both sides: a producer with points who is also a co-writer on the track configures a term affecting their own writer share — at which point D-03's visibility guard is pointing at the author, and DT-11's `unwitnessed` stamp is the only thing left. |
| DT-11 | D-03's mitigation works — the payee sees the term, so the payer cannot quietly author it against them | D-03, and the entire justification for allowing DT-02's unsafe default to stand | ❌ **REJECTED for the modal case, and this is the most uncomfortable finding in the file.** Visibility requires a viewer. The payees under these terms are session players and independent producers, and they are **overwhelmingly not on the platform** — they are `02.03`'s unclaimed shells, people the platform computes money for and cannot reach (`10.03.01`'s "correct, and unpayable"). **So the feature's only real guard is inoperative exactly where the exposure is greatest**: a Producer recording a term against someone with no account faces a notification that reaches nobody and a visibility surface with no viewer. The mitigation D-03 offers is, in the common case, a mitigation in name. There is no fix available at this layer — the platform cannot manufacture a witness. What it can do is **refuse to let the absence expire**: the term is stamped `unwitnessed` at recording and carries that stamp permanently, so the day that person claims their account, the first thing their statement says is *"this term was recorded by X on 2024-03-04, while you had no account and could not see it."* That is not protection. It is a receipt for a lack of protection, kept honestly, and it is strictly better than the silence that is the industry default. It also strengthens Q-02 considerably: counter-signature is unavailable against a party who does not exist, which means the strong version of the guard has a hole the weak version merely exposes. |
| DT-12 | Recording a deal's terms is a setup step — onboarding configuration, done once, before the money flows | The natural reading of any "enter your deal" screen, and how it will be built unless someone says otherwise | ❌ **REJECTED — recording a term is a restatement event, and the ordinary act of onboarding a real artist is `10.03.04`'s unbounded cascade, fired on day one.** Terms are bitemporal (D-04): `effective from` and `recorded at` are independent. The modal way a term enters the platform is *someone finally types in the PDF* — a term effective from 2019, recorded in 2026. That is not configuration; it is a correction to six years of already-computed arithmetic, cascading transitively through calculations, recoupment positions, statements and payouts (`10.03.04` D-05). **A working musician's back catalogue of four deals, entered on their first afternoon, restates every quarter the platform has ever calculated for them.** `10.03.04` Q-03 asks at what scale the cascade becomes infeasible on the locked persistence/DB constraint and files it as an exceptional-event concern — this shows the trigger is not exceptional at all, it is **signup**, and it is therefore load-bearing on the platform's first-run experience rather than on its tail. The behavioural consequence is non-negotiable: the restatement's scope is stated **before** commit — *"this will restate 9 periods and change 3 payments already made, net −£240.18"* — never discovered afterward in a notification. |
| DT-13 | Terms are independent values — record each one and apply them | Every term is a number in a clause; the clauses are separate | ❌ **REJECTED — a term set is a small dependency graph, and storing resolved values silently breaks it.** *"Foreign sales at 75% of the otherwise applicable rate"* is not a rate; it is a **function of another term**. All-in producer points are a fraction of the artist's rate, not an independent percentage. A controlled-composition cap is a percentage of a statutory rate that changes by legislation. Store the foreign rate as `13.5%` and the day the escalation lifts the base from 18% to 20%, the foreign rate should become 15% and does not — a wrong number, plausible, self-consistent, and undetectable, arriving only on foreign lines and only after a threshold nobody was watching. **Derived terms must be stored as relations and resolved at calculation time**, and the derivation shown to the payee: *"13.5% = 75% × 18%"*, not a bare `13.5%`. It is `10.03.03` DT-01's "derived, never stamped" reaching a third independent site — provenance tiers (02), recoupment positions (10.03.03), and now rates — which is strong evidence it is a platform-level truth rather than three coincidences. |

Sharpens the sub-domain index's own Role Matrix finding into a rule. Role-based config hands DT-02's anti-persona act to every Producer-flagged account as a default permission. |
| D-13 | A term whose only affected parties are the author or account-less shells is stamped **`unwitnessed`**, permanently, and surfaces on the payee's **first** statement after they claim their account | DT-11. Not protection — a receipt for the absence of protection, kept honestly. Strictly better than the industry's silence, and strictly weaker than Q-02's counter-signature. |
| D-14 | Terms are **never deleted** — only superseded, with a reason and an author | Parent D-04. A deleted term makes every historical calculation beneath it unexplainable, which forfeits D-08's explainability deliverable at exactly the moment it is contested. |
| D-15 | **Recording a term is a restatement event, not a setup step.** A backdated effective date cascades through `10.03.04`, and its scope is stated **before commit** | DT-12. Onboarding a real artist's back catalogue restates every period the platform has calculated. `10.03.04` Q-03's cascade is triggered by signup, not by an exception. |
| D-16 | **Derived terms are stored as relations, not resolved values**, and their derivation is shown ("13.5% = 75% × 18%") | DT-13. A foreign rate stored as 13.5% does not move when the base escalates to 20%. Third independent arrival at "derived, never stamped" (02's provenance tiers; `10.03.03`'s positions; now rates). |
| D-17 | A term set that yields an **arithmetically impossible** result (points exceeding the artist's share; a rate outside 0–100%) is **blocked and named** — never computed, never floored at zero | Impossible arithmetic means a term is wrong. Computing through it produces a plausible number and buries the defect. A *legitimately* near-total deduction stack is not impossible — that is what D-11's effective rate is for. |
| D-18 | A recorded term below a **published statutory floor** is **flagged as a factual comparison** and **still applied as recorded** | DT-09's line. The statutory rate is a published number; comparing against it is arithmetic. Refusing to apply the term would be interpreting the contract (D-02); staying silent would waste a fact the platform already holds. |
| D-19 | A Must-level calculation with contradictory terms enters a **minimal bilateral reconciliation flow**: the exact conflicting terms are shown to all recorded counterparties, who may select or replace them; the calculation resumes only after recorded consent from every affected counterparty. Unresolved cases remain held and are explicitly routed to the later full dispute system | Agent decision under owner autonomy (2026-08-02). This creates a bounded, consented release-era exit without promoting the full dispute engine or authoring an interpretation |
