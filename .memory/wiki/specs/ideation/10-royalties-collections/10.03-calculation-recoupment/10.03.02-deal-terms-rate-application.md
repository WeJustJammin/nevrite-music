# Feature: Deal Terms → Royalty Rate Application

> **Number**: 10.03.02
> **Parent**: [Royalty Calculation & Recoupment](./10.03-calculation-recoupment-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate

## Overview

The **executable interpretation of a contract**: turning "18% of net receipts escalating to 20% after 100,000 units, less a 25% packaging deduction, with 3 producer points payable from artist's share, recoupable against a £40,000 advance" into rules a calculation engine can run against a royalty line.

The sweep proposed a "Royalty Calculation Engine" with no parameters. But an engine with no terms computes only the raw split, which is correct for a self-released independent writer and wrong for everyone who has ever signed anything. The terms *are* the calculation — the arithmetic is trivial and the interpretation is where every royalty dispute in the history of the industry has lived.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |

They cannot edit it; a payee editing the terms that determine their own payment is the obvious integrity failure. What they gain is the thing almost no artist has: their own deal, legible, with its consequences computed in front of them. |
| Producer | Config | Records terms on deals they administer or pay out under — their own points, an advance they fronted, an admin fee. `personas.md` names "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed" as their worst intentional behaviour; this feature is where that act would be committed, at leisure, in a settings screen, months after the room emptied. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

A deal term is a **rule with a temporal scope**: (what it applies to, what it does to the money, when it is in effect, who it favours). The terms that recur in real music deals and must be representable:

- **Rate** — a percentage, of *something* (net receipts vs gross vs PPD — and which one is chosen changes the answer by a factor of two).
- **Escalation** — the rate changes at a threshold, which must itself be tracked cumulatively across periods.
- **Deduction** — packaging, reserves, free goods. Historic, often absurd, still in live contracts.
- **Points** — a producer's share, and the fight is always *from whose share* it comes.
- **Recoupability** — which costs are recoupable against which income (`10.03.03`).
- **Term and territory** — when and where it applies (`10.01.07` D-02's bitemporal problem again).

The platform's position must be modest and explicit: **it applies terms as recorded; it does not interpret the contract.** A human decides what "net receipts" means in this agreement and records it. The platform is a calculator with a memory, not a lawyer — and pretending otherwise is a liability surface of exactly the kind `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 refused elsewhere.

### Happy Path

1. A deal exists (domain 09's agreement, or domain 23's contract vault)
2. Its terms are recorded as executable rules, with their scope and effective dates, by someone who read the contract
3. Terms are visible in plain language to every party they affect — including the payee
4. `10.03.01` applies the terms in effect **for the usage period**, not for today
5. The payee statement (`10.04.02`) shows the term, its effect and its source clause

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| "Net receipts" is undefined in the contract | Genuinely ambiguous, and the ambiguity is worth a lot of money. **The most litigated phrase in music** | Recorded as an interpretation with an author — never resolved silently by the platform |
| Term recorded incorrectly | Every calculation under it is wrong, plausibly, and reconciles perfectly against itself | Correction → `10.03.04` restatement + clawback |
| Escalation threshold spans periods | Cumulative counting across statements from multiple sources | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Points from artist's share vs from the top | Changes who pays the producer. Constantly disputed | Must be explicit; there is no safe default |
| No contract exists at all | The handshake deal. Extremely common between a musician and a producer | Raw split, terms flagged unknown (`10.03.01` D-06) |
| Contract exists as a PDF nobody typed in | The modal case | The gap must be visible; an untyped deal is an uncomputable one |
| Producer records terms favouring themselves | `personas.md`'s named anti-persona behaviour, executed here | Every term carries an author and is visible to the payee. See DT-02 |
| Term changes mid-usage-period | Old usage, old term | Bitemporal; parent D-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No deal | Nothing recorded | Raw split applies; stated plainly, not assumed |
| Recorded | Terms entered | Plain-language view, visible to all affected parties |
| Contested | A party disputes the recording | → `10.08`; affected amounts held |
| In effect | Within scope for a period | Applied by `10.03.01` |
| Expired | Past term | No longer applied; historical periods unaffected |
| Superseded | Terms corrected | New version; restatement (`10.03.04`) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Deal terms are contract data and belong in domain 23's vault or domain 09's agreements — this is not a domain 10 feature | Node Classification Gate; 23 explicitly has "Deal & Contract Vault with Option/Reversion Alerts" and 09 has "Producer Agreements" | ❌ **REJECTED, with a real boundary rather than a land grab.** The **contract** is 23's (the document, its dates, its alerts) and the **agreement** is 09's (who agreed what). Neither models the term as an **executable rule applied to a royalty line as of a usage period** — 23 stores a PDF and reminds you about it, 09 records that a producer has 3 points. Turning "3 points from artist's share of net receipts" into arithmetic that runs against £4.11 of PRS income is collections work and exists nowhere else on the map. **But three domains now touch one deal**, which is precisely the multiple-owners failure this platform exists to prevent — hence parent `Q-02` and Q-01 below. The honest position: the rule lives here, the document does not, and somebody must ratify that. |
| DT-02 | Whoever administers the deal records its terms — that is just how administration works | The obvious operational model, and how every label system does it | ❌ **REJECTED as unsafe by default.** The person recording the terms is the person who **pays** under them, and `personas.md` already names their worst intentional behaviour as "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention". This feature is that act's ideal venue: months after the room emptied, in a settings screen, against a term the payee has never seen and cannot check. The platform's whole pitch is that facts get captured while everyone is present and friendly — and then this feature lets one party author the money rule alone, later, in private. **Mitigation is visibility, not permission**: every term carries an author, is visible in plain language to every party it affects, and a change to a term that reduces someone's money must reach them. That is weaker than counter-signature (Q-02) and it is the minimum. |
| DT-03 | This is royalty-specific machinery | The feature sits in the royalties domain and speaks the royalties dialect | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the most useful structural finding in the sub-domain.** "Compute money from receipts under agreed terms, net against what was fronted" is **not a royalty concept**. A live settlement is a guarantee versus a door percentage after costs, recouping the guarantee — which is an advance and a rate, in different clothes (domain 17). Management commission is a rate on receipts (domain 23). A publishing admin fee is a rate. Marketplace and service fee schedules are rates (05, 13, 14). **They are one engine**: terms, thresholds, deductions, recoupment against a float, all applied to receipts as of a date. Building it four times guarantees four dialects, four rounding policies and four sets of bugs — and the live-settlement one will be written by someone who has never heard of packaging deductions. Proposed as the **Deal Terms & Settlement Rules** cross-cut. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The **executable rule** lives here; the document lives in 23; the agreement lives in 09 | DT-01. A real boundary — but three touchers of one deal needs ratification (Q-01). |
| D-02 | The platform **applies terms as recorded; it never interprets a contract** | "Net receipts" is a legal judgement with money attached. Interpreting it would be the liability `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 refuses. |
| D-03 | Every term carries an **author** and is **visible in plain language to every party it affects** | DT-02. The recorder is the payer; visibility is the only available check. |
| D-04 | Terms are **bitemporal** — applied as of the usage period | Parent D-02. Same as `10.01.07` D-02. |
| D-05 | An unrecorded deal produces a **flagged gap**, never an assumed standard term | `10.03.01` D-06. Assuming a term invents a fact. |
