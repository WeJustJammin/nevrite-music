## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Deal Terms ↔ Calculation Engine

**Relationship**: The sweep proposed a "Royalty Calculation Engine" and no terms to run it on. That engine computes a raw split — which is correct for a self-releasing independent writer and wrong for every person who has ever signed anything. **The arithmetic is trivial; the interpretation is where every royalty dispute in the history of the industry has lived.**

The boundary that keeps this honest: the engine applies, it does not interpret (`10.03.02` D-02). A human decides what "net receipts" means in a given agreement and records it, with their name on it. The platform is a calculator with a memory, not a lawyer — the same refusal `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 makes about adjudicating truth, arriving here as a refusal to adjudicate a contract.

Where terms are absent — the modal case, because most independent deals are a PDF nobody typed in — the engine flags them unknown rather than assuming a standard (`10.03.01` D-06). Assuming a term invents a fact, which is `problem-statement.md`'s reconstruction failure committed by the system built to prevent it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: read-only on both. Their deal, legible, with its consequences computed — which is more than most artists have ever had.
- **Producer**: config on terms, read-only on the engine. The payer records the rule; the engine cannot be touched by anyone.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Terms are versioned rules with temporal scope; the engine holds no terms of its own. Neither is user-editable at calculation time (parent D-05) — a fix goes to the term, never to the number.
2. **Trigger chain**: Line matched → split as-of (09) → **terms in effect for that period** → deductions itemised → per-party amounts with derivation. A term correction re-enters at step 3 and cascades (CX-05).
3. **Permission intersection**: Sharp — a Producer configuring terms parameterises a Musician's money, and the Musician cannot alter any of it. Mitigated only by visibility (`10.03.02` D-03).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A term change notifies every party it affects, in plain language. This is `10.03.02` D-03's only real enforcement.
5. **State transition conflict**: A term edited mid-calculation must not mutate an in-flight run. New version, new calculation, old retained (parent D-04).

### CX-02: Calculation Engine ↔ Recoupment

**Relationship**: The separation that carries the sub-domain's moral weight. `10.03.01` DT-02 refuses to let recoupment into the engine: the artist **earned** £4,200 and **receives** £0, and those are two facts rather than one.

Every label system in existence collapses them, which is why an artist reads `£0.00` and concludes their record made no money. The engine computes the earning; `10.03.03` computes payability; `10.04.02` gets to say the true and useful thing — *"you earned £4,200; £4,200 went against your advance; £11,800 remains"* — instead of a technically-accurate zero that conceals everything the payee needs.

`10.03.03` DT-03 sharpens why this matters beyond schema hygiene: the reason "am I recouped?" is unanswered in music is not computational difficulty. It is that the only party able to compute it benefits from the artist not knowing. WeJammin computes it from statements **the artist supplied themselves** (`10.02.01`), needing no label's cooperation and no accreditation.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: read-only on both, and this pair is the reason the read is worth anything.
- **Producer**: config on advances they fronted (payer side); identical to a Musician when they are the payee.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None by construction. Earnings are the engine's; the position is derived from earnings + terms + advances (`10.03.03` D-01). Nothing is stored twice.
2. **Trigger chain**: Calculate → apply in-scope earnings against the balance at the term's rate → payable or not → `10.04`. The engine never reads the position back.
3. **Permission intersection**: A Producer's advance record determines whether a Musician gets paid at all — the same asymmetry as CX-01, with a bigger switch.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Clearing an advance is the most welcome notification in the domain. Un-clearing it (CX-06) is the worst.
5. **State transition conflict**: Both are derived, so a restatement re-derives both consistently. This is exactly what a stored balance would have broken (`10.03.03` DT-01).

### CX-03: Restatement ↔ Calculation Engine + Recoupment

**Relationship**: The reason the sub-domain looks the way it does. `10.03.04` DT-01: **retroactive change is the normal weather in royalties, not an edge case** — and the sweep's 18 candidates contained none of it.

Every structural decision here is downstream of that fact:
- Calculations are **immutable and versioned** (parent D-04) — because a clawback is the delta between two versions and is undefined if one is gone.
- Recoupment is **derived, never stored** (`10.03.03` D-01) — because a stored balance survives the correction that unmade it and keeps paying someone who is no longer recouped.
- Splits are read **as of the usage period** (parent D-02) — because a restatement arriving in 2026 for 2024 usage must pay 2024's owners.

Remove restatement from the model and all three decisions look like over-engineering. Include it and they are the minimum. A sweep that omitted it would have produced a schema unable to express the correction it needs in month three.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: read-only; the party a clawback happens *to*, often for money already spent.
- **Producer**: can *cause* one by correcting a term or a split (CX-05).
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Versions never conflict — they supersede. The conflict is temporal, not structural: version N was paid, version N+1 is correct, and the difference is owed in one direction or the other.
2. **Trigger chain**: Input changes → recalculate → **transitively** re-derive positions, statements, payouts (`10.03.04` D-05) → classify delta → unpaid reduction (painless) or overpayment (not).
3. **Permission intersection**: Whoever can change an input can trigger a clawback against someone who cannot see it coming. CX-05 is the acute case.
4. **Notification fan-out**: `10.03.04` D-02 — **causation is the message**, not metadata. Four identical negative numbers with four different causes are four different conversations.
5. **State transition conflict**: A restatement racing an open dispute (`10.08`) — the dispute must absorb it rather than argue about a number that moved underneath it. Same shape as `credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-08's dispute-absorbs-amendment rule.

### CX-05: Deal Terms ↔ Restatement

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's sharpest conflict of interest, and it is structural rather than hypothetical. `10.03.02` DT-02 establishes that the party recording a deal's terms is the party who **pays** under them. `10.03.04` establishes that correcting a term restates every calculation beneath it — and if money already moved, that is a clawback.

Composed: **a Producer can, months after the session, in a settings screen, alone, adjust a term in their own favour and thereby claw back money already paid to a Musician who never saw the term and cannot alter it.** `personas.md` names precisely this behaviour ("assigns themselves a larger split than agreed and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention") as the Producer's worst intentional act. The platform's entire thesis is that facts get captured while everyone is present and friendly (`D-18`); this pair is the back door out of that room.

The mitigations are weak and must be named as weak: every term carries an author, is visible in plain language to every affected party, and any change reducing someone's money must reach them with its cause attached (`10.03.04` D-02). None of that is counter-signature (`10.03.02` Q-02), which is what the thesis actually implies.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the actor. Config on terms, and thereby indirect control of another party's paid money.
- **Musician**: the affected party. Read-only, notified after the fact, holding no veto.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The term is the Producer's record; the money is the Musician's. One party owns the state, the other bears the consequence — the definition of an asymmetry.
2. **Trigger chain**: Term edited → new version → restatement cascade → overpayment → **offset against the Musician's future earnings** (`10.03.04` DT-02 forbids doing this silently, and silence is the default everywhere else in the industry).
3. **Permission intersection**: The central problem. Config on A determines money in B, and B has no vote.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Mandatory, plain-language, with causation (`10.03.04` D-02, D-03). "Your producer changed a term" and "Spotify restated Q1" must never render as the same sentence.
5. **State transition conflict**: A term change racing a payout run — the run must hold rather than pay a figure it knows is being restated. Paying and then clawing back is strictly worse than a delay.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 10.03.03 Recoupment | 10.03.01 Calculation Engine _(as a **reverse** edge)_ | Rejected in that direction, and the direction is the entire point. CX-02 is one-way: the engine feeds recoupment and **never reads the position back**. A reverse edge would let recoupment state alter the earning figure — which is precisely `10.03.01` DT-02's error, the mechanism that turns "you earned £4,200" into "£0.00" and convinces an artist their record failed. The one-directionality is not a data-flow preference; it is the moral content of the sub-domain, and logging a back-edge would invite exactly the optimisation that destroys it. |
| R-02 | 10.03.02 Deal Terms | 10.03.01's rounding policy | Rejected: rounding is a **platform-wide arithmetic policy** (`10.03.01` D-04), not a negotiable term. If rounding were per-deal, two payees on one line could round in opposite directions and the parts would not sum to the whole — breaking the reconciliation guarantee that D-04 exists to hold. No real contract specifies rounding; treating it as a term would invent a negotiation nobody has ever had, and hand a payer one more lever over a payee's money. |
| R-03 | 10.03.04 Restatement | 10.03.02 Deal Terms _(as a **cause** edge from restatement to terms)_ | Rejected in that direction. Terms cause restatements (CX-05); restatements never rewrite terms. The distinction matters because a system that "corrects" a term in response to a restatement would be inferring what a contract says from what the money did — reconstruction (`problem-statement.md`) in its purest and most dangerous form, with the platform reverse-engineering an agreement from arithmetic. |
| R-04 | 10.03.03 Recoupment | 10.03.02's escalation thresholds | Rejected as a pair despite the surface similarity — both are cumulative counters that cross a line and change the money. But an escalation is a **rate change on future earnings** and recoupment is a **balance consuming them**; they are independent, can be live simultaneously, and interact only inside `10.03.01`'s ordered resolution, which already sequences them (terms before payability). Wiring them directly would suggest an escalation affects recoupment or vice versa, which is false and would produce a plausible wrong number in exactly the deals that are large enough to have both. |
