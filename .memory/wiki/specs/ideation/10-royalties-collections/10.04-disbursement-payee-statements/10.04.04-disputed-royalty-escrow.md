# Feature: Disputed-Royalty Escrow & Withholding

> **Number**: 10.04.04
> **Parent**: [Disbursement & Payee Statements](./10.04-disbursement-payee-statements-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

**Holding money while people argue about who it belongs to.** A split is disputed (domain 09), a credit is contested (`02.05`), a statement is challenged (`10.08`) — and a royalty line whose ownership is in question cannot be paid to either party without the platform silently deciding the argument.

The design principle is inherited and simple: `02.05` D-02 holds a contested credit rather than deleting or publishing it, because either would let a dispute decide a fact. The money version is the same act with a higher stake. **Paying is a verdict. Holding is not.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees their money held, how much, why, since when, and what would release it. Cannot release it — including by winning an argument with the other party privately, which is exactly the pressure the hold exists to remove. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, from either side. Notably a Producer administering a payout **cannot** release escrow on money they are disputing, which is the whole point: `personas.md` names "withholds stems as leverage in a payment dispute" as their worst intentional act, and this feature refuses them the same leverage over money. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

Escrow is triggered by a **contested claim on a specific amount**, not by a general state of bad feeling. It is scoped as tightly as the dispute: if one work's split is disputed, that work's royalties hold and everything else in the payee's run pays normally. A dispute is not a freeze on a relationship.

Release has exactly three triggers, and none of them is either party's decision:
- **Resolution** — the dispute closed (domain 09 / `02.05` / `10.08`). The money follows the outcome, including retroactively (`10.03.04`).
- **Withdrawal** — the disputant dropped it. Releases to the original allocation.
- **Expiry** — a dispute that never resolves. **This is the hard one**, because an indefinite hold means filing a dispute is a free way to freeze someone's income forever — the same weapon `credits-attribution-index.md` Q-03 identifies for contested credits, with cash instead of reputation.

The parent's D-03 governs the build: **escrow is a cross-cut mechanism** (`CX-M01` — "escrow with defined release triggers"), serving gear, digital goods, services, booking and ticketing. What is domain-owned is narrow and real: what triggers a *royalty* hold, how tightly it scopes, and what the statement says about it (`10.04.02` D-01 — held money appears, named, never omitted).

### Happy Path

1. A dispute is filed on a split, credit or statement line
2. Affected amounts — and only those — are held at the next payout run (`10.04.01`)
3. Every party's statement names the held amount, its cause, and its age
4. The dispute resolves in its owning domain
5. The money follows the outcome, retroactively where needed, and releases

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Dispute never resolves | Money frozen forever. **Filing becomes a weapon** | Expiry policy required. See Q-01 |
| Dispute filed to apply pressure | The `personas.md` anti-persona behaviour, in its money form | Pattern signal → domain 24; invisible from inside one case |
| Both parties partly right | `02.05` D-04 — joint/split outcomes are first-class | The release must support a split outcome, not just a winner |
| Held money earns interest | Whose? Real at scale, and legally loaded (parent `Q-02`) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Restatement during escrow | The held amount was wrong | Re-derived; escrow is not immune to `10.03.04` |
| Dispute on 1 of 40 works | Only that work holds | Scope is per-amount, never per-relationship |
| Disputant is the payer | They hold the money and dispute the claim on it | **They cannot release it either.** The feature's core protection |
| Platform is the disputant | A platform-detected leakage or misattribution | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — who adjudicates against the house? |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Free | No dispute | Pays normally |
| Held | Dispute filed on this amount | Amount, cause, since-when, on every party's statement |
| Resolving | Dispute in progress | Held; still visible; still theirs-or-not |
| Released | Resolution / withdrawal | Follows the outcome, retroactively where needed |
| Expired | Dispute stale past policy | **Unresolved** — see Q-01 |
| Aged | Held a long time | Escalating alarm, same as `10.04.03` D-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Royalty escrow needs its own mechanism, because royalty disputes are the domain's core and money is special | Domain-importance reasoning; the sweep listed escrow as a peer capability alongside calculation and disbursement | ❌ **REJECTED — this is `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03's argument arriving intact in a second domain.** Holding funds against a defined release trigger is identical machinery for gear (13), digital goods (14), services (05), booking (17) and ticketing (19); `CX-M01` already names it. A second escrow engine here would fork the ledger and guarantee two divergent release behaviours across one platform's money. **What is genuinely domain-specific is narrow**: royalty holds are triggered by *ownership* disputes rather than delivery disputes, they scope per-amount rather than per-transaction, and — uniquely — the disputed amount keeps *growing* while the argument runs, because the work keeps earning. That is a feature riding a cross-cut, which is why this is `10.04.04` and not a folder. |
| DT-02 | An indefinite hold is the safe default — nobody is harmed by money sitting still | The conservative instinct, and it feels obviously right: holding is neutral, paying is a verdict | ❌ **REJECTED — an indefinite hold is a verdict rendered by inaction, and it hands a weapon to whoever files first.** If money freezes on filing and unfreezes only on resolution, then **filing a dispute is a free, unilateral, unappealable way to stop a rival's income for as long as you can avoid resolving it** — and the incentive to never resolve is exactly proportional to how much you dislike the other party. `credits-attribution-index.md` Q-03 identifies this weapon for contested *credits* (a permanent dispute permanently suppresses a credit); this is the same exploit with cash, where the pressure to settle is immediate and material rather than reputational. **Holding is not neutral once it is unbounded**, and an expiry policy is therefore mandatory — while also being genuinely hard, because expiry means paying someone whose entitlement is still in question. There is no clean answer and Q-01 must not be allowed to close by default. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Rides the escrow cross-cut; **no royalty-specific escrow engine** | DT-01. Same reasoning as `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03 for disputes. |
| D-02 | **No party can release escrow** — not the payee, not the payer, not the administrator | Whoever can release it can use it as leverage. `personas.md` names that behaviour explicitly. |
| D-03 | Scope is **per-amount**, never per-relationship | One disputed work does not freeze forty. A dispute is not a divorce. |
| D-04 | Held money is **named on every affected party's statement** | `10.04.02` D-01. Silent withholding is the industry's move. |
| D-05 | **An expiry policy is mandatory** | DT-02. Unbounded holding makes filing a weapon and non-resolution a strategy. |
| D-06 | Release supports **split/joint outcomes** | `02.05` D-04. Creative contribution is often genuinely joint; a winner-takes-all release manufactures a loser. |
