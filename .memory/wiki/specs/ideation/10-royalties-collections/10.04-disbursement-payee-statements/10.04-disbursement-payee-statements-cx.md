## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Payout Run ↔ Payee Statements

**Relationship**: The pair that decides whether domain 10 delivered anything. `10.04.02` DT-01 makes the case: every upstream decision in this domain — as-of splits (parent D-02), immutable derivations (`10.03.01` D-01), itemised deductions (`10.03.01` D-05), the earned-vs-paid separation (`10.03.01` DT-02) — is **only ever experienced in one document**. A payee never sees the calculation engine. They see a statement.

So the rule is absolute (parent D-04): **a payout without a traceable statement is forbidden**, and a statement is issued even when nothing moved. `£0.00` alone is the industry's characteristic concealment; the four-line form (earned / deducted / applied / paid) is the honest one.

`10.04.02` DT-02 identifies where the chain must reach: not the source line — Curve and Exactuals already do that — but **the split as of the usage period, and the session where that split was agreed**. Those two links are unreproducible by anyone who was not in the room, which is `problem-statement.md`'s root cause stated as a product feature.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: config on payout method, full on the statement. The statement is the deliverable; the transfer is plumbing.
- **Producer**: additionally *issues* statements to payees they administer — for most, the first royalty statement they have ever sent worth reading.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The run owns the transfer; the statement owns the explanation. The derivation is `10.03.01`'s and is read by both — never copied, or the two would eventually disagree about one payment.
2. **Trigger chain**: Select → aggregate → net → pay → **statement always**, including on the zero-paid and excluded paths. The statement is not conditional on money moving.
3. **Permission intersection**: A payer-issued statement exposes session economics to a payee entitled to their share and not to everyone else's (`10.04.02` Q-02) — an unresolved redaction boundary.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every payee, every period. Including — especially — the ones paid nothing, because that is where the five causes of non-payment get distinguished.
5. **State transition conflict**: A restatement after a statement was issued requires a new version with an explicit delta and its cause (`10.03.04` D-02), never a silent reissue of a document the payee already has.

### CX-02: Unpayable Balances ↔ Payout Run

**Relationship**: The counter-intuitive fact that reshapes the run. `10.04.01` DT-02: **by volume, the most common outcome for a payee in an early-stage run is not being paid** — below threshold, no payout method, unrecouped, escrowed, unattributed. Five causes, five meanings, and every one renders as "no payment" unless non-payment is a first-class output.

`10.04.03` DT-01 explains why this cannot be delegated to the rails. `CX-M01` handles a *failed transfer*. It has no concept of money owed to **a person who does not exist on the platform** — a shell payee from a session roll call (`02.03`) holding a real credit and a real neighbouring-rights claim (`10.01.06`), who has never heard of WeJammin. That balance is not a payment failure; it is a black box, and it is definitionally what `10.05.01` sells recovery from.

This is the parent domain's D-09 in one sentence: **the platform building the cure must not quietly accumulate the disease.**

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: read-only on their held money, with a cause they can usually act on. A cause turns a hold into a task.
- **Producer**: threshold config, and visibility of money owed to people not on the platform — generated by sessions they ran, reachable only by them.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The balance is the payee's money, held (`10.04.03` D-01). Not float, not revenue. The run selects against it; it does not own it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Select → payee cannot be paid → hold **with a cause** → surface as a task where the payee can act → next run. Where they cannot act (shell, deceased), the balance ages and escalates.
3. **Permission intersection**: The sharp one — a Producer's threshold policy determines whether a Musician gets paid this quarter or next.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Ageing alarms, money-denominated. And DT-02's unresolved question: is *"£340 is waiting for you"* an acceptable message to someone who never signed up? It is simultaneously the strongest growth lever on the map and an unsolicited financial communication to a non-user.
5. **State transition conflict**: A held balance is not immune to restatement (`10.03.04`) — it re-derives like everything else. A balance held for two years may be a different number by the time it is payable.

### CX-03: Escrow ↔ Payout Run

**Relationship**: The narrow, deliberate one. A contested amount is excluded from the run **per-amount, not per-payee** (`10.04.04` D-03): one disputed work holds; the payee's other thirty-nine pay normally. A dispute is not a freeze on a relationship, and building it that way would make filing a dispute an instrument of general harm rather than a claim about a specific fact.

`10.04.04` DT-02 is the pair's hardest content: **an indefinite hold is a verdict rendered by inaction.** If money freezes on filing and thaws only on resolution, filing becomes a free, unilateral way to stop a rival's income for as long as you avoid resolving it. Holding feels neutral and stops being neutral the moment it is unbounded — which is why an expiry policy is mandatory and genuinely unpleasant to design (`10.04.04` Q-01).

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: read-only from both sides. Critically, **the payer cannot release money they are disputing** — `personas.md` names withholding-as-leverage as the Producer's worst intentional act, and this pair refuses them the lever.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The dispute owns the hold; the run reads it. Neither party owns the release (`10.04.04` D-02) — the one place on this platform where "let the parties sort it out" is refused, because whoever holds the money would win by default.
2. **Trigger chain**: Dispute filed → amount held at the next run → resolution/withdrawal/expiry → release, retroactively where needed (`10.03.04`).
3. **Permission intersection**: The core protection. Administering a payout does not grant release over money you are arguing about.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Held amounts appear on **every** affected party's statement (CX-05) — including the party who may turn out not to be entitled, which is uncomfortable and correct.
5. **State transition conflict**: A disputed amount keeps growing while the argument runs (`10.04.04` Q-03) — the work keeps earning, which quietly makes a slow dispute more valuable to whoever benefits from the delay.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 10.04.04 Escrow | 10.04.03 Unpayable Balances _(as one "held money" concept)_ | Rejected as a merge, despite both being money-that-does-not-move. Escrow holds because **entitlement is contested** — the platform knows the payee and cannot pay them without rendering a verdict. Unpayable balances hold because **the payee cannot receive** — entitlement is undisputed and the route is missing. Opposite causes, opposite remedies (resolve an argument vs add a bank account), opposite parties (two claimants vs one absent one). CX-06 records what they genuinely share: an ageing problem. Merging them would produce a "held" state whose cause has to be inferred, which is precisely what `10.04.03` D-02 forbids. |
| R-02 | 10.04.01 Payout Run | 10.03.03 Recoupment _(as a direct edge)_ | Rejected: the run pays what is **payable**, and payability is already resolved upstream in `10.03` (CX-02 there). A direct edge would put recoupment logic in the payment layer — meaning two places decide whether someone gets paid, and they would eventually disagree about the same artist. The run reads a payable amount; it does not compute one. |
| R-03 | 10.04.02 Payee Statements | 10.02.01 Source Ingestion _(as a statement-to-statement edge)_ | Rejected as a direct pair, and the confusion is worth pre-empting: both features involve "a royalty statement". `10.02.01` ingests statements **the user receives from others** (evidence, parent 10.02 D-03). `10.04.02` issues statements **the platform sends to payees** (an assertion the platform stands behind). Inbound evidence and outbound assertion, mediated by the entire calculation chain. A direct edge would suggest the platform can pass a source's statement through to a payee — which is the industry's behaviour, and precisely the reconstruction-without-derivation this domain exists to replace. |
| R-04 | 10.04.03 Unpayable Balances | 10.04.04's expiry policy | Rejected: they are both "what happens to money nobody claims", and the temptation to solve them once is strong. But an expired **escrow** must resolve a live question of entitlement between two identified parties (`10.04.04` Q-01), while an expired **unpayable balance** has an undisputed owner who simply cannot be reached (`10.04.03` Q-01). One needs an adjudication rule; the other needs an escheat/return policy. One shared "expiry" would force a single answer onto two questions that only share a clock. |
