# Feature: Royalty Payout Run & Split Disbursement

> **Number**: 10.04.01
> **Parent**: [Disbursement & Payee Statements](./10.04-disbursement-payee-statements-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The **periodic run that actually moves the money**: gather everything payable since the last run, per payee, across every work and source and period; net it against holds, escrow and unrecouped balances; and pay it out in one transfer per person rather than one per line.

This is not a marketplace checkout. A checkout splits one payment three ways at the moment of sale, synchronously, against a known cart. A royalty run pays 400 people fractions of pennies aggregated across 60,000 lines, quarterly, in six currencies, where a third of the payees have no payout method and a tenth of the amounts are smaller than the fee to send them. The money rails are shared (`CX-M01`); this shape is not.

## Behavior

A run is: **select payable amounts → aggregate per payee → net → pay → record**. Each step has a royalty-specific hazard:

- **Select** — payable is not the same as earned (`10.03.01` DT-02). Held, escrowed (`10.04.04`), unrecouped (`10.03.03`) and unattributed (`10.02.05`) amounts are all excluded, for different reasons that the statement must distinguish.
- **Aggregate** — one transfer per payee per run, never one per line. A payee is owed £0.0003 from 4,000 lines and £12.40 total; paying per line is 4,000 fee-consuming transfers of nothing.
- **Net** — recoupment, overpayment offset (`10.03.04` D-03 — never silent), platform fee, withholding tax (`CX-M02`).
- **Pay** — via the rails (`CX-M01`), in the payee's currency, at a realised rate that is a distinct fact from the display rate (`10.02.04`).
- **Record** — immutably. A payout is the boundary that turns a reducible number into a clawback (`10.03.04`).

**The run must be idempotent and interruptible.** It moves real money to hundreds of parties; a half-completed run that cannot be safely resumed is how someone gets paid twice and someone else not at all. This is more important than it is interesting, and it is the reason the run is a first-class feature rather than a cron job.

### Happy Path

1. Run opens for a period; payable amounts are selected per payee with their reasons
2. Amounts aggregate to one figure per payee; nets are applied and itemised
3. Payees below threshold or without a method are excluded — **and told why** (`10.04.03`)
4. Transfers execute via the rails; realised FX is recorded where currencies differ
5. Every payee gets a statement (`10.04.02`) — including the ones who were paid nothing, and especially them

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Amount is smaller than the transfer fee | Common. Paying it destroys it | → `10.04.03` threshold; the money accrues, it does not evaporate |
| Payee has no payout method | Very common at launch. Money is real and unmovable | → `10.04.03`. The platform's own black box |
| Run interrupts mid-flight | Some paid, some not, state ambiguous | **Idempotent resume.** The single hardest engineering requirement here |
| Payee is an unclaimed shell (`02.03`) | Correct money, no person, no channel | → `10.04.03`; `credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-04's break, with cash attached |
| Restatement lands mid-run | The figure being paid is being corrected | Run **holds**; paying then clawing back is strictly worse than a delay (`10.03` CX-05) |
| Payee is dead | Estate, probate, an account nobody can access | Domain 09 has "Estate, Succession & Catalog Legacy" — this is its money-side twin. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| FX moved since calculation | Realised ≠ displayed | `10.02.04` Q-01 — whose gain or loss, still unanswered |
| Payee is sanctioned / KYC-failed | Cannot pay legally | Rails' problem (`CX-M01`), but the money's status here must reflect it honestly |
| Same payee, many entities | One human paid as themselves, as their band, and as their publisher | Correct, not a duplicate. Same shape as `10.01.01` D-01 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Open | Run created for a period | Selection in progress |
| Selected | Payable amounts gathered | Per-payee totals with exclusion reasons |
| Held | Restatement or dispute in flight | Delayed, with the cause named |
| Paying | Transfers executing | In progress; idempotent |
| Paid | Transfers settled | Statement issued (`10.04.02`) |
| Excluded | Threshold / no method / escrow | **Explained, not silent** — the money still exists |
| Failed | Transfer bounced | Retried; balance returns to payable, never lost |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the platform's multi-party split payment mechanism (`CX-M01`) applied to royalties — no domain-owned node needed | Node Classification Gate. The global cross-cut explicitly names "multi-party split payments" and lists royalties-collections among its consumers | ❌ **REJECTED, narrowly — the rails are shared, the shape is not.** A marketplace checkout splits one known payment three ways, synchronously, at the moment of sale, against a cart. A royalty run pays hundreds of parties **aggregated across tens of thousands of lines**, periodically, where the modal per-line amount is a fraction of a penny, a third of payees have no payout method, and many totals are below the cost of the transfer. None of those properties exist in a checkout, and all of them determine the design: aggregation-before-payment, thresholds, idempotent resume, and an unpayable-balance concept that a checkout has no reason to invent. **The rails are `CX-M01`'s (D-02); the run semantics are domain-owned** — the same split `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03 made for the dispute engine. |
| DT-02 | The run's job is to move money | The feature's name | ❌ **REJECTED as half of it — the run's job is to move money *and account for the money it did not move*.** By volume, the most common outcome for a payee in an early-stage run is **not being paid**: below threshold, no payout method, unrecouped, escrowed, unattributed. Five different reasons, five different meanings, and every one of them renders as "no payment" unless the feature treats non-payment as a first-class output. A run that silently skips is how a platform accumulates unexplained balances — which is the industry's black box, rebuilt (`10.02.05` DT-01, parent domain D-09). **Every payee gets a statement, including — especially — those paid nothing.** |
| DT-03 | Idempotency is an implementation detail | It is a technical property of a batch job | ❌ **REJECTED as a product decision with a moral edge.** A royalty run moves real money to hundreds of people at once. A crash mid-run leaves some paid, some not, and the platform unsure which — and the recovery options are both bad: re-run and double-pay (→ `10.03.04` clawback against people who never did anything wrong), or don't and leave people unpaid with no signal that anything failed. Both are exactly the harms the domain exists to prevent, self-inflicted, at the single moment the user is most exposed. **Resumability is not engineering hygiene here; it is the difference between a payout system and a hazard**, and it belongs in the product spec rather than being discovered during implementation. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The rails are `CX-M01`'s; the **run semantics are domain-owned** | DT-01. Aggregation, thresholds, resume and unpayable balances have no analogue in a checkout. |
| D-02 | **One transfer per payee per run**, never one per line | A payee owed £12.40 across 4,000 lines is one payment. Per-line transfers destroy the money in fees. |
| D-03 | **Every payee gets a statement, including those paid nothing** | DT-02. Non-payment is the modal outcome and has five distinct meanings. |
| D-04 | The run is **idempotent and interruptible** | DT-03. The alternatives are double-payment or silent non-payment, both self-inflicted versions of the harm the domain fights. |
| D-05 | A run **holds** rather than paying a figure under restatement | Paying then clawing back is strictly worse than a delay (`10.03.04` DT-02). |
| D-06 | A failed transfer returns the balance to **payable** — money is never lost in a run | The balance is the payee's; a transport failure is not a forfeiture. |
