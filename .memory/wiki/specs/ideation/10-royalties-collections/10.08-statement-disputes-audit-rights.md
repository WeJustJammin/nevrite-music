# Feature: Statement Disputes & Audit Rights

> **Number**: 10.08
> **Parent**: [Royalties & Collections](./royalties-collections-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The path for a royalty statement that someone says is **wrong** — and the far end of that path, the contractual **right to audit** a label's or publisher's books. Royalty disputes are endemic, and the reason is structural: the statement is composed by the party who owes the money, from splits they reconstructed and terms they interpreted, and the payee has no way to check any of it.

**Classified as a feature, not a sub-domain**, on exactly the precedent `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03 set: the dispute machinery — case, parties, evidence, adjudicator, SLA, appeal — is a platform-wide cross-cut serving services (05), gear (13), digital goods (14), booking (17), ticketing (19), rights (09) and credits (02). What is domain-owned here is narrow: what counts as evidence about a royalty line, and what a disputed amount does while the argument runs.

## Behavior

The two cases are structurally opposite and must not share a flow:

**Disputing WeJammin's own statement** is cheap, and it is the domain's proudest claim. The statement carries its full derivation (`10.04.02` D-03): amount → line → source statement → split as of the usage period → the session where the split was agreed. A dispute is therefore mostly **reading the record together**. Someone is usually mistaken about which split applied, and the record settles it — the same structure as `02.05` DT-02's finding that disputes about captured work are *cheap*, not *settled well*.

**Disputing a third party's statement** is the industry's problem, unchanged. WeJammin holds an ingested PDF and a belief about what the splits should be; the label holds the books. The platform can say *"our expectation was £X, your statement says £Y"* — which is genuinely more than the artist had before — and then it stops. It has no standing, no access, and no power.

**Audit rights** are the honest limit. A contractual audit clause lets an artist send an accountant into a label's books, at their own expense, within a window. WeJammin cannot conduct an audit and should never imply it can. What it can do is real but modest: surface that the clause exists and when its window closes (domain 23's contract vault), and produce **the data pack an auditor would need** — every expectation, every statement, every discrepancy, already assembled. That turns a £40,000 audit into a cheaper one, which is a genuine service and not a product claim.

### Happy Path

1. A payee disputes a figure — on a WeJammin statement or an ingested one
2. The platform shows what it knows: the derivation (if ours) or the expectation-vs-statement gap (if theirs)
3. **Most disputes about our own statements end here** — the record answers it
4. If not, the dispute engine runs it (cross-cut); the disputed amount is held (`10.04.04`)
5. Resolution applies, retroactively where needed (`10.03.04`), and the argument stays on the record

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Dispute about a third party's statement | The platform has an opinion and no standing | Honest: "here is the gap; we cannot compel them" |
| Dispute is really a term dispute | Extremely common. The arithmetic is fine; "net receipts" is the fight | Routed to `10.03.02`; different question, same anger |
| Dispute is really a split dispute | Also common. The money is contested because the ownership is | → domain 09. Two live cases at once (`02.05` Q-02's twin) |
| Restatement lands mid-dispute | The number under argument moves | The dispute **absorbs** it, never races it (`credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-08's rule) |
| The platform's own bug caused the discrepancy | `10.02.02` Q-03 | The platform names it as its own (`10.03.04` D-04) |
| Audit clause exists, window closing | Domain 23's alert; a real deadline with money behind it | Surfaced; the platform still cannot audit |
| Serial disputant | Pattern invisible from inside one case | → domain 24 |
| Disputed amount keeps earning | The work is still being played | `10.04.04` Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Undisputed | Normal | No dispute surface |
| Under review (ours) | Disputed our statement | The derivation, together. Most end here |
| Under review (theirs) | Disputed an ingested statement | Our expectation vs their number. No standing beyond that |
| Escalated | Not resolved by the record | Dispute engine (cross-cut); amount held |
| Held | Any live dispute | `10.04.04` escrow |
| Resolved | Outcome applied | Retroactive where needed; the argument is retained |
| Audit-eligible | Clause + window open | Data pack available. **Not an audit** |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Royalty disputes need their own engine — money is different and this is a core domain | Domain-importance reasoning; the sweep listed it as a peer capability alongside calculation and disbursement | ❌ **REJECTED — `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03's argument, arriving intact in a second domain.** Case management, evidence handling, adjudicator assignment, SLAs and appeals are identical across 02, 05, 09, 13, 14, 17, 19 and 24. A second engine here forks the machinery and guarantees two divergent appeal experiences on one platform. **What is genuinely domain-specific is narrow**: what counts as evidence about a royalty line, and what a disputed amount does while held (`10.04.04`). Two domains reaching this conclusion independently is the strongest available evidence that the dispute engine is a real cross-cut rather than an evasion. |
| DT-02 | The platform's own statements will be disputed like everyone else's | Symmetry — we issue statements, statements get disputed | ❌ **REJECTED, and the asymmetry is the entire point of the domain.** Every other royalty statement in music is disputed because it is **unauditable by construction**: composed by the party who owes the money, from splits they reconstructed and terms they interpreted. WeJammin's statement carries its derivation to the session where the split was agreed (`10.04.02` D-03) — so a dispute about our statement is mostly **reading the record together**, and someone is usually simply mistaken about which split applied. That is `02.05` DT-02's finding reached independently from money rather than from credits: **disputes about work we captured are cheap, because the record already exists.** Not "we settle disputes well" — a claim about evidence cost, not about verdicts. |
| DT-03 | Audit rights are a feature — surface the clause and support the audit | The sweep's framing; the clause is real and the money is real | ❌ **REJECTED, and it must be rejected loudly, because the temptation to over-claim here is severe.** An audit is an accountant, physically, in a company's books, under a contractual clause, at the artist's expense, for months. **WeJammin cannot conduct one, cannot compel one, and cannot be one.** Any product surface that implies otherwise is selling a legal service it does not provide — a liability of exactly the kind `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 refuses. What is genuinely available is smaller and honest: surface the clause and its window (domain 23 owns the document), and **assemble the data pack an auditor would otherwise spend £40,000 of the artist's money constructing** — every expectation, every statement, every discrepancy, already gathered. That is a real service and it is not an audit, and the copy must never blur the two. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Rides the dispute cross-cut**; no royalty-specific engine | DT-01. Same as `credits-attribution-index.md` D-03. Two domains, one conclusion. |

| D-03 | **The platform does not audit and never implies it can** | DT-03. It assembles the pack; an accountant does the audit. Blurring this sells a legal service. |

| D-06 | The platform **names its own faults** when it caused the discrepancy | `10.03.04` D-04. The easiest lie in the domain to tell. |
