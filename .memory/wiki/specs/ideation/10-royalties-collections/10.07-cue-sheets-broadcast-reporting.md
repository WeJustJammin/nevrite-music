# Feature: Cue Sheets & Broadcast Performance Reporting

> **Number**: 10.07
> **Parent**: [Royalties & Collections](./royalties-collections-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

**Cue sheets** are the documents that tell a PRO which music was used in a film, TV show, advert or game, for how long, and in what manner (featured, background, theme). They are the sole basis on which broadcast performance royalties get distributed — and they are filed by **the production**, not by the composer. When a production does not file one, or files it wrong, the composer's broadcast royalties simply do not exist. There is no error, no notification, and frequently no recourse.

This is one of the largest and most notorious leaks in music, and it is also the one where WeJammin has the least leverage: **the platform cannot file a cue sheet.** The party who must act is a production company that is not a user and never will be.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Composers with sync placements (domain 11) track whether a cue sheet exists for each one, and chase when it does not. What they gain is not filing — they cannot file — but **knowing**, which is more than they have today, and having the chase be a task rather than a memory. |
| Producer | Full | Same, where they hold a share in a placed work. Often the party with the actual relationship to the music supervisor, and therefore the only one who can realistically chase. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The model is an **expectation with a chase**, and its shape follows `10.05.03`'s exactly: a placement exists (domain 11 licensed a work for a production), therefore a cue sheet should exist, therefore its absence is a finding. The platform can track:

- Which placements have a known cue sheet and which do not
- Whether the broadcast income that should follow one ever arrived (`10.02`)
- Who to chase — the production, the music supervisor, the sync agent

What the platform **cannot** do is file. The obligation sits with a production company whose incentive to file is weak and whose deadline is nobody's problem. A feature that implies WeJammin can fix this would be selling something it does not have.

The one genuine leverage point is upstream: **the licence (domain 11) is where a cue-sheet obligation could be made a term.** That is a contract design idea rather than a collections feature, and it is the most useful thing in this file.

### Happy Path

1. A sync placement is licensed (domain 11) for a named production
2. A cue-sheet expectation is created against that placement
3. The production files; the cue sheet is confirmed (by the PRO's records, or by the supervisor)
4. Broadcast income arrives on the PRO's distribution schedule (`10.09`) and reconciles in `10.02`
5. The chain from placement to money is closed

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Production never files | **The characteristic failure.** No money, no error, no notice | A finding and a chase — and, honestly, often no remedy |
| Cue sheet filed with wrong details | Money goes to the wrong composer or nowhere | Only discovered by income that never arrives (`10.05.03`) |
| Composer cannot see the PRO's cue-sheet records | Frequently true — the records are the PRO's and the production's | The expectation is inferable; **confirmation may not be** |
| Placement not on the platform | Most back-catalogue sync | Manual entry; the value collapses without domain 11 |
| Foreign broadcast | Cue sheets are territorial, like everything else | Per-territory expectation; the same shape as `10.01.04` D-01 |
| Production is defunct | Nobody left to file | Honest dead end |
| Chase has no channel | The supervisor is not a user and has no reason to be | The feature is a task list pointed off-platform. See DT-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No placement | Nothing licensed | Feature is empty and irrelevant |
| Expected | Placement licensed | Cue sheet should exist |
| Confirmed | Filed and verified | Income expected on the PRO's calendar |
| Missing | Expected, not evidenced | Finding + chase, with an honest note on likely futility |
| Unverifiable | Cannot see the PRO's records | The most probable state. Honest uncertainty |
| Collecting | Income observed | Closed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the same feature as `10.06` setlist reporting — a usage report to a PRO | The sweep listed them adjacently and they rhyme: both tell a PRO what was performed so money flows | ❌ **REJECTED — they are opposites on the only axis that matters: who can act.** `10.06`'s setlist is reported by **the performer**, who is a user, holding their own PRO membership, standing in a venue the platform already knows about, at a moment the platform can prompt (`10.06` D-03). A cue sheet is filed by **a production company** that is not a user, has no incentive, and will never install anything. `10.06` is a capture problem WeJammin is uniquely positioned to solve; this is a **third-party compliance problem it can only observe**. Merging them would imply a symmetry that does not exist and would produce a feature promising to file something it cannot touch. The rhyme is in the output format; the product is entirely different. |
| DT-02 | If the platform can detect the gap, it can chase it | `10.05.03`'s logic — surface the finding, name the remedy, the user acts | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the honest core of the feature.** The chase targets **a music supervisor at a production company who is not a user, has no account, no notification channel, and no reason to care** — the same structural break as `credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-04 (a shell has no channel), except the target here is not even a shell: they are a stranger with no interest in ever becoming one. The platform can produce a task; it cannot produce an outcome. **Meanwhile the real leverage is upstream and belongs to a different domain**: domain 11's licence is where "licensee shall file a cue sheet within 30 days" could become a **term**. That is the only mechanism on this map that would actually move the number — and it is a contract-design decision made at licensing time, not a collections feature. This file's most valuable content is a recommendation to another domain. |
| DT-03 | Cue sheets are a major leak, so this is a major feature | It is genuinely a large industry problem, widely written about | ❌ **REJECTED as a MoSCoW conclusion.** The leak is real and large — for **composers with film/TV/game placements**, which is a small and specific slice of `personas.md`'s Musician and a segment WeJammin has no particular claim on. It is entirely downstream of domain 11 (no placement, no cue sheet), and per DT-02 the platform's power is limited to observing and nagging a stranger. The pattern is worth naming for the rest of the drill: **"large industry problem" and "feature this platform can move" are different tests**, and the sweep's candidate list conflates them. A big leak the platform cannot reach is still a big leak the platform cannot reach. |
