## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Black Box Search ↔ Claim Submission

**Relationship**: A deliberately unbalanced pair, and the imbalance is the design. `10.05.01` D-02 inverts `10.02.03`'s error budget: because the platform controls none of the money in an unclaimed pool, a false positive costs a rejected claim rather than a theft — so search should be **aggressive**, and the failure to fear is the false negative, money the user never hears about and never gets a second chance at.

That aggression is only safe because of what sits downstream. `10.05.01` DT-03 sets out the hazard plainly: 400 low-confidence candidates plus a frictionless claim button equals 400 claims, filed cheerfully and in good faith by someone reasoning *"I'll claim it and they'll tell me if I'm wrong"*. At scale that makes WeJammin the largest source of bad claims a society has ever seen — aimed at the exact institutions the whole of `10.01` needs a relationship with.

`10.05.02` D-02 is the answer: **a claim must cost something to make**, and the evidence pack is the cost. The friction is not UX debt to be optimised away in a later sprint; it is the thing that stops the product weaponising its own users against its own counterparties. Anyone who later proposes "one-click claim all" is proposing to destroy `10.01`.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: searches freely, claims with evidence. The asymmetry should be invisible to them and non-negotiable in the system.
- **Producer**: the highest-yield searcher (more sessions, more works) and the **corroborating witness** for other people's claims — `personas.md`'s trust broker, whose attestation outweighs a self-claim.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None. A candidate is the platform's inference; a claim is the user's assertion to a third party. The transition between them is a deliberate, evidenced act — not a state flag.
2. **Trigger chain**: Search → over-produce candidates → user confirms plausibility → **evidence assembled** → submit (or hand off) → expected-by → accepted/rejected/silent.
3. **Permission intersection**: The load-bearing one. Search is ungated (`10.05.01` D-01); claiming needs a mandate and standing (parent `Q-03`). Same user, same money, two different worlds.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Candidates notify the user. Claims notify a society — the platform's reputation rides on every one, which is why volume claiming is an existential risk rather than a support cost.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two users claiming the same unclaimed work — WeJammin filing against itself, at a body it needs to trust it. `10.05.02` routes it to domain 09, and the black box gets deeper rather than shallower.

### CX-02: Leakage Detection ↔ Black Box Search

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's causal spine, and the reason these are one sub-domain instead of two unrelated features filed near each other. Parent D-02: **an unregistered work is why its royalties sit unattributed at the MLC.** Leakage is the cause; black box is the effect, observed years later, from outside.

Run the causality both ways and both directions are useful:
- **Forward**: close a leak → stop producing black box. Prevention.
- **Backward**: a black-box hit → evidence of a specific past leak → tells the detector where its blind spots are. Calibration.

And then the uncomfortable consequence, which `10.01.04` DT-02 reached first from society registration: **if prevention works, WeJammin destroys its own recovery pitch's supply.** The platform's headline activation event — *"we found you £800"* — is powered by a failure the platform is simultaneously trying to eliminate. Both arguments are correct and they point at different roadmaps.

The honest resolution, and the one this drill proposes: **recover the back catalogue once; prevent from today, forever.** Recovery is a one-off per user (parent D-04: a once-event, not a habit). Prevention is permanent, compounding, and ungated. Only one of those is a business — but the other is the thing that gets people through the door.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: experiences recovery as a moment and prevention as an ongoing absence of bad news — which is a much harder thing to sell and a much better thing to have.
- **Producer**: highest yield on both, for the same reason: more works, more territories, more sessions, more gaps.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None. Detection reads internal expectations vs internal reality; search reads internal credits vs an external corpus. They share a subject (missing money) and no state.
2. **Trigger chain**: Leak → unregistered work → society collects → cannot attribute → black box → search finds it → claim (`10.05.02`) → **the leak is closed** (CX-03) → no future black box from that work.
3. **Permission intersection**: None between them — and their *external* permissions differ completely, which is CX-04.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both are money-denominated findings. `10.05.03` D-03 rate-limits to one at a time; a black-box hit is the rare finding that has earned an interruption.
5. **State transition conflict**: A leak remedied while a black-box claim on the same work is in flight — registration and claim arrive at the same body, about the same work, from the same platform. Almost certainly a conflict at the society. `10.05.02` Q-03's neighbourhood, and unresolved.

### CX-04: Black Box Search ↔ Leakage Detection _(the gating asymmetry)_

**Relationship**: The pair that decides the sub-domain's MoSCoW, and it inverts the obvious answer.

**Search** (`10.05.01`) needs: a credits graph the new user does not have (DT-02's circular dependency), which needs `02.03.01` import, which is gated on source licensing (`credits-attribution-index.md` Q-10); plus bulk access to unclaimed corpora (parent `Q-01`). **Claim** (`10.05.02`) adds three more gates: a mandate, standing with each body (parent `Q-03`), and societies that actually read evidence (`10.05.02` Q-01). Five gates, four outside the platform's control.

**Detection** (`10.05.03`) needs: nothing external. Every input is internal state the platform already holds — registration status, affiliations, cadences, calendars, ledger sums. Zero gates.

`10.05.03` DT-02 draws the conclusion: **the naive roadmap has these backwards.** Recovery gets built first because it has the money and the drama; detection gets built last because it is a list of gaps. But detection is cheaper, ungated, compounding, and causally upstream of the thing recovery exists to clean up.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: identical access to both. The asymmetry is entirely institutional, invisible to the user, and decisive for the build order.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None.
2. **Trigger chain**: Not a runtime chain — a **roadmap** chain. Detection ships without asking anyone's permission; search waits on a licence; claiming waits on a relationship.
3. **Permission intersection**: The whole content of the pair. Internal state vs external institutions.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both money-denominated, both subject to `10.05.03` D-03's one-at-a-time discipline.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — but the **narrative** conflict is real and unresolved: the product's first screen either shows the gap or shows the money (`10.05.03` Q-02), and those are different products.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 10.05.03 Leakage Detection | 10.05.02 Claim Submission _(as a **direct** producer of claims)_ | Rejected in that direction, and the direction matters. A leak is a **gap in registration** — the remedy is to register the work (`10.01`), not to claim against a pool. Claims arise only from `10.05.01`'s search against an external corpus. Wiring detection straight to claiming would suggest every gap has money sitting in a box waiting to be claimed, which is false: most leaks are *future* money that will now never be collected, not past money sitting unattributed. The genuine edge is CX-03, and it runs the other way — a successful claim closes a leak. |
| R-02 | 10.05.01 Black Box Search | 10.04.03 Unpayable Balances _(as one "find the money" feature)_ | Rejected as a merge despite parent D-05 naming them the same phenomenon. `10.05.01` searches **other institutions'** unclaimed pools using public data the platform does not control. `10.04.03` holds **the platform's own** unpayable balances, where WeJammin knows the amount, the work and the person exactly and simply cannot reach them. One is a discovery problem against an opaque third party; the other is a reachability problem the platform created. They rhyme — which is precisely why parent D-05 flags the hypocrisy risk — but merging them would imply the platform must *search* for money it already knows it holds, which would be an admission of a much worse failure than the one it has. |
| R-03 | 10.05.02 Claim Submission | 10.01.02 Work Registration Payload | Rejected as a pair despite both being "assemble a submission for a society". `10.01.02` projects a **registration** — a forward-looking assertion of ownership, built from domain 09's ledger, summing to 100%, with a publisher chain. `10.05.02` assembles a **claim** — a backward-looking assertion about a work someone *else* already registered or that nobody claimed, built from evidence rather than from a ledger. Different artifacts, different intake processes, different failure modes. They share a counterparty and the expected-by/alarm pattern (which is routed as a cross-cut), and nothing else. Merging them would put ownership-projection logic inside an evidence-assembly feature and produce a claim that argues from a ledger the society has never seen. |
