# Feature: Black Box & Unclaimed Royalty Search

> **Number**: 10.05.01
> **Parent**: [Recovery & Leakage](./10.05-recovery-leakage-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Split from sweep candidate 07 — the search half, separated from the claim (parent `D-01`, domain `D-13`)

## Overview

Searching the **unclaimed royalty pools** — the MLC's unmatched data, SoundExchange's and PPL's unregistered-performer lists, PRO unclaimed-works lists — against a user's identity and credits graph, and telling them what looks like theirs. *"There are 43 entries across three pools that match you."*

The parent domain's Overview names this as **the strongest activation event in the corpus**, and the reason is exact: it is real money, already collected, belonging to a specific person who cannot find it because matching requires precisely the credits-plus-identity graph this platform builds. It is also, per parent `D-04`, **a once-event rather than a habit** — which is why it is the activation and not the wedge (`D-18`).

The deepening pass changed the feature's shape in three ways, each of which moves the ship date or the risk:

1. **"The unclaimed pool" is not one thing** (`DT-04`). It is at least three corpora, keyed on **opposite sides of the work/recording duality**, asking different questions, and answered by different evidence. Treating them as one corpus produces a matcher that is wrong about all three.
2. **The activation event has a graph-free entry point** (`DT-05`). The performer pools (SoundExchange, PPL) are keyed on **performing name + recording** — the exact fields a brand-new user has before they have done anything. This partially breaks `DT-02`'s circular dependency, which was the feature's stated viability risk.
3. **The pool has an expiry** (`DT-07`). Unclaimed money is not held forever; under the MMA it is swept to publishers **by market share**. The clock belongs to the institution, it is running, and the platform did not start it.

## Role Lens

Their leverage is knowledge no corpus records: shown `SUNSET DRIVE / feat. Jonny Blaze / ISRC GBxxx`, **they know whether they played on it**, and no ranking function does. ## Behavior

The search matches **the user's identity and credits graph against public unclaimed corpora**. It is `10.02.03`'s machinery pointed at a different target: instead of *"which of your works is this statement line about?"*, it asks *"which of these unclaimed entries is yours?"*

### The error budget is inverted, and the inversion is the design

It inherits `10.02.03`'s discipline in a **softer** form:

- `10.02.03` refuses to auto-accept because a wrong match **pays the wrong person** (`10.02.03` D-01). The platform controls that money, the failure is silent, and it is theft.
- Here, a wrong match costs **a rejected claim**. The pool is not money the platform controls; a candidate is a suggestion to investigate. The cost of a false positive is disappointment and some wasted effort.

So this feature can be **aggressive where `10.02.03` must be conservative** — and it should be, because the failure mode that matters is a false *negative*: money the user never learns about, which under `DT-07` does not sit and wait but is **swept to somebody else on a schedule** (`D-02`).

**The scope of that aggression is precise, and `DT-09` bounds it**: `D-02` governs the **match** between a credit and a pool entry. It does **not** govern whether the credit is evidence at all. A fuzzy tolerance on a real credit is a designed risk; a candidate generated from a self-asserted credit is a fabrication with a confidence score on it (`D-11`).

### The corpus is three corpora (`DT-04`)

The single most consequential finding of this pass. The pools are keyed on **opposite sides of the work/recording duality** (domain 09), and the platform's evidence answers them with wildly different strength:

| Pool | What it holds | What is missing | The question it asks | The platform's answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Performer pools** (SoundExchange, PPL) | Recording + **performing/artist name** + ISRC | A registered payee for the performer | *"Who is this artist, and where do we send it?"* | **Strongest and earliest.** Keyed on the performing name — which is what the credits graph holds natively, and what a brand-new user has before they have a graph at all (`DT-05`) |
| **Mechanical unmatched** (MLC) | **Recording** metadata — ISRC, recording title, artist, sometimes duration/release | **The work and its writers** — which is *why* it is unmatched | *"Which musical work is this recording of, and who wrote it?"* | **Weakest.** The platform must supply the recording→work→writer chain. For the forward catalogue the session capture has it; for the **back catalogue, where the money is**, it does not |
| **Works-side unclaimed** (PRO lists, MLC works with share gaps) | Work + registered writers + **shares that do not sum** | The owner of the **missing share** | *"Whose is the 25% nobody has claimed?"* | **Sharpest, and the thesis cashing out** — the gap has a *size*, and the size is evidence (`DT-06`) |

Three consequences, all load-bearing:

- **A candidate is typed by its pool and its right type, and candidates are never merged across them** (`D-06`). This is `10.02.03` `D-16` reached independently from the other end: one recording can produce a performer candidate at SoundExchange *and* a writer candidate at the MLC, both correct, both the same human, and **neither of them the same money**. Collapsing them to "43 works" triple-counts and destroys the number.
- **The naive build order is backwards.** The obvious first target is the MLC, because it is the famous pool with the famous $424M. It is also the pool where the platform's evidence is weakest and the required chain longest. The performer pool is easier, earlier, and needs no graph.
- **`10.02.03` DT-06's name gap inverts here.** There, the credits graph holds *performing* names while societies print *registered* names, so the party discriminator silently fails on exactly the multi-hyphenate population `personas.md` centres. Here the performer pools print the **performing** name, because that is what DSP usage reporting carries. **The same gap that breaks statement matching is what makes this search work on day one.**

### A pool entry is not atomic (`DT-06`)

A works-side entry is frequently not *unclaimed* but **partially claimed**: a work registered with 75% accounted for and 25% with no valid payee. The unclaimed thing is a **share**, held by a role, on a work — not the work.

So a candidate is `(pool, work-or-recording, share, role, right type)`, never a work (`D-08`). And the gap's **size is a signal class in its own right**: if the pool shows a 25% writer share unclaimed, and domain 09 holds a split — agreed in the room, dated, signed — that gave this user 25% of that work, the arithmetic corroborates the name. That is **two independent signal classes**, which clears `10.02.03` `D-15`'s floor, from a discriminator no competitor can hold because no competitor was at the session.

The split must be read **as of the usage period, not as it stands today** (parent `D-02`, `Q-05`). A 25% gap on a 2019 usage is corroborated by the 2019 split, not the 2026 one.

### The pool has an expiry, and the clock is not ours (`DT-07`)

Unclaimed royalties are not held forever. Under the MMA, accrued unmatched mechanical royalties the MLC cannot attribute are, after a statutory holding period, **distributed to copyright owners on a market-share basis** — which means the money leaves the pool and goes, permanently and lawfully, to the publishers with the largest market share. It is not stolen; it is swept.

This changes three things:

- **`D-02`'s false-negative fear becomes concrete.** A missed candidate is not vaguely "lost forever" — it is transferred to a major publisher on a **specific date**.
- **Ranking is not by size** (`D-09`). A £40 candidate sweeping in six weeks outranks a £400 candidate with three years on the clock, because only one of them can still be lost.
- **This is parent `D-04` inverted.** The domain's defining discipline is *"an absence produces no event, so declare an expectation and let a clock manufacture one."* Here the **institution already has a clock** and simply does not tell the beneficiary it is running. The platform does not need to manufacture the event; it needs to **surface an event that already exists and is invisible** — which is cheaper than everything else in domain 10 and at least as valuable.

The exact holding period per pool is `Q-04` — the *mechanism* is certain and the calendar is not, and `D-09` needs the calendar.

### The search is a subscription, not a query (`DT-11`)

A user who searches at signup, finds nothing, and is told "nothing" once has been told the truth exactly once and then abandoned. Both operands move: the pools **refresh** (the MLC publishes new unmatched data on a cadence; new usage accrues continuously), and the user's **graph grows** as they capture sessions and import catalogue. A null result is a fact about one moment.

So the search re-runs on pool refresh and on material graph growth (`D-13`), and a finding arrives as a notification. **This does not contradict parent `D-04`** — the *haul* (the back-catalogue "we found you 43 entries") is genuinely a once-event, and a standing search is not a daily habit; it is an absence of one. The user never returns to search. The platform returns to them.

The reframe that matters: **`D-16`'s tension resolves into a loop.** The domain fears that prevention (`10.01.04`) destroys recovery's supply. But prevention is imperfect by construction — `D-10` establishes that registration is blocked on IPIs the platform cannot mint or look up, so WeJammin will keep producing black box **for its own users, out of its own forward catalogue**. A standing search against the pools is therefore the **audit of prevention**: it is how the platform discovers that its own registration silently failed, from the outside, in money. Recovery is not only the door. It is the check on the competence.

### What the platform will not do

- **It will not promise.** The corpora are thin, several publish works without sums, and nothing is yours until a body agrees. *"43 possible matches"* is honest; *"£800 is yours"* is not (`D-03`).
- **It will not leave the amount blank and let hope fill it** (`DT-10`, `D-12`). Omitting the number is not neutrality — a user told "43 entries" with no figure imagines a life-changing sum and receives £12. The platform states, in the same breath as the count, **that it does not know and will not guess, and why**.
- **It will not search past the mandate** (`D-10`). The graph reaches people the searcher has no standing over.
- **It will not build a candidate from an assertion** (`D-11`).

### Happy Path

1. User signs up and confirms their **performing name(s)** and identity (domain 01) — no credits, no import, no work required
2. System searches the **performer pools** (SoundExchange, PPL) on performing name + any identifiers held. This is the graph-free door (`DT-05`) and it is the only thing on the screen at t=0
3. Candidates return **typed by pool and right type** (`D-06`), ranked by `D-09`'s order — sweep-imminent first, money second — each carrying **its evidence** (`D-05`): *"SoundExchange holds an unregistered-performer entry for `SUNSET DRIVE`, featured artist `Jonny Blaze`, ISRC GBxxxxxxxxxxx"* — and carrying, explicitly, **the statement that the platform does not know what it is worth** (`D-12`)
4. User recognises the recordings — they are the discriminator no ranking function replaces — and confirms or dismisses each
5. In parallel, the platform invites the user to **import a catalogue** (`02.03.01`) or capture a session (`02.02`), stating plainly that it widens the search into the work-side pools
6. Graph arrives. System re-sweeps (`D-13`) and now runs the **works-side** cascade: identifier → **share-gap arithmetic corroborated against 09's split as of the usage period** (`DT-06`) → fuzzy on title + party + credits graph
7. Share-gap candidates surface with the strongest evidence the feature produces: *"The MLC shows this work 75% claimed. The 25% writer share has no payee. Your split sheet from 14 March 2019, signed by four people, gives you 25%."*
8. Candidates whose evidence names **someone else in the user's session graph** are **not shown to the searcher**. They are routed to that person if they are on the platform (`D-10`, `DT-08`), and to nobody if they are not
9. User confirms which are plausibly theirs; dismissals are permanent (`D-14`)
10. Confirmed candidates go to `10.05.02` for evidence assembly and claim — where the friction lives, and where `DT-03` is answered
11. The search **stays running** (`D-13`). Pool refreshes and graph growth re-sweep silently; a new finding arrives as a notification, money-denominated, one at a time (`10.05.03` D-03's discipline)
12. A finding on a work the user **already registered** is not a claim — it is a **registration failure**, routed to `10.01.04` (`D-15`). The prevention audit closing the loop (`DT-11`)

### States

Two state models, deliberately separate: the candidate's fact, and the surface.

#### Candidate state

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Matched (performer pool)** | Performing name + recording hit, no graph required | The t=0 activation candidate. Evidence is the name's distinctiveness + recording metadata the user can recognise (`DT-05`) |
| **Matched (identifier)** | ISRC/ISWC on a pool entry hits a known recording/work | Near-certain, and the earliest place domain 09's identifier discipline pays off outside statements |
| **Candidate (share gap)** | Works-side gap whose **size** corroborates 09's split as of the usage period | The strongest evidence the feature produces (`DT-06`). Name *and* arithmetic |
| **Candidate (fuzzy)** | ≥2 independent signal classes agree | Ranked, evidence shown, **no amount promised** (`D-03`, `D-12`) |
| **Below floor** | Single signal class only (bare name, bare title) | Never surfaced as confirmable (`D-05`). Counted, so the user knows silence was a decision |
| **Routed away** | Evidence names a third party (`D-10`) | Invisible to the searcher, by design. Sent to the subject if reachable; discarded if not |
| **Expiring** | Sweep date inside the alarm window (`DT-07`) | Ranked above everything larger and safer (`D-09`) |
| **Expired** | Sweep executed; entry gone from the pool | Named with its date and mechanism — **never silently removed** |
| **Confirmed** | User asserts it is plausibly theirs | → `10.05.02`. Not a claim yet, and the difference is stated |
| **Dismissed** | User says no | Permanent (`D-14`). Retained as evidence and as training data |
| **Unclaimable** | Found, but no route to claim (parent `D-03`) | A named gap with the requirement stated, not a dead link |
| **Reclassified → finding** | Match on a work the user already registered | Leaves this feature entirely → `10.01.04` (`D-15`, `DT-11`) |

#### Surface state

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Sweep running | Progress denominated in **pools searched / inputs searched** — never a spinner over a number the user was never shown |
| **Empty (no input)** | No credits **and** no distinctive performing name | The true cold wall, now narrow (`DT-05`). Routed to `02.03.01` with the reason stated in money |
| **Empty (searched, nothing found)** | Sweep complete, zero candidates | Honest and common for genuinely new artists. **On a dense graph this is a signal, not a result** — and is stated as one, because a silent zero is indistinguishable from an outage |
| **Populated** | Candidates found | Ranked by `D-09` — **sweep-imminence first, money second**. Typed by pool and right type; never merged (`D-06`) |
| **Partial** | Some pools unreachable, or rate limit hit mid-sweep | Named and quantified. A partial sweep **never presents as complete** |
| **Error** | No pool reachable; identity unresolved | Explicit and blocking. Never an empty state — "you have no money" is the one lie this feature must never tell |
| **Watching** | Sweep complete; subscription standing (`D-13`) | The destination state, and it is quiet: *"nothing new. We are still looking."* Findings arrive as notifications, one at a time, money-denominated |

| DT-04 | "The unclaimed pool" is one corpus with inconsistent data quality, so one matcher with tuned thresholds covers it | How every source describes it, and how the sweep candidate was named ("Black Box & Unclaimed Royalty Recovery" — singular) | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the largest finding of the pass.** They are at least three corpora keyed on **opposite sides of the work/recording duality**, and the difference is not data quality — it is *which question is being asked*. **Performer pools** (SoundExchange, PPL) hold recording + performing name and lack a payee: *"who is this artist?"* **Mechanical unmatched** (MLC) holds recording metadata and lacks the work and its writers — that absence is definitionally *why* it is unmatched, so a matcher built expecting a writer column finds an empty one and reports zero. **Works-side unclaimed** (PRO lists, MLC share gaps) holds the work and its registered writers and lacks the owner of a **specific missing share**. Three consequences: (1) a candidate must be **typed by pool and right type and never merged across them** — which is `10.02.03` D-16 reached independently from the other end, and the reason "43 works" is a triple-counting bug rather than a headline; (2) **the naive build order is backwards** — the famous pool (MLC, the famous $424M) is the one where the platform's evidence is weakest and the required chain longest; (3) the platform's advantage is not uniform, so a single confidence number across three corpora is meaningless. |
| DT-05 | DT-02's circular dependency is uniform — every pool needs a graph, so the activation event is gated on import behind a licensing question | DT-02's own conclusion, which treated "the pool" as one thing (DT-04's error) | ✅ **CONFIRMED for the work-side pools and REJECTED for the performer pools — and the exception is the feature's ship date.** SoundExchange and PPL hold **unregistered-performer** entries keyed on the **performing/artist name** and the recording, because that is what DSP and broadcast usage reporting carries. Matching against them requires: a name. Not a credits graph, not an import, not a licence to ingest MusicBrainz — **a name a user types in their first sixty seconds.** And per `D-11` those bodies **admit performers directly**, so the user's own membership is the credential and parent `D-03`'s accreditation gate does not apply either. **The activation event has a graph-free door, and DT-02 missed it by treating three corpora as one.** The symmetry is the pleasing part: `10.02.03` DT-06 establishes that the credits graph holds *performing* names while societies print *registered* names, so the party discriminator silently fails on exactly the multi-hyphenate population `personas.md` centres — the platform's biggest matching weakness. **Here that same fact is the strength**: the performer pools print the performing name, which is precisely what the graph (and the user) has. The same gap, load-bearing in opposite directions, in two features of one domain. |
| DT-06 | A pool entry is atomic — a work is claimed or it is not, so the match is work-level and the evidence is the name | The intuitive model, and the one "unclaimed works" as a phrase implies | ❌ **REJECTED — the unclaimed thing is a *share*, and its *size* is evidence the platform uniquely holds.** Works-side entries are frequently not unclaimed but **partially claimed**: a work registered with 75% accounted for and 25% with no valid payee. That is a large fraction of what "black box" actually is — not an anonymous work, but a known work with a hole in it. So a candidate is `(pool, work/recording, share, role, right type)`, never a work. **And then the finding that matters**: the hole has a **size**. If the MLC shows a 25% writer share unclaimed, and domain 09 holds a split agreed in the room, dated and signed, giving this user 25% of that work — **the arithmetic corroborates the name.** That is a second independent signal class, clearing `10.02.03` D-15's floor, produced by a discriminator no competitor can have because no competitor was at the session. This is `D-18`'s thesis cashing out as a *matching primitive* rather than as marketing, and it is the strongest evidence the feature generates. Two constraints keep it honest: the split must be read **as of the usage period** (parent `D-02`), and where 09 cannot answer as-of (`Q-05`) the arithmetic signal is **withheld rather than approximated** — a corroboration computed from the wrong year's split is a confident wrong answer, which is worse than no answer. |
| DT-07 | The pool is a static backlog — money sits unclaimed indefinitely, so the search can be leisurely and ranked by size | Every description of the black box, which frames it as money *sitting there*; and the parent's "once-event" framing, which implies no clock | ❌ **REJECTED — the pool drains on a schedule, and the platform did not start the clock.** Under the MMA, accrued unmatched mechanical royalties the MLC cannot attribute are, after a statutory holding period, **distributed to copyright owners on a market-share basis**. The money is not stolen and it does not wait: it is **swept, lawfully, to the publishers with the largest market share** — which is to say, to precisely the institutions the platform's pitch is aimed against, by operation of a statute written to solve the problem. Three consequences: (1) **DT-01/D-02's false negative acquires a date** — a missed candidate is not vaguely "lost forever," it is transferred on a specific day; (2) **ranking by size is wrong** (`D-09`) — a £40 candidate sweeping in six weeks outranks a £400 candidate with three years left, because only one can still be lost, and this is the only ranking in domain 10 where money is the *second* key; (3) **parent `D-04` inverts.** The domain's defining discipline is that an absence produces no event so the platform must declare an expectation and manufacture a clock. Here **the institution already has the clock** and simply never tells the beneficiary. The platform does not need to manufacture the event — it needs to **surface one that already exists and is invisible**, which is the cheapest high-value thing in domain 10. The calendar itself is `Q-04`: the mechanism is certain, the per-pool periods are not, and `D-09` cannot rank without them. |
| DT-08 | Search scope is "everything the graph can match" — the graph is the user's, so its reach is theirs | The obvious wiring, and the one a first implementation produces without noticing | ❌ **REJECTED — the graph knows about other people, and this is a privacy failure disguised as a feature.** A Producer's session graph contains five musicians. A performer-pool entry for that recording is frequently the **drummer's** neighbouring-rights money. Show it to the Producer as a candidate and the platform has taken a *public* pool entry, joined it to a *private* credits graph, and handed the resulting inference — *"Sarah has unclaimed money"* — to Sarah's employer. **The pool being public is what makes this feel safe and is exactly why it isn't**: one operand is public, the join is not, and the inference is a private financial fact about a third party manufactured by the platform. Claiming it would be theft; merely *showing* it is a leak. **But the correct wiring inverts it into the best mechanic in the corpus.** The platform now knows something valuable about Sarah, discovered through someone else's search: **tell Sarah.** A cold outreach carrying real money, sourced from a collaborator's session, is `D-18`'s network effect in its most concentrated form — one Producer's back catalogue becomes an acquisition channel for every musician who ever played in their room, each arriving to a screen that already has their money on it. So `D-10` scopes the search by **mandate, not by graph reach**, and third-party candidates route to their subject or are discarded. Whether the routing is a delight or an incident is `Q-05` and it is a product decision, not an engineering one. |
| DT-09 | More candidates is better — DT-01's inverted error budget says be aggressive, so generate from every credit the graph holds | `D-02`, applied consistently and without noticing what it governs | ❌ **REJECTED at the input, confirmed at the match — and the distinction is load-bearing.** `D-02`'s aggression governs the **match between a credit and a pool entry**. It says nothing about whether the credit is evidence at all, and conflating the two builds the platform's worst attack surface. `personas.md` names the Musician's worst intentional behaviour as *"inflates credits to claim work they didn't do"*; give that user an ungated search and the platform does the expensive half of the fraud for them — it **finds the money, ranks it by value, and hands back a list of which lies are worth telling.** That is strictly worse than DT-03's optimistic volume-claimer: it is deliberate, it is cheap, and the platform is the reconnaissance tool. The mitigation is not a confidence threshold — a fabricated credit produces a *genuinely* high-confidence match, because the fabricator chose it to. **Candidate generation is gated on credit provenance** (`D-11`): `02.04`'s attestation tiers decide whether a credit is an input, and a self-asserted credit with nobody behind it produces no ranked candidate. And the inversion is the good part: **the search's yield becomes the reason to get your credits attested**, which is domain 02's entire product. The activation event stops being a standalone hook and starts *pulling the wedge* — which is the pairing `D-18` claims and rarely gets to demonstrate this cleanly. |
| DT-10 | Refusing to state an amount (`D-03`) is the honest, safe position | `D-15` (state what you know), applied literally — and it is the right rule | ❌ **REJECTED as sufficient — silence is not neutral, and the blank gets filled.** `D-03` is correct that "£800 is yours" is a lie. But *"43 entries"* with no figure does not leave the user with no expectation; it leaves them with **their own**, and their imagination is calibrated on the $424M headline, not on a mechanical royalty for a 2019 album track. They picture a deposit. They receive £12, four months later, having filled in an evidence pack. The platform told no lie and destroyed the trust anyway — and it did it at the **activation moment**, which is the one impression it gets. This is `D-15` under-applied: the rule is *state what you know **and say so when you don't***, and the second clause was being satisfied by omission. So the count and the not-knowing arrive **in the same breath** (`D-12`): *"43 entries. We don't know what they're worth — this pool publishes works, not sums — and nobody does until a body agrees."* Naming the unknown is the honest act; leaving a blank and letting hope fill it is the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` blames the industry for, committed by silence instead of by assertion. |
| DT-11 | The search is a query — run it once at signup, return the haul, done. Parent `D-04` says it is a once-event | Parent `D-04`, correctly read; and the "we found you £800" framing, which is a moment, not a process | ❌ **REJECTED as a mechanism, CONFIRMED as an experience — and the gap between them resolves `D-16`.** Both operands move. The **pools refresh** (the MLC publishes unmatched data on a cadence; new usage accrues continuously) and the **graph grows** (sessions captured, catalogue imported). A null result is a fact about one moment, and a user told "nothing" once has been told the truth exactly once and then abandoned — permanently, because they will never think to search again. So the search is a **standing subscription** (`D-13`), re-swept on pool refresh and material graph growth, with findings arriving as notifications. **This does not contradict parent `D-04`**: the *haul* is genuinely a once-event, and a standing search is not a daily habit — it is the *absence* of one. The user never returns; the platform returns to them. **And then the reframe, which is the real content**: `D-16` fears that prevention (`10.01.04`) destroys recovery's supply, and treats that as a strategic tension to be chosen between. It is a **loop**, not a tension. Prevention is imperfect *by construction* — `D-10` establishes that registration is blocked on IPIs the platform can neither mint nor look up, so WeJammin will keep producing black box **out of its own users' forward catalogue**. A standing search against the pools is therefore the **audit of prevention**: the mechanism by which the platform discovers, from outside and in money, that its own registration silently failed. Recovery is not only the door. It is the check on the competence — which is a much better reason to build it than the hook, and it survives prevention working. |
| DT-12 | `10.02.03` DT-08 kills the recovery pitch: the credits-graph advantage is ~zero at launch (imported public data any competitor has) and lands on the *forward* catalogue, while recovery points at the *back* one | `10.02.03` DT-08 and `Q-03`, which state this explicitly and pointedly name this feature | ✅ **CONFIRMED as a marketing constraint, ❌ REJECTED as a functional one — and the conflation would cost the feature for no reason.** DT-08 is about **competitive advantage in matching**: at launch the graph is MusicBrainz/Discogs data anyone can import, so WeJammin's matcher is not better than a hypothetical competitor's. Every word of that is true, and none of it is about this feature, because **this feature does not need an edge — it needs an input.** Imported public credits cover the **back catalogue**, which is exactly where the black box concentrates; the fact that a competitor *could* import the same corpus is irrelevant while **no competitor is pointing any matcher at this user's name.** The advantage here is not the data's exclusivity — it is that the search is being run at all, on behalf of someone who did not know they could ask. So DT-08 forbids one specific sentence (*"our matching finds more than theirs"*) and permits the one that actually sells: *"nobody has ever searched for your money, and we did."* And DT-05/DT-06 supply what DT-08 says the graph cannot at launch: the performer pool needs no graph, and the share-gap arithmetic corroborates against a **split**, which is domain 09's contemporaneous record — not a matching heuristic at all. |
