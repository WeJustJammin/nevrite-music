# Feature: Black Box & Unclaimed Royalty Search

> **Number**: 10.05.01
> **Parent**: [Recovery & Leakage](./10.05-recovery-leakage-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Searching the **unclaimed royalty pools** — the MLC's public unmatched database, PRO unclaimed lists, SoundExchange's unregistered performer pool — against a user's credits graph, and telling them what is theirs. *"There are 43 works in the MLC's unmatched pool that match your name and your sessions."*

The parent domain's Overview names this as **the strongest activation event in the corpus**, and the reason is exact: it is real money, already collected, belonging to a specific person who cannot find it because matching requires precisely the credits-plus-identity graph this platform builds. It is also, per parent `D-04`, **a once-event rather than a habit** — which is why it is the activation and not the wedge.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Searches for their own money against their credits. **The signup moment**: an artist who has never registered anything with the MLC almost certainly has unmatched works there, and this is the first product that will ever tell them. `personas.md` names their switching trigger as "losing money or a gig to a fact they couldn't prove" — this is the fact and the money in one screen. |
| Producer | Full | Own works plus entities they administer. A producer with a back catalogue of sessions is the highest-yield search on the platform, because the pool is keyed on the works they touched. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The search matches **the user's credits graph against public unclaimed corpora**. It is the same machinery as `10.02.03` line matching, pointed at a different target: instead of "which of your works is this statement line about?", it asks "which of these unclaimed works is yours?".

Crucially it inherits `10.02.03`'s discipline in a **softer** form, and the difference is the whole design:

- `10.02.03` refuses to auto-accept because a wrong match **pays the wrong person** (`10.02.03` D-01). The cost of a false positive is theft.
- Here, a wrong match costs **a rejected claim**. The pool is not money the platform controls; a candidate is a suggestion to investigate. The cost of a false positive is disappointment and some wasted effort.

So this feature can be **aggressive where `10.02.03` must be conservative** — and it should be, because the failure mode that matters is a false *negative*: money the user never learns about, which stays lost forever.

What it must not do is promise. The pool's public data is thin — often a title and a writer surname. "43 possible matches" is honest; "£800 is yours" is not, until `10.05.02` files something and a body agrees.

### Happy Path

1. User connects their identity and whatever credits they have (`02.03.01` import, or a captured session graph)
2. System searches the public unclaimed corpora against those credits
3. Candidates return, ranked, with the evidence for each — "this unmatched work's writer surname matches you, and the title matches a session you played"
4. User reviews and confirms which are plausibly theirs
5. Confirmed candidates go to `10.05.02` for claim assembly

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| User has no credits on the platform yet | The search has no input. **The activation event requires the graph to exist first** | The cold-start problem, sitting directly on the acquisition moment. See DT-02 |
| Common name | "J. Smith" matches hundreds of unclaimed works | Ranked with corroborating evidence; never a bare name match |
| Pool data is a title and a surname | Frequently all there is | Low confidence is honest; the ceiling is set by the corpus, not the algorithm |
| Candidate is a different J. Smith's work | Claiming it would be fraud, cheerfully committed by an optimistic user | The platform must not encourage volume claiming. See DT-03 |
| Pool is not public / not bulk-ingestible | Parent `Q-01`. MLC is open; PRO lists are patchy | The feature's reach is set by data access, not by ambition |
| Money found, cannot be claimed | Search is ungated; claim is not (parent `D-03`) | Honest: "this looks like yours, and here is what claiming requires" |
| Amounts aren't published | Some pools list works, not sums | "43 works" is the honest headline; "£800" may be unknowable |
| User claims everything plausible | The predictable behaviour once money is visible | Rate-limited; false claims are a Trust & Safety pattern (domain 24) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No graph | User has no credits yet | The cold-start wall — the activation event's precondition |
| Searching | Graph exists, corpora queried | In progress |
| Candidates | Matches found | Ranked, with evidence, **without a promised amount** |
| Empty | No matches | Honest and common for genuinely new artists |
| Confirmed | User asserts theirs | → `10.05.02` |
| Unclaimable | Found but no route to claim | Named gap, not a dead link |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is `10.02.03` line matching pointed at a different corpus, so it should reuse it wholesale — including `10.02.03` D-01's no-auto-accept rule | The machinery genuinely is the same: fuzzy match, candidates, confidence, confirmation | ❌ **REJECTED on the rule, confirmed on the machinery — and the asymmetry is the design.** `10.02.03` D-01 forbids auto-accept because a wrong match **pays the wrong person**: the platform controls that money, the failure is silent, and it is theft. Here the platform controls nothing. A wrong candidate costs a rejected claim — disappointment, not theft. **So the error budgets invert**: `10.02.03` must fear false positives, this feature must fear false *negatives*, because money the user never hears about stays lost forever and there is no second chance. Same engine, opposite tuning. A spec that inherits `10.02.03`'s conservatism here would suppress exactly the finds that justify the feature. |
| DT-02 | This is the acquisition hook — lead with it, because "we found you £800" is the strongest message the platform has | The parent domain's Overview says exactly this, and it is right about the message | ❌ **REJECTED as an acquisition strategy, though the message is real — and the reason is a circular dependency nobody has named.** The search needs a **credits graph** to match against. A brand-new user has no graph (`02.03`'s cold start). So the activation event that is supposed to acquire the user **requires the user to have already done the work the platform is trying to acquire them for.** The only escape is `02.03.01` external import — MusicBrainz, Discogs, PRO data — which is itself gated on source licensing (`credits-attribution-index.md` Q-10). **The strongest hook on the map is downstream of the platform's most legally uncertain feature**, and that dependency should be visible at MoSCoW rather than discovered in month six. |
| DT-03 | Show the user everything plausible and let them claim what they recognise | The user knows their own work better than any algorithm; this is just empowerment | ❌ **REJECTED — the incentive structure is a fraud machine and the platform builds it knowingly.** Once money is visible, a user with 400 low-confidence candidates and a claim button has every reason to claim all 400 and see what sticks; the cost to them is zero and the upside is real. That is claim fraud at scale, committed by ordinary people who are not thinking of it as fraud, using a tool the platform gave them — and `personas.md` already names "inflates credits to claim work they didn't do" as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour. **The corollary is that this feature cannot be separated from `10.05.02`'s evidence requirement**: a claim must cost something to make, or the platform becomes an industrial false-claim generator pointed at institutions it needs a relationship with. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Search is ungated** — public corpora, no accreditation needed | Parent D-03. This is why it is the domain's only genuinely free activation event. |
| D-02 | The error budget is **inverted vs `10.02.03`**: fear false negatives, not false positives | DT-01. The platform controls no money here; an unfound pound is lost forever. |
| D-03 | **No promised amounts.** "43 possible matches", never "£800 is yours" | The corpus often publishes works without sums, and nothing is yours until a body agrees. |
| D-04 | Cannot be shipped without `10.05.02`'s evidence requirement | DT-03. Search + a frictionless claim button = an industrial false-claim generator. |
| D-05 | Candidates carry **their evidence**, never a bare name match | A common surname matches hundreds of works; a name alone is not a finding. |
