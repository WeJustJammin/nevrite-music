# Feature: Distribution Calendar & Money-in-Flight

> **Number**: 10.09
> **Parent**: [Royalties & Collections](./royalties-collections-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not a sweep candidate

## Overview

Making the **royalty pipeline legible**: when each society and source distributes, for which usage periods, with what lag — and therefore what money is in flight right now, from where, arriving roughly when.

*"I released in January, it's July, where is my money?"* is the most common question in music finance and it has a precise, knowable, published answer that nobody assembles: usage happens → the DSP reports it (~2-3 months) → the distributor processes (~1-2 months) → the society distributes (quarterly or half-yearly, on a published calendar) → you are paid. Six to eighteen months, entirely predictable, and completely opaque to the person waiting.

The sweep proposed "Royalty Forecasting" (`10.10`) and not this. They are different: forecasting predicts what you might earn; this states **what has already happened and where it currently is.**

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Sees their money in flight: which registered works are earning, at which bodies, for which usage periods, due on which distribution dates. `personas.md` names their success criterion as "income visible in one place" — this is the half of that which is *not yet income* and is the half nobody has ever shown them. |
| Producer | Reports | Same, across administered entities. More sources, more lag, more confusion to dispel. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

Two layers, and the distinction is what keeps the feature honest:

- **The calendar** — reference data: per society, per territory, per income type, when do they distribute and for which usage periods? PRS distributes Q1 performance in October. SoundExchange pays monthly. The MLC pays monthly on matched usage. This is **published, institutional, mostly stable, and nobody has collected it in one place.**
- **Money-in-flight** — the calendar applied to *this user's* registered works: "your Q1 2026 PRS performance is due in the October distribution."

The claim must be scoped precisely. The platform can say **when money is due**, because that is published. It usually cannot say **how much**, because it does not have usage data at the society. So the honest output is *"Q1 2026 PRS performance — due October 2026 — amount unknown"*, and inventing the amount would be the temptation and the lie.

The feature's quiet, load-bearing job is to make **`10.05.03` leakage detection possible.** Knowing money should arrive in October is the only way to notice in November that it didn't. Without a calendar there is no expectation, without an expectation there is no absence, and without an absence there is no leak — the money simply never appears, forever, unremarked. This is the domain's recurring pattern for the fifth time (`10.01.03` D-03, `10.01.04` D-02, `10.02.01` D-02, `10.02.05` D-05, `10.04.03` D-03): **declare an expectation so the absence has something to violate.**

### Happy Path

1. A work is registered at a body for a territory (`10.01.04`)
2. The calendar says that body distributes that income type on that schedule for those usage periods
3. Money-in-flight shows: this work, this body, this usage period, due this date
4. The distribution lands; the statement arrives (`10.02.01`); the expectation is met
5. Anything that did not arrive becomes a `10.05.03` finding — automatically, because the expectation existed

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Amount is unknowable | The normal case. The platform knows *when*, not *how much* | Date without amount. **Never a fabricated figure** |
| Society changes its calendar | Happens. The expectation silently becomes wrong | Calendar is data, not code (`10.01.03` D-02's pattern) |
| Work isn't registered | Nothing is in flight, and the user may believe otherwise | The most valuable finding this feature produces — nothing is coming |
| Distribution late | Societies slip | Tolerance before alarming; a slipped distribution is not a leak |
| Money arrives from an unexpected source | A distribution nobody predicted | Reconciles anyway; the calendar is not a gate on income |
| Pipeline is 18 months | The user's frustration is **correct** and the platform cannot fix it | Legibility is the deliverable, not speed. Honest and unsatisfying |
| Territory-specific calendars | Every society, every income type, differs | The reference data is the work. See DT-03 |
| Black-box holding period | Unmatched money is held for years before redistribution | Explains where the black box comes from — a rare teachable moment |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Nothing in flight | No registrations | Honest emptiness — and a leakage finding |
| In flight | Registered, usage period closed | Body, period, due date; amount usually unknown |
| Due | Distribution date reached | Expecting a statement |
| Arrived | Statement ingested (`10.02.01`) | Reconciled |
| Overdue | Past the distribution date + tolerance | → `10.05.03` finding |
| Unknown | No calendar for that body | The platform says it does not know, rather than guessing |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is part of forecasting (`10.10`) — both are "money you don't have yet" | The natural merge; the sweep proposed only forecasting, and they overlap on the calendar | ❌ **REJECTED — they differ on the property that matters most in this domain: whether the platform is asserting a fact or a guess.** Money-in-flight is **deterministic**: this work is registered, this society publishes this calendar, therefore this money is due on this date. Forecasting is **statistical**: based on your run rate you might earn roughly this much. A wrong forecast is embarrassing; a wrong in-flight claim is a broken promise about money the user is now counting on. Merging them would blend a published institutional fact with a regression output under one confidence label, and the user could not tell which they were reading. In a domain whose central discipline is refusing to invent numbers (`10.05.03` D-02, `10.01.07` D-03, `10.02.04` D-01), that blend is exactly the failure to avoid. |
| DT-02 | This is a reporting feature — a nice dashboard of expected income | It is literally a list of dates | ❌ **REJECTED — it is the **precondition for leakage detection**, and that is the fifth independent occurrence of this domain's defining pattern.** `10.01.03` D-03 (expected-by on submissions), `10.01.04` D-02 (silence synthesised into an event), `10.02.01` D-02 (a source registry that exists to make absence visible), `10.02.05` D-05 (ageing alarms on unattributed money) and `10.04.03` D-03 (ageing alarms on held balances) all reduce to one principle: **an absence produces no event, so you must declare an expectation first, then let a clock manufacture the event the world will never send.** This feature is that principle applied to society distributions — and without it, `10.05.03` cannot detect a missing distribution at all, because nothing ever said one was coming. Five features converging independently is what promotes this to a domain-level position rather than a local trick. |
| DT-03 | The calendar is reference data — look it up and load it | It is published information; societies announce their distribution dates | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED that it is data, REJECTED that it is easy — and the gap is the feature's real cost.** Per society × per territory × per income type × per usage-period convention, published across dozens of institutions in their own formats, in their own languages, with their own definitions of a "quarter", changing without notice. **Nobody has assembled this.** That is simultaneously why the feature is valuable (it is genuinely missing from the world) and why it is expensive (someone maintains it by hand, forever). It is the same shape as `10.01.04` Q-01's per-society expected-by turnarounds — institutional knowledge held by practitioners, not published as a dataset — and it means the moat here is **curation, not code**, which is an unusual and uncomfortable thing to find in a product spec. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Separate from forecasting** | DT-01. Deterministic fact vs statistical guess; blending them hides which is which. |
| D-02 | **Dates without amounts.** The platform states when, not how much | It does not have usage data at the society. Inventing the amount is the tempting lie. |
| D-03 | The calendar is **data, maintained by hand, forever** | DT-03. The moat is curation, not code. Uncomfortable and true. |
| D-04 | **This feature is the precondition for `10.05.03`** | DT-02. Fifth independent occurrence of the domain's expectation-then-clock pattern. |
| D-05 | Late ≠ leaked | Societies slip. Tolerance before alarm, or the feature cries wolf and gets muted. |
