# Feature: Royalty Forecasting

> **Number**: 10.10
> **Parent**: [Royalties & Collections](./royalties-collections-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Predicting **what a catalogue will earn**: given historical distributions, current streaming velocity, the works registered and the territories covered, roughly how much money is coming over the next four quarters.

Distinct from `10.09` money-in-flight, which states what is deterministically due. This is a **guess with a confidence interval**, and in a domain whose entire discipline is refusing to invent numbers (`10.05.03` D-02, `10.02.04` D-01, `10.01.07` D-03, `10.09` D-02), a feature whose whole output is an invented number needs to justify itself carefully — or not exist.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | A range for what their catalogue will earn. Useful for exactly one thing: deciding whether they can afford to do something. `personas.md` describes a portfolio of small income streams — the value here is not precision, it is knowing whether the number is £200 or £2,000 next quarter. |
| Producer | Reports | Same across administered catalogues, where the volume makes a forecast statistically meaningful rather than noise. |
| Operator | None | No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The forecast composes two layers of very different quality, and **presenting them as one number would be the feature's defining mistake**:

- **High confidence** — `10.09`'s money-in-flight. Registered, earned, due on a published date. Not a forecast at all; a fact awaiting arrival.
- **Low confidence** — everything else. Future usage from current velocity, distributions from historical run rate, territories from prior geography.

The second layer is genuinely weak for exactly the population WeJammin serves. Royalty income for a working musician is **lumpy, seasonal, and dominated by events nothing predicts**: a sync placement, a playlist add, a song used in an advert. `personas.md`'s Musician is not a catalogue with a smooth decay curve — they are three good quarters and a dead one. Statistical forecasting works on large catalogues with stable behaviour, and the platform's core persona is the opposite of that.

The honest version is therefore modest: **a range, not a number**; explicitly separated from in-flight; and silent where the data is too thin to say anything.

### Happy Path

1. A catalogue has enough distribution history to be statistically meaningful
2. In-flight money (`10.09`) is shown as itself — deterministic, dated, not a forecast
3. Beyond it, a **range** with a stated confidence, derived from history and velocity
4. The user can tell at a glance which part is known and which part is guessed
5. Actuals arrive; the model is calibrated against them, publicly

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Thin history | New or small catalogue. **The default for most users** | **Silence.** No forecast is better than a fabricated one |
| Lumpy income | The norm for a working musician: a sync lands and the quarter is 6× | Ranges must be wide enough to be useless-looking, which is honest |
| Forecast used for a decision | Someone commits to rent based on it | The liability question. See DT-02 |
| One-off in the history | A past sync inflates the trend | Outliers must not become a baseline |
| Catalogue changed | Rights reverted, a work sold, a split changed | The forecast's basis moved; recompute or say nothing |
| Registration gap | Forecasting income from territories that were never registered | Nonsense. Gated on `10.01.04`; unregistered = no forecast |
| User compares to a peer | "Similar artists earn £X" | Domain 22's benchmarking, not this. Different data, different claim |
| Model is wrong | It will be | Calibration must be visible; a forecast that never reports its own error is marketing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Insufficient data | Thin history | **No forecast.** The most common and most honest state |
| In-flight only | Registrations exist, history doesn't | `10.09`'s deterministic view, unadorned |
| Forecast | Enough history | A range, clearly separated from in-flight |
| Stale | Catalogue or registration changed | Recomputed or withdrawn |
| Calibrated | Actuals arrived | The model's own error, shown |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Forecasting is valuable — every finance product has it, and artists want to know what's coming | Sweep candidate 18; a reasonable ask, and it is on every competitor's screenshot | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED as a want, REJECTED as reliable for this platform's core persona — and the mismatch is the finding.** Statistical forecasting needs stable, high-volume, smoothly-decaying behaviour. `personas.md`'s Musician is the precise opposite: **a portfolio of small, lumpy, seasonal income streams dominated by unpredictable events** — a sync lands and the quarter is 6×; a playlist drops them and it halves. The forecastable users (large stable catalogues) are the ones this platform is *not* built for; the users it is built for are structurally unforecastable. That inverts the feature's apparent value and is the clearest argument for its MoSCoW placement. The genuinely useful thing WeJammin has here is not a model — it is `10.09`'s **deterministic** in-flight, which needs no statistics at all. |
| DT-02 | A forecast is a projection; the user understands it is not a promise | Standard disclaimer thinking — put "estimate" on it and the problem is handled | ❌ **REJECTED.** Money is not projected — it is **relied upon**. A musician who reads "£3,000 next quarter" makes a decision about rent, about turning down a gig, about buying gear. When £600 arrives, the platform did not merely mis-predict; it caused a harm, and it did so as the product whose entire pitch is that the music industry mishandles their money. The gap between a UI disclaimer and a user's actual reliance is where this feature's real risk lives, and in this domain the surrounding discipline is severe: `10.05.03` D-02 refuses to size leakage without data, `10.09` D-02 refuses to state amounts it cannot know, `10.01.07` D-03 refuses to reverse-engineer a gross. **This feature would be the one place the platform states a number it made up** — which is a coherence problem, not just a UX one. |
| DT-03 | This is analytics and belongs in domain 22 | Node Classification Gate. 22 is Analytics & Market Intelligence and this is a projection over historical data — textbook | ❌ **REJECTED, on the same pattern as `10.05.03` DT-01 — the fourth instance in this drill.** The *statistics* are generic and could live anywhere. The **semantics are domain 10's and exist nowhere else**: society distribution lag, per-territory registration state, the black-box holding period, the difference between earned and payable (`10.03.01` DT-02), the difference between deterministic in-flight and statistical projection (`10.09` DT-01). A forecast built in 22 would project *revenue* — a smooth curve over historical numbers, with no notion that Q1 money arrives in October, that unregistered territories earn nothing, or that an unrecouped artist receives none of it. The reporting surface rides `CX-M30`; **the model's semantics stay here.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **In-flight and forecast are never one number** | `10.09` DT-01. A published fact and a regression output must not share a confidence label. |
| D-02 | **A range, never a point figure** | DT-02. A point figure invites reliance the model cannot support. |
| D-03 | **Silence on thin data** | DT-01. Most users are unforecastable, and no forecast beats a fabricated one. |
| D-04 | Gated on registration state | Forecasting income from unregistered territories is fiction with a chart. |
| D-05 | **Calibration is public** — the model reports its own error | A forecast that never grades itself is marketing. |
| D-06 | The semantics stay in domain 10; the surface rides `CX-M30` | DT-03. Fourth instance of the shared-machinery/domain-semantics split in this drill. |
