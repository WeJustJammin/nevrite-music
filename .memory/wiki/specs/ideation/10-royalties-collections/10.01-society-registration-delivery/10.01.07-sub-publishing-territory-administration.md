# Feature: Sub-Publishing & Territory Administration

> **Number**: 10.01.07
> **Parent**: [Society Registration & Delivery](./10.01-society-registration-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Appointing and administering **local publishers in territories the platform cannot reach directly** — the standard mechanism by which a UK writer's German performance income actually gets collected: a German sub-publisher registers the work locally with GEMA, collects, takes a cut at source, and remits the rest upstream. Sub-publishing is the industry's answer to the fact that collection is national and music is not.

It is included here for completeness and honesty rather than ambition. Sub-publishing is a network of negotiated commercial relationships, one territory at a time, each with its own term, retention rate and reversion — the deepest end of the collections pool and the part least amenable to being built by a platform.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sees which territories their catalogue is administered in, by whom, at what retention, and until when — and, more usefully, which territories it is **not** covered in. Most self-administering writers have zero sub-publishing and therefore collect essentially nothing outside their home territory without knowing it. |
| Producer | Config | Same, plus the publisher-entity side where they administer others' works. |

| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The model is a **territorial administration grant**: (catalogue or work set, territory, sub-publisher, retention rate, term, reversion date, at-source vs at-receipt accounting). Two things about it are structurally nasty and must not be smoothed away:

- **Money is reduced before the platform ever sees it.** The sub-publisher deducts at source. A statement arriving upstream is already net, and reconciling it back to the gross the local society actually paid is frequently impossible from the outside. This is where the calculation engine (`10.03.01`) loses its ability to explain a number, and it directly limits the "every line traceable" promise `10.04.02` wants to make.
- **The chain of who collects what where is time-varying.** A territory's administration changes hands, terms expire, catalogues revert. A royalty for a 2024 usage in Germany must be attributed to whoever administered it *in 2024* — which is the parent domain's D-02 (as-of resolution) in its most acute form.

The platform's realistic role is to hold the map and surface the gaps: which territories are covered, at what cost, expiring when. Building the sub-publisher network itself is a business, not a feature.

### Happy Path

1. Writer's catalogue is administered directly in their home territory
2. A sub-publishing agreement is recorded for a target territory — counterparty, retention, term, reversion
3. The sub-publisher registers the works locally with that territory's society
4. Local income is collected, netted at source, and remitted upstream
5. The remittance arrives as a statement (`10.02`), already net, and is attributed to the correct territorial chain **as it stood in the usage period**

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No sub-publishing anywhere | The default for essentially every independent writer. Foreign income is uncollected and invisible | A `10.05.03` leakage finding — but one with no cheap fix, which makes it honest rather than actionable |
| Statement arrives net at source | Gross is unknowable from the remittance. **The explainability promise breaks here** | The statement must say "net at source; gross not available", never invent a gross |
| Term expires mid-period | Usage before and after the expiry belongs to different administrators | Requires as-of resolution (parent D-02); a current-state model gets it silently wrong |
| Catalogue reverts | Works must be re-registered locally by the new administrator, or income stops | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the re-registration cascade |
| Sub-publisher under-reports | Only an audit finds it, and only if the contract allows one (`10.08`) | The platform holds the expectation; it cannot see the local books |
| Double deduction | Society commission plus sub-publisher retention plus admin fee, compounding | The stack must be itemised or the writer cannot understand their own rate |
| Territory has no society worth the trouble | Real. Some territories collect less than the cost of administering them | Honest omission beats a coverage claim |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncovered | No arrangement in a territory | The gap, with an honest note that fixing it is a commercial act, not a click |
| Covered | Agreement recorded and live | Counterparty, retention, term |
| Expiring | Reversion date approaching | Alerted — this is domain 23's contract-vault pattern applied to territories |
| Reverted | Term ended | Re-registration exposure flagged |
| Collecting | Remittances observed | Net income attributed to the territory |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should build a sub-publisher network so users get worldwide collection | The consolidation instinct (`D-03`: maximal ambition), and what Songtrust actually did | ❌ **REJECTED as a feature; it is a business.** A sub-publishing network is dozens of negotiated bilateral relationships with local publishers, each with its own term sheet, each requiring the platform to *be* a publisher (parent `Q-03`) with local standing. It cannot be shipped; it can only be signed, over years. What is a feature is **holding the map and naming the gaps** — which is genuinely useful (most writers do not know they collect nothing abroad) and requires no relationship with anyone. The distinction matters because a roadmap that lists "sub-publishing" as a deliverable is committing to a corporate development programme under a product name. |
| DT-02 | Territorial administration is a current-state fact — who administers Germany | The obvious model, and how a settings screen would naturally be built | ❌ **REJECTED — and this feature is where the parent's D-02 (as-of resolution) hurts most.** A statement arriving in 2026 for 2024 German usage must be attributed to the 2024 administrator, at the 2024 retention rate, under the 2024 term — even though the territory reverted in 2025. A current-state model does not error; it produces a plausible, wrong number and pays the wrong party. This is bitemporal by necessity (usage period vs knowledge date), it is not unique to this feature (`10.03.01`, `10.03.04` and `11` all need it), and it is why the parent proposes **Point-in-Time / As-Of Record Resolution** as a cross-cut rather than solving it locally seven times. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The platform **holds the territorial map and names the gaps**; it does not build a sub-publisher network | DT-01. The network is corporate development, not product. Shipping the map is honest and useful. |
| D-02 | Territorial administration is **bitemporal** — attributed as of the usage period, not current state | DT-02. A current-state model silently pays the wrong party. |
| D-03 | At-source netting is **disclosed, never reverse-engineered** | The gross is genuinely unknowable from a net remittance. Inventing one would be the reconstruction failure `problem-statement.md` blames the industry for. |
| D-04 | The deduction stack (society commission → sub-publisher retention → admin fee) is **itemised** | Compounding deductions are why writers cannot explain their own effective rate. |
