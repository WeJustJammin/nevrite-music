# Feature: Mechanical Rights Administration

> **Number**: 10.01.05
> **Parent**: [Society Registration & Delivery](./10.01-society-registration-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Registering works for the **reproduction right** with the bodies that collect it — the MLC (US, statutory blanket licence for streaming), HFA, MCPS (UK), and their equivalents elsewhere — and administering the claim against those bodies' matching. Mechanical income is the reproduction half of a composition's earnings, structurally separate from performance income even though both come from the same stream of the same song.

The MLC deserves special attention: it was created because the US mechanical system was broken, it holds a large and public pool of **unmatched royalties**, and its data is publicly searchable. That makes it simultaneously a registration target here and the single best source for `10.05.01` black-box search — an unusual case where the platform's registration counterparty is also its discovery corpus.

## Behavior

Mechanical registration is `10.01.02`'s payload delivered to a different family of bodies with different rules. What is genuinely distinct and cannot be folded into performance registration:

- **The eligibility test differs.** A performer with no writing share has performance-adjacent claims (`10.01.06`) and zero mechanical claim. A writer has a mechanical claim whether or not they ever performed. The right, not the person, determines the registration.
- **The MLC's model is matching, not registration alone.** You register a work; the MLC matches usage lines to it. A registered work with bad metadata still fails to match, and the money stays in the unmatched pool. Registration is necessary and insufficient — which means `10.01.04`'s "registered" state is a weaker promise here than at a PRO.
- **Cover songs invert the flow.** When someone else records your song, their mechanical is owed to you; when you record someone else's, you owe theirs. Domain 11 owns compulsory/cover licensing; the money that results lands here.

The platform's honest position: it can register, and it can *observe* the unmatched pool because the MLC publishes it. It cannot make a match happen — that is the MLC's process on the MLC's schedule.

### Happy Path

1. Work is registration-ready (`10.01.02`) and the writer has a mechanical-collecting affiliation (`10.01.01`)
2. Payload delivered to the relevant body via `10.01.03`
3. Acknowledgement returns; work registered (`10.01.04`)
4. Body matches usage lines to the work over subsequent cycles
5. Mechanical income appears in statements (`10.02`) and reconciles against the expectation (`10.09`)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Registered but unmatched at the MLC | The characteristic failure. Registration succeeded; money is still in the pool | Must be visible, because the MLC publishes it — this is the domain's most checkable claim |
| Writer registered with a PRO but not the MLC | Half their streaming composition income is uncollected. Extremely common | A `10.05.03` leakage finding of the highest confidence |
| Producer expects mechanical from points | Points are usually master-side; there is nothing to register | Explained explicitly — a silent absence reads as a bug |
| Cover of someone else's song | Mechanical owed **outward**, not collected | Routed to domain 11; the obligation is not this feature's |
| Same work, MLC + MCPS + others | Territorial mechanical is a per-body registration, like performance | Per-body, per-territory state (`10.01.04` D-01) |
| Work registered by a distributor too | Duplicate/conflicting registration at the same body | Conflict → domain 09; a common consequence of using DistroKid *and* a publishing admin |
| Pre-1972 / statutory oddities (US) | Genuinely different rules | Not covered anywhere in the ideation tree. Promoted to **Q-04** (`[OWNER]`, `/create-prd`) — a jurisdictional scope line only the owner can draw |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Mechanical is just performance registration to a different address, and should be merged into `10.01.02` | The shapes look identical: a work, writers, shares, a body, a payload | ❌ **REJECTED** — the **eligibility test differs by right, not by address**. A session drummer with no writing share is eligible for neighbouring rights and ineligible for mechanical; a writer who never entered a studio is the reverse. Merging would produce one registration record with a nullable eligibility column per right type and a UI that offers people registrations they cannot have — the exact `listing`-with-40-nullable-columns error that global D-14 rejected for marketplaces. The payload *generator* is shared (`10.01.02`); the right is not. |
| DT-02 | "Registered" means the money will come | The natural reading, and what `10.01.04` says everywhere else | ❌ **REJECTED for the MLC specifically, and it is the most interesting fact in this feature.** The MLC *matches*; registration only makes matching possible. A registered work with weak metadata sits unmatched indefinitely, and — uniquely — **the MLC publishes the unmatched pool**, so the platform can see the failure from outside. That makes this the one place in the whole domain where the platform can verify its own registration actually worked, using public data, rather than inferring from silence (`10.01.04` D-03). It is also why the same public corpus is `10.05.01`'s search target: the registration counterparty and the black-box discovery source are the same institution. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Mechanical stays a **separate feature from performance registration** | DT-01. Eligibility follows the right, not the party or the address. |
| D-02 | "Registered / unmatched" is a **first-class state**, not a variant of registered | DT-02. At the MLC it is the modal outcome for weak metadata, and it is publicly observable. |
| D-03 | The platform observes the unmatched pool; it does not claim to drive matching | Matching is the body's process on the body's schedule. Claiming influence would be a promise it cannot keep. |
| D-04 | A producer's points do **not** imply a mechanical claim, and the UI says so | Silent absence reads as a bug and generates permanent support load. |
