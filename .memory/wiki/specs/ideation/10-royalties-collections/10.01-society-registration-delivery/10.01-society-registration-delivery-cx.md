## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Affiliation ↔ Payload

**Relationship**: The sub-domain's load-bearing dependency and the place the platform's thesis meets its limit. `10.01.02` DT-01 states it plainly: capture-at-creation produces **shares and human beings**; a society consumes **IPIs and role codes**. The session gives you "Sarah played bass and wrote 20%". The payload needs "IPI 00123456789, role CA, 20.00, territory 826". Nothing in the room produces the number, the platform cannot mint it, and it cannot look it up (`10.01.01` DT-02).

The consequence is that the modal registration is not blocked on data — it is blocked on **a person** (`10.01.02` DT-03). A user with perfectly captured splits still cannot register until a co-writer they may not have spoken to in two years supplies a number they have probably never seen. The wedge does not produce registration; it produces a registration blocked on someone else's paperwork.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: usually the blocked party, and rarely the one who can unblock it.
- **Producer**: frequently the blocker — their affiliation or their agreement's shape is the unresolved element.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Affiliations belong to the party (or to domain 01 — `10.01.01` Q-01). The payload is a projection from domain 09 plus those affiliations. Nothing writes both, and `10.01.02` D-01 forbids editing the projection.
2. **Trigger chain**: Split agreed (09) → project → validate → **blocked on missing IPI** → chase a human → affiliation asserted → re-validate → queue. The chain's slowest step is social, not computational.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and awkwardly — a user cannot see or supply another party's IPI, so the blocker is visible and unfixable by the person looking at it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The chase. The platform can prompt the co-writer; if they are an unclaimed shell (`02.03`) there is no channel at all — the same break as `credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-04, inherited whole.
5. **State transition conflict**: An affiliation added while a payload is queued must not mutate the in-flight file. Sequencing is stateful (`10.01.03` D-04); re-projection produces a new submission, never an edit to a sent one.

### CX-02: File Exchange ↔ Status & Rejection

**Relationship**: The pair that explains why `10.01.04` exists as a feature at all. CWR-family transport is **batch with no per-work status call** (`10.01.03` DT-01), so "registered at PRS" is never read — it is inferred from the last acknowledgement that happened to mention the work (`10.01.04` D-03). Registration state is a belief with an age.

The sharp edge is silence. A file goes out; nothing comes back; nothing is red. `10.01.03` DT-03 and `10.01.04` DT-02 converge on the same refusal: **an indefinite pending state is exactly the mechanism that turns a year of royalties into black-box money**. The counterparty will never send the event, so the platform must synthesise it from an expected-by clock.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: see the outcome and the expected-by date; never the file.
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: `10.01.03` owns the batch and its sequence; `10.01.04` owns the per-work belief. A batch-level failure fans out to every work in it, which is the one place batch state legitimately writes work state.
2. **Trigger chain**: Queue → emit → deliver → *(silence)* → expected-by breach → **alarm**. The interesting branch is the one where nothing happens.
3. **Permission intersection**: None between them; both are platform-internal.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Overdue alarms fan out to every rights-holder in the stalled batch — including people who did not initiate the submission and may not know it existed.
5. **State transition conflict**: A late acknowledgement arriving *after* an overdue alarm must resolve the alarm rather than create a second, contradictory belief. Retries racing a late ack are the duplicate-registration path `10.01.03` D-04 forbids.

### CX-06: Neighbouring Rights ↔ Affiliation

**Relationship**: The exception that reshapes the sub-domain's roadmap. Every other child here waits on the parent's `Q-03` — WeJammin must be an accredited publisher or administrator, or it has no counterparty to deliver to. `10.01.06` D-02 breaks that: **SoundExchange and PPL admit performers directly**, so the user's own membership is the credential and the platform needs no standing of its own.

Pair that with `10.01.06` DT-01 — neighbouring rights is keyed on a **credit**, not on an agreed split — and this is the only registration in domain 10 that is both tightly bound to the wedge (`D-18`) and ungated by the domain's largest business-model unknown. The naive roadmap builds performance and mechanical first because they sound more central. The evidence says otherwise.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the persona the feature exists for. A session player with a captured credit, an ISRC, and no PPL membership is leaving existing money uncollected.
- **Producer**: performer and, separately, the party asserting featured/non-featured about people who are not present (`10.01.06` DT-03).
- **Operator / Fan**: absent.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The performer affiliation is the performer's own, held like any other affiliation. No publisher chain exists to conflict over.
2. **Trigger chain**: Credit captured (02) → ISRC exists (09/12) → eligibility derived → **member's own filing** → income. No 100% arithmetic, no co-writer chase — the CX-01 blocker does not apply here.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the good kind: the credential belongs to the user, so the platform's own accreditation is irrelevant.
4. **Notification fan-out**: "Eligible, unregistered" is the highest-volume finding in the domain and lands on people who have never heard of neighbouring rights. For shells, money accrues to someone with no channel (`10.01.06` Q-02).
5. **State transition conflict**: A featured/non-featured assertion changing after filing is a claim amendment at the society, not a local edit.

| R-01 | 10.01.06 Neighbouring Rights | 10.01.02 Work Registration Payload | Rejected: they share a word ("registration") and nothing else. `10.01.02` builds a composition payload — writers, publisher chain, shares summing to 100/200%. Neighbouring rights is `(performer, ISRC, featured?)` with no composition, no shares and no chain. Wiring them would import an arithmetic model that has no meaning for a session drummer. The genuine shared surface is the *transport* (CX-08), and even that only partially — CWR does not carry neighbouring rights at all. |
| R-02 | 10.01.05 Mechanical | 10.01.06 Neighbouring Rights | Rejected: the tempting pair, because both are "the other royalty nobody registers for". But they are disjoint by right and by person — mechanical follows a composition share, neighbouring rights follows a performance. The same human may hold both on the same recording, via two unrelated facts. A shared node would suggest a shared eligibility test, which is precisely `10.01.05` DT-01's error. |
| R-03 | 10.01.03 File Exchange | 10.01.01 Affiliation | Rejected as a direct pair. Transport never reads affiliations — it moves a file whose content `10.01.02` already assembled. The real edge is CX-03 (the *acknowledgement* confirms an IPI), which is a return-path relationship mediated by `10.01.04`, not a delivery-time one. Logging a direct edge would imply the transport layer validates parties, which would put party logic in the byte-mover. |
| R-04 | 10.01.07 Sub-Publishing | 10.01.04 Status & Rejection | Rejected: a sub-publisher registers works with the local society **under their own submitter relationship**, not through WeJammin's transport. The platform never sees those acknowledgements and cannot infer that registration state — it learns only from remittances arriving months later (`10.01.07` Behavior). Modelling a status loop the platform has no signal for would manufacture a confident, empty state. |
