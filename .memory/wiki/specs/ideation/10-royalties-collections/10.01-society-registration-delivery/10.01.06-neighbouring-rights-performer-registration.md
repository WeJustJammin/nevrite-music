# Feature: Neighbouring Rights & Performer Registration

> **Number**: 10.01.06
> **Parent**: [Society Registration & Delivery](./10.01-society-registration-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Registering **performers** — not writers — with the bodies that pay for the use of a *recording*: SoundExchange (US digital radio), PPL (UK), and the neighbouring-rights societies across Europe and beyond. This is the money a session musician earns for having played on a record, independent of having written any of it, and it is the one income stream in the entire domain that is keyed on a **credit** rather than an ownership share.

That makes it the closest structural fit between the platform's wedge and actual money. Domain 02 exists to capture, at the session, who played what. This feature is the mechanism by which that captured fact becomes a payment to a person who, in the current industry, would have had to remember the session, know the society existed, and file a claim years later.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **The persona this feature exists for.** A session drummer with a captured credit on a played recording has a neighbouring-rights claim and, in the overwhelming majority of real cases, has never registered it. Sees their performer registrations per body, and — the high-value half — which of their captured credits are eligible and unregistered. `personas.md` describes their pain as "a portfolio of small income streams"; this is a small income stream that already exists and is going unpaid. |
| Producer | Full | Also a performer whenever they played, and separately relevant as the party who owns or controls the master in many independent sessions. The featured/non-featured distinction (see Behavior) is a judgement they are often best placed to make and worst incentivised to make. |
| Operator | None | A studio does not hold a performer's neighbouring right. Their room's credit ("recorded at X") is a `02.01` discography fact with no royalty attached — a near-miss worth naming so nobody wires one to the other. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The registration is keyed on **(performer, recording, role)** — where role means the featured/non-featured distinction that determines the share, and where the recording is identified by ISRC. Compared with `10.01.02`'s work payload this inverts almost everything: no composition, no shares summing to 100%, no publisher chain, and — decisively — **the fact being registered is a credit, which is exactly what domain 02 captures**.

Two properties make this the most tractable registration in the domain:

- **Eligibility comes from the credit graph, not from an agreement.** No split needs to have been negotiated. Nobody has to sum to 100%. If domain 02 says you played on it and the recording has an ISRC, the claim exists.
- **The performer registers themselves.** SoundExchange and PPL take direct performer memberships; you do not need a publisher or an administrator to hold a performer account. **This substantially dodges the parent's `Q-03` accreditation gate** — the user's own membership is the credential, and the platform's job may be as small as telling them what to claim.

The featured/non-featured split is the domain-specific judgement: featured performers earn materially more, the distinction is conventional rather than crisp, and it is asserted by whoever files. Domain 02's role taxonomy (`02.06`) supplies the vocabulary but not the answer — "played drums" does not say whether they were the artist or the session player.

### Happy Path

1. Session captured in domain 02; contributions recorded per person per recording
2. Recording gets an ISRC (domain 09 / domain 12 at delivery)
3. System identifies eligible performers from the credit graph — including people who would never have thought to claim
4. Performer's registration is filed against their own society membership (`10.01.01`)
5. Neighbouring-rights income arrives in statements (`10.02`) and reconciles to a person, keyed on a credit captured in a room

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Performer has no society membership | The most common case by far, and a pure leakage finding — money already exists for them | Surfaced via `10.05.03`; the platform can point but cannot join on their behalf |
| Featured vs non-featured contested | Materially different money; conventional, not crisp; asserted by the filer | Routed to the same evidence logic as `02.05`; **not** a platform verdict (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-10) |
| Featured/non-featured asserted by someone other than the performer (typically the Producer) | Confirmation prompt sent to the named performer; the assertion is usable immediately but carries an `unconfirmed` flag until they respond (D-06, resolving Q-03) | "This credit says you were a *featured* performer — confirm or correct" |
| Performer never responds to the confirmation prompt | No blocking. The filer's assertion stands, permanently tagged `asserted-by: <party>, unconfirmed` — the platform never auto-flips it and never computes its own answer (D-04) | The unconfirmed flag stays visible on the registration; a periodic nudge, not a hold |
| Credit exists, no ISRC | No key to register against. Silent dead end | Blocked, with the ISRC gap named (domain 09/12) |
| Credit is self-asserted, unattested (`02.04`) | The society does not consume WeJammin's tiers; it takes the claim. Our confidence has no standing with PPL | Tier informs the platform's own suggestion strength and nothing more |
| Two people claim the same performer slot | Society-side conflict; money held | → domain 09 / `02.05` |
| Performer is an unclaimed shell (`02.03`) | The credit exists, the person is not on the platform, and money is accruing for them somewhere | **WeJammin can see money owed to a person it cannot contact** (`credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-04). See Q-02 |
| Imported credit (`02.03.01`), not captured | Eligible on the same terms — the society does not care how we learned it | Import is a legitimate source here |
| Territory-by-territory membership | A performer registered at PPL is not registered in Germany | Per-body, per-territory (`10.01.04` D-01) |

### States

Two layers: canonical render states (how the view behaves) and the domain lifecycle a single
registration moves through.

**Render states**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Credit graph + ISRC + membership status being resolved | Skeleton list of eligible-credit rows |
| Empty | No captured credits on recordings the user played | "No performer credits yet — these appear once you're credited on a recording with an ISRC" |
| Populated | One or more eligible credits found | The eligible / registered / collecting list, each row in its lifecycle state below |
| Error | Society membership lookup or ISRC resolution failed | Row-level error with retry; other rows still render (partial, not total, failure) |

**Registration lifecycle** (per credit)

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Eligible / unregistered | Credit + ISRC, no membership | The domain's highest-value empty state: money exists, you are not collecting it |
| Blocked | No ISRC | The gap named upstream |
| Registered | Filed and acknowledged | Per body, per territory |
| Conflicted | Competing performer claim | Held; routed to 09 |
| Collecting | Income observed against it | Reconciled in `10.02` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is one more registration flow, peer to mechanical and performance | The candidate list's flat framing — three registration bullets in a row | ❌ **REJECTED — this is the domain's tightest coupling to the platform's thesis and the framing undersells it badly.** Every other registration in this sub-domain needs an *agreement*: a split negotiated, shares summing, a publisher chain. Neighbouring rights needs a **credit** — precisely and only the thing domain 02 captures in the room. It is the single case where the wedge (`D-18`: provenance) produces money with no intervening negotiation, no 100% arithmetic, and no co-writer to chase. `problem-statement.md` describes a musician whose career is "a portfolio of small income streams"; this is a stream that already exists, is already being collected by societies, and is unpaid because nobody filed. **If any part of domain 10 is the proof that capture-at-creation pays, it is this feature — and it should be weighted accordingly at MoSCoW.** |
| DT-02 | Like the rest of the sub-domain, this is gated on WeJammin being an accredited administrator (parent `Q-03`) | Inherited assumption from `10.01.01`–`10.01.05` | ❌ **REJECTED, and it is the most consequential exception in the sub-domain.** SoundExchange and PPL admit **performers directly** — the individual's own membership is the credential. The platform does not need to be a publisher or an administrator to help a member identify and file claims. So the one registration flow that is most tightly bound to the wedge is also the one least blocked by the domain's biggest business-model gate. That inverts the naive roadmap, which would build performance/mechanical registration first because they sound more central. |
| DT-03 | Featured vs non-featured is a data field | It appears as a flag on every neighbouring-rights filing | ❌ **REJECTED** — it is a **judgement with money attached, made by whoever files, about people who are not in the room when it is made**. It is conventional rather than defined, the pay gap is large, and the person best placed to assert it (the Producer) is not always disinterested — `personas.md` already names "assigns themselves a larger split than agreed" as their worst intentional behaviour, and this is the same act in a different currency. The platform must record who asserted it and treat it as contestable, consistent with `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 (the platform does not adjudicate truth). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Eligibility is derived from the **credit graph**, not from an ownership share | DT-01. This is the one registration keyed on a credit — no agreement, no 100% arithmetic. |
| D-02 | Not gated on platform accreditation — the **performer's own membership is the credential** | DT-02. Materially changes this feature's MoSCoW position relative to its siblings. |
| D-03 | Attestation tier (`02.04`) informs the platform's suggestion strength only; it has no standing with a society | Same reasoning as `credits-attribution-cx.md` R-02 — two unrelated notions of verification sharing a word. |
| D-04 | Featured/non-featured is **asserted and attributed**, never computed | DT-03. A judgement with money attached, made about absent people. |
| D-05 | Imported credits are eligible on the same terms as captured ones | The society does not care how the platform learned the fact. Unusual for this platform — everywhere else provenance tier matters. |
| D-06 | A featured/non-featured assertion made by anyone other than the performer **triggers a confirmation prompt to that performer**; the assertion is usable while unconfirmed but is permanently tagged `unconfirmed` until they respond, and non-response never auto-confirms it | Resolves Q-03. Consistent with D-04 (asserted, never computed) and `credits-attribution-index.md` D-10 (platform does not adjudicate truth). Makes the Producer's conflict of interest (DT-03) visible without blocking a legitimate filing. |
