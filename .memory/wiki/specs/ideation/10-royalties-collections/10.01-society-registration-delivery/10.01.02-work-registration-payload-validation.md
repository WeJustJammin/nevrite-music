# Feature: Work Registration Payload & Validation

> **Number**: 10.01.02
> **Parent**: [Society Registration & Delivery](./10.01-society-registration-delivery-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turning the splits graph into a **registration payload a society will accept**: the work, its title and alternate titles, its ISWC if it has one, every writer with their IPI and role code, every publisher chain with its shares, the territory scope, and the sum that must land on exactly 100% (or 200% under the writer/publisher convention). Then validating it *before* it goes anywhere, because a rejection round-trip costs weeks.

This is where the platform's whole thesis cashes out or does not. `problem-statement.md` argues the industry's defect is reconstruction from memory; WeJammin's claim is that it has the facts already, captured at the session. If that is true, this payload assembles itself. If it is not true, this feature is a form.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | For works they wrote: sees the payload the platform would send, what is missing, and who has to fix it. The value they experience is negative-space — "your work is registration-ready" or "3 of your 40 works are". A session drummer with no writing share never sees this feature for that work at all, which is correct and worth stating (`credits-attribution-index.md` D-01: a credit is not an ownership share). |
| Producer | Full | For works they hold points or a writing share in. Also the party most likely to be *blocking* someone else's payload, because their own affiliation (`10.01.01`) or their agreement's shape (domain 09) is the unresolved element. |
| Operator | None | Not a rights-holder in a composition. No surface. |
| Fan | None | No surface. |

## Behavior

The payload is **projected, never authored**. Every field has an upstream owner: shares and parties come from domain 09's ownership ledger, identifiers from `10.01.01` and 09's identifier issuance, titles from the work record, role codes from the credit taxonomy (`02.06`). This feature's job is assembly plus a hard validation gate — and, critically, **it must not be possible to fix a bad payload here**. A share that sums to 97% is fixed in the ledger, not in the outbound file, or the platform has just created the second source of truth that domain 09 exists to prevent.

Validation splits into three tiers, and conflating them makes the feature useless:

1. **Structural** — the society's format rules. Field lengths, mandatory codes, valid territory codes. Machine-checkable, cheap, and the platform can be exhaustive here.
2. **Arithmetic** — shares sum correctly per right per territory; the publisher chain reconciles; no party appears twice in one role. Also machine-checkable, and where most real failures live.
3. **Semantic** — does the society believe this? Is the ISWC already attached to a different work? Does a co-writer's PRO already hold a conflicting registration? **Not checkable locally at any price.** Only `10.01.04` finds these, weeks later.

The honest framing that must survive downstream: passing validation means "this will not be rejected for a reason we could see", not "this will be accepted".

### Happy Path

1. A work's splits are agreed and locked in domain 09
2. System projects a payload and runs structural + arithmetic validation automatically — no user action initiated it
3. Work shows **registration-ready**, with the payload viewable in human terms (not as a CWR record)
4. Payload is queued for delivery (`10.01.03`)
5. Acknowledgement returns (`10.01.04`); the work becomes registered at that society for that territory

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Shares sum to 97% | Blocked. The fix is in domain 09, not here | Named precisely, with a link into the ledger — never an editable field in this feature |
| A co-writer has no IPI (`10.01.01`) | Blocked, and the blocker is **another person**. The user cannot fix it and must chase a human | The specific person and the specific gap. This is the modal failure and the UX has to treat it as a social problem, not a validation error |
| Work has no ISWC | Not fatal — registration is often how an ISWC gets issued. Treating it as required is a common and expensive mistake | Proceeds; ISWC expected back via `10.01.04` |
| A contributor is an unclaimed shell (`02.03`) | A real person, a real share, no IPI, no account, no channel to reach them (`credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-04). Payload cannot complete | The platform's growth loop and its registration loop break at the same point |
| Splits change after registration | The payload is stale and the society holds a wrong record. Re-registration + restatement (`10.03.04`) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the cascade is real and unscoped |
| Work-for-hire / buyout (domain 09) | The writer may have no registrable share despite having written it. Correct, counterintuitive, and a support burden | Explained, not silently omitted |
| Same work, different territories, different chains | Normal for sub-published catalogues. One work, many payloads, none of them the "true" one | `[PENDING — Step 5]`; see `10.01.07` |
| Validation passes, society rejects anyway | The semantic tier. Not a bug | `10.01.04` owns this; this feature must never have promised otherwise |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not projectable | No agreed splits in 09 | Points at the real gap — the split was never captured. The thesis's failure case |
| Blocked | Structural or arithmetic failure | The specific gap and its owner, with a route to the upstream fix |
| Blocked on another party | A co-writer's affiliation is missing | The person's name and what they must do. Chase-able, not fixable |
| Registration-ready | Local validation passes | Ready; framed as "no visible reason to reject", not "will be accepted" |
| Queued | Handed to `10.01.03` | In flight |
| Superseded | Splits changed post-registration | Stale marker; re-registration prompted |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | If the splits are captured at creation, the payload assembles itself and this feature is trivial | The platform's own thesis (`problem-statement.md`, D-18), taken at face value | ❌ **REJECTED — and the reason is the most important thing in this sub-domain.** A split captured in the room gives you *shares and human beings*. A society needs *IPIs and role codes*. The gap between "Sarah played bass and wrote 20%" and "IPI 00123456789, role CA, 20.00, PRS, territory 826" is not clerical — it is a per-person, externally-issued identifier that the platform cannot mint, cannot look up (`10.01.01` DT-02), and that most working musicians cannot recite. **Capture-at-creation solves the fact and leaves the identifier problem untouched.** The wedge does not automatically produce registration; it produces a registration that is blocked on someone else's paperwork. This should be carried to `/create-prd` as a live risk to the domain's premise, not filed as an edge case. |
| DT-02 | Validation should let the user fix problems inline — it is the natural place, and the friction is real | Standard form design; the user is right there looking at the error | ❌ **REJECTED.** An inline fix writes a share into an outbound payload that disagrees with domain 09's ledger. That is a second source of truth for the exact fact the platform exists to protect, created at the moment of highest time pressure, by the person most motivated to make the error go away. The registration would then be *correct at the society and wrong on the platform*, or vice versa, and nothing would ever detect it. **The friction is the feature**: fixing it upstream is the only version that stays true. |
| DT-03 | The modal blocker is a data problem | The framing every validation feature assumes | ❌ **REJECTED** — the modal blocker is **a person**. Missing co-writer IPI, unclaimed shell, a producer who never confirmed their points. The user cannot resolve their own registration alone, which means this feature's real UX is a chase, and its real metric is "how often does the other human respond". Designing it as a form with a green tick will produce a green tick nobody can reach. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The payload is **projected from domain 09, never authored or editable here** | DT-02. An editable payload is a second source of truth for the platform's most protected fact. |
| D-02 | Validation is tiered: structural / arithmetic (local, exhaustive) vs semantic (only `10.01.04` can find it) | Conflating them promises acceptance the platform cannot deliver. |
| D-03 | "Registration-ready" means "no visible reason to reject" — never "will be accepted" | D-02. The copy is a liability surface, same reasoning as `02.04` Q-03 on the word "verified". |
| D-04 | A missing ISWC does not block | Registration is frequently how an ISWC is issued. Requiring one inverts the dependency. |
| D-05 | Blockers are attributed to **the person who must act**, not stated as field errors | DT-03. The modal blocker is another human, and the UX is a chase. |
