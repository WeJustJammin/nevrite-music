# Feature: Live Performance Setlist → PRO Reporting

> **Number**: 10.06
> **Parent**: [Royalties & Collections](./royalties-collections-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Turning **a gig that happened into a performance royalty that gets paid**. Venues pay blanket licences to PROs; PROs distribute that money based on setlist returns; and for most small and mid-level live music, **nobody files the return**. The money is collected and then distributed by sampling or by analogy — which means it flows to whoever is already big, and the band who played the show gets nothing from the licence fee their own show generated.

The parent domain's Overview names this exactly: *"the same mechanic applied to the gigs nobody reports."* WeJammin has an unusual position here because it also owns the gig (domains 17, 18) — the setlist is a byproduct of a show the platform already knows about, not a form someone has to remember to fill in three weeks later.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Reports the setlist for shows they played, against **their own PRO membership** (`10.01.01`). The killer property: they are already on the platform for the booking (17), so the setlist is one confirmation rather than a separate errand. For a working covers/originals musician playing 80 shows a year, this is real, recurring, unclaimed money. |
| Producer | Full | Where they perform, identical to Musician. No producer-specific behaviour — this is a performance fact, not a session one. |
| Operator | **Config** | **The one place Operator re-enters domain 10.** A venue holds a PRS/ASCAP blanket licence and, in several territories, is obliged to submit returns of what was performed. Their licence is what generated the money. They can confirm the show and the setlist from the room's side — independent corroboration nobody else can give. |
| Fan | None | No surface. A fan knows what was played and has no standing to report it. |

## Behavior

The flow is: **show (17/18) → setlist → match songs to registered works → identify the reporting body and territory → submit → track the response.**

Two things make this tractable in a way it never has been:

- **The show already exists on the platform.** Date, venue, territory, act — all known from the booking (17). The setlist is the only missing datum, and it is the one thing the musician has in their hand while packing down.
- **The submission is against the user's own PRO membership**, not the platform's. Like `10.01.06` D-02, this **dodges the parent domain's `Q-03` accreditation gate entirely** — the performer is already a member; the platform only formats and delivers.

The honest limits: cover songs report to the *writer's* work, not the performer's — you report that you played someone else's song and they get paid, which is correct and counterintuitive. And PROs distribute live money on their own schedules and thresholds, some of which effectively ignore small shows regardless of what is filed.

### Happy Path

1. A show completes on the platform (domain 17/18) — venue, date, territory, act all known
2. The musician is prompted for the setlist while it is still true — at pack-down, not three weeks later
3. Songs match to registered works (`10.02.03`'s machinery, against their own catalogue)
4. The return is formatted for the relevant PRO and submitted against the performer's membership
5. Performance income appears on a later distribution (`10.09`) and reconciles in `10.02`

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Covers night | Money goes to the songs' writers, not the performer. Correct and unintuitive | Explained, not silently filed. The performer earns nothing and reports anyway |
| Setlist never captured | Back to the status quo: nobody reports, nobody gets paid | The prompt's timing is the entire feature. See DT-02 |
| Songs aren't registered works | Nothing to report against | → `10.05.03` leakage finding: your own songs are unregistered |
| PRO ignores small shows | Real. Some distribution schemes have thresholds or sample-based allocation | Honest: filing may earn nothing. Do not promise money |
| Performer has no PRO membership | No route to file | → `10.05.03`; the same finding as `10.01.01` DT-03 |
| Venue and act both report | Duplicate returns from two sides | The PRO deduplicates; the platform should not double-file blindly |
| Show wasn't on the platform | The majority at launch | Manual entry; loses the whole "byproduct of the booking" advantage |
| Improvised / unreleased material | No registered work to match against, so no return line is produced. The setlist entry survives on the 17/18 record; the collections side simply has nothing to file | Marked unmatched, not an error. If it is the performer's *own* unreleased original, it routes to `10.05.03` as a registration prompt — register the work now and future performances of it become reportable |
| Setlist edited after submission | Performer corrects a wrong or missing song post-filing | Amended return re-submitted against the same show; the prior filing is superseded, not duplicated (`10.01.03`'s expected-by clock restarts on the amendment) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Show complete | Gig ended (17/18) | Prompt for the setlist |
| Captured | Setlist entered | Matched against the catalogue |
| Unmatched | Songs not registered works | Leakage finding, not an error |
| Submitted | Filed with the PRO | Expected-by (`10.01.03` D-03's pattern) |
| Amended | Setlist corrected after filing | Superseding return sent; expected-by clock restarts |
| Overdue | Silence past expected-by | Alarm |
| Distributed | Income observed | Reconciled in `10.02` |
| Not filed | No membership / no route | Honest gap with its cause |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a live/events feature and belongs with the gig in domain 17 or 18 | The show is theirs; the setlist is an artifact of the show | ❌ **REJECTED, with the boundary drawn where the money starts.** The show, the setlist as a *record*, and the fan-facing "what they played" surface are all 17/18's. What is domain 10's is the **collections half**: matching songs to registered works, identifying the reporting body and territory, formatting the return, tracking the acknowledgement, reconciling the income. Those are indistinguishable from `10.01`'s registration mechanics and share `10.01.03`'s expected-by/alarm pattern wholesale. The clean split: **17/18 own the setlist; 10 owns the return.** The setlist is an input this domain consumes, exactly as it consumes a statement from `10.02.01`. |
| DT-02 | The hard part is the PRO integration | It is the technical-sounding half — formats, submission, acknowledgements | ❌ **REJECTED — the hard part is the capture, and it is `02.02.03`'s close prompt in a different room.** Filing is mechanical once you have a setlist. **Nobody has a setlist**, because the moment it is true (pack-down, 1am, gear in the van) is the moment nobody is doing admin, and three weeks later it is a reconstruction from memory — which is `problem-statement.md`'s root cause, verbatim, in a live context. WeJammin's only real advantage is that it is **already in the room for the booking**, so the prompt can fire when the fact is still true. That is the platform's thesis (`D-18`) applied to gigs rather than sessions, and it means this feature's success is decided by a prompt's timing, not by an integration. **If the prompt mistimes, this degrades into a form nobody fills in — which is the status quo, which is free.** Precisely the failure `credits-attribution-cx.md` CX-02 identifies for session capture. |
| DT-03 | Operator is a bystander here, like everywhere else in domain 10 | The `10.01` Role Matrix finding — venues do not hold rights and do not collect | ❌ **REJECTED — this is the single exception, and it was nearly missed.** A venue holds the **blanket licence that generated the money**, and in several territories has a reporting obligation of its own. They can independently confirm the show and the setlist from the room's side. That makes Operator a *reporting party* here — the only place across this domain's 28 leaf features where they are anything at all. `personas.md` describes them as both buyer and seller, running the business at a loading dock on a phone; a setlist confirmation at pack-down is exactly that shape. It also creates the duplicate-return case above, which nobody would have predicted from the candidate's name. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **17/18 own the setlist; domain 10 owns the return** | DT-01. The setlist is an input this domain consumes, like a statement. |
| D-02 | Filed against the **performer's own PRO membership** — not the platform's standing | Like `10.01.06` D-02, this dodges the parent's `Q-03` accreditation gate entirely. |
| D-03 | **The prompt's timing is the feature** | DT-02. Same principle as `02.02.03` D-01: capture while the fact is still true, or it is a reconstruction. |
| D-04 | **Operator is a reporting party** — the one exception in domain 10 | DT-03. Their blanket licence generated the money and in some territories they must report. |
| D-05 | Covers are filed even though the performer earns nothing from them | The money goes to the writer, which is correct. A feature that only files when the user profits is a feature that files wrong. |
| D-06 | No promised money | Some PROs allocate live income by sampling and effectively ignore small shows. Filing is right; earning is not guaranteed. |
