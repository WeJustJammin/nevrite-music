# Feature: Negotiated Clearance & Revenue-Share Terms

> **Number**: 11.05.03
> **Parent**: [11.05 Sample & Derivative Clearance](./11.05-sample-derivative-clearance-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The path for everything the programmatic route cannot take: no standing terms, a scope the terms do not cover,
an interpolation whose value is a judgement, or a bootleg that already exists and needs blessing. It also owns
the terms structure that makes sample clearance affordable at all for the long tail — **revenue-share instead
of a fee**, where the sampled party takes an ongoing slice rather than a number nobody can price up front.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Both sides. As the sampled party: receives a request naming the work, what was taken, and the offer — fee or share. As the sampling party: asks, and finds out that asking is the part that takes six weeks everywhere else. Rev-share terms **change their ownership record**, not just their bank balance. |
| Producer | Full | Same. The requesting party in most cases; per personas.md carries the client deadline, so the response time *is* the product here. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A negotiated clearance is a scoped request between two works' owner sets: "I used 8 bars of yours; here are the
terms I propose." Both sides may be several people, so it inherits the multi-party routing from 11.02.04 —
**simultaneous, never sequential**, for the same latency reasons.

The terms come in two shapes with very different consequences:

- **Fee** — a number, paid once, done. Clean, and requires pricing a track whose fate nobody knows yet.
- **Revenue-share** — an ongoing slice of the new work. Self-adjusting, fair at both extremes, and it **writes
  into the ownership record** (parent Q-02) rather than producing a licence and stopping.

Per 11.05.02 DT-03, revenue-share is the structurally advantaged default on this platform: an ongoing share is
only enforceable where both works' splits live in one registry, which is exactly the thing WeJammin has and a
phone call to a publisher does not.

### Happy Path

1. Programmatic path falls through (no terms, or scope not covered) — or the source is simply not pre-priced
2. Requester composes: what was taken, where, how long, the intended release, the proposed terms
3. Routes simultaneously to all owners of the sampled work's relevant side(s)
4. Policy checked first — a derivative-use block ends it before anyone is disturbed
5. Sampled owners counter, accept, or decline with a reason
6. Agreement → instrument issues (11.08.02); rev-share terms register as an ongoing obligation
7. Encumbrance clears (11.02.03); the new work becomes clearable downstream

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Sampled work has 6 owners; one declines | **Terminal.** Unanimity is required — same rule as 11.04.02 D-01, share size irrelevant. | "Declined by [name] — this can't be cleared" |
| Rev-share proposed on a work with existing rev-share obligations | Stacking. The new work's splits must accommodate both, and the arithmetic can exceed 100%. | "This would put your obligations at 115%" — must be caught before agreement, not after |
| Sampled party asks for 60% | Legitimate and common — a famous sample commands it. Not an error. | The requester takes it or walks |
| Requester's track has no revenue yet (the normal case) | Rev-share costs nothing today, which is exactly why it works for the long tail | "£0 now, 8% of what it earns" |
| Sampled party wants a fee; requester can only offer share | An ordinary impasse; the platform does not resolve it | Both positions visible; no adjudication |
| Track is unreleased and the requester will not disclose it | **Producers will not declare if declaring leaks** — parent Q-03. May gate adoption. | See Q-03 |
| Agreement reached, then the sampled work's splits change | Rev-share follows the new split; the licence stands | Nothing visible |
| Bootleg: the remix already exists and is popular | Zero leverage — "please bless what I already did"; `#CX-04` | Handled in 11.05.05; the terms machinery is this feature's |
| Sampled party never responds | Request expires; **the sample stays uncleared.** Silence never clears. | Consistent with the domain-wide silence question (11.04.03 Q-01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Routing, policy evaluation | Brief |
| Empty | No requests | Quiet inbox |
| Populated | Request in flight | Per-owner board: accepted / countered / pending / declined |
| Error | Routing failed | Not delivered; requester told honestly. **Never silently pending.** |
| Partial | Some owners agreed | "4 of 6" — **not cleared.** Partial agreement never clears a sample. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This feature is the boring fallback; the interesting one is programmatic (11.05.02) | The domain rationale's framing, which leads with programmatic clearance as the unlock | ❌ REJECTED — inverted, on the evidence of 11.05.01 DT-03. For years, **this is the path almost every real sample takes**, because sampling skews to catalogue and catalogue is off-platform or un-pre-priced. Programmatic is the better feature on a corpus that does not exist yet; negotiated is the good-enough feature on the corpus that does. Treating it as the fallback would mean under-building the only path with users. |
| DT-02 | Revenue-share is just a pricing option — a field on the terms object | Modelling economy | ❌ REJECTED — a fee produces a **licence**; a rev-share produces a licence **and an ongoing ownership obligation that writes into domain 09's registry**. The second one means this feature mutates the rights record, which is a different class of thing entirely: it needs the splits arithmetic to be validated against existing obligations (see the 115% edge case), it needs domain 10 to pay it forever, and it survives the licence's own term. Modelling it as a price field loses all of that. |
| DT-03 | Sample clearance is bilateral: one sampling work, one sampled work | The obvious framing, and the way every clearance conversation is described | ❌ REJECTED — it is **n-lateral and recursive**. The sampled work may itself carry rev-share obligations to a third work (stacking), and the sampling work's splits must accommodate every obligation it inherits. A producer sampling a track that already owes 20% to an earlier source is taking on that 20%, not the 8% they negotiated. Nobody in the conversation is looking at that arithmetic, and it can silently exceed 100%. **The registry is the only thing that can see the whole chain — which is a genuine advantage, and only if this feature is built to walk it rather than to record a bilateral deal.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Unanimity required — any owner of the sampled work may decline | Consistent with 11.04.02 D-01; share size is irrelevant |
| D-02 | Rev-share is a distinct terms type, not a price field | Per DT-02 — it writes an ongoing ownership obligation, not a one-time consideration |
| D-03 | Obligation stacking is validated before agreement | Per DT-03 — inherited obligations can silently exceed 100%, and only the registry can see the chain |
| D-04 | Routing is simultaneous | Consistent with 11.01.05 D-01 and 11.02.04 D-01 |
