# Feature: Programmatic Instant Clearance

> **Number**: 11.05.02
> **Parent**: [11.05 Sample & Derivative Clearance](./11.05-sample-derivative-clearance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Clears a sample in seconds: the declared source resolves to an owner set the registry already holds, those
owners have standing sample terms, and the licence issues with nobody asked and nothing negotiated. It is the
domain's most concrete differentiator — the claim that clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a
lookup plus a pre-set price — and it works exactly as well as the fraction of sources that are on-platform and
pre-priced.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Both sides.** As seller: sets standing sample terms on their works ("4 bars or less: £200 or 5% writer share") and finds out they were sampled via a receipt. As buyer: clears an interpolation or a sample of someone else's WeJammin work instantly. |
| Producer | Full | The sampling persona and the feature's centre of gravity. Experiences it as "can I use this today, or is this track dead?" — the only question that matters when a beat is finished and a client is waiting. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The path: **declared source (11.05.01) → registry resolution → standing terms lookup (11.04.01 applied to
sampling) → policy check → price → issue.** Any step that fails falls through to negotiated clearance
(11.05.03) — never to "cleared". Fail-closed, per 11.02.01 D-02.

The pre-condition everyone skips: **the terms must have been pre-set.** The registry makes the *who* instant;
it does nothing about the *whether* or the *how much*. That is 11.04's job, and it is why this feature is
downstream of a sub-domain that was not in the sweep's candidate list at all (11.04 D-01).

The seller's experience is deliberately passive and is the feature's sharpest edge: **the first a writer hears
of their song being sampled is a receipt.** They pre-authorised it. That is the product working as designed
(`#CX-01` synthesis Q4), and it will still upset people.

### Happy Path

1. Producer declares a sample (11.05.01); source resolves to an on-platform work
2. System reads the source's licensing policy (11.04) — a derivative-use block ends it here, at any price
3. System reads the source owners' standing sample terms
4. Terms cover the use (duration, element, intended release scope) → price computes
5. Producer sees the number and takes it — one action, no conversation, no waiting
6. Licence issues (11.08.02); encumbrance clears (11.02.03); payment routes to the source's split
7. Source owners get a receipt, and their catalogue earned money while they slept

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Source on-platform, no standing terms set | **Falls through to negotiated.** The most common case for years. | "This one needs a conversation — [request terms]" |
| Source's co-owner blocked derivative use | Refusal outranks pricing; terminal, not a price negotiation | "Not available for sampling" — no counter-offer path, because there is no price |
| Terms cover 4 bars; producer used 16 | Falls through to negotiated for the excess scope | "Your terms cover 4 bars. You used 16 — [request]" |
| Terms change between lookup and acceptance | Quote pins the terms version at read | Producer gets the price they were shown; per 11.03.01 D-02 |
| Registry unreachable | Falls through to negotiated. **Never to cleared.** | Degrade to slow, never to wrong |
| Source is itself encumbered | Transitive: you cannot clear from someone who has not cleared | "The source has an uncleared sample — this can't be cleared yet" |
| Writer discovers via receipt that their song is in something they hate | They pre-authorised it. **The 11.04.01 DT-02 hazard in its sharpest form** — a category block was the only protection and they did not set one. | The receipt is the first notice. This is the design. |
| Producer clears, then abandons the track | Licence issued and paid for. Non-refundable? | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Sample of a work whose splits later change | Licence stands (irrevocable); payouts follow the new split, per 11.02.01 | Nothing visible to the producer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Resolution + terms lookup | Must be genuinely fast — "instant" is the entire product claim |
| Empty | Source not on-platform, or no terms | Falls through. **Should not be presented as a failure** — it is the normal case today (DT-03 in 11.05.01). |
| Populated | Terms found and covering | Price, scope, one action to take it |
| Error | Any step failed | Falls through to negotiated. Fail closed. |
| Partial | Terms cover part of the use | "Covered for 4 bars, negotiate the rest" — a split path, both routes live |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the domain's killer feature and should be a `must` | The domain rationale states it plainly: "Programmatic sample clearance is the same unlock: clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a lookup plus a pre-set price" | ⏳ **DEFERRED to the owner** — the mechanism is right and the addressable set is nearly empty. Per 11.05.01 DT-03: sampling skews to catalogue (a 1974 soul record, not last week's session), and no amount of split-at-creation reaches works made before the platform existed. This feature is spectacular on a corpus that will take years to exist. It is the clearest case in the domain of a **structurally correct feature with a cold-start denominator** — and calling it `must` or `could` is a go-to-market judgement, not an agent's. Proposed `should` with the caveat stated loudly rather than a confident `must`. |
| DT-02 | Instant clearance needs escrow or a holding period — the source owner should get a chance to object | Fairness instinct, reacting to the receipt-as-first-notice edge above | ❌ REJECTED — a holding period *is* asking, and asking is the six weeks this feature exists to delete. A "programmatic clearance with a 48h objection window" is not programmatic; it is a slow clearance with extra steps, and a producer cannot deliver on it. The protection against unwanted uses is the **category block** (11.04.02), set in advance, which is categorical and free. Owners who want to be asked simply set no standing terms — that is the opt-out, and it is complete. |
| DT-03 | Revenue-share terms are the natural default for programmatic sampling, not fees | Reasoning from what the platform can do that a phone call cannot: a fee requires agreeing a number (hard, requires knowing the track's fate), while a share requires agreeing a fraction (easy, self-adjusting, and the registry can actually enforce it because it already holds the splits) | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a genuine structural advantage that the domain rationale does not claim. Off-platform sample clearance defaults to fees **because there is no shared infrastructure to enforce an ongoing share** — you cannot trust a stranger's accounting. WeJammin holds both works' splits in one registry, so a revenue-share is enforceable by construction. That flips the default: on-platform sampling should be share-first, fee-optional. **This makes clearing cheap for the producer with no track record and fair to the source, which is precisely the long-tail case incumbents cannot serve.** Feeds 11.05.03 and parent Q-02 (rev-share writing into the ownership record). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every failure falls through to negotiated, never to cleared | Fail closed, per 11.02.01 D-02 — an instant clearance that was not verified is an unhonourable licence |
| D-02 | No holding period or objection window | Per DT-02 — a holding period is asking, and asking is the latency this feature deletes. The category block is the opt-out. |
| D-03 | Revenue-share is the proposed default; fee is optional | Per DT-03 — the shared registry makes an ongoing share enforceable, which off-platform clearance cannot do. This is a real advantage worth designing around. |
| D-04 | Policy is checked before terms | A blocked derivative use must never be priced; consistent with 11.04 D-03 |
