## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01 + CX-02: Declaration → Programmatic → Negotiated (the fall-through spine)

**Relationship**: The three form one flow with two exits. Declaration identifies the source. If the source is
on-platform **and** its owners set standing sample terms (11.04.01 applied to sampling), the programmatic path
prices and issues instantly. Everything else falls through to a human. The sub-domain's value is entirely
determined by what fraction takes the fast exit — and that fraction is not a function of the registry's
quality but of how many owners bothered to pre-set terms.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the sampling party in the overwhelming majority of cases; experiences this as "can I use this today or is this dead?"
- **Musician**: on both sides — sampling someone, and being sampled. The multi-hyphenate reality means the same person is the buyer on Tuesday and the seller on Thursday, which is a genuine argument for the two sides sharing one vocabulary.
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The declaration is owned by the declaring work; the terms are owned by the source work's owners. Neither side owns the *clearance* — it is a new object referencing both.
2. **Trigger chain**: Declare → identify → look up terms → price → issue, **or** fall through. If the lookup fails (registry unreachable), fall through to negotiated — **never** to "cleared". Same fail-closed rule as 11.02.01 D-02.
3. **Permission intersection**: The source work's licensing policy (11.04) governs. A source whose co-owner blocked "derivative use" is not programmatically clearable no matter how good the terms are — refusal outranks pricing here exactly as it does in 11.03.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An instant clearance notifies the source's owners *after the fact* — they pre-authorised it. This is 11.04.01 DT-02's hazard in its sharpest form: the first a writer hears of their song being sampled is a receipt. That is the product working as designed, and it will still upset people.
5. **State transition conflict**: Source terms change between lookup and issue. The quote pins the terms version at read (same rule as 11.03.01 D-02) — a price that moves between offer and acceptance is fraud.

### CX-03: Declaration ↔ Interpolation (the fingerprint blind spot)

**Relationship**: An interpolation is a re-recording of someone else's melody. It shares **no audio** with its
source, so fingerprinting is structurally blind to it — not weak, blind. Declaration is therefore the *only*
route by which an interpolation ever gets cleared, which means the honesty of a single human is the entire
mechanism. This is the sharpest instance of 11.02.03 DT-01's point: the undetectable cases are the ones that
produce lawsuits.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: usually the writer who did the interpolating, and usually the one who does not know they needed a licence at all — "I just played something similar" is not a defence and they do not know that
- **Producer**: runs the replay session that creates the new master; knows what was asked for
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — an interpolation creates a **new master** with no relationship to the source's master. Only the composition side is shared. This is why 11.05.04 is a separate feature (index D-02).
2. **Trigger chain**: Declare → route to the composition's owners only → publishing-side clearance, typically revenue-share (CX-05). The master-side step that sampling requires simply does not exist and must not be synthesised.
3. **Permission intersection**: The source's *master* owner has no standing at all here — a fact that surprises everyone and is worth surfacing explicitly, because a producer will otherwise chase the wrong party.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Composition owners only.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — an interpolation of a work that is itself an interpolation (chains are real; so are cycles).

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 11.05.02 Programmatic | 11.05.04 Interpolation | Considered: should interpolations clear programmatically too — the source's owners are enumerated, the terms could be pre-set, the machinery is identical? Rejected. Interpolation value is not a lookup: it depends on **how much** of the melody was used and how recognisable it is, which is a judgement no standing term can encode. Programmatic clearance works for samples because "this exact recording, 4 bars" is objective. "Is this the same melody?" is litigated for a living. Pre-set interpolation terms would be pre-setting the answer to the actual dispute. |
| R-02 | 11.05.01 Declaration | 11.05.05 Remix | No direct edge. A remix is authorised **before** it exists (you get stems, you make a remix) — there is nothing to declare, because permission preceded creation. The exception is the bootleg (unauthorised remix, retroactively legitimised), which routes through negotiated clearance (CX-04), not declaration. The asymmetry — declare-after vs permit-before — is exactly why remix is a separate feature from sampling. |
| R-03 | 11.05.02 Programmatic | 11.05.05 Remix | Rejected for the same reason as R-02: an authorised remix has no clearance step to make instant. Its permission was the stem grant. Where a remix *does* need clearance (bootleg), it needs negotiation, not a lookup — the remixer has no leverage and the price is not a rate-card question. |
