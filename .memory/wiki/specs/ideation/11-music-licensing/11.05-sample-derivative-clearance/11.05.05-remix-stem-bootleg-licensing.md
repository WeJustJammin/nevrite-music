# Feature: Remix, Stem & Bootleg Licensing

> **Number**: 11.05.05
> **Parent**: [11.05 Sample & Derivative Clearance](./11.05-sample-derivative-clearance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Licensing a work to be remade: granting stems to a remixer, setting the terms a remix comes back on, and
retroactively legitimising a bootleg that already exists and is already popular. It inverts the rest of the
sub-domain — a remix is **permitted before it exists**, so there is nothing to declare — except for the
bootleg, which arrives finished, unauthorised, and with the remixer holding no leverage at all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Licenses their own works for remix (stems out, terms set); remixes others'. As the bootlegger: asks to have an already-popular remix blessed, from a position of zero leverage. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the stem holder — per personas.md they have the session files, so a remix grant is *physically* theirs to make even where the rights are shared. **A structural hazard worth naming**: possession of stems is not authority to grant them. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | Read-only | An official remix or blessed bootleg becomes a fan-facing **release** (domain 12/20). The licence is not a fan surface; the record it produces is. Listed to make the boundary explicit. |

## Behavior

Three flows on one machinery:

- **Stem grant** — the source's owners release stems to a named remixer under terms agreed up front. Permission precedes creation, so there is no declaration and no encumbrance.
- **Remix terms** — what the resulting remix owes: a fee, a share, or nothing; who owns the remix master; whether the remixer may exploit it independently.
- **Bootleg legitimisation** — the exception that inverts everything. The remix exists, was never authorised, and is popular. Clearance runs backwards through 11.05.03's negotiated machinery (`#CX-04`) with the remixer holding nothing to bargain with.

The bootleg case is under-served everywhere and genuinely common: the unofficial edit that broke on a
dancefloor and now cannot be released, streamed, or paid for. It is also the case where the platform's
registry helps most — the remixer usually does not know *who* to ask, and that is a lookup.

### Happy Path (stem grant)

1. Owner sets remix terms on a work: who may request stems, what the remix owes, who owns the resulting master
2. Remixer requests stems; policy (11.04) is checked; co-owner blocks are terminal
3. Grant issues with terms attached; **stems are delivered under a licence, not just shared**
4. Remixer creates; the remix carries its obligation from birth — no declaration, no encumbrance
5. Remix releases; obligations flow per the agreed terms

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Producer holds the stems but not the authority to grant them | **Possession ≠ authority.** The grant must check the registry, not the file system. | "You have these stems. [Name] co-owns the master — they need to agree." |
| Bootleg already released and popular | Retroactive negotiation, zero leverage; `#CX-04` | "This is already out. That weakens your position — here's who to ask." — honest, not encouraging |
| Bootleg owner refuses; remix already has 2M plays | Not a licensing outcome — an enforcement one. Routes to domain 24. | The platform does not pretend a refusal is a negotiation |
| Remixer wants to exploit the remix independently | A distinct right from permission-to-remix, and routinely conflated | Must be an explicit term, never implied by the stem grant |
| Stems delivered, remix never made | Grant expires; stems' licence lapses. **The stems are still in their possession** — a real limitation the platform must not pretend away. | Honest expiry; no DRM theatre |
| Remix of a remix | Chains; obligations stack per 11.05.03 DT-03 | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Stem grant to a stranger leaks an unreleased work | Real risk; the stem grant is also a confidentiality decision | See Q-02 |
| Official remix contest (many remixers, one work) | A grant fan-out; plausible and unspecified | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Remix licensing is sampling with extra steps — same declare-then-clear flow | The sweep bundles it into the derivative cluster, which invites the assumption | ❌ REJECTED — **the temporal order inverts.** Sampling declares *after* creation (you made it, now clear it); remixing permits *before* (you got stems, so permission preceded the work). That inversion removes the encumbrance entirely — an authorised remix is never tainted, because it was never unauthorised. Modelling it as sampling would create an encumbrance-then-clear cycle for a work that was clean from birth, and would miss the actual product here: the **stem grant** and its terms. `#R-02` records the rejected edge. |
| DT-02 | "Bootleg" belongs in domain 24 (Trust & Safety) — it is infringement | Boundary challenge, and half right | ⚠️ **SPLIT** — bootleg-as-infringement (someone is exploiting my work, take it down) is domain 24. Bootleg-as-licensable-object (my unofficial edit broke on a dancefloor and I want to make it legal and pay for it) is **licensing**, and it is genuinely under-served: no incumbent has a route from "popular unauthorised remix" to "released, paid, legitimate". The two share a subject and nothing else — one is an enforcement action against a party, the other is a transaction between willing parties. Scoped here to the retroactive-legitimisation flow only (index D-03). |
| DT-03 | The producer holding the stems is the party who can grant them | The physical reality: per personas.md the Producer "owns the room" and has the session files. It reads as obvious. | ❌ REJECTED — and it is the sharpest hazard in this feature. **Possession is not authority.** The Producer can *physically* send stems to anyone; the registry decides whether they *may*. This is the Producer anti-persona's "withholds stems as leverage" (personas.md) turned inside out — the same asymmetry, used to grant rather than to withhold. The platform cannot stop a producer emailing a zip, but it can refuse to issue a licence, which means the remix has no legitimate existence. **The licence, not the file, is the control point** — and that is the only honest position available, since no DRM survives contact with a stem folder. |
