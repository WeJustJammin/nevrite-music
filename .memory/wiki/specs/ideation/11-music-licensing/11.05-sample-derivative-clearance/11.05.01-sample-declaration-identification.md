# Feature: Sample Declaration & Source Identification

> **Number**: 11.05.01
> **Parent**: [11.05 Sample & Derivative Clearance](./11.05-sample-derivative-clearance-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-30

## Overview

Establishes what a work is made of: which sample, from which recording, of which composition, for how long,
how prominently, and where in the track. It is the key that every downstream clearance path looks up — an
unidentified source cannot be priced, cleared, or paid — and it is the point where fingerprinting stops being
sufficient, because the most litigated borrowings are the ones no machine can hear.

It exists because the platform's thesis is capture-at-source (D-18): the only moment anyone knows what is in
the mix is the moment the mix is made, by the person who made it. Every incumbent asks this question years
later, of someone who has forgotten, when money is at stake. **This feature is that question asked on the
day** — and the entire sub-domain is downstream of whether it gets an honest answer.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Full | Declares borrowings in works they own or contributed to, **scoped to their own contribution** (D-06) — a topline writer declares the melody they lifted, not what the beat underneath contains. Reviews fingerprint suggestions raised against their own stems. Sees what they have *inherited*: which borrowings a collaborator declared on a shared work, who declared them, and when. **The persona most often unaware a declaration was needed** — personas.md names their worst accidental behaviour as "uploads a stem containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream", and the interpolation case (`#CX-03`) is worse still, because "I just played something similar" does not feel like borrowing at all. The flow must teach, not merely collect. As the **sampled** party they see nothing here — a declaration against their work is invisible to them until a clearance is requested (D-09). |
| **Producer** | Full | **The primary role.** Per personas.md they hold the stems and are "the person who *knows* who played what" — extended here to *what is in the mix*. They are the only party who can identify a source that predates the session, the only one who can name a treatment ("it's pitched down a fourth and reversed"), and the one whose client delivery is worthless if this is wrong. In the session-close flow they see the enumerated borrow prompt (D-05), the fingerprint candidate list with timestamps, and the per-contribution declaration state for every stem in the project — including **the stems they did not make**, where the honest answer is "ask the person who sent it" (D-06). Their constraint from personas.md governs the whole feature: their work happens inside a DAW, and a flow that requires leaving the session to fill in a web form will not be used. |
| **Operator** | None | **Renting the room is not being in the mix.** An Operator qua Operator sells time and space and never touches the audio's composition. The house engineer who tracked the session *does* declare — but they are wearing the Producer lens when they do it, not the Operator lens (parent index Role Matrix note; consistent with 11.02.02's identical finding). Explicit structural absence, not an oversight. |
| **Fan** | None | Not a fan surface, in either direction. A declaration is a pre-release working record about liability; there is nothing here a Fan can act on. What a Fan eventually sees is a **credit** — "contains a sample of X" on a released work — which is rendered by domain 02 from this record plus the issued licence, and only after both exist. A Fan never sees an undeclared, unidentified, or uncleared borrowing, and must not: pre-release declaration state is commercially sensitive and, per parent Q-03, potentially leak-sensitive. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None
>
> **Multi-hyphenate note (personas.md, "The Structural Fact")**: the same human is routinely on both sides at
> once — a producer declaring a sample on Tuesday is the sampled Musician on Thursday, and may sample *their
> own* earlier work, in which case they are simultaneously declarer and source owner and must still clear with
> their former co-writers (see Edge Cases). The feature must never assume declarer ≠ source owner.

## Behavior

A declaration is a **structured, attributed, revisable assertion that a specific work contains material
originating elsewhere**. It identifies the source, quantifies what was taken, and records who said so and on
what basis. It is *identification only* — it does not price, clear, notify the source's owners, judge whether
the borrowing is legally significant, or gate anything (D-08, D-09, D-11).

### What a declaration names

Every declaration carries these fields. Each field additionally records **who asserted it — human or machine**
(D-07), because a record that cannot say where a fact came from is useless in the dispute it exists to prevent.

| Field | What it captures | Why it is required |
|---|---|---|
| **Source recording** | Which master — the specific commercial release, pressing, remaster, live version, or on-platform work | Resolves the **master** owner set. A 1974 original and its 2011 remaster are different masters with different owners; clearing the wrong one clears nothing. |
| **Source composition** | Which song | Resolves the **publisher/writer** set — a *different owner set*, often with zero overlap with the master's. A producer who clears the master and not the publishing has cleared nothing (D-04). |
| **Element type** | One of: loop · one-shot / drum hit · vocal phrase · instrumental phrase · drum break · texture / atmosphere · melody replayed (interpolation → routes to 11.05.04) · full stem | Determines the route and makes the prompt enumerative rather than abstract (D-05). |
| **Extent** | Duration in **seconds to 0.1s** *and* in **bars/beats at the new work's tempo**; **every** occurrence timestamped in the new work (not just the first); the timestamp range taken from the source | Bars are how musicians describe borrowings and how publishers quote them; seconds are what a machine can verify. Both, or the record is unusable by one of its two audiences. |
| **Prominence facts** | Occurrence count; total sounded duration as a **percentage of the new work's runtime**; whether it is looped for the whole work; whether it is the hook; whether it sits under a lead vocal or in the background | **Prominence, not duration, is what prices a sample** (DT-04). These are captured as *facts*, never as a self-assessed price-determining rating. |
| **Treatment** | Pitched · time-stretched · chopped · reversed · filtered · layered · unaltered (multi-select) | Explains fingerprint confidence, feeds the "is it recognisable" judgement that 11.05.03 negotiates over, and is the honest reason the machine missed it. |
| **Fragment provenance** | Where the declarer got the audio: commercial release · sample pack with a licence · beat purchased or leased from a third party · another WeJammin work · a collaborator's unreleased material · vinyl / library disc / field recording · unknown | Distinguishes "I know exactly what this is" from "I bought a pack and assume it's fine" (Q-02) from "a record I bought in 2009". Each has a different clearance route and a different truth-value. |
| **Known clearance status** | Never cleared · believed covered by a pack/beat licence · cleared elsewhere previously · unknown | The declarer's belief, recorded as a belief. "Believed covered" is the single most common wrong answer in modern production and must be representable so it can be checked. |
| **Confidence** | Certain · probable · **unidentified** | "Unidentified" is a first-class terminal value (D-03), never a validation error. |

### Three identification routes, in descending reliability

1. **On-platform lookup** — the source is a WeJammin work; the registry enumerates its owners. This is the case
   the whole domain is built for, and it is the only route that resolves *both* sides (master and composition)
   in one step. **Its reliability is inherited, not intrinsic** (DT-08): a source work whose own splits are
   unattested resolves to a guess wearing a registry's confidence, and the declaration must carry the source's
   attestation grade forward rather than laundering it into certainty.
2. **Fingerprint suggestion** — the source is a known commercial recording. Machine proposes with timestamps;
   human confirms, rejects, or corrects. This is the **only** route where the machine outperforms the human:
   a producer's memory of *which master* is worse than a fingerprint's (DT-07).
3. **Manual declaration** — the source is a record-shop find, a friend's unreleased track, a library disc, a
   leased beat whose contents the declarer has never been told, or an interpolation with no audio in common.
   **The machine is blind and the human is the entire mechanism.**

Per 11.02.03 D-01, fingerprinting **suggests**; humans **declare**. An auto-declared sample propagates
encumbrance downward through every derivative on the strength of a probabilistic match. The division of labour
is precise and is not "human always wins": **the human declares that a borrowing exists and what it is; the
machine measures how much of it there is** (D-07). Neither substitutes for the other.

### Who is asked, and what "the declarer" actually means

The session owner is frequently **not the person who knows** (DT-05). A guest beatmaker sends a beat with a
sample already in it; a topline writer records over a leased instrumental; a mixing engineer closes a project
containing four stems from three people. Asking the work's owner "what's in this?" asks the wrong human.

Therefore **declaration attaches to the contribution, not to the work** (D-06). Each contributor is asked about
what they brought. A work's declaration state is the union of its contributions' states, and it is legitimately
**incomplete** — "stem 3 came from [name], who has not answered" is a true and useful state, and it is far more
honest than a work-level declaration signed by someone who was guessing. The contribution graph is domain 07's;
this feature reads it.

Where a contributor cannot be asked — a beat bought from an off-platform marketplace, an uncontactable
collaborator — the declaration records **"provenance asserted by the seller / unavailable"** and names who
would know. That is a boundary in the record, and it is exactly the information a dispute needs.

### When it is asked

Capture moments, in order of value:

1. **Session close** (domain 07) — the highest-value moment; everyone is present and the facts are fresh
2. **Stem/contribution upload** — asked of the contributor, about their own contribution (D-06)
3. **Work upload** — for material created off-platform
4. **Pre-release checklist** (domain 12) — the last honest moment before the record is public
5. **On a fingerprint hit arriving late** — including after release; the suggestion is raised, never merged (D-10)
6. **On attestation** (11.02.02) — "nothing borrowed is unpaid" is the half of the honesty ritual this feature supplies the facts for

**Declaration is not an event; it is a continuously revisable record** (DT-10). The happy path's "session
close" framing describes the best capture moment, not the only one. Works are reopened, stems are swapped,
remixes add sources years later. A declaration can be added, corrected, or superseded at any time — and per
domain-11 P-02 / D-06, **the old record always survives**: superseded declarations are never deleted, because
what someone declared *at the time they declared it* is the fact a dispute turns on.

### What this feature deliberately does not do

| It does not | Because |
|---|---|
| Notify the source work's owners | D-09 — declaring is not asking. Notification happens when a clearance is *requested* (11.05.02/03). Otherwise declaration is a stranger-spam vector (DT-09) and a leak vector (parent Q-03). |
| Price anything | 11.05.02 / 11.05.03 |
| Clear anything | 11.05.02 / 11.05.03 |
| Attach the encumbrance | 11.02.03 owns the taint and its downward propagation (index D-04) |
| Judge de-minimis | Parent Q-01 — the platform does not assert a threshold it cannot defend. But see DT-06: refusing to assert one and asking the question anyway is not neutral. |
| Block release | D-02 — a hard gate manufactures documented lies (DT-01) |
| Refuse a true but inconvenient fact | D-12 — a declaration that creates a cycle in the derivation graph is still true. Record it; let the walker cope. |

### Happy Path

1. **Session close** in domain 07. The system asks each contributor, in plain words and about their own contribution only: *"Is anything in what you brought from somewhere else?"* — followed by the enumerated list (D-05): a loop, a one-shot or drum hit, a vocal, a drum break, **a melody you played from another song**, a whole stem, a beat you bought. Not the word "sample" alone, and never the word "interpolation".
2. **Fingerprint analysis runs in the background** against the mix and the individual stems. The declaration form is fully usable with zero results; nothing is blocked on it (D-11). Results appear as they arrive and are labelled as machine suggestions.
3. **Producer confirms, rejects, or corrects each candidate.** Rejection is a first-class answer and is recorded with the rejector's identity — "the machine heard it and a human said no" is a materially different record from "the machine never heard it".
4. **Producer adds sources the machine missed** — the interpolation, the vinyl find, the friend's unreleased track. This step exists because it is where the lawsuits are (DT-02).
5. **For each source, identification runs.** On-platform → owners enumerate, with the source's attestation grade carried forward (DT-08). Fingerprint match → the master is named and the human confirms *which* master (DT-07). Neither → manual, and "unidentified" is offered as an explicit option, not a dead end (D-03).
6. **Extent and prominence are captured.** Where the fingerprint identified the source, occurrence count, timestamps and total sounded percentage are **measured from the audio and marked machine-asserted** (D-07). Where it did not, the human supplies them and they are marked human-asserted. The declarer is never asked to rate their own borrowing's importance (DT-04).
7. **Treatment and fragment provenance are captured** — pitched/chopped/reversed, and where the audio came from.
8. **The declaration is written and attributed** — declarer identity, timestamp, per-field assertion source. It is visible to the work's co-owners and to nobody else (D-09).
9. **The system states plainly what it now knows and what it does not**: *"This work contains 2 declared borrowings. One resolves to owners we can reach. One is unidentified. Nothing has been asked of anyone yet."*
10. **11.02.03 attaches the encumbrance**; this feature's job is done. The resolution route (11.05.02 programmatic, 11.05.03 negotiated, 11.05.04 interpolation) is offered but never forced — declaring must not immediately start a chore (11.02.03 D-03).
11. **A negative answer is also a declaration.** "Nothing borrowed" is recorded as an attributed assertion (D-05), which is what makes never-asked and asked-and-said-no different states — and only the latter can support one-stop via 11.02.02.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Fingerprint proposes a false match** | Human rejects. The rejection is recorded with identity and timestamp. **Never auto-declares** (D-01). The same candidate is not re-raised for that work unless the audio changes. | "We think we hear [track] at 0:42. Is that a sample?" — "No" is a first-class answer |
| **Fingerprint misses an interpolation entirely** | Expected — structurally blind (`#CX-03`). Declaration is the only route. | The prompt asks about "a melody you played from another song", never about "interpolation" |
| **Fingerprint returns two candidate masters for the same composition** (original vs remaster vs live) | Both surfaced with their distinguishing facts. The human picks. This is the case where the machine is *more* reliable than the human (DT-07) — but it still cannot choose for them. | "This composition, three recordings. Which one did you use? [1974 original] [2011 remaster] [1976 live]" |
| **The human's recording choice conflicts with a high-confidence fingerprint match** | The conflict is **surfaced, not silently resolved either way** (DT-07). Human declaration stands (D-01) but the disagreement is recorded on the declaration and travels with it to clearance. | "You said the 1974 original; we hear the 2011 remaster. Those have different owners. Are you sure?" |
| **Fingerprint identifies the *source record's own* sample** — a producer samples a record that itself contains a famous break, and the machine names the break's original | Both are true and both may need clearing. The machine is not wrong; it is one level too deep. Surfaced as two candidates, not one. | "We hear [Amen break] — did you sample [the 1969 original], or a record that used it?" |
| **Fingerprint service is unreachable** | Manual declaration is **fully available**. The machine is an assistant, never a dependency (D-11). The absence of results is displayed as absence, not as a clean bill. | "We couldn't listen this time. Tell us what's in here." — never "No samples found" |
| **Fingerprint result arrives after the declaration was submitted / after release** | Raised as a **new suggestion**, never merged into a closed declaration (D-10). A post-release hit on a licensed work is a dispute, not an edit. | "Something new turned up in [work]. Is this a sample?" — and if the work is licensed, routes to 11.02.03 / domain 24 |
| **Producer does not know what the sample is** ("it's off a record I bought in 2009") | **Partial declaration**: "unidentified source at 0:42, 4 bars, chopped, from vinyl". Encumbers honestly and is resolvable later. Better than nothing by a wide margin. | "Declare it as unidentified" is an offered option, not a dead end (D-03) |
| **An unidentified declaration sits unresolved for years** | **It never expires** (D-04). An expiring encumbrance is a laundering mechanism — wait long enough and your work goes clean (DT-11). | The unidentified borrowing is still there, and the work is still not one-stop, indefinitely |
| **Source is on-platform** | Owners resolve exactly; both sides in one step; programmatic path opens (`#CX-01`). The case the domain exists for. | "[Work] — owners known. [Check clearance]" |
| **Source is on-platform but its own splits are unattested** | The registry knows a *claim*, not a fact. The declaration carries the source's attestation grade forward rather than laundering it into certainty (DT-08). Clearance against it is possible but is not "instant and safe". | "We know who claims [work]. Nobody has attested that the list is complete." |
| **Source is on-platform but private / unreleased** | Identification succeeds; the source's existence is **not** revealed beyond what the declarer already knows (they named it). No notification fires (D-09). Whether clearance can proceed without disclosure is parent Q-03. | The declarer sees what they already knew. Nobody else sees anything. |
| **Source is on-platform and the declarer is one of its owners** (self-sample) | Resolves to a set that **includes the declarer** — and, critically, their former co-writers. Sampling your own record is not self-clearance if you wrote it with two other people. This surprises nearly everyone. | "You own 40% of [work]. [Name] and [Name] own the rest. You still need them." |
| **Source is a major-label recording** | Off-platform. Identification is fine; clearance is not — the platform cannot reach the owner. | "We can identify this. We can't clear it — that's a phone call." Honest and useless, which is honest. |
| **Source is a sample pack bought on-platform** (domain 14) | The platform sold it and knows its licence terms. Whether this auto-declares with terms attached is Q-02 — a genuine consolidation dividend, and a scope hazard (it may render most modern productions visibly encumbered). | Q-02 |
| **Source is a beat bought or leased off-platform** (BeatStars-style) | The declarer **cannot** declare what is inside it — they were never told. Records "provenance asserted by the seller; contents unknown" and names the seller (D-06). A lease also carries its own term limits, which are a separate encumbrance the artist usually does not know exists. | "You bought this beat. Only [seller] knows what's in it. We've recorded that." |
| **Source composition is public domain but the recording is not** | Two answers, and everyone gets this wrong. No composition clearance; the **master** still needs clearing. | "The song is public domain. The recording you used is not." |
| **Source is a field recording / PD material with a claimed arrangement** | Recorded as declared; the platform does not adjudicate whether an arrangement is protected. | Both positions recorded; contested cases route to domain 24 |
| **The declarer is not the person who introduced the sample** (guest beatmaker, session player) | **The dominant real-world case.** Declaration is per-contribution (D-06); the work-level state is the union and may be legitimately incomplete. The work owner is never asked to guess on someone else's behalf. | "Stem 3 came from [name]. They haven't answered yet." — an honest state, not an error |
| **A contributor declares a borrowing on a work others co-own** | Correct — the truth is the truth — but it reduces co-owners' asset value without their consent and is a spite vector. Contest path is 11.02.03 Q-01's; this feature records the declaration and its author, and never silently deletes it. | Co-owners: "[Name] declared a sample in [work]. [Contest]" |
| **Two contributors declare the same sample** | Deduplicated on (source recording, source composition, occurrence timestamps) with a tolerance window. **Both declarers are retained** — corroboration is signal, and the second declaration is not noise. Conflicting extents surface as a conflict, not a silent last-write-wins. | "You and [name] both declared this. You said 4 bars; they said 8." |
| **Two contributors declare simultaneously** (race) | Both persist. Declaration is additive, per-contribution, and never overwrites another contributor's assertion. There is no last-write-wins on a shared work-level field, because there is no shared work-level field (D-06). | Both appear |
| **A declaration is edited while a clearance request is in flight** | The in-flight request **pins the declaration version it was composed against** — the same rule as 11.03.01 D-02 and `#CX-01` synthesis Q5. An extent that grows between request and agreement is a new request, not an amendment. | "You changed what you declared. The request out with [owner] still says 4 bars. [Withdraw and resend] / [Leave it]" |
| **The work is edited after a licence issued on the basis of its declaration** | The licence stands — licences are irrevocable (domain D-06). The new edit is a new set of facts. If the edit *increased* the borrowing, the issued licence no longer covers the work, and that is a dispute (domain 24), not an automatic re-clear. | "You licensed 4 bars. This version uses 12. That licence doesn't cover this." |
| **Source work's ownership changes between declaration and clearance** | The declaration identifies a **work**, not an owner set — owners are resolved at clearance time, not frozen at declaration time. A catalogue sale between the two does not invalidate the declaration. | Nothing; this is the design working |
| **Declaration under-states the extent** | The declarer supplies facts that determine their own price (DT-04) — the incentive is to minimise. Mitigations: extent is machine-measured wherever the fingerprint identified the source (D-07); the human's claim is attributable; and the source's owners see the extent claim **when clearance is requested** and can dispute it there (11.05.03). Where the machine is blind (interpolation), there is no mitigation and the platform must not pretend otherwise. | Source owner at clearance: "They say 4 bars. [Dispute the extent]" |
| **Declaration removed after a rev-share clearance, to escape the ongoing share** | Not possible. Declarations are superseded, never deleted (P-02); and the rev-share is registered in domain 09's ownership record, which does not read this feature's current state. Removing the declaration would remove nothing. | The superseded declaration and the registered share both remain |
| **False declaration naming a famous work** — to manufacture an association, for attention or SEO | Self-encumbering (it makes *their* work not one-stop), which is most of the deterrent. Critically, **it notifies nobody** (D-09) and is **not public** until a licence issues, so it is not a spam or association vector while it sits as a declaration. It becomes one only when a clearance is requested — where 11.05.03's routing and domain 24 apply. | Nothing visible to the named party |
| **Malicious declaration used to spam a rights-holder** | Blocked structurally by D-09 — declaration does not notify. The abuse surface is the clearance *request*, which is 11.05.02/11.05.03's rate-limiting and abuse problem, not this feature's. | — |
| **Declaring nothing at all — silence** | The work is not one-stop (11.02.03 D-02: silence is not cleanliness). No accusation, no gate, no punishment; just an absent fact and its honest consequence. | "No declaration — this can't be one-stop. [Declare]" |
| **Declaring "no samples" when there are some** | An **attributable false statement** — materially worse for the declarer than silence, which is precisely why D-02 forbids a hard release gate: a gate would manufacture exactly this (DT-01). The platform records what was asked, what was answered, and by whom, and takes no view on truth. | Their name, on their answer, permanently |
| **Sample of a sample (chains)** | Transitive. The chain is walked and each link named — 11.02.03 owns the walk. This feature records one link honestly and does not attempt the traversal. | "Encumbered via [B] via [C]" |
| **Cycle: A samples B, B samples A** (real, via remixes) | The declaration is **accepted** — it is true (D-12). Cycle handling is the downstream walker's problem (11.02.03). The declaration layer never refuses a true fact for a computation's convenience. | Nothing unusual at declaration time |
| **Network drops mid-declaration** | Per-field, per-contribution capture; partial declarations persist as `unidentified` / incomplete rather than being lost. A half-entered declaration is worth more than a discarded one, and the loss of a session-close declaration is unrecoverable — the moment does not come back. | "Saved. You still need to say where [source] came from." |
| **The whole prompt is dismissed** ("not now") | Recorded as **asked and deferred** — a third state, distinct from never-asked and answered. Re-raised at the next capture moment (pre-release). | "We'll ask again before you release." |
| **The source work is deleted / its owner erases their account** | The declaration survives — it is a licence-relevant historical fact about *the declarer's* work, not a record about the deleted party's personal data. What identity remains attached is a `/create-prd-security` question (Q-06). | The borrowing remains declared and identified; the counterparty may be unreachable |
| **A band (entity, domain 01) is the source** | Resolves to the entity, whose members may hold individual shares. Clearance routes per domain 01's entity rules. A band is an entity, not a persona (personas.md). | "[Band] — 4 members, shares per [work]" |
| **The multi-hyphenate declares in two roles at once** — the producer who also played the interpolated melody | One human, two contributions, two declarations. The feature scopes by **contribution**, not by human (D-06), so the collision does not arise. Their producer contribution declares the loop; their performer contribution declares the melody they played. | Two entries, both theirs |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Fingerprint analysis running against the mix and stems | Background only. The declaration form is fully usable with zero results (D-11). Candidates appear as they arrive, labelled as machine suggestions. Never a blocking spinner — a producer must be able to declare and leave. |
| **Empty** | No declaration made, no question answered | **"Nothing declared" — not "clean"** (11.02.03 D-02). The distinction is the entire feature. The empty state's CTA is the enumerated prompt (D-05), not a generic "Add sample". |
| **Empty — negative** | Asked, answered "nothing borrowed" | **A different state from the above**, and only this one can support one-stop via 11.02.02. Shows the attributed assertion: "[Name] declared nothing borrowed, [date]." |
| **Populated** | One or more sources declared | Per source: element type, source recording, source composition, extent (seconds + bars, all occurrences), prominence facts, treatment, fragment provenance, identification route, per-field assertion source (human/machine), clearance status, and the offered route. Plus the plain statement of what is *not* known. |
| **Error** | Fingerprint failed or unreachable | Manual declaration fully available. Displayed as **absence of a result**, never as absence of samples: "We couldn't listen this time" — never "No samples found" (D-11). |
| **Partial — unidentified** | Declared, source not identified ("a record from 2009") | "Unidentified source, 4 bars at 0:42, chopped, from vinyl — encumbered." A **legitimate, useful, permanent-if-necessary terminal state** (D-03, D-04), not a validation failure and not an expiring one. |
| **Partial — pending contributor** | Some contributions declared, others not answered | "Stem 3 came from [name]. They haven't answered yet." The work-level state is the union of contributions and is honestly incomplete (D-06). |
| **Partial — deferred** | Prompt dismissed | "We'll ask again before you release." Distinct from never-asked; recorded. |
| **Conflict** | Two declarers disagree on extent, or a human declaration contradicts a high-confidence fingerprint | Both assertions shown with their authors. Nothing is silently resolved (D-07, DT-07). "You said 4 bars; [name] said 8." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Declaration is a compliance chore and will be skipped, so it must be enforced with a hard gate before release | Enforcement instinct; and it is true that it will be skipped | ❌ REJECTED — a hard gate produces false declarations, not true ones. People blocked from releasing will click "no samples" and move on, and the platform will have manufactured a *documented lie* that is worse than silence: an attributable false statement. The lever is **value**, not enforcement — declaring must visibly buy you something (instant clearance, one-stop status, a sale) so that the lazy path is the correct path, per personas.md's Producer principle. Where declaration is genuinely absent, the honest state is "not one-stop", which costs the owner money without accusing them of anything. |
| DT-02 | The platform can identify most samples automatically — Content ID does it at YouTube scale | The obvious technical read | ❌ REJECTED — fingerprinting recognises **recordings**, and the clearance problem is about **works**. It is blind to: interpolations (no shared audio at all, `#CX-03`), heavily processed samples (pitched, chopped, reversed — the normal case in the genres that sample most), samples of unreleased or non-commercial sources, and library/vinyl material outside any reference database. Those blind spots are not the tail — **they are where the lawsuits are**, because the recognisable ones get cleared or caught early. The machine handles the easy half. |
| DT-03 | On-platform sources make this feature's value **compound with platform age**, and that is a cold-start problem nobody has stated | Reasoning from 11.01.05 DT-03, applied to samples: the same cold-start caveat surfaces from a second direction | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is worse here than in sync, because sampling skews heavily toward **catalogue** rather than new work. A producer samples a 1974 soul record, not last week's WeJammin session. | D-01 | **Fingerprint suggests; human declares** | Per 11.02.03 D-01 — a probabilistic match must not propagate encumbrance through a derivative tree unattended. Refined, not contradicted, by D-07. |

| D-05 | **The prompt is enumerative and versioned, and a negative answer is a recorded declaration** | Per DT-06 — an abstract "did you sample anything?" silently imports a de-minimis the platform refuses to assert.
