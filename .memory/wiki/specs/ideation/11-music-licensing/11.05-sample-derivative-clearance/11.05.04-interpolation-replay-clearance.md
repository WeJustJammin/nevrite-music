# Feature: Interpolation & Replay Clearance

> **Number**: 11.05.04
> **Parent**: [11.05 Sample & Derivative Clearance](./11.05-sample-derivative-clearance-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Clearing a melody you played rather than a recording you used — an interpolation (re-singing or replaying part
of someone's song) or a replay (re-recording a sample to avoid the master owner entirely). It is a different
legal object from sampling: **the composition only, no master**, which means a different owner set, a
different price mechanism, and no fingerprint on earth that can detect it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The interpolating writer, and usually **the party who does not know they needed a licence at all** — "I just played something similar" is not a defence and nobody told them. As the interpolated party: receives a share request against their composition. |
| Producer | Full | Runs the replay session that creates the new master; knows exactly what was asked for, which makes them the honest party in the room and the natural declarer. Also the one who chases the wrong owner (the master's) if the flow does not correct them. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

An interpolation touches **the composition and nothing else**. The source's master owner has no standing —
a fact that surprises everyone and must be surfaced, because a producer will otherwise spend three weeks
chasing a label that has no say (`#CX-03` synthesis Q3).

That asymmetry is also *why* replays exist as a technique: a producer who cannot clear a master re-records the
part and only needs the publishing side. The platform should name that as an available strategy, not merely
process it — "you can't clear this master; you could replay it and clear the composition" is advice worth
more than most features.

Clearance is almost always **revenue-share, not fee** (`#CX-05`), because interpolation value depends on how
much was used and how recognisable it is — a judgement, not a lookup. Which is exactly why it does not clear
programmatically (`#R-01` in the sub-domain CX).

### Happy Path

1. Producer or Musician declares an interpolation at session close (11.05.01) — **the only route; no machine will find it**
2. System resolves the **composition's** owners only, and says so plainly: "the master owner has no say here"
3. Request routes simultaneously to the writers
4. Terms negotiated (11.05.03) — typically a writer share on the new work
5. Agreement → share registers in domain 09 → the new work is clearable downstream
6. New master is wholly the interpolator's; only the publishing is shared

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Nobody declares — the writer did not know they needed to | **The dominant case.** No fingerprint catches it; the work looks clean and is not. | The prompt must ask "did you play something from another song?" in plain words, not use the word "interpolation" |
| Producer chases the master owner | Wasted weeks. The flow must correct this at step 2. | "The recording's owner has no say in an interpolation. You need the writers: [names]" |
| Disagreement about whether it *is* an interpolation | Genuinely contested — "is this the same melody?" is litigated for a living. The platform cannot adjudicate. | Both positions recorded; escalates to domain 24 |
| Replay used specifically to dodge an unclearable master | **Legitimate and standard practice.** Not evasion. | The platform should surface it as a strategy, not treat it as suspicious |
| Interpolation of an interpolation (chains) | Transitive obligations stack, per 11.05.03 DT-03 | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — chains and cycles |
| Source composition is public domain | No clearance needed for the composition; **the specific arrangement may still be protected** | "The song is PD. The arrangement you copied may not be." — a distinction nobody makes and everyone gets wrong |
| Writers want a fee, not a share | Unusual but permitted; the terms machinery is 11.05.03's | — |
| Interpolation is 3 notes | Is that infringement? Nobody knows. Jurisdictions disagree. | Parent Q-01 — the platform should not assert a de-minimis it cannot defend |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Composition owner resolution | Brief |
| Empty | Nothing declared | The silent-failure state; **the machine cannot help here** |
| Populated | Declared, request in flight | Composition owners only, with the master's absence stated explicitly |
| Error | Resolution failed | Falls through; never auto-clears |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Interpolation is a sub-case of sampling and should fold into 11.05.02/03 | Modelling economy; the sweep listed it as a separate candidate but the reason is not obvious | ❌ REJECTED — it is a different legal object. Sampling needs **two** clearances (master + composition); interpolation needs **one** (composition). Folding it in would inherit a master-side step that does not exist, route requests to a party with no standing, and — worse — miss that replays exist *precisely because* the master side can be dropped. The distinction is not academic: it is a strategy the platform should be recommending. |
| DT-02 | Fingerprinting is weak here | Understating it, which is the trap | ❌ REJECTED — it is **structurally blind**, not weak. An interpolation shares zero audio with its source; there is no signal to match. This is qualitatively different from a chopped or pitched sample, where the audio is present and hard to recognise. The consequence is severe: **one human's honesty is the entire mechanism**, with no machine backstop of any kind. Every design decision here must optimise for eliciting a voluntary declaration from someone who does not know they owe one — which means asking in plain language ("did you play something from another song?"), never punishing the answer, and never using the word "interpolation" in the prompt. |
| DT-03 | The platform should recommend replay as a strategy when a master is unclearable | Reasoning from what the registry uniquely knows: it can see that a work's master is off-platform/blocked while its composition is reachable — a fact no incumbent tool holds in one place | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a small feature with a disproportionate payoff. "You can't clear this master. You *could* replay it and clear the composition — here are the writers, here are their terms" is professional advice that currently costs a music lawyer's hourly rate, and the platform can derive it from data it already holds. It is also a clean demonstration of the consolidation thesis (D-18): the advice only exists because master and composition sit in one registry. **Nothing in the sweep's candidate list is close to this.** |

| # | Decision | Context |

| D-01 | Interpolation resolves the composition's owners only, and says so | Per DT-01 — the master owner has no standing, and letting a producer discover that themselves costs them weeks |
