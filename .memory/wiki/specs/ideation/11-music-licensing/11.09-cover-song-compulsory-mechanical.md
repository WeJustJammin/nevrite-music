# Feature: Cover Songs & Compulsory Mechanical Licensing

> **Number**: 11.09
> **Parent**: [Music Licensing](./music-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Licensing a recording of somebody else's song. Unlike everything else in this domain, **the owner cannot say
no** — most territories provide a compulsory mechanical licence: identify the composition, file the notice,
pay the statutory rate, release. It is the one licence in music that is an administrative errand rather than a
negotiation, and it is a genuine on-ramp because the person doing it is usually a working musician putting a
cover on streaming, not a rights professional.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Both sides, and the persona this feature is for.** As licensee: recording a cover, needs it legal on Spotify. As licensor: their song was covered and they get paid without being asked — and without being able to refuse. Sees covers of their work appear as income, not as requests. |
| Producer | Full | Records the cover; per personas.md they deliver, so an unlicensed cover is their delivery problem. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The flow is fixed by statute, not by the platform: **identify the composition → confirm the territory's regime
→ file the notice → pay the statutory rate → release.** No consent round, no policy check, no veto — the
compulsory licence overrides all of it, and that makes this feature structurally unlike every other node in
domain 11.

**Why it is a feature and not a sub-domain**: one flow, one legal object, one path. The territorial variation
is a lookup table, not a second interacting capability. It routes into the existing instrument (11.08.02) and
the existing royalty machinery (domain 10) rather than owning either.

The important asymmetry: **11.04's veto does not apply here.** A songwriter who blocks tobacco ads cannot
block a cover, because the law does not let them. The platform must not present a control that does not exist.

### Happy Path

1. Musician declares: this recording is a cover of [composition]
2. System identifies the composition — on-platform is exact; off-platform requires a lookup against external registries
3. System determines the territory's regime and rate
4. Notice files with the relevant body (MLC in the US, direct to the publisher elsewhere, per territory)
5. Statutory rate accrues per unit/stream; payment flows to the composition's owners
6. Cover releases (domain 12) as a legitimate recording

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Composition owner objects | **They cannot.** Compulsory means compulsory. | Owner: "Covers don't need your permission. Here's what it pays." — must be stated plainly or the platform looks broken |
| The cover materially changes the arrangement | **May exceed the compulsory licence** — a substantially altered arrangement is a derivative work, and then 11.05.04 (interpolation) applies and consent *is* required | "This might not be covered by a compulsory licence" — the boundary is genuinely fuzzy and the platform should say so |
| Composition not on WeJammin | External lookup; identification is the hard part | The common case for years |
| Territory has no compulsory regime | The whole model fails there; consent is required | Per-territory divergence is the feature's real complexity |
| Cover of a cover | Both are covers of the same underlying composition; no chain | Simpler than sampling, unusually |
| Composition is public domain | No licence needed — **but the specific arrangement may be protected** | Same trap as 11.05.04 Q-02 |
| Cover is a parody | Different legal analysis entirely (fair use / fair dealing), and out of scope | `[PENDING]` — not something the platform should adjudicate |
| Musician releases first, files never | Common, and it is infringement | The value here is making filing cheaper than not filing |

Compare 11.03 where MFN reaches backwards and rewrites executed quotes — that is interaction. Territorial variation is a lookup table, not a capability. And the accounting half is domain 10's, not this feature's. One flow, one file. |
| DT-02 | The compulsory licence makes this the easiest node in the domain — no consent, no veto, no negotiation | It looks that way, and the legal simplicity is real | ❌ REJECTED — **the hard part moved, it did not disappear.** With consent removed, the entire difficulty is *identification*: which composition is this, and who owns it right now? That is exactly the archaeology the whole domain claims to have solved — and it is unsolved here, because a musician covering a 1970s song is looking for a composition the registry does not hold. The identification burden is the same one 11.05.01 DT-03 found for samples: **the platform's advantage evaporates the moment the target work is off-platform**, which for covers is nearly always. Third independent confirmation of the cold-start pattern (after 11.01.05 DT-03 and 11.05.01 DT-03). |
| DT-03 | This feature is a strong on-ramp for the platform's core persona | Reasoning from who actually files these: not rights professionals, but working musicians putting a cover on streaming — exactly personas.md's Musician | ✅ CONFIRMED, with a caveat that cuts. It is a **frequent, low-stakes, unambiguous need** with an existing willingness to pay (Easy Song, Songfile) and it introduces a musician to the platform's rights machinery through an errand they already have. It also has an unusual property for this domain: **the compulsory regime means it works on day one with an empty registry**, because the licence does not depend on the counterparty being present. That is the same cold-start resistance 11.06.01 DT-03 found for creator licensing — and it means the two cold-start-resistant nodes in this domain are the two the domain rationale treats as peripheral. **The caveat**: it is a commodity errand with existing cheap competitors, so it wins a user without proving anything the thesis claims. An on-ramp, not a wedge. Feeds problem-statement Q-03. |
