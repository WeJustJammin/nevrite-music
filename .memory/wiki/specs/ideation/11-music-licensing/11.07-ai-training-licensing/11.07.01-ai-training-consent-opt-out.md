# Feature: AI Training Consent & Opt-Out Registry

> **Number**: 11.07.01
> **Parent**: [11.07 AI Training Licensing](./11.07-ai-training-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Records whether a rights holder permits their work to be used for training generative music models — per
share, opt-in, unanimous, and with an honest account of what withdrawal can and cannot undo. It is the one
place in the domain where the product's value may be mostly in the **record itself**: an attributable,
timestamped, machine-readable statement of refusal that currently exists nowhere.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets AI training consent per share; sees which corpora and which shipped models contain their work, and that the list is append-only. **The persona with the strongest feelings and the least leverage** — the market's default is that their work is already in the weights and nobody asked. |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side — **and the sharper stake**: per personas.md they hold the stems, and isolated multi-track stems are far more valuable training data than mixed recordings. A stem corpus is the most commercially attractive thing this platform could assemble, which is precisely why the consent around it must be unimpeachable. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Consent attaches to a **share**, not a work (index D-02), and folds under the same unanimity rule as every
other refusal in the domain (11.04.02 D-01): one co-writer refusing removes the work. AI training is not a
special rights regime — it is a category block with a louder name (`#R-02`).

It is **opt-in, never opt-out** (index D-03). An opt-out registry makes the default exploitation and puts the
burden of discovery on the person being exploited. For a platform whose thesis is that facts about work should
be captured at source rather than reconstructed later by the more powerful party, an opt-out default would be
a self-contradiction with nothing to gain.

The hard part is telling the truth about withdrawal. **Withdrawing consent cannot un-train a model**
(`#CX-03`). The confirmation must lead with what withdrawal does *not* do, or the platform has sold a control
that is theatre — and the person who discovers that will be right to conclude the whole thing was.

### Happy Path

1. Rights holder is asked once, plainly, at a moment that is not a checkout: may your work train AI models?
2. Answer recorded against each of their shares, attributably and timestamped
3. Default is **no** — silence is refusal, not permission
4. A yes makes the work eligible for **future** corpora only; owners are notified before any deal ships
5. Rights holder can see which corpora and shipped models contain their work — an append-only register
6. Withdrawal excludes from future corpora, and says plainly that shipped models are unaffected

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Owner withdraws after a model shipped | Prospective only. **The confirmation leads with the limitation.** | "This stops future use. It can't remove your work from the 2 models already trained on it." — said first, not in a footnote |
| Three co-writers consent, one refuses | Work is excluded. Unanimity, per 11.04.02 D-01. | "[Name] declined — this work isn't eligible" |
| Owner never answers | **Refused.** Silence is not permission. | Consistent with 11.04.03 Q-01's domain-wide silence question — and here the answer is not in doubt |
| Withdrawal races corpus assembly | **Fail toward exclusion.** If consent cannot be proven live at inclusion, exclude. | Asymmetry: wrong exclusion costs a share of a deal; wrong inclusion is permanent |
| Owner consents without understanding the value | The consent is uninformed. `#CX-04` — money is the feedback that informs it, and it arrives after the decision. | See Q-02 |
| Share is sold after consent was given | Does consent travel with the share? The new owner may object to a corpus their predecessor agreed to. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| A work was scraped before it reached WeJammin | Extremely likely for anything released. The registry records a stance about the future; it cannot undo the past. | Honest: "This records what happens from now on" |
| Consent recorded but the platform itself trains on user audio | Index Q-01 — a credibility problem no feature can fix | Routed to `/create-prd-security` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fold across co-owners | Brief |
| Empty | Never asked | Refused by default. **Not a gap** — the safe state. |
| Populated | Consent recorded | Per-share stance, plus the append-only register of corpora and shipped models |
| Error | Fold failed | **Treat as refused.** Fail closed, consistent with 11.04.03 D-05 and the whole domain. |
| Partial | Some co-owners answered | Not eligible. Unanimity means partial is refusal. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This needs a bespoke consent system — AI is a special rights category | The instinct that follows from how loud the topic is, and how the sweep listed it as its own candidate | ❌ REJECTED — mechanically it is **11.04.02 with a different label**: a category block, per-share, unanimous, folded the same way. Building a parallel consent engine would give the platform two systems that must agree about refusal and will eventually not. What is genuinely distinctive is *downstream* — corpus assembly, dataset deals, and the irreversibility (`#CX-03`) — which is why the sub-domain exists at all. The consent object itself is not special, and pretending it is would be architecture chasing a headline. |
| DT-02 | The main value here is the money — a new revenue stream from AI corpus deals | The commercial framing, and the reason the sweep listed it | ❌ REJECTED — **the main value is the record.** An attributable, timestamped, machine-readable statement that a named person refused AI training for a specific work currently **exists nowhere**, and the entire industry's AI litigation turns on exactly that question: was there consent? The revenue is speculative (index Q-03: the buyers barely exist). The record is valuable on day one, to the rights holder, whether or not a single deal ever closes. This inverts the feature's justification — it is worth building as **infrastructure for refusal** even if 11.07.02 and 11.07.03 never ship, and that is a much stronger case than the revenue one. |
| DT-03 | Opt-in consent for AI is the safe, uncontroversial default | It reads as obviously correct, which is why it is worth attacking | ✅ CONFIRMED, with a cost the owner should see. Opt-in means the corpus starts empty and stays small, because most people will not opt in and the ones who would are the ones with least to lose. **The sub-domain's commercial half (11.07.02, 11.07.03) is therefore probably stillborn** — which is the honest reading, not a reason to soften the default. It also means this feature's real MoSCoW case rests entirely on DT-02: the record, not the revenue. The temptation to make it opt-out in order to have a business is exactly the pressure that has to be named and refused now, in writing, rather than in a growth meeting in two years. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Opt-in, never opt-out; silence is refusal | Per index D-03 and DT-03 — an opt-out default contradicts the platform's founding argument for no gain |
| D-02 | Consent uses 11.04.02's mechanism, not a parallel one | Per DT-01 — two refusal systems will eventually disagree |
| D-03 | Withdrawal confirmation leads with what it cannot undo | Per `#CX-03` — anything else sells a control that is theatre |
| D-04 | Failures and races fail toward exclusion | A wrongly excluded work costs a share of a deal; a wrongly included one is in the weights forever |
| D-05 | The register of corpora and shipped models is append-only and visible to the owner | It is the honest record of an irreversible fact |
