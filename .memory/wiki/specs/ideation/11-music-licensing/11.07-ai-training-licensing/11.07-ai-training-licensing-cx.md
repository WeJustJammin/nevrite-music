## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-03: Consent ↔ Corpus (the irreversibility)

**Relationship**: Every other permission in domain 11 is revocable in the direction that matters — a veto
(11.04.02) stops future licences, a policy change closes a scope, an opt-out empties a catalogue. Training is
different in kind: **once a model has trained on a work, withdrawing consent changes nothing about the model.**
The weights are not a database with a delete. So consent state ("I now refuse") and corpus state ("your work
is in three shipped models") diverge permanently, and the platform must represent both truthfully rather than
letting the UI imply a withdrawal did something it did not.

This is the sharpest instance of a principle the whole domain runs on: the platform must not represent as
reversible a thing that is not.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the party who will withdraw consent — usually after seeing an output that upsets them — and discover that withdrawal is prospective only. The moment this is explained badly is the moment they conclude the control was theatre.
- **Producer**: same, and with a sharper stake: **their stems are the most valuable training data on the platform** — isolated, labelled, multi-track. A stem corpus is worth far more than a mixed-track corpus, and per personas.md they are the only party who holds stems.
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Consent is owned by the share; corpus membership is owned by the corpus manifest, which is **immutable once shipped**. They are two records that agree until they permanently do not. Neither can be the other's flag.
2. **Trigger chain**: Withdraw → excluded from *future* corpora. It **cannot** cascade to shipped models. If the UI implies otherwise the platform has lied about the one thing it is selling here. The withdrawal confirmation must say what it does not do, first.
3. **Permission intersection**: Unanimity, per 11.04.02 D-01 — one co-writer's refusal removes the work. Which means a work in a shipped corpus can have a co-writer who has since refused, and the work stays in that corpus forever. Correct, and it will read as a betrayal to the refuser unless it was explained at consent time.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A rights holder should learn which corpora and which shipped models contain their work, and that the list is append-only. This is the honest register.
5. **State transition conflict**: Withdrawal racing corpus assembly. **Fail toward exclusion** — if the platform cannot prove consent was live at the moment of inclusion, exclude. The asymmetry: a wrongly excluded work costs a small share of a deal; a wrongly included work is in the weights forever.

### CX-01: Consent ↔ Corpus (the gate)

**Relationship**: The corpus is defined by consent — it is the set of works where every stakeholder said yes.
That makes corpus size a direct function of how many people the platform persuaded, which is the sub-domain's
real constraint: not the technology, not the deals, but whether musicians want this at all.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: Full on consent, Read-only on corpus — the asymmetry noted in the parent Role Matrix
- **Operator / Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The corpus reads consent at assembly time and pins it. A manifest records consent *as it stood*, with evidence — the same superseding pattern as 11.02.02 D-01.
2. **Trigger chain**: Consent granted → eligible for future corpora. Never retroactive into a shipped one, even favourably: a work cannot be added to a corpus that has already trained.
3. **Permission intersection**: Governed by 11.04's unanimity. AI is a category block with a louder name, not a special rights regime (index D-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: Inclusion in a new corpus notifies the owners — before the deal ships, not after.
5. **State transition conflict**: See CX-03 Q5 — fail toward exclusion.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 11.07.03 Compensation | 11.07.02 Corpus (per-output attribution) | Considered: should compensation track *which works influenced which generated output*, paying per generation rather than per corpus inclusion? Rejected — **it is not currently computable.** Attributing a generated track to specific training works is an open research problem, not an engineering task, and building a compensation model on it would be building on a claim the platform cannot substantiate. Compensation must divide the **corpus deal** (a real number, divided by a real manifest), not a per-output influence score that nobody can measure. If the research lands, this becomes a live edge; today asserting it would be fabrication. |
| R-02 | 11.07.01 Consent | 11.04.02 Veto (cross-sub-domain) | Not an edge because it is an **identity**: "never AI training" *is* a category block in 11.04.02, using the same mechanism, with the same unanimity rule. Recording a cross-cut here would imply two systems that must be kept in sync. There is one system. This sub-domain contributes the category and the surrounding product (corpus, compensation), not a parallel consent engine. |
