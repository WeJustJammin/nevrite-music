# Feature: Corpus Assembly & Dataset Licensing Deals

> **Number**: 11.07.02
> **Parent**: [11.07 AI Training Licensing](./11.07-ai-training-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Assembles the works whose owners all said yes into a licensable corpus, with a manifest recording exactly what
was included and on whose consent, and sells access to it as a dataset deal. It is the commercial half of the
sub-domain, it depends on a buyer market that barely exists, and its manifest is the artifact that makes
compensation and any future audit possible.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees which corpora contain their works and which deals included them. **Cannot negotiate individually, cannot refuse a specific buyer once consented, cannot price.** The asymmetry is the honest position, not an oversight — see the parent Role Matrix note. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same. Their **stems** are the most valuable material in any corpus the platform could assemble, which makes their Read-only status the most consequential instance of this asymmetry. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

A corpus is **the set of works where every stakeholder consented** (`#CX-01`), pinned at assembly time with a
manifest that records consent as it stood, with evidence — the same superseding pattern as 11.02.02 D-01. The
manifest is immutable once the corpus ships, because the thing it describes (a trained model) is immutable.

A dataset deal licenses access to a corpus to a named AI company for a named purpose. What it cannot do is
promise revocability: **once trained, always trained** (`#CX-03`). The deal's terms must therefore be honest
about what the buyer is buying — permanent use of a fixed snapshot — rather than pretending to a licence that
can be withdrawn.

Corpus size is a direct function of persuasion, not technology. Per 11.07.01 DT-03, opt-in consent means the
corpus starts empty and stays small.

### Happy Path

1. Platform defines a corpus scope (genre, era, instrumentation, stems vs mixes)
2. Eligible works are those where **every** stakeholder consented (11.07.01)
3. Manifest is generated: which works, which shares, whose consent, at what timestamp, with evidence
4. Owners are notified their work is in a corpus — **before** the deal ships, not after
5. Deal is negotiated with a named AI company for a named purpose; the corpus snapshot pins
6. Deal executes; the corpus becomes immutable; compensation divides by the manifest (11.07.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Owner withdraws consent after the corpus ships | Excluded from future corpora only. The shipped one is unchanged, forever. | "Your work stays in [corpus] — we can't un-train it" |
| Owner withdraws during assembly, before ship | Excluded. **Fail toward exclusion** if timing is uncertain. | Per `#CX-03` synthesis Q5 |
| A work in the corpus is later found to be encumbered (11.02.03) | **The corpus contains a work its owners had no right to license.** Nothing can remove it from the model. | The most serious failure available here — see Q-02 |
| Splits change after the corpus shipped | The manifest pinned the shares at assembly; compensation follows the manifest, not the current split | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — arguably it should follow the current split, like 11.02.01's rule for licences |
| No AI company wants to buy | **The most likely outcome.** Index Q-03 — the buyer market barely exists. | The corpus is a well-documented asset with no customer |
| Buyer trains beyond the licensed purpose | Undetectable in practice. Enforcement against a model's weights is nearly impossible. | Honest limitation; routed to 24 |
| Corpus assembled from stems | The most valuable corpus, and the one whose consent must be most unimpeachable | Producers hold these; see Role Lens |
| A consenting co-owner's identity was fraudulent | The consent record is only as strong as identity (domain 01) | Upstream dependency |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Eligibility fold across the catalogue | Batch, not interactive |
| Empty | No consenting works | **The expected state for a long time**, per 11.07.01 DT-03 |
| Populated | Corpus assembled | Manifest: size, composition, consent evidence |
| Error | Fold failed | No corpus. Fail closed — an unverified corpus is unlicensable |
| Partial | Assembly in progress | Not shippable; a partial manifest licenses nothing |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The platform should be the corpus's counterparty — aggregate consent, negotiate once, distribute the money | The natural read, and how the sweep's candidate implies it works | ✅ CONFIRMED as the only workable shape, ❌ REJECTED as unexamined — because it makes WeJammin a **collecting society for AI**, negotiating on behalf of members who cannot see the deal, cannot price it, and cannot refuse a specific buyer once they have said yes. That is a genuinely different relationship from every other sub-domain in domain 11, where the owner negotiates their own deal. It may be necessary (no individual musician can negotiate with an AI lab) and it is a large, quiet transfer of agency that should be a **conscious owner decision**, not a consequence of the mechanism. The parent Role Matrix records the asymmetry deliberately so it cannot be adopted by accident. |
| DT-02 | The manifest is bookkeeping — an implementation detail of compensation | Scope instinct; it looks like a join table | ❌ REJECTED — the manifest is **the only durable artifact this sub-domain produces**. The model is the buyer's; the money is spent; the consent may later be withdrawn. What survives is a signed, timestamped record of exactly what was included and on whose authority. That is the evidence in any future dispute about whether a given work was licensed for training — which is precisely the question the entire industry is currently litigating and cannot answer. **The manifest is the product; the deal is the occasion.** This is the same inversion as 11.07.01 DT-02 (the record, not the revenue) arriving from a second direction, which makes it a sub-domain-level pattern. |
| DT-03 | An encumbered work in a shipped corpus is an edge case | The comfortable read | ❌ REJECTED — it is the sub-domain's **worst possible failure and it is not rare**. Per 11.02.03, encumbrance is discovered late and propagates downstream; per 11.05.01 DT-02, fingerprinting is blind to interpolations. So a work with an undeclared sample can pass every consent check, enter a corpus, train a model, and be discovered afterwards — at which point the platform has licensed material its users had no right to license, into a system from which it **cannot be removed at any price**. Every other domain-11 failure is recoverable by refund, dispute, or a new licence. This one is not. **Corpus assembly must gate on full clearance (11.02.01), not merely on consent** — a requirement neither the sweep nor this feature's obvious design contains. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The manifest is the product; the deal is the occasion | Per DT-02 — it is the only durable artifact, and it answers the question the industry is litigating |
| D-02 | **Corpus assembly gates on full clearance, not merely consent** | Per DT-03 — an encumbered work in a shipped model is the one unrecoverable failure in the domain |
| D-03 | A shipped corpus is immutable, and its manifest with it | The thing it describes is immutable; pretending otherwise misrepresents the deal to both sides |
| D-04 | Owners are notified before a deal ships, not after | The last moment their consent still means anything |
