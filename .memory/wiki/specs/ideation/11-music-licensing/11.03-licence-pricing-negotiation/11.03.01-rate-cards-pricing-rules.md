# Feature: Rate Cards & Pricing Rules

> **Number**: 11.03.01
> **Parent**: [11.03 Licence Pricing & Negotiation](./11.03-licence-pricing-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-30

## Overview

A standing answer to "how much?" set once by the rights holder and applied automatically to every request it
covers — priced by usage type, media, territory, term and scale. It is the difference between a catalogue
that can be priced at machine speed and one where every enquiry waits on a human, and it is half of the
domain's claim that clearance collapses to "a lookup plus a pre-set price".

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets standing prices for their publishing side across usage bands (podcast, indie film, national ad, game). Sees what their card earned, where it fell through to a human, and where requests died on price. Copies a card across their catalogue rather than pricing 200 works one by one. |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side. Often pricing on behalf of works with several co-owners — see Q-01, which is the feature's hardest question. |
| Operator | None | No music rights to price in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Creator micro-licensing (11.06.01) buys at a flat price and never sees a card. |

## Behavior

A card maps **scope → price**. Scope uses the licence grammar from 11.08.01, whose axes are media, data use,
territory, term, exclusivity, usage category, scale, **extent of use** and grantee scope (11.08.01 § The axes).
Two of those are card keys the breadth pass's six-axis list omitted and pricing cannot do without: **extent of
use** — excerpt band, number of uses, full track vs stem vs instrumental — because a 5-second sting and a
3-minute needle-drop are not the same deal (11.08.01 D-17, DT-16); and **data use**, which no media grant
implies at any price (11.08.01 D-06). Price may be a fixed figure, a band, or a rule (per-territory
multipliers, scale tiers by audience size). Coverage is deliberately partial: a card that tries to price every possible
scope is a card nobody will finish filling in. Uncovered scopes fall through to 11.03.02, and the fall-through
queue is visible to the owner as demand they are not serving.

The card is a **template**; a quote is an **instance** that pins the card version at read time. Editing a card
never mutates a quote already issued (`#CX-01` synthesis Q1).

### Happy Path

1. Owner opens their rate card — pre-populated with platform-suggested bands from comparable executed deals (see Q-02; this is not obviously the platform's business)
2. Owner sets prices for the usage bands they care about; leaves the rest uncovered
3. Owner applies the card across a catalogue selection rather than per work
4. A request arrives; 11.04 policy is checked **first** (refusal outranks pricing), then the card is matched
5. Covered → an auto-quote issues at the card price with no human involved
6. Uncovered → falls through to 11.03.02 and appears in the owner's queue
7. Owner sees a digest: what the card earned, what fell through, where requests died on price

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Card covers the scope but 11.04 policy blocks the category | Policy wins; no quote issues. The card never prices a forbidden use. | Buyer: "Not available for this use." Never a price for something the owner refuses. |
| Card edited between quote issue and buyer acceptance | Quote holds at the pinned version. A price that moves between offer and acceptance is not a bug, it is fraud. | Buyer sees the price they were quoted, honoured |
| Co-owners have different cards for the same work | **The core problem.** Each side prices its own share; the buyer sees a combined number. Cards that disagree in *kind* (one bands by territory, one by audience) may not combine at all. | See Q-01 — genuinely unresolved |
| Owner prices absurdly (£1M for a podcast) | Permitted. It is their work. The card simply never converts. | "No requests matched this band in 6 months" — information, not correction |
| Owner never sets a card | Everything falls through to human quoting. Correct default, and it is the slow path the feature exists to remove. | "You have no rate card — every request waits on you. [Set one]" |
| MFN asserted on the share (11.03.03) | The card silently becomes a **floor**, not a price | Must be stated at MFN assertion time — see `#CX-04`; an owner who does not understand this has mispriced their catalogue |
| Buyer scrapes cards to survey market pricing | Card prices are visible only in response to a scoped request, not enumerable | Routed to `/create-prd-security` as an NFR |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Card lookup during a request | Target: card match resolves in the same round-trip as scope validation, not a second blocking call — this sits in the buyer's search path where every added hop is felt. Concrete latency budget `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]`. |
| Empty | No card set | The empty state is the pitch: "Every request waits on you until you set this" |
| Populated | Card set | Coverage map — which usage bands are priced, which fall through, and what each earned |
| Error | Lookup failed | Falls through to human quoting. **Degrades to slow, never to wrong.** |
| Partial | Some bands priced | Coverage meter naming the *unpriced* bands with demand — "you're getting podcast requests and have no podcast price" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rate cards and licensing policy (11.04) are the same object — both are standing owner preferences applied without asking | Genuine structural temptation; both are "set once, apply automatically", and merging would shrink the domain | ❌ REJECTED — a card answers **how much**, a policy answers **whether at all**. They are not points on one scale: a £5,000 card is an invitation to negotiate; a tobacco block is a refusal at any price. Merging produces the absurd UX "set your price for tobacco ads: [never]", and worse, it makes refusal *expressible as a number* — which means a big enough offer looks like it should work. Keeping refusal categorical is a moral-rights property, not a pricing one. Confirmed the 11.03/11.04 boundary. |
| DT-02 | The platform should suggest prices from comparable executed deals — the musician it serves is systematically underinformed about what their work is worth | Directly from personas.md: the Musician "makes a living from a portfolio of small income streams" and has no market visibility. This is real, sympathetic, and valuable. | ⏳ DEFERRED to Q-02 — the feature is genuinely valuable and the posture is genuinely hazardous. A platform that operates a marketplace **and** tells all sellers what to charge is coordinating prices in a market it runs. The fact that it would help the underinformed party does not make it not price-setting. This is a legal/values call above the agent's pay grade; escalated with the trade-off stated rather than quietly built. |
| DT-03 | Partial card coverage is a defect to be driven toward 100% | Completeness instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a card demanding every scope be priced is a card nobody finishes, and an unfinished card prices nothing. Partial coverage with a **visible fall-through queue** is better than notional completeness: the owner prices what they get asked about, guided by real demand. The right metric is not coverage but *fall-through rate on actual requests*. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Cards and policies are separate objects | Per DT-01 — refusal must stay categorical, not expressible as a price |
| D-02 | A quote pins the card version at read | A price that moves between offer and acceptance is fraud, not a race condition |
| D-03 | Policy is evaluated before pricing | The system must never quote a use the owner has forbidden |
| D-04 | Partial coverage is the design target; fall-through rate is the metric | Per DT-03 — an unfinished card prices nothing |
