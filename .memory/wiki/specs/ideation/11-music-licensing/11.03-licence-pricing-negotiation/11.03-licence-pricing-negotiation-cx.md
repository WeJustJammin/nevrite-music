## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-03: MFN ↔ Quotes (the retroactive rewrite)

**Relationship**: This is the interaction that defines the sub-domain. Under an MFN clause, a party who
settles at £2,000 is entitled to be lifted to £3,000 if a comparable co-owner later settles higher — after
their quote was accepted, after the licence issued, possibly after they were paid. Price is therefore **not
final at signature**; it is final when every comparable deal in the same transaction closes. A pricing model
that treats an accepted quote as immutable cannot represent MFN at all, and MFN is standard in sync.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: usually the publishing-side MFN beneficiary; sees their price rise without acting, and must understand *why* money appeared or the platform looks arbitrary
- **Producer**: usually the master-side party, and frequently the one whose higher settlement *triggers* someone else's uplift — they should know their negotiation moves other people's money
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The agreed price is not owned by the quote — it is owned by an MFN-aware settlement over the deal set. The quote records what was *agreed*; the settlement records what is *owed*. Collapsing these two into one field is the modelling error that makes MFN unimplementable.
2. **Trigger chain**: Deal B executes → MFN evaluation for all comparable parties → uplift → **payment adjustment**, which reaches into domain 10 and the payments cross-cut. If the uplift computes but the payment fails, the party is owed money the system knows about and has not sent — that is a debt, and it must be represented as one, not retried silently.
3. **Permission intersection**: MFN requires visibility into *someone else's* settled price. That is a privacy boundary crossed by contract, not by platform choice — but the platform has to implement the crossing, and should surface it as a knowing trade at assertion time (Q-03).
4. **Notification fan-out**: An uplift notifies the beneficiary (money up), the buyer (cost up — the thing they hate most), and arguably the trigger party. Silence on any of these makes the number look arbitrary.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two comparable deals executing concurrently can each trigger the other's MFN. Naive evaluation oscillates or double-counts. **Evaluation must be over the settled set at a fixed point, not pairwise on each execution.** This is the concrete correctness requirement that justified splitting MFN into its own feature.

### CX-01: Rate Cards ↔ Quotes

**Relationship**: A rate card is a standing answer to a question nobody has asked yet. When a quote request
arrives for a scope the card covers, the price already exists — no human, no delay. Where the card does not
reach (an unusual scope, a scale outside its bands), the quote falls through to a human. The card's coverage
is therefore exactly the fraction of demand that clears at machine speed, which is the sub-domain's whole
contribution to the domain's latency claim.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets the card once; every covered request answers itself
- **Producer**: same, master side
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The card is a template, the quote is an instance. A card edited after a quote issued must not mutate that quote — the quote pins its card version. Otherwise a price can change between offer and acceptance, which is not a bug, it is fraud.
2. **Trigger chain**: Request → card lookup → auto-quote, or fall through to human. Fall-through must be visible to the owner as a *queue*, not a silent nothing — an unanswered quote is a lost sale the owner never knew about.
3. **Permission intersection**: A card cannot price a scope that 11.04 policy blocks. Policy is evaluated first: refusal outranks pricing. Otherwise the system quotes a tobacco ad the owner has forbidden.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Auto-quotes should be reported to the owner (digest, not per-event) — they are earning money in their sleep and should know at what price.
5. **State transition conflict**: Card edit and quote generation can race. The quote pins the card version at read; the edit applies to subsequent requests only.
