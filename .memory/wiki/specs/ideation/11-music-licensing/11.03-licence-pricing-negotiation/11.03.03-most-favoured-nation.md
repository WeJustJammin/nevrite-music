# Feature: Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) Handling

> **Number**: 11.03.03
> **Parent**: [11.03 Licence Pricing & Negotiation](./11.03-licence-pricing-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

MFN is the sync industry's standard defence against being the one who settled cheap: a party asserting it is
entitled to match the best terms any comparable party gets on the same deal — even if that deal closes after
theirs, and even if they have already been paid. It makes an agreed price **provisional**, which is a property
no ordinary commerce system has, and it is the single hardest correctness requirement in the domain.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Asserts MFN on their share (usually the publishing side, classically against the master). Sees when it triggers, what it paid them, and which deal caused the uplift. Must be told at assertion time that MFN converts their rate card from a price into a floor — see `#CX-04`. |
| Producer | Config | Same, master side. Also frequently the party whose higher settlement **triggers** someone else's uplift — they should know their negotiation moves other people's money, because otherwise MFN is a tax they impose on the deal without seeing it. |
| Operator | None | No music rights to assert MFN over. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Creator micro-licensing (11.06) is deliberately MFN-free — flat prices, no comparables, no retroactivity. That is much of why it is a separate sub-domain. |

## Behavior

An MFN assertion attaches to a **party's share on a deal** and names a comparable set — usually "the other side"
(publishing MFN against master) but sometimes "any writer on this work". When any deal in the comparable set
settles above the MFN party's terms, that party is lifted to match.

The critical modelling consequence: **the agreed price and the owed price are different fields.** The quote
records what was agreed; the settlement records what is owed after MFN evaluation over the settled set. A
system with one price field cannot represent MFN at all.

The second consequence: **evaluation is over the settled set at a fixed point, never pairwise on each
execution.** Two comparable deals executing concurrently can each trigger the other's MFN; naive pairwise
evaluation oscillates or double-counts (`#CX-03` synthesis Q5).

### Happy Path

1. During quoting (11.03.02), a party asserts MFN and names its comparable set
2. System discloses to the buyer: **this deal's cost is not final** — this is not a footnote
3. Deal executes; the MFN party's price is marked provisional, and they are paid the agreed amount now
4. A comparable deal in the set later settles higher
5. MFN evaluates over the whole settled set at a fixed point → uplift computed
6. Beneficiary is paid the difference; buyer is charged it; both are told which deal caused it
7. Comparable set fully closes → the price is final and the provisional marker clears

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two comparable deals execute concurrently, each triggering the other | **Evaluate over the settled set at a fixed point.** Pairwise evaluation oscillates. This is the feature's core correctness requirement. | One uplift each, computed once — never a visible flapping number |
| Uplift computes but the payment fails | The party is owed money the system knows about and has not sent. **That is a debt** and must be represented as one — not a silent retry. | Beneficiary: "£340 owed to you from an MFN uplift — [status]" |
| Buyer refuses to pay the uplift | Contractual dispute, not a system state. Escalates to domain 24. | The reason disclosure at step 2 is what makes this rare |
| MFN asserted but no comparable deal ever settles higher | Inert. The party got their agreed price. | Provisional marker clears at set close |
| Comparable set is ambiguous ("comparable" is a judgement) | **The hard part.** "Comparable" is contractual language, not a computable predicate. | See Q-01 — this may be irreducibly human |
| MFN party is also the trigger party (multi-hyphenate holds both sides) | One human triggering their own uplift. Must be detected — otherwise a person can lift their own publishing by settling their own master high, at the buyer's expense. | **Self-triggering MFN must be excluded from the comparable set.** A genuine exploit the multi-hyphenate structure creates. |
| Uplift arrives months after the buyer closed their production budget | Real and routine. Disclosure at step 2 is the entire mitigation. | Buyer: "Cost increased £340 — triggered by [deal]" |
| Rate card holder asserts MFN | Their card is now a floor, not a price | Must be stated at assertion — `#CX-04` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Evaluation over the settled set | Not user-facing; runs on deal execution |
| Empty | No MFN asserted | Prices are final at agreement. The normal, simple case. |
| Populated | MFN live | "Provisional — may increase if a comparable deal settles higher" on **both** buyer and seller views |
| Error | Evaluation failed | Price stays provisional; **never silently finalised.** An un-evaluated MFN that resolves to "final" is theft from the beneficiary. |
| Partial | Some comparables settled, some open | "Provisional — 2 of 3 comparable deals settled" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | MFN is a niche contract term that could be deferred or handled as a manual side-agreement | Scope instinct: it is complex, it is rare in most commerce, and cutting it would simplify the sub-domain enormously | ❌ REJECTED — MFN is **standard** in sync, not exotic; publishing MFN against master is close to a default expectation. More decisively: it is not a term you can bolt on later. It requires agreed-price and owed-price to be different fields, and it requires evaluation over a settled set. Both are schema-level. Deferring MFN means rebuilding the pricing model when it arrives. The complexity is load-bearing and belongs in the breadth pass even though the feature itself may be a `could`. |
| DT-02 | "Comparable" can be computed from the scope grammar — same work, same scope, same deal | Reasonable, and it is what a naive implementation would do | ⏳ DEFERRED to Q-01 — the grammar gets close but "comparable" is *contractual* language whose whole purpose is to be argued about. Is a 2-year UK licence comparable to a 2-year US licence? Same deal, different territory, different value. If the platform computes comparability, it is making a legal determination on the parties' behalf and will sometimes be wrong in a way that moves money. If it does not, MFN cannot be automated and this feature is a notification service. **A genuine fork, and it decides what this feature is.** |
| DT-03 | The multi-hyphenate creates a self-triggering MFN exploit nobody has named | Reasoning from the personas.md structural fact ("one human routinely occupies three of these in one day") crossed with MFN mechanics | ✅ CONFIRMED — one human holding both the master and the publishing share can assert publishing MFN, then settle their own master high, and lift their own publishing at the buyer's expense. Both moves are individually legitimate; the combination is self-dealing. **The comparable set must exclude deals where the trigger party and the beneficiary are the same identity** — which requires identity resolution across roles (domain 01), and is exactly the kind of exploit a persona model of "user has one role" would never surface. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Agreed price and owed price are separate fields | A single price field cannot represent MFN; this is schema-level and cannot be retrofitted (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Evaluation is over the settled set at a fixed point, never pairwise | Pairwise evaluation of mutually-triggering deals oscillates or double-counts |
| D-03 | MFN exposure is disclosed to the buyer at request time, prominently | A cost that rises after the fact is the most hated event in sync; it is survivable only if it was never a surprise |
| D-04 | The comparable set excludes deals where trigger and beneficiary are the same identity | Per DT-03 — closes a self-dealing exploit the multi-hyphenate structure creates |
| D-05 | A failed evaluation leaves the price provisional, never final | Silently finalising an unevaluated MFN takes money from the beneficiary |
