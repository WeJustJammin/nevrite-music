# Feature: Quote Requests & Negotiation

> **Number**: 11.03.02
> **Parent**: [11.03 Licence Pricing & Negotiation](./11.03-licence-pricing-negotiation-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The deal-specific path: a buyer states a scope and a budget, rights holders price it, and the two converge
through counter-offers into terms that become a licence. It handles everything a rate card cannot — unusual
scopes, big-money deals nobody standardises, and works whose co-owners have never agreed on a number.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Receives quote requests for their publishing side with the use stated plainly and their own share named in money, not percentages. Counters, accepts, declines with a reason. Sees the buyer's budget when disclosed and the other side's position when MFN makes it their business. |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side. Typically the faster-moving party and the one a buyer's deadline lands on first — per personas.md they are the de-facto project manager, which in this feature means they are the one chasing everyone else. |
| Operator | None | No music rights to quote in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. The fan-adjacent creator buyer (11.06.01) pays a listed price and never negotiates — that is a deliberate product boundary, not an omission. |

> The **requesting buyer** has no persona — parent domain Q-01. Their side of this feature (compose a request,
> compare quotes, counter, accept) is unspecifiable until that is resolved.

## Behavior

A request carries a scope (11.08.01 grammar), a budget or budget band, a deadline, and the intended use in
plain terms. It routes to every party with a stake — **simultaneously**, for the same reasons as 11.01.05 and
11.02.04. Parties whose rate card covers the scope are auto-quoted without being disturbed. The rest respond
by hand.

Negotiation converges into an **agreed price**, which is not necessarily the final price: if any party asserted
MFN (11.03.03), the number is provisional until every comparable deal in the transaction closes (`#CX-03`).
The quote records what was agreed; the settlement records what is owed. These are two fields, and collapsing
them makes MFN unimplementable.

**Lifecycle (D-05).** A request does not negotiate forever. It is bounded by the buyer's stated deadline: at
the deadline, any side still un-agreed is auto-declined and the request closes as a partial (never a licence —
D-04). When the buyer states no deadline, the request carries a default time-to-live and expires the same way
(the exact default TTL is a `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` number, not a behaviour gap). There is no fixed round
cap — deadline pressure, not a counter-limit, is what ends a stalemate, because a round cap punishes a
genuinely converging negotiation as harshly as a stalled one. This is the same disclose-the-deadline logic as
DT-03: the clock is real information both sides price against.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer composes a request: work, scope, budget band, deadline, plain description of the use
2. 11.04 policy evaluates first — a blocked category never becomes a quote
3. Request routes simultaneously to all parties with a stake; card-covered parties auto-quote
4. Uncovered parties see the request with their own share expressed in money
5. Parties price or counter; buyer sees a combined number with a per-side breakdown
6. Convergence → agreed terms → clearance gate re-checks (11.02.01 D-04) → execution (11.08.02)
7. If MFN is live, the agreed price is marked provisional until the comparable set settles

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One side auto-quotes from a card, another wants to negotiate | Normal and common — the combined number is part fixed, part in motion | Buyer: per-side breakdown showing which sides are settled |
| Buyer's budget is below every side's floor | Surfaced early, before anyone spends a round | "Below the floor on 2 of 3 sides" — with an option to proceed anyway; owners may take it |
| Parties counter into an infinite loop | Bounded by the deadline, not a round cap (D-05): at the buyer's deadline any un-agreed side auto-declines and the request closes partial | Buyer: "Deadline reached — [Name] never agreed. Not licensed." Owner: the request greys out, no further counters accepted |
| Request has no deadline and simply goes stale | Default TTL expires it the same way a deadline would; exact TTL is `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` | "This request expired without agreement." |
| Deal agreed; clearance degraded since (a co-owner set a block) | **Gate wins, execution refused**, agreement notwithstanding. Per 11.02.01 D-04. | "This can no longer be licensed: [named blocker]" — after agreement, which is genuinely painful and still correct |
| One party ghosts mid-negotiation | Request expires; buyer released; **partial agreement never becomes a licence** | "3 of 4 agreed. [Name] stopped responding." |
| Buyer accepts, then a co-owner discovers the use is not what they understood | The 11.02.04 DT-02 failure. Plain-language framing is the mitigation, not a cure. | Escalates to a dispute (domain 24) |
| Agreed price later moves under MFN | Buyer's cost rises after they thought the deal was done — the single most hated event in sync | Must be disclosed at request time when any party has MFN live. Never a surprise. |
| Negotiation moves off-platform to dodge the fee | personas.md names this as the Musician's worst intentional behaviour. Structural, not solvable here. | Noted; routed to `/create-prd` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Routing and card evaluation | Brief |
| Empty | No quote requests | Owner: quiet inbox. Not a problem state. |
| Populated | Request in flight | Per-side board: auto-quoted / countered / pending / agreed, each with elapsed time |
| Error | Routing failed | Request not delivered; buyer told honestly. **Never silently pending.** |
| Partial | Some sides agreed | "3 of 4 agreed" — **not a deal.** Partial agreement is not a licence and must never render as one. |
| Expired | Deadline reached or default TTL elapsed with any side un-agreed (D-05) | Request closes and greys out; buyer released; no further counters accepted. Distinct from Error — nothing failed, the clock ran out. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An owner's share should be shown as a percentage of the deal — that is how splits are expressed everywhere else on the platform | Consistency with domains 07/09, where the split ritual is percentage-native | ❌ REJECTED — percentages are how the Musician gets rolled. personas.md: they "sign a split sheet they don't understand and discover years later they gave away their publishing." "You get 12.5%" is unactionable; "you get £340" is a decision they can make. The percentage remains the *record*; the money is the *ask*. Same inversion as 11.02.04 DT-02 (plain-language primary, legal appendix) — and the two together are becoming a domain-wide principle worth naming at 11 level. |
| DT-02 | Quote and consent (11.02.04) are the same flow — both ask a rights holder to approve a specific use, one with a number attached | Structural observation; a priced consent *is* a quote | ✅ CONFIRMED as a mechanism, ❌ REJECTED as a merge — they converge but do not unify. A consent can be free (a co-writer approving at £0 for a friend's film); a quote can be refused on price while the *permission* was never in question. Merging them means every price refusal reads as a permission refusal, which corrupts the clearance verdict: the work would show `blocked` when it is merely expensive. They share a routing mechanism and a notification path, not an object. **Recorded as a shared mechanism in the domain CX.** |
| DT-03 | Making the deadline visible to rights holders is coercive | Fairness instinct — the buyer's urgency is not the owner's problem | ❌ REJECTED — the deadline is real information the owner needs to price with. A supervisor with a Friday air date is a *better* counterparty than one with six months, and an owner who does not know that is negotiating blind. Concealing the deadline protects nobody and mostly costs the seller. Disclose it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An owner's ask is stated in money, not percentage | Per DT-01 — percentages are how the persona the platform serves gets rolled |
| D-02 | Quotes and consents share routing but remain distinct objects | Per DT-02 — merging corrupts the clearance verdict by conflating "too expensive" with "not permitted" |
| D-03 | Buyer deadlines are disclosed to rights holders | Per DT-03 — it is pricing information, and withholding it costs the seller |
| D-04 | Partial agreement never renders as a deal | Same principle as 11.01.05 D-04 and 11.02.04 — the half-cleared representation is the domain's recurring failure mode |
| D-05 | Negotiation is bounded by the deadline/TTL, not by a round cap | Per Q-01 (resolved this step) — a counter-limit punishes a converging negotiation identically to a stalled one; the deadline is already disclosed (D-03) and is the honest, self-explaining stop condition. Exact default TTL when no deadline is given defers to `/write-be-spec`. |
