# Feature: Print & Lyric Rights

> **Number**: 11.10
> **Parent**: [Music Licensing](./music-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Licensing the words and the notation rather than the sound: sheet music and tab sold or licensed, and lyrics
displayed by third parties (streaming services, lyric sites, karaoke). Both are publishing-side rights that
exist independently of any recording, and both are served today by specialist intermediaries — Hal Leonard,
Musicnotes, Musixmatch, LyricFind — whose relationships the platform does not have.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The writer. Licenses their compositions for print or lyric display; sets terms. Realistically: a working musician with no sheet music demand and no lyric aggregator asking. **The Role Lens is honest and thin.** |
| Producer | None | **No standing.** Print and lyric are composition rights; a master share confers nothing here. One of the few places in this domain where the Producer — usually the central persona — has no seat at all. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible as a licensing surface. A fan *reading* lyrics is a domain 20 consumption surface, not a licence. |

## Behavior

Two related but distinct products sharing a publishing-side rights basis:

- **Print** — sheet music, lead sheets, tab. Licensed or sold to a publisher/retailer, or direct to a player.
  It requires an *artifact* (an engraved score) that the platform does not have and the writer usually has not made.
- **Lyric display** — an aggregator licenses the text to display it. It requires the *text*, which is trivially
  available, and a relationship with the aggregators, which is not.

Both route through the standard machinery: policy (11.04), terms (11.03), instrument (11.08). Neither needs
anything this domain does not already have — which is precisely why it is one feature and not a sub-domain.

### Happy Path

1. Writer opts a composition in for print and/or lyric licensing, with terms
2. A licensee (publisher, retailer, lyric aggregator) requests a scope
3. Standard consent/policy/pricing machinery runs
4. Instrument issues; the artifact (score) or text is delivered
5. Royalties flow through domain 10

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| No engraved score exists | **The common case.** Print licensing needs an artifact the writer never made. | "You'd need a score. We don't make one." — honest; transcription is not this platform's business |
| Lyrics not captured anywhere on the platform | Nothing to license. Lyrics would need to be a first-class field somewhere — likely domain 07 or 12. | A dependency on a field that may not exist |
| Lyric aggregator relationship does not exist | Same shape as 11.06.02 DT-02: the counterparty must want to deal | The feature has no customer |
| Co-writers disagree on lyric licensing | Standard 11.04 fold; unanimity | Nothing special |
| Composition is PD, arrangement is not | Recurring trap — same as 11.05.04 Q-02 and 11.09 | — |
| Karaoke use | Print + lyric + master, and possibly grand-rights-adjacent | Genuinely composite; unmodelled |
| A fan-made tab of the writer's song | Rampant, unlicensed, and universally tolerated. **Not something this platform should start enforcing.** | Out of scope by choice |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Standard | — |
| Empty | Not opted in | The default, and almost certainly the permanent state for most catalogues |
| Populated | Licensed | Standard instrument view |
| Error | Standard | — |
| Partial | Opted in, no artifact | "Opted in — but there's no score to license" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Print and lyric are two features, not one — different artifacts, different buyers, different intermediaries | The classification instinct: they genuinely differ | ⏳ **DEFERRED, kept as one at [SURFACE]** — they share a rights basis (composition), a policy model, a terms model and an instrument, and neither has enough substance at this depth to justify a file. Splitting them now would be structure-for-structure's-sake on two nodes with a combined MoSCoW proposal of `won't`. **If either becomes real, this splits.** Noted rather than pre-emptively built — the honest call at breadth. |
| DT-02 | This belongs in domain 11 because it is licensing | The obvious placement, and why the sweep put it here | ✅ CONFIRMED, weakly — it is licensing and it fits the machinery. But the fit is *only* machinery: it contributes no capability this domain does not already have, and its buyers (Hal Leonard, Musixmatch) are institutional intermediaries with no relationship to anything else on the platform. It is a **thin passenger** — correctly placed, contributing nothing. That is worth saying plainly rather than letting it look load-bearing because it has a number. |
| DT-03 | The platform's real print/lyric opportunity is the **fan-made transcription economy**, not institutional print licensing | Reasoning about where the volume actually is: Ultimate Guitar has millions of user-submitted tabs, all unlicensed, all tolerated; Musicnotes' formal catalogue is a rounding error next to it | ✅ CONFIRMED as an observation, ❌ REJECTED as scope. There is a genuine whitespace — a licensed tab economy where transcribers and writers both get paid — and it is a **different product** (UGC marketplace, moderation, transcription quality, a whole submission economy) that happens to touch licensing. Building it would mean starting an enforcement war against a tolerated practice that the platform's own persona participates in, on behalf of writers who have not asked. **Recorded as a rejected adjacency**: real, interesting, not this. If the owner ever wants it, it is a domain, not a feature. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | One feature, not two, at breadth | Per DT-01 — shared rights basis and machinery; splits if either becomes real |
| D-02 | The platform does not create scores | Transcription is a different business with different economics; opting in without an artifact is honest and useless |
| D-03 | The fan-transcription economy is out of scope | Per DT-03 — real whitespace, different product, and an enforcement war the platform should not start |
