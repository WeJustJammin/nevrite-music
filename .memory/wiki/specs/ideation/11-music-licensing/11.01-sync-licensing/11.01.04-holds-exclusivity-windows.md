# Feature: Holds & Exclusivity Windows

> **Number**: 11.01.04
> **Parent**: [11.01 Sync Licensing](./11.01-sync-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

A hold is a time-boxed promise: the buyer is cutting the work into an edit and needs to know it will still
be available when the client approves. It is the sync industry's core reservation grammar — first-call,
second-call, exclusive — and it is a genuine state machine with expiry, conflict detection, and a
catalogue-wide read impact, because a work held exclusively must stop appearing available to everyone else.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|

| Producer | Config | Same, for the master side. Typically the party approving the master-side hold; must see the publishing side's state or the hold is meaningless (`#CX-05`). |

## Behavior

A hold is requested against a work for a scope (usage, territory, term) and a window. Exclusivity is a
property of the hold, not a separate object: **exclusive** removes the work from other buyers' availability;
**first-call** leaves it available but gives the holder right of first refusal if a competing buyer moves;
**second-call** queues behind a first-call. Expiry is automatic and unattended — a hold nobody released is
the industry's default way of freezing a catalogue, and it is the Operator's "forgot to release a hold"
anti-persona failure (personas.md) transplanted into sync.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a hold on a shortlisted work: exclusivity type, window, intended usage scope
2. System checks for conflicts against existing holds and any category block from 11.04
3. Request routes to the controlling parties on **both** sides (master and publishing) per `#CX-05` — not just whoever the buyer found
4. Parties accept (or auto-accept per policy); the hold activates and availability is recomputed catalogue-wide
5. Work's search state changes for other buyers (suppressed if exclusive, annotated if first-call)
6. Hold expires on schedule, or converts to a licence (11.08.02), or is released early by the buyer
7. On expiry/release, availability restores with no human action

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two exclusive holds requested concurrently | Hold record is the serialization point; the second fails closed. Never both-granted. | Loser: "Just taken — [work] is on exclusive hold until [date]. Notify me if it lapses?" |
| One side accepts, the other declines | Aggregate settles to declined; the accepting side's hold releases. Buyer is told the work is **not** held — a half-hold is the trap this feature exists to prevent. | "Publishing declined — this work isn't holdable for your scope" |
| Second side never responds | Hold request expires unaccepted at a deadline; does not linger as a phantom | Buyer: "No response from the publishing side — treat as unavailable" |
| Buyer's client goes quiet; hold lapses mid-edit | Expiry fires; buyer warned before, not after | Warning at T-minus-N; on lapse: "Your hold expired. Re-request?" — never silent |
| Exclusive hold granted but search index invalidation fails | Hold grant does not commit. Fail-closed. | See `#CX-03` synthesis Q2 — a granted-but-visible exclusive sells an exclusivity the platform cannot honour |
| Buyer serially re-holds to keep a work off the market for a competitor | Detectable pattern (repeated hold→lapse→re-hold, no licence). `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — needs a policy. | — |
| Hold active while a licence executes at the moment of expiry | Licence pins the hold; execution does not race expiry | Nothing — this must be invisible |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Conflict check running | Brief check; must be fast — this is a buyer moment |
| Empty | No holds on this work | "Available" |
| Populated | Hold active | Hold card: type, holder, scope, expiry countdown |
| Error | Conflict check unavailable | Hold **not** granted. Fail closed — a hold granted without a conflict check is worse than no hold. |
| Partial | One side accepted, one pending | "Partially held — not yet secured" to the buyer. **Never rendered as held.** |

| DT-01 | Holds bundle naturally with pitching (the sweep's candidate was "Sync Briefs, Pitching & Holds") | Sweep candidate 04 | ❌ REJECTED — pitching is a submission; a hold is a reservation with a state machine, an expiry scheduler, a conflict-detection invariant, and a write impact on the entire catalogue's availability. | DT-02 | The dominant failure is the *forgotten* hold, not the contested one | Direct transfer from the Operator anti-persona: "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold" (personas.md). | DT-03 | A hold on the master is a hold on the work | Naive model; also how a buyer intuitively reads it | ❌ REJECTED — master and publishing are separately owned and separately held. This is *the* canonical sync failure (clear one side, discover the other won't clear at any price, after picture lock). The hold aggregate must span both sides or the platform is manufacturing the failure it exists to prevent. Drives 11.01.05 and `#CX-05`. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Holds fail closed — no conflict check, no hold | A double-granted exclusive is unrecoverable: two buyers both built edits. Availability is the one thing this feature must never be wrong about. |
| D-02 | Hold expiry is automatic and warns the owner as well as the buyer | Per DT-02 — the forgotten hold costs the *seller* silently, and the seller is the persona the platform serves |
| D-03 | A hold spans both master and publishing, or it is not a hold | Per DT-03 — the half-hold is the failure mode, not an acceptable intermediate product |
