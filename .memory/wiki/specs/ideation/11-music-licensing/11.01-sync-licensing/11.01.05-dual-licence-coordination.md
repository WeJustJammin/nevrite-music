# Feature: Master + Publishing Dual Licence Coordination

> **Number**: 11.01.05
> **Parent**: [11.01 Sync Licensing](./11.01-sync-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Every sync placement needs two licences, not one: a **master use** licence from whoever owns the recording,
and a **synchronisation** licence from whoever owns the composition. They are separately owned, separately
priced, separately refusable, and the industry's most expensive routine failure is clearing one and
discovering the other will not clear at any price — after picture lock. This feature makes the two sides
move as one transaction, so "cleared" is never half-true.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Usually the publishing-side party (writer share). Sees whether the master side has cleared, at what price, and to whom — because MFN (11.03.03) makes the other side's number their business. Acts on their own side through 11.03.02 and 11.04, not here; this feature is the coordinator, not the approval surface. |
| Producer | Full | Usually the master-side controller — owns the recording, often owns the room it was made in. Sees the publishing side's state and the aggregate. The party most likely to notice that a co-writer who never showed up to the session still holds a publishing veto. |
| Operator | None | No sync catalogue surface. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

The work's clearability is an **aggregate over two independently-owned sides**. This feature owns that
aggregate: it solicits both sides, tracks their independent states, blocks any representation of "cleared"
until both are true, and coordinates price when MFN is in play. WeJammin's structural advantage is that the
split-at-creation ritual (domain 07 → domain 09) often means *both sides are on-platform and already
enumerated* — which is exactly how the platform manufactures one-stops as a byproduct rather than buying
catalogue like Musicbed or Artlist.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer requests a licence (or hold) for a work at a defined scope
2. System resolves both sides from the rights registry (domain 09): who controls the master, who controls the composition
3. Both sides are solicited simultaneously — not sequentially; sequential solicitation is how deals die in week three
4. Each side responds independently (auto-approve per 11.04 policy, or explicit approval, or a quote via 11.03.02)
5. System holds the aggregate at "pending" until both are terminal; MFN coordination (11.03.03) runs if either side asserted it
6. Both approved → the pair executes atomically as one transaction; two certificates issue (11.08.02), one deal
7. Either declined → the aggregate settles to declined and the other side releases

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Master clears, publishing declines | Aggregate = declined; master's approval releases; **nothing is charged** | Buyer: "Publishing declined. This work is not clearable at this scope." Master side: "Deal died on the publishing side — you're released." |
| One side is not on WeJammin at all | The work is **not one-stop** regardless of how complete the on-platform side is. Handed to 11.02.02 (completeness attestation). | "Off-platform publisher — we can't clear this side. [Invite them] / [Filter to one-stop]" |
| Same human controls both sides | Two approvals are still required, and still separately recorded — but presented as one action with both effects named | "Approving as: master owner **and** writer (2 of 2 sides)" — never silently one-click |
| The two sides price incompatibly (master wants 3×) | Not an error — it is the negotiation. Aggregate stays pending; MFN may force alignment if asserted. | Both sides see the gap; buyer sees a combined number |
| Publishing side has 5 co-writers, one unreachable | Aggregate blocks on the unreachable party; the platform must not "assume yes" | "4 of 5 writers cleared. [Name] hasn't responded since [date]." Escalation path per 11.02.04. |
| Composition is cleared but the buyer used the wrong recording | Master licence is per-recording; a composition licence does not travel to another master | Named at scope-selection time, not discovered at delivery |
| One side approves, then revokes before the other responds | Revocation before execution is allowed; after execution it is not (licences are irrevocable instruments) | Clear line drawn at execution, shown as such |
| Someone who controls **neither** side tries to approve (e.g. a band member without a share) | Rejected at the registry check — approval authority is per-side and derived from domain 09, never from project membership | "You don't control the master or the publishing side of this work." No approval control is rendered for them |
| Two buyers request an exclusive-scope licence on the same work at once (concurrency) | The hold (11.01.04) is the arbiter — first request to place a valid hold spanning both sides wins; the second is queued behind the hold, not silently lost | Second buyer: "This work is on hold for another buyer until [date]. [Notify me if it releases]" |
| Buyer represents a still-pending deal as "cleared" to a third party externally | Out of the platform's control post-export, but no certificate (11.08.02) exists until execution, so the claim is unbacked; the platform never issues a document that says "cleared" while either side is pending | Certificate download is disabled until aggregate is executed; the pending state names itself "1 of 2 sides — not yet licensable" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Resolving both sides from the registry | "Working out who controls this" — resolution can be genuinely slow on deep credit graphs |
| Empty | No licence request active | Work shows its standing clearability from 11.02.01, not a deal state |
| Populated | Both sides resolved, request in flight | Two-column state view: master side / publishing side, each with its own status |
| Error | Registry resolution failed | **Not clearable.** Fail closed — an unresolvable rights graph is not a clearable work. |
| Partial | One side terminal, one pending | "1 of 2 sides cleared — not yet licensable." The word "cleared" is forbidden in this state. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Sequential solicitation (master first, then publishing) is safer — it avoids bothering the publishing side about deals that die early | Plausible efficiency argument; also how manual sync brokering actually works | ❌ REJECTED — sequential is exactly why sync clearance takes months. Each hop adds a human response latency, and the second side learns about the deal only after the first has anchored a price. Simultaneous solicitation is the whole point of holding both sides in one registry, and it is the mechanised version of the advantage D-18 predicts. |
| DT-02 | When one human controls both sides, the two approvals can collapse into one click | Multi-hyphenate reality (personas.md structural fact: "one human routinely occupies three of these in one day"). Two dialogs for one person feels like bureaucracy. | ❌ REJECTED — with a caveat. The *action* may be one click, but the two approvals must remain two records, because ownership of the two sides can diverge later (a writer share is assigned to a publisher; a master is sold). A collapsed approval becomes unattributable the moment the sides split. This is the multi-hyphenate trap: the UX may unify what the data model must not. Presented as one action naming both effects. |
| DT-03 | The platform's split-at-creation ritual is what makes dual-side coordination tractable at the long tail | Thesis D-18 + domain rationale ("split-at-creation MANUFACTURES one-stops as a byproduct") | ✅ CONFIRMED — but with a hard limit that the domain rationale glosses: it only holds for works *created on WeJammin after the ritual existed*. Back-catalogue and works with any off-platform writer are exactly as bad as they are everywhere else. The one-stop catalogue therefore starts empty and grows with platform age — a cold-start property nobody has stated. **This is the load-bearing caveat on the domain's entire value proposition.** Escalated to Q-03 and to the domain index. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Both sides are solicited simultaneously, never sequentially | Per DT-01 — sequential solicitation is the incumbent's latency, and the registry exists precisely to remove it |
| D-02 | The pair executes atomically — both licences or neither | A charged buyer holding one of two licences owns nothing usable. Partial execution is worse than failure. |
| D-03 | Two approvals remain two records even when one human performs both | Per DT-02 — the sides can diverge in ownership later, and a collapsed record becomes unattributable |
| D-04 | "Cleared" is never rendered while either side is pending | The half-cleared representation is the exact failure this feature exists to prevent |
