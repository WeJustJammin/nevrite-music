## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-03: Holds ↔ Supervisor Search

**Relationship**: Placing an exclusive hold changes what the catalogue is willing to show. The hold is
written against the work, but its observable effect is in every other buyer's result set. Non-exclusive
("first-call") holds should still surface the work but annotate it as contested; exclusive holds must
suppress it or show it as unavailable for the hold window. When the hold lapses, availability must
restore without anyone taking an action — expiry is a scheduled state transition, not a chore.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees that their work is on hold and by whom (or anonymised, per Q-02); cannot pitch it elsewhere while exclusive
- **Producer**: same, for the master side; often the party actually approving the hold
- **Operator**: not affected — no visibility into sync catalogue
- **Fan**: not affected — this sub-domain has no fan surface

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The work's availability is owned by the hold record, not the search index — search reads it. If two exclusive holds are attempted concurrently, the hold record is the serialization point and the second must fail closed, not queue.
2. **Trigger chain**: Hold granted → availability recomputed → search index invalidated. If index invalidation fails, the work keeps appearing as available — a **fail-open leak that sells an exclusivity the platform cannot honour**. Invalidation must be synchronous with hold grant, or hold grant must not commit.
3. **Permission intersection**: A co-owner who blocked the category in 11.04 must not be able to be overridden by a hold accepted by another co-owner. Policy outranks holds.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Hold grant notifies all co-owners of both sides (per 11.01.05), not just the accepting party. Expiry notifies the buyer before it lapses, or the platform has silently cost them the placement.
5. **State transition conflict**: Hold expiry and licence execution can race — a licence executing at the moment its hold lapses must not fail. The licence must pin the hold, not depend on it.

### CX-05: Holds ↔ Dual Licence Coordination

**Relationship**: The single most expensive failure in sync is a buyer who cleared the master and
discovered the publishing side would not clear at any price — after the edit was locked. A hold that
covers only one side manufactures exactly that failure with the platform's name on it. The coordination
child exists to make "held" mean *both sides held, or not held*.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: usually the master-side controller; must see the publishing side's state, not just their own
- **Musician**: usually the publishing-side controller (writer share); must see the master side's state
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Neither side owns "the work is held". A joint hold is an aggregate whose state is a function of both sides' states — it needs its own record, not a flag on either side.
2. **Trigger chain**: One side accepting should trigger a solicitation to the other, with a deadline. If the second side declines, the first side's hold must release — otherwise a work is frozen by a dead deal. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`: whether release is automatic or requires the first party's acknowledgement.
3. **Permission intersection**: Master-side control does not confer publishing-side authority even when the same human holds both. The multi-hyphenate makes this look like one person; the rights model must not collapse it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A one-sided hold must notify the buyer that the work is **not yet clearable** — presenting it as held is the misrepresentation.
5. **State transition conflict**: Both sides can accept concurrently; that is the good case and must not deadlock. Both sides can decline concurrently; the aggregate must settle to "declined" once, not notify twice.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 11.01.01 Tagging | 11.01.04 Holds | No shared state and no trigger dependency. A hold does not change a work's mood, tempo, or instrumentation, and re-tagging a held work has no effect on the hold. The interaction people imagine here (held works being deprioritised in results) is really CX-03, mediated by search, not by tags. |
| R-02 | 11.01.01 Tagging | 11.01.03 Briefs | Tempting — briefs are expressed in tag vocabulary — but the coupling is entirely via the search matcher (CX-01 + CX-02). Asserting a direct edge would double-count one relationship and imply briefs read tags directly, which would fork the matching logic. |
| R-03 | 11.01.05 Dual Licence | 11.01.02 Search | Considered: should search filter out works where only one side is on-platform? Rejected as an edge here — that is the **clearance** computation's job (11.02.01), and the one-stop flag is the interface. Duplicating it in search would give two definitions of "clearable" that could disagree. |
