## Overview

Third parties acquiring permission to use music — sync for screen, sample clearance, covers and compulsories, remix and stem rights, AI training licences, and micro-licensing for creators.

**Why this is a top-level domain**: Structurally a fourth marketplace: buyers (supervisors, brands, creators, producers) who never touch the ownership registry, its own search paradigm (mood/tempo/lyric/reference, not artist name), its own negotiation grammar (briefs, holds, MFN, coordinated master+sync dual licences), and its own clearance gate. It earns top-level status because the platform's split-at-creation approach MANUFACTURES one-stops as a byproduct of its own core loop — every session that runs the split ritual adds a clearable asset. Musicbed, Artlist and Songtradr solve one-stop by acquiring catalog, which is expensive and caps at what they can buy; owning the registry scales to the long tail. Programmatic sample clearance is the same unlock: clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a lookup plus a pre-set price. Sync briefs stay here rather than in Opportunities & Casting because they are inseparable from clearance status and catalog search — an acknowledged reuse of the post→submit→triage mechanism across a domain boundary, and a deliberate wrong-cut avoidance.

**Interacting capabilities** (what justifies domain status):

- sync catalog, tagging & supervisor search
- briefs, pitching & holds
- quoting, MFN & dual-licence coordination
- clearance status & one-stop flag
- sample/cover/remix/AI licence workflows
- licence certificate issuance
