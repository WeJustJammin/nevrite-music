# Feature: Licence Persistence After Subscription Ends

> **Number**: 11.06.04
> **Parent**: [11.06 Creator Micro-Licensing](./11.06-creator-micro-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Answers the question every creator asks before they subscribe and every one of them asks again the day they
cancel: **do the videos I already made stay licensed?** It is a product decision dressed as a policy detail,
it is the defining trauma of this market, and getting it wrong once is a reputational event rather than a
support ticket.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what their catalogue is permanently committed to. **The uncomfortable half**: opting into a subscription catalogue commits their work to videos that exist forever, for revenue that stopped. They should learn that at opt-in, not from a support thread. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, master side. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | Full | **As creator buyer.** Sees exactly what survives cancellation, before cancelling. The audience for this feature is a person deciding whether to trust the platform with two years of work. |

## Behavior

Two coherent models, and the platform must pick one and mean it:

| Model | What it sells | At cancellation |
|---|---|---|
| **Access** (rental) | The right to license while you pay | Nothing survives. Your back catalogue becomes claimable. |
| **Grant** (ownership) | A permanent licence per work used | Everything licensed while live survives forever |

The honest position — and the one consistent with the platform's own thesis — is **grant**. WeJammin's entire
argument (D-18) is that the facts of work should survive: *"the work gets done, but the proof of it evaporates"*
is the problem statement's opening line. A licensing model where the creator's proof evaporates on cancellation
is that same failure, sold as a subscription, by the platform that exists to stop it.

The mechanical requirement follows directly: **the whitelist entry is a property of the licence, not of the
subscription** (`#CX-03` synthesis Q1). Modelling it as subscription state is the bug that creates the cliff.
Cancellation must cascade to *new licensing capability* and nothing else.

### Happy Path

1. Creator subscribes; licenses 200 tracks across two years
2. Creator cancels
3. System states plainly what survives: all 200 licences, all 200 whitelists, forever
4. What ends: the ability to license anything **new**
5. Back catalogue keeps working. Nothing is claimed. Nothing to do.
6. Rights holders' commitments to those 200 videos persist — they knew that at opt-in

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Creator cancels with 200 licensed videos | All persist. Whitelists persist. | "All 200 stay licensed forever. You just can't license anything new." |
| Rights holder opts out of the catalogue | Existing licences persist; **their exit cannot claim videos licensed while they were in** | Owner: "This won't affect the 340 videos already using this track" |
| Creator cancels and re-subscribes | Old licences never lapsed; new licensing resumes | No reconciliation drama, because there was no lapse |
| Creator disputes a charge / chargebacks | `[PENDING — /create-prd (Q-04)]` — does a reversed payment reverse a permanent grant? The only case with a real argument for revocation, and it is a legal/policy call, not a behavior gap. Interim behavior: the grant persists until the dispute is adjudicated (fail toward persistence, per D-04). | "Your licences remain active while this payment dispute is reviewed." |
| Platform shuts down | **The whitelists live in YouTube's system.** Do they survive WeJammin? A licence whose enforcement depends on a company that no longer exists is a licence that quietly stops working. | See Q-03 — a genuine and rarely-asked question |
| Cancellation races an in-flight purchase | Completed (persists forever) or not (does not). **No middle state may exist.** | Per `#CX-03` synthesis Q5 |
| Subscription model is never adopted (per-track only) | This feature collapses to "purchases persist", which is trivially true | Parent Q-02 decides whether this feature exists at all |
| Creator's licence persists; the rights holder is later found to not have owned the work | Upstream defect — 11.02 territory. The creator relied on a platform assertion. | Relates to 11.02 Q-01 (warranty posture) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Cancellation cascade in progress | Cancellation confirms immediately (nothing to wait for); the licence-scoped cascade runs async. The cancel screen resolves in one interaction — it does **not** block on the cascade, because the licences are already permanent and unaffected. |
| Empty | No subscription | Feature does not apply |
| Populated | Active subscription | "N licences — all permanent" stated **during** the subscription, not only at cancellation. It is a reason to stay, not a consolation. |
| Error | Cancellation cascade failed | **Fail toward persistence.** If the system cannot determine what survives, everything survives. The asymmetry is not close. |
| Partial | Cancelled, cascade running | "Your licences are unaffected" — said first, before anything else |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Persistence is a pricing/policy detail that belongs in the subscription's terms, not a product feature | The sweep's framing — "Rights Persistence After Subscription Ends" reads as a T&C line item | ❌ REJECTED — it is the **product's central trust claim**, and it is the question that decides the purchase. A creator choosing between Epidemic and WeJammin is choosing between "my back catalogue is hostage to my card details" and "it isn't". That is not a term; it is the reason to buy. It also has real mechanical consequences (the whitelist must be licence-scoped, not subscription-scoped) that a T&C cannot express and an implementer would otherwise get wrong by default. |
| DT-02 | The access model (nothing survives) is defensible — it is what the market does, and it is what makes subscriptions worth renewing | Commercial realism; Epidemic Sound's model, and it works | ❌ REJECTED **on thesis grounds, not commercial ones.** The problem statement's opening line is *"the work gets done, but the proof of it evaporates"* — and the access model is precisely that failure, monetised: the creator's right to their own back catalogue evaporates when they stop paying. A platform whose whole argument is that records should survive cannot sell a licence that expires when the card does. **This is the clearest case in the domain of the thesis making a product decision** rather than being decorative. It costs renewal leverage. That is the price of meaning it, and it should be the owner's decision to pay knowingly. |
| DT-03 | Persistence is a promise the platform can actually keep | The assumption underneath the whole feature | ❌ REJECTED — **the whitelist lives in YouTube's system, not WeJammin's** (11.06.02 DT-02). A "permanent" licence is enforced by an entry in a third party's database, maintained by a relationship that only exists while WeJammin does. If the platform folds, or loses Content ID access, every "permanent" licence quietly stops being enforced — the creator's videos become claimable and nobody tells them. **The platform can promise permanence in its own record and cannot promise its enforcement.** That gap is honest to state and almost never stated by anyone in this market. Escalated to Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Proposed: the grant model — licences persist forever** | Per DT-02 — the access model is the exact failure the problem statement opens with. **Owner's decision** (Q-01); the agent's role is to state that the thesis has an opinion here. |
| D-02 | The whitelist is a property of the licence, not the subscription | Per `#CX-03` — modelling it as subscription state is the bug that creates the cliff |
| D-03 | Cancellation cascades to new licensing capability only | The single most consequential cascade in the sub-domain |
| D-04 | A failed cascade fails toward persistence | If the system cannot determine what survives, everything survives — the asymmetry is not close |
| D-05 | Permanence is stated during the subscription, not only at cancellation | It is a reason to stay, not a consolation prize |
