## Overview

Music for people who are not in the music industry: podcasters, streamers, YouTubers and small brands who
need a track for a video today, at a listed price, with no negotiation and no lawyer. The product is not
really the licence — it is the **promise that nothing bad happens afterwards**: no Content ID claim, no
demonetisation, no takedown, and no cliff when a subscription lapses.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature** — 2+ interacting capabilities: the catalogue sells a licence;
whitelisting must then reach into YouTube/Meta's rights systems so the licence is *honoured* by machines that
have never heard of WeJammin; claim release handles the claims that land anyway; and persistence determines
whether a licence bought today survives a cancelled subscription tomorrow. A licence that a platform's
matching system claims anyway is worse than no licence — so selling and whitelisting are one product with two
halves that constantly interact.

> ### The Fan mapping is strained, and this is the honest record of it
>
> A podcaster or streamer buying a track is **not** a Musician, Producer, or Operator. Fan is the closest of
> the four ratified personas (D-19) because it is the only one defined by having **no professional music
> stake** — personas.md: *"will not tolerate professional-tool complexity, will not read a rider, and will
> never file a split sheet."* That description fits the creator buyer precisely.
>
> But it fits for the wrong reason. Fan is defined as someone who **follows and consumes** artists; the
> creator buyer is running a **business** that happens not to be a music business. They have commercial
> intent, a monetised channel, and a real dispute appetite — none of which personas.md's Fan has.
>
> **Was recorded as a persona gap** (parent Q-01 — **now resolved**; see the section immediately below),
> distinct from the buyer gap in 11.01/11.03: there the buyer
> matches *no* persona; here they match one *badly*, which is more dangerous, because a spec writer will read
> "Fan: Full" and build for a music enthusiast instead of a small-business operator.
>
> #### Resolved (D-05 / parent D-10) — a profile, not a fifth persona
>
> The creator buyer is **not** a fifth persona and is **not** the Fan persona either. They are described by the
> **creator micro-licence buyer** counterparty profile in `meta/counterparties.md` — podcaster, streamer,
> small-business channel — carrying personas.md's six fields plus **Workflow** and **Anti-Persona Behavior**,
> under a header stating it is not a persona and adds **no column** to the matrix above. The Fan ✅ Full cells
> stand, because that is the account the buyer holds; the profile is what a spec writer must read *before*
> building the surface, since "Fan: Full" alone is exactly the misread this note was written to prevent.
>
> It is a **separate artifact** from the professional licence buyer profile (11.01/11.03/11.08) rather than one
> broad buyer document, because the two differ in **kind**: there the buyer matches no persona, here they match
> one badly. One artifact covering both would diverge on almost every field — pain, workaround, budget,
> tolerance for professional-tool complexity — and produce exactly the generic text the Persona Specificity
> rubric rejects, reproducing this sub-domain's defect one level up. The two must be kept consistent where the
> buy side is genuinely common: checkout, verdict rendering, and the licence instrument.
>
> This does **not** answer personas.md Q-01 (the dealer persona for 13/14/15). A dealer is a *seller*, not a
> buyer, and parent D-10 deliberately declines to generalise the form to the nine other non-persona actors —
> see the non-closure list in parent Q-01.
>
> **Operator ❌ throughout**: no stake in this capacity.
