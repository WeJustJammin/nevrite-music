# Feature: Creator Licence Catalogue & Instant Purchase

> **Number**: 11.06.01
> **Parent**: [11.06 Creator Micro-Licensing](./11.06-creator-micro-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

A browse-and-buy catalogue for people who need a track for a video today: listed prices, no negotiation, no
quotes, no lawyer, no waiting. Every work in it is there because its owners opted in and accepted a flat
price — which makes the catalogue a direct expression of 11.04 policy, and makes its size a measure of how
many rights holders were willing to stop being asked.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Opts works into the catalogue and accepts the flat price tier. **Never negotiates** — that is the entire deal, and it is why this catalogue can exist. Sees earnings as a stream, not as deals: "£43 from 9 licences this month". |
| Producer | Config | Same, master side. Both sides must opt in or the work is not in the catalogue. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | Full | **As buyer** — searches by vibe and video type, auditions against their own footage, buys in one action, gets a licence and a whitelist. Never sees a rights holder, a split, a percentage, or the word "clearance". |

> **The Fan mapping is strained** — a podcaster running a monetised channel is a small business, not a music
> enthusiast. Fan is the closest ratified persona only because it is the one defined by having no professional
> music stake. See the parent index Role Matrix note and Q-01. A spec writer reading "Fan: Full" here must
> not build for a listener.

## Behavior

The inverse of everything else in domain 11. Sync (11.01) is a negotiation; sample clearance (11.05) is a
conversation; this is a **shop**. No quotes, no MFN, no counter-offers, no consent rounds — the rights holders
answered every question in advance by opting in (index D-04).

What the creator is actually buying is **the absence of a claim** (`#CX-01`), so the purchase is not complete
when the money moves: it is complete when the whitelist is written. A purchase that completes without a
whitelist has sold a promise the platform is not keeping.

Search is by **video context**, not musical facet: "podcast intro", "travel vlog", "60-second ad", "no vocals,
under a voiceover". A creator does not know they want 118 BPM in D minor. This is a different search paradigm
from the supervisor's (11.01.02), which is itself a different paradigm from the platform's general search —
three, not two.

### Happy Path

1. Creator searches by video type and vibe; every result carries one listed price and no conditions
2. Creator auditions — ideally against their own footage, because that is the only real test
3. Creator buys: one action, one price, no counterparty visible
4. Licence issues (11.08.02) with a fixed scope; whitelist writes (11.06.02)
5. Creator uses the track; nothing bad happens; that is the product
6. Rights holders see a monthly earnings digest, not 40 notifications

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Whitelist write fails after payment | **Purchase must not silently complete.** Either fail, or complete with a prominent "whitelisting pending". Never silence. | "Paid — whitelisting your channel now. Don't publish yet." Honest and annoying, which beats a claim. |
| One co-owner opts in, another does not | Work is **not** in the catalogue. Unanimity, consistent with the domain. | Opting-in owner: "[Name] hasn't opted in — this work isn't listed" |
| Rights holder opts out later | Work leaves the catalogue for **new** purchases; existing licences persist forever (11.06.04) | "This won't affect the 12 videos already licensed" — stated at opt-out, because they will assume otherwise |
| Creator uses the track in something the owner would hate | Category blocks (11.04.02) are the only protection, and creator use is broad by nature. **A real hazard**: opting into a creator catalogue is a much wider grant than most owners will realise. | Must be stated at opt-in in plain terms |
| Price changes between audition and checkout | **Race.** Owner may re-price or opt out while the creator is auditioning. Rule: the **price shown at checkout is the price honoured** for that transaction; an opt-out mid-session removes the track from a not-yet-completed purchase and the creator is told, not silently charged. Never charge a price the creator did not see. | If re-priced up: "Price changed to £X since you looked" before the buy button commits. If pulled: "This track was just withdrawn — [similar tracks]" |
| Payment authorises then declines/reverses | Money is the gate before the licence and whitelist (D-02): **no whitelist write until settlement confirms.** A reversal after a completed purchase routes to claim/dispute (11.06.03), not a silent whitelist removal. | Ordinary checkout decline: "Payment didn't go through — try again." Post-settlement reversal is handled as an account matter, not a licence revocation the creator discovers via a claim. |
| Bulk/automated scraping of the catalogue | **The obvious abuse.** The catalogue is a browsable price list; a competitor or bot can harvest it wholesale. Rate-limit and bot-detect the browse/search surface; auditioning against footage stays gated. Detail deferred. | Nothing for a legitimate creator; throttled/blocked for automation. `[PENDING — /create-prd-security: rate-limit + bot policy]` |
| Creator buys, then abandons the video | Licence issued and unused. Fine — no refund, no state. | — |
| Creator wants exclusivity | Not offered. Exclusivity is a negotiation and this catalogue does not negotiate. | "Not available here — [sync path]" |
| Same track in 40,000 videos | Correct and expected. Ubiquity is the model, not a failure. | Owners should understand this at opt-in |
| Creator is a minor with a monetised channel | Age assurance duties (D-13); routes outside this domain | `[PENDING]` — routed to `/create-prd-security` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is the same catalogue as sync (11.01), filtered to cheap works with a simpler checkout | Modelling economy; both are "search music, license it" | ❌ REJECTED — three things differ, not one. **Search paradigm**: supervisors search by reference track and musical facet; creators search by video context ("podcast intro"), and would not know a BPM if asked. **Price mechanism**: sync negotiates; this cannot, ever. **Product**: sync sells permission; this sells *the absence of a claim*, which is a downstream integration promise, not a licence term. A shared catalogue with a "simple mode" toggle would drag negotiation, MFN and clearance vocabulary into a surface whose entire premise is their absence. |
| DT-02 | Rights holders will opt in readily — it is free money on works that earn nothing | The obvious commercial read, and how the catalogue gets stocked | ❌ REJECTED — opting in means accepting that your song may appear in **40,000 videos you will never see**, made by strangers, forever, for a flat fee you set once. That is a much larger grant than "free money on a dead track", and it is close to the *shape* of the harm personas.md names — the Musician who "signs a split sheet they don't understand and discovers years later they gave away their publishing", except it is their sync future and it is 40,000 videos. **The opt-in must state the scale in plain terms** ("this could appear in thousands of videos; you won't be asked about any of them"), which will reduce opt-in rates and is the honest cost of not repeating the harm the platform exists to prevent. |
| DT-03 | The catalogue's cold-start problem is the same as sync's (11.01.05 DT-03) and samples' (11.05.01 DT-03) | Pattern-matching across the three cold-start findings in this domain | ❌ REJECTED — **it is materially better here, and that is a finding worth acting on.** Sync needs *specific* works (a supervisor wants that song, and the catalogue either has it or is useless). Samples need *catalogue* (a 1974 soul record the registry will never hold). But a creator needs *any* competent track that fits a 60-second travel vlog — the demand is **substitutable**. A catalogue of 500 WeJammin-native works is genuinely useful to a podcaster and genuinely useless to a music supervisor. That inverts the domain's internal priority: **the cold-start-resistant surface is the one the sweep and the domain rationale treat as peripheral.** This is a real input to the beachhead question (problem-statement Q-03) and it argues against this sub-domain's low MoSCoW proposal. Escalated to Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No negotiation, ever — flat listed prices only | Per index D-04; a creator will not negotiate and asking them destroys the product |
| D-02 | The purchase completes on whitelist write, not on payment | Per `#CX-01` — the product is the absence of a claim, not the licence |
| D-03 | Opt-in states the scale of the grant in plain terms | Per DT-02 — the alternative reproduces the exact harm personas.md names, at scale |
