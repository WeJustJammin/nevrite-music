# Feature: Content ID Whitelisting & Channel Registration

> **Number**: 11.06.02
> **Parent**: [11.06 Creator Micro-Licensing](./11.06-creator-micro-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Makes a licence legible to machines that have never heard of WeJammin. A creator registers their channel; when
they license a track, the platform reaches into YouTube's and Meta's rights systems and registers that this
asset, on this channel, is permitted — so the automated matcher does not claim it. It is the half of the
creator product that actually delivers the promise, and it depends entirely on a relationship with two
companies that WeJammin does not have.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees that their opted-in works are whitelisted for licensed channels. **Cannot un-whitelist a licensed video** — that would retroactively claim a video someone paid for, which is the cliff (`#CX-03`). |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, master side. |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | Full | **As creator buyer.** Registers and verifies their channel(s); sees whitelist status per video; this is the feature they actually came for, even though they would never name it. |

## Behavior

Two writes, in two systems the platform does not own. The creator connects a channel (OAuth against YouTube,
Meta, Twitch); the platform verifies ownership; on each purchase it writes a whitelist entry — asset ×
channel — into the external rights system.

The consequence that shapes everything: **the source of truth is split across two companies** (`#CX-01`
synthesis Q1). WeJammin holds the licence; YouTube holds the whitelist. They can disagree, and when they do,
YouTube wins in the only way that matters to the creator — their video gets claimed. So reconciliation is a
permanent background obligation, not a one-time write.

**Whitelisting is preventive and it leaks** (`#CX-02`). It cannot stop claims from parties outside the
arrangement — a co-owner's other distributor, a PRO, an aggregator with a stale catalogue, a false fingerprint
match. That is what 11.06.03 exists for.

### Happy Path

1. Creator connects and verifies a channel via OAuth
2. Creator buys a licence (11.06.01)
3. Platform writes the whitelist entry to the external system
4. Platform confirms the write landed — **confirmation, not fire-and-forget**
5. Creator publishes; the matcher sees a whitelisted asset on a whitelisted channel; no claim
6. Background reconciliation re-verifies entries; drift is repaired without anyone asking

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| External write fails | **Purchase does not silently complete.** Prominent pending state. | "Don't publish yet — whitelisting your channel" |
| Creator publishes before propagation | Claim lands, whitelist arrives after. **Entirely predictable and not their fault.** Routes to 11.06.03. | "This is our race, not your mistake — releasing the claim" |
| Creator uses the track on a channel they never registered | Not whitelisted; claim lands legitimately | "This licence covers [channel]. Add [other channel]?" |
| Channel changes hands / creator loses access | Reconciliation (D-04) detects the OAuth grant is revoked or the channel is no longer theirs. The whitelist entry **is not silently deleted** — a licensed video already published stays covered; deleting it would retroactively claim it (the cliff, `#CX-03`). New writes to that channel are blocked and the entry is flagged stale. | "We can no longer verify [channel]. Videos already licensed stay covered; reconnect to license new uploads there." |
| YouTube/Meta relationship does not exist | **The feature does not work.** Not degraded — absent. The sub-domain's central promise fails. | Parent Q-03 — an existential dependency, not a risk |
| External API changes or deprecates | The platform's core promise is subject to a third party's roadmap | Routed to `/create-prd` (integration architecture) |
| Rights holder opts out of the catalogue | Existing whitelists **persist**. Opt-out affects new licences only. | Per `#CX-03` — anything else is the cliff |
| Claim arrives from a party outside the arrangement | Whitelisting cannot prevent it — that is 11.06.03's job | "You're covered. We'll get this released." |
| Creator connects a channel they don't own | Verification must be real; a fake channel connection whitelists someone else's | OAuth ownership proof, not a URL paste |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | External write in flight | "Whitelisting" — a real state with real duration, not a spinner over an instant action |
| Empty | No channels connected | "Connect your channel — otherwise a licence won't stop a claim" |
| Populated | Channels connected, whitelists live | Per-channel, per-video status |
| Error | External write failed | **Loud.** The one notification in the sub-domain that cannot be batched (`#CX-01` synthesis Q4). |
| Partial | Some channels whitelisted | Explicit per-channel status; never a summary that implies all |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Whitelisting is an integration detail — architecture, not product | The Node Classification Gate's not-product test, applied honestly: this is an API integration | ❌ REJECTED — the *integration* is architecture, but **what it delivers is the entire product**. A creator buys a licence to prevent a claim; whitelisting is the only mechanism that prevents one. Routing this to `/create-prd` as an integration concern would leave the product as "we sell you a PDF and hope", which is not a product. The API mechanics are architecture; the promise, its states, its failure modes and its honesty when it fails are product. **The split is real, and the product half is the bigger half.** |
| DT-02 | The platform can whitelist because it holds the rights — it is the rights holder's agent | The natural assumption from owning the registry | ❌ REJECTED — **holding the rights is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.** Content ID access is a commercial relationship YouTube grants to entities it chooses, at its discretion, typically to established rightsholders and aggregators. WeJammin having perfect rights data does not confer it. This is the sub-domain's **existential dependency** (parent Q-03): the central promise of a whole sub-domain rests on a third party's willingness to deal, and no amount of provenance quality substitutes. This deserves to be an explicit risk on the owner's desk, not an assumption buried in an integration spec. |
| DT-03 | Whitelisting prevents claims, so claim release (11.06.03) is a rare edge case | The optimistic read, and the one the sweep implicitly took by omitting claim release entirely | ❌ REJECTED — whitelisting only binds parties **inside** the arrangement. Claims routinely arrive from outside it: a co-owner's other distributor with a stale catalogue, a PRO's automated sweep, an aggregator who never got the memo, a false fingerprint match, or the creator's own pre-publication race (edge case above). "I have a licence and got claimed anyway" is the **#1 complaint in this entire market** — Epidemic Sound and Artlist both run whole support functions for it. Treating release as an edge case is how you ship the product with its most common failure unhandled. Forced 11.06.03 into existence (index D-03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The write is confirmed, never fire-and-forget | Two sources of truth in two companies; drift is silent and lands on the creator |
| D-02 | A failed whitelist blocks or loudly flags the purchase — never silence | The creator would otherwise hold a licence and a live claim risk with no idea |
| D-03 | Channel ownership is proven via OAuth, not asserted | An unverified connection whitelists someone else's channel |
| D-04 | Reconciliation is a permanent background obligation | Per `#CX-01` synthesis Q1 — external state drifts and the platform does not control it |
