# Feature: Grand Rights & Dramatic Performance

> **Number**: 11.11
> **Parent**: [Music Licensing](./music-licensing-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Licensing music for **dramatic** use — a musical, a ballet, an opera, a staged narrative work where songs
carry a plot. Grand rights sit deliberately outside every PRO blanket licence: ASCAP, BMI and PRS explicitly
do not cover them, so every dramatic use is a bespoke negotiation with the composition's owners. It is the
oldest licensing category in music and the least automatable, and it is the one place in this domain where an
Operator holds Full access.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | The writer. Receives a request to use their song in a staged production; negotiates bespoke terms. Rare, high-value, and entirely unlike anything else they do on the platform. |
| Producer | None | **No standing.** Grand rights are composition rights. A master share confers nothing — a staged production performs the song live and does not use the recording. |
| Operator | Full | **The Operator's only Full access in all of domain 11.** A theatre or venue staging a musical is the licensee. Per personas.md they "sell time and space" — here they are buying the right to stage a narrative work in that space. Thin, real, and the only place the supply-side persona is a music licensee. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. They buy a ticket (domain 19); the licence is invisible to them. |

## Behavior

Every grand rights deal is bespoke: scope covers the production, the number of performances, the venue or
tour, the territory, and often approval over the treatment itself. There is no rate card, no programmatic path,
no standard term — the writer of a song being staged frequently wants **approval over how it is used**, which
is a creative veto, not a price.

The platform's honest contribution here is small: it can identify the composition's owners, route a request,
and hold the instrument. It cannot price it, standardise it, or speed it up meaningfully — the negotiation is
the product and the negotiation is bespoke by nature.

### Happy Path

1. A theatre/producer requests dramatic rights for a composition, describing the production
2. System resolves the **composition's** owners; the master side is irrelevant and is not solicited
3. Bespoke negotiation (11.03.02) — no cards, no auto-approve
4. Writer may condition on treatment approval, not only on fee
5. Instrument issues (11.08.02) with production-scoped terms
6. Royalties flow per performance or per the agreed structure (domain 10)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue assumes their PRO blanket licence covers it | **It explicitly does not.** The most common and expensive misunderstanding in this category. | "Your PRS/ASCAP licence doesn't cover dramatic use" — worth saying loudly to an Operator |
| Writer wants approval over the treatment | Legitimate and standard. It is a creative veto, not a price term. | The scope grammar (11.08.01) has no axis for "I approve the staging" — see Q-02 |
| Production changes after licensing | A new treatment may exceed what was approved → amendment (11.08.04) | The approval-based structure makes this fuzzier than a media-axis delta |
| A jukebox musical uses 30 songs | 30 bespoke negotiations, any one of which kills the production | The real reason these take years |
| Auto-approve policy set on the work | **Must not apply.** No writer means "auto-approve a musical" when they tick "auto-approve film". | Grand rights must be excluded from any auto-approve default, or the policy silently grants something enormous |
| Where does a concert with staging sit? | The boundary between a dramatic performance and a theatrical concert is genuinely contested | `[PENDING]` — not something the platform should adjudicate |
| Amateur/school production | Different rate conventions, often licensed through specialist agents (MTI, Concord) | Out of scope |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Grand rights are exotic and should be cut entirely | The scope instinct, and the MoSCoW proposal here is `won't` | ❌ REJECTED **as a deletion, confirmed as a deferral** — and the distinction matters. The node must exist because of what its absence *does*: without it, a writer's auto-approve policy (11.04.01) has no category to exclude, and "auto-approve everything under £500" could silently grant dramatic rights to a musical. That is a catastrophic mis-grant produced by *omitting* a feature. **A `won't` node still has to exist in the grammar so that policy can refuse it.** This is a real argument for breadth coverage over minimalism (D-03) that generalises: the taxonomy must be complete even where the product is not. |
| DT-02 | Grand rights are just another media axis value in the scope grammar (11.08.01) | Modelling economy; it would make this nearly free | ❌ REJECTED — because it carries a right the grammar cannot express: **approval over the treatment**. Every other licence in this domain is "you may use this, in this scope, for this money". Grand rights are routinely "you may use this *if I like what you're doing with it*" — a subjective, ongoing creative veto with no axis, no value, and no machine-checkable meaning. Adding it as a media value would let the system quote and auto-approve something whose defining term it cannot represent. **It is the one licence type in the domain that is not reducible to scope.** |
| DT-03 | The Operator's presence here is trivial — one node, thin usage, safely ignored | Reasonable given the MoSCoW proposal | ✅ CONFIRMED as *thin*, ❌ REJECTED as *ignorable* — and it produced a real finding. The finding is not that Operator matters here; it is that **the persona doc's coverage claim survived contact with the domain**, which is a small but genuine validation of D-19. The other half — a venue's PRO blanket licence for ordinary public performance — is real Operator work and is **not this domain's** (it is domain 10 collections / domain 16 compliance). Recorded as a cross-domain touchpoint so it is not lost. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The node exists even though the product is `won't` | Per DT-01 — without the category, auto-approve policy has nothing to exclude and can silently grant dramatic rights |
| D-02 | Grand rights are never auto-approvable | No writer ticking "auto-approve under £500" means "you may stage a musical" |
| D-03 | Not modelled as a scope-grammar media value | Per DT-02 — treatment approval is not expressible as scope, and pretending otherwise makes it quotable |
| D-04 | Master-side owners are not solicited | Composition rights only; a staged production performs the song, it does not use the recording |
