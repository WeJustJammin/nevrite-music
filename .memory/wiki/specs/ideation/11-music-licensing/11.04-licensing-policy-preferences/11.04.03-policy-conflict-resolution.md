# Feature: Policy Conflict Resolution Across Co-Owners

> **Number**: 11.04.03
> **Parent**: [11.04 Licensing Policy & Preferences](./11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Computes the one answer a work gives when its four owners gave four different ones. It folds every
individual share policy and every veto into an **effective work policy** — a document nobody authored, that
nobody can edit, and that governs what the work can actually be licensed for. It also has the unglamorous
job of telling an owner that their policy lost, and to whom.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the work's effective policy alongside their own, and specifically **where the two differ and why** — "your auto-approve for ads is inert; [name] blocked the category". Cannot edit the aggregate; can only change their own input to it. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same. The heaviest consumer: per personas.md they carry the buyer's deadline, and the effective policy is what tells them which works can move this week. Reading four people's policies by hand is the fragmentation the platform exists to end. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible — **and silently governed.** A creator buying a micro-licence (11.06.01) experiences this feature's output as "unavailable" with no explanation. The population most affected has the least visibility, which is correct and worth naming. |

## Behavior

The fold is a lattice operation over all share policies, and it is **asymmetric by construction**:

- **Refusals combine absolutely** — any veto (11.04.02) closes the scope. Not weighted, not voted.
- **Permissions combine restrictively** — a scope auto-approves only if *every* party's policy covers it.
- **Thresholds do not combine at all** — each owner's auto-approve ceiling is tested against **that owner's own
  share of the deal value** (11.04.01 D-17, D-18), so the fold emits an **attributed per-owner threshold set**
  and never a single work-level figure (D-06, D-07). Auto-approve still requires *every* owner to pass their
  own test — the conjunction is unchanged — and the first owner who does not is **named** as the binding owner
  (D-04). The trailing-window aggregate that qualifies each ceiling is denominated the same way, per owner and
  in that owner's own share values (11.04.01 D-06, D-19).
- **Silence is not permission** — a party with no policy falls through to a human; they do not default to yes.

Consequence: a work's licensability is governed by its **most restrictive owner**, and the aggregate is
order-independent — the fold must not depend on who wrote their policy first (`#CX-02` synthesis Q5).

The aggregate is **derived and never editable**. An editable aggregate is an override, and an override lets a
majority strip a co-owner's veto — the one thing music co-ownership does not permit.

> **Correction (D-06, ratified DQ-09.A).** This file previously stated that thresholds "take the minimum — two
> owners with £500 and £2,000 ceilings yield £500". That sentence, its edge-case row below, and
> `11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-cx.md#CX-02` synthesis Q5 were **wrong, not merely imprecise**, and are
> superseded rather than refined. The two ceilings are denominated on two different quantities — each owner's
> own share — so `min(£500, £2,000)` names a work-level ceiling only if both ceilings were denominated on the
> whole deal, which is the very thing that was in dispute. A work-level minimum would also make every owner's
> policy constrain every *other* owner's share, erasing the policy-vs-veto line 11.04.02 D-02/DT-02 draws: a
> policy governs its author's share, a veto constrains everyone's.

### Happy Path

1. Any owner writes or changes a share policy, or sets a veto
2. Fold recomputes the effective work policy for every scope
3. Clearance recomputes (11.02.01); catalogue availability updates
4. Owners whose own policy just became inert are told, and by whom
5. Owners see the effective policy next to their own, with the differences explained in outcomes
6. Buyers see availability; the aggregate itself is not exposed to them

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Three auto-approve, one has no policy at all | **Not auto-approved.** Silence is not permission — falls through to a human for the silent party. | "Auto-approve blocked: [name] hasn't set a policy" — actionable, they can be asked |
| Two owners set £500 and £2,000 ceilings | **No work-level ceiling exists** (D-06, superseding the "minimum wins: £500" this row previously stated). Each ceiling is tested against its own owner's share of the deal value, so which owner binds depends on the deal value and the split and cannot be answered before a request exists. On a £3,000 deal across a 60/40 split, £1,800 is tested against the first owner's ceiling and £1,200 against the second's — the £500 owner fails, the £2,000 owner passes, and the request falls through. | Each sees the **attributed threshold set** — their own ceiling and every co-owner's, with whose it is. Per request, both see pass-or-fall-through with the binding owner named (D-07) |
| Concurrent policy edits by different owners | Fold is order-independent for refusals (absolute) **and** for thresholds — each threshold stays attributed to its author and is never combined, so there is no aggregated figure whose write order could matter (D-06, D-07) | Nothing — this must be invisible |
| An owner asks "what can this work auto-clear?" with no request in hand | They get **a rule, not a figure**: the attributed threshold set, plus the statement that an answer requires a deal value. **Accepted comprehension cost** of D-07 — the alternative is a synthetic number that is either arithmetically meaningless (a literal minimum across different denominations) or a derived quantity nobody set. | "Every owner's ceiling, and whose. Give us a deal value and we'll tell you whether it clears and who stops it." |
| Owner's policy is entirely inert (every scope blocked by someone else) | They should know. Otherwise they believe their catalogue earns and it has been dead a year. | "None of your policy on this work is active" — the silent-inert failure `#CX-04` exists to prevent |
| Aggregate recompute fails | **Fail closed** — treat as not-auto-approvable; fall through to humans | Degrades to slow, never to permissive. Consistent with 11.04.01 D-04 and 11.02.01 D-02. |
| Work under a hold when the aggregate changes | Hold's buyer must be notified — availability changed under them | The recurring fail-open leak; see Q-03 |
| A share is sold or assigned mid-flight | The new owner's policy replaces the old one's in the fold; the aggregate can change with no policy having been edited | "This work's policy changed because [share] changed hands" |
| Owners disagree in *kind*, not degree (one bands by territory, one by media) | Folds fine — the key space is the shared scope grammar (11.08.01), so both project onto it | This is why a shared grammar is load-bearing |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fold running | Must be fast — it is in the buyer's request path |
| Empty | Single-owner work | Aggregate = that owner's policy. This feature is invisible, which is right. |
| Populated | Multi-owner work | Effective policy per scope, with attribution for each constraint. **Thresholds render as an attributed per-owner set — every owner's ceiling and whose it is — never as one effective work-level figure** (D-06, D-07; this state previously promised "the effective ceiling and whose it is"). A ceiling question is answered **per request**: supply a deal value and the fold returns pass or fall-through, naming the binding owner |
| Error | Fold failed | Fail closed — nothing auto-approves |
| Partial | Some owners have policies, some do not | Effective policy computed with silence-as-fall-through; the un-policied owners are **named**, because they are the actionable blocker |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This is a lookup, not a feature — fold the policies, return the answer, no product surface needed | Scope instinct: it looks like plumbing inside 11.04.01 | ❌ REJECTED — the fold has a **user-facing output nobody else produces**: telling an owner their policy is inert and naming who made it so. Without that surface, three of four co-writers believe their catalogue is earning while it has been closed for a year by a block they never saw. That is a silent failure with a money consequence, and it needs a screen, an explanation and a notification. The aggregate is a document with a readership, not a return value. |
| DT-02 | The most restrictive owner should not be able to govern a work they own 5% of — there should be a tiebreak, a vote, or a buyout trigger | The commercial objection, arriving from a different angle than 11.04.02 DT-01 | ❌ REJECTED, and it is worth rejecting twice because it will be raised twice. Any tiebreak **is** an override with better manners. The fold's asymmetry is not a policy choice the platform gets to tune — it is a restatement of how music co-ownership works. The platform's contribution is making the constraint **visible early** (before a pitch is built on a work that cannot take it) rather than at deal time. Visibility is the product; overriding is not on the table. **Scope note (D-06)**: this rejection governs *refusal and governance* — who may block, and with what weight. It does **not** govern thresholds. Under D-06 each owner's ceiling is tested against their own share, so threshold force does scale with share size. That is an accepted consequence of DQ-09.A, not a contradiction of this row: a 5% owner's veto is still absolute, and their ceiling still only ever governs their own 5%. |
| DT-03 | Silence should default to permissive — otherwise one unengaged co-writer makes every work non-auto-approvable, and most co-writers are unengaged | Practical necessity; and it is the same shape as 11.02.02 Q-01 (does silence count?) and 11.02.04 D-03 | ❌ REJECTED here, and flagged as needing a **domain-wide** answer. Silence-as-permission means a person who never opened the app licensed their work to an oil company. The three places this question arises (completeness attestation, consent routing, policy folding) **must be answered consistently or the domain is incoherent** — a platform where silence means "yes" for policy and "no" for consent is not expressing a principle, it is expressing whichever answer was convenient. Escalated to the domain index as a single cross-cutting decision. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The aggregate is derived and never editable | An editable aggregate is an override, and an override strips co-owner veto — which music co-ownership does not permit |
| D-02 | Refusals absolute, permissions conjunctive, ~~thresholds minimum~~, silence non-permissive — **the thresholds-minimum clause is superseded by D-06**; the rest stands unchanged | The fold's asymmetry restates how music rights work; it is not a tunable. The conjunctive-permissions half is untouched by D-06: every owner must still pass |
| D-03 | The fold is order-independent | Whoever wrote their policy first must not determine the outcome |
| D-04 | Owners are told when their own policy is inert, and by whom | Per DT-01 — the silent-inert failure is this feature's reason to have a surface at all |
| D-05 | Fold failure fails closed | Consistent with 11.02.01 D-02 and 11.04.01 D-04 |
| D-06 | **Thresholds are per-owner and share-denominated; there is no work-level ceiling and no minimum across owners.** Each owner's ceiling is tested against that owner's own share of the deal value (11.04.01 D-17, D-18); auto-approve still requires **every** owner to pass, per D-02's conjunctive permissions | Ratified by user 2026-07-22 (DQ-09.A). **Supersedes the "thresholds minimum" clause of D-02, this file's £500/£2,000 edge-case row, and `11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-cx.md#CX-02` synthesis Q5** — all three were wrong rather than imprecise. The per-share base is stated four times in the [DEEP] file that owns the policy object (11.04.01 Behavior ×2, Happy Path 7, D-17), is the literal product promise in its Role Lens ("up to £500 **of my share**"), and carries a dependent rule (unresolved splits fall through). `min(£500, £2,000)` names a work-level ceiling only if both ceilings were denominated on the deal — the point in dispute. **Accepted**: this is the strictly *more permissive* reading — everything a minimum auto-approved, this auto-approves too, plus more — and threshold force becomes proportional to share size, the instinct DT-02 rejects for *governance* in this same file |
| D-07 | **The fold emits an attributed per-owner threshold set plus a per-request pass/fall-through answer naming the binding owner. It never emits a synthetic work-level ceiling** | Ratified by user 2026-07-22 (DQ-09.B). A literal minimum is foreclosed by D-06 (it compares quantities in different denominations and is materially misleading). A divisor-derived work-level equivalent is arithmetically correct but appears in no source file and would attribute a *derived number* to an owner where D-04 attributes a **constraint**. Per-owner thresholds are already the fold's inputs, so this invents nothing. It also survives 11.04.01 D-06's trailing 90-day window without contortion: no standing figure has to stay true across buyers and media |
| D-08 | **Per-owner threshold data is exposed to the co-owner view only.** It is a display surface, not a clearance input — 11.02.01 consumes this fold's verdict and the per-request binding-owner answer, never the individual share policies or their ceilings | Stated explicitly because D-07's attribution requires the fold to return per-owner thresholds to owners, and an unqualified "the fold exposes individual thresholds" would read as licence for the gate to consume individual policies — which would fork the fold and produce two definitions of what a work permits (11.02.01 Cross-Cut Notes) |
