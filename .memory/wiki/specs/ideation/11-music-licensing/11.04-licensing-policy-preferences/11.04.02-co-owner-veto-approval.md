# Feature: Co-Owner Veto & Approval Requirements

> **Number**: 11.04.02
> **Parent**: [11.04 Licensing Policy & Preferences](./11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The right of a rights holder to refuse a use at any price, and the machinery that makes that refusal stick —
category blocks (never tobacco, never political, never AI), buyer blocks (never this company), and per-request
refusals that survive every other owner's enthusiasm. It is the part of the domain that is not about commerce
at all: it is the place where a musician gets to say no to something that offends them and have the system
agree.

It is also the feature most likely to promise more than it can deliver. A veto is absolute **within the
discretionary licensing the platform controls** — and that boundary has three real edges the platform did not
choose and cannot move: a vetoer only reaches the rights side they hold standing in (D-07), the veto belongs to
whoever holds the approval right rather than whoever holds the share (D-08), and no one can refuse a statutory
compulsory licence (D-17). Stating those edges plainly is this feature's central honesty obligation. A control
that quietly does less than its label is worse than no control, because the person who discovers the gap
discovers it at the moment it has already cost them.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets category blocks against the shares they hold ("never political ads"); blocks named buyers; refuses individual requests with an optional reason; converts a one-off refusal into a standing block in one tap. Sees which of their blocks are actually biting (`N requests turned away`) and — critically — **which of their blocks are decorative** because they hold no standing on the rights side that would be licensed (D-07). **Often the party with the smallest share and the strongest feelings — whose refusal this exists to protect.** |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side. Also the party who most needs to *discover* other people's blocks early: per personas.md the buyer's deadline lands on them, and finding a veto at deal time is the failure this feature must prevent. Their master-side block does **not** stop a composition-only re-record (D-07, DT-06) — the industry's standard workaround for a refused master, and the one they will assume they are protected against. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. Sees nothing, sets nothing. The one Operator licensing surface in the domain (11.08.03 verification) reads issued licences, which no veto can touch (D-04). |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and **silently governed**. A creator buying a micro-licence (11.06.01) sees only "unavailable for this use" — never the category, never the refuser, per D-06. The population most affected by this feature has no representation in it, which is correct and worth naming. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

A veto is a **constraint over everyone's shares authored by one party**, which is what distinguishes it from a
policy (11.04.01, which governs only its author's share). No majority overrules it; share size is irrelevant.
A 1% writer and a 90% writer have identical veto power. This will surprise every user who thinks in percentages
and it is correct — co-ownership in music is unanimous by construction.

### Who holds a veto

Not the share holder — **the party in the consent graph** for that rights side (11.02.01 D-07, D-08 below).
Where a share is administered, the approval right typically sits with the administrator: a writer signed to a
publisher may have assigned their sync-approval right in a deal they signed years ago. The platform must not
render a veto toggle to a person who does not hold the veto. That failure is precisely the harm personas.md
names as the Musician's worst accidental outcome — *"signs a split sheet they don't understand"* — served back
to them as a false sense of control.

### What a veto reaches

Its author's **standing**, not the whole work (D-07). Rights sides are derived from the requested scope, never
assumed (11.02.01 D-06). A drummer holding only a master share blocks the master; they do not block a
composition-only licence, because they hold nothing in the composition. This is not a platform limitation — it
is the shape of the right — and it is the mechanism behind the sound-alike re-record (DT-06).

### The three shapes, and their two key spaces

| Shape | Key space | Evaluated | Reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Category** (never gambling) | The scope grammar (11.08.01) — media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage, scale | **At authoring time.** Catalogue availability changes immediately, across every work where the author holds standing | All shares, on the author's rights sides |
| **Buyer** (never this company) | **Party identity** (domain 01) — *not* an axis of the scope grammar | **At request time only.** A buyer must exist before a buyer block can fire; the catalogue cannot show a work as unavailable to a party who has not asked | Same |
| **Per-request** (no, not this one) | The specific request | At the moment of decline (11.02.04) | That request; convertible to a standing block in one tap |

The two key spaces are the feature's most consequential structural fact (D-09). 11.04.01 states the policy key
space *is* the scope grammar — true for categories, false for buyers. The grammar has no counterparty axis, so
a buyer block cannot fold into an effective work policy the way a category block does (11.04.03's fold operates
over the grammar). A category block is a property of the catalogue; a buyer block is a property of the
transaction.

### Defaults

The block table's default is **empty — nothing refused**. Permissive, deliberately: a platform that ships with
refusals nobody authored is asserting stances on its users' behalf.

**One category inverts this.** AI training defaults to **refused** (11.07 index D-03: opt-in, never opt-out).
11.07.01 states plainly that AI training "is not a special rights regime — it is a category block with a louder
name" — so it runs on this feature's mechanism while carrying the opposite default (D-12). The mechanism is
shared; the default is not. An implementer who reads "permissive default" and applies it uniformly ships the
single most damaging bug available in this domain.

### Timing and in-flight work

A veto set mid-flight **withdraws in-flight quotes and consent rounds** for the blocked scope — it does not
merely apply to future requests, or an in-flight deal completes against a refusal that already exists
(`#CX-01` synthesis Q2). It never touches an issued licence (D-04).

There is **no window in which a blocked scope can pass the gate** (D-15). Authoring is atomic; evaluation is at
request time against the aggregate as it stands then (11.04.01 D-03); the gate re-checks fresh at issuance
(11.02.01 D-04). Any catalogue or search view is an advisory snapshot labelled "as of" (11.02.01 D-16) and may
lag the block. The lag is a display property. It is never a licensing property.

### Time-limited blocks

A block may carry an expiry ("no ads until the album is out" — a holdback, and ordinary industry practice). The
scheduler is not new work: **Scheduled Jobs / Time-Triggered State** is already a domain cross-cut carrying hold
expiry and licence expiry.

An expiring block **lapses to fall-through, never to permission** (D-11). The passage of time is not a yes. An
owner who sets "no ads until March", forgets, and watches the album slip to September must not discover their
work licensing to ads in March because a date passed and nobody acted. On lapse the scope falls through to a
human — the same destination as having no policy at all (11.04.01) — and the owner is notified 7 days before
and on the day.

### Refusing, re-asking, and being worn down

A decline is not a permanent block unless the owner makes it one. But an unbounded re-ask is a harassment
vector: a buyer re-submits weekly until the owner taps approve by accident — and a raised offer is *also*
legitimate, which is the whole of 11.03. The line (D-14):

1. A decline suppresses automatic re-ask of the same **(work, scope, buyer)** triple.
2. The buyer may submit **exactly one** improved counter on that triple, routed as a new request.
3. A second decline **hard-blocks that buyer on that work for 12 months** — no third ask, no exceptions.
4. "Never ask me about this again" makes it permanent immediately and converts to a standing buyer block.

One counter is negotiation. Two is attrition. Numbers pending owner ratification (Q-04).

### Attribution and reason

The block is **attributed** to its author for co-owners; the **reason is optional and separately disclosable at
the author's election** (D-13). Attribution is what lets a co-owner negotiate with a person rather than fight a
system. The reason is the author's to volunteer — a refusal is not a thing anyone owes an explanation for, and
a mandatory reason field on a moral refusal is a small coercion.

### Happy Path

1. Owner opens the work (or their owner-level defaults) and sets a category block — or refuses a live request
   and taps "never ask me about this again"
2. System confirms **reach before committing**: "This blocks the master. You hold no composition share in this
   work — a re-recorded version can still be licensed for this use." (D-07 — stated at authoring time, not
   discovered at breach time)
3. Owner optionally adds a reason and chooses whether to share it with co-owners (D-13)
4. Block is authored atomically; aggregate recomputes (11.04.03); clearance recomputes (11.02.01)
5. Work becomes unavailable for that scope — catalogue-wide across every work where the author holds standing,
   for a category block; at next request, for a buyer block (D-09)
6. In-flight quotes and consent rounds for the blocked scope are withdrawn; any active hold's buyer is notified
   (11.04.03 Q-03 — the domain's recurring availability-changed-under-you pattern)
7. Co-owners are told their earning capacity for that scope is gone and **by whom** — so they can negotiate with
   a person rather than fight a system (D-06, 11.04.03 D-04)
8. Buyers see the work as unavailable for that use, and learn neither the category nor the refuser
   (D-06, per 11.02.01 D-11)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Three co-writers auto-approve, one blocks tobacco | Work does not license to tobacco. Not at a higher price, not with three votes, not ever. | Co-owners: "[Name] blocked tobacco — this scope is closed". Buyer: "Unavailable for this use" — no category, no name (D-06) |
| The blocking owner holds 1% | Identical veto power to a 90% owner | The percentage is irrelevant and the UI must not imply otherwise — no share figure anywhere near the block control |
| A co-owner blocks everything, out of spite or inertia | The work is commercially dead and no mechanism overrules it. **By design** — see `#R-01` in the sub-domain CX. | Co-owners' recourse is buyout or negotiation (11.02.04 Q-01), never override |
| **Master-side owner blocks; buyer re-records the song instead** | **The block does not fire.** A composition-only licence needs no master-side standing (D-07, 11.02.01 D-06). The sound-alike re-record is the industry's standard answer to a refused master and the platform cannot close it. | Stated **at authoring time** (happy path 2), never discovered afterward: "You hold no composition share — a re-recording can still be licensed" |
| **Writer blocks; their share is administered by a publisher** | **The writer does not hold the veto.** The approval right sits with the administrator in the consent graph (11.02.01 D-07, D-08). | No veto control is rendered. "Your approval right for this share is administered by [Publisher]." Honest, and it is the whole reason D-08 exists — see Q-02 for reserved rights |
| **Political campaign releases a cover of the blocked song** | **The block does not fire.** A statutory compulsory mechanical cannot be refused by anyone (11.09 — "is covered without being asked"). | The block's copy must never claim absoluteness: "This blocks licences we issue. It cannot stop a compulsory cover — nobody can" (D-17) |
| Veto set while a licence is executing | The gate's fresh re-check at issuance (11.02.01 D-04) is the serialization point. Per 11.02.01 D-13 a verdict change inside the checkout window **aborts the whole transaction** — no charge, no licence. | Buyer: "No longer available" — painful, correct. No money moved |
| Veto set microseconds after commit | **Licence stands** (D-04). Per 11.02.01 D-13 the vetoer is told a licence issued before their block took effect — this notification exists specifically to stop a dispute manufactured by entirely correct behaviour. | Vetoer: "A licence for this use issued at 14:02, before your block at 14:03. It stands." Said immediately, not discovered later |
| Veto set after a licence issued | **Licence stands.** Irrevocable instrument (domain D-06). The block applies to future licences only. | "This doesn't affect the licence already issued" — stated plainly, because they will assume otherwise |
| **Owner blocks a buyer who is a subsidiary of an allowed buyer** | **The block does not resolve corporate structure.** The platform matches the **declared transacting party** and the **declared end client** (D-10). It holds no corporate-ownership graph, and inferring one from a name is worse than an honest gap — a wrong inference either blocks a stranger or waves through the blocked party under a confident label. | At authoring: "This blocks [Company] as a named party. It does not automatically cover subsidiaries, parents, or agencies acting for them. [Add related companies]" |
| **Ad agency licenses on behalf of the blocked brand** | **The real evasion, and it needs no subsidiary.** The counterparty is the agency; the brand never appears. D-10's end-client declaration is the only thing standing between a tobacco block and a tobacco ad: advertising scopes must declare the advertised brand, not merely the buyer. | Buyer-side: end client is a required field for ad-category scopes. Misdeclaration is a licence breach — remedy is liability, not revocation (domain D-06, 11.08.04 DT-03); see Q-06 |
| Buyer learns who blocked them and applies pressure off-platform | The reason D-06 exists. The refuser is often the least powerful party; the buyer has money and lawyers. | Buyer never learns the name or the category. **Insufficient on its own** — see DT-08, the pattern leaks by correlation |
| **Buyer probes the catalogue to infer the block** | Binary-searching scopes reveals *that* a work blocks tobacco without anyone being told. Across a catalogue it reveals *who*: if every work Sarah co-wrote is tobacco-blocked and no work she didn't is, Sarah is identified. **D-06 protects the name, not the pattern** (DT-08). | Enumeration defence is an NFR already routed to `/create-prd-security` at domain level; this feature contributes a sharper reason than commercial leakage — see Q-03 |
| Same human holds several shares in the work (multi-hyphenate) | **One block, authored once**, applying to every share they hold — reach per rights side still bounded by D-07. Not several vetoes from one person. Notification consolidates to one message naming all their stakes (11.02.04 D-04, which governs *routing*, not reach). | One control, one confirmation, one notification |
| Multi-hyphenate is a co-owner **and** works for the blocked buyer | No special case. Their block binds as any owner's; their employment is not the platform's business and creates no conflict the platform can adjudicate. | Nothing. Deliberately |
| **Blocking owner dies** | Block **survives**. "Death terminates authority, never the record" (01.10). The estate gains scoped rights-decision access (01.10) and *can* lift it — but only as an **attributed, explicit act** (D-16). It never lapses silently. | Estate: "This block was set by [Name] on [date]. Lifting it is recorded as your decision, not theirs." Co-owners see the block's origin unchanged. Jurisdictional moral-rights half → Q-01 |
| **Blocking owner erases their account (GDPR)** | Share survives pseudonymously; identity does not (11.02.01 D-17). Scopes needing that party's fresh consent are permanently `consent-needed` — **so the scope is unclearable whether or not the block survives**. Block retention changes the *reason shown*, never the outcome. | Co-owners: "Blocked by a former owner" — attribution decays to a role. Whether a pre-erasure standing refusal may legally be retained is 11.02.01 Q-03's question, not an agent's call |
| Share sold or assigned while a block is live | The block goes with the author, not the share. The seller's standing ends; their block ends with it. The buyer of the share brings their own (or none). | "This work's blocks changed because [share] changed hands" (mirrors 11.04.03's assignment row) |
| Owner-level category block fans out across a 200-work catalogue | Authoring is atomic; the fan-out is a recompute of a derived view. **No licence can leak mid-fan-out** because evaluation is at request time and the gate re-checks fresh (D-15). | "Applying to 187 works" — progress on a *display*, never a gate. The block is already in force |
| Two co-owners set contradictory blocks concurrently | Refusals combine absolutely and the fold is order-independent (11.04.03 D-02, D-03). Two blocks are simply two blocks; there is nothing to reconcile. | Nothing — this must be invisible |
| Owner lifts their own block while a co-owner's identical block stands | Scope stays closed. Lifting removes one constraint, not the aggregate. | "Still blocked — [Name] also blocks this category" — otherwise they believe they reopened the scope and they did not |
| Owner sets a block on a scope nobody has ever requested | Fine and common. Most blocks never fire. | "0 requests turned away" is not a failure state and must not read as one |
| **Owner's every block is decorative** (no standing on any requested side) | They should know. Otherwise they believe they are protected and are not — the silent-inert failure `#CX-04` exists to prevent, in its most damaging form: not lost income, but a violated conscience. | "None of your blocks on this work can fire — you hold no composition share and every request is composition-only" |
| Recompute fails while a block is set | **Treat as blocked.** Fail closed (D-05, domain D-09). | Degrades to unavailable, never to permitted |
| Block set against a category the scope grammar cannot express | Refused at authoring. The grammar is the key space (11.08.01 D-01); a block outside it cannot be evaluated and would be theatre. | "We can't express that yet" — honest, and it is a grammar gap to route (11.08.01 D-02: axes are a product decision) |
| Abusive free-text reason ("because Sarah is a thief") | The reason is disclosed to co-owners (D-13) and is user-generated content on a surface with a named target. Moderation is domain 24's. | Reportable like any UGC. The block itself is never moderated — only the prose attached to it |
| Party with a **contested** ownership claim sets a block | Their block holds while contested (fail-closed, domain D-09). **This is not a new attack surface**: a disputed share already yields verdict `unknown` from domain 09, which fails closed and makes the work unclearable regardless of any block (DT-11). | Dispute routes to 24 at its own priority. The veto adds nothing an attacker did not already have |
| Buyer block set against a party who is also a co-owner | Blocks them as a *buyer*; does not touch their rights as an owner. The two capacities are unrelated. | Nothing surprising — but the confirmation names both capacities so the author knows what they did and did not do |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aggregate recompute after authoring | Brief. **Not in the buyer's request path** — authoring is; evaluation is separate and is where latency matters (11.04.01) |
| Empty | No blocks set | "Nothing refused" — the permissive default. **Except AI training, which reads "Refused" with nothing set** (D-12, 11.07 D-03). The one category whose empty state is a decision |
| Populated | Blocks set | What is blocked, by whom, **what it has actually turned away** (`N requests`), and — per D-07 — which blocks **cannot fire** for want of standing |
| Error | Recompute failed | **Treat as blocked.** Fail closed — a veto that fails open is the worst outcome available in this sub-domain (D-05) |
| Partial | Block set, in-flight rounds withdrawing | "Withdrawing 2 live requests" — visible, not silent. The block is **already in force** during this state; the withdrawal is cleanup, not a precondition (D-15) |
| Partial (reach) | Block set, author holds standing on one rights side only | "Blocking the master. Not the composition — you hold no share there." The most important partial state in the feature: a block that is half a block, said so at authoring time |
| Lapsing | Time-limited block within 7 days of expiry | "This lifts on [date] — requests will start reaching you again." Falls to **fall-through, never permission** (D-11) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Veto power should be weighted by share — a 1% writer blocking a deal for a 90% writer is unjust | Strong commercial intuition, and the objection every user will raise | ❌ REJECTED — it is how music rights actually work, and the injustice runs the other way. **Weighted veto would let a majority license a work over a co-writer's objection**, which is precisely the Producer anti-persona's "pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" given a legitimate mechanism and a fair-sounding name. The platform serves the multi-hyphenate who holds *small* shares in *many* works (personas.md) — weighting veto would systematically disenfranchise its own core persona. The commercial problem is real; the answer is buyout or negotiation (11.02.04 Q-01), never override. |
| DT-02 | A veto is just a policy with the threshold set to zero | Modelling economy; it would collapse this feature into 11.04.01 | ❌ REJECTED — the scopes differ, and that is the whole thing. A policy governs **its author's share**; a veto constrains **everyone's**. Modelling a veto as a zero-threshold policy means it only blocks its author's share, and the other 75% licenses happily — which is not a veto, it is an abstention. The distinction is the difference between "I won't sell you my part" and "this cannot be sold". |
| DT-03 | The refuser needs protection from the parties they refused | Reasoning about who actually holds power at this intersection: the refuser is often the smallest shareholder, and the parties they just cost money are their collaborators, their band, the producer who books them | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it makes disclosure a safety question rather than a transparency question. Disclosing the refuser's identity to co-owners is necessary (they must be able to negotiate). Disclosing it to the **buyer** invites off-platform pressure on the person least able to resist it. Exercising a veto should not cost you your working relationships. The platform cannot fix the social dynamic, but it can decline to broadcast the target. **Independently confirmed by 11.02.01 D-11**, reached from the blocker-naming direction — two features arriving at audience-scoping separately is the strongest evidence available that it is right. Resolved as D-06. |
| DT-04 | A veto blocks the work outright, on every rights side | The feature's own framing in the breadth pass ("constrains everyone's shares"), and what every user will assume | ❌ REJECTED — **reach is bounded by standing**. 11.02.01 D-06 already establishes that the verdict decomposes per required rights side and that sides derive from the scope. A master-side owner has nothing to say about a composition-only licence; asserting otherwise would let a session drummer veto a cover, which is not a right anyone holds. D-02 survives but only *within* the author's sides — qualified by D-07. This is the difference between a veto that works and a veto that lies, and the breadth pass had it wrong. |
| DT-05 | The veto belongs to whoever holds the share | The obvious reading, and 11.04.01's framing (policy attaches to a share) | ❌ REJECTED — **it belongs to the party in the consent graph**, which is not the share graph (11.02.01 D-07: administered shares resolve to the administrator). A writer signed to a publishing deal has, in the ordinary case, assigned their sync-approval right. Rendering that writer a veto toggle is worse than rendering nothing: they will believe they blocked tobacco, and discover at the ad break that their publisher licensed it. The platform would have manufactured the exact harm it exists to prevent — a person losing control of their work to a document they didn't understand — and put its own UI's name on it. D-08. |
| DT-06 | A veto is absolute | The feature's premise, and its copy | ❌ REJECTED at the boundary, and this is the finding a music-industry expert would reach for first. Two escapes exist that the platform neither built nor can close: (1) **the sound-alike re-record** — refuse the master and the buyer licenses the composition and hires a session band; standard practice, decades old, and DT-04's consequence; (2) **the statutory compulsory mechanical** — nobody can refuse a cover, which 11.09's own Role Matrix already concedes ("is covered without being asked"). A veto is absolute over *discretionary licences the platform issues*. It is not absolute over the use of the music. The copy must say the smaller true thing rather than the larger false one (D-17). |
| DT-07 | The buyer block lives in the same key space as the category block | Both are "blocks"; 11.04.01 declares the policy key space *is* the scope grammar | ❌ REJECTED — **the scope grammar has no counterparty axis** (11.08.01: media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage, scale). A buyer block keys on party identity (domain 01), which means it cannot fold into an effective work policy the way a category block does — 11.04.03's fold operates over the grammar. The consequence is behavioural, not cosmetic: a category block changes catalogue availability at authoring time, a buyer block cannot be evaluated until a buyer exists. The breadth pass's "unavailable catalogue-wide, immediately" was true of one shape and false of the other. D-09. |
| DT-08 | Audience-scoped naming (D-06 / 11.02.01 D-11) protects the refuser | The mitigation both features landed on | ✅ CONFIRMED as necessary, ❌ **insufficient — and this is a live gap, not a footnote.** D-06 protects the *name*; it does not protect the *pattern*. A buyer who probes scopes learns the work blocks tobacco. A buyer who probes across a catalogue learns *who*: if every work Sarah co-wrote blocks tobacco and no work she didn't, Sarah is identified without the platform ever naming her. Worse, the inferred fact is not commercial — **a "never political ads" block is an expression of political opinion, and "never gambling / never alcohol" can evidence religious belief. That is GDPR Article 9 special-category data**, inferred from a public licensing surface by correlation. The domain already routes "catalogue scraping / enumeration defence" to `/create-prd-security` as an NFR leaking *commercial relationships*; this feature contributes a materially sharper reason with a statutory floor under it. Q-03. |
| DT-09 | The provenance wedge makes works easier to license | The domain rationale: split-at-creation "MANUFACTURES one-stops as a byproduct of its own core loop" | ✅ CONFIRMED as true **and** incomplete — a complete owner set is a complete *refusal* set. Every contributor the split ritual captures is a party who can now say no; a 6-person session manufactures a one-stop and six vetoes in the same act. A supervisor comparing a WeJammin work (six named people, each with an absolute veto on their side) against a hazy 1998 track (one label who says yes) may rationally pick the 1998 track. The honest rebuttal is that the 1998 track was always encumbered and nobody knew — the platform did not create the risk, it made it legible, and legible risk is what a licence is worth paying for. But "we surfaced a risk our competitor conceals" is a hard sell against a deadline, and D-07's cold-start caveat compounds it: early on, the works with complete owner sets are also the works nobody has heard of. This does not contradict D-18 — it prices it. Q-05. |
| DT-10 | A deceased owner's blocks pass to the estate's discretion like any other asset | 01.10 grants the estate scoped rights-decision access; the share is property and property is inherited | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **the block is not the same kind of thing as the share**. A tobacco block is a moral position; the estate is a commercial administrator with a financial interest in lifting it, and music history is substantially a history of estates licensing what the artist refused in life. 01.10's own invariant — "death terminates authority, never the record" — points the other way: the block is record. It survives, and lifting it is an **attributed, explicit act** recorded as the estate's decision, never a silent default and never a migration side effect (D-16). The platform does not adjudicate probate and takes no view on whether the estate *may* lift it — in some jurisdictions moral rights are perpetual and inalienable and the answer is no (Q-01). It simply refuses to let a conscience evaporate quietly at the moment its author stops being able to defend it. |
| DT-11 | A bogus ownership claim weaponises the veto — file a claim, get standing, hold a catalogue hostage | Adversarial reasoning; bogus Content ID claims are an industry plague and the analogue is exact | ❌ REJECTED **as a veto-specific concern** — the attack exists but this feature does not enable it. A disputed share already yields verdict `unknown` from domain 09, `unknown` fails closed (domain D-09), and the work is unclearable before any block is authored. An attacker who can file a claim already holds the catalogue hostage; adding a veto to their claim changes nothing about the outcome. The defence belongs where the attack lands — claim verification (09) and dispute priority (24) — and duplicating it here would build a second, weaker gate on a door already locked. Worth logging precisely because the obvious move is to build the mitigation in the wrong place. |
| DT-12 | The platform should detect coerced veto-lifting — a cooling-off period, or an anomaly flag when a block is lifted shortly after co-owners were notified | Follows directly from DT-03: if co-owners can see who blocked, they can lean on them, and a block lifted 20 minutes after the notification is a suspicious event | ❌ REJECTED — **the platform cannot distinguish coercion from persuasion**, and the two are the same signal. A co-owner explaining what the deal means for the band's rent is legitimate; a co-owner threatening to drop the drummer is not; both produce "block lifted 20 minutes after notification". A cooling-off period patronises the owner, delays legitimate reversals, and — worse — implies the platform is adjudicating a relationship it cannot see. The refuser's protection is D-06 (the buyer never learns the target) plus the fact that co-owners *must* be told to negotiate at all. Some of this is a social problem and the honest position is that the platform declines to pretend otherwise. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Veto is unweighted — share size is irrelevant | Per DT-01 — weighting would let a majority license over a co-writer's objection, disenfranchising the platform's core persona |
| D-02 | A veto is a distinct object from a policy: it constrains all shares, not its author's | Per DT-02 — a zero-threshold policy is an abstention, not a veto. **Qualified by D-07**: "all shares" means all shares *on the rights sides where its author holds standing* |
| D-03 | A veto withdraws in-flight quotes and consent rounds, not merely future requests | Otherwise an in-flight deal completes against a refusal that already exists |
| D-04 | A veto never touches an already-issued licence | Licences are irrevocable instruments (domain D-06); the alternative makes every licence worthless |
| D-05 | Recompute failure fails closed — treat as blocked | A veto that fails open is the worst outcome available in this sub-domain. Consistent with domain D-09 |
| D-06 | **The refuser is named to co-owners and never to the buyer.** Neither the refuser nor the blocked category is disclosed buyer-side | **Resolves former Q-02.** Per DT-03 and independently per 11.02.01 D-11 (audience-scoped blocker naming) — two features reaching this separately is the strongest available evidence. Co-owners must be able to negotiate with a person; a buyer with money and lawyers must not be handed a target. **Protects the name, not the pattern** — see Q-03 |
| D-07 | **A veto's reach is bounded by the rights side(s) where its author holds standing** | Per DT-04 and 11.02.01 D-06 — sides derive from the scope. A master-side owner cannot block a composition-only licence. Stated at authoring time (happy path 2), never discovered at breach time |
| D-08 | **The veto holder is the consent-graph party, not the share holder.** An administered share's veto sits with the administrator | Per DT-05 and 11.02.01 D-07. Rendering a veto control to a writer who assigned their approval right manufactures the exact harm the platform exists to prevent |
| D-09 | **Two key spaces**: category blocks key on the scope grammar and take effect at authoring time, catalogue-wide; buyer blocks key on party identity and can only be evaluated at request time | Per DT-07 — the grammar has no counterparty axis (11.08.01), so buyer blocks cannot fold (11.04.03) and cannot change catalogue availability before a buyer exists |
| D-10 | **Buyer blocks match the declared transacting party and the declared end client. No corporate-ownership inference** | **Resolves the breadth pass's subsidiary gap.** The platform holds no ownership graph; a wrong inference blocks a stranger or waves through the blocked party under a confident label. The limitation is stated at authoring time. Advertising scopes must declare the advertised brand — the agency-fronting evasion needs no subsidiary and is the real one |
| D-11 | **A time-limited block lapses to fall-through, never to permission** | **Resolves former Q-03.** Holdbacks are ordinary practice and the scheduler is an existing domain cross-cut, so the cost objection is void. But the passage of time is not a yes — an owner whose album slips must not find their work in ads because a date passed. Consistent with 11.04.03 D-02 (silence is not permission) |
| D-12 | **The block table's default is empty/permissive — except AI training, which defaults to refused** | 11.07 D-03 (opt-in, never opt-out) inverts this feature's default for exactly one category. The mechanism is shared; the default is not. Uniform application of the permissive default is the most damaging bug available in this domain |
| D-13 | **Attribution is mandatory to co-owners; the reason is optional and separately disclosable at the author's election** | Attribution enables negotiation. A mandatory reason field on a moral refusal is a small coercion — a refusal is not something anyone owes an explanation for |
| D-14 | **A decline permits exactly one improved counter on the same (work, scope, buyer) triple; a second decline hard-blocks that buyer on that work for 12 months** | One counter is negotiation (and is the whole of 11.03); two is attrition. Unbounded re-ask is a wear-down attack on the persona this feature protects. Numbers pending ratification (Q-04) |
| D-15 | **No window exists in which a blocked scope passes the gate.** Authoring is atomic; evaluation is at request time; the gate re-checks fresh at issuance. Catalogue lag is a display property, never a licensing one | Per 11.04.01 D-03 and 11.02.01 D-04/D-16. Resolves the whole class of fan-out and cache-staleness races without inventing architecture |
| D-16 | **A block survives its author's death. The estate may lift it only as an attributed, explicit act** | Per DT-10 and 01.10 ("death terminates authority, never the record"). A conscience must not evaporate silently at the moment its author stops being able to defend it. Whether the estate *may* lift it at all is jurisdictional — Q-01 |
| D-17 | **The platform never claims a veto is absolute over the use of the music — only over the licences the platform issues** | Per DT-06 — the sound-alike re-record and the statutory compulsory mechanical (11.09) are both outside any owner's refusal. Copy says the smaller true thing rather than the larger false one |
