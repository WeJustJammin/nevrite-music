# Feature: Co-Owner Veto & Approval Requirements

> **Number**: 11.04.02
> **Parent**: [11.04 Licensing Policy & Preferences](./11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The right of any co-owner to refuse a use at any price, and the machinery that makes that refusal stick —
category blocks (never tobacco, never political, never AI), buyer blocks, and per-request refusals that
survive every other owner's enthusiasm. It is the part of the domain that is not about commerce at all: it
is the place where a musician gets to say no to something that offends them and have the system agree.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets category blocks on their shares ("never political ads"); refuses individual requests with a reason; converts a one-off refusal into a standing block in one tap. Sees which of their blocks are actually biting. **Often the party with the smallest share and the strongest feelings — whose refusal this exists to protect.** |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side. Also the party who most needs to *discover* other people's blocks early: per personas.md the buyer's deadline lands on them, and finding a veto at deal time is the failure this feature must prevent. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A creator buying a micro-licence never learns why a track was unavailable — see Q-02, which is not obviously the right answer. |

## Behavior

A veto is a **constraint over everyone's shares authored by one owner**, which is what distinguishes it from a
policy (11.04.01, which governs only its author's share). Any co-owner may set one; no majority overrules it;
share size is irrelevant. A 1% writer and a 90% writer have identical veto power. This will surprise every
user who thinks in percentages and it is correct — co-ownership in music is unanimous by construction.

Vetoes come in three shapes: **category** (never gambling), **buyer** (never this company), and **per-request**
(no, not this one). All three are absolute for their scope.

A veto set mid-flight **withdraws in-flight quotes and consent rounds** for the blocked scope — it does not
merely apply to future requests, or an in-flight deal completes against a refusal that already exists
(`#CX-01` synthesis Q2).

### Happy Path

1. Owner sets a category block on their share — or refuses a live request and taps "never ask me about this again"
2. Aggregate recomputes (11.04.03); clearance recomputes (11.02.01)
3. Work becomes unavailable for that scope, catalogue-wide, immediately
4. In-flight quotes and consent rounds for the blocked scope are withdrawn
5. Co-owners are told their earning capacity for that scope is gone and by whom — so they can negotiate with a person rather than fight a system
6. Buyers see the work as unavailable for that use; whether they learn *why* or *who* is Q-02

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Three co-writers auto-approve, one blocks tobacco | Work does not license to tobacco. Not at a higher price, not with three votes, not ever. | Co-owners: "[Name] blocked tobacco — this scope is closed" |
| The blocking owner holds 1% | Identical veto power to a 90% owner | The percentage is irrelevant and the UI must not imply otherwise |
| A co-owner blocks everything, out of spite or inertia | The work is commercially dead and no mechanism overrules it. **By design** — see `#R-01` in the sub-domain CX. | Co-owners' recourse is buyout or negotiation (11.02.04 Q-01), never override |
| Veto set while a licence is executing | The gate's fresh re-check at issuance (11.02.01 D-04) is the serialization point — this is exactly what that decision is for | Buyer: "No longer available" — painful, correct |
| Veto set after a licence issued | **Licence stands.** Irrevocable instrument (11.08). The block applies to future licences only. | Owner: "This doesn't affect the licence already issued" — stated plainly, because they will assume otherwise |
| Owner blocks a buyer who is a subsidiary of an allowed buyer | Corporate-structure resolution is beyond the platform's knowledge | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Buyer learns who blocked them and applies pressure off-platform | The reason Q-02 is hard. The refuser is often the least powerful party. | See Q-02 |
| Same human holds several shares in the work (multi-hyphenate) | One block, all their shares. Not several vetoes from one person. | Consistent with 11.02.04 D-04 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aggregate recompute | Brief |
| Empty | No blocks | "Nothing refused" — the permissive default |
| Populated | Blocks set | What is blocked, by whom, and what it has actually turned away |
| Error | Recompute failed | **Treat as blocked.** Fail closed — a veto that fails open is the worst outcome in the sub-domain. |
| Partial | Block set, in-flight rounds withdrawing | "Withdrawing 2 live requests" — visible, not silent |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Veto power should be weighted by share — a 1% writer blocking a deal for a 90% writer is unjust | Strong commercial intuition, and the objection every user will raise | ❌ REJECTED — it is how music rights actually work, and the injustice runs the other way. **Weighted veto would let a majority license a work over a co-writer's objection**, which is precisely the Producer anti-persona's "pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" given a legitimate mechanism and a fair-sounding name. The platform serves the multi-hyphenate who holds *small* shares in *many* works (personas.md) — weighting veto would systematically disenfranchise its own core persona. The commercial problem is real; the answer is buyout or negotiation (11.02.04 Q-01), never override. |
| DT-02 | A veto is just a policy with the threshold set to zero | Modelling economy; it would collapse this feature into 11.04.01 | ❌ REJECTED — the scopes differ, and that is the whole thing. A policy governs **its author's share**; a veto constrains **everyone's**. Modelling a veto as a zero-threshold policy means it only blocks its author's share, and the other 75% licenses happily — which is not a veto, it is an abstention. The distinction is the difference between "I won't sell you my part" and "this cannot be sold". |
| DT-03 | The refuser needs protection from the parties they refused | Reasoning about who actually holds power at this intersection: the refuser is often the smallest shareholder, and the parties they just cost money are their collaborators, their band, the producer who books them | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it makes Q-02 a safety question rather than a transparency question. Disclosing the refuser's identity to co-owners is necessary (they must be able to negotiate). Disclosing it to the **buyer** invites off-platform pressure on the person least able to resist it. This is a real asymmetry: exercising a veto should not cost you your working relationships. The platform cannot fix the social dynamic, but it can decline to broadcast the target. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Veto is unweighted — share size is irrelevant | Per DT-01 — weighting would let a majority license over a co-writer's objection, disenfranchising the platform's core persona |
| D-02 | A veto is a distinct object from a policy: it constrains all shares, not its author's | Per DT-02 — a zero-threshold policy is an abstention, not a veto |
| D-03 | A veto withdraws in-flight quotes and consent rounds, not merely future requests | Otherwise an in-flight deal completes against a refusal that already exists |
| D-04 | A veto never touches an already-issued licence | Licences are irrevocable instruments (11.08); the alternative makes every licence worthless |
| D-05 | Recompute failure fails closed — treat as blocked | A veto that fails open is the worst outcome available in this sub-domain |
