# Feature: Per-Work Licensing Policy & Auto-Approve

> **Number**: 11.04.01
> **Parent**: [11.04 Licensing Policy & Preferences](./11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A rights holder's standing answer to requests they have not yet received: which uses are pre-approved, up to
what value, in what territories, for which media — so a buyer's request can clear without anyone being asked.
It is the mechanism that turns the domain's claim of instant clearance from a slogan into a system, because
the registry can tell you *who* to ask in milliseconds and that is worthless if the asking takes three weeks.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Sets standing policy on each share: "auto-approve podcasts and indie film under £500; ask me above that; never political." **The persona this feature most serves** — per personas.md they hold small stakes across many works and a portfolio of small income streams; they cannot field every request and will not. Sees what their policy approved while they slept and what it turned away. |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side. Also the party who most needs to *read* other people's policies, because per personas.md the buyer's deadline lands on them and they need to know which works can move today. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A fan-adjacent creator buying a micro-licence (11.06.01) is silently governed by these policies and never sees one — correct, and worth stating explicitly. |

## Behavior

A policy attaches to **a party's share of a work** — not to the work, and not to the party. Its key space is
the licence scope grammar (11.08.01): media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage category, plus a value
threshold. Policies are **defaulted at the owner level and overridable per work**, because nobody will
configure 200 works and everybody has one song they feel differently about.

Evaluation order is a correctness property, not an optimisation:

1. **Refusal** (11.04.02 veto / category block) — if it fires, nothing else runs
2. **Auto-approve threshold** — covered → approved without disturbing anyone
3. **Fall through** → 11.02.04 consent routing (a human is asked)
4. Only then → 11.03 pricing

Evaluation happens at **request time**, against the aggregate as it stands then — never at policy-authoring
time. Otherwise stale permission survives a newer refusal (`#CX-01` synthesis Q3).

### Happy Path

1. Owner sets an owner-level default policy once — a handful of decisions, not a form
2. Owner overrides on specific works where they feel differently
3. A request arrives; the effective aggregate (11.04.03) is computed for the requested scope
4. Refusals checked first; then this party's threshold
5. Covered → auto-approved, no notification interrupt, no delay
6. Not covered → falls through to consent routing with the specific reason ("above your £500 ceiling")
7. Owner sees a digest: approved while you slept (N, £X), turned away (N), fell through to you (N)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Owner auto-approves; a co-owner blocks the category | Refusal wins. This owner's auto-approve is inert for that scope. | "Your auto-approve for ads is dead — [co-owner] blocked the category" — per `#CX-04`, silence here means an owner believes they are earning and are not |
| Policy set, then a new veto appears from a co-owner | Aggregate recomputes; **in-flight quotes for the blocked scope are withdrawn**, not merely stopped for future requests | Buyer: "Withdrawn — no longer available for this use" |
| Owner sets no policy | Everything falls through to a human. Correct default, and the slow path this feature exists to remove. | "No policy — every request waits for you. [Set one]" |
| Auto-approved licence turns out to be a use the owner would have hated | **The feature's core hazard.** See DT-02 and parent Q-02. | Mitigation is category granularity, not a cure |
| Owner has 200 works and will not configure them | Owner-level default + per-work override; the default does the work | Configuring one work at a time is a failure state |
| Two owners set different auto-approve ceilings | Lowest wins (11.04.03). Deterministic and order-independent. | Each sees the effective ceiling and whose it is |
| A band entity holds a policy (domain 01 models bands as first-class) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — does a band policy bind members' individual shares? See parent Q-03. | — |
| Value threshold set in a currency; deal is in another | Threshold is currency-aware; an FX move must not silently widen a ceiling | Explicit currency on the threshold |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aggregate evaluation | Must be fast — it is in the buyer's request path |
| Empty | No policy | The pitch: "Every request waits on you" |
| Populated | Policy set | Coverage view: what auto-approves, what asks, what refuses — expressed in outcomes, not rules |
| Error | Evaluation failed | **Fall through to human.** Degrade to slow, never to auto-approved. Fail-closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02. |
| Partial | Owner default set, no per-work overrides | Normal and desirable — not an incomplete state |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The registry alone delivers instant clearance — knowing who owns what is the hard part, and WeJammin knows it | The domain's own rationale: "clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a lookup plus a pre-set price" | ❌ REJECTED — and this rejection created the whole sub-domain (D-01). The registry removes the **archaeology** (finding the parties). It does nothing about the **asking** (getting them to say yes), which is where most of the six weeks actually goes. The rationale's own phrase contains the tell — *"plus a pre-set price"* — and nothing in the sweep's 18 candidates pre-sets anything. **The domain's central claim was resting on a mechanism nobody had specified.** |
| DT-02 | Auto-approve is straightforwardly good — it is the owner's own standing instruction | The feature's premise | ⏳ DEFERRED to parent Q-02 — with real discomfort. An auto-approved licence is a licence the owner never saw, for a use they imagined in the abstract months earlier. That is structurally the same shape as the harm personas.md names as the Musician's worst accidental outcome — *"signs a split sheet they don't understand"* — except pre-authorised and at scale. The mitigation is **category granularity** (approve "indie documentary", not "film") plus a visible digest, but granularity fights the entire point, which is not having to think. **The feature that makes the domain viable is also the one most likely to hurt the persona it serves.** That tension is a product decision, not an agent call. |
| DT-03 | Policy belongs on the work, so all co-owners share one setting | Simplicity instinct; it would eliminate 11.04.03 entirely | ❌ REJECTED — a work-level policy requires co-owner agreement to set, which means it needs a governance mechanism, which means it will never get set. Worse, it implies someone can author a policy binding *other people's* shares — which is the Producer anti-persona's "assigns themselves a larger split and pushes it through" wearing a different hat. Policy attaches to a **share**, and the work's effective policy is a derived aggregate nobody can edit (11.04.03). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Policy attaches to a **share**, never to a work | Per DT-03 — a work-level policy means authoring rules that bind other people's property |
| D-02 | Owner-level default with per-work override | Nobody configures 200 works; everybody has one song they feel differently about |
| D-03 | Evaluation order is refusal → threshold → fall-through → pricing, at **request time** | Order is a correctness property; request-time evaluation stops stale permission surviving a new refusal |
| D-04 | Evaluation failure falls through to a human, never to auto-approve | Fail closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02 |
