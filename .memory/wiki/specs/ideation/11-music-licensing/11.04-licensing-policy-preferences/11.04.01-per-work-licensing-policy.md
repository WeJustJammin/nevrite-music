# Feature: Per-Work Licensing Policy & Auto-Approve

> **Number**: 11.04.01
> **Parent**: [11.04 Licensing Policy & Preferences](./11.04-licensing-policy-preferences-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

A rights holder's standing answer to requests they have not yet received: which uses are pre-approved, up to
what value, in what territories, for which media — so a buyer's request can clear without anyone being asked.
It is the mechanism that turns the domain's claim of instant clearance from a slogan into a system, because
the registry can tell you *who* to ask in milliseconds and that is worthless if the asking takes three weeks.

The feature exists because of a hole in the domain's own rationale (D-01 at sub-domain level): "a lookup plus
a pre-set price" only works **if something pre-set the price**, and nothing in the sweep's 18 candidates did.
This is the pre-setting. Its whole value is measured in requests that never reach a human.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Authors a standing policy over each share they hold: "auto-approve podcast and indie film, non-exclusive, UK+EU, up to £500 of my share; ask me above that; never political." **The persona this feature most serves** — per personas.md they hold small stakes across many works and live on a portfolio of small income streams; they cannot field every request and will not. Reads a digest of what their policy approved while they slept, what it turned away, and what it handed to them. Cannot see other parties' policies — only the effective aggregate (11.04.03) and which of their own rules it rendered inert. |
| Producer | Full | Same, master side — and their thresholds are typically an order of magnitude higher than the publishing side's, so a single owner-level default across both rights types will be wrong for one of them (see edge case *Multi-hyphenate holds several shares in one work*). Also **the heaviest reader of policy output**: per personas.md the buyer's deadline lands on them, and the effective policy is what tells them which works can move today. Also the persona most exposed to D-07 — they are frequently the buyer's counterparty *and* a co-owner, which is the self-dealing shape. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake **in this capacity**. Per personas.md a persona is a lens, not an account type: a studio operator who holds a master share through a work-for-hire arrangement holds it *as a Producer* and authors policy through that lens. The Operator lens — selling time and space — never touches a licensing policy. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and never mentioned. This feature's most consequential decisions are still experienced by two populations with no row here; they now have a described reader, not a Role Lens entry. |

## Behavior

A policy attaches to **a party's share of a work** — not to the work, and not to the party. Its key space is
the licence scope grammar (11.08.01): media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage, scale, plus a **value
threshold on that party's own share of the deal**. Policies are **defaulted at the owner level and overridable
per work**, because nobody will configure 200 works and everybody has one song they feel differently about.

Evaluation order is a correctness property, not an optimisation:

1. **Refusal** (11.04.02 veto / category block) — if it fires, nothing else runs
2. **Auto-approve eligibility bars** — exclusivity, self-dealing, MFN, dormancy, dispute (D-05..D-09, D-15). Any bar → skip to 3
3. **Auto-approve threshold** — covered *and* under the trailing-window aggregate → approved without disturbing anyone
4. **Fall through** → 11.02.04 consent routing (a human is asked)
5. Only then → 11.03 pricing

Evaluation happens at **request time**, against the aggregate as it stands then — never at policy-authoring
time. Otherwise stale permission survives a newer refusal (`#CX-01` synthesis Q3). It runs **synchronously
inside the request that renders the buyer's quote** — never as a background job the buyer waits on. Its
latency budget is therefore the *search* budget, not a job budget; the numeric value is set by
`/create-prd-compile`, to which the domain already routes "clearance graph traversal performance". If
evaluation exceeds that budget it **fails closed to fall-through** rather than blocking the response (D-04).

### Scope matching rules

- **Multi-value scopes are conjunctive.** A request for `media: {social, ad}` auto-approves only if the policy
  covers *both*. Covering `social` alone is not a partial yes.
- **Hierarchy runs one way.** Axes are hierarchical (11.08.01 D-04): a policy permitting `film` covers a
  request for `indie documentary`. A policy permitting `indie documentary` does **not** cover a request for
  `film` — the coarse request is broader than the permission.
- **An unset axis never auto-approves.** Per 11.08.01 D-05 an unspecified territory is "unspecified", not
  "worldwide". A request with an unset axis falls through.
- **The threshold is currency-explicit.** It is denominated in the currency the owner declared. A request in
  another currency converts at the rate pinned at request receipt; **an FX move must never silently widen a
  ceiling**, so conversion is evaluated at request time and the applied rate is shown in the digest.
- **The threshold is on the party's own share.** It therefore requires the split to be resolved. An
  unresolved or provisional split means the party's share value is unknown → fall through (fail closed).
  This is the gate's denominator everywhere: **there is no work-level ceiling and no minimum taken across
  owners** (D-18; 11.04.03 D-06). Every owner is tested against their own share and all must pass. The
  trailing 90-day window that qualifies the ceiling (D-06) is denominated the same way — per owner, in that
  owner's own share values (D-19).

### Happy Path

1. Owner sets an **owner-level default** once — a handful of decisions, not a form. Per rights type (publishing / master), because the economics differ by an order of magnitude.
2. Owner overrides on specific works where they feel differently. The UI states the blast radius before confirming a default change: "This changes 187 works and withdraws 3 live quotes."
3. A buyer's request arrives carrying a scope expressed in the grammar (11.08.01) and a declared deal value.
4. The effective aggregate (11.04.03) is computed for the requested scope, at request time.
5. **Refusals checked first** — any co-owner's veto (11.04.02) closes the scope and nothing else runs.
6. **Eligibility bars checked** — exclusivity, self-dealing, MFN, dormancy, disputed share. Any bar → step 8.
7. **This party's threshold checked** — this party's share value of the request, plus their trailing 90-day auto-approved total **in their own share values, each carrying the share value it had when it cleared** (D-19), against their own ceiling. Every owner is tested this way and all must pass; the owner whose ceiling binds is named in the fall-through (11.04.03 D-04, D-07). No figure is ever compared across owners (D-18).
8. Covered → **auto-approved. No notification interrupt, no delay, no human.** Not covered → falls through to consent routing (11.02.04) carrying the **specific** reason: "above your £500 ceiling (£640)", not "requires review".
9. The buyer's quote renders in the same request. The gate re-evaluates fresh at issuance (11.02.01 D-04) — a policy that tightened in between fails the issuance, not the licence.
10. Owner receives a digest: approved while you slept (N, £X), turned away (N), fell through to you (N, oldest waiting M days), and **which of your rules are inert** (`#CX-04`).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Owner auto-approves; a co-owner blocks the category | Refusal wins. This owner's auto-approve is inert for that scope. | "Your auto-approve for ads is dead — [co-owner] blocked the category" — per `#CX-04`, silence here means an owner believes they are earning and are not |
| Policy set, then a new veto appears from a co-owner | Aggregate recomputes; **in-flight quotes for the blocked scope are withdrawn**, not merely stopped for future requests | Buyer: "Withdrawn — no longer available for this use" |
| Owner sets no policy | Everything falls through to a human. Correct default, and the slow path this feature exists to remove. | "No policy — every request waits for you. [Set one]" |
| Auto-approved licence turns out to be a use the owner would have hated | **The feature's core hazard.** See DT-02 and parent Q-02. | Mitigation is category granularity + the digest, not a cure |
| Owner has 200 works and will not configure them | Owner-level default + per-work override; the default does the work | Configuring one work at a time is a failure state |
| Two owners set different auto-approve ceilings | **No lowest-wins and no work-level ceiling** (D-18 — this row previously read "Lowest wins (11.04.03)", a citation back to a clause now corrected as wrong). Each ceiling is tested against its own owner's share of the deal value; the request auto-approves only if **every** owner passes their own test, and falls through naming the owner whose ceiling bound it. Deterministic and order-independent, because nothing is combined. | Each sees the attributed threshold set — their own ceiling and every co-owner's, with whose it is; the binding owner is named per request, never as a standing figure (11.04.03 D-07) |
| **A band entity holds a policy** (domain 01 models bands as first-class) | **Resolved (D-12).** A band policy governs exactly the shares the **band entity owns** — because policy attaches to a share (D-01), and the band does not own its members' personally-held shares. A member's own share is governed by that member's own policy, or falls through if they have none. **A band policy never binds a member's individual share.** Whether a member may *delegate* their share's policy to the band is a separate mechanism — Q-03. | Member: "The band's policy covers the band's 40%. Your personal 15% has no policy — every request still waits for you." |
| Same human sits behind both a band share and a personal share in one work | **Two shares, two policies, two inputs to the fold** — the band is a separate legal person (personas.md: bands "own and get paid independently of their members"). This is *not* the multi-hyphenate rule (one human, several shares, counts once), because the owners genuinely differ. For **veto** the distinction is inert: a veto is absolute, so a second one adds nothing and there is no double-vote exploit. | "You influence 2 of this work's 4 policies (yours, and [Band]'s)" — stated, because it is otherwise invisible |
| Value threshold set in a currency; deal is in another | Threshold is currency-aware; converted at the rate pinned at request receipt | Digest shows the applied rate: "£487 (US$640 @ 1.314)" |
| **Buyer structures one £4,500 use as 10 × £450 requests** | **The threshold's defining exploit** (DT-05). Threshold evaluates against the **trailing 90-day cumulative auto-approved total for the same buyer, same work, overlapping media values** — not the isolated request. The total is kept **per owner and in that owner's own share values** (D-19), never in whole-deal values. Request 2 onward falls through once *that owner's* cumulative share total crosses *their* ceiling. | Owner: "[Buyer] has auto-cleared £450 **of your share** on this work in 90 days; this request takes them over your £500 ceiling — your call." |
| **An owner's share changes mid-window** (a split resolves, a share is acquired, a reversion lands) | **Past auto-approvals keep the share value they had when they cleared** (D-19). The running total is never restated. An owner whose share **grew** therefore carries a total computed at their old, smaller stake and has proportionally generous headroom for the rest of the 90 days; an owner whose share **shrank** carries a larger historical total against the same ceiling. Revaluing at the current share would move a ceiling the owner never touched — precisely the silent widening DT-14 rejects. | Digest figures never change retroactively: "£450 of your share auto-cleared in the last 90 days" is the same number the gate used and the same number the digest already showed |
| Fixed-template micro-licences from one high-volume creator (11.06.01) | **Exempt from the window.** The creator catalogue is a flat price, fixed scope, unlimited-quantity product — quantity *is* the product, and the owner opted into exactly that (11.06.01). Applying the structuring window here would break the micro-licensing product for a legitimate buyer. Volume still appears in the digest. | "[Creator] has licensed this track for 14 videos" — informational, not a gate |
| **Request is for an exclusive or category-exclusive scope, value under the threshold** | **Never auto-approves, at any threshold** (D-05). A £499 exclusive that forecloses the category for 5 years is worse than a £5,000 non-exclusive, and no owner setting "podcasts under £500" meant to authorise it. | "Exclusive requests always come to you" — stated in the policy UI at authoring time, not discovered later |
| **The buyer is a co-owner of the work, or their affiliate** | **Auto-approve suspends for every *other* owner's share** (D-07); the request routes for explicit consent with the conflict disclosed. The self-dealing party's own share may still auto-approve — nobody else is harmed by them licensing to themselves. | Co-owners: "[Name] is both a co-owner and the buyer here. This one needs your explicit yes." — the machine-assisted version of the Producer anti-persona's "pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention" |
| **The work carries an active MFN commitment on any share** | **Auto-approve suspends for the affected scope** (D-08). Auto-approve's function is removing the human — and the human is exactly who would have noticed that this deal repriced someone else's. | "This work has a most-favoured-nation commitment — deals here always come to you." Resolution of the MFN itself is 11.03.03's. |
| **Owner has not opened the platform in 24 months** | **Auto-approve suspends; refusals stay in force** (D-09). A standing instruction needs a standing principal. Refusals are unaffected — dormancy must never open a door, only close one. | On return: "We paused auto-approve on your 43 works while you were away. Nothing was refused that you didn't refuse." |
| **Three consecutive digests hard-bounce** | **Auto-approve suspends** (D-09). Per DT-15 the digest is the only evidence the owner ever saw what their policy did; a policy whose owner cannot be reached is a policy nobody is standing behind. | Reachable channel: "We couldn't reach you, so we paused auto-approve." |
| **A share is sold, assigned, or reverts** | **The policy dies with the transfer** (D-10). The new owner did not author it. Their own owner-level default takes over; if they have none, the share falls through to a human. Consistent with 11.04.03's "the new owner's policy replaces the old one's in the fold". | New owner: "You acquired 3 shares. Your default policy now governs them." Co-owners: "This work's policy changed because a share changed hands." |
| **A catalogue buyer with a permissive default acquires 500 shares** | Their default applies to all 500 immediately — which is a real consequence of D-02 + D-10 and is *their* decision to make. The blast-radius warning (Happy Path 2) is what makes it a decision rather than an accident. | "Your default will auto-approve on 500 newly acquired works. Review before it takes effect?" |
| **Owner's ownership is territorial (UK only); their policy says worldwide** | **Policy is clipped to the registry's ownership footprint** (D-13). A party cannot pre-authorise territories where they hold nothing. Territorial *scope* and territorial *ownership* are different axes (11.08.01) and both bind. | "Your policy covers worldwide, but you hold this share in the UK only — it applies to the UK." |
| **Owner sets a policy on a work they do not own** | Rejected at authoring. Policy requires an existing share in the registry (D-01, domain 09). | "You don't hold a share in this work" |
| **The share is under active ownership dispute** (domain 24) | **Auto-approve suspends for that work** (D-15). Auto-approving on a contested share sells something whose owner is unknown — the exact false positive D-09 (domain) exists to prevent. | "Auto-approve is paused on this work while ownership is disputed." |
| **Policy edited while a request is mid-evaluation** | Evaluation pins the policy version at request receipt. A policy that became **more restrictive** is caught by the gate's fresh re-check at issuance (11.02.01 D-04) and the issuance fails. A policy that became **more permissive** does not retroactively auto-approve a request that already fell through — the human ask stands. | Buyer, on tightening: "No longer available for this use" — before execution, never after |
| **Two co-owners concurrently edit; one drops a ceiling below an in-flight deal** | Fold is order-independent for refusals (absolute) **and** for thresholds — each ceiling stays attributed to its author and is never combined, so there is no aggregated figure whose write order could matter (D-18; 11.04.03 D-03 order-independence, D-06/D-07). This row previously read "deterministic (minimum) for thresholds (11.04.03 D-02/D-03)" — a citation back to the thresholds-minimum clause D-18 corrects as wrong; 11.04.03 D-06 supersedes it. The gate's re-check at issuance is still the serialization point. | Nothing until issuance; then the buyer is told before execution, not at the deadline — and the dropped ceiling binds only its own author's share |
| **Buyer misstates the scope to fit under a threshold** (declares "podcast" for what is an ad) | The policy evaluates the **declared** scope and the licence issues for the declared scope only (D-16). Use outside it is unlicensed, and is 11.08.03 verification's and domain 24's problem. **The policy is not a fraud detector** — the licence's pinned scope is the defence. | Nothing at request time. Post-hoc, the certificate is the evidence. |
| **The buyer's blocked-affiliate structure** — a blocked or windowed buyer requests through a subsidiary | Corporate-structure resolution is beyond the platform's knowledge. The window and the block key on the buyer identity the platform has. Same limitation 11.04.02 records for buyer blocks. | Honest limitation — Q-06 |
| **Registry unavailable / evaluation errors** | Falls through to a human. Degrade to slow, never to auto-approved (D-04). | Buyer: "This one needs the owner's answer" — indistinguishable from a legitimate fall-through, deliberately |
| **The policy has never fired** because the work is not clearable for unrelated reasons (cold start, D-07; missing attestation, 11.02.02) | Policy is set, valid, and inert — not through anyone's refusal but because clearance never reaches `consent-needed`. The owner will otherwise conclude there is no demand. | "Your policy on this work has never fired — the work isn't clearable yet: [Sarah hasn't confirmed her 25% writer share]" — blockers always named (11.02.01 D-05) |
| **Multi-hyphenate holds several shares in one work** (writer 20%, master 50%, performer) | One policy may cover all their shares, or they may set per-share policies where the economics differ (master vs publishing). In the fold their shares are their shares; for **veto** they count once (consistent with 11.04.02 and 11.02.04 D-04), but **thresholds apply per share** — a £500 publishing ceiling is not a £500 master ceiling. | "You hold 3 stakes here. One policy for all, or separate for master and publishing?" |
| **An amendment is requested on an already-auto-approved licence** (11.08.04) | An amendment is a new scope. It **re-evaluates against policy at amendment time** — it does not inherit the original's auto-approval. | "This extends the term to perpetual — that's a new decision" |
| **Owner sets a policy, then a co-owner's veto makes every scope inert** | The whole policy is dead. They must be told, or they believe their catalogue earns and it has been closed for a year. | "None of your policy on this work is active" — the silent-inert failure `#CX-04` exists to prevent |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Aggregate evaluation, inside the buyer's request | Synchronous and inside the search path — never a spinner the buyer waits on. Exceeding the budget fails closed to fall-through (D-04), so the buyer sees an answer, not a wait. Budget owned by `/create-prd-compile`. |
| Empty | No policy set | The pitch, stated as a cost: "Every request waits on you. 4 are waiting now." — never an empty illustration |
| Populated | Policy set and active | **Coverage view expressed in outcomes, not rules**: what auto-approves, what asks, what refuses, and what it has actually done (approved N / £X, turned away N, handed you N) |
| Error | Evaluation, registry, or fold failure | **Fall through to human.** Degrade to slow, never to auto-approved. Fail closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02 and domain D-09. The buyer's view is indistinguishable from a legitimate fall-through. |
| Partial | Owner default set, no per-work overrides | **Normal and desirable — not an incomplete state.** Never nagged as "incomplete"; the default doing all the work is the design target (cf. 11.03.01 D-04: partial coverage is the target, fall-through rate is the metric). |
| Partial (inert) | Policy set but wholly overridden by others' refusals, or never fires (cold start) | Distinct from Partial-above and must not be conflated: the policy exists and does nothing. Names the cause and whose it is (`#CX-04`, 11.02.01 D-05). |
| Suspended | Dormancy, digest failure, active dispute, or MFN on the work | **A named state, not an error.** Auto-approve is off; refusals still bite; requests fall through. Says which bar fired and how to clear it. Without this state the owner experiences D-07/D-08/D-09/D-15 as a silent stop. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The registry alone delivers instant clearance — knowing who owns what is the hard part, and WeJammin knows it | The domain's own rationale: "clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a lookup plus a pre-set price" | ❌ REJECTED — and this rejection created the whole sub-domain (D-01). The registry removes the **archaeology** (finding the parties). It does nothing about the **asking** (getting them to say yes), which is where most of the six weeks actually goes. The rationale's own phrase contains the tell — *"plus a pre-set price"* — and nothing in the sweep's 18 candidates pre-sets anything. **The domain's central claim was resting on a mechanism nobody had specified.** |
| DT-02 | Auto-approve is straightforwardly good — it is the owner's own standing instruction | The feature's premise | ⏳ DEFERRED to parent Q-02 — with real discomfort. An auto-approved licence is a licence the owner never saw, for a use they imagined in the abstract months earlier. That is structurally the same shape as the harm personas.md names as the Musician's worst accidental outcome — *"signs a split sheet they don't understand"* — except pre-authorised and at scale. The mitigation is **category granularity** (approve "indie documentary", not "film") plus a visible digest, but granularity fights the entire point, which is not having to think. **The feature that makes the domain viable is also the one most likely to hurt the persona it serves.** That tension is a product decision, not an agent call. DT-05..DT-09 and DT-15 are the structural mitigations found since; none of them dissolve it. |
| DT-03 | Policy belongs on the work, so all co-owners share one setting | Simplicity instinct; it would eliminate 11.04.03 entirely | ❌ REJECTED — a work-level policy requires co-owner agreement to set, which means it needs a governance mechanism, which means it will never get set. Worse, it implies someone can author a policy binding *other people's* shares — which is the Producer anti-persona's "assigns themselves a larger split and pushes it through" wearing a different hat. Policy attaches to a **share**, and the work's effective policy is a derived aggregate nobody can edit (11.04.03). |
| DT-04 | A value threshold is a sufficient risk control — cheap uses are low-stakes, expensive ones deserve a human | The feature's implicit model, and the reason "under £500" feels like a safe thing to type | ❌ REJECTED — **value does not rank harm in licensing, and the threshold silently assumes it does.** A £499 *exclusive* podcast licence forecloses every other podcast for its term; a £5,000 non-exclusive one costs nothing but the fee. A domain expert would catch this instantly and nobody in the sub-domain had said it. The grammar already lets an owner bound term, territory and media (11.08.01), so those axes are self-defending — but **exclusivity is the axis no owner will think to set and the one where the default is catastrophic.** Hence D-05: exclusivity is barred from auto-approve categorically, not thresholded. This is the general lesson the sub-domain was missing: *the cheap grant is the dangerous one*, which inverts the threshold's entire intuition. |
| DT-05 | The threshold is safe because it is checked on every request | Reading the evaluation order as written — it is per-request by construction | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **defect**, and it is the feature's defining exploit. A per-request ceiling is trivially structured around: a buyer wanting £4,500 of use sends ten £450 requests and every one auto-approves. The owner set a £500 ceiling and licensed £4,500 without being asked once. This is not exotic — **ad agencies already buy in flights and campaigns are already segmented by market, cut, and channel**, so the structured version is indistinguishable from the honest version at request level. It only resolves in aggregate. Hence D-06: the ceiling is evaluated against a trailing 90-day cumulative total per (buyer, work, overlapping media), not per isolated request. The window is why the exemption in D-06 for fixed-template micro-licences (11.06.01) has to be explicit — there, quantity *is* the product. |
| DT-06 | Auto-approve is neutral about who the buyer is | Nothing in the feature keys on buyer identity, and the scope grammar has no buyer axis | ✅ CONFIRMED as a **hole**. The multi-hyphenate is the platform's structural fact (personas.md), and its licensing consequence is that **the buyer is routinely also a co-owner**: a Producer co-owns the track and wants it for their client's ad. Their auto-approve fires on their own request. Worse, their co-owners' auto-approves fire too — against a price the buyer's side effectively set. The co-owner typed "£500 podcasts" imagining strangers, not their bandmate arriving as the counterparty on both sides of the table. personas.md names this exact person: *"assigns themselves a larger split and pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention"* — and auto-approve is the first mechanism in the domain that does the pushing **for** them, at machine speed, with the platform's endorsement. Hence D-07. Note the asymmetry that makes the rule cheap: the self-dealer's *own* share can still auto-approve, because nobody is harmed by someone licensing to themselves. |
| DT-07 | Policy and pricing are cleanly separable — policy says whether, the rate card says how much (D-02 at sub-domain level) | The sub-domain's own boundary, which is correct as far as it goes | ✅ CONFIRMED **with a leak**, and it is a leak only a sync practitioner would see. Most-favoured-nation clauses are standard on the publishing side of sync (11.03.03 exists for this). Under MFN, one share's deal terms **propagate to another share's**. So an owner's auto-approve does not only dispose of their own share — it can set the economics of co-owners who never saw the deal. The separation of policy from pricing survives (a threshold is a ceiling, not a price), but the *human who would have noticed the MFN implication* is exactly who auto-approve removes. That is the whole hazard, stated precisely. Hence D-08: an active MFN commitment on any share suspends auto-approve for the affected scope. Determining comparability remains 11.03.03's problem (its Q-01), not this feature's — this feature's obligation is to **not fire while it is unresolved**. |
| DT-08 | A standing policy stands until it is changed | The plain meaning of "standing" | ❌ REJECTED — **a standing instruction requires a standing principal, and the platform is the only party that knows whether one exists.** A musician who set "auto-approve everything under £500" while unknown, and has not opened the app in three years, is not expressing a preference; they are leaving a machine running. Their catalogue may now be worth ten times what it was. Parent Q-02 asks whether auto-approve is valid consent at all — and the **dormant** case is the weakest possible version of that claim, the one where the answer is clearly no even if it is yes everywhere else. Expiry is the tempting fix and it is wrong: expiring every policy annually breaks the buyer's certainty, which is the product. Suspension on *dormancy* is the fix, because it fails in the correct direction (slow, never permissive), it targets exactly the population that cannot defend the claim, and it costs an active user nothing. 24 months, per D-09 — owner may tune the number, but not the direction. |
| DT-09 | A policy travels with the share it governs — it is an attribute of the share | Modelling instinct from D-01 ("policy attaches to a share"), which reads as if the policy *is* share metadata | ❌ REJECTED, and D-01 is the reason rather than the counterexample. A policy attaches to a share but is **authored by a principal about their own property**. Sell the property and the instruction has no author — the acquirer never said any of it. A surviving policy would mean a share sale silently transfers someone else's standing consent to a stranger, which is the DT-03 harm ("a policy binding other people's shares") arriving through time instead of through co-ownership. Hence D-10: the policy dies with the transfer; the acquirer's own owner-level default takes over, or the share falls through. This lines up exactly with 11.04.03's edge case — "the aggregate can change with no policy having been edited" — and now explains *why* it does. |
| DT-10 | Auto-approve should be on by default for new users, or the feature will have no adoption and clearance stays slow | The cold-start pressure (D-07): an empty policy set means every request falls through, so the domain's headline claim never demonstrates itself | ❌ REJECTED, without hesitation, and the strength of the argument for it is the reason to write the rejection down. Opt-out auto-approve is a licence granted by a person who never made a decision — it is *literally* personas.md's "signs a split sheet they don't understand", with the platform doing the signing.
