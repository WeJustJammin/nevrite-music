# Feature: Multi-Party Consent Routing & Approval

> **Number**: 11.02.04
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The workflow that turns a "no" into a "yes": when a work is not one-stop because named parties have not
approved a use, this feature finds them, asks them, chases them, and writes the result back into the
clearance verdict. Without it the one-stop flag is a read-only judgement and every non-one-stop work is
commercially dead — which, in year one, is most of the catalogue.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Receives consent requests on works they co-own, with the scope stated in language they can act on ("a documentary, UK only, 5 years, £2,400 your share") — never a legal blob. Approves, declines with a reason, or asks a question. Also initiates: chasing co-writers so their own work becomes licensable. |
| Producer | Full | Same, and frequently the **initiator** — per personas.md they are the de-facto project manager and the party a buyer's deadline lands on. The one chasing four people to unblock a delivery. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

> The **buyer** is the party whose deadline drives every consent round and who has no persona (parent Q-01).
> Their experience — "4 of 5 cleared, one person hasn't opened the email, your air date is Tuesday" — is the
> single most emotionally loaded surface in the domain and currently has no persona to specify it for.

## Behavior

A consent request is scoped, time-boxed, and addressed to a **named party with a stake**, derived from the
rights registry rather than from whoever the buyer could find. Requests carry the specific use, not a blanket
permission — approving a documentary sync is not approving a tobacco ad, and a system that blurs the two will
produce consents nobody meant to give.

The feature's real job is **latency**. Clearance that takes six weeks is the incumbent experience; the entire
point of holding the registry is that the parties are already enumerated, already identified, and already
reachable. Standing policy (11.04) is the pre-emption: a party who set an auto-approve rule never sees a
request at all, which is the only way clearance gets to instant.

### Happy Path

1. Clearance computation returns `consent-needed` with N named parties for a given scope
2. System routes a request to each — simultaneously, never sequentially (same reasoning as 11.01.05 DT-01)
3. Parties with a standing policy that covers the scope (11.04.01) are **auto-approved without being asked**
4. Remaining parties see a plain-language request: what, who, where, how long, how much is theirs
5. Each responds: approve / decline with reason / ask a question (routes to a thread)
6. All approvals in → clearance recomputes → work becomes clearable for this scope
7. Any decline → terminal for this scope; the buyer is told immediately, not at the deadline

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One party never responds | Request expires; **clearance does not silently improve.** The work stays non-one-stop. | Buyer: "3 of 4 approved. [Name] hasn't responded since [date]." Initiator: escalation options. |
| A party declines on principle (won't license to gambling) | Terminal for this scope, and it should become a **standing policy** so they are never asked again | "Never ask me about gambling again?" — one tap converts a decline into a policy (11.04.02) |
| Party approves, then wants to withdraw before execution | Allowed before execution, refused after. Licences are irrevocable instruments. | The line is drawn at execution and shown as such |
| Party approves a scope they misunderstood | The failure this feature must design against — see DT-02 | Scope stated in outcome terms, with a plain-language summary that is the *primary* text, not a tooltip |
| Consent requests become spam (a work with 12 co-writers, 40 buyers) | Standing policy is the only real answer; without it the feature drives its own users away | Digest + policy prompt: "You've approved 12 podcast syncs. Auto-approve these?" |
| Buyer's deadline passes mid-round | Round continues (the consent has value beyond this deal — it can become a policy) but the buyer is released | Buyer: released. Parties: "The deal expired, but your answer still helps." |
| A party is unreachable / dead / erased | Permanently non-one-stop for scopes needing them | See 11.02.01 Q-03 and 11.02.02 Q-04 |
| Same human holds multiple shares (multi-hyphenate) | **One request, all their stakes named** — not four separate emails to the same person | "You hold 3 stakes in this work (writer 20%, master 50%, performer). Approve all?" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Routing / policy evaluation | Brief |
| Empty | No consents needed | Work is already one-stop for this scope — this feature is invisible, which is the goal |
| Populated | Round in flight | Per-party status board: approved / declined / pending / auto-approved by policy, with elapsed time each |
| Error | Routing failed | Consent not requested; clearance unchanged. **Never assume approval.** |
| Partial | Some approved, some pending | "3 of 5" — the work is **not** clearable. Partial consent is not consent. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This feature is where clearance latency actually lives — and standing policy is the only thing that can remove it | Reasoning from the domain rationale's claim that "clearance collapses from months of archaeology to a lookup plus a pre-set price" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it exposes a gap in that claim: **a lookup plus a pre-set price only works if the price was pre-set.** The registry removes the *archaeology* (finding who owns what) but not the *asking* (getting them to say yes). Nothing in the sweep's candidate list covers pre-setting terms. This is what forced 11.04 (Licensing Policy & Preferences) into existence as a whole sub-domain. **The domain rationale overstates what the registry alone buys.** |
| DT-02 | Consent requests should state legal scope precisely — this is a legal instrument and precision protects everyone | Obvious compliance instinct; also what every existing split sheet does | ❌ REJECTED — precision and comprehension are different things, and personas.md names the cost of confusing them: the Musician's worst accidental outcome is "signs a split sheet they don't understand and discovers years later they gave away their publishing." A legally precise request that nobody reads produces consents that are technically valid and morally void. The plain-language summary must be the **primary** artifact and the legal scope its appendix — inverting the industry default. The instrument still has to be precise; the *ask* must not be. |
| DT-03 | Sequential routing (ask the biggest shareholder first) reduces noise for minor parties | Efficiency instinct; mirrors how manual clearance is done | ❌ REJECTED — same reasoning as 11.01.05 DT-01. Sequential adds a human response latency per hop, and the whole competitive claim is latency. It also lets the first responder anchor terms before others see the deal, which is quietly how minor contributors get rolled. Simultaneous, always. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Requests are routed simultaneously to all parties | Per DT-03 — sequential routing is the incumbent's latency and lets early responders anchor terms |
| D-02 | Plain-language summary is the primary artifact; legal scope is the appendix | Per DT-02 — personas.md names incomprehension, not imprecision, as the harm that actually lands |
| D-03 | Non-response never improves clearance | Silence is not consent. This is the same principle as 11.02.02 Q-01 and should be resolved consistently with it. |
| D-04 | A multi-hyphenate gets one request naming all their stakes | personas.md structural fact: one human routinely holds several roles. Four emails to one person is the fragmentation the platform exists to end. |
