# Feature: Encumbrance & Uncleared-Sample Declaration

> **Number**: 11.02.03
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Records that a work contains something borrowed and not yet paid for — an uncleared sample, an
un-negotiated interpolation, a loop used outside its pack licence's terms, a source the producer
cannot even name — and propagates that taint to every work derived from it. It exists because
personas.md names this exact event as the Musician's worst accidental behaviour: *"uploads a stem
containing an uncleared sample and exposes every collaborator downstream."* A clearance model with no
upstream taint cannot represent the failure the persona doc says will happen.

It answers a question the ownership registry structurally cannot: **what is this work made of?**
Domain 09 answers *who owns it*. Both answers can be perfect and the work still unlicensable.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Declares encumbrances on works they own or are credited on. Sees encumbrances **inherited** from someone else's contribution, named to the node (`via Dave's stem → via "Loop Kit 4"`), never as "a work you own is affected". Sees their blast radius: every work of theirs the taint reaches. Sees, per-scope, what the encumbrance actually costs them — a work can be encumbered for sync and clear for a podcast, and the Musician's view must show both rather than a red badge. **The persona this feature protects**: personas.md classes the harm as *accidental*, so the design catches it for them rather than punishing them after. |
| Producer | Full | **The critical role.** They hold the stems and are the only party who knows what is actually in the mix (personas.md: "the person who *knows* who played what", extended to *what is in it*). Declares at session close and on delivery. Also the most exposed party: an encumbered delivery is their professional liability, not the sampler's — the client sues the person who handed them the file. Sees the delivery-side view: "is what I am about to hand my client encumbered for the use my client intends?" |
| Operator | None | No composition or master stake in the Operator capacity — they sell time and space (personas.md). A studio owner who holds a master share is wearing the Producer lens, and declares there. The explicit absence is a finding, not an oversight (sub-domain index Role Matrix). |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and deliberately so. A Fan buying a creator micro-licence (11.06) consumes the **verdict** `encumbered` from 11.02.01 — never this feature's detail. Showing a buyer "contains an uncleared sample of [Track X]" broadcasts an infringement admission to a stranger (D-13, DT-10). |

## Behavior

An encumbrance is a **typed, scoped, graded defect attached to a work**, and it flows downward through
the derivation graph. Its central property is that it **outranks a clean local computation**: a work
whose own splits are perfect and fully attested is still not clearable if it samples something
uncleared. Clearance must therefore walk *upstream* — "what is this made of?" — not merely across the
local ownership set (`#CX-02`).

Declaration is the honest half of the ritual whose other half is completeness attestation (`#CX-05`):
"nobody is missing" and "nothing borrowed is unpaid" are the two facts only a human present at creation
can supply, both unfalsifiable at assert time, both catastrophic when false.

### The four boundaries this feature is defined by

| Boundary | Rule |
|---|---|
| **vs. Attestation (11.02.02)** | If the missing person should be **a party to this work**, it is an attestation gap. If the material came from **another work**, it is an encumbrance. The topline case decides it: a friend who wrote your topline is a co-writer (attestation); a topline lifted from another song is an interpolation (here). The declaration flow must ask a question that separates them — "did they write it *for this*, or did it come *from somewhere*?" (D-12) |
| **vs. Identification (11.05.01)** | 11.05.01 holds *what the source is*. This feature holds *the taint and its propagation*. An unidentified source is still a first-class encumbrance (11.05.01 D-03). |
| **vs. Resolution (11.05.02 / 11.05.03)** | Declaring the problem and fixing it are different features. Conflating them means declaring starts a chore, and people stop declaring — losing the platform the fact it most needs (D-03). |
| **vs. Claims (24 / 11.06.03)** | A declaration is a **confession about your own work**. A third party asserting "you sampled me" is an **allegation** and belongs to disputes. Different object, different burden of proof, different remedy (D-07, DT-09). |

### Anatomy of a declared encumbrance

What the declarer supplies and every affected party sees:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| **Type** | `uncleared-sample` (recorded material from another master) · `uncleared-interpolation` (melody/lyric re-performed; no shared audio) · `restricted-source-licence` (material used, or about to be used, outside its source licence's granted scope — see D-11) · `unidentified-source` (per 11.05.01 D-03) · `provenance-unknown-generative` (per DT-11) |
| **Source** | On-platform work · fingerprint-identified commercial recording · free-text description (untrusted; never rendered as a link, never fetched — D-15) |
| **Status** | `declared-uncleared` · `clearing` (resolution in flight via 11.05) · `cleared-evidenced` · `cleared-asserted` · `lapsed` (a previously-cleared source whose licence term ended — DT-06) |
| **Cleared scope** | Only when status is `cleared-*`. Expressed in 11.08.01's scope grammar — the same vocabulary the licence certificate serialises. A clearance without a scope is meaningless (DT-04). |
| **Declarer** | Named identity + timestamp. This is the entire deterrent (11.02.02 D-03). |
| **Blast radius** | The set of descendant works the taint reaches, and their owners. |

### The three properties that make this feature hard

**1. Encumbrance is per-(source, scope), never a boolean.** It inherits domain D-08. The most common
real-world sample-clearance failure after outright non-clearance is *the clearance you hold does not
cover the use you were just offered*: a sample cleared for a record, then a sync request arrives and the
audiovisual right was never granted. So a work is simultaneously `one-stop` for one scope and
`encumbered` for another, and both statements are true at once (DT-04).

**2. "Cleared" is self-asserted unless the platform can see the instrument.** A producer typing
"yes, it's cleared" removes the taint on their word alone — exactly as unfalsifiable as declaring
nothing, but with the opposite effect. The status therefore carries a grade: `cleared-evidenced` (an
instrument the platform can read — an 11.05.02 on-platform clearance, or an uploaded licence document)
vs `cleared-asserted` (the declarer's word). Without the grade, "cleared" is a free pass and the feature
is a formality (DT-05, D-06).

**3. Encumbrance is time-dependent — it can resurrect.** Sample clearances are routinely term-limited or
configuration-limited ("this album, not compilations"). A work that is one-stop today re-encumbers on the
day the underlying clearance lapses, with no user action (DT-06). Which forces the invariant below.

### The scope-intersection invariant

> **A downstream licence's granted scope can never exceed the intersection of every upstream
> clearance's granted scope. The gate (11.02.01) refuses anything larger.**

This is the direct consequence of D-08 (per-(work, scope)) + D-09 (fail closed) + D-06 (licences are
irrevocable). Issuing a 10-year worldwide sync on a work whose sample clearance has 3 years left sells
something the platform provably cannot honour — and since the licence cannot be revoked, the platform
would be selling its own liability. (D-05)

### Propagation invariants

| Direction | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Bad news** (an encumbrance appears) | All-or-nothing across the descendant set; anything unreachable is treated as **encumbered** until reconciled | A partially-propagated encumbrance leaves some descendants falsely clearable — the worst outcome in the sub-domain (`#CX-02` synthesis Q2) |
| **Good news** (an encumbrance clears) | May lag; unreached descendants stay encumbered until the recompute reaches them | The asymmetry is deliberate. Lagging good news costs a delayed sale; lagging bad news sells an unhonourable licence (D-09). |
| **Traversal** | Each node visited at most once; cycles (A samples B, B remixes A) terminate and the cycle is reported, not hidden | Remix culture makes cycles normal, not pathological |
| **Naming** | The full chain is named (`encumbered via B via C`), never just the nearest node | An owner who cannot see *where* the taint entered cannot act on it (11.02.01 D-05: blockers are always named) |
| **Notification** | One notification per affected owner per encumbrance, listing every work of theirs it reaches — never one per work | 11.02.04 D-04's multi-hyphenate principle: four messages to one person is the fragmentation the platform exists to end |
| **Timing** | Fan-out is never held for a digest window | An encumbrance notification is the exposure notice; per `#CX-02` the notification set *is* the exposed set |

### Happy Path

1. A session closes (domain 07) or a work is uploaded. The system asks plainly, in the same moment as the split ritual and the completeness attestation (`#CX-05`): **"Does this contain anything you didn't make here? Samples, loops, a melody you played from another song, anything from a pack?"**
2. Audio fingerprinting (cross-cut, shared with 11.05.01) runs in the background and **suggests** candidates with timestamps. Declaration is never blocked on it (D-01).
3. The Producer answers. For each item the flow separates the two boundaries: *did this person write it for this work* (→ 11.02.02 attestation) or *did this material come from another work* (→ here). (D-12)
4. For each borrowed item, identification is collected by 11.05.01: which master, which composition, what was taken, how long, where. "I don't know what it is" is an offered answer, not a dead end (11.05.01 D-03).
5. Declarer states clearance: **not cleared** / **cleared — here's the licence** (→ `cleared-evidenced`) / **cleared — take my word** (→ `cleared-asserted`). The three are visibly different, and the flow does not pretend otherwise (D-06).
6. If cleared, the granted scope is captured in 11.08.01's grammar: which uses, which territories, how long. "Cleared" with no scope is refused as an answer (D-05).
7. System attaches the encumbrance and recomputes clearance **per scope** for this work and every descendant, under the propagation invariants above.
8. Every co-owner and every downstream derivative owner is notified — one message each, naming the chain and listing every affected work of theirs. The sampled party is **not** notified (D-08).
9. Owner is offered the resolution route: 11.05.02 (programmatic, if the source is on-platform) or 11.05.03 (negotiated, if not). Where neither can reach the owner, the honest answer is offered instead: "we can identify this; we can't clear it — that's a phone call."
10. On licence execution, status → `cleared-evidenced` with the granted scope attached; clearance recomputes upward across the descendant set for exactly the scopes the new licence covers, and no wider.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Declaration fan-out to descendants partially fails | **All-or-nothing, or fail toward "not clearable"** for anything unreachable. The encumbrance record is never partially written: either it exists with its full blast radius computed, or it does not exist. | Affected owners: "[Work] is now encumbered via [chain]. It also affects: [list of their works]." Declarer: "Declared. 12 of 14 downstream works updated; 2 pending — they're treated as encumbered until we reach them." |
| A collaborator declares an encumbrance on a work others co-own | Correct — the truth is the truth (`#CX-02` synthesis Q3) — but it reduces co-owners' asset value without their consent, and is a spite vector. Contest path required. | Co-owners: "[Name] declared an uncleared sample in [work]. It is no longer one-stop for [scopes]. [Contest]" |
| A **1% shareholder** declares, killing a work everyone else wants to sell | Permitted. Any co-owner may declare; declaration is a statement of fact, and fact does not scale with share size. It is also the spite vector at its cheapest. | Same as above. Contest window and consequence model: **Q-01**. |
| A **credited non-owner** (session player, no share) knows about the sample | **Permitted to declare; not permitted to resolve.** They are frequently the person who knows ("that loop's off a pack, I was in the room") and the platform runs on knowledge, not standing. Widens the abuse surface into Q-01's contest path — accepted. (D-10) | Declarer: normal flow. Owners: "[Name], credited as [role], declared…" — the declarer's standing is stated, because it is how co-owners weigh the claim. |
| Fingerprint suggests a sample that isn't one (false positive) | **Never auto-declares.** A suggestion the human rejects, with the rejection recorded so the same suggestion is not re-raised on the same work. | "We think we hear [track] at 0:42. Is that a sample?" → "No" is a first-class answer |
| Encumbrance declared *after* a licence has issued | Licence stands — licences are irrevocable instruments (D-06, domain 11 D-06). Creates a dispute (domain 24) and a platform liability question. Future licences for affected scopes stop immediately. | Buyer: "A defect was declared in a work you licensed. Your licence is unaffected." — and then the hard part, see parent Q-01 / Q-04 |
| Nobody declares anything — silence | **Silence must not mean clean.** A work with no declaration and no attestation is not one-stop. No hard gate on release (11.05.01 D-02) — a gate manufactures documented lies, which are worse than honest silence. | "No sample declaration — this work can't be one-stop. [Declare]" |
| The sample source is itself encumbered | Taint is transitive; the walk handles chains and cycles (A samples B samples A is normal in remix culture) | "Encumbered via [B] via [C]" — the chain named, not just the nearest node |
| A loop from a sample pack (Splice-style) is used **within** the pack licence's terms | **Not an encumbrance.** It is a `cleared-evidenced` source — the pack licence *is* the instrument, and clearing it is what the pack was for. (D-11 — resolves the breadth pass's deferred marker on this row) | Nothing. Silence here is correct: the flow must not make a normal, licensed production look defective. |
| The same loop is used **outside** the pack licence's terms — e.g. the work's stems are licensed for remix (11.05.05) or sold as a stem pack (14), exposing the loop in isolated reusable form, which most pack licences forbid | **`restricted-source-licence` encumbrance, scoped to exactly those uses.** The work stays one-stop for record and sync; it is encumbered for stem/remix/redistribution scopes only. This is D-08 applied to a source licence rather than an owner policy. (D-11) | "This track is one-stop for sync. It is **not** clearable for stem licensing — [Pack] forbids the loop being redistributed in isolated form." |
| Pack purchased on WeJammin (domain 14) | The platform sold it and knows the terms. The source self-declares with its licence attached and the scope evaluation is automatic. **The consolidation dividend the thesis predicts** — and the first time "did I clear that loop?" is answerable. (11.05.01 Q-02) | "3 loops from [Pack], bought here 2027-04-02. Cleared for sync and record; not for stem licensing." |
| Pack obtained off-platform, terms unknown | The platform cannot evaluate a licence it cannot read. **Q-06.** | — |
| Declarer says "cleared" and has no document | `cleared-asserted`, not `cleared-evidenced`. Taint lifts for computation purposes but the grade is carried into the verdict and visible to the owner. | "Recorded as cleared on your word. [Upload the licence] to make this verifiable — buyers and the gate treat evidenced clearances differently." |
| The sample's clearance **expires** | Work re-encumbers on the expiry date with no user action; status → `lapsed`. Issued licences stand (D-06); new issuance for affected scopes stops. **Owner is warned before the date, not after.** | 90 / 30 / 7 days out: "Your clearance for [source] ends [date]. [N] of your works go non-one-stop for [scopes] that day. [Renew via 11.05.03]" |
| A buyer requests a 10-year sync on a work whose sample clearance has 3 years left | **Gate refuses** — the requested scope exceeds the upstream intersection (D-05). It does not silently issue and hope. | Buyer: "Available for up to 3 years, not 10 — the underlying sample clearance ends [date]." Naming the ceiling is the product; a bare refusal is not. |
| A clearance is granted for scope X; buyer requests scope Y | Work is `one-stop` for X and `encumbered` for Y **simultaneously**. Both are true. | "Clear for podcast. Not clear for film — the sample was never cleared for audiovisual use." |
| Producer samples **their own earlier work**, co-owned with three other people | **An encumbrance.** It feels clean ("it's my track") and it is not — the 2019 band track is co-owned, and sampling it needs the same permission as sampling anyone. But the source is on-platform, the owners are enumerated, and the programmatic route (11.05.02) opens instantly. **The case WeJammin is uniquely able to fix.** (DT-07) | "This samples [your 2019 track] — Ana, Dev and Priya co-own it. [Request clearance] — usually minutes, they're all here." |
| Declarer retracts ("actually it wasn't a sample") | The declaration is **superseded, never deleted**. The retraction is itself an attributable act with a named declarer and timestamp. Otherwise the pattern is declare → panic → delete → clean record, and the audit trail is worthless. (D-09; consistent with 11.02.02 D-01 and domain CX P-02) | "Retracting is recorded, like declaring. Both stay on the work's history." Co-owners notified of the retraction as they were of the declaration. |
| Two co-owners declare the same encumbrance independently | Deduped to one encumbrance with two named declarers. **This strengthens it** — it is the counter-attestation asymmetry from 11.02.02 applied here, and a doubly-declared encumbrance is far harder to contest as spite. | "Declared by [A] and [B]." |
| Declaration lands mid-flight in an 11.02.04 consent round | The round's premise changed. Round is **invalidated, not silently continued** — parties consented to a work that is no longer the work they were shown. | Parties: "The request you were asked about has changed — a defect was declared. Your answer was not recorded." Initiator: told immediately. |
| Declaration lands while the work is under an active hold or a live sync pitch (11.01.04) | Availability changes; **buyer and pitcher must both be notified.** A silent availability change is the fail-open case. This is also the spite vector at its most valuable — a rival's live deal dies at declaration time, and the contest path is slower than the deal. | Buyer: "This work is no longer clearable for your scope: [chain]." Owner: "Your pitch was withdrawn." Contest handling: **Q-01**. |
| The blast radius is enormous — a producer's drum loop is in 400 works | Everyone in the exposed set is told; per `#CX-02` the exposed set *is* the notification set, and shrinking it to reduce noise fails open. One message per owner listing all their affected works keeps 400 works from becoming 400 notifications. | "A loop you used in 6 of your tracks is encumbered. [List]" |
| Derivation traversal times out on a deep chain | Fail closed. Verdict `unknown` → rendered as not clearable (11.02.01 D-02). Latency budget is not this feature's to set — routed to `/create-prd-compile` (domain index, Not-Product Concerns). | "Can't verify what this is made of — not offered as clearable." |
| The work has **no derivation record at all** (uploaded, not created in a session) | The machine has no answer to "what is this made of?" and declaration is the only input. This is the back-catalogue case, and per D-07 it is **most of the catalogue at cold start** — the feature's coverage is inversely proportional to its need. | "We weren't in the room for this one. Everything here is what you tell us." |
| A descendant exists but its owner set is unresolvable (domain 09 failure) | Cannot notify. Descendant fails closed and the un-notifiable owner is named as a gap, never silently skipped. | Declarer: "1 downstream work is affected but we can't resolve its owners — it's held as encumbered." |
| Declarer's account is deleted (GDPR erasure) after declaring | The declaration survives the identity — it is a fact about the work, not about the person. Attribution degrades to a tombstone. The taint does not lift because the declarer left. | "Declared by a since-removed account on [date]." Interacts with 11.02.01 Q-03. |
| The encumbered **ancestor work is deleted** by its owner | Descendant taint does **not** lift. Deleting the record of what you sampled does not un-sample it. The chain names a removed node rather than losing the link. | "Encumbered via a work that has been removed. The clearance requirement stands." |
| Malicious input in a free-text source description (URL, markup, injection) | Source description is untrusted text: escaped on render, never linked, never fetched by the platform. A source description is a claim, not an address. (D-15) | Rendered as plain text. |
| The multi-hyphenate declares on a work where they are also a co-owner | They are both declarer and affected party. They are **not** notified of their own declaration. Their co-owner view shows the encumbrance as authored by them. | No self-notification. |
| An AI stem generator's output is in the mix, model training provenance unknown | `provenance-unknown-generative` encumbrance — the *borrowed-and-unpaid* half is genuinely unknown, and unknown is not clean (D-02 generalised). The *uncopyrightability* half is an ownership fact and belongs to domain 09, not here. (DT-11) | "You marked this as AI-generated. We can't tell what the model learned from — this is declared as unknown provenance, not as clean." |
| Sampled party is on-platform and would learn of an unreleased work from the declaration | **Declaration does not notify the sampled party.** Declaring is not claiming, and a notification here would both leak an unreleased work and hand the sampled party a lawsuit for a track that may never come out. They are told when the owner initiates resolution (11.05), which is the owner's choice. (D-08 — resolves the product half of 11.05.01 Q-03) | Nothing to the sampled party. Declarer: "Declaring doesn't tell [Owner] anything. Clearing does — that's your call, when you're ready." |
| The encumbered work is already live on DSPs via domain 12 | Distribution is not withdrawn (no hard gate — 11.05.01 D-02). But the platform now holds a written record of a declared uncleared sample on a work it also distributes. **The platform's own exposure, not the user's.** | Owner: "This is live on [DSPs]. A declared uncleared sample is what Content ID claims are made of — [resolve]." Platform posture: **Q-05.** |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fingerprint analysis running, or a derivation-graph walk in flight | "Checking what this is made of." Runs in background; **declaration is never blocked on it**. Deep chains are genuinely slow — do not fake instantaneity (mirrors 11.02.01). |
| Empty | No declaration made | **"Nothing declared" — not "clean".** The distinction is the entire feature. The empty state is also the sales pitch: "This can't be one-stop until someone says what's in it. [Declare]" |
| Populated | One or more encumbrances attached | Per encumbrance: type, source, status **with its grade** (`cleared-evidenced` vs `cleared-asserted`), the cleared scope where one exists, the resolution route, the named chain, and the blast radius (N downstream works, listed). Per scope: which scopes this work is still one-stop for. |
| Error | Graph walk failed, or fan-out could not complete | **Fail closed.** This work and every descendant the walk could not reach are treated as encumbered until it succeeds. Never degrades to optimistic. "Can't verify — held as not clearable." |
| Partial | (a) Declared, resolution in flight via 11.05 · (b) Declared as `unidentified-source` · (c) Cleared for some scopes, not others · (d) `cleared-asserted` with no instrument | (a) "Clearing — [source], requested [date]" with 11.05's ETA. (b) "Unidentified source at 0:42, 4 bars — encumbered." **A legitimate terminal state, not a failure** (11.05.01 D-03). (c) "One-stop for podcast · encumbered for film." (d) "Cleared on your word — not verifiable." |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Audio fingerprinting can detect samples automatically, so declaration should be automatic and the human step is friction | Content ID precedent; also the appealing "the platform just knows" story | ❌ REJECTED — twice over. (1) Fingerprinting catches recognisable recorded samples; it cannot hear an interpolation played by a session musician, a topline written by an uncredited friend, or a chord progression lifted from a voice note. The undetectable cases are the ones that produce lawsuits. (2) An auto-declared encumbrance lets a **false positive poison a catalogue with no human in the loop** — and encumbrance is a downward-propagating taint, so one bad match damages every derivative. Fingerprint suggests; human declares. |
| DT-02 | Encumbrance is really a domain 09 (Rights) concern — it is a defect in the ownership record | Boundary challenge: why does licensing own this? | ❌ REJECTED — an encumbrance is not an ownership fact. Domain 09 answers "who owns this work"; the answer can be complete, correct and unanimous while the work remains unlicensable because of what it is *made of*. Composition (`what is this made of?`) and ownership (`who owns it?`) are orthogonal graphs, and only licensing needs both traversed together. It sits here. |
| DT-03 | The declaring persona and the harmed persona are the same person, at different times | Direct reading of personas.md: the Musician's worst *accidental* behaviour is exposing collaborators via an undeclared sample; the Musician is also the one exposed when a collaborator does it to them | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it dictates the tone of the entire feature. This is not an enforcement surface aimed at a bad actor; it is a **safety net for a well-intentioned person who does not know they are creating a liability**. That means: ask directly and early (not buried in an advanced panel), never punish the declaration, make declaring cheaper than not declaring, and route straight to resolution. A feature designed as compliance policing would suppress the very declarations it needs. |
| DT-04 | Encumbrance is a boolean taint on a work — either something is uncleared or it isn't | The file's own prior framing, and how the concept is usually described | ❌ REJECTED — encumbrance inherits domain D-08 and is per-**(source, scope)**. A music-industry expert would expect this immediately and nobody in the sub-domain had stated it: **the most common real-world sample failure after outright non-clearance is that the clearance you hold does not cover the use you were just offered.** You clear a sample for the record; a sync request arrives; the audiovisual right was never granted; you are back at the label's door with a deadline. Clearances are also configuration-limited ("this album, not compilations") — the greatest-hits problem is decades old and still catches people. So a work is `one-stop` for one scope and `encumbered` for another **at the same time**, and any UI rendering a red badge is lying about half of it. Forces D-05 (scope-intersection invariant) and makes 11.08.01's scope grammar a hard dependency of this feature. |
| DT-05 | Declaring "it's cleared" is sufficient — the taint lifts and the work is licensable | The natural reading of the prior file: declaration collects `cleared: yes/no` | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the largest hole in the feature as previously specified. "Cleared" typed by the owner is **exactly as unfalsifiable as declaring nothing**, but it has the opposite effect: silence costs you one-stop status, while an unevidenced "cleared" *buys* it. That inverts the incentive the whole sub-domain runs on and makes the lazy path the wrong path — the precise failure personas.md warns about for the Producer. Every producer would learn within a week to type "cleared" and the feature would become a checkbox. Fix: `cleared-evidenced` vs `cleared-asserted` (D-06). This is the same insight as 11.02.02 DT-03's *captured vs reconstructed* grade, arriving from a second direction — which makes parent Q-02 (confidence gradient) look less like a preference and more like a structural requirement. |
| DT-06 | Once resolved, an encumbrance stays resolved | Implicit in "encumbrance clears on licence execution → clearance recomputes upward" | ❌ REJECTED — clearances **lapse**. Term-limited sample licences are the normal shape of a low-budget clearance, and a work that is one-stop in 2027 re-encumbers in 2032 with nobody touching it. Consequences nobody had designed for: (1) clearance state is time-dependent and needs the **Scheduled Jobs / Time-Triggered State** cross-cut, which this feature had not claimed; (2) the owner must be warned *before* the date, because after it their catalogue silently left the one-stop pool; (3) most sharply — the gate must **refuse to issue a licence whose term outlives the upstream clearance** (D-05), or the platform sells a 10-year sync backed by 3 years of rights and cannot revoke it (D-06). Without this, D-09's fail-closed principle holds at issuance time and quietly fails four years later. |
| DT-07 | Sampling your own back-catalogue is clean — it's your work | The instinct every producer has, and the reason this case is litigated | ❌ REJECTED as a fact, ✅ CONFIRMED as the feature's best moment. Your 2019 band track is co-owned by four people; lifting its drum break into your 2027 solo release needs their permission exactly as much as lifting James Brown's. Band members sampling old band material is a well-worn dispute. But this is also the one case where **both works are in the registry**: owners enumerated, reachable, policy-covered, resolvable through 11.05.02 in minutes rather than months. It is a genuine expression of D-18 — the platform can only fix this because it was in the room for both works. The flow should treat it as the showcase, not the edge case. |
| DT-08 | A declaration is purely a fact about a work — it has no consequence for the platform itself | Implicit framing; the file's Q-02 asks about the *declarer's* liability but not the platform's | ✅ CONFIRMED as a live structural risk, escalated. A declaration is a written, timestamped, attributable record that a specific work contains uncleared material — and the same platform hosts that work, may distribute it to DSPs (domain 12), and sells licences to it. **Intermediary safe-harbour regimes generally turn on the host not having actual knowledge of infringing material.** This feature manufactures actual knowledge, at scale, as its core function. The agent will not assert what the legal outcome is — that is a lawyer's call across at least US/EU/UK regimes and it is exactly the kind of confident fabrication this file must not contain. But the *structure* is not in doubt: **the platform's single best integrity mechanism may also be its single largest liability generator, and nobody has looked at it from that side.** This may outrank Q-02 (the declarer's liability), because the declarer can choose not to declare; the platform cannot choose not to know. → **Q-05**. |
| DT-09 | The sampled party should be able to attach an encumbrance to a work that sampled them — they are the injured party and they know | Symmetry instinct: the victim knows more than the offender in the cases that end in court | ❌ REJECTED — that is not a declaration, it is an **allegation**, and the two must not share an object. A declaration is a confession about your own work: self-incriminating, therefore credible, therefore needing no adjudication. A third-party assertion is a claim: adversarial, therefore requiring evidence, notice, and a right of reply. If a stranger can attach a downward-propagating taint to your catalogue by assertion, the platform has built a weaponised takedown with no due process — and Content ID's abuse history is the entire precedent for how that ends. Third-party assertions belong to domain 24 (disputes) and 11.06.03 (claim release). The distinction is also what keeps the declaration flow safe enough that people use it. |
| DT-10 | Buyers should see the encumbrance detail — they are the ones taking the risk, and hiding it is dishonest | Transparency instinct, and it has real force: fail-closed means the buyer is being refused something | ❌ REJECTED for the detail, ✅ CONFIRMED for the verdict. The buyer must see `encumbered` — 11.02.01's fail-closed verdict already guarantees that, and it is what protects them. The buyer must **not** see "contains an uncleared sample of [Track X by Major Label] at 0:42". That sentence is an infringement admission published to a stranger, and it is a finished map for the sampled party's lawyer — served by the platform that persuaded the user to write it. The buyer's legitimate need is "can I license this, for my use, and when" — which the verdict, the named blocker class, and the time-to-clear satisfy without publishing the confession. Detail is scoped to the owner set and the credited set. → D-13. |
| DT-11 | AI-generated audio in a mix is an encumbrance | 2026 reality: generative stems are in ordinary sessions and no node in this domain covers the *buy* side of it (11.07 covers consenting your work *into* a corpus, the sell side) | ✅ CONFIRMED, split in two — and the split is DT-02's own logic reused. (1) **Provenance**: a generative element whose model's training corpus is unknown is material that may be borrowed and unpaid, and *unknown is not clean* (D-02 generalised). That is an encumbrance of type `provenance-unknown-generative`, and it belongs here. (2) **Copyrightability**: whether a generated portion is ownable at all is an *ownership* fact — it changes what domain 09 can record, not what this work is made of. That is not this feature's, and pulling it in here would repeat exactly the boundary error DT-02 rejected. The agent takes no position on which jurisdictions hold what; the type exists so the fact is captured rather than guessed at. Feeds domain Q-06 (does WeJammin train on user audio?) from the opposite direction — the platform is about to hold a labelled record of which works contain generative material. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Fingerprinting suggests; humans declare. No automatic encumbrance. | Per DT-01 — a false positive propagates downward through every derivative with nobody in the loop |
| D-02 | Silence is not cleanliness — an undeclared work is not one-stop | Silence-means-clean would make the whole feature opt-in for exactly the people least likely to opt in. Generalises: **unknown is not clean** (applied again in DT-11). |
| D-03 | Declaration and resolution are separate features | Per Behavior — if declaring immediately starts a chore, people stop declaring, and the platform loses the fact it most needs |
| D-04 | The feature is a safety net, not an enforcement surface | Per DT-03 — the harm is accidental and the declarer is the victim's future self |
| D-05 | **A downstream licence's scope can never exceed the intersection of every upstream clearance's granted scope. The gate refuses anything larger, and names the ceiling.** | Per DT-04 + DT-06, and forced by D-08 (per-(work, scope)) + D-09 (fail closed) + domain D-06 (licences are irrevocable). Selling a 10-year sync on 3 years of underlying rights is selling something unrevocable that the platform cannot honour. |
| D-06 | Cleared status carries a grade: `cleared-evidenced` (the platform can read the instrument) vs `cleared-asserted` (the declarer's word) | Per DT-05 — without the grade, an unevidenced "cleared" is a free pass that *buys* one-stop status, inverting the incentive the sub-domain runs on. Same insight as 11.02.02 DT-03's captured-vs-reconstructed grade, reached independently. |
| D-07 | An encumbrance is a **confession about your own work**, never an accusation about someone else's | Per DT-09 — a third-party assertion is a claim, and claims need evidence, notice and reply. Conflating them builds a weaponised takedown. |
| D-08 | **Declaring does not notify the sampled party.** They are told when the owner initiates resolution (11.05). | Notifying on declaration leaks an unreleased work and hands the sampled party a lawsuit over a track that may never come out. Producers will not declare if declaring leaks — resolves the product half of 11.05.01 Q-03. |
| D-09 | A retraction supersedes; it never deletes. Both the declaration and the retraction are attributable and both survive. | Otherwise the pattern is declare → panic → delete → clean record, and the audit trail is decorative. Consistent with 11.02.02 D-01 and the domain's "the old record always survives" (domain Q-07). |
| D-10 | A **credited contributor may declare; only an owner may resolve** | Per the Behavior boundary — the feature runs on knowledge, and the person who knows is often the session player with no share. Accepted cost: it widens the abuse surface, which Q-01's contest path must absorb. |
| D-11 | **Pack/plugin licence restrictions are scope-conditional encumbrances, not blanket ones.** A loop used within its pack licence is `cleared-evidenced` and produces no encumbrance. The encumbrance exists only for the scopes the pack licence excludes — typically stem/remix redistribution, and increasingly AI-training use. | **Resolves the breadth pass's deferred marker on this row, and narrows Q-03.** The either/or framing ("in scope or over-reach") was a false choice: treating it as blanket would make most modern productions non-one-stop for everything, which is absurd; treating it as out of scope leaves the one case that is genuinely broken — selling stems containing a pack loop — silently wrong. D-08's per-(work, scope) shape dissolves it. A pack licence is a policy attached to a *source* rather than to an owner, and it constrains downstream scope the same way. |
| D-12 | The attestation boundary: **a person who should be in the party list is an attestation gap (11.02.02); material that came from another work is an encumbrance (here).** The declaration flow must ask a question that separates them. | The topline case sits exactly on the line and would otherwise land in whichever feature the user happened to be in. `#CX-05` treats these as two halves of one ritual — which only works if the halves are cleanly cut. |
| D-13 | Buyers see the **verdict**, never the detail. Detail is scoped to the owner set and the credited set. | Per DT-10 — "contains an uncleared sample of [X] at 0:42" published to a stranger is an infringement admission the platform talked its user into writing. |
| D-14 | Bad-news fan-out is all-or-nothing (unreached ⇒ encumbered); good-news fan-out may lag | Asymmetric costs, per D-09. A lagging clearance costs a delayed sale; a lagging taint sells an unhonourable licence. |
| D-15 | A free-text source description is untrusted: escaped on render, never linked, never fetched | A source description is a claim, not an address. Rendering it as a link makes the declaration form an injection surface and an SSRF vector. |
