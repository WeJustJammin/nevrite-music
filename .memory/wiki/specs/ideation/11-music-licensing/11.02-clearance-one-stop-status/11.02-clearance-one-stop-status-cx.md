## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Completeness Attestation ↔ Clearance Computation

**Relationship**: Computation walks the rights graph and can prove a closed arithmetic fact — the known
shares sum to 100%. It cannot prove the *set* is complete. A work with two writers on WeJammin whose splits
sum to 100% looks identical to a work with three writers where the third has never heard of the platform.
Attestation is the human assertion that closes that gap, and it is the only input to the one-stop flag that
cannot be computed from anything else.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: attests the writer list is closed for their work; the attestation is attributable to them by name
- **Producer**: attests for the master side; per personas.md they are "the person who *knows* who played what" — the platform's best-informed attester, and the reason counter-attestation is worth more than self-claim
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The attestation is a distinct record from the split graph, not a flag on it. If a new writer is later added, the prior attestation is not *edited* — it is invalidated and superseded, because the old attestation remains evidence of what someone claimed and when. This is the provenance principle applied reflexively.
2. **Trigger chain**: Attestation → recompute one-stop → catalogue availability changes. Adding a co-writer invalidates attestation → work silently leaves the one-stop pool. **If it leaves while under a hold or a live pitch, both must be notified.** An availability change that nobody is told about is the fail-open case.
3. **Permission intersection**: Can one co-owner attest for all of them? This is the crux — see Q-01 in 11.02.02. A single-party attestation is fast and is exactly how a bad actor manufactures a one-stop; unanimous attestation is honest and rarely achievable.
4. **Notification fan-out**: An attestation should notify the other co-owners — "X asserted this is the complete writer list; contest?" That converts an unfalsifiable claim into a socially checked one at near-zero cost, and it is the platform's cheapest integrity mechanism.
5. **State transition conflict**: An attestation and a co-writer addition can race. The addition must win — always. Fail toward "not one-stop"; the cost of a false negative is a lost sale, the cost of a false positive is a lawsuit naming the platform.

### CX-02: Encumbrance Declaration ↔ Clearance Computation

**Relationship**: Encumbrance is a poison that flows downward. A work containing an uncleared sample is not
clearable regardless of how perfect its own split graph is — and every derivative of it inherits the defect.
Computation must therefore walk *upstream* through the derivation graph, not just across the local ownership
set. This is the difference between "who owns this?" and "what is this made of?", and only the second one
catches the failure personas.md actually names.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sees that their work is encumbered and by what; per personas.md this is their worst accidental behaviour — the design should catch it *for* them, not punish them after
- **Producer**: the declaring party in practice — they hold the stems and are the only one who knows what is in the mix
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The encumbrance belongs to the work, but its *effect* belongs to every downstream derivative. The derivation graph is owned by domain 09/07; this sub-domain reads it. Two owners, one traversal.
2. **Trigger chain**: Declaring an encumbrance must recompute clearance for the work **and every descendant**. If that fan-out fails partially, some descendants remain falsely clearable — the worst possible failure. Must be all-or-nothing, or must fail toward "not clearable" for anything it could not reach.
3. **Permission intersection**: One collaborator's declaration encumbers a work that others co-own — it reduces *their* asset's value without their consent. That is correct (the truth is the truth) but it is also an abuse vector: a spiteful collaborator can poison a work by false declaration. Needs a contest path. See 11.02.03 Q-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Every co-owner and every downstream derivative owner must be told. Per personas.md the harm "exposes every collaborator downstream" — the notification set is exactly the exposed set.
5. **State transition conflict**: An encumbrance declared *after* a licence has issued does not revoke the licence (licences are irrevocable instruments, 11.08). It creates a dispute (domain 24) and a liability question. Escalated to 11.02 Q-01.

### CX-05: Completeness Attestation ↔ Encumbrance Declaration

**Relationship**: These are the same ritual pointed in two directions. Attestation says "nobody is missing
from this work's ownership"; encumbrance declaration says "nothing in this work is borrowed and unpaid".
Both are facts that only a human present at creation can supply, both are unfalsifiable at the moment of
assertion, and both are catastrophic when false. They should therefore share a moment (the session close,
while everyone is still present and friendly — personas.md's "moment that matters") and share a consequence
model.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: per personas.md, "the design must make the lazy path the correct path" — this pair is the concrete test of that principle. Both assertions must be captured as a byproduct of closing a session, not as a separate compliance errand.
- **Musician**: co-signs; the counter-attestation is what makes it worth more than a self-claim
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan**: not affected

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Distinct records, one ritual. Do not merge into a single "work is clean" boolean — they fail independently and are contested independently.
2. **Trigger chain**: Both fire on session close (domain 07). If either is skipped, the work is not one-stop — which is the *correct* default and the honest one.
3. **Permission intersection**: Both are attributable assertions. Whoever signs, owns the consequence. That is the entire deterrent, and it is why the identity (domain 01) must be real.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both notify the co-owner set for counter-attestation or contest.
5. **State transition conflict**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether a work can be one-stop with attestation but no encumbrance declaration (i.e. is silence on sampling equivalent to "no samples"?). **Silence must not mean clean.** Provisional: both required.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 11.02.02 Attestation | 11.02.04 Consent Routing | Considered: is attesting completeness a consent request routed to co-owners? Rejected — an attestation is a *claim about a fact*, a consent is a *decision about a permission*. They have different failure modes (a false claim vs a withheld permission), different remedies (contest vs escalate), and different actors (the knower vs the owner). Conflating them would make "I confirm nobody is missing" and "I agree to this deal" the same object, which is how consent theatre gets built. The relationship they do have runs through computation (CX-01, CX-03), not directly. |
| R-02 | 11.02.01 Computation | 11.02.03 Encumbrance | Directional only — encumbrance writes, computation reads (CX-02). Computation never writes an encumbrance back; inferring an encumbrance from audio fingerprinting is tempting but belongs in 11.05.01 (sample declaration) as a *suggestion*, never as an automatic assertion. An auto-declared encumbrance would let a false fingerprint match poison a catalogue with no human in the loop. |
