# Feature: Clearance Computation, One-Stop Determination & Gate

> **Number**: 11.02.01
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turns the rights registry into a licensing verdict: given a work and a proposed usage scope, can it be
licensed, **by whom**, and what specifically is in the way. It produces the **one-stop flag** — the single
signal that makes WeJammin's catalogue worth searching — and it owns the **gate** that refuses issuance when
the verdict says no. This is the point where provenance (the wedge, D-18) becomes money.

It is a **projection**, not a source. It owns no ownership facts, no attestations, no encumbrances and no
policies. Its entire product is the *combination* of those four, computed against one scope, and the routing
decision that follows: not merely "no", but "no, because of this named thing, which this named person fixes
by doing this". A verdict that says only "no" is the incumbent experience with lower latency.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees each of their works' verdict **per scope**, decomposed per rights side ("clearable on the master, blocked on the composition"), with every active blocker *named* and routed ("Sarah hasn't confirmed her 25% writer share → [Ask Sarah]"; "contains an undeclared sample → [Declare]"). Sees when **they are the blocker themselves** — the multi-hyphenate holding a writer share that auto-approves and a master share that vetoes blocks their own work, and nothing else in the platform would tell them. **Cannot override the computation** — an owner-overridable clearance flag is a self-assertion, which is the thing the platform exists to replace. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, plus the **delivery view**: one screen answering "is what I am about to hand my client actually clearable, and for what?" across every work in a deliverable, not one at a time. Per personas.md they deliver "with metadata intact" — this is the check that makes that claim true rather than hopeful. They are also the party who *eats* a bad verdict discovered late: an encumbered delivery is their professional liability, not the sampler's (11.02.03 Role Lens). |

## Behavior

Clearance is computed **per (work, scope, rights side)**, never as a global property of a work (D-08, domain).
"Clearable" is meaningless without a usage — a work may be freely licensable for a podcast and refused for a
political ad because a co-writer set a category block (11.04.02). A scope is a point in the 11.08.01 grammar
(media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage, scale); an unset axis is `unspecified`, never permissive
(11.08.01 D-05). **No scope, no verdict** (D-14).

The verdict is a function of five inputs:

1. **The consent graph** (domain 09) — the parties whose approval the licence needs. **This is not the share
   graph** (D-07, DT-05): it errs in both directions if taken literally.
2. **The completeness attestation** (11.02.02) — is the party set actually closed, or merely arithmetically closed
3. **The encumbrance set** (11.02.03) — is anything upstream tainted, walked through the derivation graph
4. **The effective work policy** (11.04.03) — the fold of every co-owner's policy and veto for this scope.
   Consumes the aggregate, never the individual policies.
5. **The required rights sides** (D-06) — derived from the scope, not assumed

### The verdict

Each required side yields a side verdict. The combined verdict is the **most terminal** of the sides:

| Verdict | Meaning | Remedy owner (D-09) |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown` | The system cannot resolve the graph, or the registry is internally inconsistent | Domain 09 — escalation, not a user task |
| `blocked` | A standing policy or veto refuses this scope | **None.** Terminal for this scope. |
| `encumbered` | Upstream taint — something borrowed and unpaid, in this work or an ancestor | 11.05 (sample clearance) |
| `incomplete` | The record is not closed — shares do not sum, or no valid completeness attestation exists | 11.02.02 (attest) |
| `consent-needed` | Licensable after N named consents; no policy either permits or refuses | 11.02.04 (consent routing) |
| `clearable-now` | Every required side is clean, attested, unencumbered, and policy-permitted for this scope | — |

**Precedence** (D-08): `unknown > blocked > encumbered > incomplete > consent-needed > clearable-now`. The
order is by **terminality, not severity** — the point is never to send someone to do work that cannot help.
Telling an owner to spend six weeks clearing a sample for a scope a co-writer has permanently vetoed is a
cruelty the precedence exists to prevent. The dominant verdict is what is *displayed*; **every** active
blocker is still listed (D-05), because the owner needs to know the whole distance to clearable, not the
first step.

**`one-stop` is not a verdict.** It is a separate derived boolean, true only when the combined verdict is
`clearable-now` **and** the buyer transacts with exactly one counterparty (D-10, DT-06). A fourteen-writer
work where all fourteen pre-cleared is `clearable-now` with fourteen counterparties — which is an excellent
verdict and is **not** what a music supervisor means by one-stop.

The gate is the enforcement face of the same verdict: 11.08.02 cannot issue a certificate the gate refuses.
One definition of "clearable", one place, read by everyone.

### Happy Path

1. A scope is proposed — a buyer's search filter, a brief's requirement, a quote request, or a licence attempt. It is expressed in the 11.08.01 grammar. If no scope is supplied, the system returns **no verdict** and asks "clearable for what?" — it never substitutes a default scope (D-14).
2. System derives the **required rights sides** from the scope: a sync needs master + composition; a cover needs composition only (11.09); a print/lyric licence needs composition only (11.10); a sample needs both (11.05); AI training needs both (11.07). Defects on a side the scope does not require are ignored (D-06).
3. For each required side, system resolves the **consent graph** from the registry (09) — administered shares resolve to the administrator, not the writer; unreleased performers are counterparties at 0% share; credited-but-shareless parties are **not** counterparties (D-07).
4. For each required side, system evaluates in order: shares close → a valid, uncontested completeness attestation exists (11.02.02) → no encumbrance in this work or any ancestor (11.02.03) → the effective work policy (11.04.03 fold) permits this scope.
5. Each side yields a side verdict; the combined verdict is the most terminal (D-08).
6. Verdict returns as a record, not a word: dominant state · **full** blocker list, each named and routed · counterparty count (distinct humans/entities, multi-hyphenates counted once — D-07) · time-to-clear, only where derivable (D-15).
7. Verdict renders inline wherever the work appears. In search it is **advisory and timestamped "as of"** (D-16); at the gate it is authoritative.
8. On a licence attempt the gate **re-evaluates fresh** (D-04) — never on the cached verdict a buyer saw an hour ago. If the scope is exclusive, issuance excludes any concurrent exclusive attempt for the same (work, exclusivity scope) (D-12). Payment and issuance commit together (D-13).
9. `one-stop` is displayed only if the combined verdict is `clearable-now` **and** the counterparty count is exactly 1 (D-10). Otherwise the buyer sees `clearable-now` with the counterparty count stated.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Shares sum to 100% but no completeness attestation exists | Verdict = `incomplete`. **Arithmetic closure is not set closure** (DT-02). | "Splits add up, but nobody has confirmed the writer list is complete. [Attest]" |
| Shares sum to 97% | `incomplete`; the missing 3% is named as unallocated, not silently rounded | "3% unallocated — clearance needs it resolved" |
| Shares sum to 103% | Registry inconsistency; verdict = `unknown`, **not** `clearable-now`. Escalates to domain 09. | "Ownership records conflict — we can't say who to license from" |
| Registry resolution times out, or the derivation walk cannot complete | Verdict = `unknown`. **Fail closed** (D-02, domain D-09). | "Can't verify — not offered as clearable" |
| Cached search verdict says clearable; fresh gate check says blocked | Gate wins; issuance refused. The staleness is *expected*, not a bug — which is why search verdicts carry "as of" (D-16). | "This changed since you searched: [named blocker]" — the reason, never a bare denial |
| Work has 14 co-writers, all on-platform, all attested, all policy-permitted | `clearable-now`, **14 counterparties**. Not displayed as one-stop (D-10). Fourteen parties is not a blocker if they are enumerated and pre-cleared — but it is not one signature either. | "Clearable now — 14 rights holders, all pre-cleared for this scope" |
| **A writer's share is administered by an off-platform publisher** | The registry says Sarah holds 25%; Sarah **cannot consent** — her administrator can, and is unreachable. Verdict = `consent-needed` with an off-platform counterparty, regardless of how clean the splits are (DT-05). Routing Sarah a consent request would produce an approval from someone with no authority to give it — a void licence the platform asserted. | "Sarah's 25% is administered by [publisher], who isn't on WeJammin. We can't clear this here." |
| **An unreleased session performer holds 0% of the master** | They are a **counterparty at 0%** where performers' rights attach and no release exists (DT-05). Verdict = `consent-needed`, naming them. A 0% share is not the same fact as no standing. | "Dave played on this and never signed a release — his approval is needed" |
| **A credited-but-shareless party believes they have a veto** | They do not. Credit confers **no** clearance standing (D-07, resolves Q-02). They are not routed a consent request and cannot block. The platform states this **explicitly on the work**, per-work — never per-person, because the same human is a shareless performer on track A and a 25% writer on track B. | Credited party: "You're credited on this work. You don't hold a rights share in it, so licensing doesn't need your approval." |
| **Multi-hyphenate blocks their own work** | One human holds a writer share whose policy auto-approves ads and a master share whose policy vetoes them. Refusals are absolute (11.04.03 D-02) → `blocked`, and the named blocker **is them**. Counted as one counterparty, two stakes. Nothing else in the platform would surface this. | "This is blocked for ads — by your own master-side policy. Your writer-side auto-approve is inert here." |
| **Scope requires only one side** (a cover — 11.09) | Master-side defects are irrelevant and must not appear. A work with a catastrophic master and a clean composition is `clearable-now` for a cover (D-06). | Only composition-side blockers listed |
| **Encumbered AND blocked AND consent-needed at once** | Dominant = `blocked` (most terminal). All three blockers still listed (D-05). The owner is not sent to clear a sample for a scope that is permanently refused. | "Blocked for this use by [name]'s policy. Also: 1 uncleared sample, 2 consents outstanding — none of which would unblock it." |
| **Two buyers attempt an exclusive licence for the same (work, scope) concurrently** | Both fresh gate checks see no existing exclusive and would both pass. Second attempt is **refused, not queued** (D-12) — queueing makes a buyer wait for a certainty they can never get. Issuing both would create two contradictory *irrevocable* instruments (domain D-06), which no downstream remedy can undo (DT-08). | Second buyer: "Someone else exclusively licensed this while you were checking out. Nothing has been charged." |
| **Verdict changes between gate-pass and payment commit** (co-owner sets a veto in the window) | Transaction aborts; **the buyer is never charged for a licence that does not issue, and no licence issues without payment** (D-13). | Buyer: "This became unavailable during checkout — [named blocker]. You haven't been charged." |
| **Co-owner sets a veto seconds after a licence commits** | Licence stands — irrevocable instrument (domain D-06). Future verdicts change immediately. The veto-setter believes they blocked it and **must be told they did not**; nobody designs this notification and its absence produces a dispute. | Veto-setter: "Your block is live from now. A licence for this use issued 12 seconds before it took effect — it stands." |
| **Splits change after a licence issued** | Licence stands; payouts move (domain 10 — parent Q-03 owns the reconciliation). The verdict for *future* licences changes immediately. | Owner: "Your splits changed. Existing licences unaffected; new ones follow the new split." |
| **A co-writer's account is deleted (GDPR erasure)** | The **share survives; the identity does not.** The share cannot vanish — it would break the arithmetic and every issued licence that references it. It persists as a pseudonymous rights-holder handle with no personal data. Any scope requiring **fresh** consent from them is permanently `consent-needed` with an unreachable counterparty. Scopes already covered by their pre-erasure standing policy: the platform's behaviour is to **honour the policy**, on the reasoning that a policy is an instruction attached to a *share*, not to a person — but whether erasure law permits retaining it is a legal question, not an agent's call (Q-03). | Owner: "A rights holder in this work exercised erasure. Their 20% stands; their approval can't be sought. This work can't clear for uses needing a new consent." |
| **A co-writer is dead or permanently unreachable** | Same shape as erasure but with an estate that may exist. Permanently `consent-needed` unless an estate is enumerated in 09. This is common, not exotic (11.02.02 Q-04). | "This work needs [name]'s approval. We have no reachable party for that share." |
| **A named party has never onboarded** | Distinguish **"not on platform"** from **"on platform, silent"** — the first is a reachability problem (route via the owner who knows them), the second is a response problem (11.02.04). Collapsing them produces a chase aimed at nobody. | "Dave isn't on WeJammin. [Invite Dave]" vs "Dave hasn't responded since 14 Jul." |
| **Attestation is contested mid-gate** | Contest wins immediately; verdict → `incomplete`; issuance refused. Fail closed (11.02.02 CX-01 Q5 — the addition always wins). | Buyer: "The ownership record for this work is being contested. Not available right now." |
| **Encumbrance fan-out to descendants is mid-flight or partially failed** | Any work the walk could not reach is treated as `unknown` → not clearable (11.02.03 D-01, fail-closed). A partially-propagated encumbrance leaving descendants falsely clearable is the worst outcome in the sub-domain. | "Can't verify what this is made of — not offered as clearable" |
| **Derivation graph contains a cycle** (A samples B, B remixes A) | The walk terminates on cycle detection; the encumbrance set is the union along the path. **A cycle is not itself a defect** — it is normal in remix culture. If the walk cannot terminate within bounds, `unknown`. | "Encumbered via [B] via [C]" — the chain named, not just the nearest node |
| **Share is sold or assigned mid-flight** | The new owner replaces the old in both the consent graph and the fold. The verdict can change with **no policy having been edited and no split having moved** (11.04.03). | "This work's availability changed because a share changed hands" |
| **Buyer's real use straddles two media** (social **and** ad) | Scope is multi-value (11.08.01 D-04). The gate evaluates the **actual** scope, not the searched one — a work that passed for "social" can fail for "social+ad" if anyone blocks advertising. This is the most likely honest cause of a search→gate reversal. | "You searched social. This use is also an ad, which [blocker] refuses." |
| **Malicious input: a scope with an unknown or malformed axis value** | Rejected. **Never coerced to the nearest known value** — coercion silently licenses something nobody agreed to. Verdict = `unknown`. | "We don't recognise that use — we can't clear against it" |
| **Verdict probing to map who refuses what** | A buyer or competitor sweeps scopes across a catalogue to infer stances ("which artists refuse tobacco / gambling / political ads"). Named blockers (D-05) would disclose a named person's ethical and political positions to strangers. Naming is therefore **audience-scoped** (D-11): co-owners see who and why; buyers see only that a scope is unavailable. | Buyer: "Not available for this use." Co-owner: "[Name] blocked alcohol on 3 Jun." |
| **Cold start — no response history for any party** | No time-to-clear is shown for `consent-needed` (D-15). At launch (D-07) the platform genuinely does not know how fast anyone answers, and a fabricated ETA given to a supervisor with an air date is the worst possible first impression. | "Needs 3 approvals. We don't have enough history to estimate how long." |
| **A single-owner work** | **Not automatically one-stop** (DT-14). A solo writer-performer may still hold only the composition while a label holds the master, may have an undeclared sample, may have assigned admin, and may never have attested. Single owner ≠ single counterparty ≠ clearable. | Whatever the four inputs actually say — the shortcut is never taken |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Consent-graph resolution and derivation walk running | "Checking clearance" — deep credit graphs are genuinely slow to walk (routed to `/create-prd-compile` as a budget); **do not fake instantaneity**. A verdict that appears instantly and is wrong is worse than one that takes a beat. |
| Empty | Work not in the registry at all | Not a clearance state — the work does not exist here. Route to domain 09. Distinct from `unknown`, which means *we have it and cannot resolve it*. |
| Populated | Verdict computed for a supplied scope | Dominant verdict badge · every active blocker, named (audience-scoped, D-11) and routed to its remedy owner · counterparty count · time-to-clear where derivable · per-side decomposition where sides disagree |
| Error | Traversal, fold, or attestation check failed | Verdict = `unknown`, rendered as **not clearable**. Never degrade to optimistic (D-02). Distinguished from `blocked` in the UI — "we can't tell" and "the answer is no" are different facts and conflating them destroys trust in both. |
| Partial | One required side resolved, one pending | "Checking" — never `clearable-now`, never one-stop. The words are reserved for a complete, fresh, positive verdict across **every** required side. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | One-stop is a property of a **work** — compute it once, cache it, render a badge | The sweep's framing ("Clearance Status & One-Stop / Pre-Cleared Flag" reads as a work-level flag), and how every incumbent presents it | ❌ REJECTED — one-stop is a property of **(work, scope)**. A work that clears for a podcast and is blocked for a tobacco ad has no single truth value. A work-level flag would be right most of the time and catastrophically wrong exactly where the money and the offence are. This reframing propagates into 11.01.02 (results must be scope-aware), 11.04 (policy is per-category), and the whole of 11.06. **The sweep's candidate was subtly but materially wrong.** Ratified as domain D-08. |
| DT-02 | Splits summing to 100% proves the work is one-stop | The obvious arithmetic reading; also the reading that makes the domain's value proposition sound easy | ❌ REJECTED — it proves nothing about *missing* parties. Two writers claiming 50/50 on a song a third person co-wrote sums perfectly and is a lie. This is the load-bearing gap in the entire domain: the registry can verify arithmetic, never completeness. Forced the creation of 11.02.02 (D-02). **Without it the one-stop flag is unfalsifiable and the wedge is decorative.** |
| DT-03 | The verdict should fail open when the registry is slow or unreachable — a false "not clearable" costs a sale | Availability reasoning; a tempting product instinct under a latency budget | ❌ REJECTED — asymmetric costs. A false negative costs one sale. A false positive sells a licence the platform cannot honour, which is a lawsuit naming *the platform* as the party that asserted clearability. Fail closed, always, everywhere in this sub-domain. Ratified as domain D-09. |
| DT-04 | Owners will demand an override ("I know it's fine, let me sell it") and refusing them is user-hostile | Reasoning from the Musician persona's autonomy and their tolerance for professional-tool friction | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real pressure, ❌ REJECTED as a feature — an overridable clearance flag *is a self-assertion*, and self-assertion is precisely what Jaxsta/Muso.AI do and why the problem statement says they fail. The moment an owner can override, the badge means "someone claimed" rather than "the system verified", and the differentiator evaporates. The pressure is real and must be answered with a *fast consent path* (11.02.04), not an override. |
| DT-05 | The parties whose consent a licence needs are the parties who hold shares — the share graph **is** the consent graph | The assumption this feature was built on, and the one every "we hold the registry, so we can clear it" claim rests on | ✅ CONFIRMED as the assumption, ❌ REJECTED as true — **and it errs in both directions, which is why nobody catches it.** (1) **A share-holder who cannot consent**: most professional writers have assigned administration to a publisher. The registry says "Sarah, 25% writer"; Sarah has no authority to license it. Routing her a consent request yields an approval from someone who cannot give it — a **void licence the platform asserted**, which is worse than no licence. (2) **A non-share-holder who must consent**: a session performer with 0% of the master may hold performers' rights where no release was signed. A verdict computed from shares alone omits them entirely. The consent graph is `who controls`, not `who owns` — and domain 09 must model administration and performer releases as first-class facts or **every verdict this feature emits is unsound for signed writers and unreleased sessions**, which is most professional music. This also **sharpens D-07** (cold-start): the one-stop catalogue does not merely start empty and grow with platform age — it grows only among parties who *control their own rights*. A signed writer never becomes clearable here unless their publisher is on-platform. That is a cap, not a ramp, and the domain rationale does not acknowledge it. Resolves Q-02; opens Q-05. |
| DT-06 | "One-stop" means what this feature says it means — licensable now, by parties already enumerated | The feature's own inherited definition, and the domain rationale's claim that split-at-creation "MANUFACTURES one-stops" | ✅ CONFIRMED as the current definition, ❌ REJECTED as legitimate — **"one-stop" is an industry term of art and the platform is quietly redefining it in its own favour.** To a music supervisor, one-stop means *one entity controls both master and composition, so one negotiation and one signature clears the track*. It is a statement about **counterparty count**, not about cleanliness. Musicbed, Artlist and Songtradr are one-stops because they *acquired both sides*. A fourteen-writer work where all fourteen pre-cleared is an outstanding asset and is **not** one-stop — the supervisor who filters on the badge and receives it will feel lied to, and the badge is the domain's entire search proposition. **The finding underneath is larger than the naming**: a multi-party work becomes functionally one-stop **only if the platform itself is the single counterparty** — issuing the certificate, taking the fee, splitting it. Which makes WeJammin a **principal in the transaction, not a directory that publishes verdicts** — and that is the same question as parent Q-01 (warranty/indemnity/best-effort) arriving from a completely different direction. Q-01 is not a legal footnote about a badge; it is "what is the platform in the deal?". Opens Q-06. |
| DT-07 | The verdict is one value per (work, scope) | Direct extension of DT-01; the natural reading once scope-awareness is accepted | ❌ REJECTED — it must decompose **per required rights side**. Two reasons, both load-bearing. (1) **The remedies route differently**: a master-side defect goes to the label/producer, a composition-side defect goes to the writers and publishers. A combined "consent-needed" tells a Producer to chase, without telling them *whom* — which is the fragmentation the platform exists to end, reproduced inside the feature meant to end it. (2) **Not every scope needs both sides**: a cover needs composition only (11.09), a print/lyric licence needs composition only (11.10), a sample needs both (11.05). A single combined verdict marks a work with a messy master as unclearable for a cover it could clear today — **suppressing exactly the catalogue that survives cold-start** (domain Q-04 identifies cover/compulsory as one of the two nodes whose demand survives an empty registry). The single-value verdict would have hidden the domain's own beachhead. |
| DT-08 | A fresh gate check is sufficient to prevent an unhonourable licence | D-04's own logic, taken to its conclusion — if the verdict is fresh at issuance, the certificate reflects the truth | ❌ REJECTED — **freshness is not exclusion.** Two buyers attempt an exclusive licence for the same (work, exclusivity scope) simultaneously. Both gate checks are fresh. Both see no existing exclusive, because neither of the other's certificate exists yet. Both pass. The platform issues **two contradictory exclusive licences**, and licences are **irrevocable instruments** (domain D-06) — so there is no downstream remedy. Not a rare race: exclusivity is where the money is, holds (11.01.04) already contest the same slot, and deadline-driven buyers converge on the same tracks at the same time. The behavioural invariant is **at most one exclusive licence per (work, exclusivity scope) can ever exist** — the mechanism is architecture. This is the sharpest thing D-04 alone does not cover, and nothing in the sub-domain had named it. |
| DT-09 | Blockers should always be named — "not clearable" is useless, "Sarah hasn't confirmed her 25%" is the product | This feature's own D-05, inherited unqualified | ✅ CONFIRMED for co-owners, ❌ REJECTED as unqualified — **D-05 as written leaks.** The verdict is probeable: sweep scopes across a catalogue and the named blockers disclose which musicians refuse tobacco, gambling, alcohol, political advertising, or a named brand. Those are a person's ethical and political positions, published to any stranger who queries a licensing form, attached to their real name (identity is real by design, 11.02.02 D-03). The catalogue-enumeration concern is already routed to `/create-prd-security` as an NFR — but this half is **product**: it is a disclosure decision about a named individual, not a rate-limit. Naming must be **audience-scoped**: co-owners see who and why (they cannot negotiate with an anonymous constraint — 11.04.03 Q-02 recommends exactly this); buyers see that a scope is unavailable, full stop. |
| DT-10 | The verdict should carry an estimated time-to-clear — a buyer with an air date needs to know if this is a day or a month | The existing happy path asserted it without examining it; and it is genuinely what the buyer most wants | ✅ CONFIRMED as a need, ❌ REJECTED as specified — **an unexamined ETA is a fabrication, and it is a prediction about a human being the platform does not control.** Worse, it is the number a supervisor will rely on to commit an air date, which converts a guess into a liability that lands on Q-01. It is only honest where it is derived: `instant` for a scope fully covered by standing policy (11.04 — genuinely knowable, and the only path to real-time clearance per 11.02.04 DT-01); a per-party historical median where that party has responded enough times to have one; **"unknown" otherwise, said plainly.** And the cold-start consequence is specific: at launch **no party has a response history**, so the honest launch answer for every `consent-needed` verdict is "we don't know" — a direct, dated cost of D-07 on the feature the buyer cares most about. |
| DT-11 | Where most of a work is cleared, the platform should let a buyer license the cleared shares and proceed at their own risk | Not a hypothetical — this is **real industry practice**. Buyers routinely clear the master and "risk the publishing" on samples, with an indemnity, especially under deadline | ❌ REJECTED — and it is worth rejecting explicitly because a domain expert will propose it and it sounds pragmatic. **A partial licence to a work is not a licence to the work.** Using it still infringes the un-cleared share; the platform would be selling an instrument whose only function is to make an infringement feel authorised, and would be an accessory to every use of it. It also destroys the one asset the domain has: a badge that sometimes means "cleared" and sometimes means "cleared except the bit that will sue you" is worth less than no badge. The pressure is real and the answer to it is 11.02.04 (fast consent) and 11.04 (pre-set policy) — **the platform's whole thesis is that it can make asking cheap enough that risking becomes irrational.** Conceding partial licensing concedes the thesis. |
| DT-12 | Gate-pass is the commit point — once the gate says yes, the licence issues | The natural reading of "the gate refuses issuance"; the gate looks like a boolean check before an action | ❌ REJECTED — there is a window between gate-pass and commit, and money moves inside it. Two distinct failures live there. (1) **Verdict changes mid-checkout**: charge the buyer, then refuse to issue, and you have taken money for nothing; issue without charging and the rights holders are unpaid for an irrevocable instrument. The invariant is behavioural: **no licence without payment, no charge without a licence.** (2) **The "you were too late" case**: a co-owner sets a veto seconds after a licence commits. The licence stands (domain D-06). The veto-setter has no reason to suspect it and will discover it as a betrayal — the platform let a use through *after* they blocked it, from their point of view. **Nobody designs this notification**, and its absence manufactures a dispute (domain 24) out of a system working exactly as specified. Telling them costs one message; not telling them costs the relationship. |
| DT-13 | Works with an `unknown` verdict should be hidden from buyers entirely — fail closed means don't show it | Consistent application of D-02; the tidy reading of fail-closed | ❌ REJECTED — fail-closed governs **what the platform asserts**, not what it displays. Hiding `unknown` works deletes real catalogue from search over a transient graph failure, and at cold-start `unknown` will be common. The honest behaviour is narrower: an `unknown` work **appears in unfiltered search, is excluded from clearable-only filters, is never badged, and cannot pass the gate**. The platform declines to make a claim; it does not pretend the work does not exist. Erasing catalogue is not caution, it is a different error with the same excuse. |
| DT-14 | A single-owner work is automatically one-stop — the whole point of a solo writer-performer is that there is nobody to ask | The obvious simplification, and the one that makes the cold-start story ("bedroom producers are instant one-stops") sound good | ❌ REJECTED — **single owner ≠ single counterparty ≠ clearable**, and all three failures are ordinary. The solo artist may hold the composition while a label holds the master (two counterparties, one human). They may have assigned administration to a publisher (DT-05 — zero counterparties they control). Their track may contain an undeclared Splice loop or an uncleared sample (11.02.03 — `encumbered` with a perfect ownership record). And they may simply never have attested, which is the *default* state and yields `incomplete` (DT-02). The shortcut is tempting precisely because it would rescue the cold-start story, and taking it would put the platform's cleanest-looking badge on its least-verified works. No shortcut: every work goes through all four inputs. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Clearance is computed per **(work, scope)**, never per work | Per DT-01 — a work-level flag is wrong exactly where the money and the offence are. Ratified as domain D-08. |
| D-02 | The verdict always fails closed | Per DT-03 — the cost asymmetry between a lost sale and an unhonourable licence is not close. Ratified as domain D-09. |
| D-03 | Owners cannot override the verdict | Per DT-04 — an overridable flag is a self-assertion, which is the incumbent failure mode |
| D-04 | The gate re-evaluates fresh at issuance, never on the buyer's cached verdict | Clearance degrades between search and purchase (a co-owner sets a block); the certificate must reflect the moment it issues |
| D-05 | Blockers are always named, never generic — **qualified by D-11** | "Not clearable" is useless; "Sarah hasn't confirmed her 25% writer share" is actionable and is the product. Per DT-09 the naming is audience-scoped, not universal. |
| D-06 | The verdict decomposes per **required rights side**; required sides are derived from the scope, never assumed | Per DT-07 — remedies route to different parties per side, and composition-only scopes (covers, print) would be falsely suppressed by a combined verdict |
| D-07 | The verdict's party set is the **consent graph**, not the share graph. Administered shares resolve to the administrator; unreleased performers are counterparties at 0%; a credit alone confers no standing. | Per DT-05 — the share graph errs in both directions. **Resolves Q-02**: credit-vs-ownership confusion is prevented by never deriving consent from credits, and by stating standing explicitly per-work to credited-shareless parties rather than by omission. |
| D-08 | Verdict precedence is `unknown > blocked > encumbered > incomplete > consent-needed > clearable-now`. The dominant verdict is displayed; **all** active blockers are listed. | Ordered by terminality, not severity — the point is never to send someone to do expensive work that cannot change the answer. The full blocker list still shows the whole distance to clearable. |
| D-09 | Each verdict names exactly one remedy owner (`blocked` → none · `encumbered` → 11.05 · `incomplete` → 11.02.02 · `consent-needed` → 11.02.04 · `unknown` → 09) | The verdict is a routing decision, not a judgement. A verdict that does not name who fixes it is the incumbent "no" with better latency. |
| D-10 | **"One-stop" is displayed only when the buyer transacts with exactly one counterparty.** Otherwise the verdict reads `clearable-now` with the counterparty count stated. Multi-hyphenates count once. | Per DT-06 — one-stop is an industry term of art meaning one signature, not one clean record. The platform does not redefine a term of art in its own favour; a supervisor who filters on the badge and gets fourteen counterparties has been lied to. |
| D-11 | Blocker naming is **audience-scoped**: co-owners see who blocked and why; buyers see only that a scope is unavailable | Per DT-09 — universal naming publishes a named individual's ethical and political stances to any stranger who probes a licensing form. Aligns with 11.04.03 Q-02's recommendation for co-owners. |
| D-12 | **At most one exclusive licence per (work, exclusivity scope) can ever exist.** A concurrent second exclusive attempt is refused, not queued. | Per DT-08 — freshness is not exclusion, and two irrevocable contradictory exclusives have no downstream remedy. Queueing sells a buyer a wait for a certainty they cannot get. Mechanism is architecture; the invariant is product. |
| D-13 | **No licence without payment; no charge without a licence.** A verdict change inside the checkout window aborts the whole transaction. A change after commit never touches the issued licence, and the changer is told a licence issued before their change took effect. | Per DT-12 — the gate-pass-to-commit window is where money moves, and the "you were too late" notification prevents a dispute manufactured by correct behaviour. |
| D-14 | A scope is required to compute a verdict. No scope → no verdict, never a default scope. | A default scope is a permissive assumption wearing a convenience costume, and it contradicts 11.08.01 D-05 (unset axis = unspecified, never permissive) |
| D-15 | Time-to-clear is shown only where derived: `instant` for policy-covered scopes; a per-party historical median where that party has a response history; **"unknown", stated plainly, otherwise**. Never a point estimate for a party with no history. | Per DT-10 — an ETA is a prediction about a human the platform does not control, and it is the number a supervisor commits an air date against. At cold-start (D-07) the honest answer is "we don't know". |
| D-16 | Search verdicts are advisory snapshots labelled "as of"; the gate is authoritative. `unknown` works appear in unfiltered search, are excluded from clearable-only filters, are never badged, and cannot pass the gate. | Per DT-13 — fail-closed governs what the platform *asserts*, not what it displays. Hiding catalogue is a different error with the same excuse. |
| D-17 | On erasure the **share survives pseudonymously; the identity does not.** Scopes needing fresh consent from an erased party are permanently `consent-needed`. | The share cannot vanish — it would break the arithmetic and every issued licence referencing it. The person must. Whether a pre-erasure standing policy may be retained is legal, not an agent's call (Q-03). |
