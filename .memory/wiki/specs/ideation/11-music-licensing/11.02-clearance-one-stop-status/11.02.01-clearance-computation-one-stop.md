# Feature: Clearance Computation, One-Stop Determination & Gate

> **Number**: 11.02.01
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turns the rights registry into a licensing verdict: given a work and a proposed usage scope, can it be
licensed, by whom, and what specifically is in the way. It produces the **one-stop flag** — the single
signal that makes WeJammin's catalogue worth searching — and it owns the **gate** that refuses issuance when
the verdict says no. This is the point where provenance (the wedge, D-18) becomes money.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees each of their works' verdict and the *named* blocker ("Sarah hasn't confirmed her writer share", "contains an undeclared sample"), with a route to fix it. **Cannot override the computation** — an owner-overridable clearance flag is a self-assertion, which is the thing the platform exists to replace. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, plus the delivery view: "is what I'm about to hand my client actually clearable?" Per personas.md they deliver "with metadata intact" — this is the check that makes that claim true rather than hopeful. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in recordings or compositions in the Operator capacity. Structural absence, not an oversight. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. No professional stake, no clearance surface. |

> The verdict's **primary consumer is the licence buyer**, who has no persona (parent domain Q-01). Every
> row above is a seller-side row. The buyer-facing rendering lives in 11.01.02 and 11.06.01.

## Behavior

Clearance is computed **per work per scope**, never as a global property of a work. "Clearable" is meaningless
without a usage — a work may be freely licensable for a podcast and refused for a political ad because a
co-writer set a category block (11.04.02). The verdict is a function of four inputs:

1. **The ownership graph** (domain 09) — who controls each side, do the shares close
2. **The completeness attestation** (11.02.02) — is the party set actually closed, or merely arithmetically closed
3. **The encumbrance set** (11.02.03) — is anything upstream tainted
4. **The policy set** (11.04) — has anyone blocked this category, this territory, this buyer

The gate is the enforcement face of the same verdict: 11.08.02 cannot issue a certificate the gate refuses.
One definition of "clearable", one place, read by everyone.

### Happy Path

1. A scope is proposed (a buyer's search filter, a brief's requirement, a quote request, a licence attempt)
2. System resolves both sides from the registry (via 11.01.05 for sync; directly for single-side uses)
3. System checks: shares close → attestation present and valid → no encumbrances upstream → no policy blocks for this scope
4. Verdict returns: `one-stop` (licensable now, by parties already enumerated) / `consent-needed` (licensable after N named consents) / `blocked` (a policy refuses this scope) / `encumbered` (upstream taint) / `unknown` (registry cannot resolve)
5. Verdict renders inline wherever the work appears to a buyer, with the blocker named and an estimated time-to-clear
6. On licence attempt, the gate re-evaluates fresh — never on the cached verdict a buyer saw an hour ago

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Shares sum to 100% but no completeness attestation exists | **Not one-stop.** Arithmetic closure is not set closure. | "Splits add up, but nobody has confirmed the writer list is complete. [Attest]" |
| Shares sum to 97% | Not one-stop; the missing 3% is named as unallocated | "3% unallocated — clearance needs it resolved" |
| Shares sum to 103% | Registry inconsistency; verdict = `unknown`, **not** `one-stop`. Escalates to domain 09. | "Ownership records conflict — we can't say who to license from" |
| Registry resolution times out or fails | Verdict = `unknown`. **Fail closed.** | "Can't verify — not offered as clearable" |
| Cached verdict says one-stop; fresh gate check says blocked | Gate wins. Issuance refused. | "This changed since you searched: [named blocker]" — with the reason, never a bare denial |
| Work has 14 co-writers, all on-platform, all attested | One-stop. Fourteen parties is not a blocker if they are enumerated and their policies permit. | "One-stop — 14 rights holders, all pre-cleared for this scope" |
| One co-writer's account is deleted (GDPR erasure) | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a share cannot vanish because a person exercised erasure, but the person must vanish. Escalated to Q-03. | — |
| Splits change after a licence issued | Licence stands (irrevocable instrument); payouts move. Not this feature's problem — but the verdict for *future* licences changes immediately. | Owner: "Your splits changed. Existing licences unaffected; new ones will follow the new split." |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Graph traversal running | "Checking clearance" — deep credit graphs are genuinely slow to walk; do not fake instantaneity |
| Empty | Work not in the registry at all | Not a clearance state — the work does not exist here. Route to domain 09. |
| Populated | Verdict computed | Verdict badge + named blocker(s) + estimated time-to-clear + a fix route |
| Error | Traversal failed | Verdict = `unknown`, rendered as **not clearable**. Never degrade to optimistic. |
| Partial | One side resolved, one pending | "Checking" — never `one-stop`. The word is reserved for a complete, fresh, positive verdict. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | One-stop is a property of a **work** — compute it once, cache it, render a badge | The sweep's framing ("Clearance Status & One-Stop / Pre-Cleared Flag" reads as a work-level flag), and how every incumbent presents it | ❌ REJECTED — one-stop is a property of **(work, scope)**. A work that clears for a podcast and is blocked for a tobacco ad has no single truth value. A work-level flag would be right most of the time and catastrophically wrong exactly where the money and the offence are. This reframing propagates into 11.01.02 (results must be scope-aware), 11.04 (policy is per-category), and the whole of 11.06. **The sweep's candidate was subtly but materially wrong.** |
| DT-02 | Splits summing to 100% proves the work is one-stop | The obvious arithmetic reading; also the reading that makes the domain's value proposition sound easy | ❌ REJECTED — it proves nothing about *missing* parties. Two writers claiming 50/50 on a song a third person co-wrote sums perfectly and is a lie. This is the load-bearing gap in the entire domain: the registry can verify arithmetic, never completeness. Forced the creation of 11.02.02 (D-02). **Without it the one-stop flag is unfalsifiable and the wedge is decorative.** |
| DT-03 | The verdict should fail open when the registry is slow or unreachable — a false "not clearable" costs a sale | Availability reasoning; a tempting product instinct under a latency budget | ❌ REJECTED — asymmetric costs. A false negative costs one sale. A false positive sells a licence the platform cannot honour, which is a lawsuit naming *the platform* as the party that asserted clearability. Fail closed, always, everywhere in this sub-domain. |
| DT-04 | Owners will demand an override ("I know it's fine, let me sell it") and refusing them is user-hostile | Reasoning from the Musician persona's autonomy and their tolerance for professional-tool friction | ✅ CONFIRMED as a real pressure, ❌ REJECTED as a feature — an overridable clearance flag *is a self-assertion*, and self-assertion is precisely what Jaxsta/Muso.AI do and why the problem statement says they fail. The moment an owner can override, the badge means "someone claimed" rather than "the system verified", and the differentiator evaporates. The pressure is real and must be answered with a *fast consent path* (11.02.04), not an override. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Clearance is computed per **(work, scope)**, never per work | Per DT-01 — a work-level flag is wrong exactly where the money and the offence are |
| D-02 | The verdict always fails closed | Per DT-03 — the cost asymmetry between a lost sale and an unhonourable licence is not close |
| D-03 | Owners cannot override the verdict | Per DT-04 — an overridable flag is a self-assertion, which is the incumbent failure mode |
| D-04 | The gate re-evaluates fresh at issuance, never on the buyer's cached verdict | Clearance degrades between search and purchase (a co-owner sets a block); the certificate must reflect the moment it issues |
| D-05 | Blockers are always named, never generic | "Not clearable" is useless; "Sarah hasn't confirmed her 25% writer share" is actionable and is the product |
