# Feature: Catalogue Completeness Attestation

> **Number**: 11.02.02
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The named, attributable human assertion that **nobody is missing** — that the rights-holder set on a work is
closed, not merely arithmetically balanced. It is the one input to the one-stop flag that cannot be computed
from any other record the platform holds, and without it the flag is an unfalsifiable claim. This feature was
not in the sweep's candidate list; it exists because clearance computation cannot distinguish a genuinely
complete work from a work with an unlisted co-writer (11.02.01 DT-02).

It is not an exotic invention. Every properly-drafted split sheet already carries this assertion as a
**warranty clause** ("each writer warrants that no other person has any claim in the Composition"). This
feature unbundles that clause, makes it first-class, timestamps it, attributes it, and — the only part that
is genuinely new — **captures it at the moment it is most likely to be true** rather than years later when it
is most likely to be contested.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Full | Attests, **to their knowledge** (D-06), that the composition rights-holder set on their work is closed. Sees who else attested, against which list version, at what grade, and the corroboration count. Prompted once per attestation event to corroborate or contest a co-owner's attestation — one tap for corroborate, a two-field form for contest (which party is missing / on what basis). Sees their own name on every attestation they made, permanently, including superseded ones. Bears the named consequence if the attestation is knowingly wrong. Cannot attest for a side they neither hold a share in nor participated in (D-05). |
| **Producer** | Full | Attests for the **master rights-holder set** and, separately, the **performer list** (which does not gate clearance — D-02). **The platform's best-informed attester and frequently a zero-stake one**: a producer on a flat fee with no points holds nothing and knows the most. Their authority comes from the domain 07 session participant record, not from ownership (D-05) — which is precisely the third-party asymmetry personas.md identifies as their structural value. In the session-close flow they see the participant list the platform recorded and are asked to close it. Sees, per work, the delta between "who the platform recorded as present" and "who is listed as a rights holder" — the gap that surfaces unlisted writers (DT-06). |
| **Operator** | None | **Not a participant in the work's creation record.** Renting the room is not being in the room. The house engineer who tracked the session *is* a participant — but they are wearing the Producer lens when they do it, not the Operator lens (parent index, Role Matrix note). Under D-05 authority derives from the session participant record or the rights registry; the Operator qua Operator appears in neither. Explicit structural absence, not an oversight. |
| **Fan** | None | Not visible on the seller side. A Fan browsing a work in a buyer-adjacent surface (11.06 creator micro-licensing) sees the *output* — the one-stop badge and its corroboration count (D-04) — but never the attestation surface, never the attester's identity beyond what the badge shows, and has no attest, corroborate, or contest affordance. The one exception is the unlisted-claimant path (DT-05): any verified identity, including an account that is only ever used as a Fan, may assert "I hold a share in this work" — because the person the feature exists to surface has, by definition, no professional stake *on this platform*. |

## Behavior

An attestation is a **knowledge assertion**, scoped to one work and one side, made by a named verified
identity, at a timestamp, against a **specific version of that side's party list**. It asserts *"to the best
of my knowledge and belief, no person other than those listed holds a share in this side of this work."*
It does **not** assert strict truth (D-06) — no human can honestly warrant the non-existence of an unknown
claimant, and a mechanism that demands they do will either be lied to or avoided.

It is **not a flag on the work**. It is a record that references the party list as it stood. Adding or
removing a party does not edit the attestation; it **invalidates and supersedes** it, and the old one
survives as evidence of what someone claimed and when. That is the provenance principle applied to the
provenance mechanism itself.

### The three attestable lists — and only two of them gate clearance

The single most consequential correction this feature makes to the sub-domain's framing (DT-02):

| List | What it is | Gates clearance? | Consumed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Composition rights-holder set** | Who holds a copyright interest in the *work* (writers/composers, and their heirs) | ✅ **Yes** — any one of them can refuse a sync | 11.02.01 (composition side) |
| **Master rights-holder set** | Who holds a copyright interest in the *sound recording* (label, artist, producer with points) | ✅ **Yes** — any one of them can refuse a sync | 11.02.01 (master side) |
| **Performer list** | Who played/sang on the recording | ❌ **No** | 10 (neighbouring rights accuracy), 02 (credit completeness) |

A missing session drummer does not block a sync — under a work-for-hire or union session agreement they hold
no veto. A missing writer does, because they hold a copyright and can sue. Conflating "performers" with
"rights holders" would make the platform demand a fact it does not need and skip the fact it does.

**But the performer list is not decorative here.** It is the **discovery surface** for the other two
(DT-06): the topline singer who "just sang" and actually wrote the melody appears on the performer list and
nowhere else. Enumerating performers is how you find the unlisted writer. This is the reconciliation of the
persona doc's claim — the Producer's knowledge of "who played what" is exactly what makes them the best
attester of the lists that *do* gate.

### Attestation authority — presence, not ownership

Per D-05, a party may attest a side if **either**:
1. They are listed on that side in the rights registry (domain 09), **or**
2. They are a recorded participant in the work's creation (domain 07 session participant record)

This is on-thesis and not incidental. **The platform can confer attestation authority because it was in the
room** (D-18). A zero-stake producer who ran the session may attest; a 50% owner who bought their share last
Tuesday and was never present may also attest, but their attestation is the weaker kind of knowledge and the
grade records why. For back-catalogue works with **no session record**, condition 2 is unavailable and only
listed rights holders may attest — which is the mechanical reason back-catalogue attestations are graded
`reconstructed` (DT-03). The grade is not a judgement; it is a structural consequence of the absence the
problem statement names.

### Grade and corroboration — two independent axes, both shown to the buyer

| Axis | Values | Determined by |
|---|---|---|
| **Grade** | `captured` — made inside the domain 07 session-close flow, against a party list derived from the session participant record. `reconstructed` — everything else: later attestation, imported catalogue, bulk, or any work with no session record. | Mechanical. Not a user choice. |
| **Corroboration** | Integer: count of **distinct verified human identities** (D-07) other than the attester who have corroborated, over the count of distinct human identities on the attested list. | Counted at read time against the current list version. |

Per D-04 both are rendered to the buyer, not held as an internal score. `One-stop — attested by 1 of 4 named
parties, reconstructed` and `One-stop — captured at session, confirmed by 4 of 4` are both true statements
about clearable works and they carry very different risk. The platform's job is to say which one this is, not
to collapse them into a badge that means "someone claimed".

**This is what dissolves the "does silence count?" dilemma** (DT-04, Q-01). The false choice was between
stranding catalogue (demand unanimity) and dressing silence as agreement (timeout-as-consent). The third
option is to **ship the number**: the attestation stands on the attester's name from the moment it is made,
silence never converts to agreement because nothing is being agreed to, and the buyer prices the difference.

### What does and does not invalidate an attestation

Precision here is load-bearing — an over-broad invalidation rule turns every routine registry event into a
re-attestation errand, which is the D-02 failure mode.

| Event | Invalidates? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A party is **added** to the attested side | ✅ Yes — superseded | The attested set is no longer the set. The claim was falsified. |
| A party is **removed** from the attested side | ✅ Yes — superseded | The set changed. Removal is the *more* dangerous direction — it is how a share gets stolen — and the superseded record is the evidence of who attested to the pre-removal set. |
| A party's **identity** on the list is replaced | ✅ Yes — superseded | Different human, different set. |
| **Percentages change** among the same party set | ❌ No | The set is still closed. The claim was about the set, not the arithmetic. 11.02.01 recomputes the arithmetic independently. |
| A **publisher or administrator** is appointed over a listed party's share | ❌ No | Control changed; the party set did not. A writer signing to a publisher does not add a writer. Clearance changes (the publisher now grants) — the *completeness* claim does not. |
| A listed party **dies** | ❌ No | Copyright is inheritable property. The share passes to the estate; the set is unchanged. Death is a **corroboration** problem and a **consent** problem (11.02.04), never a completeness problem (DT-07). |
| A listed party **exercises GDPR erasure** | ❌ No | The share cannot vanish; the person must. The set is unchanged. The attestation *record's* retention is a separate legal question — see Q-05. |
| The attester **leaves the platform** | ❌ No | The attestation is evidence of a past assertion. Evidence does not expire because its author left. |
| **Time passes** | ❌ No | D-08. The claim "this set is closed" does not decay, and forced re-attestation is a compliance errand that D-02 forbids. Age is visible; expiry is not imposed. |
| The work becomes **encumbered** (11.02.03) | ❌ No | Orthogonal claims (`#CX-05`). "Nobody is missing from the ownership set" remains true of a work built on an uncleared sample. Clearance drops; the attestation stands. |

### Happy Path

1. A session closes in domain 07 with splits captured and participants enumerated by the platform's own record of who was in the room
2. System presents both gating lists back, side by side with the participant record: *"Composition — Alice, Bob. Master — Alice, Bob, Nadia. We recorded 4 people in this session. Priya isn't on either list. Is that right?"* — the delta is surfaced by default, not hidden behind a review step (DT-06)
3. Producer (holding attestation authority via the session participant record, D-05) confirms Priya was an engineer with no share, then attests: to their knowledge, both lists are closed
4. System records two attestations — one per side — each carrying: attester identity, timestamp, side, party-list version, grade `captured`, and the session id that conferred authority
5. System notifies every other listed party on each attested side, and every recorded session participant not on either list: *"Alice says this is the full writer list — confirm, or tell us who's missing"*
6. Co-owners corroborate (one tap) or contest (names the missing party and the basis)
7. Clearance recomputes → the work can become one-stop for scopes that permit it, rendered with grade and corroboration: `One-stop — captured at session, confirmed by 3 of 3`
8. The contest window stays open permanently (D-09) — corroboration continues to accrue after the work goes live, and a contest at any future date invalidates

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **One party attests; others never respond** | The attestation stands from the moment it is made, at corroboration `1 of N`. Silence never converts to agreement — **nothing is being agreed to** (DT-04). Silence also never *created* the improvement: a positive act by a named party did. Consistent with 11.02.04 D-03 ("non-response never improves clearance"): the state did not improve through silence, it improved through the attestation. One-stop floor = ≥1 valid uncontested attestation per gating side (D-03); the corroboration ratio is displayed, not gated. | Buyer: "One-stop — attested by 1 of 4 named parties, reconstructed." Attester: "Attested. 1 of 4 confirmed. We'll keep asking." Non-responders: reminder at 7 and 30 days, then it lives in their work's status, never as a chase |
| **A co-owner contests: "you forgot Dave"** | Contest by a **listed party** invalidates immediately — they have a stake to lose, they are accountable, and this is the honest-omission case the feature is mostly for. Work leaves the one-stop pool. Dave's addition routes to domain 09; on his addition the attestation is superseded (already invalid) and the list version increments | All parties: "Attestation contested by Bob — this work is no longer one-stop." Bob's stated basis is shown verbatim to the attester. Attester: "[Add Dave] or [Dispute]" |
| **A non-listed stranger claims "I wrote on that"** | Does **not** auto-invalidate — an unverified stranger's assertion cannot outrank a named, staked attestation, or every catalogue is griefable to death by anyone with an email address (DT-05). Requires a **verified identity** (domain 01); the claim is attributable and rate-limited to 5 open claims per identity. Work moves to `contested`: visible to buyers, the **gate blocks new issuance** (11.02.01 D-04 re-evaluates fresh), issued licences stand (11.08, irrevocable). Routes to domain 24 as a dispute. Both sides are attributable and both bear consequence — symmetric with false attestation | Buyer: "Contested — an authorship claim is open on this work." Attester: "[Name] claims a share. [Acknowledge — adds them] or [Dispute]." Claimant: dispute thread, not a resolution |
| **Contest is a threshold argument, not an omission** — "Dave was in the room; his contribution isn't authorship" | The species of contest that no product can resolve. Attestation assumes the party set has a truth value; co-authorship threshold is a contested legal question, not a fact. The platform must **not** adjudicate it. Work goes `contested`, fails closed to non-one-stop, routes to domain 24. The system explicitly distinguishes this from omission-of-fact at contest time — the contest form's first field is "did you forget them, or do you disagree that they qualify?" because the two have different remedies | Contest form branches: omission → "[Add them]" (one tap, resolves). Threshold → "This is a dispute about authorship. We can't decide it. Here's what happens next." — honest, not a fake resolution flow |
| **A writer is added months later** | Prior attestation invalidated and superseded, not edited. Work drops out of one-stop until re-attested against the new list version. Corroboration resets — corroborations were of the old set | Stated **before** the add, never after: "Adding a writer invalidates the completeness attestation and this work leaves the one-stop pool until someone re-attests. Continue?" The consequence is not a surprise |
| **Work is under an active hold or a live pitch when attestation invalidates** | Availability changes — **buyer and pitcher must both be notified** (`#CX-01` synthesis Q2). Silent availability change is the fail-open case. Issued licences unaffected (11.08). Notification is immediate, not batched into a digest — a buyer with an air date cannot learn this from a Tuesday summary | Buyer: "This work is no longer one-stop: an authorship claim was raised." Owner: "Your pitch to [buyer] was withdrawn because the attestation was contested." |
| **Attestation races a party addition** — Alice attests against list v7; Bob adds Dave, producing v8, in the same window | **The addition always wins** (`#CX-01` synthesis Q5). The attestation carries the version it was made against; on commit, version ≠ current → rejected as invalid-on-arrival. Never partially applied, never silently attached to v8 | Alice: "The writer list changed while you were reading it — Dave was added by Bob. Here's what's different. [Re-attest against the new list]" — with the diff, not a bare failure |
| **Two parties attest the same side concurrently** | No conflict. Both are valid records against the same version; the second is recorded as an independent attestation and counts as corroboration of the first (D-10) — an independent assertion is stronger evidence than a tap on someone else's claim | "Alice and Bob both attested. 2 of 4." |
| **A co-writer is dead or unreachable** | **Not a completeness problem** (DT-07). Copyright is inheritable; the share passes to the estate and the set is unchanged, so the attestation is unaffected and remains valid. Death costs exactly **one corroboration point**, not the work's commercial life. Under D-03's floor the work stays one-stop at a lower corroboration ratio. Getting the estate to *consent* to a use is 11.02.04's problem and a genuinely hard one; getting them to corroborate a completeness claim is not required | "One-stop — attested by Alice, confirmed by 2 of 4. Ray Dolan (deceased) — share held by estate, not confirmed." The reason is named, never rendered as an unexplained gap |
| **The multi-hyphenate: one human, several stakes on the same work** — Alice wrote it, produced it, and played on it | **Corroboration counts distinct verified human identities, never stakes** (D-07). Alice attests → corroboration is `1 of 2` on a work with 3 stakes held by 2 people, not `1 of 3` and not `3 of 3`. Self-corroboration is not corroboration. Her master-side attestation does **not** corroborate the composition side — different lists, different facts | "You hold 3 stakes in this work. Attesting the writer list and the master list are two separate statements." — one flow, two records, never conflated |
| **Solo work: one writer, one master owner, same person, nobody who can corroborate** | Attestable; corroboration `0 of 0`, permanently. This is the most common case for a bedroom producer and it means the one-stop flag rests on exactly one person's word — which is the self-assertion 11.02.01 DT-04 rejects as a *badge*. Resolved by D-04 rather than by refusal: the platform does not pretend, it **shows** `attested by 1, no corroborating parties exist`. The risk is entirely the attester's and the buyer can see that it is | Buyer: "One-stop — sole rights holder, self-attested." Not dressed up. Not hidden. |
| **A band entity holds a share** (personas.md: bands are first-class entities) | One attestation by the entity covers N humans who may disagree, and none of them are individually asked. A real abuse vector: route the share into an entity you control, attest as the entity, and no member is ever notified. Attestation by an entity notifies **every member of that entity** for corroboration, and an entity attestation counts as **1** corroboration regardless of membership size (D-07 applies to entities identically). Whether a single member can bind the entity is domain 01's authority model — see Q-06 | Members: "The Band attested the writer list on [work]. You're a member. Confirm or contest." Entity attestations are labelled as such to the buyer: "attested by The Band (entity)" |
| **Bulk attestation of an imported back catalogue** — 400 works from a distributor, one click | Allowed (D-11). Refusing it means the back catalogue never becomes one-stop and most of the catalogue is commercially dead — the cost the sub-domain index says it cannot pay. But one click asserting 400 facts in 3 seconds is not a considered assertion, so: bulk attestations are **always** `reconstructed` grade, the **batch id and batch size are on every record**, and both are disclosed. If one is later contested, "this was item 137 of a 400-work batch attested in 4 seconds" is evidence about the care taken. Per-work confirmation at n=400 is theatre and would be clicked through; recording the batch is honest and costs the user nothing | Before: "You're about to attest 400 works at once. These will be marked as reconstructed, and we'll record that you did them in a batch." After, to buyers: "attested in a 400-work batch" — shown, not buried |
| **Back-catalogue work uploaded years after creation** | Attestable, `reconstructed`, and honestly so (DT-03). No session record exists, so D-05 condition 2 is unavailable and only listed rights holders may attest. The attestation is a *memory*, which is precisely the reconstruction the thesis rejects — the platform does not refuse it, it **grades** it | "Reconstructed — no session record. We weren't there for this one." |
| **Attester's identity is a sockpuppet** | Attribution is the entire deterrent, so the deterrent is exactly as strong as identity verification. An unverified account attesting has **zero** deterrent value while producing a badge that implies otherwise — strictly worse than no attestation. Attestation therefore requires a **verified identity** (D-07), not merely an account. This is a hard requirement levied on domain 01 | Unverified user: "Verify your identity before attesting. Your name is the only thing standing behind this." — the reason stated, not a bare gate |
| **A participant drops out of a live jam room (domain 08) before the session close** | They are a recorded participant, so they appear in the delta at step 2 and the closing party must resolve them explicitly. They cannot corroborate (they're gone) but their absence from the list is a **surfaced** question rather than a silent omission — which is the entire value of being in the room | Closer: "Priya was in the room for 40 minutes and isn't on either list. Contributor, or not?" — an explicit "not a contributor" is a first-class answer and is recorded |
| **Attesting a remix or derivative work** | Attestation is scoped to the work's **own** party set only. Attesting a remix's list complete does not assert anything about the original's list — which you may not know and are not authorised to speak for. Upstream defects are 11.02.03's problem (encumbrance), and they override a clean local attestation (`#CX-02`) | "You're attesting who owns *this remix*. Anything it's built from is a separate question. [Declare samples]" |
| **Recompute fails after an attestation** | Work treated as not attested. **Fail closed** (11.02.01 D-02). The attestation record itself persists and is not lost — only the derived verdict is withheld | "Attested. We're still checking clearance." — never "one-stop" on a failed recompute |
| **Attestation on a work that is subsequently deleted** | The attestation survives as an orphan evidence record. A false attestation must not be erasable by deleting its subject — that would make deletion the fraud-cleanup tool | Not user-facing. Surfaces only in a domain 24 dispute |
| **Contest floods** — a competitor files authorship claims across a rival's catalogue before a sync deadline | Rate limit of 5 open claims per verified identity (above) caps the blast radius. Claim volume from one identity across one owner's catalogue is an abuse pattern reported to domain 24. The cost to the griefer is a verified identity attached to N attributable false claims — the same deterrent the feature levies on false attesters, pointed the other way | Owner: "3 claims from the same person in 2 days. We've flagged this." Griefing has a name on it too |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Clearance recompute running after an attestation, corroboration, or contest | "Checking clearance" — never a fake instant confirmation; 11.02.01 says deep graphs are genuinely slow to walk |
| **Empty** | No attestation exists for a gating side | "Nobody has confirmed this writer list is complete — this work can't be one-stop. [Attest]" — the empty state **is** the sales pitch for the feature, and it is the honest default: silence is not completeness (symmetric with 11.02.03 D-02) |
| **Populated** | Valid uncontested attestation present on the side | Attester name, timestamp, side, list version, grade (`captured` / `reconstructed`), corroboration ratio, who corroborated and when, who has not and why (unreached / deceased / declined to respond) |
| **Error** | Recompute failed, or registry unresolvable | Work treated as not attested. Fail closed. The attestation record persists; only the verdict is withheld |
| **Partial** | Attested by one, corroboration below the list size | "Attested by Alice — 1 of 4 confirmed." One-stop **holds** at the D-03 floor; the ratio is a displayed quality, not a gate. Partial corroboration is not partial truth |
| **Contested** | A listed party contested, or a verified non-listed claimant asserted a share | Listed-party contest → invalidated, not one-stop. Non-listed claim → `contested`, gate blocks new issuance, issued licences stand. Both render the contesting party's name and stated basis to the attester |
| **Superseded** | The attested party set changed (add / remove / identity replace) | "Superseded on [date] when Dave was added — attested by Alice on [date] against 2 writers." The old record is **visible, not archived**. It is the evidence trail, and hiding it would defeat the point of keeping it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This feature is unnecessary — if the platform was in the room (D-18), the party list is complete by construction | The strongest objection, and it follows directly from the thesis: capture-at-source means nobody is missing | ❌ **REJECTED** — but it took work to see why. Being in the room captures everyone *who came through the platform*. It does not capture: the writer who sent a topline by WeTransfer before the project existed; the producer who contributed a chord change in a voice note; the sample flip that predates the session; a work whose first session predates the account. The room is not the whole provenance. **The thesis is a strong prior, not a proof** — and this feature is the honest acknowledgement of the gap between them. |
| DT-02 | The attestable lists are "writers" and "performers", and both gate one-stop | The feature's own prior framing, and the intuitive reading of personas.md's "the person who *knows* who played what" | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the correction that most changes the feature.** Three lists, two gates. A missing **writer** blocks a sync (they hold a copyright and can sue). A missing **master rights holder** blocks a sync. A missing **performer does not** — a session player under a work-for-hire or union agreement holds no veto; their completeness gates neighbouring-rights royalty accuracy (domain 10) and credit completeness (domain 02), not clearance. Demanding performer-list completeness as a clearance condition would make the platform refuse works for a reason the law does not recognise, while the persona doc's celebrated Producer attestation would be pointed at a non-gating list. The parent CX file was already more precise than this file was ("Producer: attests for the master side") — this resolves the file *toward* it and adds the third list explicitly. |
| DT-03 | An attestation about a back-catalogue work is categorically weaker than one made at session close | Reasoning from the problem statement's root cause: "no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction" | ✅ **CONFIRMED** — and DT-08 later gave it a mechanical basis rather than a vibe. Attestations carry a **grade**: `captured` (made in the session-close flow, against a list derived from the platform's own participant record) vs `reconstructed` (everything else). The platform's entire pitch is that these are different things; refusing to grade them would be the platform conceding its own thesis. Feeds parent Q-02 (confidence gradient). Now D-04. |
| DT-04 | Silence from co-owners should count as counter-attestation after a timeout — otherwise one unresponsive party kills the work's commercial life forever | Practical necessity; every multi-party consent system faces this and most choose timeouts | ❌ **REJECTED — the question is malformed, and seeing that dissolves it.** Timeout-as-consent was rejected because it is how splits get stolen from people who weren't paying attention (personas.md, Producer's worst intentional). But the alternative was never unanimity-or-nothing. **An attestation is a claim about a fact; a consent is a decision about a permission** (parent CX R-01). Silence cannot be consent because consent is a decision only that person can make — nobody else can decide for them. Silence is not relevant to a *factual* claim at all: a co-owner's silence does not make the writer list incomplete, and their tap does not make it complete. Nothing is being agreed to, so there is nothing for silence to be mistaken for. What silence actually withholds is **corroboration** — evidence strength, not permission. So: the attestation stands on the attester's name from the moment it is made, and the corroboration ratio ships to the buyer (D-04). This is **coherent with 11.02.04 D-03** ("non-response never improves clearance"): clearance did not improve through silence, it improved through a named party's positive act; silence merely failed to strengthen it. That resolves 11.02.04 Q-03's demand that the two be answered together — *same principle, different outcome, because the starting state and the necessity structure differ*. Consent starts at "no" and every party's yes is individually necessary. Attestation starts at "unknown" and one knowledgeable party's assertion is sufficient to make a gradeable claim. Q-01 survives only as a tuning knob on a fully-specified mechanism. |
| DT-05 | Counter-attestation defends against the fraud case — notifying co-owners catches the manufactured one-stop | The implicit assumption in the feature's original notification design, and the reason "notify the co-owners" felt like the cheap integrity win | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the feature's most uncomfortable finding.** The counter-attestation notification reaches only parties **already on the list**. The entire purpose of attestation is to catch parties who are **not** on it. The people who could contest a fraudulent attestation are exactly the people who are not notified, *because they were omitted* — which is the omission. Two writers claiming 50/50 on a song a third person co-wrote: both are notified, both corroborate, corroboration reads `2 of 2`, and the badge says fully confirmed. **Corroboration is at its most confident precisely when the fraud is cleanest.** Consequences: (1) the platform must never claim corroboration defends against collusion — it catches *honest omission* among cooperative co-owners ("oh, you forgot Dave"), which is the common case and is genuinely worth having, and nothing more; (2) the only real defences against the fraud case are attribution (a name on the claim) and **retrospective discovery by the excluded party** — which requires the attestation to be **discoverable by someone not on the list**. If Dave can never learn that Alice and Bob claimed his song's writer list was closed, the deterrent is theoretical. That forces the unlisted-claimant path (edge case 3) and a new hard requirement on domains 02/24: **a person must be able to find a claim made about a work they contributed to.** Now D-12 and a cross-cut. |
| DT-06 | The performer list is a non-gating list and therefore a lower-priority capture | The efficient reading of DT-02 — if it doesn't gate, why chase it at session close? | ❌ **REJECTED** — it inverts the value. The performer list does not gate, but it is the **discovery surface for the lists that do**. The unlisted writer is almost never a stranger; they are usually someone who was *in the room in another capacity* — the topline singer who "just sang" and wrote the melody, the session guitarist who suggested the pre-chorus, the friend who changed a lyric. They appear on the performer list and nowhere else. So the highest-value moment in the whole feature is **step 2 of the happy path**: showing the delta between who the platform recorded as present and who is listed as a rights holder, and asking about each gap by name. That is a question only a platform that was in the room can ask, it is the concrete cash-out of D-18 inside this feature, and it converts the Producer's knowledge ("who played what") into exactly the fact clearance needs. Capture the performer list **first**, use it to interrogate the gating lists. |
| DT-07 | A dead or unreachable co-writer is a completeness problem and this feature must answer it | The file's own prior framing (old Q-04), and the intuitive reading — an unreachable party feels like a hole in the record | ❌ **REJECTED** — a category error, and rejecting it deletes an open question rather than deferring one. Copyright is inheritable property; on death a writer's share passes to their **estate**. The party set is therefore **unchanged** — the party is now the estate. Death does not invalidate an attestation and cannot, because the set the attestation described is still the set. What death actually costs is **one corroboration point** (a dead person cannot tap confirm) and, separately, a genuinely hard **consent** problem when a use needs the estate's permission — which is 11.02.04's, not this feature's. Under D-04's shipped-ratio design this costs the work a displayed number, not its commercial life. The old Q-04 ("do those works sit non-one-stop forever?") assumed a gate that D-04 removes; it is withdrawn. |
| DT-08 | The `captured` / `reconstructed` grade is a policy judgement the platform imposes on its users | Self-challenge on DT-03: is grading back-catalogue works second-class a defensible call, or is the platform inventing a hierarchy to flatter its own thesis? | ❌ **REJECTED as a policy judgement, ✅ CONFIRMED as a structural fact** — the distinction is not imposed, it **falls out of D-05**. Attestation authority derives from *either* a registry listing *or* a session participant record. A back-catalogue work has no session record, so condition 2 is unavailable and the attestation is necessarily made from memory by a listed party. The grade is not the platform deciding your old work is worth less; it is the platform **reporting which of the two authority conditions was available**. That also removes the perverse incentive DT-03 risked: since the grade is a displayed quality and **not** a gate (D-03's floor is ≥1 valid attestation regardless of grade), nobody is punished into not attesting their back catalogue. It has a go-to-market cost — the back catalogue is visibly second-class — and that cost is the thesis being true out loud. Q-03 survives only as an owner's call on whether to *show* the grade to buyers; the platform must record it either way. |
| DT-09 | The attestation is written to protect the platform, and it will be read to blame the musician | Adversarial reading of the feature's own consequence model: who actually gets hurt by this record, in the median case rather than the designed case? | ✅ **CONFIRMED — and it forced D-06, the single most important decision in this file.** Everyone designing this imagines it deterring a bad actor. Its most common real use will be as **evidence against a well-meaning musician who forgot someone**. 11.02.03 Q-02 asks whether declaring an encumbrance creates a written admission; the same question is sharper here, because an attestation is affirmative rather than confessional and reads in court as a warranty. A platform whose core mechanism manufactures written admissions by its own users is **building a liability generator for the people it serves**. The mitigation is not to soften the record — it is to **scope the assertion to what a human can honestly make**: `to the best of my knowledge and belief` rather than a strict warranty. That is the exact language real split sheets use, and it is not a hedge — it is a **negligence standard rather than strict liability**. It preserves the whole deterrent (you can be shown to have *known* about Dave) while not converting every honest mistake into a breach. It also partly answers Q-02 without deciding the policy: the consequence attaches to **knowing** falsity, not to being wrong. The counter-argument is real and should be told to the user plainly: this warranty is one they already give on every split sheet they sign — the platform is not creating new liability, it is increasing the *evidentiary weight* of an existing warranty, in exchange for capturing it at the moment it is most likely to be true. That is a real trade, and it is a good one, but the user must be told it is a trade. |
| DT-10 | Attestation authority should require an ownership stake — you may not speak about a work you have no interest in | The obvious permission model, and the one that would fall out of any RBAC-shaped instinct | ❌ **REJECTED — it excludes the best-informed attester on the platform.** A producer on a flat fee with no points holds **nothing** and knows **the most**. personas.md says exactly this: their attestation "is worth more than a self-claim precisely because they are a third party to most of the shares they are attesting about". An ownership-gated model would hand the assertion exclusively to the parties with the strongest incentive to shade it and lock out the disinterested witness. So authority derives from **presence, not ownership** (D-05): listed on the side in domain 09, **or** a recorded participant in domain 07. This is the most on-thesis decision in the file — **the platform can confer attestation authority because it was in the room.** It also gives DT-08's grade its mechanical basis: no session record, no presence-based authority, therefore `reconstructed`. |
| DT-11 | A DSP distribution ownership tick (domain 12) is a separate concern from a completeness attestation | The domain-boundary default — distribution and licensing are different domains, so their assertions are different objects | ❌ **REJECTED — they are the same assertion and must be the same record.** Every distributor makes you tick "I own this / I have the rights to distribute this" before it will ship to Spotify. That tick **is** a completeness attestation, worded worse and stored somewhere else. If domain 12 holds its own ownership tick and domain 11 holds an attestation, the platform will hold **two contradictory claims about the same fact by the same person**, and the first competent lawyer to notice will use whichever one hurts more. Worse: a user could tick "I own this" to ship a release while the same work sits attestation-empty and non-one-stop — the platform simultaneously asserting to Spotify that the rights are clear and to a sync buyer that they cannot be verified. **One assertion, one record, read by both domains.** Distribution should consume this feature's record and refuse to ship a release with no valid attestation on both gating sides — which also makes distribution a *second* capture moment for back catalogue, and a well-motivated one (a user importing their catalogue to distribute is already in the frame of mind to assert ownership). Now a cross-cut to domain 12, and probably the highest-value cross-cut in this file after domain 07. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An attestation references a party list **version**; adding, removing or replacing a party supersedes rather than edits it | The superseded attestation is evidence of what was claimed and when — the provenance principle applied reflexively. Versioning is also what makes the race (`#CX-01` Q5) resolvable: an attestation against a stale version is rejected invalid-on-arrival, never silently reattached |
| D-02 | Capture rides the domain 07 session close, never a standalone errand | personas.md: "the design must make the lazy path the correct path". A separate compliance step will not be taken, and a catalogue full of clearable works that cannot prove it is the result |
| D-03 | **One-stop floor = ≥1 valid, uncontested attestation per gating side.** Grade and corroboration ratio are displayed qualities, not gates | Per DT-04. The floor is the narrowed form of Q-01 and the one knob left for the owner. Zero attestations is already refused by 11.02.01; unanimity would strand every work with a dead, unreachable or merely busy co-owner, including every solo work by construction |
| D-04 | **Grade and corroboration ship to the buyer.** The badge never collapses them | Per DT-03/DT-04/DT-08. "One-stop — attested by 1 of 4, reconstructed" and "One-stop — captured at session, 4 of 4" are both true and carry very different risk. Hiding the difference is how the badge degrades into "someone claimed", which is the incumbent failure 11.02.01 DT-04 rejects. Shipping the number is also what dissolves Q-01's false choice between stranding catalogue and dressing silence as agreement |
| D-05 | **Attestation authority derives from presence or listing, not from ownership**: listed on the side in domain 09, **or** a recorded participant in domain 07 | Per DT-10. The best-informed attester is frequently a zero-stake producer; personas.md builds their entire structural value on that asymmetry. An ownership gate would hand the assertion exclusively to the parties most incentivised to shade it. **The platform can confer this authority because it was in the room** — the most on-thesis decision in the file |
| D-06 | **The assertion is knowledge-scoped, not a strict warranty**: "to the best of my knowledge and belief, no other person holds a share" | Per DT-09. No human can honestly warrant the non-existence of an unknown claimant; a mechanism demanding it will be lied to or avoided. This is the exact language real split sheets use — a negligence standard, not strict liability. It preserves the deterrent (you can be shown to have known) without making every honest mistake a breach, and it is the difference between a feature and a liability generator aimed at the platform's own users |
| D-07 | **Attestation, corroboration and unlisted claims require a verified identity. Corroboration counts distinct verified humans, never stakes** | Attribution is the entire deterrent; an unverified attestation has zero deterrent value while producing a badge implying otherwise — strictly worse than none. The multi-hyphenate makes stake-counting actively wrong: one human with three stakes is one corroboration, and self-corroboration is not corroboration. A band entity is one corroboration regardless of membership |
| D-08 | **Attestations do not expire** | The claim "this set is closed" does not decay, and periodic re-attestation is precisely the compliance errand D-02 forbids. Age is visible; expiry is not imposed. Freshness is already carried by the grade and by the supersession rule |
| D-09 | **The contest window is unlimited** | A time-boxed window would convert the passage of time into a defence for a false attestation — the opposite of the deterrent. Corroboration also continues to accrue after the work goes live, so the ratio improves with time rather than freezing at go-live |
| D-10 | An independent second attestation counts as corroboration of the first | Two people independently asserting the same fact is stronger evidence than one tapping confirm on the other's claim. Concurrent attestations against the same version therefore never conflict |
| D-11 | **Bulk attestation is allowed, always graded `reconstructed`, with batch id and size on every record and disclosed to buyers** | Refusing it kills the back catalogue commercially, which the sub-domain cannot afford. Per-work confirmation at n=400 is theatre and gets clicked through. Recording the batch costs the honest user nothing and gives a contest "item 137 of a 400-work batch attested in 4 seconds" as evidence about care taken |
| D-12 | **A listed party's contest invalidates immediately; a non-listed party's claim marks the work `contested` and blocks new issuance without auto-invalidating** | Per DT-05. A listed party has a stake to lose and is the honest-omission case. Letting an unverified stranger's assertion instantly outrank a named, staked attestation makes every catalogue griefable by anyone with an email address. Both paths are attributable and both bear consequence — symmetric. The residual (whether a claim must *drop* one-stop) depends on parent Q-01's warranty posture; see Q-04 |
