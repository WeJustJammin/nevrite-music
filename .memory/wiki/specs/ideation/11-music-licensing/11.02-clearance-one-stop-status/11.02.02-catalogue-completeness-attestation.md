# Feature: Catalogue Completeness Attestation

> **Number**: 11.02.02
> **Parent**: [11.02 Clearance & One-Stop Status](./11.02-clearance-one-stop-status-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The named, attributable human assertion that **nobody is missing** — that the writer list and the performer
list on a work are closed, not merely arithmetically balanced. It is the one input to the one-stop flag that
cannot be computed from any other record the platform holds, and without it the flag is an unfalsifiable
claim. This feature was not in the sweep's candidate list; it exists because clearance computation cannot
distinguish a genuinely complete work from a work with an unlisted co-writer.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Attests that the writer list on their work is complete. Sees who else has attested, and is prompted to counter-attest or contest when a co-owner attests. Bears the named consequence if the attestation is wrong — their name is on it. |
| Producer | Full | Attests for the master/performer side. **The platform's best-informed attester**: personas.md describes them as "the person who *knows* who played what". Their attestation is worth more than a self-claim precisely because they are a third party to most of the shares they are attesting about — the counter-attestation asymmetry the persona doc identifies as their structural value. |
| Operator | None | No ownership stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

An attestation is a signed statement scoped to a work and a side, made by a named identity, at a timestamp,
against a specific party list. It is **not a flag on the work** — it is a record that references the party
list as it stood. Adding a party does not edit the attestation; it *invalidates and supersedes* it, and the
old one survives as evidence of what someone claimed and when. That is the provenance principle applied to
the provenance mechanism itself.

The capture moment is the session close (domain 07), while everyone is present and friendly — personas.md's
"the moment that matters". It must ride along with the split ritual, never be a separate compliance errand.
Per personas.md's Producer anti-persona, **"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"**: if
attesting is a second trip, the catalogue will be full of works that are clearable and cannot prove it.

### Happy Path

1. A session closes in domain 07 with splits captured and contributors enumerated
2. System presents the party list back: "These are the writers. These are the performers. Anyone else?"
3. Producer (and/or Musician) attests: the list is complete
4. System notifies every other listed party: "X says this is the full list — confirm or contest"
5. Co-owners counter-attest (cheap, one tap) or contest (opens a dispute path)
6. Attestation validates → clearance recomputes → the work can become one-stop

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| One party attests; others never respond | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether silence counts as counter-attestation after a timeout. **See Q-01, this is the crux of the feature.** | — |
| A co-owner contests: "you forgot Dave" | Attestation invalidated immediately; work leaves the one-stop pool; Dave's addition routes to domain 09 | All parties: "Attestation contested — work is no longer one-stop" |
| A writer is added months later | Prior attestation invalidated and superseded, not edited. Work drops out of one-stop until re-attested. | "Adding a writer invalidates the completeness attestation. Re-attest after?" — stated *before* the add, so the consequence is not a surprise |
| Work is under an active hold or live pitch when attestation invalidates | Availability changes — **buyer and pitcher must both be notified.** Silent availability change is the fail-open case (`#CX-01` synthesis Q2). | Buyer: "This work is no longer one-stop." Owner: "Your pitch was withdrawn." |
| Someone attests falsely to manufacture a one-stop | The attestation is attributable by name — that is the entire deterrent. Discovery is retrospective, via the excluded party surfacing. Consequence model is a policy gap. | See Q-02 |
| A co-writer is dead or unreachable | The list can be complete and still not fully counter-attestable. This is a real and common case, not an edge. | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Back-catalogue work uploaded years after creation | Attestable, but the attestation is a *memory*, not a capture — precisely the reconstruction the thesis rejects. Should it be marked as a weaker grade of attestation? See Q-03. | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Recompute after attestation | Brief |
| Empty | No attestation exists | "Nobody has confirmed this writer list is complete — this work can't be one-stop. [Attest]" — the empty state *is* the sales pitch for the feature |
| Populated | Valid attestation present | Who attested, when, who counter-attested, against which party list |
| Error | Recompute failed | Work treated as not attested. Fail closed. |
| Partial | Attested by one, not yet counter-attested | "1 of 4 confirmed" — one-stop status depends on Q-01's resolution |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | This feature is unnecessary — if the platform was in the room (D-18), the party list is complete by construction | The strongest objection, and it follows directly from the thesis: capture-at-source means nobody is missing | ❌ REJECTED — but it took work to see why. Being in the room captures everyone *who came through the platform*. It does not capture: the writer who sent a topline by WeTransfer before the project existed; the producer who contributed a chord change in a voice note; the sample flip that predates the session; a work whose first session predates the account. The room is not the whole provenance. **The thesis is a strong prior, not a proof** — and this feature is the honest acknowledgement of the gap between them. |
| DT-02 | Silence from co-owners should count as counter-attestation after a timeout — otherwise one unresponsive party kills the work's commercial life forever | Practical necessity; every multi-party consent system faces this and most choose timeouts | ⏳ DEFERRED to Q-01 — this is a **product/values decision, not an agent call** (per `decision-classification.md`). Timeout-as-consent is how splits get stolen from people who weren't paying attention, which is the Producer's worst-intentional behaviour in personas.md ("pushes it through while contributors aren't paying attention"). Requiring positive confirmation is honest and will strand real catalogue. Both options have a body count. Escalated with options. |
| DT-03 | An attestation about a back-catalogue work is categorically weaker than one made at session close | Reasoning from the problem statement's root cause: "no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it implies attestations carry a **provenance grade**, not just a boolean: *captured* (made at session close, with the platform present) vs *reconstructed* (made later, from memory). The platform's entire pitch is that these are different things. Refusing to grade them would be the platform conceding its own thesis. Feeds parent Q-02 (confidence gradient). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An attestation references a party list snapshot; adding a party supersedes rather than edits it | The superseded attestation is evidence of what was claimed and when — the provenance principle applied reflexively |
| D-02 | Capture rides the domain 07 session close, never a standalone errand | personas.md: "the design must make the lazy path the correct path". A separate compliance step will not be taken. |
| D-03 | Attestations are attributable by named identity | Attribution is the only deterrent available against a claim nothing can falsify at assert time |
