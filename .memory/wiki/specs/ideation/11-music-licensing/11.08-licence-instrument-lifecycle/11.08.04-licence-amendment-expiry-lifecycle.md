# Feature: Licence Amendment, Expiry & Termination

> **Number**: 11.08.04
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens to a licence after it exists: the festival film that sold to a streamer and needs its media scope
widened, the five-year term running out on a campaign still running, the MFN uplift that changes what was
paid, and the narrow set of circumstances in which a licence ends. It handles mutations of an instrument that
other parties are actively relying on — which is why the answer to almost every question here is *supersede*,
never *edit*.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Approves amendments touching their share — a scope widening is a new grant and needs new consent, at a new price. Sees expiry approaching on licences against their works (a renewal is a sale). **Cannot unilaterally terminate.** |
| Producer | Config | Same, master side. Also the party whose client triggers most amendments: "the film got picked up, we need worldwide streaming now" lands on them first. |
| Operator | None | Not a party to licences in this capacity. |
| Fan | Read-only | As creator buyer: sees that their licence has **no expiry** (11.06.04). The absence of this feature is the product for them — a creator licence that could expire is the cliff. |

## Behavior

Three mutations, one consent model, one invariant.

**Amendment** — a scope delta expressed in the grammar (`#CX-05`): "festival → +streaming", "UK → +worldwide".
A widening is **a new grant**: it needs fresh consent from every party whose share it touches and a fresh
price. It **supersedes** rather than edits (`#CX-03`) — the original survives as evidence of what was agreed
when, exactly as a superseded attestation does (11.02.02 D-01).

**Expiry** — a term ends. Automatic, unattended, and it must warn **both** sides: the buyer (their use is about
to become unlicensed) and the owner (a renewal is a sale they will otherwise miss). Same lesson as 11.01.04
DT-02 — the forgotten expiry costs the seller silently.

**Termination** — narrow, and mostly it does not exist. Licences are irrevocable (index D-05). Termination is
for breach and material failure, not for regret, a later veto (11.04.02 D-04), or a post-issue encumbrance
(11.02.03). Four sub-domains depend on that invariant.

### Happy Path (amendment)

1. Buyer's use outgrows the scope — the film sold to a streamer
2. Buyer requests an amendment: the delta, in grammar terms
3. Every party whose share the delta touches is solicited **simultaneously** (11.02.04 D-01)
4. Policy re-evaluates against the **new** scope — a party who permitted festival may block streaming, and that is not a breach of anything
5. Priced as a new grant (11.03); agreed
6. Amended instrument supersedes; the original remains visible as history
7. Verification (11.08.03) now answers on the new state, and shows that an amendment occurred

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Amendment refused by one party | The widening does not happen. **The original licence is untouched** — a refused amendment is not a termination. | "Streaming declined. Your festival licence still stands." |
| A party permitted the original scope, blocks the new one | Legitimate; not a breach. Policies change; a new grant is a new decision. | Per 11.04 — refusal is categorical |
| Term expires while the use is live | Use becomes unlicensed. **The buyer must be warned well before**, not at the moment. | Warning at T-minus-N to both sides; expiry is not a surprise |
| Owner wants to terminate because they now hate the use | **Not available.** Regret is not breach. | Per index D-05 — the alternative makes every licence worthless |
| Post-issue encumbrance discovered (11.02.03) | Licence stands. A liability question, not a termination. | 11.02 Q-01 — the domain's hardest open question |
| MFN uplift lands (11.03.03) | The **consideration** amends; the licence does not reissue. A price change is an amendment of one field. | `#CX-03` — supersede |
| Buyer breaches (uses beyond scope) | Grounds for termination — the narrow real case | Routes to domain 24 |
| Amendment while a verification is in flight | The answer names the version it saw | `#CX-04` — the race is made visible, not eliminated |
| Perpetual licence (creator, 11.06.04) | No expiry. The lifecycle must accommodate an instrument that never ends. | The absence of expiry is the product |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Amendment routing / policy re-evaluation | Brief |
| Empty | No lifecycle events | The normal state — most licences issue and are never touched |
| Populated | Amendment in flight, or expiry approaching | Per-party approval board; countdown with both sides warned |
| Error | Routing failed | **No amendment. The original stands.** Degrade to unchanged, never to modified. |
| Partial | Some parties approved the amendment | Not amended. Partial approval is not a grant — consistent with the whole domain. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An amendment is an edit — update the scope, re-render the certificate | The obvious model, and how a document-centric design (11.08.02 DT-01's rejected framing) would do it | ❌ REJECTED — **supersede, never edit.** The original licence is evidence of what was agreed and when, and other parties have relied on it: a broadcaster verified it, a client filed it, a payment settled against it. Editing it destroys the record of a state that other people acted on. This is the same principle as the superseded attestation (11.02.02 D-01) and it is the domain's most repeated structural rule — which is itself a signal: **"the old record survives" is arguably a platform-level invariant, not a domain-11 one**, and belongs in the global CX. Flagged as a cross-cut. |
| DT-02 | Expiry is a buyer-side concern — it is their use that lapses | The obvious read: the buyer is the one who loses something | ❌ REJECTED — **it is a seller-side revenue event.** A term ending is a renewal opportunity, and an unattended expiry is a sale the owner never knew was available. This is the Operator's "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold" (personas.md) arriving for the third time in this domain — after 11.01.04 DT-02 (holds) and now here. **The same persona insight keeps generating the same design requirement across unrelated features: perishable state must warn its owner, not only its counterparty.** That recurrence suggests it is a domain-level principle worth stating once rather than rediscovering in each feature. |
| DT-03 | Termination should exist for the case where a licence should never have issued — a bad split graph, an undeclared sample, a fraudulent party | The apparently reasonable escape hatch, and the case 11.08.02 DT-03 makes uncomfortably plausible | ❌ REJECTED, and it is the hardest rejection in the sub-domain. If a licence can be unmade when the platform's record turns out to be wrong, then **every licence is contingent on the platform's data quality forever** — and the buyer's whole reason to license here (certainty, purchased once) evaporates. A licence that can be revoked because someone else lied is not an instrument; it is an opinion with an invoice. The remedy for a wrongly-issued licence has to be **liability, not revocation**: the platform (or the misrepresenting party) pays, and the buyer keeps what they bought. That is expensive and it is the price of the certainty being sold. **It also means 11.02 Q-01 (the warranty posture) is not a legal footnote — it is the question that decides whether this domain has a business model.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Amendments supersede; they never edit | Per DT-01 — other parties relied on the original, and it is evidence of what was agreed when |
| D-02 | A widening is a new grant: fresh consent, fresh policy check, fresh price | A party who permitted festival never agreed to streaming |
| D-03 | Expiry warns the owner as well as the buyer | Per DT-02 — an unattended expiry is a sale the seller never knew about |
| D-04 | Termination is for breach only; regret, later vetoes and post-issue encumbrances do not terminate | Per DT-03 and index D-05 — four sub-domains depend on the irrevocability invariant |
| D-05 | A refused amendment leaves the original untouched | A refused widening is not a termination |
