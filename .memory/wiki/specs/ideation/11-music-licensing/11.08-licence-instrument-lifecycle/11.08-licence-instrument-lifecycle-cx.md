## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Scope Grammar ↔ Certificate

**Relationship**: The certificate is not a document that happens to describe a licence — it is a **serialised
instance of a structured type**. That distinction decides whether the rest of the domain works. If a licence's
scope is free text, then policies (11.04) cannot match against it, rate cards (11.03.01) cannot key on it,
clearance (11.02.01) cannot be computed per-(work, scope), and MFN (11.03.03) cannot determine comparability.
Every one of those features is downstream of the grammar being a type rather than prose.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: consume the vocabulary when setting policies and cards; never author it
- **Operator**: not affected
- **Fan** (as creator buyer): never sees the grammar; their licence is a fixed template with no choices

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The grammar is a platform-owned type; certificates are instances. Instances pin the grammar version at **request creation** and carry it unchanged through pricing to issue (11.08.01 D-03) — a grammar change must never retroactively alter what an issued licence means, and pinning upstream of pricing is what stops a v4-priced quote issuing under v5.
2. **Trigger chain**: Grammar evolution → new instances only. Adding a media axis (a distribution channel that did not exist when the licence issued) cannot retroactively grant or deny it. **The unforeseen-medium problem** — see 11.08.01 DT-03.
3. **Permission intersection**: Policy (11.04) and grammar must share a vocabulary or the fold in 11.04.03 cannot project onto anything. This is why 11.04.03 can fold policies that "disagree in kind".
4. **Notification fan-out**: None — grammar is infrastructure.
5. **State transition conflict**: Grammar version pinned at **request creation** (11.08.01 D-03), which is *upstream* of 11.03.01 D-02's card-pins-at-read — the two pins agree rather than race, which is exactly why D-03 moved off the breadth pass's "pins at issue" (11.08.01 DT-13).

### CX-04: Lifecycle ↔ Verification (the point-in-time problem)

**Relationship**: Verification answers "is this licence valid?" — and the honest answer has a timestamp
attached, because expiry, amendment and termination all mutate the thing being verified. A verification result
cached, screenshotted, or filed by a client's legal team is a claim about a moment. The failure mode is a
party relying on a verification that was true when they got it and is not now.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: their client's legal team verifies once, at delivery, and files it. If the licence later expires, that filed answer is stale and nobody will re-check.
- **Operator**: verifying that music used on their premises was licensed — a point-in-time check about a term-limited thing, which is exactly the shape that goes stale
- **Fan** (as creator buyer): verification is their claim-dispute evidence (11.06.03); a stale answer loses a dispute they should win
- **Musician**: can point a doubter at the record

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The licence is the truth; verification is a **projection at time T**. Verification must never cache in a way that outlives its accuracy, and must state its timestamp prominently rather than in small print.
2. **Trigger chain**: Expiry/amendment → prior verification answers become stale. **The platform cannot recall them** — they are screenshots in someone's file. Mitigation is a verification result that carries its own timestamp and validity window, so a stale answer is self-evidently stale.
3. **Permission intersection**: Whether the verifier may see the scope (not just validity) is index Q-03. A stranger may need "is this valid?" without being entitled to the commercial terms.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Should a party who previously verified be notified when the licence changes? It would require tracking who asked, which turns an anonymous check into a surveillance record — and the same surface already keeps references unguessable (11.08.03 D-05) precisely because it leaks commercial relationships. **Owned as [11.08.03 Q-04](./11.08.03-third-party-licence-verification.md)** — owner's call at `/create-prd-security`; the deferral was re-pointed off the completed `/ideate-discover` Step 5 run, which closed without recording an answer. Today's mitigation is 11.08.03 D-02: the answer carries its timestamp, so a stale one is visibly stale.
5. **State transition conflict**: Verification racing an amendment. The answer states the version it saw — which makes the race visible rather than eliminating it.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 11.08.01 Grammar | 11.08.03 Verification | No direct edge. Verification answers questions **about a certificate** (CX-02), and the certificate carries its own pinned grammar version. Asserting a grammar→verification edge would imply the verifier resolves scope against the *current* grammar rather than the instrument's pinned one — which is precisely the bug CX-01 synthesis Q2 exists to prevent, and it would let a grammar change silently alter what an old licence appears to permit. |
| R-02 | 11.08.02 Certificate | 11.08.02 Certificate (revocation) | Considered and rejected as a capability, not merely as an edge: should a certificate be revocable? **No** — licences are irrevocable instruments (index D-05), and four other sub-domains depend on that invariant (11.02.03 post-issue encumbrance, 11.04.02 D-04 veto-after-issue, 11.05.02 splits-change-after-issue, 11.06.04 persistence). A revocable licence is worth nothing to a buyer, which makes the whole domain unsellable. Where a licence *should not have issued*, the remedy is a dispute (domain 24) and a liability question (11.02 Q-01) — never a quiet unmaking of a thing someone relied on. |
