# Feature: Licence Scope Grammar

> **Number**: 11.08.01
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The structured vocabulary that says what a licence permits: what media, where, for how long, how exclusively,
in what usage, at what scale, to whom. It is the domain's **shared type system** — policies (11.04), rate cards
(11.03.01), clearance verdicts (11.02.01) and MFN comparability (11.03.03) all key off it — and it was not in
the sweep's candidate list, which listed the certificate but never the language the certificate is written in.

The problem it solves is not "how do we store a licence". It is: **every automated claim domain 11 makes
depends on a licence's scope being a machine-decidable thing rather than a paragraph a human has to read.**
Prose scope is exactly why incumbent licensing is manual. Nobody experiences this feature directly — musicians
and producers see plain-language pickers, buyers see a form that never names an axis, and the word "grammar"
must never appear in any surface. Its whole product value is that four other features work.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Read-only | Consumes the vocabulary when authoring a policy (11.04.01) or a rate card (11.03.01) — picks "podcast · worldwide · perpetual" from plain-language controls. Never authors an axis, never sees a version number, never sees the word "grammar". The one place the grammar surfaces to them by name is the **inert-value notice** (D-11): "Advertising was added as a new use. Your 'all media' policy does not cover it — review?" |
| **Producer** | Read-only | Same authoring lens on their master share. Plus the **delivery-side containment question** — "is my client's cut inside what we licensed?" — which is a `contains` check they read as a yes/no on a delivery checklist, not as a scope diff. Per D-14 they can hold two contradictory verdicts on one work at once (master permits, administered publishing refuses) and the UI must show both rather than reconcile them. |
| **Operator** | None | No stake — **and the reason is specific, not incidental.** The Operator's one question in domain 11 is "was the music played on my premises licensed?" (11.08.03). That is a **public-performance blanket licence** question, which is a PRO instrument this grammar does not model and which currently has no home in the product (domain index Q-05). An Operator who verified a certificate written in this grammar would be reading an instrument that has nothing to say about their question. The gap is real; it is not this feature's. |
| **Fan** | None | Not visible, and the invisibility is the product. A creator licence (11.06) is a **fixed point in this space** with no choices exposed. Its mechanical consequence (D-15): 11.06 opt-in *is* the question "does your policy contain the template point?" — an owner whose policy does not contain it cannot be listed in the creator catalogue, and that is the entire eligibility rule. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None
>
> **Nobody has Full.** Consistent with the sub-domain index: this is machinery. The grammar is platform-owned;
> humans supply *instances* of it from elsewhere (11.03, 11.04) and read *outcomes* of it here.

## Behavior

A scope is **a point in a structured space**, not a sentence. Three things define the feature: the axes, the
algebra over them, and the versioning rules.

#### The axes

Revised from the breadth pass. Two changes matter more than the rest: **AI training is removed from the media
axis** (DT-04), and **exclusivity stops being an axis value and becomes scope-valued** (DT-06).

| Axis | Values | Why it exists | Verifiable post-issue? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Media** | podcast · film · TV · advertising · game · social/UGC · radio · in-store · trailer-house reels · physical release | Where the recording is **heard**. The primary axis; most policies key on it alone. | ❌ No |
| **Data use** | *(none by default)* · AI training · analysis/indexing | **Separate from media by construction** (DT-04, D-06). Uses where the recording is consumed as data and no human hears it. **Never implied by any media grant, however broad.** | ❌ No |
| **Territory** | a resolved ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country set, plus the label it was chosen by ("worldwide", "EU", "UK & Eire") | Rights differ per territory; ownership does too (domain 09), and they are **not the same territory** (D-09). | ❌ No |
| **Term** | a **commencement trigger** (on issue · on first broadcast · on release · a stated past date) **+ a duration form** (N years · campaign duration · perpetual · life of copyright) | Drives expiry (11.08.04). Not a date range — DT-07. | ❌ No |
| **Exclusivity** | *(none by default)* · **a scope** (a sub-region of this same space) **+ its own term** | "Exclusive" alone is meaningless — exclusive *within what*? Recursive by necessity (DT-06). | ⚠️ Platform-verifiable **only for licences it issued** (D-10) |
| **Usage** | in-context · out-of-context · main title · end credits · trailer/promo · theme · underscore | Fees differ by an order of magnitude across this axis. Granularity is **Q-03**. | ❌ No |
| **Scale** | an audience / spend / channel band | The band that makes flat pricing viable (11.03.01). **The only axis the buyer's own success can invalidate** (DT-12). | ❌ No — self-declared |
| **Extent of use** | excerpt duration band · number of uses · full track vs stem vs instrumental | A standard rate-card key that the breadth pass omitted (DT-16). A 5-second sting and a 3-minute needle-drop are not the same deal. | ❌ No |
| **Grantee scope** | the licensee **+ permitted assignees / sublicensees**, bounded by a stated purpose | Whether the deal is worth anything (DT-15). An agency licence that does not run to the brand is worthless paper. | ❌ No |

**Rights side is not an axis — it is derived.** 11.02.01 D-06 states that required rights sides (master /
composition) are *derived from the scope, never assumed*. That is a hard constraint on axis choice, not a
downstream detail: **every point in this space must map deterministically to exactly one set of required rights
sides.** A cover recording distributed resolves to composition-only; a sample resolves to both; a master-use
sync resolves to both by a different route. If any reachable point leaves the side-set ambiguous, 11.02.01 D-06
has nothing to derive from and the verdict cannot decompose. D-05 records this as an acceptance test on the
axes themselves.

#### The algebra

The grammar must let a rights holder express, and the system decide, three things:

| Construct | Concretely | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| **Union** (multi-value) | "social **and** advertising" — a branded TikTok is both, and real uses straddle media routinely | Every straddling use is unrepresentable or silently mis-filed |
| **Containment** (hierarchy) | "film" contains "indie documentary"; "worldwide" contains "UK" | 11.04.03 cannot fold policies that "disagree in kind"; 11.03.01 cards cannot key coarsely |
| **Exclusion** (carve-out) | "worldwide **except** China"; "all media **except** advertising" | An owner must enumerate ~248 countries to carve out one. Policies become unwritable (DT-10) |

**The one question the grammar must always answer** is containment: *is the requested scope wholly inside the
permitted scope?* That answer must be **total and binary** — Yes or No, for every pair of points, with no third
value and no human in the loop. Where it cannot decide, it answers **No** (domain D-09, fail closed).

**The question the grammar cannot answer is similarity.** Containment is a partial order; commercial
comparability is a judgement. Two scopes can be mutually incomparable in the lattice ("UK · podcast ·
perpetual" vs "worldwide · podcast · 1 year") and obviously comparable to a music supervisor. This is a real
limit, and it is the honest input to 11.03.03 Q-01 (DT-11).

#### Versioning — instruments pin, policies float

| Object | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** (issued licence, quote, request) | Pins the grammar version **at request creation** and carries it unchanged through pricing to issue (D-03) | An instrument is a historical fact. A grammar change must never retroactively alter what it means |
| **Policy** / **rate card** | **Floats** — always expressed in the current grammar | A policy is *current intent*, not a record of a past act. An owner's live standing permission should not be frozen in a 2026 vocabulary |
| **A new value added to the grammar** | **Inert in every existing policy.** Not covered by any pre-existing broad grant. Requires affirmative adoption by the owner | D-11 / DT-05 — the single most consequential rule in this file |

That last row is the unforeseen-medium problem in its **policy** form, and it is worse than the licence form
that `#CX-01` synthesis Q2 already covers. An old *licence* that pins v4 neither grants nor denies a v5 axis —
inert, harmless. An old *policy* that says "auto-approve all media" and floats onto v5 would **silently
auto-approve the value that was just added**. That is how "AI training" would get consented to by musicians who
never heard of it, under a policy they wrote about podcasts — the exact harm personas.md names as the
Musician's worst accidental outcome, executed at catalogue scale by the platform that exists to prevent it.

### Happy Path

1. Platform publishes grammar version **v5**: axes, values, hierarchy, and the country sets each territory label resolves to today
2. Musician opens their policy (11.04.01) and sees plain-language controls — "Where can this be used?", never "select media axis values". They pick **podcast + social**, **worldwide**, **perpetual**, **non-exclusive**, and set a price band on their rate card (11.03.01). The policy stores v5 values and **floats**
3. Buyer opens a request form that names no axis. They answer "what are you making?" (a podcast), "where will it run?" (worldwide), "for how long?" (forever)
4. System **pins v5 to the request at creation** (D-03) and resolves the answers to a point: `media:{podcast} · territory:{249 codes, label "worldwide"} · term:{trigger: on-issue, duration: perpetual} · exclusivity: none · usage:{in-context} · scale:{band 2} · extent:{full track, 1 use} · grantee:{Buyer Ltd, no sublicence} · data-use: none`
5. System derives the required rights sides from that point — master **and** composition — and hands them to 11.02.01, which computes a verdict **per side** (11.02.01 D-06)
6. Policy evaluation asks the grammar exactly one question per party: **does the party's permitted scope contain the requested point?** It does, for every party
7. Rate card keys on the same point and returns a price. The card pins at read (11.03.01 D-02); the **grammar pins at request** — the two pins now agree, which is the whole reason D-03 moved (DT-13)
8. Buyer accepts. The gate re-evaluates clearance fresh (11.02.01 D-04) — **against the point resolved under v5**, not re-resolved under whatever version is current at issue
9. Licence issues (11.08.02) carrying the scope as data, the resolved country set, and `grammarVersion: 5`
10. Six months later the grammar ships v6, adding **advertising** to the media axis. The issued licence is untouched — it pins v5 and neither grants nor denies advertising. The musician's *policy* floats to v6, and advertising arrives **inert**: their "podcast + social" grant does not silently widen, and they get a notice offering the choice (D-11)
11. Verification (11.08.03) and amendment (11.08.04) both read the licence's **pinned** v5 structure, never the current one (`#R-01`)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A medium exists that the grammar has no value for | **The unforeseen-medium problem** — genuinely unsolvable (DT-03). The grammar cannot be extended retroactively into an issued instrument | Nothing good; see Q-01 |
| Buyer's real use straddles two media values | Union is a first-class construct — a branded TikTok is `{social, advertising}` | A multi-select, not a radio group |
| Owner wants "everything except advertising" | Exclusion is a first-class construct (DT-10). Stored as a carve-out, not as an enumeration of the complement | "All uses, except…" with one control |
| Owner wants "worldwide except China" | Same. Stored as `label: worldwide, exclude: {CN}` → 248 resolved codes | One control, not 248 checkboxes |
| **A new value is added to the grammar and an owner's live policy says "all media"** | **The value is inert. It is not granted.** The owner is notified and asked (D-11). Fail closed, consistent with 11.04.03 D-02 (silence non-permissive) | "Advertising is new. Your policy does not cover it yet — review?" |
| **A buyer requests AI training under a broad media grant** | Refused. Data use is a separate axis, defaults to none, and **no media grant of any breadth implies it** (D-06) | `data-use not granted` — routes to 11.07's opt-in, never auto-approved |
| **An axis value is deprecated** (a dead platform) after licences pinned it | The licence still renders it. Deprecated values are removed from *pickers*, never from *resolution* — an old instrument must remain readable forever (D-12) | The 2026 licence still says what it said |
| A pinned grammar version cannot be resolved when rendering an old licence | **Fail closed.** Render the raw stored values with an explicit "could not resolve v4 vocabulary" banner. **Never re-resolve against the current version** — that is the `#R-01` bug with a friendly face | A degraded but honest view; no silent reinterpretation |
| **Territory label's meaning changed since issue** (`EU` on a 2019 licence) | The **resolved country set is authoritative**; the label is decoration. The UK ceased EU membership 2020-01-31 and the transition ran to 2020-12-31 — so even *which date* "EU" changed on is arguable (DT-08). Snapshotting at request is what makes the argument unnecessary | The set, shown; the label, secondary |
| A country ceases to exist / a code is reassigned after issue | The pinned set keeps the code. Resolution is historical, not current (D-12) | An obsolete code, shown as issued |
| **A territory the standard cannot name** (Kosovo has no assigned ISO 3166-1 code; `XK` is user-assigned) | A territory grammar that is *exactly* ISO 3166-1 cannot express a Kosovo carve-out, and contested entries (Taiwan) make the code list a political statement the platform is publishing | Feeds Q-02 — the interop-vs-fit trade has a political edge nobody costed |
| **"Life of copyright, worldwide"** | Has **~249 different expiry dates** — life+70 in the EU/UK, life+100 in Mexico, 95-from-publication for US works-for-hire. There is **no single expiry date** (DT-07) | 11.08.04 must handle a term with no computable end. See D-08 |
| **Term commences "on first broadcast"** and the broadcast is off-platform | The platform **cannot observe the trigger**. Expiry is uncomputable until the buyer declares the date. Per D-08 the licence is `term-uncommenced`, expiry warnings cannot fire, and the buyer is obliged to declare | Owner sees "term has not started — no expiry date yet", not a false date |
| Buyer never declares the commencement date | The term never starts; the licence never expires. **This favours the buyer**, so the obligation to declare must sit with them and be chased (11.08.04) — an undeclared trigger is a silent perpetual licence | Escalates as an open lifecycle item, not a silent success |
| **Retroactive licence** — the track already shipped, clearance is after the fact (routine in samples, 11.05) | **Permitted.** A stated past commencement date is a valid trigger. Without it a large slice of real sample clearance is inexpressible (DT-07) | A date picker that allows the past, deliberately |
| **Buyer's channel outgrows the scale band** | The scope self-invalidates through the buyer's success — and the platform **cannot observe it** (DT-12). Not a licence event; a compliance event. Routes to 11.08.04 (a widening = new grant) or to dispute (24) | The buyer is told the band at purchase, prominently, because it is the term most likely to be breached by accident |
| **Exclusivity term outlives the use term** (30-day campaign, 12-month category exclusivity) | Correct and common. Exclusivity carries its **own** term, independent of the licence term (D-07) | Two terms shown, never conflated |
| **Two exclusive requests overlap in scope space** | Refused, not queued — 11.02.01 D-12 ("at most one exclusive licence per (work, exclusivity scope)"). The grammar's job is to make "overlap" **decidable**: two exclusivity scopes conflict iff their intersection is non-empty | The blocker names the conflict's shape, audience-scoped per 11.02.01 D-11 |
| An exclusive licence exists that the platform did not issue (a prior off-platform deal) | The platform cannot know. This is 11.02.03's encumbrance declaration, and it is why exclusivity is only ⚠️-verifiable | `unknown` verdict, per 11.02.01 D-16 |
| **One human, two contradictory verdicts** — a multi-hyphenate holds the master (their policy: auto-approve) and a writer share administered by a publisher (11.02.01 D-07 → the administrator's policy: refuse) | **Correct behaviour, not a bug.** One scope → two derived sides → two parties → two answers. The UI must **not** reconcile them (D-14) | "Your master share clears. Your publishing is administered by X, who has not." |
| A scope arrives with an unset axis | **`unspecified`, never permissive.** Containment returns **No**. An unset territory is not "worldwide"; an unset term is not "perpetual" (D-04) | The form cannot submit; the axis is required, not defaulted |
| No scope at all | No verdict. 11.02.01 D-14 — "a default scope is a permissive assumption wearing a convenience costume" | The form has no defaults to accept |
| **Grammar version changes between quote and issue** | Closed by D-03's refinement: the pin is taken at **request creation** and travels through pricing to issue, matching 11.03.01 D-02's card-pins-at-read. Before the refinement, a v4-priced quote could issue under v5 and the certificate's scope would not be the scope quoted (DT-13) | Nothing — the race is designed out |
| Two co-owners' policies are written at different granularities ("film" vs "indie documentary") | Hierarchy makes them foldable — the coarse value contains the fine one, so 11.04.03's fold projects both onto the same lattice | This is *why* 11.04.03 can fold policies that "disagree in kind" |
| **Adversarial buyer probes scope space to enumerate an owner's policy** | A scope request is an **oracle**. Repeated probes map a rights holder's private policy shape — and 11.02.01 D-11's audience-scoped naming exists precisely because that shape is a named individual's ethical and political positions. **Finer axes = higher-resolution oracle** (DT-17) | Rate-limiting and the enumeration-defence NFR are already routed to `/create-prd-security` (domain index). The *grammar-specific* half — granularity is a leak surface, not only an expressiveness choice — is recorded here and feeds Q-03 |
| Adversarial buyer crafts an absurd-but-valid scope (term 9999 years, every country, unbounded scale) to fish for a rate-card gap | Not exploitable for **price** — 11.03.01 D-04 falls through to a human, and 11.04.01 D-03 evaluates policy *before* pricing. It **is** exploitable as a **denial-of-attention attack** on the owner. The grammar's contribution is bounding the axes (no unbounded values); throttling is the cross-cut's | Owner is not flooded; limits are the platform's problem, not theirs |
| Standard (DDEX/RIN) vocabulary lacks a value the platform needs | Q-02 — the interop-vs-fit trade. Sharpened by the Kosovo/Taiwan row: adopting a standard also adopts its politics | — |
| **Creator licence (11.06) has no scope choices** | Correct — a fixed point in the space, presented as no choice at all. Its consequence is mechanical (D-15): 11.06 eligibility *is* `contains(policy, templatePoint)` | The simplicity is the product |
| A creator licence has no expiry (11.06.04) but the creator's channel grows past the template's scale band | The one place the fixed template leaks. Either the template is scale-unbounded, or 11.06's "no expiry, no negotiation" promise quietly has an asterisk | Flagged to 11.06 as a cross-cut, not resolved here |
| An option / step-deal — "1-year festival, with an option to extend to all-media worldwide at a pre-agreed fee" | **Expressible.** The option is a *conditional grant* — a second scope, consented and priced at issue, activated by exercise + payment. Exercising it is **not a widening**, so 11.08.04 D-02 ("a widening is a new grant: fresh consent, fresh price") is untouched (D-16) | A licence that shows what it grants **now** and what it can become |
| A real sync licence's obligations — credit format ("courtesy of…"), final-cut approval, no-lyric-change | **Not scope.** Scope is what is *permitted*; an obligation is what the licensee must *do* (D-13). Enumerable restrictions ("no political use") are scope carve-outs; obligations are the instrument's payload (11.08.02) | See Q-04 — if the instrument carries no obligations, real sync deals are not expressible, and that is a product-shaped hole, not a schema one |

### States

The breadth pass wrote this table off as "N/A — grammar is static infrastructure". That was wrong: the grammar
is **versioned data that outlives its own current version**, and rendering a 2026 licence in 2031 is a real
operation with real failure modes.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | A surface needs a vocabulary version to render — a picker (current version) or an old instrument (its pinned version) | Skeleton controls. **Never a picker with partial values** — a half-loaded vocabulary is a form that silently cannot express what the user means |
| **Empty** | No axis has been set on a new request | Not an empty state — a **required** state. Nothing defaults; the form cannot submit. Per D-04, empty is not "permissive", it is "unanswered" |
| **Populated** | A version resolved | Plain-language pickers and plain-language summaries. The grammar itself is never rendered — no axis names, no version numbers, no set notation |
| **Error** | A pinned version cannot be resolved | **Fail closed and say so.** Raw stored values plus "this licence was written in a vocabulary version we could not load — showing it exactly as stored." Never re-resolve against the current grammar (`#R-01`) |
| **Partial** | A scope with unset axes | **Ambiguous, and never permissive.** Unset territory = "unspecified", not "worldwide". Containment returns No. Fail closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02 and D-14 |
| **Inert** *(new)* | A new grammar value exists that a floating policy does not cover (D-11) | "Advertising was added. Your policy doesn't cover it — review?" **The only state in which a Musician meets the grammar as a thing that changed**, and the only one with a notification. Mirrors 11.04.03 D-04's silent-inert lesson: an owner must be told when their own policy has a hole |
| **Term-uncommenced** *(new)* | Term trigger is an event the platform cannot observe (first broadcast, release) — DT-07 | Owner: "term has not started — no expiry date yet." Buyer: "you must tell us your first-use date." **Not a fabricated date**, and not a silent perpetuity |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Scope is naturally free text — licences are legal documents and lawyers write prose | How licences actually look today, everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — and this rejection is why the feature exists at all. **Four features collapse without a structured scope**: 11.04 policies cannot match a free-text use, 11.03.01 cards cannot key on prose, 11.02.01's per-(work, scope) verdict has nothing to compute against, and 11.03.03's MFN comparability is undecidable. Prose scope is precisely why incumbent licensing is manual — every question about a licence requires a human to read it. The structured grammar is the unglamorous precondition for everything the domain claims to automate, and the sweep listed the certificate while missing the language it is written in. |
| DT-02 | The grammar is architecture, not product — it is a schema, and schemas route to `/create-prd` | Honest application of the not-product test; it looks exactly like a data model | ❌ REJECTED, narrowly. The *encoding* is architecture. But **the axes are product decisions with money attached**: whether "usage" distinguishes in-context from trailer is worth an order of magnitude in fee; whether "media" separates social from ad decides whether a musician's block on advertising catches a branded TikTok. Choosing the axes is choosing what rights holders can express and what buyers can buy. That is not a schema, it is the product's expressive range — and a schema chosen by an implementer would quietly decide it. Kept as product; the serialisation is architecture. |
| DT-03 | The grammar can be extended as new media appear — just add an axis value | The obvious maintenance story | ❌ REJECTED — **the unforeseen-medium problem is genuinely unsolvable and this is the honest place to say so.** A licence issued for "all media now known or hereafter devised" is the industry's answer, and it is why artists lost streaming revenue on contracts signed before streaming existed. A licence issued for an enumerated media set cannot cover a medium invented later. Neither option is neutral: broad grants transfer future value to buyers; enumerated grants force renegotiation on every new channel. **The platform must choose a default and it will be wrong for someone** — and given the platform's persona is the musician who signs things they do not understand (personas.md), the catch-all is the one that has historically hurt them. Recommendation: enumerated by default with a cheap amendment path (11.08.04). **Owner's call** (Q-01). |
| DT-04 | AI training is a media value — the breadth pass listed it alongside podcast and film | The sweep's framing; it is a "use" so it looked like a use | ❌ REJECTED — **a category error with a live consent bug inside it.** Media is *where the recording is heard*; in AI training nobody hears it, and the output is not a use of the recording in any sense the other values share. The bug is mechanical: 11.07.01 D-01 is **"opt-in, never opt-out; silence is refusal"**. If AI training sits on the media axis, then an owner who auto-approves "all media" for podcast convenience **has consented to training** — silently, under a policy they wrote about something else. That is not an edge case; it is the default path for any owner who ever picks a broad media grant, and it inverts 11.07's founding decision through a vocabulary choice made three sub-domains away. **Data use is a separate axis, defaults to none, and is never implied by any media grant however broad** (D-06). Independently, this is the strongest available argument on Q-01: "hereafter devised" is *precisely* the clause under which AI training is currently being claimed against contracts signed decades before it existed. |
| DT-05 | A new grammar value is automatically covered by an existing broad policy ("all media" should mean all media) | The intuitive reading, and what a floating policy does if nobody stops it | ❌ REJECTED — **the highest-value finding in this file.** `#CX-01` synthesis Q2 solved the unforeseen-medium problem for *instruments* (they pin; a new axis neither grants nor denies). Nobody solved it for **policies**, which float — and the policy form is worse, because an instrument's silence is inert while a policy's silence **grants**. Adding a value to the media axis would retroactively widen every "all media" policy on the platform without a single owner acting. Fail closed (domain D-09) and non-permissive silence (11.04.03 D-02) both say the same thing: **a new value is inert in every existing policy until its owner affirmatively adopts it** (D-11). The alternative is the platform performing, at catalogue scale, exactly the harm personas.md names as the Musician's worst accidental outcome — giving away rights via a document they did not understand. |
| DT-06 | Exclusivity is an axis value — `non-exclusive · category-exclusive · exclusive` | The breadth pass's own axis table | ❌ REJECTED — **"exclusive" is meaningless without saying exclusive *within what*.** Real category exclusivity in advertising is "exclusive to carbonated soft drinks, US, 12 months" — a **sub-region of this same scope space**, which makes the grammar recursive: a scope contains a scope. The confirming evidence is a sibling's ratified decision: 11.02.01 D-12 already says "at most one exclusive licence per **(work, exclusivity scope)**" — it presupposes an exclusivity scope as a distinct object, which the axis-value model cannot supply. Second consequence the flat model hides: **exclusivity carries its own term**, routinely outliving the use term (a 30-day campaign with 12-month category exclusivity is a standard shape). |
| DT-07 | Term is a date range — start date and end date | The obvious model, and the one every calendar UI suggests | ❌ REJECTED, three times over. (a) **Commencement is a trigger, not a date** — sync terms routinely start on first broadcast or release, which the platform cannot observe, so the expiry date is not computable at issue. (b) **"Life of copyright" has no single date** — life+70 in the EU/UK, life+100 in Mexico, 95-years-from-publication for US works-for-hire; a "life of copyright, worldwide" term has ~249 different expiry dates and no scalar answer. (c) **Commencement can be in the past** — retroactive clearance is routine in sampling (11.05), where the track already shipped. Term is a **commencement trigger + a duration form**, and 11.08.04's unattended expiry must handle "no computable end date" as a first-class case rather than a bug (D-08). |
| DT-08 | Territory is a country list | The obvious model | ❌ REJECTED — **territory is a time-varying set with politics in it.** Real territories are named blocks with legal meaning ("World excluding US & Canada", "UK & Eire", "the Universe" — a genuine term of art in film contracts), and the blocks **change**: the UK ceased EU membership on 2020-01-31 with a transition to 2020-12-31, so an "EU" licence issued in 2019 has a *contested* meaning today and even the date of the change is arguable. Countries also appear (South Sudan, 2011) and vanish. Resolution: **the resolved country set is stored at request and is authoritative; the label is decoration** (D-09). The label alone is an argument waiting five years to happen. Sharpest consequence for Q-02: Kosovo has **no assigned ISO 3166-1 code** (`XK` is user-assigned) and Taiwan's entry is contested — so a territory grammar that is exactly ISO 3166-1 both cannot express a real carve-out and publishes a political position on behalf of every user. |
| DT-09 | Territorial scope and territorial ownership are the same axis | They are both "territory", and reusing one model is the obvious economy | ❌ REJECTED — they are different facts about different things, and conflating them is a wrong answer that looks right. Scope territory is *where the buyer may exploit*; ownership territory (domain 09) is *who controls the right in a given country* — sub-publishing means the same composition has different controlling parties in different territories. A worldwide grant over a work with territorially-split control needs consent from a **different party set per country**, which 11.02.01's consent graph must resolve. Both are needed; the registry owns the second; **the grammar must not model it** or it will be authoritative about something it does not know. |
| DT-10 | The grammar needs only union and enumeration | The minimal model that handles multi-value | ❌ REJECTED — **without exclusion, policies are unwritable.** "Worldwide except China" and "all media except advertising" are among the most common real carve-outs in the business, and enumerating the complement means clicking 248 countries to exclude one, or re-listing the media axis minus one value every time the axis grows. The second failure is worse: an enumerated complement **does not track the axis**. When advertising is added to the media axis, an owner whose "all media except X" was stored as an enumeration silently acquires the new value — which is DT-05's bug entering through the algebra instead of the versioning. Set algebra is union, containment, **and exclusion**. |
| DT-11 | A structured grammar makes MFN comparability computable — 11.03.03 could automate it | The tempting inference from "scope is a point in a space" | ❌ REJECTED — **containment is decidable; similarity is a judgement, and the grammar cannot supply it.** "UK · podcast · perpetual" and "worldwide · podcast · 1 year" are mutually incomparable in the lattice — neither contains the other — and obviously comparable to any music supervisor. No distance metric over these axes reconstructs that judgement, because the axes are not commensurable (how many countries is a year worth?). This is the honest input to **11.03.03 Q-01**: the grammar supports option (b), *declared*, and cannot honestly support (a), *computed*. Stated as a limit rather than smuggled in as a feature, because a computed comparability that is "usually right" is a platform making legal determinations that move money and are sometimes wrong. |
| DT-12 | The platform can enforce the scope it sells | What "we issue the licence" implies to a reader | ❌ REJECTED — **the grammar states permission; it never observes use.** Of nine axes, **zero** are platform-verifiable post-issue (see the axis table's right-hand column), and exclusivity only partially, for licences the platform itself issued. The platform cannot tell whether a licensee ran the track in a tobacco ad in Brazil. The sharpest case is **scale**: the band is self-declared, and the buyer's own success invalidates it — a podcast licensed at "under 50k downloads" that succeeds is out of scope by growing, and nobody is watching. Consequence for the whole domain: enforcement is Content ID (11.06.02) and dispute (24), and **both need the scope to be readable, not observable** — which is a second, independent argument for the grammar existing at all (DT-01 argued it from automation; this argues it from enforceability). Anything the surfaces say about "protection" must not imply monitoring the platform does not do. |
| DT-13 | Pinning the grammar at issue is sufficient (the breadth pass's own D-03) | The original decision in this file | ❌ REJECTED — **it races 11.03.01 D-02.** A rate card pins **at read** (quote time); the grammar pinned **at issue**. Those are different moments, so a quote priced against v4 could issue under v5, and the certificate's scope would not be the scope the buyer was quoted — a price/permission mismatch on an irrevocable instrument, which 11.03.01 D-02 calls "fraud, not a race condition" when it happens to the price. Fixed by moving the pin **upstream of both**: the grammar version pins at **request creation** and travels unchanged through pricing to issue (D-03). This does not weaken 11.02.01 D-04 (the gate re-evaluates clearance fresh) — the *policies* are re-evaluated live; the *scope* they are evaluated against is resolved under the pinned version, which is the only way the buyer's meaning survives the round trip. |
| DT-14 | One human resolves to one verdict per scope | The natural reading of "party" | ❌ REJECTED — **the multi-hyphenate breaks it, correctly.** One human can hold the master share (theirs, auto-approve) and a writer share in the same work that is administered by a publisher (11.02.01 D-07: administered shares resolve to the administrator, whose policy may refuse). One scope → two derived rights sides → two parties → two contradictory answers about one person. This is not a data-quality problem to reconcile; it is how music rights actually work, and it is precisely the structure personas.md calls the defining identity characteristic of the industry. **The UI must show both and must never helpfully collapse them** — a collapsed view either fabricates a refusal on a share that cleared or fabricates clearance on a share that did not. |
| DT-15 | The licensee is a name on the instrument, not part of the scope | The instrument names its parties; that seemed enough | ❌ REJECTED — **the grant runs to a party *set*, and getting it wrong makes the deal worthless.** "Licensee and its successors, assigns and licensees" is boilerplate in real sync for a reason: an ad agency licences on behalf of a brand, a production company delivers to a broadcaster who must air it, a film needs its distributors covered. A licence issued to the agency that does not run to the brand is paper nobody can use — and the failure surfaces at delivery, after payment, on an irrevocable instrument. **Grantee scope is an axis**: the licensee plus permitted assignees/sublicensees, bounded by a stated purpose. It also connects to 11.03.03 D-04, which already reasons about "beneficiary" identity to close the self-dealing exploit — the concept was in use before it had a home. |
| DT-16 | The six breadth-pass axes cover what rate cards key on | The original axis table | ❌ REJECTED, one omission and it is a standard one. **Extent of use** — excerpt duration band, number of uses, full track vs stem vs instrumental — is one of the most common rate-card keys in sync, and it was absent. A 5-second sting and a 3-minute needle-drop under a montage are priced very differently, and a card that cannot key on it must either overprice the sting out of the market or underprice the needle-drop. The stem/full distinction is separately load-bearing for 11.05.05 (remix and stem licensing), which cannot express its own product without it. |
| DT-17 | Finer axes are strictly better — more expressiveness, better matching, better pricing | The obvious direction of travel, and what Q-03 implicitly assumes | ❌ REJECTED — **granularity is a leak surface, not only an expressiveness choice.** A scope request is an **oracle on a rights holder's private policy**: probe the space, read the yes/no, map the shape. 11.02.01 D-11 makes blocker naming audience-scoped precisely because that shape is a named individual's ethical and political positions — who they will not work with, what causes they refuse. The finer the axes, the higher the oracle's resolution, and the more precisely a stranger can reconstruct a musician's private views by probing a licensing form. The domain already routes **catalogue enumeration defence** to `/create-prd-security` as an NFR; the *product* half — that **Q-03's granularity answer is simultaneously a privacy decision** — has no other home and is recorded here. Rate-limiting mitigates volume; it does not change the fact that a finer grammar leaks more per probe. |
| DT-18 | Options and step-deals cannot be expressed without contradicting 11.08.04 D-02 | Reading D-02 ("a widening is a new grant: fresh consent, fresh policy check, fresh price") against the real shape of a sync option | ✅ CONFIRMED that they are expressible — the contradiction is apparent, not real. Modelled as a *conditional grant* — a second scope, consented and priced at issue, activated by exercise plus payment — an exercise is **not a widening**, so 11.08.04 D-02 is untouched: it governs scope the parties never agreed to, and an option is scope they agreed to in advance. | DT-19 | The creator template (11.06) is just a licence with fewer choices | The natural reading of "fixed template" | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it yields a mechanism 11.06 did not have. Its consequence: **11.06 opt-in eligibility *is* `contains(ownerPolicy, templatePoint)`** — an owner whose policy does not contain that point cannot be listed in the creator catalogue, and that single containment check is the entire eligibility rule (D-15). It also exposes the template's one leak: 11.06.04 promises no expiry, but a creator's channel can outgrow the template's **scale** band, and the platform cannot see it (DT-12). Either the template is scale-unbounded, or 11.06's "no expiry, no negotiation" promise has an asterisk nobody has written. Raised to 11.06 as a cross-cut; not resolved here. |
| DT-20 | Rights side (master / composition) should be a scope axis | It is the most important distinction in music licensing, so it looks like it belongs on the list | ❌ REJECTED — **it is derived, and a ratified sibling decision says so.** 11.02.01 D-06: "required sides are derived from the scope, never assumed." But the rejection imposes a hard constraint back onto axis design: **every reachable point in the space must map deterministically to exactly one set of required sides.** If any point leaves the side-set ambiguous, D-06 has nothing to derive from and the verdict cannot decompose. That makes side-derivation totality an **acceptance test on the axes themselves** (D-05), not a downstream implementation concern — and it is the test most likely to fail quietly as new values are added. |
