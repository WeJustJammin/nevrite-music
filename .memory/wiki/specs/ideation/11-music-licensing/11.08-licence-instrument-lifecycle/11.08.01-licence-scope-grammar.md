# Feature: Licence Scope Grammar

> **Number**: 11.08.01
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The structured vocabulary that says what a licence permits: media, territory, term, exclusivity, usage
category, and scale. It is the domain's shared type system — policies, rate cards, clearance verdicts and MFN
comparability all key off it — and it was not in the sweep's candidate list, which listed the certificate but
never the language the certificate is written in.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Consumes the vocabulary when setting policy (11.04) and rate cards (11.03.01) — "auto-approve *podcast*, *worldwide*, *perpetual*". Never authors it. Should never see the word "grammar". |
| Producer | Read-only | Same. Also reads scope when checking whether a delivery is covered — "is my client's use inside what we licensed?" |
| Operator | None | No stake in this capacity. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A creator's licence (11.06) is a fixed template with no scope choices — that absence is much of the product. |

## Behavior

A scope is a point in a structured space, not a sentence. The axes:

| Axis | Examples | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Media** | podcast, film, TV, ad, game, social, AI training | The primary axis; most policies key on it alone |
| **Territory** | worldwide, UK, EU, per-country | Rights can differ per territory; ownership can too |
| **Term** | perpetual, 5 years, campaign duration | Drives expiry (11.08.04) |
| **Exclusivity** | non-exclusive, category-exclusive, exclusive | Interacts with holds (11.01.04) |
| **Usage** | in-context, out-of-context, trailer, theme | Prices differ by an order of magnitude |
| **Scale** | audience tier, spend tier, channel size | The band that makes flat pricing viable |

Everything downstream depends on this being a **type** rather than prose (`#CX-01`): free-text scope means
policies cannot match, cards cannot key, clearance cannot compute per-(work, scope), and MFN cannot determine
comparability. Four features collapse if this one is prose.

Instances **pin the grammar version at issue**. A grammar change must never retroactively alter what an issued
licence means.

### Happy Path

1. Platform defines the axes and their vocabularies
2. Rights holders express policies and rate cards in that vocabulary — in plain words, never in schema language
3. Buyers express requests in the same vocabulary, usually via a form that never names an axis
4. Clearance, pricing, and policy all evaluate against the same structured scope
5. The licence issues carrying its scope as data, and its grammar version
6. Verification (11.08.03) and amendment (11.08.04) both read that pinned structure

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| A medium exists that the grammar has no axis value for | **The unforeseen-medium problem** — see DT-03. The grammar cannot be extended retroactively. | Nothing good; see Q-01 |
| Buyer's real use straddles two media values | Grammar must permit sets, not just single values — a use is often "social **and** ad" | Multi-value scope, not one-of |
| Territory rights differ per country for one work | Scope territory and *ownership* territory are different things and both must be modelled | The registry (09) owns the second |
| Grammar gains an axis after licences issued | Old licences pin the old version; they neither grant nor deny the new axis | Per `#CX-01` synthesis Q2 |
| Two policies express scope at different granularities ("film" vs "indie documentary") | Hierarchical axes — a coarse value contains fine ones; the fold (11.04.03) projects onto the lattice | This is why 11.04.03 can fold policies that "disagree in kind" |
| Standard (DDEX) vocabulary does not have a value the platform needs | Index Q-02 — the interop-vs-fit trade | — |
| Creator licence (11.06) has no scope choices | Correct — a fixed point in the space, presented as no choice at all | The simplicity is the product |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | N/A — grammar is static infrastructure | — |
| Empty | N/A | — |
| Populated | Always | Users see plain-language scope pickers, never the grammar itself |
| Error | N/A | — |
| Partial | A scope with unset axes | **Ambiguous, and must not be treated as permissive.** An unset territory is "unspecified", not "worldwide". Fail closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Scope is naturally free text — licences are legal documents and lawyers write prose | How licences actually look today, everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — and this rejection is why the feature exists at all. **Four features collapse without a structured scope**: 11.04 policies cannot match a free-text use, 11.03.01 cards cannot key on prose, 11.02.01's per-(work, scope) clearance verdict has nothing to compute against, and 11.03.03's MFN comparability is undecidable. Prose scope is precisely why incumbent licensing is manual — every question about a licence requires a human to read it. The structured grammar is the unglamorous precondition for everything the domain claims to automate, and the sweep listed the certificate while missing the language it is written in. |
| DT-02 | The grammar is architecture, not product — it is a schema, and schemas route to `/create-prd` | Honest application of the not-product test; it looks exactly like a data model | ❌ REJECTED, narrowly. The *encoding* is architecture. But **the axes are product decisions with money attached**: whether "usage" distinguishes in-context from trailer is worth an order of magnitude in fee; whether "media" separates social from ad decides whether a musician's block on advertising catches a branded TikTok. Choosing the axes is choosing what rights holders can express and what buyers can buy. That is not a schema, it is the product's expressive range — and a schema chosen by an implementer would quietly decide it. Kept as product; the serialisation is architecture. |
| DT-03 | The grammar can be extended as new media appear — just add an axis value | The obvious maintenance story | ❌ REJECTED — **the unforeseen-medium problem is genuinely unsolvable and this is the honest place to say so.** A licence issued in 2026 for "all media now known or hereafter devised" is the industry's answer, and it is why artists lost streaming revenue on contracts signed before streaming existed. A licence issued for an enumerated media set cannot cover a medium invented later. Neither option is neutral: broad grants transfer future value to buyers, enumerated grants force renegotiation on every new channel. **The platform must choose a default and it will be wrong for someone** — and given the platform's persona is the musician who signs things they do not understand (personas.md), the "hereafter devised" catch-all is the one that has historically hurt them. Recommendation: enumerated by default with a cheap amendment path (11.08.04). **Owner's call** (Q-01). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Scope is a structured type, never free text | Per DT-01 — four downstream features collapse without it; prose scope is why incumbent licensing is manual |
| D-02 | The axes are a product decision; the encoding is architecture | Per DT-02 — axis choice sets the expressive range of every policy and every deal |
| D-03 | Instances pin the grammar version at issue | A grammar change must never retroactively alter what an issued licence means |
| D-04 | Axes are multi-value and hierarchical | Real uses straddle media; policies are expressed at different granularities and must still fold |
| D-05 | An unset axis is "unspecified", never permissive | Fail closed, consistent with 11.02.01 D-02 |
