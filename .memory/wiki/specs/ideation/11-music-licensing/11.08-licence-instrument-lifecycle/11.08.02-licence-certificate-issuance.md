# Feature: Licence Certificate Issuance

> **Number**: 11.08.02
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Mints the instrument: on a cleared, agreed, paid transaction, the platform issues a licence — a structured
record of who permitted what to whom, under which scope, for what consideration, at what moment. Every route
in domain 11 terminates here, and once it has fired the result is irrevocable, which is the property the whole
domain's buyer-side value rests on.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees certificates issued against their works and what each earned. **Cannot issue or withhold one** — the gate (11.02.01) decides, not the owner. An owner-issuable licence would be a self-assertion, which is the incumbent failure the platform rejects. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same. Also the **delivery-side consumer**: per personas.md they deliver "with metadata intact", and a client asking "where's the licence?" is answered by this record instead of a search through email. |
| Operator | None | Not a party to licences in this capacity. Their touchpoint is verification (11.08.03). |
| Fan | Read-only | As creator buyer: receives a certificate they will never open. Per personas.md the Fan "will not read a rider" — this population is one step past that. The certificate matters not because they read it but because 11.06.03 can cite it when a claim lands. |

## Behavior

Issuance is a **transaction, not a document render**. Its preconditions:

1. The gate re-evaluates clearance **fresh** — never on the buyer's cached verdict (11.02.01 D-04)
2. Every required party has consented (11.02.04) or pre-authorised via policy (11.04.01)
3. Terms are agreed (11.03) — or fixed, for creator licences (11.06.01)
4. Payment has cleared, or is atomically bound to issuance

Then the instrument mints: scope as structured data (11.08.01), parties resolved from the registry (09),
consideration, timestamp, grammar version. **The PDF is a rendering of this record, not the record.** Document
generation and e-signature are a platform cross-cut (index D-01) shared with split sheets, booking contracts
and service agreements — building them inside this domain would fork them five ways.

For a dual-sided sync licence, **two instruments issue as one atomic transaction** (11.01.05 D-02). A buyer
holding one of two licences owns nothing usable.

### Happy Path

1. All preconditions met; the gate re-checks fresh and says yes
2. Instrument mints: parties, scope, consideration, timestamp, grammar version
3. Payment settles; fee disburses across the splits (domain 10)
4. Certificate renders (cross-cut) and is delivered to the buyer
5. Rights holders see the licence and their share
6. The record is now permanent, verifiable (11.08.03), and irrevocable

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cached verdict said one-stop; fresh gate check says blocked | **No issuance.** Agreement notwithstanding. | "This changed since you agreed: [named blocker]" — painful, correct, per 11.02.01 D-04 |
| Payment fails mid-issuance | No instrument. Issuance and payment are atomic. | A licence issued unpaid, or a payment taken unlicensed, are both unrecoverable |
| Dual-sided sync: one side's instrument fails | **Neither issues.** Per 11.01.05 D-02. | Partial execution is worse than failure |
| Certificate render fails but the instrument minted | **The licence exists.** The PDF is a projection; re-render it. | The distinction matters: a failed render is cosmetic, a failed mint is the deal |
| Buyer disputes the licence later | The instrument stands. Disputes go to domain 24; they do not unmake the record. | Per `#R-02` — irrevocability |
| Post-issue encumbrance discovered (11.02.03) | Licence stands. Liability question, not a revocation. | 11.02 Q-01 — the domain's hardest open question |
| Splits change after issue | Instrument fixed; payouts follow the new split | Per 11.02.01 — the licence is not the payout |
| Same human is both parties (multi-hyphenate licensing their own work) | Legitimate — sampling your own back catalogue is common. Must not be blocked, must be recorded honestly. | Both roles named on one instrument |
| An MFN uplift lands months later (11.03.03) | The instrument's **consideration** amends (11.08.04); the licence does not reissue | `#CX-03` — supersede, never edit |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Gate re-check + mint | Short and consequential — this is the moment money moves |
| Empty | No licences | Buyer: an empty library. Owner: nothing sold yet. |
| Populated | Issued | The instrument, its scope in plain words, its verification link |
| Error | Any precondition failed | **Nothing issued, nothing charged.** The named reason, never a bare denial. |
| Partial | **Must not exist.** | Issuance is atomic — there is no half-issued licence. The absence of this state is a requirement. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Issuance is document generation — render a PDF from a template, sign it, store it | The sweep's candidate reads exactly this way ("Licence Certificate & Contract Generation"), and it is how every incumbent works | ❌ REJECTED — **the document is a projection; the instrument is a record.** Conflating them means the PDF is the truth, which makes the licence unverifiable by anyone who was not sent the file (11.08.03), unamendable without a re-render (11.08.04), and unmatched by policy or MFN because its scope is prose (11.08.01 DT-01). It would also fork document generation into a domain-specific implementation when it is a platform cross-cut serving five domains (index D-01). The sweep's candidate name encoded the wrong model, and inheriting it would have quietly broken three other features. |
| DT-02 | The rights holder issues the licence — it is their work, their permission | The intuitive model, and how a manual licensing conversation ends: the owner sends the paperwork | ❌ REJECTED — **the gate issues; the owner does not.** An owner-issuable licence is a self-assertion, and self-assertion is precisely what Jaxsta/Muso.AI do and why the problem statement says they fail. It also breaks the one-stop promise mechanically: a work with four co-owners would have four people able to issue, three of whom lack the authority, and the first one to click would sell something they did not control. The owner's control is exercised **upstream** — policy (11.04), consent (11.02.04), price (11.03) — and by the time issuance fires, every decision they were entitled to make has been made. Same principle as 11.02.01 DT-04: the pressure to let owners override is real and the answer is a fast consent path, not an override. |
| DT-03 | Issuance is the natural moment to capture provenance — the platform is finally holding a complete, agreed, paid record | Reasoning from the thesis (D-18): capture facts when they are true and everyone is present | ⏳ **DEFERRED — and it exposes a tension worth naming.** The thesis says capture at the *moment of creation* (the session, per personas.md's "the moment that matters"). Issuance is months or years later — it is the moment of *transaction*, not creation. So this is not a provenance capture point at all; it is where earlier provenance **cashes out**. That reframing matters: it means the licence record's quality is entirely inherited from domain 07's split ritual and domain 09's registry, and **this feature can never be better than its inputs.** A licence minted on a bad split graph is a well-formed, verifiable, irrevocable instrument that is wrong — and it will be relied upon precisely because it looks authoritative. **The domain's most authoritative artifact is the one furthest from the truth's source.** Escalated to Q-03. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The instrument is a record; the PDF is a projection of it | Per DT-01 — conflating them breaks verification, amendment, and policy matching |
| D-02 | The gate issues, not the owner | Per DT-02 — an owner-issuable licence is a self-assertion and breaks one-stop mechanically |
| D-03 | Issuance and payment are atomic; dual-sided licences issue atomically as a pair | A licence issued unpaid or a payment taken unlicensed are both unrecoverable |
| D-04 | Issued licences are irrevocable | Index D-05 — four sub-domains depend on this invariant |
| D-05 | There is no partial-issue state | Atomicity is a requirement, and the absence of the state is how it is expressed |
