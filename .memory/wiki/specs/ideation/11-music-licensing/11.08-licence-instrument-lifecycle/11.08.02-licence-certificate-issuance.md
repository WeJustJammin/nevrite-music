# Feature: Licence Certificate Issuance

> **Number**: 11.08.02
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Mints the instrument: on a cleared, agreed, settled transaction, the platform issues a licence — a structured
record of who permitted what to whom, under which scope, for what consideration, at what moment. Every route in
domain 11 terminates here — sync (11.01), samples (11.05), covers (11.09), creator micro-licences (11.06) and
AI corpus deals (11.07) are all different roads to this one event. Once it has fired the result is irrevocable
(index D-05), which is the property the whole domain's buyer-side value rests on: the buyer is purchasing
**certainty, once**, and a licence that could be unmade is an opinion with an invoice attached (11.08.04 DT-03).

The feature's whole difficulty is that issuance is the point where a graph of platform-held facts (splits,
consents, policies, prices) becomes a **liability-bearing artifact that strangers will rely on** — and it is the
last moment at which anything can be checked.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees certificates issued against their works, what each earned, and — per DT-09 — **the issue notification is their only signal that a policy (11.04.01) auto-approved something on their behalf.** They never saw the request; this is where they find out, and they cannot undo it. **Cannot issue or withhold** — the gate (11.02.01) decides, not the owner. An owner-issuable licence is a self-assertion, which is the incumbent failure the platform rejects (DT-02). Their control was exercised upstream: policy (11.04), consent (11.02.04), price (11.03). |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, master side. Also the **delivery-side consumer**: per personas.md they deliver "with metadata intact", and a client's "where's the licence?" is answered by handing over a verification reference (11.08.03) instead of searching an inbox. Per DT-10 they are the reason the certificate is delivered as a **file at all** — their switching trigger is "a client demanding delivery in a form they can't produce". Per DT-11 they hold the production details a cue sheet needs and nobody asks them for. |
| Operator | None | Not a party to licences in this capacity — they sell time and space and hold no recording or composition share (music-licensing-index finding 3). Their only touchpoint in domain 11 is verification (11.08.03). |
| Fan | Read-only | As creator buyer: receives a certificate they will never open. Per personas.md the Fan "will not read a rider"; this population is one step past that. The certificate matters not because they read it but because 11.06.03 cites it when a claim lands. **Per DT-08 they are also the only party here who may be a *consumer* at law** — which is where the domain's irrevocability invariant collides with a statutory withdrawal right (Q-04). **The reader for that path is the creator micro-licence buyer counterparty profile** (`meta/counterparties.md`, domain D-10 — see D-17); the professional buyer profile is the reader for every other route into this feature. Neither is a persona and neither adds a column here. |

## Behavior

Issuance is a **transaction, not a document render** (DT-01). The certificate is a projection of the instrument;
the instrument is the record.

### Preconditions — all required, all fail closed (domain D-09)

1. **Fresh clearance.** The gate re-evaluates the exact (work, scope) at issue time and returns `clearable-now`
   — never the buyer's cached verdict (11.02.01 D-04). Any other verdict aborts. Clearance degrades between
   search and checkout; the instrument must reflect the moment it issued, not the moment it was quoted.
2. **Consent or pre-authorisation.** Every party in the **consent graph** (11.02.01 D-07 — not the share graph)
   has consented (11.02.04) or is covered by a standing policy (11.04.01).
3. **Terms agreed** (11.03) — or fixed and unnegotiable, for creator licences (11.06.01 D-01).
4. **Exclusivity is available.** No live exclusive over the requested (work, exclusivity scope) (11.02.01 D-12);
   and where the request is *itself* exclusive, no live overlapping **non**-exclusive (D-13, per DT-13).
5. **Consideration settled.** Funds irrevocably committed (D-07), or £0 (D-08).
6. **The licensee is named and bindable** (D-11) — which may not be the purchaser.

### What the instrument records

| Fact | Detail | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Pinned at issue — name, role, share, capacity — **plus** a live link to the registry entity (D-09) | Who agreed (historical, immutable) and who to talk to now (current) are two different facts and both are needed |
| Licensee | Named separately from the purchaser (D-11) | The agency buys; the brand is licensed (DT-04) |
| Scope | Structured data in the grammar (11.08.01), never prose | Free-text scope means nothing downstream can match, price, or compare it (11.08.01 DT-01) |
| Grammar version | Pinned at issue (11.08.01 D-03) | A grammar change must never retroactively alter what an issued licence means |
| Consideration | A fee, **£0**, or a **rev-share** — a distinct type, not a price field (11.05.03 D-02) | A rev-share is a licence *and* an ownership obligation that writes into domain 09 |
| `issued-at` | Platform-asserted, never client-supplied (D-14) | Exclusivity races and MFN comparability both key off it |
| `effective-from` | May be later than `issued-at` | A March-executed sync for a June campaign is ordinary |
| `term-start` | Absolute date, **or an unresolved trigger** ("first commercial exploitation") (D-10) | The industry's standard term commencement is an event the platform cannot observe (DT-05) |

### The atomic boundary (D-06) — the feature's central rule

> An effect belongs **inside** the atomic boundary if and only if the platform can guarantee it atomically
> **and** the grant is meaningless without it.

| Inside — all of it, or none of it | Outside — post-issue obligations that can never unmake a licence |
|---|---|
| Instrument mint | Fee disbursement across the splits (domain 10) |
| Irrevocable commitment of funds (D-07) | Certificate render + delivery (cross-cut) |
| Rev-share obligation registration in domain 09 (11.05.03 D-02) | Content ID whitelist write (11.06.02) |
| The **paired** instrument, for dual-sided sync (11.01.05 D-02) | Cue sheet emission to domain 10 (DT-11) |
| | Notification fan-out to the parties (D-12) |

Two consequences follow, and they resolve tensions that otherwise sit unowned across three sub-domains:

- **A rev-share licence whose registry write fails must not issue.** The sampled party granted permission in
  exchange for an ongoing interest; a licence recording that interest without registering it has taken the
  permission and silently delivered nothing forever. This is inside the boundary because the platform owns both
  writes and the grant is meaningless without the second.
- **A licence whose whitelist write fails still issued.** The whitelist lives in YouTube's system; atomicity
  cannot span another company's API. Making the instrument contingent on a third-party API would make WeJammin's
  licences revocable-by-outage, which contradicts D-04. Per 11.06.02 D-02 the failure is **loud, never silent** —
  but it is a post-issue obligation, not a precondition. 11.06.01 D-02's "the purchase completes on whitelist
  write" describes the buyer's *purchase experience*; it does not make the instrument contingent.

For a dual-sided sync licence, **two instruments issue as one atomic transaction** (11.01.05 D-02) and remain
two records even where one human performs both approvals (11.01.05 D-03). A buyer holding one of two licences
owns nothing usable.

### Happy Path

1. Buyer commits at checkout: agreed scope (11.08.01), agreed consideration (11.03), **named licensee** (D-11),
   declared `effective-from`, and — where the buyer is a consumer — an express waiver of the withdrawal right
   (D-12b / Q-04).
2. The gate re-evaluates clearance **fresh** for that exact (work, scope) and returns `clearable-now`
   (11.02.01 D-04). Any other verdict aborts the transaction unissued and uncharged.
3. Exclusivity is checked both directions (D-13): no live exclusive blocks this request; and if this request is
   exclusive, no live overlapping non-exclusive contradicts it.
4. Funds are **irrevocably committed** — authorisation or escrow, never settlement (D-07). £0 commits trivially
   (D-08).
5. **Atomic commit** (D-06): the instrument mints — parties pinned (D-09), scope as data with its grammar
   version pinned, consideration, `issued-at`/`effective-from`/`term-start` (D-10) — and, in the same
   transaction, any rev-share obligation registers in domain 09 and any paired instrument mints alongside it.
6. Post-issue obligations fire, **none of which can unmake the licence**: funds settle and disburse across the
   splits (10); the certificate renders carrying its verification reference (D-16); the Content ID whitelist
   writes where applicable (11.06.02); the cue sheet payload emits to 10 (DT-11); every party whose share the
   licence touches is notified, **including parties whose standing policy admitted it without their seeing the
   request** (D-12).
7. The buyer holds a reference they can hand to a doubter (11.08.03). Rights holders see the licence, what it
   earned, and **which policy admitted it** — the only feedback loop that tells them their policy is wrong
   (DT-09).
8. The record is permanent, verifiable (11.08.03), and irrevocable (D-04).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cached verdict said one-stop; fresh gate check says blocked | **No issuance.** Agreement notwithstanding. Nothing charged. | "This changed since you agreed: [named blocker]" — painful, correct, per 11.02.01 D-04. Blocker naming is audience-scoped (11.02.01 D-11): the buyer sees that the scope is unavailable, not who blocked it |
| Payment fails mid-issuance | No instrument. Commit and funds-commitment are atomic (D-06). | A licence issued unpaid, or a payment taken unlicensed, are both unrecoverable (11.02.01 D-13) |
| Funds committed but the mint fails (platform defect) | Commitment released; nothing issued. The failure is the platform's and the buyer is told so plainly. | "We couldn't complete this. You have not been charged." — never a silent retry that might double-issue |
| Buyer double-clicks / client retries the request | **At most one instrument per committed transaction** (D-15). The retry returns the same instrument, not a second one. | One licence, one charge. Two licences for one intent is a defect the buyer discovers in their bank statement |
| Dual-sided sync: one side's instrument fails | **Neither issues.** Per 11.01.05 D-02. | Partial execution is worse than failure |
| Two buyers race for the same exclusive scope | **First to commit wins; the second is refused, never queued** (11.02.01 D-12). Refusal happens before the loser's funds commit (D-07). | "This became exclusive to another licensee while you were checking out." Never a charge, never a wait for a certainty that cannot arrive |
| Exclusive requested over a **live non-exclusive** for an overlapping scope | **Refused** (D-13, per DT-13). The non-exclusive is irrevocable and cannot be cleared. | "This can't be sold exclusively — it's already licensed non-exclusively for [scope]." The alternative is selling an exclusivity that is already a lie |
| Consideration is a rev-share; the domain-09 registry write fails | **No instrument.** The registry write is inside the boundary (D-06). | Nothing issued, nothing committed. A rev-share licence without a registered share is a silent theft |
| Rev-share stacking would push the new work past 100% | Refused at 11.05.03 D-03, before issuance. If it somehow reaches the gate, **fail closed** (domain D-09). | Per 11.05.03 — the arithmetic is shown before agreement, not discovered at issue |
| Fee disbursement to one split party fails (no payout account) | **The licence stands.** Disbursement is outside the boundary. The platform holds their money and owes it. | A debt, not a defect. The party is told they have money waiting and why it hasn't moved |
| Content ID whitelist write fails (creator licence) | **The licence stands.** Outside the boundary — atomicity cannot span YouTube's API. | "Whitelisting pending" — prominent, per 11.06.02 D-02. Never silence; the creator is holding a licence and a live claim risk |
| Certificate render fails but the instrument minted | **The licence exists.** The PDF is a projection; re-render it. | A failed render is cosmetic; a failed mint is the deal. The distinction is DT-01's whole point |
| Payment method settles at T+2 (bank transfer for a £40k sync fee) | Issuance binds to **irrevocable commitment**, not settlement (D-07). Escrow or authorisation satisfies it; a hopeful "pending" does not. | The supervisor gets their licence at commitment, not two days later when the cut has already shipped |
| £0 / gratis licence (student film, favour, promo) | **Issues.** The precondition is "consideration settled", not "payment taken" (D-08). £0 settles trivially. | The long-tail one-stop the domain claims to manufacture is frequently free, and refusing to mint it would delete that claim |
| Buyer and owner set a £0 price to settle in cash off-platform | **Issues** — and the platform's fee is evaded. Per DT-07 the gratis path must exist and is therefore the fee-evasion surface. | personas.md's Musician "worst intentional" — takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees. Detection is domain 24's; the invariant is that a £0 licence is still a real licence. Q-07 |
| Buyer is an agency; the licensee is their client brand | The instrument names **both** (D-11). The purchaser warrants authority to bind the licensee. | A licence naming the agency is worthless to the brand and the delivery is rejected (DT-04) |
| Purchaser cannot actually bind the named licensee | Issues on the purchaser's warranty; the platform cannot verify corporate authority. | An honest liability question, not a solvable check. Q-05 |
| A party is a **band entity** (domain 01), not a person | Entity capacity: who may bind the band is domain 01's model, not this feature's. The instrument names the entity as the party. | The instrument records what domain 01 says binds; it does not invent a capacity rule |
| Same human is both parties (multi-hyphenate licensing their own back catalogue) | **Legitimate** — sampling your own catalogue is common. Must not be blocked; must be recorded honestly as two roles. | Both roles named on one instrument. Per 11.01.05 D-03 two approvals remain two records — the sides can diverge in ownership later |
| Same human sits on both sides of a **dual-sided** sync (owns master and composition) | Two instruments still, two approvals still (11.01.05 D-02, D-03). | Collapsing them makes the record unattributable when the sides diverge |
| A party is erased (GDPR) between consent and issue | Per 11.02.01 D-17 the scope becomes permanently `consent-needed`. **No issue.** | Fail closed. The share survives pseudonymously; the identity does not, and consent cannot be inferred from a person who is gone |
| A party is erased **after** issue | The instrument names them, and a buyer relies on it as evidence. Whether the name may be scrubbed from an issued instrument is not an agent's call. | **Q-06** — the sharpest collision in the file: an erasure right against an irrevocable evidentiary record |
| A party dies between consent and issue | Party set **unchanged** — the party is now the estate (11.02.02 DT-07). Consent already given stands; copyright is inheritable property. | Issues. Death is not a completeness defect |
| Buyer's account is deleted after issue | **The licence stands.** It is not the account's property; it is a record of a grant between parties. | Irrevocability does not depend on the buyer's continued patronage |
| Buyer is a consumer with no express withdrawal waiver | **Cannot issue as irrevocable** (DT-08). The waiver is a precondition, not a footnote. | Per constraints.md's flagged distance-selling trigger. Q-04 |
| Buyer is a minor | Capacity to contract is absent; age gating is a flagged compliance trigger (constraints.md). Not this feature's rule to invent. | Routed to `/create-prd-security`. The instrument must not pretend a minor bound themselves |
| `term-start` is trigger-based and the buyer never declares the trigger | **The term never starts; expiry never schedules** (11.08.04). The licence is effectively perpetual, and the buyer has every incentive to leave it that way. | DT-05's nasty finding. Q-05 |
| Grammar gains/changes an axis between quote and issue | The instrument pins the version **in force at issue** (11.08.01 D-03). If the agreed scope is not expressible in that version, **abort** — do not silently re-map it. | Fail closed. A silent re-map sells a scope nobody agreed to |
| Rate card changed between quote and issue | The card **pinned at read** (11.03.01 D-02); the agreed price stands. | The buyer is not repriced at the till |
| An MFN uplift lands months later (11.03.03) | The instrument's **consideration** amends (11.08.04); the licence does not reissue. | `#CX-03` — supersede, never edit |
| Splits change after issue | Instrument fixed; **payouts follow the new split**. | Per 11.02.01 — the licence is not the payout |
| Post-issue encumbrance discovered (11.02.03) | Licence stands. A liability question, not a revocation. | 11.02 Q-01 — the domain's hardest open question |
| Buyer disputes the licence later | The instrument stands. Disputes route to domain 24; they do not unmake the record. | Per `#R-02` — irrevocability |
| Forged certificate presented to a third party | Irrelevant to the record. Verification runs against the platform's data, not the document (11.08.03). | The forgery fails at 11.08.03, which is why DT-01's distinction matters |
| Notification fan-out to the parties fails | **The licence stands.** Notification is outside the boundary (D-06). | Retried; the owner's feed is the durable surface, not the email |
| Buyer will not name the production (cue sheet payload unavailable) | The sync licence **issues** — a cue sheet is not a precondition of a grant. But the writer's performance back-end is now at risk and nobody else will notice. | DT-11 / Q-08 — the platform is the only party positioned to see this and say so |
| Licence minted on a bad split graph | Issues, verifies perfectly, and is **well-formed and wrong**. | DT-03 — the domain's most authoritative artifact is the one furthest from the truth's source. Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fresh gate re-check → exclusivity check → funds commitment → atomic mint | Short and consequential — this is the moment money moves. **If the bound is exceeded, the transaction aborts unissued and uncharged; it never resolves optimistically.** The numeric budget is `/create-prd-compile`'s (clearance graph traversal is already routed there as an NFR); the *behaviour on exceeding it* is this feature's and is fail-closed |
| Empty | No licences | Buyer: an empty library. Owner: nothing sold yet — **and, per DT-09, no policy has ever fired**, which is itself information about a policy that may be too narrow |
| Populated | Issued | The instrument, its scope in plain words (never grammar language, per 11.08.01), its consideration, its three time facts (D-10), its verification reference, and — for the owner — which policy admitted it |
| Error | Any precondition failed | **Nothing issued, nothing charged.** The named reason, audience-scoped (11.02.01 D-11), never a bare denial |
| Partial | **Must not exist.** | Issuance is atomic (D-05). There is no half-issued licence, no half-charged buyer, no rev-share licence with an unregistered share. **The absence of this state is a requirement, and it is testable: no query against the instrument store may ever return one side of a dual pair, or an instrument whose rev-share obligation has no registry entry.** The visibly *incomplete* things — whitelisting pending, disbursement pending, cue sheet unemitted — are post-issue obligations of a **complete** licence, and must be presented as such rather than as a partial licence |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Issuance is document generation — render a PDF from a template, sign it, store it | The sweep's candidate reads exactly this way ("Licence Certificate & Contract Generation"), and it is how every incumbent works | ❌ REJECTED — **the document is a projection; the instrument is a record.** Conflating them means the PDF is the truth, which makes the licence unverifiable by anyone who was not sent the file (11.08.03), unamendable without a re-render (11.08.04), and unmatched by policy or MFN because its scope is prose (11.08.01 DT-01). It would also fork document generation into a domain-specific implementation when it is a platform cross-cut serving five domains (index D-01). The sweep's candidate name encoded the wrong model, and inheriting it would have quietly broken three other features. |
| DT-02 | The rights holder issues the licence — it is their work, their permission | The intuitive model, and how a manual licensing conversation ends: the owner sends the paperwork | ❌ REJECTED — **the gate issues; the owner does not.** An owner-issuable licence is a self-assertion, and self-assertion is precisely what Jaxsta/Muso.AI do and why the problem statement says they fail. It also breaks the one-stop promise mechanically: a work with four co-owners would have four people able to issue, three of whom lack the authority, and the first one to click would sell something they did not control. The owner's control is exercised **upstream** — policy (11.04), consent (11.02.04), price (11.03) — and by the time issuance fires, every decision they were entitled to make has been made. Same principle as 11.02.01 DT-04: the pressure to let owners override is real and the answer is a fast consent path, not an override. |
| DT-03 | Issuance is the natural moment to capture provenance — the platform is finally holding a complete, agreed, paid record | Reasoning from the thesis (D-18): capture facts when they are true and everyone is present | ⏳ **DEFERRED — and it exposes a tension worth naming.** The thesis says capture at the *moment of creation* (the session, per personas.md's "the moment that matters"). Issuance is months or years later — it is the moment of *transaction*, not creation. So this is not a provenance capture point at all; it is where earlier provenance **cashes out**. That reframing matters: it means the licence record's quality is entirely inherited from domain 07's split ritual and domain 09's registry, and **this feature can never be better than its inputs.** A licence minted on a bad split graph is a well-formed, verifiable, irrevocable instrument that is wrong — and it will be relied upon precisely because it looks authoritative. **The domain's most authoritative artifact is the one furthest from the truth's source.** Escalated to Q-03. |
| DT-04 | The buyer is the licensee — whoever paid is who is licensed | The default every checkout in the world assumes, and the shape of every other transaction on this platform | ❌ **REJECTED — and it is the most consequential omission in the file.** The domain's own index names the buyers as "supervisors, brands, and agencies" — that is not one party wearing three hats, it is **three parties in one transaction**. A music supervisor is an agent; an ad agency purchases on behalf of a brand; the party who must be licensed is the brand, because the brand is who exploits the music and who gets sued if it is unlicensed. A licence issued to "Sarah's Music Supervision Ltd" for a Coca-Cola campaign is **worthless to Coca-Cola** and the delivery is rejected by the client's legal team — which is the exact Producer switching trigger personas.md names ("a client demanding delivery in a form they can't produce"). So the instrument names purchaser **and** licensee, separately (D-11). This also sharpens the domain's Q-01: the licence buyer does not merely lack a persona — the buyer is **plural**, and a persona model built for one of the three would still miss the one that matters. |
| DT-05 | Issuance has one timestamp — the moment it fired | Modelling economy, and true of almost every other record on the platform | ❌ **REJECTED — there are three time facts, and one of them the platform cannot observe.** Music licences routinely separate execution from commencement: a sync executed in March for a June campaign, with a term of "12 months from **first broadcast**". "Term commences on first commercial exploitation" is the industry's standard construction, and 11.08.01's grammar already implies it by listing "campaign duration" as a term value. The consequence nobody has drawn: **if the term starts at an event the platform cannot see, expiry (11.08.04) cannot be scheduled** — and the buyer, who is the only party who knows the trigger fired, has a permanent incentive never to declare it. An undeclared trigger silently converts a 12-month licence into a perpetual one, and it does so through correct behaviour by every system involved. The instrument must therefore record `term-start` as *unresolved* rather than guessing (D-10); what to do about the buyer's incentive is a product decision (Q-05). |
| DT-06 | The atomic boundary is "instrument + payment" — mint and money, nothing else | The file's own prior framing (old D-03), and it sounds complete | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED AS FAR AS IT WENT, AND MATERIALLY TOO NARROW.** Three sibling features each independently bind an external effect to issuance and none of them says whether it is inside the transaction: 11.05.03 D-02 registers a rev-share **ownership obligation** in domain 09; 11.06.01 D-02 says the creator purchase "completes on whitelist write"; domain 10 disburses the fee across the splits. Left unstated, an implementer resolves this three different ways in three places. The rule that resolves all three: **an effect is inside the boundary iff the platform can guarantee it atomically AND the grant is meaningless without it** (D-06). Rev-share registration passes both — the platform owns the write, and a rev-share licence without a registered share has taken a permission and delivered nothing forever. The whitelist fails the first — it lives in YouTube's system, and making the instrument contingent on a third party's API would make WeJammin's licences revocable-by-outage, contradicting D-04 and index D-05. Disbursement fails the second — the money is already committed and held; failing to move it is a **debt**, not a broken licence. This reconciles all three siblings without contradicting any: 11.06.01 D-02 describes the *purchase experience*, not the instrument's existence, and 11.06.02 D-02's "loudly flags, never silence" is exactly the right treatment for a post-issue obligation. |
| DT-07 | Every licence has a price — payment cleared is a precondition | The file's own prior framing, and the commercial instinct | ❌ **REJECTED — £0 licences are real, common, and load-bearing on the domain's central claim.** A musician letting a student film use a track, a favour for a friend's podcast, a promo grant to build a relationship: these are ordinary music-industry transactions and they are exactly the long-tail one-stops the domain claims to *manufacture* as a byproduct of its core loop. A precondition of "payment cleared" deletes them. The precondition is **"consideration settled"**, where £0 settles trivially (D-08). **And the honest consequence: the gratis path is the fee-evasion surface.** Set the price to £0 on-platform and settle in cash — which is personas.md's Musician worst-intentional ("takes the deal off-platform to dodge fees") arriving through a door the platform must leave open. There is no design that both permits genuine free licensing and prevents priced-at-zero laundering, because they are the same transaction seen from different sides. Detection is domain 24's; the invariant here is that **a £0 licence is a real licence** (Q-07). |
| DT-08 | Irrevocability is the platform's decision to make — index D-05 asserts it and four sub-domains rely on it | The whole sub-domain proceeds as though this were settled | ❌ **REJECTED — irrevocability is not free, and this is where the domain's central invariant meets statute.** The Fan-as-creator-buyer is potentially a **consumer**, not a business (music-licensing-index finding 2 already flags that this mapping is strained — "a podcaster with a monetised channel is a small business"; the hobbyist buying one track is not). Consumer distance-selling regimes give consumers a right of withdrawal on distance contracts, and `meta/constraints.md` already flags "Consumer protection / distance selling" as a live, unresolved compliance trigger for exactly this reason. The standard carve-out for immediately-supplied digital content is an **express waiver obtained at checkout** — which is why every digital storefront makes you tick it. So D-04's irrevocability is not a decision the platform gets to assert; it is a property the platform must **earn at checkout, per buyer, for consumer buyers**. If the waiver is not obtained, the licence is not irrevocable, and every downstream feature that relies on the invariant (11.02.03, 11.04.02 D-04, 11.05.02, 11.06.04) is relying on something that isn't there. **The invariant the whole sub-domain is built on has a statutory hole in it that nobody costed.** Q-04 — legal, escalated, not an agent's call to resolve. |
| DT-09 | A policy-approved licence needs no issue notification — the owner already said yes when they set the policy | The efficient read of 11.04.01: pre-authorisation *is* the consent, so notifying again is noise | ❌ **REJECTED, and rejecting it reframes the notification from courtesy to mechanism.** The notification looks cruel and useless: "we sold something of yours; you cannot undo it." Both halves are true — it is informational only, because the licence is irrevocable the instant it exists. But it is the **only feedback loop that tells an owner their policy is wrong.** A musician who set "auto-approve advertising" and then sees "your track licensed for a tobacco ad, £400, via your auto-approve policy" has learned something they can act on — for the *next* licence, by changing the policy (11.04.01). Without it the policy is a blind grant and they discover it in a royalty statement, or in a friend's text, or never. This is 11.08.04 DT-02's principle arriving for the fourth time in this domain (holds, expiry, and now issuance): **perishable or consequential state must reach its owner, not only its counterparty** — which by four independent instances is a domain-level principle, not a local rule. It also generates a real disclosure question: on a policy-approved licence the owner never chose the buyer, so **do they learn who is using their music?** Probably yes — but 11.02.01 D-11 established that naming is audience-scoped, and this is the mirror case, so it needs a rule rather than an instinct (Q-09). |
| DT-10 | The certificate should not be delivered as a file — the record is the truth, so hand over a reference and refuse the PDF | The purist reading of DT-01, and it follows logically: an emailed PDF is precisely the artifact 11.08.03 exists to devalue ("anyone can produce a PDF") | ❌ **REJECTED — it is a correct principle that loses the user it is meant to serve.** The Producer's client's legal team wants **a file to file**. They will file *something* regardless; refusing to supply it means they file a screenshot, or an email, or the agency's own paperwork — artifacts the platform has no relationship with at all. personas.md names "a client demanding delivery in a form they can't produce" as a Producer **switching trigger**: the form the client demands is a document. So deliver the file — and make the file a **pointer to the record rather than a substitute for it**, carrying the verification reference prominently (D-16, and 11.08.03's happy path already assumes a QR or link on the certificate). This is the difference between a design that is right and a design that works: the purist version wins the argument about what a licence *is* and loses the Producer at the exact moment they were about to switch. |
| DT-11 | Everything issuance needs to emit, it emits to parties inside the transaction | The scope instinct — the licence is between the parties, so what else is there? | ✅ **CONFIRMED AS A GAP — the cue sheet, and no one has mentioned it in 34 features.** For sync, the broadcaster files a **cue sheet** with the PRO recording what music was used, where, for how long, in what context. The cue sheet is how the *writer* gets their performance royalty — which for a broadcast sync is routinely **larger than the sync fee itself**. A sync licence issued without a cue sheet reaching a PRO means the writer got paid once and loses the back-end permanently, and the failure is invisible: nothing errors, no one is notified, the money simply never arrives. **Issuance is the only moment any system holds the whole payload** — this work, these writers, this production, this territory, this usage, this duration. Domain 10 (Royalties & Collections) is where it belongs and domain 10 cannot know it happened. This is DT-03's reframing paying off: issuance is where provenance *cashes out*, and the cue sheet is the single clearest instance of the platform being positioned to convert a record into money that would otherwise evaporate — which is the thesis (D-18) in one concrete artifact. Logged as a trigger dependency, not built here (Q-08). |
| DT-12 | The certificate renders its parties by resolving them from the registry (09) at read time — one source of truth, always current | The obviously correct data-modelling instinct, and the one every implementer will reach for | ❌ **REJECTED — a live-resolved certificate is not evidence.** Party identity is mutable: a musician changes their legal name, a company assigns its catalogue to a label, a writer dies and the estate takes over, a user exercises erasure (11.02.01 D-17). Live resolution means **next year's render of the same instrument shows different names than the render the client filed** — and the certificate's entire job is to be evidence of what was agreed *when*. But pure snapshotting fails the other way: the buyer holds a licence naming a party who no longer exists and cannot be found. So: **pin the identity at issue AND carry a live link to the registry entity** (D-09). Two facts, both true, neither sufficient — who agreed (historical, immutable) and who to talk to now (current). This is the same shape as grammar-version pinning (11.08.01 D-03) and rate-card pinning (11.03.01 D-02), which makes it the **fourth** instance of "the old record survives" and further evidence for 11.08.04 Q-02's suspicion that this is a platform-level invariant rather than a domain-11 rule. It also surfaces the file's hardest collision: if identity pins at issue, an erased party's name is **frozen into an irrevocable instrument a stranger relies on** (Q-06). |
| DT-13 | 11.02.01 D-12 ("at most one exclusive per (work, exclusivity scope)") is sufficient to protect exclusivity at issue | It reads as the complete rule, and it was written to be | ❌ **REJECTED — it covers exclusive-vs-exclusive and leaves exclusive-over-live-non-exclusive wide open.** Sell a non-exclusive podcast licence on Monday and an exclusive podcast licence on Friday, and the exclusive is **a lie the moment it issues**: someone else already holds a live, irrevocable grant for an overlapping scope, and irrevocability (D-04) means it cannot be cleared at any price. The buyer of the exclusive has purchased the one thing the domain sells — certainty — and received the one thing it cannot fix. D-12's remedy for the exclusive-vs-exclusive race is refusal, and refusal is the only remedy available here too, because every other option requires unmaking something. So the exclusivity check runs **both directions at issue** (D-13). Worth naming why it was missed: 11.02.01 reasoned about two buyers racing *at the same moment*, which is the vivid case; this one is two buyers months apart, which is the common one. |

Inside: mint, funds commitment, rev-share registration (09), the paired instrument. | D-10 | The instrument records three time facts: `issued-at`, `effective-from`, and `term-start` — the last of which **may be an unresolved trigger** | Per DT-05 — "term commences on first exploitation" is the industry standard and the platform cannot observe it. | D-11 | **Licensee ≠ purchaser.** The instrument names both; the purchaser warrants authority to bind the licensee | Per DT-04 — the agency buys, the brand is licensed, and a licence naming the agency is worthless to the brand |
