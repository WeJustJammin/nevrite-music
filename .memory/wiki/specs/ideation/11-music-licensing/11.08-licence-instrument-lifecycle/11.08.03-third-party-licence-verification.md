# Feature: Third-Party Licence Verification

> **Number**: 11.08.03
> **Parent**: [11.08 Licence Instrument & Lifecycle](./11.08-licence-instrument-lifecycle-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Lets someone who does not trust the licence holder check that the licence is real — a broadcaster's clearance
department, a client's legal team, a Content ID claimant, a festival asking a venue for proof. It exists
because **a certificate the holder hands you proves nothing**: anyone can produce a PDF. It was not in the
sweep's candidate list, and it is what converts the platform's record into evidence.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Can point a doubter at a verifiable record instead of arguing. Per personas.md their core pain is "re-proving who they are and what they've played on" — this is that problem's licensing-side twin, and the same answer: cited, not claimed. |
| Producer | Read-only | **The heaviest user.** Their client's legal team is the doubter, every time, on every delivery. Per personas.md a switching trigger is "a client demanding delivery in a form they can't produce" — a verifiable licence link is exactly that form. |
| Operator | Read-only | **The Operator's only touchpoint in all of domain 11.** A venue or studio asked to demonstrate that music used on their premises was licensed. Thin, real, and the whole of their stake here. |
| Fan | Read-only | As creator buyer: this is the **evidence that wins a claim dispute** (11.06.03). They will never visit the verification page; the platform cites it on their behalf. |

## Behavior

Verification answers a narrow question — **is this licence real, and is it valid?** — to a party who holds a
licence ID or a certificate, without requiring them to have an account or to trust anyone.

The honest complication: **it is a point-in-time answer about a mutable thing** (`#CX-04`). Expiry, amendment
and termination all change the answer. A verification result screenshotted by a client's legal team in March
is a claim about March, and nobody will re-check it. So every answer carries its timestamp and validity window
prominently — a stale answer must be **self-evidently stale**, because the platform cannot recall it.

The verifier reads the certificate's **pinned grammar version**, never the current one (`#R-01`) — otherwise a
grammar change would silently alter what an old licence appears to permit.

### Happy Path

1. A doubter receives a licence reference (ID, link, or QR on the certificate)
2. They open it — no account, no login, if Q-01 lands on public
3. System confirms: this licence exists, it covers [scope in plain words], it is valid as of [timestamp]
4. What is disclosed beyond validity is scoped — see Q-02; a broadcaster needs the scope, a claimant may need only "yes"
5. The answer carries its own timestamp and validity window
6. Doubter is satisfied without ever trusting the holder

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Licence expired since it was verified | The old answer is stale and **cannot be recalled** — it is a screenshot in someone's file | Mitigation is the timestamp on the answer, not a recall mechanism |
| Verifier enumerates IDs to survey who licensed what | **A real attack.** Public verification is enumerable by construction; it leaks commercial relationships. | IDs must be unguessable; rate limits; routed to `/create-prd-security` |
| Licence amended since issue | Verification shows the **current** state and the fact of amendment; the superseded version is visible as history | Per `#CX-03` — supersede, never edit |
| Verifier wants the fee | Almost certainly not disclosed — commercial terms are between the parties | See Q-02 |
| Forged certificate presented | The whole point: verification is against the platform's record, not the document. The forgery fails. | "No licence with this reference exists" |
| Verifier has no reference — just a track and a video | Cannot verify. Verification answers questions about a **known** licence, not "is this use licensed by anyone?" | A different and much harder feature; see Q-03 |
| Platform is unreachable | The licence's provability depends on the platform's uptime — and on its continued existence | Same shape as 11.06.04 DT-03: permanence promised, enforcement dependent |
| Licence issued on a bad split graph | Verifies perfectly. **Well-formed and wrong.** | 11.08.02 DT-03 — verification confirms the record, never the truth behind it |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Lookup | Fast — this is a stranger with no patience and no account |
| Empty | No such licence | "No licence with this reference" — the forgery-detection answer |
| Populated | Found | Validity, scope in plain words, timestamp, validity window |
| Error | Lookup failed | **"Cannot verify" — never "invalid".** The distinction is everything: an outage must not brand a real licence a fake. |
| Partial | Found but expired/amended | Full honesty: what it was, what it is, when it changed |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The certificate is self-sufficient — sign it cryptographically and anyone can verify it offline | The elegant answer, and technically sound | ❌ REJECTED — a signature proves the platform **issued** it; it cannot prove the licence is **still valid**. Term-limited licences expire, amendments supersede, terminations happen. An offline-verifiable certificate would be provably authentic and possibly wrong — which is worse than an unverifiable one, because it carries false confidence. Signature and lookup answer different questions: *did we issue this?* vs *is it good right now?* The second is the one the doubter is actually asking, and only a live record can answer it. |
| DT-02 | This is a niche feature — most licences are never questioned | The scope instinct, and the reason the sweep omitted it | ❌ REJECTED — it is **the point where the platform's record becomes evidence**, and three separate features are load-bearing on it. 11.06.03 (claim release) wins disputes by citing it. The Producer's client demands it on every delivery — personas.md names "a client demanding delivery in a form they can't produce" as a *switching trigger*. And the Operator's only presence in this entire domain is here. A platform whose thesis is that proof should survive (D-18) and which cannot let a third party check that proof has built a private diary, not a registry. **Verification is where provenance stops being internal.** |
| DT-03 | Verification should answer "is this use licensed?" not just "is this licence valid?" | The question doubters actually have — a claimant with a video and a track does not have a licence ID | ⏳ **DEFERRED to Q-03 — and it is the more valuable feature.** "Here is a video and a track; is this licensed?" requires fingerprinting the use, resolving the work, finding any licence covering that user for that scope, and answering without a reference. That is a genuinely different and much harder feature — and it is what would let the platform answer Content ID claims **automatically and at scale** rather than one dispute at a time (11.06.03). It is also a serious privacy surface: it lets anyone ask "does this person have a licence?" about anyone. Recorded rather than built, because inventing its behaviour now would be fabrication — but it is the direction this feature wants to grow, and 11.06.03's economics probably depend on it eventually. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Verification is a live lookup, not an offline signature check | Per DT-01 — a signature proves issuance, not current validity, and false confidence is worse than none |
| D-02 | Every answer carries its timestamp and validity window prominently | Per `#CX-04` — the platform cannot recall a stale answer, so the answer must age visibly |
| D-03 | An outage returns "cannot verify", never "invalid" | Branding a real licence a fake because of a lookup failure is unrecoverable reputationally |
| D-04 | The verifier reads the certificate's pinned grammar version | Per `#R-01` — otherwise a grammar change silently alters what an old licence appears to permit |
| D-05 | Licence references are unguessable | Public verification is enumerable by construction; enumeration leaks commercial relationships |
