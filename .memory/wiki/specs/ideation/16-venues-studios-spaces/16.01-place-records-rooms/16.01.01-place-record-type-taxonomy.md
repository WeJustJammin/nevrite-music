# Feature: Place Record & Type Taxonomy

> **Number**: 16.01.01
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The canonical record for a physical music place — a venue, a recording studio, a rehearsal
facility, or a trade/facility (luthier, rental house, pressing plant) — and the taxonomy that
decides which technical spec sheet its rooms carry. A place record exists independently of
whether anyone has claimed it: most records are born unclaimed and seeded from public data
(16.05.01), which is what gives the registry coverage before it has a single transaction.

The type is not cosmetic. `type=venue` attaches the venue spec (16.02), `type=studio` attaches
the studio spec (16.03) — and the platform's most common real-world record is a place that is
**two types at once** (a venue with a tracking room upstairs, a studio that hosts small shows).

The record is also the platform's **address of record for provenance**. A credit that says
"tracked at The Bunker, March 2026" resolves here. Under D-18 (provenance is the wedge), that
makes this file's mutation rules a thesis concern and not a data-modelling detail — see DT-15.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full (own claimed record) | Owns the record once claimed (16.05.02). Sets name, aliases, address, location precision (D-10), contact, description, permanence posture. Adds and retires rooms — which is how the type set actually changes (D-05); they never edit the type set directly except to *pre-declare* a type before a room exists. Disputes any community edit. May **grant** a room's operating org to another org — the cooperative default, instant, no claim unit required (D-12). **Cannot end that grant unilaterally**: revocation is an adjudicated dispute and the room stays live and bookable throughout (D-13). Where they will not cooperate or have vanished, the room operator may instead **claim** the room independently (D-12; 16.05.02 D-16). Cannot delete a record — only mark it closed (16.01.05), because the record carries other people's gig history. Cannot raise a statutory ceiling by editing their own record (CX-02). |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Reads the record to decide whether to enquire: where is it, who runs it, is the address real, when was any of this last verified. Creates a place record that does not yet exist (the rehearsal room they use) — 3 fields, no spec, no claim. Suggests edits (16.05.03) to identity/location fields on any record. **Cannot** set commercial fields (16.05.03 D-03), **cannot** remove a type from the set, and **cannot** set location precision — creating is not owning (16.05.02 D-04). |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | Same rights as Musician, and the highest-value editor on studio-type records — they are in the room and know the record is wrong. Frequently *is* the Operator of a record and the Musician on another in the same week (`personas.md` multi-hyphenate); the discriminating fact is the **org** (01), never the human. When a Producer edits a record their own org claims, the write lands as an owner-write, not a suggestion (see Edge Cases). |
| Fan | Read-only (venue half only) | Sees a record iff `venue ∈ typeset`, as a show-discovery surface — where is this gig, how do I get there, is it accessible (audience route only, 16.01.04). On a `[venue, studio]` record the studio rooms, studio spec and all rates are absent from the Fan view, not merely collapsed. Reaches an `ad-hoc` record via its show page but never via search. Studio, rehearsal and trade records are not surfaced to Fans at all — see DT-11 for why "where was my favourite album made" is answered by 02 Credits and not by this record. Cannot suggest, cannot claim, cannot create. |

## Behavior

A place record is an **entity**, not a listing. Nothing about it is transactable — everything
transactable (spec, calendar, rate) hangs off its rooms (16.01.02 D-01). The record answers four
questions and no others: *what is this place called, where is it, what kinds of place is it, and
who — if anyone — controls it.*

### What the record holds

| Group | Fields | Write class |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Canonical name; aliases; former names, each with a date range (`"The Boileroom" → 2019-04`) | Community-correctable fact |
| **Location** | Address (structured), geo point, `location_precision`, load-in access point (a distinct pin from the front door — the two are routinely 100m+ apart and only one of them matters at 3am) | Community-correctable fact; **anchor class** (D-09) |
| **Type** | `typeset` — a set, never an enum (D-01) | Asserted, invariant-constrained (D-05) |
| **Rooms** | Composition — 0..N rooms (16.01.02) | Operator / room operator |
| **Claim** | Claim state, controlling org (01), claim history | 16.05.02 |
| **Permanence** | `permanent` · `seasonal` · `ad-hoc` (D-08) | Operator; `ad-hoc` set at creation by 18, never editable to `permanent` without promotion |
| **Contact** | Public phone, public domain, enquiry routing target (16.06.06) | Operator; **anchor class** (D-09) |
| **Provenance** | Per-field source class, contributor, timestamp, evidence, freshness clock | 16.05.03 — never directly writable by anyone |

Every field carries where it came from and when it was last verified. There is no record-level
"verified" flag — see DT-13.

### The type set and its invariants

Type is a **set, not an enum** (D-01). The set decides which spec sheets are attachable to the
record's rooms: `venue` → 16.02, `studio` → 16.03, `rehearsal` → 16.04, `trade` → 16.01.07's
trade profile. A place typed `[venue, studio]` has rooms carrying 16.02 sheets and rooms carrying
16.03 sheets, under one record, one org, one enquiry inbox.

The set is **asserted at birth and derivation-constrained thereafter** (D-05). Two invariants,
both hard:

1. **Auto-extend.** Adding a room whose spec type `T ∉ typeset` sets `typeset ∪= {T}` silently, with
   no prompt. An Operator adding a tracking room to their venue has just become a studio; asking
   them to confirm it is asking them to re-state what they did.
2. **Removal is blocked, never cascaded.** Removing `T` from `typeset` while any non-retired room of
   type `T` exists is rejected with the blocking room list. Cascading would silently orphan a live
   room's spec — and its calendar, and its bookings. The Operator retires the room; the type leaves
   on its own.

A record may assert a type it has no room for (`typeset = {venue}`, 0 rooms). That is the seeded
and freshly-created state, and it is the majority of the registry at cold start — the type set
must not require a room, or the whole coverage-at-zero-transactions premise fails (DT-05). The
converse never holds: a room's type is always in the set.

The set **never auto-empties.** Retiring the last venue room does not remove `venue` — a venue with
no rooms configured is a real and common data state; a venue that has *ceased to be a venue* is a
closure (16.01.05), which is a different fact with a different meaning and a different audience.

### Containment: one building, N operating orgs

A studio subleasing a room inside a venue is **one record** (a record is a building — D-04), with
the subleased room carrying a different **operating org** from the place's claimant (D-06).

- The **place claim** (16.05.02) proves control of the *premises* and grants: identity, location,
  permanence, contact, and the default operating org for every room.
- A **room operating org comes into existence by either of two paths** (D-12). Neither is a fallback
  for the other; they answer different situations:
  - **Grant** — the place claimant hands the room to another org. Cooperative, **instant**, one
    action, no claim unit and no review (16.05.02 D-10's cooperative-transfer shape, one level down).
    This is the path most subleases take, and it costs the sublessee nothing.
  - **Room-scoped claim** — the room operator proves control of the *room* through 16.05.02's ladder
    scoped to the room (D-16 there), anchored to their own pre-existing facts and to the sublease or
    licence agreement, never to the place's. This is the path for the landlord who will not
    cooperate or has vanished, and it is why a sublessee is not at the landlord's mercy.
- The place claimant is notified of an incoming room claim within 24h and may dispute it. A disputed
  room claim escalates to 24 and **the room stays live and bookable throughout** — 16.05.02 D-03 one
  level down, for the same reason: a dispute between two parties who both say they run the room must
  not take the room off the market.
- **A grant cannot be revoked unilaterally** (D-13). Initiating revocation opens a counter-window and
  escalates to 24 if answered; the room stays live and bookable while it runs, and a revocation that
  completes may never orphan a booking whose counterparty has already paid a deposit (16.01.02 D-16).
- **Which path produced a room's authority is recorded on the room**, because the two can meet on one
  room and no source yet defines what happens when they do — Q-10.
- The room operator writes their room's spec, calendar, rate card and booking posture. They cannot
  write the record's address, cannot close the place, and cannot change the type set except by
  adding/retiring their own rooms.
- **Statutory facts stay premises-level** (16.01.06, CX-02). Where the record's **regime profile**
  declares a premises-level statutory instrument (16.01.06 D-05), the sublessee trades under it and
  cannot exceed its occupancy ceiling or its operating-hours condition by asserting otherwise on
  their room. Where the profile declares no such instrument, there is nothing premises-level to
  inherit and the room's own claims stand as claims (16.02.03 D-01) — the containment rule is
  unchanged either way, only its input is. This is still the hardest argument for containment being
  one record: a premises instrument is one document about one building, and two records would have
  to hold two copies of it.

### Permanence: three postures

| Posture | What it is | Searchable | Claimable | Rooms | Decays | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `permanent` | The default. A building with a controller | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ (16.05.05) | Any venue, studio, rehearsal room |
| `seasonal` | Permanent premises, opening posture is intermittent | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | A festival site; a summer-only outdoor stage |
| `ad-hoc` | A pointer to where a one-off thing happened. **Not supply** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ (frozen at event date) | A warehouse show; a one-time private space |

`ad-hoc` records are created **by 18 Show Production** when a show resolves to an address that is
not in the registry, and never by the registry's own creation flow. They carry name + address +
geo + the show that created them, and nothing else — no spec, no calendar, no rate, no claim path,
no freshness clock (there is no fact to keep fresh; the record is a statement about one night that
has already happened). Creating one still runs duplicate detection first: if the address matches an
existing `permanent` record, the show resolves to that record and no `ad-hoc` record is created.

**Promotion**: when **3 distinct shows from 2+ distinct promoters** resolve to the same `ad-hoc`
address within **24 months**, the platform proposes promotion to `permanent` and opens a claim
path. Rationale for the two-promoter half: one promoter with repeat access to a space is a private
arrangement (their friend's warehouse), which is not supply; two independent promoters reaching the
same space means the space is available to the market, which is what supply means. The thresholds
are proposed, not validated — Q-04.

A festival site is **not** `ad-hoc` (DT-09). It is a permanent place with a real load-in, a real
capacity, whatever statutory permission its regime requires for the event (16.01.06 D-05 — the
instrument is profile-declared, and in a profile that declares none the test is unaffected), and a
controller who can claim it. It is open five days a year, which is a posture, not an ontology.

### Location precision

`location_precision ∈ {exact, approximate}` is a property of the record, resolved per audience.

| Precision | Public sees | A hirer with a **confirmed** reservation sees |
|---|---|---|
| `exact` | Full structured address + geo point + load-in access point | Same |
| `approximate` | Locality + postal district; geo point snapped to the district centroid; no street line, no house number, no load-in pin | Full `exact` payload, released on reservation confirmation (16.06.03) |

Defaults: `exact` for any record with `venue ∈ typeset` — a venue's address is public information
already printed on a ticket, and hiding it breaks 19. `approximate` for a `studio` / `rehearsal`
record whose address is flagged residential. Operator-settable on a claimed record; **not**
settable by the community on an unclaimed one (a suggestion that reveals a home address is not a
correction).

Two hard constraints:
- A record with an **on-sale ticketed show** (19) cannot be `approximate`. The ticket carries the
  address; an approximate address on a ticket is a wrong ticket.
- Precision downgrade is **forward-only in effect**: a hirer who already received the exact address
  keeps it. The platform cannot un-tell someone a fact. Stated because the alternative is a
  guarantee the product cannot honour.

### Field classes and who writes them

| Class | Fields | Who writes | Beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Statutory** | Derived from the statutory record (16.01.06) — **only where the record's regime profile declares the slot or instrument** (16.01.06 D-05) | Nobody — derived | Everything (16.05.03 D-02) |
| **Anchor** | Address, public phone, public domain | Owner-write, or community suggestion with a stability window (D-09) | Community |
| **Fact** | Name, aliases, former names, load-in access point, description | Owner-write; community suggestion (16.05.03) | Seeded |
| **Commercial** | Rate, deal terms — **not on this record**, they live on the room (16.06.07) | Room operator only | — |
| **Structural** | `typeset`, `permanence`, `location_precision` | Operator; `typeset` invariant-constrained (D-05) | Community (community cannot remove a type) |

### Happy Path

1. Musician searches "rehearsal rooms near Guildford"; the room they use every Tuesday is not in the results
2. Musician taps **Add a place** and enters three fields: name, address, type — no spec, no room, no photos
3. System geocodes the address and returns a map pin; Musician drags the pin 40m to the actual door
4. System runs duplicate detection (16.05.07) against committed state and shows two near-matches — "The Old Dairy, 4 Mill Lane" at 60m and "Mill Lane Studios" at 110m — with a **This is a different place** confirmation, biased toward under-merging (16.05.07 D-02)
5. Musician confirms it is distinct; record is created `unclaimed`, `permanence: permanent`, `typeset: {rehearsal}`, 0 rooms
6. Every field is stamped provenance class **5 — community suggestion, unreviewed on an unclaimed record** (16.05.03), and the freshness clock starts (16.05.05)
7. Record is publicly visible and indexable. It is **not bookable** — it has no rooms, therefore no calendar. It shows an enquiry-only posture and a "never verified" marker (D-11)
8. Six weeks later the Operator finds the record and claims it (16.05.02) — proof anchors to the record's *pre-claim* public phone number, which the Operator did not supply (D-09)
9. Operator adds Room A (`rehearsal`, cap 8). `typeset` is already `{rehearsal}`, so nothing auto-extends. The room layer stays invisible — one room, so the Operator never meets the word "room" (16.01.02 D-03). The record is now bookable, and the Musician's contributed identity and address survive the claim with their provenance intact (16.05.02 happy path step 4)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Place already exists under a former name ("The Boileroom" → "The Bunker") | Duplicate detection (16.05.07) matches on geo + alias date-range; offers merge instead of create | "A place at this address already exists — is this it?" with the existing record |
| Venue moves address, keeps name | New location is a **new record**; old record → `relocated` (16.01.05) with a successor link. Gig history stays attached to the building where it happened (D-04) | Both records live, cross-linked "moved to / moved from" |
| **Studio subleases a room inside a venue — the landlord cooperates** | One record (D-04). The venue **grants** Tracking Room's operating org to the studio: one action, instant, no claim unit, no review (D-12). This is the default and the majority case | Studio: "You now operate Tracking Room. The premises are operated by {venue org}." Venue: confirmation of the grant it just made |
| **…the landlord will not cooperate, or has vanished** | The studio **claims** the room instead — 16.05.02's ladder scoped to the room, running on **L7 document review** with the sublease/licence agreement as the document and the venue's claimant org as the external cross-check (16.05.02 D-16). Place claimant notified within 24h, may dispute. **Provisional until a human clears the document**, so no calendar and no rate card until then — the grant path stays instant, the claim path does not | Studio: "We'll need your sublease — reviewed within 5 working days. You can describe the room now; you can't take bookings yet." Venue: "{studio org} has claimed Tracking Room — Confirm / Dispute" |
| Venue Operator disputes the sublessee's room claim during a rent row | Escalates to 24. **Room stays live and bookable throughout**; existing reservations unaffected; the claim freezes, the room does not (16.05.02 D-03) | Both parties: "Room operator under review — bookings unaffected". Hirers: nothing |
| **Venue Operator tries to revoke a grant during a rent row** | **Not an act — a dispute** (D-13). Initiating revocation opens a counter-window with maximal notification; if the room operator answers, it escalates to 24, which becomes the arbiter of whether the sublease genuinely ended. **The room stays live and bookable throughout** and no paid deposit is ever orphaned (16.01.02 D-16). Accepted cost: a landlord whose tenant has genuinely vacated must wait the window out before re-letting the room on the platform | Venue: "Ending this arrangement is a dispute, not a switch. {studio org} has 14 days to respond." Studio: "{venue org} says your arrangement has ended — respond by [date]." Hirers: nothing |
| **A granted room's operator later claims it, or a room claim lands on an already-granted room** | **Unspecified.** Two authority provenances can exist on one room and no source in this domain defines precedence, whether the second is refused, or whether the grant survives a successful claim. Recorded rather than guessed — **Q-10** | `[PENDING — Q-10]` |
| Sublessee's room asserts a capacity above the premises statutory ceiling | Blocked **where the record's regime profile declares an occupancy-ceiling instrument** (16.01.06 D-05/D-06). Statutory class outranks every org on the record, including one that is not the place claimant (CX-02, 16.01.06 D-02). Where the profile declares none, there is no ceiling to breach and the figure publishes as an unverified Operator claim (16.02.01 Claimed state) | Ceiling present: "The occupancy ceiling for these premises is 200. This room cannot exceed it." Ceiling absent: "Capacity not verified against a statutory record" |
| **Place is a one-off pop-up (warehouse show)** | 18 creates a `permanence: ad-hoc` pointer record — name, address, geo, originating show. Not searchable, not claimable, no rooms, no spec, no calendar, no decay clock (D-08) | Fan reaches it from the show page and gets an address and a map. Nobody else ever sees it |
| Same `ad-hoc` address used by 3 shows / 2 promoters in 24 months | Promotion proposed: record becomes `permanent`, becomes searchable, opens a claim path | "This space has hosted 3 shows — is it a venue? Claim it" |
| `ad-hoc` creation for an address that is already a `permanent` record | No `ad-hoc` record created. The show resolves to the existing record. Duplicate detection runs **before** creation, not after | 18 shows the matched venue for confirmation |
| **Festival site** | `permanence: seasonal`. A full record: rooms (stages), specs, capacity, licence, claim, decay — open 5 days a year is a posture, not an ontology (DT-09) | Normal record with a seasonal availability posture (16.06.01) |
| Home studio at a residential address | `location_precision: approximate` — locality + postal district, geo snapped to district centroid, no street line, no load-in pin. Escalates to `exact` for a hirer on reservation confirmation only (D-10) | "Exact address shared once your booking is confirmed" |
| Community suggests an edit that would reveal an `approximate` record's street address | Rejected at class level — `location_precision` is a structural field, and a suggestion that publishes a home is not a correction of a fact | "Only the operator can change this place's address precision" |
| Ticketed show goes on sale against an `approximate` record | Blocked. The ticket carries the address (19); an approximate address on a ticket is a wrong ticket | "This place's address is hidden. Set it to exact before putting tickets on sale." |
| **Attacker edits an unclaimed record's address to a building they control, then claims by utility bill** | The claim-anchor attack, one field over from 16.05.02 DT-02. Address is **anchor class**: an edit within the stability window **invalidates the field as claim evidence** rather than merely logging it. Proof falls back to a pre-edit anchor value or to a stronger rung (D-09, DT-10) | "This address was changed recently and can't be used to verify a claim. Verify by phone or domain instead." |
| Attacker edits the phone, waits out the window, then claims | The window is the whole defence and its length is the whole question — Q-01. Default: the field must be stable for **90 days, or since seeding, whichever is shorter**, and any anchor edit notifies a claimed record's Operator immediately | Operator: "Your listed phone number was changed" |
| Operator removes `studio` from the type set while a studio room has live bookings | **Blocked, never cascaded** — cascading orphans the room's spec, calendar and bookings in one write (D-05, CX-01) | "Tracking Room uses the studio spec and has 4 bookings. Retire the room first." |
| Operator adds a tracking room to a venue-typed record | `typeset` auto-extends to `{venue, studio}` silently. No prompt — they already made the decision by adding the room | Room appears; studio spec sheet attaches; no dialog |
| Operator retires the last `venue` room | `venue` **stays** in the set. A venue with no rooms configured is a normal data state; a venue that has stopped being a venue is a closure (16.01.05) — different fact, different audience | Record stays a venue, 0 bookable rooms, Empty view state |
| Seeded record has a type but zero rooms (the cold-start majority) | Valid and expected. Searchable, unclaimed, not bookable, no calendar. This is what coverage-at-zero-transactions *is* (DT-05) | Record page + "Do you run this place? Claim it" + "Never verified" |
| Two Musicians create the same place within seconds | Creation is **not** blocked on a race — blocking is worse than a duplicate. Writes serialise on the normalised-address key; the second write re-runs detection against committed state and surfaces the first record before accepting | Second creator: "This was just added by someone else — is this it?" |
| Network drops mid-create; Musician retries | Creation is idempotent on (account, normalised address, name) within 15 minutes. The retry returns the existing record; it does not create a second | "You already added this place" → the record |
| Operator on a loading dock, no signal, adds a place | Creation queues locally and submits on reconnect; duplicate detection runs **at submit**, not at capture, so near-matches reflect committed state. The Operator persona works "on a phone at a loading dock" — a flow that requires signal at the moment of typing is a flow that is not used | "Saved — will be added when you're back online" |
| Bulk record creation from one account (scripted) | Creation is capped at **20 records per account per 24h**; more than **5 within 1 hour** holds the batch for review. Records already created stay live — a hold blocks new writes, it does not retract facts | "You've added a lot of places today — these are queued for review" |
| Malicious fabricated record for a place that does not exist | Nothing to verify. Never claimed, never harvested, decays to the freshness floor (16.05.05), suppressed from search at the floor (16.01.05 D-03). The registry does not need to detect the lie — it needs to never present it as true | "This record has never been verified — it may not exist" |
| Competitor creates a record for a fake venue at a rival's address to poison the rival's claim anchor | Address alone never merges (16.05.07 D-03) so the rival's record is untouched; the fake record's own anchor is fresh, so it backs no claim (D-09). The rival's pre-existing anchors are unaffected | Rival: nothing. The attack has no effect on their record |
| Two genuinely different venues both called "The Grand", different cities | Not duplicates. Name alone never matches (16.05.07 D-03 in mirror) — geo is the discriminator | Both records, both live |
| Two **claimed** records, different orgs, same address | Not a merge (16.05.07: two claimed records is a contested claim). But it is often neither — it is containment recorded wrong. Escalates to 24 with a **containment migration** option: fold the contained record's rooms into the premises record, transferring their operating org (D-06) | "These may be one building with two operators — merge as premises + operator?" |
| Producer edits a record their own org claims | Lands as an **owner-write**, not a suggestion. The persona lens is not the permission — the **org** is (16.05.02 D-05). One human is a Producer here and an Operator there in the same session, and the record must not ask which hat they are wearing | Direct edit, no review queue |
| Producer suggests an edit to a room whose **operating org** is theirs, inside a record claimed by someone else | Owner-write on the room; suggestion on the record. The permission boundary follows containment, not the page | Room fields editable; record fields show "Suggest an edit" |
| Multi-site operator group: 4 rooms in 4 cities | 4 records, 1 org (01). Not one record — a record is a building (D-04) | One org dashboard, 4 records |
| Geocode fails: rural site, new-build, no postal code, or a country with thin coverage | Record accepts a **manually dropped pin** and stores the address as free text with `geocode: manual`. It is not blocked — a place the geocoder has never heard of is exactly the place nobody else has listed, which is the coverage the registry exists to have | "We couldn't find that address — drop a pin instead" |
| Address geocodes 200m off (bad source data) | The record is not wrong, the geocode is. Community suggestion on the geo point with the map pin as evidence; anchor-class stability window applies to the address line, not to a pin correction that leaves the address text unchanged | "Pin looks wrong? Move it" |
| Name contains RTL text, emoji, 400 characters, or an HTML fragment | Name is stored as text and always rendered escaped; length capped at 200 characters; no markup interpreted anywhere. A venue genuinely called "❤" is a real venue | Over-length: "Names are limited to 200 characters" |
| Building is demolished | `permanently closed` (16.01.05), never deleted. The record carries other people's gig history and harvested reports (D-03) | Read-only historical record; still linked from every past gig |
| Operator demands the record be deleted entirely (a business that never wanted listing) | Closure + delisting from search is available today. **Hard deletion is not**, and whether a business has a right to it against a record it never created is Q-05 — the domain's Q-14, acute here because this feature is where the record is born | "This place has been removed from search" — record retained, gig history intact |
| A merged record is cited by a live show (17/18) or an on-sale ticket (19) | The loser record's identifier must keep resolving to the survivor indefinitely. A redirect that expires breaks a ticket someone already bought | Transparent — the show resolves to the survivor |
| A credit (02) cites a place that has since relocated, renamed, or merged | The citation must resolve to the record **as of the cited date**, not to its current state — otherwise a later edit silently rewrites where a session happened (DT-15). Owned by 02; flagged as Q-06 | "Recorded at The Boileroom (now The Bunker), March 2019" |
| Listings-site seeding creates "Jazz at the Vaults" as a place, at a venue's address | It is not a place — it is a **promoter-branded night** (an 18/21 event brand) wearing a venue's address. Detection sees name mismatch + exact address match and would under-merge by rule (16.05.07 D-03). This class needs a negative rule of its own: an event brand is not an alias (DT-14, Q-07) | Currently: two records, one building. Wrong, and flagged |
| A live room used for tracking on weekdays and shows on Sundays | One room, two spec sheets, one calendar. The room's type is a **set** for exactly the reason the place's is (DT-06). Two rooms would let both be booked at once — 16.01.02 DT-03's rejected error one level down. Owned by 16.01.02; flagged as Q-08 | — |
| Fan opens a `studio` record via a direct link | Not rendered. Not a 404 — a "this is a professional record" page with no address, no rate, no contact (DT-11) | "This place isn't part of the public listings" |
| Fan opens a `[venue, studio]` record | Venue half only: name, address, doors/transport, audience accessibility route. Studio rooms are **absent from the payload**, not collapsed in the UI | A venue page. The studio does not exist to them |

### Record Lifecycle States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unclaimed | Seeded (16.05.01) or user-created; no verified controller | Public record + "Do you run this place? Claim it" banner |
| Claim pending | Claim submitted, proof incomplete (16.05.02) | Record unchanged and fully live; only the claimant sees progress |
| Claimed | Control proven (16.05.02); org holds write access | Org-controlled fields; community suggestions queue for review |
| Contested | 2+ place claims, or an Operator disputes a community edit | **Record stays live and bookable.** Disputed fields show last-agreed value + a dispute marker (D-05, domain) |
| Room-contested | A room's operating-org claim is disputed by the place claimant | Record and **room** both stay live and bookable; the room claim freezes; 24 escalation |
| Room-authority disputed | The place claimant has initiated revocation of a **granted** room and the counter-window is running (D-13) | Record and **room** both stay live and bookable; the grant stands until the window closes or 24 rules; both parties see the deadline; hirers see nothing |
| Ad-hoc | Created by 18 for a one-off show (D-08) | Address + map, reachable only from its show. Absent from search |
| At-risk | Orthogonal to all of the above (16.01.05 D-01) — a fully trading record can be at risk | Normal record + at-risk banner + follow CTA |
| Relocated | Operator sets, links successor (D-04) | Both records live, cross-linked; gig history stays on the old building |
| Closed | Marked closed (16.01.05) — temporarily or permanently | Read-only historical record; not bookable; still linked from past gigs |
| Merged | Resolved as a duplicate (16.05.07) | Loser identifier redirects to the survivor, indefinitely, for tickets already sold |

### View States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Record requested | Skeleton: name line, address line, map placeholder. Nothing that implies a fact — a skeleton that renders a plausible-looking address before the real one lands is a lie with a spinner on it |
| Empty | Record exists, **0 rooms** — the cold-start majority (seeded or freshly created) | Identity + location + type + provenance. No calendar, no spec, no rate. Enquiry-only posture (16.06.06). "Never verified" marker. Operator CTA: "Add your rooms" |
| Populated | Claimed, ≥1 room, spec above the minimum bar (16.01.02 States) | Full record: rooms, specs, availability, freshness age per field |
| Partial | Record has rooms but fields are missing, decayed past the warning threshold, or under dispute | Record renders with **per-field** markers — "load-in: not stated", "capacity: last verified 3 years ago", "curfew: disputed". Never a global "incomplete" banner: per-field provenance means the record is simultaneously fresh and stale, and one banner must lie about most of it (16.05.03 DT-01) |
| Error | Record fetch fails | "Couldn't load this place" + retry. **Never** a cached-but-unlabelled render — a stale venue address shown as current is the failure this domain exists to prevent |
| Conflicted | ≥1 field under dispute (16.05.04) | Record fully live; disputed fields show last-agreed value + marker + "why?" link to the field's history |

Rooms are multi-hyphenate too — a venue with a tracking room, a studio that does Sunday shows, a rehearsal facility with a live room. When the load-in changes, the two records disagree — which is fact-duplication-across-systems, the thing the registry exists to end, reproduced inside the registry. The sublessee does not need a record. They need a **room** — the unit that already carries the spec, the calendar and the rate (16.01.02 D-01). Containment is **room-level operating org**. |

Naming one anchor field and not the others leaves the ladder's weakest rung undefended. **Anchor class (address, public phone, public domain): an edit within the stability window invalidates the field as claim evidence** — it is not enough to log it. | DT-12 | A record for one person's home studio is the same object as a record for a 2000-cap venue | One entity, one type set, one address field — the whole premise of DT-02's merge | ❌ REJECTED on location. Publishing everything the geocoder returned is a defensible policy for venues and an indefensible one for the majority of studio supply. **Precision is a property of the record with per-audience resolution** (D-10). Note the asymmetry this creates with 19: a ticketed show *cannot* be approximate, so precision is not a free preference — it is constrained by what the record is used for. |
| DT-13 | A claimed record should carry a "verified" badge | It is what every directory does, and 16.05.02 already produces exactly this signal | ❌ REJECTED as a single badge — **claim state and freshness are orthogonal, and one badge must lie about one of them.** A claimed record whose spec has not been touched in four years is *worse* than an unclaimed one carrying a band's report from Tuesday, and a "verified" badge would rank them the other way. The Operator's accidental anti-persona is literally "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show" — a badge that reads as *accurate* when it means *claimed* is the mechanism that ships that failure to the band. Treating them as aliases pollutes the alias set with brands the building's Operator does not control and cannot correct; treating them as separate places splits the calendar. | DT-15 | A credit citing a place is a live pointer to the current record | It is the default relational read, and every other consumer (17, 18, 19) genuinely does want the current record | ❌ REJECTED for 02, and it is a **thesis-level** finding. "Tracked at The Bunker, 4 High St, March 2026" is a fact **about the past**. If the venue relocates (D-04 makes that a *different record*), renames, or merges into a survivor, a live pointer silently rewrites where the session happened — the credit ends up claiming a session occurred in a building it did not occur in. D-18 says provenance is the wedge; a provenance record that mutates underneath its own citation is not provenance, it is a cache.
