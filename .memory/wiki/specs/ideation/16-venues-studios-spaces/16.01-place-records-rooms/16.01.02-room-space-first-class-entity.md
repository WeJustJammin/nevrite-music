# Feature: Room / Space as First-Class Child Entity

> **Number**: 16.01.02
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The bookable, spec-carrying unit inside a place. A venue is not one thing — it is a 400-cap main
room and a 80-cap basement with different stages, different curfews and different deals. A studio
is Live Room A, the vocal booth, and the mix suite. **The room, not the place, is what has a
spec, an availability calendar, and a rate.**

This is the structural spine of the domain. Getting it wrong collapses everything downstream:
if the spec hangs off the place, a band books "the venue" and arrives to the wrong room; if the
calendar hangs off the place, a studio cannot sell its mix suite while a band is tracking. It is
also the domain's contribution to D-18: the room is the resolution at which "where the work
happened" becomes a *fact* rather than an address — a session credit that says "Studio X" is a
claim, one that says "Live Room A, 14 March, this reservation" is provenance.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Creates and names rooms on a **claimed** record; attaches each room's spec (16.02/16.03); sets per-room availability, rates and booking posture. Declares each room's **dependencies** (which other rooms and shared facilities it consumes) — from which the platform derives exclusion, rather than asking them to declare N(N−1)/2 pairwise rules (DT-05). Sees the blast radius before every destructive act: retiring, subdividing, or adding a dependency names the bookings it would touch. May **grant** a single room to another org (D-09) without surrendering the record — instant, one action — and **cannot take it back unilaterally**: revocation is an adjudicated dispute (D-16). Where they refuse or have vanished, the other org may **claim** the room instead, without them (D-15). At N=1 room, is never shown the word "room" at all (D-03). |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Browses and books **a specific room, not a place** — "the Basement, 8pm", never "the venue". Compares rooms within one record on the two numbers a band decides on: capacity (16.02.01) and fee (16.06.07). Suggests edits to any room's spec via 16.05.03, and — after a booking completes — is the highest-authority corrector on load-in and access facts (16.05.06). Reads the room's *performer* accessibility profile (16.01.04); the place-level question "is this venue accessible?" is unanswerable and this feature is why. |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | The heaviest reader — **the room choice IS the creative decision** (16.03.01). Reads acoustics, sightlines, isolation, signal chain, and specifically which rooms can run *simultaneously* (a mix in the control room while a band tracks next door is either the studio's second revenue stream or a physical impossibility, and only the dependency graph knows which). Highest-authority editor on studio room specs. When they are also a room's **delegate** (D-09), they hold Full on their room and Read-only+suggest on every sibling room of the same record — the multi-hyphenate permission intersection this feature creates. |
| Fan | Read-only (name, route, access) | Sees the room **name** on a ticket ("Main Room" vs "Basement"), the room's **entrance/route** (the basement's door is round the back — a place-level address sends them to the wrong door), and the room's **audience** accessibility profile (16.01.04). Never sees specs, rates, availability, dependencies or the graph. A multi-room venue running two shows at once means a Fan can be at the right venue and the wrong room — the room name on the ticket is the only thing that prevents it. |

## Behavior

A room belongs to **exactly one place record** and carries: name, aliases, type (live room /
control room / booth / stage / rehearsal room / mix suite / lockout unit), the spec sheet
appropriate to its type, capacity and configurations (16.02.01), photos (16.01.03),
accessibility (16.01.04), a rate card (16.06.07) and an availability calendar (16.06.01).

**Room aliases are load-bearing, not cosmetic.** The door says "Room 2", the promoter says "the
Cellar", the calendar says "Basement". The harvest (16.05.06) receives free text from a band who
were there, and the rider (16.07) names a room in the hirer's vocabulary. Without aliases, a
correct report attaches to nothing.

### The spec is fixed ∪ mobile — the room owns only the fixed half

A room's spec splits on a fact the industry takes for granted and no data model states (DT-07):

| Class | Owner | Examples | Contended? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | The room | Dimensions, ceiling height, treatment, sightlines, installed PA, tie-lines, load-in route, power | No — it is the room |
| **Mobile** | The **place** | Mic locker (16.03.02), outboard on a trolley, backline, a wheeled desk, a hired-in PA | **Yes** — one U47, three rooms |

A room's *usable* spec on a given date is its fixed spec plus the mobile assets allocated to that
booking. Two rooms booked simultaneously contend for one locker. This is the same mechanism as
the shared-PA case below, and it is why contention had to be modelled (D-06).

### Rooms interlock — three typed relationships, one graph

The critical modelling fact is that rooms are **not independent resources**. 16.06.05 D-03
establishes that exclusion and compounding are one resource graph read in two directions; this
feature owns that graph's declaration, and its edges are typed:

| Kind | Direction | Enforcement | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`requires`** | **Directional** | Hard. Booking A atomically reserves B as a compound member (16.06.05) | Live Room A *requires* Control 1 to track. Control 1 does **not** require Live Room A — a mix sells alone. |
| **`excludes`** | Symmetric, **derived** — never declared | Hard. The calendar shows unavailable **with the reason** (16.06.01 D-05) | Live A and Live B both `require` Control 1 ⇒ booking either excludes the other. Nobody typed that rule. |
| **`part-of`** | Directional, symmetric in effect | Hard. Booking the whole excludes every part; booking a part excludes the whole but **not** its sibling parts | A hall with an airwall: "Main" is `part-of`-parent to "Main North" + "Main South" (DT-08) |
| **`contends`** | Symmetric | **Soft.** Both bookable. The second booking is admitted only after the contended facility and its Operator-authored rule are shown and acknowledged; both parties are notified | Main Room and Basement share one PA — both can trade, but not both loud. Studio A and B share one load-in lift. |

**Exclusion is derived, not declared.** The Operator declares what each room *needs*; the platform
computes what that blocks. This is not an implementation nicety — pairwise declaration cannot
express the control-room asymmetry, and asymmetry is where the studio's second revenue stream
lives (DT-05).

**Relationship changes are effective-dated.** A studio genuinely does knock through next month.
An edge added today must be true for future bookings without retroactively invalidating a session
that already happened.

### Bookability requires a claim

Most of the registry is unclaimed at cold start. A room on an unclaimed record can be *described*
(via 16.05.03, like any other field) but **can never leave Draft**, because there is nobody to
honour a booking (D-11). `Live` requires all three: the place is claimed (16.05.02), the room's
spec meets the minimum bar, and a posture is set (16.06.06).

### Room authority: granted, or claimed — and never revoked by an act

A room's **operating org** may differ from the record's claimant (16.01.01 D-06). It arrives by one
of two paths, and the room records **which** (D-15):

| Path | Who initiates | Cost | Speed | When it is the right one |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grant** (D-09) | The place claimant | None — no claim unit, no review | **Instant** | The cooperative sublease. The majority case |
| **Room-scoped claim** | The room operator | 16.05.02's L7 document review (D-16 there) — sublease/licence agreement, place claimant org as external cross-check | **5-business-day SLA**; Provisional meanwhile, so **no calendar and no rate card** until a human clears it | The landlord who will not cooperate, or has vanished |

**Extinguishment is symmetric with neither.** A grant is not a switch the claimant can flip back
(D-16): initiating revocation opens a counter-window, escalates to 24 if the room operator answers,
and **the room stays live and bookable throughout**. The booking-safety rule that used to govern
revocation is carried into the dispute path unchanged — a revocation that completes is
**effective-dated after the last confirmed booking**, or requires an explicit Operator-fault
cancellation of each (16.06.03 D-05). **No paid deposit is ever orphaned by a revocation.**

There is no anchor snapshot to re-derive here — a grant is not a claim, so 16.05.02's anchor
machinery has nothing to read. What the room operator's response asserts is that the arrangement
**subsists**, and adjudicating that is 24's, deliberately (16.01.01 D-13, Q-11).

**The cost this imposes, stated rather than left implicit**: a place claimant whose tenant has
genuinely vacated must still wait out the counter-window — and, if it is answered, 24 — before they
can re-let that room on the platform. That is the deliberate price of never letting a revocation
take a trading room offline, and it falls on the honest landlord as well as the leveraging one.

### A room is a physical volume, not a name

16.01.01 D-04 rules that a record is a building, not a business — a relocation is a new record
because nothing physical survives a move. **The same rule applies one level down** (D-08):

| Event | Physical volume | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Rename** ("the Cellar" → "the Vault") | Unchanged | Same room. Old name retained as an alias. History follows. |
| **Refit** (new treatment, new desk, new PA) | Unchanged | Same room. Field-level freshness resets on affected fields (16.05.05). History follows — otherwise every refit orphans a discography. |
| **Reconfigure** (seated 200 / standing 400) | Unchanged | Same room, same calendar. A capacity property (D-04, 16.02.01 D-03). |
| **Subdivide** (one room → two) | **Changed** | Parent → `Superseded`. Two new rooms, `formed-from: [parent]`. |
| **Merge** (two rooms → one) | **Changed** | Both → `Superseded`. One new room, `formed-from: [A, B]`. |

**History is never re-attributed.** Bookings, harvested reports (16.05.06), reviews and credits
attach to the room that was **live on the event date**. Lineage links make history *traversable*
— they never move it. "I tracked drums in Live Room A" is a fact about a volume that no longer
exists; copying it onto the two rooms that replaced it would manufacture provenance, which is the
one thing this platform must never do (D-18).

The Operator declares which event occurred, and their incentive is skewed — declaring a *refit*
keeps the reviews and the reputation, declaring a *subdivision* loses them. That is
`personas.md`'s Operator anti-persona ("misrepresents the room's specs") applied to lineage, so a
lineage change on a room carrying prior harvested reports is a suggested edit subject to contest
(16.05.03 / 16.05.04), not a unilateral write.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims a place (16.05.02). System asks one question: **"Is more than one space bookable here?"**
2. Operator says no → a default room is auto-created, inherits the place's name, and the room layer is never rendered. Operator lists, prices and sells without meeting the concept. **For most of the registry the happy path ends here.**
3. Six months later the Operator takes over the unit next door and adds a second space. The default room is **promoted, not replaced** — it keeps every booking, photo, review and harvested report — and the Operator is asked for one blocking field: a name for it ("Room 1").
4. Operator adds Room B: name, type (`rehearsal room`), capacity. System attaches the spec sheet for that type; Room B opens in `Draft` with a spec-completeness meter.
5. System asks the only relationship question it ever asks: **"What does Room B need in order to run?"** — offering the place's existing rooms and shared facilities (the PA, the lift, the desk, the locker).
6. Operator selects the shared load-in lift. System records `Room A contends Room B (facility: goods lift)` — both stay bookable; a second hirer overlapping the first's load-in window will be shown the lift rule and asked to acknowledge it.
7. Operator completes Room B's spec; posture set (16.06.06); Room B goes `Live`. Both calendars now sell independently — the outcome D-01 exists to produce.
8. A Producer later books the studio's Live Room A. System reads `Live Room A requires Control 1`, holds both atomically as one compound (16.06.05), and prices them as one.
9. A second Producer requests **Control 1 alone** for a mix on the same afternoon. The derived graph says Control 1 is taken (Live A requires it) → `Blocked (cascade)`, reason named: *"Control 1 is in use with Live Room A."*
10. That evening, the same second Producer requests **the Mix Suite**, which requires nothing. It sells. **The studio bills two sessions in one afternoon** — the concrete revenue D-01 unlocks.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Place has exactly one room (most rehearsal rooms, most small venues) | Default room auto-created; the room layer is not rendered. Hidden **iff the place has exactly one room record in any state** (count = 1) | Place-level booking; room is invisible plumbing |
| N=1 → N=2 (the growth path this business actually takes) | Default room is **promoted**, never replaced — all bookings, photos, reviews, harvested reports and its calendar survive. One blocking prompt: name it | "You're adding a second space — what should we call the one you already have?" |
| N=2 → N=1 (Operator retires the second room) | The layer **stays visible permanently**. Once a second room record has ever existed, re-hiding would make the retired room's history and bookings unreachable | Room layer remains; retired room listed under "Retired" |
| Room is reconfigurable (seated 200 / standing 400) | Configuration is a property of capacity (16.02.01), not a separate room — same physical space, same calendar | One room, capacity varies by config |
| Room is **divisible** (airwall; two rehearsal rooms combine for a big band) | **Not** a configuration — two clients can hold the halves simultaneously, which reconfiguration can never do. Parent + two child rooms with `part-of`. Booking Main excludes both halves; booking North excludes Main but leaves South sellable | Three calendars, one physical volume, no contradiction possible |
| Two rooms share a PA — both bookable, but not both loud | `contends (facility: PA)`. Both stay sellable. The **second** booking to overlap is shown the Operator's rule and must acknowledge; both hirers are notified of the other's presence | "The Basement shares the PA with the Main Room, which has a show 8–11pm. Loud playback isn't available in that window." |
| Two rooms want the same mobile mic (one U47, three rooms) | Same mechanism — mobile assets are **place**-owned and contended, not room spec (DT-07). Allocation is per-booking | "The U47 is allocated to Live Room A on this date" |
| Mix booked in the control room while the live room sits empty | **Must not block.** `requires` is directional: Live A requires Control 1; Control 1 requires nothing. A symmetric pairwise flag would silently destroy the mix-suite revenue stream | Control 1 sells. Live A remains available |
| Two live rooms share one control room | Exclusion is **derived**, not declared: both `require` Control 1 ⇒ booking either excludes the other. The Operator never typed a rule and cannot type an inconsistent one | "Live Room B unavailable — Control 1 is in use with Live Room A" |
| Operator adds a `requires`/`excludes` edge while both rooms have live conflicting bookings | **Blocked.** Offered as an **effective-dated** change instead, from the first date with no conflict | "2 bookings would conflict (14 & 21 March). Apply this from 22 March?" |
| Two exclusive siblings confirmed at the same instant (race) | The graph is evaluated at hold **and re-evaluated at confirm**. First commit wins; the loser's hold is released with a named reason, not a generic failure | "Live Room B was confirmed first — Control 1 is no longer free. Your hold is released." |
| Operator deletes a room with future bookings | Blocked. Retire (no new bookings, history intact) is offered instead. Retire itself is blocked while ≥1 **confirmed** booking has a start time in the future | "3 bookings use this room (next: 14 March) — retire it instead?" |
| Room is subdivided or merged (studio knocks through) | Parent(s) → `Superseded` with lineage; children created `formed-from`. History stays on the superseded room and is **never** copied down. Blocked while future confirmed bookings exist unless the Operator explicitly reassigns each one | "This ends Live Room A as a space. Its 12 sessions and 4 reports stay on its record and will remain linked from both new rooms." |
| Room renamed with no physical change | Same room, old name kept as an alias. History follows. **The common case an Operator will mistake for a subdivision** | "Renaming — history and bookings stay with this room" |
| Room refit (re-treated, new desk, new PA) | Same room. Freshness resets on the affected fields only (16.05.05). Not lineage | "Spec updated — this is still the same room" |
| Booking reassigned across a subdivision boundary (hirer booked a 400-cap parent; only 180-cap children now exist) | A **material** change to a confirmed booking, therefore an Operator-initiated variation: the hirer must consent, and the deposit ladder inverts (16.06.03 / CX-04) | Hirer sees the substituted room, its capacity, and accept/refund |
| Room floods; PA dies; heating fails mid-winter | **`Out of Service`** — not Retired (that is terminal-ish and hides the room), not Blocked (that means a sibling took it), and not place closure (16.01.05 — the studio is trading). Requires a reason and an **expected-return date**. Live bookings on that room only are cancelled Operator-fault | "Live Room A is out of service until 3 August (flood). Your 21 July session has been cancelled — deposit refunded in full." |
| Out-of-service date passes with no update | Field freshness decays (16.05.05); the room is de-ranked in search rather than silently presented as available. Escalation to closure is **Q-04** | "Availability last confirmed 6 weeks ago" |
| Place marked permanently closed while rooms hold live bookings | **Place status wins unconditionally** (CX-04). Closure cascades to every room calendar and cancels live reservations Operator-fault. If the refund step fails, the cancellation still stands and the refund retries — a money failure must never leave a closed room bookable | Every reservation holder + follower notified; 19 notified for any on-sale show |
| Operator adds a room whose type is not in the record's type set (a tracking room in a venue) | Room stays `Draft`; a one-tap prompt extends the record's type set (16.01.01 D-01). Not auto-applied — the type set is the record owner's declaration | "Adding a tracking room makes this a studio too. Add `studio` to this place?" |
| Operator removes a type from the record's set while a room of that type exists | **Blocked, never cascaded** (CX-01) — cascading would orphan a live spec and a live calendar | "A studio room exists here. Retire it before removing the studio type." |
| Room created on an **unclaimed** record | Suggestible via 16.05.03 like any other field, and permanently `Draft` — no calendar, no rate, no posture. **Bookability requires a claim** (D-11) | "Do you run this place? Claim it to make this space bookable" |
| Malicious room-spam on an unclaimed competitor record (12 fake rooms) | Blast radius is bounded to spec pollution and search dilution because unclaimed rooms are never bookable. Rooms on unclaimed records enter the same review queue as any suggested edit (16.05.03) | Suggested rooms shown as pending, not as inventory |
| Room granted to a second org (a studio subleases the venue's upstairs room, cooperatively) | The **room** is the delegation unit (D-09): one record, one claim, one room granted to another org, **instantly and with no claim unit**. The grantee owns that room's spec, calendar, rate and enquiries; the record — identity, address, type set, status — stays with the claimant | Room shows "Operated by [org]" |
| The claimant will not grant it, or cannot be reached at all | The other org **claims the room** instead (D-15) — 16.05.02's ladder scoped to the room, running on L7 with the sublease as the document. Provisional until reviewed: describable, **not sellable**, no calendar, no rate card. The grant path stays instant; this one does not, and that asymmetry is the price of independence | "We'll need your sublease — reviewed within 5 working days." |
| **Claimant tries to revoke a grant** (with or without live bookings) | **Not an act — a dispute** (D-16). A counter-window opens with maximal notification; if the room operator answers, it escalates to 24; the **room stays live and bookable throughout**. If revocation completes, it is **effective-dated after the last confirmed booking** or requires an explicit Operator-fault cancellation of each — it must never orphan a booking whose counterparty has already paid a deposit | Claimant: "This is a dispute, not a switch — {org} has 14 days to respond." If it completes: "4 bookings run to 30 September. Revocation takes effect 1 October." |
| **A granted room is later claimed, or a room claim lands on an already-granted room** | Two authority provenances on one room. **Undefined** — precedence, refusal and survival-after-revocation are all unspecified (16.01.01 Q-10, Q-06 here) | `[PENDING — Q-06]` |
| Multi-hyphenate: a Producer who is Room B's delegate opens Room A of the same record | Full on Room B, Read-only + suggest on Room A. Permission is per-room, not per-record, the moment any delegation exists — the intersection this feature creates | Room A shows the suggest-an-edit affordance, not the editor |
| Multi-hyphenate: an Operator books their own room for their own band | It is a **block**, not a booking — no counterparty (16.06.01). Consequence: no reservation ⇒ no proof-of-presence ⇒ **no harvest trigger** (16.05.06). The rooms an Operator knows best generate the least fresh data | Block on the calendar; no post-session prompt |
| Two records merge (16.05.07) and both carry rooms | Rooms **never** auto-merge — two rooms at one address are the normal case, not duplicates (16.05.07 D-03/DT-03). Rooms are carried onto the survivor as distinct rooms; only the Operator may then declare two of them the same physical volume, which is a **merge** (lineage), not a dedup | "Both records list a 'Main Room'. Are these the same space?" — defaults to **no** |
| Harvested report names a room the registry does not know ("the cellar") | Resolved against room name + aliases. No match ⇒ the report is held against the **place** and queued for the Operator to attribute, not discarded and not guessed onto a room | "A band reported a load-in issue here — which space?" |
| Operator pushes a room `Live` with an empty spec | Blocked at the minimum bar. The meter names the missing fields rather than reporting a percentage — an Operator on a phone at a loading dock cannot act on "62% complete" | "Add capacity and a load-in note to make this bookable" |
| Room created but the spec sheet fails to attach (partial write) | Room persists in `Draft` with the spec absent and re-attachable. A room that exists without a spec is recoverable; a spec without a room is not | Draft room + "Finish setting this up" |
| Two Operator sessions edit the room graph concurrently (phone at the loading dock + laptop in the office) | Last write wins on independent rooms. On the **same** edge, the second write is rejected with the current value shown — a silently-merged dependency graph is a double-booking generator | "This changed while you were editing — here's what it says now" |
| Fan arrives at the right venue, wrong room (two shows, one building, one night) | The room name and its **own entrance/route** are on the ticket and the show page. The place-level address is insufficient by construction and this feature is the reason it is available | "Basement — entrance on Mill Lane, not the main door" |
| Show downgraded from Main Room to Basement after on-sale (routine live-music practice) | **Room reassignment must be first-class** — never delete+recreate, which would drop the hold rank, the deposit, and the ticket link. 16 releases the old slot, books the new, re-runs the graph and **emits an event**. Capacity drops 400→80, so 19's manifest is now oversold and 17's guarantee was negotiated against the wrong number. **Who owns that event is Q-03** | Operator sees the blast radius (tickets sold vs new capacity) before confirming |

### States

**Room lifecycle**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Room created; spec incomplete, **or** the place is unclaimed (D-11), **or** its type is not in the record's type set | Not bookable. A named list of what is missing — never a percentage |
| Live | Place claimed **and** spec meets the minimum bar **and** posture set (16.06.06) | Bookable/enquirable per posture; appears in search |
| Blocked (cascade) | A `requires`/`excludes`/`part-of` sibling is held or booked | Unavailable **with the reason** — "Control 1 is in use with Live Room A" (16.06.01 D-05) |
| Out of Service | Operator declares a fault; reason + expected-return date **required** | Room hidden from search; live bookings cancelled Operator-fault; reason and return date shown to anyone holding one |
| Retired | Operator retires it | No new bookings. History, reports and past bookings intact and reachable. **Reversible** |
| Superseded | Subdivision or merge executed | Terminal. Read-only. History stays here; `formed-from` links reach the successor rooms — history is traversable, never re-attributed (D-08) |

**View states — Operator room manager**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Room list or graph being fetched | Skeleton room cards; the dependency graph renders last (it is derived) |
| Empty | Claimed place, no rooms — only reachable on a claim of a seeded record | "Is more than one space bookable here?" — the single question of the happy path |
| Populated | ≥1 room | Room cards with state, calendar summary, spec meter; dependencies rendered as *"needs: Control 1"*, never as a graph diagram |
| Partial | Rooms load; the derived graph or a sibling's calendar does not | Rooms shown; **booking actions disabled**, not silently permitted. An unknown graph must fail closed — a room sold against an unverified graph is the double-booking this domain exists to prevent |
| Error | Room write fails | The room's prior value with an inline retry. A destructive act (retire, subdivide, revoke) that errors is **never** retried automatically |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rooms are independent bookable resources | The obvious model — each room gets a calendar, done | ❌ REJECTED: rooms interlock physically. A live room and its control room are one booking. Independent calendars produce a double-booking of a shared resource — which is precisely the Operator's stated switching trigger ("a costly double-booking"). Room exclusion/dependency rules are mandatory, not an enhancement. |
| DT-02 | The room layer will confuse single-room Operators | A rehearsal room owner does not think "I have one room" — they think "I have a room" | ✅ CONFIRMED: the abstraction must be invisible at N=1. Auto-create a default room and never surface the concept. The Operator persona works "on a phone at a loading dock" — hierarchy they didn't ask for is a defection trigger. |
| DT-03 | Reconfigurable capacity needs separate room records | Seated vs standing feel like different products (different capacity, different ticket map) | ❌ REJECTED: same physical space, same calendar, same load-in, same curfew. Only capacity and the ticket map change. Modelling it as two rooms would let both be booked at once — a physical impossibility the data model would happily allow. Configuration belongs to capacity (16.02.01). |
| DT-04 | Room specs are what 19 Ticketing needs for a seat map | Ticketing needs to know where people stand/sit | ⚠️ **RESOLVED (Step 5) — SPLIT.** The room carries the **configuration** (named layout + capacity per layout + per-section seating mode: `ga`/`seated`/`mixed`), which is exactly what 19.01.04's happy path step 1 consumes and what its DT-02 demands ("capacity is per-configuration, not per-room" — 16.02.01 D-01/D-03 already satisfies this; **19.01.04 Q-01 is answered: yes, per configuration**). The room does **not** carry **seat identities or map geometry**. Reason: the seat map fails every test that makes something belong to 16 — no Musician suggests an edit to seat J14, it does not decay, it is never harvested post-gig, and it has exactly one consumer. Owning it would import a geometry editor into a curation registry for 19's sole benefit. **Note the collision**: 19.01.04 currently asserts "seat maps are a room asset (domain 16)". Both domains are pointing at each other, exactly like the unowned cross-domain compound (16.06.05 DT-05). 16's side is now decided (D-05); the reconciliation is Q-02. |
| DT-05 | Pairwise mutual exclusion is the right way to declare how rooms interlock | The breadth pass's own happy path ("system asks whether A and B can be booked at the same time"); it is how a human would describe it | ❌ **REJECTED — the sharpest finding in this feature.** Two fatal defects. (1) **It cannot express asymmetry.** Live Room A needs Control 1 to track; Control 1 needs nothing to host a mix. A symmetric "A and B are mutually exclusive" flag blocks the mix — silently deleting the studio's second revenue stream, and contradicting this file's own Overview ("a studio cannot sell its mix suite while a band is tracking" is the failure we exist to prevent). (2) **It scales as N(N−1)/2 and admits contradiction.** A 4-room studio needs 6 hand-typed rules that can disagree with each other; a rehearsal complex with 8 rooms needs 28. The fix inverts the question: the Operator declares what each room **needs** (O(N), and a fact they know), and exclusion is **derived**. A derived graph cannot be internally inconsistent, and 16.06.05 DT-03 — "exclusion and compounding are one graph read in two directions" — becomes literally true rather than aspirational. |
| DT-06 | Room contention is binary — rooms either can or cannot be co-booked | The breadth pass's model; its unresolved shared-PA edge case (two rooms, one PA, both bookable but not both loud) | ❌ **REJECTED.** Binary forces every shared facility to become a hard block, which is a lie the Operator will route around.
