## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Place Record ↔ Room Entity

**Relationship**: The place record holds identity, location, claim state and type set. The room
holds everything transactable — spec, capacity, calendar, rate, photos, accessibility. A place
with `type=[venue, studio]` does not carry two spec sheets; its *rooms* do, individually. This is
what makes the multi-hyphenate building modellable: the venue's main room carries a venue spec,
the tracking room upstairs carries a studio spec, and both sit under one record with one org, one
claim and one enquiry inbox.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: creates the record once, then rooms. At N=1 room the layer is invisible (16.01.02 D-03).
- **Musician / Producer**: search and book rooms; the record is context (where is it, who runs it).
- **Fan**: sees the record (the venue) and only the room's *name* on a ticket.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The record owns identity/claim; the room owns spec/calendar. No field is writable from both. Type-set changes on the record can *orphan* a room's spec (removing `type=studio` while a studio room exists) — must be blocked, not cascaded.
2. **Trigger chain**: Record claim (16.05.02) grants write access to all rooms. Record closure (16.01.05) blocks all room calendars — see CX-04. Async is unacceptable for closure: a booking taken between closure and cascade is a real harm.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — write access is granted at the record level and inherited by rooms. There is currently no per-room delegation, which Q-01 (containment/sublease) will likely force.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Record-level events (claim, closure, at-risk) notify everyone with a live reservation on **any** room.
5. **State transition conflict**: Record closure racing a room booking is the live risk. Closure must win and roll back in-flight holds.

### CX-02: Licences & Statutory ↔ Place Record

**Relationship**: 16.01.06 is not just another field group — it introduces a **second provenance
class** onto the same record. "Curfew 23:00 because the premises licence says so" and "curfew
23:00 because the Operator prefers it" are the same value with different authority. The first is
non-negotiable and outranks Operator edits; the second is a claim like any other. Licensed capacity
behaves identically: it is a ceiling the Operator cannot raise by editing their own record.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: records the licence; discovers that some fields are no longer theirs to change.
- **Musician / Producer**: see *why* a constraint exists — "licence condition" is a different conversation from "house rule", and it stops the promoter negotiating for a midnight finish that is not available at any price.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Licence-derived fields are read-only downstream. The Operator can update the licence record; they cannot edit the derived value in the spec.
2. **Trigger chain**: Licence record change → derived spec fields (16.02.03) update → any conflicting live advance in 18 is invalidated. Failure to propagate leaves a spec claiming an illegal curfew.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and inverted — being the record owner grants *less* authority here than elsewhere in the domain.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Licence expiry notifies the Operator and, per 16.01.06, is visible to hirers with live bookings.
5. **State transition conflict**: A licence lapsing mid-booking-window is unresolved — see 16.01.06 Q-02.

### CX-03: Photo Checklist ↔ Accessibility Profile

**Relationship**: Accessibility is the field class where a text claim is least trustworthy and a
photograph is most decisive — "step-free entrance" is contradicted instantly by a picture of two
steps. Because 16.01.03 tags photos to the spec field they evidence, the accessibility route photo
carries the accessibility field's freshness and makes a contradiction machine-visible rather than
a matter of argument.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician (disabled)**: the primary beneficiary — the photo is the reason to believe the claim.
- **Operator**: cannot make an unevidenced accessibility claim look as strong as an evidenced one.
- **Producer**: reads it when booking a room for contributors they have not met.
- **Fan**: gets the audience-route photos on the public show page.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The photo does not write the field — it evidences it. A contradiction raises a flag (16.05.04); it does not auto-flip the value.
2. **Trigger chain**: Photo upload → field freshness reset (16.05.05). Photo contradicting text → conflict flag. Both async and both tolerant of failure.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and contested — a band-contributed photo the Operator wants removed is 16.01.03 Q-01, and it is the same tension as the accessibility conflict exception (16.01.04 DT-04).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A contradiction flag notifies the Operator; no wider fan-out proposed.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — evidence and claim are independent records.

### CX-04: Place Status ↔ Room Entity

**Relationship**: Status lives on the place; bookability lives on the room. A permanent closure
must reach every room calendar and every live reservation, and the direction is strictly one-way
(a room cannot reopen a closed place). The Operator's anti-persona behaviour — "forgets to release
a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold" — has a mirror image here: a place marked
temporarily closed for a refit that silently keeps taking bookings.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: one status change, N room calendars affected — they must see the blast radius before confirming.
- **Musician / Producer**: holders of live reservations get an Operator-initiated cancellation, which inverts the deposit ladder (16.06.03).
- **Fan**: sees a dead venue page instead of buying a ticket to a room that no longer exists.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Place status wins over room state unconditionally.
2. **Trigger chain**: Closure → block room calendars → cancel live reservations (Operator-fault path) → refund per 16.06.03. If the refund step fails, the cancellation must still stand and the refund retried — money failures must not leave a closed room bookable.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — only the claimed Operator can close. Community reports never cascade (16.01.05 D-02), precisely because this cascade is destructive.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Large — every live reservation holder, every follower, and 19 Ticketing for any on-sale show.
5. **State transition conflict**: Closure racing an in-flight booking is the concrete risk; closure must win and roll back.

### CX-05: Licences & Statutory ↔ Place Status

**Relationship**: A licence review is one fact wearing two hats — a statutory record in 16.01.06
and an at-risk reason in 16.01.05. Recorded at Medium confidence because the two features were
derived independently and the overlap may indicate the licence record should *emit* at-risk
signals rather than duplicate them.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: may not want a licence review public — it is factual, damaging, and a matter of record.
- **Musician / Fan**: the at-risk signal is the point (16.01.05 D-04).

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. The Medium
confidence is exactly why: whether the licence record is the *source* of the at-risk signal or
merely correlated with it is unresolved, and 16.01.05 Q-02 (is at-risk moderated?) sharpens here —
publishing a licence review is publishing a true fact with reputational consequences.

### CX-06: Place Record ↔ Trades Directory

**Relationship**: A trade is `type=trade` on the same record — same identity, address, claim,
provenance and decay machinery, with a trade profile where the technical spec would be. It
inherits everything from 16.01.01 and touches almost nothing else in the domain.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: read + suggest; they are the only ones who know these places exist.
- **Operator**: the trade *is* an Operator, in the domain's weakest form — enquiry-only, no calendar.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None new — the record model is shared, the profile is exclusive to the subtype.
2. **Trigger chain**: None. Trades have no booking, therefore no post-transaction harvest (16.01.07 DT-02) — the absent trigger chain is the finding.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical to any claimed record.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.
