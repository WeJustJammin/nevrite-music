# Feature: Place Status & At-Risk Signalling

> **Number**: 16.01.05
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The lifecycle state of a place — open, temporarily closed, permanently closed, relocated — plus
the **at-risk signal**: the community-visible fact that a room is under threat from a
redevelopment, a licence review, a noise complaint, or a rent increase. Grassroots venues and
studios close constantly, and the industry's response is always reactive, because nobody knows
until the closure post appears.

Status is also a data-integrity problem: a registry seeded from public sources (16.05.01) will
contain places that closed years ago, and nothing corrodes trust in a directory faster than
sending a band to a room that no longer exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Sets status on their claimed place. Can declare at-risk themselves (a licence review, a redevelopment notice) — turning the community into a constituency. Cannot delete the record; closure is the terminal state and is reversible. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Sees status before booking. Can report "this place has closed" or "this place is at risk" — usually the first to know, because they were there. Follows an at-risk place to get updates. |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | Same as Musician; for studios, the Producer is typically the earliest signal (they hear a lease is up before the public does). |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees open/closed on the venue page so they don't turn up to a dead room. Sees the at-risk signal and can follow/act on it — the Fan is the *constituency* an at-risk campaign needs. |

## Behavior

**Status** is a small state machine on the place record: `open` · `temporarily closed` ·
`permanently closed` · `relocated` (with a link to the successor record, per 16.01.01 D-04).
Status gates bookability at the place level and cascades to every room.

**At-risk** is orthogonal to status — an open, trading, fully bookable venue can be at risk. It
carries a reason (redevelopment / licence review / noise complaint / rent / unknown), a source,
and a date. It is a public signal, and a follow surface.

The write model is deliberately asymmetric: a **claimed Operator** can set status directly, but
community reports of closure are a **signal**, not a state change — because "I turned up and it
was dark" is not the same as "it has closed", and a false closure flag on a trading venue is a
direct commercial harm.

### Happy Path

1. Musician plays a venue; two months later reads it is being redeveloped
2. Musician opens the venue record and reports at-risk with a reason and a link
3. System flags the record; the Operator (if claimed) is notified and can confirm, correct, or dispute
4. Once confirmed, the at-risk state is public; Fans and Musicians who follow the venue are notified
5. The room stays fully bookable throughout — at-risk is a signal, not a closure

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Seeded record for a venue that closed in 2019 | Never verified, decayed to the floor (16.05.05); presented as unverified/possibly-closed rather than open | "This record has never been verified — it may no longer exist" |
| Malicious closure report against a trading competitor | Community reports do not change state — they raise a signal requiring Operator response or corroboration | Operator notified; record stays open |
| Operator never responds to a corroborated closure report | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — needs a timeout after which an unclaimed/unresponsive record flips to "possibly closed". The threshold is unresolved. | — |
| Venue closes with bookings on the calendar | Status change must cascade to reservations (16.06.03) — cancellation is Operator-initiated, and the deposit ladder is inverted (Operator cancelled, not hirer) | Hirers notified; refunds per policy |
| Place reopens under new ownership | Status → open; claim transfers (16.05.02). Same building, same record, new org | Record continuity, ownership history visible |
| At-risk becomes a campaign | Out of scope here — this feature emits the signal; organising is 03/21 territory | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Open | Default / Operator confirms | Bookable; normal record |
| At-risk | Reported + confirmed/corroborated | Bookable, with an at-risk banner, reason and follow CTA |
| Temporarily closed | Operator sets (refit, licence suspension, seasonal) | Not bookable; expected-return date if given |
| Permanently closed | Operator sets, or corroborated + timeout | Read-only historical record; still linked from past gigs |
| Possibly closed | Corroborated report, no Operator response | Unverified warning; suppressed in search |
| Relocated | Operator sets, links successor | Pointer to the successor record (16.01.01 D-04) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Closure is the interesting state | The obvious lifecycle model — open/closed | ❌ REJECTED as sufficient: closure is the *outcome*, and by the time it is recordable, the room is gone and the platform has only archived a loss. The at-risk signal is where the value is, and it is orthogonal to status — a fully trading venue can be at risk. Two independent axes, not one. |
| DT-02 | Community members can mark a place closed | Wiki-style — trust the crowd | ❌ REJECTED: a false closure flag on a trading venue is direct commercial harm and an obvious griefing vector against a competitor. Community reports raise a **signal**; only the Operator (or corroboration + timeout) changes state. This is the one place in the domain where the write model is deliberately more conservative than the rest of 16.05. |
| DT-03 | A seeded record for a long-dead venue is a bug in seeding | It shouldn't be there | ❌ REJECTED: it is unavoidable — public sources are full of dead venues, and there is no source of truth for closure. The fix is not cleaner seeding but **honest presentation**: an unverified record must not look like a live one. This makes 16.05.05 (decay) a correctness requirement, not a nicety. |
| DT-04 | Fans are the wrong audience for an at-risk signal | This is professional data on a professional surface | ❌ REJECTED: `personas.md` D-11 makes the Fan a first-class user, and the Fan is exactly the constituency a threatened venue needs — attendance and objections come from the crowd, not the industry. The at-risk signal is one of the few places in this domain where the Fan is the *primary* consumer, not a bystander. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | At-risk is orthogonal to status, not a status value | A trading, bookable venue can be at risk; closure is the outcome, not the signal (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Community reports raise a signal; only the Operator (or corroboration + timeout) changes state | A false closure flag is a commercial attack and an obvious griefing vector (DT-02) |
