# Feature: Licences, Insurance & Statutory Records

> **Number**: 16.01.06
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's candidate list

## Overview

The documents that make a place legal to operate and a booking legal to happen: premises licence
and its conditions, licensed capacity, public liability insurance, PAT/electrical testing, fire
certification — plus the mirror-image fact of **what the place requires from the hirer** (most
venues require the band or promoter to hold their own PLI; many rehearsal rooms require PAT-tested
gear).

This is absent from the sweep entirely and it is a hard gate on real bookings. It also carries the
domain's most dangerous field: **licensed capacity**, which is a legal ceiling that 19 Ticketing
must not exceed and that Operators routinely overstate.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Records licence type, licensed capacity, conditions (curfew, noise limits attached to the licence), PLI cover level and expiry, PAT date. Declares what the hirer must produce. Sees expiry warnings before they bite. |
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what they must produce to book ("this venue requires £2m PLI"), and whether the place's own cover is current. A band without PLI discovering the requirement at the door is a cancelled gig — this is the field that prevents it. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, for studios. Also reads PAT status because they are the person plugging in a client's amp — and liability lands on them if it goes wrong. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A Fan has no action here and licensed capacity is not a fact to expose to ticket buyers — it is an internal ceiling consumed by 19. |

## Behavior

Two structured sets on the place (some fields per-room):

**The place's own records**: licence type and reference, licensed capacity per configuration
(feeds 16.02.01), licence conditions that constrain operation (curfew, decibel limits, permitted
hours — these are the *source* of several 16.02.03 facts), PLI insurer/cover/expiry, PAT date,
fire safety.

**Requirements imposed on the hirer**: PLI minimum, PAT for hirer-supplied gear, risk assessment,
named responsible person, age/safeguarding requirements for under-18 attendance.

Records are **declarations with expiry dates**, not verified certificates. The platform is not a
certification authority and must not present a declared PLI as a verified one. Expiry is what
makes this feature more than a text field: an expired declaration is worse than no declaration,
because it is a false assurance.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims a venue; onboarding asks for licensed capacity and licence conditions
2. Operator enters licensed capacity 350, curfew 23:00 (a licence condition), decibel limit 98dB
3. System derives the curfew and limiter facts into the venue spec (16.02.03) with provenance "licence condition" — a strictly stronger provenance than "Operator says"
4. Operator declares hirer requirements: £2m PLI
5. Every enquiry now surfaces the PLI requirement to the hirer before they commit (16.06.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| PLI expires with future bookings on the calendar | Warning to Operator ahead of the expiry date; on the date the declaration flips to expired (never silently stale), and each affected hirer with a live booking is notified the place's cover lapsed | Operator: "Your PLI declaration expired — 4 upcoming bookings affected." Hirer: "This venue's public liability cover is no longer current." |
| Advertised capacity (400) exceeds licensed capacity (350) | Direct contradiction; licensed capacity wins as the statutory ceiling and the higher claim is flagged, not silently overwritten (16.02.01) | Contested capacity marker on the venue record |
| Hirer cannot produce the required PLI at enquiry | The requirement surfaces at enquiry (16.06.06), never at the door; the platform does not silently allow the booking. Whether this **hard-blocks**, **warns-and-proceeds**, or triggers an **insurance upsell** is a product decision (Q-02) — the Level-1 guarantee is only that the gap is made visible before commitment | "This venue requires £2m public liability cover. Confirm you hold it to continue." |
| Musician/Producer/Fan attempts to edit a statutory record | Rejected — these fields are Operator-write, everyone-else read-only (Fan: no visibility at all). A statutory declaration is an assertion by the party legally responsible; a hirer editing it would break the provenance the record exists to carry | No edit affordance is shown; the record renders read-only |
| Place has a live booking enquiry but no statutory records declared at all | Enquiry is not blocked by absence, but the place shows "Not stated" for cover/capacity and the Operator is nudged to declare before confirming; the hirer sees the absence rather than a false assurance | Hirer: "This venue has not stated its licensing or insurance." Operator: nudge to complete records |
| Operator overstates cover or declares a lapsed policy as current | Declared-not-verified label always present (D-01); an expired-then-marked-current entry is caught by the expiry engine and shown as expired regardless of the Operator's claim. Deliberate misrepresentation is a Trust & Safety signal, not a validation error | "Declared by operator — not verified by WeJammin" persists on every value |
| Jurisdiction differs (PRS/PPL/PAT are UK-shaped; US/EU are separate regimes) | `[PENDING — /ideate-validate]` — the record's field set is jurisdiction-shaped and the sweep assumed one regime; the launch-market decision (Q-01) determines which field vocabulary ships first | — |
| Operator uploads an actual certificate document | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` — document storage, retention, and whether the platform ever inspects a certificate are unresolved (Q-03). Declaration-with-expiry, not document custody, is the current assumption | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Undeclared | Nothing recorded | "Not stated"; no assurance shown; Operator nudged |
| Declared | Operator recorded it | Values + expiry + "declared by operator, not verified by WeJammin" |
| Expiring | Within warning window | Operator warning; hirers see the expiry date |
| Expired | Past expiry | Declaration invalidated — shown as expired, never as current |
| Contested | Contradicts another field (e.g. capacity) | Flagged (16.05.04) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Licences and insurance are boring admin, not a product feature | Nothing in the 22 candidates mentions them | ❌ REJECTED — this is a real gap in the sweep. PLI is a hard gate on booking a UK venue: the band turns up, cannot produce cover, the show does not happen. A booking platform that never mentions it has not consolidated the workflow, it has hidden a landmine. |
| DT-02 | Licensed capacity belongs in the venue spec (16.02.01) with the other capacity numbers | It is a capacity number | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: it is *consumed* there, but it **originates here** as a statutory fact with a different provenance class. A licence condition is not "the Operator says" — it is externally imposed and outranks the Operator's own claim. Origin here, consumption in 16.02.01, and the provenance distinction is what lets a contradiction be detected at all. |
| DT-03 | The platform should verify certificates | Verified badges build trust | ❌ REJECTED: becoming a certification authority creates liability the product cannot carry and cannot staff. Declarations with expiry dates, explicitly labelled unverified. The honest label is the feature. |
| DT-04 | Curfew and decibel limits are Operator preferences | They read like operating choices in the spec sheet (16.02.03) | ❌ REJECTED: they are usually **licence conditions** — legally binding, not negotiable, and not the Operator's to waive. That changes the field's provenance and makes it non-overridable by a promoter's "can we run to midnight just this once". Deriving them from the licence record is what makes the spec truthful. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Licences/insurance are declarations with expiry, never platform-verified certificates | Certification is liability the product cannot carry; the honest label is the feature (DT-03) |
| D-02 | Licence conditions (curfew, dB limit) originate here and are derived into the spec | They are legally binding facts with a stronger provenance class than Operator claims (DT-04) |
| D-03 | Licensed capacity originates here; 16.02.01 consumes it as a ceiling | Statutory provenance is what makes an overstated capacity detectable (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Hirer requirements are a first-class part of the record | The band that cannot produce PLI at the door is a cancelled show this field prevents (DT-01) |
