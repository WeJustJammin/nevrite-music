# Feature: Licences, Insurance & Statutory Records

> **Number**: 16.01.06
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's candidate list

## Overview

The records that make a place legal to operate and a booking legal to happen — and the mirror-image
fact of **what the place requires from the hirer**.

The *slots* are universal; the *instruments that fill them* are not. A UK venue's occupancy ceiling
comes from a premises licence; a US venue's does not. So this feature is modelled as a fixed set of
**statutory slots** resolved against a **regime profile** (D-05), and the instrument names live in
the profile, never in the slot. At launch **only the US profile is authored** — every other territory
is an *unauthored* profile whose statutory fields resolve to an explicit **unknown**, never to
another regime's default (D-05).

This is absent from the sweep entirely and it is a hard gate on real bookings. It also carries the
domain's most dangerous field: the **occupancy ceiling**, which is a legal ceiling that 19 Ticketing
must not exceed and that Operators routinely overstate.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Fills the **slots their profile declares** (D-05, D-06): occupancy ceiling and its conditions, liability cover level and expiry, electrical/fire-safety record, performing-rights licence status — each with its issuer and its expiry. Declares what the hirer must produce. Sees expiry warnings before they bite. Never sees a slot their profile does not declare. |
| Musician | Read-only | Sees what they must produce to book ("this venue requires £2m/$X liability cover"), and whether the place's own cover is current. A band without cover discovering the requirement at the door is a cancelled gig — this is the field that prevents it. **This dependency is on the slot, not the instrument**, and survives the profile change unchanged. |
| Producer | Read-only | Same, for studios. Also reads the **electrical/fire-safety record** because they are the person plugging in a client's amp — and liability lands on them if it goes wrong. **This dependency is on the slot, not the instrument**, and is why the slot survives D-06 rather than being dropped. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A Fan has no action here and the occupancy ceiling is not a fact to expose to ticket buyers — it is an internal ceiling consumed by 19. |

## Behavior

### The regime profile and its capabilities (D-05)

A place record resolves **one regime profile** from its address. The profile is deliberately thin —
it declares **capabilities**, not rules — and the vocabulary of what a profile may declare is closed
and short, because the moment it grows past this it becomes a rules engine:

| A profile may declare | It may not declare |
|---|---|
| **Which of the five statutory slots exist** in this regime (D-06) | Any computation over a slot's value |
| For each existing slot, **the instrument that fills it** and **who issues it** | Any threshold, rate or fee |
| Whether a **per-premises public register class** exists for the occupancy/premises slot — *the class only*; actual availability resolves **per licensing authority** for the record's address (16.05.01 D-16) | Whether any particular authority publishes one |
| Whether a **temporary-permission instrument** exists that can vary a statutory condition for a single date | Any allowance, quota or lead time |

**Exactly one profile is authored at launch: the US** (ideation D-32). Every other territory
resolves to an **unauthored** profile, whose statutory slots render an explicit `unknown` — never a
silent inheritance of another regime's vocabulary. The UK vocabulary this feature was originally
written in (premises licence, PAT, PLI, TEN) is **retired into an unauthored profile**: the worked
example is lost, and if the second market is the UK its vocabulary must be re-derived rather than
un-commented.

The axis is kept even though only one profile fills it, because D-32 does not merely say US-first —
it says *keep the data model jurisdiction-parameterized so later international expansion is additive,
not a rewrite*. Deleting the axis, or abstracting it to labels only, contradicts the second half.

### The five slots (D-06)

Universal, regardless of profile. Each carries an **issuer** and an **expiry**:

| Slot | What it holds | Consumed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Occupancy ceiling** | The maximum lawful occupancy of the premises, per configuration | 16.02.01 (as a date-scoped hard ceiling), 19 |
| **Liability cover** | The place's own public-liability-equivalent cover: insurer, level, expiry | Hirers, 24 |
| **Electrical / fire-safety record** | The place's electrical-test and fire-safety record | Producers (they plug in the amp), 16.06.06 |
| **Performing-rights licence status** | Whether the place holds a blanket performing-rights licence | 16.02.06 |
| **Hirer requirements** | What the place requires *from* the hirer: minimum liability cover, tested gear, risk assessment, named responsible person, age/safeguarding | 16.06.06 (surfaced at enquiry) |

**The US instrument names for these slots are deferred** — see D-06 and the live `[PENDING]` below.
The slot set, the issuer requirement, the expiry semantics and the declared-not-verified rule are
product decisions and are settled here; *what a US occupancy ceiling is called and who issues it in
each state* is a per-state legal fact no ideation source contains, and asserting one here would be an
unsourced claim wearing a ratified decision's clothes.

> `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` **The US profile's instrument names and issuers, per slot.**
> This is a **live** deferral, not an expired one: it stays open through the rest of ideation and is
> the empirical work D-32 hands to `/create-prd-security`. Until it lands, the slots exist, the
> issuer and expiry fields exist, and the instrument name renders as not yet determined.

Records are **declarations with expiry dates**, not verified certificates (D-01). The platform is not
a certification authority and must not present a declared cover level as a verified one. Expiry is
what makes this feature more than a text field: an expired declaration is worse than no declaration,
because it is a false assurance.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims a venue. The record resolves the **US profile** from its address; onboarding asks only for the slots that profile declares
2. Operator enters the **occupancy ceiling** (350) into that slot, with its **issuer** and its **expiry**. *What the instrument is called and who issues it renders as not yet determined* until Q-04 lands (D-06's live `[PENDING]`) — the slot's behaviour is fully specified regardless
3. **Nothing is derived into the venue spec (16.02.03)**: the US launch profile declares **no operating-hours or decibel-limit instrument** (D-05; 16.02.03 D-01, D-13), so there is no statutory condition to derive. Any curfew or dB limit the Operator publishes there is their own claim, **labelled as a claim**, and is never silently promoted to statutory. In a profile that *does* declare such an instrument, D-02's derivation fires and carries **statutory** provenance — strictly stronger than "Operator says"
4. Operator declares hirer requirements: minimum liability cover
5. Every enquiry now surfaces the hirer requirement before they commit (16.06.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Liability cover expires with future bookings on the calendar | Warning to Operator ahead of the expiry date; on the date the declaration flips to expired (never silently stale), and each affected hirer with a live booking is notified the place's cover lapsed | Operator: "Your liability-cover declaration expired — 4 upcoming bookings affected." Hirer: "This venue's liability cover is no longer current." |
| Advertised capacity (400) exceeds the occupancy ceiling (350) | Direct contradiction; the ceiling wins as the statutory value and the higher claim is flagged, not silently overwritten (16.02.01) | Contested capacity marker on the venue record |
| **The record's profile declares no occupancy-ceiling slot at all** | There is nothing to contradict. No ceiling exists, no contradiction detection is possible, and every configuration is an unverified Operator claim — the branch 16.02.01 already authors as its `Claimed` state. The slot renders **unknown**, never zero and never "unlimited" | "Capacity not verified against a statutory record" |
| **The record is in an unauthored profile** (any territory but the US at launch) | Every statutory slot resolves to an explicit **unknown**. The record is still fully usable — searchable, claimable, bookable — it simply has no statutory layer, and says so. **It never inherits another regime's vocabulary** | "We don't yet model statutory records in this territory" |
| Hirer cannot produce the required liability cover at enquiry | The requirement surfaces at enquiry (16.06.06), never at the door; the platform does not silently allow the booking. Whether this **hard-blocks**, **warns-and-proceeds**, or triggers an **insurance upsell** is a product decision (Q-02) — the Level-1 guarantee is only that the gap is made visible before commitment | "This venue requires liability cover of £X/$X. Confirm you hold it to continue." |
| Musician/Producer/Fan attempts to edit a statutory record | Rejected — these fields are Operator-write, everyone-else read-only (Fan: no visibility at all). A statutory declaration is an assertion by the party legally responsible; a hirer editing it would break the provenance the record exists to carry | No edit affordance is shown; the record renders read-only |
| Place has a live booking enquiry but no statutory records declared at all | Enquiry is not blocked by absence, but the place shows "Not stated" for cover/capacity and the Operator is nudged to declare before confirming; the hirer sees the absence rather than a false assurance | Hirer: "This venue has not stated its licensing or insurance." Operator: nudge to complete records |
| Operator overstates cover or declares a lapsed policy as current | Declared-not-verified label always present (D-01); an expired-then-marked-current entry is caught by the expiry engine and shown as expired regardless of the Operator's claim. Deliberate misrepresentation is a Trust & Safety signal, not a validation error | "Declared by operator — not verified by WeJammin" persists on every value |
| Jurisdiction differs from the launch market | **Resolved (D-05).** The record resolves a regime profile from its address; the profile declares which slots exist and what instrument fills each. Only the US profile is authored, so every other territory is an unauthored profile rendering explicit unknowns. The sweep's UK vocabulary (premises licence, PAT, PLI, TEN) is retired into an unauthored profile, **not deleted and not defaulted to** | "We don't yet model statutory records in this territory" |
| **A profile-less field is read by a downstream consumer** (16.02.01's ceiling, 16.05.07 D-08's decisive key, 16.02.03's derived curfew, 16.05.02's L2 rung) | Every one of those rules is **capability-gated**: it has an input where the profile declares the slot and **no input** where it does not. None of them falls back to a default, and none of them treats absence as zero | Each consumer's own authored degraded branch |
| Operator uploads an actual certificate document | `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` — document storage, retention, and whether the platform ever inspects a certificate are unresolved (Q-03). Declaration-with-expiry, not document custody, is the current assumption | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not modelled | The record's profile does not declare this slot, or the profile is unauthored (D-05) | Explicit **unknown** — never "not stated" (which implies someone could have stated it), never a blank, and never another regime's value |
| Undeclared | Slot exists in the profile; nothing recorded | "Not stated"; no assurance shown; Operator nudged |
| Declared | Operator recorded it | Values + expiry + "declared by operator, not verified by WeJammin" |
| Expiring | Within warning window | Operator warning; hirers see the expiry date |
| Expired | Past expiry | Declaration invalidated — shown as expired, never as current |
| Contested | Contradicts another field (e.g. capacity) | Flagged (16.05.04) |

| DT-01 | Licences and insurance are boring admin, not a product feature | Nothing in the 22 candidates mentions them | ❌ REJECTED — this is a real gap in the sweep. **Liability cover** is a hard gate on booking a venue: the band turns up, cannot produce cover, the show does not happen. A booking platform that never mentions it has not consolidated the workflow, it has hidden a landmine. **⚠️ Refined (owner ratification, DQ-14.1/14.2 → D-05/D-06):** this annotation originally named UK PLI as *the* instrument. The **slot** (liability cover, with issuer and expiry) is universal and is what carries the argument; the instrument that fills it is profile-declared, and the US names are deferred (Q-04). |
| DT-02 | Licensed capacity belongs in the venue spec (16.02.01) with the other capacity numbers | It is a capacity number | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: it is *consumed* there, but it **originates here** as a statutory fact with a different provenance class. | DT-03 | The platform should verify certificates | Verified badges build trust | ❌ REJECTED: becoming a certification authority creates liability the product cannot carry and cannot staff. Declarations with expiry dates, explicitly labelled unverified. The honest label is the feature. |

Whether such an instrument exists at all is **profile-declared**, and the US launch profile declares none for the operating conditions — so in it these fields are Operator claims labelled as claims (16.02.03 D-01), which is the branch 16.02.03 had already authored. The rejection stands wherever the instrument exists and has **no input** where it does not. |

Nothing computational | Owner decision (DQ-14.1).
