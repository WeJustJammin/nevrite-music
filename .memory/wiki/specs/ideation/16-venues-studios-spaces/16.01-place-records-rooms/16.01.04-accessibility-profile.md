# Feature: Accessibility Profile (Audience & Performer)

> **Number**: 16.01.04
> **Parent**: [Place Records & Rooms](./16.01-place-records-rooms-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Structured accessibility facts for a room, covering **both sides of the barrier**: the audience
route (entrance, viewing platform, accessible toilet, companion policy) and the **performer and
crew route** (load-in, stage access, backstage, control room, toilets you can reach in a
wheelchair while carrying a bass).

The audience half is partially served by existing tools and by statutory pressure. The performer
half is effectively unbuilt: a disabled musician has no way to know whether they can get onto the
stage of a room that will happily sell them a booking, and finds out at the load-in door.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Declares the audience route and the performer route as separate structured profiles. Prompted for the honest answer, not the flattering one ("step-free with a 2-step lip" is a real and useful answer). Sees enquiries they lose to unanswered accessibility fields. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Filters rooms by performer accessibility before enquiring — the whole point. Post-booking, confirms or contradicts the profile (16.05.06). A disabled Musician is the highest-authority editor of this field, and their correction must outrank the Operator's claim. |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | Reads it when booking a room on behalf of contributors — the Producer books the room for other people, so they need the profile for a session member they may not have met. |
| Fan | Read-only | The audience half, on the public show page: step-free entry, accessible toilet, viewing platform, companion ticket policy, and how to arrange it. The performer half is not shown — irrelevant and confusing. |

## Behavior

Two structured profiles per room, deliberately not merged:

**Audience route**: step-free entry, ramp/lift, viewing platform (and its capacity), accessible
toilet, companion ticket policy, assistance dog policy, quiet space, strobe/haze warnings, seating
availability, how to request assistance and who to contact.

**Performer/crew route**: step-free load-in, door width and any lip/threshold, distance from
unloading point, lift access to stage, stage access (steps/ramp/lift), accessible backstage and
toilet, accessible control room (for studios), height-adjustable/reachable desk positions.

Both are **claims, not certifications** — subject to decay (16.05.05), community correction
(16.05.03), and post-visit confirmation (16.05.06). The design must make "partially accessible
with a specific caveat" as easy to state as a yes/no, because binary answers are what drive
Operators to lie and disabled users to distrust the data.

### Happy Path

1. Musician filters rooms: performer accessibility = step-free stage access
2. Results show matching rooms, plus near-matches with the specific caveat visible ("ramp to stage, 1 step at load-in")
3. Musician opens a room and sees the accessibility photos (16.01.03) evidencing the claim
4. Musician enquires with confidence; the Operator receives the accessibility requirement in the enquiry
5. After the gig, Musician is asked whether the profile was accurate (16.05.06); a contradiction flags the field

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Field unanswered | Never rendered as "no" — absence is absence | "Not stated — ask the venue" + a prompt to the Operator that they are being filtered out |
| Operator claims step-free; band arrives to 3 steps | Post-visit contradiction (16.05.06) flags the field; enters conflict resolution (16.05.04) | Field shows contested; Operator notified |
| Access is conditional ("lift works, but not after the bar staff leave at 23:00") | Structured caveat field required — conditionality is the norm, not the exception | Caveat shown inline, not hidden in a note |
| **Empty** — performer filter matches zero rooms | Never a blank page; the search widens to near-matches with their caveats surfaced, so the disabled Musician chooses rather than being shown nothing | "No fully step-free stages in this area. 4 near-matches — see caveats" + list |
| **Permission** — Fan opens or deep-links the performer/crew route | The performer half is never rendered on any Fan surface, even by direct URL — only the audience route is exposed to Fan (Role Lens) | Audience accessibility panel only; performer fields absent, not "hidden" |
| **Concurrency** — Operator edits the profile while a disabled Musician's correction is in flight | The Musician's post-visit correction wins per DT-04; the Operator's simultaneous edit does not silently overwrite it — the field goes contested and enters 16.05.04 | Operator sees "a visitor has contested this field" rather than a saved confirmation |
| **Abuse** — Operator re-saves/re-affirms the profile repeatedly to reset the decay clock (16.05.05) and bury a contested marker | Re-affirmation without new evidence does not clear a contested flag or reset decay; only a post-visit confirmation (16.05.06) from someone who was there does | Contested marker persists; "last verified" reflects the last real visit, not the last edit |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unstated | No profile provided | "Not stated" per field + Operator nudge; room is excluded from accessibility-filtered search |
| Stated | Operator declared the profile | Structured profile with caveats and last-verified date |
| Evidenced | Profile + tagged photos of the route | Profile with photo evidence inline |
| Contested | A visitor contradicted a claim | Last-agreed value + contested marker (16.05.04) |
| Stale | Decayed past threshold | Profile with "last verified" age; drops out of confident filtering |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Accessibility is an audience concern | This is how every venue directory and ticketing platform models it — the disabled person is assumed to be in the crowd | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the domain's clearest whitespace. Disabled musicians and crew exist and are structurally invisible: no tool describes stage access, load-in thresholds, or backstage. `personas.md` says the Musician is a working professional; the platform's supply side cannot assume performers are non-disabled. Two profiles, both required. |
| DT-02 | A yes/no accessibility flag is sufficient and simpler | Binary filters are easy to search and easy to answer | ❌ REJECTED: real rooms are conditionally accessible ("ramp to the stage, one step at the load-in, lift stops at 23:00"). A binary forces the Operator to choose between lying and being filtered out — both bad. Structured caveats are mandatory; conditionality is the norm. |
| DT-03 | Unanswered should default to "not accessible" (safe default) | Fails safe — nobody is misled | ❌ REJECTED: it is not safe, it is wrong. It silently deletes accessible rooms from search and gives the Operator no signal to fix it. Absence must render as absence, and must be visible to the Operator as lost enquiries — the incentive is what fills the field. |
| DT-04 | A disabled Musician's post-visit contradiction should outrank the Operator's claim | Generic conflict policy (16.05.04) gives the Operator the last word on their own record | ✅ CONFIRMED as a needed **exception**: the Operator has both an incentive and a blind spot here (they can walk). This is the strongest case in the domain for a field class where the owner does not win by default. Flagged to 16.05.04 as a policy exception, not a general rule. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Two separate profiles: audience route and performer/crew route | The performer half is unbuilt industry-wide and is the whitespace (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Structured caveats, never a binary flag | Real rooms are conditionally accessible; a binary forces a lie (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Unanswered renders as "not stated", never as "no" | A false negative deletes accessible rooms and gives the Operator no signal (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Proposed exception: a visitor's accessibility contradiction outranks the owner's claim | The Operator has both incentive and blind spot; escalated to 16.05.04 (DT-04) |
