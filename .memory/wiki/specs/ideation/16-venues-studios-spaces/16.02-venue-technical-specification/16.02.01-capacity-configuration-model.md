# Feature: Capacity & Configuration Model

> **Number**: 16.02.01
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How many people fit in the room, in which layout, on which date, and under whose authority.
"Capacity" is a single number everywhere in the industry and it is three different numbers in
reality: the **licensed** capacity (a legal ceiling), the **configuration** capacity (350 standing,
180 seated, 120 cabaret — same room), and the **advertised** capacity (what the venue's website
says, which is frequently the most flattering of the three).

Worse, none of those three numbers states **what it counts**. A licensed figure counts every body
in the building — staff, crew, the band, the sound engineer. The figure a band cares about counts
paying audience. They differ by 10–15% and no venue, directory or spec sheet in the industry says
which one it is quoting. Every capacity number in live music is therefore two numbers wearing one
label.

This field is load-bearing far outside this domain: 19 Ticketing cannot allocate above the licensed
ceiling, 17 Live Booking's deal maths runs off the config capacity, 16.07's conformance check reads
it, and a band choosing between two rooms is choosing on this number.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Declares the named configurations their room supports, each with a capacity, a **counting basis**, a **stated footprint assumption**, and optional **derate rules** ("full-width backline: −40"). Declares the **house party size** (staff + crew + performers typically inside) so an audience figure can be reconciled against a total-occupancy ceiling. **Cannot write the licensed ceiling** — it is derived from 16.01.06 and is the one field in this sub-domain where the record owner is not the authority. Sees every contradiction the system detects on their own room, before anyone else does. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Reads capacity **by configuration** when choosing a room — this and the fee are the two numbers a band decides on (CX-06). Sees the counting basis explicitly, so "350" is never ambiguous. Sees the ceiling that applies **on their show date**, not the ceiling today. Can suggest an edit to a configuration capacity, and can file a post-gig **implausibility flag** (16.05.06) — never a replacement number (D-09). |
| Producer | Read-only | Rarely the primary reader — capacity is a venue concern and the Producer's interest in this domain is studio rooms (16.03). Reads it when producing a live recording: the configuration determines where the stage sits (CX-01) and therefore where a recording rig, its cable runs and a split can physically live, and the **derate rules** tell them what their rig costs the room in places — which is what determines whether the Operator will accept it at all. |
| Fan | None | Never sees a capacity number, on any basis, at any time. Experiences the number only as remaining ticket availability (19) and as the room name on a ticket (16.01.02). Licensed capacity is a statutory internal ceiling; exposing it to ticket buyers has no upside for the Fan and invites scrutiny of the Operator's compliance that the platform did not earn the right to invite. Aligned with the 16.02 Role Matrix (`❌ None — sees ticket availability only`). |

## Behavior

Capacity is not one field. It is **three layers with different owners, different provenance classes
and different lifetimes**, plus a fourth number that is not a fact at all.

### Layer 1 — The ceiling (statutory, not writable here)

Licensed capacity originates in **16.01.06** as a licence condition (16.01.06 D-03). It is:

- **Total-occupancy basis, always** — it counts every body inside, including staff, crew and the band.
- **Date-scoped, not constant** (DT-06). A place can hold a premises licence for 200 *and* a
  temporary licence for one weekend at 499. The ceiling is resolved **for a date**, never in the
  abstract. A consumer asking "what is the cap?" without a date is asking the wrong question.
- **Not contestable by anyone** — neither the Operator nor the community gets a vote (16.05.04 D-04).
- **Freshness is expiry-driven, not decay-driven** (D-11, consistent with 16.05.05's licence row).

### Layer 2 — Published configurations (Operator-declared, room property)

Configurations are a property of the room (16.01.02 D-04 — a reconfigurable room is one room, one
calendar). Each configuration declares:

| Element | Required? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Yes | "Standing", "Seated", "Cabaret" — free text |
| Capacity | Yes | The number |
| **Counting basis** | Yes | `audience` or `total-occupancy` — no default, no inference (D-05) |
| Footprint assumption | Yes | The stage/production state the figure assumes: "stage depth ≤ 3m, no side-fills" |
| Derate rules | Optional | `(condition → delta)` pairs: "full-width backline → −40"; "in-the-round → −55" |

A configuration capacity must be **≤ the ceiling effective on the date being evaluated**, after
basis reconciliation (below). The Operator may declare *less* than the ceiling freely — choosing to
sell 300 of 350 is legitimate and common.

### Layer 3 — Effective capacity for an event (derived, never stored here)

The room publishes configurations and rules. An **event** resolves them into one number, at read
time, computed by whoever needs it (17's deal maths, 19's allocation, 16.07's check). 16 owns the
rule; it does not own the computation and does not store the result — mirroring CX-06's existing
position that potential gross is a read-time computation.

The subtraction chain, in order (DT-07):

```
ceiling effective on the event date          (16.01.06, total-occupancy)
  − house party size                          (16.02.01, Operator-declared)
  = maximum audience on that date
  ∩ chosen configuration capacity             (16.02.01, audience basis)
  − production derate                         (16.02.01 rules, triggered by 18's rider)
  − accessible-space displacement             (16.01.04 — see DT-15)
  − guest-list allocation                     (16.02.04 / CX-04)
  = sellable allocation                       (consumed by 19)
```

**Capacity is the input to that chain, not the answer.** A ticketing system that treats the capacity
number as the allocation ceiling has skipped four subtractions, every one of which is a real
oversell in live music.

### The fourth number — advertised (a claim, not a fact)

Whatever the venue's own website says. Ingested during seeding (16.05.01), provenance class
**seeded — weakest** (16.05.03 D-02). It is never a ceiling, never an input to the chain, and never
displayed as the room's capacity. It exists for exactly one reason: **so that the contradiction can
be detected**.

Where advertised exceeds licensed, the record is flagged. This is not a rare edge case — it is one
of the most common data conditions in live music, and detecting it is a genuinely new capability.
Where advertised is *lower* than licensed, nothing is flagged: a venue that sells fewer than it may
is compliant and unremarkable.

### Contradiction detection — what this feature actually adds

| Detection | Condition | Why it is new |
|---|---|---|
| **Overstatement** | advertised > ceiling (same date scope) | Nobody holds both numbers, so nobody can compare them |
| **Basis collapse** | config capacity (audience) == ceiling (total-occupancy) | Arithmetically impossible — staff and the band are bodies inside. Equality is the signature of a silently mixed basis (DT-12) |
| **Stale ceiling** | our own settled ticket count (19) for a past show in this room > the recorded ceiling | The licence record is probably stale; presented as a prompt to fix it, never as an accusation (DT-11) |
| **Stranded allocation** | ceiling decreases below a live allocation | The only spec field whose *decrease* invalidates a commitment already sold (DT-09) |
| **Implausibility** | post-gig flag from a band that played the room (16.05.06) | Bands cannot count a crowd, but they know a 150-cap pub claiming 350 (D-09) |

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims a 350-cap venue. Licence record (16.01.06) records **licensed capacity 350,
   total-occupancy basis**, no end date.
2. Operator opens the room's capacity panel. It is empty; the room is excluded from every
   capacity-filtered search and the Operator sees the lost-enquiry nudge.
3. Operator adds configuration **Standing / 350**. The system asks the basis. Operator answers
   `audience`. The system responds: *"350 audience under a 350 total-occupancy licence leaves nobody
   for your staff or the band. Did you mean 350 total, or a lower audience figure?"* (basis collapse,
   DT-12).
4. Operator corrects to **Standing / 320 / audience**, and declares **house party size 30**. The two
   figures now reconcile against the 350 ceiling.
5. Operator adds **Seated / 180 / audience** and **Cabaret / 120 / audience**, each with a footprint
   assumption. Each is timestamped and provenance-tagged `owner-declared` (16.05.03).
6. Operator attempts **Standing / 400**. The save is rejected; the prior value stands; the panel
   shows *"400 exceeds the licensed capacity of 350 effective on this date. Licensed capacity comes
   from your premises licence and cannot be raised here."*
7. Operator adds a derate rule to Standing: *"full-width backline → −40"*, with the footprint
   assumption *"stage depth ≤ 3m"*.
8. Seeding (16.05.01) has already ingested **advertised 400** from the venue's website. The record
   flags the contradiction and shows both numbers with provenance to the Operator, who can correct
   their website or accept the flag.
9. Musician searches for a **300–400 cap** room. The venue appears **once**, matched on its
   best-fitting configuration (Standing / 320 / audience), with the basis stated on the card — not
   three times, once per configuration (D-13).
10. Musician is offered a show on 2026-11-14. The room resolves the ceiling **for that date**, and
    16.07 checks their rider against the room; the rider's full-width backline triggers the derate,
    so the check reports **effective audience 280, not 320**.
11. Booking is confirmed against the **Standing** configuration (16.06.03). The configuration is
    selected per booking, not per room — the room publishes the set; the event picks one.
12. 19 Ticketing allocates against the subtraction chain, not against "350": ceiling 350 − house
    party 30 = 320, ∩ Standing 320, − backline derate 40, − 4 accessible spaces and their
    displacement, − 10 guest list = **266 sellable**.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Advertised (400) > licensed (350) | Flagged contradiction. Licensed wins as the ceiling; the record shows both with provenance. Advertised never enters the subtraction chain | "Venue advertises 400; licensed capacity is 350" — both values, both provenance classes |
| Advertised (300) < licensed (350) | **Not a contradiction.** A venue selling fewer than it may is compliant. No flag, no marker | Nothing — the advertised figure is simply not displayed |
| **No licence record exists (the majority case at cold start)** | No ceiling to enforce. Every configuration is an unverified Operator claim. The room is still searchable — excluding the unlicensed majority would empty the registry — but every figure carries the label, and no contradiction detection is possible | "Capacity not verified against a licence" on every configuration |
| **Capacity includes/excludes staff, crew and guest list** | **Resolved (D-05)**: every figure carries a required counting basis. Licensed is always `total-occupancy`. Configurations declare theirs. House party size is a declared room field. The industry's silent ambiguity becomes an explicit, reconcilable pair | Basis stated inline: "320 audience · licensed 350 total occupancy · 30 house party" |
| Operator declares a configuration with no basis | Save is rejected. Basis has no default and is never inferred — inferring it recreates the exact ambiguity this model exists to kill | "Does 320 count your audience, or everyone in the building including staff and the band?" |
| **Config capacity (audience) == licensed (total-occupancy)** | Basis collapse detected. Save is not blocked — it may be a `total-occupancy` config, which is legal — but the Operator is challenged once, and if they confirm `audience`, the configuration is flagged `basis-suspect` and drops out of confident matching (16.07) | "350 audience under a 350 total-occupancy licence leaves nobody for your staff or the band" |
| **Room capacity varies with the stage footprint (a big production eats 40 places)** | **Resolved (D-07)**: the configuration states a footprint assumption and carries derate rules. An event resolves the effective capacity at read time by applying the rules triggered by 18's rider. The room's published number never changes; the event's number does | Musician sees "Standing 320 — assumes stage depth ≤ 3m. Your rider's full-width backline: 280" |
| Operator declares no derate rules and the production is large | No rule, no derate — the effective capacity equals the config capacity and the room is silently optimistic. This is the honest failure of an optional field, and it is exactly the condition CX-01 names ("two independent fields that must agree and have no mechanism to"). 16.07 reports the footprint assumption alongside the match so the band can see what was assumed | "Standing 320 — assumes stage depth ≤ 3m. Your rider states 6m. The venue has not stated a derate." |
| **Licensed capacity is time-bounded (a temporary licence for one weekend at 499 over a 200 premises licence)** | The ceiling is date-scoped (D-06). Resolution takes the ceiling **effective on the evaluated date**. A booking on the temporary weekend resolves 499; a booking the following week resolves 200 | Calendar-aware: "Licensed 200 · 499 on 14–16 Aug (temporary licence)" |
| **Licensed capacity is reduced after inspection (fire officer cuts 350 → 280) with tickets already sold above 280** | 16 detects the stranded allocation the moment the ceiling drops and notifies the Operator, 19 and 17. **16 does not cancel tickets** — it is not the ticketing system. The room enters `over-allocated`; every configuration above 280 is **suspended**, not silently clamped; no new allocation is possible above the new ceiling | Operator: "Your licensed capacity dropped to 280. 3 shows have 340, 310 and 290 tickets sold. Ticketing has been notified." |
| Licensed capacity is reduced below a published configuration, no tickets sold | The configuration is **suspended**, not deleted and not clamped. Silently rewriting an Operator's declared 350 to 280 is the platform putting words in their mouth | "Standing (350) is suspended — it exceeds your new licensed capacity of 280. Restate it to publish again." |
| **The licence declaration expires (16.01.06 state `Expired`) with a recorded ceiling of 350** | **The ceiling stands. Fail closed** (D-14). An expired declaration is not evidence the ceiling rose; it is evidence we no longer know. The last-known ceiling continues to bind, and the figure is presented as unverified | "Licensed capacity 350 — declaration expired 2026-05-01, not re-verified" |
| Mutually exclusive rooms sold simultaneously | Room exclusion (16.01.02 D-02) prevents it. **Combined capacity of exclusive rooms is never additive** — a place with a 350 main room and an 80 basement that share a fire exit is not a 430-cap place, and no view may sum them | Calendar blocks the sibling room; place-level capacity is never presented as a sum |
| **Outdoor / temporary space (courtyard, beer garden)** | **Resolved (D-15)**: the test is the *room*, not the roof. A courtyard that is a bookable room on a place record with a licence uses this model unchanged. A **festival site** is not a room, has no place record, and its per-event licensed capacity is an event property owned by 17/18 — 16 does not model it | An outdoor room looks like any other room |
| **Community suggests a capacity edit** | Permitted — capacity is a *fact*, not a term (16.05.03 D-03/D-05), so it is suggestible. But it lands as class `community suggestion`, which cannot overwrite `statutory` (the ceiling) and does not outrank `owner-declared` (a configuration). In practice it queues for the Operator or, on an unclaimed record, is governed by 16.05.03 Q-01 | Pending-edit marker on the configuration |
| **Post-gig report proposes a capacity number ("felt like 200 in there, not 350")** | Recorded as an **implausibility flag**, never as a value (D-09). No band counts a crowd. Two independent flags on one configuration mark it `implausible` and drop it out of confident matching until the Operator responds — the flag is a question, not a correction | Operator: "2 bands who played here have flagged Standing (320) as implausible." Musician: "Capacity flagged as implausible by bands who played here" |
| **The multi-hyphenate: an Operator plays a gig in their own room and files a post-gig report on it** | Provenance class is derived from the contributor's **relationship to the record**, not from the persona lens in use (D-12). A self-report on a record you own is `owner-declared`, full stop. Switching from the Operator lens to the Musician lens cannot launder a self-claim into first-hand class. This is the field with the strongest incentive to try | Silent — the report is accepted and classed correctly; no accusation is made |
| **The multi-hyphenate: an Operator's own band books a room the Operator does not own** | No conflict. They are a Musician on that record and their report is genuine first-hand class. The relationship test (D-12) resolves this correctly in both directions | Normal first-hand report |
| **Two of the Operator's staff edit configurations concurrently** | Edits to different fields never collide — each field settles on its own. When both writers touch the same field, the second to save is shown the other's value and the time it landed and chooses which stands; the change is never applied silently, and the superseded value stays readable in the configuration's change history (16.05.03 D-04). The exception is a **decrease that would strand an allocation** — blocked regardless of who wrote it, because the consequence is external to the record | Second writer: "Standing was changed to 300 by [name] 40 seconds ago. Your value: 320. Keep yours, or theirs?" |
| **Race: Operator lowers a configuration while 19 has tickets in carts against it** | The new value applies to allocation **from that moment**; carts already issued are 19's to honour or release. 16 never retroactively voids an allocation it did not make. If the decrease strands tickets already sold, this becomes the `over-allocated` case above | Operator sees the consequence before confirming: "Lowering Standing to 300 — 312 tickets are already sold for 14 Nov." |
| **Race: a hold exists on the room (16.06.01) when the ceiling drops** | A hold is not an allocation — no tickets exist. The hold survives; the configuration suspends. The holder is notified so they can decide before it converts | Holder: "The room's licensed capacity changed to 280 since your hold. Your hold is still valid." |
| **Operator declares many near-identical configurations to appear in every search band** (Standing 300 / 310 / 320 / 330…) | Search dedupes by room, not by configuration: a room appears **once**, matched on its best-fitting configuration (D-13). Declaring 12 configurations buys exactly the same visibility as declaring 2 | Nothing — the gaming has no effect, so it is not worth a warning |
| Two configurations with the same capacity and different names (Standing 320 / GA 320) | Harmless. Both publish; search matches once. No dedupe, no warning — naming is the Operator's business | Both listed |
| **Room is subdivided or merged (a wall goes in; the 350 room becomes 200 + 120)** | Retire + create (16.01.02 Q-03). Historical bookings keep the retired room's configurations frozen at their then-current values — a 2024 settlement must remain readable against the room as it was. New configurations attach to the new rooms. Neither new room inherits the 350 licence figure; the ceiling must be re-derived from 16.01.06 | "Main Room (retired 2026-11-01). This room's capacity history is preserved for past bookings." |
| **Operator on a phone at a loading dock loses connection mid-edit** | The configuration panel is group-wise and each configuration commits independently (16.02.02 D-02). An interrupted edit loses that one configuration's unsaved fields, never the others, and never a previously saved value | "Couldn't save Standing — you're offline. Retry" with the entered values retained |
| Malicious input: capacity entered as 999999, or 0, or negative | Non-positive rejected outright. A figure above the ceiling is rejected by the ceiling rule. **With no licence record there is no ceiling** — so an unlicensed room can claim 999999, and only the unverified label stands between that and search. This is the honest cost of not requiring a licence to list, and it is what makes the licensed-ceiling detection valuable rather than decorative | "Capacity must be a positive number" / (unlicensed) the figure publishes with "not verified against a licence" |
| **A room genuinely has one configuration (most small venues)** | Configuration is not surfaced as a concept. The Operator answers "how many people fit?" and a single configuration named after the room type is created invisibly — mirroring 16.01.02 D-03's treatment of the single-room place. **The abstraction must be invisible at N=1** | "Capacity: 320 audience" — the word "configuration" never appears |
| 19 Ticketing requests a capacity for a room with configurations but no booking (a speculative on-sale) | No event, no chosen configuration, no derate — so no effective capacity exists. 16 returns the ceiling and the configuration set and refuses to pick one. Guessing a configuration on 19's behalf would be 16 inventing an event's shape | 19's concern — 16 states "configuration not selected" |
| **The room has never been booked on-platform, so nothing ever refreshes the capacity** | Structurally fine, uniquely. Buildings do not shrink and the ceiling is expiry-driven, so capacity is one of the very few fields where the domain's freshness hole (Q-10, lockout generates no completion events) does not bite. **Capacity does not need the harvest** (D-11) | "Licensed capacity 350 — licence current" with no staleness caveat |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Capacity panel or search card requested | Skeleton rows sized to the configuration count; the ceiling resolves first and renders before the configurations |
| **Empty / Unstated** | No configuration declared | Room excluded from every capacity-filtered search; Operator sees the lost-enquiry nudge: "You're not appearing in capacity searches" |
| **Claimed** | Configurations declared, **no licence record to check against** | Every figure + "Capacity not verified against a licence". No contradiction detection is possible in this state |
| **Verified / Populated** | Declared, basis stated, within the ceiling effective for the date | Figures + basis + licensed-capacity badge + the ceiling's licence provenance |
| **Partial** | Some elements missing — a configuration with a capacity but no footprint assumption, or a ceiling with no house party size | The figures that exist, with the gap named explicitly: "Standing 320 — footprint assumption not stated". Never dyed green by 16.07 (16.07: unknown is never a pass) |
| **Contested** | A community suggestion is disputed, or advertised exceeds licensed | Last-agreed value + both positions + provenance + contested marker (16.05.04 D-01 — last-agreed stands, contest visible) |
| **Implausible** | 2+ independent post-gig flags (16.05.06) on one configuration | Value + "flagged as implausible by bands who played here"; drops out of confident matching until the Operator responds |
| **Basis-suspect** | Config capacity == ceiling on a mixed basis, and the Operator confirmed `audience` | Value + "this figure may be counting everyone in the building"; drops out of confident matching |
| **Suspended** | The ceiling dropped below a published configuration | Configuration hidden from search, retained on the record, not clamped: "Standing (350) is suspended — it exceeds your licensed capacity of 280" |
| **Over-allocated** | The ceiling dropped below a **live** allocation | Operator + 19 + 17 notified with the affected shows and counts. The room shows the new ceiling; the sold overage is 19's to resolve. 16 reports; it does not cancel |
| **Ceiling-expired** | The licence declaration passed its expiry (16.01.06) | Last-known ceiling still enforced (fail closed) + "declaration expired [date], not re-verified" |
| **Error** | The write failed, or the ceiling could not be resolved | Ceiling unresolvable: the room falls back to `Claimed` and every figure is labelled unverified — it never falls back to *no ceiling* and never presents an unchecked figure as checked. Write failure: entered values retained + "Couldn't save — retry" |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Capacity is one number on a room | How every venue directory and every venue's own website models it | ❌ REJECTED: it is three numbers with three provenance classes — licensed (statutory ceiling), configuration (Operator claim), advertised (marketing). Collapsing them is exactly why capacity data is untrustworthy industry-wide, and separating them is what makes "this venue advertises 400 but is licensed for 350" **detectable**. That detection is new. |
| DT-02 | The Operator is the authority on their own room's capacity | The general rule everywhere else in this domain — the owner wins on their own record | ❌ REJECTED: licensed capacity is imposed by a licensing authority, not chosen. This is the clearest case in the domain of a field the record owner cannot write, and the Operator's anti-persona behaviour ("misrepresents the room's capacity to win bookings") is precisely why the ceiling must not be theirs to edit. |
| DT-03 | Reconfiguration should be modelled as separate rooms | Seated and standing have different capacities and different ticket maps — they look like different products | ❌ REJECTED (inherited from 16.01.02 DT-03): one physical space, one calendar. Two room records would let both be sold at once — a physical impossibility the data model would permit. Configurations belong to capacity. |
| DT-04 | Capacity is a venue-only concept | This feature sits in the venue spec sub-domain | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: studios and rehearsal rooms have occupancy limits too (how many people fit in the live room; fire limits). But the *machinery* — configurations, ticket ceilings, deal maths — is venue-specific and would be dead weight on a studio. Keeping it venue-scoped and accepting that a studio's occupancy is a simple spec field in 16.03.01, not this model. |
| DT-05 | "Capacity" means the same thing to everyone | It is the industry's most-used number | ❌ REJECTED: licensed capacity counts every body in the building (staff, crew, guests); the number a band cares about is paying audience. These differ by 10–15% and nobody states which they mean. **Resolved in this pass by D-05** — the basis is a required, explicit attribute of every figure, exactly as D-01 refuses to collapse the three numbers. The industry's silent ambiguity becomes a reconcilable pair. |
| DT-06 | The licensed ceiling is a single constant | It reads like a property of the building | ❌ REJECTED — and this is the finding a licensing officer would expect and nobody in the sweep raised. A place can hold a premises licence for 200 **and** a temporary licence for one weekend at 499. The ceiling is a **function of the date**, not a scalar. Every consumer of this field — 19's allocation above all — must resolve it **for the event date**. A ticketing system that caches "the cap" has cached a number that is wrong on the exact dates that matter most (the ones where the venue paid for extra headroom). |
| DT-07 | Licensed capacity is the ceiling on ticket allocation | Stated as fact in this file's own first draft, in 16.01.06, and in the domain index | ❌ REJECTED as stated — it is the **first term of a subtraction chain**, not the ceiling. Sellable = ceiling − house party − production derate − accessible displacement − guest list. Every one of those subtractions is a real oversell that happens in live music, and the reason they happen is that the industry hands 19 a single number and calls it the cap. Capacity is the **input**, not the answer. This is the sharpest correction in this pass and it changes what 19 must consume. |
| DT-08 | The advertised number is harmless marketing — a vanity figure worth flagging for tidiness | It lives on a website, not in a contract | ❌ REJECTED: the advertised figure is what a band reads **before** they negotiate, so it anchors the deal maths on a door split (CX-06). A venue advertising 400 against a licensed 350 has inflated the band's perceived earning ceiling by 14% at the moment of negotiation. The contradiction flag has **commercial teeth, not just compliance teeth** — it protects the Musician's negotiating position, not the licensing authority's records. That reframing is what makes it a product feature rather than an audit report. |
| DT-09 | A capacity change is an ordinary field edit | Every other spec field works this way — write the new value, reset the freshness clock | ❌ REJECTED: capacity is **the only field in this sub-domain whose *decrease* can invalidate a commitment already sold**. A fire officer cuts a room from 350 to 280 and 340 tickets exist. A PA downgrade disappoints; a capacity cut creates people with tickets and no legal right to be in the building. Nothing in the domain's write model contemplates a field edit with an external commercial consequence, and nobody designs for it because the capacity field is normally inert. Requires a trigger chain into 19 and 17 (D-10), and it is the strongest argument that this field is not a spec field wearing a spec field's rules. |
| DT-10 | The band is a good first-hand source for capacity — the domain's own principle (16.02 D-07, 16.02.02 DT-03) | The whole freshness engine rests on the post-gig harvest | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED, and scoping D-07 rather than contradicting it. **Nobody counts a crowd.** A band's estimate of 350 people is worthless as a number and would be actively harmful if it overwrote a licensed figure. But bands know **gross implausibility** — a room claiming 350 that is visibly a 150-cap pub is obvious to anyone who has stood on its stage. So the harvest yields a **flag, not a value** (D-09). The general principle holds; this field is where it has the least purchase, and saying so is better than pretending the harvest keeps capacity fresh. |
| DT-11 | The platform can learn a room's true capacity from actual attendance | We own the box office (19); we can see what really fits | ❌ REJECTED as a **measurement** — a half-sold show tells you nothing, and inferring capacity *downward* from weak sales would turn poor ticket sales into a permanent, published slur on the room. ✅ CONFIRMED as a **contradiction detector**: a settled ticket count is proof the room held *at least* that many — a **lower bound with first-hand provenance**. A floor above the recorded ceiling means the licence record is stale (the common, innocent explanation) or the room oversold. Surfaced to the Operator as a prompt — "your licence record says 280; we sold 400 here in March" — never as an accusation, and never automatically to anyone else. |
| DT-12 | A configuration capacity equal to the licensed capacity is fine — it just means they sell right up to the limit | It looks like an Operator maximising their room | ❌ REJECTED: it is **arithmetically impossible** across the two bases. Licensed counts every body; staff, crew and the band are bodies. 350 audience under a 350 total-occupancy licence means zero staff inside a running venue. Equality is therefore the **signature of a silently mixed basis** — and it is the commonest way DT-05's ambiguity surfaces in real data. This is the check that turns D-05 from a data-modelling nicety into a detector. |
| DT-13 | The multi-hyphenate cannot cause trouble on a mostly-read field | Reading a number is not adversarial | ❌ REJECTED — and this field has the strongest incentive of any in the domain. An Operator who plays a gig in their own room can file a post-gig report (16.05.06) on their own venue and thereby **launder an owner claim into first-hand class**, which is the one class positioned to challenge an owner claim (16.05.03 D-02). Provenance must be computed from the contributor's **relationship to the record**, not from which persona lens the UI happens to be in (D-12). `personas.md` is explicit that one human holds several roles simultaneously — so any provenance model keyed on the *active* role is exploitable by construction, on every record in the platform. |
| DT-14 | Outdoor and festival capacity is out of scope — the record model assumes a permanent room | 16.01.01 Q-02 raised exactly this | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED, and the breadth pass's deferral resolved: **the test is the room, not the roof.** A courtyard, beer garden or arches space that is a bookable room on a place record, with a licence and a calendar, uses this model completely unchanged — outdoor-ness is not a modelling property. A **festival site** is genuinely different: it is not a room, it has no place record, its licensed capacity is granted per-event by a temporary licence, and it exists for one weekend. That is an **event** property, owned by 17/18, and 16 must not grow it. Drawing the line at "is it a room?" keeps a real and common case in and a large scope creep out. |
| DT-15 | Accessible spaces are 16.01.04's problem, not capacity's | Accessibility is its own feature with its own profile | ❌ REJECTED: a wheelchair bay **physically displaces standing places** — it occupies floor area that would otherwise hold other bodies. So accessible provision is a term in the subtraction chain (DT-07), not a parallel concern. The displacement ratio is a **room fact the Operator declares**, not a constant the platform may invent — it depends on the bay's position, sightline requirements and the companion seat. Nobody models this, which is precisely why accessible allocations get "found" at the last minute by subtracting them from a number that was never adjusted for them. This is a concrete instance of 16.01.04's cost landing on a disabled musician or Fan through an arithmetic gap nobody owns. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Three capacity numbers with three provenance classes: licensed, configuration, advertised | Collapsing them is why industry capacity data is untrustworthy; separating them makes overstatement detectable (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Licensed capacity is a hard ceiling the Operator cannot raise | It is imposed, not chosen — and the Operator's anti-persona is overstating capacity (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Configurations are a property of the room, not separate rooms | One physical space cannot be sold twice (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Capacity machinery stays venue-scoped | Configurations and ticket ceilings are dead weight on a studio (DT-04) |
| D-05 | **Every capacity figure carries a required, explicit counting basis** (`audience` \| `total-occupancy`). Licensed is always `total-occupancy`. House party size is a declared room field. No default, no inference | Resolves Q-01 by applying D-01 one level down: refuse the conflation rather than pick a side. Choosing one meaning would hide the ambiguity in exactly the way the industry already does; making it explicit makes the two figures reconcilable and makes DT-12's detector possible (DT-05) |
| D-06 | **The ceiling is date-scoped, resolved for an event date, never a scalar** | A temporary licence is a real, common second ceiling over the premises licence. A cached "cap" is wrong on precisely the dates the venue paid extra for (DT-06) |
| D-07 | **The room publishes configurations + footprint assumptions + derate rules; effective capacity is computed per event at read time and never stored here** | Resolves Q-02. Only the Operator knows a full-width backline eats 40 places, so the rule is a room property; only the event knows the rider, so the result is an event computation. Mirrors CX-06's existing position that derived figures are read-time, not stored |
| D-08 | **16 does not model seat geometry.** It publishes capacity, configuration and layout intent; a geometric seat map, if one is needed, is 19's | Resolves this feature's side of Q-03 and honours 16.01.02 DT-04 — 16 must not grow a seating-chart editor without 19 confirming it owns it. Whether 19 needs one is 19's question, not a reason for 16 to hedge |
| D-09 | **The post-gig harvest yields an implausibility flag on capacity, never a value** | Nobody counts a crowd, so a band's number is worthless; but a 150-cap pub claiming 350 is obvious to anyone who played it. Scopes 16.02 D-07 rather than contradicting it (DT-10) |
| D-10 | **A ceiling decrease below a live allocation is detected and notified to 19 and 17; 16 never cancels a ticket and never silently clamps a configuration** | Capacity is the only field here whose decrease invalidates a sold commitment. Silently rewriting the Operator's 350 to 280 puts words in their mouth; cancelling tickets is 19's job, not the registry's (DT-09) |
| D-11 | **Capacity freshness is expiry-driven via the licence record, not decay-curve-driven** | Buildings do not shrink. This makes capacity immune to the domain's structural freshness hole (Q-10) — a room that is never booked still has a trustworthy capacity |
| D-12 | **Provenance class is derived from the contributor's relationship to the record, not the active persona lens** | `personas.md`: one human holds several roles simultaneously. Any provenance model keyed on the active role lets an Operator-Musician launder a self-claim into first-hand class — the class specifically positioned to challenge owner claims (DT-13) |
| D-13 | **A room appears once in a capacity-filtered search, matched on its best-fitting configuration** | Kills configuration-spamming as a visibility tactic without an arbitrary cap on how a room describes itself |
| D-14 | **An expired licence declaration does not lift the ceiling — the last-known ceiling stands. Fail closed** | An expired declaration is not evidence the ceiling rose; it is evidence we no longer know. Failing open would make expiry an incentive |
| D-15 | **Outdoor is a room test, not a roof test.** A licensed, bookable outdoor room is in scope unchanged; a festival site is not a room and its per-event capacity belongs to 17/18 | Resolves the outdoor deferral left by the breadth pass. Keeps a real, common case (courtyards, beer gardens, arches) in, and keeps a large scope creep (temporary event sites) out |
