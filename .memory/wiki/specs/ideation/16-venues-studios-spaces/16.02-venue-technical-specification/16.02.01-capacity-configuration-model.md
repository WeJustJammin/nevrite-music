# Feature: Capacity & Configuration Model

> **Number**: 16.02.01
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How many people fit in the room, in which layout, and under whose authority. "Capacity" is a
single number everywhere in the industry and it is three different numbers in reality: the
**licensed** capacity (a legal ceiling), the **configuration** capacity (350 standing, 180 seated,
120 cabaret — same room), and the **advertised** capacity (what the venue's website says, which is
frequently the most flattering of the three).

This field is load-bearing far outside this domain: 19 Ticketing cannot allocate above the licensed
ceiling, 17 Live Booking's deal maths runs off the config capacity, and a band choosing between two
rooms is choosing on this number.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Defines the configurations their room supports and the capacity of each. **Cannot exceed the licensed capacity** derived from 16.01.06 — the one field in the domain where the record owner is not the authority. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Reads capacity by configuration when choosing a room — this and the fee are the two numbers a band decides on. Can flag an implausible number post-gig (16.05.06). |
| Producer | Read-only | Rarely relevant — capacity is a venue concern and the Producer's interest in this domain is studio rooms. Reads it when producing a live recording (crowd size affects the approach). |
| Fan | Read-only (indirect) | Never sees a capacity number as a number. Experiences it as ticket availability and as "this is a small room" context on a show page. Exposing licensed capacity to Fans has no upside and invites scrutiny of the Operator's compliance. |

## Behavior

Capacity is modelled as a **set of named configurations** on a room (16.01.02 D-04 — a
reconfigurable room is one room), each with a capacity figure, and each optionally with what the
configuration implies (seated rows vs standing floor vs cabaret tables changes the stage position,
the sightlines and the ticket map).

Three numbers, three provenance classes:
- **Licensed** — from 16.01.06, statutory, a hard ceiling, Operator cannot raise it
- **Configuration** — Operator-declared, must be ≤ licensed
- **Advertised** — whatever the venue's own website says; ingested during seeding (16.05.01) and treated as a *claim*, not a fact

Where advertised exceeds licensed, the record is flagged. This is not a rare edge case — it is one
of the most common data conditions in live music, and detecting it is a genuinely new capability.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims a 350-cap venue; licence record (16.01.06) sets licensed capacity 350
2. Operator adds configurations: Standing 350, Seated 180, Cabaret 120
3. Operator attempts Standing 400; system blocks it against the licensed ceiling
4. Musician searching for a 300–400 cap room finds this venue on the Standing config
5. 19 Ticketing allocates against the selected configuration, capped at 350

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Advertised (400) > licensed (350) | Flagged contradiction; licensed wins as the ceiling; the record shows both with provenance | "Venue advertises 400; licensed capacity is 350" |
| No licence record exists (the common case at cold start) | No ceiling to enforce; capacity is an unverified Operator claim and is labelled as such | "Capacity not verified against a licence" |
| Capacity includes/excludes staff, crew and guest list — nobody agrees which | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the licensed figure usually includes everyone in the building; the config figure usually means paying audience. The two are silently different numbers. | — |
| Room capacity varies with the stage footprint (a big production eats 40 places) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — production-dependent capacity is real (a full-width backline reduces the floor) and is where 16 meets 18's rider | — |
| Mutually exclusive rooms sold simultaneously | Room exclusion (16.01.02 D-02) prevents it; combined capacity of exclusive rooms is never additive | Calendar blocks the sibling room |
| Outdoor/festival capacity | `[PENDING]` — likely out of scope; the record model assumes a permanent room (16.01.01 Q-02) | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unstated | No capacity declared | Room excluded from capacity-filtered search; Operator nudged with lost-enquiry signal |
| Claimed | Operator declared, no licence to check against | Capacity + "not verified against a licence" |
| Verified | Declared and within the licensed ceiling | Capacity + licensed-capacity badge |
| Contested | Advertised/declared exceeds licensed, or a post-gig flag | Both values shown with provenance + contested marker |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Capacity is one number on a room | How every venue directory and every venue's own website models it | ❌ REJECTED: it is three numbers with three provenance classes — licensed (statutory ceiling), configuration (Operator claim), advertised (marketing). Collapsing them is exactly why capacity data is untrustworthy industry-wide, and separating them is what makes "this venue advertises 400 but is licensed for 350" **detectable**. That detection is new. |
| DT-02 | The Operator is the authority on their own room's capacity | The general rule everywhere else in this domain — the owner wins on their own record | ❌ REJECTED: licensed capacity is imposed by a licensing authority, not chosen. This is the clearest case in the domain of a field the record owner cannot write, and the Operator's anti-persona behaviour ("misrepresents the room's capacity to win bookings") is precisely why the ceiling must not be theirs to edit. |
| DT-03 | Reconfiguration should be modelled as separate rooms | Seated and standing have different capacities and different ticket maps — they look like different products | ❌ REJECTED (inherited from 16.01.02 DT-03): one physical space, one calendar. Two room records would let both be sold at once — a physical impossibility the data model would permit. Configurations belong to capacity. |
| DT-04 | Capacity is a venue-only concept | This feature sits in the venue spec sub-domain | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: studios and rehearsal rooms have occupancy limits too (how many people fit in the live room; fire limits). But the *machinery* — configurations, ticket ceilings, deal maths — is venue-specific and would be dead weight on a studio. Keeping it venue-scoped and accepting that a studio's occupancy is a simple spec field in 16.03.01, not this model. |
| DT-05 | "Capacity" means the same thing to everyone | It is the industry's most-used number | ❌ REJECTED, and unresolved: licensed capacity counts every body in the building (staff, crew, guests); the number a band cares about is paying audience. These differ by 10–15% and nobody states which they mean. Flagged as Q-01 — this ambiguity is silently present in every capacity number in the industry. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Three capacity numbers with three provenance classes: licensed, configuration, advertised | Collapsing them is why industry capacity data is untrustworthy; separating them makes overstatement detectable (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Licensed capacity is a hard ceiling the Operator cannot raise | It is imposed, not chosen — and the Operator's anti-persona is overstating capacity (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Configurations are a property of the room, not separate rooms | One physical space cannot be sold twice (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Capacity machinery stays venue-scoped | Configurations and ticket ceilings are dead weight on a studio (DT-04) |
