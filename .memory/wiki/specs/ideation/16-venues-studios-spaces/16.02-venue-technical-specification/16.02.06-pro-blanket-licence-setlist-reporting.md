# Feature: PRO Blanket Licence & Setlist Reporting Status

> **Number**: 16.02.06
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Whether the venue holds a performing-rights blanket licence, and — the part that matters —
**whether performances in that room are reported to the collecting body**, which is the difference
between a songwriter getting paid for their own gig and not.

*Which* bodies apply is **not asserted here**: it is the performing-rights licence status slot in
16.01.06 D-06, filled by whatever bodies the record's regime profile declares, and the US profile's
names are deferred to `/create-prd-security` with the rest of its instrument names (16.01.06 Q-04).
The UK vocabulary this feature was originally written in retires into an unauthored profile.

Live performance royalties are one of music's largest routinely-unclaimed income streams. The money
exists: the venue pays the blanket licence, the PRO collects it, and it is distributed against
reported setlists. When nobody reports, the money is distributed by proxy to whoever is already big.
This is the domain's most direct connection to the thesis — **provenance of a live performance,
captured at the moment it is true, turning into money**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Declares blanket licence status and reference. Declares whether they report setlists and by what route. A licensed venue that reports is a venue artists want to play — this field becomes a competitive asset. |
| Musician | Read-only + act | Sees whether the room is licensed and reporting **before** the gig, and is prompted to submit their setlist after it. For a songwriter playing their own material this is directly their money. The highest-value read in the venue spec. |
| Producer | Read-only | Peripheral — matters when a live recording generates a separate performance/mechanical question, and when the Producer is the one holding the setlist. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A Fan has no action and no interest; PRO licensing is invisible infrastructure to the audience. |

## Behavior

Two linked facts, deliberately separated:

**Licence status** — which PRO(s), reference, current/expired/unknown. This is the
**performing-rights licence status slot** in 16.01.06 D-06, so *which* bodies apply is named by the
record's **regime profile** (16.01.06 D-05) rather than assumed: the US profile is the only one
authored at launch and its instrument names are deferred with the rest (16.01.06 Q-04), and an
unauthored profile renders the slot as an explicit unknown rather than defaulting to another
territory's bodies. Where a profile declares a publicly checkable PRO register and the record's
authority resolves (16.05.01 D-16), status can be seeded (16.05.01); otherwise it is Operator-declared
like every other slot.

**Reporting posture** — does the venue report setlists, by what mechanism (venue-submitted /
artist-submitted / not reported), and for which kinds of night. A venue can be fully licensed and
report nothing. That combination is extremely common and is invisible to every artist who plays
there.

The feature's real output is a **prompt at the right moment**: after a confirmed gig at a licensed,
reporting room, the artist is asked for their setlist while they still remember it — the same
capture-at-source pattern as the domain's other harvest (16.05.06), applied to money.

The boundary of this feature is fixed even where a downstream decision is not: **16 always captures
the setlist and routes it to domain 10.** Whether the platform then *submits* that setlist directly
to the PRO (an integration owned by 10) or hands it back to the artist to submit themselves (a
lighter, non-integration path) is a domain-10 product decision, not a gap in this feature — tracked
as Q-01. Either way, the venue-side fact and the post-gig capture described here are unchanged.

### Happy Path

1. Operator declares the **performing-rights licence status slot** (16.01.06 D-06) as licensed against the body their record's regime profile names, reference on file, and declares that they report setlists via artist submission. *Which body the US profile names is deferred (16.01.06 Q-04)* — until it lands the Operator declares status against a body rendered as not yet determined, and the reporting posture is unaffected either way
2. Musician books the room; the spec shows "licensed + reporting" before they agree
3. Gig happens; the reservation completes (16.06.03)
4. Musician is prompted post-gig: "You played a licensed, reporting room — submit your setlist" with the gig's date, venue and their own catalogue pre-filled. The copy names the **slot**, never a body, so it is correct in every profile — including an unauthored one, where the room's status is an explicit unknown and no prompt fires at all
5. Setlist is captured while it is true; routed to 10 Royalties & Collections

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue is licensed but reports nothing | The common, invisible case this feature exposes | "Licensed, does not report setlists" — a fact artists have never been able to see |
| Venue is unlicensed | Structured fact. Playing there means no performance royalty, and it is also a licensing risk for the venue | "Not licensed" |
| Artist played covers only | Setlist is captured and routed anyway — the royalty flows to the writers of the covered songs, not to the performer. Reporting is still correct (the writers get paid); the performer's own performance royalty is simply zero for that night | "Reported for the songwriters. You performed these — you didn't write them, so this doesn't earn you a performance royalty" |
| Status unknown (the default for every seeded record) | "Unknown" must be visible as unknown, not absent | Explicit unknown state |
| Artist submits a setlist for a gig that did not happen | Fraud vector against a royalty pool. A completed reservation (16.06.03) auto-attaches as evidence and the submission is trusted; a submission with no matching completed reservation is accepted but flagged **off-platform / unverified** and carries lower trust weight when it reaches domain 10. Detection of the off-platform case is deferred to 24 (Q-03) | On-platform: "Matched to your gig on {date}." Off-platform: "We can't see this gig on WeJammin — mark where it happened; unverified setlists are reported with lower confidence" |
| Non-operator tries to change licence/reporting status | Rejected. Licence and reporting fields are Operator-config only; a Musician who believes the status is wrong files a community correction through 16.05 flagging, not a direct edit | Musicians see the field read-only with a "Report incorrect status" link, not an edit control |
| Two operators (or an operator + a register check) disagree on licence status | Last confirmed write wins but the prior value and its source are retained; a register-sourced value and an operator-declared value that conflict are surfaced, not silently overwritten. The register half only exists **where the profile declares a checkable PRO register and the record's authority resolves** (16.05.01 D-16) | "Register shows {body} active; operator marked expired — {which} is authoritative" `[PENDING — /write-be-spec: precedence rule]` |
| Same setlist submitted twice for one gig | Deduplicated on (artist, gig) — the second submission edits the first rather than creating a duplicate report into the pool | "You already submitted a setlist for this gig — editing it" |
| Musician dismisses / ignores the post-gig prompt | Capture is not lost. The prompt persists as a pending action tied to the completed gig; the setlist can still be submitted later (memory decays, so it is nagged, not expired) | A standing "Setlist not submitted for {venue}, {date}" reminder in their post-gig actions |
| Territory has a different regime entirely | **Resolved by the profile model** (16.01.06 D-05): the slot exists universally, the bodies that fill it are profile-declared, and an unauthored profile renders an explicit unknown. The **reporting posture** — does this room report setlists — is regime-independent and survives every profile unchanged, which is the half of this feature that carries D-02's value | "PRO status unknown — we don't yet model performing-rights licensing in this territory" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unknown | Seeded or unstated | "PRO status unknown" — the honest default for most of the registry |
| Licensed | Operator declared / register-checked | Licensed badge + PRO name |
| Licensed + reporting | Both declared | "Licensed and reports setlists" — the state artists should filter for |
| Licensed, not reporting | Both declared | Visible fact; the gap becomes legible for the first time |
| Not licensed | Declared | Visible fact; feeds 16.01.05 at-risk if a licence review follows |
| Expired | Statutory-record expiry — the performing-rights-licence-status slot (16.01.06 D-06) | Expired, not silently current |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | PRO licence status is a compliance checkbox on the venue record | Candidate 21 framed it as "compliance status" | ❌ REJECTED, and the reframing is the point. Compliance is the venue's problem. The **artist's** problem is that their live performance royalty is never claimed — and licence status is only interesting because it is the precondition for the setlist being worth reporting. Framed as compliance, it is a dead field nobody fills. Framed as "this is how you get paid for your own gig", it is one of the strongest reasons in the domain for a Musician to care about a venue record. |
| DT-02 | Licensed implies reported | If the venue pays the licence, the performances must get reported | ❌ REJECTED — this is the load-bearing error the feature exists to correct. Licensed and reporting are **independent**. A licensed venue that reports nothing is common, and the resulting money is distributed by proxy to acts who are already large. No artist can currently see which rooms report. Separating the two fields makes an invisible, structural income leak legible. |
| DT-03 | This is domain 10's (Royalties) feature, not 16's | The money and the PRO relationship belong to 10 | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: 10 owns the royalty, the PRO relationship and the collection. But the **licence status is a property of the room**, decaying and community-correctable like every other venue fact, and the **capture moment** is anchored to a booking in this domain. The seam: 16 owns the venue-side fact and the post-gig prompt; 10 owns everything the setlist becomes. Recorded as a high-confidence cross-domain touchpoint. |
| DT-04 | Post-gig setlist capture is the same mechanism as post-gig spec harvesting (16.05.06) | Both are "ask the band after the show" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a strong argument for one post-gig moment, not two. A band will tolerate one prompt after a show; two prompts is one too many. The setlist ask and the spec-correction ask should share the same surface. Escalated to the domain CX file, because it changes 16.05.06's shape. |
| DT-05 | This is where 16 touches the thesis most directly | D-18: provenance is the wedge | ✅ CONFIRMED: this is capture-at-source applied to live performance. The setlist is a fact that is true for exactly one night and is reconstructed — if ever — months later from memory. It is the same failure the platform exists to prevent, in the one domain where the platform is provably present (there is a booking). |
