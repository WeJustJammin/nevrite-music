# Feature: PRO Blanket Licence & Setlist Reporting Status

> **Number**: 16.02.06
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Whether the venue holds a performing-rights blanket licence (PRS/PPL in the UK; ASCAP/BMI/SESAC in
the US), and — the part that matters — **whether performances in that room are reported to the
PRO**, which is the difference between a songwriter getting paid for their own gig and not.

Live performance royalties are one of music's largest routinely-unclaimed income streams. The money
exists: the venue pays the blanket licence, the PRO collects it, and it is distributed against
reported setlists. When nobody reports, the money is distributed by proxy to whoever is already big.
This is the domain's most direct connection to the thesis — **provenance of a live performance,
captured at the moment it is true, turning into money**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Declares blanket licence status and reference. Declares whether they report setlists and by what route. A licensed venue that reports is a venue artists want to play — this field becomes a competitive asset. |
| Musician | Read-only + act | Sees whether the room is licensed and reporting **before** the gig, and is prompted to submit their setlist after it. For a songwriter playing their own material this is directly their money. The highest-value read in the venue spec. |
| Producer | Read-only | Peripheral — matters when a live recording generates a separate performance/mechanical question, and when the Producer is the one holding the setlist. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. A Fan has no action and no interest; PRO licensing is invisible infrastructure to the audience. |

## Behavior

Two linked facts, deliberately separated:

**Licence status** — which PRO(s), reference, current/expired/unknown. Sourced from 16.01.06
(statutory records) and, in some territories, publicly checkable against the PRO's own register
during seeding (16.05.01).

**Reporting posture** — does the venue report setlists, by what mechanism (venue-submitted /
artist-submitted / not reported), and for which kinds of night. A venue can be fully licensed and
report nothing. That combination is extremely common and is invisible to every artist who plays
there.

The feature's real output is a **prompt at the right moment**: after a confirmed gig at a licensed,
reporting room, the artist is asked for their setlist while they still remember it — the same
capture-at-source pattern as the domain's other harvest (16.05.06), applied to money.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — whether WeJammin *submits* setlists to PROs, or
merely captures and hands them to the artist, is unresolved and is the difference between a spec
field and an integration.

### Happy Path

1. Operator declares: PRS licensed, reference on file, reports setlists via artist submission
2. Musician books the room; the spec shows "licensed + reporting" before they agree
3. Gig happens; the reservation completes (16.06.03)
4. Musician is prompted post-gig: "You played a PRS-licensed room — submit your setlist" with the gig's date, venue and their own catalogue pre-filled
5. Setlist is captured while it is true; routed to 10 Royalties & Collections

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Venue is licensed but reports nothing | The common, invisible case this feature exposes | "Licensed, does not report setlists" — a fact artists have never been able to see |
| Venue is unlicensed | Structured fact. Playing there means no performance royalty, and it is also a licensing risk for the venue | "Not licensed" |
| Artist played covers only | The royalty flows to the writers of the covers, not the performer — still worth reporting, but the incentive to the performer is zero | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Status unknown (the default for every seeded record) | "Unknown" must be visible as unknown, not absent | Explicit unknown state |
| Artist submits a setlist for a gig that did not happen | Fraud vector against a royalty pool. Reservation completion (16.06.03) is the natural gate, but it does not cover off-platform gigs | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Territory has a different regime entirely | Same jurisdiction wall as 16.01.06 Q-01 | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unknown | Seeded or unstated | "PRO status unknown" — the honest default for most of the registry |
| Licensed | Operator declared / register-checked | Licensed badge + PRO name |
| Licensed + reporting | Both declared | "Licensed and reports setlists" — the state artists should filter for |
| Licensed, not reporting | Both declared | Visible fact; the gap becomes legible for the first time |
| Not licensed | Declared | Visible fact; feeds 16.01.05 at-risk if a licence review follows |
| Expired | Licence record expiry (16.01.06) | Expired, not silently current |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | PRO licence status is a compliance checkbox on the venue record | Candidate 21 framed it as "compliance status" | ❌ REJECTED, and the reframing is the point. Compliance is the venue's problem. The **artist's** problem is that their live performance royalty is never claimed — and licence status is only interesting because it is the precondition for the setlist being worth reporting. Framed as compliance, it is a dead field nobody fills. Framed as "this is how you get paid for your own gig", it is one of the strongest reasons in the domain for a Musician to care about a venue record. |
| DT-02 | Licensed implies reported | If the venue pays the licence, the performances must get reported | ❌ REJECTED — this is the load-bearing error the feature exists to correct. Licensed and reporting are **independent**. A licensed venue that reports nothing is common, and the resulting money is distributed by proxy to acts who are already large. No artist can currently see which rooms report. Separating the two fields makes an invisible, structural income leak legible. |
| DT-03 | This is domain 10's (Royalties) feature, not 16's | The money and the PRO relationship belong to 10 | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: 10 owns the royalty, the PRO relationship and the collection. But the **licence status is a property of the room**, decaying and community-correctable like every other venue fact, and the **capture moment** is anchored to a booking in this domain. The seam: 16 owns the venue-side fact and the post-gig prompt; 10 owns everything the setlist becomes. Recorded as a high-confidence cross-domain touchpoint. |
| DT-04 | Post-gig setlist capture is the same mechanism as post-gig spec harvesting (16.05.06) | Both are "ask the band after the show" | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is a strong argument for one post-gig moment, not two. A band will tolerate one prompt after a show; two prompts is one too many. The setlist ask and the spec-correction ask should share the same surface. Escalated to the domain CX file, because it changes 16.05.06's shape. |
| DT-05 | This is where 16 touches the thesis most directly | D-18: provenance is the wedge | ✅ CONFIRMED: this is capture-at-source applied to live performance. The setlist is a fact that is true for exactly one night and is reconstructed — if ever — months later from memory. It is the same failure the platform exists to prevent, in the one domain where the platform is provably present (there is a booking). |
