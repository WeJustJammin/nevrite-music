# Feature: Standing Deal Model & Commercial Terms

> **Number**: 16.02.05
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The venue's **published standing terms** — the deal shapes it works on (door split, guarantee,
guarantee-vs-percentage, hire / dry-hire, promoter's night), the typical percentages, what comes
off the top (in-house sound engineer, security, PRS levy, cleaning), and its merch commission.
This is **not a specific show's deal** — it is the commercial shape a band can read *before*
enquiring, so the conversation starts from a shared baseline instead of a fishing expedition.

The boundary here is one of the sharpest in the whole ideation: **16 publishes standing terms;
17 Live Booking negotiates the actual deal and settles it.** Get this seam wrong and you either
duplicate 17's contract model here or leave a band unable to learn a venue's basic commercial
shape without sending an email. The standing terms are a *property of the room* (like capacity),
readable and decaying; the negotiated deal is a *property of the show*.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Publishes their standing deal shapes and merch terms. Marks each field as indicative rather than fixed. Chooses disclosure posture per field — public / on-enquiry / not-disclosed. Disclosing nothing is a legitimate posture, not a gap to be shamed. Edits terms over time; the room shows the terms' age. |
| Musician | Read-only | Reads standing terms to filter rooms before spending an enquiry. Answers the single most-requested, least-available fact in live music: "what deal does this room do?" Filters a city's rooms by deal shape (e.g. door split, no hire fee). Cannot see per-field detail the Operator marked on-enquiry — only that it exists. |
| Producer | Read-only | Low relevance — venue commercial terms are a band/promoter concern, not a session concern. Reads them only when producing a live release where the venue takes a cut of recording or merch, so the split maths has to account for the room. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and must never be. A Fan seeing "the venue takes 20% of merch" reframes their purchase, seeds resentment, and helps nobody. There is no Fan-facing view of commercial terms anywhere in this feature. |

## Behavior

Structured, and deliberately **indicative**. An Operator publishes, per field, some subset of:

- **Deal shapes offered** — door split, guarantee, guarantee-vs-percentage (band takes the
  greater), dry hire, promoter's night. A room can offer several.
- **Typical split range** — e.g. 70/30 to the act after costs. A range, not a fixed number,
  because the real split depends on the show (billing, draw, night of week).
- **Off-the-top items** — what is deducted before the split: in-house sound, security, PRS levy,
  cleaning. This is where a "70/30" quietly becomes a 55/45.
- **Minimum guarantee or hire fee** — if the room publishes one; for a dry-hire room this points
  at the rate card (16.06.07) rather than duplicating it.
- **Merch commission** — the percentage (standardly 20–25%), whether it applies to physical only
  or to physical-plus-digital, and whether the band runs the merch table or the venue does.
- **Disclosure posture** — per field: public / on-enquiry / not-disclosed.

Merch terms carry their own emphasis. A 20–25% merch commission is standard, is frequently
discovered *at the merch table on the night*, and is one of the few venue terms that materially
changes whether a small band's show made money. Publishing it structurally is a genuinely
band-favourable act — which is precisely why some Operators will decline to.

Every term is framed as **indicative, not a quote** wherever it renders. The negotiated deal for
a specific show is created and settled in 17, which references these terms but is not bound by
them. The deal-shape taxonomy above is a first cut; the exact off-the-top items and typical
percentages are territory- and scale-dependent, and refining that taxonomy per region is
`[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]`.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is routing a tour and filters rooms in a city by deal shape (e.g. "door split, no
   hire fee").
2. Rooms with public standing terms appear with their indicative split inline; rooms whose terms
   are on-enquiry appear flagged as such rather than hidden.
3. Musician opens a room and reads: 70/30 after costs, in-house sound off the top, 20% merch,
   physical only, band runs the table.
4. Musician enquires already knowing the shape — the enquiry is about the date and the draw, not
   the basics.
5. The actual negotiated deal is created and settled in **17 Live Booking**, referencing but not
   bound by these standing terms.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator declines to publish any terms | "Not disclosed" is a first-class value, not a gap. No shaming, no completeness penalty. | "Terms on enquiry" on the room; no split figures shown |
| Musician filters by a deal shape a not-disclosed room might offer | The room is not excluded — undisclosed ≠ "doesn't do it". It appears in a separate "terms on enquiry" band below the rooms with matching public terms. | Room listed under "on enquiry — may match" |
| Merch commission discovered at the merch table on the night | The exact failure this feature exists to prevent. When published, it is surfaced upfront on the room and echoed at enquiry. | Merch % shown before enquiry, not at the door |
| Published term differs from the deal actually offered in 17 | Expected — terms are indicative. A one-off gap is normal; whether a *systematic* gap is measured as a trust signal is deferred (Q-03). | Indicative framing on the term; the 17 deal is authoritative |
| A published term is cited as leverage in a settlement dispute (17/24) | Indicative terms are **not a contract** and must never render as one. The authoritative deal is the one settled in 17. The liability framing itself is `[PENDING — /create-prd-security]` (Q-02). | "Indicative — not a quote" persists on every surface the term appears |
| Terms vary by night, billing, or how badly the room wants the act | The norm, not an exception (same wall as 16.02.04 DT-04, 16.02.03 DT-04). Terms are stored as ranges + off-the-top items, never as one fixed number. | Indicative ranges, not a single figure |
| Venue is dry-hire only (no revenue deal at all) | Deal shape = dry hire; the hire fee lives on the rate card (16.06.07) and this feature is nearly empty by design. | "Dry hire — see rate card", link to 16.06.07 |
| Terms are years old and never updated | Commercial terms decay; a 3-year-old split is misinformation. The room shows the terms' age rather than hiding it. | Terms with a visible "last updated" age badge |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Room's standing-terms panel is being fetched | Skeleton rows where the deal shapes and merch line will render |
| Empty | Operator has published no terms and set no posture | "Terms on enquiry" — the neutral default; treated as a valid state, never an "incomplete profile" nag |
| Not disclosed | Operator's explicit chosen posture (distinct from Empty: a deliberate silence) | "Terms on enquiry" with no completeness penalty; filters treat the room as "may match" |
| Published | Operator declared standing terms | Indicative terms (deal shapes, split range, off-the-top, merch) + persistent "indicative, not a quote" framing |
| Populated (partial) | Some fields public, others on-enquiry | Public fields shown inline; on-enquiry fields shown as "on enquiry" placeholders, not blanks |
| Stale | Terms not updated past the decay threshold | Terms rendered with a visible age badge; the room is flagged for the Operator to refresh |
| Error | Terms failed to load | "Couldn't load this room's terms" with a retry; the enquiry path stays open so a load failure never blocks contact |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The deal model belongs in 17 Live Booking with the rest of the money | 17 owns booking and settlement; deals are money | ❌ REJECTED, but narrowly. The **negotiated deal for a specific show** is 17's — absolutely. The venue's **standing terms** are a property of the room, like its capacity: readable before any enquiry exists, decaying, community-correctable. Without them here, a band cannot filter rooms by deal shape and must email every venue to learn what is effectively public information. The seam: 16 publishes, 17 negotiates and settles. |
| DT-02 | Publishing standing terms will be refused by Operators | Commercial terms are private; negotiating position is leverage | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — hence "not disclosed" as a first-class posture rather than a shaming gap. But the incentive runs the other way for rooms that want fill: publishing "we do 70/30, no hire fee" attracts the acts they want and filters out the ones they don't. The feature must not assume disclosure; it should **reward** it (better filtering visibility) without **punishing** silence. |
| DT-03 | Merch commission is a minor line item | It is one field among many commercial terms | ❌ REJECTED: for a small band it is often the difference between the show making money and not. It is standardly 20–25%, standardly discovered on the night at the merch table, and is one of the few venue terms with a direct, immediate, personal cost to the Musician persona. Publishing it structurally is the single most band-favourable field in this domain. |
| DT-04 | Standing terms are a contract | They are published, structured commercial terms | ❌ REJECTED, and this needs guarding in the UI, not only the data model. Indicative terms presented as firm invite a settlement dispute ("your page said 70/30"). They must be framed as indicative everywhere they render, and the authoritative deal must live in 17. This is a liability boundary, not a copy decision — flagged as Q-02. |
| DT-05 | An undisclosed room should be excluded from deal-shape filters | Filtering exists to narrow to matching rooms; a room with no published shape can't be confirmed to match | ❌ REJECTED: exclusion would punish the "not disclosed" posture DT-02 protects, hiding good rooms from touring bands and pushing the whole feature back toward the email-everyone status quo. Undisclosed rooms surface in a separate "on enquiry — may match" band beneath confirmed matches — visible, honestly labelled, never silently dropped. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | 16 publishes **standing terms**; 17 negotiates and settles the **actual deal** | Standing terms are a property of the room; the deal is a property of the show (DT-01) |
| D-02 | "Not disclosed" is a first-class posture, not a gap | Many Operators will not publish terms, and shaming them is not a product (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Merch commission is a headline field, not a line item | It is the venue term with the most direct cost to the Musician, and it is standardly discovered too late (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Terms are framed as indicative everywhere they appear | Published terms presented as firm are a settlement-dispute liability (DT-04) |
