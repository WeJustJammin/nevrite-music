# Feature: Standing Deal Model & Commercial Terms

> **Number**: 16.02.05
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The venue's **published standing terms** — the deal shapes it works on (door split, guarantee,
hire/dry-hire, promoter's night), the typical percentages, what comes off the top (sound engineer,
security, PRS), and its merch commission. Not a specific show's deal: the terms a band can read
*before* enquiring, so the conversation starts from a shared baseline instead of a fishing
expedition.

The boundary here is one of the sharpest in the whole ideation: **16 publishes standing terms; 17
Live Booking negotiates the actual deal and settles it.** Getting this seam wrong either duplicates
17's contract model here or leaves a band unable to learn a venue's basic commercial shape without
sending an email.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Publishes their standing deal shapes and merch terms. Can mark terms as indicative rather than fixed. Chooses how much to disclose publicly — many will disclose nothing, and that is a legitimate posture. |
| Musician | Read-only | Reads the standing terms to filter rooms before wasting an enquiry. The most requested and least available fact in live music: "what deal does this room do?" |
| Producer | Read-only | Low relevance — venue commercial terms are a band/promoter concern. Reads them when producing a live release where the venue takes a cut. |
| Fan | None | Not visible, and must never be. A Fan seeing "the venue takes 20% of merch" reframes their purchase and helps nobody. |

## Behavior

Structured, and deliberately **indicative**: deal shapes offered (door split / guarantee /
guarantee vs percentage / dry hire / promoter night), typical split range, what comes off the top
(in-house sound, security, PRS levy, cleaning), minimum guarantee or hire fee if published, merch
commission (percentage, whether it applies to physical only, whether the band sells or the venue
does), and a disclosure posture (public / on enquiry / not disclosed).

Merch terms deserve their own emphasis: a 20–25% merch commission is standard, is frequently
discovered at the merch table on the night, and is one of the few venue terms that materially
affects a small band's income from the show. Publishing it structurally is a genuinely
band-favourable act, which is also why some Operators will decline to.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — deal shape taxonomy is a first cut. The
percentages and off-the-top items vary by territory and by venue scale, and the sweep did not
supply a taxonomy.

### Happy Path

1. Musician is routing a tour and filters rooms in a city by deal shape (door split, no hire fee)
2. Rooms with public standing terms appear with their indicative split; rooms with "on enquiry" appear flagged
3. Musician reads: 70/30 after costs, in-house sound off the top, 20% merch, physical only
4. Musician enquires already knowing the shape — the enquiry is about the date, not the basics
5. The actual negotiated deal is created and settled in 17, referencing but not bound by these terms

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator declines to publish any terms | Legitimate posture — "not disclosed" is a valid value, not a gap to shame | "Terms on enquiry" |
| Published terms differ from the deal actually offered | Expected — terms are indicative. But a systematic gap between published and actual is a trust signal | `[PENDING — Step 5]` whether that gap is measured at all |
| Merch commission discovered at the merch table on the night | The failure this feature exists to prevent | Published upfront |
| Terms vary by night, by billing, or by how much the promoter wants the act | The norm. Same wall as 16.02.04 DT-04 and 16.02.03 DT-04 | Indicative ranges, not fixed values |
| Venue is a dry hire only (no deal at all) | Deal shape = dry hire; the rate card (16.06.07) carries the fee and this feature is nearly empty | Hire fee via rate card |
| A published term is used as leverage in a settlement dispute | `[PENDING]` — indicative terms are not a contract, and must not be presented as one. This is a real liability question | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not disclosed | Operator's chosen posture | "Terms on enquiry" — a valid state, not an incomplete one |
| Published | Operator declared standing terms | Indicative terms + "indicative, not a quote" framing |
| Stale | Decayed | Terms with visible age — commercial terms age badly and a 3-year-old split is misinformation |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The deal model belongs in 17 Live Booking with the rest of the money | 17 owns booking and settlement; deals are money | ❌ REJECTED, but narrowly. The **negotiated deal for a specific show** is 17's — absolutely. The venue's **standing terms** are a property of the room, like its capacity: readable before any enquiry exists, decaying, community-correctable. Without them here, a band cannot filter rooms by deal shape and must email every venue to learn what is effectively public information. The seam: 16 publishes, 17 negotiates and settles. |
| DT-02 | Publishing standing terms will be refused by Operators | Commercial terms are private and negotiating position is leverage | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — hence "not disclosed" as a first-class posture rather than a shaming gap. But the incentive runs the other way for the venues that want fill: publishing "we do 70/30, no hire fee" attracts the acts they want and filters out the ones they don't. The feature should not assume disclosure; it should reward it (better filtering visibility) without punishing silence. |
| DT-03 | Merch commission is a minor line item | It is one field among many commercial terms | ❌ REJECTED: for a small band it is often the difference between the show making money and not. It is standardly 20–25%, it is standardly discovered on the night at the merch table, and it is one of the few venue terms with a direct, immediate, personal cost to the Musician persona. Publishing it structurally is the most band-favourable single field in this domain. |
| DT-04 | Standing terms are a contract | They are published, structured commercial terms | ❌ REJECTED, and this needs guarding in the UI, not just the data model. Indicative terms presented as firm invite a settlement dispute ("your page said 70/30"). They must be framed as indicative and the actual deal must live in 17. This is a liability boundary, not a copy decision — flagged as Q-02. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | 16 publishes **standing terms**; 17 negotiates and settles the **actual deal** | Standing terms are a property of the room; the deal is a property of the show (DT-01) |
| D-02 | "Not disclosed" is a first-class posture, not a gap | Many Operators will not publish terms, and shaming them is not a product (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Merch commission is a headline field, not a line item | It is the venue term with the most direct cost to the Musician, and it is standardly discovered too late (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Terms are framed as indicative everywhere they appear | Published terms presented as firm are a settlement-dispute liability (DT-04) |
