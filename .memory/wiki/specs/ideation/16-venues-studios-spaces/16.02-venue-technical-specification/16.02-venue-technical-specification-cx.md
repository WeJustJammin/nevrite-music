## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Capacity & Configuration ↔ Stage, PA & Backline

**Relationship**: These two features describe one physical room from two angles and they are not
independent. A seated configuration puts the stage somewhere different from a standing one. A
full-width backline eats floor space and therefore capacity. A room configured for cabaret has
sightlines a standing room does not. The naive model treats capacity as a number and the stage as
a set of dimensions; reality couples them, and 16.02.01 Q-02 is exactly this coupling surfacing.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: declares configurations and the stage spec separately, and is the one who knows they interact — but has no field in which to say so.
- **Musician**: chooses a room on capacity and discovers the stage constraint at load-in.
- **Producer**: cares when the production footprint is large (live recording rig, big backline).
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: No field is written by both — but the *values* are dependent, which is worse: two independent fields that must agree and have no mechanism to.
2. **Trigger chain**: None implemented. A configuration change should arguably invalidate a stage footprint claim; currently nothing does.
3. **Permission intersection**: Both are Operator-config; the **occupancy ceiling** is the exception (not writable) — **where the regime profile declares that slot** (16.01.06 D-05, 16.02.01 D-16). Where it does not, there is no exception and every figure is an Operator claim.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: The real risk is silent inconsistency — capacity 350 standing with a stage spec that only holds at 300 with a full backline. Unresolved; see 16.02.01 Q-02.

### CX-03: Access & Logistics ↔ Stage, PA & Backline

**Relationship**: The sound limiter is a fact in the access/logistics group that **invalidates**
facts in the PA group. A room can honestly advertise a large, capable PA and a 95dB limiter, and
both facts are true, and the combination means a rock show cannot happen there. No existing tool
surfaces this because both facts live in prose in a PDF, if they exist at all. Structured, the
contradiction is computable — and it is one of the most useful things this domain could tell a
band.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the beneficiary — "this room has a great PA and a limiter that will cut you off" is the single most valuable warning in the spec.
- **Producer**: same, for live recording — a limiter trip mid-take is a lost take.
- **Operator**: may not want the juxtaposition surfaced; the PA is a selling point, the limiter is not.
- **Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — independent fields, both Operator-declared (the limiter statutory-derived only where the record's regime profile declares an instrument that imposes it — 16.02.03 D-01/D-13).
2. **Trigger chain**: Should a limiter value below a threshold annotate the PA spec? Proposed yes, unimplemented. This is a derived-insight opportunity, not a data dependency.
3. **Permission intersection**: The limiter may be statutory-derived and read-only — **only where the profile declares an instrument that imposes it** (16.02.03 D-13) — while the PA spec is freely editable — an Operator can upgrade the PA claim but cannot touch the limiter.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — but the *absence* of a limiter threshold (16.02.03: "present, threshold not stated") makes the contradiction undetectable, which is the common case.

### CX-04: Hospitality ↔ Standing Deal Model

**Relationship**: Guest list is filed under hospitality by every rider in the industry and is
settled as money by every venue in the industry. It comes off the top, it is comped ticket stock
(19's concern), and it is negotiated as part of the deal. 16.02.04 DT-03 flagged this rather than
silently resolving it, and this CX entry is where the tension is recorded.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: experiences it as hospitality ("how many can I put on the list?") and pays for it as money (it comes off settlement).
- **Operator**: models it as both, because it is both.
- **Fan**: invisible — but a guest-list place is a ticket that 19 did not sell.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Currently one field in 16.02.04 with a commercial consequence in 16.02.05 and a stock consequence in 19. One fact, three owners — unresolved and flagged as 16.02.04 Q-01.
2. **Trigger chain**: Guest list allocation → ticket stock reduction (19) → settlement line (17). None modelled.
3. **Permission intersection**: The Operator sets the allowance; the act consumes it; 19 must not oversell against it.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: Real — guest list places sold as tickets is a double-allocation of the same seat. It is 19's bug but it originates in this field's ambiguity.

### CX-05: PRO Licence ↔ Standing Deal Model

**Relationship**: The **performing-rights levy** appears in 16.02.05's off-the-top list. *Which*
body collects it is the performing-rights licence status slot, declared by the record's regime
profile (16.01.06 D-05/D-06), and the US profile's names are deferred (16.01.06 Q-04) — the
relationship below holds under every profile because it is about the levy, not the body. The venue pays a blanket
licence; the levy is standardly deducted from the door before the split. So the act pays — via the
settlement — for a licence whose royalties they may never receive because the venue does not report
setlists (16.02.06 DT-02). Recorded at Medium confidence because the settlement mechanics belong to
17, but the observation is sharp enough to be worth carrying forward.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: pays into the pool through the settlement and is not paid out of it. This is the concrete shape of the income leak 16.02.06 exists to expose.
- **Operator**: holds the licence and controls whether reporting happens.
- **Fan / Producer**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the settlement
mechanics are 17's and the royalty flow is 10's. What this domain can assert is the venue-side
fact; whether the platform should surface "you are paying a levy into a pool this room does not
report to" is a product decision with real teeth. Escalated to the domain index.

### CX-06: Capacity & Configuration ↔ Standing Deal Model

**Relationship**: Every door-split deal has a ceiling: capacity × ticket price. A band evaluating
"70/30 after costs" cannot evaluate it without the capacity, and the two facts are published in
different features. Together they answer the only question a band actually has — "what can I earn
here on a good night?" — which no venue directory currently answers.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: the primary consumer; this pair is the tour-routing decision.
- **Operator**: publishing both makes their room comparable, which helps the rooms that offer good deals and exposes the ones that don't.
- **Producer / Fan**: no visibility.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — independent fields with a derived insight.
2. **Trigger chain**: None. The derived "potential gross" is a read-time computation, not stored.
3. **Permission intersection**: Capacity has a statutory ceiling the Operator cannot raise; terms are freely publishable. An Operator cannot inflate the earning potential by editing capacity — which is precisely why 16.02.01 D-02 matters commercially, not just legally.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.
