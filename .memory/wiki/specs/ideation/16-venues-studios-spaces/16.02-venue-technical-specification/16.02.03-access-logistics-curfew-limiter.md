# Feature: Access & Logistics (Load-In, Parking, Curfew, Sound Limiter)

> **Number**: 16.02.03
> **Parent**: [Venue Technical Specification](./16.02-venue-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23
> **MoSCoW**: Must (D-20 — all 195 Musts ratified)

## Overview

The physical and temporal facts of getting a show into a room and out again: the load-in route (door
aperture, threshold lip, steps, lift, carry distance), parking and unloading restrictions, get-in and
soundcheck windows, the several distinct times the industry collectively calls "curfew", and the
**sound limiter** — the device that cuts stage power when the room gets too loud, and that ends more
UK shows than any other single piece of equipment.

These are the facts a tour manager needs and cannot get. They exist today as prose in a PDF, or in the
head of a house engineer, and they are re-asked by email for every show. Several of them may not be the
Operator's opinion at all: curfew and decibel limits are **statutory conditions wherever the record's
regime profile declares an instrument that imposes them** (16.01.06 D-05) — externally imposed, and
not the Operator's to waive on request. **The US launch profile declares none**, so at launch these
are the Operator's own claims, labelled as claims (D-01, D-13).

**Why this feature earns its Must**: it is the only place in the domain where a wrong fact costs money
*before* anyone plays a note. A band that accepts a fee without knowing about a 40-metre carry has
mispriced the show. A band that accepts a fee without knowing about a 95 dB limiter has agreed to a
show that cannot happen. Both discoveries currently land at 4pm on the day, after the travel is spent.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Operator** | ⚙️ Config | Declares the route, the vehicle restrictions, the standing time windows, and the limiter. **Cannot write the statutory-derived fields** (music-off curfew, dB threshold) **where the record's regime profile declares an instrument that imposes them** — those are read-only here and edited via the statutory record (16.01.06 D-02/D-05). **Where it declares none — as the US launch profile does not — nothing is derived, the same numbers are the Operator's own claims labelled as claims (D-01, D-13), and the Operator writes them here.** **Can** add date-range exceptions (the lift is out until 12 Aug; and — **where the record's regime profile declares a temporary-permission instrument** (16.01.06 D-05) — a temporary permission extends the operating-hours limit for one date, D-13) and day/time-of-day exceptions (curfew 23:00 midweek, 00:00 Fri–Sat). Must answer the limiter tri-state (D-06) before the access group reads as complete. Sees which unstated fields produce `unknown` in conformance checks and therefore lose enquiries (16.07 D-06). |
| **Musician** | 👁️ Read-only + suggest | Reads load-in, parking and the resolved times **before agreeing a fee** — a 40 m carry up two flights changes whether the show is worth doing, and the load-out curfew decides whether they can drive to the next city tonight or sleep in the van. Acting as tour manager, reads this instead of writing an advance email (18). **Post-gig, the highest-authority corrector of the carry, the steps and the limiter's real behaviour** (16.05.06) — they carried the gear and they got cut off. |
| **Producer** | 👁️ Read-only + suggest | Relevant when recording live or bringing a mobile rig: where the truck goes, is there power and of what kind, how long is the get-in for a rig (longer than a band's), and — the one that costs a session — **whether a limiter trip mid-take destroys a take that cannot be re-performed**. Their attestation on power and limiter behaviour outweighs a self-claim (`personas.md`: trust broker). |
| **Fan** | 👁️ Read-only (partial) | Sees only the audience slice: doors time, "show ends by" derived from the **effective** music-off curfew (D-09 — the earliest binding one, not the statutory one), and public parking/transport. **Never** sees the load-in, the limiter, the get-in, or the load-out curfew. Reason: a Fan cannot act on a limiter threshold and cannot act on a load-in route; surfacing either is professional-tool noise on a show page (`personas.md`: the Fan "will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"). |

## Behavior

Four structured groups on the **room** (16.01.02 D-01 — the room is the spec-carrying unit), each
field independently statable, independently provenance-tagged, and independently decayed (16.05.05
D-01).

### 1. Load-in route

Door aperture (W × H, at its **narrowest point**, not the doorframe), threshold lip height, step count
and direction (up/down), the **turn** (a 2 m door at the end of a 1 m corridor with a 90° bend will not
take a 4×12 or a Hammond — the corridor is the door), lift presence with **internal W × D × H, door
aperture, and weight limit** (a lift that will not take a bass cab is not a lift), carry distance in
metres from the unloading point, whether the carry is level, dock vs street pull-up, and **whether the
route runs through the public/front-of-house room** — which is a scheduling fact wearing a route
costume (see group 3).

### 2. Parking / vehicle

Unloading restriction type and its controlled hours (loading bay with a time limit / red route with no
stopping / residents' bay / dock), permit or bay-suspension requirement, **vehicle height limit** (a
2.0 m barrier excludes most of the touring fleet's high-top vans), vehicle length/weight limit,
**emission or congestion zone** the room sits inside, overnight options, and whether overnight parking
is secured (yard/gated) or street.

### 3. Times — standing defaults only

Get-in window, soundcheck window, doors, and the curfew set (below). **These are room-level standing
defaults, not the times for your show** (D-08). The actual get-in for a specific date is a function of
what is booked before you (a matinee, a daytime hire, a wedding) and of whether staff with keys are on
site; that is a show property owned by 17/18 and resolved at booking/advance. This mirrors the domain's
sharpest seam: 16 publishes standing terms, 17 negotiates the deal (domain D-11).

### 4. Curfew — three named times, not one

"Curfew" is not one field (D-09). At minimum:

| Time | What it means | Possible provenance |
|---|---|---|
| **Music off** | Amplified music must stop | Statutory condition **where the profile declares an operating-hours instrument** — *or* Operator commercial (a club night takes the room) — *or*, in a profile that declares none, an Operator claim labelled as a claim (D-01, D-13) |
| **Premises close** | Everyone out of the building | Statutory where declared; otherwise an Operator claim labelled as a claim |
| **Load-out / vehicle movement** | No loading, no vehicle movements after this time | Statutory where declared, common in residential areas; otherwise an Operator claim labelled as a claim |

Each carries its own provenance independently, and **which provenances are even available is
capability-gated** (16.01.06 D-05): the three times exist in every profile, the statutory class does
not. A room can have a statutory premises-close of 02:00 and
an Operator-set **commercial** music-off of 22:30 because the DJ night starts at 23:00 — the commercial
one is not statutory and is just as binding in practice. The **effective** music-off is the earliest
binding value across all sources, and it is what the Fan's "show ends by" derives from.

The **load-out curfew is the one nobody designs for** (DT-08) and it strands bands: a 23:00 music-off
with a 23:00 vehicle-movement condition means the gear stays in the room until 07:00 and the driver
does not leave tonight. That is a tour-routing fact (18) that currently exists nowhere.

### 5. Sound limiter — four attributes, not a number

A limiter threshold is not one fact. "95 dB" is four facts collapsed into one number, and the collapse
is exactly why the number is useless today (DT-06, D-07). Each is separately statable and separately
`not stated`:

| Attribute | Values | Why it changes the answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Presence** | none · present · not stated (**forced choice**, D-06) | Silence is read as "no limiter" — absence is not neutral here |
| **Threshold** | value in dB | The headline number |
| **Weighting** | dB(A) · dB(C) · dual-band · not stated | dB(C) captures low frequency. A band with a kick and a bass trips a C-weighted limiter at a level where an A-weighted one is comfortable. Same number, different room. |
| **Measurement point** | FOH · fixed room mic · stage · boundary / noise-sensitive receptor · not stated | Decides **who controls the reading**. At the boundary, crowd noise counts and the band cannot control it at all. |
| **Averaging** | instantaneous/peak · Leq over a stated window · not stated | A peak limiter punishes a snare hit. An Leq window permits dynamics. Wholly different shows. |
| **Trip behaviour** | warn (lights only) · cut stage power · trip the breaker | Whether a trip ends the song or the night |
| **Reset** | auto after a stated delay · manual by duty manager · requires a key held by a named person | **This is what decides whether a trip ends the show.** A key-held reset at 22:45 on a Saturday is a cancelled set. |
| **Source** | statutory condition (available **only where the profile declares an instrument that imposes it** — D-01, D-13) · planning condition · landlord/freeholder device · Operator | Decides whether it is negotiable and by whom |

Presence and threshold remain separate fields (D-04) because presence is common knowledge and the
threshold usually is not — "limiter present, threshold not stated" is the single most common true
statement about a UK room, and the model must be able to say it.

### 6. Conditionality — base value + typed exceptions

Resolves the breadth pass's Q-02 and answers the sub-domain's Q-01 / domain Q-03 from this feature's
side (D-05).

The spec is **not a set of constants** — but neither is the variance arbitrary, and that is the finding
that makes this tractable. Every varying case observed in this feature reduces to one of **exactly
three** condition types:

| Condition type | Real case |
|---|---|
| **Day-of-week** | Curfew 23:00 Sun–Thu, 00:00 Fri–Sat |
| **Time-of-day window** | The loading bay is a red route until 10:00; the lift is unusable after the bar staff leave at 23:00 |
| **Date range** | The lift is out until 12 Aug; roadworks close the loading bay for six weeks; a temporary permission extends the operating-hours limit for one date **where the record's regime profile declares such an instrument** (D-13) |

So: **every field carries one base value plus an optional, ordered list of typed exceptions drawn from
a closed vocabulary of three condition types.** No general rule engine. No free-text caveat.

**The date-range exception type is regime-independent; its *statutory* variant is not** (D-13). A
temporary permission is a date-range exception carrying statutory provenance — which requires a
statutory condition to vary and an instrument that varies it. Where the profile declares neither,
there is **no statutory temporary permission**, because a statutory exception with no statutory
source is a contradiction. The date-range exception itself survives unchanged under D-05; only its
provenance class changes, to the Operator claim D-01 already governs.

Why this shape and not the alternatives:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| One value per field, most restrictive | ❌ Forces the Operator to publish their worst night. Loses them the bookings the good nights would win, and lies about Saturday. |
| One value per field, most permissive | ❌ Causes the failure this feature exists to prevent. |
| Free-text caveat | ❌ Honest and **uncheckable** — kills 16.07, which is the domain's headline capability and the only reason the spec is structured at all (16.07 D-02). |
| General conditionality model | ❌ Unfillable. The Operator is "on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk" (`personas.md`) and will not build a schedule matrix. 16.02.02 DT-02's wall, rebuilt higher. |
| **Base + typed exceptions, closed vocabulary** | ✅ The Operator who does not vary pays nothing — one value, one tap, exceptions never surfaced. The Operator who does vary adds two rows. And it stays **machine-resolvable**: given a show's date and time, the rule evaluates to a single value, which is exactly what 16.07 needs to compare. |

**The consequence that matters beyond this feature**: 16.05.04 DT-05 found that a meaningful share of
"conflicts" are conditionality failures — both parties told the truth and the model was too rigid to
hold it. A band contesting "curfew is midnight" against an Operator's "23:00" is not a conflict if the
band played a Saturday. Under base + exceptions, **that contest never opens.** Fixing conditionality
removes conflicts rather than resolving them, and that is cheaper than any policy.

### 7. The measurement is one fact with two lenses

Resolves the breadth pass's Q-01 (D-03).

The load-in facts and 16.01.04's performer accessibility route are **the same staircase**. DT-03 flagged
the real defect: two fields describing one physical object can silently disagree, and then decay
independently, and then get contested independently. That is a bug, not an overlap.

Resolution: **the physical measurements are recorded once** — step count, step direction, threshold lip
height, door aperture, carry distance, level/not-level, lift internal dimensions and weight limit — as
room-level physical facts with **one provenance, one verification timestamp, one decay clock**.
16.02.03 and 16.01.04 are two **read lenses** over that one set, asking different questions and deriving
different answers:

| Lens | Question | Derives from |
|---|---|---|
| 16.02.03 load-in | *Can the gear get in?* | Door aperture at its narrowest, the turn, lift internal size + weight limit, carry distance, dock vs street |
| 16.01.04 performer route | *Can **I** get in?* | Step count, lip height, ramp presence + gradient, width, level/not-level |

The answers diverge legitimately — a 30 m level carry is a nuisance to one band and a hard blocker to a
wheelchair user — because the *derivations* differ, not because the *facts* differ. Neither feature owns
the other; either may prompt for the measurement, and both write the same field.

**The consequence** (D-11): sharing the fact means sharing the conflict policy. 16.05.04 D-02 sets policy
per field class, and this is now one field class — so the step count inherits accessibility's
**first-hand-wins** exception (16.05.04 D-03). This is correct, not accidental: the rationale for
first-hand-wins there was that the Operator has both an incentive *and* a blind spot (they can walk).
That reasoning holds identically for the load-in — the Operator walks past those three steps daily and
stops seeing them; the band carrying a Hammond up them sees nothing else.

### 8. Capture reality

The Operator is best placed to record the load-in while **standing in it** — which is a basement, a
back alley, or a service corridor, i.e. exactly where a phone has no signal. Capture must not lose the
Operator's input when connectivity drops mid-group and must not require them to start again. Field
groups complete independently (16.02.02 D-02); a photo can evidence any measurement (16.01.03).
Mechanism is `/create-prd`'s; the requirement is behavioural and non-negotiable — a capture flow that
fails in a basement will not capture load-ins.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims The Bunker. The access group is empty; the room does not appear in logistics-filtered search and returns `unknown` on every conformance check (16.07).
2. Operator opens the load-in group on their phone, standing at the load-in door. Records: street pull-up, door 1.4 m × 2.0 m at narrowest, 3 steps down, lip 40 mm, no lift, 30 m carry, not level, route runs through the FOH bar.
3. Operator photographs the steps; the photo tags to the measurement as evidence (16.01.03). **The same measurement now populates 16.01.04's performer route** — recorded once, read twice (D-03).
4. Operator opens the parking group: loading bay, 20-minute limit, controlled 08:00–18:00, height barrier 2.0 m on the adjacent car park, room sits inside a low-emission zone, no secure overnight.
5. Operator opens the limiter group. Presence is a **forced choice** — they cannot mark the group complete without answering (D-06). They select `present`, threshold 95, weighting `not stated`, measurement point `fixed room mic`, averaging `not stated`, trip behaviour `cut stage power`, reset `manual by duty manager`, source `statutory condition`.
6. What that means is decided by the record's regime profile. **Where it declares an instrument that imposes the limit**, the system rejects the Operator as the writer — the threshold derives from the statutory record (16.01.06 D-02) and renders read-only with a statutory badge. **Where it declares none — as the US launch profile does not (D-01, D-13)** — there is nothing to derive from: the source resolves to the Operator's own claim, the threshold stays theirs to write, and it renders labelled as a claim. Either way the Operator's other five limiter attributes stand as their own claims.
7. Operator adds one exception to music-off: base 23:00, exception `day-of-week: Fri–Sat → 00:00`. Two rows. Total interaction: one dropdown, one time.
8. A tour manager (a Musician) is offered a Saturday. They open the room. Times **resolve against the show's actual date**: music-off 00:00 (the Fri–Sat exception fired), premises-close 00:30, **load-out curfew 23:00 — before the music stops.**
9. The load-out curfew is the fact that decides the night: the gear cannot leave until 07:00. The TM prices an extra night's accommodation into the fee, or declines. **Either way, they knew in February.**
10. The limiter card reads: *"Limiter present — 95 dB, carrying whichever provenance step 6 resolved. Weighting not stated. Measured at a fixed room mic. Reset is manual by the duty manager."* The TM runs a conformance check (16.07) against the band's rider (18): the limiter returns **`unknown`, not `match`** — 95 dB(A) and 95 dB(C) are different shows and the room has not said which (D-07).
11. TM raises exactly one question at advance instead of writing a nine-question email: *"is the 95 A or C weighted?"* The Operator asks their engineer and answers dB(A). The field is filled; the next band never asks.
12. Post-gig, the band gets one prompt (16.05.06 D-04): *"Load-in listed as 3 steps and a 30 m carry. Right?"* → No → *"How far?"* → 45 m. One contest opens on the shared measurement — and under the shared-fact policy it is **first-hand-wins** (D-11), so the carry corrects and 16.01.04's performer route corrects with it, from one answer.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

#### Load-in & parking

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Door aperture stated from the doorframe; the real constraint is a 90° turn in a 1 m corridor | The **turn** is a required field precisely because the door is not the constraint. Aperture alone is stated at its narrowest point, and a stated door with an unstated turn returns `unknown` for anything long | "Door 1.4 m — turn not stated" |
| Lift exists; internal size, aperture and weight limit unstated | `Lift: present` is not an answer. A lift that will not take a bass cab is not a lift (D-04's logic applied to lifts). Presence without dimensions returns `unknown`, never `match` | "Lift present — dimensions and weight limit not stated" |
| Route runs through the front-of-house room, which is trading | Route fact with a **scheduling consequence**: load-in must complete before doors and load-out cannot start until the room clears. Surfaced against the get-in window, not buried in the route | "Load-in is through the public bar — get-in must complete before doors (19:00)" |
| Van exceeds the car park's 2.0 m height barrier | Stated as a fact, not a verdict. The platform publishes the barrier height; it does not model the band's van (D-12) | "Adjacent car park: 2.0 m height barrier" |
| Room sits inside a low-emission or congestion zone | Published as a **cost** fact in the parking group, not a footnote. On a sub-£200 fee a daily charge is a material share of the show's economics | "Low-emission zone — daily charge may apply to older vehicles" |
| Gear left in a van overnight; no secure parking | `Overnight: street, not secured` is a first-class value, because it is a risk fact, not a convenience one. **`Not stated` must not read as `secure`** — the same asymmetry as the limiter (D-06's logic) | "Overnight parking: street, not secured" |
| Loading bay suspended by roadworks for six weeks | **Date-range exception on the field** (D-05, D-10) — not a place-status change, not a permanent spec edit, not an at-risk signal | "Loading bay unavailable until 30 Aug — nearest alternative not stated" |
| Carry distance stated; whether it is level is not | Two facts. A 30 m level carry and a 30 m carry with a flight of stairs are different shows. Level/not-level is required alongside the distance | "30 m carry — level not stated" |
| Route measured for a band; a Producer arrives with a mobile recording rig | Same facts, different sufficiency. The spec publishes measurements; it does not certify them adequate for an unmodelled load (D-12) | Measurements, no verdict |

#### Times & curfew

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Curfew varies by night (23:00 midweek, 00:00 Fri–Sat) | **Resolved** — day-of-week exception (D-05). Resolves against the show's actual date; the TM never sees a rule, only their night's value | "Music off 00:00 (Sat)" |
| Loading bay is a red route until 10:00 | **Resolved** — time-of-day exception (D-05) | "No stopping before 10:00; loading bay 10:00–18:00, 20 min limit" |
| A promoter (a **Musician** or **Operator** in a booking role — not a fifth persona) wants to negotiate a statutory curfew | Read-only with statutory provenance. **But not "no at any price"** (DT-07) — **where the record's regime profile declares a temporary-permission instrument** (16.01.06 D-05), it can extend the condition for one date: scarce, needs lead time, the Operator's to spend | "Music off 23:00 — statutory condition. Extensions are possible only via a temporary permission the venue applies for. Ask early." |
| A temporary permission is granted for one date | It **is** a date-range exception (D-05) carrying **statutory** provenance — the shape already exists. Elegant, and not a coincidence: a temporary permission is literally a time-boxed condition change | "Music off 00:30 on 14 Sep (temporary permission)" |
| **The profile declares no statutory operating-hours condition at all** (the US profile at launch) | Two consequences, both already authored: the curfew set is **Operator claims labelled as claims** (D-01), and there is **no statutory temporary permission** to model, because there is no statutory condition to vary (D-13). The Operator may still publish a date-range exception to their own stated curfew — it is a claim varying a claim, which the closed vocabulary already permits and 16.07 can still resolve to a single value | "Music off 23:00 — stated by the venue, not verified against a statutory record" · "Music off 00:30 on 14 Sep (venue)" |
| **A municipal ordinance really does bind a US venue's hours, and the platform cannot represent it as statutory** | **Accepted gap.** No instrument is named anywhere in the tree for it, and inventing one would commission a per-municipality permission model and reopen Q-03 in the launch market. The fact is publishable as an Operator claim and is *labelled* as one, which understates its force. Recorded rather than papered over | The ordinance's effect appears as a venue-stated curfew, with no statutory badge |
| Room has a 02:00 statutory operating-hours limit but a hard 22:30 music-off because a club night takes the room | **Two sources, one field.** The commercial curfew is Operator-provenance and non-statutory, and is just as binding. Effective music-off = earliest binding value (D-09). The statutory half of this case only arises **where the profile declares an operating-hours instrument** (D-01, 16.01.06 D-05); where it does not, both values are Operator claims and the same earliest-binding rule resolves them | "Music off 22:30 (venue — club night from 23:00). Statutory limit 02:00." |
| Music-off 23:00, load-out curfew 23:00 | The failure nobody designs for. The gear is legally trapped until 07:00. **Surfaced against the load-out group and pushed to 18's routing**, not buried in a times list | "⚠️ No vehicle movements after 23:00 — load-out cannot start tonight" |
| Get-in listed 16:00; a matinee overruns | Get-in here is a **standing default** (D-08). The actual get-in is a show property (17/18), fixed at booking against the room's calendar (16.06.01) | "Get-in usually 16:00 — confirmed per booking" |
| Get-in listed 16:00 because that is when someone with keys arrives | Same field, different reason, and the distinction is worth nothing to the reader — the time is the time. **Rejected** as a field: reason-for-get-in is a note nobody will fill and nobody will read | Time only |
| Support act overruns; the headliner's set is cut by a hard curfew | Out of scope here (D-08). This feature publishes the constraint; who eats the cut set is a settlement question (17) and a running-order question (18) | — |

#### Sound limiter

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Limiter present, threshold unknown (**the most common true statement about a UK room**) | Presence and threshold are separate fields (D-04) so the model can say it | "Limiter present — threshold not stated" |
| Threshold stated, weighting not | **Not a fact** (D-07). 95 dB(A) and 95 dB(C) are different rooms. 16.07 returns `unknown`, never `match` | "95 dB — weighting not stated (ask: A or C?)" |
| Threshold and weighting stated; measurement point not | Still `unknown` for a band that can control FOH but not the crowd. The measurement point decides **who controls the reading** | "95 dB(A) — measurement point not stated" |
| Measured at the boundary / nearest noise-sensitive receptor | The band cannot control the reading **at all** — crowd noise counts. This is the value that most changes the answer and it is the one least often stated | "Measured at the site boundary — audience noise counts toward the limit" |
| Limiter not stated at all | **The asymmetry** (DT-05, D-06). For every other spec field, absence is neutral and renders as absence (16.01.04 D-03 — correct there). For a limiter, the reader's prior is **"no limiter"**, so a blank actively misleads. Presence is a forced tri-state and `not stated` renders with hazard weight, never as a gap | "⚠️ Limiter: not stated — this room has not said whether it has one" |
| Room soundchecks fine at 16:00 and trips at 21:30 with a full house | **The limiter measures the room, not the band** (DT-06). A sold-out crowd raises the noise floor, so limiter headroom is a function of **occupancy/configuration** (16.02.01) — the same threshold is a different constraint at 120 and at 350. Nobody models this and it is the classic mid-set trip | "Headroom is lower at capacity — audience noise counts at this measurement point" |
| Room has a large, capable PA **and** a 95 dB limiter | Both facts are true and the combination means a rock show cannot happen (CX-03). Structured, the contradiction is computable — and it is one of the most useful things this domain can say | "Large PA, 95 dB(A) limiter — this room will not run a loud show at volume" |
| Reset requires a key held by a duty manager who has gone home | **This is what decides whether a trip ends the song or the night**, and it is nowhere in the industry's data. Reset is a required attribute of the limiter group | "Reset: manual, duty manager" |
| Limiter is a landlord's or planning-condition device, not statutorily imposed | Provenance differs (Operator/landlord claim vs statutory); the practical effect is identical. Source is a stated attribute, and it decides **who** a promoter would even be negotiating with | "95 dB(A) — landlord's device, not a statutory condition" |
| Trip behaviour is warn-only (lights, no cut) | Materially different feature of the room and a legitimate "safe" value — a warn-only limiter is a nuisance, not a show-stopper | "Warning lights only — no power cut" |
| Producer's take is destroyed by a trip mid-session | The Producer-specific cost the venue lens misses: a cut show resumes, a cut take is gone. Surfaced in the Producer's read of the limiter group, not just the Musician's | "Reset: manual — a trip will end the take" |

#### Provenance, permissions & conflict

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator edits a **statutory-derived** curfew (only reachable **where the profile declares an operating-hours instrument**) | Rejected at write. The field is not theirs (16.01.06 D-02/D-05, sub-domain D-04). The edit path is the statutory record, and it is a different action with a different meaning. **Where the profile declares none, this rejection has no input**: the curfew is the Operator's own claim (D-01, D-13) and they edit it here | "Curfew is a statutory condition — edit it on the venue's statutory record" |
| Operator disputes a band's contested carry distance | The shared measurement is **first-hand-wins** (D-11) — inherited from accessibility (16.05.04 D-03), because the Operator has both the incentive and the blind spot | Corrected value + dispute recorded and visible |
| Community contests a statutory curfew | **Not contestable by anyone** (16.05.04 D-04). A statutory condition is neither the Operator's opinion nor the community's. **This row is reachable only where the profile declares an instrument that imposes the curfew**; where it declares none the curfew is an Operator claim and the ordinary contest rules apply (D-01, D-13) | "Statutory conditions cannot be contested here" |
| No statutory record exists (the common case at cold start), **or the profile declares no such instrument** | Nothing to derive from, from either cause. Curfew and dB threshold fall back to **Operator claims, explicitly labelled as claims** — never silently promoted to statutory (D-01, D-13) | "Curfew 23:00 — stated by the venue, not verified against a statutory record" |
| Operator omits the limiter to win bookings | The anti-persona (`personas.md`: "misrepresents the room's specs to win bookings"), and precisely what D-06 exists to defeat: omission is no longer a neutral act, because `not stated` is loud. A pattern of omitted-then-contested limiters is a trust signal (16.05.04 Q-03) → 24 | "⚠️ Limiter: not stated" |

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Curfew is an Operator preference like any other spec field | It reads like an operating choice and sits among operating choices | ❌ REJECTED **wherever the record's regime profile declares an instrument that imposes them**: they are then **statutory conditions** — imposed by an authority, legally binding, not the Operator's to waive. Modelling them as preferences invites a promoter to negotiate something that is not available on request, and lets an Operator "agree" to a curfew extension they cannot lawfully grant. Provenance is the feature. *Refined by DT-07 — the absolutism was overstated.* **⚠️ Further refined (owner ratification, DQ-14.1/14.4 → D-01, D-13):** whether such an instrument exists at all is profile-declared, and the US launch profile declares none — there these fields are Operator claims labelled as claims, which is the branch D-01 already authored. |
| DT-02 | The sound limiter is a minor spec detail | It is one field among dozens | ❌ REJECTED: it is one of the highest-consequence facts about a UK room. A limiter set at 95 dB will not run a rock show — the power cuts mid-set. Bands discover this at soundcheck, having already loaded in and travelled. It deserves its own structured group, not a line in a notes field. *Extended by DT-06 — the group needed is bigger than presence/threshold/behaviour.* |
| DT-03 | Load-in facts duplicate the performer accessibility profile (16.01.04) | Both describe steps, thresholds, door widths and distances | ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — a real overlap, and the flagged defect was correct: two fields describing one staircase can silently disagree, decay independently, and be contested independently. **Resolved in D-03**: one measured fact, two derivations. |
| DT-04 | One value per field is sufficient | Simplest model | ❌ REJECTED: curfew varies by night, loading bays vary by hour, get-in varies by whether there is a matinee. A single-value model forces the Operator to state the most restrictive case (losing bookings) or the most permissive (causing failures). **Resolved in D-05**: base + typed exceptions. |
| DT-05 | An unstated limiter is safe, like every other unstated field | 16.01.04 D-03 establishes that absence renders as absence and must never render as "no" — a settled, correct domain principle | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the sharpest new finding here.** The principle is right and its application to the limiter is wrong, because it assumes the reader's prior is neutral. For accessibility, users assume nothing and absence is genuinely informative. **For a limiter, every band on earth assumes there isn't one unless told.** A blank does not render as absence; it renders as "no limiter", which is a claim the venue never made and the platform just implied. Absence is only neutral where the default assumption is neutral. Hence the forced tri-state (D-06): the Operator must positively say `none`, and `not stated` must be loud. |
| DT-06 | The limiter measures the band | It exists to stop the band being too loud, and it is the band that gets cut off | ❌ REJECTED, twice over. **(a) It measures the room** — crowd noise, an audience singalong, the extractor fan all count toward the reading. So limiter headroom is a function of **occupancy** (16.02.01): the same 95 dB is a different constraint at 120 and at 350, and this is the mechanical explanation for the industry's most familiar mystery — *"we soundchecked fine and tripped at 21:30."* Nobody models it. **(b) The threshold is not one fact but four**: value, weighting (dB(A) vs dB(C) — a kick and a bass trip a C-weighted limiter where an A-weighted one is comfortable), measurement point (who controls the reading — at the boundary, nobody), and averaging (peak punishes a snare hit; Leq permits dynamics). Collapsing four facts into "95 dB" is exactly why the number is useless today, and un-collapsing it is free. |
| DT-07 | A statutory curfew is non-negotiable at any price | DT-01's conclusion, stated absolutely | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — DT-01 is right in spirit and **factually overstated**, and the platform must not repeat the overstatement to a promoter. It is not the Operator's to waive *unilaterally*, but a profile-declared temporary-permission instrument, where one exists, extends it for a single date. It is **scarce** (a capped annual allowance), needs lead time, and is the Operator's to spend. So the honest field is not "23:00, no" but "23:00 standing; extensions only via a temporary permission, ask early". This refines D-01 without contradicting it: the Operator still cannot *edit* the field — a temporary permission changes the underlying licence for one date. **And it needs no new machinery: such a permission is a date-range exception (D-05) carrying statutory provenance.** The convergence is a good sign the conditionality shape is the right one. Exact mechanics are jurisdiction-bound (domain Q-13). **⚠️ Refined (owner ratification, DQ-14.4 → D-13):** the *shape* is confirmed and the *instrument* is now profile-declared. Where a profile declares no temporary-permission instrument — as the US launch profile does not — the statutory variant of the date-range exception simply does not exist, and this annotation's original UK example retires with the UK vocabulary into an unauthored profile. |
| DT-08 | "Curfew" is one time | It is one word, and the industry uses it as one | ❌ REJECTED: at minimum three distinct times get called curfew — **music off**, **premises close**, and **load-out / vehicle movement** — and they carry different provenances and different consequences. Plus a fourth source: a **commercial** curfew (the club night takes the room at 22:30 in a room licensed to 02:00), which is Operator-provenance, non-statutory, and just as binding in practice. **The load-out curfew is the failure mode nobody designs for**: finish at 23:00, no vehicle movements after 23:00, gear trapped until 07:00, band not driving to the next city tonight. It appears in no directory, no tech spec, and no advance email — and it is a tour-routing fact (18) worth an unbudgeted night's accommodation. |
| DT-09 | The get-in time is a property of the room | It sits in the room's spec with the other times | ❌ REJECTED: get-in is a function of **what is booked before you** (a matinee, a daytime hire) and of whether staff with keys are on site. A room-level constant would be wrong for most shows. **Resolution (D-08): the room publishes a standing default; the actual get-in is a show property fixed at booking/advance (17/18).** This mirrors the domain's sharpest seam exactly (domain D-11: 16 publishes standing terms, 17 negotiates the deal) — which is corroboration, not coincidence: the seam is real and it cuts through every temporal field in this feature. |
| DT-10 | Parking is a nice-to-have logistics detail | It reads as the least consequential group here | ❌ REJECTED: three parking facts change whether a show is financially viable, and none appear in any venue directory. **(a) Vehicle height limits** — a 2.0 m car park barrier excludes most of the touring fleet's high-top vans, which is a hard blocker presented as trivia. **(b) Emission / congestion zones** — an old diesel van into a charged zone is a per-day cost against a fee that is frequently under £200; it is a material share of the show's economics and it is invisible until the charge arrives. **(c) Overnight security** — gear left in an unsecured van is the single most common way a working band loses its livelihood in one night. These are **cost and risk facts wearing a logistics costume**, and (c) shares the limiter's asymmetry: `not stated` must not read as `secure`. |
| DT-11 | An Operator who plays their own room is just a Musician for the harvest | The persona model is lenses on behaviour, not account types (`personas.md`) — so the Musician lens is simply active | ❌ REJECTED, and this is where the multi-hyphenate structurally breaks a domain mechanism. 16.05.06's entire authority rests on the reporter being **disinterested** — the band has no incentive to flatter the room. Not this feature's model to change → Q-05, cross-cut to 16.05.03/16.05.06. |
