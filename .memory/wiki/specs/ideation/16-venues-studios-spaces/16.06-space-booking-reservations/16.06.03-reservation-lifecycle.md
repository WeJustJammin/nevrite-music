## Overview

The reservation from request to completion: minimum blocks, buffers, deposits, the session running
over, cancellation ladders, and — the state that matters most to the rest of the domain —
**completion**, which is what proves someone was actually in the room and triggers the harvest
(16.05.06).

This is the domain's transactional core. It is also where the Operator's inventory reality bites:
their stock is **time**, it is perishable, and an unsold Tuesday is gone forever. Every mechanic here
exists because of that fact.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Sets minimum blocks, buffers, deposit terms, cancellation ladder and overtime rules. Confirms or declines requests. Cancels (rarely, and it inverts the refund). Marks completion, or it is inferred. |
| Musician | Full (own booking) | Requests, pays a deposit, cancels (against a ladder they were shown before paying), runs over, settles. Sees exactly what a cancellation costs *before* committing — which is not how the industry works today. |
| Producer | Full (own booking) | Same, and the persona most likely to run over — a session that is nearly there does not stop at 6pm. Overtime is a Producer feature. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. A Fan buys a ticket to a show (19); they never touch the reservation behind it. |

## Behavior

States: requested → confirmed → deposit paid → in progress → completed, with cancellation possible at
several points and different consequences at each.

**Minimum blocks and buffers** are the Operator's protection against their inventory's physics: a
studio cannot sell a 45-minute session because setup and teardown consume the room; a venue needs
load-in and load-out buffers around a show. Buffers are **invisible inventory** — time consumed by the
booking that is not part of it, and a booking system that ignores them sells slots that do not exist.

**Deposits** hold the slot. **Cancellation ladders** are graduated (full refund > 30 days, 50% > 7
days, none inside 48h) and must be shown before payment, not after. **Overtime** is the studio
reality: the session runs over, and the rate for the extra hour was agreed either in advance or in an
argument.

**Completion** is quiet and load-bearing: it triggers the post-gig harvest (16.05.06 D-02), it gates
setlist submission (16.02.06), and it is the evidence that a first-hand report is first-hand.

and whether the platform holds funds materially changes this feature's shape.

### Happy Path

1. Producer requests Live Room A, Tuesday 10:00–18:00 (above the 4-hour minimum block)
2. System adds a 1-hour teardown buffer; the room is unavailable until 19:00, invisibly
3. Producer sees the rate, the deposit, and the cancellation ladder **before** paying
4. Deposit paid; reservation confirmed; it appears in the Operator's Google Calendar (16.06.02)
5. Session runs to 19:30 → overtime at the pre-agreed rate → completed → harvest prompt fires (16.05.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Session runs over into the next booking | The buffer absorbs some of it; beyond that it is a real conflict between two paying clients | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — who is ejected, and who pays? |
| Client cancels inside the no-refund window | The ladder they saw before paying applies. The Operator's anti-persona ("keeps deposits outside the stated policy") is countered by the ladder being stated and enforced | Ladder applied automatically |
| **Operator** cancels | The ladder inverts — this is the Operator's fault, so the refund is full and there is arguably a penalty. A band with a cancelled show has costs | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Venue closes permanently with live bookings | 16.01.05 cascade — Operator-fault cancellation for every future booking at once | Bulk refund path |
| No-show | The Operator's named cost. Distinct from a cancellation: no notice, so no backfill was possible (16.06.04) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Booking never marked complete | The harvest never fires and the domain's engine stalls for that booking. **Completion must be inferred, not manual** — nobody will tap "completed" | Auto-complete after the slot passes |
| Deposit paid, then the room is merged (16.05.07) or claimed by another org (16.05.02) | The money and the room have moved. Real, and unresolved in both directions | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Overtime not pre-agreed | The argument at 2am. Pre-agreeing the rate is the whole intervention | Rate shown at booking |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Requested | Client requested (enquiry-posture rooms) | Pending Operator response |
| Confirmed | Operator accepted / instant-book | Confirmed + deposit due |
| Deposit paid | Payment cleared | Slot secured; ladder active |
| In progress | Slot start passed | Live |
| Completed | Slot end passed (inferred) | **Triggers harvest (16.05.06) + setlist prompt (16.02.06)** |
| Cancelled (client) | Client cancelled | Ladder applied; slot released to waitlist (16.06.04) |
| Cancelled (operator) | Operator cancelled | Inverted ladder: full refund + `[PENDING]` penalty |
| No-show | Slot passed, nobody came | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Completion is a housekeeping state | It is just the end of a booking | ❌ **REJECTED — completion is the domain's most load-bearing state.** It is the proof a person was in the room, which is what makes their post-gig report *first-hand* rather than an anonymous claim (16.05.06 D-02). It gates setlist submission against royalty fraud (16.02.06 Q-03). Without it the harvest cannot fire, and without the harvest the registry rots (16.05.05 DT-05). **This is the mechanical reason booking belongs in domain 16 and not in 17** — the reservation is what puts the platform in the room. |
| DT-02 | Completion should be marked by someone | Somebody confirms the session happened | ❌ REJECTED: nobody will tap "completed". Not the band at 1am, not the Operator at the loading dock. **Completion must be inferred** from the slot passing without cancellation. A manual step here would silently stall the domain's engine for most bookings, and the failure would be invisible — no error, just a registry that stops refreshing. |
| DT-03 | Buffers are an Operator preference | Nice-to-have padding around a booking | ❌ REJECTED: buffers are **invisible inventory**. A studio's teardown hour is time consumed by the booking that is not part of it; a venue's load-out is not optional. A system without buffers sells slots that do not physically exist, and manufactures the double-booking the Operator came here to escape. They are physics, not preference. |
| DT-04 | The cancellation ladder is a policy field | The Operator writes their terms | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — it is a **mechanism**, and the mechanism is the product. The Operator's anti-persona is "keeps deposits on cancellations outside the stated policy"; the Musician's is "abuses refunds". Both are defeated by the same design: the ladder is shown before payment and enforced automatically by the platform. A ladder that is text in a page is the status quo (an argument); a ladder that executes is the feature. |
| DT-05 | Operator-cancels and client-cancels are the same event with the parties swapped | Symmetry | ❌ REJECTED: they are asymmetric in fault and in harm. A client cancelling costs the Operator a perishable slot they may backfill (16.06.04). An **Operator** cancelling costs a band a show — travel booked, a bill announced, tickets sold (19), other dates routed around it. The refund inverts, and a full refund is arguably not sufficient compensation. The industry has no norm here, which is precisely why the platform must state one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Completion is inferred**, never manually marked | Nobody taps "completed"; a manual step stalls the domain's engine invisibly (DT-02) |
| D-02 | Completion is the domain's proof of presence — the mechanical reason booking lives in 16 | It makes a post-gig report first-hand and gates setlist submission (DT-01) |
| D-03 | Buffers are invisible inventory, not preference | A system without them sells slots that do not physically exist (DT-03) |
| D-04 | The cancellation ladder is **shown before payment and enforced automatically** | It simultaneously defeats the Operator's deposit-keeping and the client's refund abuse (DT-04) |
| D-05 | Operator-cancellation inverts the ladder and is not symmetric with client-cancellation | An Operator cancelling costs a band a show, travel, and ticket sales (DT-05) |
| D-06 | Candidate 14's waitlist/backfill split into 16.06.04 | Backfill is a demand-side capability with its own model, not a reservation state |
