# Feature: Reservation Lifecycle

> **Number**: 16.06.03
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Merged from**: sweep candidate 14 (deposits, minimum blocks, buffers, overtime, cancellation ladders) — waitlist backfill split to 16.06.04

## Overview

The reservation from request to completion: minimum blocks, buffers, deposits, the session running
over, cancellation ladders, and — the state that matters most to the rest of the domain —
**completion**, which is what proves someone was actually in the room and triggers the harvest
(16.05.06).

This is the domain's transactional core. It is also where the Operator's inventory reality bites:
their stock is **time**, it is perishable, and an unsold Tuesday is gone forever. Every mechanic here
exists because of that fact.

Two things this feature is not. It is **not pricing** — the number comes from the computed quote
(16.06.07 D-01); this feature consumes it and never authors it. And it is **not the deal** — for a
show, the commercial terms are negotiated and settled in 17 (domain D-11). What this feature owns
regardless of who set the terms is **room physics**: blocks, buffers, occupancy, overrun, completion.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Operator** | Full | Sets per-room minimum block, setup/teardown buffers, minimum changeover, deposit percentage, cancellation ladder, overtime grace and increments, and the no-show assertion. Confirms or declines requests on enquiry-posture rooms (16.06.06). Cancels — rarely, and it inverts the ladder against them (D-05). Asserts no-show inside a 12-hour window, and is the only party who can (DT-07) — which is also a conflict of interest the design must name, because they are the party who profits from the assertion. **Cannot** override a licence-derived curfew to sell overrun (DT-14). May book their own room as a **zero-value reservation** for their own session (D-12). Never marks completion — it is inferred (D-01). |
| **Musician** | Full (own reservation) | Requests, sees the ladder before paying, pays the deposit, reduces or reschedules under stated rules, cancels against a ladder they were shown **before** committing — which is not how the industry works today. Runs over on a rehearsal (rarely; rehearsal rooms are back-to-back and the hard stop is real). Contests a no-show assertion within 72h. For a **show**, they see 17's negotiated ladder here, not the room's standing one (D-15). |
| **Producer** | Full (own reservation) | Same, and the persona most likely to run over — a session that is nearly there does not stop at 6pm. Overtime is a Producer feature: they see, at the moment of booking, whether the room has overrun headroom at all, and at what rate. They compose compounds (16.06.05), so they meet the case where one cancellation produces two different refund outcomes (D-16). The **Operator-Producer** — one human running a room and a session in it — is the multi-hyphenate case this feature must not drop (D-12). |
| **Fan** | ❌ None | No visibility, no surface, not even a read. A Fan buys a ticket to a show (19); the reservation behind it is the venue's commercial position and an empty Tuesday is not public information. The one place a Fan is downstream of this feature and never knows it: an Operator-fault cancellation of a show with tickets sold cascades into 19's refund path (CX to 19). |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None
>
> **The multi-hyphenate rule for this feature** (`personas.md`: roles are simultaneous, not
> sequential): fault, ladder side and refund direction attach to **the role the actor was acting in
> on this reservation**, recorded at booking time — never to the human. A studio's house engineer
> who books their employer's room for their own client is the client on that reservation, and their
> cancellation is a client cancellation, even though they can also press the Operator's buttons.

## Behavior

A reservation binds **one client party** to **one room** (16.01.02 D-01 — the room is the
calendar-carrying unit, never the place) for **one contiguous slot**, under **terms captured at
booking time**.

### The capture-at-booking rule

Everything that will be argued about later is copied onto the reservation the moment it is created,
and the copy governs forever: the ladder, the deposit percentage, the overtime rate and grace, the
buffers, the minimum block, the session archive policy (16.03.04 D-02), and the identity of the
counterparty org. If the Operator edits their ladder tomorrow, live reservations keep the ladder
their clients were shown. This is the same principle 16.03.04 D-02 already states for archive policy,
generalised: **a dispute three years later must cite the terms as they were, not as they now read.**

### Bookability requires a claim

A reservation cannot exist on an **unclaimed** place record (16.05.02). A booking needs a payee, and
an unclaimed record has no one to pay. Unclaimed records are **directory**; claimed rooms are
**inventory**. This is the line between the registry's value at zero transactions and its value as
supply — and it is a positive answer to "why would an Operator ever claim?": claiming is what makes
the room sellable.

### Minimum blocks and buffers — the inventory's physics

A studio cannot sell a 45-minute session because setup and teardown consume the room; a venue needs
load-in and load-out around a show. The **minimum block** is Operator-set per room (proposed
defaults: studio 4h, rehearsal 2h, venue show 1 day — 16.06.07 D-04 governs what "a day" means).

Buffers are **invisible inventory** — time consumed by the booking that is not part of it. A booking
system that ignores them sells slots that do not physically exist and manufactures the
double-booking the Operator came here to escape (DT-03).

**Buffers between two adjacent bookings collapse; they do not stack** (DT-09):

```
changeover(A → B) = max(A.teardown_buffer, B.setup_buffer, room.min_changeover)
```

Summing them would silently destroy the inventory the buffer exists to protect — a room would lose
two hours to a one-hour changeover, and the feature meant to defend the Operator's stock would eat
it. `room.min_changeover` exists for the case where max() is genuinely wrong (a full drum teardown
followed by an orchestral setup); it is Operator-set, default 0.

The client never sees the buffer in availability. They see it exactly once, and indirectly: as
**overrun headroom** — "the room is yours until 18:00; it is free until 19:00 if you need it."

### Duration is elapsed time, not wall-clock arithmetic

Occupancy, billing, minimum-block checks and ladder thresholds are computed as **elapsed real time in
the room's IANA zone**, never as a subtraction of clock faces. A 22:00–06:00 session on a fall-back
night is **9 real hours**; on a spring-forward night, 22:00–06:00 is **7**. The band occupies the
room for the real duration and the Operator's stock is consumed for the real duration. Every naive
calendar gets this wrong, and it gets it wrong on exactly the nights studios sell overnight blocks.

### Deposits and the payment window

The deposit is an Operator-set percentage of the **computed quote** (16.06.07 D-01), default 25%,
bounded 0–100%. 0% is legitimate and supported — plenty of rehearsal rooms take nothing.

Confirmation creates an **exclusive lock on the slot** and opens a payment window:

```
payment_window = clamp(0.25 × lead_time, 30 minutes, 24 hours)
```

A slot four days out gets 24h. A slot 48h out gets 12h. A slot 4h out gets 1h. A slot 45 minutes out
gets the 30-minute floor. A fixed 24h window would be absurd on a next-morning session and is how
booking tools lose perishable inventory to a lapsed card. Window lapses → reservation `expired`, the
slot returns to available, and 16.06.04 is triggered exactly as it is by an expired hold.

### The cancellation ladder — a mechanism, not a policy field

The ladder is **shown before payment and enforced automatically** (D-04). That single design defeats
both anti-personas at once: the Operator's "keeps deposits on cancellations outside the stated
policy" and the client's "abuses refunds". A ladder that is prose on a page is the status quo — an
argument. A ladder that executes is the feature.

A ladder is **a total, non-overlapping partition of lead time over [0, ∞)**, validated at publish
(DT-19). The platform rejects a ladder with a gap or an overlap, because an ambiguous ladder
reproduces exactly the argument the ladder exists to prevent. Proposed default:

| Lead time before slot start (elapsed, room-local) | Refund of amount paid |
|---|---|
| ≥ 30 days | 100% |
| 7–30 days | 50% |
| 48 hours – 7 days | 25% |
| < 48 hours | 0% |

### The three ways a reservation shrinks — and why only one is free

The ladder is trivially defeated unless all three are named:

| Operation | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Cancel** | The captured ladder applies to the full amount paid | The base case |
| **Reduce** (8h → 4h) | The ladder applies to **the released delta**, priced at the quote's rate for that time. The remainder must still satisfy the minimum block, or the reduction is refused | Otherwise "reduce to the minimum block" is the universal ladder bypass (DT-11) |
| **Reschedule** | **Once** per reservation, free of ladder, if: ≥7 days notice, the new date is within 90 days, and the new slot is confirmed **atomically** with the release of the old one. Anything else — a second reschedule, a move beyond 90 days, a move inside 7 days, or a move with no new date chosen — is a **cancellation**, and the ladder fires | Otherwise "move it to next year, then cancel that" is the ladder bypass (DT-10) |

**Extension** before the session (6h → 8h) is not a shrink: it is priced as new time at the captured
rate, subject to availability, and adds no ladder exposure.

### Overtime — pre-agreed, capped by law, priced not policed

Overrun availability is a fact the room knows **before the session starts**, and the Producer is told
it at booking:

- **Headroom exists** (nothing booked after the changeover, and the room's licence permits the hour):
  overrun is available at the captured overtime rate. Grace period default 15 minutes
  (Operator-settable 0–30), then billed in 30-minute increments, rounded up. Default overtime rate
  1.5× standard hourly (16.06.07 owns the number).
- **No headroom** (the changeover is followed by another paying client, or the hour breaks the
  premises licence curfew): the session has a **hard stop**. The system warns at **T-30** and
  **T-10** before the changeover begins.

**The licence is a ceiling the Operator cannot lift** (DT-14). Curfew and dB limit are
licence-derived fields the Operator does not own (16.01.06 D-02, 16.02.03). The platform refuses to
**sell** an overrun past curfew even when the Operator would wave it through in real life. It does
not pretend to stop the music — it declines to be the instrument that sells the breach.

**Overrun into another client's slot**: the platform **never ejects; it prices** (D-08). Software
cannot remove people from a live room, and pretending otherwise is theatre. What it can do is know
whose time was consumed and by whom:

- The incumbent's overrun into sold time is billed at **2× the overtime rate** — that time was sold
  to someone else.
- The displaced client is **credited from that charge**, automatically, on the reservation that was
  eaten.
- Both parties and the Operator are notified in real time; the Operator, not the platform, decides on
  the night whether to enforce or absorb. The platform supplies the arithmetic, not the eviction.

### Completion — quiet, inferred, and load-bearing

**Completion is inferred**, never marked (D-01). Nobody taps "completed" — not the band at 1am, not
the Operator at the loading dock. A manual step here would stall the domain's engine for most
bookings and the failure would be invisible: no error, just a registry that quietly stops refreshing.

Completion fires at **slot end + changeover**, and it is what triggers the post-gig harvest (16.05.06
D-02) and gates setlist submission (16.02.06). It is the evidence that a first-hand report is
first-hand — **the mechanical reason booking lives in domain 16** (domain D-04).

Because it is inferred from silence, completion is **forgeable** (DT-06): book a cheap Tuesday, never
go, get asked spec questions at midnight, answer them — and the answers now carry the first-hand
credential the whole domain rests on. With 16.02.06, the same path produces a setlist for a show that
never happened, which is royalty fraud. The mitigation is **fire fast, commit late**:

| Moment | What happens |
|---|---|
| slot end + changeover | Completion inferred. Harvest prompt fires immediately (freshness is 16.05.06 D-03's whole point) |
| slot end + changeover → slot end + 12h | Reservation is `completed (provisional)`. Harvested reports are held provisional and are **not** published to the registry |
| slot end + 12h | No-show assertion window closes. Unasserted → `completed (settled)`; provisional reports commit |
| Operator asserts no-show first | Reservation → `no-show`; **every provisional report from that booking is voided** |

The deposit is the low-end anti-fraud mechanism that costs nothing to build: forging presence
requires paying for a room and leaves a payment trail. It is not sufficient for the royalty path —
16.02.06 Q-03 already flags that setlist submission needs a stronger gate than 16's completion, and
this feature confirms it rather than solving it.

### No-show — asserted, not inferred, by the party who profits from it

A no-show is **not a cancellation**: no notice was given, so no backfill was possible (16.06.04). It
is the Operator's named cost.

It **cannot be inferred** (DT-07). Completion is inferable from silence; its inverse is not — the
system has no idea whether anyone walked through the door. Only the Operator can assert it, and the
Operator is the party with the financial interest in asserting it. That asymmetry is structural and
must be designed around, not wished away:

- **Window**: opens at slot start + 30 minutes, closes at slot end + 12 hours. Default on silence is
  **completed**, not no-show. The default favours the client because the asserting party profits.
- **Money**: the deposit is retained in full. The balance is **not** auto-charged (see Q-06).
- **Contest**: the client has **72 hours**. A contested no-show routes to 24, and the funds are held
  pending resolution — which is only possible if the platform holds them (DT-08).
- **Reputation**: recorded, **not published**. Counted privately; surfaced to Operators as a
  booking-risk signal only after **3 uncontested no-shows in a rolling 12 months**, and disputable
  throughout. A published no-show flag asserted unilaterally by the counterparty is a commercial
  attack and an obvious griefing vector — the identical reasoning to 16.01.05 D-02.

### The Operator's own session — a zero-value reservation

An Operator booking their own room for their own session is **a reservation, not a block** (D-12). A
block has no counterparty, no completion and therefore **no harvest** (16.06.01: a private event is a
block). But an Operator-Producer's own sessions are a large share of studio use, and modelling them
as blocks means the platform's most engaged supply generates zero provenance from the work it does
most. The zero-value reservation has no deposit, no ladder and no payment; it consumes inventory,
completes, and triggers the harvest and the archive policy exactly like any other.

This is one instance of a **class of bug worth naming**: *every path that consumes a room without
producing a completion is a hole in the domain's engine.* The other known instance is lockout
tenancy (CX-08 / domain Q-10) — a band uses a room weekly for two years and is never asked a single
question about it. Same shape, different feature.

### Escrow is not an implementation detail of this feature

D-04 says the ladder is **enforced automatically**. That is only implementable if the platform
controls the funds at cancellation time (DT-08). On a pass-through model the platform can compute the
refund and then politely ask the Operator to honour it — which is the status quo, and the Operator's
anti-persona is precisely that they will not. And an Operator-fault cancellation caused by insolvency
(16.01.05 closure cascade) is a refund owed by a business that has no money (DT-13). **Q-01 is not a
stack preference; it decides whether D-04 and D-05 exist at all.**

### Happy Path

1. Producer opens Live Room A's calendar and selects Tuesday 10:00–18:00 — above the room's 4-hour minimum block.
2. System resolves the compound (16.06.05), computes the quote (16.06.07), and displays, on one screen **before any payment**: the total, the 25% deposit, the full four-band cancellation ladder, the overtime rate and grace, and the session archive policy (16.03.04).
3. System states overrun headroom: "Yours until 18:00. The room is free until 19:00 — overrun at £X/30min after a 15-minute grace." (It knows this because the next booking starts at 20:00 and the changeover is `max(1h teardown, 0h setup, 0) = 1h`.)
4. Producer confirms. The slot is exclusively locked; a 24-hour payment window opens (lead time is 3 weeks).
5. Deposit paid; reservation `secured`. Terms are captured onto the reservation and are now immutable. The booking appears in the Operator's Google Calendar within seconds (16.06.02).
6. Tuesday 17:30 — system sends the T-30 notice: overrun available to 19:00, rate as agreed.
7. Session runs to 19:30. 18:00–19:30 minus 15 minutes grace = 75 minutes → billed as 3 × 30-minute increments at the captured overtime rate. The room's changeover pushes to 20:30; the 20:00 booking is inside sold time, so the last 30 minutes bill at 2× and the 20:00 client is credited from it, automatically.
8. Slot end + changeover → completion inferred → `completed (provisional)`. The harvest prompt fires on the Producer's phone at 20:30 (16.05.06 D-03: three taps, pre-filled).
9. Next morning, 12h elapsed with no no-show assertion → `completed (settled)`. The harvested corrections commit to the registry; the setlist path opens for the show case (16.02.06).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Session runs over, room free after** | Grace 15 min, then 30-min increments at the captured overtime rate. Auto-extends; no approval step at 2am | "Overrun: 30 min · £X. Running." |
| **Session runs over into another paying client's slot** | The platform never ejects; it prices (D-08). Incumbent billed 2× overtime for time inside sold time; displaced client auto-credited from that charge; Operator notified live and decides on the night | Incumbent: "You are 20 min into the next booking — £X at 2×." Displaced: "Your 20:00 start is delayed ~20 min. £Y credited." |
| **Overrun would break the premises licence curfew** | Refused. Licence-derived fields are not the Operator's to override (16.01.06 D-02). The platform declines to sell the breach; it does not claim to stop it | "Hard stop 23:00 — the room's licence. No overrun available tonight." |
| **Client cancels inside the no-refund window** | The captured ladder applies, automatically. Slot released to 16.06.04 with a clock running | "Cancelled. 0% refund — you were shown this before paying." + the ladder as shown at booking |
| **Client "reduces" 8h → 45min to dodge the ladder** | Refused — the remainder breaks the 4h minimum block. If it had cleared the block, the ladder applies to the released delta (DT-11) | "Minimum block is 4h. Reduce to 4h (ladder applies to the 4h released) or cancel." |
| **Client reschedules, then reschedules again** | First move is free under the rules (≥7d notice, new date ≤90d, atomic). The second is a cancellation and the ladder fires (DT-10) | "This is a second change — it is a cancellation. You'd get 50% back. Continue?" |
| **Client reschedules with no new date chosen** | Not a reschedule. A move without a destination is a cancellation with optimism attached | "Pick the new date now, or this is a cancellation." |
| **Operator cancels** | Ladder inverts (D-05). 100% refund + a platform-issued credit at the proposed default (Q-02). Slot released; waitlist fired; if a show with tickets sold, cascades to 19 | "Cancelled by the venue. Full refund + £X credit. This is their fault, not yours." |
| **Operator cancels because the venue went insolvent** | 16.01.05 cascade: bulk Operator-fault cancellation of every future booking at once. **The refunding party has no money** (DT-13) — deliverable only under escrow | Bulk refund path; under pass-through, an unfundable promise |
| **Client paid a deposit on a venue that is flagged at-risk** | The at-risk signal (16.01.05 D-04) is surfaced **at the point of deposit**, not buried on the place page (DT-17). A deposit on a venue that may not exist in March is a risk the client is entitled to price | "This venue is flagged at risk of closure. Your deposit is £X." |
| **No-show** | Operator-asserted only, inside a 12h window; default on silence is completed. Deposit retained; balance not auto-charged; recorded not published; contestable 72h (DT-07) | Client: "The venue reported you did not attend. Deposit retained. Contest within 72h." |
| **Operator falsely asserts a no-show** | The named risk of DT-07 — the asserting party profits. Countered by: default-to-completed, 72h contest, funds held pending 24, private-not-published, and the 3-in-12-months threshold before any signal exists | Contest flow → 24 |
| **Client never marks the booking complete** | Nobody ever does. Inferred at slot end + changeover (D-01) | Nothing. That is the point. |
| **A no-show that auto-completed and got harvested** | The forgery vector (DT-06). Reports stay `provisional` until slot end + 12h; a no-show assertion voids every provisional report from that booking | The report simply never appears in the registry |
| **Chargeback after a completed session** | Does **not** reverse completion, the harvest, or the credential. Presence was real; a payment dispute is not a presence dispute (DT-16). Routes to 24 as a payment dispute only | Money dispute proceeds; the provenance fact stands |
| **Card declined / payment window lapses** | Reservation → `expired`; exclusive lock released; slot available; 16.06.04 fires exactly as on an expired hold | "Your hold on Tuesday lapsed — payment didn't complete." |
| **Two clients confirm the same slot in the same second** | Exclusive lock at confirmation. The second is refused **before capture**, never refunded after — a refunded double-charge is a dispute the design can simply not create | "Taken 4 seconds ago. Nearest free: Wednesday 10:00. Join the waitlist?" |
| **Client taps Pay twice / network drops mid-payment** | The reservation exists before the payment; payment is idempotent against it. One reservation, one charge, always | Single confirmation |
| **Booking spans a DST fall-back** | Duration is elapsed real time in the room's zone: 22:00–06:00 is 9 real hours, billed as 9 and blocking 9 (+changeover). Clock-face subtraction would give the band a free hour and consume an hour of unsold stock | "8 hours (9 on the night the clocks go back)." |
| **Booking spans a DST spring-forward** | 22:00–06:00 is 7 real hours. Billed as 7. Inverse of the above, and the case that gets angry phone calls | "8 hours (7 on the night the clocks go forward)." |
| **Touring client reads the ladder from another time zone** | Every ladder threshold is elapsed hours before slot start in the **room's** local time, displayed with the room's zone named | "0% refund after 20:00 Tue 12 Mar (venue local, GMT)." |
| **Operator edits the ladder after a deposit is paid** | The captured ladder governs. The edit applies only to reservations created after it | Operator: "Live reservations keep the terms their clients agreed." |
| **Room merged into another record (16.05.07) while a deposit is held** | A **correct** merge is a no-op for the reservation — same physical room, one record; the reservation binds to the room identity and both ids resolve forward. A **wrong** merge is why 16.05.07 D-04 makes merge non-destructive and reversible: the reversal carries the reservation back untouched. Reservations are never silently relocated to a different physical room | Nothing changes for the client |
| **Room's record is claimed by another org while a deposit is held** | Cannot arise on an unclaimed room — bookability requires a claim (DT-18). If a **counter-claim** or a change of ownership lands: the reservation's counterparty is frozen at booking time. The incoming org must **accept the novation** or the booking becomes an Operator-fault cancellation | "This room changed hands. The new operator has accepted your booking." / full refund + credit |
| **Inbound calendar event lands on top of a paid booking** | A sync event **cannot** cancel a paid reservation — money and a counterparty are involved. It also cannot be discarded — the Operator believes their calendar. Surfaced to the Operator as a conflict for a human to resolve (CX-09) | Operator: "Google Calendar shows 'Wedding' on Sat 14 — you have a paid booking. Which is real?" |
| **Compound cancellation with divergent member terms** | The ladder is computed **over the compound**, exactly as the quote is (CX-04 symmetry). The room refunds 100% at 31 days; the 05 engineer's terms say 50%. The client sees one **blended outcome per date band**, never two ladders | "Cancel now: £340 of £500 back (room 100%, engineer 50%)." |
| **A show reservation booked via 17** | This feature owns the slot, buffers, occupancy, overrun and completion. The ladder and the money come from 17's negotiated deal (domain D-11). The reservation carries a **terms source** (D-15) | The 17 deal's terms, in this feature's UI |
| **Rival books a room purely to deny a date** | Real in live music. The ladder is the only honest defence: the denial costs real money and the cost rises as the date nears; a no-show records it. A funded adversary can still do it. Named, not solved — the pattern is visible to 24 | Nothing at booking time |
| **Operator books their own room for their own session** | Zero-value reservation, not a block (D-12). No deposit, no ladder, no payment. Consumes inventory, completes, triggers the harvest and archive policy | "Your session. No charge. You'll get the usual prompt after." |
| **A studio's house engineer books their employer's room for their own client** | The multi-hyphenate case. Fault and ladder side attach to **the role on this reservation** (client), not to the human — even though the same person can press the Operator's buttons | Client-side ladder, client-side refunds |
| **Client cancels a lockout "booking"** | Does not exist here. A lockout is a tenancy: notice, not a ladder; eviction, not a refund; nothing completes (16.06.09 D-02, CX-08) | Handled entirely by 16.06.09 |
| **A ladder is published with a gap (e.g. 48h–7d undefined)** | Rejected at publish. A ladder must be a total, non-overlapping partition of `[0, ∞)`. An ambiguous ladder reproduces the argument the ladder exists to end (DT-19) | Operator: "48 hours to 7 days isn't covered. What's the refund there?" |
| **Operator sets a 0% deposit** | Legitimate and supported — most rehearsal rooms take nothing. Consequence: no payment window, no ladder leverage, and the no-show cost lands entirely on the Operator. Stated at configuration, not discovered later | Operator: "No deposit means no-shows cost you the whole slot." |

### States

**Reservation lifecycle states**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| `requested` | Client requested on an enquiry-posture room (16.06.06) | "Awaiting the venue." Requester's identity, credits and history travel with it (16.06.06 D-03) |
| `declined` | Operator declined the request | "Not available." Waitlist offered (16.06.04) |
| `confirmed` | Operator accepted, or instant-book | Slot exclusively locked. Deposit due; payment window counting down |
| `expired` | Payment window lapsed | "Payment didn't complete — the slot is back on sale." Fires 16.06.04 |
| `secured` | Deposit cleared (or 0% deposit + confirmation) | Slot secured; captured ladder active and displayed; appears in the Operator's external calendar (16.06.02) |
| `in progress` | Slot start passed | Live. Overrun headroom stated. T-30 / T-10 warnings if there is a hard stop |
| `completed (provisional)` | Slot end + changeover, no cancellation | **Harvest prompt fires now** (16.05.06). Reports held, not published |
| `completed (settled)` | + 12h with no no-show assertion | Terminal. Provisional reports commit. Setlist path opens (16.02.06) |
| `cancelled (client)` | Client cancelled, reduced below the block, or made a disqualifying reschedule | Captured ladder applied automatically; slot released to 16.06.04 |
| `cancelled (operator)` | Operator cancelled, or 16.01.05 closure cascade | Inverted ladder: 100% refund + credit (Q-02). Cascades to 19 if tickets are sold |
| `no-show (asserted)` | Operator asserted inside the 12h window | Deposit retained; balance not auto-charged; provisional reports voided; 72h to contest |
| `no-show (contested)` | Client contested within 72h | Funds held; routed to 24. Neither party's version wins by default |
| `rescheduled` | Qualifying reschedule | Terminal pointer to the successor reservation. Not a cancellation; no ladder |

**View states**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Reservation list or detail opening | Skeleton rows carrying date + room name from cache — never a bare spinner; an Operator at a loading dock is checking one fact |
| Empty | No reservations (a new client; an Operator with no bookings this week) | Client: "Nothing booked. Find a room." Operator: "Nothing this week" + the 16.06.04 waitlist depth for those dates — the empty state is where their perishable stock is *visible* |
| Populated | Reservations exist | Grouped: needs action (payment window, T-30, contest window) → upcoming → past. Money and countdowns are never below the fold |
| Error | Payment, sync or quote resolution failed | Names which one failed and what is still true: "Deposit taken. Your Google Calendar didn't update — your booking is secure, the venue may not see it yet. Retry." A generic failure here reads as "did my money go?" |
| Partial | Quote resolvable but a compound member's price is missing (CX-04's silent failure); or 16.06.02 sync is degraded and availability is possibly-stale (16.06.02 D-04) | The gap is named, never averaged over: "Assistant engineer — price not set by the venue. Total excludes it." / "This venue's calendar last synced 6h ago — availability may be stale." A quote that is wrong and looks right is the failure this state exists to prevent |
