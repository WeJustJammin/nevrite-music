# Feature: Availability Calendar & Holds

> **Number**: 16.06.01
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The room's calendar: when it is open, when it is taken, and — the part that matters in live music —
when it is **held**. A hold is not a booking. It is the industry's universal soft-reservation: a
promoter asks for a Tuesday, the venue pencils it, and everyone waits. First hold, second hold,
challenge, release. It is how live music actually works and no general-purpose calendar models it.

`personas.md` names the Operator's switching trigger — "a costly double-booking" — and their
accidental anti-persona — "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold."
Both failures live in this feature. The first is prevented by being truthful (which this feature
cannot achieve alone — see 16.06.02); the second is prevented by construction, because **every hold
in this system dies on a clock**.

The Operator's inventory is *perishable and physically constrained* (`personas.md`, Unique
Constraint). An unsold Tuesday is gone forever. That single fact sets every number in this file: a
hold's expiry is not bounded by the holder's convenience, it is bounded by **the Operator's remaining
ability to sell the slot to somebody else**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Operator** | ✅ Full | Owns the calendar. Sets per-room opening hours (recurring weekly) and dated blackouts; sets hold policy within platform bounds (TTL floor/ceiling, challenge window, concurrent-hold cap, resale floor); grants, ranks, overrides and releases holds; places holds **for off-platform counterparties** (a promoter with no WeJammin account — see DT-06); sees every holder's identity, hold age and renewal count; sees the "clearing the book" queue of holds approaching their resale floor. Their calendar **is** their business, and it currently lives in a paper diary and Google Calendar simultaneously (16.06.02). |
| **Musician** | 👁️ View + request hold | Sees availability at whatever granularity the Operator's posture permits (16.06.06) — which for many venues is "nothing, enquire". Requests a hold for a tour date; holds a slot while routing a tour that may not happen. Sees **their own** rank, TTL countdown (in their local zone), renewal budget, and the option to challenge the rank directly above them. Sees competing holds as **rank + age only, never identity** (DT-09). Acts as a person or on behalf of a band entity (01) — the drummer in two bands may legitimately hold one date twice, and gets warned (DT-12). |
| **Producer** | 👁️ View + request hold | Same mechanics, studio rooms. Producers hold speculatively too — a client's project that may or may not get funded is a held week. Shorter natural windows than a tour hold (24h floor / 14d ceiling vs 72h / 45d). |

A Fan (or a scraper wearing a Fan account) reading held dates across venues could reconstruct a tour before its announce, which is a real commercial harm to the artist this platform exists to serve (DT-14). |

## Behavior

The calendar attaches to a **room** (16.01.02 D-01), never a place. It exists only for **claimed**
rooms — most of the registry at launch is unclaimed and seeded from public data, and an unclaimed
record has no calendar at all, only a contact path (16.06.06).

### The availability model

Availability is a set of **intervals**, not a slot grid (DT-13). A venue's Saturday is one 14-hour
block; a rehearsal room's Saturday is fourteen 1-hour blocks; a studio's is a day, a half-day, or a
lockout. A fixed grid forces two of those three to lie, and it cannot express 16.06.03's buffers,
which are deliberately off-grid.

- **Granularity**: intervals resolve to 15 minutes. Anything finer is not a real booking unit for a
  physical room.
- **Opening hours**: a recurring weekly pattern per room, plus dated exceptions (bank holidays, the
  Operator's fortnight in Spain). The pattern is authored in the room's local wall-clock time.
- **Minimum block** governs sale, not the calendar (16.06.03). A studio may show 09:00–21:00 open and
  refuse to sell a 45-minute session.

Each interval carries exactly one state: `available`, `held` (with rank), `booked`, `blocked`
(Operator), or `cascade-blocked` (an exclusive sibling room is booked or hold-blocked, 16.01.02 D-02).
A cascade-block **always carries its reason** — "Live Room is booked" is actionable, "unavailable" is
not (D-05).

### Time is wall-clock, not an instant (DT-10)

A slot is `2026-03-14 19:00–23:00 Europe/London` — a **local wall-clock time plus the room's IANA
zone** — never a UTC instant. The room is a physical place; 19:00 is the fact that the licence, the
curfew, the load-in and the neighbours all agree on. Zone rules are political and do change (Brazil
abolished DST in 2019; Lebanon moved a transition with 48 hours' notice in 2023). A booking stored as
an instant silently migrates relative to the room's curfew when a government legislates. Stored as
wall-clock + zone, a 19:00 show stays a 19:00 show, which is what every party meant.

Three consequences, each of which a naive calendar gets wrong:

| Rule | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Duration is elapsed real time; wall-clock is the label.** | A 23:00–03:00 booking on the fall-back night is **5 real hours** — the room is occupied for 5, the engineer is paid for 5, and 16.06.07 prices 5. On the spring-forward night the same wall-clock span is **3 real hours**. The UI states it: "23:00–03:00 · 5 hrs (clocks go back)". |
| **Non-existent local times are rejected, never shifted.** | 02:30 on a spring-forward night does not exist. The request fails with `SLOT_TIME_NONEXISTENT` and the system offers the two adjacent real times (01:30 / 03:30). Silently shifting is how a load-in gets missed. |
| **Ambiguous local times are disambiguated explicitly.** | 01:30 on a fall-back night occurs twice. The picker offers "01:30 (first · BST)" and "01:30 (second · GMT)". A span that crosses the transition is unambiguous by construction — it is start + elapsed duration. |

**Rendering rule — room facts in room time; personal deadlines in viewer time.** Slots always render
in the room's zone as primary, with the viewer's zone as a secondary annotation only when they differ:
"Sat 14 Mar 19:00 GMT · 20:00 your time". A tour manager reading "19:00" from Berlin must be reading
19:00 *at the venue*. But a hold's TTL countdown renders in **the viewer's** zone — it is their
deadline, not a property of the room.

**All durations in this feature (TTL, challenge windows, grace) are elapsed real hours**, so a
transition night neither gifts nor steals an hour from a deadline.

### Holds are ranked, challengeable and always expiring

First hold has priority; a second sits behind it; rank N+1 — and only N+1 — may **challenge** rank N,
which forces rank N to confirm or release inside a window. Only one challenge may be live per slot at
a time. This is the mechanism live music has used forever and it is entirely absent from generic
booking tools — it is the clearest case in the domain of an industry practice a general tool cannot
express.

**Holds are attributed to the entity, not the individual** (`personas.md`: bands, labels and venues
are first-class entities). A venue booker changes jobs constantly; a promoter's holds belong to the
promoter's company. Removing a staff account must never release or orphan a hold. Corollary: an entity
cannot challenge itself — if the same entity holds rank 1 and rank 2, the ranks collapse.

**Holds are free** (DT-08). Charging for a hold would be the platform inventing a practice the
industry does not have; squatting is bounded by clocks and caps instead.

#### The two clocks

**1. TTL — the hold's absolute expiry.** Not a constant (DT-07):

```
lead_time  = slot_start − granted_at
expires_at = min(
               granted_at + clamp(0.25 × lead_time, floor, ceiling),
               slot_start − resale_floor
             )
```

| Room type | floor | ceiling | resale_floor | Holds default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Venue — performance slot | 72h | 45 days | **42 days** (the announce window — DT-11) | ON |
| Venue — non-performance hire (daytime, corporate, filming) | 48h | 21 days | 7 days | ON |
| Studio — session | 24h | 14 days | 48h | ON |
| Rehearsal room | — | — | — | **OFF** (instant-book — DT-15) |

Operator override bounds: floor ≥ 24h, ceiling ≤ 90 days, resale_floor ≥ 0. **The Operator cannot set
a hold to never expire.** That option is the anti-persona (D-02).

The `slot_start − resale_floor` term is the load-bearing half. It says: *a hold expires no later than
the last moment the Operator could still sell this slot to somebody else.* For a performance slot that
moment is not "a few days before the gig" — it is **the announce**, because a show that cannot be
announced, marketed and put on sale cannot be sold at all (19). The 42-day default is the on-sale lead
time, not a resale guess.

**2. Challenge window — runs only when a challenge is fired:**

| Room type | Window |
|---|---|
| Venue — performance | 48h |
| Venue — other · Studio | 24h |

Operator-settable 12h–120h. **Windows are elapsed real hours, and never expire on a Saturday, Sunday
or a public holiday in the holder's country — expiry rolls to 09:00 the next working day in the
holder's local zone, capped at a 120h total window.** A 48h clock fired at Friday 18:00 would
otherwise die on a Sunday evening, and "musicians are not at a desk" is not an edge case, it is the
population. This is also why the window is not "business hours" — that phrase has no meaning for a
touring musician in another zone.

#### Expiry is never a surprise

No hold expires without its holder being asked exactly once. At `expires_at − challenge_window` the
system fires a **confirm-or-release prompt** to the holder (an auto-challenge with no challenger).
Confirming converts to a booking (16.06.03) if the room is instant-book, or renews the hold if the
holder is not yet ready to commit. This is the platform performing the Operator's real, unglamorous,
recurring chore — **clearing the book** — automatically, on every hold, forever.

**Renewals are bounded**: max 3 per holder-entity per slot; permitted only while
`lead_time > 2 × resale_floor`; each renewal recomputes TTL by the same formula. The Operator sees the
renewal count; competing holders see **hold age in days** — enough to know a hold is stale, not enough
to know who it is (DT-09).

**Concurrent-hold cap**: 4 live holds per holder-entity per room (Operator-settable 1–20).
**Challenge rate limit**: 1 challenge per challenger-entity per slot per 14 days; 10 per
challenger-entity per Operator per 30 days.

Expiry is evaluated at **≤ 60s granularity** — a hold never outlives its `expires_at` by more than a
minute — and each expiry emits a release event that is the highest-value backfill trigger in the
domain (CX-02, 16.06.04 D-02).

#### The challenge protocol, precisely

1. Rank 2 challenges. The challenge is announced to rank 1 with the window's end rendered in rank 1's
   local zone. Rank 3+ are told a challenge is live but may not join it.
2. Rank 1 **confirms** → converts to a booking (16.06.03); ranks 2+ are released and notified with a
   reason. Or rank 1 **releases** → rank 2 promotes to rank 1, inherits a **freshly computed TTL**
   (not the remainder of rank 1's), and 3+ each promote one rank.
3. Rank 1 **does not answer** before the window closes → treated as a release. Silence is not consent
   to hold a perishable slot.
4. **A challenger who releases mid-window voids the challenge**: rank 1's original TTL resumes with the
   consumed time credited back, and rank 1 is told "challenge withdrawn". Forcing a commitment decision
   on behalf of a party who has left is punitive for no gain. The obvious abuse — challenge-sniping to
   force a rival to commit early or expose that they are not serious — is bounded by the rate limit
   above; repeat patterns are a Trust & Safety signal (24), not a punishment this feature invents.
5. Confirmation is **idempotent**. A retried confirm after a network failure is a no-op that reports
   success, not a double-booking.
6. **Grace**: a confirm arriving within **120s** after expiry is honoured if the slot has not been
   re-allocated. Beyond 120s, or if re-allocated, it fails with `HOLD_EXPIRED` and the expiry
   timestamp. Countdowns render from server-authoritative time — a holder's laptop clock is not a
   party to the contract.

#### The challenge protocol generalises to the exclusion graph (DT-16)

Exclusive sibling rooms (16.01.02 D-02) create the hardest case: Live Room has a live first hold, and
somebody tries to **book** Control Room, which cannot coexist with it.

- If a soft hold could block an exclusive sibling outright, one speculative pencil freezes the whole
  building — and the Operator loses money to a hold, which is precisely D-02's failure wearing a
  different hat.
- If it could not, holds are worthless on every multi-room site, which is most studios.

**Resolution**: a hold on room A makes exclusive sibling B **hold-blocked** — B can still be *held*,
but B cannot be *booked* until A's hold resolves, and the booking attempt **auto-challenges A's first
hold** on the spot. The pencil is honoured, the clock is short, and the money is not turned away — it
is queued behind a 48h answer. The challenge protocol was already the right tool; it just was not
pointed at the exclusion graph.

#### What the Operator can do that the platform will not prevent (DT-04)

An Operator may **book over a live hold**. It is their room and their business, and a platform that
forbids it will be routed around within a week — back to the diary, which is DT-03's failure. But the
override requires an explicit acknowledgement naming the holder, the holder is notified with a reason,
and the event is recorded. **The platform's power here is visibility, not prohibition.** A pattern of
overrides is a reputation signal (24), and reputation is the only correct instrument.

Symmetrically: an Operator wanting to keep a slot for themselves places a **block**, not a hold. A hold
has a counterparty and a clock; a block has neither.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims The Lexington's Live Room, connects their Google Calendar (16.06.02), and sets
   opening hours: Tue–Sat 18:00–02:00, `Europe/London`. Hold policy defaults to the venue-performance
   row: 72h floor / 45d ceiling / 42d resale floor / 48h challenge window.
2. A promoter (Musician, acting for the entity **Rough Trade Presents**) is routing a March tour and
   requests a hold on **Sat 14 Mar 2026, 19:00–23:00** — 240 days out. Posture is enquiry-only
   (16.06.06), so the request arrives as a structured enquiry carrying the act's credits (02).
3. Operator grants **first hold**. TTL = `min(240d × 0.25 = 60d → clamped to the 45d ceiling,
   14 Mar − 42d = 31 Jan)` → **expires 31 Jan 2026**, the announce boundary, not the ceiling. The
   holder sees "First hold · expires 31 Jan · 2 renewals left after that" in their own zone.
4. Six weeks later a second promoter requests the same date. They are granted **second hold**, and see
   "2nd of 2 · first hold is 43 days old" — age, not identity. Their TTL is computed the same way.
5. Second holder **challenges**. Fired Fri 12 Dec 18:20 GMT; the 48h window would land Sun 14 Dec, so
   it rolls to **Mon 15 Dec 09:00** in the first holder's zone. Both parties see that exact deadline
   and why it moved.
6. First holder confirms inside the window → the hold converts to a booking (16.06.03: deposit due,
   cancellation ladder shown before payment). Second and third holds release automatically with a
   reason. The booking appears in the Operator's Google Calendar within seconds (16.06.02).
7. Had the first holder released or stayed silent, the second promotes to first with a **freshly
   computed TTL**, and the released slot — if no other hold existed — emits a release event that
   reaches the waitlist within 60s (16.06.04).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Hold expires unnoticed | Auto-releases at ≤60s granularity; holder and Operator notified; backfill fires (16.06.04). **This is the point** — the Operator's anti-persona failure fixed by construction, and the failure never happens silently because of the confirm-or-release prompt one window earlier | "Your hold on 14 March expired" / Operator: "1 slot returned to sale" |
| Operator books the room in Google Calendar, not here | The core failure — the platform becomes the fourth incompatible calendar. Only 16.06.02 prevents it, and only if the Operator *has* a digital calendar | Availability marked possibly-stale when sync is degraded (16.06.02 D-04) |
| **Operator has no digital calendar at all — a paper diary** | 16.06.02 offers them nothing, so our calendar is confidently wrong and *we* cause the double-booking that is their switching trigger. **Interlock**: a newly-claimed room defaults to enquiry-only posture (16.06.06) and stays there until the calendar earns a trust signal — sync connected, **or** 5 completed bookings with zero conflicts (DT-17) | "Enquiry-only until your calendar is connected or proven" |
| Exclusive sibling room booked | Slot shows `cascade-blocked` **with the reason**, never a bare "unavailable" | "Live Room is booked" |
| Exclusive sibling has a live hold; someone tries to book this room | Sibling is `hold-blocked`, not blocked. The booking attempt **auto-challenges** the sibling's first hold (48h/24h). Money is queued, not refused; the pencil is honoured, not indulged (DT-16) | "Held by another booking — they have until Mon 09:00 to confirm. We'll tell you either way." |
| **DST fall-back**: 23:00–03:00 booking | Duration is **5 elapsed hours**, not 4. Priced, staffed and buffered on 5 | "23:00–03:00 · 5 hrs (clocks go back)" |
| **DST spring-forward**: 23:00–03:00 booking | **3 elapsed hours**. Same rule, opposite sign | "23:00–03:00 · 3 hrs (clocks go forward)" |
| Booking requested at a **non-existent** local time (02:30, spring-forward) | Rejected — never silently shifted | `SLOT_TIME_NONEXISTENT`: "02:30 doesn't exist on 29 Mar. Did you mean 01:30 or 03:30?" |
| Booking requested at an **ambiguous** local time (01:30, fall-back) | Requires explicit disambiguation | Picker: "01:30 (first · BST)" / "01:30 (second · GMT)" |
| **Curfew vs a DST-ambiguous end time** | Curfew is a licensed wall-clock ceiling (16.01.06 D-02). The safe reading of "stop by 23:00" is the **later** real instant. Applied as default; flagged for licence confirmation (Q-02) | Conformance warning via 16.07 |
| Tour manager reads the calendar from another zone | Room's zone is always primary; viewer's zone is a secondary annotation. Never inverted | "Sat 14 Mar 19:00 GMT · 20:00 your time" |
| Challenge window would expire on a Sunday | Rolls to 09:00 the next working day in the **holder's** zone, capped at 120h total | "You have until Mon 15 Dec 09:00 (moved — weekend)" |
| Hold challenged while the holder is on stage | The 48h window plus the weekend/holiday roll exists for exactly this. A shorter default is a hostile default for the population this platform serves | Countdown in their zone + reminder at 25% remaining |
| Challenger withdraws mid-window | Challenge **voids**; first hold's TTL resumes with consumed time credited back; first holder notified | "Challenge withdrawn — your hold stands, expires 31 Jan" |
| Challenge-sniping (repeated challenges with no intent) | Rate-limited: 1/slot/challenger/14d, 10/Operator/challenger/30d. Patterns escalate to 24; this feature does not invent a punishment schedule | Rate-limit message with the reset time |
| **Hold squatting** — one promoter holds every Friday for six months | Bounded by three independent clocks: concurrent-hold cap (4/room, Operator-settable 1–20), renewal cap (3), and the `slot_start − resale_floor` term. A holder exceeding 50% of a room's live holds is **surfaced to the Operator**, not auto-enforced | Operator: "Rough Trade holds 6 of your 9 held Fridays" |
| Two hold requests land on the same available slot in the same instant | **Not a conflict** — the ranked ladder absorbs it. Ranked by server-received order, ties broken deterministically. Both succeed; nobody sees an error. This is a structural benefit of the hold model | "You're 2nd on this date" |
| Two confirmations race for one slot | The booking transition is serialised. Loser gets the winner's timestamp, never a generic failure | `SLOT_ALREADY_BOOKED`: "Booked at 14:03. Your hold has been released." |
| **Operator books over a live hold** | Permitted — it is their room. Requires an explicit acknowledgement naming the holder; holder notified with a reason; event recorded; pattern is a 24 reputation signal (DT-04) | Operator: "This releases Rough Trade's 1st hold. Confirm?" · Holder: "The Lexington sold 14 March" |
| Operator blackouts a range containing live holds | A blackout **cannot silently swallow** a hold. The Operator is shown exactly what they would destroy and must release each explicitly (bulk action available, holder names shown). Live **bookings** block the blackout entirely — cancelling them fires the Operator-fault ladder (16.06.03 D-05) | "This blacks out 4 held slots and 1 paid booking. Holds: release. Booking: cancel (Operator-fault refund)." |
| Opening hours or curfew change shrinks an already-held slot | The hold survives (a hold is a claim on a slot, not on a spec), the holder is notified with the delta, and 16.07 flags the conformance break | "The Lexington's curfew moved to 23:00 — your held slot ran to 01:00" |
| Compound hold: room free, engineer not (16.06.05) | **Atomic — nothing is held.** All members are pre-held for **90s** while the compound resolves; failure anywhere releases everywhere. The UI names the failing member (16.06.05 D-04) | "Sam (assistant engineer) isn't free Tuesday — the room is" |
| **Compound release fails partway** — the orphan case | The nastiest failure in the sub-domain: an orphaned hold is the platform committing the Operator's anti-persona at machine speed, systematically, to the persona it sells a fix to (CX-03). **Hard guarantee: no hold unattached to a live compound or a confirmed booking survives longer than 15 minutes.** Release is idempotent and retried; a stuck hold surfaces in an Operator-visible queue and is swept | Operator (rare): "1 stuck hold auto-released" |
| Two compounds race for one shared member (same engineer, two studios) | The ladder handles it — the engineer's calendar ranks them. Second compound is held, not refused (16.06.05 CX-03) | "2nd in line for Sam on Tuesday" |
| **The same human holds one date twice — for two different bands** | Legitimate and common: holds are entity-attributed, so a drummer in two bands is two holders. Not blocked, but **warned** — they can only play one gig | "Kaiser Chiefs also hold 14 March. You're in both." |
| **The same entity ends up at rank 1 and rank 2** | Ranks collapse to the earliest grant time. You cannot challenge yourself; a self-challenge is the sockpuppet path and is closed by construction | "You already hold this date" |
| **Cross-venue conflict the platform alone can see** | The same act held on the same night at two on-platform venues, or a band double-held across two rooms. Nobody else in the industry has both books. Warn both holders and neither Operator (it is the holder's routing problem, and revealing it to a venue exposes a rival's routing) (DT-05) | "You hold 14 March at The Lexington and at Brudenell" |
| Room merged into a duplicate record (16.05.07) | Holds move to the surviving record. Duplicate holds by the same entity across both records **collapse to the earliest grant time**, preserving rank fairness — the promoter who asked first asked first, regardless of which duplicate they landed on | "Your hold moved to the merged listing — rank unchanged" |
| Room claimed by an org (16.05.02) mid-hold | Cannot occur: unclaimed records have no calendar, so there are no holds to inherit | — |
| Venue closes permanently (16.01.05) | Every live hold released with a reason; every booking becomes an Operator-fault cancellation (16.06.03 D-05) | "The Lexington has closed — your hold on 14 March is released" |
| Room's time zone was seeded wrong and gets corrected (16.05) | Wall-clock times stay; the **real instants shift**. Never silent — every holder and every booked party on affected future slots is notified with the delta | "This room's zone was corrected to Europe/Dublin. Your 19:00 slot is now 19:00 IST (was BST)." |
| Holder's account suspended (24) mid-hold | Hold **freezes**, does not auto-release: their confirm rights are paused, the Operator is told, and the Operator may release immediately without the usual courtesy. Auto-releasing on suspension would let a false report clear a rival's holds | Operator: "This holder is suspended — release now?" |
| Room has opening hours but no rate card (16.06.07) | Holds are **allowed** — a hold is not a purchase, and every enquiry-only room has no published price anyway. Only the **booking** is blocked | "Hold placed — the Operator will quote you" |
| Network failure while confirming a challenge | Confirm is idempotent; retry is a no-op reporting success | "Confirmed" (once) |
| Holder's device clock is wrong | Countdowns render from server-authoritative time, not the client's | Correct deadline regardless |
| Calendar scraped to reconstruct an unannounced tour | Holder identity is never public; availability visibility is posture-gated (16.06.06); reads are rate-limited. **A hold pattern is tour routing** and leaking it harms the artist (DT-14) | Fan/anon: no calendar at all |
| Overlapping holds on a lockout room | Lockout rooms have no availability semantics at all (16.06.09, CX-07) — the calendar concept does not apply. A lockout is a tenancy | Handled by 16.06.09 |
| Rehearsal room, hold requested | Holds are **off** by default for rehearsal-type rooms — instant-book. A ranked, challengeable ladder on a £12 hour is ceremony (DT-15). Operator may enable | "Book it — this room doesn't do holds" |
| Hold is live while a 17 deal is being papered | A challenge that kills a 90%-done deal is a real loss. **One** extension, of one challenge-window length, granted automatically when a live 17 deal thread exists on the slot — visible to both parties, once only | "Extended 48h — terms are being agreed" |

### States

#### Slot states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **No calendar** | Room unclaimed (most of the registry at launch) | Not bookable; contact path only (16.06.06) |
| **Available** | Inside opening hours, no claim | Bookable / enquirable per posture (16.06.06) |
| **Held (1st)** | Hold granted | Holder: rank + TTL + renewal budget, in their zone. Others: "held · 43 days old" + challenge option |
| **Held (2nd+)** | Subsequent hold | Rank + age of the hold above + challenge option (rank N+1 only) |
| **Challenged** | Rank N+1 challenged, or auto-challenge at `expires_at − window`, or an exclusive sibling's booking attempt | 1st holder: countdown to confirm-or-release with the exact deadline and any weekend roll explained. Others: "a challenge is live" |
| **Hold-blocked** | Exclusive sibling has a live hold | Holdable, not bookable; names the sibling and the challenge deadline (DT-16) |
| **Booked** | Confirmed (16.06.03) | Unavailable |
| **Blocked** | Operator blocked it | Unavailable, no counterparty |
| **Cascade-blocked** | Exclusive sibling booked | Unavailable **+ the reason** (D-05) |
| **Frozen** | Holder suspended (24) | Operator sees "holder suspended — release?"; holder cannot confirm |

#### View states

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Calendar fetch in flight | Skeleton month grid; no availability is rendered optimistically — a guessed "available" is a double-booking |
| **Empty (no calendar)** | Room unclaimed, or claimed with no opening hours set | Operator: onboarding CTA — "Set your opening hours to start selling". Musician/Producer: "Not bookable here" + contact path (16.06.06) |
| **Empty (nothing free)** | Opening hours set, every interval taken | "No availability in March" + waitlist CTA (16.06.04) — the highest-intent moment in the feature |
| **Populated** | Intervals resolved | Month/week view; room's zone primary, viewer's secondary when they differ |
| **Partial (possibly stale)** | Sync degraded (16.06.02 D-04) | Availability shown **with a staleness banner and a last-synced time**. Loud, never a green tick over a frozen calendar |
| **Partial (posture-limited)** | Enquiry-only room hiding availability (16.06.06) | "Enquire for dates" — no grid. The absence is the Operator's product, not a bug |
| **Error** | Fetch failed | "Couldn't load the calendar" + retry. **Never renders a fallback availability grid** — an availability error must fail closed |

Completeness beats purity: a book that is 100% of the truth and 60% interactive beats one that is 40% of the truth and beautiful. |
| DT-07 | A hold TTL is a constant per room type ("14-day holds") | It is how the industry talks about it, and it is what a settings page wants to be | ❌ REJECTED — **wrong at both ends of the lead time.** A flat 14-day TTL on a date 8 months out forces the holder to re-hold 17 times, so the Operator trains them to treat expiry as noise. It also inverts the wedge: a promoter who must pay to pencil will pencil by email, off-platform, which is D-18's whole failure. Squatting is bounded by clocks and caps (TTL, resale floor, renewal cap, concurrent cap), which cost the honest holder nothing. |

A hold that resolves 10 days out has technically left the Operator a room and practically left them nothing. This also closes the sockpuppet path: an entity cannot challenge itself. |
| DT-13 | Availability is a grid of slots | Every calendar UI ever drawn | ❌ REJECTED. A ranked, challengeable, TTL-bounded ladder on that is ceremony nobody asked for, and it slows the one room type where speed is the product. **Holds default OFF for rehearsal-type rooms**; Operator may enable. The pencil is honoured, the clock is 48h, and the money is queued rather than turned away. | DT-18 | Hold expiry is background hygiene | A sweep that tidies up stale rows | ❌ REJECTED — it is the domain's **freshness engine**. TTLs are therefore *generative*: they do not merely prevent a loss, they manufacture a stream of sellable inventory the Operator never knew existed. That reframes "your holds expire" from a restriction into the feature the Operator is actually buying. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16.06.02 External Calendar Sync** ([./16.06.02-external-calendar-sync.md](./16.06.02-external-calendar-sync.md)) — **the pair that decides whether this feature is useful or harmful** (DT-03, CX-01). Sync is what makes availability true; without it this calendar is the fourth incompatible place. *New*: sync **cannot** cover the paper-diary Operator, so DT-17's posture interlock is the residual protection.
- Touches **16.06.06 Booking Posture** ([./16.06.06-booking-posture-enquiry-rfq-routing.md](./16.06.06-booking-posture-enquiry-rfq-routing.md)) — bidirectional. Posture decides whether this calendar is visible at all (CX-05); and **DT-17 pushes back**: the calendar's trust state should decide the room's *default* posture. A room whose calendar is unproven must not instant-book. This is a new dependency direction, not recorded in CX-05.
- Touches **16.06.05 Compound Booking** ([./16.06.05-compound-multi-resource-booking.md](./16.06.05-compound-multi-resource-booking.md)) — atomic holds across multiple calendars, 90s pre-hold, and the 15-minute orphan sweep that makes CX-03's guarantee real rather than aspirational.
- Touches **16.06.04 Waitlist & Backfill** ([./16.06.04-waitlist-backfill.md](./16.06.04-waitlist-backfill.md)) — a released hold is the highest-value backfill trigger there is (CX-02). Expiry emits a release event within 60s. Note the fan-out risk is theirs (16.06.04 DT-04): my expiry sweep is their notification storm.
- Touches **16.06.03 Reservation Lifecycle** ([./16.06.03-reservation-lifecycle.md](./16.06.03-reservation-lifecycle.md)) — a confirmed hold converts to a reservation; buffers are invisible inventory this calendar must reserve but never sell (16.06.03 D-03); an Operator-forced blackout over a booking fires their Operator-fault ladder (D-05).
- Touches **16.06.09 Recurring & Lockout** ([./16.06.09-recurring-bookings-lockout-tenancy.md](./16.06.09-recurring-bookings-lockout-tenancy.md)) — inversion: a lockout room has no availability at all (CX-07). Any assumption that every room has a calendar breaks here.
- Touches **16.01.02 Room Entity** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md)) — the calendar attaches to the room; the exclusion graph cascades into it, and **DT-16 makes the exclusion graph a participant in the challenge protocol**, which is new.
- Touches **16.01.06 Licences & Statutory Records** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.06-licences-insurance-statutory-records.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.06-licences-insurance-statutory-records.md)) — curfew is a wall-clock ceiling, so DST ambiguity on a fall-back night is a question about the **constraint record**, not a UI one (Q-02) — resolved against the statutory record where the regime profile declares an instrument (16.01.06 D-05), and against the Operator's own claim where it does not (16.02.03 D-01/D-13).
- Touches **16.05.07 Dedup & Merge** ([../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md](../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md)) — merging records joins calendars; duplicate holds by one entity collapse to the earliest grant time so rank fairness survives the merge.
- Touches **16.07 Spec Conformance Check** ([../16.07-spec-conformance-check-rider-room.md](../16.07-spec-conformance-check-rider-room.md)) — a spec change during a live hold (curfew, capacity) can invalidate the held slot without touching the hold.
- Consumed by **17 Live Booking & Settlement** — a confirmed hold becomes a show; 17 owns the deal, 16 owns the slot. *New tension*: the hold's clock **is** the deal's clock in practice, and a challenge can kill a deal that is 90% papered. One automatic extension is this feature's answer; the seam itself is 16.06 Q-06.
- **Depends on 19 Ticketing** for its most important number — **the announce/on-sale lead time sets the performance-slot resale floor** (DT-11). A hold that resolves inside the on-sale window has left the Operator a room and no show. This is a hard dependency on another domain's physics and is not recorded anywhere in the CX map.
- **Constrains 20 Fanbase / 21 Promotion** by exclusion — hold data is unannounced tour routing (DT-14) and must never reach any fan-facing surface, however aggregated.
- Touches **24 Trust & Safety** — hold-override patterns, challenge-sniping patterns, squatting signals and holder suspension all surface here and are adjudicated there. This feature reports; it does not punish.
- Touches **01 Identity** — holds are attributed to **entities** (band, promoter org, venue org), not accounts, so staff churn cannot orphan a book (DT-12).
- **Cross-cut mechanism**: availability calendar & conflict-free reservation serves 05, 06, 08, 17 — recorded in the domain CX file (DT-05). The **hold ladder + challenge protocol is domain-owned** and must not be pulled into that mechanism.
