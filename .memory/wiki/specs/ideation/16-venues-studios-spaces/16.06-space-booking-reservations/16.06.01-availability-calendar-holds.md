# Feature: Availability Calendar & Holds

> **Number**: 16.06.01
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The room's calendar: when it is open, when it is taken, and — the part that matters in live music —
when it is **held**. A hold is not a booking. It is the industry's universal soft-reservation: a
promoter asks for a Tuesday, the venue pencils it, and everyone waits. First hold, second hold,
challenge, release. It is how live music actually works and no general-purpose calendar models it.

`personas.md` names the Operator's switching trigger — "a costly double-booking" — and their
accidental anti-persona — "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold."
Both failures live in this feature.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Owns the calendar. Sets opening hours and blackouts, places and releases holds, sees the whole room's book. Their calendar **is** their business, and it currently lives in a paper diary and Google Calendar simultaneously (16.06.02). |
| Musician | 👁️ View + request | Sees availability at whatever granularity the Operator's posture permits (16.06.06) — which for many venues is "nothing, enquire". Requests a hold for a tour date; holds a slot while routing a tour that may not happen. |
| Producer | 👁️ View + request | Same, for studio rooms. Producers hold speculatively too — a client's project that may or may not get funded is a held week. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. A Fan sees a show that is on sale (19), never the calendar behind it. A venue's empty Tuesday is commercially sensitive. |

## Behavior

The calendar attaches to a **room** (16.01.02 D-01), never a place. Slots carry: available, held
(with rank), booked, blocked (Operator), or unavailable-by-cascade (an exclusive sibling room is
booked, 16.01.02 D-02).

**Holds are ranked and expiring.** First hold has priority; a second hold sits behind it; the second
holder can **challenge**, which forces the first holder to confirm or release within a window. This
is the mechanism live music has used forever and it is entirely absent from generic booking tools —
it is one of the clearest cases in the domain of an industry practice that a general tool cannot
express.

**Every hold must expire.** The Operator's stated anti-persona is forgetting to release one; a hold
without a TTL is a slot removed from sale by inattention. Expiry is not a nicety, it is the direct
counter to a named failure mode.

### Happy Path

1. Promoter (Musician) is routing a tour and requests a hold on 14 March
2. Operator grants first hold; a 14-day TTL starts
3. Another promoter requests the same date; gets second hold and can challenge
4. Challenge fires: first holder has 48 hours to confirm or release
5. First holder confirms → becomes a booking (16.06.03); or releases → second hold promotes to first

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Hold expires unnoticed | Auto-releases; both parties notified. **This is the point** — the Operator's anti-persona failure fixed by construction | "Your hold on 14 March expired" |
| Operator books the room in Google Calendar, not here | The core failure — the platform becomes the fourth incompatible calendar. Only 16.06.02 prevents it | Double-booking risk |
| Exclusive sibling room booked | Slot shows unavailable with a reason, not a bare "unavailable" | "Live Room is booked" |
| DST transition | A 01:30 booking on a fall-back night exists twice; a spring-forward 02:30 does not exist. Real, and every naive calendar gets it wrong | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Tour across time zones | Slots must be unambiguous in the room's local time, and a touring musician is reading them from another zone | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Hold challenged while the holder is on stage | 48-hour windows exist because musicians are not at a desk. Too short a window is a hostile default | — |
| Operator wants to hold for themselves (a private event) | That is a block, not a hold — different intent, no counterparty | Block |
| Overlapping holds on a lockout room | Lockout rooms have no availability semantics at all (16.06.09) — the calendar concept does not apply | Handled by 16.06.09 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Available | Default within opening hours | Bookable/enquirable per posture (16.06.06) |
| Held (1st) | Hold granted | Holder sees rank + TTL; others see held-with-challenge-option |
| Held (2nd+) | Subsequent hold | Rank + the option to challenge |
| Challenged | 2nd holder challenges | 1st holder sees a countdown to confirm or release |
| Booked | Confirmed (16.06.03) | Unavailable |
| Blocked | Operator blocked it | Unavailable, no counterparty |
| Cascade-blocked | Exclusive sibling booked | Unavailable + the reason |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A calendar is available/booked | Every booking tool ever built | ❌ REJECTED — **the hold is the whole point**, and it is the clearest example in this domain of an industry practice that generic tools cannot express. Live music runs on ranked, challengeable, expiring soft-reservations: a promoter pencils six venues for a tour that may not happen, and five of those holds evaporate. A tool with only available/booked forces the Operator to either block slots they'd sell (losing money) or verbally track holds outside the system (which is the paper diary they came here to escape). |
| DT-02 | Holds can be indefinite | The Operator will release them when they know | ❌ REJECTED, on the persona's own words: "forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold." A hold without a TTL is a slot removed from sale by inattention, and the platform would be automating the exact failure it is selling a fix for. **Every hold expires.** Auto-expiry is a named-failure-mode counter, not a convenience. |
| DT-03 | The platform's calendar is the source of truth | We built it; it is authoritative | ❌ **REJECTED — the domain's most dangerous assumption.** The Operator's calendar already exists in Google Calendar and a paper diary (`personas.md`: "lives in three incompatible places"). Declaring ours authoritative does not make it so; it makes us **the fourth incompatible place**, which is a strictly worse outcome than not existing. The platform earns authority only by syncing (16.06.02), never by asserting it. This is why external sync is a Must, not a nice-to-have. |
| DT-04 | Challenge is an advanced feature to defer | Holds alone are enough for v1 | ❌ REJECTED: without challenge, a first hold is a free indefinite option on a room — the second promoter has no recourse and the Operator has no mechanism to convert a stale hold. Challenge is what makes the hold system *fair* rather than a queue that favours whoever asked first. Holds without challenge would be actively worse than the paper diary, where at least the Operator can pick up a phone. |
| DT-05 | Calendars and holds are generic booking machinery | Every marketplace with time-based inventory has this | ⚠️ SPLIT. The **calendar/conflict-free-reservation engine** is generic and serves 05, 06, 08, 16 and 17 — recorded as a cross-cut. The **hold ranking and challenge protocol** is not generic: it is a live-music practice, and no other domain on this platform needs it (a mixing engineer does not pencil a session behind another). Mechanism cross-cut; the hold protocol is domain-owned. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Holds are first-class**: ranked, challengeable, expiring | Live music runs on soft-reservations; available/booked forces the Operator back to the paper diary (DT-01) |
| D-02 | **Every hold has a TTL and auto-releases** | Directly counters the Operator's named anti-persona failure — a forgotten hold is a slot lost to inattention (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The platform's calendar is **not** assumed authoritative | It earns authority by syncing (16.06.02); asserting it makes us the fourth incompatible calendar (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Challenge ships with holds, not after | Without it a first hold is a free indefinite option and the system is worse than a phone call (DT-04) |
| D-05 | The calendar attaches to the **room**; exclusion cascades carry a reason | "Unavailable" is useless; "Live Room is booked" is actionable |
