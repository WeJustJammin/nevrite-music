# Feature: Recurring Bookings & Lockout Tenancy

> **Number**: 16.06.09
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's 22 candidates (lockout appears only as a word inside candidate 14)

## Overview

Two things the sweep folded into ordinary booking that are not ordinary bookings:

**Recurring bookings** — "every Tuesday 7–10pm" — a series with a shared identity, where skipping one
week is not a cancellation and the band expects the slot to be theirs indefinitely. It is a soft,
standing claim on a repeating slot, not N independent bookings.

**Lockout tenancy** — a monthly rental of a dedicated room that becomes the band's own. Gear stays in
it. They pay whether they use it or not. There is no availability, because the room is never
released. It is a **tenancy**, not a booking, and it is how a large share of rehearsal-room economics
actually works (16.04 DT-02).

The two are grouped because both break the ordinary booking model in the same direction — a
continuing relationship rather than a one-off transaction — and both are highest-frequency supply
(16.04 DT-04), the part of the domain most likely to build the daily habit D-18 says wins the user.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Lists a room as lockout-only (monthly rent, notice period, deposit) or offers a recurring slot. Sees lockout as their **best revenue**: predictable, monthly, no per-session admin, no empty-Tuesday risk. Manages notice periods and rent, ends a recurring series by notice — not availability, not a cancellation ladder. |
| Musician | Full | The band holding the lockout or the weekly slot. Creates/skips/moves individual recurring instances without dissolving the series; holds a lockout room as effectively their own premises with fob access and stored gear. Sees rent as a recurring cost, not a per-session charge. |
| Producer | Full | Occasionally holds a lockout as a project studio — the point where a lockout quietly turns into a `type=studio` place record (16.01.01) with its own bookings, making the tenant a supplier of a room they do not own. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. Lockouts and recurring bands are back-of-house supply relationships with no fan-facing surface. |

## Behavior

**Recurring bookings** are a series with an identity: a rule (weekly, Tuesday, 19:00–22:00), an
end (or none), and instances that can individually be skipped, moved or cancelled without dissolving
the series. The band expects the slot to be *theirs* — the series is a soft claim on a repeating slot,
closer to a standing hold (16.06.01) than to N independent bookings. Skipping one instance is not a
cancellation and does not fire the cancellation ladder (16.06.03); ending the whole series is notice
of ending a relationship, not the cancellation of one booking.

**Lockout is not a booking at all.** It has: monthly rent, a notice period, a deposit that is a
tenancy deposit rather than a booking deposit, gear stored on site, keys or fob access, and **no
availability semantics** — the room is off the market for the duration. Modelling it as 30 recurring
bookings gets the billing wrong (it is rent, not 30 slot fees), the calendar wrong (there is nothing
to release), the cancellation wrong (it is notice, not a ladder), and the entity wrong (a tenant is
not a client).

**Proposed platform position (D-04)**: domain 16 **represents** lockout — the room is off-market,
here is the rent figure, here is the notice period — and does **not operate** the tenancy (no rent
collection, no eviction, no deposit-protection scheme). Whether the platform crosses into operating
tenancies is a scope decision the owner still owns (Q-01).

### Happy Path (recurring series)

1. Band books Tuesday 19:00–22:00 in Studio A and chooses "repeat weekly" with no end date
2. System creates a **series** with an identity and materialises the first N instances on the calendar
3. Each week the instance confirms automatically against the series claim — no fresh availability race
4. Band skips one Tuesday (holiday): the instance is marked skipped, the series stays intact, and the
   freed instance is offered to the waitlist (16.06.04) as perishable one-off inventory
5. The band's soft claim on Tuesdays persists indefinitely until either side gives notice to end the series

### Happy Path (lockout)

1. Operator lists Room 3 as lockout-only, £400/month, one month's notice, deposit equal to one month's rent
2. Band takes it; pays a tenancy deposit and the first month's rent
3. Room disappears from the availability system entirely — it is not bookable by anyone, including the band
4. Band leaves gear in it and uses it whenever they like, with a fob
5. Monthly rent recurs; ending the tenancy is a notice period, not a cancellation ladder — on notice
   an end date is set and the room returns to market only after the period elapses

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Recurring instance skipped one week | **Not a cancellation.** Series survives; the single instance is marked skipped and offered to the waitlist (16.06.04) as one-off inventory the Operator did not know was free | "Skipped this week — series intact"; the Tuesday shows as available-once |
| Operator ends a recurring series | Ending a **relationship**, not cancelling one booking. Notice to the band; remaining materialised instances are removed; no per-instance cancellation ladder fires | "Series ended — no further Tuesdays booked" |
| Recurring series with no end date, second band wants the same slot permanently | First-come soft claim holds; the second band cannot book over an active series. Whether an indefinite claim can be indefinitely held is a policy corner | Second band sees the recurring slot as unavailable; `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth: indefinite-claim policy]` |
| Lockout band stops paying | An **eviction**, not a refund dispute — genuinely outside what this platform models and possibly outside what it should. Platform surfaces "rent overdue"; resolution is off-platform | Operator sees an overdue flag; no automated eviction flow |
| Lockout room re-listed as a project studio taking bookings | The tenant becomes an Operator sub-letting a room they do not own. **Not the two-authorities case 16.01.01 D-12 settles** — a lockout tenant holds neither a grant nor a room-scoped claim, so there is no authority provenance on the room at all. The residual collision question is 16.01.01 Q-10 (Q-01 there is closed) | `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth: sub-supply with no authority provenance]` |
| Band tries to sublet an unused lockout | Real and common; turns a tenant into a supplier the platform has no model for. Not permitted in v1 representation | "Subletting not supported"; `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| External calendar shows a recurring event (16.06.02) | Two recurrence rules from different systems interact — the imported event and the native series can disagree on a given week | Conflict flagged on the affected instance; `[PENDING — Step 5: recurrence reconciliation]` |
| Lockout deposit vs booking deposit confusion | A tenancy deposit is not a booking deposit and must not enter the 16.06.03 deposit/refund ladder | Deposit shown under the tenancy, never in booking-refund UI |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Series active | Recurring booking created | The series plus its materialised instances |
| Instance skipped | Band skips one week | Series intact; that slot possibly released to the waitlist |
| Series ended | Notice given by either side | Historical series; no future instances |
| Tenancy active | Lockout taken | Room off-market; rent recurring |
| Tenancy notice | Notice served | End date set; room returns to market after the period |
| Tenancy overdue | Rent unpaid past due date | Overdue flag surfaced to Operator; resolution off-platform |
| Tenancy ended | Notice period elapsed | Room re-listed and bookable again |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A lockout is a long booking | It reserves a room for a period of time — that is what a booking is | ❌ **REJECTED — a lockout is a tenancy, and the difference is total.** Billing is rent, not slot fees. There is no availability, because the room is never on the market. Ending it is notice, not a cancellation ladder. Non-payment is eviction, not a refund dispute. The counterparty is a tenant, not a client. Every single mechanic in 16.06 is wrong for it, and modelling it as 30 recurring bookings produces a system that is incorrect in four independent ways at once. |
| DT-02 | Recurring bookings are N independent bookings with a helper to create them | The convenience framing — a bulk-create button | ❌ REJECTED: the **series has an identity** and the band has an expectation. Skipping a week is not cancelling a booking; ending the series is not cancelling either; and the band believes Tuesday is theirs. As N independent bookings, every skip triggers a cancellation ladder (16.06.03) and every week is a fresh availability race against other bands. The series is closer to a **standing hold** than to a set of bookings. |
| DT-03 | The sweep covered this | Candidate 14 mentions "lockout" | ❌ REJECTED: it appears as a **word in a parenthetical**, alongside buffers and overtime, as though it were another booking mechanic. It is a different commercial relationship. Its absence as a first-class concept is a real gap, and it matters because lockout is a large share of rehearsal-room revenue (16.04 DT-02) — the domain's highest-frequency supply. |
| DT-04 | This belongs in domain 16 | It is about rooms, and rooms are 16's | ⚠️ **GENUINELY UNCERTAIN — flagged rather than assumed.** A tenancy is property management: rent, notice, deposits, eviction. That is not a booking domain's competence and it may not be this platform's business at all. But a lockout room is unarguably a **place record with rooms** (16.01), and pretending lockouts do not exist would misrepresent the rehearsal supply badly. Honest position: 16 must *represent* lockout (the room is off-market, here is the rent, here is the notice) and should probably not *operate* a tenancy (no rent collection, no eviction). Q-01. |
| DT-05 | Lockout is a niche edge case | Most bookings are ordinary slots | ❌ REJECTED on the economics. Lockout is a large share of rehearsal-facility revenue, and 16.04 DT-04 identifies rehearsal as the domain's **frequency play** — the weekly habit that D-18 says wins the user. A booking model that cannot express how that supply actually sells is not a small gap; it is a gap in the part of the domain most likely to build the daily habit. |
| DT-06 | A skipped recurring instance should silently vanish | Simplest behaviour — the band isn't coming, nothing to do | ❌ REJECTED: the skipped Tuesday is **perishable inventory the Operator did not know was free** (16.06.04 waitlist). Silently vanishing throws away recoverable revenue on the domain's highest-frequency supply. A skip must offer the freed instance to the waitlist — the one place a recurring series behaves like ordinary bookable inventory. |
