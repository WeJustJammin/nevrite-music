# Feature: Off-Peak, Seasonal & Dynamic Pricing

> **Number**: 16.06.08
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Varying a room's rate by when it is booked: cheaper Tuesdays, cheaper daytimes, cheaper Januarys —
and, at the far end, algorithmically adjusting price against demand the way hotels and airlines do.

The economic argument is genuine. The Operator's inventory is perishable: an unsold Tuesday is
annihilated, and any price above marginal cost beats zero. The counter-argument is also genuine and
is stronger at this stage: **there is no demand data to price against at cold start**, and surge
pricing a community's rehearsal room is a good way to lose the community.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | ⚙️ Config | Sets off-peak windows and seasonal rates — most already do this manually and informally ("£20 an hour, £15 before 6"). Would set dynamic rules if offered, which is part of the concern. |
| Musician | 👁️ Read-only | Sees the price for their slot. Benefits from cheap Tuesdays; is harmed by a Saturday that costs more than it did last week for no reason they can see. Rehearsal booking is a price decision, so this lands directly on them. |
| Producer | 👁️ Read-only | Same. More able to move a session to an off-peak week than a band with day jobs — off-peak favours the flexible, which is not the same as favouring the needy. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

Three distinct things wearing one name, and they should not have been one candidate:

**Off-peak** — static, rule-based, published: daytime and weekday discounts. Operators already do
this. It is a rate-card variant (16.06.07) and it is uncontroversial.

**Seasonal** — static, rule-based, published: January is dead, December is not. Also uncontroversial.

**Dynamic** — algorithmic response to demand. A different animal entirely: opaque, unpredictable,
requires demand data that does not exist yet (16.06.04's waitlist is the only plausible input), and
carries a real relationship cost with the community that domain 16's supply depends on.

**Deepened and decided.** The candidate is **split** (D-01): the static half is a rate-card extension
riding with 16.06.07 as a `could` (D-03), and the dynamic half is **`wont`** (D-02) — not deferred,
declined, because the demand data does not exist and the community cost is real (DT-01, DT-03, DT-04).
What is still open is whether the static half survives as its own node at all (Q-01,
`/decompose-architecture`).

### Happy Path

1. Operator sets: standard £25/hr, off-peak (Mon–Thu before 18:00) £18/hr
2. Band searching for a Tuesday afternoon sees £18 and books — a slot that would have gone unsold
3. The Operator recovers value from perishable inventory; the band gets a cheaper room
4. Both sides understand the rule, because it is a published rule and not an algorithm
5. No dynamic pricing is involved at any point

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Price changes between browsing and booking | Trust damage out of proportion to the revenue. A quote (16.06.07) must be honoured for its window | Price lock |
| Surge on a Saturday because demand is high | The community-relationship cost. These are not hotel guests; they are the same people every week and they talk to each other | — |
| No demand data exists (cold start) | Dynamic pricing has **nothing to price against** — it would be a random number generator with a confident UI | — |
| Off-peak discount cannibalises full-price bookings | Standard yield-management risk: bands who would have paid Saturday rates move to Tuesday | `[OPEN — Q-02 → /create-prd, owner commercial decision]` |
| Operator sets dynamic rules they do not understand | Handing a small business an algorithm that prices against their own regulars is not obviously a favour | — |
| Lockout tenancies (16.06.09) | Not applicable — a tenancy has no per-slot price | N/A |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Flat | Default | One rate |
| Off-peak rules | Operator set windows | Rate varies by published rule; the rule is visible |
| Seasonal rules | Operator set periods | Same |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Perishable inventory implies dynamic pricing | Hotels and airlines proved it; the Operator's Tuesday is annihilated if unsold | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED. The **economics** are sound; the **preconditions** are absent. Dynamic pricing needs demand data, and at cold start there is none — the only plausible input is waitlist depth (16.06.04 DT-05), which requires liquidity the domain will not have for a long time. A dynamic pricer with no demand signal is a random number generator with a confident interface. |
| DT-02 | Off-peak, seasonal and dynamic are one feature | The sweep bundled them as candidate 22 | ❌ REJECTED: off-peak and seasonal are **static published rules** that Operators already run informally and that nobody objects to. Dynamic is **algorithmic and opaque**. They share the phrase "price varies by time" and nothing else — not the mechanism, not the data requirements, not the trust profile. Bundling them lets the uncontroversial half smuggle the controversial half into scope. |
| DT-03 | Operators want dynamic pricing | More revenue from the same room | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the strongest argument against. The Operator's customers are **the same people every week**, they know each other, and they talk. Surge-pricing a local band's rehearsal slot is not a pricing decision, it is a relationship decision — and the supply side of this domain runs on relationships, not yield curves. `personas.md` describes an Operator who "often runs the business on the side of engineering or performing themselves". They are in the community, not extracting from it. |
| DT-04 | This is a `could` — a later optimisation | It is nice-to-have | ❌ REJECTED for the dynamic half specifically — **`wont`**, and for a positive reason rather than a scheduling one. It requires data that will not exist, it damages the relationship the domain's supply depends on, and it contributes nothing to the thesis (D-18): it is neither a provenance mechanism nor a consolidation hook. The static half (off-peak/seasonal) is a small extension of the rate card and can ride along with 16.06.07 as a `could`. |
