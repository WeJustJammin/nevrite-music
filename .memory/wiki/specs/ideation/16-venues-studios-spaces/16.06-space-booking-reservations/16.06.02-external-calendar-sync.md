# Feature: External Calendar Sync

> **Number**: 16.06.02
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's 22 candidates

## Overview

Two-way synchronisation between a **room's** calendar here and wherever the Operator's calendar
already lives — Google Calendar, Outlook/Microsoft 365, iCloud, or an iCal feed. WeJammin bookings
and holds appear there; Busy events there block availability here.

The Operator persona's primary pain point, verbatim: *"Their calendar is the business, and it lives
in three incompatible places — email enquiries, a paper diary or Google Calendar, and a phone."*
The sweep produced 22 candidates for this domain and **not one of them addresses it**. Without sync,
WeJammin does not consolidate the Operator's calendar; it becomes the fourth incompatible place, and
the double-booking that is their stated switching trigger becomes something the platform causes.

This feature is the precondition for D-18 in this domain: consolidation puts the platform *in the
room*, and being in the room is what makes the completed reservation — and therefore the harvest
(16.05.06) — possible at all. A calendar nobody maintains produces no bookings, no completions, and
no provenance.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | ✅ Full | The only persona who touches sync. Connects one or more external calendars **per room** (16.01.02 D-01 — the room carries the calendar, never the place). Runs the mandatory **impact preview** before inbound is enabled and sees exactly how many bookable hours it will remove. Sets the mirror's field set, the buffer-inheritance toggle, and the all-day rule. Resolves conflicts. Receives the loud degradation alerts. Overrides individual inbound blocks ("that Google event is not a room booking"). **This is the feature that decides whether they adopt or churn.** |
| Musician | ❌ None (benefits) | Never sees the feature, never knows it exists, has no setting and no surface. Benefits from it entirely: the availability they see is real, so the room they book is actually free. **One sync-derived artefact reaches them** — if the Operator confirms another act in their own calendar on a date the Musician holds, the Musician gets "The venue has confirmed another event on 14 March — your hold has been released" (16.06.01). It is never framed as a sync event; it is framed as the venue's decision, which is what it is. |
| Producer | ❌ None (benefits) | Same. Note the multi-hyphenate case: a Producer who also **operates** a studio touches this feature as an Operator, on their own room, with their own personal calendar as the hazard (see Edge Cases). The persona boundary is by context, not by account. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility, no artefact, no notification. A Fan never books a room (16.06 Role Matrix — `❌ None` across all nine children); they buy a ticket to a show (19). A venue's empty Tuesday is commercially sensitive and a sync surface would leak it. |

## Behavior

### The governing principle — the harm asymmetry

Sync is never instantaneous and never certain. Every rule below derives from one asymmetry:

> **A false block costs a possible sale. A false available costs a double-booking — the Operator's
> stated switching trigger.** When sync is uncertain, degraded, disconnected, or ambiguous, the
> platform **fails closed**: it keeps the block and stops selling. It never opens a slot on a guess.

This is why the disconnection path freezes blocks rather than clearing them, why an unblocking delta
is never auto-applied, and why staleness downgrades posture instead of hiding the room.

### The unit of mapping is the room, not the account

A connection is `room ↔ external calendar ↔ direction`. Consequences:

- **Outbound**: exactly **one** target calendar per room. A single canonical mirror is the only thing
  the platform can reconcile against; two mirrors means two deletion surfaces and no answer to "which
  one is the mirror?"
- **Inbound**: **any number** of source calendars per room. Blocks are a **union** — inbound is
  monotone in the fail-closed direction, so more sources can only add blocks, never remove them. Each
  block names its source calendar.
- **Contact-only rooms (16.06.06) are not offered sync.** They transact nothing here, so there is no
  mirror to write, and blocking availability nobody can buy is meaningless work.
- **Lockout rooms (16.06.09) are not offered sync.** A tenancy has no availability semantics at all
  (CX-07); there is nothing to block and nothing to mirror.

### Inbound scoping — the three rules that stop a dentist blocking a live room

This is the half that can silently destroy a room's availability. Three rules, applied in order:

**1. Room-scoped mapping.** Any calendar mapped inbound to a room is the Operator's declaration that
it is *that room's* calendar. The platform's recommended path for the one-calendar-four-rooms
Operator is per-room calendars in their own account (see Q-02). Where they refuse, **coarse mode** is
offered explicitly: one calendar mapped inbound to four rooms blocks **all four**, and the preview
says so in bookable hours before they commit.

**2. Busy/Free semantics — the Operator's own convention, not a new one.** The platform reads the
transparency and status the Operator has *already* set. Nothing new to learn:

| External event property | Effect here |
|---|---|
| Busy (`TRANSP:OPAQUE`), status confirmed | **Blocks** |
| Free (`TRANSP:TRANSPARENT`) | **Ignored** — this is how an Operator already marks "in my diary, not in my room" |
| Status tentative | **Provisional block** — not sellable, labelled "tentative in your calendar", and the highest-value backfill candidate if it clears (16.06.04) |
| Status cancelled | Ignored; removes any prior block from that event |
| Invitation declined by the mapped calendar's owner | Ignored |
| All-day, Busy | Blocks the room's opening hours for that date **in the room's local timezone** (all-day events are date-only and carry no zone). Operator-toggleable, **default ON** |
| Zero-duration / point-in-time event | Ignored — a reminder is not an occupancy |
| Event wholly outside the room's opening hours | Ignored — nothing to block |
| Event tagged as created by WeJammin (our own mirror) | **Ignored — echo guard** (DT-06) |
| Single event longer than **30 days** | **Not applied.** Raised as a conflict: "This event would block Room 3 for 4 months." If it is real, it is a lockout (16.06.09), not a block |

**3. The mandatory impact preview.** Inbound is **never enabled without showing its damage first.**
Before the Operator confirms, the platform reports, for the **last 30 days and the next 90**:

- total events that would block, and how many are all-day
- **total bookable hours removed** from the room (the number that matters, not the event count)
- the 5 largest blocks by hours, with their titles
- how many of those events are the Operator's own WeJammin bookings at *other* rooms (the
  multi-hyphenate case)

The Operator confirms, picks a different calendar, or turns inbound off. Nothing is silently
destroyed, because nothing is enabled blind.

### Buffer inheritance — the venue-day problem

A venue's Google entry for a show says `BAND X · 19:00–23:00`. The room is actually consumed from
14:00 load-in to 01:00 load-out. **Inbound events systematically under-block**, because Operators
write the *show* time, not the *occupancy* time — and 16.06.03 D-03 already established that buffers
are invisible inventory, not preference.

So: **inbound blocks inherit the room's configured buffers**, exactly as an on-platform booking does.
The Operator sees the effective span: *"Your event 19:00–23:00 blocks 18:00–00:00 with your load-in
and load-out buffers."* Per-connection toggle; defaults by room type:

| Room type | Buffer inheritance default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Venue | **ON** | The calendar entry is the show; the day is the occupancy |
| Studio | **ON** | Teardown is real and the entry is the session |
| Rehearsal | **OFF** | The calendar entry *is* the slot; a rehearsal room turns over in minutes |

### Outbound — the mirror is advisory, never control

WeJammin bookings and holds appear in the Operator's calendar. What syncs, and how:

| WeJammin state | Outbound event |
|---|---|
| Confirmed / deposit-paid booking (16.06.03) | Busy, confirmed. Title: `WeJammin · {Room} · {Client display name}` |
| Hold, 1st rank (16.06.01) | **Tentative + Busy.** Title prefixed `HOLD (1st)`. A tentative event is exactly what a pencil is — the convention already exists in every calendar app |
| Hold, 2nd+ rank | **Tentative + Free.** It must not block the Operator's own free/busy: a second hold is not an occupancy, it is a queue position |
| Hold expired / released | Event deleted |
| Booking cancelled | Event deleted |
| Operator's manual block here | Busy, confirmed. Title: `WeJammin · Blocked` |
| Inbound-derived block | **Never mirrored back** — it came from there (DT-06) |

**Mirror field set** — the mirror crosses into a third-party system the Operator often shares with
staff who have no WeJammin account (CX-01 point 3):

| Included by default | Excluded by default |
|---|---|
| Room name | Client's phone / email |
| Start, end, room-local IANA timezone | Price, rate, deposit status |
| Client **display name** (already public on the platform) | Split, deal terms, settlement |
| Booking reference | Any 16.02.05 commercial terms |
| Deep link to the booking here | Contract or rider content |
| A one-line notice: *"This is a mirror. Deleting or moving this event changes nothing. Cancel or reschedule on WeJammin."* | |

The Operator can add price to the mirror; they cannot add the client's contact details (that is the
client's data, not the Operator's, and a shared calendar is not a place to put it — Q-01).

### The precedence ladder — who wins, and who gets told

An inbound Busy event lands on a slot. The slot's state here decides the outcome. **Money and a
counterparty are never overridden by a title string typed into a calendar app** (DT-10):

| Slot state here | Result | Notified |
|---|---|---|
| Available | Block applied silently — the normal case, ~99% of inbound | Nobody |
| **Held** (any rank, incl. mid-challenge) | **Inbound wins.** Hold auto-released; the queue does not promote — the slot is gone, not passed on | Every holder + the Operator: *"Your calendar event released a 1st hold from {promoter} on 14 March"* |
| Enquiry accepted, deposit **unpaid** | **Conflict.** Slot frozen — nothing else can be sold into it | Operator + client |
| **Confirmed / deposit paid** | **Conflict. The booking is never cancelled, never moved, never overwritten.** Slot frozen | Operator immediately; client only when the Operator resolves — a client must not be told their session might be gone because of a race the platform lost |
| Manual block here | No-op — merged; the block already exists | Nobody |
| Lockout room (16.06.09) | Ignored — the room has no availability | Nobody |
| Our own mirror (source-tagged) | Ignored — echo guard | Nobody |

**Why inbound beats a hold**: a hold carries no money, and the Operator writing an event in their own
calendar is a real commercial commitment they have already made. Auto-release is also the correct
behaviour against the Operator's named anti-persona — *"forgets to release a hold"* — and it matches
16.06.01 D-02: every hold dies, and dying is the design. What is **not** acceptable is dying
silently, so both sides are told what happened and why.

**Conflict resolution.** A conflict is never auto-resolved. The Operator sees both claims side by
side and picks one: *keep the WeJammin booking* (the external event is marked ignored, permanently,
by event ID) or *cancel the WeJammin booking* (which routes into 16.06.03's **Operator-fault**
cancellation — inverted ladder, full refund, and whatever penalty 16.06.03 Q-02 settles). The
platform never makes that choice, because one branch of it takes money from a band.

Escalation: push + email on creation, repeated every 12 hours. A conflict on a slot starting **within
48 hours** escalates immediately and is marked critical — that is the window in which a band is
already travelling.

### The race window — narrowed, never closed, and always assigned

Sync is asynchronous. A slot can be sold here at 14:59:58 while an event is written there at
14:59:59. **The platform does not pretend to close this. It bounds it and states it:**

| Path | Target |
|---|---|
| Outbound propagation (state change → visible in the external calendar) | P95 < 30s, P99 < 2 min |
| Inbound on push-capable calendars | P95 < 60s |
| Inbound on polled calendars / iCal feeds | Every 5 min |
| **Stated worst-case inbound lag, shown to the Operator** | **5 minutes** |

The Operator is told this in plain words at connect time: *"Availability here can lag your Google
Calendar by up to 5 minutes. In that window, a slot can be booked here that you have just filled
there. We will never cancel a paid booking to fix it — we will show you both and let you decide."*

The race outcome is **assigned, not resolved**: whoever committed here with money keeps the slot; the
Operator gets the conflict. Precision about who loses is worth more than a promise the platform
cannot keep.

### The blast-radius guard — the silent-destruction circuit breaker

A single sync delta must never be able to wreck a room. Any inbound delta that would:

- block **> 20%** of the room's bookable hours in the next 30 days, **or**
- block a contiguous span **> 72 hours**, **or**
- **remove > 20% of the room's existing inbound blocks**

…is **quarantined** and requires Operator approval, presented in bookable hours with a sample.

The third condition is the one nobody designs for, and it is the most dangerous of the three: an API
that returns an empty or truncated event list on a partial failure would otherwise **open 300 hours
for sale in one cycle**. A blocking delta auto-applies after **7 days** unapproved (fail-closed). An
**unblocking delta never auto-applies** — it waits for a human, forever, because there is no timeout
after which guessing "the room is free" becomes safe.

### Sync health — a first-class, user-visible property of the room

Sync health is not plumbing. It is **availability truth**, so it is a visible state with a staleness
clock (DT-04, DT-09):

| Tier | Trigger | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Healthy** | Last successful inbound sync ≤ 10 min | Normal. Last-sync time shown on the room's calendar |
| **Degraded** | > 30 min with no successful sync | **Loud**: in-app banner + push + email to the Operator. Room badged "calendar may be stale". Availability still sells |
| **Stale** | > 2 hours with no successful sync | Instant-book slots **auto-downgrade to enquiry-only** (16.06.06). Alert escalates. The room stays visible and sellable — a human just has to say yes |
| **Disconnected** | > 24 hours, **or** token revoked / permission removed / calendar deleted | Connection marked disconnected. **Inbound blocks freeze in place**, labelled "from a disconnected calendar — verify". Posture stays enquiry-only until reconnected. Daily reminder for 7 days, then weekly. Outbound suspends and queues |

**The 2-hour downgrade is the safety valve, and it works because of 16.06.06 D-01**: enquiry-only is
a first-class posture, not a degraded mode. A stale calendar cannot sell a slot the Operator has not
personally confirmed — **the Operator becomes the fallback source of truth**, which they already are.
Two hours is chosen because an Operator at a loading dock cannot fix OAuth in thirty minutes; two
hours is roughly one load-in.

Failure classification:

| Failure | Retry |
|---|---|
| Transient (5xx, 429, timeout) | 3 retries, exponential backoff 2s / 8s / 32s, then queue for the next cycle. Health tier advances on the clock regardless |
| Auth (401, `invalid_grant`, revoked consent, permission removed) | **No retry.** Straight to Degraded and alert — retrying a revoked token cannot succeed and only delays telling the Operator |
| Calendar deleted or unshared at the source | Straight to Disconnected; blocks freeze |

### Time, zones and DST

- Outbound events carry the **room's IANA timezone identifier** (`19:00 Europe/London`), never a
  fixed UTC offset. A booking created in October for a date in March survives the DST change; a UTC
  offset does not.
- Inbound events are converted to **room-local** time before blocking.
- **Fall-back night**: a local 01:30 exists twice. An inbound event spanning the fold blocks **both**
  instances (fail-closed, per the harm asymmetry).
- **Spring-forward**: a local 02:30 does not exist. An inbound event starting there is clamped to
  03:00 local, and the block is flagged to the Operator as adjusted.
- All-day events are date-only and are resolved against the **room's** zone, not the reader's.

### Recurring inbound events

- Recurrences are expanded for a rolling **18 months** forward. The horizon is set by the domain, not
  by convenience: tour routing and festival programming run 12–18 months ahead (16.06.01 DT-01).
- Beyond the horizon, instant-book downgrades to enquiry-only for those dates. The platform does not
  instant-sell a date it has not verified.
- Exceptions must be honoured: `EXDATE` and modified instances unblock or move that date here. A
  cancelled week in Google that stays blocked here forever is a slot lost to a bug.
- **An inbound recurrence never becomes a 16.06.09 recurring series.** It is expanded to N
  independent blocks with no series identity, no band, and no skip semantics. The series identity
  lives in the external calendar, where the Operator put it. This closes the "recurring external
  events and recurring bookings interact badly" note in 16.06.09 from this side: they do not interact
  at all, by construction.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims their venue (16.05.02) and reaches the room's calendar. Prompt: **"Where does this
   room's calendar live?"** — Google, Microsoft, iCloud, iCal URL, or "nowhere / paper".
2. They choose Google and authorise. The platform lists their calendars and asks which one **is Room
   3** — not "which account", which room.
3. **Outbound switches on immediately** and is not gated: it cannot destroy anything. Existing
   WeJammin bookings and holds mirror into that calendar within 30 seconds.
4. **Inbound is offered, not assumed.** The preview runs: *"Turning this on blocks 47 events and
   removes 312 bookable hours from Room 3 over the next 90 days. Largest: 'Refurb' — 5 days.
   4 are all-day events."*
5. Operator confirms. Blocks appear; their room's availability here is instantly real, with zero data
   entry. Buffer inheritance is on (venue room), so the 19:00–23:00 show blocks 18:00–00:00 and the
   Operator is shown the effective span.
6. A band books Tuesday 10:00–18:00 here. The event appears in the Operator's Google Calendar within
   30 seconds, titled `WeJammin · Room 3 · The Haar`, carrying a deep link and the line *"Deleting
   this event changes nothing."*
7. Operator never opens WeJammin to manage the calendar, and is never double-booked. When their
   token expires eight months later, they hear about it in 30 minutes — not from a band standing in
   the car park.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator's personal calendar has a dentist appointment | Prevented by construction: inbound is room-scoped, honours Busy/Free, and cannot be enabled without the preview. If they map a personal calendar anyway, the preview names the damage in bookable hours before it happens | Preview: *"312 bookable hours removed. Largest: 'Dentist' — 1h."* |
| Slot booked here and filled in Google in the same minute | The unavoidable race, bounded at 5 min. The on-platform booking **wins and is never cancelled**; the external event becomes a conflict; the slot is frozen from further sale | Operator: side-by-side conflict + resolve. Client: nothing, until the Operator decides |
| Sync breaks silently (token expires) | **Prevented — the worst failure gets the loudest path.** 401 is not retried. Degraded at 30 min (banner + push + email); Stale at 2h auto-downgrades instant-book → enquiry-only; Disconnected at 24h freezes blocks | *"Room 3's Google Calendar hasn't synced for 2 hours. Instant booking is paused until it reconnects."* |
| Operator has one calendar for four rooms | Two honest options, never a silent guess: per-room calendars (recommended — Q-02), or **coarse mode** where the event blocks all four rooms and the preview quantifies it | *"This calendar will block Rooms 1–4 together. 1,248 bookable hours over 90 days."* |
| Operator deletes a WeJammin booking in Google Calendar | **The mirror is advisory, never control.** The booking is untouched — a calendar app is not a contract surface. The mirror is re-created, up to 3 times in 24h, then the platform stops and raises a conflict, because a fourth deletion means a human is trying to say something | *"A WeJammin booking was deleted from your calendar 3 times. We put it back — it's a paid booking. Cancel it here if that's what you meant."* |
| Operator **moves** a WeJammin booking in Google (14:00 → 16:00) | Reverted at the next sync. A booking's time is a term agreed with a paying counterparty; a drag in a calendar app is not consent from both sides | *"You moved a WeJammin booking in Google. That change wasn't applied — reschedule here so the band is told."* |
| Recurring event / lockout in the external calendar | Expanded 18 months forward, `EXDATE`/exceptions honoured, never promoted to a 16.06.09 series. A > 30-day single event is refused as a block and flagged as a probable lockout | *"This event blocks Room 3 for 4 months. Is it a lockout?"* |
| **Our own mirror comes back inbound** (the echo/loop) | Source-tagged and ignored. Ungarded, a booking's mirror re-enters as a duplicate block, a 1st-hold's tentative mirror re-enters as a provisional block, and the room silently double-blocks every slot it sells (DT-06) | Nothing — the guard is invisible when it works |
| **Inbound sync returns an empty event list** (partial API failure) | **The dangerous one.** > 20% of existing blocks removed ⇒ quarantined, never auto-applied, no timeout. Without this the platform opens 300 hours for sale on an API hiccup | *"An update would unblock 47 events on Room 3. That looks wrong — approve or dismiss."* |
| Operator has no digital calendar (a paper diary) | **Resolved: their product is not sync.** It is **enquiry-only posture** (16.06.06 D-01) — nothing sells without them saying yes, so the paper diary stays authoritative and the platform never claims a slot they did not approve. A read-only **iCal feed URL** is offered for the day they get a phone calendar: no OAuth, no account, one link | *"You don't need to change how you work. Nothing gets booked without your reply."* |
| Venue's calendar is owned by a **promoter or booking agent**, not the Operator | Common in small UK venues. Inbound events are written by a third party the platform has no relationship with — and they are authoritative (DT-11). Sync works; the disconnect path fires when the *calendar owner*, not our Operator, revokes access | *"Room 3's calendar was shared by dave@promoter.co. Access was removed — ask them to re-share."* |
| **Multi-hyphenate**: Producer operates a studio and maps their personal calendar to their own Room 1 | Their sessions **as a client** at other studios now block their own room. Correct — they cannot track elsewhere and run Room 1 — but surprising, so the preview names it: *"47 of these are your own WeJammin bookings at other rooms."* Not hidden, not prevented | Preview line-item, before enabling |
| **Multi-hyphenate**: Musician-Operator on tour; tour dates block their rehearsal room | Correct only if the room needs them present. A self-access (fob) room does not — access model is a room property (16.02.03 / 16.06.09). The preview flags the mismatch rather than silently deleting 6 weeks of rehearsal availability | *"Room 2 is self-access. These tour dates will block it anyway — is that what you want?"* |
| Operator leaves the venue's org / the place is re-claimed (16.05.02) | **All connections created by that user suspend immediately.** Blocks freeze (fail-closed). Outbound stops — a departed booker's personal calendar continuing to receive the venue's bookings is an ongoing data leak, not an inconvenience | New Operator: *"Room 3's calendar was connected by a former member. Reconnect it."* |
| Place records merged (16.05.07) — two rooms become one with two connections | Inbound survives as a **union** (safe). **Outbound suspends on both** and the Operator must pick one target before it resumes. Existing mirrors in the losing calendar are left untouched — the platform does not delete events from a calendar it no longer has a mandate for | *"This room now has two connected calendars. Which one should bookings be written to?"* |
| Two Operators of the same venue connect their own calendars to one room | Allowed. Inbound: union of both. Outbound: one target, chosen explicitly — no last-writer-wins | *"Bookings are written to booking@venue.com. Change target."* |
| Malicious or malformed inbound event (script in the title, 999999-count RRULE, 10-year duration) | Titles render as text, never markup (`security-first`). RRULE expansion is bounded by the 18-month horizon. A > 30-day event is refused as a block. Any of it that survives still hits the blast-radius guard | Conflict or quarantine — never a silent mass-block |
| Connected calendar has **zero** events | Legitimate for a new room, and also the exact signature of picking the wrong calendar | *"No events in the last 30 or next 90 days. If that's not right, you may have picked the wrong calendar."* |
| Inbound event lands on a **lockout** room (16.06.09) | Ignored. The room has no availability to block (CX-07). Sync is not offered on lockout rooms at all | Sync unavailable, with the reason |
| Inbound block sits on a slot, then clears (tentative → cancelled) | Slot reopens and becomes a **backfill trigger** — a slot the Operator had mentally sold and silently got back (CX-02's shape, arriving from outside) | Waitlist notified (16.06.04) |
| Operator says an inbound block is wrong ("that's not a room booking") | Per-event override, durable by external event ID, so re-sync never re-blocks it. Per-event only — no fuzzy "ignore events like this" rule that would silently swallow a real one later | *"Unblocked. This Google event will be ignored for Room 3."* |
| Concurrent syncs for one room (push webhook races the scheduled poll) | Serialised per room; the second queues. Observable guarantee: **a block is never applied twice and a delta is never half-applied** | Nothing |
| Operator revokes WeJammin's calendar access from inside Google | Detected on the next cycle as an auth failure ⇒ Degraded ⇒ Stale ⇒ Disconnected on the normal clock. Blocks freeze. **Revocation is not deletion** — nothing they built here is lost | *"Access to Room 3's calendar was removed. Blocks are frozen — reconnect to resume."* |
| Inbound-derived block is used for a real show, which then completes | **No completion fires. No harvest fires (16.05.06).** An inbound block is not a WeJammin booking — there is no band, no counterparty, no proof of presence. See DT-13: this is a structural hole, escalated, not fixable here | Nothing — and that is the problem |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Not connected** (Empty) | Default for every new room | *"Where does this room's calendar live?"* — Google / Microsoft / iCloud / iCal URL / nowhere. Calendar is manual-only and at risk of divergence. This is a legitimate resting state, not an error |
| **Connecting** (Loading) | OAuth in flight | Provider's consent screen, then a calendar picker naming the room: *"Which calendar is Room 3?"* |
| **Previewing** (Partial) | Inbound offered, not yet enabled | The impact preview: events, all-day count, **bookable hours removed**, 5 largest, own-bookings count. Confirm / pick another / outbound-only |
| **Outbound only** (Populated) | Connected; inbound declined or never offered | Bookings and holds mirror out. Availability here is Operator-maintained. Honest, safe, and a complete product on its own |
| **Two-way — Healthy** (Populated) | Both directions live, last sync ≤ 10 min | Two-way active + last-sync time + block count. Normal operation; sync is not surfaced beyond this line |
| **Syncing** (Loading) | Delta in progress | Not surfaced. A spinner on a background sync teaches the Operator to watch a spinner |
| **Quarantined** (Partial) | Delta breached the blast-radius guard | *"An update would block/unblock N hours on Room 3."* Approve / dismiss. Blocking auto-applies in 7 days; **unblocking never does** |
| **Degraded** (Error) | > 30 min without a successful sync, or an auth failure | **Loud**: banner + push + email. Room badged "calendar may be stale". Still selling |
| **Stale** (Error) | > 2 hours without a successful sync | Instant-book auto-downgraded to enquiry-only (16.06.06). *"Instant booking is paused until Room 3's calendar reconnects."* Escalated alerts |
| **Disconnected** (Error) | > 24 hours, token revoked, or calendar deleted/unshared | Blocks **frozen and labelled** "from a disconnected calendar — verify". Posture stays enquiry-only. Outbound queued. Reconnect CTA. Daily reminder ×7, then weekly |
| **Suspended** (Error) | Connecting user left the org, or the place was re-claimed (16.05.02) | Both directions stop immediately. Blocks frozen. New Operator prompted to reconnect. **Outbound stops first** — it is a live data leak |
| **Conflicted** (Error) | Both sides claim a slot | Explicit side-by-side conflict, slot frozen from further sale, **never silently resolved**. Critical if the slot starts within 48h |

| DT-02 | Building a great calendar makes Operators switch to it | Ours will be better — venue-shaped, with holds and challenges | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the reasoning error the whole feature corrects. Their calendar is not a tool preference, it is where their **whole life** already is — shared with staff, on their phone, backed by ten years of history. A better calendar does not win; an **integrated** one does. 16.06.01 DT-03 states it: we earn authority by syncing, never by asserting. |
| DT-03 | Two-way sync is the obvious goal | More sync is better | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: the two directions have very different risk profiles. **Outbound** (bookings → their calendar) is low-risk and high-value: it makes us visible in their daily tool and cannot destroy anything. **Inbound** (their events → block us) is where the value and the danger both live. **Resolved at Step 5 (D-06)**: the answer is not to pick a direction — it is to *gate them differently*. Outbound on by default at connect; inbound opt-in per room behind a mandatory damage preview. |
| DT-04 | Sync failures are a technical concern | It is plumbing — retries and tokens | ❌ REJECTED: **a silently frozen calendar is worse than no calendar.** It looks live, so the room sells slots it does not have, and the platform manufactures the Operator's switching trigger (a costly double-booking) while displaying a green tick. Degradation must be loud and must mark availability as possibly stale. That is a product requirement about trust, not a retry policy. |
| DT-05 | Calendar sync is architecture, not product | It is protocols, tokens, webhooks and polling | ⚠️ SPLIT, deliberately. The **protocol layer** (iCal vs CalDAV vs Google push channels, token refresh, rate limits, webhook renewal, OAuth app verification) is architecture — routed to `/create-prd`. What stays product: the guarantee, the inbound scoping rule, the precedence ladder, the blast-radius guard, the health tiers and the failure UX. Those are user-visible decisions with real consequences, and none of them are protocol choices. |
| DT-06 | Sync is two independent pipes | Outbound writes, inbound reads — they do not interact | ❌ **REJECTED — they form a loop, and the loop is the classic sync bug.** Our outbound mirror is an event in a calendar we also read. Unguarded: a booking's mirror re-enters as a duplicate block on the slot it already occupies; a 1st-hold's *tentative* mirror re-enters as a provisional block; that block is not mirrored (correctly) but the duplicate block is now indistinguishable from a real one, so the room silently double-blocks every slot it sells and the Operator watches their availability rot for reasons nobody can explain. Every event the platform writes must be source-tagged and ignored on the way back. Nobody mentioned this and it would have shipped. |
| DT-07 | The mirror is the Operator's data, in the Operator's calendar | It is their booking, in their room | ❌ **REJECTED — the mirror exports a third party's personal data into a system with neither of our consents.** The client is a Musician who booked a room; they did not agree to their name, phone and session times being written into a Google Calendar shared with four bar staff who have no WeJammin account, indexed by a processor they never chose. This is the feature that quietly turns a booking platform into a data exporter. Resolved by minimising the field set (D-09): display name yes (already public here), contact details and price no. The lawful basis is a real question, not a rhetorical one — Q-01, routed to `/create-prd-security`. |
| DT-08 | Deleting the mirror cancels the booking — it is the Operator's calendar, so it is their decision | Symmetry, and it is what the Operator literally did | ❌ **REJECTED.** A calendar app is not a contract surface. The person who deleted it is plausibly a bar manager tidying next week, who has no idea a £200 deposit and a band's van hire sit behind that rectangle. Deletion carries no intent, no authentication, no audit trail and no counterparty notice. The mirror is **advisory**: re-create it, tell them it is a mirror, and after 3 re-creations in 24h stop and ask — because at that point a human is trying to say something and the right response is a question, not a fourth event. |
| DT-09 | Sync health is a status page concern | The Operator does not want to think about plumbing | ❌ REJECTED: **sync health *is* availability truth**, so it belongs on the room, not on a status page. And the mitigation is already in the domain: at 2 hours stale, instant-book auto-downgrades to **enquiry-only**, which 16.06.06 D-01 establishes as a first-class posture rather than a degraded mode. A stale room stays visible and sellable — a human just has to say yes. **The Operator is the fallback source of truth, which is exactly what they already are.** This is the whole feature's philosophy applied to its own failure: do not assert, defer to the person who knows. |
| DT-10 | A conflict is resolvable with "last write wins" | It is the standard answer for two-writer sync | ❌ REJECTED — the two sides carry incomparable evidence. An on-platform booking has money, a counterparty, a cancellation ladder shown before payment (16.06.03 D-04) and an audit trail. An external event has a title string typed by whoever had the tab open. Last-write-wins is precision-blind: it would let a mis-typed Google event cancel a paid session, which is the platform *causing* the harm it sells a fix for. Hence a **precedence ladder** keyed on what each claim actually carries: money and a counterparty are never overridden by a rectangle. |
| DT-11 | An external event's times are the room's occupancy | The event says 19:00–23:00, so the room is busy 19:00–23:00 | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the domain-expert failure nobody mentioned.** A venue's calendar entry for a show is the *show*, not the *day*. The room is actually consumed from a 14:00 load-in to a 01:00 load-out: backline in, soundcheck, doors, curfew, load-out. An Operator writes `BAND X · 19:00–23:00` because that is what the entry means to them. Taken literally, sync sells a 14:00 rehearsal slot to a band on a show day and the platform has manufactured a collision in the physical world — a van at the loading dock during soundcheck. 16.06.03 D-03 already established buffers as **invisible inventory, not preference**; the rule follows: **inbound blocks inherit the room's buffers**, defaulted by room type (venue/studio ON, rehearsal OFF), with the effective span shown. The same insight in reverse also holds: an Operator who *does* write 14:00–01:00 must be able to turn inheritance off, or we double-count their load-in. |
| DT-12 | An inbound event is either a booking or noise | Binary: it blocks or it does not | ❌ REJECTED — **the Operator pencils in their own calendar too.** Live music runs on holds (16.06.01 DT-01), and an Operator routing a tour date marks it *tentative* in Google long before it is confirmed. Treating tentative as a hard block loses them speculative dates they would sell; treating it as noise sells a date they are actively negotiating. Both are wrong. Resolution: tentative ⇒ **provisional block** — not sellable, labelled "tentative in your calendar", and the **highest-value backfill candidate** when it clears (16.06.04). It cannot be challenged (16.06.01 D-01) because there is no counterparty the platform can reach — the challenge protocol requires two parties who are both here. |
| DT-13 | Sync makes the calendar truthful, which helps the whole domain | Sync is unambiguously good for 16 | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED WITH A REAL HOLE — escalating.** An inbound-derived block is *not* a WeJammin booking: no client, no counterparty, no completion (16.06.03 D-01), and therefore **no harvest** (16.05.06). So the better sync works, the more of a busy venue's real activity arrives as opaque blocks — and **the busiest rooms generate the least freshness data**. This is structurally identical to the lockout hole already escalated in CX-08 ("the freshest data comes from the least-used rooms"), arriving from a second, independent direction. That makes it a pattern rather than a coincidence: **the domain's data engine is powered by on-platform transactions, and every feature that respects an Operator's existing workflow starves it.** Not fixable inside this feature — the harvest's value is that it is first-hand from the band (16.05.06 D-02), and an inbound block has no band. Q-04 / Step 6. |
| DT-14 | We should ship a WeJammin calendar app or widget so the Operator does not need sync | Meet them where they are — on their phone | ❌ REJECTED — it is DT-02 wearing a hat. A widget is still our calendar; it does not contain their ten years of history, it is not shared with their bar staff, and it does not have their dentist appointment in it. It moves our calendar closer to their thumb without making it their calendar. The distance that matters is not pixels. |
| DT-15 | Musician and Producer correctly have no surface here | 16.06 Role Matrix: `❌ None (benefits)` | ⚠️ **CONFIRMED FOR THIS FEATURE, BUT IT REVEALS AN UNOWNED CAPABILITY.** A touring Musician wants their confirmed session in their own phone calendar, and a Producer running four rooms this week wants the same — that is the *client* side of sync, and by the exact logic of DT-02 it is worth as much to them as the Operator's side is to them. Nobody owns it: this feature is Operator↔room-calendar, 16.06.03 owns the reservation but not its export. I am **not** expanding my Role Lens to take it — that would silently contradict the ratified 16.06 Role Matrix from a leaf file. Raised as a cross-cut for Step 6 and as Q-03. The honest position: the gap is real, and it is not mine to close unilaterally. |
| DT-16 | The paper-diary Operator is a segment sync cannot serve, so they are out of scope | Sync needs a digital calendar; they have none | ❌ REJECTED — **the framing is the error.** They do not need sync; they need the platform not to sell their Tuesday without asking. That product already exists in this sub-domain: **enquiry-only posture** (16.06.06 D-01), where nothing transacts without their reply, so the paper diary remains authoritative and the platform never claims a slot they did not approve. This is the same principle as DT-09's staleness downgrade — when the platform cannot verify, it defers to the human. A read-only iCal feed URL (no OAuth, no account) covers the day they get a phone. Q-04 in the previous revision is closed by D-10. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16.06.01 Availability Calendar & Holds** ([./16.06.01-availability-calendar-holds.md](./16.06.01-availability-calendar-holds.md)) — **the pair that decides whether the calendar is useful or harmful** (16.06.01 DT-03). Three specific seams now, not one: (a) **inbound beats holds** and auto-releases them without promoting the queue (D-05) — the notification to the losing holder is the only sync artefact a Musician ever sees; (b) **holds mirror outbound as tentative events**, 1st as Busy and 2nd+ as Free (D-08); (c) an external **tentative** event creates a provisional block that **cannot be challenged**, because 16.06.01's challenge protocol needs two parties who are both on the platform (DT-12).
- Touches **16.06.03 Reservation Lifecycle** ([./16.06.03-reservation-lifecycle.md](./16.06.03-reservation-lifecycle.md)) — **trigger dependency, two ways**: (a) an inbound event **never** cancels a paid booking; resolving a conflict *toward* the external event routes into 16.06.03's **Operator-fault** cancellation (D-05, inverted ladder — so 16.06.03 Q-02's penalty question now has a second caller and its answer is load-bearing here); (b) **inbound blocks inherit the room's buffers** exactly as bookings do (16.06.03 D-03 / DT-11) — the buffer model must therefore be a room property, not a booking property.
- Touches **16.06.04 Waitlist & Backfill** ([./16.06.04-waitlist-backfill.md](./16.06.04-waitlist-backfill.md)) — **trigger dependency**: an external **tentative** block that clears is the highest-value backfill trigger the domain has, because it is a slot the Operator had mentally sold and silently got back from *outside* the platform (the same shape as CX-02's expired hold, arriving through sync). Also inverse: a hold released by an inbound event frees a slot that is *immediately* blocked, so it must **not** reach the waitlist — 39 people notified about a date that is gone.
- Touches **16.06.06 Booking Posture** ([./16.06.06-booking-posture-enquiry-rfq-routing.md](./16.06.06-booking-posture-enquiry-rfq-routing.md)) — **state dependency, and this pair is a new one worth adding**. The CX file's R-02 rejected sync↔posture as orthogonal (truthfulness vs saleability). That was right on the axis it tested and it misses the safety valve: **at 2 hours stale, instant-book auto-downgrades to enquiry-only** (D-04, DT-09), and **contact-only rooms are not offered sync at all**. Posture is the mechanism by which an unverifiable calendar stops selling without disappearing. **Step 6 should revisit R-02**: they are orthogonal in intent, coupled in failure.
- Touches **16.06.09 Recurring & Lockout Tenancy** ([./16.06.09-recurring-bookings-lockout-tenancy.md](./16.06.09-recurring-bookings-lockout-tenancy.md)) — **resolved from this side**: an inbound recurrence is expanded to N independent blocks over an 18-month horizon and **never** becomes a 16.06.09 series (D-07) — there is no band, no skip semantics, no series identity to own. And **lockout rooms are not offered sync** (no availability to block, CX-07). The one live seam left: an inbound single event longer than 30 days is refused and surfaced as *"is this a lockout?"* — a discovery path into 16.06.09 that nothing else provides.
- Touches **16.01.02 Room Entity** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md)) — **shared entity**: the connection binds to the room (16.01.02 D-01), never the place. Consequence for the exclusion graph (16.01.02 D-02): an inbound block on Room A **cascades** to exclusive siblings exactly as a booking does, and the cascade reason must name the source — *"Live Room is blocked by your Google Calendar"*, not a bare "unavailable".
- Touches **16.05.07 Duplicate Detection & Merge** ([../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md](../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md)) — **shared entity, state race**: merging two place records joins two rooms that may each carry a connection. Inbound survives as a union (fail-closed); **outbound suspends on both** until the Operator picks one target; mirrors in the losing calendar are left untouched (D-11). 16.05.07 must know that a merge can suspend a live integration.
- Touches **16.05.02 Claim & Verification** ([../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.02-claim-verification-ownership-transfer.md](../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.02-claim-verification-ownership-transfer.md)) — **permission**: a claim transfer or an org-membership loss must **suspend every connection created by the departing user, immediately**. Outbound to a former booker's personal calendar is an ongoing export of the venue's booking data and its clients' names — a live data leak, not an inconvenience. This is a hard dependency: if 16.05.02 does not emit that event, this feature cannot be safe.
- Touches **16.05.06 Post-Gig/Session Harvesting** ([../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.06-post-gig-session-data-harvesting.md](../16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity/16.05.06-post-gig-session-data-harvesting.md)) — **DT-13, escalate**: an inbound block produces no completion and therefore no harvest. The busiest, most Google-native venues will generate the fewest first-hand facts. Structurally the same hole as CX-08's lockout finding, from a second direction.
- Touches **16.02.03 Access & Logistics** ([../16.02-venue-technical-specification/16.02.03-access-load-in-logistics.md](../16.02-venue-technical-specification/16.02.03-access-load-in-logistics.md)) — **field dependency**: the room's access model (staffed vs self-access/fob) decides whether the Operator's *own* absence is the room's unavailability. A self-access room does not need them present, so mapping a personal calendar to it is almost always wrong — the preview flags it. Also the source of the load-in/load-out figures DT-11's buffer inheritance depends on.
- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** ([../../17-live-booking-settlement/](../../17-live-booking-settlement/)) — **notification / seam**: 16 owns the slot, 17 owns the deal (16 index D-11). A sync conflict on a show date is a 16 problem *until* it forces a cancellation, at which point a deal, a settlement, an announced bill and sold tickets (19) are in play. The conflict UI must not pretend a show is a slot.
- **Reconciliation note for Step 6**: the parent index Q-01 and CX-01 point 5 both record the outbound-vs-two-way and conflict-policy questions as unresolved. **They are resolved here** (D-05, D-06). Step 6 should reconcile the CX file rather than treat this file as contradicting it.
- **Not-product**: sync protocol layer (iCal / CalDAV / Google push channels / Microsoft Graph subscriptions), token lifecycle and refresh, polling vs push strategy, webhook channel renewal, rate-limit handling, per-room sync serialisation, **and OAuth app verification for calendar write scopes** → `/create-prd-architecture` (DT-05). The verification review on Google and Microsoft is a **launch-schedule dependency**, not a build task — flag it early rather than discovering it at launch.
