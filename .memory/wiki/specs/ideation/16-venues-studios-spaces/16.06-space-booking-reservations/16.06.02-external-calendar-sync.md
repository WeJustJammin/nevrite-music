# Feature: External Calendar Sync

> **Number**: 16.06.02
> **Parent**: [Space Booking & Reservations](./16.06-space-booking-reservations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — not in the sweep's 22 candidates

## Overview

Two-way synchronisation between the room's calendar here and wherever the Operator's calendar
already lives — Google Calendar, iCloud, Outlook, or an iCal feed. Bookings made here appear there;
events created there block availability here.

The Operator persona's primary pain point, verbatim: *"Their calendar is the business, and it lives
in three incompatible places — email enquiries, a paper diary or Google Calendar, and a phone."*
The sweep produced 22 candidates for this domain and **not one of them addresses it**. Without sync,
WeJammin does not consolidate the Operator's calendar; it becomes the fourth incompatible place, and
the double-booking that is their stated switching trigger becomes something the platform causes.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Connects their existing calendar once. Sees WeJammin bookings appear in the tool they already use, and their existing commitments block availability here without re-entry. **This is the feature that decides whether they adopt or churn.** |
| Musician | ❌ None (benefits) | Never sees it. Benefits from it entirely: the availability they see is real, so the room they book is actually free. |
| Producer | ❌ None (benefits) | Same. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. |

## Behavior

Two directions, with different risk profiles:

**Outbound (here → their calendar)**: WeJammin bookings and holds appear as events in the Operator's
calendar. Low risk, high immediate value, and it is what makes the platform visible in their daily
tool rather than a website they must remember to check.

**Inbound (their calendar → here)**: events in their calendar block availability here. High value,
high risk — a naive implementation blocks a room because the Operator has a dentist appointment.
Inbound needs a scoping rule (which calendar, which events, or an explicit convention) and it is the
half that can silently destroy availability.

The **conflict** case is the hard one: a slot booked here and double-booked there, between syncs.
Sync is never instantaneous, so a race window always exists, and the platform must decide what
happens when both sides claim a slot. Getting this wrong causes exactly the harm the feature exists
to prevent.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — inbound scoping rules, conflict-resolution policy,
and whether one-way outbound is a safer launch position than two-way.

### Happy Path

1. Operator claims their venue and is prompted: "Where does your calendar live?"
2. Connects Google Calendar; picks the specific calendar that represents the room
3. Existing events import as blocks — the room's WeJammin availability is instantly real, with no data entry
4. A band books here; the booking appears in their Google Calendar within seconds
5. Operator never opens WeJammin to manage the calendar and is never double-booked

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator's personal calendar has a dentist appointment | Inbound scoping must prevent a dentist blocking a live room. Without a rule, the room's availability is destroyed by their private life | Scoped calendar selection |
| Slot booked here and elsewhere in the same minute | The unavoidable race. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — an inbound event cannot cancel a paid on-platform booking, but it also cannot be ignored | — |
| Sync breaks silently (token expires) | **The worst failure**: the calendar looks live and is frozen, so the room sells slots it does not have. Silent staleness is worse than a visible disconnection | Must alert loudly, not fail quietly |
| Operator has one calendar for four rooms | Everything blocks everything. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — per-room mapping is needed and most Operators will not have per-room calendars | — |
| Operator deletes a WeJammin booking in Google Calendar | Does that cancel a paid booking with a deposit? Almost certainly not — but then the two calendars disagree and the Operator believes theirs | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Recurring events / lockouts in the external calendar | A weekly rehearsal in Google Calendar is a recurring block; interacts with 16.06.09 | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Operator has no digital calendar (a paper diary) | A real segment of this supply. Sync offers them nothing, and they are the ones most at risk of double-booking | Manual calendar only |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not connected | Default | Prompt to connect; calendar is manual-only and at risk of divergence |
| Connected | Auth complete, calendar mapped | Two-way sync active + last-sync time |
| Syncing | In progress | Normal operation, not surfaced |
| Degraded | Sync failing (token, rate limit) | **Loud** alert to the Operator; availability marked as possibly stale |
| Conflicted | Both sides claim a slot | Explicit conflict surfaced; never silently resolved |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The sweep's 22 candidates covered this domain's booking needs | 22 candidates including a detailed booking child (candidate 14) | ❌ **REJECTED — this is the single largest gap in the sweep.** The Operator persona's primary pain point is a calendar in three incompatible places, and not one of 22 candidates mentions synchronising with any of them. The sweep enumerated booking *mechanics* (deposits, buffers, overtime, cancellation ladders) and missed the *precondition*: an Operator whose calendar lives elsewhere will not adopt a calendar that lives here. Without sync, every other booking feature is built on a calendar nobody maintains. |
| DT-02 | Building a great calendar makes Operators switch to it | Ours will be better — venue-shaped, with holds and challenges | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the reasoning error the whole feature corrects. Their calendar is not a tool preference, it is where their **whole life** already is — shared with staff, on their phone, backed by ten years of history. A better calendar does not win; an **integrated** one does. 16.06.01 DT-03 states it: we earn authority by syncing, never by asserting. |
| DT-03 | Two-way sync is the obvious goal | More sync is better | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: the two directions have very different risk profiles. **Outbound** (bookings → their calendar) is low-risk and high-value: it makes us visible in their daily tool and cannot destroy anything. **Inbound** (their events → block us) is where the value and the danger both live — it prevents double-bookings and it can silently delete a room's availability because someone has a dentist appointment. Outbound-first may be the correct launch position, with inbound gated on a scoping rule. Q-01. |
| DT-04 | Sync failures are a technical concern | It is plumbing — retries and tokens | ❌ REJECTED: **a silently frozen calendar is worse than no calendar.** It looks live, so the room sells slots it does not have, and the platform manufactures the Operator's switching trigger (a costly double-booking) while displaying a green tick. Degradation must be loud and must mark availability as possibly stale. That is a product requirement about trust, not a retry policy. |
| DT-05 | Calendar sync is architecture, not product | It is protocols, tokens, webhooks and polling | ⚠️ SPLIT, deliberately. The **protocol layer** (iCal vs CalDAV vs Google push channels, token refresh, rate limits, webhook renewal) is architecture — routed to `/create-prd`. What stays product: the guarantee ("you will never be double-booked"), the inbound scoping rule (what blocks a room), the conflict policy (who wins), and the failure UX (loud, not silent). Those are user-visible decisions with real consequences, and none of them are protocol choices. |
