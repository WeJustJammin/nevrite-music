## Overview

How a room accepts business, and where the request goes. **Booking posture** is the discriminator:
instant-book (pick a slot, pay, done), enquiry-only (tell me what you want and I will answer), or
contact-only (here is a number). **Routing** is what happens to an enquiry — who receives it, in what
form, and whether it becomes a structured negotiation or an email.

The posture is not a setting; it is a statement about the business. **Most venues will never
instant-book** — a Saturday night is a curated bill, not a slot, and a venue that lets anyone buy one
has lost control of its programming. A platform that treats enquiry-only as a degraded mode has
misunderstood most of its supply.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | ⚙️ Config | Sets posture per room, per slot type, or per period. Receives enquiries in a structured form with the requester's context attached. Sets who receives them — often a booker, not the owner. |
| Musician | Full | Sees which rooms they can book instantly and which need a conversation. Sends an enquiry with their requirements structured, not typed into a paragraph. Sees the hirer requirements (16.01.06) before committing. |
| Producer | Full | Same. Studios instant-book far more readily than venues — a Tuesday in a studio is a slot, not a curated bill. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. A Fan never books a room; they buy a ticket to a show (19). |

## Behavior

**Posture** is per-room and may vary by slot: a venue may instant-book its weekday rehearsal use and
enquiry-only its Friday and Saturday shows. Instant-book, enquiry-only and contact-only are three
distinct commercial stances, and the platform's job is to represent all three honestly rather than
pushing everyone toward the one that transacts.

**Routing** turns an enquiry into a structured object: what, when, who, what they need. The enquiry
carries the requester's context — their profile (01), their credits (02), their history on the
platform — which is the thing an email cannot do, and which is exactly what a booker needs to decide.

The enquiry then becomes a **thread**: enquiry → response/quote → hold (16.06.01) → booking
(16.06.03). Each step is real, and the industry currently does all of it in email, where nothing is
structured, nothing is tracked, and the thread dies silently.

**Hirer requirements surface here** (16.01.06 D-04) — the PLI requirement must be visible at enquiry,
not at the door.

### Happy Path

1. Musician finds a venue for a tour date; posture is enquiry-only
2. Sends a structured enquiry: date, act, expected draw, requirements — pre-filled from their profile (01)
3. Booker receives it with the act's credits (02) and platform history attached — context an email never carries
4. Booker responds with a hold (16.06.01) and indicative terms (16.02.05)
5. Thread converts: hold → confirm → booking (16.06.03), or dies with a reason, tracked either way

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|

| Enquiry is really a deal negotiation (a tour date with a fee) | **The 17 seam.** At some point this stops being "can I have the room" and becomes "what's the deal" | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Instant-book is the goal; enquiry-only is a stepping stone | Marketplaces push toward instant transaction — it is the metric | ❌ **REJECTED, and this is the feature's central point.** Most venues will never instant-book, and that is not conservatism. A Saturday night is a **curated bill** — the venue is choosing which acts play together, in what order, to which audience. Letting anyone buy that slot destroys their programming, which is their product. Treating enquiry-only as a degraded state misreads the majority of this supply and would push the platform to optimise for the wrong thing. |
| DT-02 | Posture is a per-room setting | It is a room's booking mode | ❌ REJECTED: it varies **by slot**. A venue may instant-book weekday rehearsal use and enquiry-only its Friday/Saturday shows — same room, same calendar, two commercial stances. A per-room flag forces them to pick the restrictive one, which costs them the weekday business. |
| DT-03 | An enquiry is a message | It is someone asking about a room | ❌ REJECTED: it is a **structured request carrying the requester's identity, credits and history**. That is the only thing the platform has that email does not. A booker deciding on an unknown act is currently doing forensics — googling, listening, guessing. An enquiry that arrives with verified credits (02) attached is a categorically different object, and it is where domain 16 touches the provenance thesis: **the credit graph makes the enquiry credible.** |
| DT-04 | Enquiry routing is a mechanism serving many domains | 05 (quote a mixing engineer), 13 (offer on gear), 18 (production hire) all have enquiry → quote → accept | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a **cross-cut**. The thread machinery (structured request → quote → accept → convert) is generic. What stays domain-owned: the **posture discriminator** (instant/enquiry/contact) as a statement about a *room's* commercial stance, and the enquiry's specific payload (date, act, draw, requirements, hirer compliance). Mechanism cross-cut; posture and payload domain-owned. |
| DT-05 | The enquiry thread ends where the booking begins | Clean handoff to 16.06.03 | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — for a **show**, the thread does not end, it becomes 17's deal negotiation. The seam is genuinely blurry: "can I have March 14th" (16) and "what's the split" (17) arrive in the same message from the same person. Q-02. This is the same boundary that 16.02.05 D-01 draws for terms, arriving from the conversational side. |
