# Feature: Suggested Edits & Field-Level Provenance

> **Number**: 16.05.03
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The write model that makes this a registry rather than a set of listings: **anyone can propose a
change to any field, and every field carries where its current value came from, who supplied it,
when, and how strong that source is.** Revision history is per-field, not per-record.

This is what the domain index means by "facts are contested between owner and community". A gear
listing has one writer. A venue's load-in has four people who know it — the owner who describes it,
the tour manager who carried through it, the community editor who fixed the door width, and the
dataset that guessed the address. Field-level provenance is how those coexist without the loudest
one winning.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full (own record) | Writes their claimed record directly. Receives suggested edits, accepts or disputes them. Sees the provenance of every field, including which of their own claims the community has contradicted. |
| Musician | Suggest | Proposes edits to any field on any record. On an **unclaimed** record their suggestion may apply immediately (nobody is there to review) — which is what keeps the unclaimed majority of the registry alive. Sees their own edit history and whether it stuck. |
| Producer | Suggest | Same. On studio records the Producer's suggestion carries more weight than a general edit — they were in the room. Whether "carries more weight" is formalised is Q-02. |
| Fan | Suggest (limited) | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a Fan can plausibly correct a venue's address or opening hours; they have no business editing a load-in spec. Field-class-scoped suggestion rights are the likely shape, unresolved. |

## Behavior

Every field value carries a **provenance record**: source class, contributor, timestamp, and any
evidence (a tagged photo, 16.01.03). Source classes, ranked:

1. **Statutory / licence-derived** (16.01.06) — strongest; not the Operator's to overwrite
2. **Owner-declared, verified claim** (16.05.02)
3. **First-hand post-transaction report** (16.05.06) — a band that just played the room
4. **Community suggestion, accepted**
5. **Community suggestion, unreviewed on an unclaimed record**
6. **Seeded from a public dataset** (16.05.01) — weakest

The ranking is the feature. It is what stops re-ingestion overwriting a human (16.05.01 D-02), what
makes a licence condition beat an Operator's preference (16.01.06 D-02), and what makes a band's
first-hand report a legitimate challenge to an Operator's claim (16.02.02 DT-03) rather than
vandalism.

Suggestions on a **claimed** record queue for the Operator. Suggestions on an **unclaimed** record
have no reviewer — and the majority of the registry is unclaimed at launch, which makes "what happens
when nobody is there to review" the load-bearing question, not the edge case.

### Happy Path

1. Musician plays a venue and finds the load-in is 40m, not the 10m claimed
2. Opens the record, suggests an edit to the load-in field with a photo (16.01.03)
3. Record is claimed, so the suggestion queues for the Operator with the evidence attached
4. Operator accepts; the field updates, provenance becomes "community, accepted by owner", freshness resets (16.05.05)
5. If the Operator disputes it instead, it enters conflict resolution (16.05.04)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Record is unclaimed — nobody reviews | The majority case at launch. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — auto-apply keeps the registry alive and opens it to vandalism; queueing forever means the registry never improves | — |
| Operator ignores suggestions indefinitely | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a timeout that auto-applies is coercive; no timeout makes the community's work pointless. The most consequential unresolved policy in the sub-domain | — |
| Suggestion is vandalism or a competitor's sabotage | Provenance ranking limits the blast radius; a weak-class suggestion cannot overwrite a strong-class value. Beyond that, 24's territory | — |
| Two suggestions contradict each other | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — no arbitration model exists between two equal-class community claims |  — |
| Operator "accepts" a suggestion then silently reverts it | Provenance history is per-field and immutable, so the revert is visible. Whether anyone looks is another matter | Field history |
| Suggestion to a commercial field (rate, deal terms) | Rejected by design — commercial fields are Operator-only even on unclaimed records (16.01.01 Role Lens). The community can correct facts, never prices | "This field can only be set by the operator" |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Proposed | Suggestion submitted | Contributor sees pending; field shows a pending-edit marker |
| Applied | Accepted, or auto-applied on an unclaimed record | New value + provenance + reset freshness |
| Disputed | Operator disputes | Enters 16.05.04; last-agreed value stands |
| Superseded | A stronger-class value replaces it | Old value in history, not deleted |
| Rejected | Operator declined | Contributor notified with the reason `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Provenance belongs at the record level | Simpler — the record was contributed by someone, edited by someone | ❌ REJECTED: a single venue record routinely mixes a seeded address, an owner-declared PA, a licence-derived curfew, and a band-corrected load-in. Record-level provenance would have to pick one and lie about the rest. **Per-field provenance is the only honest model**, and it is what makes selective trust possible — believe the curfew, doubt the backline. |
| DT-02 | Suggested edits are a wiki feature | It is Wikipedia's model — propose, review, apply | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED. The wiki model assumes a reviewer community that cares about every article. Here, **the reviewer is the subject** — the Operator reviews claims about their own room, and has an incentive to reject accurate corrections that are unflattering (16.02.02 DT-03). That inverts the wiki's trust model: the reviewer is the least neutral party available. It is why 16.05.04 must exist as a separate feature rather than being an accept/reject button. |
| DT-03 | The unclaimed-record case is an edge case | Most records will be claimed | ❌ **REJECTED — it is the majority case at launch**, by construction (16.05.01: seed the registry, then wait for claims). An edit model that only works when an Operator is present describes a state the registry has not reached. What happens to a suggestion on an unclaimed record is the load-bearing question of this feature, and it is unresolved: auto-apply keeps the registry alive and invites vandalism; queue-forever means the community's work vanishes. |
| DT-04 | Community curation is a mechanism serving many domains | Artist profiles, credits, gear catalogues all have contested-fact problems | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a **cross-cut**. Suggest → review → apply, with per-field provenance and revision history, is platform machinery serving 01, 02, 12 and 15. What stays domain-owned: the **source-class ranking** (statutory > owner > first-hand > community > seeded) is venue-shaped, because only this domain has licence-derived facts and post-transaction eyewitnesses. Mechanism cross-cut, ranking domain-owned. |
| DT-05 | The community can correct any field | Open curation means open | ❌ REJECTED: commercial fields (rates, deal terms) are Operator-only even on unclaimed records. A community-suggested price is not a correction of a fact — it is a fabrication of a term, and the Operator would be bound by a number they never set. The line: **the community corrects facts about the room; only the Operator sets what the room costs.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Provenance is **per-field**, never per-record | One record mixes seeded, owner-declared, licence-derived and band-corrected values; record-level provenance must lie about most of them (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Source classes are **ranked**: statutory > owner > first-hand > community > seeded | The ranking is what makes selective trust, re-ingest safety, and legitimate challenge all work |
| D-03 | The community corrects **facts**; only the Operator sets **terms** | A community-suggested rate is a fabrication, not a correction — and would bind an Operator to a number they never set (DT-05) |
| D-04 | Revision history is per-field and immutable | It is what makes a silent revert visible |
