# Feature: Place Data Seeding & Ingestion

> **Number**: 16.05.01
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Populating the registry from public sources before a single Operator has signed up — OpenStreetMap,
Wikidata, MusicBrainz, licensing registers — so that a musician searching for a room in Leeds on day
one finds rooms in Leeds rather than an empty page.

This is the domain's answer to the cold-start problem, and the domain index names it as one of the
six capabilities justifying domain status: **the unit of value is coverage and accuracy at zero
transactions.** An empty directory has no users, and users are what make it non-empty. Seeding is
the only way out of that loop.

It carries two non-obvious constraints, and both are load-bearing:

1. **Source licences.** The best venue geodata in the world (OSM) carries the heaviest obligations
   (ODbL share-alike). That analysis is legal, not product — routed out (D-04).
2. **Seeding writes the fields that the claim ladder later trusts as proof.** OSM is world-writable.
   An attacker edits a phone number upstream, waits for ingest, and claims the venue against a number
   they control — an attack that 16.05.02 D-02 and 16.01.01 D-09 do **not** see, because no edit ever
   happened on our platform (DT-06). This is the feature's most dangerous property and the reason
   ingest must capture the source's own per-object modification time, not just its own.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Read-only (indirect) | Never invokes this feature; no user-facing surface exists. Experiences its **output**: they search for their own venue and find a record that already exists, seeded from public data, unclaimed, waiting to be claimed (16.05.02). That discovery is the domain's primary acquisition path (D-01). Post-claim, ingestion stops writing to their record entirely (D-08) — they never find a stranger's OSM edit changing their phone number. |
| Musician | Read-only (indirect) | Experiences it as a registry that is not empty on day one. Sees seeded records marked never-verified with per-field source attribution ("address — OpenStreetMap, ingested Mar 2026, never verified"); a seeded record must never present as a curated one (D-03, 16.01.01 D-11). Cannot trigger an ingest ("import this venue from OSM") — record creation (16.01.01) already covers the gap and produces a **stronger** provenance class (DT-10). |
| Producer | Read-only (indirect) | Same, and thinner: studio seeding yields the studios that need seeding least (DT-11). The multi-hyphenate hazard lands squarely here — a Producer who runs a home studio may discover a seeded record publishing **their home address**, created by a stranger from a public dataset. Seeded studio/rehearsal records therefore default to `approximate` precision (D-09, 16.01.01 D-10). |
| Fan | Read-only (indirect, minimal) | Sees seeded venue records attached to show pages. Sees the never-verified marker and nothing else — no source attribution, no provenance class, no ingest mechanics. Per 16.05.05's Fan lens, confidence machinery is professional-tool complexity the Fan will not tolerate; at most "this venue's details may be out of date". |

> No persona has write access. Ingestion is a system process with **four read-only audiences and
> zero operators** — which is exactly why the unclaimed-record review question (16.05 Q-03) is
> load-bearing: seeding is notification-silent by construction, because at seed time there is
> nobody to notify.

## Behavior

Ingestion runs per source, mapping external records to place records (16.01.01) with **per-field
provenance** — every seeded field carries which dataset it came from, when *the source last changed
it*, when we ingested it, and under what licence. Records land unclaimed, never-verified, with
freshness clocks already running (16.05.05) and duplicate detection (16.05.07) applied within and
across sources.

### Source classes

| Source | Licence | Coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenStreetMap** | **ODbL 1.0 — share-alike** | The best geographic coverage of small venues that exists; world-writable | **Blocked pending D-04's legal answer.** Ingest must be built to survive losing it (D-06) |
| **Wikidata** | CC0 — no obligations | Notable venues good; small rooms poor. Also world-writable | Usable |
| **MusicBrainz** | CC0 for core data (place entities, coordinates, aliases, begin/end dates). Supplementary data is understood to be CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0 — **confirm the split before relying on any non-core field** | Venues with recorded event history; carries a **liveness signal** nothing else has (DT-09) | Usable, core fields only |
| **Licensing / premises registers** | Public records; patchy machine access; per-authority formats; terms vary by territory | Authoritative on licensed capacity, licensable activities, conditions | **Not "seeded" class — statutory class** (D-05). Jurisdiction-shaped; see domain Q-13 |

**The ODbL question is not a detail.** Share-alike may create obligations on a derived database, and
the answer decides whether the best venue dataset in the world is usable at all. That analysis is a
legal/architecture decision routed out of the product to `/create-prd` (D-04) — this feature's job is
to flag it loudly and to **not be blocked by it** (D-06).

### Provenance class is per-source, not per-mechanism

16.05.03 D-02 ranks `statutory > owner > first-hand > community > seeded`. Seeding touches **both
ends of that ladder**, and collapsing it into one "seeded" class is wrong:

- OSM / Wikidata / MusicBrainz → **`seeded` (weakest)**. Anyone can write them.
- A local authority premises-licence register → **`statutory` (strongest)**. It *is* the statutory
  source; arriving by ingest does not weaken it.

The corollary is a hard mapping rule: **a `seeded`-class source may never write a statutory-class
field** (licensed capacity, curfew, licensable activities — 16.01.06 D-02). OSM carries a `capacity`
tag and Wikidata carries P1083; writing either into licensed capacity would **launder the weakest
source into the strongest class**, and 16.07's rider↔room conformance would then match riders
against a number a stranger typed into a wiki (D-05, DT-08).

### Per-source field mapping

Resolves the field-mapping gap left by the breadth pass. `—` = not ingested from that source.
**Empty is a legitimate
output**: an unmapped field is never-verified, which makes it the least-certain field, which puts it
at the top of harvest's question queue via decay (16.05.05, CX-03). Declining to guess is how
seeding feeds the freshness engine without touching it (respects CX R-01).

| Field (16.01.01 / 16.02) | OSM | Wikidata | MusicBrainz | Class written | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | `name` | label | name | `seeded` | Mandatory — an unnamed record is neither discoverable nor matchable |
| **Aliases / former names** | `old_name`, `alt_name`, `short_name` | also-known-as | aliases | `seeded` | **The alias is worth more than the name** (DT-07) |
| Geo | node/way centroid | P625 | coordinates | `seeded` | Geo **or** a normalised address is mandatory |
| Address | `addr:*` | — (unreliable) | address field | `seeded` | Anchor class — see the anchor rule below |
| Place type set | tag → type map | P31 chain | place type | `seeded` | A **set**, never an enum (16.01.01 D-01). Superset-of-rooms is vacuous at seed: zero rooms (DT-12) |
| Public phone | `phone`, `contact:phone` | — | — | `seeded` | **Anchor class.** Highest-risk field in the feature (DT-06) |
| Public website | `website`, `contact:website` | P856 | — | `seeded` | **Anchor class.** Displayed source-attributed, never as a platform-vouched link |
| Stated capacity | `capacity` | P1083 | — | `seeded` | Into **stated** capacity only. **Never** licensed capacity (D-05). Public data reports headline/max, not the sellable figure for a configuration (16.02.01) |
| Opening hours | `opening_hours` | — | — | `seeded` | Weak claim; **is not curfew** (16.02.03 — licence-derived) |
| **Accessibility** | `wheelchair` | — | — | **not ingested** | **Refused (D-07).** A false "accessible" costs a wheelchair user the journey and the gig. 16.01.04 D-04 makes first-hand win here precisely because the stakes are physical; a seeded value is the opposite of first-hand. A blank prompts a phone call; a wrong "yes" prompts a wasted trip |
| Rooms | — | — | — | **never** | Seeding cannot know rooms (DT-12). Consequence: **nothing seeded is bookable** |
| Rates, deal terms | — | — | — | **never** | Commercial fields are Operator-only (16.05.03 D-03). A seeded rate is a fabrication |
| Licensed capacity, curfew, licensable activities | — | — | — | **never** | Statutory class. Only a register may write these (D-05) |
| Status (open/closed) | — | — | — | **never** | Seeding may raise a **closure signal** to 16.01.05; it may not set status (D-10) |
| Photos, virtual tours | — | — | — | **never** | Licence-encumbered and separately routed (16.01.03) |

### Admission — the precision/recall rule

Resolves Q-02, previously deferred here. The trade-off is not a threshold to tune; it is a question
about what the source actually said:

> **Ingest records carrying a positive music signal. Never ingest records of a type that merely
> *could* host music.**

- **Positive signal (admit)**: `amenity=music_venue`; `live_music=yes`; `amenity=nightclub` with a
  music tag; `amenity=theatre` / `arts_centre` with a music signal; a MusicBrainz place with ≥1
  recorded event; a Wikidata entity whose instance-of chain resolves to a music-venue class.
- **No signal (refuse)**: bare `amenity=pub` / `bar` / `restaurant` / `community_centre`. Most pubs
  are not music venues, and a registry where most venues are not venues is noise that destroys the
  search result the whole feature exists to produce.
- **`live_music=occasional`**: admit, typed `venue`, flagged low music-confidence. This is the honest
  middle — the Windmill-shaped case where a pub genuinely is a venue.

The recall cost is real and accepted: a genuine grassroots room that no mapper tagged with a music
signal will not be seeded. It will be created by a human instead (16.01.01) — at a **stronger**
provenance class than seeding would have given it. Under-inclusion degrades to the community path;
over-inclusion degrades to noise with no path out. The asymmetry decides it (D-11).

### The anchor rule — the feature's security core

16.01.01 D-09 makes address, public phone and public domain **anchor class**: an edit inside the
stability window invalidates the field as claim evidence. **That rule is blind to edits made on the
source**, because no edit happened here — we ingested a value an attacker had already planted
(DT-06). Therefore:

1. Ingest **must** capture the source's own per-object last-modified timestamp (OSM version +
   timestamp; Wikidata revision; MusicBrainz edit history). A seeded field's provenance carries
   **two** times: `source_modified_at` and `ingested_at`.
2. The anchor stability window runs from **`source_modified_at`**, never from `ingested_at`. A phone
   number added to OSM three weeks before we ingested it is **not** valid claim evidence, even though
   our record is one day old.
3. OSM exposes modification time per *object*, not per *tag*. The conservative rule stands: **if the
   object changed inside the window, no anchor field on it is claim-valid.** This over-blocks — a
   mapper fixing a building outline invalidates the phone — and over-blocking is the correct error
   here, because the failure it prevents is a hijacked business (16.05.02 Overview).
4. **A source that does not expose a per-object modification timestamp may not supply anchor-class
   fields at all.** General, and it scales to sources not yet chosen.

### Re-ingestion

Resolves the refresh-cadence and re-ingest gaps left by the breadth pass. Re-ingestion is
**a coverage mechanism, not a freshness
mechanism** — DT-04 already establishes it can never overwrite a human, and 16.05.05 D-05 establishes
that harvest is what produces fresh facts. So re-ingest does exactly three things:

| Re-ingest may | Re-ingest may never |
|---|---|
| Create records that did not exist before | Overwrite **any** human-supplied value (D-02) |
| Fill fields that are **empty** and whose class it is permitted to write | Write to a **claimed** record (D-08) |
| Raise a **closure signal** to 16.01.05 from a lifecycle prefix (D-10) | Set status, resurrect a rejected record, or revive a merge loser |

**Cadence: monthly per source, per target region.** Justified, not arbitrary: coverage grows slowly,
and since re-ingest cannot refresh anything a human touched, a faster cadence buys no accuracy while
multiplying dedup churn, ODbL exposure, and the anchor-poisoning window. MusicBrainz event data
(liveness only) is quarterly. Licensing registers publish irregularly per authority and are ad-hoc.
*The scheduling mechanism is architecture and is out of scope; the cadence is a product claim about
how current the registry says it is.*

**When a community edit meets a re-ingest**: the field's current provenance is `community` — a
stronger class than `seeded` — so re-ingest skips it. The source's contradicting value is recorded in
the field's history as a **superseded seeded observation** (16.05.03 States) and is **not** surfaced
on the record. This is consistent with CX-05 Q4: the Operator is notified of contests, never of
auto-supersessions. A weakest-class value losing to a human is the expected case, not news, and
surfacing it would train everyone to ignore provenance.

### Per-source purgeability

The ODbL answer (D-04, Q-01) is unknown and may be "no". Waiting for it is not a plan (DT-13).
Therefore, decided now: **every seeded field is tagged with its source, and each source's
contribution is independently purgeable** without destroying the record or anything built on top of
it. Losing OSM must cost coverage, not the registry.

Purge collides with 16.05.03 D-04 (per-field history is **immutable**) — the history contains the
licensed values (DT-14). Resolution: history entries are **tombstoned, not deleted**. The event
survives ("address changed by ingest, 12 Mar 2026"); the licensed value is redacted from it. The
audit trail's integrity is preserved; the encumbered content is not retained.

### Happy Path

1. Ingestion pulls candidate places for a target city from a compatible source (Wikidata; OSM only if
   D-04 clears it), bounded to the city's region
2. Admission filter applies: records carrying a positive music signal pass; a bare `amenity=pub`
   does not (D-11)
3. Each passing record maps to a place record — name, aliases, geo, address, type set, and the
   permitted contact fields, each tagged with source, `source_modified_at`, `ingested_at` and licence
   (D-12). Accessibility, rooms, rates, and statutory fields are **not** written
4. Anchor evaluation runs: any anchor field whose source object changed inside the stability window is
   marked **not claim-valid** at ingest (DT-06)
5. Duplicate detection (16.05.07) runs within the source, across sources, and against existing
   records — with cross-source agreement **discounted for shared ancestry** (DT-15)
6. Records land **unclaimed**, **zero rooms**, never-verified, provenance-tagged, freshness clocks
   already running (16.05.05)
7. A musician searches "rehearsal rooms in Leeds" and gets results instead of an empty page — every
   one marked never-verified, from public data
8. An Operator searches for their own venue, finds it, and claims it (16.05.02) — the acquisition
   loop (D-01)
9. Claim succeeds; **ingestion stops writing to that record permanently** (D-08)
10. The Operator adds their first room (16.01.02) — **the record becomes bookable for the first
    time**, because seeding never made it bookable (DT-12)
11. A band books it and plays; the completed reservation triggers the harvest (16.05.06); the
    seeded guesses get replaced by first-hand facts, and the record leaves seeded quality behind
    for good

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Source licence is share-alike (ODbL) | **Blocked pending a legal decision routed to `/create-prd`** (D-04). Ingest is built per-source purgeable so the answer is survivable either way (D-06) | Fewer seeded records in OSM-only territories; no error — the registry is simply thinner |
| **Attacker plants a phone in OSM, waits, then claims the venue** | **The feature's central attack** (DT-06). Anchor validity is computed from `source_modified_at`; the planted value is not claim-valid. 16.05.02 D-02 alone does **not** catch this — no platform edit occurred | Claimant falls to a weaker rung of the ladder; "this number was recently changed at its source and can't be used to verify" |
| Source object has no modification timestamp | Anchor-class fields are not ingested from it at all (anchor rule 4). Non-anchor fields still ingest | Record has no seeded phone; claim uses another rung |
| Source record is a venue that closed in 2019 | Lands unclaimed, never verified, decays to the floor (16.05.05); presented as unverified, never as open (16.01.05 DT-03). MusicBrainz's last-event date is the only real liveness signal available (DT-09) | "Never verified — may no longer exist. No recorded activity since 2011" |
| Source has a pub with occasional live music tagged as a venue | Admission requires a **positive music signal**, not a hostable type (D-11). A bare `amenity=pub` is never ingested; `live_music=occasional` is ingested, typed `venue`, low music-confidence | The pub simply is not in the registry. If wrongly admitted and later judged not a music place → Rejected, suppressed |
| Same venue in three sources with three spellings and two addresses | Cross-source duplicate detection (16.05.07) — the load-bearing dependency. Aliases (`old_name`) are what make this work (DT-07) | Merge candidates; both live until confirmed |
| **Three sources agree — but two copied the third** | Wikidata imports geo from OSM; MusicBrainz links to Wikidata. **Agreement is not corroboration when the sources share ancestry** (DT-15). Confidence must discount by provenance lineage, not count sources | No user-visible surface; silently prevents a false-confident auto-merge |
| Community edits a seeded field, then the source is re-ingested | **Re-ingestion never overwrites a human edit** (D-02). The field's class is `community` > `seeded`, so re-ingest skips it | Their value, intact. The source's contradicting value sits in field history as a superseded observation; **no notification** (CX-05 Q4) |
| Re-ingest lands on a **claimed** record | **Blocked entirely** (D-08). An Operator's silence about a field is not an invitation for OSM to speak | Nothing. Their record is theirs |
| A claimed venue closes and the owner abandons the account; OSM marks it `disused:` | Claim blocks field writes but **not closure signals** (D-10). The signal reaches 16.01.05 regardless of claim state — the abandoned-record case is exactly why | At-risk/closure signal on the record; status unchanged until a human acts |
| OSM lifecycle prefix contradicts a human's `status=open` | Seeded data **cannot contest** anything (CX R-04). It raises a signal to 16.01.05, never a field write (D-10) | Closure signal visible; the human's status stands |
| Source data is wrong (bad geocode, 200m off) | Seeded facts are claims at the weakest class, correctable by anyone (16.05.03) | Low-confidence marker; source attribution; one-tap correction |
| Source record has a name but no geo and no parseable address | **Dropped** — not ingested, not remembered as rejected. A record that cannot be located cannot serve "rooms in Leeds", and cannot be deduped on geo | Nothing; it never existed |
| Source record has geo but no name | **Dropped.** An unnamed record is neither discoverable nor matchable | Nothing |
| **A venue does not want to be listed** | Suppression is honoured — but **only to someone who proves they run the place**, via 16.05.02's ladder. Delisting is a claim followed by an opt-out (D-14). Otherwise a competitor delists a rival. Suppression ≠ deletion: no hard delete (16.01.01 D-03), because the record carries third-party gig history and harvested reports that are not the venue's to erase | De-indexed, unsearchable, `noindex`; existing gig references still resolve |
| **An unlicensed DIY/house-concert space gets seeded** | A registry entry is *evidence* — seeding can help get a grassroots space shut down (DT-16). Records with no licence evidence and a residential flag default to `approximate` precision (D-09, 16.01.01 D-10), and the suppression path above is a **safety** mechanism here, not a GDPR one | Approximate location only; suppression available on proof of control |
| **A seeded studio record publishes a Producer's home address** | The multi-hyphenate case: the same human is the Operator whose home is exposed and the Musician the coverage serves. Seeded studio/rehearsal records default to `approximate` unless the source positively indicates commercial premises (D-09) | Neighbourhood-level location; exact released only on reservation confirmation (16.01.01 D-10) |
| A seeded capacity is read as licensed capacity | **Barred by construction** (D-05). Public data reports the headline/max figure; a band needs the sellable figure for their configuration (16.02.01), and 16.07 would match riders against a stranger's wiki edit | Capacity shown as "stated, per Wikidata, never verified" — visibly not a licensed figure |
| A mass-vandalism changeset alters hundreds of records upstream | Blast radius is bounded by construction: ingest touches only unclaimed, unedited, non-statutory fields. Velocity anomalies (one account, one changeset, N records) are an abuse-detection concern → 24 | Low-confidence seeded values; no human work destroyed |
| **Rejected record resurrects under a new source id** | OSM ids are **not stable** — a pub redrawn from node to polygon gets a new id, so keying rejection to source id alone leaks (DT-17). Rejection is keyed to source id **and** the 16.05.07 dedup fingerprint | The rejected place stays rejected |
| Re-ingest races an in-flight merge and resurrects the loser | Merged-away source ids **map to the survivor**. Re-ingest of a merge loser updates the survivor's source-attributed fields; it never recreates the loser (CX-01 Q5) | One record, not two |
| Two sources ingest concurrently and both create the same venue | Not a race worth locking. Merge is human-confirmed anyway (16.05.07 D-02), so a transient duplicate is the expected state; only a **permanent** duplicate is the failure | Merge candidate flagged |
| A claim completes mid-ingest | Claim wins; the in-flight ingest's writes to that record are discarded (D-08). Losing one month of coverage on one record is free; writing over a new owner is not | Nothing |
| A user asks to "import this venue from OSM" on demand | **Refused** (DT-10). 16.01.01 record creation already covers the gap at a **stronger** class, and on-demand ingest lets a user pull an attacker-planted record past every scoping control | "Add this venue" — the creation flow, not an import |
| Ingest fails, times out, or a source API changes shape | **The user sees nothing** — no error, no empty state, no degraded page. The registry is just smaller than it would have been, silently. **This is the honest and dangerous property**: seeding failure is invisible to every persona, and only operational alerting catches it (→ `/create-prd` observability) | Nothing. That is the problem |

### States

**User-visible states.** This feature has no UI; these are what each persona encounters as a
consequence of it.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | — | **Never.** Ingestion is asynchronous and pre-user; no persona ever waits on it. Deliberate: any UI that made a user wait on a public-dataset fetch would be architecture leaking into the product |
| Empty | Registry unseeded, or seeded for other regions only | "No rooms found in Aberdeen yet — add one" → 16.01.01 creation flow. Honest: an empty region is a coverage gap, not an error, and the creation path is the correct answer to it |
| Populated | Seeded records exist for the searched region | Results, each marked never-verified with per-field source attribution and a claim CTA |
| Partial | Record ingested with the mandatory fields and little else — **the normal case** | Record with name/geo/type; empty fields shown as empty, never guessed (D-07). Empty fields rank top of harvest's queue via decay (CX-03) |
| Error | Ingest failed | **Nothing.** No persona has a surface on which this could appear. The failure is invisible by construction and only operational alerting catches it |

**Record lifecycle states.** What a seeded record does over its life.

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Seeded | Ingested, unclaimed, unverified, zero rooms | Record + "unverified, from public data" + claim CTA. **Not bookable** — no rooms (DT-12) |
| Enriched | Community added or corrected fields (16.05.03) | Mixed provenance visible per field; corrected fields outrank the seeded ones |
| Claimed | Operator claimed it (16.05.02) | Provenance history retained; record now org-controlled; **ingestion permanently stops** (D-08) |
| Live | A human added the first room (16.01.02) | Bookable for the first time. The seed→claim loop only closes here (DT-12) |
| Merged | Duplicate resolved (16.05.07) | Redirects to survivor; source ids remap so re-ingest cannot resurrect it |
| Rejected | Ingested then judged not a music place | Suppressed. Remembered by source id **and** dedup fingerprint, so re-ingest cannot resurrect it under a new id (DT-17) |
| Suppressed | Delisting honoured on proof of control (D-14) | De-indexed, unsearchable; existing references still resolve. Not deleted (16.01.01 D-03) |
| Purged | A source's licence answer came back "no" (D-06) | That source's values removed; the record and everything built on it survive; history tombstoned, not deleted (DT-14) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Seeding is a data-loading task — architecture, not product | It looks like an ETL job, and the classification gate asks exactly this | ⚠️ SPLIT, deliberately. The **pipeline** (scheduling, fetching, mapping, retries) is architecture and is routed out. What stays is genuinely product: *which* sources, *what a seeded record looks like to a user*, whether a seeded record can masquerade as verified (no), what happens when a human edit meets a re-ingest (the human wins), and the delisting question. Those are product decisions with user-visible consequences, and none of them are ETL. |
| DT-02 | Seeding is a growth hack | It fills the directory before there are users | ❌ REJECTED as a framing — it is the **acquisition mechanism itself**. The Operator does not sign up and create a record; they *discover a record for their own venue that already exists* and claim it (16.05.02). Seeding and claiming are one loop, and the loop is the domain's entire cold-start answer. Calling it a hack understates it: the domain index says the unit of value is coverage at zero transactions. |
| DT-03 | Any public dataset can be ingested | It is public data | ❌ **REJECTED — the most dangerous assumption in this domain.** ODbL (OpenStreetMap) is share-alike, and OSM is simultaneously the best venue geodata that exists and the source with the heaviest obligations. Whether share-alike reaches a derived database that mixes OSM geo with community-contributed specs is a real legal question with a real answer, and getting it wrong contaminates the platform's core dataset. Routed to `/create-prd` as a **not-product legal/architecture decision** — flagged loudly rather than assumed away. |
| DT-04 | Re-ingestion keeps the data fresh | Sources update; pull the updates | ❌ REJECTED as stated: re-ingestion that overwrites human edits destroys the exact value the domain creates. A community-corrected load-in beats OSM's guess in every case. Provenance ranking must make source data the **weakest** class, never the winner. Re-ingestion may add new records and fill empty fields; it may never overwrite a human. |
| DT-05 | Studios can be seeded like venues | Same mechanism, different type | ❌ REJECTED: public datasets know where venues are (they are public-facing businesses with licences and addresses) and largely do not know where studios are (many are residential, unlisted, and deliberately private). Studio supply must be acquired, not ingested — which means the cold-start answer is **venue-shaped only**, and 16.03's supply has no seeding story at all. That asymmetry is a real strategic finding, not a data gap. |
| DT-06 | 16.05.02 D-02 and 16.01.01 D-09 already defend the claim ladder against anchor poisoning | Both rules exist specifically to stop edit-then-claim | ✅ **CONFIRMED as a hole — the feature's most important finding.** Both rules watch **our** edit history. OSM and Wikidata are world-writable: an attacker edits the phone upstream, waits, we ingest, and they claim against a number they control — with **no platform edit to detect**. D-09's window runs from our ingest, which is *newest* exactly when the data is *most suspect*. The fix is behavioral and specifiable now: capture `source_modified_at` per object, run the anchor window from it, and refuse anchor-class fields from any source without one. **Seeding writes the evidence the claim ladder trusts** — that sentence is the whole risk, and nothing in 16.05.02 says it. |
| DT-07 | The venue's name is the field that matters | It is the identifier | ❌ REJECTED — **the alias is worth more than the name.** Venues rebrand constantly while the room stays the same, and the *former* name is what the gig history, the ticket stubs, and every band's memory are filed under. 16.05.07 already trips over this ("The Boileroom"/"The Bunker"). OSM carries `old_name`/`alt_name`; MusicBrainz carries aliases. Nobody proposed ingesting them. Ingesting aliases improves dedup recall **and** search recall for free, from data already in the payload. |
| DT-08 | Ingesting capacity from public data is a coverage win | Capacity is a headline venue fact and the sources have it | ❌ REJECTED as stated, for two independent reasons. (1) **Class laundering**: licensed capacity is statutory class (16.01.06 D-02) and OSM `capacity` is the weakest class; writing one into the other makes a stranger's wiki edit outrank an Operator. (2) **Wrong number**: public data reports the headline/max figure, and a band needs the sellable figure for *their* production (16.02.01). 16.07's rider↔room conformance — the domain's headline capability — would then match riders against a fiction. Admitted only as a source-attributed **stated** capacity, never statutory, never conformance-eligible. |
| DT-09 | Seeding cannot tell a live venue from a dead one | No source has a closure field | ❌ REJECTED — **MusicBrainz has a liveness signal nothing else does.** It carries begin/end dates, an `ended` flag, and place↔event links: a venue whose last recorded event was 2011 is probably gone. OSM adds lifecycle prefixes (`disused:`, `demolished:`, `was:`). Neither is authoritative and neither may *set* status (D-10, CX R-04) — but both are real signals for 16.01.05's at-risk surface, and 16.01.05 DT-03 currently assumes seeding offers nothing here. It offers the best evidence available at zero cost. |
| DT-10 | Users should be able to trigger an ingest ("import this venue from OSM") | Obvious coverage mechanism; the user knows the venue exists | ❌ REJECTED, and the reason is instructive. (1) It is **redundant** — 16.01.01 record creation already covers it, and a human-created record is `community` class, **stronger** than anything seeding produces. On-demand ingest would *downgrade* the record. (2) It is an **attack primitive**: it lets a user pull an attacker-planted OSM record into the registry on demand, past every scoping and admission control. The right button says "Add this venue", not "Import it". |
| DT-11 | Seeding gives studios thin but useful coverage | OSM does carry `amenity=studio` + `studio=audio`; Abbey Road is mapped | ⚠️ CONFIRMS DT-05 rather than softening it. Seeding does yield *some* studios — and they are **precisely the studios that need seeding least.** Abbey Road does not need discovering; the bedroom studio in Leeds does, and it is in no dataset. Seeding's studio yield is **inversely correlated with need**, which is a sharper statement of D-05, not a retreat from it. The strategic finding stands and Q-04 gets harder, not easier. |
| DT-12 | A seeded record is a working registry entry | It has a name, a location and a type | ❌ **REJECTED — the failure mode nobody designs for.** 16.01.02 makes the **room** the spec- and calendar-carrying unit, and 16.01.01 D-05 concedes a seeded record has "a type and zero rooms". Draw the consequence nobody has drawn: **no rooms → not bookable → no completed reservation → no harvest (D-08 of 16.05 index) → no fresh facts, ever.** A purely seeded registry is frozen at ingest quality permanently, and 16.05.05's "room never booked on-platform" worst case is not an edge case — **it is the default state of every seeded record.** The loop only closes when a human adds the first room, and the gap between "claim succeeds" (16.05.02 Happy Path ends here) and "record is bookable" is currently owned by nobody. Surfaced to 16.05.02 and 16.01.02; it is not this feature's to fix, but it is the thing most likely to make the cold-start answer fail quietly. |
| DT-13 | Wait for the ODbL answer before building ingest | You cannot design against an unknown legal constraint | ❌ REJECTED — the design that survives **either** answer is specifiable today: per-source tagging and **per-source purgeability** (D-06). Seeding then depends on the legal answer for *coverage*, not for *existence*. Waiting would block the domain's entire cold-start mechanism on a question the product team does not own and cannot schedule. |
| DT-14 | Purging a source is just deleting its values | It is a data-removal operation | ❌ REJECTED — **purge collides head-on with 16.05.03 D-04**, which makes per-field history **immutable**, and the history contains the licensed values. Two correct rules, mutually exclusive, and neither file noticed. Resolution: **tombstone, don't delete** — the history event survives ("address changed by ingest, 12 Mar 2026"), the licensed value is redacted from it. The audit trail keeps its integrity; the encumbered content is not retained. A collision worth logging precisely because both sides look obviously right in isolation. |
| DT-15 | Three sources agreeing is strong corroboration | Independent confirmation is how confidence works | ❌ **REJECTED — the sources are not independent.** Wikidata imports coordinates from OSM; MusicBrainz links places to Wikidata. Three sources agreeing may be **one source, laundered through two others** — and 16.05.07's matching confidence is exactly the kind of model that would count sources rather than trace lineage. Cross-source agreement must be discounted by shared ancestry, not multiplied by count. Left unfixed, this produces confident auto-merges built on a single mapper's typo. |
| DT-16 | A seeded record is harmless to the venue — it is public information already | The data is public; we are only reorganising it | ❌ REJECTED — **aggregation changes the harm.** A DIY space, a house-concert venue, or an unlicensed room is materially safer scattered across a wiki than it is indexed in a searchable music-venue registry, because **a registry entry is evidence** and the registry's whole value is being findable. Seeding can help get a grassroots space shut down — and grassroots DIY spaces are a real and important part of the ecosystem this platform serves. This makes the delisting path (Q-03) a **safety** mechanism first and a data-protection one second, which is not how it was framed. |
| DT-17 | Remembering a rejected record by its source id is enough | Every source has stable ids | ❌ REJECTED — **OSM ids are not stable.** A pub redrawn from a node to a building polygon is a *new object with a new id*, and the rejected place walks straight back in at the next re-ingest. Wikidata QIDs are stable; MBIDs are stable; OSM's are not, and OSM is the source with the most churn. Rejection (and merge-loser mapping) must be keyed to the source id **and** the 16.05.07 dedup fingerprint, or the rejection loop is infinite — the exact failure CX-01 Q5 warns about, arriving through a different door. |
| DT-18 | GDPR gives a venue the right to be delisted | It is a right-to-erasure case | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **it is two questions wearing one coat.** GDPR protects natural persons, not companies: a limited-company venue has no erasure right over its own trading address. But a **sole trader's venue or a residential studio** — where the address *is* someone's home and the phone *is* a personal number — is personal data, and the right plausibly applies. So the product answer splits by whether the record is a business or a home, which is the same axis 16.01.01 D-10 already uses for location precision. The legal residue genuinely belongs to `/create-prd-security` (Q-03); the **behavior** does not, and is specified here (D-14). |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16.05.07 Duplicate Detection & Merge** ([./16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md](./16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md)) — **hard dependency** (CX-01): seeding without dedup produces a garbage registry on day one. **Two new inputs from this pass**: (a) ingested aliases/`old_name` materially improve matching recall (DT-07); (b) **cross-source agreement must be discounted for shared ancestry** — Wikidata imports from OSM, MusicBrainz links to Wikidata, so counting agreeing sources overstates confidence and risks confident auto-merges on one mapper's typo (DT-15). 16.05.07's Q-03 (same-class ties) should absorb this.
- Touches **16.05.02 Claim & Ownership Verification** ([./16.05.02-claim-ownership-verification.md](./16.05.02-claim-ownership-verification.md)) — seed + claim is one acquisition loop (CX-02, DT-02). **Escalation**: 16.05.02 D-02's "proof must predate the claim" is **blind to upstream edits** — seeding supplies the anchor fields from world-writable sources, so the poisoning happens off-platform where D-02 cannot see it (DT-06). 16.05.02's ladder must consume `source_modified_at`, not `ingested_at`. **Second escalation**: 16.05.02's Happy Path ends at "Operator controls the spec, calendar and rates" on a record with **zero rooms** — i.e. not bookable (DT-12). The claim→room gap is owned by nobody.
- Touches **16.01.02 Room as First-Class Entity** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.02-room-space-first-class-entity.md)) — **trigger dependency, and the domain's quietest structural hole**: seeding creates zero rooms, so **no seeded record can ever produce a completed reservation, therefore none can ever be harvested, therefore a purely seeded registry can never refresh itself** (DT-12). The cold-start loop closes only when a human creates the first room.
- Touches **16.01.01 Place Record & Type Taxonomy** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.01-place-record-type-taxonomy.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.01-place-record-type-taxonomy.md)) — **shared entity + permission**: seeding writes the type set with zero rooms (D-05 there); it writes the anchor-class fields D-09 governs; and its residential-precision default (D-10) is the only thing standing between an OSM `studio=audio` node and a Producer's home address on a public page (D-09 here, DT-16).
- Touches **16.01.05 Place Status & At-Risk Signalling** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.05-place-status-at-risk-signalling.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.05-place-status-at-risk-signalling.md)) — **new cross-cut, not in the CX map**: MusicBrainz last-event dates and OSM lifecycle prefixes are real closure **signals**. Seeded data may never *contest* a value (CX R-04), so these raise a signal to 16.01.05 and never set status (D-10). 16.01.05 DT-03 assumes seeding offers nothing here; it offers the best evidence available at zero cost (DT-09). **Recommend CX-10 be added to the sub-domain CX map.**
- Touches **16.05.03 Suggested Edits & Provenance** ([./16.05.03-suggested-edits-field-provenance.md](./16.05.03-suggested-edits-field-provenance.md)) — provenance ranking is what stops re-ingestion overwriting humans (D-02, DT-04). **Refinement required**: D-02's ladder must be read as **per-source, not per-mechanism** — a licensing register arriving by ingest is `statutory`, not `seeded` (D-05). Also a **collision**: D-04's immutable per-field history and this feature's per-source purge (D-06) cannot both hold literally; tombstoning resolves it (DT-14).
- Touches **16.05.05 Verification Decay** ([./16.05.05-verification-decay-freshness-scoring.md](./16.05.05-verification-decay-freshness-scoring.md)) — seeded records start with a running clock and no verification. **Sharpened**: 16.05.05's "room never booked on-platform" worst case is not an edge case — per DT-12 it is the **default state of every seeded record** until a human adds a room.
- Touches **16.02.01 Capacity & Configuration Model** ([../16.02-venue-technical-specification/16.02.01-capacity-configuration-model.md](../16.02-venue-technical-specification/16.02.01-capacity-configuration-model.md)) and **16.07 Spec Conformance** ([../16.07-spec-conformance-check-rider-room.md](../16.07-spec-conformance-check-rider-room.md)) — **data contract**: a seeded capacity is a headline figure, not the sellable figure for a production. ## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Seeding + claiming is **one acquisition loop**, not a growth hack | The Operator discovers their own record rather than creating one — the domain's cold-start answer (DT-02) |
| D-02 | Re-ingestion may add and fill; it may **never** overwrite a human edit | Source data is the weakest provenance class; a corrected load-in beats OSM's guess always (DT-04) |
| D-03 | A seeded record must never present as verified | It is an unverified claim from a third-party dataset, and much of it is years stale (16.01.05 DT-03, 16.01.01 D-11) |
| D-04 | ODbL share-alike analysis routed to `/create-prd` as a legal/architecture decision | The best venue dataset carries the heaviest obligations; not a product call to make here (DT-03) |
| D-05 | Studios cannot be seeded — supply must be acquired | Public data knows venues; it does not know studios. The cold-start answer is venue-shaped only (DT-05). **Refined**: seeding *does* yield a handful of notable studios — precisely the ones that need seeding least (DT-11). The strategic finding is unchanged |
| D-06 | **Every seeded field is source-tagged and each source is independently purgeable** — decided now, without waiting for the ODbL answer | Losing OSM must cost coverage, not the registry. The design that survives either legal answer is specifiable today; waiting blocks the cold-start mechanism on a question the product does not own (DT-13) |
| D-07 | **Seeding never writes accessibility fields.** The field stays empty | 16.01.04 D-04 makes first-hand win on accessibility because the stakes are physical; a seeded value is the opposite of first-hand. A blank prompts a phone call; a wrong "yes" costs a wheelchair user the journey and the gig. Empty ranks top of harvest's queue via decay — declining to guess *feeds* the freshness engine (DT-07, CX-03) |
| D-08 | **A successful claim permanently terminates ingestion of field values on that record** | An Operator's silence about a field is not an invitation for OSM to speak. Not claim-freezes-verification (R-02 correctly rejects that) — this is narrower: the org is now the authority on its own contact details whether or not it has touched them |
| D-09 | Seeded studio/rehearsal records default to **`approximate`** precision unless the source positively indicates commercial premises | Seeding cannot know residential status, and the failure publishes a Producer's home address on a record they never created. Default-safe (16.01.01 D-10, DT-16) |
| D-10 | Seeding may raise a **closure signal** to 16.01.05; it may never **set status** | Seeded data cannot contest a human value by construction (CX R-04). A signal is not a field write — and the signal survives claim (D-08) because the abandoned-record case is real (DT-09) |
| D-11 | **Admission requires a positive music signal, not a hostable type** | Resolves Q-02. Over-inclusion is noise with no path out; under-inclusion degrades to the community creation path at a *stronger* provenance class. The asymmetry decides it |
| D-12 | Every seeded field carries **two timestamps** — `source_modified_at` and `ingested_at` — and the anchor stability window runs from the **former**. A source without a per-object modification time may not supply anchor-class fields at all | The claim ladder's "proof must predate the claim" rule is blind to edits made upstream, where the poisoning actually happens (DT-06). This is the feature's security core |
| D-13 | **A `seeded`-class source may never write a statutory-class field** — even when it has the data | Otherwise the weakest source is laundered into the strongest class, and 16.07 matches riders against a stranger's wiki edit (DT-08). Premises registers write statutory because they *are* the statutory source (D-05 refinement: class is per-source, not per-mechanism) |
| D-14 | **Delisting is a claim followed by an opt-out** — suppression on proof of control via 16.05.02's ladder; suppression is de-indexing, never deletion | Unauthenticated delisting lets a competitor remove a rival. No hard delete because the record carries third-party gig history and harvested reports that are not the venue's to erase (16.01.01 D-03). The safety case (DIY/unlicensed spaces) is the primary driver, not GDPR (DT-16, DT-18) |
| D-15 | **Re-ingest cadence: monthly per source per region** (MusicBrainz liveness quarterly; registers ad-hoc) | Re-ingest is a coverage mechanism, not a freshness one (DT-04). Since it cannot refresh anything a human touched, a faster cadence buys no accuracy while multiplying dedup churn, licence exposure, and the anchor-poisoning window |
