# Feature: Owner-vs-Community Conflict Resolution

> **Number**: 16.05.04
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The policy for the moment the registry's premise breaks: the Operator says the load-in is step-free
and the band that carried through it says there are three steps. Both are on the record. One is
wrong. The platform has to decide what a user sees.

This is the hardest product decision in domain 16, and it cannot be avoided by any amount of
mechanism. It is the direct consequence of 16.05.03 DT-02 — **the reviewer is the subject**. Every
other write model in the platform has a neutral arbiter or a single writer. This one has two
interested parties and a fact.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full (dispute) | Disputes a community edit to their record and states why. Sees which of their claims are contested. Cannot make a contested field silently revert to their preferred value — the contest is visible. |
| Musician | Full (contest) | Contests an Operator's claim with a first-hand report and evidence. Sees whether their contest stood, was overruled, or is unresolved. This is the persona whose trust is lost if contests are quietly discarded. |
| Producer | Full (contest) | Same, for studio facts. Their attestation is worth more than a self-claim — `personas.md` makes exactly this point about the Producer as trust broker. |
| Fan | 👁️ Read-only | Sees a contested marker on a venue's public facts (an accessibility claim, say) but has no part in resolving it. The marker itself is the honest disclosure — "these parties disagree" is information. |

## Behavior

When a field is contested, the platform has four possible policies, and it must pick one per **field
class** rather than globally:

| Policy | Effect | Where it fits |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| **Owner wins** | The Operator's value stands; the contest is recorded and visible | Commercial terms, subjective descriptions |
| **Last-agreed stands, contest visible** | Neither value wins; the field shows the disagreement | Default for spec facts |
| **First-hand wins** | The eyewitness report replaces the claim | Accessibility (16.01.04 DT-04); arguably backline |
| **Escalate** | Human review (24) | High-stakes or repeated |

The **default is "last-agreed stands, contest visible"** — the honest position when the platform
cannot know. It is deliberately not "owner wins": that would make the registry a listings product
with extra steps, and it would systematically favour the party with a commercial incentive to
flatter.

The exceptions matter more than the default. **Accessibility is the confirmed exception** (16.01.04
DT-04): the Operator has both an incentive and a blind spot, and a wrong answer costs a disabled
musician a gig. **Statutory fields are not contestable at all** — a statutory condition is not the
Operator's opinion or the community's (16.01.06 D-02).

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the field-class → policy map. This is a product
decision for the owner, not an agent call: it sets who the platform believes by default, which is a
values question before it is a mechanism.

### Happy Path

1. Band contests "step-free load-in" with a photo of three steps (16.05.03)
2. Operator disputes: "the ramp was in the van that week"
3. Both are plausibly true. Field enters **contested** state: the last-agreed value stays displayed, but now carries a marker and shows both positions plus evidence to anyone who opens the field (16.05.03 provenance surface)
4. A second band's post-gig report (16.05.06) independently corroborates the steps — the field's `accessibility` class routes this to the **first-hand-wins** policy (D-03), so first-hand reports carry weight the self-claim does not
5. The corroboration count is displayed against the contest. Whether reaching a threshold **auto-flips** the field to the first-hand value or **escalates to 24 review** is Q-02 — an owner values-call, not an agent one, because it decides whether the crowd can overwrite an owner without a human in the loop. Until answered, the field stays contested-and-visible: the honest state, never a silent revert.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Operator disputes every correction reflexively | The contests accumulate visibly. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a pattern of disputed-then-corroborated contests is a trust signal, and nothing currently consumes it | — |
| Community is wrong (the ramp genuinely exists) | Contested marker on a true claim damages an honest Operator. **This is the real cost of not defaulting to owner-wins** and must be stated plainly, not hidden | Contested marker on a correct field |
| Contest on an unclaimed record | Nobody to dispute. Falls to 16.05.03 Q-01 — the majority case, unresolved | — |
| Both parties are right at different times (a lift that works until 23:00) | Not a conflict at all — a **conditionality** failure (16.02.03 DT-04, 16.02.04 Q-03). A meaningful fraction of "conflicts" are really the spec model being too rigid to express a true statement | — |
| Contest is a competitor's sabotage | Provenance class limits blast radius; repeated pattern escalates to 24 | — |
| Fan sees a contested accessibility claim and doesn't come | The honest outcome. Suppressing the contest to protect the booking would be the platform choosing the Operator over the disabled Fan | Contested marker |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Uncontested | Normal | Value + provenance |
| Contested | Owner disputes a community edit, or vice versa | Last-agreed value + both positions + evidence + marker |
| Corroborated | Multiple independent first-hand reports agree | Still shows the contested marker, now with a visible count of corroborating first-hand reports. The count-that-triggers-action and whether it flips or escalates are Q-02 (owner) — the render is complete, the consequence is deferred |
| Escalated | High-stakes or repeated | 24 review |
| Resolved | One side accepted, or review concluded | Value + resolution history, permanently visible |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The owner should win on their own record | It is their business, their room, their liability — and every listings product works this way | ❌ REJECTED as a **default**, and this is the domain's defining decision. Owner-wins makes the registry a listings product with a suggestion box, and it systematically favours the one party with a commercial incentive to flatter. `personas.md` names the Operator's anti-persona behaviour precisely: "misrepresents the room's specs to win bookings" and "publishes a stale gear list, so a band arrives to a room that can't run their show". A write model that resolves every dispute in favour of that party has chosen the wrong side of the domain's whole purpose. Default: last-agreed stands, contest visible. |
| DT-02 | The community should win — more eyes, no incentive to lie | The wiki instinct; the crowd is the corrective | ❌ REJECTED equally: community-wins makes every venue record a sabotage target for a competitor, and a single loud wrong claim damages an honest Operator with no recourse. Neither party is trustworthy by default. **The honest answer is that the platform often does not know — and "we do not know, here are both positions" is a legitimate product state**, not a failure to design. |
| DT-03 | One policy fits all fields | Consistency is a virtue; users should not have to learn a matrix | ❌ REJECTED: the stakes are wildly asymmetric across fields. A contested green-room capacity costs nothing; a contested accessibility claim costs a disabled musician a gig (16.01.04 DT-04); a contested rate is not a fact at all (16.05.03 D-03); a statutory field is not contestable by anyone (16.01.06 D-02). Policy must be **per field class**, and the map is a product decision for the owner. |
| DT-04 | This is a mechanism, so it is a cross-cut | 16.05.02 and 16.05.03 both resolved to "mechanism cross-cut, specifics domain-owned" | ❌ REJECTED — and the distinction is worth being precise about. The *dispute workflow* is generic machinery. But **what this feature actually is** is a policy: who the platform believes when two users disagree about a physical fact. That is a values decision, specific to this domain's adversaries (an Operator with a sales incentive vs a band with first-hand experience), and it does not generalise to 01 or 02 where the conflict shapes are different. Policy stays; the workflow rides the curation cross-cut. |
| DT-05 | A conflict means someone is lying | Two contradictory claims about one physical fact | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the most useful finding here: a meaningful fraction of "conflicts" are **conditionality failures**. The lift works until 23:00. The load-in is step-free if the ramp is out. The curfew is 23:00 midweek and midnight on Saturdays. Both parties told the truth and the spec model was too rigid to hold it. Three features independently hit this wall (16.02.03 DT-04, 16.02.04 DT-04, 16.02.01 Q-02) — **fixing conditionality would remove conflicts, not just resolve them.** |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | **Default: last-agreed stands, contest visible.** Not owner-wins, not community-wins | Owner-wins favours the party with the incentive to flatter; community-wins makes every record a sabotage target. The platform often does not know, and saying so is legitimate (DT-01, DT-02) |
| D-02 | Policy is **per field class**, not global | A contested green room costs nothing; a contested accessibility claim costs a gig; a rate is not a fact; a statutory condition is nobody's opinion (DT-03) |
| D-03 | Accessibility is the confirmed **first-hand-wins** exception | The Operator has both an incentive and a blind spot, and the cost of being wrong lands on a disabled musician (16.01.04 DT-04) |
| D-04 | Statutory fields are not contestable by either party | A statutory condition is externally imposed; neither the owner nor the community gets a vote |
| D-05 | The false-positive cost is stated, not hidden | A contested marker on a *true* claim damages an honest Operator. That is the real price of not defaulting to owner-wins |
