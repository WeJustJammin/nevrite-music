# Feature: Duplicate Detection & Merge

> **Number**: 16.05.07
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — named in the domain index's rationale ("the data needs provenance, revision history, dedup/merge and freshness decay") but absent from the sweep's 22 candidates

## Overview

Finding the records that are the same place and joining them into one, without losing what either
side knew. Three sources seeded (16.05.01), a musician created a fourth, and the Operator claimed a
fifth — five records, one room, one calendar that is now split five ways.

This is not a hygiene task. **Duplicates make the registry actively worse than an empty page**: a
band books room A while the Operator's calendar is on record B, and the double-booking the Operator
came here to escape is now caused by the platform.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full (own records) | Finds their venue listed three times, merges them. Must choose which values survive, and the merge decides which of their bookings live where — the highest-stakes action in the domain. |
| Musician | Suggest | Flags "these are the same place" — often the first to notice, because they are the one comparing search results. Cannot execute a merge; suggesting a merge and performing one are different powers. |
| Producer | Suggest | Same. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility. A Fan sees whichever record the show is attached to and has no reason to know the other exists. |

## Behavior

Detection runs at three moments: at seeding (within and across sources), at record creation
(16.01.01 happy path — near-matches shown before accepting), and continuously as records are edited
toward each other.

Signals: geo proximity, name and alias similarity (including former names — "The Boileroom" and "The
Bunker" at one address), address normalisation, phone, domain. None is decisive alone. Two rooms in
one building are not duplicates; one venue geocoded 200m off by a bad source is.

Merge is where the danger is. It must preserve: **per-field provenance from both sides** (16.05.03),
revision history, claim state, and — critically — **bookings, reviews and harvested reports attached
to either record**. A merge that drops the loser's data is a data-loss event dressed as a cleanup.

Merges must be **reversible**. A wrong merge joins two real, distinct rooms into one, and unpicking
it by hand afterwards is not possible if the merge was destructive.

agent-assisted or human-confirmed, and field-level survivorship rules.

### Happy Path

1. Seeding ingests "The Bunker, 4 High St" from one source and "Bunker Club, 4 High Street" from another
2. Detection matches on normalised address + name similarity; confidence is high but not certain
3. Records are flagged as merge candidates rather than auto-merged
4. Operator claims one, sees "we think these are the same place", and confirms
5. Records merge: strongest-provenance value wins per field, both histories retained, the loser redirects (16.01.01 States)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two distinct rooms in one building | **Not duplicates** — 16.01.02: one record, two rooms. Merging them destroys a real distinction and merges two calendars into a double-booking machine | Detection must not fire on shared address alone |
| Venue relocated; old and new records | **Not duplicates** — 16.01.01 D-04: a record is a building. Same name, different building, deliberately separate | Linked, never merged |
| Both records have live bookings | The highest-stakes case. Bookings from both must survive onto the merged calendar — and may now collide with each other | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Both records are claimed, by different orgs | Not a merge — a **contested claim** (16.05.02). Two orgs claiming one room is 24's problem, not dedup's | Escalates |
| Merge is wrong | Must be reversible. A destructive merge of two real rooms cannot be unpicked by hand | Reversal path required |
| Fields conflict at merge | Survivorship by provenance class (16.05.03 D-02) — the strongest source wins per field, not per record | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Aggressive matching merges two genuinely different venues | The false-positive cost is far worse than the false-negative. Two rooms merged is a corrupted booking system; two duplicates left alone is untidy | Bias toward under-merging |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Candidate | Detection above threshold | "Possible duplicate" flag; both records live |
| Confirmed | Operator or reviewer confirms | Merge executes |
| Merged | Merge complete | Survivor record; loser redirects; both histories retained |
| Rejected | Confirmed as distinct places | Pair remembered as not-duplicates so detection stops re-flagging |
| Reversed | Bad merge undone | Records separated, history intact |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Dedup is a data-hygiene chore, not a product feature | It sounds like cleanup, and the sweep omitted it entirely from 22 candidates | ❌ REJECTED — **and its absence from the sweep is the sharpest gap I found in this domain.** The domain index's own rationale names dedup/merge as a justifying capability. It is a precondition for seeding having any value: three sources on day one guarantees duplicates, and a duplicated registry is *worse than an empty one* — it splits calendars and manufactures the exact double-booking the Operator persona came here to escape. It is not hygiene; it is the thing that makes 16.05.01 shippable. |
| DT-02 | Auto-merge high-confidence matches | Confidence is high; save everyone the work | ❌ REJECTED: the **failure costs are wildly asymmetric**. A missed duplicate is untidy — two records, some confusion, fixable later. A wrong merge joins two real rooms into one, merges two calendars, and corrupts live bookings. Bias hard toward under-merging, flag rather than execute, and require confirmation. This is the rare case where the timid option is straightforwardly correct. |
| DT-03 | Same address means same place | The strongest single signal | ❌ REJECTED, twice over. A venue with a studio upstairs is one address, two types (16.01.01 D-01). A rehearsal complex is one address and eight bookable rooms (16.01.02). Address is a **signal**, and on its own it is a signal that would merge a building's rooms into one — destroying the domain's spine. |
| DT-04 | Merge picks a winner record and discards the loser | The standard dedup pattern | ❌ REJECTED: the loser holds real data — bookings, harvested reports (16.05.06), provenance history, possibly a claim. Discarding it is a data-loss event dressed as cleanup, and it would silently destroy the domain's most expensive asset (first-hand reports). Survivorship is **per field by provenance class** (16.05.03 D-02), and both histories are retained. |
| DT-05 | Dedup is a mechanism serving many domains | Artists (01), releases (12), gear catalogue (15) all have duplicate problems | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a **cross-cut**, bundled with ingestion. Match → flag → confirm → merge with per-field survivorship is generic machinery. What stays domain-owned is the **signal set and the negative rules**: same-address-is-not-same-place, relocation-is-not-a-duplicate, two-rooms-are-not-two-records. Those rules are venue-shaped and getting them wrong here is uniquely destructive. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Added as a Deep Think child despite absence from the sweep | The domain index names dedup/merge as a justifying capability; without it, seeding produces a registry worse than empty (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Bias toward **under-merging**; flag rather than auto-execute | A missed duplicate is untidy; a wrong merge corrupts two calendars and live bookings (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Address alone never triggers a merge | A venue with a studio upstairs, and a rehearsal complex with eight rooms, are both one address (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Merge is **non-destructive and reversible**; survivorship is per-field by provenance class | The loser holds bookings, harvested reports and history — discarding it destroys the domain's most expensive asset (DT-04) |
| D-05 | Rejected pairs are remembered | Otherwise detection re-flags the same two distinct rooms forever |
