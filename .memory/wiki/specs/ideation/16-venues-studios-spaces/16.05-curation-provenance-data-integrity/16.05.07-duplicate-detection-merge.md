# Feature: Duplicate Detection & Merge

> **Number**: 16.05.07
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17
> **Origin**: Deep Think addition — named in the domain index's rationale ("the data needs provenance, revision history, dedup/merge and freshness decay") but absent from the sweep's 22 candidates

## Overview

Finding the records that are the same place and joining them into one, without losing what either
side knew. Three sources seeded (16.05.01), a musician created a fourth, and the Operator claimed a
fifth — five records, one room, one book of business now split five ways.

This is not a hygiene task. **Duplicates make the registry actively worse than an empty page**: a
band books room A while the Operator's calendar is on record B, and the double-booking the Operator
came here to escape is now caused by the platform.

It is also **two operations, not one** (DT-06). A place record is a building and carries no calendar;
the calendar hangs off the **room** (16.01.02 D-01). Merging two place records re-parents rooms and
joins nothing. Only merging two **rooms** joins two calendars — which is where every gram of both the
value and the danger lives. A dedup that operates only on place records is cosmetic: it removes the
duplicate search results and leaves the split calendar exactly where it was.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full (own claimed records) | Sees open candidates as a marker **on the record itself**, not only in a queue — detection failure is silent and cumulative (CX-01), so a candidate that lives only in a list is a candidate nobody sees. Confirms or rejects at Tier 1; the natural moment is the claim itself (Happy Path 6–8). Executes the **room** merge — the one that joins calendars, gated on zero overlapping future confirmed bookings. Reverses a bad merge. **Cannot** merge a record their org does not claim, and cannot merge across two orgs' claims (→ 24, N-4). |
| Musician | Suggest | Usually the first to notice, because they are the one comparing search results. Search collapses an open candidate pair into one result with a "possibly the same place" marker and a one-tap **Same place** / **Different places** — which is how under-merging (D-02) stays affordable (DT-15). Their tap is a Tier 1 **input**, never an execution. Capped at 20 suggestions/24h. Never shown a candidate involving an `approximate` record (DT-11). |
| Producer | Suggest — **or required confirmer** | Same as Musician on venue records. **Different where it counts**: where they hold a room's **operating org** (16.01.01 D-06/D-07) — a studio room subleased inside a venue — a merge that re-parents that room requires *their* confirmation, because it moves their business's room under a different premises record. The discriminator is the **org**, never the persona (16.01.01 D-06); the same human is a suggester on one record and a required confirmer on the next. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No visibility, no suggestion, no marker. But not unaffected: the merge redirect is what keeps a **ticket they already bought** resolving to a venue page (16.01.01 — an expiring redirect breaks a sold ticket). They experience dedup as one venue existing instead of three, and should never learn the other two existed. |

## Behavior

### Detection: two keys, and one of them must be location

Detection runs at three moments: **at seeding** (within a source and across sources, 16.05.01), **at
record creation** (16.01.01 Happy Path 4 — near-matches shown before accepting, at submit rather
than at capture so they reflect committed state), and **continuously as records are edited toward
each other**.

A pair becomes a candidate only if it carries **at least two independent signals, of which at least
one is a location signal and at least one is an identity signal.** Neither class alone ever fires.

| Class | Signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Location** | Geo distance ≤ **150 m** | Deliberately loose. 16.01.01 records that a place's **load-in access point and its front door are routinely 100 m+ apart, and only one of them matters at 3am** — a tight radius fails on exactly the pair that matters (a seeded front-door pin vs a musician's load-in pin for one building) |
| **Location** | Normalised address equality | House number + street + postcode, after normalisation. Catches the 200 m-off geocode that the radius misses |
| **Identity** | **External identifier equality** — Wikidata QID, MusicBrainz place MBID, source-native venue ID | **The strongest inferential signal available, and it was missing from this file** (DT-08). OSM carries `wikidata=*`; MusicBrainz links to Wikidata. Cross-source dedup is mostly solved by the sources' own cross-references; fuzzy matching is the fallback, not the method |
| **Identity** | Normalised name equality | NFKC + case-fold + strip diacritics + strip leading articles + strip legal suffixes (Ltd/LLC/CIC) + strip place-type words (Club/Bar/Studios/Rehearsal Rooms) + collapse punctuation and whitespace. `"The Bunker"` ≡ `"Bunker Club"` |
| **Identity** | Alias or **former-name** match | 16.01.01 holds former names with date ranges. `"The Boileroom" → 2019-04` matches `"The Bunker"` at one address |
| **Identity** | Fuzzy name similarity above the platform threshold | Weak. **Never sufficient for Tier 0** (see tiers). The threshold is a recall parameter, not a product rule — routed to `/create-prd` |
| **Identity** | Public phone equality (E.164) or registrable-domain equality | Anchor-class fields (16.01.01 D-09) — see the merge-laundering rule |

**The decisive key is statutory, and it is the only one that can refute.** Two records carrying the
same **premises licence number** (16.01.06) are the same building by construction, not by inference —
a licence is one document about one building. Two records carrying **different** premises licence
numbers are **different buildings**, and the pair is auto-rejected and remembered, whatever every
other signal says. The statutory class outranks every org on the record (16.01.01 CX-02); it outranks
the detector too.

### Detection emits classes, not a boolean

The seed sources' unit of record is **not a place** (DT-09), and each is wrong in its own direction.
A detector that answers "duplicate: yes/no" answers the wrong question for three of these four:

| Class | Trigger | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| `duplicate` | Two keys, location + identity, negative rules pass | Merge (tiered below) |
| `relocation` | Identity signal present, **location signal absent** | **Never a merge.** Offered as a relocation-link suggestion: old record → `relocated` with a successor link (16.01.05, 16.01.01 D-04). A record is a building; nothing physical survives a move |
| `not-a-place` | Name mismatch + **exact** address match against a `venue`-typed record with ≥ 1 room | The **promoter-branded night** (16.01.01 DT-14, Q-07). "Jazz at the Vaults" at The Vaults' address is an **event brand** (18/21), not an alias and not a place. It fails the two-key rule so it is correctly never merged — and is therefore left standing as a phantom, which is 16.01.01's actual complaint. Resolution is **suppression from search + a pointer to the venue record**, never merge: merging would make an event brand an alias of a building its Operator does not control and cannot correct. Always human-classified; the venue's Operator is the natural human ("that's a night that happens here, not us") |
| `same-place-different-room` | Address-equal + name-similar **with an ordinal/positional discriminator** ("2", "II", "Upstairs", "Basement", "Studio B") | The mirror of `not-a-place`, and the same underlying defect: **OSM indexes the room, listings sites index the night.** "Academy" + "Academy 2" at one address are one place with two rooms (16.01.02 D-01), not two places and not one place with an alias. Resolution is **absorb-as-room** — the loser becomes a **room** on the survivor carrying its own name. Flattening it into the alias set destroys a room, and the room is the calendar-carrying unit. **Always Tier 1**: the ordinal suffix is what routes to a human with the right question ("same place, or same place different room?"), never what decides it |

### Negative rules — the domain-owned half

DT-05 confirmed the *mechanism* is a platform cross-cut. What is venue-shaped, and uniquely
destructive to get wrong, is the list of things that look identical and are not:

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| N-1 | **Rooms are not records.** Two rooms in one building are not duplicates. Detection compares place to place, never place to room | 16.01.02: a rehearsal complex is one address and eight bookable rooms. Merging them destroys the domain's spine and merges eight calendars |
| N-2 | **Relocation is not duplication.** Same name, different building → `relocated` + successor link | 16.01.01 D-04: a record is a building. Deliberately two records, cross-linked, gig history staying where it happened |
| N-3 | **An event brand is not an alias** → `not-a-place`, suppress, never merge | 16.01.01 DT-14 |
| N-4 | **Two records claimed by different orgs is never a merge.** Route to 24 with both options: **contested claim** (16.05.02) or **containment migration** (16.01.01 D-06 — fold the contained record's rooms into the premises record, transferring their operating org) | CX-09: merging would decide an ownership dispute by silently picking a survivor, with no process and no appeal. And it is *usually* neither — it is containment recorded wrong |
| N-5 | **`ad-hoc` records are never survivors** (16.01.01 D-08 — a pointer, not supply). An `ad-hoc`↔`permanent` match is not a merge: the **show re-resolves** to the permanent record and the pointer retires. An `ad-hoc`↔`ad-hoc` match **is** merged — see the promotion-counter edge case | An `ad-hoc` record has no spec, calendar, claim or clock; there is nothing to apply survivorship to |
| N-6 | **Rejected pairs are remembered and never re-fire automatically** — only a human reopens them. Sole exception: a later **premises-licence-number match**, the one class of evidence that overturns a human's judgement | Otherwise detection re-flags the same two distinct rooms forever. Rejection is a statement about the *places*, not about their current field values — so a later edit must not resurrect it |
| N-7 | Address alone never merges. Name alone never merges | A venue with a studio upstairs is one address (16.01.01 D-01); two "The Grand"s in different cities are two venues (16.01.01) |

### Who confirms: the tier is set by what the merge can destroy

D-02 bias hard toward under-merging. **The confirmation requirement scales with destroyable value,
not with detector confidence** (DT-07) — confidence is a property of the evidence; destruction is a
property of the record.

| Tier | Conditions (**all** must hold) | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0 — auto** | Both records `unclaimed` · both **0 rooms** · **every field on both sides is seeded-class** (16.05.03 class 6) · no first-hand reports · no bookings · no open claim · location key = **address-normalised equality** · identity key = **external-identifier equality or normalised-name equality** (never fuzzy) | Executes at ingest, unconfirmed, reversible in one tap. **Destroys nothing**: no calendar, no booking, no claim, no human's work. The alternative is a registry that fails on launch day at seeding scale (CX-01) |
| **Tier 1 — human-confirmed** | Anything else that is not Tier 2: any claim, any room, any booking, any first-hand report, any owner-declared or community-class field, any fuzzy name match, `same-place-different-room`, `not-a-place` | Flagged, never executed. Confirmed by the Operator of a claimed side, or a platform reviewer where neither side is claimed. DT-02 stands here unmodified |
| **Tier 2 — blocked** | Both claimed, different orgs (N-4) · premises-licence mismatch (auto-rejected) · `ad-hoc` as proposed survivor (N-5) · a merge that would leave a record with an **on-sale ticketed show** at `approximate` precision | Routed, not queued. The merge is the wrong instrument |

**Tier 0's safety rests on one condition doing the work**: *every field on both sides is
seeded-class*. An attacker-created record's fields are community-class, so any pair an attacker can
influence is Tier 1 by construction. Tier 0 is machine-to-machine only, at ingest, before a human
has touched either side.

### Survivorship: per field, by provenance class — with three exceptions

Survivorship is **per field by provenance class** (D-04, CX-06, 16.05.03 D-02), never per record. A
merged record legitimately takes its address from one source, its PA from an Operator, and its load-in
from a band's harvest. **Same-class ties resolve per class, not by one global rule** (Q-03, resolved):

| Class | Same-class tie | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory** | **Refutes the pair.** Two different licence-derived values for one building means one record's licence linkage is wrong — or they are two buildings | The only class where a disagreement is evidence the *merge* is wrong, not the field |
| **Owner-declared** | Freshness wins; loser to per-field history | Cannot occur across orgs (N-4). Same org saying two things at two times = the later one is current |
| **First-hand** | **Neither wins — the field opens a contest (16.05.04)** | Two bands reporting different values is usually a **conditionality failure**, not a conflict (CX-04, 16.05.04 DT-05 — the ramp was out one week and not the other). Picking by freshness would silently destroy the corroboration signal, which is the domain's only independent evidence |
| **Community** (accepted or unreviewed) | Freshness wins; loser to per-field history | Same authority, so recency is the only honest discriminator |
| **Seeded** | **Neither wins.** Survivor keeps its own value; the other lands in history; the field's freshness drops to the **floor** so decay flags it and the next harvest asks about it first (16.05.05 → 16.05.06) | Both are stale by construction, so freshness is meaningless. **A seeded-vs-seeded conflict is not resolved — it is converted into a harvest question.** The registry does not guess; it asks the next band |

**Exception 1 — `location_precision`: the stricter value always wins**, regardless of provenance
class. Merging an `exact` record into an `approximate` one (a home studio, 16.01.01 D-10) must not
publish a home address. The asymmetry is DT-02's, one field over: a wrongly-hidden address is
recoverable in one tap by the Operator; a wrongly-published home is not — *the platform cannot
un-tell someone a fact*. Blocked at Tier 2 where the survivor would carry an on-sale ticketed show.

**Exception 2 — `typeset` is unioned, never chosen.** Rooms re-parent, and 16.01.01 D-05's invariant
`place.typeset ⊇ ⋃ room.typeset` must hold after the write. **Merge is the one operation that can
violate that invariant silently**, because it is the only operation that moves rooms between records.
Union is mandatory, not a preference.

**Exception 3 — anchor class does not launder.** See below.

### Merge is not verification

A merged value keeps **its original provenance timestamp and class**. A merge resets no freshness
clock and confirms nothing. This is R-02's reasoning exactly ("claiming is not verifying") applied to
the adjacent operation: treating a merge as a verification event would make a data-cleanup operation a
free credibility upgrade for every field it touched (DT-12).

### Merge laundering — the anchor attack

Survivorship by class means **the loser's community-class phone number beats the survivor's
seeded-class one.** So: create a near-duplicate carrying your own phone → get it merged → the phone
is now the record's phone → claim by phone (16.05.02). The claim ladder was never touched; the merge
did the work (DT-10).

Two rules close it:
1. A value arriving on the survivor via merge carries its **original** provenance stamp — the
   anchor stability window (16.01.01 D-09, default 90 days) is computed from that stamp, never from
   the merge.
2. An **anchor-class field whose value changes as a result of a merge is invalidated as claim
   evidence** for the full stability window measured **from the merge date**. Invalidated, not
   logged — 16.01.01 D-09's standard.

The adjacent case that *looks* like an attack and is not: an attacker claims a junk record, then
confirms a merge pulling in a real unclaimed venue record. They now control the venue's record — but
that is exactly what claiming the venue's record directly would have given them, at the same bar. The
merge adds no attack surface here. It would if the survivor were choosable, so it is not: **the
survivor is the claimed record where exactly one side is claimed, else the record with the earliest
creation timestamp.** Both identifiers resolve to the survivor indefinitely, so the choice is
cosmetic for every consumer and is not a lever for anyone.

### Place merge vs room merge

| | Place merge | Room merge |
|---|---|---|
| Joins calendars | **No** | **Yes** — this is the whole point |
| Moves bookings | No — rooms re-parent intact, calendars stay separate | Yes — both rooms' bookings land on one calendar |
| Blocks on bookings | **Never.** Blocking would leave the busiest records permanently duplicated (DT-14) | **Yes** — on overlapping **future confirmed** bookings |
| Risk | Low | The highest-stakes action in the domain |

**Room-merge preflight**, in order:

1. **Future confirmed bookings that overlap** → merge **blocked**. The preflight lists every
   collision; the Operator resolves each (cancel or move one side) and re-runs. Blocking is correct:
   the alternative is executing a merge whose output is a double-booked calendar — manufacturing the
   exact failure the domain exists to prevent.
2. **Past bookings never block.** The room was physically one room; whatever happened, happened.
   They land on the merged calendar as history.
3. **Holds (16.06.01) never block.** Overlapping holds re-rank on the merged calendar by their
   **original grant timestamp**, preserving first-hold-wins. A hold overlapping a confirmed booking
   is auto-released with a reason — a hold is not a booking.
4. **An open hold challenge on either room blocks.** A 48-hour challenge window whose calendar
   changes underneath it is a broken protocol; resolve the challenge, then merge.

A place merge that re-parents a room with the same normalised name as one of the survivor's rooms
**emits a room-merge candidate immediately** and disambiguates the display names until it is resolved.
It never auto-merges rooms — not even at Tier 0, which by definition has no rooms at all.

### Reversal: always available, degrading fidelity

Merges are reversible (D-04). **Availability is unbounded; fidelity is not** (DT-16). A wrong merge
of two real rooms is discovered when someone tries to book the wrong one, which can be months.

| Mode | Condition | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean reversal** | No post-merge writes to affected fields, no new bookings on re-parented rooms | Full restoration. Both records return to pre-merge state; the redirect is removed; the loser's identifier resolves to itself again |
| **Split reversal** | Post-merge activity exists | Records separate; each keeps the rooms, bookings, provenance and history that **originated with it**. Post-merge-created objects (new rooms, bookings, edits) stay with the survivor, listed for the Operator to move individually. **Nothing is deleted** |

Reversal **flags, never silently re-points**, every show (17/18), ticket (19) or credit (02) created
during the merged window that cites the survivor — the platform cannot know which building it
happened in. And per 16.01.01 DT-15 a credit citing a place resolves to the record **as of the cited
date**: neither a merge nor its reversal may rewrite where a past session happened. A provenance
record that mutates underneath its own citation is not provenance, it is a cache (D-18).

Standing to reverse: the survivor's claiming org, a platform reviewer, **any org that held a claim on
either side**, and any account that contributed provenance to either side. The wrongly-merged party
may have no record left to act from — their record redirects to a rival's — so standing cannot be
"the survivor's owner".

## Happy Path

1. Seeding ingests "The Bunker, 4 High St" (OSM, `wikidata=Q123`) and "Bunker Club, 4 High Street" (Wikidata, `Q123`) in one run
2. Detection: location key = normalised address equality; identity key = external identifier equality (`Q123` = `Q123`). Two keys, both exact, neither fuzzy
3. Tier gate: both unclaimed, 0 rooms, every field seeded-class, no reports, no bookings → **Tier 0**. The merge executes at ingest, unconfirmed
4. Survivorship: both values seeded-class, so the seeded rule applies — the survivor (earliest creation) keeps its own values, the others land in per-field history, and each conflicting field's freshness drops to the floor so the first harvest asks about it. Typesets union. Both identifiers redirect to the survivor. No clock is reset — a merge is not a verification
5. Six weeks later the Operator searches for their venue and finds **one** result, not two
6. They claim it (16.05.02), proving control against the record's **pre-claim** public phone
7. The record shows a marker: a third record, "The Bunker Guildford", created by a musician, with one room and two bookings. Fuzzy name + community-class fields + a room → **Tier 1**. The claim was the merge's natural confirmation moment (CX-02)
8. Operator confirms. The **place** merge executes: the room re-parents, `typeset` unions, both provenance histories are retained, the loser's identifier redirects indefinitely. **No calendar is joined and no booking moves**
9. The re-parented room normalises to the same name as the Operator's own Main Room → a **room-merge** candidate fires. The Operator confirms it, and only now do two calendars become one. Preflight finds one collision: two confirmed bookings on 14 March
10. The merge is **blocked** until they resolve it. They move one booking, re-run, and the room merge executes — one record, one room, one calendar. The double-booking the duplicate was hiding was surfaced rather than merged into

## Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two distinct rooms in one building | **Not duplicates** (N-1). One record, two rooms (16.01.02). Merging them merges two calendars into a double-booking machine | Never flagged — address alone never fires |
| Venue relocated; old and new records | **Not duplicates** (N-2). Identity signal without a location signal → `relocation` class | "Did this place move?" → both records live, cross-linked "moved to / moved from" |
| **Both records have live bookings** | Bookings exist only on rooms, and rooms exist only under a controlling org — so both-with-bookings means both claimed. Different orgs → **N-4, not a merge**. Same org → **place merge does not touch calendars**; the risk is entirely in the follow-on room merge, which blocks on overlapping future confirmed bookings | Place merge: "2 rooms moved. Calendars unchanged." Room merge: "2 bookings collide on 14 March — resolve these before merging" |
| Both records claimed, by different orgs | **Tier 2 → 24**, with both options: contested claim (16.05.02) or containment migration (16.01.01 D-06). Usually the latter — one building, two operators, recorded wrong | "These may be one building with two operators — merge as premises + operator?" |
| Fields conflict at merge | Per field, by provenance class (D-04). Same-class ties resolve **per class**: statutory refutes the pair · first-hand opens a contest · seeded becomes a harvest question · owner and community resolve by freshness with the loser kept in history | Field history shows both values; the superseded one is one tap from being restored |
| **A merge opens a contest on the Operator's own record** | Expected, not a fault. Record A carries an owner-declared "step-free"; record B carries a band's first-hand "3 steps". Post-merge the field holds an owner claim contradicted by a stronger-class first-hand report → 16.05.04, last-agreed stands | Operator: "Merging opened 1 dispute on this record" — stated *before* they confirm, never discovered afterwards |
| **A merge closes a contest** | Corroboration counts **combine**. Two records each holding one first-hand report of "3 steps" become one record with two independent reports — which can cross a corroboration threshold a 1-vs-1 stalemate never would | Contest resolves; Operator notified |
| **Merge laundering**: attacker's community-class phone beats the seeded phone, then claims by phone | Merged values keep their original stamp; an anchor field that **changes value via merge** is invalidated as claim evidence for the full stability window from the merge date (16.01.01 D-09) | "This number was changed recently and can't be used to verify a claim. Verify by domain instead." |
| **The candidate flag is a discovery oracle** | An `approximate` record hides a home address (16.01.01 D-10). "These are the same place" against an attacker's exact-address record **confirms the hidden address**. Candidates involving an `approximate` record are never surfaced to non-privileged users; the suggest affordance never reveals the counterpart's hidden fields | Musician/Producer/Fan: the candidate does not exist. Operator and reviewer only |
| Merging `exact` into `approximate` | Stricter precision wins (Exception 1). A wrongly-hidden address is one Operator tap to fix; a published home is permanent | "Address precision is now approximate — set it back if this is a public venue" |
| …but the survivor has an **on-sale ticketed show** | **Tier 2 — blocked.** 16.01.01 D-10: the ticket carries the address, and an approximate address on a ticket is a wrong ticket | "Set this place's address precision before merging — a show is on sale" |
| Aggressive matching merges two genuinely different venues | The false-positive cost is far worse than the false-negative (D-02). Two-key rule + negative rules + Tier gates + unbounded reversal | Bias toward under-merging — made affordable by search-time collapse (DT-15) |
| Under-merging leaves three search results for one room | The cost D-02 accepts, and it is only affordable because it is **paid at read time**: an open candidate pair collapses into one search result with a "possibly the same place" marker. Dedup delivers most of its user-visible value **before any merge executes** | One result + "possibly the same place · Same / Different" |
| **OSM lists a room as a venue** ("Academy" + "Academy 2") | `same-place-different-room` (DT-09). **Absorb-as-room**, never merge-as-alias: flattening the loser's name into the alias set destroys a room, and the room is the calendar-carrying unit. Always Tier 1 — the ordinal suffix routes the question, it does not answer it | "Is 'Academy 2' the same place, or a different room in it?" |
| **Listings site lists a night as a venue** ("Jazz at the Vaults") | `not-a-place` (16.01.01 DT-14). Name mismatch + exact address at a venue-typed record. Fails the two-key rule so it is never merged — and therefore stands as a phantom, which was the real complaint. Suppressed from search + pointed at the venue record. **Merging it would make an event brand an alias of a building its Operator does not control** | Venue Operator: "'Jazz at the Vaults' is listed at your address — is that a night here, or a different place?" |
| Licensing register seeds a record at the **licensee's correspondence address** | A record at a solicitor's office bearing a venue's name. Location signal fails against the real venue, so no candidate — correctly, because they are not the same building. **Dedup cannot fix it**; it is a seeding precision defect of the same family as the two rows above (the source's unit of record is the licensee, not the place) | Nothing. Flagged to 16.05.01 as Q-05 |
| **Two `ad-hoc` records at one address** | Merged. Otherwise they **split the promotion counter** (16.01.01 D-08: 3 shows / 2+ promoters / 24 months) — a real recurring space never crosses the threshold, never becomes searchable, never opens a claim path. A silent failure that costs supply | Transparent; both shows resolve to one pointer |
| `ad-hoc` matches a `permanent` record | Not a merge (N-5). The **show re-resolves** to the permanent record; the pointer retires. An `ad-hoc` record has no spec, calendar, claim or clock — there is nothing to apply survivorship to | 18 shows the matched venue for confirmation |
| A **closed** record (16.01.05) and a live record for one place | Merged normally. The survivor takes the **live** status — a closure on the loser was a statement about the loser's identifier, not about the building. Gig history from both survives | One live record; both histories intact |
| **A re-parented room's operating org differs from the survivor's claimant** | The merge just moved an independent business's room under a different premises record (16.01.01 D-06/D-07). **That org is a required confirmer**, not a bystander. The place claimant is notified within 24h. **The room stays live and bookable throughout** — 16.05.02 D-03, inherited verbatim | Room operator: "A merge would move Tracking Room under {org}'s record — Confirm / Dispute". Hirers: nothing |
| The multi-hyphenate in two roles at once | A Producer who operates a room in the building *and* holds a booking there as a Musician. They are **one org for the room and one account for the booking** — the confirmation is requested of the **org**; the booking notice goes to the account. Never two contradictory asks, and never a "which hat are you wearing?" prompt (16.01.01 D-05, 16.05.02 D-05) | One confirmation request, one booking notice, no ambiguity |
| Merge racing a claim | The claim **does not block** — CX-02 proposed it should, and that is backwards. The Operator claiming is the person best placed to confirm the merge, and making them wait for a review to claim their own venue is hostile. **The merge resolves through the claim** (Happy Path 6–8). Claim proof still anchors only to the claimed record's pre-claim values | "There are two records for The Bunker — does your claim cover both?" |
| Two merges in flight on one record (A→B and A→C) | Merges serialise per record. A record that is a loser cannot be a party to another merge; later operations target the survivor. **Redirects are transitive and flattened** (A→B, B→C ⇒ A resolves directly to C). A merge whose proposed survivor is already a loser of the other party's merge is rejected — the cycle guard | Second confirmer: "This record was just merged — here's the survivor" |
| Network drops mid-merge | **Atomic or not at all.** A half-merge — rooms re-parented, provenance not — is a corrupted registry with nobody alerted. Retry is idempotent on the ordered pair within 15 minutes | "Merge didn't complete — nothing was changed. Retry" |
| Re-ingestion resurrects a merged-away record | Rejected and merged pairs are remembered (D-05, N-6); a re-ingest of the loser's source record resolves to the survivor. Without this the loop is infinite (CX-01) | Nothing — the ingest is a no-op |
| Attacker edits an unclaimed record toward a target to force a match | Detection re-fires on edit, so the match is drivable. It is Tier 1 by construction (community-class fields), so it burns a reviewer's time and nothing else. Capped: **20 merge suggestions per account per 24h; more than 5 in 1 hour holds the batch for review** — the same shape as 16.01.01's creation cap | "You've suggested a lot of merges today — these are queued for review" |
| Detection silently fails and the registry forks | **The dangerous property**: nothing alerts, the fork is cumulative, and no user is in a position to notice (CX-01). The mitigation is structural, not operational — the candidate marker lives **on the record**, and search collapses open pairs, so an undetected duplicate is the *only* failure mode, not an undetected-and-unresolved one | — |
| A rejected pair is later found to share a premises licence number | The one exception to N-6. Statutory is the only class that can refute a candidate; symmetrically it is the only class that can overturn a human's rejection | "New evidence: these share a premises licence" |
| Merge suggested on a record the suggester cannot fully read | Comparison renders **redacted** — the fields the suggester has no rights to (rates, an approximate address, another org's room) are absent from the payload, not blurred in the UI | Partial view state |

## States

### Candidate Lifecycle States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Candidate | Two keys matched, negative rules passed, Tier 1 | "Possible duplicate" marker **on the record**; both records live; search collapses the pair with a Same/Different tap |
| Auto-merged | Tier 0 conditions all held | Nothing — it happened at ingest before any human saw either record. Reversible in one tap from the survivor's history |
| Confirmed | Operator, required room org, or reviewer confirmed | Merge executes atomically |
| Merged | Merge complete | Survivor record; loser's identifier redirects **indefinitely** (a sold ticket outlives any expiry); both provenance histories retained; no clock reset |
| Rejected | Confirmed as distinct places | Pair remembered; never re-fires automatically (N-6) |
| Refuted | Premises-licence mismatch | Auto-rejected and remembered; overrides every other signal |
| Routed | Tier 2 | Handed to 24 (N-4), 16.01.05 (`relocation`), 18/21 (`not-a-place`), or 16.01.02 (`same-place-different-room`) — never queued as a merge |
| Blocked | Room merge with overlapping future confirmed bookings, or an open hold challenge | Collision list; merge unavailable until resolved |
| Reversed | Merge undone | Clean or split reversal; records separated; nothing deleted; citing shows/tickets/credits flagged for human re-resolution |

### View States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Candidate comparison requested | Skeleton of the comparison frame only — never a rendered field value that later changes. A skeleton showing a plausible address before the real one lands is a lie with a spinner on it |
| Empty | No open candidates for this record | "No duplicates detected" — and **not** "no duplicates exist". The registry cannot know; detection failure is silent (CX-01) |
| Populated | ≥ 1 open candidate | Side-by-side field comparison with **provenance class and freshness age per field on both sides**, the survivorship outcome pre-computed per field, and the consequences stated before confirmation: rooms moving, contests opening, orgs to be notified |
| Partial | Confirmer lacks rights to some fields on one side (rates, `approximate` address, another org's room) | Those fields **absent from the payload**, not blurred. "Some fields on the other record aren't visible to you" |
| Error | Detection or merge fails | "Couldn't complete — nothing was changed." **Never** a partially applied merge, and never a stale comparison rendered as current |

Detection compares against the **stored** address. So: create a record with your guess at the street address, wait for "these look like the same place", and the platform has just confirmed a stranger's home for you — a fact D-10 exists to withhold and which, once told, cannot be untold. **Candidates involving an `approximate` record are never surfaced to non-privileged users**, and the suggest affordance never renders the counterpart's hidden fields. |

Resetting clocks on merge would hand every field a free credibility upgrade for a data-cleanup operation — and would let an Operator refresh a four-year-old spec by merging a junk record into it. **Merged values keep their original timestamp and class.** |
| DT-13 | The Operator confirming a merge is the last word on it | They own the record, and 16.01.01 D-06 makes the place claim the top of the tree | ❌ REJECTED: **a place merge can move an independent business's room under someone else's record.** 16.01.01 D-07 established that a contained room's operating org is *claimed, not granted* — expressly so the venue Operator is not the gatekeeper of a sublessee's platform presence, because in a rent dispute a grant is a lever. That is not untidiness, it is the registry failing at its one job at the moment of use. D-02's bias toward under-merging is correct *and* it is only affordable because an open candidate pair **collapses into one search result** with a "possibly the same place" marker and a one-tap Same/Different. **Dedup therefore delivers most of its user-visible value before any merge executes** — and the collapse is what turns every unresolved candidate into a cheap crowdsourced question instead of a broken search result. |

A bounded window guarantees that the exact class of error D-02 is engineered to prevent becomes permanent precisely when it is finally noticed. **Availability is unbounded; fidelity degrades** — clean reversal early, split reversal later, and nothing is ever deleted. The cost is state; the alternative is an irreversible corruption of two real businesses' records, which is the failure mode this whole feature exists to make impossible. |
