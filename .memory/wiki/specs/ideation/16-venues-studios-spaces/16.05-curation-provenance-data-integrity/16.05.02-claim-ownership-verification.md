# Feature: Claim & Ownership Verification

> **Number**: 16.05.02
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How an unclaimed place record — seeded from public data or created by a musician — becomes an
Operator's own: they prove they control the place, and write access transfers to their org. This is
the other half of the cold-start loop (16.05.01 DT-02): most records are born unclaimed, and claiming
is how supply arrives on the platform.

It is also where the domain meets its adversaries. A claim grants control of a business's public
record, its calendar and its money. A wrongly-granted claim is not a data error — it is a hijacked
business.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Discovers a record for their venue that already exists, claims it, proves control, and gains write access for their **org** (01) — not their person. On sale of the business, transfers it. Disputes a rival claim. |
| Musician | Read-only + create | Cannot claim a place they do not run. **Can** create an unclaimed record (16.01.01) and keep editing it via suggestions (16.05.03) — creating is not owning, and that distinction is what stops record creation being a land grab. |
| Producer | Full (for their own studio) | The Producer persona frequently **is** the Operator — they run the room. The multi-hyphenate fact: the same human claims a studio and books a venue in the same week. |
| Fan | None | No claim path. A Fan has no place to claim; exposing the flow to them is pure attack surface. |

## Behavior

Claiming is a proof-of-control flow, not a proof-of-ownership one — the platform cannot adjudicate
who legally owns a business, and must not pretend to. Proof methods, roughly in strength order:

- **Phone verification at the number already on the public record** (before the claim) — strong, because the number was not supplied by the claimant
- **Email at the place's own domain**
- **DNS or a token on the venue's own website**
- **A document** (licence, utility bill, lease) — weakest, human-reviewed, forgeable
- **Existing org linkage** (01) — an already-verified org claiming another site

The critical design rule: **proof must be anchored to data that predates the claim.** A claimant who
first edits the record's phone number and then verifies against it has proved nothing — and this is
the single most obvious attack on the whole domain.

Contested claims (two parties, one venue) are not resolvable by the platform on the evidence
available. They escalate to 24, and — importantly — the record stays live and bookable throughout,
because a contested claim must not take a trading business offline.

### Happy Path

1. Operator searches for their venue; a seeded record already exists, unclaimed
2. Operator clicks Claim; system offers phone verification against the number **already on the record**
3. Operator receives the code at the venue's landline and enters it
4. Write access transfers to their org (01); provenance history is retained — claiming does not erase what the community contributed
5. Operator now controls the spec, calendar and rates; community can still suggest edits (16.05.03)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Claimant edits the phone number, then verifies against it | **Blocked** — proof must anchor to data predating the claim. The central attack | Verification uses the pre-claim value only |
| Two parties claim the same venue | Both claims held; record stays live and bookable; escalates to 24. The platform cannot adjudicate ownership | "Claim under review" — booking unaffected |
| Venue is sold; the old Operator still controls the record | Transfer flow needed, and the departing owner has no incentive to cooperate. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — the strongest case for a re-verification path that can override an incumbent | — |
| Booker claims the venue rather than the owner | Common and mostly benign — the booker *is* the operational controller. Proof-of-control is the right test precisely because it does not ask who owns the freehold | Claim succeeds; control is what is proven |
| Record has no public phone number to anchor to | Falls to weaker methods; `[PENDING — Step 5]` whether weak proof grants provisional control |  — |
| Claimant is a squatter with a forged document | Document proof is the weakest rung and is human-reviewed. This is why the ladder exists | Manual review |
| A claim would gain control of a live calendar with bookings | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — a successful hostile claim inherits other people's money. Does a claim on a record with live bookings need a higher bar? | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unclaimed | Default for seeded/community records | Public record + claim CTA |
| Claim pending | Claim submitted, proof incomplete | Record unchanged; claimant sees progress |
| Claimed | Proof accepted | Verified badge; org-controlled |
| Contested | 2+ claims | Record live and bookable; claim frozen; 24 escalation |
| Transferred | Ownership moved between orgs | Ownership history visible |
| Revoked | Claim overturned | Reverts to unclaimed; edits made under the bad claim `[PENDING — Step 5]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Claiming proves ownership | It is called "ownership verification" in the candidate list | ❌ REJECTED — and the reframe matters. The platform cannot determine who legally owns a business; it can determine who **controls** it (answers its phone, holds its domain, has the keys). Proof-of-control is achievable, sufficient for every product purpose, and honest. Proof-of-ownership is a claim the platform would be making without evidence — and it would make every contested claim the platform's legal problem instead of 24's process problem. |
| DT-02 | Verify against the contact details on the record | The obvious mechanism — call the venue's number | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **this is the domain's central vulnerability**. If the claimant can edit the record first, they verify against themselves. The rule must be: proof anchors only to data that **predates the claim** and that the claimant did not supply. Stated explicitly because it is the kind of thing that is obvious once said and catastrophic if unsaid. |
| DT-03 | A contested claim should freeze the record | Two claims means the data is untrustworthy; lock it | ❌ REJECTED: the record is a **trading business's** public presence and calendar. Freezing it takes a real venue offline over a dispute between two parties who both say they run it — punishing the business for the platform's inability to adjudicate. The claim freezes; the record stays live and bookable. |
| DT-04 | Creating a record should grant control of it | The person who added it cares most about it | ❌ REJECTED: it would make record creation a land grab, and a musician who adds their local rehearsal room has proved nothing about controlling it. **Creating ≠ owning.** This separation is what lets the community populate the registry (16.01.01) without the community capturing it. |
| DT-05 | Claim verification is a mechanism serving many domains | Artist profiles (01), gear (15), credits (02) all have claim-an-unclaimed-entity flows | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a **cross-cut**. The generic flow (prove control → transfer write access → handle contests) is platform machinery. What stays domain-owned is the venue-specific **proof ladder** (a phone at the listed number, a premises licence, a venue's domain) and the venue-specific stakes (a claim inherits a live calendar and other people's deposits). Mechanism cross-cut, proofs domain-owned. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Proof-of-**control**, not proof-of-ownership | The platform cannot adjudicate legal ownership; control is achievable, sufficient and honest (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Proof must anchor to data that **predates the claim** and that the claimant did not supply | Otherwise a claimant edits the phone number and verifies against themselves — the central attack (DT-02) |
| D-03 | A contested claim freezes the **claim**, never the record | Freezing takes a trading business offline over the platform's inability to adjudicate (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Creating a record does not grant control of it | Otherwise record creation is a land grab and community population becomes community capture (DT-04) |
| D-05 | Claims grant control to an **org** (01), not a person | Multi-site operators, staff turnover, and business sale all require it |
