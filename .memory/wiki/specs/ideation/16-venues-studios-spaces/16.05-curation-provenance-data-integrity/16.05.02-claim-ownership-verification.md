# Feature: Claim & Ownership Verification

> **Number**: 16.05.02
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

How an unclaimed place record — seeded from public data or created by a musician — becomes an
Operator's own: they prove they control the place, and write access transfers to their org. This is
the other half of the cold-start loop (16.05.01 DT-02): most records are born unclaimed, and claiming
is how supply arrives on the platform. The Operator never onboards in the conventional sense; they
search for their own venue, find a record that already exists, and take it over (CX-02).

It is also where the domain meets its adversaries. A claim grants control of a business's public
record, its calendar and — if the model is designed carelessly — its money. A wrongly-granted claim is
not a data error; it is a hijacked business.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Discovers a seeded record for their venue, claims it, picks a rung off the proof ladder the record's **anchor set** supports, and gains write access for their **org** (01) — never for their person, so the record does not leave when they do. Post-claim they write spec fields directly, set rate cards (16.06.07), and review suggestions (16.05.03). Sees the inherited-booking settling period on any bookings taken before their claim. Transfers the claim on sale; defends it against a challenger (D-10). |
| Musician | Read-only + create | Cannot claim a place they do not run. **Can** create an unclaimed record (16.01.01) and keep editing it via suggestions (16.05.03). The values they supply at creation are **community class**, and community-class values are never anchor-eligible (D-07) — so a creator cannot verify a future claim against data they themselves typed. This is what makes "creating ≠ owning" (D-04) an enforced mechanism rather than a stated principle. Sees any record's claim status, because a claim is public (D-13). |
| Producer | Full (for their own studio) | The Producer persona frequently **is** the Operator — they run the room. The multi-hyphenate fact: the same human claims a studio and books a venue in the same week. **Structurally the worst-served role in this feature**: studios are not seeded (16.05.01 D-05), so a studio record has no seeded contact values, therefore no anchor set, therefore no strong rung — document review is their only path (DT-10). Compounded at shared premises, where the postal rung cannot tell six studios in one building apart (DT-11). |
| Fan | None | No claim path, no claim UI, and the claim route returns 404 rather than 403 for a Fan session — a 403 confirms the flow exists. A Fan has no place to claim; exposing the flow to them is pure attack surface. They see only the public consequence: a "Verified operator" badge, or its absence. |

## Behavior

Claiming is a **proof-of-control** flow, not a proof-of-ownership one — the platform cannot adjudicate
who legally owns a business, and must not pretend to (D-01).

### The anchor rule — the mechanism the whole feature rests on

**Proof must anchor to data the claimant did not supply.** A claimant who first edits the record's
phone number and then verifies against it has proved nothing, and this is the single most obvious
attack on the domain (DT-02).

Operationally, at claim open the system takes an **anchor snapshot** of the record's contact fields
(name, phone, email, website, postal address) by reading each field's provenance history (16.05.03
D-01, D-04) and selecting its most recent value of class **`seeded` or `statutory`** — even where that
value has since been superseded by a human edit.

Two properties fall out, and both matter:

1. **Class restriction kills the attack at the root.** Every human-supplied value — owner-declared,
   first-hand, community-suggested, and record-creation values — is excluded from the anchor set. An
   attacker who overwrites the seeded phone with their own mobile has changed the *live* value and not
   the *anchor*, because the seeded value is still in the immutable per-field history.
2. **The snapshot is immutable for the claim's lifetime.** A rival cannot break a pending claim's
   anchor by editing the record, and a contest running for weeks does not need the record's contact
   fields frozen — which is what keeps this compatible with D-03 (freeze the claim, never the record).

> **This feature is unimplementable without 16.05.03 D-01 and D-04.** Per-field, immutable provenance
> is not a nice property here — it is the only reason the anchor rule can be enforced at all. Record-
> level provenance, or mutable history, and D-02 becomes a slogan.

### The proof ladder

Rungs are offered strongest-first, and **only the rungs the record's anchor set supports are offered**.

| Rung | Method | Anchors to | Strength | Grants | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Existing org linkage** (01) — an already-verified org claiming another of its sites | The org's prior proven claim | Strong | Full, immediate | Instant |
| L2 | **Statutory register match** — claimant's org name matches the premises licence holder on the public licensing register (16.01.06) | Statutory | Strongest external | Full | ≤ 24h (register lookup) |
| L3 | **Phone/SMS code** to the number in the anchor set | Seeded | Strong | Full | 6-digit code, valid 10 min, 3 attempts, max 3 code requests per record per 24h |
| L4 | **Email** to an address at the record's own domain | Seeded | Strong | Full | Token valid 7 days |
| L5 | **DNS TXT record or a token file** on the record's website | Seeded | Strong | Full | Token valid 7 days, polled hourly |
| L6 | **Postal code** to the premises address in the anchor set | Seeded | Strong | Full | Code valid 30 days |
| L7 | **Document** — premises licence, business rates bill, utility bill ≤ 90 days old, signed lease, or headed PLI certificate (16.01.06) | **Nothing — claimant-supplied** | Weak | **Provisional on submission; Full only on human accept** | Review SLA 5 business days; provisional lapses at 30 days |

**L6 is the floor and the guarantee.** Every seeded record has a postal address (16.05.01 Happy Path
step 2), so the postal rung is available on every seeded record even when nothing else is. It is slow,
unglamorous, and the only rung that cannot be absent (DT-06). A bad geocode (16.05.01's 200m-off case)
does not break it — mail is delivered against the address string, not the geocode.

**L7 is not a rung so much as an escape hatch.** It anchors to nothing, because a document is by
definition supplied by the claimant. What makes it survivable is the reviewer: a human checking (a)
the document's address against the record's address, (b) the document's named party against the
claimant's org name (01), and (c) for a premises licence, the named holder against the public
licensing register (16.01.06). Check (c) is the only one that anchors externally — where the register
is available, it silently upgrades L7 to L2's strength. Where it is not (domain Q-13, jurisdiction),
L7 is a human reading a forgeable PDF, and the feature should be honest that that is what it is.

Documents contain PII — a residential address where the studio is someone's home, account numbers on
a utility bill. The document is never public, never shown to the community, never part of the record,
and only the **decision and its date** are visible on the record's history. On reject: reason given,
one resubmission within 30 days, second reject → 30-day cooldown on that record for that org, three
rejects across any records → 24.

### Two control tiers

| | **Provisional** (L7 pre-review) | **Full** (L1–L6, or L7 accepted) |
|---|---|---|
| Write spec fields (16.02, 16.03) | ✅ — tagged `owner-provisional`, reversible, community-visible | ✅ `owner-declared` |
| Respond to enquiries (16.06.06) | ✅ — they are messages, not commitments | ✅ |
| Set / publish rate cards (16.06.07) | ❌ — commercial terms bind | ✅ |
| Calendar: accept, decline, cancel, hold (16.06.01, 16.06.03) | ❌ | ✅ |
| Verified badge shown publicly | ❌ — public sees "Claim under review" | ✅ |
| Delist, merge (16.05.07), transfer the claim | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Change payout destination** | ❌ | ❌ — **never granted by a claim** (D-06) |

Provisional exists because an Operator who claims and receives nothing for five business days churns.
It grants exactly the actions whose worst case is a reversible, attributable, provenance-tagged wrong
value — and nothing whose worst case is someone else's money or someone else's plans.

### What a claim does not do

- **It does not move money.** The payout destination lives on the org's finance profile behind its own
  KYC (01/23). A claim grants control of the *record*; it never grants or changes where the money goes
  (D-06, DT-07). This is the decision that collapses the threat model.
- **It does not verify anything.** An Operator can claim a record and never confirm a single field. The
  claim proves control, not that the PA spec is true. No freshness clock resets on a claim (CX R-02).
- **It does not erase history.** Provenance survives the claim: the load-in a band corrected last March
  is still credited to that band and still first-hand class (CX-07).
- **It does not settle ownership.** Contested claims escalate to 24, and the record stays live and
  bookable throughout, because a contested claim must not take a trading business offline (D-03).

### Overriding an incumbent — sale, departure, and the absent owner

The venue is sold; the old Operator's org still holds the claim and has no incentive to cooperate. Or
— the commoner and duller case — the person who claimed it left the company two years ago and nobody
has the login. Both are the same problem: a live business locked under a claim nobody is behind.

**The cooperative path is one action**: org A initiates transfer to org B, org B accepts, done. No
re-verification. This covers every amicable sale and should never be harder than that.

**The challenge path** exists for the uncooperative or absent incumbent (D-10):

1. Challenger opens a re-verification challenge and must complete a strong rung (L1–L6) against a
   **freshly taken** anchor snapshot.
2. The asymmetry that makes this safe: **the anchor set follows the premises, not the org.** The new
   owner answers the venue's phone and collects its mail. The departed owner does not.
3. On challenger success, the incumbent gets a **14-day counter-window** and is notified through every
   channel on file — immediately, at day 7, and at day 12. Silence is what transfers control, so the
   notification burden must be maximal.
4. If the incumbent re-proves control against the same anchor set, the challenge is **dismissed and
   escalated to 24 as a contested claim** — two parties both proving control of one premises is a real
   dispute or a handover in progress, and neither is the platform's to call.
5. If the incumbent does not respond within 14 days, control transfers to the challenger's org, and
   the inherited-booking settling period (D-09) runs from the transfer.

Throughout, the incumbent keeps Full control and the record stays live. You do not strip a trading
operator's calendar because somebody knocked.

**Why this is not itself the hijack vector**: the challenger still has to prove control of the
premises. If they can answer the venue's phone or receive its post, they arguably *do* control it —
which is the entire premise of D-01. The counter-window is what protects the incumbent who genuinely
still controls it: they answer the same phone.

### Claim dormancy

A claim is not a one-time event; it is a perishable assertion about a fact that changes (DT-09). An
**abandoned claim is worse than an unclaimed record**, because an unclaimed record's suggestions have
a live policy (16.05.03 Q-01) while a claimed one's queue forever.

A claim goes **Dormant** when all three hold: no org action on the record for 12 months, an unreviewed
suggestion queue with items older than 90 days, and no response to three re-verification pings over 30
days. Dormancy keeps the claim and keeps the badge — a venue that has not changed is not a venue that
has vanished — and changes exactly one thing: the pending queue falls back to the unclaimed-record
write policy (16.05.03's to define). **Dormancy touches no freshness clock**, which is what keeps it
clear of CX R-02: this is about responsiveness, not accuracy.

### Revocation

A claim is revoked when 24 overturns it, a challenger wins, or the proof is shown to be forged (D-11).

- **Fields revert.** Every field written under the revoked claim reverts to its pre-claim value —
  or to empty where the claim filled an empty field. The revoked values are retained in per-field
  history marked `revoked-origin`, never as the live value. Reverting rather than demoting is
  deliberate: the claim's authority is what admitted those values and the authority was void. Some of
  them were probably true — a squatter who correctly fixed the PA spec still fixed it — but a true
  value is cheap to recover (the community re-suggests it, or the harvest confirms it, 16.05.06) and a
  subtly-wrong value left live at community class is not. Same instinct as 16.05.07 D-09: prefer the
  conservative failure.
- **Bookings are honoured.** A band booked in good faith and has plans; cancelling punishes them for
  the platform's failure. Bookings accepted under the revoked claim carry forward to whoever the record
  reverts to, flagged: *"accepted under a claim later revoked — either party may cancel with no penalty
  until [date +7 days]."* Bookings priced against a reverted rate card honour the price they were
  booked at.
- **Money mostly has not moved**, because of D-09's settling period. Whatever did is 24/finance's
  recovery problem — flagged here, not solved here.
- **The record reverts to its prior state**: unclaimed if it was unclaimed, or to the prior org if the
  revocation came from a won challenge. The revoked org is barred from re-claiming that record; a
  pattern across records is a 24 matter.

### Happy Path

1. Operator searches "The Lexington, London". A seeded record already exists, unclaimed, marked
   "unverified — from public data" (16.05.01 D-03). They select it and choose **Claim**.
2. System takes the **anchor snapshot**: for each contact field, the most recent value of class
   `seeded` or `statutory` from the field's provenance history (16.05.03 D-01). Human-supplied values
   are excluded — so nothing the claimant could have typed is verifiable against.
3. System offers only the rungs that snapshot supports, strongest first. The postal rung (L6) is always
   offered, because every seeded record has an address. This record's source entry also carries a
   contact phone, so the phone rung (L3) is offered above it.
4. Operator picks the phone rung. A 6-digit code is placed to **the number in the anchor set** — never
   to a number the Operator typed. The message names the venue and states plainly that someone is
   claiming its WeJammin record and the code should not be shared unless they requested it.
5. Operator answers the venue's landline and enters the code inside its 10-minute window.
6. Control transfers to the Operator's **org** (01), not their personal account (D-05). Status becomes
   Claimed; the verified badge appears.
7. Every field's provenance survives (CX-07). The load-in a band corrected in March is still credited
   to that band, still first-hand class. Claiming grants the right to write, not to rewrite. No
   freshness clock resets (CX R-02) — the Operator has proved control, not accuracy.
8. Operator now writes spec fields directly and sets rate cards (16.06.07). The community keeps
   suggesting (16.05.03); those suggestions now queue to them.
9. The record carried 4 bookings taken before the claim. These are **inherited**: they stay live, their
   deposits do not settle to the new org for 14 days, and cancelling them is rate-limited and notified
   to the counterparty (D-09). Nothing the Operator does in the first 14 days moves an inherited
   booking's money.
10. Payouts are **not** part of this. To be paid, the org completes payout setup on its finance profile
    under its own KYC (01/23). A claim never moves money (D-06).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Claimant edits the phone number, then verifies against it | **Blocked at the root.** The anchor snapshot reads the field's most recent `seeded`/`statutory` value from immutable per-field history (16.05.03 D-04). Their edit changed the live value, not the anchor. The domain's central attack, dead by construction | Code goes to the pre-existing number; the claimant never sees it |
| A sock puppet plants a phone number as a community suggestion, then the attacker claims against it | Blocked — community class is **never** anchor-eligible (D-07), regardless of age or apparent independence. No link-detection required for the base case; linked-account signals (24) are defence in depth, not the defence | Rung not offered |
| Record has no phone, no website, no email — only name/address/geo/type | The realistic majority (16.05.01 seeds exactly this). Falls to **L6 postal**, which is always available. Slow, and correct | "We'll post a code to the venue. Usually 2–5 days." |
| Record has **no seeded values at all** — a musician created it (16.01.01) | No anchor set exists. Every value on the record is community class. Only **L7 document review** is available. This is every studio (16.05.01 D-05) — see DT-10, Q-01 | "We'll need a document — reviewed within 5 working days" |
| Two parties claim the same venue | Both claims held; **record stays live and bookable**; escalates to 24. The platform cannot adjudicate ownership (D-03) | "Claim under review" — booking unaffected |
| Two claimants **both** complete a strong rung within minutes | Not first-past-the-post. Both proved control of the same premises — that is a real dispute (a venue and its booking agent both answer the phone), not a race to settle by timestamp. Both held → contested → 24 | Both see "Another party has also proved control — under review" |
| Venue is sold; old Operator still controls the record and won't cooperate | Challenge path: challenger proves control against a fresh anchor set → incumbent gets a 14-day counter-window with maximal notification → silence transfers (D-10). The anchor follows the premises, which is why the new owner passes and the departed one does not | Incumbent: "Someone has proved control of this venue. Re-prove by [date] or control transfers." |
| Person who claimed leaves the company | Nothing happens — the claim is the **org's** (D-05). Staff turnover cannot orphan a record | No change |
| The claiming org dissolves; every member account is deleted | No one is behind the claim, but the badge and the lock persist. Falls to **dormancy** (D-12) after 12 months + a stale queue + 3 unanswered pings, then to a challenge from anyone who can prove control | "This record's operator has not responded in 12 months" |
| Booker claims the venue rather than the owner | Common and mostly benign — the booker *is* the operational controller. Proof-of-control is the right test precisely because it does not ask who owns the freehold (D-01) | Claim succeeds; control is what is proven |
| Claimant is a squatter with a forged premises licence | L7 → **Provisional only**. Reviewer cross-checks the named holder against the public licensing register (16.01.06), which is where most forgeries die. Never touches calendar, terms, payouts, or the badge | Manual review; no badge |
| A claim would gain control of a live calendar with bookings | **No higher proof bar** — the bar cannot discriminate (a hostile claimant who passes L3 passes any bar), and a higher bar punishes the busiest, most obviously-real venues. Instead the blast radius shrinks: 14-day settling period on inherited deposits, ≤3 inherited cancellations per 24h (4th → 24), counterparty contacts revealed per-booking not in bulk, every inherited cancellation notified with the claim's age attached (D-09) | Band: "This venue was claimed 3 days ago. Your booking and deposit are unaffected." |
| Fresh org (created 5 minutes ago) + L7 document + a record with 40 live bookings | Allowed to claim — everyone starts somewhere — but the combination is the highest-risk state the feature can be in. Provisional only, auto-flagged to 24, settling period runs from any eventual transfer | Provisional; no badge |
| Hijacker messages every inherited counterparty from behind the verified badge | The badge is a **phishing asset** (DT-12). During the settling period, outbound messages to inherited counterparties are stamped "This venue's record changed hands on [date]" and rate-limited to the same ≤3/24h as cancellations. Payment instructions can never be changed by messaging, because payouts were never in the claim (D-06) | Band sees the stamp before the message body |
| Claim opens while a merge is in flight (CX-09) | Claim **blocks on the merge**. Claiming the wrong one of two duplicates is expensive to unpick | "This record has a merge under review — claiming will be available once it resolves" |
| Two duplicate records, each claimed by a different org | **Not a merge.** Two parties asserting they run one room is a contested claim (16.05.02 + 24), never a survivorship decision. Dedup must hand off, not resolve (CX-09) | Both orgs + 24 notified |
| A claimed record is later merged away as a duplicate | The claim **follows the survivor**. A claim is a fact about a place, not about a row | Redirect; claim intact |
| The org already holds a claim on a duplicate of the record they're claiming | Offer **merge** (16.05.07), not a second claim | "You already operate a record for this place — merge them?" |
| Someone reads the phone code out to a caller who social-engineered them | The person answering the venue's phone *is* exercising control, so the rung is not wrong — but the human is the weak point. The code message states the venue name and what the code does, unprompted. Beyond that, 24 | — |
| Claimant's org name is a homoglyph of the real business ("Тhe Lexington", Cyrillic Т) to pass the register match | Register matching is Unicode-normalised and confusable-folded before comparison, and L2 matches are human-spot-checked; homoglyph and lookalike detection is 24's | Rung fails; flagged |
| Claim spam across many records | 5 claim opens per org per 24h; 20 per 30 days; beyond → 24. Rate limits are per **org**, not per account, so spinning up accounts inside one org buys nothing | "Claim limit reached — try again tomorrow" |
| Network failure after the code is entered; response lost | The code stays valid for its full window and re-entry is idempotent — the same code succeeds. A dropped response must never cost the user a rung | Retry works |
| Postal address is right but the geocode is 200m off (16.05.01) | Postal rung unaffected — mail routes on the address string. The strongest anchor is also the one most tolerant of the seeding defect most likely to be present | No impact |
| Shared premises — 6 independent studios at one address; mail lands in a common hallway | **The postal rung is not anchor-eligible where the address resolves to 2+ distinct place records** (D-14). It cannot tell the businesses apart, and neither can the postman. Falls to a rung that discriminates: phone, domain, or register. For a studio with none of those, there is no path but L7 (DT-11, Q-03) | "Postal verification isn't available at a shared address" |
| A new business opens at a closed venue's old address and claims the old record | They can claim it — but the record **is the business, not the address**. Renaming a claimed record to a materially different business name within 30 days of the claim flags to 24, and the prior name and its full provenance history stay publicly visible. Otherwise this is reputation laundering: inheriting a decade of another venue's verified reports by taking its postbox | Record history shows "Previously listed as …" |
| Record was suppressed by seeding as "not a music place" (16.05.01 Rejected state) | **Claimable.** An Operator claiming a suppressed record is the correction signal — a real venue wrongly filtered out. A successful strong-rung claim un-suppresses it | Record returns to the registry, claimed |
| Territory has no public premises-licence register (domain Q-13) | L2 does not exist there and L7 loses its external cross-check. The ladder degrades to postal + a human reading a PDF. Stated rather than papered over — see Q-05 | Fewer rungs offered |
| Provisional claim never upgrades within 30 days | Lapses back to Unclaimed. Spec edits made under it are **retained** and demoted to community class — they were reversible, attributable, and possibly correct, and the claimant was never shown to be acting in bad faith. This is the one place demotion beats reversion, because no authority was ever void — it simply expired | "Your claim expired. Your edits remain as community contributions." |
| Fan navigates to the claim route by URL | **404, not 403.** A 403 confirms the flow exists and that this record is claimable | Not found |

### States

**Claim lifecycle** — the substantive states:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unclaimed | Default for seeded/community records | Public record + "unverified — from public data" + claim CTA |
| Claim pending | Claim opened, anchor snapshot taken, strong rung in flight (code posted/sent, DNS token awaiting) | Record unchanged and fully bookable; claimant sees rung progress + expiry |
| Provisional | L7 document submitted, awaiting human review | Public: "Claim under review", no badge. Claimant: spec editing only, calendar and terms locked |
| Claimed | Strong rung completed, or L7 accepted | Verified operator badge; org-controlled; suggestions now queue to them |
| Settling | Control transferred on a record carrying pre-existing bookings; 14-day window running | Operator: "Inherited deposits settle from [date]". Counterparties: "This venue changed hands on [date]; your booking is unaffected" |
| Contested | 2+ claims, or a challenger and incumbent both prove control | **Record live and bookable**; claim frozen; 24 escalation; no badge change for an existing incumbent |
| Challenged | Re-verification challenge succeeded; 14-day counter-window running | Incumbent: re-prove by [date] or control transfers. Public: no change — the business is trading |
| Dormant | 12 months no org action + queue items > 90 days + 3 unanswered pings | Badge retained; pending queue falls back to the unclaimed write policy (16.05.03). No freshness clock touched |
| Transferred | Cooperative org → org handover, accepted | Ownership history visible on the record |
| Revoked | 24 overturns, challenger wins, or proof shown forged | Reverts to prior state; fields written under the claim revert with history marked `revoked-origin`; bookings honoured with a 7-day mutual no-penalty exit (D-11) |
| Lapsed | Provisional hit 30 days without upgrade | Back to Unclaimed; edits retained at community class |

**Interface states** — what the claimant sees while a rung runs:

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Anchor snapshot being computed; register lookup in flight | Skeleton on the rung list; "Checking what we can verify against…" — never a spinner with no copy, because the wait is explained by what it is doing |
| Empty | Anchor set is empty — no `seeded`/`statutory` contact values (every studio, every musician-created record) | Not a dead end: "We have no independent data for this place, so we'll need a document." L7 presented as the path, not as a failure |
| Populated | Anchor set has ≥1 eligible value | Rung list, strongest first, each showing what it will do ("We'll call 020 7…37 — the number on public record") and its expected duration |
| Error | Code expired, 3 attempts exhausted, DNS token not found, register unreachable | Specific cause + the remaining rungs, never a generic failure. Register unreachable degrades to "we'll try again — meanwhile, these rungs work now" |
| Partial | Rung completed but the record is contested, blocked on a merge, or Provisional pending review | Exactly what is granted now, what is not, and what unblocks it, with a date |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Claiming proves ownership | It is called "ownership verification" in the candidate list | ❌ REJECTED — and the reframe matters. The platform cannot determine who legally owns a business; it can determine who **controls** it (answers its phone, holds its domain, has the keys). Proof-of-control is achievable, sufficient for every product purpose, and honest. Proof-of-ownership is a claim the platform would be making without evidence — and it would make every contested claim the platform's legal problem instead of 24's process problem. |
| DT-02 | Verify against the contact details on the record | The obvious mechanism — call the venue's number | ❌ REJECTED as stated — **this is the domain's central vulnerability**. If the claimant can edit the record first, they verify against themselves. The rule must be: proof anchors only to data that **predates the claim** and that the claimant did not supply. Stated explicitly because it is the kind of thing that is obvious once said and catastrophic if unsaid. |
| DT-03 | A contested claim should freeze the record | Two claims means the data is untrustworthy; lock it | ❌ REJECTED: the record is a **trading business's** public presence and calendar. Freezing it takes a real venue offline over a dispute between two parties who both say they run it — punishing the business for the platform's inability to adjudicate. The claim freezes; the record stays live and bookable. |
| DT-04 | Creating a record should grant control of it | The person who added it cares most about it | ❌ REJECTED: it would make record creation a land grab, and a musician who adds their local rehearsal room has proved nothing about controlling it. **Creating ≠ owning.** This separation is what lets the community populate the registry (16.01.01) without the community capturing it. |
| DT-05 | Claim verification is a mechanism serving many domains | Artist profiles (01), gear (15), credits (02) all have claim-an-unclaimed-entity flows | ✅ CONFIRMED — recorded as a **cross-cut**. The generic flow (prove control → transfer write access → handle contests) is platform machinery. What stays domain-owned is the venue-specific **proof ladder** (a phone at the listed number, a premises licence, a venue's domain) and the venue-specific stakes (a claim inherits a live calendar and other people's deposits). Mechanism cross-cut, proofs domain-owned. |
| DT-06 | The proof ladder needs a rung that is always available | DT-02's anchor rule is only as good as the anchor set; if a record has nothing to anchor to, the rule has no teeth and the flow has no path | ✅ CONFIRMED — **postal-to-premises is the anchor of last resort, and it is the one rung that cannot be absent.** 16.05.01 seeds name, address, geo, type on every record, so the address is the only anchor guaranteed to exist. It is slow, unglamorous, and structurally superior: physically receiving mail at the premises is an excellent proxy for controlling the premises, it is what Google Business Profile has leaned on for two decades for exactly this reason, and it survives the seeding defect most likely to be present (a 200m-off geocode — mail routes on the address string). The corollary is uncomfortable and worth stating: if 16.05.01 seeds *only* name/address/geo/type, then **the phone rung — this feature's own headline, and its original Happy Path — does not exist for most records at launch**, and postal is not the fallback but the default. See Q-02. |
| DT-07 | A successful claim should give the Operator everything about their venue, payouts included | It is their business; withholding the money surface is artificial and will read as friction | ❌ REJECTED — **and this is the highest-leverage decision in the feature.** Every attack here is ultimately an attack on money. Sever the claim from the payout destination — making a payout change an independently KYC-verified action on the org's finance profile (01/23) — and a *successful* hijack yields: reversible spec edits, sight of a calendar (a privacy harm), and the ability to cancel bookings (griefing). All real, none of them the payoff that makes hijacking a trading business worth the effort. The threat model collapses from "someone steals a venue's income" to "someone vandalises a venue's page", and those need very different amounts of defence. It costs the legitimate Operator nothing they would not expect: they set up payouts once, through finance, with KYC, exactly as every other platform makes them. **Design the claim so that winning it is not worth the crime.** |
| DT-08 | A claim can be a private fact between the org and the platform | It is administrative metadata; the public cares about the badge, not the claim | ❌ REJECTED, on two independent grounds. First, trivially: a "verified operator" badge *is* a public claim — a secret claim with a public badge is incoherent. Second, and the reason it matters: the multi-hyphenate makes claim status a **conflict-of-interest signal**. An Operator who holds a claim on a 200-cap room can suggest edits to a rival 200-cap room down the road (16.05.03 lets anyone suggest anything). "Capacity is actually 80" from a competitor is sabotage wearing curation's clothes, and it is invisible unless the suggester's claims are queryable. So: **a claim is public and queryable, and a suggestion carries its author's competing-claim status as a declared interest.** This feature owns the fact; **16.05.04 owns what to do with it** — this is a boundary, not a land grab. |
| DT-09 | A claim is a one-time event — prove it once, hold it forever | Every claim flow works this way, and re-verifying a settled operator is friction for no gain | ❌ REJECTED. Everything in this sub-domain is perishable — 16.05.05 is a whole feature about that — and a claim is no exception: the claimant left in 2028, the org folded, the venue sold twice. The finding that matters is sharper than "claims go stale": **an abandoned claim is strictly worse than an unclaimed record.** An unclaimed record has a live write policy for suggestions (16.05.03 Q-01); a claimed one queues them to a reviewer who no longer exists, forever. Abandonment converts an editable record into a frozen one and calls it progress. Hence **Dormant** (D-12). Deliberately kept clear of CX R-02, which rejects Claim ↔ Decay on the grounds that "claiming is not verifying" — that is about a claim resetting *field* clocks and it is right. Dormancy touches no field clock. It measures the **operator's responsiveness**, not the record's accuracy, and they are different things with different consequences. |
| DT-10 | The proof ladder works for studios as well as venues | Same flow, same rungs, different `type` on the record | ❌ **REJECTED — the anchor model is venue-shaped and structurally excludes the Producer.** Compose two decisions written elsewhere and the hole appears: 16.05.01 D-05 says studios cannot be seeded (public data knows venues, not studios — many are residential, unlisted, deliberately private), and D-07 here says only `seeded`/`statutory` values can anchor. Therefore **a studio record has no seeded values, therefore no anchor set, therefore no strong rung — ever.** L7 document review is not the studio's fallback; it is the studio's *only* path, at cold start and permanently. The weakest, slowest, most forgeable, most expensive-to-operate rung is the primary rung for an entire persona — and not a marginal one: the Producer is the persona `problem-statement.md` calls "the capture point", without whom the provenance wedge has no mechanism. Neither sibling feature is wrong; the gap is only visible where they meet, which is exactly the kind of thing that survives to production. The tempting fix — a "corroborated presence" rung (N≥3 completed bookings by ≥3 distinct counterparty orgs, all naming the claimant as operator) — is a genuinely strong anchor the platform generates itself and no claimant can fabricate alone, and it is **useless at cold start**, because transacting requires a claimed record. Circular. Recorded honestly as Q-01/Q-06 rather than resolved with a mechanism that does not work when it is needed. |
| DT-11 | The postal rung is universally safe because an address is an address | DT-06 establishes postal as the floor; a floor should hold everywhere | ❌ REJECTED — **the anchor is the premises; the claim is the business; at a shared address those are not the same thing.** A rehearsal complex with 12 rooms is one org and fine. A studio building where six engineers rent rooms independently is **six orgs at one postal address**, and mail cannot discriminate between them — it reaches whoever collects the post, which is the landlord or the first person down the hall. The floor has a hole in it, and it is under the Producer again (DT-10), because shared buildings are how studios overwhelmingly exist. Hence D-14: postal is not anchor-eligible where an address resolves to 2+ distinct place records; the claim falls to a rung that discriminates below building level (phone, domain, register) — and a small studio has none of those. It also surfaces a structural question this feature cannot answer: domain D-06 makes the **room** the spec- and calendar-carrying unit, while the claim model is **place**-level. Six independently-operated rooms in one building need six claims at one address, and whether the place record supports per-room claims by different orgs is 16.01.02's to answer (Q-03). |
| DT-12 | The damage window after a hijack is about data | The claim grants writes; the worst case is wrong values and a wrecked calendar | ❌ REJECTED — **the verified badge is a phishing asset, and it converts far better than the data does.** A hijacker holding a venue's record for 48 hours can message every band with a booking, from inside the platform, wearing the platform's own trust signal, with the venue's real name attached: *"our card machine is down, send the balance here."* That is not a data attack and no amount of provenance ranking touches it — the platform is the one vouching. D-06 removes the on-platform payout vector; it cannot remove an off-platform payment instruction sent through a channel the recipient trusts *because we told them to*. Hence the settling period gates outbound messaging to inherited counterparties, not just money: stamped with "this venue's record changed hands on [date]", rate-limited identically to cancellations. The general lesson is worth carrying to every claim flow on the platform (DT-05's cross-cut): **a trust signal is a capability, and transferring it is transferring a capability** — so it should transfer at the speed of trust, not the speed of the database write. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16.05.01 Seeding** ([./16.05.01-place-data-seeding-ingestion.md](./16.05.01-place-data-seeding-ingestion.md)) — **hard dependency, and the sharpest unresolved seam in the sub-domain.** The anchor set is *entirely* 16.05.01's output: no seeded contact values, no strong rung. Their Happy Path step 2 seeds "name, address, geo, type — nothing more, because nothing more is reliable", which silently deletes rungs L3/L4/L5 from this feature for every record at launch and makes postal the default rather than the fallback (DT-06, Q-02). Their D-05 (studios cannot be seeded) deletes the *whole ladder* for studios (DT-10, Q-01). Seed + claim is one acquisition loop (CX-02) and the loop's strength is set by what seeding carries.
- Touches **16.05.03 Suggested Edits & Field Provenance** ([./16.05.03-suggested-edits-field-provenance.md](./16.05.03-suggested-edits-field-provenance.md)) — **this feature is unimplementable without their D-01 (per-field provenance) and D-04 (immutable history)**: the anchor rule reads a *superseded* seeded value out of history, which record-level or mutable provenance cannot provide. Their D-02 class ranking *is* the anchor eligibility test. Claiming flips the write model — org writes directly, everyone else suggests (CX-07) — and their Q-01 (suggestions on unclaimed records) is what Dormant falls back to (D-12).
- Touches **16.05.04 Owner-vs-Community Conflict Resolution** ([./16.05.04-owner-vs-community-conflict-resolution.md](./16.05.04-owner-vs-community-conflict-resolution.md)) — a claim is public and queryable (D-13), so a suggester's competing-claim status is available as a **declared interest** on suggestions to rival records. This feature owns the fact; 16.05.04 owns the policy (DT-08).
- Touches **16.05.05 Verification Decay** ([./16.05.05-verification-decay-freshness-scoring.md](./16.05.05-verification-decay-freshness-scoring.md)) — **explicitly a non-relationship, and deliberately kept that way.** CX R-02 stands: claiming is not verifying, and no claim, transfer, challenge, dormancy transition, or revocation touches a field's freshness clock. Recorded so a future reader sees the silence as a decision.
- Touches **16.05.06 Post-Gig Harvesting** ([./16.05.06-post-gig-session-data-harvesting.md](./16.05.06-post-gig-session-data-harvesting.md)) — the completed booking as credential (sub-domain D-08) is the seed of the rejected "corroborated presence" rung: a platform-generated anchor no claimant can fabricate alone, useless at cold start (DT-10, Q-06).
- Touches **16.05.07 Duplicate Detection & Merge** ([./16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md](./16.05.07-duplicate-detection-merge.md)) — CX-09, three distinct rules: a claim **blocks** on an in-flight merge; two *claimed* duplicates are a contested claim and must be **handed off, never merged** (merging would decide an ownership dispute by picking a survivor); a claim **follows the survivor** when its record is merged away.
- Touches **16.01.01 Place Record / 16.01.02 Rooms** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/](../16.01-place-records-rooms/)) — **structural tension**: domain D-06 makes the *room* the spec- and calendar-carrying unit while the claim is *place*-level. Shared premises need per-room claims by different orgs at one address (DT-11, Q-03). Also: musician-created records (16.01.01) carry only community-class values and therefore cannot anchor their creator's later claim — this is what makes D-04 enforceable.
- Touches **16.01.06 Licences, Insurance & Statutory Records** ([../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.06-licences-insurance-statutory-records.md](../16.01-place-records-rooms/16.01.06-licences-insurance-statutory-records.md)) — the public premises-licence register is simultaneously the **strongest rung (L2)** and the **only external check inside the weakest (L7)**. Where it is absent (domain Q-13), the ladder loses both at once.
- Touches **16.06 Space Booking & Reservations** ([../16.06-space-booking-reservations/](../16.06-space-booking-reservations/)) — the stakes. Inherited bookings (16.06.03) keep their deposits unsettled for 14 days; inherited cancellations are rate-limited; rate cards (16.06.07) and the calendar (16.06.01) are Full-tier only, never Provisional. A hostile claim inherits other people's money and other people's plans, and D-09 is the answer to both.
- Touches **01 Identity, Profiles & Organizations** ([../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/](../../01-identity-profiles-organizations/)) — **high confidence**: control grants to an **org**, not a person; multi-site groups are one org with N claims; staff turnover cannot orphan a record; org dissolution is what dormancy catches. The generic claim mechanism (prove control → transfer write access → handle contests) is 01's platform machinery serving 01/02/15 (DT-05); only the ladder and the stakes stay here.
- Touches **23 Career & Finance / payout profile** ([../../23-career-finance/](../../23-career-finance/)) — the other half of D-06. The payout destination is an independently KYC-verified property of the org's finance profile, never reachable through a claim. This is the boundary that makes hijacking not worth doing (DT-07).
- Touches **19 Ticketing** ([../../19-ticketing/](../../19-ticketing/)) — a hijacked venue record with ticketed shows attached exposes ticket revenue that is 19's, not 16's. D-06's principle must hold there too: controlling a venue record must not redirect a ticket settlement.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([../../24-trust-safety-disputes/](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — the escalation target for: contested claims, both-parties-proved-control, L7 document review, homoglyph/confusable org names, linked-account detection, the 4th inherited cancellation in 24h, revocation, recovery of money settled to a revoked org, and repeat-claimant patterns. The platform does not adjudicate ownership; 24 runs the process.
- **Cross-cut mechanism**: entity claim & control verification serves 01, 02, 15 — recorded in the domain CX file (DT-05). DT-12's lesson generalises to all of them: transferring a trust signal is transferring a capability.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Proof-of-**control**, not proof-of-ownership | The platform cannot adjudicate legal ownership; control is achievable, sufficient and honest (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Proof must anchor to data that **predates the claim** and that the claimant did not supply | Otherwise a claimant edits the phone number and verifies against themselves — the central attack (DT-02) |
| D-03 | A contested claim freezes the **claim**, never the record | Freezing takes a trading business offline over the platform's inability to adjudicate (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Creating a record does not grant control of it | Otherwise record creation is a land grab and community population becomes community capture (DT-04) |
| D-05 | Claims grant control to an **org** (01), not a person | Multi-site operators, staff turnover, and business sale all require it |
| D-06 | **A claim never grants or changes a payout destination.** Payout setup is an independently KYC-verified action on the org's finance profile (01/23) | The decision that collapses the threat model: a successful hijack yields vandalism and griefing, not income. Winning the crime is not worth committing it (DT-07) |
| D-07 | The **anchor set** is the record's contact fields' most recent values of class `seeded` or `statutory`, read from immutable per-field history and snapshotted at claim open. No human-supplied value — owner, first-hand, community, or record-creation — is ever anchor-eligible | Makes D-02 operational rather than aspirational. Kills both the edit-then-verify attack and the sock-puppet-plant attack at the root, without relying on link detection. Depends entirely on 16.05.03 D-01/D-04 |
| D-08 | Two control tiers: **Provisional** (L7 pre-review) grants spec writes and enquiry replies only; **Full** grants everything except payouts | Provisional exists so an Operator is not dead for 5 business days. It grants exactly the actions whose worst case is a reversible, attributable wrong value — never the calendar, terms, badge, or money |
| D-09 | A claim on a record with pre-existing bookings faces **no higher proof bar** — it faces a **14-day settling period** on inherited bookings: deposits unsettled, ≤3 cancellations/24h, per-booking (not bulk) counterparty contacts, stamped and rate-limited outbound messaging | A higher bar cannot discriminate (whoever passes L3 passes it) and would punish the busiest, most obviously-real venues. The risk is not the claim; it is the first destructive action after it. Shrink the blast radius, not the door (Q-02, DT-12) |
| D-10 | An incumbent claim is overridable: a challenger proves control against a **fresh** anchor set, then the incumbent gets a **14-day counter-window** with maximal notification. Silence transfers; both proving control escalates to 24. The cooperative transfer path stays one action | The anchor follows the **premises**, not the org — which is why the new owner passes and the departed one does not, and why this is not a hijack vector: the challenger must still prove control (Q-01) |
| D-11 | On revocation: fields written under the claim **revert** to pre-claim values (history retained, marked `revoked-origin`); bookings taken under it are **honoured**, with a 7-day mutual no-penalty exit; the org is barred from re-claiming that record | Revert rather than demote because the authority that admitted those values was void. A true value is cheap to recover; a subtly-wrong one left live is not. Cancelling the bookings would punish bands for the platform's failure (Q-04) |
| D-12 | A claim has its own lifecycle and can go **Dormant** (12mo no action + 90-day-old queue + 3 unanswered pings): badge retained, pending queue falls back to the unclaimed write policy. **Dormancy touches no freshness clock** | An abandoned claim is worse than an unclaimed record — it converts an editable record into a frozen one. Kept clear of CX R-02: this measures operator responsiveness, not record accuracy (DT-09) |
| D-13 | **A claim is public and queryable**, and carries as a declared interest onto its holder's suggestions to competing records. 16.05.04 owns what to do with it | A secret claim with a public badge is incoherent; and the multi-hyphenate makes an Operator's suggestion to a rival's record a conflict of interest that is invisible unless claims are queryable (DT-08) |
| D-14 | The claim unit is the **place record**. The postal rung is **not anchor-eligible** where an address resolves to 2+ distinct place records | Mail cannot tell six studios in one building apart; neither can the postman. The floor has a hole and it is under the Producer (DT-11) |
| D-15 | Rate limits are per **org**, not per account: 5 claim opens/24h, 20/30 days | Per-account limits are defeated by adding accounts to the org. The org is the unit that holds the claim, so it must be the unit that is limited |
