## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Seeding ↔ Dedup & Merge

**Relationship**: These ship together or neither ships. Seeding pulls one venue from OSM, Wikidata
and MusicBrainz with three spellings and two addresses; without dedup the registry presents three
records for one room on launch day. That is not untidy — it splits the calendar, so a band books
record A while the Operator manages record B, and the platform *causes* the double-booking the
Operator persona came here to escape. **A duplicated registry is worse than an empty one**, because
an empty one at least does not lie.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: finds three records for their venue and has to merge before they can trust anything.
- **Musician**: sees three search results for one room and cannot tell which is live.
- **Producer**: same, though studio seeding is thin (16.05.01 DT-05) so they feel it less.
- **Fan**: sees a show attached to one of three records; the other two look like different venues.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Merge decides which field values survive — per field by provenance class (CX-06), never per record.
2. **Trigger chain**: Ingest → detect within source → detect across sources → detect against existing → flag. Detection failure is silent and cumulative, which is the dangerous property: nothing alerts, the registry just quietly forks.
3. **Permission intersection**: Detection is automatic; merge requires the Operator or a reviewer (16.05.07 D-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A claimed record with a new merge candidate notifies the Operator.
5. **State transition conflict**: Re-ingestion racing a merge could resurrect a merged-away record. Rejected/merged pairs must be remembered (16.05.07 D-05) or the loop is infinite.

### CX-03: Decay ↔ Harvest — *the engine*

**Relationship**: The single most important pair in domain 16. Decay is a **measurement**: it says
"this PA claim is 26 months old" and changes nothing about the world. Harvest is the **only**
mechanism that produces a fresh, first-hand, disinterested fact. Run decay without harvest and you
have built a system that documents its own rot with great precision — every field correctly labelled
stale, nothing ever refreshed, and a registry that is honest about being useless.

The pair also sets each other's shape: decay's per-field confidence is what tells harvest *which*
3–5 questions to ask (the least-certain fields), and harvest's answers are what reset decay's clocks.
Neither has a purpose alone.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: supply the harvest (`✅ Full`), read the decay. They are the engine.
- **Operator**: reads the decay as lost enquiries, receives the harvest as free QA (`📊 Reports`). Notably **not** the source of the strong facts about their own room.
- **Fan**: sees neither, beyond a possible "may be out of date" note.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — decay reads timestamps, harvest writes them. Clean separation.
2. **Trigger chain**: Reservation completes → prompt selects the least-certain fields (from decay) → answers write provenance events → clocks reset. If the prompt fails or is ignored, nothing breaks — the field simply stays stale. Graceful degradation, but degradation to a stale registry.
3. **Permission intersection**: Inverted from every other domain — the record owner cannot supply the strongest evidence about their own room; only a booked visitor can.
4. **Notification fan-out**: One prompt per completed reservation, shared with the PRO setlist ask (16.02.06 DT-04). The prompt budget is the constraint every feature wanting a post-gig question competes for.
5. **State transition conflict**: A harvested correction racing an Operator's own re-verification of the same field. Whichever lands second wins on timestamp — but they are different provenance classes, so timestamp order is the wrong tiebreak. Unresolved; see 16.05.05 Q-01.

### CX-04: Harvest ↔ Conflict Resolution

**Relationship**: 16.05.04's central problem is that when the Operator and a band disagree about a
physical fact, the platform has no way to know who is right. Harvest is the only thing that can
change that: a **second, independent, booked** visitor reporting the same three steps is evidence in
a way that neither party's assertion is. Without harvest, conflict resolution is permanently stuck at
"we do not know" — which 16.05.04 accepts as a legitimate state, but only as a resting position, not
a destination.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician / Producer**: the corroborators. Their independence is the entire value — they have no stake in the record.
- **Operator**: the party whose claim gets corroborated or contradicted, with no ability to influence the process.
- **Fan**: sees the contested marker; never participates (16.05.06 Role Lens — a Fan did not test the load-in).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The contested field's last-agreed value stands while corroboration accumulates.
2. **Trigger chain**: Harvest answer contradicts a claim → contest opens (or an existing one gains corroboration) → threshold reached → `[PENDING — Step 5]`: does the field flip, or escalate to 24?
3. **Permission intersection**: A harvest answer is only weighted if it came from a completed reservation (16.05.06 D-02) — the booking is the credential.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A contest notifies the Operator; corroboration probably should too, but a stream of "more people say you're wrong" is a hostile UX with a real defection risk.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two harvest answers contradicting *each other* (one band says 3 steps, another says step-free) — which is usually a **conditionality failure**, not a conflict (16.05.04 DT-05: the ramp was out one week and not the other). This is the strongest evidence for fixing conditionality upstream rather than arbitrating downstream.

### CX-05: Provenance ↔ Conflict Resolution

**Relationship**: The provenance class ranking (statutory > owner > first-hand > community > seeded)
does most of conflict resolution's work before a conflict is even declared. A seeded value never
contests an owner's claim — it is simply superseded. A statutory value is not contestable at all. The
conflicts that reach 16.05.04 are the ones the ranking **cannot** settle: owner vs first-hand, which
is precisely the pair where both are credible and the stakes are real.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: their class beats community and seeded; it does not beat statutory, and 16.01.04 D-04 proposes it should not beat first-hand on accessibility.
- **Musician / Producer**: first-hand class, contingent on a completed booking.
- **Fan**: limited suggestion rights, unresolved field scoping (16.05.03 Q-03).

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The ranking IS the merge strategy for same-field disagreements; 16.05.04 handles only same-class or exception cases.
2. **Trigger chain**: Suggestion → class comparison → auto-supersede (weaker) or contest (comparable) → 16.05.04.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes and deliberately inverted for statutory fields — being the record owner grants *less* authority there (16.01 CX-02).
4. **Notification fan-out**: The Operator is notified of contests, not of auto-supersessions.
5. **State transition conflict**: The unresolved core — does first-hand outrank owner generally (16.02.02 DT-03 argues yes for spec facts), or only on accessibility (16.01.04 DT-04)? This is 16.05.04 Q-01 and it is an owner decision, not an agent call.

### CX-02: Seeding ↔ Claim

**Relationship**: The domain's acquisition loop, in two features. The Operator never onboards in the
conventional sense — they search for their own venue, find a record that already exists, and claim
it. Seeding without claiming is a dead directory; claiming without seeding has nothing to claim.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: the entire journey — discover, claim, prove control, inherit a record the community has already been maintaining.
- **Musician / Producer**: created or corrected the record before the Operator ever arrived; keep suggesting afterwards.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Claiming does not erase community provenance — the record's history survives the ownership change (16.05.02 Happy Path 4).
2. **Trigger chain**: Seed → discover → claim → prove → transfer write access to the org.
3. **Permission intersection**: Pre-claim, nobody holds `✅`; post-claim, the org does — but only on non-statutory, non-exception fields.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A claim on a record with community contributors — do they get told the venue took it over? Unresolved and worth asking; they did the work.
5. **State transition conflict**: A claim landing while a merge is in flight (CX-09) — the claim should block on the merge, since claiming the wrong one of two duplicates is a mess to unpick.

### CX-09: Claim ↔ Dedup & Merge

**Relationship**: Recorded because the boundary is easy to get wrong. Two **unclaimed** duplicate
records are a merge. Two **claimed** records for one place, by different orgs, are a *contested
claim* — two parties both asserting they run the room — which is 16.05.02's problem and then 24's, not
dedup's. Dedup must recognise the case and hand off rather than attempt to resolve it, because
merging two claimed records would silently decide an ownership dispute by picking a survivor.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: both parties; each believes the other's record is the duplicate.
- **Musician / Producer / Fan**: no role.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Unresolvable by merge — it is an ownership question wearing a data-quality costume.
2. **Trigger chain**: Detection flags → both claimed → route to contested-claim (16.05.02), never to merge.
3. **Permission intersection**: Neither org can merge the other's record. Correct, and the reason the handoff must be explicit.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Both orgs; 24.
5. **State transition conflict**: The dangerous silent failure — auto-merging two claimed records would decide an ownership dispute by choosing a survivor record, with no process and no appeal.

### CX-06, CX-07, CX-08 — summary detail

**CX-06 (Provenance → Dedup survivorship)**: at merge, the strongest-class value wins **per field**,
not per record — so a merged record can legitimately take its address from one source, its PA from an
Operator, and its load-in from a band's harvest. Same-class ties are unresolved (16.05.07 Q-03).

**CX-07 (Claim → Provenance)**: claiming flips the write model — the org writes directly, everyone
else suggests. What it must **not** do is erase or downgrade what the community contributed before
the Operator arrived; the history survives, and so does the provenance of every field they did not
personally set.

**CX-08 (Provenance → Decay)** — *Medium confidence*: an accepted edit resets a clock. The open
question is whether the **class** of the edit changes the **strength** of the reset — an Operator's
one-tap self-confirmation is cheap and interested; a booked band's contradiction is neither. Treating
them identically means the Operator can keep a false claim permanently "fresh" by tapping a button.
`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — see 16.05.05 Q-01.
