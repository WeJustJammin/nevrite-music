# Feature: Post-Gig / Post-Session Data Harvesting

> **Number**: 16.05.06
> **Parent**: [Curation, Provenance & Data Integrity](./16.05-curation-provenance-data-integrity-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The moment after a gig or a session when the platform asks the one party who just tested every claim
on the record: **was it as described?** The load-in, the backline, the monitor count, the accessible
route, the green room that turned out to be a corridor.

This is the domain's engine. Everything else in 16.05 is scaffolding around it: decay (16.05.05)
labels staleness but cannot fix it; provenance (16.05.03) ranks sources but needs a strong one to
rank; conflict resolution (16.05.04) needs an independent witness to corroborate against. **Harvest
is the only mechanism in the domain that produces fresh, first-hand, disinterested facts** — and it
is the same capture-at-source pattern the whole platform is built on (D-18), applied to rooms
instead of splits.

Its entire value rests on three properties, in this order: the contributor **was actually there**
(the completed reservation proves it), they **actually observed the thing being asked about**
(nothing else earns first-hand class), and they have **no stake in the answer** (which the platform
must protect, both from the Operator's retaliation and from the Operator's own participation).
Remove any one and what remains is a review with a stronger provenance label on it — which is worse
than no harvest at all, because the registry would then rank fiction above fact.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | ✅ Full (primary contributor — **gig profile**) | The strongest source the registry has. After a gig they were **present at**, gets **one** prompt containing at most 5 questions, each quoting the record's current claim verbatim ("Load-in listed as *step-free via the rear yard*. Was it?"), each answerable in one tap with **"Didn't check" costing exactly the same one tap** as yes or no. First affordance is always "We didn't use the room". Sees precisely what their answer changed ("The next band now knows there are 3 steps"). Never nagged, never scored, never ranked. |
| Producer | ✅ Full (primary contributor — **session profile**) | Same mechanism, different profile: the session ran 8 hours and they used the mic locker item by item, so their question pool is gear-and-room-shaped and their prompt rides the **session-close flow they are already completing** (07/02) rather than arriving as a separate notification. `personas.md` makes them the trust broker — their attestation on studio facts (gear, sightlines, isolation) is the highest-value first-hand evidence in 16.03. |
| Operator | 📊 Reports (**receives; may never contribute**) | Sees what came back about their room as **one actionable item per field** ("Booked visitors report steps at load-in — update the field or dispute it"), never as a stream of accusations. Reports are **unattributed** and no finer-grained than the month (D-13) — the Operator learns the fact, not who said it. They cannot reply inside the harvest (disputes go through 16.05.04), cannot suppress it, and **cannot harvest their own room**: a completed reservation on a record they control produces an owner-class event, never first-hand (D-11). The party who owns the record is structurally barred from supplying its strongest evidence. That is the point. |
| Fan | ❌ None | No harvest path, and the exclusion is principled rather than protective. A Fan attending a gig did not carry an amp up the load-in, did not plug into the PA, and did not look at the mic locker — they have no first-hand knowledge of anything on the spec, so a Fan answer would manufacture a strong-class fact from no observation, which is the exact failure D-06 exists to prevent. A Fan's ticket is not a reservation and never triggers this. Their post-gig relationship is with the artist (20), not the room's data. |

## Behavior

### The trigger and the credential

Triggered by a **completed reservation** (16.06.03) — which is the whole reason booking belongs in
this domain rather than in 17 or 05. The booking is what proves the person was in the room; without
it, a "first-hand report" is an anonymous claim.

Completion in 16.06.03 is **inferred**, not declared ("nobody will tap completed"), which means the
trigger is load-bearing but not trustworthy on its own: a slot passes whether or not anyone turned
up. So the reservation earns the contributor the *right to be asked*; it does not earn their answer
its class. Two further gates do that:

1. **Attendance** — the prompt's first affordance is "We didn't use the room", because an inferred
   completion is a guess about the physical world (D-16).
2. **Observation** — every question offers "Didn't check" at equal cost, because being in the
   building is not the same as having tested the claim (D-06).

### Who gets asked

The reservation names a **party**; the eyewitness is a **person**. Those are routinely different —
`personas.md` models the Band as an entity, and gigs are booked by managers, agents and the drummer
who does admin, none of whom necessarily carried anything through the load-in. Sending the prompt to
the booking contact by default sends it to the person least likely to know.

The prompt therefore goes to the reservation's **named on-site party**. Where the booking is held by
a band entity (01) with named attendees, it goes to the attendees. Where no on-site party is named,
it goes to the booking contact with a single one-tap reassignment — "I wasn't there → ask [member]"
— which may be exercised **once**, to one recipient, and never re-reassigned (D-21). A prompt that
can be passed around indefinitely is a prompt nobody answers.

### What gets asked — suppression is the default

The prompt is **suppressed unless it earns its delivery**. Two independent conditions must both hold:

**(a) The record has something worth asking.** Candidate fields are ranked by uncertainty:

| Rank | Field state | Source | Why it is asked first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contested** — an open contest exists and this contributor is not already a party to it | 16.05.04 | Independent corroboration is the only thing that moves a contest; nothing else the platform can do resolves one |
| 2 | **Stale** — past its decay window | 16.05.05 | The registry is actively presenting an aged claim as a fact |
| 3 | **Never verified** — seeded, never touched by a human | 16.05.01 | Most of the registry at launch; the weakest provenance class there is |
| 4 | **Ageing** — past half its decay window | 16.05.05 | Cheap to refresh before it goes stale |
| — | **Fresh and uncontested** | — | **Never asked.** There is no question here |

Candidates are then filtered by **observability**: a band that played the main room cannot speak to
the studio's mic locker; a rehearsal band cannot speak to the venue's stage PA; nobody who booked a
daytime session can speak to the curfew. Only fields this contributor's reservation plausibly put
them in contact with survive the filter.

The top **3** ranked survivors are asked; **5** is the hard ceiling and is only reached when three or
more fields are contested. If **zero** survive, **no prompt is delivered at all** — which on a
weekly-rehearsal room is the normal outcome after the first two sessions, and is the correct one.

**(b) The contributor's budget allows it.** Two caps, both hard (D-14):

| Cap | Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Platform-wide | **1 harvest prompt per contributor per rolling 7 days**, across every room and every domain sharing this moment | A band playing four rooms on a week's tour gets asked once, not four times. Prompt fatigue costs the domain its engine; there is no field worth that |
| Per room | **1 per contributor per room per 90 days** | The weekly-rehearsal case. The 90-day cap is **waived** when the contributor's report would be independent corroboration of an open contest (rank 1) — corroboration is the scarcest thing the domain has. The 7-day cap is **never** waived |

Suppression is not a degraded state. It is the mechanism working: the platform asks only where it is
genuinely uncertain, and stops asking the moment it is not.

### How the question is asked

**The question quotes the claim verbatim** (D-07). "Was the load-in ok?" and "Load-in listed as
*step-free via the rear yard* — was it?" produce different, incomparable facts. The band that loaded
through the side door and found three steps has not contradicted a claim about the rear yard; they
have answered a different question. A paraphrased prompt harvests noise wearing the registry's
strongest provenance class.

**Confirmation is not symmetric with correction** (D-08). "Yes, as described" from a tired band at
1am is subject to acquiescence — the affirmative answer is the one that ends the interaction fastest,
so it is the answer the prompt's own design pressures them toward. A correction has no such
pressure: nobody types "3 steps" to get rid of a notification faster. Both write a first-hand
provenance event; a confirmation resets a shorter clock than a correction sets.

**Straight-lining is detected, not designed around** (D-09). An all-affirmative response submitted
faster than ~2 seconds per question — less time than reading the quoted claim takes — is recorded as
a weak confirmation and **resets no clock**. Polarity reversal (mixing "was it step-free?" with "were
there steps?" to defeat tapping) was considered and rejected: it is known to add measurement noise,
and a tired band at 1am is precisely the population on which it misfires.

**The last slot is free text, and it is optional** (D-18). The most valuable thing a band learns is
usually the fact the spec has no field for — "the load-in goes through the bar and you can't do it
after 6pm because of dinner service". A closed question set is structurally incapable of capturing
it. So one optional free-text box sits last, is never required, resets **no** clock (it has no field
and therefore no provenance), and routes to a **spec-gap queue** rather than to the record. It is the
domain's discovery channel for missing fields and for the conditionality problem four features have
independently hit (domain Q-03).

### What an answer is worth

Every answer becomes a **first-hand provenance event** (16.05.03 class 3), with two properties that
matter downstream:

- **The event's timestamp is the observation time — the reservation's slot — not the submission
  time** (D-10). A band answering from a van with no signal three days later observed the room on the
  night, and that is when the fact was true. This also gives CX-03's unresolved race a real answer:
  a harvested correction and an Operator's re-verification of the same field are ordered by **when
  each party observed the room**, not by which write landed second.
- **The class is earned by the reservation *and* by the absence of a control relationship** (D-11).
  An Operator who books their own room is not a first-hand witness to it; they are the owner, using a
  booking to launder an owner-class claim into a first-hand one. Their answer is recorded at
  owner-class. Same for anyone in the claiming org.

### One prompt, not many

16.02.06 DT-04 established that the PRO setlist ask and the spec correction ask must share this
moment. A band will tolerate one post-gig interruption. Two is one too many, and losing the harvest
to prompt fatigue costs the domain its only engine.

The prompt is therefore a **shared container with independently suppressible sections**. A licensed,
reporting room with nothing uncertain on its spec delivers a setlist-only prompt. An unlicensed room
with three stale fields delivers a harvest-only prompt. A room that is both delivers one prompt with
both sections, and the 3–5 question ceiling applies to the harvest section, not to the container.

This treaty is bilateral and the moment is not. 02 (credits), 17 (settlement), 18 (show report), 19
(ticketing) and 20 (fan follow-up) all want the same post-gig attention, and nothing above domain 16
owns the budget — see DT-10 and Q-05.

### When the prompt arrives

Timing is the whole design, and the two profiles need different answers (D-15):

| Completion time (local) | Delivered | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Between **21:00 and 06:00** (the gig case) | **10:00 local the following morning** | They are loading out, driving, or asleep. The facts asked about are structural — three steps do not survive the night any less well than the setlist does |
| Otherwise (the session / daytime-rehearsal case) | **Completion + 2 hours** | The session ended at 18:00, they are in the car, the mic locker is fresh, and it is not 1am |

The prompt **expires 96 hours after delivery** and there are **zero reminders** — D-05 is absolute,
not a default. An answer submitted after expiry (the van had no signal) is still accepted, because
the answer's validity is a property of when it was observed, not when it was uploaded (D-10).

### Anonymity is a precondition, not a courtesy

The music scene is small and venues, promoters and agents talk. A band that publicly corrects a
venue's spec and can be identified as having done so has a live risk of not being booked there — or
elsewhere — again. **An attributable harvest is an unanswered harvest**, and the domain loses its
engine to a chilling effect it built itself.

Harvest answers are therefore **never attributed to a named contributor in the Operator's view**, and
the Operator's report view is no finer-grained than the **month** (D-13). Identity is retained
internally — corroboration independence (D-12) and 24 escalation both require it — and is disclosed
to no one else, including the Operator, at any stage short of 24's own process.

This is a mitigation, not a solution, and the residual is stated plainly: a room with one gig a month
cannot be de-identified by coarsening a timestamp. See Q-07.

### Response rate — the domain's largest unvalidated assumption

The entire domain's data quality rests on an assumption nobody has tested: **that bands will
answer.**

The arguments for are real: the ask is three taps, they have just experienced the thing, and there is
reciprocity (they used this registry to pick the room). The arguments against are equally real: they
are tired, they are unpaid, the beneficiary is the next band, and correcting a venue carries a scene
risk that anonymity mitigates but does not erase.

**The design's honest position is that it must degrade to something, and what it degrades to is a
stale registry that is accurate about being stale.** Nothing breaks at a 0% response rate; decay
(16.05.05) simply labels every field correctly and forever. The design does three things about it and
claims no more:

1. **Reciprocity is the only compensation offered** — the answer screen shows what changed and who it
   helps ("The next band now knows there are 3 steps"). Not points, not badges, not a leaderboard
   (DT-13).
2. **The ask is made as small as it can be made** — suppression by default, 3 questions, one tap,
   "Didn't check" free.
3. **The response rate is measured from day one and is a validation gate, not a metric** (Q-01).

If validation shows the rate is near zero, the incentive design reopens as a **product decision for
the owner**, not an agent call — and the domain's MoSCoW changes with it, because a registry whose
engine does not turn is a directory.

### Where the trigger does not fire

Two structural holes, stated rather than papered over:

- **Off-platform gigs have no reservation, so no trigger, no credential, and no harvest.** At launch
  that is most gigs. The loop only works where the booking already lives here — which is the thesis
  (D-18) working exactly as described, and is also an inversion of the domain's launch sequence
  (Q-04).
- **Lockout tenancies generate no completion events** (16.06 CX-08; domain Q-10), so the harvest never
  fires on the supply that is used most and tested hardest. **The fix is that a tenancy's recurring
  billing cycle is the completion-equivalent** (D-19): a monthly renewal is proof of continued
  presence in exactly the way a completed reservation is proof of presence on a night, and a monthly
  cadence sits comfortably inside both prompt caps. The tenant is the single best-informed party
  about a rehearsal room's backline in the entire registry, and until this fires they are silent.

### Happy Path

1. Band completes a 4-hour load-in-to-curfew reservation at The Bunker; the slot ends 23:40 and
   16.06.03 infers **completed**
2. Selection runs against The Bunker's record: `load-in step-free` is **contested** (rank 1, an open
   contest from a previous band), `house kit` is **stale** (rank 2, verified 19 months ago), `PA make`
   is **ageing** (rank 4). Green room and curfew fail the observability filter for this reservation's
   room and slot. Three survivors → three questions
3. Budget check: the drummer is the reservation's named on-site party, has answered no prompt in 11
   days and none for this room in 6 months → both caps clear → prompt is queued
4. Completion fell between 21:00 and 06:00 → delivery scheduled for **10:00 the next morning**
5. 10:00: one prompt — "Quick check on The Bunker — 3 questions". First affordance: **"We didn't use
   the room"**. The drummer did play, so they continue
6. Q1 quotes the claim verbatim: *"Load-in listed as **step-free via the rear yard**. Was it?"* →
   **No** → "How many steps?" → **3**
7. Q2: *"House kit listed as **Yamaha Stage Custom, kick/snare/2 toms/hats**. Was it usable?"* →
   **Yes**
8. Q3: *"PA listed as **d&b Y-Series**. Right?"* → **Didn't check** — same single tap as the other two
   answers, no penalty, no follow-up
9. Optional free text, skipped. Submitted at 10:00:41 — 41 seconds, well past the straight-lining
   floor, and one of three answers is a correction, so acquiescence is not indicated
10. Three first-hand provenance events are written, all timestamped to **the reservation's slot**, not
    to 10:00 the next day:
    - `load-in` → **correction**: corroborates the open contest with an independent second report
      (16.05.04); the contest's corroboration count rises to 2 distinct unrelated booking parties
    - `house kit` → **confirmation**: resets the decay clock, on the shorter confirmation clock (D-08)
    - `PA make` → **"didn't check"**: recorded as an observability signal, resets nothing, and lowers
      `PA make`'s rank for future band-profile prompts
11. Drummer sees: *"Thanks — the next band now knows there are 3 steps at the rear yard."* No score, no
    badge, no follow-up. Total elapsed: 41 seconds
12. The Operator's report view gains **one actionable item**: *"Booked visitors report steps at the
    rear-yard load-in (July). Update the field or dispute it."* — no name, no date finer than the
    month, no way to reach the drummer

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Nobody answers.** The domain's central risk; the response rate is unmeasured | Nothing breaks and nothing improves. Every field decays correctly and forever; the registry becomes precisely accurate about being useless (16.05.05 DT-05). Measured from day one as a **validation gate** (Q-01), not a dashboard metric | The Musician sees nothing (the prompt expired quietly, no reminder). The Operator sees their record's freshness fall and their enquiries with it. A searching band sees "verified 3 years ago" on every field |
| **Band straight-lines all-yes to dismiss the prompt** | Worse than no answer — a false confirmation resets a clock on a false fact. All-affirmative submitted faster than ~2s/question → recorded as a **weak confirmation, resets no clock** (D-09). Slower all-yes answers are accepted but on the **shorter confirmation clock** (D-08) | Nothing — the contributor is not accused of anything, and the mechanism is invisible. Accusing a tired customer of lying is a worse product than absorbing the noise |
| **Band is angry about the gig and trashes the spec** | A retaliation vector against the room's data, and distinct from a review — a spec fact is not an opinion (DT-01). A single first-hand report never flips a field (16.05.04 D-01: last-agreed stands); it opens or joins a contest requiring **independent** corroboration (D-12), which one angry band cannot supply alone | Operator: a contest, unattributed. Musician: their report recorded, the field still showing the last-agreed value |
| **Gig happened off-platform** | No reservation, no trigger, no credential, no harvest. Most gigs at launch. **The loop only works where the booking already lives here** — the thesis (D-18) as a structural constraint, and an inversion of the launch sequence (Q-04) | Nothing. There is no prompt, and no honest way to manufacture one — a self-asserted "I played there" is exactly the anonymous claim D-02 exists to exclude |
| **Prompt arrives during load-out** | It does not. Completion between 21:00–06:00 defers to **10:00 local next day**; otherwise **completion + 2h** (D-15). The facts asked about are structural and survive a night; a prompt at 01:00 does not survive a band | A prompt at 10:00, once, with no reminder |
| **Same room, same band, every week (rehearsal)** | Prompt fatigue is guaranteed, and the fix is upstream of scheduling: after two sessions **nothing on the record is uncertain**, so selection returns zero fields and **no prompt is ever generated**. Backstopped by the **90-day per-room cap**, itself backstopped by the **7-day platform-wide cap** (D-14) | Nothing — and that silence is the mechanism succeeding, not failing |
| **Answers contradict the Operator** | Exactly what it is for → 16.05.04 opens or corroborates a contest | Contest opened; Operator sees an unattributed actionable item |
| **Reservation auto-completed but the gig never happened** (van broke down; venue flooded; promoter pulled it) | 16.06.03 infers completion from a passed slot, so it fires on shows that did not occur. **The prompt's first affordance is "We didn't use the room"** (D-16): it cancels the harvest, writes **zero** provenance, and feeds back to 16.06.03 that the completion inference was wrong — which is information 16.06.03 has no other way to get | "We didn't use the room" → "Thanks — we won't ask about this one." Nothing else. Never a follow-up asking why |
| **The booker was never in the room** (manager, agent, or the drummer who does admin booked it) | The reservation names a party; the eyewitness is a person. Prompt routes to the **named on-site party**; absent one, to the booking contact with **one** reassignment (D-21) | Booking contact: "I wasn't there → ask [member]" — one tap, one hop, no further passing |
| **Contributor is asked about a field they never observed** (green room they never entered) | The worst possible outcome is a manufactured first-hand fact, because it wears the registry's strongest class. **"Didn't check" costs exactly one tap, same as yes or no** (D-06), and is never penalised or followed up | "Didn't check" — accepted silently, no clock reset, no reprompt on that field |
| **A field accumulates "didn't check" answers** | It leaves the harvest pool for that contributor profile — which is honest and is also the mechanism's ceiling: **the fields no visitor can observe are exactly the fields most in need of independent verification**, and they stay owner-attested forever (DT-14). Not a bug in this feature; a boundary of it | Nothing. The field simply stops being asked about, and its provenance stays owner-class with an ageing clock |
| **Operator books their own room** | **Provenance laundering** — a completed reservation would otherwise upgrade an owner-class self-claim to first-hand class for the price of a booking. Blocked: any harvest from a party with a **control or claim relationship** to the record (16.05.02) is recorded **owner-class**, never first-hand (D-11) | The Operator answers a prompt and it lands as what it is: an owner self-confirmation, on the owner's clock (16.05.05 Q-01) |
| **Operator's friend books and confirms** (sockpuppet corroboration) | Independence is the entire value of corroboration, so it is checked rather than assumed: corroboration counts **distinct booking parties with no control relationship to the record and no shared org**; repeated reports from one party count **once** (D-12). One friend cannot corroborate anything; a coordinated ring is a **24** pattern | Contest corroboration count does not move |
| **Rival Operator books a competing room and reports downward** | The reservation is real and so is the observation — they *did* test the load-in, so the class is earned by the booking, not withheld for the persona. But a **pattern** of one Operator's reports consistently contradicting a competitor in the same market is a 24 signal, not 16's to adjudicate (Q-06) | Report accepted, unattributed. Pattern routes to 24 |
| **Band books the minimum slot, never attends, answers falsely to sabotage** | The **cheapest attack in the domain** — the price of a first-hand-class lie is one minimum booking. Mitigated, not solved: a single report never flips a field (16.05.04 D-01), corroboration requires independent parties (D-12), and repetition is a 24 pattern. Stated because it is the residual risk of making the booking the credential | Contest opened on one field; the last-agreed value still stands |
| **Record is merged (16.05.07) between the booking and the prompt** | The reservation points at record A; A merges into B. The harvest **follows the surviving record** and its answers land on B's fields. Merge is non-destructive and reversible (16.05.07 D-09), so an unmerge carries the harvest back with it. R-05 exists precisely because a destructive merge would silently destroy the domain's most expensive asset | Contributor sees the surviving record's name in the prompt — which may not be the name they booked. Prompt states both: "The Bunker (you booked it as Bunker Studios)" |
| **Record is claimed (16.05.02) between the booking and the prompt** | The prompt is unaffected — selection ran against the fields, not the ownership. What changes is downstream: the answers now queue against a record with a reviewer, so a correction becomes a disputable suggestion rather than an unreviewed one (16.05.03 Q-01) | Contributor: no difference. New Operator: an actionable item about a night that predates their claim, which is exactly the free QA the claim was worth taking |
| **No connectivity** (band in a van, rural, no signal) | The prompt is answerable offline and submits when connectivity returns. Real and common — the population is literally in a van | Answers accepted locally; "we'll send this when you're back online" |
| **Submission lands after the 96h expiry** | **Accepted.** The answer's validity is a property of when the room was observed, not when the bytes arrived (D-10). Expiry stops the platform asking; it does not stop the contributor answering | Normal thanks screen |
| **Contributor answers 2 of 4 questions and closes the app** | Each answer is an independent provenance event, so a partial prompt is **not a failed prompt**: the 2 answers are kept and applied, the remaining 2 are dropped, and there is **no re-prompt for the remainder** (D-17). Re-prompting for a remainder is nagging with extra steps | "Thanks — here's what changed" for what they answered. No mention of what they skipped |
| **Nothing on the record is uncertain** | **No prompt is generated.** The most common outcome on any well-maintained or high-frequency room, and the normal steady state | Nothing — for the contributor, the Operator, and the record |
| **Two members of the same band both answer, and contradict each other** (one says 3 steps, one says step-free) | Usually **not a conflict** — it is the same conditionality failure 16.05.04 DT-05 identifies: they used different entrances, or the ramp was out for one and not the other. Both are first-hand and both count as **one** party for corroboration (D-12: distinct *parties*, not distinct people). The disagreement routes to the **spec-gap queue** as a conditionality signal, not to 16.05.04 as a contest | Both see their own answer recorded. Neither is told the other contradicted them — that is a band's business, not the platform's |
| **Contributor retracts an answer** (they realise they used the wrong entrance) | A wrong first-hand report is expensive: it puts a contested marker on a true claim and damages an honest Operator (16.05.04 D-05). Retraction is available on the contributor's own answers, is **recorded, never erased** (16.05.03 D-04: per-field history is immutable), and **removes their corroboration weight** from any open contest — which can drop a contest back below threshold | "Retracted — the field no longer counts your report." Operator's item updates; the retraction itself is visible in field history |
| **The Operator updates the claim between the gig and the answer** (new PA installed the next morning) | The answer is about the room **as observed at the slot** (D-10), so it does not contradict a claim that postdates it. The harvest event is written against the field's history at its observation time and is **superseded**, not contested, by the newer owner-class value (16.05.03 "Superseded") | Contributor: normal thanks. No contest opens |
| **Harvest contradicts a statutory field** (curfew, occupancy ceiling) | A band's first-hand report cannot flip it — statutory fields are not contestable by anyone (16.05.04 D-04; 16.01.06 D-02). But a band reporting a curfew that differs from a statutory condition is reporting a **breach**, which is a different and more serious thing than a data error, and it routes to 24, not to 16.05.04. Statutory fields are excluded from the question pool for this reason — the platform does not ask a band to check a statutory condition. **The exclusion keys on the field's *resolved class*, not on its name**: where the record's regime profile declares no such instrument, the same curfew is an Operator claim (16.01.06 D-05; 16.02.03 D-01), and it is asked about and contested like any other claim | Statutory: the field is never asked about; a free-text breach report routes to 24. Operator-claimed: an ordinary harvest question |
| **Harvest contradicts a field that 16.07 matched on at booking time** | Not only a data correction: the band hired a room on a conformance check that has now been falsified — the room did not match on X. The harvest **routes the signal** to the booking party and to 17/18 as a possible dispute, and **never adjudicates it**. This is the moment a spec misrepresentation becomes evidence in a commercial dispute, which is a large part of what the structuring bet is for | Contributor: "You booked this room partly on that claim — do you want to raise it with the venue?" — a route out, not a verdict |
| **Unstaffed room, no Operator present** (24h fob rehearsal room) | The harvest works normally — the contributor's observation does not require a counterparty. What is missing is a **disputer**: on an unclaimed record there is nobody to review the correction, which is 16.05.03 Q-01's majority case, not this feature's to resolve | Contributor: normal. Nobody else: nothing, until someone claims the record |
| **Reservation is retro-cancelled or refunded after the harvest is answered** | The credential is withdrawn after the fact. Answers are **voided** — their provenance events are marked withdrawn (not deleted; history is immutable) and their corroboration weight is removed. A cancelled booking is not proof of presence, and a fact that rests on a withdrawn credential cannot keep the class the credential bought | Field history shows the withdrawal. The contributor is not notified — telling someone their honest report was voided by an unrelated refund is a poor trade |
| **Lockout tenancy** | No completion event, so historically no harvest — on the supply used most and tested hardest (domain Q-10). Fixed: the tenancy's **recurring billing cycle is the completion-equivalent** (D-19). A monthly renewal is proof of continued presence, and a monthly cadence sits inside both caps | The tenant gets at most one prompt per month, and only if something on the room is uncertain — which, for a room they use weekly, it rarely will be |
| **Contributor deletes their account after answering** (GDPR erasure) | The fact is about a **room**, not about the person. The provenance event survives with the identity **severed** — class, observation timestamp and value are retained; the contributor link is destroyed. The cost is real and is accepted: a severed event can no longer be checked for corroboration independence (D-12), so it counts toward no future threshold | The field keeps its value and its age. Nobody sees a gap |
| **Contributor is already a party to the open contest being asked about** | They are not independent, so their second report corroborates nothing (D-12). The field **fails selection** for this contributor and drops out of the question pool | The question is simply not asked |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Suppressed (Empty)** | Selection returns zero fields (nothing uncertain, or all candidates failed the observability filter), **or** a prompt cap is spent | **Nothing — no prompt is ever created.** The default and most common state, and the correct one. The record is fresh or the contributor's budget is spent; either way there is no question to ask |
| **Queued (Pending)** | Reservation completion inferred (16.06.03), selection returned 1–5 fields, caps clear | Nothing yet — delivery is scheduled per D-15 (10:00 next morning for 21:00–06:00 completions; completion + 2h otherwise) |
| **Delivered (Prompted)** | Delivery time reached | One prompt: "Quick check on {room} — {N} questions". First affordance "We didn't use the room". 96h to expiry, zero reminders |
| **Loading** | Contributor opens the prompt | Skeleton of {N} question rows while each field's **current claim** is fetched — the questions cannot be shown until the claims are, because the claim *is* the question (D-07). If the claims have not arrived in 3s: "Getting the current listing…" |
| **Answering (Partial)** | Contributor answered some questions and left | Answers are **applied as they are given** — each is an independent provenance event, so a half-answered prompt is a half-successful harvest, not a failure. Reopening within the window resumes at the next unanswered question; the remainder is dropped at expiry with **no re-prompt** (D-17) |
| **Not attended** | Contributor taps "We didn't use the room" | "Thanks — we won't ask about this one." **Zero provenance written.** Signal routes back to 16.06.03: the completion inference was wrong (D-16) |
| **Answered (Populated)** | Submitted | **What changed, and who it helps**: "The next band now knows there are 3 steps." Per-answer outcome — corrected / confirmed / didn't check. No score, no badge, no rank (DT-13) |
| **Error** | Submission fails (no signal; the van) | Answers held locally; "we'll send this when you're back online". Accepted on arrival **even after expiry** (D-10). A failed submission is never a lost answer — the population is in a van, and this is the normal case, not the exception |
| **Harvested** | Answers applied | First-hand provenance events written at **observation time** (D-10); decay clocks reset (confirmations on the shorter clock, D-08); contests opened or corroborated (16.05.04); "didn't check" recorded as an observability signal; free text routed to the spec-gap queue |
| **Expired (Ignored)** | 96h passed with no response | **Nothing. The prompt disappears.** Never nagged — a nagged musician is a lost one, and the domain needs them next time (D-05). No "you missed this", no digest, no second chance |
| **Voided** | The reservation is retro-cancelled/refunded, or the record is deleted (16.01.05) | Prompt withdrawn if undelivered. If already answered, the provenance events are marked **withdrawn** (never deleted — history is immutable) and corroboration weight is removed. Contributor is not notified |

| DT-01 | This is a review/feedback feature | It is a post-transaction prompt, which is what review systems are | ❌ REJECTED, and the distinction is sharp. A review is an **opinion** about an experience ("great room, lovely staff"). This harvests **facts** about a physical object ("3 steps, not step-free"). Facts are verifiable, corroborable, and correctable; opinions are none of those. Reviews are a platform cross-cut serving 05/13/14/16. This is not that, and merging them would turn falsifiable facts into vibes. |
| DT-02 | This is a nice-to-have data-quality enhancement | It is one of six curation features | ❌ REJECTED — **it is the domain's engine, and everything else in 16.05 is scaffolding around it.** Decay labels staleness and fixes nothing (16.05.05 DT-05). Provenance ranks sources but needs a strong source to exist. Conflict resolution needs an independent witness. Seeding produces a registry that immediately begins to rot. Harvest is the only mechanism that generates fresh first-hand disinterested facts. Cut it and the domain is a stale directory with good intentions. |

The platform's argument is that facts must be captured while they are true, because reconstruction from memory is contested and lossy. A venue's load-in is exactly such a fact: known perfectly by the band on the night, and never by anyone afterwards. **The booking is what puts the platform in the room** — the same causal chain the thesis describes for splits, applied to spaces. This is why booking must live in this domain: without the reservation there is no trigger, no proof of presence, and no harvest. |
| DT-04 | Ask everything while we have their attention | It is the one moment they are engaged | ❌ REJECTED: a band at 1am has approximately three taps of patience. | DT-05 | Bands will answer | The whole domain's data quality assumes it | ⚠️ **UNVALIDATED — the domain's largest single risk, and it must be stated plainly.** Every freshness guarantee in 16 rests on an untested behavioural assumption. Arguments against: they are tired, they are unpaid, and the beneficiary is the next band. **If the response rate is near zero, decay (16.05.05) does not degrade gracefully — it just accurately documents a rotting registry.** This assumption should be tested before the domain's MoSCoW is finalised. |
| DT-06 | A completed reservation is sufficient to make an answer first-hand | It is the domain's stated credential (D-02) | ❌ **REJECTED — and this is the most important correction in this file.** The reservation proves **presence**; first-hand class requires **observation**, and they are not the same thing. A guitarist arrives after load-in and never sees it. A band books the main room and never enters the green room. A daytime session never meets the curfew. Forcing an answer out of a contributor who did not observe the thing manufactures a fabricated fact **wearing the registry's strongest provenance class** — strictly worse than seeded data, which at least advertises its weakness. Therefore: **every question offers "Didn't check" at exactly the same cost as yes or no** (D-06), and observability filters the question pool before the prompt is ever built. Presence is necessary; it is not sufficient. |
| DT-07 | A closed question set can capture what a band learned | The prompt must be 3 taps, so it must be closed | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — and the residue is the domain's best discovery channel.** The most valuable thing a band learns is usually the fact the **schema has no slot for**: "the load-in goes through the bar and you can't do it after 6pm because of dinner service." A closed set is structurally incapable of holding it — not because the questions are wrong but because the *field* does not exist. Domain Q-03 records four features independently hitting the same wall (conditional curfew, production-dependent capacity, billing-dependent hospitality, conditional accessibility), and **every one of them is a fact a band knows and the spec cannot express.** Resolution: one **optional** free-text slot, last, resetting no clock (no field ⇒ no provenance), routed to a **spec-gap queue** rather than to the record (D-18). It is not a harvest answer; it is how the platform finds out which questions it should have been asking. |
| DT-08 | The Operator receives the harvest as free QA on their own listing | That is the honest framing, and it is what it is | ⚠️ **PARTIALLY REJECTED — the framing is true and the delivery will be experienced as accusation.** The CX file already flags that "a stream of *more people say you're wrong*" is a hostile UX with a real defection risk (CX-04). Worse, the **naïve** implementation is actively dangerous: an Operator who can see *which band* corrected their spec has everything they need to not book them again, and the scene is small enough that the risk generalises past one venue. **An attributable harvest is an unanswered harvest** — the domain would lose its engine to a chilling effect of its own construction. Resolution (D-13): reports are **unattributed**, no finer than the **month**, and surfaced as **one actionable item per field**, never a stream. Identity is retained for corroboration independence and 24 escalation and disclosed to nobody else. Residual, stated: a room with one gig a month is not de-identified by coarsening a timestamp (Q-07). |
| DT-09 | An Operator cannot game the harvest, because it requires a booking they cannot make | The credential is the reservation, and it is theirs to sell, not to hold | ❌ **REJECTED — it is trivially gameable, and this is a laundering vector, not a nuisance.** An Operator can book their own room. The reservation completes. The harvest fires. Their self-claim — which is owner-class, weaker than first-hand precisely because they have an incentive to flatter (16.05.05 DT-03) — is now reborn as a **first-hand** report about their own room, for the price of a booking they made to themselves. Their false "step-free" claim outranks the band that contradicted it, using the mechanism built to catch exactly that. Blocked at the source (D-11): **class is earned by the reservation *and* by the absence of a control or claim relationship to the record.** A party who controls the record is never a first-hand witness to it, however many bookings they make. Every one has a legitimate claim on the same tired band at the same 1am, and each will independently reason its way to "just one small prompt". **The post-gig attention budget is a platform-scarce resource with no owner** — the classic tragedy-of-the-commons shape, and the commons here is the only thing keeping domain 16's registry alive. Two prompts is one too many; six is a deleted app. Escalated as a **platform cross-cut** (Q-05): the budget needs an owner above domain 16, and 16 cannot allocate what it does not own. |
| DT-11 | The prompt should go to whoever made the booking | They are the party on the reservation, and the reservation is the credential | ❌ REJECTED: **the reservation names a party; the eyewitness is a person**, and in live music those routinely differ. `personas.md` models the Band as an **entity** (01), and gigs are booked by managers, agents, promoters and the drummer who does admin — none of whom necessarily carried anything through the load-in, and some of whom were never in the building. Sending the prompt to the booking contact sends it, systematically, to the person least likely to know. Resolution (D-21): route to the reservation's **named on-site party**; absent one, to the booking contact with **exactly one** reassignment hop. Unlimited forwarding was rejected — a prompt that can be passed around is a prompt nobody owns, and diffusion of responsibility is the well-documented death of exactly this kind of ask. |
| DT-12 | The provenance event should be timestamped when the answer is submitted | That is when the platform learned it, and it is the only time the platform can observe | ❌ **REJECTED — the timestamp must be the observation time (the reservation's slot), not the write time** (D-10). The reasoning is the thesis: the fact was true **in the room**, and the submission is merely transport. Three consequences follow, and all three are load-bearing. (1) The band answering from a van three days later is not reporting a stale fact; they are reporting a fact from the night, late. (2) An answer arriving after the 96h expiry is still **valid** — expiry stops the platform asking, not the contributor answering. (3) It resolves **CX-03's open race**: a harvested correction and an Operator's re-verification of the same field are ordered by **when each party observed the room**, not by which write landed second. The CX file correctly diagnosed that "whichever lands second wins on timestamp" is the wrong tiebreak; observation time is why. (What each event is *worth* remains 16.05.03's ranking and 16.05.05 Q-01 — this decides *when*, not *how strong*.) |
| DT-13 | Gamify the harvest — points, badges, contributor leaderboards, a "top corrector" rank | It is the standard fix for a volunteer contribution mechanism, and Q-01 says the response rate is the risk | ❌ REJECTED, on three independent grounds. **(1) It rewards volume over accuracy** — the metric a leaderboard optimises is *number of corrections*, and the cheapest way to climb is to find things wrong, which is precisely the bias that damages honest Operators (16.05.04 D-05). **(2) The reward is a reputation nobody wants** — a public rank as the scene's most prolific venue-corrector is a liability in a small industry, not a prize; it inverts the anonymity DT-08 establishes as a precondition. **(3) It answers the wrong question** — Q-01 asks whether bands will answer a 3-tap ask about a room they just played. If the answer is no, a badge does not fix it; if the answer is yes, a badge is not needed. The only compensation offered is **reciprocity made visible**: "the next band now knows there are 3 steps." If validation (Q-01) shows the rate is unworkable, incentive design reopens as a **product decision for the owner** — not as an agent's default reach for a leaderboard. |
| DT-14 | The harvest can verify every field on the record, given enough gigs | It is the domain's verification engine, so it verifies | ❌ REJECTED, and the boundary is worth stating because the domain leans on this feature so heavily. **The harvest can only ever verify what a visitor observes.** Fields that no booked visitor encounters — internal capacity calculations, insurance, the parts of the deal model that are not on the invoice, anything about a room the contributor did not hire — accumulate "didn't check" and drop out of the pool. And those are, by construction, **the fields most in need of independent verification and the least likely ever to get it**: they stay owner-attested forever, with only an ageing clock to show for it. This is not a defect in the mechanism; it is its ceiling, and it means 16.05.05's decay labels are permanently the *only* thing standing behind an entire field class. 16.05.05 Q-04 asks the adjacent question (is there any freshness path that does not require a transaction?) and this is the strongest argument that it needs one. |
| DT-15 | Gig harvest and session harvest are the same feature with the same shape | Both are "ask the customer after they used the room" | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED — one mechanism, **two profiles**, and the differences are not cosmetic. **Timing**: a gig ends at 01:00 and a session ends at 18:00, which is the entire reason D-15 needs a branch rather than a constant. **Question pool**: a band tested the load-in, the PA and the backline in 90 minutes; a Producer used the mic locker item by item for 8 hours and can speak to isolation, sightlines and headphone lines that no band ever sees. **Incentive**: the band may never return; the Producer books that room again next month and is the trust broker (`personas.md`) whose attestation already outranks a self-claim. **Surface**: and this is the one that matters — the Producer is *already in the platform at session close*, capturing credits and splits (D-18, 02/07). The studio harvest should **ride that flow**, not arrive as a separate notification, because the thesis says the capture that works is the one that is a byproduct of work already happening. The venue harvest has no such flow to ride, which is exactly why it needs a prompt and why the prompt is fragile. |

| D-25 | **A harvest answer that falsifies a field 16.07 matched on at booking time routes to the booking party and to 17/18 as a possible dispute** — the harvest never adjudicates it | The room did not match on X. | D-27 | **On erasure, the provenance event survives with the identity severed** — value, class and observation time retained; contributor link destroyed; corroboration independence forfeit | The fact is about a **room**, not about the person.
