# Feature: Rehearsal & Practice Space Specification

> **Number**: 16.04
> **Parent**: [Venues, Studios & Spaces](./venues-studios-spaces-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The spec for a rehearsal room: what backline is in it and whether that is included or hired, the
access model (staffed hours vs 24-hour fob), room size and what it comfortably holds, and whether it
is sold by the hour or as a **lockout** — a monthly tenancy on a room that becomes the band's own.

A rehearsal room is the least glamorous and most frequently used space in music. It is also
deliberately a **feature, not a sub-domain**: unlike a venue or a studio, it has no interacting
capability clusters. There is one spec sheet, it is short, and pretending otherwise would inflate the
structure to match its neighbours rather than its reality.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | Declares backline, inclusion model, access model, room size, and whether the room is hourly or lockout. Rehearsal Operators are the most price-and-availability-driven supply in the domain — the spec is short because the decision is short. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | **The primary consumer.** Picks a room on: is there a kit, is there a bass rig, can we get in at 10pm, and what does it cost. Post-session, the corrector — the backline claim is tested every single time someone uses the room. |
| Producer | Read-only | Peripheral. Occasionally books a rehearsal room for pre-production, in which case they care about backline quality and whether they can leave gear set up overnight. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Structured fields: backline present (kit, bass rig, guitar amps, PA/vocal mics, keys) with an
inclusion model per item (included / hire extra / bring your own), access model (staffed hours with
a schedule / 24h fob / by arrangement), room size and comfortable band size, storage (can gear be
left), and sale model (hourly / lockout / both).

**Lockout is the fact that makes this feature more than a stub.** A lockout is not a booking — it is
a monthly tenancy on a dedicated room that the band treats as their own, leaves gear in, and pays for
whether or not they use it. Modelling it as 30 recurring bookings would be wrong in every respect:
wrong billing, wrong calendar, wrong availability semantics. It is handled in 16.06.09.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — backline condition. A "drum kit" that is a shell
with two heads and no snare is technically a drum kit. Every rehearsal room in the world has this
problem and no vocabulary exists for it.

### Happy Path

1. Band needs a room tonight at 8pm with a kit and a bass rig
2. Filters rehearsal rooms: backline included, available tonight, within 5 miles
3. Results show rooms with backline listed and per-item inclusion visible
4. Band books hourly (16.06.03); the backline claim is on the reservation
5. Post-session, the band is asked whether the kit was as described (16.05.06) — the fastest-decaying claim in the domain, tested nightly

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Backline is listed but broken (the universal rehearsal-room condition) | Per-item status, same pattern as 16.03.02 D-02. Post-session harvest is the only real corrective | "Kit — reported issue: no snare" |
| Room is lockout-only | Not hourly-bookable at all; a different product with different economics (16.06.09) | "Lockout only — £X/month" |
| Access is 24h fob but the fob takes 3 days to issue | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — access lead time is a real booking constraint and is unmodelled |  — |
| Room is included in a venue or studio (a rehearsal room in the basement) | Place type is a set (16.01.01 D-01) — one record, `type=[venue, rehearsal]`, separate rooms | Handled by the type set |
| PA present but with two working mics out of four | Same condition problem as the kit; same absent vocabulary | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Band wants to leave gear between sessions without a lockout | Storage field — a distinct, commonly-sold thing (a locker, a cage) | Storage: lockers available |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Rehearsal spaces deserve a sub-domain like venues and studios | Symmetry — the other two place types got sub-domains, and the sweep listed rehearsal alongside them | ❌ REJECTED. Applying the classification gate honestly: a rehearsal room has **one** spec sheet with no interacting capability clusters. A venue's curfew constrains its running order which constrains its deal; a studio's staffing model changes what a booking is. Nothing in a rehearsal room's spec constrains anything else in it. Symmetry with its neighbours is an aesthetic argument, and inflating the structure to match would be exactly the domain-count inflation D-17 warns about. Feature. |
| DT-02 | Lockout is a booking with a long duration | It is a room reserved for a period of time — that is a booking | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the feature's most important finding. A lockout is a **tenancy**: billed monthly regardless of use, no availability semantics (the room is never released), gear left in situ, and often a rolling agreement with notice rather than a fixed end. Modelling it as 30 bookings gets the billing, the calendar and the cancellation model all wrong. It is a different product sold through the same door, and it needs its own treatment (16.06.09). |
| DT-03 | Backline condition can be captured in the spec | If we can structure a mic locker we can structure a drum kit | ⚠️ UNRESOLVED and honest: "drum kit" is a claim that spans a maintained Gretsch and a shell pack with no snare, and the industry has no vocabulary for the difference. This is the fastest-decaying claim in the entire domain — it is tested every single night by whoever books the room. Post-session harvest (16.05.06) is the only mechanism that could plausibly track it, which makes rehearsal rooms the **best test case** for whether the harvest loop works at all: highest frequency, lowest stakes, fastest feedback. |
| DT-04 | Rehearsal rooms are a low-value corner of the domain | Cheap, unglamorous, small transactions | ❌ REJECTED on the consolidation half of the thesis (D-18). They are the **highest-frequency** place transaction in music — a working band is in one weekly, versus a venue a few times a year and a studio once. Frequency is what makes a daily reason to show up, and D-18 says consolidation is what wins the user. The lowest-value transaction may be the highest-value habit. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Classified as a **feature**, not a sub-domain | One spec sheet, no interacting capability clusters. Symmetry with venues/studios is an aesthetic argument (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Lockout is a **tenancy**, not a long booking — handled in 16.06.09 | Monthly billing, no availability semantics, gear in situ, rolling notice. 30 bookings gets all three wrong (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Per-item backline inclusion model (included / hire extra / BYO) | "Backline available" is three different commercial facts |
| D-04 | Rehearsal is the domain's **frequency** play, not its value play | Weekly use beats annual use for building the daily habit D-18 requires (DT-04) |
