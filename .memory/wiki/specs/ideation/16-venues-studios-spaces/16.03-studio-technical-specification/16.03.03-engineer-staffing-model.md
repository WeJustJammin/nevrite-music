# Feature: Engineer Staffing Model

> **Number**: 16.03.03
> **Parent**: [Studio Technical Specification](./16.03-studio-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

How a studio sells its people alongside its room: **dry hire** (you get the keys and the room, bring
your own engineer), **assisted** (a house assistant sets up, patches and runs the machine), or
**full-service** (a house engineer records your session). Plus the rules around it — can you bring
your own engineer at all, is an assistant mandatory, is the house engineer named or whoever is on.

This is a small field with a large blast radius. It determines what a booking *is* (a room, or a
room and a person), it determines whether the booking is compound (16.06.05), and it sits directly
on the seam with 05 Services Marketplace — because a house engineer and a freelance engineer are
the same job sold through two different doors.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Declares the staffing models offered, whether an assistant is mandatory, and whether outside engineers are permitted. Many studios mandate an assistant — protecting the gear from strangers — and that is a booking rule, not a preference. |
| Producer | Read-only | **The persona this field is about.** A Producer booking a dry hire *is* the engineer; a Producer booking full-service is buying someone else's ears. Also: a Producer may be on the other side, working as a studio's house engineer — the multi-hyphenate fact, live in one field. |
| Musician | Read-only | A band without a Producer needs full-service and needs to know it exists. "Dry hire only" is a hard filter for them, and discovering it after booking is a wasted day. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Per-room (not per-place — a mix suite may be dry-hire-only while the live room is never let dry):
staffing models offered, whether an assistant is mandatory, whether outside engineers are permitted,
whether the house engineer is named, and what each model costs (feeding 16.06.07).

The **named** engineer question is the interesting one. If the house engineer is named, they are a
person with an identity (01), a credit history (02), and probably a service listing (05) — and the
studio is partly selling *them*. If they are unnamed, the studio is selling a role. Both are real;
they behave differently and they are the reason this field cannot be a simple enum.

credits reconcile with their independent presence in 05 is unresolved and is a genuine
architectural fork.

### Happy Path

1. Musician's band wants to record but has no engineer; filters for full-service studios
2. Results show studios offering full-service, some with named engineers and their credits (02)
3. Musician opens a room: full-service available, named engineer, £X/day inclusive
4. Booking creates a compound reservation (16.06.05) — the room and the engineer, together
5. If the engineer is unavailable, the booking is unavailable — the compound is atomic

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Assistant is mandatory even on a dry hire | A booking rule, not an upsell — it must be surfaced pre-booking and priced into the quote | "Assistant required — included in rate" |
| Producer wants to bring their own engineer; the studio forbids it | Structured field; a hard filter. Discovering this after booking is a wasted day and a refund argument | "Outside engineers not permitted" |
| Named house engineer is also a freelance service provider on 05 | The multi-hyphenate fact. `[PENDING — Step 5]` — one identity, two doors, and possibly two prices | — |
| House engineer is booked; the room is free | The compound fails even though the room is available (16.06.05) — correct behaviour, surprising UX | "Room available, engineer unavailable on this date" |
| Studio is dry hire only and the client has no engineer | The client needs 05 Services. This is a **cross-sell the platform can uniquely make** — dry hire + a freelance engineer is a compound spanning two domains | `[PENDING — Step 5]` |
| Engineer leaves the studio | Their credits and reputation are theirs (01/02), not the studio's. The staffing field goes stale; the person's history does not | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unstated | Nothing declared | Ambiguous — the client cannot tell what they are buying; Operator nudged |
| Declared | Models declared | Available models + rules + rates |
| Named | House engineer is a platform identity | Model + engineer's profile and credits (01/02) |
| Unavailable | Model offered but staff booked | Room shows available; the staffing model does not, for that date |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Staffing is a rate-card line item | It has a price and it varies by model — it looks like an extra | ❌ REJECTED: it changes **what the booking is**. Dry hire reserves a room; full-service reserves a room *and a person*, which means two calendars, two availability constraints, and a compound that can fail on either side. That is a booking-model fact, not a price. It is also why 16.06.05 exists at all. |
| DT-02 | A house engineer and a freelance engineer are different things | One is studio staff, one is a service listing in 05 | ❌ REJECTED — this is the multi-hyphenate fact from `personas.md` appearing structurally, and it is a boundary problem, not a modelling convenience. The same human engineers at a studio on Tuesday and takes freelance work on Wednesday. If they are two entities, their credits fragment across two records — which is the exact failure the platform exists to prevent. **One identity (01), one credit history (02), two sales channels.** |
| DT-03 | Dry hire is the simplest case | No people, just a room | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: it is the simplest *reservation* and the most complex *sale*, because the client who wants dry hire and has no engineer needs 05. This is the platform's clearest structural cross-sell — no competitor can offer "this room + this freelance engineer, both booked, one transaction" because no competitor holds both sides. Consolidation as the thesis describes it (D-18), instantiated in one booking. |
| DT-04 | Mandatory assistants are an upsell | They add cost the client didn't ask for | ❌ REJECTED: they are usually risk management — a studio does not let a stranger loose on a Neve. Framing it as an upsell misreads the Operator and produces a hostile UI. It is a **booking rule** with a price attached, and it belongs in the pre-booking rules, not the extras list. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Staffing is a **booking-model** fact, not a rate-card line | It changes what is reserved: a room, or a room and a person with their own calendar (DT-01) |
| D-02 | A named house engineer is **one identity** (01) with one credit history (02), sold through two channels | Two entities would fragment their credits — the precise failure the platform exists to prevent (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Staffing is declared per-room, not per-place | A mix suite may be dry-hire-only while the live room never is |
| D-04 | Mandatory assistants are a booking rule, not an upsell | It is risk management; framing it as an upsell misreads the Operator (DT-04) |
