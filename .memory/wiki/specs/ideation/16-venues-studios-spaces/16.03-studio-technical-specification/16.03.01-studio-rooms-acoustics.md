# Feature: Studio Rooms & Acoustics

> **Number**: 16.03.01
> **Parent**: [Studio Technical Specification](./16.03-studio-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The acoustic and physical character of a studio's rooms — live room dimensions and volume, ceiling
height, treatment and variability (dead/live, adjustable), isolation booths, sightlines between
rooms, control room monitoring and its acoustic treatment, and the practical facts (how many
players fit, can the drummer see the singer).

For the Producer persona this is not metadata — **the room choice is the creative decision**. A
band tracking drums needs a live room with height; a singer-songwriter needs a dead booth; a mix
engineer needs a control room they trust. And unlike a venue's spec, almost none of this is
expressible as a number that means anything on its own.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | The studio owner declares each room's dimensions, treatment, isolation and sightlines. Their room is their product, so unlike venue Operators they are motivated to describe it — the constraint is that the vocabulary is subjective, not that the will is missing. |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | **The primary consumer of this entire sub-domain.** Chooses a room on acoustics, reads sightlines and isolation because they determine whether the session can be tracked live. Post-session, the highest-authority corrector — they have heard the room. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Reads it when booking their own session without a Producer. Cares about the practical facts (does the band fit, is there a piano) more than the acoustic vocabulary. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Studio-type records are not surfaced to Fans at all (16.01.01 Role Lens). |

## Behavior

Per-room structured fields: type (live room / control room / booth / mix suite), dimensions and
volume, ceiling height (the single most consequential number for drums), treatment character
(dead / live / variable, with what mechanism), isolation between rooms, sightlines (which rooms see
which — a machine-checkable fact that determines whether a band can track together), headphone/cue
provision (how many mixes), and monitoring for control rooms (mains, nearfields, treatment).

The honest problem: **acoustic quality is not expressible in the structured vocabulary this domain
uses everywhere else.** "Is it a good room?" is answered by a photo, a discography, a reputation
and a visit — not by a field. This feature can carry the *physical* facts (dimensions, height,
isolation, sightlines) rigorously and must not pretend to carry the acoustic judgement.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — treatment vocabulary, and whether any acoustic
measurement (RT60, a room impulse response) is realistically capturable or is a fantasy that no
studio will supply.

### Happy Path

1. Producer needs to track a 5-piece live; filters studio rooms by live-room volume and sightlines
2. Results show rooms where the live room sees the control room and the drum booth sees the live room
3. Producer opens a room: 6m ceiling, variable treatment, 3 booths, full sightlines, photos (16.01.03)
4. Producer books the compound (live room + control room + assistant engineer) via 16.06.05
5. Post-session, Producer confirms or corrects the sightline and isolation claims (16.05.06)

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| "Is it a good-sounding room?" | Unanswerable in structured fields. Carried by photos, the studio's discography and reviews — none of which this feature owns | Physical facts + evidence elsewhere |
| Live room and control room are one room (a project studio) | Extremely common. The room model must not assume separation, and "no isolation" is a first-class fact | One room, isolation: none |
| Treatment is variable (movable gobos, hinged panels) | Structured as variable + mechanism; the range matters more than any single state | "Variable — gobos and hinged panels" |
| Rooms are separable, so the mix suite is bookable while tracking runs | Room exclusion (16.01.02 D-02) — this is the case exclusion rules exist for; the control room is usually exclusive to its live room | Calendar blocks the sibling |
| Home/project studio at a residential address | Address precision reduced (16.01.01) — and this is most of the market's supply, not an edge case | Area-level until booked |
| Studio is refitted / re-treated | Decay (16.05.05) plus post-session correction; a 3-year-old acoustic claim is a claim about a room that may not exist | Field age visible |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unstated | Nothing declared | Room invisible to acoustic/physical filters |
| Declared | Operator filled the room spec | Physical facts + last-verified age |
| Evidenced | Spec + tagged photos | Facts with photo evidence inline |
| Contested | Post-session contradiction | Last-agreed value + contested marker |
| Stale | Decayed | Facts with visible ages |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Acoustics can be structured like a venue's PA spec | The whole domain's thesis is that specs should be structured | ❌ REJECTED, and this is the sub-domain's honest limit. "Great-sounding live room" is a judgement, not a field, and the industry's own vocabulary ("warm", "punchy", "dead") is irreducibly subjective. What **is** structurable is the physical substrate: dimensions, ceiling height, isolation, sightlines, treatment mechanism. This feature must carry those rigorously and refuse to fake the rest. Pretending to structure a judgement produces a field every studio fills with "excellent". |
| DT-02 | Sightlines are a nice-to-have detail | They sound like a minor ergonomic note | ❌ REJECTED: sightlines decide whether a band can **track live**, which decides what kind of record can be made in the room. It is one of the very few genuinely structurable, genuinely decisive, and genuinely unpublished facts about a studio — no studio directory carries it, and every Producer asks. High-value, machine-checkable, unbuilt. |
| DT-03 | Ceiling height is one dimension among three | It is part of the room's dimensions | ❌ REJECTED: for drums it is the dominant variable, and it is the fact most often omitted from studio listings (which lead with floor area, because it flatters). Carrying it as a first-class field — and making its absence visible — is a small change with disproportionate value to the Producer. |
| DT-04 | The Operator is motivated to fill this spec, unlike a venue Operator | 16.02.02 DT-02 concluded venue Operators resist the form | ✅ CONFIRMED — a real asymmetry worth designing around. A studio's room **is** its product; describing it is marketing they already do. Venue Operators fill a spec so bands stop emailing; studio Operators fill it to sell. So the studio spec can be richer and longer than the venue spec without hitting the same wall. Note the inversion: the field most likely to be inflated here is the acoustic judgement (DT-01) — which is exactly the field this feature refuses to structure. |
| DT-05 | A control room is a room like any other | It is in the room list | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: it carries a different spec (monitoring, not acoustics-for-tracking), it is usually **exclusive** to its live room rather than independently bookable, and it is the one room that can be sold alone (a mix session). It is a room, but its exclusion rules and its rate behaviour are distinct — which is exactly what 16.01.02 D-02 exists to express. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Structure the physical substrate; refuse to structure the acoustic judgement | "Warm" is not a field. Faking it produces a column every studio fills with "excellent" (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Sightlines are a first-class, machine-checkable field | They decide whether a band can track live — decisive, structurable, and published nowhere (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Ceiling height is a first-class field, and its absence is visible | Dominant for drums; routinely omitted because floor area flatters (DT-03) |
| D-04 | The studio spec can be richer than the venue spec | The studio's room is its product — the Operator is selling, not submitting (DT-04) |
