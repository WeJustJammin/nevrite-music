# Feature: Session Archive & Recall Policy

> **Number**: 16.03.04
> **Parent**: [Studio Technical Specification](./16.03-studio-technical-specification-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16
> **Split from**: sweep candidate 17 ("Rate Cards, Inclusions, Extras & Session Archive Policy") — archive policy is a studio property, not a booking mechanic

## Overview

What the studio does with your session data after you leave: how long the files are kept, on what,
whether a recall costs money, whether they are backed up, and what happens if you never come back.
Every studio has an answer to this and almost none of them publish it — which is why a band asking
for their stems from a session five years ago is a phone call with an uncertain outcome.

This is a small, unglamorous field that sits directly on the platform's thesis. **The session
archive is where provenance physically lives** — the stems, the session file, the take that proves
who played what. A studio's retention policy determines whether that evidence still exists.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Config | Declares retention period, medium, backup posture, recall fee, and what happens at end-of-retention (deleted / archived / returned). Publishing it turns a vague liability into a stated term. |
| Producer | Read-only | **The persona who feels this.** They are the one asked for a recall three years later, by a client who assumes the files exist. They read this before booking because they know the question is coming. |
| Musician | Read-only | Reads it if they think about it at all — which is the problem. The band that discovers their stems were deleted is the band that never asked. Making it visible pre-booking is the whole intervention. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. |

## Behavior

Structured fields: retention period, storage medium (drive / LTO / cloud / "we haven't thought
about it"), backup posture (single copy / redundant / off-site), recall fee and turnaround,
end-of-retention behaviour (deleted / archived indefinitely / returned to client / client must
collect), and who owns the archive copy (which is a rights question wearing a storage costume).

The last field is the dangerous one. "The studio keeps a copy" and "the studio owns a copy" are
different sentences with different legal weight, and the industry routinely conflates them. This
feature can state the studio's *policy*; it must not pretend to state the *rights*, which belong to
09 Rights & Ownership.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is choosing between two studios for a client project with a long tail
2. Studio A: 12 months on a drive, no backup, £150 recall. Studio B: indefinite, LTO, off-site, free recall
3. Producer factors it into the choice — a decision they have never before been able to make on data
4. Books Studio B; the archive policy is captured on the reservation as a term
5. Three years later the recall request references a stated policy, not a memory

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Studio has no policy (the common case) | "Not stated" — and the absence is itself informative | "Retention not stated — ask before booking" |
| Studio deletes files inside its stated retention | A dispute (24) against a stated term, which is a strictly better position than a dispute against a memory | Policy cited on the reservation |
| Client wants a recall after the studio has closed (16.01.05) | The nightmare case. The record is closed; the drives are in someone's loft. Nothing the platform can do — but the policy at least records what was promised | — |
| Session files are also on-platform via 07 | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — if 07 holds the stems, the studio's archive is a backup, not the source. This may deprecate the feature or make it the bridge for studios that will never upload | — |
| Studio's archive contains an uncleared sample or another client's material | A rights and liability question, not a storage one. Belongs to 09/24 | — |
| "The studio owns the archive copy" | Stated as a policy claim, never as a rights determination. 09 owns rights | Policy shown as a studio term |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not stated | No policy declared | "Not stated" + a nudge to ask — the honest default |
| Stated | Operator declared the policy | Structured policy + last-verified age |
| Captured | Policy attached to a reservation at booking time | The policy as it was on the day, frozen on the booking |
| Stale | Decayed | Policy with a visible age — a 5-year-old retention promise is not a current one |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Archive policy is a rate-card extra | The sweep bundled it into candidate 17 with rate cards and extras | ❌ REJECTED — hence the split. A recall **fee** is an extra; the **policy** is a durable property of the studio, like its treatment or its mic locker. It decays, it is community-correctable, it is read before booking, and it outlives any individual booking. Bundling it with pricing hid a provenance-bearing fact inside an invoice line. |
| DT-02 | This is a minor operational detail | It is storage admin; nobody chooses a studio on backup posture | ❌ REJECTED on the thesis (D-18). The session archive is **where provenance physically lives** — the stems and session file that evidence who played what. The platform's whole argument is that facts must be captured at the moment of creation rather than reconstructed from memory; if the artefact that carries those facts is silently deleted after 12 months, the capture was theatre. A retention policy is provenance infrastructure. |
| DT-03 | If 07 Music Projects stores session files, this feature is redundant | On-platform storage makes the studio's drive irrelevant | ❌ REJECTED, though it is the strongest objection. Most studios will never upload a full session to a platform — the files are huge, the DAW is not a browser (`personas.md`: "their work happens inside a DAW"), and the habit is decades deep. This feature is the honest bridge to the supply that will not change its workflow: it records what the studio does, rather than pretending the platform holds the data. Where 07 *does* hold the stems, this becomes a backup declaration — still useful, less critical. |
| DT-04 | "The studio keeps a copy" and "the studio owns a copy" are the same statement | The industry uses them interchangeably | ❌ REJECTED and guarded: they are different sentences with different legal weight. This feature states a **policy**; 09 Rights & Ownership determines **rights**. Letting a storage field imply an ownership claim would be the domain quietly making rights determinations it has no authority to make — exactly the confusion the platform exists to end. |
