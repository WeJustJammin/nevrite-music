# Feature: Signal Chain, Mic Locker & Instrument Inventory

> **Number**: 16.03.02

> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Everything in the studio that a signal passes through or is made by: the desk, preamps,
converters, outboard (compressors, EQs, reverbs), the mic locker down to individual pairs, the DAW
and its version, monitoring, and the instruments — the piano and its last tuning, the drum kit, the
amps, the Rhodes that half the studio's bookings actually exist for.

The domain index calls this out explicitly: **"the mic locker IS the booking decision"**. A
Producer does not book a studio and then discover what is in it; they book a studio *because* of a
pair of C12s and a 1073. This is the single most persuasive listing content a studio has, and it is
currently a bulleted list on a website that was last updated when the Neve was still working.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Operator | Full | The studio owner lists their gear — the thing they most want to talk about and the thing most likely to be aspirational (the vintage compressor that has been in for repair for a year). Marks items as available / in repair / by request. |
| Producer | Read-only + suggest | **The primary consumer.** Reads it before booking to plan the session, and reads it as a proxy for whether the studio is serious. Post-session, the highest-authority corrector — they used the gear and know what actually worked. |
| Musician | Read-only + suggest | Reads it for instruments more than signal path — is the piano tuned, is there a decent kit, do I need to bring my amp. Cares about "what do I need to bring", which is the same question inverted. |
| Fan | None | Not visible. Studio records are not surfaced to Fans. |

## Behavior

Structured inventory grouped by function: console/desk (make, model, channel count, recall
capability), preamps, converters and clocking, outboard, mic locker (make/model/quantity/pairs —
pairing matters, a single C414 is a different fact from a matched pair), DAW and version, monitoring,
and instruments (piano + last tuning date, drum kits, amps, keys, percussion).

Per-item status is the field that makes it honest: **available / in repair / by request / hire
extra**. A gear list where everything is implicitly available is a list of aspirations. And gear
that is a paid extra rather than included is a rate-card fact (16.06.07) discovered on the invoice
today.

The open structural question is whether this inventory is *this domain's data at all* — see DT-03.

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the taxonomy depth (is "1073" enough, or does it
need revision, serial, mod state?) is unresolved and is exactly where this feature either duplicates
or defers to 15 Gear Registry.

### Happy Path

1. Producer is planning a session that needs a specific vocal chain
2. Filters studio rooms by mic (C12 or U47) and preamp (Neve/API)
3. Results show rooms with matching gear, with per-item availability status
4. Producer opens the room: matched pair of C414s (available), 1073 ×2 (available), LA-2A (in repair)
5. Producer books knowing what is actually working — the LA-2A does not appear in their session plan

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Gear listed but broken | Per-item status exists precisely for this — "in repair" is a first-class value | "LA-2A — in repair" |
| Gear list is aspirational (things sold years ago) | The most common failure. Decay (16.05.05) + post-session correction (16.05.06) are the only correctives | Item age + community flag |
| Gear is a paid extra, not included | Structured field; feeds the rate card (16.06.07). Today this is discovered on the invoice | "Hire extra — £40/day" |
| Single vs matched pair | Distinct facts. A matched pair is a different capability, not two of the same item | Quantity + pairing |
| Piano is in the room but has not been tuned since 2019 | Last-tuning date is a structured field — a piano is the one instrument whose condition is a date | "Last tuned: 2019" |
| Studio owns 400 items | `[PENDING — Step 5]` — full inventory entry is a data-entry wall. Tiered detail (headline gear vs full list) is likely necessary |  — |
| Producer brings their own gear | Not modelled here; it is a session fact. Possibly 07's, possibly 15's | — |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | No inventory | Room drops out of gear-filtered search — for a studio this is close to being invisible |
| Partial | Headline gear listed | Inventory + "full list on request" |
| Complete | Full inventory with statuses | Searchable, filterable inventory |
| Stale | Decayed | Items with visible ages — a 3-year-old gear list is a claim, not an inventory |
| Contested | Post-session contradiction | Contested item marker |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A gear list is marketing copy | Every studio website treats it that way — a bulleted list under a photo | ❌ REJECTED: it is the booking decision (domain index: "the mic locker IS the booking decision"). Structured, it becomes searchable ("who has a C12 within 50 miles?") — a query no tool answers today and one that would route real bookings to studios that currently rely on word of mouth. This is the strongest single argument for the domain's structuring bet. |
| DT-02 | Everything on the list is available | The implicit assumption of every gear list ever published | ❌ REJECTED: studios are full of gear that is in repair, on loan, or a hire extra. An implicitly-available list is aspirational, and the Producer discovers the truth mid-session with the take waiting. Per-item status is what separates an inventory from a brochure. |
| DT-03 | The mic locker is this domain's data | It is in the studio spec, it describes the room | ⚠️ **UNRESOLVED — the most consequential open question in this sub-domain.** A mic locker is *gear owned by an org*, which is precisely domain 15 (Gear Registry & Ownership). If 15 models gear with make/model/serial/condition/ownership, then this feature is a **view over 15 scoped to the studio's org**, not a parallel inventory. Building it twice gives two gear taxonomies that will diverge — and it forfeits the interesting consequence: a studio's gear having provenance, service history, and a resale path into 13. Escalated as a high-confidence cross-domain touchpoint. |
| DT-04 | Full inventory entry is achievable | Studios want to show off their gear (16.03.01 DT-04) | ⚠️ PARTIALLY REJECTED: the will exists; the volume defeats it. A well-equipped studio has hundreds of items. Tiered detail is likely necessary — headline gear structured and searchable, the long tail as a list. If 15 owns the taxonomy (DT-03), the entry cost drops sharply, because items are picked from a catalogue rather than typed. |
| DT-05 | Pairing is a quantity detail | Two C414s is quantity 2 | ❌ REJECTED: a matched pair is a distinct capability (stereo/overheads/Blumlein) and two unmatched singles are not a substitute. Quantity alone loses the fact a Producer is actually filtering on. Small modelling point, real consequence for search. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Candidate 03 kept whole — signal chain, mic locker and instruments are one decision | A Producer evaluates the chain and the room's instruments as a single question; splitting them serves no persona |
| D-02 | Per-item status (available / in repair / by request / hire extra) is mandatory | An implicitly-available list is a brochure; status makes it an inventory (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Pairing is a first-class fact, not a quantity | A matched pair is a distinct capability; quantity alone loses what Producers filter on (DT-05) |
| D-04 | Ownership of the gear taxonomy is **deferred to a 15 cross-check**, not decided here | Building a second gear taxonomy is a divergence risk and forfeits provenance and resale paths (DT-03) |
