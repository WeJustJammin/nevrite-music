## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Rooms & Acoustics ↔ Signal Chain & Inventory

**Relationship**: These are not two facts about a studio; they are two halves of one question. A
Producer choosing a room is choosing "this live room through this desk with these mics" as a single
creative decision. The domain index states it plainly — the mic locker IS the booking decision —
and the practical consequence is that neither feature is usable alone. A room spec without an
inventory does not tell a Producer whether the session is possible; an inventory without a room does
not tell them whether it will sound like anything.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: the whole point. Filters on both simultaneously; a match on one is not a match.
- **Musician**: cares about the instruments half (is there a piano, is it tuned) more than the chain.
- **Operator**: fills both; the inventory is what they want to talk about (16.03.02 DT-01), the acoustics is what they cannot honestly describe (16.03.01 DT-01).
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — separate fields, both Operator-owned. But the *search* must join them, and the join is what makes either useful.
2. **Trigger chain**: None. Independent lifecycles: gear moves constantly, the room's dimensions never change. That difference in decay rate is worth carrying — a stale inventory is likely, a stale ceiling height is impossible.
3. **Permission intersection**: Identical.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None — but the decay-rate asymmetry means a room can show fresh physical facts and a rotten inventory, and present as "verified" overall. Freshness must be per-field, not per-room (reinforces 16.05.05).

### CX-02: Engineer Staffing ↔ Signal Chain & Inventory

**Relationship**: The mandatory assistant is not an upsell — it exists because the inventory is
valuable and irreplaceable. The causal direction runs from gear to staffing rule: the studios that
mandate an assistant are the studios with a Neve, and the studios that offer dry hire are the ones
where the worst a stranger can do is unplug something. This means the staffing model is partly
*predictable* from the inventory, and an inconsistency between them (a vintage-heavy studio offering
unsupervised dry hire) is a mild signal that one of the two is wrong.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: reads the rule and understands it instantly — they would do the same.
- **Operator**: sets it as risk management, and needs it framed that way in the UI (16.03.03 DT-04).
- **Musician**: experiences it as a cost, and needs the reason visible or it reads as a gouge.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None.
2. **Trigger chain**: None modelled. Whether adding vintage gear should *prompt* a staffing rule review is a speculative nicety, not a requirement.
3. **Permission intersection**: Both Operator-config.
4. **Notification fan-out**: None.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-05: Rooms & Acoustics ↔ Engineer Staffing

**Relationship**: The most operationally consequential pair here. A full-service booking must satisfy
**two independent availability constraints** — the room (with its exclusion rules: booking the live
room blocks the control room) and the person (with their own calendar, which may also be a 05
services calendar). Either can fail. A room that is free with an engineer who is not is an
unavailable booking, and the UI must explain which half failed or the client concludes the calendar
is broken.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: hits this constantly — the room they want is free, the engineer they want is not.
- **Operator**: manages two resource types with different scarcity; the engineer is usually the binding constraint, not the room.
- **Musician**: needs full-service, so is entirely at the mercy of the engineer's calendar.
- **Fan**: none.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The room and the person are separate resources with separate calendars; the compound (16.06.05) owns the atomicity.
2. **Trigger chain**: Compound booking → hold room → hold engineer → confirm both, or release both. A partial hold that persists is the Operator's stated anti-persona failure ("forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold") arriving by machine rather than by forgetfulness.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes and unresolved — if the engineer is a named 05 provider, whose permission grants the booking, the studio's or theirs? 16.03.03 Q-03.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A compound booking notifies the Operator and the engineer, who may not be the same party.
5. **State transition conflict**: Real and concrete — two calendars racing. Both holds must be atomic or the compound is a double-booking generator, which is the Operator's switching trigger.

### CX-04: Archive Policy ↔ Engineer Staffing

**Relationship**: Recorded at Medium confidence because it is a human dependency rather than a data
one. The recall request three years later goes to the person who ran the session, and the archive
policy is the studio's. When the engineer has moved on (16.03.03: their credits are theirs, the
studio's archive is not), the client is asking a studio about a session run by someone who no longer
works there, against a policy that may have changed.

**Role scoping**:
- **Producer**: both sides — the one asked for the recall, and the one who left.
- **Operator**: holds the archive and the policy, may not know what is on the drive.
- **Musician / Fan**: no direct role.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. The Medium
confidence is honest: the pair is real in practice and thin in data terms. What it does establish is
that the archive policy must be **captured onto the reservation** (16.03.04 D-02) rather than read
live — because the studio, the policy, and the engineer may all have changed by the time it matters.
