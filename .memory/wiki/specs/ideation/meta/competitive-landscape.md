## The Structural Observation

> This is the sweep's central competitive finding. It is a **hypothesis for the owner to
> confirm or kill**, not a locked conclusion.

**Every competitor above is a point solution.** No single product spans identity → credits →
collaboration → rights → commerce → live → audience. A working musician today runs their
career across Reverb + SoundBetter + Bandsintown + Splice + DistroKid + a spreadsheet — with
no shared identity, no shared credit history, and no shared ownership record between any of
them.

**The credits-database failure mode** (relevant to domain 02, the map's `whitespace` pick):
Jaxsta, Muso.AI, Sound Credit and Discogs all exist *because* credits are broken — and all
fail the same way. They **reconstruct credits after the fact** from whoever remembers, from
liner notes, from label submissions. None of them were in the room. A platform that hosts the
session can capture the credit at source, at the moment it is true and uncontested.

The same asymmetry applies to splits (domain 09): reconstructing a split sheet years later,
after a track earns money and relationships have soured, is the single most litigated failure
in music. Capturing it on the day costs one signature.

## Unique Differentiators

`[PENDING — /ideate-discover]`

Candidates from the sweep, requiring owner ratification:

1. **Split-at-creation** — capture ownership at the moment of creation, not in reconstruction.
   Unrepeatable by any competitor who was not present at the session.
2. **A verified credit graph** derived from actual sessions rather than self-assertion — makes
   the professional network real by construction rather than a graph of "accept?" clicks.
3. **Compound effects no point solution can replicate** — e.g. gear provenance that follows an
   instrument across owners; warm intros weighted by verified collaboration; payouts that
   settle against a rights record the platform already holds.
