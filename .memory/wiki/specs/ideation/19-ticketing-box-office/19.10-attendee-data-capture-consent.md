# Feature: Attendee Data Capture & Event-Party Consent

> **Number**: 19.10
> **Parent**: [Ticketing & Box Office](./ticketing-box-office-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Who gets the attendee list after the show, and did the fan agree to it? The artist wants it (they built the audience), the venue wants it (they filled the room), the promoter wants it (they paid for it), and the fan agreed to hear from — at most — one of them.

Ticketing is where the artist↔fan link is created, which makes this domain the point of capture for the most contested asset in live music: **a list of people who demonstrably showed up**. This feature owns the narrow, ticketing-specific question of *which party receives what, on what basis, for this event*. The consent **mechanism** — capture, preference centre, withdrawal, DSAR — is a cross-cut serving every domain and is not owned here.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Receives the attendee data the fan consented to share **with them specifically** — which is the whole point: today the artist plays to 300 people and leaves with none of their details, while the venue and the promoter keep the list. Sees consented contacts, aggregate geography for the rest. Cannot change what was consented to. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Configures which parties are offered at purchase and what each is asking for, receives their own consented share, and answers for the disclosure being accurate. |
| Fan | Full | Decides, at purchase, who may contact them — per party, not as a single blanket tick. Can withdraw at any time and the withdrawal must actually propagate. This is their data and the platform's position is that it stays theirs. |

## Behavior

At purchase, the fan provides what the transaction needs (identity for the ticket, an email for delivery) and is asked, separately and per party, who may contact them afterwards: **this artist**, **this venue**, **the promoter**.

The parties are named — not "our partners". A fan agreeing to hear from an artist they love is not agreeing to hear from a venue they will visit once.

Post-show, each party receives only the contacts that named them. Everyone else gets aggregate-only. Withdrawal propagates to whoever received the data, which is the part that is hard and the part that is the actual duty.

### Happy Path

1. Operator configures the show's parties: the artist, the venue, and (where relevant) the promoter.
2. Fan buys. Alongside the transaction, they see a plain, per-party question: who may contact you about future shows?
3. Fan ticks the artist; leaves the venue and promoter unticked. This is the common and expected outcome.
4. Ticket is delivered; the transaction data (name, email) is used for that purpose regardless of the marketing consent, because delivering the thing they bought is not marketing.
5. Post-show, the artist receives that fan's contact in their fanbase (domain 20). The venue receives an aggregate count.
6. Fan later withdraws; the withdrawal reaches the artist's list, and the artist can no longer contact them.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buyer is not the attendee | Bought 4 for friends; consented for themselves | **The structural defect** — the platform has one buyer's consent and four attendees. Recorded as a *buyer* record, never claimed as four attendees (D-05). See DT-01 |
| Transferred ticket (19.09.02) | The holder never made a consent decision at all | Same defect; the holder is a person the platform knows nothing about. Transfer does **not** carry the original buyer's consent to the new holder — consent is personal and untransferable |
| Party wants the data regardless of consent | Commercial pressure applied to the Operator who configures the screen | The disclosure must be accurate; a party that did not get consent gets aggregate-only, never contacts. Overriding it is a Trust & Safety incident (domain 24) |
| Consent bundled as one tick for "the organisers" | Blocked at configuration — the Operator cannot collapse parties into one checkbox | Per-party or nothing. See D-01, D-02 |
| A named party is removed from the bill after consent given | Support act drops off; fan had ticked them | Consent was to a specific named party; if that party never becomes a recipient, the consent is simply never acted on. No contact shared to a party that left the event |
| Ticket cancelled or refunded before the show | Fan didn't attend; artist should not gain a "fan who came" they never met | Marketing consent given at purchase is **not** revoked by a refund (it was a free-standing decision), but a refunded ticket is excluded from the attendee/buyer distribution — a no-show is not audience |
| Fan changes their consent between buying two tickets to the same show | Bought again for a friend, ticked differently | Latest per-party choice wins for that fan's own record; the cross-cut preference centre is the single source of truth, not the individual checkout |
| Fan withdraws after the artist exported the list to Mailchimp | The data is gone from WeJammin's control | Withdrawal propagates inside the platform immediately; propagation past the boundary is a duty the platform can signal but not enforce. `[PENDING — /create-prd-security, Q-03]` |
| Externally-ticketed show (19.07) | The incumbent captured the fan; WeJammin has no relationship at all | This feature does not exist for those shows — an honest, structural hole, not a bug |
| Walk-up sale (19.05.04) | Bought at a window with a queue behind them; nobody is ticking consent boxes at a door | Realistically no marketing consent captured; the sale is a *buyer* record with transactional data only. An honest gap, disclosed not papered over |
| Comp / guest list (19.03) | Never bought anything; on someone's list | No purchase transaction means no consent screen was ever shown; the guest yields aggregate attendance only, never a consented contact, unless they separately opt in via their own account |
| Fan buys 6 shows by the same artist; consents each time | Per-event capture would ask six times | Level-1: each purchase captures per-event; de-duplication and "don't ask me again for this artist" live in the consent cross-cut's preference centre, not here. Per-event vs per-artist basis is `[PENDING — user decision, Q-02]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not configured | Show has no parties defined | Operator prompted; defaults to artist + venue |
| Asked | At purchase | Per-party question, plainly worded, named parties |
| Consented (partial) | Fan ticked some | Normal — most fans tick one |
| Declined all | Fan ticked none | Also normal. The transaction proceeds identically; nothing is withheld |
| Shared | Post-show distribution | Each party sees their consented contacts and aggregate for the rest |
| Withdrawn | Fan withdrew | Propagates to recipients; the party can no longer contact them |
| Unattributed | Buyer ≠ attendee | The gap the platform cannot close (DT-01) — recorded as a buyer, not an attendee |
| Excluded | Ticket refunded/cancelled | Consent record stands, but the buyer is dropped from the distribution — a no-show is not audience |
| Capture error | Consent write fails at checkout | Ticket still issues (transactional path is independent, D-03); consent defaults to **none granted** and the fan is not silently opted in |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The buyer is not the attendee, and the whole model quietly assumes they are** | Fan persona's Role Lens across this domain; the transfer feature (19.09.02) existing at all; the ordinary reality that one person buys four tickets for a group | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a defect that runs through the entire feature rather than an edge case at its margin. One person buys four tickets and answers one consent question. Three attendees never saw it, never agreed to anything, and never appear on any list — while the buyer's consent is recorded as though it covered the party. So the "attendee list" is really a *buyer list*, and it under-represents the audience by whatever the average group size is (2–3×). Every downstream use is affected: domain 20's superfan scoring sees one fan where there were four, the artist's audience looks smaller than it is, and the three people who actually came are invisible. Ticket personalisation (asking for each attendee's name) is the industry's answer and it is a checkout-conversion catastrophe for an £18 club ticket. **Recorded honestly rather than solved**: the platform should say "buyer" where it means buyer, and not claim an attendee list it does not have. |
| DT-02 | This should be a sub-domain — capture, party-scoped sharing, handoff and withdrawal are several interacting capabilities | The initial classification instinct while running the gate | ❌ REJECTED, and the reasoning is what keeps this a feature. Once the **consent mechanism** is correctly routed out as a cross-cut — the preference centre, the withdrawal propagation, DSAR, the lawful-basis machinery, all of which serve 20, 24, 05, 13, 14 identically — what remains that is genuinely ticketing-owned is a single capability: *for this event, which of these named parties receives this fan's contact details, and on what basis*. That is one thing. The pieces that looked like interacting capabilities were mostly the cross-cut's, borrowed. Flagged for promotion under the Reactive Depth Protocol if Step 5 finds the party-scoped rules to be substantive — the multi-party bill case (a headliner, two supports, a venue and a promoter, each wanting the list) could plausibly justify it. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Consent is per named party, never a single blanket tick | Agreeing to hear from an artist is not agreeing to hear from a venue; bundling is legally weak and substantively dishonest |
| D-02 | Parties are named explicitly — never "our partners" | A fan cannot consent to a party they cannot see |
| D-03 | Transactional use (ticket delivery, reschedule notices) is independent of marketing consent | Delivering the thing they bought is not marketing, and a fan declining marketing must still get their ticket and hear about a date change |
| D-04 | Declining all consents changes nothing about the transaction | Nothing is withheld, nothing is degraded |
| D-05 | The platform says "buyer" where it means buyer | It does not have an attendee list and must not claim one (DT-01) |
| D-06 | The consent *mechanism* is a cross-cut; only party-scoped event disclosure lives here | What remains after routing the mechanism out is one capability (DT-02) |
