# Feature: Purchase Limits & Bot Mitigation

> **Number**: 19.09.01
> **Parent**: [Ticketing Fraud, Bot & Resale Controls](./19.09-ticketing-fraud-bot-resale-controls-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Stopping one actor from taking inventory meant for many. A per-buyer limit ("4 per person") is the oldest control in ticketing and the easiest to defeat; bot mitigation is the arms race that follows, and it is an arms race the defender loses on effort every time.

The US **BOTS Act** makes circumventing a ticket purchase limit unlawful — which is useful and does not help much, because the actors doing it are not deterred by a statute nobody enforces against them.

**The posture** (parent D-03): the structural controls already decided elsewhere in this domain do most of the work here. Queue randomisation (19.02.04 D-01) removes the bot's speed advantage without detecting anything. This feature covers what is left.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sets or requests the limit for their show — it is their fans being crowded out, and 2-per-person on a 200-cap room is an artist's decision about who gets in. Sees the outcome (how many hit the limit), not the detection internals. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sets limits per show and per window, sees limit hits and suspected circumvention, and decides what to do about it. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the limit stated **before** they start choosing, not at checkout when their cart is full. A limit discovered at the last step is a hostile design that costs a sale and a mood. |

## Behavior

A **purchase limit** caps a buyer's accumulated holding for a show — not their basket. The distinction is the whole feature: capping the basket stops nothing, because four baskets is four baskets. Capping the *holding* requires knowing who is holding, which requires identity, which is where it gets hard.

Limits can be set per show and per presale window (19.02.02), which is common: 2 in the fan-club presale, 4 on public sale.

**Bot mitigation** in this feature is what remains after the structural controls: identity-bound queue positions (19.02.04), rate and pattern signals, and the shared fraud cross-cut's device/account signals. It is deliberately the smaller half.

### Happy Path

1. Musician requests 4 per person; Operator sets it on the show and 2 on the fan-club window.
2. Fan browses; the limit is stated plainly on the show page, before selection.
3. Fan selects 4; the limit is checked against their existing holding for this show, not just this cart.
4. Purchase completes; the holding is 4.
5. Fan returns and tries to buy 2 more; blocked, with the reason and their current holding stated.
6. A bot attempts the same across 40 accounts; queue randomisation (19.02.04 D-01) has already removed its speed advantage, and identity-bound positions mean 40 accounts is 40 positions competing with everyone else's one — not 40 head starts.
7. Limit hits and circumvention signals surface to the Operator as information, not as automated punishment.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Buy 4, transfer them out, buy 4 more | **The standard defeat.** The limit constrains a purchase; transfer converts it into accumulation | See `19.09-ticketing-fraud-bot-resale-controls-cx.md#CX-01`. This is why transfer policy and limits are one decision |
| One household, two fans, one card | Legitimate; looks exactly like limit evasion | Card-based limits punish families. See DT-01 |
| 40 accounts, 40 real-looking identities | The professional's approach; no signal distinguishes them from 40 real fans | Structural controls beat detective ones here — randomisation makes 40 accounts worth little (parent D-03) |
| Limit stated only at checkout | Fan builds a cart of 6 and is rejected at the last step | Stated before selection. A hostile pattern that costs a sale and a fan |
| Limit hit by a genuine group of 8 friends | They must split across accounts, which is exactly what a scalper does | No system can tell them apart. The honest answer is that the limit is a blunt instrument and should be set where the artist wants it, not where an algorithm does |
| Legitimate fan blocked by a false positive | A fan who did nothing wrong cannot buy | The asymmetry that governs this feature — see DT-02 |
| Limit on a show with no demand | Pure friction for zero benefit | Limits should be off by default at the beachhead. See parent Q-01 |
| Fan buys 4, gives 2 to friends, wants 2 more for themselves | Genuine, common, and indistinguishable from laundering | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No limit | Default | Nothing; most shows need none |
| Limited | Limit set | Stated on the show page before selection |
| Within limit | Fan holding < limit | Normal purchase; remaining allowance shown if close |
| At limit | Fan holding = limit | Blocked with their current holding stated plainly |
| Suspected circumvention | Pattern signals | Surfaced to the Operator as information |
| Under review | Operator investigating | Fan is unaffected unless the Operator acts. See D-04 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Limits should bind to **payment card**, since that is the strongest identity signal available at checkout | Standard industry practice — most incumbents do exactly this | ❌ REJECTED — it punishes precisely the wrong people. A household with one card is two or three fans; a couple sharing finances is two fans; a parent buying for their kid and themselves is normal. All of them hit a card-based limit and none of them is a scalper. Meanwhile the professional has fifty virtual cards, which cost nothing and defeat the control entirely. So a card limit has the worst possible profile: it reliably blocks families and reliably fails against the actor it was built for. Account identity is weaker in theory and better in practice, because the professional evades both while the household evades neither — and at least an account limit does not make a shared bank account into a suspicious pattern. This is a case where copying the incumbent would import a control that has been quietly failing for a decade. |
| DT-02 | When a purchase looks like circumvention, the system should block it automatically | Security instinct; and it is what "mitigation" implies | ❌ REJECTED — the error asymmetry is brutal and runs the opposite way to normal fraud. A false positive here is a real fan, who did nothing wrong, publicly unable to buy a ticket to see an artist they love, at the one moment it is available, with no recourse and no explanation they will accept. A false negative is a scalper who got four tickets. The first is a story on social media and a fan lost forever; the second is a Tuesday. Nothing in this feature justifies automated punishment on probabilistic evidence. The correct model is **surface, don't block**: show the Operator the signal, let a human act on it, and lean on the structural controls (parent D-03) which have no false positives at all — randomisation does not punish anyone, it just makes early arrival worthless. Detection is the last resort in this domain and it should never be the automatic one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Limits cap a buyer's accumulated **holding** for a show, not their basket | Four baskets is four baskets; capping the cart stops nothing |
| D-02 | Limits bind to account identity, not payment card | Card limits reliably block households and reliably fail against professionals with virtual cards (DT-01) |
| D-03 | The limit is stated before selection, never at checkout | A limit discovered with a full cart costs a sale and a fan for no benefit |
| D-04 | Suspected circumvention is surfaced, never auto-blocked | A false positive is a real fan publicly unable to buy; a false negative is a Tuesday (DT-02) |
| D-05 | Limits are off by default | Most shows at the beachhead have a selling problem, not a scalping problem |
| D-06 | Structural controls are preferred; this feature is deliberately the smaller half of bot mitigation | Randomisation (19.02.04 D-01) has no false positives; detection always does (parent D-03) |
