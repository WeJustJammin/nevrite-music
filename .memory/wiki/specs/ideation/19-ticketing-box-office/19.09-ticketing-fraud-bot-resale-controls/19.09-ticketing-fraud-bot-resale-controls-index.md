## Overview

Keeping tickets in the hands of the people the artist wanted to have them, at the price the artist set. Purchase limits, bot mitigation at on-sale, transfer controls, and a regulated resale path with a price ceiling.

**Why this is ticketing-specific and not the generic fraud cross-cut**: the generic fraud/abuse detection engine is a mechanism serving domains 05, 13, 14, 19 and 24 alike — a stolen card is a stolen card whether it buys a ticket, a guitar or a plugin, and that mechanism is recorded as a cross-cut. What lives *here* is the body of controls that exist **only** because tickets are scarce, dated, and resellable at a markup: the BOTS Act, resale price caps, transfer locks, face-value exchange. The parent domain's rationale names exactly this — *"its own regulatory regime (BOTS Act, resale price caps, fee-disclosure and junk-fee rules)"*.

**The posture that shapes everything here**: the cheapest and most effective controls in this domain are **structural, not detective**. Randomising the queue (19.02.04 D-01) removes the bot's advantage without detecting a single bot. Keeping the ticket use-once (19.04.03 D-01) preserves the double-scan signal for free. Detection is the last resort, not the first.
