# Feature: Ticket Transfer & Delivery Controls

> **Number**: 19.09.02
> **Parent**: [Ticketing Fraud, Bot & Resale Controls](./19.09-ticketing-fraud-bot-resale-controls-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Whether a ticket can move from the person who bought it to someone else, and how. This sounds like a minor setting and is in fact the **most consequential control in the sub-domain**: transfer policy *is* resale policy (`#CX-02`), transfer is the escape hatch that defeats purchase limits (`#CX-01`), and the delivery mechanism (static barcode vs rotating token) determines whether a ticket can be screenshotted and sold on Facebook.

It is also the control most likely to hurt innocent people, because the overwhelming majority of transfers are one friend giving another friend a ticket.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sets the transfer posture for their show — it is their fans on both ends of every transfer, and their name on any resale listing. The strongest artist voice in the domain. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Executes the policy, sees transfer activity, and handles the support case when a transfer went to the wrong email. |
| Fan | Full | Transfers a ticket to a friend and receives one. This must be **trivially easy in the common case** — it is the second-most-common thing a fan does with a ticket after presenting it at a door. |

## Behavior

Three transfer postures, per show:

| Posture | Behaviour | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Free transfer to anyone, any time | Uncapped off-platform resale (`#CX-02`); purchase limits become theatre |
| **Tracked** | Transfer permitted, recorded, holder identity updated | Limits are enforceable; resale still invisible but the chain is known |
| **Locked** | No transfer | Kills resale — and kills the fan who genuinely cannot go |

**Delivery mechanism** interacts directly: a **static barcode** (PDF, printout, screenshot) can be duplicated and sold and the platform never knows. A **rotating token** cannot be screenshotted usefully — and requires the fan's phone to have signal in a basement, which 19.04.02 establishes it does not.

That is the trade this feature cannot escape: the anti-fraud mechanism requires exactly the connectivity the venue does not have.

### Happy Path

1. Musician sets the posture — for a club show, **tracked**: transfer freely, but we know who holds it.
2. Fan buys 2, cannot go, sends one to a friend from their ticket view — two taps, no ceremony.
3. Recipient gets the ticket in their own wallet (19.12); the sender's copy is void.
4. Holder identity updates; the sender's holding for limit purposes decrements (or does not — see Q-01).
5. At the door, the friend scans their own ticket normally (19.04.01).
6. The transfer chain is recorded — who held it when — which is the only artifact that makes resale visible at all.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Rotating token, no signal at the door | The pass cannot rotate; the fan cannot get in | **The unavoidable collision** (19.04.01 edge cases). A static fallback is required, and the static fallback is exactly the screenshot the rotation existed to prevent. See DT-03 |
| Transfer sent to the wrong email | Transfer sits **pending** — not yet claimed — so the sender cancels it and re-sends (D-06) | Sender's ticket view shows the transfer as pending with a **Cancel** control; recipient's copy is not live until they claim it |
| Transfer claimed by a stranger before the sender notices the typo | Once claimed, the ticket has moved; reversal is now a support case, not a self-serve cancel | Sender sees "already claimed"; Operator handles it as a disputed transfer (the reason tracked posture records the chain). Deep clawback rules `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |
| Ticket screenshotted and sold twice | Both buyers turn up; first scan wins | Double-scan refusal (19.04.03) with specifics; the second buyer is out of pocket and furious at the venue |
| Locked transfer; fan genuinely cannot go | Ticket wasted, seat empty, fan resentful | See DT-02 |
| Refund on a transferred ticket | Money goes back to the payer; the ticket belongs to the holder | 19.06.01 Q-03; a genuine tangle |
| Reschedule notification on a transferred ticket | The notification goes to the payer; the holder turns up on the wrong night | **Why the wallet pass matters** (19.06.03 DT-02) — the pass is on the holder's phone and updates itself |
| Transfer of a VIP package | The M&G roster names a person the artist agreed to meet | 19.08.01 Q-03 |
| Transfer of an accessible ticket | The accessible position may not suit the recipient | `[PENDING]` |
| Buy 4, transfer out, buy 4 more | The limit defeat (`#CX-01`) | The reason transfer and limits are one decision |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not transferable | Locked posture | Stated at purchase — a fan should know before buying that they cannot pass it on |
| Transferable | Open or tracked | Transfer available from the ticket view, two taps |
| Sending (loading) | Sender confirms the recipient | Brief inline progress on the transfer button; the ticket is not yet void and the recipient is not yet invited until the send succeeds |
| Transfer pending | Sent, not claimed | Sender sees it is pending and can cancel (D-06); recipient is invited |
| Transferred | Claimed | Recipient holds it; sender's copy void; chain recorded |
| Chain visible | Tracked posture | Operator sees the holding history; the fan sees only their own ticket |
| Rotating | Rotating-token delivery | Fan sees a live pass; static fallback available (DT-03) |
| Empty (nothing to transfer) | Fan holds no transferable tickets | Ticket view shows no transfer control — not a broken button, simply absent |
| Transfer error | Send failed (network, invalid recipient, recipient blocked) | The ticket stays with the sender, unchanged; an inline error names the cause and offers retry — a failed send never leaves a ticket in limbo |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Transfer policy is a minor delivery setting | The framing the sweep's candidate list implies, by burying transfer inside "fraud controls" | ❌ REJECTED — it is the **keystone of the entire sub-domain** and arguably the most consequential single setting in domain 19. It determines whether purchase limits are real (`#CX-01`), whether resale can be capped at all (`#CX-02`), and whether the platform has any visibility into where its tickets go. Every other control here is downstream of it: a resale price cap on a marketplace WeJammin operates is meaningless if free transfer lets the market happen on Facebook; a purchase limit is theatre if the tickets can be laundered onward. Treating it as a delivery preference is how a platform ends up with an elaborate anti-scalping apparatus that a scalper walks straight past. |
| DT-02 | Locked transfer is the right default for artists who care about scalping | The obvious anti-scalping answer, and artists ask for it | ❌ REJECTED — it punishes the wrong population overwhelmingly. The vast majority of transfers are a fan who genuinely cannot go giving their ticket to a friend, and locking transfer means that seat is empty, that fan is out of pocket and resentful, and their friend did not come to the show. The scalper, meanwhile, sells the *account credentials* — an inconvenience, not a barrier. So locked transfer reliably harms real fans and mildly inconveniences professionals: the same failure profile as card-based purchase limits (19.09.01 DT-01), and it recurs because both are attempts to solve an economic problem with a mechanical prohibition. **Tracked** is the right default: transfer stays easy, the chain is recorded, and the artist who genuinely wants to stop resale gets a better tool in the capped face-value exchange (19.09.03) which gives the fan who cannot go a legitimate exit. |
| DT-03 | Rotating barcodes are the strong anti-screenshot control and should be used on shows that need one | Industry direction of travel — SafeTix and equivalents; and it does work | ✅ CONFIRMED with a **hard constraint that mostly defeats it here**, which is worth recording precisely because the industry answer does not survive contact with this domain's own facts. Rotating tokens require the fan's phone to reach the network at the door. 19.04.02 establishes as a *precondition of the whole sub-domain* that music venues do not have connectivity — basements, thick walls, and a few hundred phones saturating one cell at doors-open. The arena that deploys SafeTix has DAS and venue wifi; the 200-cap basement WeJammin targets has neither. So the control that works for Ticketmaster's estate is the one that strands fans at WeJammin's. Any rotating implementation needs a static fallback, and the static fallback is precisely the screenshot the rotation existed to prevent — which means rotation degrades to theatre at exactly the venues in scope. Recorded as confirmed-but-constrained rather than rejected, because it becomes correct the moment WeJammin serves rooms with real connectivity. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Transfer policy is the keystone control of this sub-domain, not a delivery setting | It determines whether limits are real and whether resale can be capped at all (DT-01) |
| D-02 | **Tracked** is the default posture: transfer stays easy, the chain is recorded | Locked transfer harms the fan who cannot go and barely inconveniences the scalper, who sells credentials (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Transfer in the common case is two taps with no ceremony | It is the second-most-common thing a fan does with a ticket |
| D-04 | Any rotating-token implementation must carry a static fallback | The door has no network, and neither does the fan's phone (DT-03) |
| D-05 | A locked-transfer show states it at purchase | A fan should know before buying that they cannot pass it on |
| D-06 | Transfer is reversible until claimed | Wrong-email transfers are common and currently unrecoverable |
