# Feature: Resale, Price Caps & Face-Value Exchange

> **Number**: 19.09.03
> **Parent**: [Ticketing Fraud, Bot & Resale Controls](./19.09-ticketing-fraud-bot-resale-controls-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

A fan cannot go. Someone else wants to. The seat should move between them at the price the artist set — not at four times it, with the difference going to a stranger with a script.

**Face-value exchange** is the model that works: a fan lists their ticket at what they paid, it goes to the next person who wants one (or to the waitlist, 19.02.05), the original fan gets their money back, the new fan gets a real ticket, and the artist's pricing survives contact with demand. Twickets and DICE have both demonstrated it. It is not a compromise between the fan and the scalper — it is the removal of the scalper from a transaction that never needed them.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sets the resale posture for their show. **The strongest artist voice in the domain**, and correctly so — it is their price, their fans getting gouged, and their name on a £200 listing for an £18 ticket. An artist who wants face-value-only should get it. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Executes the policy and operates the exchange where WeJammin runs one. |
| Fan | Full | Both sides: the seller who cannot go and wants their money back without guilt, and the buyer who wants a real ticket at a real price for a show that says "sold out". |

## Behavior

Where transfer is **tracked** (19.09.02 D-02), a fan who cannot attend can list their ticket into the **face-value exchange**:

1. Seller lists at face value (what they paid, including or excluding fees — Q-01).
2. The seat is offered to the waitlist first (19.02.05 D-01), because that list is already ordered and already proven to want it.
3. Buyer pays; seller is refunded; the ticket's holder updates; the chain records it.
4. No margin exists anywhere in the transaction, so there is nothing for a scalper to extract.

**Price caps** apply where WeJammin operates the exchange and are jurisdiction-shaped (parent Q-02). The hard limit on all of it: WeJammin can only cap the market it runs. Off-platform resale executes as a transfer (`#CX-02`) and the money is invisible.

### Happy Path

1. Musician sets face-value-only resale for their show.
2. Fan buys 2; a month later they cannot go.
3. Fan opens their ticket and lists it — one action, no fee, no guilt, no Facebook post.
4. The waitlist (19.02.05) has 40 people; the seat goes to the first claimant as a time-boxed private claim (19.02.03).
5. Buyer pays face value; seller is refunded in full; ticket transfers.
6. Show is full, the artist's price held, and the fan who could not go is not out of pocket.
7. The transaction is recorded — a real fact about where the ticket went.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan sells off-platform at 4× | Executes as a transfer; the money is invisible | **The structural limit.** WeJammin caps only the market it runs (`#CX-02`) |
| Face value + fees — what does the seller get back? | They paid £18 + £3 fee. Do they list at £18 or £21? Does the buyer pay a new fee? | The defining policy question. See Q-01 |
| Nobody wants the ticket | Show is not sold out; the exchange has no buyers | Listing sits unsold; the fan should be told plainly rather than left hoping |
| Exchange competes with the venue's own unsold inventory | A fan reselling at face value undercuts nothing but takes a sale the venue wanted | Real tension. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — probably the venue's inventory sells first |
| Seller lists, then the show is cancelled | Listing voids; the original buyer is refunded normally | 19.06.02 |
| Buyer pays; the transfer fails | Money moved, ticket did not | Must be atomic. `[PENDING]` |
| Artist wants resale banned entirely | Locked transfer (19.09.02) — which harms the fan who cannot go | The exchange is the *better* answer to what the artist actually wants, and should be offered as such (19.09.02 DT-02) |
| Reserved seating exchange | The seat identity moves with the ticket | Straightforward |
| Jurisdiction bans above-face resale | The cap is not a choice | Parent Q-02 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Face-value exchange is the right primitive, not a price-capped resale marketplace** | Fan persona's success criterion — *"money reaches the artist rather than a reseller"*; the Musician's interest in their own pricing surviving demand; the observation that the waitlist (19.02.05) already supplies an ordered queue of proven buyers | ✅ CONFIRMED — and the distinction is not semantic. A price-capped marketplace still *is* a marketplace: it has listings, browsing, pricing decisions, discovery, and a margin small enough to be capped but large enough to be optimised — which means it still attracts people whose business is optimising it. A face-value exchange has **no margin at all**, which does not merely regulate the scalper's profit, it deletes the reason they were ever in the transaction. It also composes with machinery that already exists: the waitlist is the buyer queue, the code mechanism (19.02.03) is the claim delivery, the refund rail returns the seller's money. So the exchange is both the stronger anti-scalping design and the cheaper build — it is mostly wiring together three features that exist for other reasons. Build the exchange; do not build a marketplace and then try to regulate it. |
| DT-02 | WeJammin should operate a resale marketplace to keep resale on-platform where it can be controlled | The pragmatic "if they'll do it anyway, better here" argument, and it is how several incumbents justify their resale business | ❌ REJECTED — the argument is self-serving when incumbents make it and it does not survive here. Operating a marketplace means taking a cut of a transaction that adds no value to the artist, the fan or the show; it means building discovery, pricing and trust surfaces for a product whose whole purpose is that a ticket changed hands; and it means the platform now has a revenue line that grows when tickets are scarce and expensive — an incentive pointing precisely against the Fan persona and against the artist. That is the exact structural corruption that made the incumbents hated, and it would be adopted deliberately. The face-value exchange (DT-01) captures the legitimate need — a fan who cannot go, a fan who wants in — with no margin and therefore no perverse incentive. And the honest answer to "they'll do it anyway" is: yes, off-platform, and WeJammin will see a transfer and nothing else (`#CX-02`). Accepting that limit is better than monetising the problem. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The primitive is a face-value exchange, not a price-capped marketplace | No margin means no scalper, rather than a regulated one; and it is mostly wiring together the waitlist, codes and the refund rail (DT-01) |
| D-02 | WeJammin does not operate a margin-taking resale marketplace | A revenue line that grows when tickets are scarce is an incentive pointing against the artist and the fan — the exact corruption that made incumbents hated (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Listed seats are offered to the waitlist first | The list is already ordered and already proven to want it |
| D-04 | The exchange is offered to artists as the better answer to "I want to stop resale" | Locked transfer punishes the fan who cannot go; the exchange gives them a legitimate exit (19.09.02 DT-02) |
| D-05 | An unsold listing tells the fan plainly | Leaving them hoping is worse than telling them the show is not sold out |
