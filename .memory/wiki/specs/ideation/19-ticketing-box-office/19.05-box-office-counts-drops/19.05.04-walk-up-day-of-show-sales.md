# Feature: Walk-Up & Day-of-Show Sales

> **Number**: 19.05.04
> **Parent**: [Box Office Counts, Drops & Day-of-Show](./19.05-box-office-counts-drops-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Selling at the window on the night. The literal box office: a person, a table, a card reader, a cash float, and a queue in the rain. At the club level that WeJammin targets, walk-up is not a rounding error — for a large share of shows it is a substantial fraction of the gate, and for some it is most of it.

It is also the last thing to write to the count, which makes it the gate on the close (19.05.05), and it happens *during* the scanning window, when every scanner is holding a replica that predates the sale (`#CX-03`).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. They see the resulting count and the certified statement; the window is not theirs to operate. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sells at the window: takes payment (card or cash), issues the ticket, admits the buyer. Sees remaining inventory live. Reconciles the cash float at close. |
| Fan | Full | Turns up without a ticket, pays at the window, walks in. The simplest transaction in the domain and the one with the least tolerance for friction — there is a queue behind them and a band on stage. |

## Behavior

Walk-up sells from the same manifest as everything else (19.01.02) — it is not a separate pool. The window's price may differ from advance (day-of-show pricing is standard), which is a price level, not a separate product.

Two payment realities coexist and both must work: **card** (which needs a rail and therefore a network) and **cash** (which needs neither and which small venues still run on). Cash is not legacy — it is the fallback when the card reader has no signal, which in these rooms is often.

The buyer walks in immediately. Usually they are admitted by the same person who just sold to them, at the same table — which is the resolution to `#CX-03` (see DT-01).

### Happy Path

1. Doors are open; Operator opens the window on a device that is also a scanner.
2. Fan arrives with no ticket; Operator sees remaining inventory live.
3. Operator selects the day-of-show price level and takes payment — card, or cash into the float.
4. System issues the ticket; paid count increments; remaining decrements.
5. The same device admits the buyer directly — no scan of a barcode that was created three seconds ago on this same device (DT-01).
6. Fan walks in.
7. At close, the cash float is reconciled and the window's takings join the certified statement (19.05.05).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Card reader has no signal | Extremely common in these rooms | Cash fallback must work with zero network. If cash is not first-class, the window stops |
| Sold at 20:30; the door scanner synced at 19:00 | The ticket does not exist on the scanner | The window device admits directly (DT-01). If they *are* separate devices, the sale must reach the scanner — which needs peer gossip (19.04.02 DT-03) |
| Show sold out; walk-ups queueing | Nothing to sell — but holds may be unreleased (19.01.02) | Held-inventory alert should have fired days ago. At the window it is too late, which is exactly why it fires on remaining inventory rather than a date |
| Cash float does not reconcile | The oldest problem in the box office | Float reconciliation is part of close; a discrepancy is recorded, not hidden. See Q-02 |
| Fan wants a refund at the window minutes later | Bought the wrong night, changed their mind | `[PENDING]` — routes to 19.06.01 but the window is a different context (cash out of the float) |
| Walk-up on an externally-ticketed show (19.07) | The window is selling against inventory WeJammin does not control | Cannot sell what it cannot see. `[PENDING]` — likely out of scope for external shows |
| Two windows selling concurrently | Both draw from the same remaining inventory, possibly offline | Same coordination problem as multi-scanner (19.04.03), with money attached — worse, because an oversell here is a person who paid |
| Fee structure differs at the window | Day-of-show all-in price must still be disclosed (19.01.03) | Displayed price is the charged price, at a table as much as online |
| Buyer wants a receipt | Card gives one; cash does not | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Closed | Before doors / after window close | Window not selling |
| Open | Operator opens the window | Remaining inventory, day-of-show price level, payment mode |
| Selling | Transaction in flight | Amount, payment method, issue-and-admit as one action |
| Sold out | Remaining reaches zero | Window shows nothing to sell; held inventory surfaced if any remains |
| Offline (card) | No signal for the rail | Cash-only state, stated plainly, window keeps working |
| Reconciling float | Window closing | Cash count vs expected; discrepancy recorded |
| Closed for close | Window shut | Gates the certified statement (`#CX-04`) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The selling device admits the buyer directly** — a walk-up buyer should never be issued a barcode and then asked to present it to a scanner | The `#CX-03` race (sale at 20:30 vs replica from 19:00); the physical reality that the window and the door are frequently the same table and the same person | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it dissolves the sub-domain's nastiest race rather than solving it. If the sale and the admission are one action on one device, there is no replica to be stale, no barcode to propagate, no window between issuance and scan for anything to go wrong in. The ticket still exists as a record (for the count, the statement, and the fan's proof of purchase), but it is never *validated* — it is admitted at birth, by the device that created it, which has perfect knowledge of it. This is why walk-up sits in this sub-domain and not with the door (parent D-02): its problem is the count, not access control. The residual case — a separate window and door, physically apart — still needs the sale to reach the scanner, and that is 19.04.02's peer-gossip question (DT-03), not a new problem. |
| DT-02 | Cash is a legacy path that can be deferred; card-only is acceptable for a modern platform | Product simplification instinct; every fintech says cash is dying | ❌ REJECTED, decisively, and it is the kind of assumption that fails a product in the field rather than in review. Two independent reasons. **Connectivity**: the card reader needs a network, and the whole premise of this domain's door (19.04.02, parent D-02) is that these rooms do not have one. A card-only window is a window that closes when the signal drops — at the exact moment a queue exists. Cash is not the legacy fallback, it is the *offline-capable* payment method, which is the same argument that makes offline scanning non-negotiable. **The market**: small venues run cash floats today, and a platform that cannot take a tenner at the door is a platform they cannot use for the busiest 30 minutes of their week. Deferring cash would fail the Operator persona precisely where they said their business lives — *"much of their work is on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk."* |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The selling device admits the buyer directly; walk-up tickets are admitted at birth, never scanned | Dissolves the sale-vs-stale-replica race instead of solving it (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Cash is a first-class payment method, not a legacy fallback | It is the only offline-capable payment method, and these rooms are offline (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Walk-up sells from the same manifest; day-of-show pricing is a price level, not a separate pool | A separate pool is a second manifest and a second chance to oversell |
| D-04 | All-in price disclosure applies at the window exactly as online | The regulation does not care whether there is a table |
