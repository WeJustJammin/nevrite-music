# Feature: Walk-Up & Day-of-Show Sales

> **Number**: 19.05.04
> **Parent**: [Box Office Counts, Drops & Day-of-Show](./19.05-box-office-counts-drops-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Selling at the window on the night. The literal box office: a person, a table, a card reader, a cash float, and a queue in the rain. At the club level WeJammin targets, walk-up is not a rounding error — it is routinely 20–50% of the gate and for some shows the majority of it, which makes it a material fraction of the split settlement computes (domain 17).

It is also the **last writer to the count**, which makes it the gate on the close (19.05.05, `#CX-04`), and it happens *during* the scanning window, when every scanner is holding a manifest replica that predates the sale (`#CX-03`). The defining design move (DT-01) is that the selling device admits the buyer directly, so a walk-up ticket is *admitted at birth* — sale and admission are one action, and the sale-versus-stale-replica race dissolves rather than being solved.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | The window is not theirs to operate — they have no box-office surface. They see only the *downstream* result: the walk-up figure as a distinct line on the certified statement (19.05.05) and, if their deal grants it, the drop (19.05.02). **Sharp exception (DT — multi-hyphenate)**: when the same human is *both* the venue Operator and the performing Musician tonight, they operate the window as Operator, and every sale they ring feeds the gate their own split is computed from. The persona boundary holds (the Musician *role* still has no window access), but the human does — see the multi-hyphenate edge case and 19.05.05 Q-02. |
| Producer | None | No box-office surface at all. The Producer is the platform's trust broker *in a studio*, not at a door. Nothing in this feature is visible to the Producer role. Listed explicitly so FE/BE spec writers do not render any walk-up affordance for it. |
| Operator | Full | Opens the window on a device that is also a scanner; sees remaining inventory live (with a freshness stamp when offline); selects a price level; takes payment (card on the rail, or cash into the float); the same device issues **and admits** in one action; reconciles the float at close. Selling and float-handling is a **distinct permission** from scanning (D-08) — not every door volunteer holds it. Every sale is attributed to the operating human by name. |
| Fan | Full | Turns up without a ticket, pays at the window, walks straight in. The simplest transaction in the domain and the one with the least tolerance for friction — a queue behind them, a band on stage. No account, no contact details, and no barcode are required to complete a cash sale (D-06): the ticket record *is* the proof of purchase, and a delivery channel (SMS/email/QR slip) is offered but optional. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

Walk-up sells from the **same manifest** as everything else (19.01.02) — it is not a separate pool (D-03). The window's price may differ from advance (day-of-show pricing is standard), which is a **price level**, not a separate product, and the all-in disclosed price is the charged price at a table exactly as online (D-04, 19.01.03).

Two payment realities coexist and both must work: **card** (which needs the rail, therefore a network) and **cash** (which needs neither). Cash is not legacy — it is the *offline-capable* payment method (DT-02), the fallback when the reader loses signal, which in these rooms is often, and the money small venues still run their busiest 30 minutes on.

Sale and admission are one action (DT-01): the buyer walks in immediately, admitted by the same device that just created the ticket, which therefore has perfect knowledge of it. The ticket still exists as a record — for the count, the certified statement, the fan's proof of purchase, and any later refund — but it is never *validated*, because it was admitted at birth.

Refund at the window is the mirror of the sale: **payment method dictates the refund path** (D-05) and, because sale *is* admission, a refund is also an *un-admission* (DT-03). Both must land before the close; a post-close refund is a settlement adjustment (19.05.05 D-06), not a statement edit.

The platform **reconciles** the float — counts it, computes expected, flags the discrepancy — but does **not take custody** of the cash (D-10): the money stays physically in the Operator's float. WeJammin is the ledger, not the till.

### Happy Path

1. Doors open. The Operator (holding the sell+float permission, D-08) opens the window on a device that is also a scanner. The device shows remaining inventory live.
2. A Fan arrives with no ticket. The Operator sees, e.g., `Remaining: 34` for the relevant tier.
3. The Operator selects the **day-of-show price level** (e.g. £15 all-in, disclosed as the charged figure) and takes payment — tap/insert on the card rail, or a note into the float.
4. The system issues the ticket record and, atomically: `paid += 1`, `remaining -= 1`, `admitted += 1` (issue-and-admit, DT-01). The sale is attributed to the operating human by name.
5. The same device admits the buyer directly — no barcode is generated for presentation, no scan of a ticket created three seconds ago on this same device.
6. The Fan walks in. A receipt/delivery channel is *offered* (SMS/email/QR slip) but not required; the ticket record is itself the proof of purchase (D-06).
7. At close (19.05.05), the window drains (rejects new sales, lets the in-flight one finish), the cash float is reconciled against expected cash takings, and the window's figures — walk-up total, split by card vs cash — join the certified statement as their own distinct lines (D-05 of 19.05.05: no aggregation).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Card reader has no signal** | Extremely common in these rooms. The rail is unreachable | Window drops to an explicit **cash-only** state, stated plainly; selling continues. If cash were not first-class the window would stop mid-queue (D-02) |
| **Whole device offline (no server)** | Sells against last-known `remaining`; sales queue locally for sync; issue-and-admit still works fully offline (DT-01 needs no network) | Remaining shown with a freshness stamp: `Remaining 34 (as of 19:00, offline)` (mirrors 19.05.01 D-02). Sales sync on reconnection or at close reconcile |
| **Sold at 20:30; the door scanner synced at 19:00** | On the common single-device setup this cannot arise — the selling device *is* the scanner and admits at birth (DT-01) | Nothing to see; the race does not exist. On a genuinely separate window+door, the sale must reach the scanner — the residual peer-gossip case (Q-01, 19.04.02 DT-03) |
| **Two windows selling concurrently, both offline, on the last few tickets** | Each sells against its own last-known `remaining`; combined they can exceed true stock → **oversell** | Not hard-preventable across two offline tills. Each is honored (D-09); the oversell surfaces as a named exception at close (19.05.05 step 3), never as a refusal at the door |
| **Offline oversell produces a paid ticket for stock that no longer exists** | A real person paid for a seat the room does not have | The paid admission is **never voided at the door** (D-09, DT-04). The oversell is resolved after the fact by refund or upgrade/comp, recorded as a close exception — turning a paying customer away is the one outcome the flow forbids |
| **Show sold out; walk-ups queueing** | Nothing to sell — unless holds remain unreleased (19.01.02) | Window shows `Sold out`; any remaining held inventory is surfaced so the Operator can release it. The held-inventory alert should have fired days ago on remaining-inventory, not on a date — at the window it is too late by design-acknowledgement, which is why the alert triggers earlier |
| **Sale in flight when the Operator initiates close** | The close must quiesce writers (`#CX-04`), but a transaction is mid-payment | Window enters a **draining** state: new sales are rejected with a plainly-stated *window is closing* message; the in-flight sale is allowed to complete; only then is the window quiesced and the count freezable |
| **Fan wants a refund at the window minutes after buying** | RESOLVED (D-05, DT-03). Payment method dictates the path: **cash → cash out of the float**; **card → reversal on the rail (19.06.01)**. The ticket returns to the manifest (`paid -= 1`, `remaining += 1`) *and* the admission is voided (`admitted -= 1`) or the room count over-states occupancy | Only the selling Operator's device, same session, **before close**. After close the window refuses the at-window refund as *after-close* and routes it to a settlement adjustment (19.05.05 D-06), not a statement edit |
| **Buyer wants a receipt** | RESOLVED (D-06). Card produces a rail receipt automatically; for cash the ticket record itself is the proof of purchase, retrievable by its reference code. A delivery channel (SMS/email/QR slip) is offered | Optional and zero-required-contact: an anonymous cash walk-up is valid and completes without capturing any buyer detail. Friction stays near zero because a queue is waiting |
| **Walk-up on an externally-ticketed show (19.07)** | RESOLVED (D-07). WeJammin cannot sell inventory it cannot see (D-03 same-manifest principle) | Pure external shows: **walk-up does not run on WeJammin** — the external provider owns the window. Only if a WeJammin-held allocation block exists does the window sell, and then **only from that allocation**. Selling against inventory WeJammin does not control is refused |
| **Unrecorded cash sale (staff skim)** | The Operator pockets a note without ringing the sale, or admits a friend without selling | Not preventable at the point of the note, but it is *detectable*: an unrung admission diverges `admitted` from `paid`, and unrung-but-taken cash makes the float reconcile **over** expected. The reconciliation records the discrepancy as its own line (D-05 of 19.05.05); it is surfaced, never netted away. Attribution (D-08) names whose window it was |
| **Card walk-up later charged back by the bank** | A card sale is disputed weeks after the night | The certified statement is a record of the close, not a live figure (19.05.05 D-06); the chargeback is a post-close settlement adjustment routed through 19.06 / domain 24, not a statement rewrite |
| **Person without the sell+float permission tries to open the window** | Door volunteers may scan but not necessarily sell or handle the float (D-08) | The window refuses to open, stating the operator lacks box-office (sell+float) permission. The venue-scoped role (domain 01/16) gates opening the window; scanning permission alone is insufficient |
| **The operating human is also the performing Musician tonight** | Multi-hyphenate (D-19): the same person operates the window as Operator while being a payee on the split the gate feeds (17) | The Musician *role* still has no window access; the human operates as Operator. Every sale is attributed to them (D-08), and the count they create feeds their own split — a conflict of interest named here and resolved at certification by counter-attestation (19.05.05 Q-02) |
| **Fee/price differs at the window** | Day-of-show is a distinct price level (D-03), all-in-disclosed | The displayed price is the charged price at the table exactly as online (D-04, 19.01.03) — no separate service fee revealed at payment |
| **Duplicate/counterfeit walk-up ticket presented at the door** | Cannot arise for walk-up: there is no barcode issued for later presentation (DT-01) | N/A — admit-at-birth removes the artifact a forger would need. (Advance tickets remain 19.04.01's concern) |

| Offline / Partial (card) | Card rail unreachable | **Cash-only** state, stated plainly; window keeps working |
| Offline / Partial (data) | No server; selling against a cached replica | Remaining stamped `(as of HH:MM, offline)`; sales queued for sync |
| Error | Sync push failed, or a payment failed on the rail | Specific cause + retry; a failed card attempt never issues a ticket or admits |

| Reconciling float | Window closing | Cash count vs expected; discrepancy shown as its own figure, not hidden |
| Closed for close | Window quiesced | Gates the certified statement (`#CX-04`) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **The selling device admits the buyer directly** — a walk-up buyer should never be issued a barcode and then asked to present it to a scanner | The `#CX-03` race (sale at 20:30 vs replica from 19:00); the physical reality that the window and the door are frequently the same table and the same person | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it dissolves the sub-domain's nastiest race rather than solving it. If the sale and the admission are one action on one device, there is no replica to be stale, no barcode to propagate, no window between issuance and scan for anything to go wrong in. The ticket still exists as a record (for the count, the statement, the fan's proof of purchase, and a later refund), but it is never *validated* — it is admitted at birth, by the device that created it, which has perfect knowledge of it. This is why walk-up sits in this sub-domain and not with the door (parent D-02): its problem is the count, not access control. The residual case — a genuinely separate window and door, physically apart, offline — still needs the sale to reach the scanner, and that is 19.04.02's peer-gossip question (Q-01), not a new problem. |
| DT-02 | Cash is a legacy path that can be deferred; card-only is acceptable for a modern platform | Product simplification instinct; every fintech says cash is dying | ❌ REJECTED, decisively — the kind of assumption that fails a product in the field rather than in review. Two independent reasons. **Connectivity**: the card reader needs a network, and this domain's whole premise (19.04.02) is that these rooms do not have one. A card-only window is a window that closes when the signal drops — at the exact moment a queue exists. Cash is not the legacy fallback, it is the *offline-capable* payment method, the same argument that makes offline scanning non-negotiable. **The market**: small venues run cash floats today, and a platform that cannot take a tenner at the door is a platform they cannot use for the busiest 30 minutes of their week. Deferring cash fails the Operator persona precisely where they said their business lives — *"much of their work is on a phone at a loading dock, not at a desk."* |
| DT-03 | A walk-up refund is only a payment reversal — return the money and be done | Instinct: a refund is a money operation | ❌ REJECTED — a walk-up refund is a payment reversal **and an un-admission**, because DT-01 fused the sale with the admission. If refunding only reverses the payment and returns the ticket to stock (`paid -= 1`, `remaining += 1`) but leaves `admitted` untouched, the room count now claims one more body inside than is true, and every downstream number — occupancy against capacity/curfew (16), the scanned figure on the statement (19.05.05), the split (17) — inherits the error. So the refund must also decrement `admitted`. And the path is dictated by how they paid (D-05): you cannot reverse cash on a rail that never saw it, nor hand a note back for a card sale without creating a float shortfall. This is the whole reason walk-up refunds cannot be delegated wholesale to the generic refund rail (19.06.01) — the cash leg and the un-admission are box-office-specific. |
| DT-04 | An offline oversell should be prevented by refusing the sale the moment local `remaining` hits zero | Correctness instinct: never sell stock you do not have | ❌ REJECTED — you cannot prevent an oversell across two offline tills that each believe they hold the last ticket, and refusing a sale on a *stale* zero would deny real inventory to a paying customer standing in the rain. The correct stance inverts the priority: **never turn away someone who paid, never void a paid admission at the door** (D-09). Honor every walk-up sale, detect the oversell at reconcile, and resolve it after the fact by refund or upgrade/comp as a named close exception (19.05.05). The failure the platform must avoid is not "we sold one too many" — that is recoverable arithmetic; it is "a paying fan was refused entry with a valid receipt," which is the exact unacceptable-and-predictable outcome `#CX-03` warns against. Correctness here means honoring the human, not the counter. |
| DT-05 | The platform should take **custody** of the cash float to fully close the provenance loop | Thesis instinct — capture at source implies owning the money at source | ❌ REJECTED, for now — and the distinction matters. Taking custody of physical cash turns WeJammin into a money transmitter: KYC/AML on the float, state-by-state or per-jurisdiction licensing, and liability for notes it never physically touches. The provenance win this domain actually needs is the **record** of the float — count, expected, discrepancy, attribution — not the **custody** of it. WeJammin is the ledger, not the till (D-10): it makes the cash *legible and attested* (the thing that does not exist today) without ever holding it. Whether the platform ever moves into genuine cash settlement/custody is a real product-and-regulatory decision, not an implementation detail — it is Q-02, owned by the user and `/create-prd-security`, and DT-05 records why the default is deliberately ledger-only. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The selling device admits the buyer directly; walk-up tickets are admitted at birth, never scanned | Dissolves the sale-vs-stale-replica race instead of solving it (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Cash is a first-class payment method, not a legacy fallback | It is the only offline-capable payment method, and these rooms are offline (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Walk-up sells from the same manifest; day-of-show pricing is a price level, not a separate pool | A separate pool is a second manifest and a second chance to oversell |

| D-06 | Every walk-up sale produces a ticket record that is itself the proof of purchase; a delivery channel is offered but optional, and no buyer contact is required to complete a cash sale | Anonymous cash walk-up is valid; friction must stay near zero with a queue waiting; card additionally yields a rail receipt |
| D-07 | Walk-up does not run on purely externally-ticketed shows; where a WeJammin-held allocation exists, the window sells only from that allocation | WeJammin cannot sell inventory it cannot see (D-03 same-manifest principle) |

| D-09 | A paid walk-up admission is never voided at the door; an offline oversell is honored and resolved as a named close exception (refund/upgrade), never by refusing entry | Turning away a paying fan with a valid receipt is the one forbidden outcome (DT-04) |
| D-10 | The platform reconciles the float (count, expected, discrepancy, attribution) but does not take custody of the cash — it is the ledger, not the till | Custody would make WeJammin a money transmitter; the provenance win is the record, not the money (DT-05). Custody itself is Q-02 |
| D-11 | A refund is also an un-admission — decrementing `admitted`, not only `paid`/`remaining` | Sale is admission (DT-01); leaving `admitted` stale over-states occupancy for capacity, the statement, and the split (DT-03) |
| D-12 | Closing while a sale is in flight puts the window in a **draining** state — new sales rejected, the in-flight one allowed to finish — before the count is quiesced | Quiescing mid-transaction would either drop a real sale or freeze a number that is about to change (`#CX-04`) |
