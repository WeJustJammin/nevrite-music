# Feature: Drop Reports & Counterparty Distribution

> **Number**: 19.05.02
> **Parent**: [Box Office Counts, Drops & Day-of-Show](./19.05-box-office-counts-drops-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The **drop** is live music's oldest recurring report: a periodic statement of how a show is selling, sent to the parties who have money on it. Agents chase them, promoters send them late, artists never see them at all. They arrive as an email with numbers typed by hand, or a screenshot of a dashboard, or a text message saying "we're at about 200".

This feature makes the drop an artifact instead of a message: an immutable snapshot of the count (19.05.01), scoped to what each party's deal entitles them to see, delivered on a cadence, and quotable weeks later.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | **Receives** the drop — for most artists this is their only visibility into how their own show is selling, and today it usually does not reach them at all (it reaches their agent). Sees what their deal grants: typically sold, remaining, and pacing (19.05.03). Cannot see the venue's costs, other shows, or the fee split unless the deal says so. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Configures the cadence and recipients, sees every drop that has gone out and to whom, and can send an ad-hoc drop. Sees the unscoped count themselves. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this. It is a commercial document between the parties. |

## Behavior

A drop is a **snapshot**: a frozen copy of the count at a moment, with a scope applied per recipient. It is generated on a cadence (daily during on-sale, weekly during the long tail, daily again in show week — this shape is the industry's, not an invention), delivered to each party through the notification cross-cut, and retained.

Immutability is the property that matters. A drop is used to make decisions and then cited to defend them: "on the Tuesday drop we were at 180, that's why we released the holds". If the Tuesday drop re-renders from live data, that sentence becomes unfalsifiable and the drop becomes a dashboard with a date on it.

### Happy Path

1. Operator configures the drop for a show: cadence, recipients, and each recipient's scope (defaulted from the domain 17 deal where available).
2. Cadence fires. System takes a consistent snapshot of the count (`#CX-01`).
3. Snapshot is scoped per recipient and rendered — sold, remaining, movement since the last drop, pacing (19.05.03).
4. Drop is delivered to each party and retained immutably.
5. Musician reads it on their phone; it is a small number of numbers, not a dashboard.
6. Weeks later, any party can open the Tuesday drop and see exactly what it said on Tuesday.
7. On show night, the certified statement (19.05.05) supersedes the drop entirely.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Snapshot taken mid-write | Internally inconsistent numbers — paid=142 with a gross implying 141 | Snapshot must be consistent; one bad drop destroys trust in every number the platform ever shows. See `#CX-01` Q5 |
| Recipient's scope not defined by the deal | Default is unclear — over-share leaks the venue's business, under-share blinds the artist | Defaults must be conservative and stated; see Q-01 |
| Delivery fails (bad email, notification bounces) | The snapshot exists; only the delivery failed | Re-deliverable. A missed drop must never mean a missing fact |
| Show is externally ticketed (19.07) | Numbers are ingested, delayed, possibly disputed | Drop must state the source and freshness of each number (19.05.01 D-02) — a drop that presents an ingested count as authoritative is lying with confidence |
| Drop cadence continues after the show | Meaningless; the statement supersedes it | Cadence ends at close |
| Party wants a drop right now | Common — an agent asking mid-week | Ad-hoc drop; it is still a snapshot and still immutable |
| Numbers move backwards between drops (refunds) | Alarming to a reader who does not expect it | Movement is shown explicitly, including negatives, with the reason where known |
| Artist's agent should receive it, not the artist | The real-world routing, and the model has no Agent | See Q-03 and parent Q-03 |
| Recipient's scope changes mid-cadence (deal renegotiated) | New scope applies to future drops only; drops already delivered keep the scope they were sent under | Old drops render exactly as they were quoted; a scope change never rewrites history — the drop is immutable in content *and* scope. `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` for the exact scope-versioning contract |
| Recipient removed before the next cadence fires | They stop receiving; drops they already hold are unaffected | Removal is forward-only. A recipient who saw the Tuesday drop still legitimately holds it |
| Show is postponed / rescheduled | Cadence is meaningless against a moving on-sale; the last drop may now misrepresent a paused sale | Cadence pauses on postponement and resumes with the rescheduled on-sale; a drop must carry the show status so a paused number is never read as a live one |
| First drop of a show (no prior snapshot to diff against) | Movement column has no baseline | Movement shows "first drop — no prior comparison", not a fabricated zero-delta |
| Two recipients are the same party via two channels (agent email + artist app) | Same scope, delivered twice | De-duplicate on party-scope, not on channel; the fact delivered is one drop, however many places it lands |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not configured | Show has no drop set up | Operator sees a prompt with the deal's reporting terms if available |
| Scheduled | Cadence configured | Next drop time; recipients and their scopes |
| Generated | Cadence fired, snapshot taken | Immutable drop exists |
| Delivered | Sent to all recipients successfully | Delivery status per recipient — all green |
| Partially delivered | Some recipients delivered, at least one failed | Operator sees which parties received it and which did not; the snapshot is complete, delivery is not |
| Delivery failed | Bounce or channel failure | Operator sees the failure; drop is re-deliverable — the fact exists even when the delivery did not |
| Paused | Show postponed / on hold | Cadence suspended; last drop is flagged stale until the sale resumes |
| Superseded | Show closed, statement certified | Drops become history; the statement (19.05.05) is the artifact |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A drop must be an **immutable snapshot**, not a live view with a timestamp | Problem statement — *"the facts are reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable"*; the thesis (D-18) that provenance means capturing the fact when it is true | ✅ CONFIRMED — and the reason is that a drop is *evidence*, not information. Every drop is a fact that a decision was made from: holds released, marketing spent, a second night added, a support act booked. When those decisions are questioned — and on a show that loses money they are always questioned — the drop is the artifact that says what was known and when. A live view with a date on it cannot do that job; it can only say what is true now, which is the one thing nobody is arguing about. This is the platform's whole thesis applied to a report: capture the fact at the moment it is true, or reconstruct it later from a dashboard that has moved on. |
| DT-02 | Drops should be replaced by a live dashboard — the parties can look whenever they want, and a periodic report is a legacy of fax machines | Modernisation instinct; a dashboard is strictly more information | ❌ REJECTED, and the rejection matters for adoption. Three reasons. **Pull vs push**: a dashboard requires the artist to remember to look, and the Musician persona is not sitting at a desk — they are in a van. A drop arrives. **Attention economics**: the cadence *is* the product. A drop is a small number of numbers at a moment the industry already expects them; a dashboard is an infinite surface that gets opened once in week one and never again. **Evidence**: per DT-01, the dashboard cannot be cited. The correct design is a dashboard for the Operator (who is at the desk, and needs 19.05.01 live) **and** drops for everyone else. Replacing the drop with a dashboard would be a modernisation that made the artist's visibility worse, which is the opposite of the platform's purpose. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A drop is an immutable snapshot, retained and quotable | It is evidence of what was known when a decision was made, not information about now (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Drops (push) coexist with the Operator's live count (pull) — neither replaces the other | The Operator is at a desk; the Musician is in a van (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Each recipient's scope is a deal term, defaulted conservatively | Over-share leaks the venue's business; under-share blinds the artist |
| D-04 | A drop states the source and freshness of every number | An ingested count presented as authoritative is a confident lie |
| D-05 | Movement between drops is shown explicitly, including negatives | Numbers going backwards without explanation destroys trust faster than bad numbers |
| D-06 | Scope and recipient changes are forward-only; already-delivered drops are never rewritten | Immutability (D-01) applies to a drop's *scope* as well as its numbers — a renegotiated deal or removed recipient changes future drops, never the ones a party already holds and may have decided from |
