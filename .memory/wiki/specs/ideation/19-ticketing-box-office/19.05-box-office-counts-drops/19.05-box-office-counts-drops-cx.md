## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Live Count ↔ Drop Reports

**Relationship**: The live count is current truth and changes constantly. A drop is a *snapshot* of it, taken on a cadence, distributed to counterparties, and used to make decisions — release the holds, add a second night, start spending on marketing. Because those decisions are made from the drop and defended by reference to it later, the drop must be immutable: the Tuesday drop must say tomorrow what it said on Tuesday.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: owns the count, configures the drop cadence and its recipients.
- **Musician**: receives the drop; it is frequently the only visibility their deal grants them into the room's performance.
- **Producer**: not affected — no box-office surface.
- **Fan**: never sees any of this.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The count is owned by the Operator's box office. A drop is a derived, frozen copy — no write-back, no conflict.
2. **Trigger chain**: Cadence fires → snapshot taken → distributed. If distribution fails, the snapshot still exists and must be re-deliverable; a missed drop is a support issue, not a lost fact.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the substance of 19.05.02 — what a party may see in a drop is a deal term, not a UI preference.
4. **Notification fan-out**: The drop *is* the notification.
5. **State transition conflict**: A snapshot taken mid-write must be internally consistent. Paid=142 with a gross that implies 141 is a report that destroys trust in every other number on the page.

### CX-02: Live Count ↔ Pacing

**Relationship**: Pacing reads the count over time and compares it to a trajectory. Its value is entirely in the action it triggers: an on-sale that is 40% behind at four weeks out is recoverable — release the artist hold to sale, open a discount window, spend on promo. The same fact at four days out is just a description of a bad night.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: acts on it — the actions available (hold release, price, promo) are theirs.
- **Musician**: sees it for their show; their action is different (post about it, ask for the second night to be cancelled).
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — pacing is read-only over the count.
2. **Trigger chain**: Pacing threshold → alert → action on the manifest (19.01.02) or the ladder (19.02.02). The chain must actually connect; an alert that requires the Operator to go and find the hold-release screen is an alert that gets dismissed.
3. **Permission intersection**: The alert's recipient must be someone who can act. Alerting a Musician about a manifest they cannot touch is noise.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Via the drop (CX-06) rather than a separate channel.
5. **State transition conflict**: None.

### CX-03: Walk-Up ↔ Live Count

**Relationship**: The window sells at 20:30. The scanners downloaded their replicas at 19:00 (19.04.02). The ticket that was just sold does not exist on any scanner, so the person who bought it 30 seconds ago cannot be scanned in. This is the seam where the sub-domain's placement decision (parent D-02 — walk-up belongs with counts, not with the door) has a real cost, and the cost must be paid explicitly.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: sells at the window and scans at the door, often at the same table, sometimes as the same person.
- **Fan**: buys a ticket and is refused entry with it, ten metres away, thirty seconds later. Unacceptable and entirely predictable.
- **Musician** / **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The manifest. Walk-up writes to it; scanner replicas read a frozen copy of it. On the counters themselves there is **no** conflict: the window writes `paid` and its own `admitted`, the door writes `scanned`, and the canonical all-bodies number `admissions_total` is derived from both (19.05.01 D-09) rather than contended by both.
2. **Trigger chain**: Sale → count mutation → the scanners must learn. Either the walk-up device *is* a scanner (the common physical reality), or the sale must reach the replicas, or walk-up tickets need a validation path that does not depend on the replica. See 19.05.04 DT-01.
3. **Permission intersection**: No.
4. **Notification fan-out**: No.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes, and it is the sharp one — a sale and a scan of the same ticket seconds apart, across two devices, with no network.

### CX-05: Live Count ↔ Close

**Relationship**: The transition from *observation* to *attested fact*. Before the close, the count is a thing the system believes; after it, it is a thing the parties have agreed and signed, and the money is computed from it. Every writer to the count — sales, comps (19.03.02), door adds (19.03.03), walk-up, scans, refunds — is a party to this transition and must be quiesced or reconciled before it can complete.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: certifies. Note the conflict of interest — they certify the number that determines what they pay (parent Q-02).
- **Musician**: the counterparty. Counter-attestation is the provenance-shaped answer.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: This is the resolution of every conflict in the sub-domain — the close is where they all have to be settled.
2. **Trigger chain**: Close → quiesce writers → reconcile devices (19.04.02 D-04) → freeze → certify → hand to settlement (17).
3. **Permission intersection**: Certification authority vs box-office authority. See parent Q-02.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Certification notifies every party to the settlement.
5. **State transition conflict**: The whole feature. See 19.05.05.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 19.05.02 Drop Reports | 19.05.04 Walk-Up | Considered because both are "box office activity". Rejected: drops run on a cadence over weeks of advance sales and are consumed by absent parties making forward decisions; walk-up happens once, on the night, in front of the person. They meet only inside the count (CX-01, CX-03), never with each other. The final drop and the certified statement are also different objects — the statement (19.05.05) supersedes the drop entirely on the night. |
| R-02 | 19.05.03 Pacing | 19.05.05 Close | Tempting — both look like "reporting on the show" — but they sit on opposite sides of the show's existence. Pacing is only useful while the outcome can still be changed; the close is the record of what the outcome was. A pacing read after the close is a description of history with no action attached, which is domain 22's business (post-hoc analysis), not this sub-domain's. |
| R-03 | 19.05.01 Live Count | 19.05.04 Walk-Up — as one feature | Rejected the *merge*, not the pair (CX-03 confirms the interaction). It is tempting to model walk-up as "the count, but you can also add to it". Rejected because walk-up is a full commerce flow with a money rail, a fee structure, a receipt, a refund path and a cash-handling reality that the count has nothing to do with. Merging would put a payment terminal inside a reporting feature. |
