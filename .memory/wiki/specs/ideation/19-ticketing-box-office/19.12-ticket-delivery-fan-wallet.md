# Feature: Ticket Delivery & Fan Ticket Wallet

> **Number**: 19.12
> **Parent**: [Ticketing & Box Office](./ticketing-box-office-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

How the fan actually gets the ticket, and where it lives afterwards. Email, wallet pass, in-app, or a printed page — and then a single place showing everything they hold: what they bought, for when, where, and what changed since.

**Deep Think addition** — the sweep's candidate list did not include it, which is a striking omission: it is the fan's *entire* relationship with this domain. Every other feature in 19 is machinery the Fan never sees. This one is the product, from their side.

**And it turns out to be load-bearing.** 19.06.03 DT-02 established that a self-updating wallet pass is the only channel that reliably reaches a fan when a show reschedules — because notifications go to the payer, land in promotions, or arrive at a dead address, while the pass is in the pocket of whoever is actually coming. That reframes this feature: it is not "how the fan gets the ticket", it is **how the fan stays correct about the show**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. How a fan stores their ticket is not the artist's concern. A Musician who is *also* a Fan (bought a ticket, or holds a comp/+1 for a show they are playing) sees the wallet through the Fan lens, on the same identity — the two lenses never merge (see the multi-hyphenate edge case). |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface — except via 19.11, whose RSVPs deliver through this feature. |
| Operator | Config + Reports | **Config**: chooses which delivery channels a show offers (wallet / in-app / email / print), and whether print is available for fans with no smartphone. **Reports**: sees per-ticket delivery status and a delivery-failure list — a bounced ticket is a person who will arrive with nothing. Sees holder identity only to the degree 19.10 consent permits; a failure row shows the order and contact-channel state, not PII the fan withheld. |
| Fan | Full | **The primary user, and the only one.** Receives the ticket, holds it, sees it update when the show moves, transfers it (19.09.02), lists it (19.09.03), refunds it (19.06.01), and presents it at a door in a dark room with 4% battery. The wallet is the Fan's whole view of domain 19. |

## Behavior

Delivery runs on **every issuance path** — a paid sale, a comp (19.03.02), a walk-up, a VIP package (19.08), an RSVP (19.11). One mechanism, no special cases; a comp that arrived differently from a ticket would be a comp that scans differently (D-04).

| Channel | Property |
|---|---|
| **Wallet pass** (Apple/Google) | Self-updating, offline-readable, survives a dead inbox. The strongest channel |
| **In-app / web** | Full view; needs signal |
| **Email** | Universal, unreliable — promotions folder, dead addresses, forwarded screenshots |
| **Print** | Works with no phone at all; static, screenshot-equivalent |

The **wallet** (the fan's view of everything they hold) shows the ticket, the show, the door time, the venue, the age restriction (19.04.04 D-02), any package components (19.08.03 D-02), and — critically — the *current* truth about all of it.

**The pass is a projection, not a snapshot.** Its rendered content (date, venue, door time, validity) is derived from live ticket + show state at render/refresh time, never frozen at issuance. This is the mechanism behind D-02: a value that changed after issuance (a reschedule, a room swap, a refund) is reflected the next time the pass renders or is pulled, without the fan doing anything. It is also what makes update ordering safe (see the concurrency edge cases) — there is no stale copy to go wrong, only a projection that re-derives.

**Delivery-failure handling is concrete.** Email is dispatched immediately on issuance; a hard bounce or repeated soft failure retries up to **3 times over ~24h**, after which the ticket enters **Delivery Failed** and the Operator's delivery dashboard flags it. Wallet-pass provisioning and in-app delivery do not "bounce" — they are pull surfaces — so a fan who added the pass is never in Delivery Failed for that channel.

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys. The ticket is delivered immediately through every channel the show offers.
2. Fan adds the wallet pass; it is now on their phone, offline, self-updating.
3. Two months pass. The fan forgets everything about this purchase.
4. The show reschedules (19.06.03). An email goes to the promotions folder, unread. **The pass updates itself** — the date on their phone is now correct.
5. On the night, the fan opens their wallet, sees the door time and the venue, and goes.
6. At the door, the pass renders offline in a basement with no signal (19.04.02).
7. Scan (19.04.01). In.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees / Resolution |
|----------|-------------|------------------------|
| Email bounces | The fan has nothing and does not know | Retried 3× / ~24h → ticket enters **Delivery Failed**; Operator dashboard flags it; name lookup at the door (19.04.01 D-01) is the safety net that makes this survivable |
| Dead phone at the door | Universal | Name lookup (19.04.01 D-01). Print if offered. This is why lookup is first-class |
| No signal at the door | The pass must render offline | Wallet passes do; a web view does not. The single strongest argument for the pass in these venues (19.04.02) |
| Screenshot passed to a friend | Static artifacts are copyable; both present it | First scan wins; second scan returns a **double-scan refusal with specifics** (19.04.03) — same time/gate/device shown, not a silent reject |
| **Concurrent wallet adds** — fan taps Add-to-Apple-Wallet and Add-to-Google-Wallet, and opens in-app, near-simultaneously across two devices | Naive design mints multiple pass serials for one ticket → several passes that all scan valid | Pass serial is **derived deterministically from the ticket ID per platform** (one Apple serial + one Google serial per ticket); concurrent adds are **idempotent** and resolve to the same serial. One ticket = one scannable identity, regardless of how many devices hold the projection |
| **Race: reschedule vs transfer at the same instant** — show moves (19.06.03) while the fan transfers the ticket (19.09.02) | Ordering could push a stale date to the wrong holder, or update a pass that just voided | Because the pass is a **projection re-derived from live state at render time**, ordering cannot produce a stale pass: if transfer commits first, the sender's pass renders VOID and the recipient's renders the new date; if the update pushes first, the recipient's freshly-issued pass still re-derives the already-updated date on first render. No snapshot to desync |
| **Rapid transfer chain A→B→C** before B's pass provisions | Intermediate holder's pass may never render | Only the **current holder** (resolved at push/render time) has a valid pass; B's pass, if it renders at all, renders already-void. The chain is recorded (19.09.02) |
| Rotating token, no signal | Cannot rotate; static fallback required | 19.09.02 DT-03 / D-04 — the anti-fraud control the venue's connectivity defeats; static fallback is mandatory, and it degrades to a screenshot-equivalent at exactly the venues in scope |
| Show reschedules; notification missed | The fan would turn up on the wrong night | **The pass is the fix** (19.06.03 DT-02, D-03). This is the feature's most valuable behaviour |
| Ticket transferred (19.09.02) | The pass must reach the new holder and leave the sender | Sender's pass voids; recipient's issues via a single-use claim link (DT-04) |
| **Recipient has no WeJammin account** | A transfer or shared ticket lands on someone who has never signed up | A **signed, single-use claim link** provisions the wallet pass directly — no account required (DT-04, D-08). Account creation is offered, never forced at the door-critical moment |
| Fan has no smartphone | Real, especially for older audiences and all-ages shows | Print must remain available (D-03). See DT-02 |
| Fan bought 6 months ago and has no memory of it | Extremely common | The wallet is the answer — one place, everything they hold |
| Venue changed (a room swap) | Same class as a date change | The pass re-derives and updates; treated as material only if it invalidates a sold seat (19.01.04 D-03) |
| Package with a partially-specified component (19.08) — e.g. ticket confirmed, meet-and-greet time TBA | One purchase, several promises, one not yet pinned | **Partial view**: the pass shows the ticket plus the M&G component marked "time to be announced", not a hidden component and not a blocked pass. One package view, not four notifications (19.08.03 D-02) |
| **Void pass sitting in a wallet** — refunded/cancelled/transferred ticket | A void ticket that still looks valid is a fan turned away at a door | The pass front updates to **VOID / REFUNDED / TRANSFERRED** and its barcode is invalidated so a scan returns void at 19.04.01, not a silent valid-looking pass (D-06) |
| **Empty wallet** — account holds zero tickets | Fan opens the wallet with nothing in it | Empty state with a **"Discover shows"** CTA (domains 20/21) — an empty wallet is a starting point, not an error |
| **Permission probe** — someone opens an add-to-wallet / pass link that is not theirs (guessed or leaked serial) | Naive design would render a stranger's ticket | The provisioning link is **single-use and bound to a signed claim token or the recipient identity**; an unauthorized request returns **403 `{ error, code: "PASS_NOT_YOURS" }`** and renders nothing. Serials are not sequential/guessable |
| **Malicious input — tampered static artifact** — a screenshot or PDF whose *printed date/barcode* was edited | Fraud attempt at the door | The scannable barcode is **signed** (crypto is not-product → architecture); a tampered barcode fails validation at 19.04.01 (returns invalid, not admitted). A tampered *visual* is irrelevant — the door scans the token, never the printed date |
| **Malicious input — malformed provisioning deep link** | Crafted/oversized/garbage add-to-wallet URL | Rejected before provisioning; returns **400 `{ error, code: "INVALID_PASS_LINK" }`**; no pass issued, nothing logged with PII |
| Pass-update push (APNs/FCM) fails or is throttled | The push channel is unreliable | The pass **reconciles on next open via pull** from the pass web service; if online it fetches latest, if offline it shows last-synced content plus a **"last updated {timestamp}"** so a stale date is at least detectable (Partial state) |
| **Multi-hyphenate holding two roles at once** — the Operator of a venue who has *also* bought a ticket to a different show (or holds a comp/+1 to a show at their own venue) | Risk of conflating the Operator's delivery-failure dashboard with their personal wallet | The two are **distinct surfaces on one identity**: the Fan-lens wallet shows tickets they hold; the Operator-lens dashboard shows delivery status for shows they run. Buying a ticket to their own venue's show delivers a normal Fan ticket and never appears in their Operator config. Same person, two lenses, no bleed |
| Duplicate delivery to one address (two tickets, one email) | Fan orders 2 → both must arrive distinctly | Each ticket is its own pass with its own serial; one email may carry both, but they add as **two** wallet passes, not one merged pass |

### States

**Wallet-view states** (the fan's list of everything they hold):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Wallet opened; tickets being fetched | Skeleton ticket cards |
| Empty | Account holds zero tickets | Empty state + "Discover shows" CTA (20/21) — not an error |
| Populated | One or more tickets held | Ticket cards with add-to-wallet offered prominently, each showing the *current* show truth |
| Error | Wallet fetch failed (backend/network) | Retry affordance; a pass already in Apple/Google Wallet still renders offline **independently** of this surface |
| Partial | Some ticket data incomplete (package component TBA) **or** a pass is showing last-synced state offline | Ticket rendered with a missing-data / "time to be announced" indicator, or a "last updated {timestamp}" stamp |

**Per-ticket lifecycle states** (one ticket's journey):

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Issuing | Purchase / comp / RSVP created | Immediate confirmation |
| Delivered | Sent on every offered channel | Ticket in hand; add-to-wallet offered prominently |
| In wallet | Pass added | Offline, self-updating, on the lock screen near the venue |
| Delivery failed | Email bounced after 3 retries / ~24h | Operator sees it; fan may not know — the dangerous state; name lookup is the net |
| Updated | Show changed (date, venue, time) | Pass re-derives and changes itself; the fan need do nothing |
| Transferred out | Sent to someone else (19.09.02) | Sender's pass voids; recipient claims via single-use link |
| Listed | In the face-value exchange (19.09.03) | Pending; delistable |
| Scanned | Admitted (19.04.01) | Pass shows used |
| Void | Refunded / cancelled / transferred | Pass front reads VOID/REFUNDED/TRANSFERRED; barcode invalidated (D-06) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The wallet pass is a **convenience channel** among several, and email is the real delivery mechanism | The default assumption — email is universal, the pass is a nice extra. It is also the framing under which the sweep omitted this feature entirely | ❌ REJECTED, decisively, and the rejection is what promotes this feature from plumbing to product. The pass has three properties no other channel has, and each maps onto a failure this domain has already established as real. It **self-updates**, which is the only reliable answer to a reschedule reaching a fan whose email is dead or whose ticket was transferred (19.06.03 DT-02). It **renders offline**, which matters because 19.04.02 establishes that these venues have no signal — a web view is useless in a basement. And it **lives on the lock screen near the venue**, so the fan does not have to find anything. Email does none of this: it is a delivery event that ages instantly and is wrong the moment anything changes. So the correct hierarchy is pass-first, everything else as fallback — and the whole feature's purpose is not delivery at all but *keeping the fan correct about a show they bought six months ago and forgot*. |
| DT-02 | Print can be dropped — it is 2026, everyone has a phone, and print is the screenshot vector | Modernisation instinct, plus a real security argument: a printed ticket is trivially copyable | ❌ REJECTED — on the same reasoning that kept cash at the window (19.05.04 DT-02), which is not a coincidence but the domain's actual character asserting itself twice. Both are the **no-technology fallback**, and both are needed precisely because this domain's venues are the ones where technology fails: a dead phone, a cracked screen, a fan with no smartphone at an all-ages show, a basement with no signal. Dropping print does not eliminate the copyable artifact either — the screenshot already exists and is far more common than a printout. What dropping print eliminates is the fan who cannot get in. The double-scan signal (19.04.03 D-01) is the actual control against duplication, and it works identically on paper and on a screenshot, which means print costs nothing in security and buys real accessibility. |
| DT-03 | The lock-screen pass, being the platform's most valuable fan surface, should carry marketing — a "see you tonight" nudge, a merch link, an upsell | It is genuinely the highest-value real estate the platform owns over a fan, and the pull to monetise it is strong (this was Q-03, deferred to Step 5) | ❌ REJECTED as a promotional surface, ✅ CONFIRMED for *logistics*. The distinction is the whole answer. A merch link or a marketing nudge trains the fan to associate the pass with spam and **delete it** — and deleting the pass destroys the one reliable channel for a reschedule (19.06.03 DT-02), breaking D-01 and D-02 in exchange for a click-through. The cost is wildly asymmetric: a few impressions bought against the loss of the correctness guarantee that is the feature's entire reason to exist. So the pass carries **only show-correctness content plus day-of logistics** on its back fields — door time, venue address, entry/ID instructions, age restriction (19.04.04 D-02), package components (19.08.03) — because those *serve* D-02, they are not marketing. Anything whose purpose is a click, a sale, or a nudge is out. If the platform wants a "see you tonight" moment, that belongs in the notification/feed surfaces (domain 20/21), not on the artifact whose value is that the fan keeps it. |
| DT-04 | A wallet pass requires a WeJammin account behind it, so a transferred ticket whose recipient has no account cannot be delivered until they sign up | The natural assumption that the wallet is "a view of a fan's account" (Cross-Cut to domain 01) — implying login is a precondition (this was Q-04, deferred to Step 5) | ❌ REJECTED — the pass is **identity-light**: the ticket *is* the pass, and the pass can live in an Apple/Google Wallet with no WeJammin login behind it. Forcing account creation at the moment a friend passes you a ticket is exactly the friction that strands a recipient at the door, and it contradicts the domain's own posture that transfer should be two taps with no ceremony (19.09.02 D-03). The mechanism: transfer (19.09.02, reversible-until-claimed, D-06) sends a **signed, single-use claim link** over the channel the sender used (email / SMS / shareable link). Opening it provisions the wallet pass directly — Add-to-Apple/Google-Wallet — without a signup wall. Account creation is *offered* (it unlocks the full wallet, transfer, and updates-if-your-email-changes), never *required*. The claim link is single-use and expires; an unclaimed transfer remains reversible by the sender. This makes the Cross-Cut to domain 01 precise: the *in-app wallet view* is a view of an account, but an *individual pass* is not — which is what lets a ticket reach someone the platform has never met. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Wallet pass first; every other channel is a fallback | It is the only channel that self-updates, renders offline and is on the lock screen — each mapping to a failure this domain has proved real (DT-01) |
| D-02 | This feature's purpose is keeping the fan **correct** about the show, not delivering a barcode once | Delivery is an event; correctness is a duration, and the show changes during it |
| D-03 | Print remains available | Same reasoning as cash at the window: it is the no-technology fallback in venues where technology fails, and it costs nothing in security (DT-02) |
| D-04 | One delivery mechanism for every issuance path — paid, comp, walk-up, package, RSVP | A comp that arrives differently is a comp that behaves differently downstream |
| D-05 | Delivery failures are surfaced to the Operator; email retries 3× over ~24h before a ticket enters Delivery Failed | A bounced ticket is a person who will arrive with nothing, and only the Operator can act before that happens |
| D-06 | A void pass says so — front reads VOID/REFUNDED/TRANSFERRED and the barcode is invalidated | A refunded ticket sitting in a wallet looking valid is a fan turned away at a door |
| D-07 | The pass carries only show-correctness content and day-of logistics; no marketing, upsells, or merch links | A promotional pass gets deleted, and deleting the pass destroys the reschedule channel that is the feature's reason to exist (DT-03, resolves Q-03) |
| D-08 | A pass is identity-light: a transferred ticket reaches an account-less recipient via a signed single-use claim link; account creation is offered, never required | Forcing signup at the door-critical moment strands the recipient and contradicts two-tap transfer (DT-04, resolves Q-04) |
| D-09 | The pass is a projection re-derived from live ticket+show state, never a snapshot frozen at issuance | It is the mechanism behind self-update (D-02) and it makes concurrent update/transfer ordering safe — there is no stale copy to desync |
| D-10 | One ticket resolves to one scannable identity per platform (deterministic serial); concurrent adds are idempotent | Otherwise multiple wallet adds mint multiple valid passes for one seat |
