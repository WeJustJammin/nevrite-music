## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Allocation & Submission ↔ Comp Issuance

**Relationship**: An allocation is a party's hold-backed comp budget — a number, agreed in advance, usually as a deal term, with matching manifest units reserved before it opens. Submission spends it: each name submitted issues a real comp ticket by moving a bound unit from `held` to `comp`. The comp never changes `paid`. That asymmetry is the whole point: settlement's door split reads the paid count, so every comp issued is money the artist chose to spend on a guest rather than take home.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: spends their own allocation; sees their budget, their spend and their remaining headroom. Does not see other parties' budgets.
- **Operator**: sets the budgets (within the deal), sees every party's spend, and owns the manifest the comps come from.
- **Producer**: not affected — no box-office surface.
- **Fan**: a comped guest is a Fan who was given a ticket; they receive it exactly like a bought one (19.12) and never see the allocation machinery.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The manifest (19.01.02) is the shared entity. Allocation creation or a raise atomically writes matching units into the party's hold; submitted names are owned by the granted party. Two owners, one resource — but no merge conflict: public sales write `remaining` and guest-list issuance writes its already-bound `held` units.
2. **Trigger chain**: Allocation/hold creation → submission → comp issuance (`held → comp`) → live count update (19.05.01). A capacity race can fail allocation creation before it promises any budget; once issued, a comp does not wait behind a public sale.
3. **Permission intersection**: Holding an allocation grants the right to name guests **from its reserved hold**, not an unbounded claim on the room. Public sell-out leaves the party's held units intact until the allocation deadline; after release, they return to sale.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Submission notifies the guest (they get a ticket). Exhausted allocation notifies the party. Approaching a deadline notifies the party — see 19.03.01 DT-01.
5. **State transition conflict**: Allocation/hold creation and a public sale can race for the last unreserved unit; the paid sale wins by default, so the allocation creation or raise fails before promising capacity. Once a hold-backed allocation exists, its comp issuance cannot race a public sale. Resolved by 19.03.01 D-11.

### CX-02: Door-Time Changes ↔ Allocation & Submission

**Relationship**: At the door, allocation discipline collapses. The deadline has passed, the list is printed or loaded onto a scanner, and someone is standing in front of the Operator saying they are with the band. Door-time additions spend allocation retroactively — or spend allocation that does not exist, which is the same conversation with more volume.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: makes the call, under time pressure, with a queue forming behind the person.
- **Musician**: is frequently the one asking, and is frequently not physically present to ask.
- **Fan**: the guest — experiences it as being let in or humiliated at the door.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The allocation is the contended resource and it is being spent by someone other than its owner, without the owner present.
2. **Trigger chain**: Door add → over-allocation → the overage lands on *someone's* settlement. Who absorbs it is the entire question, and answering it at 7pm by argument is the status quo.
3. **Permission intersection**: Door staff act on the Operator's authority but are not the Operator. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — door staff are an Operator sub-role, not a persona (see parent domain Q-03).
4. **Notification fan-out**: A door add against the Musician's allocation must reach the Musician — not tomorrow, but as it happens. They are usually 30 feet away in a dressing room, not unreachable.
5. **State transition conflict**: Door adds race the box office close (CX-03).

### CX-03: Door-Time Changes ↔ Comp Issuance

**Relationship**: Comps issued at the door land on the live count while the box office is being closed and settlement is being computed from that same count. A comp issued at 20:14 and a statement certified at 20:15 disagree by one person and one seat — and the disagreement is discovered weeks later, if ever.

**Role scoping**: **Operator** issues and certifies (a conflict of interest worth naming); **Musician** is the counterparty whose money the discrepancy touches; **Fan** unaffected; **Producer** unaffected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The live count (19.05.01). Both this feature and the close (19.05.05) write to and read from it.
2. **Trigger chain**: Close freezes the count. A door add after the freeze must either be rejected or force a re-certification — silently mutating a certified statement is the failure the certified statement exists to prevent.
3. **Permission intersection**: The right to add a guest and the right to certify a statement should not sit with the same person unchecked. `[PENDING]`
4. **Notification fan-out**: Any post-close mutation must notify every party to the settlement.
5. **State transition conflict**: This is the race. See 19.05.05 for the close's side of it.
