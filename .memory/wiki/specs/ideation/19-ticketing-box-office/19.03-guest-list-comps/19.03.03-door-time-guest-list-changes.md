# Feature: Door-Time Guest List Changes & Conflict Resolution

> **Number**: 19.03.03
> **Parent**: [Guest List & Comps](./19.03-guest-list-comps-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The 7pm argument. Someone is at the door who is not on the list, the deadline passed yesterday, the person asking for them is on stage in forty minutes, and there is a queue forming. This feature exists because that conversation happens at every show, currently resolves by force of personality, and lands on someone's settlement without anyone deciding that it should.

Named explicitly in the parent domain's rationale as *"a nightly source of door-time conflict"*. It is the one place in this domain where the product's job is not to prevent a thing but to **record who decided it**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Can add a name from their phone at the door **if** their allocation has headroom — the common case that currently requires finding a human. Over-allocation requires the Operator. Sees adds made against their allocation in real time, including ones they did not make. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | The decision-maker. Approves or refuses over-allocation adds, records who authorised and who absorbs, and closes the box office. Door staff act under this role — see D-03. |
| Fan | None | The guest sees only whether they get in. They never see the allocation, the argument, or the fact that someone just spent £14 of someone else's money on them. |

## Behavior

After the submission deadline (19.03.01), the guest list is closed and any change routes here. A door-time add has two paths:

- **Within allocation** — the party has unspent headroom. Add is self-service, instant, logged.
- **Over allocation** — no headroom, or no allocation at all. Requires an explicit Operator decision that records **who authorised it** and **whose settlement absorbs it**.

The second path is the feature. The system does not prevent over-allocation — Operators must be able to say yes, because sometimes the answer is yes. It makes the yes *attributable*.

### Happy Path

1. Guest arrives; door staff find no ticket for the name.
2. Door lookup shows the party they claim to be with, and that party's remaining headroom.
3. Headroom exists → door staff add the name; a comp issues (19.03.02); the guest is admitted; the party is notified in real time.
4. No headroom → the system presents the over-allocation decision: the overage count, the face value at stake, and the absorb options (artist's settlement, venue's, split).
5. Operator picks; the decision is recorded with authoriser, timestamp, reason and absorb party.
6. Comp issues; guest is admitted.
7. At close (19.05.05), over-allocation adds appear as a distinct, attributed line on the certified statement — not buried in the comp count.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Over-allocation add with the Musician not present | Someone spends the artist's money without the artist knowing | Real-time notification to the party, as it happens, not at settlement. They are usually 30 feet away — see DT-01 |
| Room is sold out; guest at the door | No inventory, so no comp is possible regardless of allocation | Blocked. This is the failure 19.03.01 should have surfaced days earlier |
| Door staff add over-allocation without Operator authority | The control that matters is defeated | Over-allocation requires the Operator role; door staff hold a narrower one (D-03) |
| Add lands after box office close | Certified count mutates after certification | Rejected, or forces re-certification with all parties notified. See `19.03-guest-list-comps-cx.md#CX-03` |
| Guest is genuinely with the band, band cannot be reached to confirm | The everyday case | Operator decides on the spot; the record captures that they decided without confirmation |
| Two door staff add the same guest (near-simultaneous) | The add is keyed on guest identity + party, so it is idempotent: the manifest holds one comp per (guest, party) pair. The second write resolves to the existing comp rather than issuing a duplicate — no second seat is drawn | Second staffer sees "Already added by [name] at [HH:MM] — 1 comp issued", not a fresh add. No duplicate barcode, no double-decrement of headroom or inventory |
| Same guest added by two *different* parties (each claims them) | Two distinct (guest, party) pairs, so two comps against two different allocations — a real double-admit and a settlement question, not a de-dupe case | Blocked pending Operator: the system flags the collision ("this name already has a comp against [other party]") and routes it to the over-allocation decision so one authoriser resolves who the guest belongs to. Long-tail split-billing detail: `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` |
| No network at the door | Adds cannot issue against a live manifest | Offline behaviour is genuinely hard here — an offline add cannot know if inventory exists. See 19.04.02 and Q-02 |
| Operator refuses; guest escalates loudly | Not a system problem | Record shows a refusal was made and by whom; that is the extent of the product's job |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Closed | Submission deadline passed | Party sees the list as closed with a door-time route |
| Door open | Doors opened | Door lookup active; adds route through this feature |
| Self-service add | Party has headroom | Instant add, logged, party notified |
| Decision required | Over-allocation attempted | Operator sees overage, money at stake, absorb options |
| Authorised | Operator decided | Comp issues; decision recorded with authoriser and absorb party |
| Refused | Operator declined | Recorded; guest is not admitted |
| Frozen | Box office closed | Adds rejected or force re-certification |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The product's job here is **attribution, not prevention** — record who authorised the overage and whose money it came from, at the moment of the decision | Problem statement — *"the facts are reconstructed years later, from memory, usually at the exact moment they become contested and valuable"*; Producer persona — *"they are the person who knows… and they have no tool that records it at the moment it's true"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — and this is the thesis (D-18) applied to a £14 comp instead of a songwriting split. The structure is identical: a fact becomes true at a moment when everyone involved is present and friendly, nobody records it, and it is reconstructed weeks later when the settlement is short and the relationship is not friendly any more. A system that blocks over-allocation gets switched off by 8pm on night one because the answer genuinely is sometimes yes. A system that says *"yes — and this went on the artist's tab, authorised by Dave at 19:42"* survives contact with the door and produces a fact that ends the argument before it starts. Prevention fails; attribution works. |
| DT-02 | Door-time changes are an edge case of 19.03.01 and should live there as a state | It is the same list, after a deadline | ❌ REJECTED — the actor, the authority model, the time pressure and the money consequence are all different. 19.03.01 is a Musician spending their own budget at leisure, days ahead; this is an Operator spending *someone else's* money in ten seconds with a queue behind them, and it produces an attributed settlement line that ordinary submission never does. Modelling it as a late state of submission would inherit submission's permission model, in which the Musician can spend their allocation freely — which is exactly wrong at the door, where the Musician is on stage and the person adding names is not them. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Over-allocation is permitted and attributed, never blocked | A system that blocks gets switched off on night one, because the answer is sometimes genuinely yes (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Every over-allocation add records authoriser, timestamp, reason and absorb party | This is the fact that ends the argument; it is only capturable at the moment it happens |
| D-03 | Door staff are an Operator sub-role with a narrower authority; over-allocation requires the Operator | The control that matters is *who* said yes, and door staff cannot be that person for someone else's money |
| D-04 | Over-allocation adds are a distinct line on the certified statement, not folded into the comp count | Burying them in comps hides the one number a disputing party needs |
