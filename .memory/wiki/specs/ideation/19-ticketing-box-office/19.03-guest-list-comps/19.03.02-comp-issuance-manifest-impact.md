# Feature: Comp Issuance & Manifest Impact

> **Number**: 19.03.02
> **Parent**: [Guest List & Comps](./19.03-guest-list-comps-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Turns a submitted name into a real, scannable ticket, and records what that costs the room. A comp decrements sellable inventory and increments the comp count — but never the paid count, which is the number the door split is computed from. That asymmetry is the entire reason comps belong in Ticketing and not in a CRM.

This feature owns the accounting truth of a giveaway: 20 comps on a 300-cap room is 20 seats that could not be sold and, on a 70/30 door split, a specific amount of money the artist chose not to take home. Today that number is computed by someone with a calculator after the show, if at all.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the comp count on their show and — the part that matters — **what it cost them**: comps issued × face value × their split percentage, shown before the deadline while they can still change their mind. Cannot issue comps directly; they submit names (19.03.01) and this feature does the rest. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Issues, voids and reissues comps; sees comp count against paid count in the live view; owns the manifest impact. Can issue a comp directly without a guest-list submission (press, industry, a favour). |
| Fan | Read-only | A comped guest receives a ticket indistinguishable from a bought one and sees only that. They see no price, no "COMP" label, and no indication anyone spent anything on them. |

## Behavior

Issuance draws one unit from the manifest (19.01.02), marks it `comp`, and delivers it through the ordinary ticket delivery path (19.12). The unit is gone from sellable inventory the moment the comp is issued — not at the door, not at scan.

The count model has three separate numbers that are routinely and expensively conflated:
- **Paid** — sold, money received. The settlement number.
- **Comp** — issued, no money. Reduces sellable inventory.
- **Scanned** — walked through the door. Not the same as either, because comps no-show at a far higher rate than paid tickets.

### Happy Path

1. Musician submits a guest name against their allocation (19.03.01).
2. System draws one unit from the manifest at the price level the allocation is bound to.
3. System issues a comp ticket with a real barcode and delivers it to the guest (19.12).
4. Live count (19.05.01) updates: sellable −1, comp +1, paid unchanged.
5. Musician's view updates with the running cost of their guest list against their split.
6. Guest arrives and scans exactly like a paying customer (19.04.01).
7. At close (19.05.05), the certified statement carries paid, comp and scanned as three distinct lines.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Comp issued with no inventory left | Cannot issue — the allocation was a right to name guests, not a right to conjure seats | Blocks at submission (19.03.01), surfaced early rather than at the door. See `19.03-guest-list-comps-cx.md#CX-01` Q3 |
| Comp guest no-shows | Very common — comp no-show rates run far above paid | Scanned count reflects reality; the seat is gone regardless. This is why comp and scanned must be separate numbers |
| Comp counted in the paid count | Settlement is wrong in the artist's favour, and the venue finds out | Structurally impossible — three separate counters, not one with a flag |
| Comp voided after issuance | Inventory returns to sale if the deadline has not passed | Operator voids; guest's ticket is invalidated and they are notified |
| Guest arrives, comp was never delivered (bad email) | Name is on the list, but there is no ticket in their hand | Door lookup by name against issued comps (19.04.01); the record exists even when delivery failed — which is the whole benefit of D-02 |
| Comp on a reserved-seating show | Takes a specific seat; scattered comps damage adjacency exactly as hold release does | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Room charges a facility fee on a comp | A real practice — a "free" ticket that costs £3 | `[PENDING]`; see Q-02 and `#R-02` |
| Comp issued at the door after box office close | Mutates a certified count | See `#CX-03` and 19.05.05 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Pending | Name submitted, issuance in flight | Party sees the entry as issuing |
| Issued | Comp ticket created and delivered | Guest has a ticket; party sees the name as confirmed with a delivery status |
| Delivery failed | Bad email / no contact channel | Party is prompted to fix the contact detail — not the guest's problem to solve at the door |
| Scanned | Guest admitted | Scanned count +1; party can see who actually came |
| No-show | Show ended, comp never scanned | Visible in post-show reporting; genuinely useful to a Musician deciding next time's list |
| Voided | Operator voided pre-deadline | Inventory returned; guest notified |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | A comp must be a **real ticket with a real barcode**, not a name on a door list | Problem statement's root cause — *"no system is present at the moment of creation, so the record is always a reconstruction"*; Producer persona's *"capture as a byproduct, zero extra admin"* | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is the sub-domain's load-bearing decision. A name on a list cannot be scanned, so it never enters the scanned count; it cannot be reconciled at settlement, so the comp number is reconstructed by memory or by counting biro marks on paper; and it cannot be revoked. Issuing a real ticket makes the comp a first-class object that flows through scanning, counting and settlement with no special-casing anywhere downstream. The cost is that comps now consume inventory at issuance rather than at the door — which is not a cost, it is the accurate model. |
| DT-02 | Paid, comp and scanned can be one counter with a type flag, derived on read | Modelling economy — they are all "units of admission" | ❌ REJECTED — they have different *arrival times* and different *consumers*, and collapsing them is how settlement goes wrong. A comp is consumed at issuance (days before), a paid ticket at purchase, a scan at 20:30 on the night. Settlement reads paid; the fire officer reads scanned; the artist's guest-list cost reads comp. A single counter with a flag makes every consumer filter correctly every time — and the one place someone forgets is the place the artist is underpaid. Three counters make the wrong answer unrepresentable rather than merely unlikely. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A comp is a real ticket with a real barcode, drawn from the manifest at issuance | A name on a list cannot be scanned, counted or reconciled — it is the reconstruction failure in miniature (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Paid, comp and scanned are three separate counters, not one counter with a type flag | Different arrival times, different consumers; conflation is how the artist gets underpaid (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The Musician sees the running cost of their guest list against their split, before the deadline | The information is only useful while the decision is still open |
| D-04 | Comped guests are shown nothing indicating a comp | Indistinguishable from a bought ticket, by design |
