# Feature: Guest List Allocation & Submission

> **Number**: 19.03.01
> **Parent**: [Guest List & Comps](./19.03-guest-list-comps-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Each party to a show gets a comp budget — the artist gets 20, the support act gets 6, the venue keeps 10, press gets whatever is left. This feature owns the budget and the spend: who has how many, who they named, by when, and what happens to unspent allocation.

The budget is almost always a **deal term** agreed in domain 17 months earlier and then re-keyed here by hand, which is exactly where the artist's understanding and the venue's understanding begin to differ. That divergence is invisible until 7pm on show night.

The unit model is the load-bearing part: an allocation is a count of **admission units**, not a count of names. A named guest is one unit; each `+1` on that guest is another unit; each unit issues its own scannable comp ticket (19.03.02). "Jane +1" spends 2 units and produces 2 barcodes, because two bodies walk through the door and both must be individually countable — the fire officer, the scanner, and the settlement all count people, not names.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Owns their allocation. Submits guest entries (name + optional `+1` count + optional note) against it, sees their budget, spend and remaining headroom as a live unit count, edits any entry up to the deadline, and is prompted on an escalating schedule before it passes. Cannot see other parties' allocations, cannot raise their own budget, and cannot see the room's remaining sellable inventory. Full — not Config — because this budget is theirs to spend, not the venue's to approve name by name. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. A Producer who is *also* gigging the show experiences this feature through their Musician lens, never a Producer one. |
| Operator | Full | Creates one allocation per performing/entitled party (headliner, support, venue house comps, press), sets each party's quantity within the deal's terms, sets the submission deadline, sees every party's spend, and is the only role that can raise a budget or reopen a deadline. Also holds their own venue allocation and spends it identically. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this feature. A guest experiences only the ticket that arrives (19.12) — identical to a bought one. They are not told they were comped, which is deliberate: the guest does not need to know the artist spent budget on them, and the artist may not want them to. A guest who is also a WeJammin Musician still receives the ticket through their Fan/wallet surface, not this one. |

> **Access levels**: Full · Config · Read-only · Reports · None

## Behavior

An allocation has: an owning party, a **funding party** (whose settlement absorbs the comp cost — usually the same as the owner, but see support acts below), a quantity in admission units, a submission deadline, and a lifecycle state. It is **hold-backed**, never a free claim against future capacity: creation or a raise atomically reserves matching manifest inventory in the party's guest-list hold. Every allocation unit therefore corresponds to a held unit at its price level. Submission spends it: each entry is a guest name, an optional `+1` count, and an optional note (press outlet, plus-one relationship). Each unit in an entry issues a real comp ticket (19.03.02) by moving its bound unit from `held` to `comp`.

**Spend is atomic per entry.** An entry consumes `1 + (+1 count)` units. If the entry would breach remaining headroom, the *whole entry* is rejected — there is no partial spend. A half-seated `+1` is a person turned away at the door, which is the exact failure this feature exists to prevent.

**The server is the authority on headroom, always.** The client's headroom display is optimistic and can be stale (another device, an Operator raise, an Operator lowering). Every submission is validated against server-side allocation and bound-hold headroom at write time and rejected if it breaches, regardless of what the client believed. Public sale cannot consume a bound hold.

**Submission is idempotent.** Each submit carries a client-generated idempotency key so a retried request after a network timeout does not double-spend the budget or double-issue tickets.

Unspent allocation at the deadline releases its matching held units to sellable inventory — this is the same design call as hold auto-release in 19.01.02, and it exists for the same reason. It returns to **sale**, not to a venue comp pool: the deal granted those units to the party, and silently repurposing them would invert the deal's intent (see DT-05).

**Support acts and multi-party bills.** The Operator can create an allocation for *any* entitled party, not only the headliner. Each allocation names its funding party explicitly, so the settlement knows whose money a support act's comps came from. A support act is a Musician who is not *the* deal's Musician; they spend their allocation through the identical UX. What remains open is the *default* funder for a support allocation and whether a flat-fee support act can hold a settlement-bearing allocation at all — that needs the multi-party bill model (Q-02), but the per-party, explicitly-funded structure is settled here (D-06).

### Happy Path

1. Deal in domain 17 grants the artist 20 comps; the Operator atomically reserves 20 manifest units into the artist hold and binds the artist allocation to it, funder = artist. If support is billed, the same transaction shape creates a 6-unit support hold-backed allocation funded per the bill's terms.
2. Operator sets the submission deadline — typically 24–48h before doors, so the room can sell anything unspent.
3. Musician sees their allocation on the show — budget 20, spent 0, headroom 20 units — and submits entries as they accumulate: partner (`+0`, 1 unit), the drummer's brother (`+0`, 1 unit), two journalists (`+0` each), the promoter from the next town (`+1`, 2 units).
4. System validates each entry against server headroom, issues a comp ticket per unit (19.03.02), and delivers each to its guest.
5. As the deadline approaches, the system prompts the Musician on an escalating schedule (T-48h informational, T-12h actionable, T-2h final): "8 units unspent, deadline in 2 hours, unspent returns to sale."
6. At the deadline, unspent units return to sellable inventory automatically; the Musician's allocation locks.
7. Door works from a list where every name — and every `+1` — has a real, scannable ticket.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Entry exceeds remaining headroom (over-submission) | Blocked at the budget — atomic, no partial spend. Over-submission is where the door conflict is manufactured | Rejected as ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED, showing units-needed vs units-remaining, with a request-more route to the Operator |
| `+1` count exceeds headroom on the last entry (partial data) | Entry is atomic; the `+1` is not half-seated. Blocked | ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED; the form offers "drop the `+1`" (submits the named guest alone if 1 unit fits) or "request more" |
| Concurrency: two devices submit against one allocation at once | Server headroom is authoritative; whichever write lands second and would breach is rejected even though its client showed headroom | The losing device is rejected as ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED and refreshes to the true remaining count |
| Race: allocation creation or raise vs a public sale for the last unreserved unit | The two mutations serialise on the manifest. Paid sale wins by default; the requested hold-backed allocation or raise fails before it can promise capacity | `HOLD_NO_INVENTORY` at allocation creation or raise. Once an allocation exists, public sale cannot consume its held units. |
| No allocation set for the party (empty) | Not a blank screen — the party is entitled but unbudgeted | Musician sees "No guest-list budget set for this show" with a route to request one from the Operator; Operator sees a prompt pre-filled with the deal's comp terms from domain 17 if available |
| Deal says 20, Operator keyed 10 | Silent divergence until the Musician tries to submit the 11th unit | ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED on unit 11. Root cause is re-keying; see Q-01. The Musician sees a cap they did not agree to and no explanation of why |
| Permission: Musician tries to raise their own budget | Rejected — only the Operator raises | FORBIDDEN_RAISE; the UI shows the request-more route instead |
| Permission: Musician tries to view another party's allocation or spend | Hidden entirely — the API returns only the caller's own allocation | Other parties' budgets never appear in any Musician response |
| Network failure: submit request times out | Entry is not confirmed and no units are spent. The idempotency key makes a retry safe | Entry shows as `unsent` (not `issued`) with a retry action; a retry of the same key never double-spends |
| Malicious input: scripted flood of submissions to drain inventory or harass | The hard server-side unit cap makes over-spend impossible; the submit path is rate-limited (stricter for the guest-list surface); each entry is validated | Excess requests are throttled (rate-limited); nothing beyond the allocation's units can ever be issued |
| Malicious input: guest name is a script payload or 10k characters | Name is length-capped (≤ 200 chars) and escaped everywhere it renders — door list, Operator spend view, notifications | INVALID_NAME on overflow; no markup ever executes |
| Duplicate guest: same name **and** contact submitted twice against one allocation | Flagged as a probable duplicate; the second issuance requires an explicit confirm to prevent accidental double-spend | "You already comped this guest — comp again?" If the contact differs, it is allowed silently (two real people can share a name) |
| Multi-hyphenate: one human is both the Musician **and** the Operator (artist runs the night) | Authority is role-scoped, not person-scoped. They may raise the budget, but the raise executes as an **Operator** action and is recorded/attributed as such — a self-raise is still an auditable Operator decision, never a silent bump | The raise appears in the Operator audit trail with authoriser = themselves |
| Multi-hyphenate: one human plays two bands on the same bill (headliner + support), holding two allocations | The two allocations are independent budgets. Submitting the same guest against both issues two comps (two seats) unless caught | A cross-allocation duplicate warning fires when the same name+contact is submitted against a second allocation the same human owns (D-05) |
| Guest name submitted with no contact detail (partial data) | Entry is accepted and the record exists, but no unit can be delivered — routes to 19.03.02 delivery-failed | Entry shows `delivery-pending`; the party is prompted to add a channel. Door lookup by name still works because the record exists — the benefit of D-02 |
| Support act's allocation | The Operator creates a per-party allocation with an explicit funder; the support Musician spends it identically | Default funder and flat-fee-support entitlement remain a product/model decision — see Q-02. The *structure* is specified: per-party, explicitly funded (D-06) |
| Operator lowers an allocation below what is already spent | Rejected — the floor is the current spend. To reclaim, comps must first be voided (19.03.02) | BELOW_SPENT, showing spent-count as the floor |
| Musician submits an entry after the deadline | Deadline passed; the allocation may already have returned to sale | DEADLINE_PASSED; routes to 19.03.03 (door-time changes) — the honest place for it |
| Operator reopens a deadline after auto-return | The budget number is restored but the returned units may already be sold | Reopening surfaces as allocation-without-inventory (Blocked state) if the room has since sold through |
| Public sellable inventory sold out; party has unspent allocation | The allocation's units remain held for that party until its deadline | Party continues to submit against its held headroom. Only after release can those units enter public sale. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fetching the allocation and spend for the show | Skeleton of the budget bar (budget / spent / headroom) |
| Empty (Not allocated) | Show has no guest-list budget for this party | Musician: request-a-budget route. Operator: prompt pre-filled with the deal's domain-17 comp terms if available |
| Open (Populated) | Allocation set, deadline in future | Party sees budget, spend, headroom in units, deadline, and a submit form |
| Deadline approaching | Escalating thresholds before deadline (T-48h / T-12h / T-2h) | Party is prompted with the unspent unit count and the consequence (returns to sale) |
| Partial | Some entries are `delivery-pending` (missing/bad contact) | Entries list with per-entry delivery badges; a prompt to add contact details so the ticket can reach the guest |
| Error | Submit failed (headroom, network) | Inline error keyed to the code (`ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`, timeout) with a retry-safe retry action. Inventory is reserved before an allocation opens. |
| Exhausted | Party spent their full allocation in units | Submit is closed; request-more route offered |
| Closed | Deadline passed | Submissions closed; unspent returned to sale; changes route to 19.03.03 |
| Blocked | Allocation creation, raise, or reopen cannot reserve matching held inventory | Party sees plainly that the requested budget cannot be promised; Operator sees it too, early |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Guest list needs a **submission deadline with automatic return of unspent allocation**, mirroring hold auto-release in 19.01.02 | Operator anti-persona — *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold"*; Producer anti-persona principle — *"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — an open-ended guest list is a hold by another name, and it fails identically: 14 unspent comps on a show that sells out at 95% is money nobody decided to lose. The deadline must default to hours-before-doors rather than doors, because inventory returned at doors cannot realistically be sold. Note this is the *second* place in the domain where the same pattern appears; the shared shape (a party holds inventory, forgets, and the room loses money) suggests a common mechanism rather than two implementations. |
| DT-02 | The guest list is a social/CRM object and belongs near Community (03) or Fanbase (20) | The list is literally a list of people the artist knows | ❌ REJECTED — a comp changes the settlement. Every comp is a unit of inventory removed from the paid count that the door split is computed on, which makes it a box-office object with a social wrapper, not a social object with a box-office side-effect. Placing it in Community would put the money consequence one domain away from the money, which is how it gets reconciled from memory weeks later — the exact failure named in `problem-statement.md`. The parent domain's rationale reaches the same conclusion independently. |
| DT-03 | An allocation should count **names**, and a `+1` should ride free on the named guest's ticket | Naming feels like the unit of a guest list; "plus one" reads as an attribute of a person, not a second person | ❌ REJECTED — a `+1` is a second body that occupies a second seat, is counted by the fire officer, and is scanned separately at the door. If a `+1` rode on the named guest's barcode, the gate-observed `scanned` count — and with it the derived `admissions_total` the fire officer reads (19.05.01 D-09) — would under-count real attendance by exactly the number of `+1`s, and a `+1` who arrives without the named guest could not be admitted at all. The unit of an allocation is therefore an **admission unit** — one body, one barcode — and a `+1` spends one. This is the only model in which the split-counter settlement stays honest. |
| DT-04 | A single deadline reminder is enough | One clear notification respects the user's attention | ❌ REJECTED — the whole value of DT-01 is recovering unspent inventory into sale, and the person holding it is a working musician who is mid-tour, on stage, or asleep after a drive. One notification into that life is a notification into the void, and the unspent units it was meant to recover are lost anyway. Escalating prompts (informational → actionable → final "use it or lose it") match the cost of the miss to the proximity of the deadline. The reminder is not a courtesy; it is the mechanism by which the auto-return actually recovers money rather than merely being fair about losing it. |
| DT-05 | Unspent allocation should roll into a **house comp pool** the Operator can redistribute, not return to sale | The venue could paper a thin room with the artist's unused comps | ❌ REJECTED — the deal *granted* those units to the party. Returning them to **sale** is neutral: it recovers money into the very door split both parties share. Returning them to a **venue pool** is a silent transfer of value from the artist to the venue, and the artist would eventually discover their entitlement was quietly repurposed to comp the venue's own guests — a betrayal of the deal term, discovered late, which is the platform's founding failure mode. Auto-return must be revenue-neutral, never a redistribution. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Allocations carry a mandatory submission deadline; unspent allocation returns to sale automatically | An open-ended guest list is an unreleased hold and fails identically (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Deadline defaults to hours before doors, not doors | Inventory returned at doors cannot be sold |
| D-03 | Musician holds Full access to their own allocation, not Config | The budget is theirs to spend; name-by-name venue approval is a workflow nobody runs and would be rejected |
| D-04 | Guests are not told they were comped | The artist spent budget on them; that is between the artist and the room |
| D-05 | An allocation counts **admission units** (one body, one barcode); a `+1` spends a unit and issues its own ticket; entry spend is atomic (no partial spend); duplicate name+contact across a person's own allocations warns | Only the per-body unit model keeps the gate `scanned` count, the derived `admissions_total` and settlement honest (DT-03); a half-seated `+1` is a guest turned away |
| D-06 | Allocations are **per-party with an explicit funder**; the Operator can create one for any entitled party (headliner, support, venue, press) | The settlement must know whose money each comp came from; support acts are Musicians funded by someone else's budget |
| D-07 | Authority is **role-scoped, not person-scoped** — a raise or reopen always executes and is attributed as an Operator action, even when the same human also holds a Musician allocation | Prevents a musician-operator from silently self-raising; keeps every budget change auditable |
| D-08 | Submission is idempotent (client key) and the server is authoritative on headroom; over-spend is impossible regardless of client state | Retries after network failure must not double-spend; concurrent devices cannot both win the last unit |
| D-09 | An Operator cannot lower an allocation below its current spend; the floor is the spent count | To reclaim units, comps must be voided first (19.03.02) — lowering below spend would orphan issued tickets |
| D-10 | Deadline prompts escalate (T-48h / T-12h / T-2h) rather than firing once | A single reminder to a touring musician is lost, defeating the auto-return recovery (DT-04) |
| D-11 | Every guest-list allocation is **hold-backed**: creation or a raise atomically reserves matching manifest units; submission moves those units from `held` to `comp` | An allocation must not promise capacity that a paid sale can take. Public sale may win only while an allocation/hold is being created, never after it exists. |
