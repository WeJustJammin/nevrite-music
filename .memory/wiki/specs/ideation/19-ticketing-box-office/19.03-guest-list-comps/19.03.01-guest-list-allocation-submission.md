# Feature: Guest List Allocation & Submission

> **Number**: 19.03.01
> **Parent**: [Guest List & Comps](./19.03-guest-list-comps-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Each party to a show gets a comp budget — the artist gets 20, the support act gets 6, the venue keeps 10, press gets whatever is left. This feature owns the budget and the spend: who has how many, who they named, by when, and what happens to unspent allocation.

The budget is almost always a **deal term** agreed in domain 17 months earlier and then re-keyed here by hand, which is exactly where the artist's understanding and the venue's understanding begin to differ. That divergence is invisible until 7pm on show night.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Owns their allocation. Submits guest names (and +1s) against it, sees their budget, spend and remaining headroom, edits up to the deadline, and is prompted before it passes. Cannot see other parties' allocations, cannot raise their own, and cannot see the room's remaining sellable inventory. Full — not Config — because this budget is theirs to spend, not the venue's to approve name by name. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sets each party's allocation within the deal's terms, sets the submission deadline, sees every party's spend, and is the only role that can raise a budget or reopen a deadline. Also holds their own venue allocation. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this feature. A guest experiences only the ticket that arrives (19.12) — identical to a bought one. They are not told they were comped, which is deliberate: the guest does not need to know the artist spent budget on them, and the artist may not want them to. |

## Behavior

An allocation has an owning party, a quantity, a submission deadline, and a state. Submission spends it: each entry is a guest name, an optional +1 count, and an optional note (press outlet, plus-one relationship). Each submitted entry issues a real comp ticket (19.03.02).

Unspent allocation at the deadline returns to sellable inventory — this is the same design call as hold auto-release in 19.01.02, and it exists for the same reason.

### Happy Path

1. Deal in domain 17 grants the artist 20 comps; the Operator sets the allocation on the show.
2. Operator sets the submission deadline — typically 24–48h before doors, so the room can sell anything unspent.
3. Musician sees their allocation on the show and submits names as they accumulate: partner, the drummer's brother, two journalists, the promoter from the next town.
4. System issues a comp ticket per entry (19.03.02) and delivers it to the guest.
5. As the deadline approaches, system prompts the Musician: 6 unspent, deadline in 12 hours, unspent returns to sale.
6. At the deadline, unspent allocation returns to sellable inventory automatically.
7. Door works from a list where every name has a real, scannable ticket.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Party submits more names than their allocation | Blocked at the budget — over-submission is where the door conflict is manufactured | Musician sees their cap, their spend, and a request-more route to the Operator |
| Allocation unspent at deadline | Returns to sellable inventory automatically | Musician was warned; Operator sees the recovered inventory in the live count |
| Show sold out; party has unspent allocation | The allocation is real but the inventory is gone — the party holds a right they cannot exercise | See `19.03-guest-list-comps-cx.md#CX-01` synthesis Q3. Must surface **early**, not at the door — this is the failure this feature must catch |
| Deal says 20, Operator keyed 10 | Silent divergence until the Musician tries to submit the 11th name | Root cause is re-keying; see Q-01. The Musician sees a cap they did not agree to and no way to see why |
| Guest name submitted with no contact detail | No ticket can be delivered; the guest arrives as a name on a list with no barcode | Requires a delivery channel per entry; see 19.03.02 DT-01 |
| Support act's allocation | Support acts are Musicians but not the deal's Musician; their budget comes from the headliner's or the venue's | `[PENDING]` — see parent Q-03. Multi-party bills are not modelled at breadth |
| Musician submits a name after the deadline | Deadline passed; allocation may have already returned to sale | Routes to 19.03.03 (door-time changes) — the honest place for it |
| +1 count exceeds allocation on the last entry | Partial spend | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not allocated | Show has no guest list budgets set | Operator sees a prompt with the deal's comp terms from domain 17 if available |
| Open | Allocation set, deadline in future | Party sees budget, spend, headroom, deadline, and a submit form |
| Deadline approaching | Configurable threshold before deadline | Party is prompted with unspent count and the consequence |
| Exhausted | Party spent their full allocation | Submit is closed; request-more route offered |
| Closed | Deadline passed | Submissions closed; unspent returned to sale; changes route to 19.03.03 |
| Blocked | Room sold out, allocation unspent | Party sees plainly that inventory is gone; Operator sees it too, early |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Guest list needs a **submission deadline with automatic return of unspent allocation**, mirroring hold auto-release in 19.01.02 | Operator anti-persona — *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold"*; Producer anti-persona principle — *"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — an open-ended guest list is a hold by another name, and it fails identically: 14 unspent comps on a show that sells out at 95% is money nobody decided to lose. The deadline must default to hours-before-doors rather than doors, because inventory returned at doors cannot realistically be sold. Note this is the *second* place in the domain where the same pattern appears; the shared shape (a party holds inventory, forgets, and the room loses money) suggests a common mechanism rather than two implementations. |
| DT-02 | The guest list is a social/CRM object and belongs near Community (03) or Fanbase (20) | The list is literally a list of people the artist knows | ❌ REJECTED — a comp changes the settlement. Every comp is a unit of inventory removed from the paid count that the door split is computed on, which makes it a box-office object with a social wrapper, not a social object with a box-office side-effect. Placing it in Community would put the money consequence one domain away from the money, which is how it gets reconciled from memory weeks later — the exact failure named in `problem-statement.md`. The parent domain's rationale reaches the same conclusion independently. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Allocations carry a mandatory submission deadline; unspent allocation returns to sale automatically | An open-ended guest list is an unreleased hold and fails identically (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Deadline defaults to hours before doors, not doors | Inventory returned at doors cannot be sold |
| D-03 | Musician holds Full access to their own allocation, not Config | The budget is theirs to spend; name-by-name venue approval is a workflow nobody runs and would be rejected |
| D-04 | Guests are not told they were comped | The artist spent budget on them; that is between the artist and the room |
