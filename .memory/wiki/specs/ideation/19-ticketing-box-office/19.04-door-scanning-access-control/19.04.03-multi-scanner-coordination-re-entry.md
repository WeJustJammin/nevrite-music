# Feature: Multi-Scanner Coordination & Re-Entry

> **Number**: 19.04.03
> **Parent**: [Door Scanning & Access Control](./19.04-door-scanning-access-control-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Most rooms have more than one door and more than one scanner, and most rooms let people back in. Both facts break the simple model where a scan means "this ticket has now been used, forever". This feature owns the coordination — deduplication across devices and gates — and the re-entry policy that decides whether a second scan is fraud or a smoker.

The two are inseparable: **re-entry destroys the double-scan signal**. If a repeat scan is legitimate, then a repeat scan is no longer evidence of a copied barcode, and the cheapest fraud detection in the domain stops working.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. The door's internal coordination is not their concern. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Registers gates, assigns devices to them, sets the re-entry policy per show, issues re-entry tokens (wristbands), and sees the deduplicated count across all doors. |
| Fan | Read-only | Experiences it as: get scanned once, get a wristband if the show allows re-entry, come back in on the wristband. Never presents a ticket twice — see D-02. |

## Behavior

Each device is assigned to a **gate** (main door, side door, VIP entrance, accessible entrance). Scans carry the gate identity, which is what makes a refusal message actionable: "scanned at 20:14, side door" tells the Operator whether they are looking at fraud or at someone who walked round the building.

**Re-entry policy** is per show:
- **No re-entry** — one scan per ticket, ever. A second scan is refused. The double-scan signal is clean.
- **Re-entry via token** — the first scan admits and issues a physical token (wristband, stamp). Re-entry is on the token, not the ticket. The ticket still scans exactly once, so the signal survives.
- **Re-entry via re-scan** — the ticket scans on the way in every time. Simple, and it destroys the signal entirely.

### Happy Path

1. Operator registers the show's gates and assigns each device to one.
2. Operator sets the re-entry policy — for most club shows, re-entry via token.
3. Doors open. Fan scans at the main door; scan is accepted and recorded against that gate.
4. Fan receives a wristband — the token that says both "you have a valid ticket" and "your ID has been checked" (`#CX-04`).
5. Fan steps outside; returns; is admitted on the wristband with no scan at all.
6. A second presentation of the same barcode at any gate is refused with the first scan's time and gate.
7. Deduplicated scanned count is accurate across every door and feeds the live count (19.05.01). It is the **gate-observed** count and stays gate-pure: a walk-up admitted at the window never scans and is counted separately (19.05.01 D-09).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Same ticket scanned at two gates, both offline | Both admit; conflict surfaces at reconciliation | See `19.04-door-scanning-access-control-cx.md#CX-02`. Live detection needs peer gossip (19.04.02 DT-03) |
| Re-entry via re-scan policy chosen | Repeat scans are now legitimate; fraud signal is gone | Warned at configuration. See DT-01 |
| Wristband transferred to someone outside | The token is bearer-grade — it admits whoever wears it | The known cost of token re-entry; also breaks the ID check (`#CX-04`) |
| Fan loses their wristband inside | Cannot re-enter; has a valid ticket that has already scanned | Operator judgement + name lookup (19.04.01 D-01) |
| Device assigned to the wrong gate | Refusal messages name the wrong door; conflicts are misattributed | Gate assignment is confirmed at scanner open |
| Two devices at one gate | Normal — a busy door runs two or three | Dedup is per ticket, not per device; both must see each other's scans (19.04.02) |
| VIP gate scanning a general ticket | Admitted. Because gates are labels not permissions (D-02), the ticket is valid regardless of which door reads it; the gate/tier mismatch is recorded, not enforced | Fan admitted; Operator sees the scan attributed to the VIP gate. A tier-vs-gate mismatch report is a `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` reconciliation nicety, not a door-time refusal |
| Accessible entrance | Accessible tickets route here; companion scans alongside | See 19.01.05 |
| Show has one door and no re-entry | The simple case, and probably the majority at the beachhead | Everything above is inert; the feature must not impose ceremony on it |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not configured | Show has no gates registered | Defaults to a single gate — a one-door room should require zero configuration |
| Configured | Gates registered, devices assigned | Operator sees gates, devices per gate, and each device's replica age |
| Live | Doors open | Per-gate scan counts; deduplicated total |
| Token issued | First scan under token re-entry policy | Fan has a wristband; ticket is spent |
| Duplicate detected (live) | Same ticket, second gate, peers connected | Refused with the first scan's gate and time |
| Duplicate detected (post-hoc) | Same ticket, second gate, both offline | Reconciliation report; both humans already inside |
| Closed | Doors closed | Per-gate counts frozen; feed the close (19.05.05) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Re-entry must be handled by a **physical token**, not by re-scanning the ticket | Reasoning about what a repeat scan means once re-entry is legal | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the feature's central call. Under re-scan re-entry, the sentence "this ticket has been scanned three times" carries no information: it might be one person having three cigarettes, or three people sharing one screenshot. The system cannot distinguish them and neither can the Operator. Token re-entry keeps the ticket a strict use-once object, which preserves the double-scan signal *for free* while giving the fan a better experience (a wristband is faster than a scan, works with a dead phone, and needs no queue). The venue industry already solved this with a rubber band; the software's job is to not un-solve it. |
| DT-02 | A gate is a permission — a VIP ticket should only scan at the VIP door | Modelling instinct from access control | ❌ REJECTED for the general case, and the reason is operational rather than technical. Doors get repurposed mid-show constantly: the VIP entrance becomes the accessible entrance when the lift breaks; the side door opens because the main queue is out into the road. If a gate is a permission, every one of those decisions becomes a configuration change made under pressure by someone holding a radio — and when they get it wrong, valid tickets get refused at a door that is physically open. A gate is a **label** that makes refusal messages and conflict reports actionable. The one genuine exception is the accessible entrance (19.01.05), where routing matters to the fan, and that is routing guidance rather than enforcement. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Re-entry is via physical token; the ticket remains strictly use-once | Re-scan re-entry destroys the double-scan signal, which is the cheapest fraud control available (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Gates are labels, not permissions | Doors get repurposed under pressure; permission-gates turn an operational decision into a config change that refuses valid tickets (DT-02) |
| D-03 | A one-door room requires zero configuration | The majority case at the beachhead must carry none of the multi-gate ceremony |
| D-04 | Scans carry gate identity | It is what makes a refusal message and a conflict report actionable |
