# Feature: Door Age & ID Verification

> **Number**: 19.04.04
> **Parent**: [Door Scanning & Access Control](./19.04-door-scanning-access-control-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The second gate. A valid ticket says the money is right; it says nothing about whether this human may lawfully be in this room. Age-restricted shows (18+, 21+, 14+-with-adult) are the norm rather than the exception in music venues, because the licence that lets the room sell alcohol is the licence that constrains who may stand in it.

This feature owns the **refusal decision and its consequences** — not the age-assurance mechanism, which is a cross-cut serving domains 19, 20, 24 and 03 (and is already flagged in D-13 as a statutory duty scaling with the Fan population).

**Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep candidate list, which is itself the finding: age restriction is invisible to product thinking and is the venue's single largest legal exposure.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. The licence is the venue's and so is the risk. A Musician has no say in who is old enough to watch them. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sets the show's age restriction, sees it on every scanner, records refusals, and carries the licensing consequence personally when a check is skipped. The restriction is theirs because the licence is theirs. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the age restriction **before purchase** — prominently, not in terms — and again on the ticket. At the door they present ID or they do not get in. A refused minor is the Fan persona's named worst-accidental case. |

## Behavior

A show carries an age restriction sourced from the room's licence (domain 16) and adjustable upward by the Operator for a specific show. The restriction is disclosed at purchase, printed on the ticket, and shown on every scanner.

At the door, the check is a human looking at a document and a face. The system's job is narrow and important: **make the restriction impossible to miss, and record the refusal when it happens** — because a refusal at the door is a paid ticket that is not being honoured, and that is a refund decision with a policy behind it.

**A restriction is not always a single number.** The common all-ages case — "14+ with an accompanying adult" — is a *composite* restriction: an under-18 is admitted only when accompanied by an adult (18+), and that adult's ID is checked too. At Level 1 the restriction is therefore modelled as a small object, not an integer floor: a minimum age plus an optional accompanied-minor exception (minimum accompanying age; whether one adult may vouch for more than one minor is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]`). The scanner surfaces the composite plainly — e.g. *"14+ · under-18 needs an accompanying adult (18+)"* — so door staff check the right thing without interpreting a policy. Everything else in this feature (disclosure, refusal recording, refund case) is identical whether the floor is a number or a composite.

### Happy Path

1. Room's licence (domain 16) supplies the default age restriction; Operator confirms or raises it for this show.
2. Restriction is disclosed prominently on the show page — before the buy button, not in a terms link.
3. Fan buys; the restriction appears on the ticket and in the delivery (19.12).
4. At the door, the scanner shows the restriction alongside every accept verdict — the Operator does not have to remember it.
5. Fan presents ID; door staff check it; fan is admitted.
6. Fan receives the wristband, which now carries both facts: valid ticket, ID checked (`#CX-04`).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Valid ticket, underage holder | Scan accepts, ID check refuses. **Scanned-but-refused** — a state the naive model does not have | Scan must be undone or the count says someone entered who did not. See `19.04-door-scanning-access-control-cx.md#CX-03` |
| Refused minor asks for a refund | The real question this feature exists to answer | Refusal creates a refund case automatically (19.06.01). See DT-01 |
| Fan has no ID | Extremely common; not a system failure | Operator judgement; refusal is recorded like any other |
| Restriction not disclosed at purchase, fan refused at door | The venue refused a ticket for a reason it never told the buyer | Refund is not really optional here. Disclosure at purchase is what makes refusal defensible — see D-02 |
| Show is 14+ with an accompanying adult | A composite rule: the minor's admission depends on another human's presence. Scanner shows "under-18 needs an accompanying adult (18+)"; door staff verify the minor AND check the adult's ID; the minor is admitted only with the adult present | Both are checked; if no adult, the minor is refused and recorded as a *no-accompanying-adult* refusal (distinct from an age refusal). One-adult-vouches-for-many ratio is `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]` |
| Restriction raised after tickets sold | Existing buyers may now be ineligible for a ticket they hold | Treated as a material change; those buyers get a refund route. `[PENDING]` |
| Wristband transferred to a minor inside | The band asserts an ID check that did not happen for this person | Known cost of token re-entry; see `#CX-04` and 19.04.03 Q-03 |
| Age assurance at *purchase* rather than at the door | A different question, and a cross-cut | Not owned here — see Cross-Cut Notes |
| No network at the door | Irrelevant — the check needs no data | The one door capability with a perfect offline story (`#R-02` in the sub-domain CX) |

### States

The restriction/refusal model and the scanner-verdict UI lifecycle are both listed — the first is the fact being tracked, the second is what door staff actually look at.

### Scanner-verdict UI lifecycle

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Ticket scanned; verdict resolving against the local manifest replica | Brief "checking…" on the verdict tile — sub-second offline (`#R-02`: the age line needs no network) |
| Empty (unrestricted) | Show carries no age restriction | Plain accept verdict; no age line, no ceremony on an all-ages show |
| Populated (restricted) | Scan accepted on a restricted show | Green verdict with the restriction line beside it — a number ("18+") or a composite ("14+ · U18 needs accompanying adult") |
| Error | Manifest replica is missing the show's restriction data | **Fail-safe, not fail-open**: verdict shows "AGE: UNKNOWN — check the licence" rather than hiding the line; never silently treats an unknown as unrestricted |

### Restriction & refusal model

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unrestricted | Show has no age restriction | Nothing shown; no ceremony on an all-ages show |
| Restricted | Restriction set from licence or by Operator | Prominent on the show page pre-purchase, on the ticket, and on every scanner |
| Accept + check required | Scan accepted on a restricted show | Verdict shows the restriction next to the green light |
| Refused (age) | Door staff refused on ID grounds | Recorded with reason; refund case opened (D-01) |
| Refused (no ID) | Fan had no document | Recorded distinctly from an age refusal — a fan who was old enough but unprepared is a different fact |
| Refused (no accompanying adult) | Composite 14+ show; under-18 arrived alone | Recorded distinctly again — the minor was eligible in principle, the condition was unmet |
| Checked | Fan admitted | Wristband issued (19.04.03), carrying both facts: valid ticket, ID checked |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An age refusal at the door must **automatically open a refund case**, not require the fan to file one | Fan persona — *"the only persona with no professional stake"*, *"will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"*; Fan anti-persona worst-accidental — *"is a minor and hits an age-gated purchase or an adult venue"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the feature's reason to exist as a node rather than as a note in 19.04.01. A refused fan is standing on a pavement, upset, possibly a teenager, holding a ticket they paid for. Expecting them to find a support form is a design that produces a chargeback (a cross-cut cost), a public complaint, and a fan who never returns — instead of a refund that the venue was probably going to grant anyway. Opening the case at the moment of refusal costs the Operator one tap on a screen they are already holding, and it captures the fact while the person is in front of them. Same structural argument as the whole platform: record it when it is true, or reconstruct it badly later. |
| DT-02 | Age assurance should happen at **purchase**, so nobody is refused at the door | The obvious prevention-first design; and D-13 already flags statutory age assurance as a live duty | ❌ REJECTED as this feature's answer — though the underlying idea is sound and belongs elsewhere. Three reasons. First, the buyer is routinely not the attendee: parents buy for kids, friends buy in groups of six, tickets get transferred (19.09.02). Purchase-time assurance verifies the wrong human. Second, it puts an identity-verification wall in front of a checkout, which is a conversion catastrophe for a fan buying a £12 club ticket. Third, and decisively: **the venue's licence requires a check at the door regardless**. Door staff cannot show a licensing officer a database record; they must have looked at a face. Purchase-time assurance is therefore additive at best, and cannot remove this feature. It belongs to the age-assurance cross-cut where it can serve 20 and 24 as well. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | An age refusal at the door automatically opens a refund case | The alternative is a chargeback and a lost fan; the fact is only capturable at the moment of refusal (DT-01) |
| D-02 | The age restriction is disclosed before the buy button, not in terms | Undisclosed restriction makes a door refusal indefensible and the refund non-optional |
| D-03 | The restriction is shown on every scanner alongside every accept verdict | Door staff should not have to remember the licence |
| D-04 | Refused-on-age and refused-for-no-ID are recorded as distinct facts | A fan who was old enough but unprepared is a different situation with a different refund answer |
| D-05 | Purchase-time age assurance does not live here | The buyer is not the attendee, and the licence requires a door check regardless (DT-02) |
