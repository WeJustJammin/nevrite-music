# Feature: Offline Manifest Sync & Reconciliation

> **Number**: 19.04.02
> **Parent**: [Door Scanning & Access Control](./19.04-door-scanning-access-control-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Gets the manifest onto the door's devices before doors, keeps the door working when there is no signal, and reconciles what happened when the signal comes back. This is the feature that decides whether WeJammin can be at the door of a real music venue at all.

**The premise**: connectivity at music venues is bad, and not incidentally bad — structurally bad. Basements, thick walls, load-in doors round the back, and at doors-open a few hundred phones saturating one cell. An online-only door does not degrade in these rooms; it stops. This feature is therefore not a resilience nicety, it is the precondition for the sub-domain existing (parent D-02).

The feature owns three phases with different lifecycles: **provision** (get a replica onto each device, before doors, while signal exists), **operate** (validate locally, log locally, push/pull opportunistically during the show), and **reconcile** (merge every device's log into the authoritative count after reconnect, and report the conflicts that could not be prevented). The Operator is the only human who touches any of it, and the design goal is that they touch it exactly once — before doors — and never again.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. Replica management is not their concern and never should be. | Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Provisions scanners before doors (downloads the replica), sees each device's replica age, readiness state, and queued-scan backlog live, marks a lost device as revoked, and reviews the reconciliation report after the show. The only role that must ever think about sync — and the design goal is that they think about it exactly once, before doors, and never again. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this. If a fan is ever made aware that a replica exists, the feature has failed. The fan's entire experience of sync is that their scan is fast whether or not the venue has signal (19.04.01). |

## Behavior

Before doors, each device downloads a **replica** of the show's manifest: every valid ticket, comp and package, plus the minimum data needed to validate them and to run name lookup (19.04.01 D-01). During the show, scans are validated locally against the replica and each verdict is written to a local, append-only **scan log** carrying device ID, gate, and a device-local timestamp (19.04.03 D-04). When connectivity is available, the device pushes its log and pulls manifest updates (refunds, transfers, voids since the last sync). When it is not, scans queue and scanning continues unaffected.

**Reconciliation** is the process of merging every device's scan log into the authoritative **gate-observed** `scanned` count (19.05.01 D-09 — the window's `admitted` counter is not this feature's and never merges here) and detecting what could not be prevented at the door — double-scans across devices, admissions of tickets refunded after the replica's last sync, admissions of tickets transferred away after sync.

Reconciliation produces a **report**, not a fix. By the time a conflict is visible, the humans are inside the building (DT-01). The report's job is to state exactly what happened, with enough specificity to settle the argument and to price the loss.

**Readiness is gated on the replica being present and complete — not on connectivity, and not on its age** (19.04.01 D-04; D-11). A device with no replica, or with an incomplete one, cannot scan. A device with a **stale** replica is ready and scans normally; it raises an **amber** warning before doors, while the problem is still fixable, and **staleness never blocks** (D-11). This file is the single author of the staleness rule and its thresholds for the whole sub-domain; 19.04.01 D-06 references it and states only the scan-side consequence.

**Every merged log must be attributable.** A scan log is only merged if it carries a recognised device identity provisioned for this show. A log from an unrecognised or unprovisioned device is **quarantined, not merged**, and surfaced to the Operator by device identity — the merge is never silent about where a record came from (D-09).

### Happy Path

1. Before doors, Operator opens the scanner on each device while still in signal (office, bar, anywhere but the door).
2. Device downloads the replica; scanner shows ready with the replica's timestamp and the ticket count it holds.
3. Doors open. Devices scan locally with no network dependency (19.04.01).
4. Where signal exists, devices push scan logs continuously and pull manifest updates; where it does not, they queue and the queued-scan count is visible to the Operator.
5. Where peers are reachable on the local network but the internet is not, devices exchange scan logs peer-to-peer, so a cross-device double-scan is caught at the second door live (behaviour specified; mechanism deferred — D-03, DT-03).
6. Post-show, devices reconnect (or are walked into signal) and push their remaining logs.
7. System merges all device logs into the authoritative scanned count.
8. Reconciliation report surfaces conflicts: N double-scans, N admissions of post-sync-refunded tickets, N post-sync-transferred, N quarantined logs, N unresolved.
9. Box office close (19.05.05) consumes the reconciled count — and cannot close while any provisioned device is unreconciled (D-04).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Device never had signal, even before doors (empty replica) | No replica means no door. Scanning is **blocked**, not degraded — a device with no manifest cannot tell a valid ticket from a forged one, and admitting everyone is not a door (DT-06) | Blocking "not ready — no manifest" screen naming the two fixes: get signal and sync, or receive the manifest from a ready device nearby. Never a silent partial mode |
| Manifest empty — zero tickets sold, comps only | Valid state, not an error | Ready with count `0 tickets, N comps`; scanning works normally |
| Device dies mid-show with unpushed scans, no peer copy | Its queued scans are lost; the scanned count is short by the queued backlog | **Unrecoverable if the log lived only on the device.** This is the strongest argument for peer gossip holding copies (DT-03). Reconciliation reports the dead device's last-seen backlog count as an explicit gap, never as zero |
| Two offline devices scan the same ticket, peers unreachable | Both admit; conflict exists only after both reconnect | Reported at reconciliation, not fixed. See `19.04-door-scanning-access-control-cx.md#CX-02` |
| Two devices scan the same ticket, peers reachable on local network | Second scan is refused live | Refusal at door B in under a second (19.04.01 latency budget) naming door A's gate and time. The double-scan never happens |
| Ticket refunded at 19:50, replica synced 19:00 | Admitted per 19.04.01 D-02 (admit and reconcile) | Reconciliation flags an admitted-refunded ticket; the money conversation is the Operator's, not the door's |
| Ticket transferred to another fan at 19:40, replica synced 19:00 | The pre-transfer barcode still validates on the stale replica; admitted | Reconciliation flags a post-sync transfer so the Operator knows the wrong human may have been admitted on a since-reassigned ticket |
| Device reconnects mid-show and pulls updates | Its replica ages backwards; refunds/transfers since last sync now apply | Replica age resets to the pull time; no user action |
| Connection drops mid-push (partial push) | Push is idempotent and resumable; the log is append-only, so a re-push re-sends only unacknowledged records | Queued count resumes draining on reconnect; no double-counting, because merge dedupes on (device ID, scan record ID) |
| Replica download interrupted (partial data) | A truncated manifest is not a manifest | Device stays in "not ready — incomplete" and re-fetches; it never enters ready with a partial manifest, which would silently refuse tickets it simply failed to download |
| Non-Operator opens the scanner (permission) | A Musician/Producer/Fan device is not provisioned for this show's replica | Provisioning refuses; no replica is issued. Any scan log later presented from an unprovisioned device is quarantined at merge (D-09) |
| Malicious injected scan log during gossip/merge (forged or replayed records) | A tampered or replayed log would corrupt the count or oversell the room | Merge admits only logs from recognised provisioned devices, deduped by record ID so replays are idempotent-dropped; unrecognised logs are quarantined and surfaced by device identity, never merged (D-09) |
| Box office close attempted with a device unreconciled | Closing on a partial count certifies a number that is wrong | Close is **blocked** until every provisioned device is reconciled or explicitly written off with a reason (D-04). See 19.05.05 |
| Clock skew between devices makes conflict ordering ambiguous | Device-local timestamps disagree; naming a "first" scan could be false precision | Reconciliation reports conflicts whose timestamps fall inside the skew-uncertainty band as **"concurrent — order undetermined"** rather than asserting a first (D-07). The door-refusal message uses the scanning device's own local time, which is internally consistent even if cross-device order is not |
| Replica contains PII (names on tickets) on a lost or stolen staff phone | A lost phone holding names is a data incident | Operator marks the device **lost**, which revokes its provisioning; the replica carries a defined lifetime and is wiped no later than 24h after close (D-08). Which PII fields the replica must carry, and on-device encryption, are a security question, not a sync one → Q-05 |
| Door-time comp added offline (19.03.03) | An offline *write* to inventory, not just a scan — the device cannot know whether the seat exists | The hardest case in the sub-domain: it is a write, not a read, so admit-and-reconcile can oversell. Behaviour: the offline comp is admitted and flagged as an **unbacked offline write** at reconciliation, distinct from a normal scan, so the Operator sees it created inventory rather than consumed it. See 19.03.03 Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No replica (blocking) | Scanner opened, never synced | Blocking — "not ready, no manifest", with both fixes stated (get signal and sync, or receive from a ready device). Scanning disabled |
| Incomplete replica (blocking) | Download started but interrupted/truncated | Blocking — "not ready, manifest incomplete"; auto-retries the fetch. Never becomes ready on partial data |
| Ready | Replica downloaded and complete — **any age** (D-11) | Ready with replica timestamp and ticket count |
| Stale (warn) | Replica older than the amber threshold (default **60 min**, per-show override), or the device learned that inventory changed after its replica's timestamp | Amber warning **before doors**, when it is still fixable. The device stays **ready** and scanning is unaffected — staleness never blocks (D-11). The alarm lives here, not on connectivity (19.04.01 D-04) |
| Live, connected | Scanning with signal | Scans push continuously; replica stays current; queued count at 0 |
| Live, peer-linked | Scanning offline but reachable to peer devices | Calm indicator; cross-device duplicates caught live; internet-sink deferred |
| Live, offline | Scanning without signal or peers | Calm indicator; queued scan count visible. Scanning is unaffected |
| Reconciling | Device reconnected, pushing log | Progress; queued count draining |
| Reconciled | Log fully pushed, acknowledged, and merged | Device shows clear; contributes to close readiness |
| Conflicted | Reconciliation found conflicts | Report to the Operator naming each conflict and what it cost |
| Revoked (lost) | Operator marked the device lost | Device is de-provisioned; its replica is invalidated and any later log from it quarantines |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Reconciliation produces a **report**, not a repair | Reasoning about what is actually reversible at a door | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the honest framing and it changes what gets built. When two offline devices admit the same ticket, both humans are in the room. There is no rollback, no compensating transaction, no clever merge that ejects one of them. The system's job is to state exactly what happened, with enough specificity to settle the argument and to price the loss. Designing for repair produces machinery that cannot work; designing for evidence produces something that does. This is the same posture as 19.03.03 (attribution, not prevention) arriving from a completely different direction — which suggests it is the domain's actual character rather than a local choice. |
| DT-02 | Preventing cross-device double-scans requires the devices to reach the server | Standard distributed-systems instinct | ❌ REJECTED — it requires the devices to reach **each other**, which is a much weaker and much more achievable condition. Two scanners at two doors of the same building are 20 metres apart with no internet and a perfectly good local network between them (shared wifi, Bluetooth, an ad-hoc link). Routing every scan through a server in another country to solve a problem that is entirely local is both unavailable in the target venues and unnecessary. The server is not the authority the door needs; the other door is. |
| DT-03 | Devices should gossip scans peer-to-peer on the local network, treating the server as an eventual sink rather than the source of truth for the night | Follows from DT-02; and from the dead-device case, where a device's log is otherwise unrecoverable | ✅ CONFIRMED as the strongest available design — with a caution. Peer gossip solves the two failures that matter most (cross-device double-scan detection *during* the show rather than tomorrow, and log survival when a phone dies), and it does so under exactly the conditions the venue provides: no internet, good local proximity. The caution: this is a materially more complex build than push-to-server, and it is an architecture decision, not a product one. What this feature *specifies* is the behaviour — a double-scan at door B on a ticket used at door A is caught at door B, live, with no internet. **How** is `/create-prd-architecture`'s call, and it may reasonably decide the cost is not worth it at the beachhead. Recorded here so the decision is made deliberately rather than defaulted away. |
| DT-04 | A replica should persist on the device after the show, so a re-open next time is instant | Convenience instinct — caching is usually good | ❌ REJECTED — the replica is a bundle of ticket-holder names and entitlements, and a music venue's scanners are borrowed phones, shared tablets, and staff BYOD that change hands constantly. A persistent replica turns every one of those devices into a standing data liability for a show that is over. The replica's value is entirely time-bound: it is worthless the moment the room is empty and the count is closed. So it must expire, not persist — wiped no later than 24h after the box office close consumes its log (D-08). The next show gets a fresh provision; the cost of re-downloading is trivial next to the cost of a lost phone full of last month's guest list. |
| DT-05 | When two device clocks disagree, reconciliation should trust the earlier timestamp and name a "first" scan | The refusal message and the conflict report both want to say who scanned first | ❌ REJECTED for cross-device ordering — trusting an unsynchronised wall clock manufactures a fact the data does not contain, and the whole domain's posture is honesty about what actually happened (DT-01). If door A's phone is 90 seconds fast and door B's is 30 seconds slow, "scanned first at 20:14" can be exactly backwards, and it would be printed as evidence to settle an argument. Confirmed instead: within a skew-uncertainty band the conflict is reported as **order-undetermined**; only outside that band is an ordering asserted. The device-local refusal message is exempt because it compares a device only to its own clock, which is internally consistent (D-07). |
| DT-06 | A device with no replica could fall back to a permissive "admit everyone" mode so the door never blocks the queue | The fear of a blocked door in front of a crowd | ❌ REJECTED — admit-everyone is not a degraded door, it is not a door. It admits forged barcodes, tickets for other shows, and refunded tickets indiscriminately, and it destroys the gate-observed `scanned` count — and with it the derived `admissions_total` that the fire officer's capacity limit reads and the `admissions_paid` projection settlement reads (19.05.01 D-09). The correct behaviour when a device cannot get a replica is to make the *provisioning* robust — peer transfer of the manifest (DT-03), and a hard requirement to sync in signal before doors — not to weaken the door itself. A blocked scanner before doors is a fixable inconvenience; an uncounted room mid-show is an unfixable liability. |
