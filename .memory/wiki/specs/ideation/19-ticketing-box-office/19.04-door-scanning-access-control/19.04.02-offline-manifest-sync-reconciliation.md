# Feature: Offline Manifest Sync & Reconciliation

> **Number**: 19.04.02
> **Parent**: [Door Scanning & Access Control](./19.04-door-scanning-access-control-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Gets the manifest onto the door's devices before doors, keeps the door working when there is no signal, and reconciles what happened when the signal comes back. This is the feature that decides whether WeJammin can be at the door of a real music venue at all.

**The premise**: connectivity at music venues is bad, and not incidentally bad — structurally bad. Basements, thick walls, load-in doors round the back, and at doors-open a few hundred phones saturating one cell. An online-only door does not degrade in these rooms; it stops. This feature is therefore not a resilience nicety, it is the precondition for the sub-domain existing (parent D-02).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. Replica management is not their concern and never should be. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Prepares scanners before doors (download the replica), sees each device's replica age and readiness, and reviews the reconciliation report after the show. The only role that must ever think about sync — and the design goal is that they think about it exactly once, before doors, and never again. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this. If a fan is ever made aware that a replica exists, the feature has failed. |

## Behavior

Before doors, each device downloads a **replica** of the show's manifest: every valid ticket, comp and package, with the checks needed to validate them. During the show, scans are validated locally and written to a local log. When connectivity is available, the device pushes its log and pulls updates.

**Reconciliation** is the process of merging device logs into the authoritative count and detecting what could not be prevented — double-scans across devices, admissions of tickets refunded after sync, scans of tickets transferred after sync.

Reconciliation produces a **report**, not a fix. By the time a conflict is visible, the humans are inside the building.

### Happy Path

1. Before doors, Operator opens the scanner on each device while still in signal (office, bar, anywhere but the door).
2. Device downloads the replica; scanner shows ready with the replica's timestamp.
3. Doors open. Devices scan locally with no network dependency (19.04.01).
4. Where signal exists, devices push scans continuously and pull updates; where it does not, they queue.
5. Post-show, devices reconnect (or are walked into signal) and push their remaining logs.
6. System merges all device logs into the authoritative scanned count.
7. Reconciliation report surfaces conflicts: N double-scans, N admissions of post-sync-refunded tickets, N unresolved.
8. Box office close (19.05.05) consumes the reconciled count — and cannot close on an unreconciled device.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Device never had signal, even before doors | The catastrophic case — no replica means no door | Replica must be transferable device-to-device (see DT-03), or the venue must prepare in signal. `[PENDING]` |
| Device dies mid-show with unpushed scans | Its scans are lost; the scanned count is short by an unknown number | **Unrecoverable if the log lives only on the device.** This is the strongest argument for device-to-device gossip (DT-03) |
| Two offline devices scan the same ticket | Both admit; conflict exists only after both reconnect | Reported, not fixed. See `19.04-door-scanning-access-control-cx.md#CX-02` |
| Ticket refunded at 19:50, replica synced 19:00 | Admitted per 19.04.01 D-02 (admit and reconcile) | Reconciliation flags an admitted-refunded ticket; the money conversation is the Operator's, not the door's |
| Device reconnects mid-show and pulls updates | Good — its replica ages backwards | Replica age resets; no user action |
| Box office close attempted with a device unreconciled | Closing on a partial count certifies a number that is wrong | Close is **blocked** until every device is reconciled or explicitly written off with a reason. See 19.05.05 |
| Clock skew between devices | Scan timestamps disagree; "already scanned at 20:14" becomes unreliable and so does conflict ordering | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — a real problem for the refusal message in 19.04.01 D-03 |
| Replica contains PII (names on tickets) sitting on a staff phone | A lost phone is a data incident | `[PENDING]` — replica scope and retention is a security question, not a sync question |
| Door-time comp added offline (19.03.03) | An offline *write* to inventory, not just a scan — cannot know if inventory exists | The hardest case in the sub-domain. See 19.03.03 Q-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| No replica | Scanner opened, never synced | Blocking — "not ready", with the fix stated (get signal, sync now) |
| Ready | Replica downloaded, fresh | Ready with replica timestamp |
| Stale | Replica older than the threshold | Amber warning **before doors**, when it is still fixable. The alarm lives here, not on connectivity (19.04.01 D-04) |
| Live, connected | Scanning with signal | Scans push continuously; replica stays current |
| Live, offline | Scanning without signal | Calm indicator; queued scan count visible. Scanning is unaffected |
| Reconciling | Device reconnected, pushing log | Progress; queued count draining |
| Reconciled | Log fully pushed and merged | Device shows clear; contributes to close readiness |
| Conflicted | Reconciliation found conflicts | Report to the Operator naming each conflict and what it cost |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Reconciliation produces a **report**, not a repair | Reasoning about what is actually reversible at a door | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the honest framing and it changes what gets built. When two offline devices admit the same ticket, both humans are in the room. There is no rollback, no compensating transaction, no clever merge that ejects one of them. The system's job is to state exactly what happened, with enough specificity to settle the argument and to price the loss. Designing for repair produces machinery that cannot work; designing for evidence produces something that does. This is the same posture as 19.03.03 (attribution, not prevention) arriving from a completely different direction — which suggests it is the domain's actual character rather than a local choice. |
| DT-02 | Preventing cross-device double-scans requires the devices to reach the server | Standard distributed-systems instinct | ❌ REJECTED — it requires the devices to reach **each other**, which is a much weaker and much more achievable condition. Two scanners at two doors of the same building are 20 metres apart with no internet and a perfectly good local network between them (shared wifi, Bluetooth, an ad-hoc link). Routing every scan through a server in another country to solve a problem that is entirely local is both unavailable in the target venues and unnecessary. The server is not the authority the door needs; the other door is. |
| DT-03 | Devices should gossip scans peer-to-peer on the local network, treating the server as an eventual sink rather than the source of truth for the night | Follows from DT-02; and from the dead-device case, where a device's log is otherwise unrecoverable | ✅ CONFIRMED as the strongest available design — with a caution. Peer gossip solves the two failures that matter most (cross-device double-scan detection *during* the show rather than tomorrow, and log survival when a phone dies), and it does so under exactly the conditions the venue provides: no internet, good local proximity. The caution: this is a materially more complex build than push-to-server, and it is an architecture decision, not a product one. What this feature *specifies* is the behaviour — a double-scan at door B on a ticket used at door A is caught at door B, live, with no internet. **How** is `/create-prd-architecture`'s call, and it may reasonably decide the cost is not worth it at the beachhead. Recorded here so the decision is made deliberately rather than defaulted away. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Reconciliation produces evidence, not repair | Nothing at a door is reversible once a human is inside; designing for repair produces machinery that cannot work (DT-01) |
| D-02 | The door's coordination problem is local, not global | Two scanners 20m apart need each other, not a server in another country (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Behaviour specified: cross-device double-scans are detected live with no internet. Mechanism deferred to architecture | The requirement is a product decision; peer gossip vs server round-trip is not (DT-03) |
| D-04 | Box office close is blocked while any device is unreconciled | Certifying a partial count is certifying a wrong number, which is worse than certifying late |
| D-05 | The Operator thinks about sync once, before doors, and never again | Any design that requires door-time sync awareness has failed the persona |
